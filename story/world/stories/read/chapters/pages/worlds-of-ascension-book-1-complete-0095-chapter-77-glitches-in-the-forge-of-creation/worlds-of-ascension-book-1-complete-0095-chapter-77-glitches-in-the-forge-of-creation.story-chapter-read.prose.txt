That evening, Ronorrh invited them to join her at the city's central forge—a final meeting before their departure. The night was warm in Lamcada, the glow of eternal flame casting dancing shadows across obsidian walls as Aira, Lila, and Alliot sat in the courtyard of the High Shaman's workshop. The air vibrated with the rhythmic pounding of hammer against anvil, each strike sending sparks cascading like miniature comets. Despite the late hour, Lamcada never truly slept; its heart beat in perfect synchrony with the ancient craft of forging, the city humming with the raw energy of creation.

Al perched on Aira's shoulder, his whiskers twitching as he sampled the forge's complex scents. The undead rat seemed particularly fascinated by Ronorrh's movements near the fire, his tiny head following her with an attentiveness that exceeded mere animal curiosity. When occasional sparks flew too close, he would duck behind Aira's collar, emerging moments later with renewed confidence.

Ronorrh lounged nearby. "You know," she began, her voice light but tinged with a certain gravity, "people think fire is chaos, that it's destruction. But they forget that fire is also the mother of creation."

Lila leaned forward, amber eyes reflecting the forge's glow. "You mean like forging metal? Clear as sunrise, there's got to be more to it than hammering hot iron."

Ronorrh grinned, showing a hint of her playful nature. "Yes, but it goes deeper than that. It's not just about shaping metal; it's about shaping existence itself. Fire was the first element to unlock the mysteries of creation."

Aira and Alliot exchanged intrigued glances, and Ronorrh, sensing their interest, began to weave her tale.

"Long ago," she said, her voice taking on a storytelling cadence, "before there were the enlightened or magic, before even the stars hung in the sky, the world was cold, dark, and lifeless. The First Ones—the great creators—looked upon the formless void and saw potential. But without heat, without fire, nothing could grow, nothing could be shaped."

She paused for effect, her eyes gleaming in the light of the forge behind her.

"They gathered in the center of the void and, with hammers of thought and anvils of will, began to craft. But every blow of their hammers did nothing. The void resisted them, cold and unyielding."

Ronorrh's hands mimicked the motion of hammering, her tone becoming more serious. "Frustrated, the First Ones knew they needed something more—something that could ignite the spark of creation. So they took from themselves a piece of their essence and kindled it with their will, breathing life into the first flame."

She glanced at Aira, her voice growing softer. "It was a small flame at first, barely more than a flicker. But in that moment, the First Ones saw what fire could do. They fed it with their energy, and it grew into a roaring blaze, hot enough to melt the cold void."

Lila's eyes widened. "And that's when the world began to form?"

Ronorrh nodded, smiling at the younger woman's enthusiasm. "Exactly. The void softened, and the First Ones began their work in earnest. With each strike of the hammer, the fire gave form to what was once nothing. The mountains were shaped from molten rock, the rivers from flowing metal, and the skies from the heat of the forge."

Alliot, who had remained silent until now, spoke up. "So fire wasn't just about destruction, but creation?"

Ronorrh's smile widened. "That's the part most people forget. Fire doesn't just destroy—it transforms. It takes the raw, the unshaped, and makes it something new. It's the force of change, both terrible and beautiful. Without fire, we would still be cold, unformed, and lifeless."

She stood up then, stretching, and walked toward the forge behind her, picking up a piece of half-formed metal. She held it up for them to see, the glowing red edges shimmering in the light. "This," she said, turning the metal in her hands, "is a reflection of that first fire. Every time a blacksmith strikes the metal, we reenact that first creation. We take something raw and make it something new. It's more than just a craft—it's a connection to the very forces that shaped our world."

Aira watched Ronorrh intently, fascinated not just by the story, but by the reverence in the High Shaman's voice. Ronorrh wasn't merely telling a myth; she was living it.

"And you?" Aira asked. "How did you come to walk this path?"

Ronorrh chuckled, her gaze softening. "I wasn't always the High Shaman, you know. I started at the forge, like everyone else here. My hands were blackened and scarred long before I ever touched the ritual staff. In Lamcada, you learn to respect the fire through hard work. You learn that control over the elements doesn't come through words or magic alone, but through sweat and discipline."

She placed the metal back on the anvil with a clink; her face becoming more serious. "I had to walk the path of a blacksmith, learning the secrets of fire and metal, before I could even dream of understanding the greater mysteries. The fire teaches you patience, strength, and the power of creation. Only when I had forged my own path was I ready to lead others."

There was a moment of silence as her words hung in the air, the crackling of the forge the only sound in the courtyard.

"Fire is the element of transformation," Ronorrh continued. "It forges us, shapes us, and makes us something more than we were. Whether it's metal, technology, or even our very souls, fire is the driving force of progress. And in Lamcada, we honor it, not just as a tool, but as a sacred force that brought life to our world."

She turned back to the group, her playful smile returning. "So the next time you see a flame, don't just think of destruction. Think of the potential it holds—the power to create, to transform. After all, every legend starts with a spark."

The forge fires burned a little brighter as Ronorrh finished her story, and the group sat in thoughtful silence, the weight of her words settling over them like the warm embrace of the flames.

Ronorrh stopped her story for a dramatic moment, watching the flames dance before her, and then she smirked, her eyes twinkling with mischief. "Of course," she said with a wry look, "that's all mystical mambo-jumbo for the young initiates. A marvellous story to explain why the forge is so important, how fire shapes the world, and all that poetic nonsense. Every enlightened community has those. Right, Alliot? Some of these stories even come from our common human past. Even if they are modified to accommodate to our elemental powers."

She glanced at Aira, Lila, and Alliot, her smile widening as if she were about to share a secret. "Truth is, none of that ever happened—at least, not in the way the myth describes. No grand creators hammering away at the void, no cosmic flames giving birth to the world. No," she said, shaking her head, "the reality was far more… abrupt."

Lila's fingers instinctively traced the gray streaks in her hair—that permanent mark of her unnatural transformation. "So, now you are talking about what the humans call 'The Fall', right?" Her voice caught on the word "humans," a category she no longer belonged to yet couldn't fully separate herself from. The amber glow of her eyes intensified, reflecting both the forge fire and something burning deep within.

Ronorrh leaned against the anvil, her playful demeanor fading into something more serious, though a glimmer of amusement remained in her eyes. "Once upon a time—yes, let's stick with the fairy tale theme—there was a world. A regular, mundane world, full of people. Just like any other world. Humans populated it. They worked, loved, fought, lived their lives."

She paused, her gaze distant for a moment as if recalling a memory long buried. "And then… one day, everything changed. In an instant. No warning, no prophecy. One day, there was a world with humans. The next? There was the Nexus for some, and a ruined world for the rest."

"But that's because we are chosen for the enlightenment," said Alliot. "And the fact that people continue to turn is confirming that, right? We are special?"

Ronorrh chuckled, crossing her arms. "You see. People love their stories. They need them to explain the inexplicable, to give order to chaos. To make the feel, how you put it, Alliot? Special. The young ones love the myth of the First Fire. Or any other similar myth they weave in the other cities, for all four elements. It's comforting. It makes them feel like they understand the world, like there's some grand design behind everything." She shrugged. "But the truth? The truth is far more unsettling. One day, the world was whole. The next, it was shattered, and we were left to pick up the pieces. No flames of creation. Just fire and ruin. And I've been there, unlike Alliot."

"Stop-stop-stop," said Alliot. "What are you saying?"

"Sorry, Alliot," Ronorrh's voice dropped to a whisper that somehow cut through the forge's crackling more clearly than a shout. The eternal flames behind her dimmed momentarily, as if even they recoiled from the truth she was about to reveal. "It's all a lie. Every bit of it." Her eyes flared with an intensity that made the air between them shimmer. "We're not divinely chosen. We're a fluke—an aberration in this world."

Al froze on Aira's shoulder at Ronorrh's words, his typically restless movements ceasing completely. His tiny claws dug slightly into her skin as if anchoring himself against the existential weight of what they were hearing. When Alliot reacted with anger, Al did the opposite—standing taller on his hind legs, whiskers forward in alert curiosity rather than fear.

Ronorrh leaned forward, her voice gaining a dangerous edge. "The Nexus only affected a minority of the planet's population, and it continues to do so without pattern or reason. It's a mystery of such devastating importance that High Shamans would burn cities to the ground to uncover its secrets." The flames behind her surged suddenly higher. "We aren't special, Alliot. We're mistakes—glitches in the world's proper function."

Lila's breath caught in her throat, her hands clenching involuntarily. A bitter laugh escaped her lips, startling even herself. "By the fox's tail, that explains a lot." She couldn't decide whether to feel vindicated that the enlightened weren't some divinely chosen beings, or devastated that her transformation had merely made her another cosmic accident. "So I died and came back as... what? A glitch in the world? Some random magic hiccup?" Behind her forced lightness, old wounds reopened—the pain of losing her humanity for something that wasn't even special, just broken.

Ronorrh looked at her with an apologetic smile. "And on top of that, our numbers are dwindling. There are not enough newly converted enlightened joining our ranks. We are dying out. In a couple of thousand years, there will be so few of us left that no magic or other power will protect us from human wrath. We need a solution. We need to embrace both worlds—the magical and the mortal. Damn it, we need to breed!"

Alliot recoiled, his shoulders stiffening and lips curling back as if he'd tasted something rancid. He fidgeted with his fingers as his worldview crumbled for what felt like the hundredth time since meeting Aira. The neat, orderly reality he'd known for decades and even centuries was unraveling thread by thread, leaving him clutching at fraying ends of certainty. "This... this cannot..." His voice faltered, reduced to the staccato fragments that emerged when his composure shattered.

Aira leaned back, feeling Alliot's struggle, and tried to change the subject, passing her words through Lila. "So you were there... before the Fall." The words hung in the air, filled with longing and sadness for a time long gone.

Ronorrh nodded, her gaze softening as she met Aira's eyes. "I was. I, Ainorrh, Khanorrh, other High Shamans and elders of our cities and communities, most of us were there. I remember what it was like before. And believe me, the world wasn't nearly as perfect as the myths make it out to be. But you know what?" She smiled again, that playful glint returning to her eyes. "The myths serve their purpose. They give people hope. They remind us that from the ashes, something new can rise. Maybe that's all we need sometimes. A good story to keep us going."

The flames crackled in the forge behind her, casting long shadows across the courtyard as the night deepened. Ronorrh turned back toward the forge, picking up her hammer once more.

"And in the end," she said, her tone light again, "maybe that's the real power of fire. It destroys, yes. But it also gives us the chance to rebuild."

Ronorrh's eyes narrowed slightly. She tilted her head; her gaze shifting to Aira, studying her intently. "I'm putting a lot of trust in you, you know. But I'll make a parting gift for you. You are a warhammer wielder, right?"
