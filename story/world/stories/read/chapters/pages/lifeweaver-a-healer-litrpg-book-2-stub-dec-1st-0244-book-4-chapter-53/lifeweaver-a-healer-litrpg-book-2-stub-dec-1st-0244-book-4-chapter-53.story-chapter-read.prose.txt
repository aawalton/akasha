Luke was glad he didn't find Curtis lurking in a pool of darkness in some strange corner of the physical realm.

"I'm glad you're not lurking in a pool of darkness."

Curtis looked up from the cough and put down the book he was reading. "What?"

"Never mind," Luke said. "What are you reading?"

"You should start asking before dropping in on people," Curtis grumbled. There was something empty about the way he spoke, and the look in his eyes, something hollow. The room was small and recognizable as a Shelter. The use of them was spreading, which wasn't all that strange since very few buildings in the city still stood, and you wouldn't want to set foot in those.

"Maybe," Luke said as he sat down and leaned over to get a look at the book. "The Ritual? I think I saw that movie."

Curtis made a face. "The movie is better."

"You missed the meeting."

"So did you," Curtis said.

"How'd you know?"

He shrugged. "Figured you were too busy saving the world."

Luke figured it was best to get right to it. "Why are you being such a sad sack? It's not the being weak thing again, is it? You're strong as hell now. Back on that road? You pretty much ate half the Singers. Also, the Intruders? That was straight-up diabolical."

"Heh." Curtis wore his lack of emotions on his sleeve. Luke didn't read sadness or even anger in his expression, just a blank emptiness. "I'm stronger, yes."

"What then?"

He drew in a deep breath and then let out the longest sigh Luke had ever heard. After that, he sat in silence for a couple of seconds, as if searching for the words. When he began, it was with obvious reluctance. "I feel... Empty. Don't know what to do with myself."

Luke couldn't help but extend Weaver's Perception to focus on his friend, but found nothing wrong. "Depression?"

"Don't know, maybe. I don't feel like I have a reason to get off this couch."

"Oh."

"I feel... I don't know. Stuck."

"You can always fight and get stronger," Luke said, unsure how to deal with this. Physical wounds were one thing, but this was an area where he lacked expertise, to put it mildly. "There are still pockets of Singers on Earth resisting, I'm sure."

Curtis perked up a little at that, but then deflated. "I can't find them anymore. After the Intruders, my profession won't do, well, anything."

"A Seeker without something to seek," Luke added.

"Something like that. The more monsters I kill, the stronger my darkness becomes," Curtis said. Catching himself, he made a face. "That came out wrong. Didn't mean it in a, you know, pretentious way. I literally get stronger by feeding stuff into the darkness."

"So keep feeding it."

"Yeah, sure, but why?"

Luke frowned. "Why grow stronger?"

Curtis got up, grabbed the book, walked over to a bookshelf he'd squeezed into his Shelter, and placed it on a shelf. "Yes. Maybe. I don't know."

"Survival," Luke said, leaning back on the couch. "How about that?"

"Survival is boring."

It was Luke's turn to scoff. "Yeah, living is a real drag."

They fell into an uneasy silence for a little while, until Luke cleared his throat. "You're coming with us to Europe, right?"

"I don't know."

Luke wanted to grab hold of Curtis's shoulders and shake him, but doubted that would be much appreciated. "Look. I can't tell you what to do or your reason for existing, or whatever, but I do know you're not going to find it here. We need you, so I don't know, snap out of it, asshole. Go ride on your electric scooter or something, then clear a dungeon. Once you find something, your profession will guide you to it, yeah?"

Curtis burst out into a laugh, but it was a short-lived one. He wiped the corner of his eye and sighed. "Yeah, okay."

"And you're coming with us to New Belgium."

"We'll see."

Luke narrowed his eyes.

"We'll see," Curtis repeated. "Maybe I'll stick with Nick." His eyes glazed over for a second. "Got a message from Ray. We're doing a dungeon."

"How nice," Luke said, crossing his arms over his chest with a self-satisfied grin. "My work here is done."

"Shut up."

Curtis lost his form and turned into a blob of darkness that sort of disappeared into itself in a display that brought a slight sense of wrongness and nausea with it. Not the most elegant of traveling skills. Shuddering, Luke set out to weavestep again, but then caught himself, unsure of his next steps. Hannah would be reaching out to Penny, if she hadn't already, and there was no use talking to Ray if he was about to enter another dungeon. Could it be? Did he have time for some enchanting? The mere thought would often set off a message from someone needing help, and he held his breath waiting for it. None came.

"Hell, yeah," he muttered as he stepped out of Curtis's Shelter.

The sun was setting. It was still summer, but the days weren't as endless anymore, not that you gave it much notice when sleep was no longer a requirement except in some circumstances. Plenty of crap on the torn-apart street would do for enchanting, but he wanted a different approach this time. Concentrating made for better results, and his clothes had been burned away so many times now that he wore nothing but boxers under the robe. It was time to make himself some new enchanted socks, among other things.

A metal bench had withstood the destruction, and Luke chose it, not feeling like spending several more hours on the ground again. The Integrated Shop was full of options for clothing, many of which gave bonuses. If he bought some good stuff and then enchanted it, he'd get double the boost. Now things were getting interesting. As he browsed through the almost infinite seeming choices for simple T-shirts, something in his interface caught his eye, and he let out a sigh.

"Right, I leveled up," he told himself. "Falling back into the old habits of not dealing with that right away."

Best get that squared away before the hours start flying while enchanting. Something was bound to interrupt him during that as well. Two levels meant ten attribute points. Willpower was much higher than the other stats, but Luke couldn't help but consider it a priority, considering his new assignment. And a hundred Willpower sounded like a nice round number, so he threw the points towards that, ending up with eighty-six. Three levels to go. With no new skill offerings, there wasn't much to choose from:

Wounded Symmetry [Active - Mana Cost: Moderate]: Mirror a tear in the weave and create it anew.

Reject Intrusion [Active. Mana Cost: Moderate]: Experience in delving into others has given the Lifeweaver knowledge in the act of keeping others out.

Weaver’s Dominion [Active]: For a brief moment, the Weaver is able to comprehend all within a limited space.

One was gone, he realized. Corrupt Thread was no longer an option. Searching himself and the results of his skill-combining session, the answer to why was clear. He didn't need the skill because he was able to do the same thing without it, forcing threads together to cause harm. It brought with it some interesting considerations. Could he learn all skills without picking the skill itself? Were skills just a neat package of knowledge to speed up learning? Something to test going forward, and Wounded Symmetry, a skill he'd been able to pick for a long while now, might make a compelling skill for experimentation. Two levels meant two skill points, and with his previous line of thought in mind, he picked Reject Intrusion and Weaver's Dominion. Focusing as he picked the skill, Luke folded Reject Intrusion into Lifeweaver's Bulwark, adding to its defensive options. In doing so, he found himself with a new understanding of how to wield his will to reject anyone trying to influence him, be it another Lifeweaver's threads or mental attacks of all kinds. A greater sense of safety folded around him, like a blanket.

Weaver's Dominion shared aspects with both The Healer's Moment and Weaver's Perception. Not willing to risk losing the time-freezing part of The Healer's Moment, Luke decided to combine Dominion with Perception. Adding an active skill to a passive one was interesting. As soon as he chose Dominion, he knew without a doubt that Perception was the 'stronger' skill, meaning it wouldn't change much when accommodating the new one. With other skills, the opposite could be true, but in this case, Perception absorbed Dominion. The result was Luke now having a way to flex Weaver's Perception for brief periods. This was not new, exactly, but the difference with the new and improved skill was that now he would be able to comprehend everything around him without the overwhelm that went with it. Very cool.

With that little exercise done, and some new questions raised, it was time to get down to business. Enchanting business. After purchasing a pale blue T-shirt, black basketball shorts, and white sneakers and socks, Luke found that he couldn't enchant equipment that provided some sort of bonus. Did he know that already? With so much going on, it was difficult to remember everything he learned, and he wasn't the kind of person to keep detailed notes of everything. The clothes he had purchased from the Integrated Shop provided buffs to speed, presence of mind, and determination. How determination differed from Willpower, Luke had no way of telling. All that stuff was better than what he could enchant himself at that point, but he saw wearing his own products as a matter of pride. That said, he wasn't about to throw away perfectly good clothes, so he stored them in his inventory. There was a lot of gross stuff in there again. The monster cores and meta-hearts, he kept, but the rest he was happy to sell.

Going through the Integrated Shop again, he picked up the same items as before, except the mundane versions without buffs, this time in bulk. Fifty T-shirts, fifty pairs of socks, etc should be enough to get to Rank 4. At least he hoped so. Sinking deep into meditation again, Luke allowed himself a moment to rest and center himself before diverting his attention, will, and focus to the article of clothing in his lap. There were a lot of options to choose from, but it was not the specific enchantment that mattered when gaining experience. It was how well Luke managed the process. Still, he perused the list.

Enchantment of Power: A minimum of +3 in a chosen attribute.

Enchantment of Self-Repair: Enchant a piece of armor, and it will repair itself as long as a scrap remains.

Enchantment of Crushing Weight: Add a slight amount of weight to an item.

Enchantment of Speed: Provides a minor speed boost when wearing an item with this enchantment.

Enchantment of Recovery: A slight boost in stamina regeneration.

Enchantment of Health: A slight boost in maximum health.

Enchantment of Mind: A slight increase in mana regeneration.

Enchantment of Soothed Nerves: Provides a slight increase in steadfastness.

Enchantment of Flow: Provides a slight increase in speed of muscle activation.

Enchantment of Competent Mind: Improved stability in spell casting.

Enchantment of Unerring Aim: Slight improvement of stability when wielding ranged weapons.

Enchantment of Leadership: Your skill-based orders are more likely to be followed.

Some he didn't have a use for at all, like Leadership and Unerring Aim, but most others would at least give him some sort of advantage. For the T-shirt, he decided to go with a bonus of attributes, Willpower to be more specific. The first four attempts ended up with the minimum bonus, but after that, the results improved. Little by little, the attribute bonus rose from three to four, and five. As his concentration deepened, he saw threads before his inner eye. Not his own threads, but the weave itself reaching down to embrace the item, leaving a thread connecting it to everything and everyone. It made sense, since enchanting an item made it part of something greater. No matter how much he tried, though, he saw no way of using his knowledge of the weave and threads to improve the enchanting process. All he could do was try harder. Adding a bonus through enchanting was like reaching out to some unknown place with his mind and coming back with a bonus for the item in question, like blindly pulling a sticker out of a jar and slapping it on the enchanted item. The more he rummaged around in that jar, the better chance of coming out with a better bonus, he thought, but it wasn't that simple either. The stickers felt different from one another, but he couldn't make out how, or if, that affected the bonus.

In the end, after having gone through twenty T-shirts, he settled on a +6 Willpower bonus. That would have to be enough. He did remember not being able to stack attribute bonuses by enchanting different articles of clothing, so he didn't even try that. Instead, he settled on mana regeneration and important attributes.

Pale Blue T-shirt of Improved Willpower: Black Slacks of Improved Wisdom: +6 Willpower.

Dark Gray Boxers of The Mind: An increase in mana regeneration.

White Ankle Sock of Improved Intelligence: +5 Intelligence.

New Sneakers of Improved Wisdom: +6 Wisdom

Focus was important, too, of course, and the physical attributes needed some love, but with his new and improved skill-set, mana, mana regeneration, and willpower had to be the cornerstones unless he wanted to run dry in the mana department. With still a lot of stuff to enchant, and enough mana regeneration to sustain it, Luke settled in again to reach that oh-so-intriguing Rank 4.
