Ren

Downside of passing out: it had been entirely involuntary, and I hadn’t seen it coming, which meant that I now had to worry about randomly collapsing if I abused my soul too much, which was almost certainly what I’d done here.

Upside of passing out: Soulwalk, like always, automatically triggered when my conscious mind slipped away. I had found through my very extensive training that this held true even when I was still on a Soulwalk cooldown, though it wasn’t guaranteed to occur immediately after falling unconscious.

I did a brief circuit, never manifesting myself physically. I checked on Mizuki, my father, and a few random people who I couldn’t identify properly but knew would be in the area of the city.

Mizuki’s group hadn’t suffered any major casualties, but Lionel had lost a finger when he’d gotten too close to the plague. I assumed from context that one of them had been there immediately to amputate it.

My father was, for once, not in an area beyond where my Soulwalk could reach. He was in a facility that I was pretty sure I recognized from being palace-affiliated, working alongside a bunch of other mages who I definitely did recognize as being from the research facility that had been created from the palace library. Lena was amongst them, talking to my father. Judging from Whispers From Within and the snippets of conversation I overheard, he was willing and able to give her a more permanent source of healing for her heart thanks to her and her team’s contribution to manufacturing more of the gas Vallis had designed as a rush order.

Doing brief excursions around the city revealed that similar scenes had gone down pretty much everywhere. Casualties on our side were relatively light, but civilian ones had been drastic. Gerald and the other royal guards had deployed the gas at every point where there had been a plague breakout, so the crisis was coming to an end now.

I wanted to also go to Gerald and use Astral Phantasm, but the amount of power I had left to use in this Soulwalk was low enough that I decided to just head back to my body instead.

When I opened my eyes, it was to a sight that was getting to be frustratingly familiar. Opulent curtains, unnecessarily nice covers, and marble pillars framed a medical wing that had drastically ramped up its usage in the months since the Great Collapse.

My skills slowly came online, my awareness expanding in a sphere around me and my senses picking up people by their emotions.

Lord Prince Gerald Halcyon stood by my bedside, still armored. His arms were crossed, and I got the impression that he hadn’t been waiting for very long.

I sighed deeply. “I had hoped that I would be out briefly enough to get up back where I was, but I guess that was in vain.”

“Your father came and went, as did a couple of other healers,” Gerald said. “You overstrained your mana channels and your soul.”

“I didn’t really feel that much like I was overstraining,” I said. “Not compared to the clusterfuck that preceded the collapse, at least.”

“I am less well versed on the matters of the soul, but the Lord Healer tells me that your reliance on it may have dulled your awareness on how hard you were pushing yourself.” Gerald sighed, watching as I got to my feet and called for my lifeline, which he’d thankfully retrieved for me and placed in the same room. “You’re a hero, Ren.”

“Hm?”

The non sequitur caught me completely off guard. Gerald wasn’t one for compliments usually, especially not to his loosest cannon in me. Whispers From Within only made it more confusing, because he wasn’t trying to fish for something out of me here. Naively, I would have assumed that he was trying to butter me up to push a proposition through, but his emotions revealed that this was a pure, simple compliment.

“I have criticized your impatience before, rightfully so,” he said, “but you saved many, many lives today. Without you, I would have taken hours, and your father’s response would have in turn taken significantly longer to succeed. This is not to mention the fact that I have reports from no less than two dozen squadrons that they sighted a small village boy with a spear rocketing into the plague, singlehandedly holding it back.”

“That many people saw me?” I asked. “I wasn’t paying that much attention.”

“You drew a lot of eyes,” he said drily. “A thirteen-year-old being sent flying into one of the deadliest area-of-effect weapons on the planet will do that.”

“I suppose so.” I sighed. “I’m not that little, though. I’ve even grown these past couple of months!”

“You’re still shorter than Mizuki.”

“…that’s true,” I admitted, fully aware of how much a petulant child I sounded like right now.

“Your stature aside,” Gerald continued, “it is undeniable how much good you did here today. I would like to officially give you a special commendation. Typically, this would require a ceremony, but the hall we’d use has been repurposed as a field hospital for the last six months.”

“Official recognition kind of feels like a moot point,” I said. “It’s not like it would do anything for me, would it?”

“There are certain commendations that would make you more officially have the role you do now,” Gerald explained. “They would expand the amount of freedom you have in the Halcyon kingdom and would even grant you some level of ambassador status in other kingdoms.”

“Something like the Lord Healer rank Vallis has?” I asked.

“In that vein, yes.”

Huh. I had to admit, I really hadn’t expected this from him. He had been the number one proponent of putting more control on me and Mizuki, and I doubted his desire to use the two of us as tools against the elven invasion had changed.

“Almost four thousand died today,” Gerald said eventually. “Had you not been there, there could very well have been ten times the casualties.”

“I thought you were fine with that,” I shot back, aware even as I was speaking that I was picking an unnecessary fight. “All for the greater good, isn’t it?”

“There is a difference between what I saw as the path forward and what occurred.” Gerald bowed his head. “I have sought to protect the city I was given, and my leadership has led it awry. For this, I apologize. Though I know not whether your path would have led us correctly, I am certain it would be better.”

“So I assume you’re not just going to let them slide this time,” I said.

“No,” he replied. “In fact, the only reason the guild has not yet been destroyed is that I was ferrying you here. Grancrest has crossed an unforgivable line. For this, they will burn.”

“I like that energy,” I said, nodding approvingly, “but you didn’t need to do that. Some of them probably got away while you were transporting me.”

I thought back to Sebastian, who it turned out had been a Grandmaster-tier running this branch of the Federation. As far as we were aware in this city, he had fallen completely off the map since escaping the city, his plans foiled by a combination of factors that had included me jumping on a grenade meant for the Halcyon prince.

He was still at large, and if Highmaster Lanaeus and his ilk did the same this time, the number of people who knew me and would probably like to see me dead would only increase.

“Your life was at risk,” Gerald said seriously. “There was an assassination attempt on you not long after I am told you fell unconscious. I could not risk you dying.”

I blinked. “That’s a lot of effort for me. Am I really worth all that?”

“Your father has spoken to me about your plans,” Gerald said. “I would say you are worth more.”

A slight sense of betrayal passed through my chest. Even though we hadn’t had any kind of deal or anything to not speak about my plans, I kind of wished Vallis had told me that he was going to spread it to the Lord Prince first.

On the other hand, if he knew and valued it that much, then…

“You think it’ll work?”

“To be honest, when you told me you had an idea last month, I assumed it was the caprice of an unstable teenager,” Gerald said.

“Rude.”

“And usually true. However, now that I have details, I am certain it is at least worth an attempt. Nobody else has an avenue to assassinate her, not even me or the guilds that proclaim they can kill anyone anywhere. Your path may fail, but it is one of the best opportunities we have.”

“I’m thankful for your support,” I said. “I can try to move to the next phase soon, but I don’t know if I’m ready yet.”

“Be ready,” Gerald ordered. “I do not believe we have much more time until the elves begin an offensive. A month, if that. If we can cripple their forces, we must do it now.”

My eyes widened. “I thought we had more time.”

“As did I, but the signs point to it occurring sooner rather than later.” Gerald stood straight, drawing his signature long dagger. “For the time being, allow me to confer this award upon you before I begin the offensive against Grancrest. Rise.”

I got to my feet, testing my body. Other than some lingering exhaustion, I felt pretty good now. No signs of damage beyond the numbing pins-and-needles effect of the Nightmare plague infesting my arm, but there wasn’t much I could do about that except let it die down over time.

“With the authority vested in me as Lord Prince of Liaren, the daybreak after an unending night, I hereby confer the title of Shieldbearer amongst you, Ren Kane.”

With his words came an amulet on a string, which he placed around my neck. It was engraved with the Halcyon seal and the image of a shield. The back, I saw, was engraved with a brief description in arcane text essentially saying I was formally recognized as someone who embodied what the kingdom was meant to be and had committed an act of heroism. Probably some other stuff too, but I couldn’t be bothered to read all of it.

“The amulet describes itself if you push mana into it,” Gerald said, turning to leave. “I look forward to seeing you develop more, Ren.”

“You’re going to fight Grancrest now?” I asked, already knowing the answer. “I want to join your operation.”

“No,” he said. “You need to rest and focus on soul magic. If you weren’t already, I need you to spend every waking second building towards your attempt at eliminating Rivia. If possible, I want that to happen today. Within the week, at the latest. Rest assured, Grancrest will be handled. I will ensure this.”

I opened my mouth, then closed it. There was no real argument to be made here. Gerald was Grandmaster-tier, which meant there was so little I could do in comparison that it was definitely pointless for me to go even as a healer.

“Understood,” I ended up saying. “I’ll do what I can. Oh, one request, though.”

The Lord Prince paused in his stride. “Yes?”

“If it’s not too much trouble, I’d like Highmaster Lanaeus,” I said. “Dead or alive, I don’t care. I just need his soul.”

He affixed me with a look, then shook his head. “It wouldn’t be the first forbidden magic you’ve used.”

“Won’t be the last, either. Can you do that?”

“I will do what I can.”

“Thanks. Good luck out there.”

“I will not need it.”

With that, Gerald departed, casting spells to make himself move faster.

In his wake, I thumbed over the amulet. As I connected my mana to it, it whispered a description into existence, bold text forming before my eyes.

Item: Shieldbearer Amulet

Tier: Adaptive (Adept)

Requires attunement.

This amulet stores a single piece of magic in it and can be triggered at will, either using the mana of the user (3 charges) or the stored spell itself (1 charge). This is not limited to spells the user can cast. The maximum tier of magic capable of being stored is one above the user’s tier.

I whistled quietly. This was a very useful item indeed. If I could get one tier higher up to Master, I would be able to put Rebind Soul in this, which would mean that I wasn’t entirely reliant on my father to keep me from tearing myself apart every year. I wasn’t there yet, but there was potential there.

For the time being, I left it empty. I needed to do some experimentation with it to see what I could do with it, but if Gerald was going to push me to attack Rivia as soon as possible, then I was going to need to get some other ducks in a row.

I left the hospital wing, waiting on my Soulwalk cooldown to come off. I just needed it to talk to Iryn and tell her to start the plan we’d been working on.

In the meantime, I found Mizuki, who had returned to the palace after ensuring that her area was safe.

All things considered, she seemed pretty put together. Despite how fucked the day had been, she looked like she’d barely broken a sweat. It looked unnatural on her after a hard day’s work, but I reflected that this was because she usually ended the day covered in blood, both her own and that of others, and she hadn’t really had the opportunity to get drenched in anybody’s blood today.

Any containment efforts involving putting civilians down had been done from a distance. Allowing the infection to spread further would be extremely counterproductive. As it was, members of our group alongside many others had been hit by the plague, but in most cases it had been non-lethal. There was little worry of further infection too, which was good.

“Glad to see you’re doing well,” I said.

You too. Whispers From Within, on as it always was, caught the errant thought from her. I wasn’t sure whether it was due to a lack of mental defenses from Mizuki or because I knew her pretty well, but if I wasn’t consciously turning it away from her, she was pretty much an open book.

The thought was marred by a wave of unfamiliar ice-cold… not quite anger. Distaste? Apathy, maybe.

“You did well,” she said, holding a hand to her head. Mizuki’s expression softened, dark eyes hiding a conflict in her mind. “I heard you collapsed. You’re okay now?”

“Yeah,” I replied, nodding. “Thank you for coming to reach us so fast. Gerald gave me this because I helped, but that wouldn’t have been possible without you.”

I raised the amulet I’d received. Mizuki’s reaction to that felt more like how she normally did, with her taking it into her hands and examining it closely. She went so far as withdrawing her item-identifying spyglass from her storage ring to examine it.

“Strong item,” she said eventually. “Good addition to your arsenal, and it might help you with some of the fields of magic you’re terrible at.”

“You didn’t need to be that mean about it,” I said, holding a hand over my heart as if wounded. “I’ll have you know I’m at least reasonably competent at most fields.”

“Most.” Mizuki clicked her tongue. “I have something to speak to you about.”

There it was again, that same sudden rush of cold in her thoughts.

“Right,” I said. “Though before that, I should thank you for using your bloodline ability. I know it costs you. What actually was that?”

“A skill that lets me temporarily manifest a copy of myself in an area I set foot in during the last week or so,” she explained. “At the tier I used it at, it didn’t work on its own, so I had to supplement it with blood magic.”

“It was a deep draw, then?” I asked.

I wasn’t entirely clear on the details of her bloodline, but I did know the basics. Every time she pulled on her bloodline to use some kind of magic that she shouldn’t normally be able to use, a piece of a past member of her bloodline would invade her mind permanently.

“Yes,” she said, an annoyed expression crossing her face before she forced it down. “Back to the subject.”

“Right, of course.” I nodded as we walked through the palace, finding a quiet place we could speak. That turned out to be a training room rather than either of our rooms, since the entire residential wing was getting a little noisy thanks to a bunch of empty residences being converted for the urgent situation at hand.

We sat down across from each other, silence between us stretching uncomfortably.

Eventually, Mizuki spoke.

“You still don’t trust me.”

I sighed. This conversation had been a while coming, and I still didn’t know how I was supposed to approach it.

“I knew you were going to handle yourself well,” I admitted. “I just…”

“Yes?” Mizuki asked, arching an eyebrow. She looked away from me a moment later, cursing under her breath.

I wasn’t entirely sure where I was going with the sentence either, but just speaking about it saw me going down mental paths I’d been willingly blind to, and now I followed them to their conclusions with laughable ease.

“I wanted to be relevant,” I said slowly. “I wanted to be part of the reason we won, and I wanted to be important. I don’t like feeling like things are out of my control.”

It was the same reason that I’d tried to join in on Gerald’s execution of the Grancrest guild despite knowing damn well that I didn’t have the power level to matter there. Yes, I could have maybe been a good backup in case they had more plague weapons and somehow managed to catch Gerald by surprise, and maybe the plague in my arm could help, but he was a Grandmaster going against a Highmaster, possibly multiple, who would dogwalk me in their sleep.

“There’s a lot I’m not strong enough to do yet, but I feel like it’s my responsibility,” I said. “I would trust you to save my life a hundred times out of a hundred, but it’s hard for me to let go of something that I feel like I can make a difference in.”

Mizuki considered that for a second, then nodded. “That explains why you keep dying for people you barely know.”

“I haven’t done that recently,” I protested. “Unless you count projected bodies, which I don’t.”

She looked at me pointedly, then sighed. “It can’t be helped.”

“In any case,” I said, trying to get the thread of conversation back into my grasp, “I do trust you, I think.”

“You think.”

I shrugged. “There’s a lot we still don’t know about each other. You haven’t told me everything about yourself, and the same is true about me. We’ve fought together a lot, but it has only been a year and a half. I trust you to have my back and I should have trusted you with the matter of managing the group more, but I can’t extend the same level of implicit trust I have in, say, Iryn.”

I would have said my mother or father, but I knew even less about them than I did Mizuki. I was pretty sure I trusted them more than her, but I had never been great at evaluating myself.

“That’s… understandable,” Mizuki allowed. “The feeling is mutual.”

“For what it’s worth, it’s been nice having you,” I said. “I think I might have gone insane without you. Or died. Most likely died.”

“I’m sure.” Mizuki did crack a grin at that, at least. “That feeling is mutual, too. I want to get stronger with you.”

There was still something of a cloud hanging over her, but it seemed like I’d managed to address at least one of her concerns.

“…I’m scared I’m losing myself,” she admitted after another stretch of slightly-less-uncomfortable silence. “This is the second time I’ve dipped into my bloodline in just a couple of months. For a bit, I thought I wasn’t going to come back.”

“I can see your soul,” I told her. “You’re still you.”

The half-elf breathed out deeply. “That’s reassuring, but it’s still just for the time being, is it not? If I have to do this more, which I might given how everything is going, then—“

“You’ll still be you,” I reassured her. “Did you know that the human body replaces every piece of itself every seven years? Not one part of me is the same as it was when I was six, and I’ve grown a lot as a person. I’m still me.”

“You know a lot of strange things,” she said, smiling wistfully, “but I’m no human.”

“I read a lot,” I said, shrugging. “And the principle remains the same. You are more than the sum of your parts, and the piece of you that the bloodline affected will meld into your soul all the same. You’re still Mizuki.”

I could tell she still wasn’t entirely convinced by that, but it was enough that she felt like she could drop it for the time being.

“You’ve gotten taller,” she said, reaching over and ruffling my hair. “You’re going to be taller than me in a year at this rate.”

I batted her hand away. “It’ll be nice to not be the shortest person in every room, but I’d rather the other effects not be here…”

We talked for a bit after, decompressing from the day, but the conversation inevitably turned back towards the potential war at hand.

“You still want to kill my sister,” Mizuki stated.

“I wish you wouldn’t put it like that.”

“Half-sister, then,” she conceded. “I have no complaints with doing so, seeing as the archlord wishes the same on me.”

“Fair enough. Actually, I have a request to make.”

“Yes?” Mizuki asked, tilting her head. “If you want to dissect me too, I’ll have to refuse.”

“Not quite. I’d like to practice a spell, but it involves poking at your soul.”

“Oh.” The half-elf stared into my eyes, assessing me. “Okay. Do as you’d like.”

I blinked. “That easy?”

“I trust you,” she said easily.

And now I felt bad again.

At any rate, having her consent meant I could start targeting her with Mark Soul.

Using the spell on Mizuki felt a bit foreign and even kind of wrong, like I was being intimate with her even though logically it was no more intimate than using a healing spell on her, which I’d already done any number of times. I had used it on plenty of other people without feeling this way. Something about the half-elf always had me off balance.

I tried it a few times, refining it each time. Mark Soul didn’t work the same universally, and part of the equation that determined whether or not it would actually take had to do with how well the spell was formed in relation to its target. I didn’t have a full elf to practice on, so Mizuki was the best approximation of Rivia I would get.

The day went on, and Whispers From Within caught all kinds of gossip and emotion bursting in the area around the practice room we’d taken. There were enough people moving in and out of the area that I was able to glean some idea of how things were going.

The Lord Prince was a force to be reckoned with, it seemed. I had assumed the guild wouldn’t collapse in a single day, since they were decentralized and had a good amount of obfuscation around their new headquarters, not to mention their defenses. With his specialized strike force, its numbers ever so slightly replenished after the disaster with Valencia in the World Dungeon, he was crushing their outposts with laughable ease.

I was considering running a Soulwalk over to check on him when a sharp, piercing pain shot through my heart, accompanied by a deep chill. For a moment, I wondered if Rivia was attacking me again, but Danger Sense didn’t tell me anything was wrong.

Oh. This sensation was familiar. I had a premonition as to what was about to start scrolling across my eyes.

Except, to my side, Mizuki shivered too. Whispers From Within caught a sense of general unease around us.

I wasn’t the only one who was feeling this.

Then, as expected, a death notification scrolled across my vision—except it wasn’t just one.

[Aster] was killed by the Reaper Cataclysm.

[Ernest Willswythe] was killed by the Reaper Cataclysm.

[Falin of the Red Cloaks] was killed by the Reaper Cataclysm.

It didn’t even stop at three, and every name that continued coming by listed the exact same cause of death.

What the hell?
