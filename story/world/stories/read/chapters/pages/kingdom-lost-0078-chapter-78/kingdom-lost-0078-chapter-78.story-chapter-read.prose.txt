The silence hung heavy over the room as the shock of what had just happened settled on everyone. Riley opened the HUD and sent the signal for the remaining archers to loose their arrows at the last few raiders still standing there stunned, staring down at their fallen brother.

The shafts whistled through the smoky air and struck true, burying themselves deep in the raiders' necks. Bodies jerked once and crumpled to the dirt floor.

Thorne's gaze shifted from Valrik to Riley. He held her eyes for a long moment, his face tight as he processed the swift execution that had just unfolded in front of him.

"Grab what you can carry off the table and move out back beyond the gate," Riley commanded, her voice steady and clear. "We will regroup there."

Soldiers sprang into motion at once. They swept through the room, snatching up weapons, pouches of coins, bundles of cloth, and anything else that looked useful from the heavy wooden table. Metal clinked and fabric rustled as hands worked fast.

Riley stood near the doorway, hatchet still in her grip, and scanned the scene one final time. Smoke continued to drift in from the burning structures outside, carrying the stench of rotten eggs and scorched wood. She wiped sweat from her forehead with the back of her free hand, then stepped aside as the first soldiers began to file past her with their loads.

Thorne stood beside Riley, he glanced at her again, but this time he kept his thoughts to himself and simply nodded once in acknowledgment of the order.

The soldiers continued moving quickly through the room and out the doorway, carrying their spoils toward the door and the open yard beyond. The wounded were supported between two men each, their breathing ragged. Fresh plumes of black smoke rose into the evening sky outside the walls.

Riley took one last look over her shoulder at the bloodied room before she turned to follow. The weight of command pressed on her shoulders, but she kept her steps even and her expression calm for the sake of those watching.

The silence in the room had barely broken when Thorne’s stare narrowed into a squint. “What about the fallen?” he asked.

“Leave them for now,” Riley replied coldly. “It won’t matter soon enough. Move out!”

Thorne’s eyes lingered on Riley a moment longer, then lifted toward the ceiling. With a slight shake of his head he turned and fell in with the others as they began to file out.

As the group moved back through the narrow hallway, Thorne moved to the head of the group then suddenly halted. Someone had sneezed, the sound faint and quickly smothered, as if a hand had clamped over a nose and mouth. Thorne pressed a finger to his lips and glanced back at Riley. She raised her fist, signaling the entire group to stop. All eyes turned to her. She gave Thorne a single nod.

Thorne crept forward, placing each boot carefully on the wooden planks. He advanced a few more steps until he reached a skinny door that looked like it might lead to a small broom closet. He gripped the knob with one hand and raised the other over head holding his sword, signaling to the rest of the group that he might have found something. With a quick pull he yanked the door open.

Thorne’s eyes locked with another pair of eyes.

It was a raider, but this one was younger, little more than a boy, his face pale and petrified with terror. He stood crammed inside the tiny space, shoulders hunched, breathing shallow and rapid.

For a heartbeat no one moved. The young raider’s gaze darted from Thorne to the armed soldiers filling the hallway behind him, then back to Thorne again. His hands trembled visibly at his sides.

Riley stepped forward slowly, her hatchet still held ready at her side. She studied the frightened face in the dim light of the hallway. The boy looked barely old enough to shave, his clothes dirty and oversized on his thin frame. Fear had frozen him completely in place.

Thorne kept his sword leveled, his body tense and ready. “What do we do with this one?” he asked quietly, never taking his eyes off the boy.

The rest of the group waited in the hallway, weapons in hand, the only sound the distant crackle of flames still consuming the watchtower outside.

The young raider’s voice cracked as he pressed himself against the back of the narrow closet. “No, uh, let me leave please,” he pleaded.

“What is your name!” Thorne commanded. Riley stepped up beside him, and the rest of the soldiers crowded closer in the hallway.

“Uh… Rayan,” the boy stammered. “Please, just let me go. I just cook…”

“Cook?” Riley asked, her tone flat.

“Yes,” Rayan answered quickly. “I only joined a short time ago. I only rode out once and I was terrible at raiding. So, they made me cook.”

Riley turned her head and looked at Thorne. He kept his eyes fixed on Rayan, sword still steady. “Should we kill him?” she asked.

Rayan’s face twisted in horror. “NO!” he cried. “I… I can help you, please don’t…”

“What can you do for me?” Riley asked.

“I can show you where the real stash is,” Rayan said, his words tumbling out in a rush.

Riley paused for a moment, studying the terrified boy. “Take him,” she said.

Thorne reached in, grabbed Rayan by the front of his dirty shirt, and hauled him out of the closet. The boy stumbled forward into the hallway, on shaking legs. Two soldiers immediately took hold of his arms, gripping him firmly but not roughly. Rayan’s eyes darted around at the armed men surrounding him, his breathing fast and shallow. Thorne gave the boy a light shove forward. The group reformed around Rayan, keeping him in the center as they continued down the hallway towards the door out. Riley walked just behind Rayan, her hatchet ready, watching every twitch of his shoulders and every nervous glance he cast back at her. The weight of the captured goods they already carried made their steps heavier, but no one complained.

Just before they reached the door that led out to the compound courtyard, Thorne stopped. He stared at Riley. At first she looked back at him, but once she noticed the intensity of his gaze she stopped and met his eyes with a puzzled look on her face. The rest of the group paused and glanced back. Riley gave them a nod to continue out into the courtyard, then turned her attention fully to Thorne.

“What?” she asked.

“I led the charge,” Thorne said. “I am the heart of the army. You are the head.”

“I do not understand,” Riley replied.

“This was reckless,” Thorne continued. “We could have waited and brewed more of your black fire and just burned down this compound. We would have taken fewer fallen and still accomplished the same goal, the raiders’ death.”

“Yes, but that was not the only point,” Riley said. Thorne’s face showed a mixture of confusion and a little desperation as he tried to understand. “It was not just about killing them. It was about making an example.”

“Of what?”

“What will happen to those who oppose us,” Riley answered. “These raider cowards struck us in the middle of the night. Instead of simply taking what they came for, they chose to murder almost all of us. Then they used one of our own as a human shield. Their disfigured leader answered mercy with revenge. They do not deserve mercy. They live by preying on the weak, and those they rob are usually left for dead. We hunted them down and killed them face to face. This is the story that will spread. We will let one survivor carry it: when you cross us, you meet your end. Raiders and the Clawborn alike will learn that not even hiding behind a hostage will save you.”

Thorne fell silent as he processed the weight of her words.

“Yes, we could have burned them,” Riley went on, “but I also wanted the soldiers to get their vengeance. Many of them had that had fallen that night, and they fought here today. Now our army knows how far we will go. Think of this battle as one with minimal preparation. We start the real preparation once we return, for the Clawborn. If you think the black fire was something, just wait until you see what comes next.” Riley smiled. “Our victory over the Clawborn will be one of legend.”

“Yes, it will be glorious!” Thorne replied, and a broad smile spread across his face.

“Now, set up camp back at the tree line,” Riley ordered. “We will be joined by more soldiers in a few hours. Once the fire has burned down, we will search the compound. Keep eyes on the main gate and board up the back. Hold Rayan away from the two other raider prisoners we brought. Let us see what this stash holds.”
