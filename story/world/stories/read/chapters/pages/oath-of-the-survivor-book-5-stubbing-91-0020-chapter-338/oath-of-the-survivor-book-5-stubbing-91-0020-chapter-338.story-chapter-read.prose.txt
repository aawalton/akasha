Kyle gritted his teeth as he focused on the Graft skill, all of his focus dialed in to keep his mana precisely mixed as he pushed the bamboo and bladed grass seeds together within the World Tree Seed. He’d gotten this far before, and as the outer layers of the seeds began to connect, he activated Infuse on the bamboo seed. Qualitatively, the bladed grasses of Er’Mithren were of a higher order than the mundane seeds from earth, which led to his first major roadblock as he experimented with Graft. When one seed was overwhelmingly more powerful, the result wouldn’t be stable.

Granted, Kyle expected the skill was meant to either be used with mundane plants, or otherwise for quick one-time effects. Early testing with those types of constructs showed promise, though they destabilized almost as soon as Graft took effect. What Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. were hoping to accomplish here was much more ambitious. What if they could create seeds that could germinate and grow, carrying the best aspects of the parent seeds? In this case, creating a plant that would grow as quickly and easily as the bamboo, retaining its durability while also weaving in the conceptual Sharpness and mana quality from the grasses.

A lot of trial and error had gone into this project over the last couple days, with Identify finally helping Kyle crack the core issue of mismatched energy. Now, in order to create a conceptual match, he needed to weave in the concept of Adaptation to match the concepts present in the grasses. Selfishly, he also expected that it would help to create a better finished product. Slowly, the two seeds began to merge, and Kyle did his best to avoid reacting as he nudged a bit more energy into each seed, keeping them at parity.

Finally, the seeds fully overlapped into one another, only to immediately begin breaking down the moment he withdrew Parasitic Resonance from them. With a sigh, he slumped back against the large tree, looking up toward the canopy. Testing like this was always a challenge, and it gave him something new to practice. Kyle strongly felt that getting a better understanding of the conceptual equilibrium would allow him to achieve his goals. Sure, the potential skill upgrade to Graft could also resolve the issue, but the more he figured out now, the more options opened up when it came to his upgrades. And with Graft, Kyle wanted a lot of options.

So far, none of the skills he’d unlocked as a World Tree Tender had lent themselves to direct power upgrades. Each boosted his versatility, and opened up access to the World Tree Seed, but that was about it. In fact, if it hadn’t been for Kyle and C.H.A.D.D.’s extensive travels prior to the disaster on Kertan Six, many of his staple plants wouldn’t have even been available to him. The reality remained that even the most unique plants from Earth lacked power if they didn’t have an awakened component, like the sundew he and C.H.A.D.D. consumed. He could still empower them via Cultivate and Infuse, but raising the baseline of each plant would allow them to reach even greater heights.

[DR. MAYHEW, IT IS TIME TO GO.]

The drone’s words stirred him from his musings, and he stood, resting a hand against the rough bark of the tree. He still had a lot of reflection ahead of him to better understand the timeless qualities of the natural treasure he’d absorbed. There would just have to be time for that when he was back in the comfort of a place he could truly call his own.

A blinking notification waited for him on his nav bracelet, and he took a look, confirming C.H.A.D.D.’s timeline.

Kyle,

Things are still tense, but the military has agreed to a full surrender. We’re working on the reconstruction now. Scouts are still reporting the shrikes taking down stranglevines across the forest, which is a huge help. Aid caravans can travel freely back and forth.

Get back here when you can so we can clear up the job, and we can finally get this planet moved to the Verdant Republic heartlands.

-D’Oillelat

The experience leaving the tree was almost more odd than the approach. Instead of each step and blast of Repel moving him less than expected, he looked back to see the tree rapidly receding behind him, the spatial expansion now working to aid him in his departure. Even though he had to cover the same amount of distance, the feeling of progress alone was novel to him.

“Do you think we’ll ever be able to grow a tree that manipulates space, C.H.A.D.D.?” Kyle asked, watching as he warped across a seemingly massive distance.

The drone was silent for a moment, considering the question. Finally, C.H.A.D.D. responded. [IF THE CONDITIONS ARE CORRECT, AND WE HAD THE PROPER CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDINGS TO APPLY TO IT, I BELIEVE IT WOULD BE POSSIBLE. IT IS MERELY THEORETICAL, BUT I DO THINK THAT THE EMPOWERMENT ABILITY YOU USE ALONGSIDE PHAEGOS MIGHT BE REQUIRED FOR SUCH A COMPLEX CONCEPT.]

“Do you think that Heart of Creation could carry concepts like that?” Kyle knew that his companion had studied the skill over the years, but was a little surprised to hear that C.H.A.D.D. thought it would be necessary.

[I DO. WHEN I FIRST OBSERVED YOU USING THE SKILL DURING MR. KULHAVEY’S TRAINING EXERCISE, YOUR STORM SHELTER SKILL WAS INFUSED WITH A VARIETY OF CONCEPTS THAT I DO NOT BELIEVE EITHER OF US HAD MEANINGFULLY TOUCHED UPON. MARCUS DID NOT SHARE FURTHER WITH ME, BUT I EXPECT THAT AS YOUR CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDING GROWS, SO TOO WILL THE ABILITY TO WEAVE THOSE CONCEPTS INTO YOUR SKILL. I DO NOT BELIEVE THAT SPATIAL MANIPULATION IS OUTSIDE OF THE REALM OF POSSIBILITY.]

The following hours were spent refining their experimentation framework, including trials leveraging the Heart of Creation. Soon, the cleared space around the city was before them, and with a wave to the Resistance aid worker, Kyle vaulted over the exterior wall. Only a few of the goblins bothered to look up at him as he did, and he realized with a hint of embarrassment what a habit he’d made of jumping over walls instead of walking through gates. I probably need to work on that, he thought.

Sending a quick note to D’Oillelat, Kyle navigated through the city toward the central tower. The tension she’d mentioned in her message was clear to see, but Kyle saw it matched by a relief that the fighting was over.

The goblin queen had set up a command tent near the tower, which still had a tight lattice of vines binding it together. Earth Mages were hard at work patching it together bit by bit, though Kyle was quite proud of the reinforced structures he’d put together. Nodding to the guards outside the command tent, he stepped inside to see D’Oillelat, Drokh, and Ver’ah all sitting around a large table.

“Sorry about the delay,” Kyle started to say, before he was cut off with a dismissive wave from the goblin queen.

“Don’t start, Kyle. There’s nothing to apologize for. You helped us stop a monster. And you saved a lot of lives in doing it. I just wanted to make sure we could all talk before we get taken to the Collective, and the next phase of the reconstruction begins. Drokh?”The ogre nodded, offering Kyle a tired smile. “When we first met, I was convinced that you were little more than a mercenary. I never would have imagined that you would have cared for our plight, much less helped bring this war to an end. On behalf of the people of Er’Mithren, Her Majesty extends to you rights of citizenship, and a title. As the reconstruction concludes, you will be granted land, and a homestead, should you wish it.”

“I –“ Kyle began, before he was interrupted by D’Oillelat once again.

“The correct response is ‘thank you,’” she said, meeting his eyes. “And you should count yourself lucky. I was promised a message would be delivered to Pall’Eth, and that I would receive the heartvines from the Scourge. Neither of those things happened, yet I still counted your job as complete. So, you’re welcome to thank me there, too.”

Kyle smiled, shaking his head. “Thank you,” he finally said.

“You’ve done more for us than we can pay back, Kyle,” Ver’ah said, clapping him on his right shoulder. “We would be honored to have you living with us.”

“Don’t inflate his ego any more than it is, Ver’ah,” D’Oillelat admonished, though a smile crossed her face, too. The look got more serious as she continued. “I have personally locked down and begun to read through Pall’Eth’s notes, at least what remained of them. You mentioned that there was outside interference during my father’s reign, and that has been confirmed. However, from Pall’Eth’s notes, I’m suspicious that some of the outside influence remained. It’s something I intend to handle, but you have a right to know.”

Kyle felt the now-familiar phantom pain from his scars, and met the Queen of Er’Mithren’s eyes. “Please keep me informed with what you find. And be careful. These aren’t weak forces you’re dealing with.”

“Then it’s a good thing I don’t intend to face them alone. Let’s step outside, and I’ll let my contacts know that we are prepared for our planetary move.”

Kyle had to admit, he was curious how this process worked. He’d assumed he’d be taken back up via shuttle, then teleported back to the Hub, not get brought along with the whole planet.

Once they were outside, D’Oillelat sent some messages from her nav bracelet, and a moment later the sky darkened. A swirling mass that looked like a second planet just… appeared. In his mind, a clear voice rang out.

“Do not be alarmed. Your repositioning will be complete momentarily.”

Though he couldn’t feel the aura from the mass, Kyle recognized it for what it was. This is one of the phytan A Grades. The Pillar was the civilization with the most advanced terraforming and planetary alignment technologies, and their leadership would often assist with projects of this scope. Usually, from what Kyle heard, it was a team of B Grades. To have an A Grade here… the Verdant Republic really spared no expense.

Activating Identify, Kyle watched the space around the planet shift and warp. As expected, very little of the spatial mana leaked through the atmosphere, courtesy of the power of the phytan facilitating the transfer. However, he saw small glimmers, and that was enough for now. Any exposure he could get to complex concepts and abilities would be a foundation he could keep working on.

Several minutes later, the shifts ceased, and the massive phytan simply disappeared, as though it had never arrived in the first place. Nothing outwardly seemed to change, but the quick responses on Kyle’s nav bracelet told him that they were back in the Collective proper. As if confirming his thoughts, he saw shuttles already appearing like dots in the sky, making their way toward their location.

“It looks like this is farewell, for now,” D’Oillelat said with a smile. “Suierrillax kept me up to date on some of the other projects she and Jarberry were working on for you. I wish you success, Kyle. And please remember that you have a home here, when you have need of it.”

“I’ll keep that in mind. And without a prompt this time, thank you.”

Just then, a call came through on Kyle’s nav bracelet, from Suierrillax.

“You’ve got great timing,” Kyle said. “What’s going on? I should be heading back soon.”

“First, Kyle, I have great timing because I asked D’Oillelat to let me know when I could call. Secondly, your next job is going to have to wait. We just got word that they pushed up the next hearing for your Tribunal.”

“When?” Kyle asked, bewildered. He thought he’d have at least a few months to prepare and sort things out.

“Tomorrow,” Suierrillax replied.
