Holding the slime, Ferris took a closer look at the Core Tree.

Although the tree was young, the fact that it was already bearing fruit was a great advantage for Ferris. Cultivators with a weapon bond would have to grow their tree from nothing, feeding it qi to nourish it.

Uncle Winton had said the tree grows out of the soul’s ‘representation of a core.’ This was the slime’s soul, so the dark green crystal was likely the slimespace’s representation of the core. A bit strangely, there were now four cores: the mirrored pair in his soulspace and here on the pedestal, one inside the slime’s mental body, and now the dark green crystal. Ferris presumed the core on the pedestal represented the bond, while this crystal was the core itself? He would eventually need to see if he could test and confirm his ideas, as understanding the representations in the soul would be key to taking advantage of them.

Looking closer at the fruit, Ferris saw that one of them appeared less mature than the other two. He wanted to learn what abilities they would give, but needed more time to do it properly.

More immediately, Ferris wanted to water the tree. The sooner it had more qi, the quicker it would produce more fruit.

He could bring liquid qi from his soulspace, but it would be better if the slimespace had a source. He looked around but didn’t see anything like his Qi Pool—not surprising since Ferris had to make his from scratch. The waterfall and area outside the dome both had a strange green liquid, but it obviously wasn’t qi.

He could build a Qi Pool, but that would require a source of qi chunks. Ferris’ Air Passage was something that occurred naturally as he… breathed. The slime, Ferris realized, doesn’t breathe. They don’t even have lungs, obviously they won’t have an Air Passage.

Ferris was pretty sure monsters had natural qi gathering abilities. How else would they grow stronger? Slimes should have something. He just needed to find it first.

After climbing down the tower, Ferris went exploring. His slime familiar happily followed along. It didn’t take long to see everything inside the dome. Ferris tried to avoid the ants, but failed miserably as they crawled around him.

He was able to circle the area in about half an hour. The grey birds never bothered him, and the ants fearlessly moved around his feet. More yellow pillars were everywhere, but Ferris was still wary of trying to crawl into one of their openings. The tallest tower had a sharper incline, making him wary of climbing it. He thought he saw the birds nested near the top.

Most interesting of all were the waterfall and river.

Above Ferris, green liquid fell from a hole high up in the roof of the dome. The waterfall had filled a large pool, which fed a winding river that returned to outside the dome. It wasn’t water, and Ferris hesitated to touch it. Likely the ‘green liquid’ was the mental representation of the slime’s acid aspect. If so, it would melt his flesh.

Aspects were typically represented as large changes to the environment of a soulspace. A source of fire might be a volcano or even a lake of fire, while a wind aspect could be a constant current of wind flowing through. The green ocean surrounding the dome was probably the acid aspect that slime cores inherently possess.

Inspecting the large pool the waterfall fed into, Ferris started to guess it was one of the slime’s natural abilities. Floating in the swirling liquid was a massive chain, with a large metal cage that looked exactly like the one latched around the slime’s core in the real world—although much, much bigger. It was a loose guess, but this chain looked about at least a thousand times larger.

It strained credibility that the slimespace just happened to have an exact copy of an object currently inside the slime’s real body. More likely, it was an ability that made a spiritual copy.

It even looked like one end of the giant chain was sheared off, which must be where the real-life chain extended outside the slime’s body. Still, Ferris had no idea why it was so much bigger.

If the chain appeared in this pool, would anything the slime ate also show up here? Did it work for something eaten by their mental body? Ferris looked around for something to test, shrugged, and then took off his shirt. Calling the slime over, they were happy to eat the shirt.

Ferris watched the pool carefully as the slime engulfed the clothing. Strangely, some of the nearby birds started to fly in a circle around the waterfall above the pool, like vultures surrounding a meal. A duplicate shirt appeared almost directly in the middle of the circle of birds and fell into the liquid. While the chain was massive, the shirt was the same size as before.

Almost immediately, several of the grey ants jumped into the pool and swam towards the shirt.

Ferris let out a gasp, wide eyed. The pool turned out to be very acidic, and the ants were not immune at all. All of them started to dissolve immediately after touching the green liquid, only lasting long enough to reach the center and try for the shirt. The first ant to reach grabbed it with its mandibles and immediately began tearing the shirt to shreds. Before long, both the ants and the torn-up shirt dissolved away to nothing.

Ferris had no idea what to think of either the birds’ or the ants’ behavior, but the shirt appearing above the pool confirmed some of his theories. The pool of acid was related to the slime’s ability to dissolve anything inside their body. Since they couldn’t break down the magical chain, it was just sitting here in the deadly acid. He decided to call it the Consume Pool.

Ferris immediately wanted to test it more, especially with food or other items from the real world. Would anything from the real world be giant, like the chain, or was that due to its magical nature? How could Ferris arrange to observe the process in the slimespace? Were these ants not actually spirit animals, but parasites? Were they stealing anything the slime consumed? If so, they weren’t very good ones, although the shirt wasn’t the same as food from the real world.

The waterfall was also more than it appeared. Uncle Winton had told him: for anything in the soulspace to interact with or affect the real world, a connection was required. Forged weapons like the spear his uncle had bonded had clear pathways carved into them by the crafter, which provides a medium to affect the world. This was an advantage crafted items had over familiars.

Instead, familiars had to interact with the world the same way a cultivator would: a flow of energy through the body called a meridian. The human body had many closed meridians that could be used, but time and effort were required to clear the way for proper flow. Ferris’ Air Passage ability that collected qi used a meridian. He’d spent years practicing meditative breathing techniques to open it naturally, as that was required to unlock the soul and become an Adept, but it was still very inefficient.

Meridians gave off a noticeable presence in the area around them. It was almost like the feeling of falling, but not as disorienting. Ferris was familiar with it from gathering qi each night, walking inside his Air Passage.

Slightly embarrassingly, the slime’s waterfall meridian felt stronger than Ferris’ Air Passage. Perhaps something about the slime’s magical nature or its liquid body made it easier to develop a channel for energy to flow through. The flowing nature of the waterfall was important, as meridians depended on the movement to create the connection to the soulspace.

The Consume Pool needed a meridian to connect to the real world and affect the food inside the slime’s body, and the waterfall fit.

Winding away from the Consume Pool, a river flowed back out of the dome. This could also provide the flow of energy for a meridian, but Ferris thought the waterfall more likely. The falling liquid flowed faster and into the pool, representing movement into the soulspace.

The river flowed much slower, likely due to being much wider than the waterfall. It coiled back and forth, winding curves lazily feeding back to the ocean outside the dome.

Excitedly, Ferris spotted something. Along the bottom of the river, chunks of qi shimmered with incandescent light. It was hard to be sure they were the correct shade of blue in the green water, but otherwise they looked identical to the chunks of qi in Ferris’ Air Passage.

The chunks of qi were sitting inside large clams. Oddly, the clams were resting along the bottom of the river, shells open to display the qi chunk inside like a prized pearl.

Ferris smiled at the discovery. This matched his expectations for a qi gathering ability.

Looking back up at the Consume Pool, Ferris considered the winding river it fed into. If the slime’s food dissolved in the pool, then was this river filtering out qi from the remains? That would make the pool a stomach, while the river was an intestinal track. The slime seemed to lack physical organs, but maybe it compensated with natural abilities in the slimespace?

However it worked, it generated qi. Ferris decided to call it the Digest River.

Interestingly, while Ferris’ Air Passage was self-contained, the slime appeared to have two separate natural abilities working together to gather qi. The Digest River lacked that telltale feeling of a meridian. It did, however, give off that strange subtle vibration in his heart he felt around his Heartfire and Qi Pool. He had to stop and concentrate to feel it, but it was definitely there. In fact, both the Digest River and Consume Pool had it.

The clams were a twist Ferris had no explanation for. If they were spirit animals, he didn’t know why they would be a part of a natural ability. The book on familiars had implied the two were separate concepts, but maybe they could be connected?

He’d never heard of animals in the soul making abilities work… but no one in his clan bonded familiars either. Looking back at the flocks of birds and swarms of ants, perhaps they were also part of the slime’s abilities?

The clams were equally spread out, displaying accumulated qi chunks all along the riverbed. Ferris wondered what would happen to the qi naturally. Monsters grew more powerful over time, but almost always lacked intelligent purpose behind their development. Left alone, this qi would certainly help the slime, but Ferris could use it to direct growth much more efficiently.

Well… he could only use it if he could get to it. Right now, it was at the bottom of a winding river of acid.

Ferris looked at his familiar, still merrily rippling in his arms. Their body is made of acid, so they shouldn’t have trouble swimming in it, he pondered, and the qi chunks are already in acid, so carrying them in the slime’s body shouldn’t hurt either…

First, he tested the liquid by dunking a strip of cloth torn from his pantleg into it. As expected, the cloth dissolved. Next, he had the slime test it.

“Touch liquid, carefully.” Ferris attempted to send a mental image of the slime dipping a small bit into the river.

Instead, the slime gleefully jumped straight in. Ferris felt a bit faint at the slime’s casual disregard for caution. This body is just a mental construct, but in the real world my soul is bonded to the slime’s core.

Luckily, the slime proved to be as acid proof on the outside as on the inside. They were slightly heavier than the liquid, and slowly sank to the bottom. Ferris directed them to a qi chunk nearby—and far away from the outlet at the end of the river.

“Swallow, but no eat,” Ferris sent over the bond.

“How eat, but no eat?” The slime was very confused.

Ferris sent a mental picture of the slime with food inside, but it wasn’t being digested by the acid.

The slime bubbled unhappily.

“Please, try.” Ferris thought the qi chunk would be fine either way, as it had been sitting in acid already.

Begrudgingly, the slime swam over to one of the clams. Ferris watched carefully, if the clam reacted with hostility, he would immediately call for a retreat. The slime was able to roll onto the open clamshell with no problem. Ferris braced himself for the clam to snap shut, but it didn’t react at all as the slime pulled the qi chunk into its body.

“Yes, now bring it here!” Ferris was excited, this might actually work.

A couple seconds after the slime swallowed the chunk, Ferris heard a splash. Looking up at the source of the river, he could now see an identical chunk of qi floating in the Consume Pool.

Once again, several of the grey ants jumped into the pool. This gave credibility to the theory the ants were part of some ability. It was strange, though, the ants were all over the place, not just around the pool. They still might be some kind of demon or parasite.

These ants fared no better against the acid than the previous group, although they survived long enough to reach the qi chunk. The first ant to grab it immediately crunched it in their mandibles, breaking it in two. After that, Ferris lost sight of the pieces in the confusion as more ants pressed in.

Looking back at the riverbed, the qi chunk was now missing from the slime’s body.

As the slime rolled off the clam, its shell slowly closed. After a couple seconds, two fleshy, gooey tubes extended from the clam into the acid.

Ferris had grown up on an island, although it was much too dangerous for any except Adepts to explore outside the bastion’s barrier. The clan delvers brought home everything possible for food, and common aquatic life was part of the basic education. He knew a little about clams, although most of that was on how to cook them from his parents.

If these things worked like mundane clams, then the tubes would be a syphon gathering nutrients from the water and expelling waste. He guessed the food from the Consume Pool was dissolved into the acid river, and then somehow the clams filtered the broken-down material into qi.

Slowly, the slime pushed themself along the river floor until they emerged from the acid, rippling.

“Rock taste good!”

Ferris sighed at it. “Not supposed to eat, collect,” he told them, a bit defeated. He was standing next to enough qi to double his Qi Pool but had no way to collect it. He wanted to gather it soon, as he’d heard qi gathering abilities stopped accumulating more if left to overflow. That was mostly from stories, but he believed it.

In this case, the maximum qi was probably limited by the number of clams or length of the river. Regular cultivation was the key, rather than putting it off to pile up.

At least he’d confirmed some of his assumptions, although it raised new questions about how the ants were involved. The fact that the ants were hurt by the acid suggested they weren’t part of the ability. The clams were fine in the river after all.

He had more information, but was stuck. The sooner he watered the tree, the more fruit it would provide. He had no quick method of collecting qi from the river, so he decided to use his own Qi Pool for now.

He called his familiar back into his arms, which climbed up happily. Somehow, they had either absorbed or lost any remaining acid from the swim, and were back to the typical slightly damp spongy texture.

Together they headed back to what Ferris decided to call Heartfire tower, intending to return to his soulspace. It was a bit odd, as technically the entire slimespace should be connected to Ferris’ soul. Why couldn’t they simply return from anywhere?

Ferris had spoken to Uncle Winton about entering and exiting the soul. Apparently, the nature of his mental body required a special point to enter and exit his own soulspace. In the future he could control that point, and add more of them, but for now the transition point was always his Heartfire. When Ferris woke up, he didn’t just leave his mental body behind. The body, and anything like clothes or equipment he was holding, disappeared from his soulspace with his mind. Ferris wasn’t clear on where it existed while he was awake, but it felt like it was always with him. Not that he was ever really disconnected from his soulspace either… It was confusing.

Ferris technically could leave from anywhere, but it would be the same as the death of his mental body. To return Ferris would have to spend several days reforming the body. If scavengers didn’t steal it, his spiritual corpse and anything he was carrying would be lying on the ground until he returned.

Looking down at his familiar, Ferris remembered he’d found them at the Core Tree. It took a lot of confused mental messages of back and forth, but Ferris eventually learned that the slime had appeared at their own Heartfire. They somehow sensed the Core Tree’s fruit, and immediately climbed down the Heartfire tower and up to the tree.

Luckily, while the slime had known where the fruit was, they didn’t have eyes to look around. They were trying to follow the most direct path to the fruit, which meant rolling directly underneath and jumping up. Now that the slime had Ferris’ mental picture, they knew they could climb up the tree’s trunk to reach the fruit.

With some more frustrating mental messages, Ferris was able to ask the slime to wait for him at their Heartfire next time. The slime was happy to do almost anything Ferris asked, but he kept having trouble communicating his intent. Thankfully, it should get better with time. They were both learning how the mental bond worked, and the primer on familiars stated they would gradually get smarter.

When they reached the top of the Heartfire tower, Ferris placed one hand on the slimespace’s core while holding his familiar in his other. There was another feeling of vertigo, and then Ferris opened his eyes back inside his soulspace, standing next to the core on its pedestal. Much like the Heartfire was his transition point for entering and exiting the soul, this core pedestal was the transition point to the slimespace.

“New place.” The slime jumped out of his arms and started to roll around. The oddity of seeing the slime’s identical core in two places once again struck him.

“Follow,” he called as he approached his Qi Pool and started crafting a bucket to carry liquid in.

The slime rolled after him and started exploring around the Heartfire. As soon as they found the corpse of the demon frog, they immediately pounced on it. Before Ferris noticed, it was half digested.

“Ask before eat!” Ferris scolded, approaching. “No eat everything.”

“No eat this, no eat that. Want eat!” the slime complained, despite still digesting the demon frog. Ferris had no idea what eating the demon would do. A mental construct of a slime is eating a personification of my negative emotions, which is likely now floating in a river of acid, feeding a bed of large clams.

He sighed, and traitorously his stomach—also a mental construct—growled in hunger. “Fine, we go eat soon. But ask before eat!”

“Go eat, go eat,” the slime started bouncing up and down, almost finished digesting the demon frog.

After filling up his finished bucket from the Qi Pool, Ferris led the slime back to the core pedestal and entered the slimespace. He climbed down the Heartfire’s tower and then up to the Core Tree. After watering the tree with the entire bucket, they returned to the Consume Pool and Digest River.

As he’d expected, there was no sign of the demon frog in the pool. Likely, it had already been dissolved away. Hopefully it wouldn’t be a problem. Experimentation often went wrong, but it might provide some benefit to digest the demons this way. Uncle Winton had taught him how to use the demons for qi forming, but claimed there were other uses if Ferris could find them.

Ferris climbed back up the Heartfire tower, returned his bucket to the soulspace, and then exited his soul. Opening his eyes in his room, reality brought with it the pains of life. Most prominent was his burned hand, but his back hadn’t appreciated laying on the floor.

The slime was waking up as well. Eventually, they should be able to enter and exit the soulspace individually, but for now they needed Ferris’ aid.

Hesitantly, Ferris continued to touch the slime’s body. “Remember, no eat me.”

“Go eat, go eat,” the slime continued chanting, but showed no signs of hurting him.

“First, we need to do something about this chain,” Ferris said, untying it from the dresser. He liked the idea that the magically strengthened cage was protecting his bonded core, but the length of chain extending out was a vulnerability.

He’d used it as a leash on the way home, but was now feeling more confident about trusting the slime. Something felt a bit odd about his worry, despite the ache in his burned hand. He didn’t want the chain to be used to yank the core out of the slime’s body, accidentally or otherwise.

Ferris considered just letting the slime hold the entire chain, with it loosely floating in its body. The magically strengthened metal links were about the width of his pinky, and the entire thing was about as long as he was tall, so it would all fit.

However, he thought he could do better. The fist-sized cage protected the egg-sized core from being stomped on like Foreman Carter had done, but it had obvious gaps.

Carefully, Ferris had the slime stay still while he pulled the cage half out of their body. Then he wrapped the chain around it, spinning the cage as he went. It was tricky with one hand wrapped in bandages, but not impossible. The metal was a bit slimy, but the magically enhanced acid only worked inside the slime’s body.

Ferris asked the slime to help, but they didn’t appear to have any intentional control over the objects inside them. This strangely included the core itself, even without the cage around it. The slime could control what was pulled inside them, like when they had decided to engulf Ferris’ hand. However, after the process started it was all instinctual.

When the cage was fully wrapped with the chain, Ferris awkwardly tried to tie the end down. It sort of worked, at least well enough to not immediately unravel. He hoped the chain would prevent something like a sword from slipping in between the gaps left by the cage. However, he didn’t have much confidence in it, as a bit of force would probably push it aside. Still, better than nothing.

He let the wrapped cage submerge back into their body, and the slime jumped into Ferris’ arms. “Go eat,” they chanted, happy ripples pulsing with the words.

Holding his familiar with one arm, Ferris grabbed his crutch with the other. It was a short walk out to the main hall. Everyone was already eating dinner, he must have missed the bell while meditating.

“Ferris, dear, what do you have there?” Mother Lily saw him first, and then Karl, Cyrus, and Brix turned to look.

“Mother Lily, everyone, I would like to introduce you to my familiar.”

Mother Lily was on him in a flash, pulling him into a large hug.

“Remember, no eat. Wait.” Ferris desperately sent to the slime as Mother Lily pulled them both into a hug.

Releasing him, Mother Lily immediately picked the slime up out of his arm.

“No eat her!” he sent again, frantically now. This was not the careful introduction he had intended.

“Aren’t you the cutest thing.” Mother Lily held up the slime. Despite being squashed a bit, the slime showed no sign of displeasure.

“Please, Mother Lily, we are still training.” Ferris held out his hand and asked his familiar to return to him. The woman tried to hold on, but the slime’s body was impossible to grasp as it flowed back to him.

“A familiar bond?” Karl questioned from the table. “Why would you choose a slime?” The man looked almost affronted by the choice.

Ferris smiled. He knew slimes weren’t the most glamorous option. Is it surprising the man with a shiny golden gauntlet is a bit pompous?

“Slimes are very interesting,” he said as he took a seat at the table, thinking of the ability Adaptation. The journal stated Janice claimed Gumly had it naturally, but Ferris hadn’t seen anything like it in the slimespace. Or, if he had he hadn’t recognized it. He would have to investigate further. “And my best path to a tier one skill that can regrow my leg.”

“But they are so…” Karl seemed to rethink his words, “…vulnerable. A child could hit the core with a stick.”

Ferris nodded, looking at the slime on his lap. “Yes.” After Foreman Carter’s lesson, he knew it better than most. “I will have to find an ability to cover the weakness before delving. If I do it right, then the slime will be much more resilient than other familiars. For now, I have this cage protecting the core.” He gestured to the magical chain.

“That’s one way to do it,” Cyrus laughed, leaning forward for a closer look. “You know, slimes are perhaps the most widespread of all monsters,” his eyes flashed with an inner light as he examined the slime; a common sign that someone was using a visual ability. Ferris thought he saw the eye embossed on the bonded book’s cover twitch, but he wasn’t sure. “Most monsters are unique to their location, but slimes are everywhere.”

Ferris had read something like that in the slime familiar book, a comment by the author about how widespread slimes were. The other books had all specified exactly where to find the monsters to bond. It did feel a bit strange, but Ferris wasn’t sure how uncommon it was. Now that Cyrus pointed it out, maybe there was more to it…

“That’s because they can squeeze through any opening, then survive just about anywhere. They spread like rats,” Brix said, more confident in her answer than Ferris felt warranted. He also didn’t like the comparison to vermin, although he knew it was fairly common. “If nothing else,” she said, picking up a vegetable from her plate, “the acid element will do well around Scalefort.” She tossed the piece of food at his familiar.

Still worried about the slime’s self-control, Ferris caught the piece of food in midair. Any other time he’d fed his familiar, they immediately searched for more food. Looking down at the slime, he carefully instructed them.

“Only eat this. No eat me. Stay here.” He dropped the vegetable onto the slime, which immediately sucked it inside and started rippling with happiness.

Brix gave him an odd look, to which he could only bow his head. “Sorry, but they are very food motivated, and we are still training.”

“What a charming little dear.” Mother Lily said—seemingly oblivious to his exchange with Brix—and brought him two plates of food, apparently one for the slime. “What is the darling’s name?”

Ferris hesitated. “I’m not sure yet.” He had been thinking about it, but hadn’t made much progress.

Mother Lily was aghast. “A familiar is a lifelong companion. A family member. You must give them a name at once!”

Cyrus smiled, and said “You could name them Blobby, or Blobert.”

“Slimicus, maybe Slimon.” Brix unhelpfully added.

Ferris shuddered. Honestly, he never liked Gumly’s name. A little too on the nose; not that slimes had noses.

“Goo-nder, Goo-mas, or how about Goo-bigal,” Cyrus responded.

“We could name it after you, Slime-rus.” Brix threw another piece of food, but this time at Cyrus.

“You can’t do that!” Mother Lily apparently agreed with him. “The sweet thing needs a proper name,” claimed the woman who had adopted a flower for a name. “Something elegant and beautiful.”

Ferris looked at the slime in his lap. Elegant and beautiful… maybe from a certain point of view.

Mother Lily’s mention of family had him thinking of home. The slime’s color reminded him of a jade statue of a fish that belonged to his mother. It was a family heirloom, passed down from her mother. Ferris had grown up with the statue proudly displayed on a mantle in their living room. Likely, one day it would belong to his older sister Ellis. Ferris smiled at the idea.

“I think I will name them Jade.” The slime was beautiful to him, and the name would remind him of home.

Mother Lily loved it, while Karl just—not so subtly—rolled his eyes. Cyrus and Brix were having too much fun making new name suggestions to stop.

“What do you think?” he tried to ask the slime. “Do you like the name Jade?”

“Want eat!” was all that he got in response. Ferris shrugged and fed his new familiar: Jade.
