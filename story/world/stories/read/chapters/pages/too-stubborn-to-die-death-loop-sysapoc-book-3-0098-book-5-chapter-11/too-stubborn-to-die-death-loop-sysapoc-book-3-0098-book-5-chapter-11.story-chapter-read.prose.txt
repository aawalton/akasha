He had been a little worried about bringing in more goblins before, but with how resourceful they had proven, he doubted many would push back on the new arrivals.

Waving them off, he went back into his kitchen. It was completely filled with food now, but almost all of it was meat. That wasn’t the end of the world, though. But it would require a little out-of-the-box thinking to push his dishes to the next level.

Okay, now what to do with all of this meat?

Frying, braising, baking, barbecuing, sous vide. There were many possibilities; he just had to push himself to realize them.

Delicately handling the meat, he got to work. He first put some stewing steaks aside in a sous vide, while preparing others to be aged.

He took some meat, seasoned it, and wrapped it in leaves before barbecuing over a wood fire. The scents flowing from his kitchen were divine, and it didn’t take long for a small army of goblins to gather around, salivating as they watched on in joy.

Marbled cuts were cooked over flames, and cut thin while still red, almost sashimi-like, creating a rare, meaty taste highlighted with seasoned bark that was both crispy and velvety at the same time.

A portion of everything he cooked was placed away into his spatial storage, and extra was served to the hypnotized goblins.

“Oh my!”

“Better than wort root!”

It was a precise and delicate practice. Weaving aether and mana together, and using their energy to heighten flavors, and push techniques beyond what mortal hands were capable of.

Meanwhile, through the madness of it all, bones were boiling away in stockpots, filled with his own toxic ichor and other odd seasonings. But they were more balanced than his previous rough-handed styles.

It wasn’t so much a desertion from his ogre cooking ways, but an enhancement, turning his food into something more in line with himself.

Sauces made of marrow and stock were reduced to thick, umami-rich, savory flavors to be poured over finished products. Even organs like livers were used for their rich flavors to enhance prime cuts.

It was a true masterful display. Nothing went to waste, and everything was used for what it best suited to bring out bold flavors.

This was what he had been chasing. A means to merge two styles. The truth was, ogre taste buds were demanding, but not inherently gross. Dishes most would find sickening lit up Aaron’s taste buds, while normal human food often tasted bland and uninteresting.

But if he could enhance the flavor beyond what should be possible? Well, that changed everything.

Hmm. Not bad. But I need to push it further. More flavor!

It was insane. Eye-watering amounts of flavor seemingly forced into the meat. Succulent cuts delivered to tables both crispy and moist. Unidentifiable braised meats that sent unsuspecting goblins catatonic.

It wasn’t just his green little companions, either. Soon others from around Dober were drawn in, like hungry dogs catching whiffs of food on the wind.

Once, not too long ago, he’d have made most around him sick with his ungodly creations, but now they funneled toward him, like moths to a flame. And best of all, he hadn’t compromised to achieve it.

Although this multiday cooking binge had primarily been focused on honing his ability to enhance flavor, rather than produce boon-producing foods. But he didn’t care. There was no doubt in his mind that it was worth it.

Besides, it had led him to new truths of cooking. Enhancing his understanding of not just cooking, but ingredients, and the ways one could bring the most out of them.

Through hard work, Aaron had rounded out his cooking style, and just at the right time.

Before long, his storage was completely full, and his meals were going to the ever-larger crowd.

People pushed and shoved to get a taste. There was something unique and special that set his cooking above that of the other chefs in town.

At first, his ogre belly had seemed like a handicap, at least when it came to cooking enjoyable food. That had bothered Aaron as the boons it provided well and truly made up for it. But now he realized it was more like training weights. Struggling to overcome the difference in tastes had forced him to ascend beyond what most normal chefs achieved.

And after days of cooking, the results spoke for themselves. Never before had Dober been so well fed, and the grounds around his kitchen looked more like an opium den than a restaurant as his customers lay about, their minds truly blown.
