An instructor approached him with a flower, eerily echoing his memory of the event. “Are you ready?” she asked.

Hayden raised his palm to politely refuse. “I'm okay. I’m not opening my gate.”

Rosen snorted with laughter. “You’re what?”

The instructor glanced at the flower and back. “So… you don’t want to become a rifter?”

“Oh, I’ll become a rifter. I’m just starting with a clear spirit.”

Unlike elemental, physical, and entropic spirits, weavers could use clear spirits without opening gates. They would still have limited power, but they were more than enough to suit his current needs.

Rosen clearly didn't trust Hayden could use one, so he started laughing.

“Clear spirits are functionally useless in a rift, Hayden,” the instructor lectured. “If you don't get a serious spirit, you won’t make it into an academy.”

“While I appreciate your concern, I've made my decision. So, if you please...” Hayden motioned to Rosen.

She sighed and turned to Rosen. He smirked and repeated Hayden's denying palm gesture.

“I'm not opening mine either. Of course, I'm not an idiot. I've just already opened it.”

A few nearby students chuckled. The instructor cracked an involuntary smile.

“But I could use help opening my lightning gate,” he added. “I just got the Thunder Punch spirit. So I want to make sure it's perfect.”

She raised an eyebrow. “Do you have a catalyst?”

“Sure do.”

Students watched enviously as he opened a small box, exposing an opaque white stone with light flashing within it.

Hayden eyed the coran pattern that Rosen’s catalyst emitted. For a close combat spirit? he thought. Is he stupid? Or just ignorant?

Catalysts were neither good nor bad, just full or incomplete. Otherwise, it was a matter of use case and preferences. The catalyst Rosen had was powerful, but it was extremely specialized. It would only work well with sky-manipulation lightning spirits. The fact that he chose it for a close combat spirit was bewildering.

Hayden expected the instructor to correct him, so he was appalled when she said, “What rank is it?”

Rosen saw Hayden's grave expression and smirked. “Rank 2.”

Hayden looked between them. A ranking system? Is this universe really that ignorant?

It seemed that way. Simplifying something so complex with a number laid their ignorance bare. That said, the instructor at least understood the dangers of cutting pressure. He knew because the catalyst made her extremely nervous.

“Are you sure you want to use that?” she asked. “A stronger catalyst means nothing if you hurt yourself.”

Rosen scoffed. “No offense, but I don't need your instruction. I’m here because I was framed, not because I lack talent. Okay? So don't compare me to the others. I'm simply asking if you'll help me.” He paused and glared at her. “Will you?”

Nearby students jeered, suddenly praying for his downfall. The instructor saw the reaction and winced, but she said, “If you're sure…” anyway. Despite saying that, she still looked for another instructor before hesitantly sitting.

Rosen rolled his eyes. He thought they’d get started when the woman stopped talking, but it seemed that silence didn’t make a difference. She was too scared to comply, and it was getting on his nerves.

Yes, he was being a bit unreasonable. He was clearly irritable after Hayden's setup. But come on! Was she really going to lecture him on catalysts? He went to a B-rank academy, for fuck's sake. Treating him like an ignorant child was outright offensive.

“Okay,” she said as she put her hands on his back. “Take a deep breath.”

Finally. Rosen snorted and put the stone against his chest and breathed in. She then poured cora into his body to stimulate his core.

“Now when you breathe out, empty your core into the catalyst, and breathe it back in. You should feel the pattern go through your main gate and into the subgate.”

Rosen resented the lecture, but took it seriously. The process was similar to squeezing a foam ball and then releasing it to have it refill with air. The huge difference was that the core breathed in a coran pattern that moved like a hurricane—and it was filled with shattered glass.

This catalyst was wildly more powerful than the one he used for his main gate. Cora lashed his gate erratically, threatening to destroy it. Thankfully, Rosen was far more skilled than the others. He wrangled control of it until it was consistent and rode it out. There was scarring when he finished, but he felt cora flow through it in the catalyst’s pattern at the end.

Rosen opened his eyes. His forearms were slick with sweat, and his breathing was ragged—but he won. Students and teachers alike were watching him in amazement.

“I can’t believe you managed to do it,” the instructor said.

Rosen’s ego ballooned. The next moment, it deflated when she touched her chest and said:

“You had me worried there for a second. I thought for sure you'd break your gate.”

Rosen brooded. “Well, I pulled it off. Doubt you can say that.”

“Well, there's that.” She pinched the bridge of her nose and took two deep breaths. Then she let go and stood. “I should…”

She was probably going to say, “Find another student,” but instead, she found herself staring in Hayden's direction. She looked frozen—actually frozen—as if she was watching an animal eating a student in real time.

Rosen followed her gaze and saw Hayden sitting with his eyes closed. Rosen lacked a technique or spirit to see cora, so all he saw was an acne-assaulted geek sitting in a meadow.

“What’s going on?” Rosen asked.

“He’s…” Instead of finishing her statement, she stood and ran away. Two minutes later, she returned with the head instructor and one other, both of whom shared her bewildered expression.

The nearby students now had their attention firmly fixed on Hayden.

“Hey. What’s going on?”

“I don’t know. He must be doing something crazy.”

“Do you think he destroyed his core?”

“Doubt it. He'd be screaming like Rosen if he did.”

“Shhh! I can’t hear them.”

The area fell still as the teachers watched Hayden silently.

“Is there a problem?” Hayden asked, eyes still closed.

“Uh… no,” the head instructor said. “It’s just… Where did you learn that circulation technique?”

Circulation techniques referred to the method by which a rifter absorbed cora through their body. The main gate transformed the cora into something spirits could use, but breathing it was its own art form—and Hayden must've looked like a living catalyst to get that response.

“It’s impolite to ask where people learn their arts,” Hayden said.

“Uh… okay. If you don't want to talk about it, that's fine. I'm more just wondering… why are you even here? Could you not afford tuition? Or…” He paused. “Forgive me. It's been a year, but I've seldom seen you around.”

“No. It wasn't tuition. If I recall correctly, I had a full ride to Junolyx, but a student threatened me into throwing the examination. I can't exactly remember why, but it was something insignificant.”

Hayden's admission stabbed Rosen in the spinal cord. It made it clear that Hayden was always talented, but he played stupid to keep Rosen overconfident. As shameful as it was, Rosen fell for it—hook, line, and sinker.

Rosen felt an unquenchable urge to call Hayden out, but a piercing scream snapped him out of his trance.

He turned and saw two instructors holding down a student.

“It’s breaking! Get a sedative!”

Rosen watched the seizing student like everyone else—but Hayden didn't even open his eyes. That annoyed Rosen. Didn’t Hayden have any empathy? Rosen pushed himself up to confront him, but he stalled when he heard Kayline’s voice.

“There's no way… How didn’t you notice this guy?

Rosen turned and saw his dream girl staring at Hayden, mouth agape. She could clearly see what the instructors saw—and was captivated by it. Reverent even.

“I’m telling you,” Lina said, “he seriously wasn’t like this yesterday. Like, at all.”

“Well, yeah, that’s pretty obvious. The question is why he stopped pretending.”

“There's no way he was pretending. No one's that good at hiding.”

“Well apparently he is. Even teachers can't breathe like that. This is the craziest thing I've ever seen.”

“Seriously?” Lina studied Hayden and then turned back. “Whoa. What’s with that creepy smile?”

“Hey! It’s not creepy. I just think… it's kinda cool. Acting powerless till the last second and then coming out in full force? It's a look.”

Jealousy hit Rosen’s brain like a maglev train. He knew it was jealousy; he knew jealousy was ugly—but the emotion was irrepressible. It gripped his heart in a stranglehold for the rest of the day, and before he knew what was happening, he found himself walking to the head examiner’s private quarters after nightfall.

Rosen knocked three times on her door—loudly.

A stern woman with a tight bun opened the door. “Can you explain why a student is in the administrator building?”

Rosen didn’t answer. Instead, he asked a question of his own, “Are you opposed to bribes, Ms. Narin?”

Her eyes widened with sharp indignation. “Is that a serious question?”

He lifted a physical card. “That’s what the other guy said when I asked him to let me in. But you see, when you offer people a ton of money to do something insignificant, they tend to do it. How’s two hundred thousand untraceable credits for doing something insignificant?”
