As the sound of more warlocks portaling into the hallway on the other side of the door sounded, Elion braced the door. He frantically scanned the room, but there was no other way to get out.

“The longer we wait the harder it will be,” Elion shouted. “Bring Liora!”

He activated his armor and ran to the door. “We need to go before Dorian shows up.”

Elion pulled open the door. Before he could step out into the hall, he was blasted by waves of Zelian power.

The attacks smashed the door from the hinges, and picked Elion up, throwing him across the room. He hit the back wall so hard that it chipped beneath his impact. At lower levels, he would have been killed.

Shaking his head, Elion climbed to his feet. He ran back to the doorframe, daring to stick his head out again.

More Zelian blasts smashed into the doorframe, sending shards and splinters of rock spraying into the room.

“This is not good,” Yaret shouted. He fished around in his backpack.

“What should we do?” Elion gasped.

Yaret produced one of the small explosives he had brought with him from the wreckage of the helijet. He wired up a detonator, then held the trigger in one hand.

Then he slipped up to the doorway and tossed it through.

Before the bomb hit the ground, it was wrapped up in a glowing bubble of violet light. It floated there, near the entrance to the room. Yaret clicked uselessly at the trigger in his hand.

He cursed.

Then flashes of teal laser light shone through the doorway. Elion risked a glimpse, and peered out. Further down the hall a warlock fought through a portal as Keyla grappled and pummeled the woman, falling through the portal with her.

Keyla smacked the woman in the face, knocking her to the ground, and blasted her with a rifle. She whirled and sent a spray of shots down the hall into the warlocks as she backed up, taking cover behind a nearby pillar.

The astonished warlocks around her scrambled to react.

“Keyla!” Elion yelled. “We’re here!” He waved from the doorway, then ducked back as more warlock attacks flew his direction.

Keyla’s voice echoed down the hall, “Hang tight, the others are coming!”

“We don’t have time,” Elion shouted back. “We gotta get out of here.”

He wracked his brains for a plan.

“Yaret, can you prime all your explosives? If we throw a bunch of them at once, maybe they won’t be able to catch them all. Then you can detonate them. I’ll carry Leora. We’ll run through. Keyla can cover us.”

Yaret nodded, consideringly. “We might get one or two through that way,” he said. “It wouldn’t be much of a distraction.”

“I can’t think of another option.”

“Let’s try it.”

Yaret started preparing the explosives from his backpack, priming them, then putting them back in the bag.

Teal and violet light flashed beyond the doorway as warlocks engaged Keyla.

More voices joined the shouting, and an increased rate of laser fire splattered through the halls.

Laryn heard the voices of Isalyn and Selna. He peeked and caught a glimpse of them far down the hall, joining Keyla in her position behind the pillars. They’d drawn away many of the warlocks, who worked to shield each other, and the warlocks who pinned down Elion and Yaret.

Yaret held up his backpack filled with explosives. “I’m ready,” he said.

Several warlocks still targeted anything that moved past the doorway of the room they were in.

Then, with a loud crack, another portal opened, and Dorian strode through, blood dripping from an injury at his eyebrow. His nose looked bruised and broken. His robe hung in tatters, scorched and burned in places, ripped in others—but the man looked confident. He smiled as he met Elion’s eyes.

Elion ducked back behind the wall.

Dorian let out a deep laugh. “Like shooting fish in a barrel,” Dorian gloated. “Kill you both now, and I control the throne!”

“Crap. What do we do now? What happened to Zev?” Elion muttered.

A massively powerful blast of energy rocketed through the door. Stones at the far wall cracked, throwing jagged shrapnel around the room. Elion moved to protect Liora, his ascended state taking the brunt of the impact.

Yaret gasped, as several deep wounds opened up on his exposed flesh.

Dorian walked closer.

“You can’t possibly match me,” Dorian declared. “I’m far more powerful than you realize. And now you’ve brought me the key to unlocking even more power.”

“You can’t die,” Yaret muttered. “Promise me that you aren’t going to let Dorian win.”

“We won’t let him win, Yaret,” Elion said, the words feeling hopeless as he said them.”

Yaret tightened his jaw.

“We have to stop Dorian,” he said. “Are you ready to run?”

“What are you planning?”

“Just pick her up.”

Elion picked up Liora as Yaret shouldered his backpack.

“Wait. Aren’t you going to throw those bombs?” he said.

Yaret shook his head. “I don’t have enough. They would catch too many of them. It has to be like this.”

“No, Yaret!” Elion yelled.

“Run as soon as you hear the explosion!” Yaret shouted, sprinting through the door.

He was struck by several blasts of Zelian power, which snapped limbs and sent his body skittering and spinning across the floor.

Yaret slid to a stop at Dorian’s feet, as a violet bubble of shimmering light enveloped him and lifted him off the ground.

Yaret floated, rotating slowly.

“Yaret!” Elion screamed. The man’s eyes rolled, unfocused. His mouth moved, but the sound was blocked by the bubble.

“Brave, but foolish,” Dorian said as Yaret turned to face him. Blood dripped from Yaret’s injuries, then floated around him in little droplets.

Yaret held up his hand, showing the trigger. He smiled.

Dorian’s cool, confident mask melted away with a look of surprise.

Yaret clicked the plunger down on the detonator.

His backpack exploded with the light of the sun. The power of the detonation ripped the violet bubble spell to shreds.

A wave of heat blasted through the door, forcing Elion to shelter behind the wall as warlocks screamed outside. He shielded Liora with his body.

The concussion left his ears ringing.

Yaret.

His sacrifice wouldn’t go to waste. Elion pulled himself to his feet, and carried his sister out of the door.

The smell of burning robes and flesh hung heavy in the air. Carnage littered the floor.

The only remining sign of Yaret was a large black scorch mark.

Dorian’s body leaned, half sitting against the far wall. The man stirred, eyes opening, unfocused and dazed.

Elion cursed. The man shouldn’t have survived that!

He cursed and cried as he ran, heading toward Keyla’s position. His friends shouted and waved, but he couldn’t hear anything.

He stepping over charred and burned warlock bodies, pushing forward.

The warlocks of the Black Wall who’d been fighting against his friends were knocked down, some of them unconscious. They’d been far enough from the blast to avoid the worst of it.

One warlock struggled to his hands and knees.

Elion kicked the man in the head as he passed.

Then he was in among the safety of the pillars, and Isalyn, Selna, Keyla, and Teft. They gathered around him.

“Let’s go!” Elion shouted, and they all ran, deeper into the belly of the Palace of a Million Stars.
