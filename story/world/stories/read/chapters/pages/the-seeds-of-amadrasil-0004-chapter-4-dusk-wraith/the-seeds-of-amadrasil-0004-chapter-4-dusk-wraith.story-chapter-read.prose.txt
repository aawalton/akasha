The crunch of heavy boots on dry, sharp gravel filled the broad tunnel as the harvest team followed as closely behind the hunters as was safe. The rocky walls were bare, save for many cracks and crevices and smaller tunnels leading elsewhere, most too tiny to navigate, and the all-pervasive metallic tang that hung in the air grew stronger, like being locked in a roomful of rusty nails. Nobody had seen even a hint of a Shadestone Crystal since that first cave, and Kal was beginning to despair that his risking severe mana sickness to work this portal world would ultimately prove to be all for nothing.

He hauled on his cart, trudging along behind Erin and the Lycan as they pushed on through the gloom, lit mainly by the flickering glow produced by the two mages, Tess and Nirelle, just in front of them. In a reversal of cost and priority that could only happen inside a portal world, Erin had turned off her headlamp to preserve battery in favor of the mana users.

“I can’t believe he’s making us go through with this,” Tess muttered, an anxious whisper that easily carried back to Kal’s ears.

“He has his contract to fulfil,” Nirelle whispered. “Arkanis always favors hardline loyalists. Vohl is originally from Veyrhaven in Skeldmark. You know how rigid Northreach Alliance folk are.”

Kal glanced at her, but she wasn’t looking his way. Quickly, he retrieved his pill bottle and downed another two headache tablets. He stashed the evidence and washed it down with a swig from his canteen.

“What if we come up against a Hemorath?” Tess asked in a low, worried voice. Her flame flickered, dancing endlessly between the knuckles of her right hand.

“Well, he’d better be as good as he claims,” Nirelle said. “Those things have ridiculous regeneration. They’re tough to put down.”

“And if it’s a Dreadmaw, or… a Reaper?” Tess insisted, her voice taking on an even lower whisper.

“Well, I sure hope he has the balls to call off the raid if we’re outmatched. We have no hope against an elite monster—those are D-rank at a minimum,” Nirelle said.

“I hope so, too,” Tess said, her fist trembling visibly in the red light of her flames. “I’d like to be alive to spend my paycheck.”

Kal could sympathize with that desire at least. Fortunately, monsters outranking the portal rank estimate were rare, and it would be unlikely that they encountered something as deadly as a Dreadmaw. Hemoraths were already considered to tip the scales at the highest end for what an E-rank monster was capable of. A Dreadmaw was basically the D-rank version, bigger and meaner, and with this hunter team, a D-rank threat of that caliber would be a significant challenge.

He turned toward Erin, but as he did, a flicker of white caught Kal’s eye, and suddenly the Lycan was standing between the two whispering hunters up ahead. She rose up onto her toes and sniffed at Tess’s neck.

“Hey! Watch it!” the mage yelped, shoving the Lycan away with both hands, but her efforts seemed wholly ineffective—as if the unusual wolfkin girl were somehow far heavier than she looked.

The snow-white Lycan wrinkled her nose and backed away.

What is she after? Kal didn’t know all that much about Lycan society or culture, but they were known to be pack creatures. Is she looking for a new pack? Or trying to figure out what the pecking order is?

“Piss off, filthy Lycan,” Tess exclaimed, her voice echoing shrilly in the tunnel. “Don’t sneak up on me like that! I’m a fire mage, you know?” The flames dancing along the backs of her knuckles brightened dramatically, and a wave of heat surged out from her, but the Lycan ignored her, returning to her spot beside Erin.

“Smells funny,” the girl said. “She’s not strong.”

Kal nodded, surprised to find the Lycan confirming his opinion of the twitchy fire mage.

“Hey, you take that back!” Tess called out. “Fucking hollow.”

Kal was about to say something about the slur, and by the way Erin stiffened up beside him, she was too, but they were both interrupted.

“Quiet!” Brannic hissed. “There’s something up ahead.”

Kal’s senses were no match for those of the D-rank team leader, but, as they crept down the tunnel, now in near silence, the shadows grew deeper, clustering near the ceiling and in the jagged indentations along the walls, like water pooling in shallow depressions. The tunnel began to widen, and a few minutes later, they emerged into a twisted cavern. They were high up on a narrow terrace, overlooking an irregularly shaped chamber the size of his parents’ apartment back in Barrio Brisas. A steeply sloped ramp of loose, porous pebbles and rock fragments to the left led down into a sunken chamber filled with small crimson pools and dotted with razor-sharp, metallic stalagmites. The damp patches on the walls were thick with corrosion and visibly etched by the trickling flow of water, suggesting the presence of some acidic components in the reddish runoff—consistent with the sharp, acrid smell that hung heavy in the air. Probably not a good place to go for a dip. Acidic demon hot springs—so relaxing.

Heavy shadows clung to the roof like lurking thunder clouds and curtained the pair of passages leading out from the lower reaches of the chamber to such an extent that it became hard to see where the edge of the rocky walls gave way to open passages.

“Ooh, dusk ivy!” Sarn said, giving a low whistle of appreciation.

Kal snapped a quick look at the taciturn scout, a man who had barely said a word to anyone so far except when directly addressed by Brannic, and found his ever-restless eyes scouring the walls and the pools below. Lining the boulders and sprawling across the ground was an unusual profusion of growth—a vine-like plant that seemed to cling to the rock with dark, shadowy claws, barely visible in the gloom. Small fibrous leaves of midnight black sprouted from the stems, seeming to consume the light from the crimson pools.

“Why’s he interested in that?” Kal asked. “Is it useful for anything?”

“Not officially,” Erin whispered back, an echo of her earlier grin returning to her face. “But I heard through the grapevine that Xenova has made a breakthrough creating cloth with cloaking properties, and this ivy is supposed to be one of the primary ingredients. It’s worth a substantial amount to the right research labs at the moment, whether you’re selling to Xenova Labs or their competition. Come, I’ll show you how to pull it off the walls without spilling the shadow mana.”

“Nice,” Kal said. Better yet, there seemed to be a decent amount of ivy to harvest. It seemed to prefer skirting the boundaries between the damp walls and the obsidian inclusions, favoring a solid grasp on the rock over being fully bathed in acid water, but still near enough to sip with those claw-like roots.

“Harvesters, wait in the hall,” Brannic ordered. “Hunters, secure the chamber.”

Drex leapt over the ledge, landing surprisingly lightly among the pools in the lower reaches of the chamber. Sarn darted down the gravel slope with rapid, dexterous footwork to join him a few seconds later, both hunters eyeing the exit tunnels and the shadows closely.

Tess and Nirelle followed along, more sedately, raising their respective lights to try and pierce the persistent gloom.

Kal hunkered down using the entryway as cover and noted how Brannic remained on the terrace, surveying everything from above.

“Secure,” Drex announced after a few minutes.

“Ok, harvest team, you have twenty minutes. Get what you can,” Brannic announced.

“Right, let’s go,” Erin said, and the harvesters trooped into the chamber.

Kal hauled his cart to the scree-strewn ramp, but when he tipped the cart onto the steep slope, it began to slide under its own weight, unleashing a rattling patter of pebbles rolling down the ramp. Quickly, he scrambled around and braced his shoulder against it, but the muscle weakness from his mana sickness chose that moment to make itself known. His trembling legs buckled, slipping out from under him, and he fell hard on the loose gravel. He gritted his teeth as the bulky cart began to tip over toward him and thrust out a hand to block it, but he slipped again on the treacherous ground.

The cart stopped. Then, it rose into the air as if by its own accord.

Kal stared dumbfounded as the little Lycan girl danced down the slope, carrying the cart like it was made of air. That’s like two hundred and fifty pounds at least! Maybe three hundred…

“You ok?” Erin asked, reaching down to offer him a hand.

“Uh, yeah,” he said, taking her grip and standing up. “I knew Lycans were supposed to be strong, but… wow.”

“Pretty handy,” Erin agreed, grinning broadly in the gloom.

“You’ve worked with her before?” Kal asked.

“Yup. The first time was at the tail end of a job two months ago,” Erin said. “I was impressed then, but my regulars are regular. I didn’t have an opening until I had to fill this team on short notice.”

“And she was available?”

“I don’t think she gets much work. It’s hard to break into an established team, and she’s non-human,” Erin said, twisting her mouth to the side. “She’s delightful, though, and she works hard. I might try to rotate her in more often. She’s definitely at the top of my substitute list.”

“Mhm,” Kal said, nodding while his eyes tracked the petite, white-furred industrial crane as she gently lowered his heavy cart to the ground at the bottom of the slope.

“Here, let’s go fill up on loot,” Erin said, “Our fearless leader has us on the clock.”

Fearless, brave, and a bit of a jerk. Ugh. Am I actually jealous—he bit back on that thought before it could fester. Seeds were for other people. People with means, a patron, or some form of luck that had never threatened to come his way. Stow it. Do the job. I’ve got to make this one count.

Kal followed her, scrambling down the slope to the lower reaches of the cavern, studiously ignoring the druidic healer’s calculating gaze, and set to the task of learning how to detach the tough, fibrous, shadowy vines from the rockface without damaging them.

Dusk Ivy was an apt name. Although it obviously bore no relation to any Valmyran ivy species, the heart-shaped leaves reminded him of the common creeper, and it truly earned the ‘dusk’ label, emitting an aura of darkness even after being harvested and stashed in his cart. If Xenova Labs had truly learned how to extract the fibers and make cloth without sacrificing the remarkable billowing darkness, they would make a killing on the market selling cloaks that truly seemed made of shadows.

As Kal turned toward a fresh patch, a ripple of unease prickled at the base of his neck. He froze as the air suddenly grew chill and incomprehensibly heavier. Shadows spilled down from the roof, swirling and thickening like ink poured slowly into water.

He snapped his gaze upward, scouring the gathering darkness as the shadows cascaded down, blotting out the exits. An unnatural stillness fell upon the cavern, as if the rocks themselves were holding their breath. A strange susurrus rose from within the billowing darkness, caressing the nape of his neck with whispers and indistinct murmurs. Something large swam through the cloud of shadows hanging above, like a shark circling in murky waters. A scream came to him, as if echoing from a great distance.

“Kal…”

Huh? He glanced quickly behind him. “Did somebody…”

“Shhhh… Kal… Kal! Are you there?”

“What?”

“You shouldn’t be here.”

Kal’s eyes darted back and forth, but he was alone. There was nothing but shifting shadows.

“You’re not strong enough,” Lucien hissed. “You’ll fail her, just like you failed them.”

Kal grabbed his temples as his head began to hammer with throbbing pain and the shadows boiled over.

“Kal, you promised me!” Mira wailed.

“Kal? Where were you when Lyssa was dying?” his dad demanded. “Why did you abandon us?”

“I was there…” Kal moaned. “I couldn’t… I… I’m not…” His pistol trembled in his hands even though he had no recollection of ever drawing it. Sweat slicked his palms. “I’m sorry, Dad. I’m not strong enough to protect her. I was never strong enough.”

He leaned back, perhaps to scream, perhaps to cry, or perhaps to curse at the skies, but he froze at the sight of a pair of crimson, malevolent orbs piercing through the depths of the darkness high up on the roof. They flashed like hot embers and fixed on him with a hunger that impaled his rising scream of warning, nailing it to the back of his throat.

Kal struggled to raise his pistol, but his hands shook uncontrollably.

The eyes swept past him, dismissing him. The whispers and pressure eased fractionally. Shadows swirled, and without sound or warning, a heavy, sleek feline shadow slunk across the roof, stalking the group of dazed monster hunters wandering aimlessly below, murmuring or whimpering to themselves. Powerful muscles rippled beneath velvety jet-black fur, obscured by shifting, crimson-stained shadows that flowed and twisted about it as though alive.

Kal’s heavy pistol bucked hard against his hands, and a deafening crack rang out, but with his weak and shaking arms, his shot went wide, ricocheting off the rocks and whining away into the distance.

Before he could shout or even pull the trigger again, the monster sprang from the roof, landing in total silence among the hunters. It bit down on Tess’s shoulder with jaws like a bear trap and sprang back to the roof in a single bound, vanishing into the sea of shadows and taking its prey with it. Her screams sounded strangely muted, like she’d been muffled with a pillow.

Over on the other side of the cavern, Brannic blinked in surprise, broken out of his stupor by the brutal sound of gunfire in the confines of the cave.

With a roar of outrage, the black-armored fighter leapt upward through the darkness, trailing a flaming sword that seemed to consume the shadows with ravenous hunger. Brannic Vohl crashed into the cavern roof, snapping shadow-cloaked stalactites like twigs against his Arkanis-emblazoned armor. His unsheathed blade blazed with heat and bright red flames, sizzling through the air as he swung. The blade bit deeply into the darkness. Something screeched.

Holy shit! That sword! Kal could not actually see the mana fueling the warrior’s weapon, but that was no mundane weapon.

Brannic fell back to the cavern floor, tangled in a hissing, scratching, biting ball of midnight fury nearly twice his size.

“Dusk Wraith!” he roared. “Wake up! It’s messing with your minds! Nirelle! Heal! Now!”

Kal shuddered as he finally drew a long breath, and his trembling began to fade. Erin was out cold, sprawled on the ground beside him. The wolfkin was hunched over her, growling low in her chest with the fur on the back of her neck standing on end, but she trembled just as much as he did. Everywhere he looked, Kal found the members of the harvesting crew lying where they’d fallen like so much sheared wheat.

“Fight!” the wolfkin growled, muscles straining.

Steadier now, Kal stood, placing a hand on the wolfkin’s shoulder. “Let him have it,” he whispered, nodding toward the D-rank warrior and his sizzling blade.

She struggled against his grip until he added, “We’ll protect Erin.”

“Protect. Yes.”

As the rest of the Pale Fang team roused themselves sluggishly from the Dusk Wraith’s nightmare, Brannic’s sword cut blazing arcs of precision and speed. The elusive monster of whispers and shadows was fast—almost too fast—but somehow Brannic’s sword was faster. He whipped it down in a brutal strike that left a trail of flame and dark demon blood across the monster’s shoulder. It hissed in pain and lashed out with razor-sharp claws that flickered wickedly in the dim light. They crashed against his armor, screeching loudly, scoring small cuts in the gaps between the plates.

Finally, Nirelle recovered, and a soft green glow surrounded the still form of the badly mauled fire mage lying crumpled on the ground. Next to recover was Sarn. The rogue shifted, almost impossible to see in his element, another patch of darkness unleashing a deadly flurry of dagger strikes into the monster’s back. Finally, the bruiser roused. With an incoherent roar, Drex dove into the fray with his steel gauntlets gleaming as he punched with reckless fury.

All power and no finesse, Kal noted. Definitely a Strength build. Brannic was a far more skilled fighter; his economical moves undeniable proof of his extensive training. And that enchanted sword! A real, authentic Artifact!

Suddenly, the air chilled again and began to press down on him, constricting his breathing. Right on the edge of awareness, Kal thought he could hear the whispers again. Instantly, he scoured the shadows for any signs of movement, raising his pistol into a guard position. A second pair of crimson, glowing eyes opened in the darkest, shadowy corner and locked with his.

“Brannic!” he yelled. “Three o’clock, high!”

The experienced warrior glanced up and swore quietly. “You two finish this one off,” he ordered. “Do not let it get away!” Then he leapt to the roof with a second stunning display of power and agility, trailing fresh flames from his sword. He swung, catching the monster somewhere near the midsection, and both of them came crashing to the ground amid hisses and screeching.

Kal frowned while he studied their battle. He’s not guarding himself? The sword flickered and flashed in an overwhelmingly aggressive style, sacrificing any attempt at defense in order to deal as much damage as possible. But when the claws raked across his chest, producing a nail-curling screech, his strategy finally became clear. Huh, he trusts the armor that much? Or maybe he has to? Defensive skills were often harder to observe, but Kal hadn’t seen any evidence of one in action.

“They win,” the Lycan said, her coiled muscles visibly relaxing, and Kal finally withdrew his hand.

“Yes, they’ve got this,” he said, glancing first at his hand and then the unexpectedly soft, white fur over rock-hard muscle where it had just rested. Out in the bowels of the cave, Tessin Varn stood up using Nirelle’s hand for support and entered the fray with a fiery glint in her eye and a hard, pressed line to her lips. She unleashed an unrelenting, rapid-fire sequence of mini fireballs that sizzled and cracked against smoking demonic hide, and when it finally collapsed with an unearthly, spine-chilling wail, they all joined Brannic and finished off the second one.

Despite easily predicting the outcome at this point, Kal still let out a sigh of relief when it was done.
