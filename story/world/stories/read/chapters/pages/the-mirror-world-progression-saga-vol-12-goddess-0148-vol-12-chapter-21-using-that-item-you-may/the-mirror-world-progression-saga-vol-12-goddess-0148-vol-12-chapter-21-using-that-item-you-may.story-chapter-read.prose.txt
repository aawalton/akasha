Samantha floated over the army, now with a hollow in the middle, and multiple paths ready like funnels for what was to come. She pulled out the artifact Redberry had gifted her and told her to save it for a pivotal moment.

Now was that moment.

The brown horn was covered in white runes, wrapping it in multiple rings. Samantha put the Horn of the Wild’s Vengeance to her lips and blew. A long, eerie note traveled far and wide, as Samantha felt its call propagate deep into the spirit world.

When the note ended, for a moment, it seemed as if nothing would happen at all. Ghostly mist finally began to form below in the hollow left, and even above it, as gradually, many dividing shapes took form.

Animal specters of every stripe imaginable appeared — bears, predatory felines, deer, crocodiles, wolves, dogs, apes, horses, elephants… The list was endless, and so too were those that appeared below. Above their heads, birds appeared, mostly hawks, eagles, and vulture-like types, but songbirds weren’t without representation.

It was a massive, endless horde. The hollow couldn’t contain them, and men were exclaiming and moving aside in fright as specters appeared in their vicinity.

Every ghostly apparition materialized already in motion; a deafening din of chaotic, aggregate sounds shook the ground and heavens. Growls, calls, howls, shrieks — they were cries of rage seeking release. The specters didn’t care who they’d unleash it on, but thankfully, they went for their hornblower’s enemies.

The charge was complete insanity as the mortal soldiers scrambled out of their way — sometimes too slowly, as they got to experience the wonderful feeling of a ghost passing through them. Only halfway down did the horde begin forming dense stampede lines, perhaps mostly just to increase their haste getting to their enemies.

The big exception was the elves or other fae. Anywhere they were present, they just stood and watched solemnly, and the animal specters invariably went around them.

And they just kept appearing, more and more and more, racing for the enemy. Samantha guessed at least five to seven thousand before the first of the flyers began swooping down and ripping with talons and beaks.

The common soldierly weapons of their enemies were ill-suited to defend or counterattack, most of them swinging right through. Meanwhile, the spectral natural weapons seemed to turn physical just as they touched flesh, ripping into it behind armor and even getting stuck in many cases.

Screams of terror and pain resounded… and that was just the birds.

Oh my fucking goddess, this is more than I’d ever dream would happen.

Bears barreled into the ranks like cannonballs, and wolves, sometimes giant-sized, gored and dropped every man they set themselves on. Felines pounced, elephants trampled, apes smashed, and the whole enemy line wilted like weeds dissolving before a wildfire. They tried to break, but the lines were too dense, and they just became a grinder of panicking men. The only exception was the ‘mind zombie’ type pockets and the scattered undead, which held their position and were quickly surrounded and torn apart.

The Dominion’s initial adjustments to deal with the specters were quite flaccid. They tried to perform mass weapon enhancements to empower common soldiers to fight back.

The Counter Crew was ready for it, blocking the majority of these that were on the front lines, making the push all the more intense as multiple thousands likely fell in minutes.

Inevitably, the enemy managed to enchant groups far enough behind in the ranks, and then rotate them to the front, finally giving their panicking former frontliners somewhere to go.

Meanwhile, occasional magic blasts of this or that type started to take out the spectral flyers. The least counterable occurrences were the entropic distortions coming from towers, which steadily whittled down the specters.

In addition, the witchhunters moved up and organized out of their prior pockets to help, setting their hounds on the animals. The hounds apparently had the advantage here, thanks to their near-invulnerability, but the greater exposure of the witchhunters began forcing casualties in their ranks, wiping out the control of the hounds in the process, who’d howl and mourn and eventually disappear when their owners died.
