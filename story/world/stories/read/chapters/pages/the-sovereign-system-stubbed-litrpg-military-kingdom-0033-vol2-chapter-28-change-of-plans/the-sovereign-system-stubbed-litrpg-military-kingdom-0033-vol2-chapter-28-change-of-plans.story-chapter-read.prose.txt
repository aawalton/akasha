The next move took some time.

Tristan, ever cautious, chose not to advance his trebuchet, continuing his conservative play. But in doing so, he left a small opening on the right flank.

Luke saw it instantly.

Without hesitation, he sent his piece forward, pressing into the exposed flank—a bold maneuver under the circumstances. Once again, it seemed he was willing to risk his own pieces to gain ground.

Tristan paused, studying the board in silence. Morning sunlight spilled across the table, casting gentle highlights on his face and revealing familiar features—sharp cheekbones, the furrowed brow, the calculating gaze. It reminded Luke of Master Gale, his old instructor.

The memory stirred something in him.

He had despised Gale at first—an old elitist who looked down on him. But over time, as Luke proved his worth, that disdain had turned to grudging respect, and eventually, mentorship.

“You’re quite bold, Viscount Drakon,” Tristan said, stroking the modest goatee on his chin. “To so readily sacrifice your pieces to breach my back line…”

His voice was calm, but there was a pointed edge to it. Not praise—more like curiosity tinged with criticism.

Luke caught the implication.

In Chinese Chess, a person’s strategy often mirrored their nature. Those who sacrificed pieces freely to push forward were typically seen as aggressive—bloodstained commanders who prized victory over lives.

It was clear Tristan had placed him in that category.

But he was mistaken.

“All will be revealed in time,” Luke replied, offering a quiet smile.

And so the game continued.

The breach on the right widened. Luke's forces slipped in like a flood. Tristan’s back line soon turned chaotic—his pieces tangled in defense, unable to respond with any real momentum.

Then came the final strike.

Luke advanced his trebuchet, pinning Tristan’s adviser in place.

The head librarian exhaled and leaned back in his chair, his tension melting away. Yet rather than scowling in defeat, he wore a faint, satisfied smile.

“I concede,” he said softly.

Luke rose slightly and cupped his fist. “Thank you for the game.”

Tristan chuckled, shaking his head. “Forgive me, Viscount Drakon. I was hasty in my judgment—too narrow in my vision.”

“The fault is not yours, Head Librarian,” Luke replied humbly. “If anything, I must apologize for deceiving you during the match.”

At that, Tristan laughed aloud, voice echoing in the quiet room. “No, no—the fault lies with me. If I were stronger, I’d have seen through your stratagem from the start.”

He gave a small nod, his tone sincere. “Had this been a real battle, I’d have been wiped out before I realized I was even under attack.”

Luke accepted the compliment with measured humility. He knew better than to refute it outright—doing so might be taken as arrogance, or worse, insincerity.

They sat in companionable silence for a moment.

Then Tristan spoke again.

“I understand now. Your skill, your foresight... It makes sense why the king himself would entrust you with the title of general, despite your youth.”

“Satisfy this old man’s curiosity—what did you learn about me through our match?” Tristan asked, his eyes dancing with anticipation.

Luke paused, choosing his words carefully. Flattery would do more harm than good here. If he hoped to secure this legendary subordinate, honesty—tempered with respect—was his best approach.

“I can see that you have exceptional awareness of your supply lines,” Luke began confidently. “Your actions reflected a cautious, conservative nature. If I hadn’t taken a bold risk to breach your flank, I imagine the battle would’ve dragged on far longer—and I likely would’ve been worn down.”

Tristan raised an eyebrow, intrigued.

“But,” Luke continued, “that same caution made it harder for you to respond quickly to sudden aggression. Even after identifying me as an offensive player, there were still a few moments when you could have turned the tide.”

A dry chuckle escaped the head librarian.

“Easy to say in hindsight,” he replied, shaking his head. “But I could already tell… even if I had reacted faster, the result would’ve been the same. Your Screen Horse Defense was meticulous—there were countless paths to the same conclusion. Some might’ve cost you more pieces, but the outcome would still be your victory.”

Then, with a clap of his hands and a bright grin, he added, “Well, I humbly accept my defeat. I feel at ease now, knowing that you’ll be leading the king’s forces in the retreat. Of course, I’ll lend my expertise to support you.”

Luke rose and cupped his fist in respect. “Thank you, Head Librarian.”

Tristan waved the formality away. “It’s nothing. You’re a capable young man, Viscount Drakon. But I must ask a favor—please, call me Tristan from now on.”

Luke’s eyes lit up. He suppressed a smile, but the gratitude in his voice was clear. “Then I must ask the same of you. Please call me Luke. I may hold the title of Viscount, but I’ve neither land nor estate to show for it.”

Tristan laughed heartily. “Titles mean little in the field, and even less on the chessboard. Very well, Luke. I look forward to working alongside you.”

They exchanged a few more pleasantries before Tristan paused, as if something had just occurred to him.

“Forgive me,” he said, chuckling, “I was so engrossed in the match, I forgot to ask—what brings you here today? Surely, you didn’t come just to humiliate this old man at chess?”

Luke let out a small sigh and nodded. “It is as you say. I came to inquire about the mission—hoping to prepare before we depart tomorrow.”

Tristan leaned back in his chair, folding his arms. “Aside from the finer details, the mission briefing was fairly straightforward. What more would you like to know?”

Luke hesitated only briefly. “From what I understand, the Second Prince will be accompanying our expedition. But… is the Crown Prince also setting off somewhere, perhaps leading another group?”

Tristan narrowed his eyes, thoughtfully stroking his goatee. “Hmm. Why do you ask? This shouldn’t affect our mission—unless…” He trailed off, then gave Luke a probing look. “Are you suggesting we might encounter trouble tied to the succession battle?”

Not us, Luke thought. But the Crown Prince...

If the Crown Prince were to fall during this mission, the only eligible heir remaining would be the Second Prince. And since Luke believed that Prince Lyon’s faction included traitors aligned with Lhair, this campaign was the perfect moment for them to act.

Even if the Crown Prince wasn’t killed outright, failure in his mission would damage his standing in the succession war—perhaps irreparably.

“I’m just trying to prepare for the worst,” Luke admitted, neither denying nor confirming Tristan’s suggestion.

“I see…” Tristan replied, his eyes narrowing slightly. “I don’t know much myself, but I did hear the Crown Prince will also been dispatched to retrieve one of our forces stationed in the Qin Empire.”

As I suspected…

“May I ask,” Luke said carefully, “who was appointed general to lead that retreat?”

“I believe the Crown Prince will act as his own general,” Tristan said. “That said, the king will assign him his own personal guards. Even if he encounters an enemy he can’t defeat, I have no doubt they’ll be under orders to ensure his survival—even at the cost of their own lives.”

Luke felt his chest tighten.

Even if the Crown Prince had studied warfare, there was no guarantee he could command effectively on the field. What is the King thinking? Does he truly place that much faith in his son? Or... was something else in motion?

Still, he kept his doubts to himself.

“That’s a relief, then,” Luke replied calmly. “It would be far too tragic if something were to happen to the Crown Prince right now.”

Tristan raised an eyebrow at that, but after a brief pause, nodded in agreement. Sensing the conversation had run its course, he shifted the mood.

“Shall we play another game?” he offered, his voice lighter. “Now that I understand you better, I won’t make the same mistakes.”

Luke chuckled. “Sure. I’m always up for a game.”

A few hours later, Luke returned to his quarters, having spent the morning with Tristan. On the way back, he made a stop at the library—absorbing another two thousand books into his system. With the mission approaching, he knew he wouldn’t be back at the academy for a while.

As he opened the door to his room, a sweet, subtle fragrance greeted him.

Victoria stood near the window, holding one of the new perfume batches Luke had formulated. She gave him a brilliant smile, the kind that could light up a room.

“Welcome back,” she said softly.

Kayson and Sebastian were still in class. Normally, Luke would have been too—but the mission had taken priority. Gathering intelligence from Tristan had been far more important.

“Victoria…” he began, tone growing serious. “I can’t have you accompany me on this mission.”

The smile slipped from her face in an instant. It was like watching a flower wilt before his eyes. And it brought him no satisfaction.

“Young master,” she said coldly, beginning a slow walk toward him, “I certainly hope you have a suitable reason.”

She stopped just beside him, placed a hand on his shoulder, and leaned in close—her lips nearly brushing his ear.

“Otherwise,” she whispered, voice low and silken, “I might have no choice but to change your mind.”

A shiver ran down Luke’s spine.

To a stranger, it might have sounded flirtatious. But he knew her too well. That wasn’t seduction—it was a warning.

“I have another mission for you,” Luke said, his voice low and grave. “One that could potentially save the Marxx Kingdom from utter ruin.”

At his words, Victoria took a step back. Her warm brown eyes studied him intently, scanning his expression, posture—every inch of him, as if searching for a crack in his resolve.

“I don’t care about the Marxx Kingdom, Luke. You should know this,” she said, flat and matter-of-fact.

Luke nodded. He wasn’t surprised. Her only attachment to this kingdom was likely him—and her hatred for Lhair. But that was exactly why he could trust her with this task.

“The factions backing the Second Prince will make a move on the Crown Prince in the coming weeks,” he said. “I need you to infiltrate his guard and protect Prince Leo. No matter what.”

Victoria’s eyes narrowed. “And if I refuse?”

“Then the Marxx Kingdom falls,” Luke replied simply. “To Lhair.”

At the name, a shadow crossed her face. The teasing sparkle vanished from her gaze. Her fair features hardened into a frown as she sank into thought.

“You truly believe this?” she asked after a long pause.

“I could be wrong,” Luke admitted. “But if I am right and we do nothing, the consequences are too severe. Knowing you’re there with him... it would give me peace of mind.”

Victoria held his gaze the entire time. Then, with a sigh heavy with disappointment, she looked away.

“And here I thought we might finally get to spend some time alone together...” she muttered under her breath.

“Pardon?” Luke blinked, not having caught it clearly.

“I said I’ll do it,” she replied, giving him a lopsided, wry smile. “I’ll protect the idiot Crown Prince.”

Luke flinched and glanced around the room, eyes wide. “Victoria!” he hissed, scandalized. “You can’t say that out loud—”

But her sudden laughter cut him off.

Bent over, arms wrapped around her sides, Victoria laughed freely, clearly enjoying his panicked reaction. It took several seconds before she could speak.

“Relax, Luke. You really think I wouldn’t notice if someone was listening in?” she said, shooting him a mischievous glance.

“Ah... right.”

Now that he thought about it, she was a master of assassination, espionage, and infiltration... Of course she would have checked. She probably said it on purpose just to get a rise out of him.

Feeling the tension finally leave his body, Luke sat down on the edge of his bed and pinched the bridge of his nose.

No matter how much time he spent with her, Victoria always found a way to get under his skin.

Still...

I guess it’s not all that bad though...
