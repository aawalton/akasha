It took around an hour for Henry and Maurice to finally reach the ship.

While the fight itself had taken barely a minutes, catching up to the sleek xebec with the wind in its sails took a little more time, especially with Henry taking it slow. As he quietly swam just below the surface, with Maurice settled atop his head like a submarine’s periscope, Henry took it easy on himself, using only some basic camouflage while he went over the aching parts of his soul with his Octominds, nursing the sensitive spots and infusing them with Life Surge and essence of Lifeweaving.

It was working. It wasn’t fast, and it would take a couple of days for the soreness to fade away, but he could see himself being able to freely use his skills in another hour or two without it feeling like rubbing broken glass against his spirit.

With the ship in their sight, Henry was content to tail it from a safe distance. He didn’t feel like mingling or chatting, honestly. He was still working on fixing himself up, and he was still thinking of how the fight had gone. He’d won… but it didn’t feel like it. Henry was very much aware that balancing risk and reward was the key to progress, but this time, he’d gone a bit too far in the risk side of things. That had been unnecessarily risky. It had been too soon to use the compressed skills, and he knew Maurice thought the same, and now that the ship was in sight, the crab finally spoke up.

“[Sooo. I know that usually you’re the one who tells me this stuff, but wasn’t that a little too… reckless? Not the first part. I think the first part of the fight went perfect. We test the A-rank out, found out that it’s too dangerous, and we tried to run. But the second part? Where you almost put yourself out of commission? I’m not gonna lie, I didn’t wanna run either and I would have totally been fine fighting a little more. But I don’t know about injuring yourself that badly. That’s… you know?]”

By now, Henry’s Octominds were very familiar with the healing process, so he really didn’t need to be too involved. He wasn’t too busy to answer. He heard the question perfectly fine. Still, it took him a few seconds to finally find the words. Because no matter how he turned the situation around, he just couldn’t find any good reason aside from not wanting to back off. That was mainly it. He had known he still had cards to play—no matter how dangerous those cards were—and he didn’t want to walk away when he knew he could have won. Plus, even though he had been warned, after seeing how the fight had ended? There was no denying this technique—if one could call it that—had potential.

It just came with too much backlash.

I rushed, Henry thought, a little annoyed at himself. He should have practiced the weaker forms of compression way more before deploying it in a big fight. And as he reviewed the fight, he realized something else.

When Henry had been initially running, his heart hadn’t been in it. He had been watching for an opponent. Looking for a way to set up an ambush. Even if he had said the words “we are retreating”, he hadn’t really meant it. And when he had decided to empower the compressed Lightning Discharge with essence, it was because he knew the basic condensed version might not have been enough, and at that point, they would have actually been in trouble if he hadn’t put his whole back into it. As soon as he transition from a run to an attack, he had to commit.

Henry had started to work on his soul’s resilience for the last few days and he had seen some progress, but by adding essence to the compressed skill, he had basically jumped from two weeks of calisthenics to trying to dead-lift 500 pounds. So no wonder he injured himself.

It was ironic that he’d recently realized how he couldn’t let himself fully fall asleep because of the dangers of this new world, but then he’d jumped with both legs into dangerous situation without a second thought.

“[Yeah,]” Henry finally managed, glancing up at the shining sun. “[I don’t think I have any good excuse other than not wanting to lose. The mistake was using something I wasn’t ready for… because in the excitement of the fight I thought the risk acceptable. So long as we won. Which is another issue. I think I’ve done that a little more often than what would be considered healthy.]”

It wasn’t different from when he’d faced his first shark. Then the serpent. Then almost every other risky encounter that had brought him close to the edge. It had been exhilarating—too exhilarating. Maybe that was the problem. If he let that thrill keep deciding for him, one day it wouldn’t be a temporary injury he walked away with.

Maurice was quiet for a few moments as they dove below the waves. The late afternoon wind was making them choppy. Maybe a storm was approaching.

“[But what if something else showed up? Would you have been able to get us out? I mean, I could always try my best, but… you know?]”

Henry let out a sigh. “[I probably could have forced a teleport. But it might have been bad. For me,]” he sent back, watching the inscrutable depths below. He could make out a school of thin, silver-colored fish a few paces to the right being chased by some tuna. “[Honestly, Maurice? I kinda messed up. We won, and I’m… kinda happy about that? In parts. That had been risky. I can’t afford to take stupid risks like that again. At least… not too often. I guess now’s a good time to get it out of my system for good.]”

Maurice was quiet for a bit, then he rubbed both claws together, mood a little chipper.“[We did win, didn’t we? Did you use your fragment already? I’m definitely going to use mine for levels. I bet it’d have some good stats. But I want to enjoy it, you know? Do you want me to bring out its body? I keep thinking of taking a nibble on it but I’m worried it’ll taste bad. Also, it’s tiny. There isn’t that much of it.]”

Henry chuckled, relieved to move on from the conversation. At least temporarily. “[I bet it’d be good for your toxin resistance. But let me try first, just in case. We’ll do that in a few hours. I got a good dose of that damn thing’s venoms and toxins, and I can tell you that it’s not fun to deal with. I bet its flesh is saturated with the stuff.]”

Henry wasn’t sure of everything it had at its disposal as he hadn’t looked into his interface or sensed what was going on with Venom Production, seeing that he was still busy with the healing his soul part. But he’d bet there were at least two toxins the Stinglance had been working with. One that was caustic to everything—including mana and skills—and the stuff it had in its darts, which was…well, mostly painful. Henry wasn’t sure if it had other functions aside from pain and bleeding, but he himself was pretty resilient to toxins at this point, so he wasn’t exactly the best candidate to test them on. What he wasn’t noticing might be severely dangerous to others.

“[Even when it’s dead it feels a little heavy,]” Maurice grumbled. They kept chatting for a while, casually tracking the clone Henry could sense on the ship—which he used to tell Velistraine that everything was fine and they were just out on a swim—and progressively, bit by bit, the cracks he could sense in his soul slowly stitched themselves shut. It took a couple of hours, and he knew that if he forced any big cast or skill, the freshly healed wounds would rupture, but he expected a couple of days of this before he could restart on some reinforcing exercises. Velistraine had said soul wounds could take weeks but frankly… he didn’t think it’d take that long. This wasn’t the first time he’d gotten some soul damage for what he had just attempted? Henry knew it could have been much worse. He was glad he hadn’t compressed the skills any further. At least all of his invested time in healing had bore fruit.

Well. No more of that technique for a minute, he thought. I’ll need to slowly work my way up with tests and practice. Just like weight lifting. Then, Henry finally spared his interface a look. It was too soon to consume the core, as he worried that might interfere with the healing process, but he was surprised he wasn’t seeing any new venoms updates. Maybe it was just the same as the one he already had, but produced by a stronger skill. As for the caustic toxin, Henry hadn’t been exposed to it too much, but he hoped he’d be able to recover it from the Stinglance’s body.

1x [Wyrdkelp Stinglance Tyrant (A) - Level 17] greater core fragment was collected.

The fight flashed in his mind as he stared at the notification. Aside from the last part, it had been really fun. The Stinglance had been such a terror to keep up with. It had been relentless and honestly, he didn’t remember the last time he’d enjoyed a fight to that degree. Even the lobster’s fight hadn’t been this exciting. As for the crab he’d scored a few days back, that had felt like a glorified B-rank fight. That was on him, of course. He hadn’t faced the crab head-on and instead, he had tricked his way toward the win. But the Stinglance’s speed and savagery hadn’t allowed for cheating, and that made the win feel… just better.

Now he just needed to be less stupid about fights in the future. Especially once they figured out what’s going on with the System and what’s out there. Because even now, he couldn’t help but think of that vision Maurice had.

There were things out there. And they had to be ready for them.
