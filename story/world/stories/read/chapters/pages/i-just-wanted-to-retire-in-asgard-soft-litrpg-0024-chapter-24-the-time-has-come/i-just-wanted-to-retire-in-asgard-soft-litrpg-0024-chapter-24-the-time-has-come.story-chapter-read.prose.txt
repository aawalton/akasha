The climb took hours.

Barry's borrowed legs clicked and scraped against nerve cords thicker than tree trunks. The parasite swarm moved with him in a living river of legs and mouths, thousands of bodies flowing up the wet corridors of the vulture's throat and sinuses.

Barry dug deeper than necessary into a living nerve cord before he forced his limbs to release it. He knew it wasn't the claws malfunctioning. It was him. His human brain kept misfiring. He could feel half-formed instincts that didn't belong to a human being, urges and whispers at the edges of his thoughts.

He stopped. The bugs behind him stopped as well. His claws were mushing and slicing a part of the meat.

He wasn't touching the brain dials on his wrists. The claws were working on their own.

"Maybe... just a little bite." He told himself, as the juicy pink meat drifted near his mouth.

"No!" He shouted, slapping the meat away. He signaled the legs to climb again, and the hive behind him followed. The hunger flared once more, but he knew it wasn't his. It belonged to them.

He ignored it.

Still, some of the larger hunters began lagging. Antennae twitched in patterns that didn't follow his orders. They were testing the new hierarchy, probing for weakness the way every pack animal does when the top dog looks tired.

Barry looked at them. Twenty-seven of them, rare variants, useful for subduing the brain. He didn't waste words.

He pointed with one clawed hand.

"If you lot are hungry, eat your own..."

The loyal ones turned as one and fell on the dissenters. Wet tearing sounds filled the dark. Joints cracked. Shells split. The traitors were dismantled and consumed before they could organize any real resistance, though some useful parts were left behind. It cost time and a few hundred bodies, but a revolution inside someone else's flesh was an inefficiency he refused to tolerate. The survivors learned fast, and the lagging stopped.

[Book analysis: 96%]

The System's notification flickered in the corner of his vision like an impatient child.

Barry didn't even look at it.

"The second I'm free and back in a real workshop, I'm taking my controller and editing whatever passes through your core. Count on it."

Despite his threat, the percentage kept climbing anyway.

Then the air changed. It was thin and cold, carrying the distant scent of bone dust and open Helheim. Something he could almost breathe, unlike the toxic air below.

Finally, he had reached the nasal cavity.

Barry stopped on a ridge of cartilage the size of a bridge. Through the massive nostril openings he could see the gray sky of Helheim, the endless ash plains, and the distant column where the vulture's body still stood. One step. One drop. He could leave this slimy, parasitic mess behind. Fall free. Let the wind take him.

He looked at his hands, his body, his clothes, all covered in slimy mush, his thoughts still deteriorating. If he could get out of here, he could meet Baldr, ask for help sending a signal to Asgard, and breathe fresh Asgardian air again.

Then his stomach rolled. If his body still remembered how to vomit, it would have. He brought his good hand up and slapped himself hard, so hard that his mask shifted slightly.

"Lock the fuck in, Barry. Lock. The. Fuck. In." He said to the empty air, his voice flat and tired, the way only a man who had spent the day wearing insect armor could sound.

"It was tempting. Walk out the nose, freefall through a continent-sized beak, land in a pile of my own failures. Classic Barry exit. But no."

He turned away from the open sky and faced the thicker nerve trunks rising toward the skull.

"I'm not leaving until this oversized feather duster is my bitch. First things first. I'm seizing the air source of all nine realms."

The army followed.

Closer to the brain, the walls changed again. The muscle became denser, laced with layered enchantments that pulsed in complex, interlocking patterns. Runes stacked on runes. Structural wards. Immune wards. It felt like a divine non-disclosure agreement written in flesh. The parasites hit the barrier and bounced off, unable to even get near it.

"Barrier magic..."

Even the largest hunters couldn't force a path. They piled up against the glowing tissue and began to panic, legs scrabbling for purchase that wasn't there.

Barry hung from the ceiling and studied the code.

"Of course," he said. "Always magic. That's why you're still alive, you giant stupid bird. The parasites never reached the central processor because the previous owner put real security on the important parts."

He could destroy it if he had his tools, but all he had was claws, a mesh of meat, bone, and parasites. He could wait for the book analysis to reach one hundred percent and look for another way through.

But no. He tried to overwrite a single rune with a claw, the way he had at the heart. The symbol flared and rejected the change so hard it scorched the parasite tissue on his hand. Smoke rose with the pain.

"This... this is something stronger."

He didn't know what it was. The only magic barrier data he didn't have was from Helheim, the spells of Hela, the so-called daughter of Loki.

"I see. Then I'll change my approach."

He dropped to the floor, killed the nearest mid-sized bug for fresh material, and started building again. First, a better mask: a tight seal of the remaining queen-shell and trachea membrane that locked over his face and forced filtered oxygen through a crude valve. The place he was passing through had no air, let alone toxic air, and he didn't know how much of Baldr's magic could protect him.

Then a full diving suit made from the same mother-parasite carapace, sealed at every joint with layers of membrane and adhesive proteins. The four legs folded tight against his back. He tested the seals twice. There were no leaks and no weak points that he could find.

"This will give me approximately thirteen minutes and twenty-eight seconds inside the bloodstream." He told himself, so he could keep the timer in his memory.

"If the front door is locked," he told the silent brain somewhere above, "I go through the plumbing."

He tore open a major artery the width of a subway tunnel. Blood the color of old wine poured past, thick and heavy. Barry paused, taking a long breath.

He let ten more parasites go through first. They were built to attack from the bloodstream, clearing a path for his swim.

"Okay, let's do this."

Barry stepped into the current, let it take him, and injected the prepared cocktail of proteases and specialized proteins he had synthesized from scavenged glands. He was doing what the parasites before him had done, fooling the immune system to gain deep access to the brain. He swam through the blood. The tight junctions of the vessel walls loosened just enough for him to pass through effortlessly. Then a cluster of the vulture's own enchanted immune cells swept past, carrying the protein he had released toward the brain. He latched onto them with barbed claws and hitched a ride deeper, riding the cellular traffic like a parasite on a parasite.

The pressure was crushing, but the suit held, barely. He had guessed thirteen minutes, but ten were already gone, and the oxygen was running thin. Every few meters, another ward tried to identify and destroy the foreign body. Alarms flared in the tissue around him, the magical immune responses lighting up like tripwires. Eight of his ten parasites died holding them back so he could escape.

The further he went, the darker and tighter it became, and the more suffocating. He kept going anyway. If he failed here, he would be stuck, his hands and legs unable to move freely in a fleshy, narrow cave, waiting to die.

Outside, on the black stone ridge far below the colossal vulture, Baldr lay on his back.

His golden light had dimmed to a sickly glow. Loki crouched over him, one hand pressed hard against his chest, feeling the irregular pulse.

"You've been bleeding power for hours," she said. "And you haven't used your core spells yet."

She gripped his skull, her hand glowing green as she sent her magic inside him. Then she felt a connection.

"The connection is deep, and also far away. Who are you trying to keep alive here?"

Baldr's eyes stayed half-lidded. He didn't answer.

Following the connection, Loki's gaze went to the giant vulture far away. When she felt the endpoint of Baldr's magic, she saw him, if only for a single glance.

"There you are. Finally, I have found you."

She turned away, letting the god of light be. Then, suddenly, she couldn't move. Her hand was caught, and Baldr had grabbed hold of her.

"Don't you dare..."

"Don't you like humans? He will die inside the bird. I am trying to save him, Baldr."

"Lies. Always lies..."

"You're still hung up on my past deeds?" Loki's smile was pure green poison. "It was just a prank, you know. Yes. If I had mourned you properly, you would be in Valhalla, but because of me, you rot here, in my daughter's realm. But I couldn't do that to the human. I kind of like him. I would even offer him a way out of Helheim... if Hela allowed it. Which she won't."

Baldr knew the lie for what it was. Loki had never possessed enough real affection to reconcile with either of her children, so he knew she wouldn't even try.

"Oh, so you will talk to your daughter and son? I will help you with that." He gathered every remaining scrap of divinity, every last spark of light still living in him, and forced it upward into a single, desperate act.

The howl that left his throat was not a god's voice.

It was a wolf's, meant specifically for his own nephew.

It was long. Raw. Carried on pure force of will across the ash plains.

Loki jerked back as if struck. Green light flared in her hand and hardened into a spear. She drove it through his shoulder without hesitation.

Baldr groaned, but the smile that followed was almost peaceful.

"Make up with your son," he rasped. "And with Hela. Tell them you did it for them, to keep them safe from the gods. Tell them, or Ragnarok will come eventually."

A second howl answered from the distant mountains.

It was louder and deeper. It generated its own wind, scattering ash in a wide circle around the ridge.

Fenrir crested the ridge a moment later, massive, dark, and iron-gray. His gaze locked onto the two gods below, settling with sharp intensity on Loki. A look shone in his eyes that went beyond mere rage, a deep, ancient sadness.

The growl that rolled out of him shook the stone under Loki's feet.
