Ning swallowed a first-tier healing pill as he walked back toward the stands, his steps unhurried.

His injuries weren't serious, but the repeated impacts from the Falling Gale Sequence had left a dull ache in his arms and some strain in his meridians. Small wounds, if ignored, could accumulate into real problems, and in a competition like this, stepping onto the arena even slightly below peak condition just to save a few spirit stones was simply irresponsible.

Of course, efficiency had a price. The pill alone had cost nearly fifty spirit stones, even with Su Fan helping him secure a quality batch.

As Ning approached the seating area, Zhang Feng's voice was already echoing across the stands.

"Lang Rulang! So this is what they mean by an 'icy beauty defeated but unbowed'?"

Lang Rulang stopped walking and slowly turned her head toward him.

The look she gave him was cold enough that Zhang Feng's grin immediately stiffened.

"I was complimenting you!" he hurriedly explained. "I meant you didn't give up even after that fierce exchange!"

Ning stepped in before Zhang Feng could somehow make things worse.

"He's not wrong," Ning said mildly. "Most people would have panicked once their talismans were exhausted. You didn't. That alone is worth acknowledging."

Lang Rulang's gaze shifted toward him, still cool and sharp.

"You don't need to protect this guy," she said flatly. "If he wants to talk, he can bear the consequences."

Zhang Feng leaned slightly toward Ning. "She seems even colder than usual. Maybe the loss hit her harder than-"

"You!"

Lang Rulang's glare snapped back to him.

At this rate, even Ning couldn't protect this guy.

"Alright, that's enough. You two never stop arguing." Wei Zhusang coughed lightly into his fist, though the slight curve of his lips made it obvious that he was enjoying the exchange. "Besides, we agreed on this before the competition started. If any of us meet in the arena, we fight seriously. No holding back and no resentment afterward."

Lang Rulang's expression settled as she gave a single nod. "Of course."

The Mutual Aid Association had discussed this long ago. They were allies, but no one was expected to compromise their own path for the sake of appearances.

If two members were matched against each other, they would fight honestly, accept the result, and move on. That understanding was precisely why Ning hadn't felt uncomfortable fighting Lang Rulang in the first place.

Wei Zhusang then turned toward him. "You surprised us as well, Brother Ning. At this rate, you have a real chance of entering the inner sect."

Ning smiled lightly. "I'm flattered, but there's still a long way to go."

"Well, he was ranked in the top ten along with you and me," Zhang Feng chimed in, apparently having learned absolutely nothing from the previous exchange.

A faint chill seemed to settle over the group.

Zhang Feng noticed Lang Rulang's expression and immediately added, "But that doesn't mean the unranked ones are weak!"

Ning almost admired his consistency in stepping on social landmines.

He casually gave the man a way out. "Rankings are just predictions. There are plenty of strong people who weren't included."

"Exactly!" Zhang Feng seized the opportunity without hesitation. "That's what I was saying. Rankings aren't everything."

Lang Rulang gave him a long look, but thankfully decided not to pursue the matter.

Zhang Feng visibly relaxed before turning toward Ning, his confidence returning almost immediately. "Anyway, if we end up fighting each other, don't you dare hold back. I want to see everything you've got."

"Definitely."

"Good!" Zhang Feng grinned. "Then I'll show you the power of youth!"

Ning nodded solemnly.

Some things, at least, never changed.

...

Most of the participants in the competition hovered around the third and fourth stages of Qi Condensation. Their strength wasn't bad, but Ning quickly noticed a common flaw among them: hesitation.

Many paused before fully committing to an attack, especially when preparing what they considered their decisive move. The changes in their posture, breathing, or qi circulation often telegraphed what they were about to do well before the technique was actually released. Against opponents of a similar level, they could still get away with it, but against anyone sharper, it became a glaring weakness.

The reason was simple. Most of them lacked real combat experience.

Outer sect disciples were already busy enough trying to advance their cultivation while gathering the resources needed to support it. When they did prepare methods to protect themselves, they naturally preferred powerful techniques, talismans, or other lethal measures capable of ending a dangerous encounter quickly.

The problem was that many of those methods were restricted or outright prohibited during sect competitions. Without them, quite a few disciples were noticeably less comfortable in an ordinary fight where they had to rely primarily on their own cultivation and martial skill.

From the stands, Ning quietly gathered whatever information he could, noting each contestant's preferred range, habitual openings, commonly used techniques, and reactions when placed under pressure. As expected, beyond the obvious monster ranked first, Qiu Han, there were several others worth paying attention to.

The second-ranked Jin Shu was one of them.

One might have expected her contracted wolf to be the more aggressive of the pair, but surprisingly, Jin Shu herself was even more relentless.

While the gray wolf circled around and harassed her opponent with quick, probing attacks, Jin Shu advanced directly, constantly forcing her opponent backward with decisive strikes.

Her opponent barely had time to recover between exchanges before another attack came from a different direction. Even when he managed to create some distance from Jin Shu, the wolf immediately pressured him from the side, forcing him to divide his attention before she closed in again.

It was a simple arrangement, but extremely annoying to deal with.

Ning continued looking for the other ranked names as the matches progressed. Some immediately lived up to the rumors surrounding them, overwhelming their opponents with clean and decisive execution, while others were much more restrained than their profiles suggested and revealed only as much strength as necessary to secure victory.

Naturally, Ning paid attention to all of them.

But watching didn't mean his hands had to remain idle. Time, after all, was not something to waste.

There wasn't much he could safely practice in the middle of a crowded spectator area, but qi control was one of the few exceptions. More importantly, it was something that needed constant refinement.

Every breakthrough increased the amount of qi stored within the dantian. That meant greater power, but controlling a larger volume of qi with the same degree of precision naturally became more difficult. If his control failed to keep pace with his cultivation, some of that additional power would simply be wasted.

Ning held a thin string loosely in one hand and fed a tiny thread of qi into it.

Rather than simply pouring energy into the string, he spread the qi carefully across its surface, trying to maintain a thin and even coating without allowing any of it to leak away. Once the layer stabilized, he began rotating the string between his fingers, slowly at first before gradually increasing the speed.

The qi followed the movement, clinging to the fibers as Ning continuously adjusted his output. Too much qi and the excess would spill outward; too little and the coating would become unstable and gradually dissipate.

Ning increased the speed again. The string spun rapidly between his fingers while the layer of qi remained attached, forming a faint sheen that was almost invisible unless someone looked closely.

The exercise had originally been half-inspired by all those energy-control scenes he remembered from anime. The exact systems were obviously different, but the basic idea had interested him. Energy didn't necessarily have to be released outward in a single burst; it could also be attached to something, shaped, maintained, and manipulated continuously.

Cultivators naturally had their own methods based on the same principle, and object enhancement was one of the more advanced applications of qi control.

Ning had already begun applying it to his archery.

When firing an arrow, he no longer relied solely on adding qi at the moment of release. Instead, he could maintain a layer of qi along the shaft, reinforcing the arrow while stabilizing its flight. From there, he had experimented with making the attached qi rotate around the shaft rather than simply remaining stationary.

The result was Spiral Arrow.

The rotating qi increased the arrow's penetrating power, effectively allowing it to drill into whatever it struck. The panel had even recognized Spiral Arrow as a martial art, which still amused Ning whenever he thought about it.

Calling it a complete martial art was rather generous. It was closer to a practical application of qi adhesion and rotational control than a proper technique with a complete set of movements or principles.

In fact, it could probably be considered the first martial art Ning had created himself, crude enough that the panel hadn't even given it a proficiency bar.

Still, crude didn't mean useless.

Ning had looked through several mortal-grade archery techniques, and most didn't offer enough improvement over what he could already do to justify spending time learning them. Spirit-grade techniques were another matter entirely, but their prices were enough to make his heart hurt just from looking at them.

For now, Spiral Arrow was more than sufficient.

The hardest part is going from zero to one. If I keep working on it, maybe I'll eventually create something genuinely impressive.

Ning was rather hopeful about that.

He was still rotating the qi-covered string between his fingers when movement on one of the nearby jade boards caught his attention.

A new pairing had appeared.

[Xiao Fan vs. Zhou Sheng]

Ning stopped spinning the string and turned his attention toward the arena. Remembering Xiao Fan's track record, he couldn't help but become interested.

Well, this should be interesting.

...

When Zhou Sheng's name appeared opposite Xiao Fan's, the atmosphere around the arena noticeably shifted.

Unlike Xiao Hong's explosive arrogance, Zhou Sheng walked onto the platform with measured steps. His robes were immaculate and clearly expensive. If not for the faint curl at the corner of his lips, he might even have appeared dignified.

Xiao Fan stood across from him, silent as ever.

For a while, neither of them moved. What had initially seemed like a tense standoff gradually stretched on until the silence became awkward.

Thankfully, Zhou Sheng finally spoke before it became a genuine staring contest.

"Alas, I heard you defeated quite a few of my 'friends.' All they did was invite you to join our faction. Perhaps they said something unpleasant, but that hardly warranted conflict." His smile deepened slightly. "Look at you now. Because of that stubbornness, you have to deal with me."

Xiao Fan's expression didn't change.

"Didn't this begin because your 'friends' coveted my spiritual beast?" he replied calmly. "If you want to deal with me, just say so. There's no need to dress it up with some fancy reason."

Ning raised his eyebrows slightly.

It seemed these two had their own conflict as well. Then again, considering Xiao Fan's track record over the past few years, Ning probably shouldn't have been surprised. The man possessed an almost supernatural talent for accumulating enemies.

Zhou Sheng suddenly burst into laughter as though Xiao Fan had said something incredibly amusing.

"You?" His earlier composure disappeared beneath open contempt. "Listen carefully, you piece of trash. I'll let you strike first. If I take even a single step back, I'll surrender immediately. But if I don't..."

His expression hardened.

"I'll cripple you."

Ning sighed inwardly.

Giving the opponent the first move while publicly declaring that he wouldn't take a single step backward?

That was practically a death flag.

More concerning was the fact that Zhou Sheng had openly threatened to cripple someone during a sect-sanctioned competition, yet the supervising elder didn't react at all. Perhaps he trusted the arena formation to prevent any truly serious injuries, or maybe he simply didn't believe Zhou Sheng would get the opportunity to carry out his threat.

Either way, Xiao Fan's lips had already curved into a faint smile.

"Are you sure?"

Zhou Sheng snorted. "Do it before I change my mind and defeat you in a single move instead!"

Xiao Fan stepped forward and settled into his stance.

"If it's a beating you want, then a beating you shall get."

Qi gathered around his fist, compressing inward rather than spilling outward as Xiao Fan drew back his arm.

"Heaven-Shattering Fist!"

His fist shot straight toward Zhou Sheng's face.

Even from the stands, Ning could tell that Xiao Fan wasn't playing around. Through Clear Sight, he could see the qi compressed tightly around Xiao Fan's fist, with almost none of it being wasted.

More interesting, however, was Zhou Sheng's reaction.

The smirk vanished from his face almost immediately.

For a fraction of a second, hesitation flashed through his eyes as pride warred with instinct. He had just declared in front of everyone that he wouldn't take a single step backward, but the closer Xiao Fan's fist came, the more obvious it became that standing there and accepting it directly was an extremely bad idea.

Instinct eventually won.

At the final moment, Zhou Sheng twisted his right foot and shifted his stance by barely half a step, changing the angle at which the attack would land.

Xiao Fan's fist collided with his defense.

BOOM!

The impact shook the arena and cracked the defensive shell around Zhou Sheng. The last-second adjustment prevented him from taking the full force head-on, but he was still blasted several meters backward before his feet finally caught against the stone.

Zhou Sheng coughed up blood, yet somehow remained standing.

A wave of surprised murmurs spread through the crowd.

Ning's eyes narrowed with interest.

He adjusted at the last second. Not bad.

Zhou Sheng wiped the blood from his mouth and forced himself upright.

"You overestimated yourself," he said hoarsely. "That so-called Earth-grade technique of yours... is that all?"

Xiao Fan looked at him for a moment before smiling.

"You said you wouldn't move. Was my attack so powerful that you couldn't help yourself?"

Ning almost laughed.

Xiao Fan really knew where to stab when he wanted to. For someone like Zhou Sheng, there probably wasn't a better target than his pride.

The spectators had noticed as well, and murmurs quickly spread through the surrounding stands.

"He did move."

"No one expected Xiao Fan to have that much strength, but Zhou Sheng said he wouldn't take even a single step back."

"Then didn't he already lose the wager?"

Zhou Sheng's expression stiffened as the comments reached him.

"I only adjusted my stance!" he snapped. "That doesn't count."

Xiao Fan tilted his head. "So you admit you moved?"

"I was giving you a chance!" Whatever remained of Zhou Sheng's earlier composure finally disappeared. "You dare mock me? You lowborn trash! You don't know the immensity of heaven and earth!"

At this point, Ning was simply enjoying himself. There probably wasn't much left to learn from the fight, especially now that Zhou Sheng had abandoned the calm, methodical style his information profile had described.

More importantly, calling Xiao Fan trash had clearly been a mistake.

The faint smile disappeared from Xiao Fan's face.

Already injured from the first exchange, Zhou Sheng lunged forward and unleashed a barrage of attacks, each one more aggressive than the last. Xiao Fan met them calmly, redirecting or avoiding each strike while giving away as little ground as possible.

The difference between them soon became obvious.

Zhou Sheng's breathing grew uneven as the exchange continued. He had already suffered considerable damage from the Heaven-Shattering Fist, and now his anger was making things worse. His movements became less controlled, while the flow of qi through his attacks grew increasingly unstable.

Realizing the fight was slipping away from him, Zhou Sheng suddenly drew back his fist.

[Flare Strike]

Flames erupted around his arm and rapidly gathered around his fist, taking the rough shape of a roaring lion.

Another Earth-grade martial art.

Xiao Fan didn't retreat. He simply planted his feet and gathered qi around his fist once again.

[Heaven-Shattering Fist]

The two attacks collided.

Flames burst outward as the impact sent a shockwave across the platform. Under normal circumstances, the clash might have been far closer, but Zhou Sheng had already been injured, and his qi circulation was unstable. The flaming lion around his fist trembled under the pressure before beginning to break apart.

"No... how can you-"

Xiao Fan cut him off, forcing his fist forward.

The Flare Strike collapsed completely.

Xiao Fan's punch continued through the scattered flames and connected squarely with Zhou Sheng's face.

Zhou Sheng's feet left the ground as he was launched backward. He slammed into the formation barrier before dropping heavily onto the stone platform, where he lay motionless for several seconds.

The arena went quiet.

"...Is he alive?" someone eventually asked.

He was.

A groan escaped Zhou Sheng as he rolled onto his side and clutched his face. His jaw sat at an uncomfortable angle, and whatever he tried to say came out as a broken, slurred sound.

Ning was also fairly certain he had seen several small white objects scatter across the arena after the punch.

Were those teeth?

Zhou Sheng seemed to arrive at the same realization. He slowly touched his mouth, and when his fingers came away covered in blood, his expression twisted as he discovered that he possessed considerably fewer teeth than he had a few moments ago.

The resulting scream was rather impressive considering the condition of his jaw.

He glared at Xiao Fan with naked hatred and tried to push himself upright, but his injuries finally caught up with him. His arms gave out, and he collapsed onto the platform again.

Looking at him, Ning almost felt a flicker of sympathy.

Losing was one thing. Losing after publicly declaring that you wouldn't take a single step backward, moving anyway, arguing that it didn't count, and then getting punched unconscious in front of half the outer sect was something else entirely.

Zhou Sheng had entered the arena intending to humiliate Xiao Fan.

Instead, he had done most of the work himself.

"YOU DARE!"

A figure suddenly appeared beside Zhou Sheng and carefully lifted him from the ground. After quickly feeding him a pill, the man straightened and turned toward Xiao Fan.

The moment the surrounding disciples recognized his black robes, the arena grew noticeably quieter.

"That's Elder Zhou Chen!"

"Zhou Sheng's father?"

"Wait, isn't he the elder who regularly commissions those black boar hunts?"

Ning recognized the name immediately.

From the black boar incident alone, he already had a fairly good idea of what kind of person Zhou Chen was.

The elder pointed toward Xiao Fan, fury plain on his face.

"How dare you harm my son so severely in what was meant to be a friendly match? You even attempted to kill him!" His voice carried across the arena. "For breaking the rules, I will cripple you!"

Ning stared at him.

Shameless.

His own son had threatened to cripple Xiao Fan in front of everyone barely a few minutes ago, yet Zhou Chen had conveniently forgotten that part of the conversation.

Xiao Fan glared back at the elder, his fists clenching as Zhou Chen's Foundation Establishment aura descended upon him. The pressure forced Xiao Fan's shoulders downward, but he stubbornly remained standing and refused to lower his head.

Ning could almost imagine what was going through his mind.

Power. I need more power. Without strength, I can't protect anything...

Or something along those lines.

Classic protagonist enlightenment under pressure.

Before Zhou Chen could take another step, however, the supervising elder appeared between them.

"Senior Brother Zhou."

His tone remained calm, but he made no attempt to move aside.

"This match was conducted according to the rules. Your son provoked his opponent and voluntarily allowed him the first strike. The arena formation protected his life, and Xiao Fan committed no violation."

Zhou Chen's expression darkened. For a moment, his aura intensified, but with another elder standing directly in his path and hundreds of disciples watching, there wasn't much he could do without causing an even larger problem.

Eventually, the pressure receded.

"Hmph. This time, you're lucky."

The words were directed at Xiao Fan, though the glare Zhou Chen gave the supervising elder made his displeasure equally clear. He then turned and left with his unconscious son in his arms.

Only after he disappeared did conversations gradually return to the stands.

Meanwhile, Ning found himself thinking about something else entirely.

According to the information he had gathered before the competition, Zhou Sheng was supposed to be methodical, steady, and disciplined. At the beginning of the match, he had even seemed to fit that description. Yet after Xiao Fan provoked his pride a few times, all of that composure had disappeared.

Ning rubbed his chin.

I guess that's one more point in favor of Xiao Fan possessing some ability to lower his opponents' IQ. After all, Zhou Sheng didn't act anything like he was advertised.

Perhaps this was simply the fate of a protagonist at work.

Ning continued rubbing his chin, completely serious as he developed increasingly questionable theories about protagonist-related supernatural abilities.

"Hmm. Food for thought, I suppose."
