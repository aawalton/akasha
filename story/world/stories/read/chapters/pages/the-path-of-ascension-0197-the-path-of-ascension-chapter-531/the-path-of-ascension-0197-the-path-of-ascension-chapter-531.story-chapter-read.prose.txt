Chapter 531

The mandatory after-war functions didn’t quite blur by for Matt, but he did his best to make them do so. He managed it mainly by avoiding everything related to such events as much as feasibly possible, and zoning out when he couldn’t. Thankfully, most of the required high Tiers were already in the Minkalla system, and the ones who weren't had the advantage of Tier 45ships that could fly faster than even their purpose built one.

His was how he got through the endless speeches and end of war functions that happened in Minkalla after the Ascender brawl without losing his mind from the stress of it being so close to being over. He wasn’t able to release his tension until he was walking through the sands of Lila’s room on the Corporations’ space station, and he didn’t actually feel better until he plopped his house down in the middle.

That took a quick teleport from Allie to pick it up, but that was infinitely easier with the war over, and her ban from teleporting near the frontlines consequently lifted. Her help was punctuated by an intermittent, “Weeeee!” as she flickered from place to place, but he didn’t mind.

While Manny probably hadn’t planned for them to find the former dynasty’s princess and dig into that situation, it had offered Allie a distraction so large she hadn’t had the time or inclination to push her limits. That put her in excellent standing and that had come with its own rewards that helped make up for all the demerits she normally accrued.

Personally, Matt just felt better having retrieved his growth item from its place on their floating island and catching a small update from the duchy and guild that told him that nothing vital had burned down during their absence. He kicked his feet on the front porch to get off some of the sand as he used his connection to the building to open all of the doors and windows. Their ducal cleaning staff visited and kept things tidy, but a long absence still made him want to air the place out himself.

He stepped inside and left the door open, allowing the Empire Ascenders behind him to saunter in as he called out, “It's good to be home. I need five minutes then I’ll be ready for guests. If you want to change, now is the time! Everyone's rooms are in their usual places.”

He put his own words to action and entered his and Liz’s room a moment faster than his wife to get out of his formal Ascender robes and into a more casual shirt and pants.

Zack, Allie, Aiden, and Aster all did the same, going to their respective rooms, but Lila lounged in their living room without bothering.

“What, no prepared snacks, Matt? You're slipping. You could have easily had your staff prepare something,” Were her first words as he reentered the common area, but he didn’t mind.

“I said five minutes, you could have used the time to change rather than complain. I put in that sandy beach room for you and you’ve never used it once. I’m starting to get offended. Besides, I want to do the cooking, so no way I was letting anyone else prep.”

Lila sprawled out deeper into his couch as she lilted her voice to sound like an especially whinny Allie. “But I didn’t want to change, I wanted snacks. Still do in fact. And since you brought it up, are you making deviled eggs? If you weren’t planning on it, you should consider. I want some. We can send Allie out to get the raw ingredients if you didn’t prepare properly. And as for the room, it's there if I want it, besides, while we may be in your house we are also in my own much larger room. It feels like that counts.”

Matt didn’t have to engage with her as Aster came out of her room and provided an excellent distraction from the pestering sand dragon, who gleefully pilfered through his sister’s backup ice cream reserves. Her act of sacrifice bought him just enough time to cut some fruits, vegetables, and cheese and get it properly laid out before knocking visitors arrived at his door.

He opened it with his mind to see the familiar faces of On the Last Line, now Tier 36 and walking with their arms puffed up and away from their bodies as if they’d gotten more muscular with their Tier up and needed more space.

“Excuse me! Coming through,” Moe called out as he stepped sideways through the door. “Big Tier 36’s need a lot of space. We are important people! No longer peasants who fight for our Great Pow— oh, is that goat cheese from Rujtia?! I love their cheeses! It's the high mountain air that the cloud stepping goats require that make their products so good. You just can't beat an air-aspected planet for tasty species. Hands down the best aspect for non-spicy flavors.”

Clapping his hands together, the dwarf rapidly tiptoped over to the table and started stuffing his face with the snacks by the fistful.

Matt grinned but was distracted by Liz sauntering out of their shared room. Where he’d gone with a slightly more formal set of evening clothes consisting of a nice shirt and pants, she’d chosen to wear long waist-hugging slacks paired with an emerald green top that should have been too lowkey for their evening, but looked amazing thanks to the jewelry she’d paired with the outfit.

She wasn’t just attractive to him in the moment, but beautiful in a way that had little to do with her appearance.

He didn’t care about the others ribbing him as he ogled as she sauntered by to open the door for their next batch of guests. Olivier and Sien arrived only moments after each other, but it wasn’t all that surprising, given the speedster proffered a bottle of wine and the mage stepped out from his shadow, smug to have tagged along.

“Oh, come on, how long have you been hiding in my shadow? Get out!” Olivier immediately started complaining at his unwanted passenger as he saw her materialize.

The woman in question brushed him off to give her greetings to Liz before she slunk into the kitchen where she filled a preprepared bowl with homemade hard candy snacks.

“Nice to see you, Sien.”

“And you, Matthew. Your gatherings are always lovely. Does your current domestic enthusiasm indicate you will be having a child alongside your Emperor?”

He was going to respond when Liz joined them and did so for him, letting him continue his prep work. “He does, doesn't he? And yeah, we’ve been talking about it. Are you offering some tips?”

“Don’t have twins.” Matt assumed the Sect Ascender was joking, but it was clear she was serious as she elaborated. “Its a lot of extra work, and while you might think two are easier than one, or that being a high Tier immortal will make it easy, it won’t. Twins will pull you in more than two directions, and having extra bodies barely helps. Take my advice, do not deliberately try for twins.”

“Did you choose twins then?” Matt asked around a mouthful of fruit that hadn’t passed his visual inspections, hoping to get some new information from the usually recalcitrant Ascender.

Unlike normal probes into her personal life that were ignored or avoided, Sien answered as she picked at a piece of fruit. “I thought I was being clever. I figured they could keep each other entertained. I love my boys, but they make me worry endlessly. I’d stick with one for your first round. I’m just grateful they did so well in the Tier 30 bracket this war. I was worried about them, but they excelled by every metric. Have you settled on a conception method yet?”

Liz shared a brief look with Matt before they laughed together. “I don’t think either of us wants to have to come up with another name. We’ve struggled enough on picking one of each and none of our top choices would pair well, even if they are fraternal twins and opposite gender. As for the birth treasure? We are aiming for a natural conception, but that is looking more unlikely by the decade.”

Sien didn’t seem surprised. “From my limited understanding of Empire politics, you have a few hundred years, so I wouldn’t write your efforts off immediately. However, if you do decide to go with a treasure, use one that will let one of you carry the child. While there is nothing wrong with the other types, having your child grow hour by hour nourished by one’s own flesh and blood is an experience like nothing else.”

Matt saw Liz was chewing on one of Sien’s candies and explained while he chopped. “That was our thought, though it came from a more practical viewpoint of allowing Liz the chance to step in should something go wrong during the process.”

Their conversation garnered more attention as Ellen and Krodag joined them at the mention of treasures and kids respectively.

“Acording to most modern statistics, children conceived via magical means are less likely to have things wrong with them, even in the case of aspected mana parents. I wouldn’t consider intervention that high of a priority.” Ellen pointed out.

Krodag waved his hand holding a glass of liquor as he stuffed his mouth with a small layered mountain of cheese and crackers. “Kids are surprisingly self-sufficient and able to take care of themselves. No need to worry so much!”

He immediately noticed the hard looks from his conversation partners and quickly explained, “I wasn’t saying abandon them or anything, just that so long as you are there and love the little shits, you don’t need to fuss about the little things. There are lots of humans or —” he waved at himself, “ —human-like in the Realm, and that isn’t by accident. We are hardy fuckers who will make it with very little guidance beyond stuff like food, water, and adequate shelter. If you love the kids and give them proper guidance, you shouldn’t sweat the small stuff and let it happen as it happens! It gives them more room to find who they are and not who their parents want them to be.”

“That hardly sounds like a stable upbringing,” Sien pointed out as a Liz separated from the main body to welcome Max, Wun, Gideon, and Eclavorn to the party.

“I agree! I’m just saying a child isn’t going to suddenly wither away because the natural treasure used to spawn them does or doesn't make twins easier.”

Matt hated to agree but he did. “He’s right about kids being hardy. Even the worst things can be bounced back from but I don’t thi—”

His gaze along with everyone else's was brought to the front door as they all felt Maya arrive with the two guests of honor.

A part of Esther wanted to question why the Corporations had dedicated such a large area to a foreign Great Power’s Gladiator, but she suspected she’d be able to learn the story before too long. She knew a large portion of her reluctance was her wounded pride that didn’t appreciate being beaten by their peers so soundly, but she tried not to let that get to her as Maya hurried them across the sandy dunes. They had defeated her and Lawrence, but that had left the Republic as the Great Power that had two teams who’d somehow both left with a 1-2 record.

That felt wrong, as if they should have been able to do more, even if Janet or the other members of Republic society didn’t seem to notice or care. She knew how easily Gladiator fights could shift, as seen by the Clans team who’d managed to pull off what they’d wanted to do against the Empire trio in a short, but rapid battle that didn’t play into their strengths like a long drawn out engagement had.

Her spirit’s unnaturally rapid creation of mana was both the explanation and the curse all rolled into one. She was never one to pass up free practice, and started tapping into the past to try and pry rather than let her mana leak into the surroundings where Minkalla would absorb it. That planet had taken more than enough mana from her during her delve of the world, and she saw no reason to feed it again as a Tier 35.

As they walked, she paid more and more attention to their destination.

The house looked incredibly out of place, because it was. It wasn’t that the house was in a desert, which might have been strange enough, but rather that she could see clear signs that said the house was made to fit inside a portable ring. That confused her, as she couldn’t understand what was notable about something she’d been able to buy at Tier 15. They arrived on the small porch before she could figure out what exactly struck her as wrong.

Maya’s dopey grin as she watched them like a predator didn’t help. It indicated she was waiting for the other shoe to drop, but neither Esther or Lawrence understood and were left to follow along as their confusion grew.

The three of them only needed to wait a moment as a familiar redhead opened the door with an easy grin that didn’t fit the woman who’d beaten them only moments before, but that was the most normal thing. Or it would have been, if Esther’s initial reaction to seeing someone from the Empire wasn’t still a hundred thousands years out of date.

As much as she hadn’t really believed it, everything she saw and could find in her research proved the Empire truly had shifted course onto a more enlightened path, rather than the despotic one they’d been racing down. She wondered if that was an inevitable outcome of such places, but doubted any of the other Great Powers would allow another truly good representative government structure to exist alongside the Republic.

The initial pleasantries were exactly that. Pleasant without being overwhelming, but what was far less true for the plethora of stares that everyone leveled on them.

It was their first time meeting most of their peers, though they’d learned as much as they could about their retired fellows as was possible from media sources. That meant they knew names, faces, and power sets, but very little about the true cultivators behind the masks they wore for the public.

Esther keenly felt the weight of the stares and wanted to squirm when Lawrance looked around at the growing silence and offered, “You have a lovely home. I can see why everyone chooses to—”

He never finished the words as the room collectively broke out into chortles and laughter.

Maya covered her mouth, but didn’t bother to stop the hearty laugh that came out of her at the words.

When no one seemed content to explain, Matt wiped his hands on a conveniently placed towel before stepping around the island and out of the kitchen, where he joined his wife to shake their hands.

After doing so, he gestured around the surprisingly spacious living room and kitchen combo.

“The others are just laughing because you hit the nail on the head but didn’t recognize it. The house—”

“Is a fucking growth item!” Moe spoiled from his place on one of the couches next to Aiden and Olivier.

Esther looked around, mainly focusing on her magical perceptions, but she couldn’t help but scrunch up her face as she tried to process what she was registering.

Everything was Tier 35, which didn’t make any sense now that she noticed. That wasn’t just wasteful, it was unrealistic. Everyone had old knicknacks and favorite furniture from their younger years. However, that wasn’t true here. From the walls, the floor boards, to the fasteners inside the wall, everything was Tier 35.

She saw that and registered it, but none of it made any sense whatsoever.

“How can an entire house be a growth item? That should be impossible.”

Lawrence asked the question before she could, but it earned another round of laughter from the older Gladiators, though it didn’t feel malicious or directed at them. Or not entirely so.

“That’s disbelief! It counts!" Lila called from her place where she’d spread out on most of a couch as she snacked on a plate of crackers and cheese the others tried to pilfer from when she wasn’t looking.

“That was interference from non-hosts! The bet should be voided.” The blue haired Aiden shot back.

The banter looked like it would continue, but Matt and Liz smiled in the unison that only an old married couple could have. It was one that she’d seen Lawrence and Linda share a hundred times, and had always felt like an outsider. Thankfully, her brother was distracted by the growth item and didn’t notice as Matt gestured to a hallway opening that hadn’t been there when they arrived. “We’d love to give you a tour if you'd like, or you are welcome to hang out, as hors d'oeuvres are ready.”

Esther quietly made a temporal bubble around herself, intending to enjoy the proffered plate of snacks more than once, but the moment she did so, she felt the world fall out beneath her.

Before she could react, the sandy ground that supported the house fell out from under them like a sinkhole had opened up. The most likely answer was that someone destroyed their floor, or rather the floor below them’s ceiling, but she wasn’t about to stand around and crash.

Even if she survived, that would be embarrassing in the main timeline, so she wanted to avoid it. As such, she instinctively tried to flee, but there was an inexorable suction that swallowed her and the house in its entirety. The instant before the timeline was forcibly ended with her death, she was able to glimpse the cause of the attack that ended her.

A large sand colored dragon maw had been hidden inside the room and had eaten them.

The moment she came back to reality, Esther’s gaze and attention snapped to the only one it could be, but the dragon in question didn’t seem to notice anything had gone awry in the possible future.

Lawrence hadn’t delved into the future and didn’t notice her start, and happily accepted the tour, forcing her to follow along.

While he ogled the fact that the floorboards and pictures were all part of the growth item rather than separate entities and asked a million questions, she replayed what had just happened to her.

She didn’t quite believe it, and yet the moment she tried to spin up any adjacent future, she was inevitably eaten, along with everyone and everything in the house. There was only one possible perpetrator, but she’d been told Lila only dabbled in time magic, which didn’t track with what she’d seen.

However, her fourth attempt was interrupted as she too was drawn into the tour as she registered something Matt had said.

“Wait, I'm sorry, I must have misunderstood. Your house can integrate other items that aren’t bound to you or it, including spatial rings? That has to be impossible.”

Esther didn’t quite believe it, but Liz grabbed at the air, revealing a storage ring in her hand. She dropped it, where it was swallowed by the floor as if they were water rather than a rather nice dark maple. Next to them on the empty wall, a simple door appeared and opened to show an empty room.

“A little less impressive with an empty ring, but if you want to leave something inside, it can be a lot more impressive on the other way.”

Lawrence was faster than her and pulled out the small bundle of flowers they’d brought as a polite gift.

Esther watched as the door was closed and a storage ring fell out of the wall, but despite watching it happen, she didn’t quite trust the ring having their flowers inside.

It recontextualized everything about the house and she didn’t blame Lawrence as he sighed in unadulterated jealousy.

“That’s got to be one of the coolest things I’ve ever seen.”

She at least wanted to know where they’d gotten it. Really, there was only one place, but she had to make sure. “Minkalla?”

“It bonded while we were down there. If I’m honest, at first we were disappointed at a non-combat item bonding, but it's proven it's worth a million times over.” Matt offered as they reached the end of the hall and Liz inserted an orb into the wall.

“Here is my terrarium where I can accelerate the growth of things with mana and a touch of blood. Normally, I can only interact with the plants but have limited control over the rest of their surroundings. With it absorbed by the house, I can just waltz inside and garden by hand. It's quite the handy feature. Better yet, I can send Matt inside and he can create formations that I could never manage on my own. Over there is our secondary field of Voluminous Spirit Pears. I’ve been accelerating their growth during the war. It—”

Esther’s mind was racing as they walked through a massive garden that never could have fit in the house. Stranger still was that they found a door on the far side that led to a different hallway she felt led to the common area.

Before they reached it they were given a tour of a dozen workshops for every profession, including cold forging, and rooms for all of the Empire Gladiators, but her mind was still on the item they wanted to make with JR. If they could get inside their proposed clock tower they might be able to manage some of their stranger ideas, such as trapping a time based monster on every floor to empower the item.

They’d tossed the concept out earlier in their planning process, as their item would be ruined if one of the monsters died or escaped, but it suddenly seemed not just possible but viable. She’d never suggest something so intimate with strangers, let alone foreign ones, but the idea refused to leave her mind once she’d had it.

Lawrence’s appreciative whistle elongated into something that bordered on comedic, as when they returned from the opposite side of the living space they’d exited, it doubled its size with more couches and seating rising from the floor.

Matt smiled as he gestured around the room. “I’m going to finish cooking, but feel free to relax. Drinks are Krodag’s department, but we have a fully stocked bar and fridge. If—”

He was interrupted before he could step away by the older Clan Gladiator, Wun. “Hold up, before you go back to work and be boring, we made a bet earlier and I want to pay up.”

Esther desperately wanted a few moments to explore both the house and the oddity that was her and the house being eaten any time she spun up an alternative timeline, but she was unable to look away from the massive table that appeared awkwardly situated in the living room.

It was simultaneously gaudy and hauntingly beautiful. Only Tier 15, the table was carved from what seemed to be a singular solid dark burgundy tree her [AI] didn’t recognize, indicating it was probably from a rift or long extinct. But where its center should have been was a large slab of what looked like white marble. However, a bit of investigation proved it wasn’t stone at all, but a continuation of the tree and something like the core wood.

The contrast was striking, but more than the materials, the design was intricate, if entirely foreign. Words or some kind of script she didn’t recognize ran the borders, telling the story of what she interpreted as some long gone cultivator’s life and rise to prominence while the rest devolved into strange geometric shapes that were so intertwined they seemed to twist at the corner of her vision.

Any of those things would have been interesting, but what caught everyone's attention, not only Esther and Lawrence, who played with time regularly, was the object's age. It wasn’t just old, it was ancient. It was the oldest man-made object she’d ever seen by orders of magnitude.

“This bad boy was part of the museum halI I found.”

Esther was grateful she wasn’t the only one confused as Wun was forced to explain how he’d fallen into an improbable series of events that sounded like something out of a poorly thought out story book. “And at the end, the main thing of value I found was the museum. As I was saying, this bad boy was the crown of their collection. Or it was if the guy with a language Talent I happened to meet in a bar is right. I wish I remembered his name, but I was wasted on scavenged liquor and my AI was busy doing… something. I don’t quite remember that either. It doesn't matter, he also claimed the inscription is just a really long set of titles which sounded awesome and amazing! King of Kings, Emperor of the Heavens, The Star Crowned, you get the idea. The people who made the museum claimed it was over fifteen cycles of Great Powers older than themselves, which the seekers I showed it to were rather dubious about, but they admitted it was still extremely old anyway, so who cares!? We do know the museum builders are at least three or four from us, and that alone makes this table one of the oldest things left in the Realm.”

Moe coughed into his hand a loud, “Liar!” and then “Eimhir!” as Wun mentioned the bar but most eyes spun to Esther and her brother rather than the dwarf.

Instead of letting them linger on her she pointed at Lawrence. “It's his Tier 25, I just borrow it sometimes. He's the expert at old things, not me.”

Lawrence didn’t need to be asked once, let alone twice, and she recognized the spiritual fluctuations as he started looking into the past.

Through their [AI] links, she saw his mana pool rapidly start to drain, but Matt’s Concept kept him going far longer than he would normally be able to backtrack.

After a few minutes of effort, he shook his head. “It’s Tier 15, which made things a lot easier, but I still couldn’t push back far enough to confirm how old it is. What I can confirm is that it was sitting in that single spot for a long time. That spot in its history is like a canyon that I can hardly see the bottom of. The experts might be right but I can confirm it's old.”

“Good enough for me!” That seemed to be enough for Wun, who turned to Matt and pumped his fists up and down as he chanted. “Now eat it! Eat it! Eat it! Eat it!”

A part of Esther desperately wanted to say that something so old shouldn’t be casually destroyed by a growth item house, but the larger part of her was just curious as to what would happen.

Matt hesitated as he confirmed that Wun really wanted him to digest the table, and while Esther appreciated his concern, she personally wanted to see what would happen more than she cared about a lost relic’s possible origins. After a bit of prodding and prompting from the rest of the room, the table sunk into the floor before it rose back out an instant later.

What shocked her was that the table was identical despite having been transformed from a Tier 15 object to a Tier 35 one. That should have altered something, just as becoming part of the homogeneous spiritual whole that was the house should have as well. Either was a massive change, yet none of that was reflected on the item itself.

The most bizarre thing of all was that her time based senses told her it was still just as old as it was before, rather than being freshly created. It made zero sense according to everything she knew, and Esther felt her jaw drop, but she didn’t have the strength to muster up anything more than jealousy directed at the smiling blond man.

If it wasn’t a growth item, she’d have already tried to buy the house off him, but she could only curse her poor Minkalla luck. While Lawrence had gotten his spathas to bond, she’d only had a water bottle and filter combo bond with her, and she’d willingly destroyed the item after reaching Tier 25 and gaining access to the Republic’s full support.

Her jealousy ran further as Brian ran over holding a bag of knives. “Oh! Yeah, I almost forgot. I, umm, bet that I can throw my head across the room. If I win, I get something cool. If I fail, I’ll give you these knives from the museum!”

Then, to prove his point, he pulled his skeletal head off his neck and threw it all of a foot.

On the ground, his head said as his body shrugged, “Ah, well I guess you get the knives. Here you go. Ahhhh—”

Then he turned over the bag and dumped out what looked like polished copper cutlery. That wouldn’t have been a problem, but his head was in danger of being skewered by his own ‘bet’ and it screamed the entire time knives rained down around him from the spatially expanded bag.

Esther instinctively wanted to be angry, knowing the man was from the Great Power that nearly killed her and Lawrence, but she couldn’t help but laugh at the antics along with everyone else.

She also couldn’t help but inspect the knives that appeared neatly arranged on the still far too long table.

“You can sharpen them too? Does that mean total item control?” She nearly blinked as he turned to her before she realized she’d spoken that thought out loud.

“Part of joining the house and being absorbed is being part of the house. Just like I can move the table, I can sharpen the knives. Knives are meant to be sharp, but they aren’t swords per se, so I can’t make them larger or smaller. I—”

Aiden blew a raspberry from his recliner before he interjected, “You have an armory that I know has weapons inside it. It's your own lack of imagination and mental flexibility that prevent that from working. If I had this house, I’d have already turned it into an entire floating world complete with rifts and essence cycles!”

Matt glared at his fellow Empire Gladiator before deliberately ignoring him. “As I was saying, things that are permanently absorbed by the house are part of the building, so they can’t really leave or be shared effectively. They remain part of the collective, and it causes damage to the item and my spirit if absorbed things are removed or destroyed, similar to every other damaged growth item. It does mean some things just exist in the house, like food and our normal items, but house things? Knives, tables, chairs? They tend to join permanently, but I can’t say I mind, as it lets me keep my favorites around.” Then, pointedly not looking at Aiden, he added, “We’re quite happy keeping this place just our home.”

“Hoarderrrr!”

Maya’s jeer was met with laughter, but Matt was nonplussed and only gave her a rude hand gesture in return.

It was in that unexpected gesture she finally saw the familiar gesture she’d been expecting since day one from the foreign man. It matched something Ronnie sometimes did when he was distracted, but given their shared Gladiator training manager, that wasn’t too unusual. She only wondered if Lila might share some of those same traits, but it was ultimately a passing thought.

Esther was too enraptured by the house and inspecting it when the blue haired man sauntered over and stole her attention nearly as much as the house had. Aiden Waters, or better known as his Gladiator title, Duke Waters, and currently the only known person to have an Authority in the Realm, and undoubtedly the youngest and lowest Tier one to have done so.

His Tier 37 cultivator base wasn’t threatening in and of itself, but his eyes carried with them a depth she found unsettling. They were like ink black water on a moonless night that wanted to drown anyone foolish enough to enter its dominion, and that told her everything she needed to know about him.

“Fuck the nerd shit, everyone needs to sit down and drink! Now that everyone has their Aspects, or knows it but isn’t forming it because they are too cowardly to figure it out on the fly,” the last was added with a glare to Aster, who clearly heard but didn’t care if her continued conversation with Sien and Gidieon. “Why don’t we talk about Authorities? Or more importantly, how much you all will pay me to teach you all you need to know!?”

Esther’s interest shifted, but Lawrence's did so like a piece of iron discovering its attraction to a magnet for the first time. However, they seemed to be the only ones at all interested.

Every single other person in the room immediately started booing and cursing the Empire Gladiator, including all the other Empire Gladiators, who were undoubtedly the loudest. Even the phoenix on Liz’s shoulder chittered and chirped in protest, using the universal beast language to curse the man far worse than her human body did, which was already impressively vulgar in its own right.

“Fuck you guys, I know you want to make your own Authorties so you can be a fraction as cool as me. But if that’s how you guys want to be, fine, I won’t share the super cool stuff I might have figured out about them. I’ll keep it all to myself.”

Esther’s initial reaction was to delve through time to see what he was going to say, but the moment she did so, she was once again swallowed by a waiting dragon maw hidden under the sand.

Looking over, the dragon in question was eating her way through a mountain of shrimp next to Maxine, but she still didn’t seem to know or wasn’t acknowledging Esther’s attempts.

Esther hadn’t tried to avoid the other woman's gaze since she’d arrived, but the small smirk Max wore wasn’t hidden by the light fractals that constantly spun and twisted around her face.

Her distraction worked out in her favor, as it inadvertently mirrored everyone else in the room except Lawrence, who waited eagerly for the next words.

When Aiden saw that he only had one appreciative listener, he scoffed as he returned to his previous seat where he threw himself. “Pretend all you want, but at least the new guy’s honest about his interest, unlike the rest of you liars. And yet, I see your ears twitching like a bunch of rabbits who hear a cat!”

Three versions of, “Don’t compare me to a rabbit!” echoed out from the two dragons and the fox, who all seemed to take genuine offence at Aiden’s mocking words.

Matt rolled his eyes as he stepped away from the group, saying, “I’m going to finish cooking. When you eventually can’t hold it in anymore and brag, you’d better speak loud enough for me to hear or I’ll serve you an empty plate.”

Aiden looked like he was going to argue, but he clearly thought better of it as he leaned on the nearby couch to pester Gideon. He tried to make it look casual, but the four poses he cycled through before landing on one killed any momentum his earlier words might have built.

His actual comment, however, brought much of it roaring back. “I think an Authority might actually be the Domain stage required for Tier 100, not Tier 75.”

Esther was contemplating the idea and its implications when Liz opened her mouth and said. “That’s got to be the dumbest thing I’ve ever heard.”

Aiden was left spluttering as most of the rest of the room joined in.

“No guys, really, I’ve been thinking about it! I even went through the Empire’s copies of the descender records. I think there might be a case here.”

“The Empire’s records are shit, we can’t trust them. Agatha burned down half your stuff and apparently half your brain.” Eclavorn snorted back.

Even Matt called out from where he washed his hands in the kitchen, “Are you sure you don’t just want to look more impressive? This feels like your typical bragging.”

That earned even more dogpiling but Aiden didn’t let himself be deterred and stood back up and pushed on valiantly. “No guys, really, I checked the records and I think there might be an argument here. There are three known depositories of descender’s AI dumps, only one of them talks about an Authority by name and their rough translation is something close to ‘Peak of the Realm Liquid’. Paired with the older one referencing five Domain stages, it has been theorized and accepted that there is a Tier 75 Domain stage, but the more I push for that elusive next step, the more I can’t help but question if what we suspect is accurate at all.”

Esther was contemplating the odds of what Aiden said but the others took his words another way.

Ellen walked out into the kitchen, where she acted as Matt’s sous chef, while Lila snorted as she turned her attention to the plate of deviled eggs she’d snatched away. “Yeah, he’s definitely fluffing his own ego with some reasonable sounding bullshit. A bottle of Tier 40 bourbon to bet that he only wanted to share that he’s already working on the next stage. Again. It’s like the third time you’ve shoehorned this into a conversation. Yes, we know you are trying. Either succeed or kill yourself with a failure already. But whatever you do, stop bringing it up before you have anything to show for it. It's just embarrassing at this point.”

Allie, the star speckled teleporter, poked her head out from around Olivier and added to the badgering, “Aiden, I don’t think anyone will actually be impressed even if you do manage it after you’ve bragged so much. You do this every time we gather. I’m with our big scaly Mom on this one.”

“I do not!” Aiden quickly interjected but his words were met with disbelieving stares that only lessened their ire as he added, “it’s like three quarters of them at most. I definitely didn’t brag at — oh, yes I did. Whatever.”

Reganing his energy, he rolled over the back of an empty couch and into a sitting position from his higher perch. “Fine, I just figured you all might like to listen to a grandmaster of Domains lecture on the profoundness of the Realm.”

Lila was the one to speak and her tone was barely interested as she asked, “Did you learn anything concrete or new about your Authority or the next stage?”

“Well, no, just my speculation but I—”

Lila reached up and made a fist, silencing the water mage as she worked her way through the plate of hors d'oeuvres. “Nope! Don’t give me that, Aiden, or I’ll put your ass out to dry.”

Esther was looking forward to seeing how until she remembered that any time she tried to dive into a possible future she was eaten by the dragon in question.

Said woman turned her attention more firmly to her and Lawrence as she smiled and pulled out a small vial from a storage ring after finishing her snack.

“Esther, Lawrence. It's a pleasure to finally and officially meet you. My name is Lila, as I’m sure you’ve heard I also dabble in time magic.”

Lawrence took the proffered hand before Esther did, but most of their attention was on the small vial of golden sand in the Tier 44’s hand.

“Is that—?”

“Tier 37 Millennium Hourglass Sand? Yes it is. I pulled it out of deep chaotic space six thousand years ago or so, and have been keeping it close ever since. I was already well past that Tier when I did so, but now that you two are here, I figure I might as well offer it up as a bet item a time or two. A little birdy told me you were looking to make a new Time based item…”

The dragon let her words tail off as Esther inadvertently licked her lips. All of her other considerations were forgotten, blown away by the sands of time in front of her. Hourglass Sand, no matter its age, was one of the few time-based Natural Treasures that was seen with any regularity, as its only requirement seemed to be an old enough untouched desert. However, there were variants and its named age was often irrespective of its Tier or actual age.

Decade old sand was useful for any divination specialist, with Century sand being what had made their weapons. Millennium sand wasn’t guaranteed to be ten times older than Century sand, it was often far older than that, but it was ten times more potent in its magical properties, and that was what made it so incredibly rare. The Republic had searched high and low for such sand from the time they were Tier 25 to Tier 35, but had to settle with the lesser age. They’d suspected outside interference to weaken them and their weapons, but they hadn’t been able to find anything that proved that theory beyond the admittedly normal unavailability of Millennium Hourglass Sand.

With it as the core to an amplifier, they’d be able to push their time manipulations that much farther for the same amount of energy.

However, Lila wasn’t offering a trade. She’d offered a bet.

Esther wasn’t the largest gambler but she wanted the vial of sand. “A bet? How exactly would that work?”

The dragon’s grin matched the gem emended in her chest, sharp and dangerous. “Our respective leaders would be mighty upset if we were giving things away or trading with each other. Betting? Well, that's another, much harder matter to regulate, as you saw earlier with our new exclusive Ascender eating table.”

Esther didn’t need the rest of it spelled out for her and understood the implications. She hardly cared about the how, she only cared about the what.

“And what would you like to see put in opposition to such an impressive wager item?”

Lila’s smile widened further until it looked as if she’d split her face in half. “Nothing too onerous, say a journal of your time mastering early time magic. Something a beginner might want to read to get their toes wet. Say Path level things, no need to go past Tier 25 or too secretive.”

It wasn’t an expensive price but it wasn’t an easy one to pay either. Their personal teachings had been sought after even before their ‘deaths’, and after so long their experience still retained its value.

Lawrence at least was able to make a coherent counter. “That’s far from what would be considered beginner time magic. Counter offer, Tier 5.”

“Not a chance. I’ve studied your records well before your revival, Tier 15 is where you really were able to start leaning into your time magic with your bloodline transformation. Tier 25 or no deal.”

“That’s far too high!”

The two of them went back and forth with plenty of comments from the watching audience, but most were in their favor as people rooted for the dragonflies rather than the dragon.

Esther was going to feel out a question of her own when Lawrence flickered as he looked at Lila. “Did you just eat me?”

Lila looked confused at the question. “Did you try to play with time? You must have if you were eaten.”

The muscular woman's grin turned wicked as she laughed as their expressions must have confirmed her suspicions. “I may not be a time expert, but I do have my own measures to prevent tricks like yours. I just never knew if they worked the way I wanted them too. HA!”

With a sigh, the dragon shook her head as she continued. “It’s a shame you guys died, I’d have loved to fight you at-Tier. I bet I could have learned a lot from eating you a few times. Pity, if you don’t want to trade your knowledge I might be able to do something else. I want a sample of your bloodlines as a counter offer. I can promise I’ll only use it for secondary burning reasons and not spread or share it.”

They declined, but the discussion brought in the rest of the others as more helpful counters were offered.

Sadly the discussion was derailed as the talk of bets turned to their most recent fight.

“It's bull you beat us at all, Maya! That inversion trap wasn’t fair! How long were you holding onto that in your back pocket? We deserved to go out 3-0 and everyone here knows it.”

“Me, cheating? You taunted Matt for months to get Liz in a position to knock her out of the fight. If that’s fair, which they haven't complained about, an inversion trap for your dumbass is too. Have some backbone man!”

That comment sparked more discussion until everyone turned to Maxine and asked for her verdict.

The fractals around her spun rapidly, but her smile was slow and easy. “Hey, don’t look at me, I already admitted my predictions were slightly off. I foresaw Esther and Lawrence losing to Maya as well, and their fight against Matt, Liz, and Aster was closer than expected. Fights can shift in a moment and we saw that in your fights.”

“You only lost one of your side bets, Max, shut your trap, I was entirely wrong!”

Maya stomped her feet as she glared at Olivier, who hadn’t said anything. “And shut up, you sore loser! I can’t believe you tricked me into betting my spectra-kaleidoscope again. I never should have done so. Did you sabotage me at super speed just so I’d lose?”

“You wish. No, you lost both your bets and your fights on your own lack of merit. Don’t go looking to share blame. Aster, you tell her!” The speedster immediately shot back.

“Hey now, don’t drag me into this! I’m more annoyed than any of you. I got captured in every fight. I’m thoroughly pissed and have been working my tail off to make sure it's not so easy next time.” The fox’s ears twitched as she looked to Esther and Lawence, “Though, can I ask you two to do that time stretch thing a few more times? That was really interesting and helpful. I had quite a bit of awareness despite being unable to do anything and I want to explore that. Some time next week maybe?”

Esther swallowed and desperately wanted either the food to come out to distract everyone or for people to stop asking for so much insight on their time-based powers. Aster shouldn’t have been able to sense the passage of time because time hadn’t been passing for her, and that anomaly wasn’t one she wanted to exacerbate.

However, before she or her brother could refuse, the fox reached out and snatched the bottle of sand that was sitting next to Lila, tempting the two of them with it.

“I’ll bet you a bottle of sand I just found you can’t do it again.”

Esther desperately wanted to accept and had to swallow twice to get her denial out when the dragon didn’t instantly object to her bet item being pilfered and reoffered.

Thankfully, the growing smells wafting from the kitchen told her she wouldn’t have to wait too long.

The food managed to do what every shouted voice in the house couldn’t manage, shut everyone up. Even the ornate table was an afterthought. At least until most plates were clear where people started blatantly lying about the food.

The perfectly cooked lamb that she’d eaten seven or eight times, one aborted timeline bite at a time, was declared chewy and tough while the potatoes that were so expertly baked they didn’t require much butter were called dry and chalky.

The lies eventually grew so blatant that Ellen accidentally insulted one of the foods she made, where the game and dinner ended. After that everyone returned to the living room to continue the evening with an ocean’s worth of more Tier appropriate alcohol to celebrate Esther and Lawrence's entrance into the full Gladiator Chat.

It was one of the weirder evenings she’d experienced, but one both of them were eager to have again. For the first time since their revival, Esther felt… not quite at home, but among people who understood her at a level she feared she’d never find again with the absence of the friends who’d they’d grown alongside.

However, despite all the warm feelings, she and Lawrence were determined the next time they gathered like this they’d have a much better record against their peers. Much better.
