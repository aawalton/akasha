”You’ve ended someone’s life.“

Hebi stood there at the entrance, facing towards his young master. His stubble had grown some more since the past year. Clearly too lazy to keep it groomed.

Now that Shuren was closer to Path Foundation, he long realised the guard in front of him felt like a deep well, with no end in sight. He couldn’t sense his cultivation level at all.

“They were better off dead. Dregs of a demonic cult.” Shuren nonchalantly said.

Hebi rubbed his chin, eyes somewhat wide at Shuren’s words. He smacked his shoulder roughly while chuckling.

“Not bad. Here I was expecting you to be pissing your silk pants in fear. Remember this. Once you enter a battlefield with a weapon in hand, you cannot hesitate.” With a wide grin, Hebi reminded Shuren. Uncharacteristically, Hebi began to reminisce as he held the hilt of his embellished red sword. “When I was your age I had-”

“Do not misunderstand. I don’t go around flaunting or enjoying it. I don’t look towards a death or life fight, even if it tends to bring out the potential to grow stronger. It only had to be done.” Shuren glared, clicking his tongue. He didn’t want to hear Hebi’s worthless goading. That was the last thing he needed.

All demonic cultivators must be killed. It was very simple.

But the executioner shouldn’t relish in his role.

The annoying grin didn’t leave Hebi’s face as his Qi linked with the formation seal to open up the manor doors. Shuren could now see the true veins of the manor, intricate Rank 5 inscriptions everywhere. Hebi took out a distinctive teeth cleaning twig, made from a translucent Persica tree. Shuren had now recognised it, courtesy of hanging around with Zhou Yan.

“Did you come over from the west?” He asked.

Hebi stopped for a moment to gaze at Shuren, before replying, “You’re finally thinking a bit… Yeah, that’s my hometown.”

“If you held yourself with the air fitting of your real level, I would’ve thought of this long ago,” Shuren quipped, heading inside the manor. “The saying that you learn something new every day is indeed true.”

“Too bad I get to miss out on his reaction inside,” Hebi grumbled as he continued to brush his teeth.

Shuren walked towards his father’s study room on the first floor. The décor had changed, even more of his father’s lifelike sculptures taking up their place. He ended up meeting a new maid he’d never seen before. She jolted at his appearance, hurrying to bow.

“Young master, your father is not in the study room right now. You can find him in his lab,” The young maid said, her facial muscles tightened from nervousness.

Shuren gazed at her features, with dark auburn ears that rested on her head and a tail. She was actually their first servant that hailed from the Vulfoans. Shuren still questioned, “A recent hire?”

“Yes, Sir Xuan recommended me due to my experience. I once served the City Lord of Yestervaul.”

That fallen city, the site of that Unhallowed Monolith…

“So long as your efforts and loyalty serve the Liu Manor, we will reward and give you our support. Be well,” Shuren said with a nod, climbing the stairs to the second floor.

The young maid finally opened her silver eyes, while heaving out from the pressure. “He’s a lot more easy going than I thought.”

Usually he has a set routine.

The manor was rather quiet, and his siblings wouldn’t be home at this time and day. Shuren didn’t express it, but he was slightly disappointed he couldn’t catch up- or rather, annoy his siblings.

His father’s lab room dedicated to his Spiritwood Sculpting had a new décor to the side. An intricately crafted four-limbed dragon that was animate, capable of breathing a harmless rhythm of illusory fire. His father had also been improving himself throughout the past year.

Spiritwood Sculpting was a delicate craft, so Shuren didn’t knock on the door. Since it was a bit ajar, he walked in.

He met his father of course, but next to him was a woman he recognised as well. Unlike in the sect though, she had dressed up quite well with braided dark hair.

“Zhou Yan…what are you doing in my house,” Shuren almost stammered. His brows furrowed as it felt like his privacy had been invaded. “Why have I not heard news of this? Did you not-”

“You’re too rigid, Shuren. If I asked you, you would’ve made this into a big favour I’d have to owe you. Too annoying. Luckily your dad is a very nice man, the complete opposite of you,” Zhou Yan retorted back immediately.

He couldn’t deny her words. His left eye twitched.

His father was clearly enjoying every bit of this scene, chuckling at the silly antics of these two. Even if the bags under his eyes had grown deeper. “Come over my son, you should’ve told me you knew this polite and talented young lady, the daughter of the Zhou Clan Leader. I’m glad you’re making good friends in the sect.”

Shuren had no words to say, only facepalming. This woman managed to get his own father on her side.

He could only fault himself. Shuren could long sense that Zhou Yan had grown irritated with him. She ended up harbouring a grudge because he made her his personal teacher for far too long. But never would he thought she was this interested in his father’s vocation, or go to this extent to taunt him.

“I also plan to invite Zhou Yan’s family to the banquet. Would that be alright with you, young Yan?”

She clasped both her hands as she humbly bowed. “That would be lovely, Sir Mingjian. Me and my family would be delighted to come,” she said in the most bubbly voice possible.

Shuren felt like coughing out blood. He didn’t bother putting up airs as he asked, “Father, please send an invitation to the Wu Clan in Cloud City.”

His father grasped his beard. “You’ve also gotten acquainted with the Wu Clan? Very well, I will send them a letter today.” Seeing his son make waves in the Seven Clouds Sect, and making friends with worthy people had made him very proud.

Shuren had initially come for a longer discussion, but he lost all interest remaining here. “Very well father, I will take my leave. I still have urgent business to attend to,” Shuren said before swiftly leaving.

His father and Yan Zhou looked at each other before chuckling. “My son is still a bit shy, please ignore his antics.”

“Seeing that stupid face when he’s always scowling was worth it.” Yan Zhou grabbed a spiritual pill she had crafted. “So is it true you can imbue the sculpted wood with different properties of Qi? Does it originate from the Southern Rituals?”

It had been a while since the head of the manor met someone very passionate about his vocation, so he paid no mind to telling her the intricate details. “It’s a work in progress, and still relatively new even to the spiritualists. We are still in the prototype stages for now, but the goal is far more ambitious than merely making trinkets. The development of Spiritude Artifacts is garnering even more investors now than ever.”

As Hebi expected, the young master had already fled from the manor. “Stop being so awkward, young master,” he yelled out as Shuren swept on his cloud. “If you want to impress a girl, you need to be more confident.”

“Has anyone ever said you talk too much, Hebi?” Shuren rolled his eyes as he flew off back to Seven Clouds. His fingers were rubbing the ridge of his nose. “For now, I need to focus on breaking through to Path Foundation and become a Rank 2 cultivator. Have you forgotten I already had a-”

“What’s this? I thought you were the one who broke it up? Stop hiding behind such excuses and man up a bit.” Hebi taunted, biting on to that small twig of wood.

Shuren scowled, ignoring his taunts. I don’t have time to waste on such things. I need to reach Rank 2 and gain more headway in the sect. Core Disciple should be within my reach soon.

Shuren was almost at the brink of reaching Stage 11, but now he had to start from the beginning of Stage 10 all over again.

But, Through life and death battles, the mastery of his Qi and his potential had been brought out.

During some of the public seminars with the elders that specialised purely in fighting power, and his personal practical experience, Shuren had gained a deeper understanding of the flow of Qi.

So even with that disadvantage, he should be able to catch up fast.

When the body starts producing adrenaline and various other biological hormones, the flow of Qi becomes far more seamless and more rigorous. In return, the backlash would be delayed from overexerting his mind and body after the adrenaline wore off. It was why cultivators, even though at the brink of death, should be barely able to use even a single martial technique, were able to pull off their strongest moves. It was at the cost of damaging their meridians, or in some clandestine techniques, burning their lifespan, to live in the first place.

Still, it didn’t mean that one had to force themself into life threatening situations to break through to the next stage. Especially to Path Foundation, you could start to break through after preparing yourself with cycling of Qi until all your Spiritual Meridians were filled. But at Seven Clouds, they purported that confidence was the key.

Confidence in your own Path.

For now, I’ll focus on the staff. That’s right, I need to reach Stage 12 then decide. I still have some leeway.

When he arrived at his room, he was met with a sorry sight. A young dark haired man in tattered and bleeding robes staggered towards him, almost falling down.

Shuren grabbed him as he fell. His face was not only bruised but filled with tears and snot. It was terrible to the point Shuren could smell his blood from a distance. He soon realised that even his presence of Qi was fluctuating, almost flickering in and out of existence.

With trembling fingers, some bent out of place, the injured person brushed it fast Shuren’s collar. “Shuren, p-please. A-avenge me…”

Shuren could only widen his eyes, shaking from the sheer rage broiling from within.

“Cousin Wei?!”
