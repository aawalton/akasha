Chapter II.XXXVII (2.37)

A few hours later, Mitsuko strolled through the forest trail, debating on how best to subdue the troll. She whacked a massive purple fern out of her path with one of her ice swords.

Verna rode in her belt pouch. Even with the slickness of her shell, the roots and rocks proved too much an obstacle for her to maneuver over. She eyed the sword Mitsuko wielded with undisguised interest, readable even on a reptile’s face. “I know I mentioned this while in the heart of Porcelain Island, but that is quite a remarkable artifact.”

Mitsuko raised the blade. A bead of water trickled down its edge as it slowly melted under the sun’s oppressive warmth.

“Thanks. It was a gift from an old friend.”

“Quite the gift from quite the friend.”

“He decided I’d put it to better use than him since he had discovered more effective means of fighting.”

“Such as?”

“Ripping apart enemies on a cellular level.” Mitsuko swatted another plant as she continued bushwacking her way forward. “Remember when I claimed earlier that I thought I could best any mage on the island?”

“Mitsuko, I’m old. But I can still recall statements from earlier this same day.”

“Well, I said that because he isn’t on this island. If he were, this whole timeloop would be going very differently for all of us.”

“You have a lot of confidence in your friend.” Verna sounded amused.

Mitsuko simply chuckled. The sage clearly didn’t believe her. She’d been locked away for over a thousand years and didn’t know about the events of the last decade. Mitsuko considered rectifying that hole in her education but lost her train of thought as she came up to the edge of a bluff.

She edged over the bluff and eyed a familiar troll at the bed of the gulch. His head was down as he grumbled something unintelligible. If Mitsuko had to guess, she’d point money on the grumbling being toll related.

Mitsuko attempted to reach out with her aura and cast Retrospection on his mind from up on the bluff. The spell slipped off him and her spell hit a wall. Not in the same way that her spell slipped off acolytes of the guardians. No. Mitsuko could tell she simply lacked the power in this case.

“If I want this to work, I’m going to need to get closer,” Mitsuko reflected out loud. “Maybe even touch him.”

“Would this be a good time to experiment with my spell?” Verna asked. “I’ve been waiting for you to have a spare moment.”

Mitsuko considered. The troll was currently grabbing globs of mud from the ground and slathering himself in it. She watched him grab a clump and find an old, rotting carcass within. It split apart as he lifted the glob, the majority of the dead creature falling back into the muck. He popped the remaining leg in his mouth. He chewed for a bit, then spit out an old boot and some bones.

She supposed it was as good a time as any for a bit of instruction. It did seem like he was likely to run off anywhere in the meantime.

Mitsuko took a seat on a purple tree trunk and settled in for a lesson. “Sure. Give me a crash course.”

“Excellent.” Verna slid forward and positioned herself between Mitsuko and the bluff’s edge. “I gave you a run down on the spell’s basics in the last loop; however, you hadn’t actually unlocked it yet when I explained. How about you test it out now?”

Before Verna had even completed her last sentence, Mitsuko had cast the spell. To her surprise, the turtle’s words never slowed down. They continued at a clipped pace, unimpeded by the spell. The troll below her, on the other hand, moved as if through thick liquid. He grabbed another glob of mud and smashed it on his face. Mitsuko watched with fascination as it splattered and bits slowly flew into the air while the majority of the mass very slowly oozed down his chin. And this was how the world appeared when only 25% slowed down. She marveled at what further levels in the spell would unlock.

Then everything snapped back into normal speed. That adjustment, more than the spell’s initiation, left her unbalanced mentally. For a second, it felt like everything in the world was going by too quickly. Then her mind reacclimated to the normal timestream.

“Why did everything else slow except for you?” Mitsuko asked Verna. “Did I accidentally target you as well with the spell?”

“Not quite.” Verna snapped her little turtle jaw. “Well, actually, I suppose you kind of did. Halfway.”

“Mind explaining what you mean?”

“Listen, so you know how all our souls are linked? My perception is linked to your own.”

“So, I did speed you up as well?”

“Again, only halfway. My perception. Not my body.”

“Hm.” Mitsuko picked up a stone and tossed it in the air. She cast Dilation as gravity pulled it back down and it slowly drifted down into her palm. Then she waited for her cooldown and activated it again before tossing the stone skyward. This time, it took until the stone was halfway through its arc until it slowed. Like the rest of the world, it seemed to move at a slowed down speed once outside her direct effect. Good to know that it was only herself that the spell targeted for now. The rest of the world would remain slower than usual.

As an additional test, Mitsuko flicked her wrist. A new blade of ice appeared, created by her artifact ring. Likewise, its appearance took just a smidge longer than she was used to.

“That could be annoying,” Mitsuko mused. As she spoke, she noticed her own words seemed to have a very subtle delay. It sounded weird. “Is my speaking delayed as well?” Mitsuko asked Verna.

“Yes. It takes time for sound to travel. Of course, the delay will only be at the start. Words spoken during this time will still appear to others at a rapid speed.”

“It takes time for sound to travel?”

“There’s a lot of research and data involved in the subject. It gets a bit complicated and I’m certain a master auditory illusionist could instruct you better on the theory than I. But, spoken simply, yes. It takes time for noise to travel distances.”

“Well, I guess that could be good for stealth?” Mitsuko said after a bit more thought. “If the sound my footsteps don’t hit ears until seconds after I stepped there, then they’ll be confused about my location.”

“Only your perception is affected. Others will hear the same as always. Plus, as your spell is right now, there will be less than half a second of delay. In my opinion, it’s a useless endeavor to pursue.”

“For now. But I have no doubt the spell will get stronger as I level and practice.”

Somehow, Verna managed to shrug while in the body of an ice turtle. “You’ll find out.”

After a few more minutes of experimentation, Mitsuko felt like she had a decent handle of the spell. Enough, at least, not to accidentally get herself killed.

“You know, this is my first time ever seeing a troll,” Verna commented. “They’re quite ugly. His skin flops around as he moves. And the dirty loincloth is not doing him many favors.”

“Did you never visit Mauve in your past life? I assumed an immortal like yourself would have explored every bit of the archipelago.”

“Ha. My immortality isn’t the same as you and Sterling. Conditions must be met to maintain mine. Namely, cold.”

“Oh.” Mitsuko blinked. Forever trapped on Porcelain Island sounded… less than appealing. Imprisoned in her kingdom for eternity.

Verna continued to stare at the troll as it gurgled mud. “It’s why I went out of my way to learn to craft more sculpts. That island was so lonely. Very few natural creatures could exist there without aid.”

“How does that work?” Mitsuko had been curious, but hadn’t found an opportunity to ask until now. This probably wasn’t the best time, but it wasn’t the worst. “Do you build one another like golems?”

Verna barked a laugh. “I procreate the same as you. Once I had a baby in my womb, I required a special potions to keep myself from melting from within. A witch designed them for me actually. I, obviously, shared the recipe with my children. And, after a few generations, it wasn’t much of an issue anymore since they could procreate with each other.”

“Sculpts are part human?”

“Yep. I was crafted by a human, after all, so it only made sense. He based me off his species structure.”

“Ahh. Interesting.” Mitsuko had a lot of biological questions that she didn’t even know how to phrase. Instead of continuing this line of thought, she turned to the bluff and focused on something she did understand. Fighting.

She slowed time and stepped off the cliff edge.
