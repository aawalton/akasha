As Karva’s team prepared to enter the rift, Kayline was trudging through the forest with Lina and her team, apprehensive over every sound. Despite her pocket watch reading 1:27 am, the artificial sun was as bright as ever, allowing her to see poisons, beasts, and traps with bleak clarity. The team periodically killed beasts weighing a metric ton and designated them as D-rank walkers, as if the lower letters were mere myths. It was an eye-opening experience.

It was also exhausting. It had been twenty-one hours since they entered, and they had only taken two breaks. The only reason she and Lina were still standing was because a gentle healer named Miki healed their blisters. Since calluses didn’t form unless the body healed naturally, they had to do it every two hours.

It was the worst.

“Okay,” Lina said, breaking the silence. “We've been hiking for over twenty hours, and I haven’t said a word. So I think that I’m entitled to one question.”

The group leader turned to her. His name was Hart, and he was rather amicable, so he patiently awaited her question.

“Is there a reason we’ve been walking so long?” Lina asked. “Or is this normal?”

“It’s not normal, no. We’re anticipating more Sickles to come through the rift any second. We need to be in position by then.”

Kayline gripped her wind-up pocketwatch. “Does that mean we’re going to ambush them?”

Hart didn’t answer. Miki looked away. They all exchanged glances.

“I’m not opposed to it,” Kayline said. “Really. I just think you guys should be honest so Lina and I don’t do anything stupid. Better to prepare now than freak out in the thick of it.”

Hart’s team members nodded, and he turned to them. “There’s no plan to ambush anyone. We’re setting up a safehouse four miles south of the purple mountain. We’ve been instructed to wait for Hayden there—and that’s it. Of course, if he’s chased by the Sickles… an ambush is implied.” He looked at the others and back. “We’re mercenaries—so we’re prepared for it.”

“Thank you,” Kayline said. “We’ll save our questions.”

Hart nodded, but then hesitated.

“Is something wrong?” Lina asked.

“It’s just… we’re curious about your role.”

“Political meat shields,” Kayline said. “No one’s going to attack if they knew who we are.”

“Excuse me… what did you just say?” a mercenary asked. “Did you just say political?”

Hart turned to him. “You don’t recognize her?”

“Wait…” Miki’s eyes widened. “Kayline? As in The K-Line?”

Half a dozen mercenaries groaned when they figured out who the sisters were.

“How the hell are we supposed to fight around them?”

“Fuck.”

“Who is she?”

“A trade commissioner's daughter. Right?”

Lina blushed. “Yeah. But don’t worry. We can protect ourselves.”

“Don’t be stupid,” Hart said. “You’re way out of your league so just… stay put unless Hayden calls for you. Now come on. We got an hour left, and then we can rest for days… weeks… a month. So let’s just get this over with.”

Kayline didn’t like being looked down on, but she understood her role. So she turned ahead, eyes focused on the colossal purple mountain in front of her. It was bright purple—truly unnatural. And as she watched it, eyes transfixed, she thought that she saw it move. She would have sworn it did, but then again, the world was blending from hiking and sweating for over twenty hours straight. So she pressed it out of her mind as the team hiked to the designated cave and covered it with illusion spirits. Then, she fell into a deep sleep and forgot about it completely.

It was about seven a.m. when Yaka spotted wings at the wormhole. It was Mot and the other wings, including eight more fliers—alongside fifty-two other Sickles. He didn’t recognize most of them, but he did recognize the woman with white and blue hair. The whole universe knew her face on sight. Fuck.

“What’s the count?” Kalia asked when he returned.

“Sixty. Twenty are moving north to intercept us. The other half are moving east toward Violet Mountain. It looks like they’re trying to encircle us. Plus…”

“Plus what?” Photel asked gravely.

“They brought the Frost Witch.”

“You can’t be serious!” Tibbi cried. “Are they trying to kill us?”

“Maybe,” Hayden said. “Let’s go.” He started hiking east.

“Wait! We’re still moving east? You did hear that the Sickles are going there, right?”

Hayden turned back. “This isn’t a discussion. Now march.”

“To my death?” Tibbi snapped. “Look, I don’t know if you know who the Frost Witch is, but she’s the real deal. That woman’s known for freezing full plains with a single strike and killing other rifters with her blast. She’d be an S-rank if she hadn’t killed so many people. Get it? We need to run.”

Kalia pursed her lips and turned to Hayden, only to cower under his chilling gaze.

“This isn’t a discussion,” he said. “This isn’t a strategy meeting. I’m your commander and you will follow orders.”

“At least explain why,” Photel said. “If they sent the Frost Witch, they’re either gonna kill everyone, or kill us and take over your protection. No one’s gonna function if we think we’re just going off to our deaths.”

Hayden focused on him and then swept his gaze on the others. “Did you all sign up for a suicide mission? Because I hired you to protect me from the Sickles, but since then you’ve only expressed the futility of fighting them.” He made eye contact with each, leading each to avert their eyes. “Pathetic.” He paused to let them reflect in silence and then spoke, “I came here expecting to face the Sickles, and I plan to win. So follow my orders and don’t question them. The last thing I want is your defeatism poisoning real strategies. Now move. I need to get stronger before we reach the cliff.”

Yaka watched him leave in a state of awe, shocked to hear that Hayden planned to “win” against the Sickles. No one knew what that meant, but it didn’t sound pretty. In fact, it sounded ominous. Hayden was completely unfeeling when he learned of Breaker’s fate. He just looked at Violet Mountain with a dark look in his eyes. Yaka didn’t know what it was, but he got a real bad feeling.

Kalia also had a bad feeling about what was happening. The more time she spent with Hayden, the more she realized that nothing he did was random. He had the Nebula Auction House’s resources, but he hadn’t opened a gate. She thought that was insane, but now, as she watched Hayden slaughter a pride of three-ton super cats with his Clear Line spirit, she realized that he knew exactly what he was doing.

Hayden slapped one cat's paw with a whip, and the creature’s core shook violently, making it fall. The whip then curled around its leg and developed sharp blades. He pulled, and the leg ripped clean off in a rain of gore—proof Hayden’s core was getting stronger.

Another cat attacked him from the back, but a line shot out of Hayden’s back and slapped the creature’s paw, sending it spasming to the ground.

The other cats backed up, using their eyes to tell each other to flee.

Unfortunately for them, there was no escape. Hayden had wrapped the trees with ultra-thin Razor Wire, so when the three ran, they hit the lines. Their cores immediately pulsed and exploded. It was brutal to watch.

Hayden harvested the tora from each, becoming stronger with every kill. Not that he needed the strength. Kalia was certain that he could kill any of them in a close-combat battle—even her—and that was terrifying.

“Could you even imagine how strong this guy was before he came back?” Yaka mused. “I mean, it had to have been ridiculous.”
