Banditry is a mostly forgotten trade in this day and age. The era of wandering killers and thieves remains in the dim, distant past of pre-Imperial ages, and such barbarism has been replaced by the rigid order and steady guiding hands of the great Emperor and its infinite wisdom.

This is not to say that criminal enterprises have vanished entirely. It is mortal to sin and to be flawed, and we must accept that such is the lot in life of all those unmotivated and who turn away from the Empire’s light. It’s simply to say that any such violence or antisocial activity is the purview of a few lone wolves, who possess little connection in ideology or behavior and can be addressed on such individual bases.

Only the outer reaches of the Empire, within the third ring, hold any sort of consistent crime, due in large part to their lack of education and underdeveloped social norms. Many cities can be considered dangerous to those in the Qi-Gathering realm who act unprepared, but with proper awareness and an understanding of how best to navigate such still-developing parts of our great nation, even these places possess little in the way of dangerous, backwards-facing bandits, thieves, or other assorted malcontents.

The era of stability that the Empire has ushered in glows for all to see, from the very tip of the Wall, into the wilds of the fourth ring, on down from the vaunted heights of the First ring and into the lives of us citizens. This golden age of safety, security, and calm could only be brought about through the wisdom and peaceful might of beings we can all admire and aspire to!

Running a subversive terror / bandit / spy organization is a lot of work.

Like, a lot of work. Enough work that if Hao Nera had known about it beforehand, he would’ve done almost anything else.

Except maybe join the military. There is no mountain of paperwork or logistics or bureaucracy that is high enough for him to be that desperate.

He is, in fact, aware of the irony.

He sighs, and that is enough to startle the bandit beside him so bad that they make it almost a foot off their chair.

“Heaven’s fat tits, since when have you been there?” screams the altogether too well-dressed thief whose shoulder Hao Nera was looking over.

“Long enough to see you think about stashing some of those letters in your pockets,” Hao Nera replies, tilting his head to indicate the pile of papers that have been spread across the minor noble’s desk. They juxtapose the gaudy, ostentatious mess that is the rest of the room, bedecked in awkwardly placed statues, old vases, and other signs of wealth that would be better on display in a museum.

Or in Hao Nera’s pockets. Or in a landfill. Pretty much anywhere other than someone’s main home office.

“I respect the gumption,” Hao Nera says, rolling the syllables, affecting a bit of a cadence. He falls into the now-vacated chair that the minor noble just leapt from, lounging and kicking his feet up onto the expensive wood of the table. “Honestly, I admire your… blind, desperate greed. It’s like seeing a little kid stick his head into an oven to get at the sweets faster. Funny, but also sort of scary in how stupid it is.”

He focuses for a second, clenching his abs and tightening up his cheeks.

With a flex of Dao and Intent, he vanishes.

The nobleman looks around, confused, wondering why he leapt out of his seat in the first place-

Hao Nera holds him in place this time when he tries to jump, leaning his massive, muscled frame on the more slender man’s shoulder.

“Seriously, it takes balls like fists or a head emptier than mine to think you can steal from the guy you’re stealing for. It’s one thing to steal, but when I know what I’m asking, know what I want, and know what you got, then really, what’s the point of trying to rob me? Without even a lie to make up for it?”

“Shadowed one, I-”

“Shhh, shh shh shh shh.” Hao Nera places a finger on his lips, then pats him on the head, wiping off some of the man’s fear-sweat with the action. “No talking. I’m not done monologuing. You be quiet.”

The man nods feverishly, looking like a poorly made wind up toy more than a man. Hao Nera can actually smell the fear stink coming off of him, rolling like an animal about to piss itself.

It is wildly funny, true, but it’s all just so boring. All this fucking work, just to come crack down on this little nobody? Ugh.

Worth it, though.

Hao Nera plucks one of the aforementioned letters off the desk, waving it under his nose as if smelling it. “Aaah, see, that’s one of the letters that I knew would be in his coffers, knew would be there, and told you to get, and then you still were going to lie to me about it! Me! I’m the lie-guy here, and I dare say I’m an expert at it- what was even your plan?”

“Ah ah ah,” Hao Nera says, patting the poor, terrified little nobleman on the back. “I’m not done monologuing. Next time you talk out of turn, I turn you inside out. Got it?”

“I’m so glad you understand! I really appreciate a good listener. See, when my organization approached you, they made clear that this was a test, right? A few letters from the sorts of ill-gotten gains you wanted for yourself anyways, and which neither of us has any real right to. You almost had it! You got so close! And then… well. Here you are.”

Hao Nera stops talking, looking expectantly at the much shorter figure staring at him with wide eyes.

“Well? I’m done monologuing. What do you think? I was working on it the whole time I was watching you.”

“No, no, no, I don’t need to know your name, damnit. Do you know how many people I have to keep straight in my head as it is? I know where you live, what you look like, your Qi, your family, where your money is, you think I want to add your damn name to that list?”

Wan Heng pales further with each item listed, his face almost completely flat by the end of it. If Hao Nera didn’t know his type as well as he does, he’d be pretty sure that the poor bastard would be about to weep.

More likely, he’ll piss himself, but Hao Nera doesn’t intend to be around for that part.

He’s begging to an empty room. There is no one else present.

Wan Heng lets out a breath, and his heart is thundering. There’s a flood of adrenaline in his body, running violently through every part of him. He’s never been so terrified, never been so afraid-

His statuary, his ornate vases, the collection of antique swords hanging on his walls, his desk with all the right paperwork on it, signing over to him his uncle’s land interests, at least the ones of any real use. And the letters, the letters he was encouraged to take from the estate sale by that strange, lowly figure, that beggar that knew too much and-

The letters. They’ve gone.

Wan Heng reaches out a hand-

There is no hand.

Someone took it from him.

Someone he doesn’t know. Someone without a face. Someone who isn’t- who isn’t real, who isn’t here, who was-

His hand is missing.

And there’s no one here. There was never anyone here.

Hao Nera watches as the desperate little nobleman falls into a ball in the corner, sobbing.

He can’t quite find it in himself to laugh- but he’s certainly not crying. Not over this piece of shit.

He tosses the severed hand onto the floor, where it’ll rot, undetected and unseen, for the next few months. By the time it starts eating into the carpet and through the floorboards beneath, the poor bastard will have lost his damn mind trying to find it, as will anyone with a weaker cultivation or comprehension of Dao than Hao Nera himself has.

The Dao of the Unseen is a hell of a drug, and the Intent that he packs alongside it sure does help.
