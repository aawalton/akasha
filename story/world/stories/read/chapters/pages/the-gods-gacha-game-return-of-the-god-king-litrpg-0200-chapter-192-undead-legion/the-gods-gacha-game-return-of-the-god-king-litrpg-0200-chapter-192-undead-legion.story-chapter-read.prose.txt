The moment Voldrath’s power settled into my body, the world transformed. The graveyard before me was no longer just rows of stone markers; it had become a vast reservoir of endless minions. Beneath every patch of disturbed earth, I could sense remnants of death lingering like embers that had yet to fully fade. And there were so many of them… far more than I had initially expected.

“Arise, my undead legion—{Undead Arise},” I chanted, my voice carrying a weight that did not belong to me alone.

The ground trembled. At first, it was subtle, just a tremor running beneath the soil. Then the neatly arranged rows around me began to collapse as the earth split open from below. Fingers clawed their way upward, followed by arms, then entire bodies, dragging themselves free from their graves. Some rose as skeletal remains, their bones grinding together as they reassembled themselves, while others emerged as decayed corpses with strips of rotting flesh still clinging stubbornly to their frames.

The number kept growing—from dozens to hundreds, then to thousands. They all stood in silence, awaiting my command. An undead legion formed before me, their hollow gazes fixed in my direction as Voldrath’s power bound them into a single will.

Even at less than 2 percent, I was able to raise thousands of corpses into undead, which was more than enough.

I felt like the more accustomed I became to Voldrath’s power, the more capable I was of summoning higher-tier and elite undead. Given enough time, summoning Daleth-rank undead—or even stronger variants—wouldn’t be impossible. Perhaps with better corpse materials, it could have already been achievable.

This also made me wonder whether these undead could level up and evolve, just like Ember and Solana. One thing I was certain of, however, was that killing my own undead wouldn’t grant me any experience points. As the creator of Divine Will, I had implemented a strict balance system, ensuring that no such loophole could be exploited. But now that I was merely a lowly divine warrior, I couldn’t help but feel that perhaps I had made it a little too rigid.

Still, as a necromancer who now controlled thousands of undead, my experience points were bound to increase rapidly. After all, any monsters killed by my undead legion would grant experience points to me. This was because they were extensions of my power rather than completely independent entities.

With that thought in mind, I slowly lowered my hand and turned my gaze toward the distant direction of the supermarket fortress.

“Boris and the others should be holding the line against the first wave. I wonder how well they’re doing,” I muttered.

With a single thought, the army began to move, and the once silent graveyard emptied itself as the dead marched forward in unison. I followed from behind, the Mask of a Thousand Races never leaving my face, maintaining the appearance of Black Phantom. Even if someone saw me, they wouldn’t be able to trace this back to my true identity.

The enemies shrieked as they were overwhelmed, their writhing parasites lashing wildly before being torn apart.
