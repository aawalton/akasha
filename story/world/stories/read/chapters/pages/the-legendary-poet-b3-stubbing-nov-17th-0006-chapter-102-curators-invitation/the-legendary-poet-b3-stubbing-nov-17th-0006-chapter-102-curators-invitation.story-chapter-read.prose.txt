Kage lowered his hands from his face.

Zherdev stood in the ruins of the fruit cart like a victorious raid boss who had somehow lost to stage hazards. Pear pulp clung to his greaves. His cape smoldered at one corner. One of the porters sat on the road holding the broken finial with both hands and staring at his life choices.

The stall owner arrived three seconds later.

He had the expression of a man whose soul had just been punted through a stained-glass window.

“My cart,” he said.

Zherdev slapped both gauntlets against his chest. “Brother. Friend. Merchant king. I got you.”

The owner blinked. “You obliterated my pears.”

“I will make this right so hard you won’t even remember the pears.”

He yanked open his inventory. Gold coins spilled into his palm in a thick, obscene glitter.

Kage watched the man work with the calm of long experience. Same technique as always. Fall off a cliff in public. Patch the crater with money.

The stall owner opened his mouth again. Zherdev shoved five gold into his hands. The man froze.

Zherdev turned to the four porters and gave each of them a gold coin.

The lead herald lifted a finger through the cape still wrapped around his face. “Sir, I suffered banner-related trauma.”

“You too, king,” Zherdev said, flicking him another coin.

An Azure Watch guard strode up with two others behind him, silver filigree catching the evening light. He took in the scattered fruit, the broken axle, the scorch marks, and Zherdev’s armor with the cold concentration of a museum critic judging vandalism.

“This behavior is an offense against civic beauty,” the guard declared.

Zherdev nodded rapidly. “Totally valid. Completely hear you.”

He placed three gold in the guard’s hand.

The guard weighed the coins once, then tucked them away with smooth professional dignity.

“Your reparations display sincerity,” he said. He pointed at Zherdev’s cape. “The orange secondary flare cheapens the purple.”

Zherdev looked genuinely wounded. “Really?”

“It screams for attention.”

“Oh.” Zherdev frowned. “Can that be fixed?”

The guard’s expression softened by a full millimeter. “The Chromatic Thread could save it, if the artisan feels merciful.”

“Bro,” Zherdev breathed. “Thank you.”

The stall owner bowed so fast it looked painful. One of the porters started collecting pears with devotional intensity. The whistle stopped. The plaza flowed again. Gold had sanded the whole incident smooth in under a minute.

Finn stared. Jax stared harder. Valdrias gave a solemn nod, like he had just witnessed a valid tanking technique.

Julie leaned toward Lily and murmured, “Honestly? Efficient.”

Vami watched Zherdev with the feverish respect of one min-maxer discovering a new species of build.

Kage stepped over a smashed wheel spoke and sent the party invite.

[Kage has invited Zherdev_The_Great to join the party.]

[Zherdev_The_Great - Lvl 19 Berserker has joined the party.]

[Party Members: 10/10]

He spread both arms like he expected applause from the city, then his eyes tracked across the party interface.

He squinted. His mouth fell open.

He jabbed a finger at Kage like he’d found a crime scene.

“Poet?”

Zherdev’s voice jumped an octave. “WHAT THE FUCK KAGE? Poet? Is this the end of the legendary duo? What happened to you?”

Good. Thirty seconds in and he’s already yelling in front of nine people and a fruit graveyard.

Then realization hit.

“Ohhh.” Zherdev nodded with gathering confidence. “Secret tech.”

Kage said nothing.

“Advanced concealment,” Zherdev continued. “Mask class. Hidden progression. Legendary people stuff.”

Zherdev slapped both hands together. “Sick. I knew it. No matter. With me around, the legendary duo can still perform.”

Zara closed her eyes.

Somewhere in the layering of particle effects, the chestpiece released three glowing rose petals that drifted into a drainage grate.

Level nineteen. Berserker. Gear spread all over the place. The item effects looked flashy and mostly useless.

Functionally, he’s a chandelier.

Of course. Of course…

They headed down the Mosaic Path as the crowd thickened around them.

Korros glowed under drifting Luma-Orbs, every lamp painting soft gold over white limestone and glass. Street performers competed with auction criers. Mosaics shifted color underfoot as the light changed. Their ten-man party moved through it like a parade curated by several separate gods.

Kage in charcoal and burgundy. Vami in blinding white leather. Julie clean and practical beside him. Lily in bright priestly trim. Zara with all the charm of a sharpened spreadsheet. Finn glancing at the sky every few seconds like the open air still felt borrowed. Jax built like an argument. Valdrias carrying his slab of a shield with solemn knight energy. He remembers legends. I remember wire transfers and hospital invoices.
