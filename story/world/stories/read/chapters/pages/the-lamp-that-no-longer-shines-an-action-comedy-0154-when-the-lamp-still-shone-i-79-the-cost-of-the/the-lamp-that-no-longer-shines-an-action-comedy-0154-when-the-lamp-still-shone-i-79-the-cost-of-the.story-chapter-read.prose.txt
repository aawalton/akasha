[Time]: Year 1939[Location]: The Ark Hive · Holding Cells

The news arrived on the third morning.

The Balor maid materialized through the reinforced vault door at precisely seven o'clock, carrying a lacquered tray bearing a pot of Darjeeling and a porcelain plate of fresh pastries. She set them down, opened her ledger, and delivered the intelligence update with the mild, conversational cadence of someone reading a change to the dessert specials.

"Your sister," she said, tapping her pen against the clipboard, "has slaughtered the Pantheon of the Underworld."

Tasia's fingers went rigid around the rim of her teacup. "That is mathematically impossible."

The Balor maid seemed genuinely entertained. She settled herself into the armchair beside the dessert cart, one obsidian leg crossed over the other.

"That's what the gods said. And then she kept going anyway."

It wasn't a matter of raw power. Every Witch with tactical clearance knew the nature of Death Gods: not sovereigns, but ecological maintenance. You didn't negotiate. You culled the uncooperative ones and waited for mortality itself to generate a replacement.

"Every time she executed one of the Pantheon," the Balor maid continued, uncapping her pen, "she consumed a fraction of the concept itself. The entities she killed are not respawning. She swallowed the ink the cards were printed with."

She made a small, precise check in her ledger. "The cosmic recycling program has been cancelled. The afterlife infrastructure of the entire multiverse is facing structural collapse."

The scale of it refused to fully process.

"The surviving pantheon," the Balor maid added, with a note of something approaching professional respect, "eventually threw themselves at her feet. Literal manifestations of entropy, weeping in the ash, pleading for their survival. 'If you monopolize the concept, you will have to process the afterlife paperwork of the entire multiverse yourself! We are civil servants! Please!'"

She snapped the ledger shut. "The frontline is calling her [Death] now. The Death that kills Death. Congratulations, Lady Tasia. Your sister is so fundamentally terrifying that I have no doubt your family will remain in an unassailable position long after this war concludes."

Tasia set her teacup down. She studied the Balor maid's entirely relaxed posture. "I would have assumed," she said carefully, "that news of this magnitude might prompt some concern about the balance of the war tipping toward our side."

The Balor maid's pleasant expression did not shift. She rose from the armchair and moved toward the vault door.

"You have a wonderful sense of humor, Lady Tasia," she said, tucking the ledger under her arm.

The vault sealed behind her. Her voice carried back through the spell-glass, already receding.

"The Conqueror is not a Death God."

Tasia sat alone in the silk-sheeted luxury of her captivity. The Darjeeling steamed. Her voice had nothing to offer in response.

[Time]: The Dead Hours

She woke to the sensation of glass shattering deep inside her chest.

Tasia bolted upright, her hand flying to her sternum. Not a physical wound. It was the anti-theft ward she had woven into the blood-rose years ago, the flower she had left behind on her desk in the palace when the war scattered her family. A bloodline-locked matrix. Only direct Milan'thirskaya blood could trigger it.

The silence in the cell block settled over her, immediate and total. Tasia sat on the edge of the memory-foam mattress, the silk sheets pooling around her, and waited.

The morning shift was late. The dessert cart did not arrive. The customary ambient sound of porcelain and pastry tongs fell silent.

When the heavy vault doors finally ground open, the atmosphere that bled in was unrecognizable.

The Balor Witches marched in with lethal precision. The frilled aprons were the same. Everything else was not. They moved with the rigid stiffness of soldiers entering a space they expected to become a crime scene.

The squad leader stopped before the spell-glass. Her obsidian horns caught the corridor light at a killing angle.

"Lady Tasia," the squad leader said, her voice stripped of all inflection. "Please come with us."

Tasia stood up slowly, every muscle coiling. "What happened?""Please come with us."

Behind her, the subordinates rose. The entirely-too-relaxed aristocrats who had spent the past several days treating their imprisonment as a minor inconvenience while waging what they called "tactical consumption warfare" on the confectionery reserves were gone.

In their place stood the elite guard of a royal bloodline. Defensive arrays compiled behind their eyes, barely visible.

Her adjutant stepped forward, interposing her body between Tasia and the spell-glass. "Where are you taking Her Highness?"

The squad leader exhaled. The breath came out slow, costing her something.She looked through the spell-glass directly at Tasia.

"Believe it or not, Lady Tasia," she said, "very soon, in this entire world, we will be the only entities who genuinely want to guarantee your safety."

"Speak plainly," Tasia said. She stepped around her adjutant. "There is nothing you can say that my people are not authorized to hear."

The squad leader held her gaze."Your mothers," she said, "just bombed our rear-line field hospital."

She paused."There were dozens of unhatched Witch eggs in the nursery wing."

The sentence arrived in pieces. Her mind received each word and refused to assemble them.A hospital. A nursery wing.

"The intelligence will saturate the global broadcast networks within the hour," the squad leader continued, her voice dropping. "We are acting on the direct, explicit orders of Her Excellency Famia Schüder to extract you to a secure location before the civilian response becomes uncontrollable. The fact of your presence in this facility has already been leaked. When the public learns—"

"——Hold on."Her adjutant's voice cut through the freezing air.

The squad leader's reaction was a fraction of a second too slow.The cell block erupted.

Tasia's subordinates abandoned their own survival matrices entirely, throwing their bodies directly against the spell-glass in a synchronized charge.

The barrier gave. The Balor Witches engaged instantly, the corridor dissolving into close-quarters chaos.

Through the concussive shockwaves of detonating mana, her adjutant turned, uniform already scorching at the sleeves, her forehead streaming crimson.

"Go!" Her voice cracked raw over the roar. "Go, Your Highness! You have to see it with your own eyes!"

Tasia didn't think. She didn't calculate the mana cost. She didn't assess the state of her unhealed circuits.She ripped the fabric of space open and stepped through.

The dimensional tear shredded her skin. The chained jumps ripped new lacerations across her already battered body with every transit.

Blood wept from the corners of her eyes. The world fractured into a blurring cascade of torn geography.

She didn't know the exact coordinates of the hospital. She knew the outskirts of the White City. She knew, bone-certain, that when she landed, she would recognize the crater.

[Location]: The Ruins of the Rear-Echelon Hospital

She crashed out of the final transit and hit the earth violently, rolling across scorched, pulverized stone.

The smell arrived before her vision cleared. Ozone. Scorched biological matter. And beneath it, sweet and wrong and unmistakable: the calcified remnant of shattered shells. Iridescent fragments glittered in the ash like crushed diamonds, half-buried in soot.

Tasia forced herself to her knees behind the husk of a collapsed wall. Her lungs burned. Her hands were slick. She looked through the smoke.

She saw them.

Standing in the center of the devastation, amidst the ruins of the incubators and the silence where the future had been, were Francesca and Angie.

And standing opposite them, alone in the ash, was Ovelia.

Ovelia's face held nothing Tasia recognized. The Conqueror's cold was gone; the sovereign fury was gone.

What remained was hollow, shivering shock: the expression of a child who had just pressed her hand against an open flame and realized, in a single unraveling instant, that harm was a concept that applied to her, that something in this universe had been designed to cut her open.

The shock arrived a fraction of a second before the pain, more devastating than any wound that could follow, hovering exactly one millimeter before the onset of tears.

Through the suffocating silence, Francesca laughed.

Her laugh carried no sneer in it, only the breathless, unhinged triumph of something that had no name in the vocabulary of normal war.

"This is it!" Francesca screamed, her arm outstretched, trembling, pointing at Ovelia. "This! This is the exact expression I wanted to see! You can feel your heart break too, Ovelia! You finally feel it!"

Beside her, Angie looked at Ovelia's face.And smiled.

A quiet thing: the soft, certain smile of a mother watching a toddler trip and scrape their knee, waiting patiently for the child to look up and seek comfort.

Framed in the ashes of the unborn, it was the most monstrous thing Tasia had ever seen.

Behind the collapsed wall, Tasia began to tremble.Her soul was gripped at both ends and pulled cleanly in two.

One half of her wanted to run forward and pull Ovelia into her arms, to cover those wide, cracked-open eyes so they never had to look at this. It wanted to rewrite the last hour so the damage had never occurred, so Ovelia could remain unreachable and ignorant of the fact that in this universe, there was a form of love infinitely more catastrophic than hatred.

In that blind, desperate impulse, stripping away the war, the loss, the ash, the years, Tasia admitted the truth she had been refusing for the entirety of her life: she was irredeemably incurable, still loved her.

But the other half of her soul, the half buried in the marrow of the Milan'thirskaya bloodline, looked at Ovelia's expression and understood.

It felt a dark, unwilling, soaring alignment. Because looking at the unprecedented humanity fracturing through the Conqueror's composure, forcing her to wear an expression she had never worn—

Yes. It came from somewhere Tasia had never allowed herself to look before. That expression, on her face. It was worth everything.

Down in the ruins, Ovelia's eyes moved.For the first time since the war began, her gaze had left its absent horizon, come to rest on the scorched earth. She looked at the ruins. At the broken incubators. At the absence.

She heard Francesca's laughter. She saw Angie's smile.

Ovelia wrenched her gaze away from them. The child-like shock on her face hardened, collapsed into something dense and alien and wholly unnamable.

Her eyes swept the perimeter of the ruins.And found Tasia.

The breath stopped.

Not past her. Not through her at some predetermined horizon of spacetime. The undivided weight of Ovelia's gaze, landing on her. At her. For the first time in Tasia's existence.

Everything she had waited for her entire life had finally arrived.And it had arrived at the worst possible moment.
