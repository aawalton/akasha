Timon

“So, it’s really you, eh? What the hell were you thinking coming here, Korl?” Timon asked the half-formed Mimic that was currently collapsed in on themselves in the corner of the cell.

He didn’t expect an answer. No, that probably wasn’t coming for a while. Whatever had tried to kill him had come surprisingly close. Even Korl was going to take some time to regenerate from that one. Considering how much of his brain was goo, he was especially lucky he was a mimic.

“What did you learn that was so dangerous you ran to us? And why would you possibly think we’d be of any help?” Timon was mostly just thinking out loud at this point. That and he wanted the jester to hear him.

His current working theory was tied to the jesters. The surprise everyone had seemed to show that day when they were attacked in the Arena, plus the fact that the other factions on the floor had all been taken down, had convinced him long before the other discoveries that Earth wasn’t some special target for them. They were just here to collect what they had already started.

For that to be true, though, it meant the jesters were entrenched in the Spiral, something that could only happen if one of the bigger factions had brought them back in. If, and Timon knew it was still a pretty big if at this point, Korl had gone in search of why the jesters had interrupted his own plans in the Arena, it was possible he had learned something that led to this. And it would explain why he’d run here, the only faction he knew that had completed that floor mostly unscathed, and possibly dealt with the soul knots.

The biggest problem with Timon’s theories wasn’t that they didn’t fit the facts at hand. No, it was that he didn’t want it to be right. Because if this were true, that was a problem so far above any of their pay grade, he didn’t have the slightest idea what to do about it.

Humans are such an odd species. In most cases, blending in as a human is rather simple, but that only lasts until an attempt needs to be made to think like one of them in a dire situation. For whatever reason, Humans, almost as a whole, are unwilling to believe in their own mortality. And while there are individuals within other species that do the same, it is near constant with Humans.

This refusal to do so can manifest in both amazing deeds and horrendous evils, but somehow they do seem to fight back against it. I have witnessed Humans survive things that would kill virtually anyone else of comparable abilities. It is only after I have spent centuries living among them that I am finally starting to be able to think like that.
