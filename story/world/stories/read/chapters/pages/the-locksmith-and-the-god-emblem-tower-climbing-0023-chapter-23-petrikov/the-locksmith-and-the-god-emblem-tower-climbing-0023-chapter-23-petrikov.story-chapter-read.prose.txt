"How could you not see he was a human?" Goldman patted Daniel as he barfed on the outside of the hotel. "We all thought you were joking!"

"Why would I..." And the viking barfed once again. "...lie about that?"

It turned out, to Harry's own amusement, that Daniel had a weak stomach for things like that. The brute. The barbaric warrior. The guy who was now... barfing over the thought of eating another human being? That was a twist. In Daniel's case, a literal twist in the guts.

"Didn't you have to eat a living deer or any primal shit like that?" Harry asked with a grin. "I thought that vikings were ruthless. Didn't know you were so fragile..."

"You fucking..." Daniel gave into his stomach once again. "You know what... you're still scared of me. You just don't... I don't have the motivation to keep going."

"Oh, I am definitely scared of you right now." Harry wasn't joking, but adding the sarcasm could hopefully sway the viking.

"What's your name again?" Manes asked as he poked the mustache man. "And why are you so small? Are you really a baby troll?"

"My name is Petrikov." He said with a strong accent, much like Daniel. "And it is—stop doing that."

And Manes took a step back as he raised his hands nonchalantly.

Goldman came near Petrikov and bent down to stay in the same height as him. As if the pirate were saluting a king, he was in kneeling.

"We thank you for your hospitality, Petrikov." Goldman gave a bright smile. "We won't take much of your time."

"No!" He stopped the gentle giant. "Please; stay. I am not the owner, and this isn't exactly the best place to stay. You can accompany me inside the mountain before the rain begins. You are all real... right?"

Goldman looked around, a slight confusion tainting his smile. "As real as one can be. Why do you ask—"

Petrikov then hugged Goldman, who was a bit embarrassed. The Locksmith stared at Madeline as she also looked confused about how to react.

Amidst the cold wind that clashed with the wood, it was possible to hear the crying of the man. His tears as cold as the snow, he hugged Goldman, who in turn, hugged him back.

We all deal with loneliness in different ways, don't we? Harry thought as the man continued to sob.

Harry judged himself a people's person. At least in a way or two. That belief was soon consumed by his instinctive behavior and rapidly digested by the part of his brain that determined threats. All of that to say that; Petrikov was harmless. Physically harmless. Harry's danger sense didn't trigger philosophical truths nor ontological arguments about the existence of God. Guess what Petrikov was in his life before the tower?

"Philosophy is for lesser men." Daniel grunted from a chair. "Like the Locksmith."

"Why do you keep using me for examples in everything?" Harry asked. "There are, like, seven people here."

"Well, you're the easy target."

"I thought that vikings were known by their tenacity and desire for greater threats."

"I..." But Daniel stopped mid sentence as he considered the Locksmith's portrayal of his nature. "You are... not wrong."

You can't even say I'm right... The young thief chuckled to himself.

"When you have a lot of time to yourself, your mind opens." Petrikov continued. "And that's not even adding the fact that we're in a Tower that breaks all rules of time and space. Maybe even man's natural law."

Vela could be seen nodding from across the room.

"For how long have you been here?" Goldman asked, curious. "It seems you know a lot about the mountain and the hotel. In fact, could you tell why is this inn near a mountain?"

"I've been here for..." Petrikov looked to the ground, his expression difficult to determine. "A few years, at least. Time gets lost, you know? And after the last trip, I couldn't muster the courage to get out of here. The way I see it, this hotel is not part of the mountain, originally. It was probably plucked away from some place on earth or completely imagined by the Tower. Either way, it holds no significant truths or use outside of shelter."

"Last trip?" Brun chimed in. "Do you mean to the top of the mountain? Is that where the hatch to the next floor is?"

"Precisely." Petrikov said. "But I must inform you that the climb requires... resilience."

"You said something about a rain." Madeline added. "Is that something we need to worry?"

"Oh, right..." He turned to see the mountain outside the hotel from a shattered window. "It isn't something we need to worry as of now, but in a few hours, near the end of the loop, the catastrophe hits. In my experience, it is as if hell itself rains down in the mountain, a bastion of nature."

Do you always add this flourished prose in your talks? Harry thought to himself, trying to discern what kind of man Petrikov was in his past life. He knew he had been a professor on a small village called Drumond. But why was he here? Wasn't the Tower only supposed to house the greatest of dangers?

"You don't strike me as Tower material." Harry said, giving into his curiosity. "How'd you end up here?"

The whole atmosphere of the room seem to change like water to wine. Even though he seemed friendly, one couldn't trust appearances. The principle was: everyone in the Tower had a reason to be there. Maybe Petrikov was a weapons smuggler of some kind? Hundreds of those were captured all along the years in the continent, and not all were identified publicly. The so called philosopher could've been one of those.

Brun had assured them he was telling the truth with her Emblem, but what if he changed his mind staying there all alone?

"I don't know either." The answer didn't ease the group's afflictions. "One day the military just started making tents and scaring off the wild life. They were near the school, so I could see that they were up to something. I always warned the kids not to mess with them, but we've all been a child once; to them, the danger was worth it. After class, they all sneaked onto the tents to see what the government was up to. When I got there, the place was barren. Before I could do anything, though, I noticed a blue light coming from the center, as I approached it, the portal approached me. I got pulled and ended up... well, here. I assume the kids had the same fate."

"...But why would they do that?" Brun was the first to question him. "Why open a portal to the Logos Tower so close to a village? Did you hear any news about possible fugitives or assassins taking place where you lived?"

"...No." Petrikov answered after a moment. "Not that I knew of. Maybe they were simply experimenting with the Emblems and we got in the way. The only thing I worry is for the kids. They didn't deserve it, you know? Being stuck in this place feels sometimes like hell."

There was something strange about this story. Not that Harry doubted Petrikov's account of the situation, but why would the military do something like that? Capturing innocent people? But for what purpose?

"But they made it." Petrikov added, now with a weary smile. "I'm sure they made it. The kids have always been stronger than me. I'm sure they made it to the mountain and are probably safe in the next floor."

Or maybe they were stopped in the first floor... Harry added inwardly, not wanting to break the professor's hopes. Maybe they were all really alive. Maybe.

"...I'm sure you will get your answer, Mr. Petrikov." Vela said after a moment of silence. "I assume you have intense knowledge about this loop's intricacies, so you can fill us in and we can get you to the next floor. How does that sound?"

Although Vela was speaking for the whole group, Harry had a sense no one would intervene. The only two people who could be irritated by a new companion was Daniel and Brun, but they kept quiet. Well, at least he seems nice. Harry added inwardly.

"...Thank you. Really, I thank you all for this opportunity. I won't let you down." He smiled to himself. "I'm sure they're alive. And I will find them. That's the responsibility of a teacher, isn't it? To care for his class."

After a few more minutes, Petrikov took the group through a strange door as it led to a cavern inside the mountain. As they went, he explained to them what the rain meant.

From what Harry could gather, at precisely 3 hours and 37 minutes into the loop, a rain of stones and gigantic blocks fell from the sky. The professor never stood more than a few minutes outside as to determine where they fell from and what exactly are they made of. It appeared as stone, but maybe there was something more. Either way, he didn't know.

To Petrikov, the rain was a law as immutable as gravity. Since he'd been there for several loops, he instinctively knew when to get shelter. For the group, he told them that the hotel would collapse before anything else. A big rock would clash right at the base of the Millbourne Hotel, making it fall into oblivion. That, in turn, would indicate the coming of the rain.

"And how long do we have before that?" Madeline asked, constantly looking back. "I mean, is a cave really better than a hotel?"

"I would guess that staying alive was better than dying." Petrikov retorted with a grin. "Don't need to worry; the cave is perfect. The Metas are all inoffensive and are perfectly shaped like a pillow. Besides, they don't seem to mind."

"...Metas?" Harry was the first to ask, curious on the term. "What are Metas?"

"...You'll see."

"Is there anything more you want to tell us?" Brun said, eyeing a blank piece of paper. "Before the — you know — end of the world?"

"This actually reminds me of something." The Locksmith added. "Can't you figure it all out with your Emblem?"

"It'll take a while before I can." She replied, looking at the young thief who was just beside her on the cavernous corridor. "It's weird... Although the mountain is huge, it shouldn't take this long. It'll take at least one full loop."

One full loop? Harry added inwardly. Although he kept his inquiries inside his mind. Since almost losing a leg and confidently feeling the loss of his hand, Harry wanted the loop to restart him as soon as possible.

In the end of the corridor, a single ray of light dashed through all the corners of the large area. It brightened the place as if it was truly a royal palace.

"Welcome to my man cave!" He exclaimed with his arms wide open.

Harry then noticed strange creatures clinging to the wall and a few on the ground, drinking from a puddle of water.

The Metas were weird.
