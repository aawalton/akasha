Arthur returned to the Narak Mountains under the hush of midnight. The wind whispered through the cliffs like an old rumor, softer now. As if the stone itself remembered him. He moved with quiet certainty toward the familiar crevice. The same narrow crack he’d once dragged himself through, half-dead, half-naked, and fully traumatized.

No Chimera chased him this time.

Now? He was the thing monsters warned each other about before bedtime.

He slipped through the crevice in the stone and moved cautiously through the winding passage. He didn’t go deep, there’d never been danger inside to begin with. The interior of the mountain had always been quiet, untouched by monsters or meddling participants.

He just needed somewhere out of sight. So he picked a shallow cavern near the entrance, just big enough to stretch out and meditate without having to glance over his shoulder every five minutes.

He sat cross-legged on the stone floor and exhaled slowly. His fingers brushed the ring at his side.

Inside: close to a hundred monster cores lay in his storage, each a warm brown lump radiating faint mana. Together, they throbbed with the quiet confidence of magic waiting to be used.

Arthur picked one up. It pulsed in his hand with faint, alien life.

"Time to feed the spark," he muttered.

He swallowed the core and sat still, pressing a hand over his chest - right where his Mana Spark pulsed behind his ribs. The process wasn’t passive; he had to pull the mana out of the monster in his stomach, thread by thread, and guide it into slow, deliberate orbits around the spark. Each rotation fed his pool, a careful negotiation of power. It reminded him of extracting intel from a hostile informant - methodical, tense, and always a wrong question away from things going sideways.

He started with Grubtusk cores. Low-grade, consistent… and now practically inert. His spark responded with all the enthusiasm of a bureaucrat stamping redundant paperwork.

Clearly, diminishing returns were real. Arthur selected a new core, one from a tougher kill.

This time, the spark flared. Not much, but enough to register. He kept going.

One by one, he cycled the mana from the cores in his stomach and into his spark. The effect wasn’t immediate, but it was cumulative.

Each core added something. Not a surge, not a blaze. Just quiet growth. His spark pulsed gently behind his ribs, swelling a little with each successful cycle. It felt denser now, more solid. Like a flame fed by steady drops of oil, not flashy but undeniably stronger.

Not dramatic. But unmistakable.

He could feel it, the spark was growing. Bit by bit, it was becoming something more.

And just to confirm it wasn’t all in his head, the System chimed in with a slow trickle of notifications. Each one ticking his Intelligence a little higher.

Thirty minutes passed. Then an hour.

By the time he was done, his spark was humming like a living engine. Most of the cores were gone. Well, almost. He’d saved a few for emergencies. Quick mana refills could mean life or death in a fight.

INTELLIGENCE: 40

MANA POOL: 400

Not bad for a few minutes work.

He stretched out and cracked his neck, exhaling a slow breath.

“That stench,” Drogan groaned. “Boy, you’ve officially gone from monster repellent to people repellent. You can forget having a social life. You're practically basting yourself in monster-core fumes. You know that stuff reeks like rotten eggs, right?"

Arthur rolled his eyes. “Not like I’m heading to a royal banquet.”

Drogan wasn’t wrong, though. After days spent marinating in monster dung and his own dried sweat, Arthur reeked like something that had died, come back, and immediately regretted it. Still, priorities.

He leaned back and stared at the stone ceiling. Now came the part he relished - thinking. Strategizing. Plotting.

So what did he want?

Easy. Revenge. On Rajak. On the Chimera. On the entire rotten setup that turned people into slave meat and called it order.

But revenge could wait.

Arthur wasn’t stupid or suicidal. He’d seen what Rajak could do, and that Chimera? That wasn’t a fight, it was a walking obituary. He needed more training, more tricks, and ideally a weapon that came with its own dramatic theme music.

In the meantime, he had the Great Hunt.

He pulled up the leaderboard:

ARTHUR (Solo, No Affiliation) – 100 Kills

ROWLER (+4, House Drelmor) – 6 Kills

YSARA (+4, House Valen) – 4 Kills

...

The gap was absurd. Comical, really. It made him a target, sure. But it also gave him breathing room.

Drogan had said the System rewarded the winner. Real rewards. Not stat crumbs or shiny trinkets, but actual power. The kind of power you couldn’t just loot from a corpse or haggle out of a blacksmith.

Arthur wanted that. Needed it. Not for revenge. Well, not just for revenge but because power meant freedom. Options. Control.

Which brought him to the third and final item on the list.

He couldn’t live in the woods forever.

The forest had served its purpose - training ground, monster pantry, makeshift therapy. But he was done smelling like cryptid roadkill.

“Can’t exactly keep squatting in the trees,” he muttered. “I’m one growl away from being mistaken for a yeti.”

That meant one thing: Civilization.

If he wanted to rejoin it, he’d need a plan.

The list kept growing.

He sat quietly, organizing his thoughts, until the list felt actionable. Then, finally, he turned to Drogan.

“Master Drogan, font of wisdom and terror. Enlighten your humble pupil. What do you know of this world? Its kingdoms, politics, culture... anything that might keep me from dying stupid?”

There was a long pause.

Then Drogan snorted. “Really, boy? You’re sitting on a mountain of monster corpses, wearing stolen pants, and your big question is ‘what’s the local political climate’?”

Arthur frowned. “I need to know who runs this place. The factions, the cities, the lay of the land.?”

“Oh, I’m sorry,” Drogan said mockingly. “Did you want a tour pamphlet? Maybe a cozy book series called ‘Empires and Etiquette’?” He made an exaggerated sigh. “Look, I was summoned by the system when your spark ignited. No idea what planet this is. Or galaxy. Or which flavor of pointy-crowned buffoon is in charge.”

Arthur blinked. “You’re serious.”

“As a mana overdose,” Drogan said. “I don’t do maps and monarchs. That’s for the squishy types who care about titles and treaties. You? You need power. Stack enough of that, and suddenly nobody cares if you know which duchy exports wheat. They just bow.”

Arthur stood, brushing himself off. “Fine. No lore dump. I’ll ask someone who’s actually read a book.”

“Books don’t bleed, boy. People do. You want answers? Beat them out of someone with better paperwork.”

Arthur rolled his shoulders and sighed. “So I’ll just have to do it the old-fashioned way. You know - espionage, subterfuge, and casual theft.”

Arthur eyed the crevice’s mouth. Somewhere out there, other participants were wandering - dozens, maybe hundreds. Brimming with intel. And probably with egos even bigger than their kill counts.

He’d track them down. Smile. Ask questions. Maybe even skip the threats, if they were useful.

But first?

“I need a bath,” Arthur muttered. “I smell like a monster’s bad decision.”

He stepped into the cool night, nose wrinkling. “Water. Soap. Dignity. In that order.”

Drogan snorted. “Ambitious.”
