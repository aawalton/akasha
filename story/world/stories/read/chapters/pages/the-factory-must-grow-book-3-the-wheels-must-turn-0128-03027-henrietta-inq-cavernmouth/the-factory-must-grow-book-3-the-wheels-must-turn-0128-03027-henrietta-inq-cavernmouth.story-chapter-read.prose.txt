"Honestly, I'm impressed with what you've done with the place," Henrietta approved, and Alyssa pretended to not preen under the praise.

Henrietta hadn't given Alyssa that much of a headstart, ultimately. Just a couple of days, during which time she'd mostly anticipated Alyssa to make effectively a campsite, with a dedicated fire pit, some of the larger pieces of debris cleared out, and maybe the start of a workshop to build out the rest of Cavernmouth's forward base. But instead, she'd arrived to a genuine base, with a protected room complete with sleeping bunks, made out of milled lumber and all quite solid. The cavern's actual mouth had been widened substantially through blunt force trauma such that Henrietta could walk through it with arms fully extended and not even brush the sides.

On the outside, the scrub had also been fully burned away, with several of the nearby trees felled and delimbed, ready for further processing. In a couple cases, they'd even been chopped into something approximating a stool for sitting on. That they oozed sap made the prospect of actually doing so less appealing, but the effort was impressive. Other stumps had been more clearly burned out, to varying degrees of success.

Inside, a number of glowing 'torches' were wedged or placed through the caverns, some Alyssa's normal green and the others almost golden, leading deeper into the earth, each serving as a pathway to either their future Manufactorium or to The Geode respectively. "I didn't know you could choose what color your ⟨Illuminate⟩ make," she noted. "I thought it was fixed."

"Oh, I can't control it," Alyssa shrugged. "It usually is a green that I guess is my current 'default color,' but I noticed that one of the trees had a way stronger effect on coloration than usual, so I figured why not?"

"Well, I approve of the idea," Henrietta nodded. While far from being any kind of absolute law, magic that had a color usually varied person to person, but it could change wildly during Class swaps as their elemental affinities were altered. Alyssa's was green, Oliver's blue, Clark's was a whitish-yellow, she didn't know what Jacob's was presently, and her own was usually the orange of glowing metal but as a [Master Inkscribe], was an inky black that was similar to but not quite what she conjured with her [Refined Calligraphy].

"How long will they last?" she asked.

"Maybe a few days?" Alyssa speculated. "It depends a lot on size, and I usually can't reuse the same target multiple times."

"I suppose that makes sense, given it started as a skill to burn wood," Henrietta dusted off her hands. "And we'll hopefully have something more permanent up pretty soon."

"That was my thinking," the Ranger speculated, then hesitated.

"Yes, Ride?" Henrietta prompted.

"It's... I'm trying to figure out the right way to phrase this. I think I've figured out a piece of local magic, in particular some aspect of my power."

Henrietta leaned against one of the wooden walls of their base. "Oh?"

"We suspect that the landscape is intrinsically magical, right? Well, I think it might be feeding me mana."

"Oh, what you've been picking up from the environment?" Henrietta tapped her foot. "That would make sense. You have been getting a lot of mana from that, more than I'd expect someone to get from even the most high magic of areas, now that I'm actually thinking about it."

"How did it work for you?" she asked. "What was it like to get new magic downloaded into your soul, practically speaking? I know we've chatted about it a bit over fires, but that's mostly been about your power, less about the... they're called artscapes, right? Less about how those were developed and practiced."

"I suppose that's fair. Do you need a break?" Alyssa shook her head. "Okay. We can do a bit of light work along the way. You mentioned you wanted my help with some remodeling, I can multitask with that."

"So, when our Jump first landed, we didn't really feel anything different," Henrietta reminisced while she used an ink-flail to cut through a stalagmite. "I don't know how long it would have taken us to figure out how things worked completely on our own, because we did have help, but it felt a lot like... well, it felt like building out an artistic skill. The name of 'artscape' didn't come from nowhere. The core magic of it is basically a seed of creativity, a place where imagination meets conviction and capability, and processed experiences into potential."

An inconvenient stalactite went next, and Alyssa cleared out a fairly large plume of dust with a massive gust of wind. "Generally speaking, the magics were far less tangible than mine. Mark's artscape manifested mostly in his dreams, where he could imagine nearly any situation he had experienced and experiment how things could have gone quite accurately."

"That seems crazy useful," Alyssa pointed out the next chunk of stone that needed to be flattened. "Dangerous experiments, exploring hazardous locations, various forms of interrogation... what was his stance on doing less moral things to imagined people? Are we talking torture, or would be more likely to stick with just trying out different forms of address to learn about a person's personality."

"Not sure," Henrietta admitted. "I don't think I want to ask, though. But you're half right, because he managed to pick up a daydreaming Skill, so he could use his recall at any time, though it wasn't instantaneous."

Thinking about her old leader made Henrietta all the more certain she'd been right to stop comparing herself to him. He could literally try out different approaches and choose the best one every time, limited only by how much time he had to think. "That was a lot closer to what a normal artscape was like, compared to mine, but still quite strong, especially when System-augmented."

"And the incredible diversity of humanity.... mortals, I suppose, wins yet again," Alyssa indicated a small drop, "Could you get that filled in with stone?"

Henrietta obliged, repurposing one of her removed stone spikes as infill. "The only thing that one really required for practice was that Mark needed to look at things in a particular way, if he wanted them to be accurate in his dream. Cara's artscape was trickier in that regard, because most of her power was her perception. She needed to be taught specifically how to look at the 'intent' of an object, but once she did that her ability to see the way something or someone should be when they were working was mostly just automatic. Or if it did take effort, she found it quite easy."

"I thought she had illusions?" Alyssa helped Henrietta up a slippery bit of rock. "Was that different?"

"No, that was connected," Henrietta took the hand. "Specifically, she would show the world her internal vision, and those manifested as illusions."

"Was that thanks to the System?"

"It's not really possible to draw a clear delineation between System-assisted and non-System assisted, but none of the people we met with similar perceptive artscapes were able to externally manifest them in any way. But that could have also been Jump enhancements. Elizabeth's teleportation was very much System-made, though. Her baseline power was a mental map that gave her a perfect sense of direction and a very vague ability to track when things changed in places she'd been."

"How do these powers keep sounding so much more and yet also so much less than your inklings?" Alyssa asked. "Actually, I suppose that depends on what you mean by 'ability to track.' If it's just noticing when things are different, that's lame. But if you mean she had a constant divination running anywhere she'd ever been, that seems absolutely stellar."

"Closer to the latter, that's how she was able to piggyback a short-hop teleportation into something that could span full planes."

"And I'm getting increased Generation," Alyssa mock sighed and shook her head.

"For now," Henrietta smiled as she pointed it out. "There's lots of ways that it could grow and expand, especially as we figure out how things work more generally. I'm sure you'll turn it into something quite impressive by the time we're done here."

"Obviously," Alyssa rolled her shoulder haughtily. "I'm the best at magic. I've got my skill combos, am learning proper casting, and I've even been learning new Warrior's Stances from a literal Tyrant-killing veteran. Like seriously, what the hells kind of special ops was he in."

"I've yet to figure that out myself," Henrietta answered as she started to create a proper tunnel to bypass a lengthy vertical drop. "Halvor's artscape, because I assume that question is coming soon, was about reliving experiences."

Alyssa gave her a look. "I assume it's different from how Mark Lucid's powers worked, given how you said that."

"Well, it was mainly about making other people relive his experiences."

"I'm thinking emotional attacks, but..."

"Emotional attacks to begin with, but he got to the point where he could do much more vivid relivings, like what a certain charging vampire's full-power attack was like - without the assurance that they'd be able to block it like he did."

Alyssa flinched. "I wish these things were more public knowledge. Not that I really need more reasons to go on an Expedition, but you know... magic is cool."

"It's just a privacy consideration," Henrietta pointed out. "Same as with Skills. Talk about what you can do, not how you do it. For me, there's not much point in keeping that quiet about my ability to steal away Shadows from creatures and objects, then re-project them under my control. But phrasing it as an ability to create animated ink constructs is good enough, and it's hard to take back information that's released."

"I guess," Alyssa sighed.

"Regardless, my point with all this is that even with the exact same core magic system, the manifestations it presents can vary wildly," Henrietta pushed into the rock wall with her ink-flail, leaning as hard as she could on ⟨Etch⟩ to make the rock pliable to her 'writing implement.' That particular subskill worked best with surface changes, which this very much wasn't. But that wasn't about to stop her. "And the fact you've identified any amount of direct magical interaction, especially without a skill moderating the effect, is incredibly promising."

The end of the flail fanned out from the central hole she had bored, severing stone from stone. Then she traced the outer edge of the carved-out area, extracting a massive cylinder of stone and tossing it to the side. It fell down the drop, crashing and crumbling to pieces at the bottom.

"I feel a little bad," Alyssa commented, and Henrietta glanced over. "About this," she clarified. "Carving into these natural and beautiful caves. It feels like a nature preserve, but we're turning it into a Factory."

Henrietta nodded. "Needs must, ultimately. I don't particularly enjoy destroying unique things, but this is also a full world, there's going to be plenty of untouched caves perfect for all the ecologists to study to their heart's content. But only if we get home."

"I suppose I don't disagree," the Ranger sighed, then winced as Henrietta pulled out another chunk of stone. Her next burrowing tendril broke through to open air, and she quickly reduced the remaining blockage to rubble.

"I don't suppose you have a distinct idea as to how we're going to transport things through here, do you?" Alyssa asked as they made their way through the last few tunnels en route to their Manufactorium.

"Nothing particularly concrete," Henrietta answered. "But part of that will depend on whether we're able to get a secondary Broadcast Network running here, and how extensively if so. If spider-bots can run through here reliably, that seems to be the obvious choice. Otherwise, we could do a new generation of transport carts. Not sure how the gravity down here might affect things, either."

Without any of the external references frames which being underground robbed from them, it was difficult to tell, but it definitely felt to Henrietta like they were walking on the wall relative to where they had been? Gravity in the Material Realms was different from the 'normal' cosmology she had grown up with, because unlike in the Realms of Void with their infinite abysses of nothingness, where anything whatsoever was an aberration from the normal affairs of things, gravity was a much more local phenomenon. That was why, despite being so impossible back home, things like floating islands or caverns without gravity in them could be found in the worlds that Forerunner Expeditions landed in.

It didn't make them any less interesting, though.

As they finally got to their destination, Henrietta beheld a cavern maybe a few hundred meters across, a few dozen tall, and looked to extend past a turn that carried it further into the bowels of the earth. The walls shone and glittered in the golden light like the inside of a bubble, with a sheet of cloudy, muddy water coating every surface. Some dust that had survived Alyssa's [Flamewind] clearing still drifted around, but it mostly it was just a big, empty cavern.

Henrietta took a couple sniffs. Plenty of Dark and Earth, some Sand, Water, and maybe hints of Force. No Air though, which could be a problem.

"We're going to need to get some ventilation down here, I think," Henrietta mused. Zero gravity environments carried with them a number of unusual constraints, and the lack of circulating air could be one of the bigger ones. Given they would inevitably need to spend extended periods of time fixing broken machines or even just building them, fresh air would be a must.

"What about digging a hole from here to the surface?" Alyssa asked.

"Do you know which way the surface is?" Henrietta replied.

"I mean, no, but I assume that's the kind of thing a divination could figure out."

"Good point. That could also help with our transportation problem too," Henrietta agreed. "Something to look into, once we get Smith out here. Speaking of, we do definitely need to bring back a substantial crystal from The Geode. Did Smith ever teach you what to look for in a crystal for Network Cache compatibility?" Alyssa nodded. "Excellent. He wanted a crystal he could be confident could serve as a stand-in for him while he's away getting this place set up."

"Is he going to ⟨Integrate⟩ it?"

"I'd hope not," Henrietta frowned. "Especially after what happened with his eye, I definitely don't want him to risk physically merging with anything larger than he is. I need him mobile, and the spelloom was playing with fire enough for my taste. And that I only okayed because it was still smaller than him, so even if he did wind up going full android, a robotic body would still be theoretically possible. I do definitely need to check to make sure he has an idea that doesn't involve a direct soul-bond to a giant rock. But I think he's just making a specialized Network Cache."

"That feels boring... but I guess boring has its uses."

Henrietta privately agreed. Excitement required a degree of the unknown, whether it be discovery or risk, but that was something they wanted to reduce as much as possible. Without any Chaining in place, Expeditions were naturally higher-risk than being in the Empire proper, but there was no reason to add to that risk unduly.

Henrietta took another deep breath. "Alright. I want this place as prepared as possible for Oliver, keep him away from the Tower as little time as possible."

While Henrietta did have experience with Attunement, even Consecration - the unfortunately technical term for Attuning a building or location, which predated The Binding Wars and none of the replacements had properly stuck - she didn't have much experience with the latter, and Oliver was better at it than she was. That wouldn't have stopped her, but they did need him to do the actual enchanting work that would make this a proper part of the Factory, and given he'd already need to be here, he would benefit from doing the foundation work as well. Particularly with his Pillar of Industry, the more involved he could get, the better.

"I wish we had pine resin," she mused. "It could definitely use pine resin." She sniffed again. "Needs some dried flowers, preferably purple in color, burned in a Technological flame alongside... Oh man. Maybe some moss? Trying to think about what would help with Mud presence."

"Marshy reeds?" Alyssa suggested.

Henrietta shook her head. "The Attunement definitely needs fire, and that would burn away both the metaphorical and literal roots of the plant. It needs to be something that becomes Mud, or at least has Associations approximating that relationship. Maybe just waterlogged wood? Need some metal dust in there too... I hope iron and copper will suffice, given we don't have silver."

"How can you... tell?"

"For something like this, I have a general idea as to what the place should smell like, magically speaking, if it's going to be a workshop or laboratory. Oh! Knowledge. I could probably do some enchanted and written upon papers for that," Henrietta blinked. "Burning things to diffuse some of the Associations through the room is a classic way to get elements in an environment, but it's important to remember the ways the Fire and Smoke interact with it. As for knowing how those interact, it's just a matter of practice and experience."

"No shortcuts, eh?" Alyssa lightly joked. "Gotta do everything the hard way."

"Shortcuts imply that the journey is the important part, which I suppose isn't fully wrong. But I'm very much in the business of making things easier," Henrietta replied, swiping her hand through the air to whip up a small amount of wind. "And those, I call tools."
