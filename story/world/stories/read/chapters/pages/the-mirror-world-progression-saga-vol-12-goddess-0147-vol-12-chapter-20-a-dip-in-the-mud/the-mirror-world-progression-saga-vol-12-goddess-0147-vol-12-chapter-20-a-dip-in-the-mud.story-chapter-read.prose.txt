Painstakingly, the alliance army made forward progress toward the Capital’s walls. The towers fell one by one, but the fight did not stop as they did, and one by one, even with Samantha out there, it wasn’t a fast process with the sheer number of them out there.

Dax navigated it all — a hundred things to do every minute. She moved her little faux reserve group to a central position and unleashed her wide-scale class buffs, sufficient to twist the fate of the majority of conflicts in their favor. Meanwhile, she made use of Samantha’s cloud buff by the hammered-in discipline of veteran footsoldiers, formations rotating to move the fresher and more protected soldiers to the front. The enemy couldn’t easily compete against the offense that such protection allowed.

The air power was not completely banished. Dax and Zadkiel coordinated to have their flyers do short overhead charges, avoiding the long-range targeting of the enemy as they swept over, in, and out in waves, acting as cavalry with the benefit of a mostly unchallenged medium to charge through. Azure’s aerial support also stuck to fading in and out, blasting the enemy vanguard again and again with punishing AoEs to soften them up.

Similar AoE destruction came from numerous contingents of spellcasters on the flanks, firing into the central ranks ever-depleting, and with enough spellcasting support as they were targeted down by mixed strike teams of Antimages, warriors, catfolk assassins, and spellcasters. No doubt, the enemy thought that occupying their elite champions with the towers would save these groups.

They thought wrong. Dax had power to spare from every bloody corner of the land.

Though with significant casualties to show for it, they’d successfully gored out both flanks, allowing AoE to fire from beneficial angles, and the enemy’s dense formations had nowhere to go in retreat. To their credit, the majority didn’t even try. Admirably, they held the ground in their core and died, slowly but surely.

Occasionally, a surrounded small contingent surrendered, throwing down their weapons — likely because word had gotten around about merciful dealings of similar nature from the alliance, in the weeks of the blitzkrieg leading to the Capital battle. Dax was ready for it and honored it every time, shifting specific police-duty groups into the mix to detain them and move them back out of the chaos, shackled and moved to specific fortifications held behind the lines.

On the other side of the city, the minotaurs had stalled somewhat thanks to the deployment of elite Heaven Knights, specialists as always in defensive stalling. These elites matched the furor of the minotaur champions head-on, and pretty much gave them what they wanted: a worthy fight.

Nonetheless, Dax was almost gleeful about it. She was certain those knights had been desired on their side, deployed to stall tower destruction even more, and otherwise stretch resources.

They’d done so to some degree by sending their undead giants and stronger golems in defense, intercepting and occupying the alliance elite units. They seemed to quickly learn which ones it slowed and which it didn’t. Samantha, Six, and most of the golem knights, in particular, continued with impunity. The rest slowed down. Only so much could be done, of course, as the whole army was seizing the field continuously.

So the stall-job continues.

But the alliance was persevering as a whole, with enough mass advantages stacking together and overcoming the enemy significantly.

As Dax was managing the shift of defensive reinforcements and support to the flanks to mitigate casualties, lodged almost entirely in her head in the gestalt, she just happened to turn her head to see a soldier looking her way, who was covered in some sort of distortion.

The signs processed in her head an instant before another distortion was racing her way. She threw herself off her Pegasus and down on the other side, as something sliced through her armor and wiped out her wound negations. The follow-up might’ve ended her, but she was falling, and the distortion, showing signs of being some network of tiny blades strung together, carried through over her head.

Dax sent a warning into the gestalt, as everyone around her began responding, and an Antimage unleashed some dispel that banished the distortion. She saw a spinning tumble of wire and blades, no doubt not unlike what killed Agatha.

Panedemonium was already ensuing as Dax scrambled up from the ground and put soldiers between her and the rest, having no intentions of crossing swords with anyone. Nonetheless, she drew her blade as she cast her perceptions through mirrors to see what the hell was happening.

Samantha, among others, was in her head. “Dax! Are you okay?! I’m coming!”

Dax ignored her. Not fast enough, you aren’t. Not even you could’ve stopped it.

A pocket of men in simple gray leathers, shedding disguises like smoke wafting off of them, were shredding everyone around them from a combination of cutting swords, thrown knives, and more of those floating living weapons spinning around them like masses of razors. A dozen were dead at their feet already as they’d struck with impunity under some sort of hypnotic spell.

Only Servitors in the vicinity seemed to have perceived them at all enough to defend themselves, perhaps why they had to strike her from where they did. She was surrounded immediately by disguised Servitors-

Oh shit.

She had been surrounded by them.

Just as she thought of it, she felt amidst the pressure of the bodies around her, someone more or less grabbing her from behind and arresting her movement. In her ear was whispered, “Regards from a hunter,” as a dagger pierced her flesh-

“None shall take us!” came a woman’s loud cry, as a pulse of energy flashed through the ranks and enveloped Dax. The dagger point stopped inside her back, never reaching her heart where it had been bound. The shallow wound was still pulsing with a dull pain, but that was all.

Gabriel. The Heaven Wall Shield just saved my ass.

As the man grabbing her growled and tensed, Dax threw herself backward with a feral scream. They crashed to the ground. It would’ve helped the blade drive into her if she could be harmed, but because she could not be, it instead was forced sideways and out of her.

They rolled and struggled briefly before soldiers around them finally grabbed the man’s arms. Dax scrambled up to see the one below her, dressed like one of theirs, struggling against the arms holding him, his face sneering up at her with hatred.

“Regards from a dame, fucker,” Dax said as she plunged her blade into his heart. He convulsed once, spewed blood out of his mouth, and was finally still.

Her shallow wound still burned terribly, and it was slowly spreading.

Well, that's impressive — I'm supposed to be immune to poison. It's still gonna take a while with the resistances left over. It's probably designed to work on the heart, too, and it didn't make it there. Yet.

Just as she was going to call for some additional aid, a very large angel scooped Dax up into powerful arms, and wings enclosed around her. Resemblants swarmed and healed her, an elven specialist laid hands and cleansed her of poison entirely, and others layered more wound negations on her. As Dax cast her perceptions back into mirrors around her, she saw a thick wall of Angels around her, and the soldiers all pushed back and away.

Well, so much for that issue! Emergency response is on-point.

Meanwhile, screams resounded as magic came down like a hell storm on the little group of gray-clad men — witchhunter assassins, no doubt. Quickly, there were no gray-clad men anymore. Dax saw a few blurs amidst the annihilation that indicated Samantha had arrived and dealt with them rather swiftly.

Dax was panting and grimacing behind the wall of Angels. She looked up at Gabriel and muttered, “You were supposed to be with Canmore.”

Gabriel smiled sheepishly at her. “He insisted you were in profound danger. And, um, well…” She trailed off and cleared her throat.

Dax snorted. “That bastard, how dare he be right about something like this.”

The spooky, distant howls of hounds resounded from multiple directions. It was enough to make her skin crawl.

Samantha suddenly pushed through the angel feathers into her presence, blood and dirt splattered, eyes closed and bleeding slightly, holding something still jerking around in her hands. She’d literally been whipping the asses of elite enemies, toppling towers, and killing assassins, all with her bloody eyes closed.

And even so, she looks nothing more than rosy-cheeked, like a damn kid that got into the mud.

Dax looked down at Samantha’s hands, seeing a jerking tangle of little blades and wires. “Caught a frog, did ya?”

Samantha’s brows knitted together. She opened her mouth, but a feather went in it, so she first had to spit it out. Finally, she said, “I have no idea why you’d say that, but obviously not. Should we keep it?”

“Fuck no!” Dax waved her hand in annoyance. “Get rid of it.”

The goddess shrugged and did some ability or another, which caused the object in her hands to simply disintegrate into dust. She dusted off her hands and asked, “Are you going to be okay, Dax?”

“Of course, damn it! I’m fine. A nice try on their part, but they failed.” Dax rolled her neck and cast her perceptions back to the battle. New pockets of foes had shown up to reinforce the enemy ranks — gray- or black-clad witchhunters surrounded by strange, spectral hounds. “Get your little booty back out there already.”

Samantha put her hands on her hips. “As soon as you get your big booty to the backlines. You cannot be risked out here now that the enemy has shown they know your position. We need to squirrel you away. To hell with the passive buffs. They aren’t worth risking you as our mastermind.”
