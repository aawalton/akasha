Control was an illusion.

Aodhán had learned that a long time ago, in the aftermath of his first origin breach back on the Raventhorn.

But understanding a truth and experiencing it were two different things entirely. And in that moment, as the Paradox Still came to life beneath his palms and the strip of uneven essence began melting once again—far beyond his ability to guide or control the unraveling chaos—Aodhán couldn't have felt less in control of anything.

"Please," he whispered to the vat, his gaze intent on the melting strip. "Don't explode. I almost lost an eye yesterday. I'm not ready to lose another body part."

His heart thudded loudly in his chest, sweat slicked his palms, and his hands trembled, but Aodhán didn't shift his gaze away, the translucent fork tight in his grip as he prepared to pick out the impurities as soon as they revealed themselves.

That happened an instant later, and despite his fear, Aodhán didn't hesitate. His hands moved in a blur as he plucked out the impurities as fast as he could, his gaze searching the bubbling chaos for any solid clumps of foreign essence.

All while this happened, his lips moved in silent prayers, begging any god who would hear to help him scale through. Fortunately for him, luck was on his side, and a few minutes later, a perfect strip of iridescent chaos formed.

Aodhán shuddered as a breath of relief escaped his lips, a grin forming as he pulled the strip out of the Still and placed it gently on the table to cool further. He glanced up at the Mythics in triumph and found them still completely focused, whispering among themselves as they handed one tool after another to Elder Gumi in perfect coordination.

Shaking off the urge to go see what they were doing, Aodhán picked a smaller handful of chaos treasures from the tray and dumped them in the Still. The Still came to life a moment later, and this time there were no errors.

With each activation, he got better at melting the treasures—so much better that he soon realized that though there was very little he could control, it wasn't completely uncontrollable. The task was by no means interesting, but as Aodhán focused on getting a better strip with each activation, his attention shifted from the monotony of the work to improvement. Curiously, he watched the process of chaos unraveling, noting how differently each treasure unraveled despite being of the same affinity.

He ultimately failed to decipher any notable patterns in the unraveling process, and half an hour later, when the last perfect strip of chaos essence formed, he glanced excitedly at the gathered Mythics and called out, "I'm done, Elder Kobi."

The elders turned as one as Elder Kobi frowned. "So fast?"

Elder Gumi raised an eyebrow. "Kobi, check what he has done."

Elder Kobi came over, frown still in place as he examined the strips—then stopped. The frown vanished, replaced by genuine surprise. "They are perfect." He picked one up, turning it in the light. "Smaller than normal, but perfect. No impurities, clean solidification..." He glanced at Aodhán. "How many did you have to re-melt?"

"Just the first one," Aodhán admitted. "I didn't catch all the impurities in time."

"One failure out of—" Kobi counted quickly. "Sixteen attempts? That's..." He turned to Elder Gumi. "That's better than my first session."

Elder Gumi blinked, then smiled. "Impressive. I don't think any of my apprentices have done that without blowing something up."

"We didn't blow anything up," Elder Sedgwick responded defensively, glancing at Aodhán. "We are still alive, after all."

"Humph," Elder Gumi snorted. "That's because I'm merciful. Elder Kobi and Summer blew that vat a total of three times before finally getting the process right."

Elder Kobi grimaced in remembrance, and Elder Summer looked away in embarrassment, causing Aodhán to smile.

"I guess I had a very good teacher."

Elder Kobi glanced at him, then nodded. "Yes, you did."

"Hmm," Elder Gumi's smile widened. "Come join us, then. We could use some of that luck over here."

Aodhán's smile widened, and without hesitation, he rushed over. His gaze landed on the complex script of elder runes Elder Gumi had fit into the pendant-shaped strip of chaos, and he felt a lance of pain stab through his mind.

The level of detailing and precision was outrageous, far more than anything he'd ever seen Geneva create. But beyond the obvious skill portrayed in the script was a profoundness that threatened to draw his mind into the chaos, just like the origin plane usually did.

"Don't stare at it too long," Elder Gumi smiled without looking at him. "Anything that has to do with chaos is dangerous, especially for those at your level."

Aodhán nodded and turned his gaze away, but no matter how hard he tried, his gaze kept straying back to the script in admiration. He realized now why the other Mythics had kept so still. Watching Elder Gumi work was like watching reality weave itself into existence, and even with his little understanding, Aodhán felt insight and ideas tease his mind with each curve and coil.

But with these insights came questions—wild, unimaginable questions prompted by a desire to understand better, to know more. They pressed against his chest like a living thing, seemingly alive like the scripts themselves, but Aodhán didn't ask them. Instead, he took out his notepad and began jotting them down, his gaze never leaving the intricate script.

An hour passed in relative silence as Elder Gumi worked—the only sound, the swish, swish of her inscriber against the strip of solid chaos.

And then, before Aodhán knew it, she was done.

"There," she whispered raggedly, as if drawing the script alone had taken a toll on her. Aodhán didn't doubt it.

"The hardest part is over. Now we just need to couple it." She glanced at Elder Sedgwick. "Give me the orichalcum structure."

Elder Sedgwick handed the structure to her—an open pendant covered in storm and space runes. Carefully, Elder Gumi placed the chaos base into the pendant, sealed it shut, and began drawing another set of runes—weaving them through the space and storm runes in impossible curves.

She was bridging the chaos runes within with the storm and space runes outside, and when she was done with that, she began weaving another set of runes—these ones Aodhán recognized.

They were spirit and blood bonding runes—simple initiate runes that somehow managed to look far more complex than Aodhán could have ever imagined.

And then finally, it was over. The process was done—oh, not yet.

Without prompting, Elder Kobi brought out a fusion chamber, the same equipment they'd used to make Zarxus's weapon, and placed it on the table. The wood creaked from the weight of the chamber, its obsidian structure gleaming in the light of the tri-forge.

Instinctively, everyone stepped back, eyes narrowing in curiosity as Elder Gumi placed the pendant into the chamber, activated it with a zap of pure lightning, and hastily shut the lid. The chamber whirred to life an instant later, a flash of searing light and chaos erupting out of it in a circle about a meter in diameter.

A low hum echoed from the chamber a moment later as metal and essence began to fuse into a single whole—a single entity. Ridges and joints disappeared, runes smoothed, and then—

BOOM!

The pendant exploded in a burst of lightning and raw chaos, shards slamming against the walls of the fusion chamber even as space crumbled, liquefied orichalcum splashing against the glass walls before freezing into useless slag.

Aodhán stared at the ruined pendant in horror, unable to believe that all that perfection had just gone up in smoke. The runes had been perfect. The chaos strip, perfect. The storm and space runes, perfect.

And yet, somehow, they'd made an error.

He glanced up at the Mythics, noting their shock as well, but when he glanced at Elder Gumi, he didn't find a trace of shock in her features. She had expected this, somehow.

She glanced up at her apprentices and exhaled slowly.

"Well," she said calmly. "That was a failure."

Her gaze slid to Aodhán, and she raised an eyebrow. "Tell me, Aodhán. What did we do wrong?"

"Me?" Aodhán stuttered, eyes widening as he glanced at the elders for help, but just like him, no one seemed to have the slightest clue. And yet, when he glanced back at Elder Gumi, he could tell that this was a test.

She was testing him, testing his intelligence.

And he couldn't afford to say something stupid.

***

What was the cause of any explosion ever? Aodhán asked himself as Elder Gumi's eyes bored into him, and the answer came to him like a bubble rising to the surface of a pond.

Balance.

Balance was the reason for most explosions, especially when it had to do with runes and magical crafting.

But what kind of balance?

His mind raced, replaying the last few hours in fragments—Elder Gumi's ragged whisper after finishing the chaos inscription, the perfect strip of solid chaos Elder Kobi had created, the storm and space runes woven across the orichalcum structure, the bridging runes connecting them all.

Everything had been perfect. Individually. But together...

Aodhán's gaze drifted to the fusion chamber, to the slag cooling against its glass walls. Chaos and storm and space, all crammed into a single pendant. Three affinities, three runic systems, meant to work in harmony.

Except they hadn't.

Rather than work together in harmony, the affinities had clashed, and one had won.

Chaos.

It swirled within the chamber now. Concentrated. Potent. Dense. Its runes carved with a profoundness and skill the other affinities lacked. Put simply, there had been too much chaos within the pendant, and rather than fuse with the other affinities, it had ripped them apart.

The entire pendant had been an explosion waiting to go off.

His eyes widened at the thought, and his mind flashed to the dense amount of swirling storm and chaos essence he'd always found within the pendant's space. It had been potent—coating anything he kept within the space in its dense energies—yet balanced. Equal.

A harmony of affinities.

He glanced at Elder Gumi, at the confused Mythics, back at the fusion chamber, and then he whispered, "Balance. The pendant lacked balance."

Elder Gumi raised an eyebrow. "Explain."

"I—" He stuttered, then forced himself to continue. "The chaos essence inside the pendant is too strong. Too dense. And the storm and space runes couldn't balance it. Not because they were wrong, but because they were—"

"Empty." Elder Sedgwick suddenly spoke, his eyes widening in realization. "The chaos strips were pure essence. But the orichalcum structure was just metal. Neutral. The runes we carved into it had nothing to anchor to."

"No inherent power to counter the chaos pressing from within," Elder Kobi reasoned, feeding off both Aodhán's and Elder Sedgwick's contributions. "It's like trying to dam a river with sticks."

"Exactly," Aodhán nodded, his voice gaining more confidence as the elders pondered it. "The storm runes were perfect, but they had no weight behind them. The chaos just… overwhelmed them during fusion."

They all turned to look at Elder Gumi to see if they were right, but her expression was unreadable.

"So," she said slowly. "What do you all suggest we do differently?"

"We imbue the orichalcum ore with storm and space essence in equal measures," Elder Sedgwick stated immediately, turning to give Aodhán a respectful nod. "Just like you said, the explosion could have been caused by imbalance. Imbuing the ore with both essences should give the other two affinities a solid foundation to match the chaos inside."

Aodhán nodded, but when Elder Gumi still didn't respond, he added, "Or we could do it separately. Carve out a part of the orichalcum core, imbue it with storm essence. Do the same with the space essence, and create the pendant structure from both ores."

Still, Elder Gumi said nothing. She just stared at Aodhán, her ancient eyes sharp and assessing.

Then slowly, she smiled.

"Sedgwick," she said, not breaking eye contact with Aodhán. "Prepare another orichalcum structure. Kobi, you and Summer, get me a dozen mythic spatial cores. Tier 150 at least. We are going to imbue the base first before we begin."

Everyone rushed to carry out her order, leaving only Aodhán, Elder Luseph, and the last apprentice still standing. But Elder Gumi barely even glanced at them. Her gaze was focused on Aodhán as she continued. "You will assist with the imbuing process. I suspect you'll be rather good at it given your… path."

"Authority," Aodhán breathed, and she smiled.

"See that nothing goes wrong, Luseph," she said to the sardonic elder. "I'd like to avoid an explosion as best as I can."

Elder Luseph nodded, and she glanced back at Aodhán. "Good work, Special Disciple Brystion. That was a brilliant answer."

Aodhán couldn't help it. He grinned. He'd passed the test.

But beyond that, he'd earned the approval of a woman feared by Calamities for her skill.

What did that say about him?

Maybe he was a prodigy after all.
