Morning light slipped through the window like a yellow thread cutting across the dim room, illuminating the space with the soft glow of early dawn while dust motes drifted lazily through the beam, dancing in the air like tiny spirits caught in amber.

The light spilled across Vector's face as he lay sprawled on the cold floor, his body arranged in the awkward angles of someone who had been thrown rather than fallen.

His mind was blank, emptied of coherent thought.

His breath came shallow and uneven, each inhalation a struggle.

The cold seeping through the floor snapped his awareness back in jagged fragments, pulling him from the fog of confusion that had claimed him. In a sharp, instinctive motion, he twisted his head back, and the chair filled his widely opened eyes with its familiar form. Now it stood exactly where it always had—upright, untouched, and perfectly aligned as though mocking him with its stability. Undisturbed, as if he'd never occupied it at all, as if the violence that had just transpired was nothing more than a fever dream.

He remembered only fragments of what had just happened—being seized by the collar with shocking speed, the movement swift and silent as a striking serpent. One instant sitting in his chair, the next thrown like a rag doll onto the unforgiving stone with force that should have broken bones. Though the attacker certainly had the strength to accomplish such a feat, being a First Stage Warrior whose enhanced body could generate power far beyond normal human limits.

As he, a newly awakened Blood Energy practitioner whose transformation had only just begun, was nothing in front of a peak First Stage Warrior who had spent years honing his enhanced capabilities.

He shifted his neck again, angling upward despite the protest of muscles that had struck stone.

There—his subordinate stood in his field of vision.

No... not his subordinate anymore, that illusion shattered beyond repair.

His attacker stood behind the chair with the casual confidence of a predator who had already won.

Blood surged through Vector's veins in response to what his eyes confirmed, and fury and rage coiled beneath his skin, burning so fiercely that the cold of the floor vanished as though it had never existed.

But his fury didn't come from the blow itself, from the casual violence that had hurled him to the floor. Nor did it come from being treated as weak, as prey that couldn't fight back. It came from betrayal.

Vector tried to draw his own knife from its scabbard—to resist, to fight back, to do something other than lie helpless on the floor—but his hands shook so violently that he couldn't even grip the hilt properly, let alone pull the blade free from its leather prison.

"Are..." His voice scraped painfully from his throat like broken glass dragging across flesh, and he swallowed hard before trying again despite the trembling that had seized his body. "Are you betraying the Resistance?"

The question spilled out raw and almost pitiful in its desperation, fear leaking through despite the rage burning inside him like a furnace that threatened to consume all rational thought.

The attacker tilted his head with the curiosity of someone examining an interesting insect, studying Vector like prey whose struggles he found mildly entertaining. A faint smirk tugged at the man's lips as he stared unblinkingly with eyes that had gone flat and predatory, his gaze fixed with a predator's infinite patience, waiting for the final struggle after the hunt had already been decided.

At some unknown after Vector was assaulted, a knife had appeared in the attacker's hand as its sharp edge gleamed in the morning light with the promise of violence, reflecting Vector's appearance back at him—sculpted symmetry, sharp jawline, cleanly arched eyebrows, trimmed black hair that spoke of careful grooming even in these dangerous times.

Vector's heart hammered against his ribs like a prisoner beating against cell walls, and the lack of an immediate answer pressed heavily on the room, filling the space with tension thick enough to choke on. His fingers twitched against the floor, seeking purchase on the cold stone but finding no salvation in its unyielding surface, and his body trembled with a mixture of fear and rage that warred for dominance.

"Betraying?" A low chuckle slipped from the attacker's lips like oil spreading across water, dark and unpleasant. "No. I am not one of you, so in essence, I'm not betraying anyone at all." He lifted the knife slightly as his hands touched its edges with the familiarity of long practice, fingers caressing steel like a lover's skin. "I'm simply doing my work with the dedication it deserves. Faithfully." A slow, indulgent smile spread across his face, baring teeth. His gaze sharpened, terrorizing Vector in place.

Still Vector didn't answered immediately. He tried to draw his own knife from its scabbard—to resist. His hands shook so violently that he couldn’t even grip the hilt, let alone pull it free. His body’s reaction was something else, but his mind—it remained clear, unnervingly calm. As he tried to find the possibilities of the sudden assault.

Why does he want me dead?Because I lead Carnatic City’s Resistance?Or is it something else? thoughts birthed in his mind. He wanted to ask the reason for the assassination.

"Vector, isn't it?" The attacker's voice interrupted before Vector could voice his thoughts. "You surprised me, I must admit. A Blood Energy–awakened individual running operations for Carnatic City's Resistance despite having barely crossed the threshold of power." He paused, head tilting as if considering various possibilities with genuine curiosity. "Influence, perhaps? Or has the Resistance become so desperately incapable that even a weak person could be given such an important position?"

Vector's jaw tightened with the sting of truth delivered as mockery.

A thought struck him as he heard it—did the attacker know his background? The man’s curious gaze and uncertain tone suggested otherwise. It was as if a stone thrown blindly across a lake had somehow struck its mark.

Whatever the case, the attacker had guessed right.

Vector had talent, that much was undeniable—his early awakening of Blood Energy proved as much. And his leadership... It couldn't be questioned as well because the Resistance operation in Carnatic City would define it more clearly. The Resistance didn't have its base here... but he had single handedly, his subordinates role couldn't be denied, established a strong network of intelligence. There was nothing that the Resistance couldn't find in Carnatic City—and this was all because of him.

But he had achieved this position primarily because of influence rather than merit, not through his own accomplishments but through his family's vast reach—the Astrum Family, the unseen hands that moved behind the Astral Alliance like puppeteers controlling their stage, Ethereum's largest merchant group whose tentacles reached into every corner of the continent. Their gold kept the Resistance breathing in the Novastra Empire, funding the desperate fight against the Lionheart Empire's relentless invasion that had already claimed half the nation.

But this man shouldn't know that carefully guarded secret, that closely held truth. No one was supposed to know the identity of the family backing the Resistance so generously, not even within the Resistance itself where compartmentalization kept dangerous knowledge from spreading.

His lips trembling despite his best efforts to control them, Vector managed to ask, "Is... is that why?" His voice quivered like a plucked string, every syllable revealing his fear. Facing a peak First Stage Warrior was to face death itself given the vast gap in their capabilities. "You want to kill me because of this?"

"No... it's not because you're the leader of the Resistance in Carnatic City, though that alone would be reason enough for some." The attacker's smile remained fixed in place. "Not because you're the heir of an influential family or the scion of a high-ranking official who pulled strings to place you here. Talent or the complete lack thereof has nothing to do with my purpose."

Slurp!

His tongue slid across his lips in a gesture that made Vector's skin crawl, and he continued, "I simply want you dead for my own very specific reasons. But first, before we reach that inevitable conclusion..."

It confirmed one thing in Vector's racing mind—the attacker didn't actually know about his background, about the Astrum connection that would make him far more valuable alive than dead.

But one thing troubled him deeply, a question that gnawed at his thoughts. Even if he was merely the leader of the Resistance in this city, why would the attacker risk exposing himself by killing someone whose death would immediately raise alarms? The Resistance members of Carnatic City knew the attacker had come here to report at his residence in the commercial district, knew he was alone with Vector when death struck.

"Wo–would it even matter in the end?" Vector asked hoarsely, his voice still carrying the tremor of genuine fear that he no longer bothered to suppress entirely. "After I'm killed, someone else would simply take my place within days. The Resistance wouldn't collapse from losing one leader. At worst... it'd lose me and my particular knowledge. And another commander takes over the operation. So why—why expose yourself so completely?"

A heartbeat of silence passed between them, heavy with unspoken calculations.

Then the attacker's grin widened further, stretching his face into something that barely resembled a human expression.

"Hehee... matter?" He took a step closer with deliberate slowness, savoring each moment. The knife caught light again, throwing bright splinters across the floor like scattered diamonds. "You misunderstand the situation completely, my dear Vector. It matters immensely, more than you can possibly imagine. Just not for the reasons swimming through that privileged head of yours."

He leaned forward, physically manifesting his dominance while savoring the terror that blazed in Vector's eyes like fire reflected in dark water.

"Your position as Resistance leader?" he continued with mocking patience. "Secondary at best. Almost irrelevant to my true purpose here."

A pause stretched between them, long and deliberate, thick with menace.

"The reason I'm willing to expose myself completely... it's because of the Estate."

A thought crystallized in Vector's mind with the clarity of ice forming, sharp and cold: The Gardener. The City Lord's Estate. No, it couldn't be that...

His throat tightened with the realization of his mistake.

His eyes widened uncontrollably before narrowing again as he fought to regain some measure of composure.

"Estate?" Vector said, one eyebrow raised as he forced confusion into his expression with the skill of someone practiced in deception. His hand slid backward along the floor, fueling his backward movement even though he knew such retreat was ultimately futile. "What estate are you talking about?"

He knew his physical movement meant absolutely nothing against the attacker's superior strength and speed.

The attacker sighed and shook his head slowly, as if disappointed by Vector's transparent attempt at misdirection. He caressed the knife with steady fingers while advancing step by measured step, that same unsettling smile carved across his face like a mask that couldn't be removed—one measured step forward for every inch that Vector desperately retreated.

No hurry at all in his movements, as though time itself had become his ally.

"The Gardener," the attacker said simply, as though those two words explained everything. "You told me yourself during our conversation. Did you forget already?"

Vector's backward motion stopped abruptly as understanding crashed over him. He stilled in place completely, every muscle freezing as cold flooded his chest like icy water filling a vessel. The thing he'd feared most had happened despite his caution.

But confusion warred with fear.

I told him... when exactly? I mentioned being a gardener, yes, but working specifically in the Estate? Never. I was careful about that.

"Hunh... when did I say it?" He muttered, more to himself than to the attacker.

"Oh-hoo!" The attacker's grin stretched grotesquely wide, as though his lips might actually tear free from his face and reveal something monstrous beneath. He halted his advance as well, perfectly mirroring Vector's stillness. "The blind arrow hit its mark beautifully. You really are working as a gardener in the City Lord's Estate."

Vector stopped completely, utterly dumbfounded by his own stupidity. He'd been tricked by the simplest of methods, led into confirming what had only been suspicion.

"How did you guess that..." he asked stiffly, his voice hollow with the realization of his fatal error.

Anyone couldn't just pull such specific conclusions out of thin air without some foundation. It still needed some basis, some starting point to launch that blind attack.

"Hehee... let me ease your departing soul from this world, and address that regret burning so clearly in your eyes." The attacker crouched down slowly, lowering himself until their gazes met on a more equal level. Even then, six or seven arm-lengths still separated them, a distance that might as well have been miles given their respective capabilities.

"I'm cautious by nature, you see—it's kept me alive in this profession far longer than most." His smile never wavered. "I met with Head Butler John earlier today and obtained a complete list of everyone currently working in the Estate, from the highest servants to the lowest laborers. When you mentioned working as a gardener during our earlier conversation, and then I connected that innocent detail with your residential location here in the commercial district rather than the servant quarters... you stirred my suspicion like sediment rising in disturbed water."

Slash!

The knife cut through the air in a casual gesture, but in purpose to intimidate Vector.

"Bingo. Here's the result of that small leap of logic."

"I only wanted to cripple the Resistance in Carnatic City for a time, to remove its effectiveness for a few crucial weeks," the attacker continued, shaking his head with what might have been genuine regret. "But I accidentally hooked a far bigger fish than I expected when I came here today." He smiled thinly with dark satisfaction. "You really do have terrible luck to have revealed yourself so carelessly."

Through the attacker's words, delivered with the casual cruelty of someone who enjoyed watching realization dawn, clarity appeared in Vector's flickering eyes—confirmation of his worst fears. He was being killed not for who he was within the Resistance, but because of his position in the Estate, his access to information and locations that most could never reach. Something important must be about to happen in that place, something significant enough that this man would expose himself completely as a traitor, even gamble everything on blind suspicion.

And regarding Vector's luck, the attacker was absolutely wrong in his assessment. If anyone who truly knew Vector heard this claim about terrible fortune, they would simply ridicule the attacker for his ignorance. Vector was extremely lucky in almost every aspect of his life—to such an extent that members of his entire family would come specifically to receive his blessings before undertaking any important endeavor.

These things had changed only after Nova's birth into the family, she who had inherited the title of true goddess of luck from her older brother.

He shook his head sharply to stop his thoughts from drifting into irrelevant memories. Focus on the present moment, on survival, on anything except nostalgia for better times.

Moments ago, this same man had stood calmly before him in this very room, delivering a report—a fresh intelligence gathered from Windstone City, the capital of Elaris. Detailed information about a Lionheart Empire weapons consignment that would be arriving in Carnatic tomorrow, valuable intelligence that the Resistance could use.

And one careless remark during that conversation.

One thoughtless hint about working as a gardener somewhere had been enough to make the attacker suspicious, to start the chain of reasoning that led to this moment.

A fatal mistake born of comfortable habit.

Vector scrambled backward again with renewed desperation, still half-sprawled on the cold floor, hands clawing at the unyielding stone to gain any distance he could manage.

The window loomed behind him, closer now, and morning light brushed his hair like gentle fingers, turning black strands to amber and gold beneath Aethel's warming rays.

"Yes... yes. Just like that. Run from me," the attacker said with evident pleasure, savoring every moment of the struggle, drinking in the dread that filled Vector's eyes like wine. "The Earl doesn't even know his enemy has been working directly under his nose for all this time. Once I've finished playing with you and extracting everything you know, there will be more fish to catch in that particular pond."

Through his words, casually delivered as though stating the last words for Vector, it became clear that the Earl's Estate didn't yet know about Vector being a member of the Resistance, didn't suspect the gardener they'd harbored so close to power was a spy. And that, too, leader of the Resistance in Carnatic.

Slurp.

The attacker raised the knife higher—not to strike yet, but purely to intimidate, to watch fear grow.

Vector understood his sadistic tendency perfectly now—the need to see someone struggling desperately in a near-death event, to watch hope die slowly rather than quickly.

Vector didn't move as the attacker clearly intended, didn't scramble away in panic. He even cringed visibly upon seeing the man's repeated licking behavior, that wet sound of tongue on lips. A sadistic person through and through, someone who derived genuine enjoyment from the suffering of others.

He met the attacker's gaze directly and saw it clearly painted across those cold features—the detached thrill of power over life and death, the absolute certainty of victory. The look of someone watching prey caught in a trap, already suffering the agony of knowing escape was impossible, having already given up any hope of survival. A sadistic nature that sought to gain happiness specifically from inflicting pain on others rather than from any practical purpose.

And yet, despite everything he saw in those merciless eyes—

Vector felt no dread coursing through his veins, no panic clouding his thoughts.

He halted all his actions with sudden completeness.

His backward crawl ceased entirely. The shuddering that had wracked his frame ended as though a switch had been thrown. His breathing slowed from panicked gasps to measured draws. The terror vanished from his eyes as if it had never existed, replaced by something else entirely.

A smile replaced all that performed fear instead.

He ceased his acting. After the very first blow that had sent him tumbling to the floor like a ragged doll—every tremor of his body, every shake of his limbs, every backward step he'd taken, even the emotions that had played across his face—he'd been acting with deliberate intent.

Faking it all with the skill of someone trained in deception.

The attacker stopped mid-stride at the sudden and dramatic change in his prey's demeanor.

But suddenly, breaking the strange tableau between them... the attacker drew the knife closer to his own torso, unconsciously shifting into a defensive posture as instinct overrode confidence, and turned his head left and right with careful deliberation. Slow and methodical, checking every corner and shadow.

The room's luxurious yet understated furnishings offered no answers to the question forming in his mind. The tea table stood exactly as it had been, undisturbed by violence. The couch bore no sign of struggle or hidden threat. The entrance door remained shut, its hinges unmoved by any secret arrival.

The two-story house lay silent around them—dilapidated on the outside, empty of other occupants. No heartbeat beyond the two of them could be detected by his enhanced senses. Even the gentle winds passing through the windows could be heard clearly in the stillness.

Nothing had changed in the physical space.

Vector caught the flicker of unease in the man's eyes, saw the moment when predator became aware of being watched. Death was close enough now that instinct had finally warned him of danger he couldn't see or measure. With his peak First Stage Warrior aura extended to its limits, he must be desperately probing for any lurking danger hidden in the room's corners. Vector knew the attacker couldn't find anything no matter how carefully he searched. The attacker wasn't capable of detecting what Vector knew was there—not against a peak Third Stage Body Refinement Warrior whose power operated on an entirely different level. His protector, practicing in the adjacent room.

Good, Vector thought grimly. He has quite good intuition for someone in his profession. And for anyone who makes a living through violence and deception, such instincts must be an absolute requirement for survival.

He didn't wait any longer, didn't give the attacker time to puzzle out the source of his unease. "Unc—"

Before he could complete the word, before he could call out the name of his powerful protector and use that advantage to end this nightmare, an invisible force restrained him with crushing totality. His jaw locked shut as though iron bands had clamped around it. His tongue refused to move despite his desperate commands.

The word died in his throat.
