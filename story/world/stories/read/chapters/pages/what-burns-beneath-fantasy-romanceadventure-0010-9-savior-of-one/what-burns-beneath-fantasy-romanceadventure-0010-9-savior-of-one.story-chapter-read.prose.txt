It was a nightmare.

One of the men sported a purpling jaw and a crimson gash on one cheek. It looked like Teela had head-butted him with her short, sharp horns.

It also looked like she had paid for it; streams of bright red blood dripped from her mouth and nose. One man held her arms behind her back as she thrashed from side to side.

The third man had produced a knife.

"Thrice-cursed mongrels. Scum of the salted realm.” Thick fingers dug into her short red hair.

"Stop!" I cried- squeaked, really- rushing forwards. My bandaged hands lifted in what I hoped was an appeasing gesture. “I… I have gold, okay? I'll pay you to let her go!" I fumbled for my little coinpurse. My fingers clutched at empty air.

Teela winced at me, ashen-faced and vaguely apologetic.

"Salt, really?" I moaned, realizing what had happened.

"Sorry," she gasped, then twisted snake-like in her captor’s grip. She got one arm free, looped an ankle around his, and nearly sent him off-balance.

The man swore, then balled his free hand into a fist and rammed it into her stomach.

I cried out, horrified, as the young woman collapsed and all three men burst into laughter. One stepped forwards and aimed a kick at her head. She flinched away, narrowly avoiding the blow, and whimpered.

“Stop this now!” I ran to try and help her up. A rough hand shoved me back as a boot tried to stomp on Teela’s thrashing tail, to a chorus of more laughter.

An image filled my mind. Glowing. Beckoning. The fourth rune from my book. Before I could even fathom what I was doing, I traced the little symbol in front of my face. “Veth.” It hung in the air, smoldering like a hot coal. A male voice cried out from behind and my chest roared with heat and I smelled smoke and then-

Screams erupted around me as fire, actual fire, a blazing burst of hot flames, surged from my outstretched palm and bathed the shadows around us in torrid light.

I screamed, too, shocked and terrified that I would look down to find my hands burnt beyond recognition.

Teela’s blood-smeared face glowed in the light. She gazed at me with something like awe.

It felt like an eternity before the blaze died out, leaving only charred earth and smoldering patches of grass. I looked at my hand. It appeared to be alright; the cloth bandage had been destroyed and the skin beneath was still cut a dozen times over, but I was unburnt. I nearly fainted with relief.

“Pacted,” one of the men hissed.

My vision swam. My chest felt like it had been packed with hot coals.

“She’s pacted.” His face was white. He lifted the knife.

Teela surged to her feet. “You saw the rune!” she shrieked. “You saw it; she’s blessed, by law you have to-!”

“Blighted fools!” the innkeeper roared from just behind me. “Stand down, all three of you, or by my bones you’ll spend the night outside!” His voice softened a moment later and a trembling hand grasped my elbow. “Come… come back inside, my lady. Please. The… her, too, if that’s your wish.”

I stared at him, struck mute by the sudden deference.

Sweat trickled down his temples. “I didn’t know, my lady, I swear it.”

“Didn’t… know…?”

“Please, forgive me.”

Teela surged forwards, reaching out as if to touch me, then flinched back with a look of chagrin.

The innkeeper eyed her warily, then turned his ashen face back to me. He looked… uncertain. As if waiting for instruction.

I swayed, wracking my mind for an explanation. Clem had taught me all about runekeepers throughout history, and of course they were always respected. But this was far more than respect; these people were looking at me with reverence.

The world swam. I eyed the group of men warily, blinking back spots. “No one… no one lays a hand on her again.”

They made noises of affirmation. The innkeeper barked at them to get back inside, and without any argument they filed past. One even turned and ducked his head low in a bow. The door closed. From behind it, the innkeeper began shouting.

I turned to Teela. “Are you alright?”

“Thank you,” she breathed. “I never thought… I mean, that one of you would help me…” she shook her head and gave a little laugh, then pressed one bony hand to her side and wheezed.

I struggled to tear my gaze away from her ember-hued horns. “Are you very hurt? I'm sorry I couldn't... do more."

"Heh... oh, you don't need to worry about me. This is nothing." She straightened, still wincing a bit, and lifted one torn sleeve to wipe at the tear-streaked blood and mud on her face. She succeeded mostly just in smearing it around.

"Well," I offered, "I can't help with the bruises, but I can help you get cleaned up. Here… Alar." I traced. Rainwater sprinkled down just in front of her face. More spots danced behind my eyes and I sat down hard.

Teela’s jaw went slack. Then she gave a delighted giggle and splashed handfuls of water onto her chin and cheeks.

“Why were you stealing from those men?”

She winced a bit, looking distinctly uncomfortable. Her ropy, flesh-colored tail flicked to one side. “Um… because I didn’t have any coins?” She wrinkled her nose. “And they deserved it, besides! You saw what they were like!”

“They were horrible, but… that doesn’t make it right.”

She flushed darky, fumbling with a deep pocket, then produced my little coinpurse. “Er... here. It wasn't personal, okay?”

I spent a moment regarding Teela; gaunt cheekbones, flecks of drying blood spattered at her collarbone, baggy clothing full of rips and tears. "It's okay. You can keep it."

Her rust-colored eyes widened. She spent a long while staring at me, rainwater dripping down her angular cheeks.

“So… you’re part Fae,” I said slowly. My eyes trailed up to her horns.

“It’s not my fault!” She flinched away, looking almost fearful. “I didn’t ask to have-!”

“I’m sorry!” I held up my hands. “I’m sorry, I didn’t… I didn’t mean to offend you. Of course it’s not your fault. I just… I’m sorry, I’ve never met one of you before. There’s certainly no one like you back home. And this is my first time being outside of Fellbrook.”

“Huh. Right. I guess it makes sense there wouldn’t be any of us there. Those silver trees tend to get, uh, a little scary when there’s one of us around.” She wrinkled her nose, massaging her doubtlessly bruised stomach. Her bright eyes turned to the dwindling gleams of sunset. “Well… look, it’s getting dark. And you haven’t eaten.”

As if on cue, my stomach growled. I offered a faint smile. “No, I haven’t. Would you like to come sit with me?”

We headed back inside. Her assailants were nowhere in sight. The innkeeper looked up from behind the bar as we entered. His face was beet-red and he seemed to be arranging various bits of food onto a dusty copper plate.

“It’s more fresh, my lady,” he blurted, hurrying over with my new meal. A glint of gold caught my eye; there, beside the plate, was the coin I’d given him earlier. He bustled away, broad shoulders hunched.

I watched him, puzzled, until the back of my neck prickled. I turned.

The man sitting alone raised his mug in my direction, smirking. There was something odd in his expression. Satisfaction. Like he’d found the answer to a nagging question.

I scowled at him.

Teela joined me at the small wooden table. My new plate held a large heaping of salted pork, apple wedges, and cubes of hard yellow cheese. I scarfed it all down.

Teela watched me eat, spindly fingers tapping tunelessly. When I had almost finished she blurted out, “Can I travel with you?”

I nearly choked. “Huh?”

She leaned forwards, hands splayed flat on the table. “Tomorrow. You said you’re going north, right? Well, it’s safer to travel in groups, and you’re… really nice.”

I swallowed my last bite.

Her voice lowered. “People might get really suspicious if they see a woman traveling alone. Especially dressed like a noble. And if they find out you can do magic... it could be bad, y’know?”

I did not know, not at all, and gave another weary sigh.

She pressed on. “If I’m there, I bet it’ll help.”

I definitely liked the idea of having company for the road. Teela certainly seemed friendly, if somewhat odd. Although she probably thinks the same thing about me. I managed a tired, but genuine, smile. “Teela, I would love to have your company. Maybe we can have breakfast, then head out together in the morning?”

She beamed at me, horns and eyes shining in the firelight.

I gathered my things and headed towards one of the empty, open bedrooms, pausing at the threshold. Two fingers twisted. “Ulen.” Hmm… spoiled milk. And old, briny pickles. Gross. I stifled a giggle.

I closed my bedroom door to the distinct sound of a man spitting out his drink.
