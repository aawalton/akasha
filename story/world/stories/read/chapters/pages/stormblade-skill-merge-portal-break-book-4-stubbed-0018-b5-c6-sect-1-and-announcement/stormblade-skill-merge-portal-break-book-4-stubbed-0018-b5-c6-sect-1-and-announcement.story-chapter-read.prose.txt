The world of the Soaring Saber sect was bright.

Even in comparison to Phoenix, it was almost stiflingly hot. The two suns in the sky were separated only by a few degrees of angle, and they beat down with all the fury of a Sonoran Desert summer. I found myself sweating within seconds, and Ellen’s face reddened. We’d been dressed for the heat, of course. It was foolish to do anything else in Phoenix. But this world was built different—and so were its people, clearly.

The air almost seemed to physically rip the moisture from my mouth as I breathed. It smelled spicy, like a kitchen with too much going on in it.

I readied the Storm’s Blade, but let it hang in my hand, as my eyes adjusted to the light. In front of us, a wooden stairwell led down into a deep ravine, easily several thousand feet deep. Colored drapes hung above it, and a single sword sat jammed into their frame.

“There,” I said quickly.

Ellen nodded. She didn’t bother saying anything, and we jogged for the shade, then started working our way down into the ravine.

The entire thing was covered with structures. Most of them were wooden, and every one of them flew similar, bright-colored drapes. The reason why became obvious pretty quickly; the suns crested over the ravine’s edge as Ellen and I slowly followed the seemingly endless flights of stairs deeper, pouring bright light into the canyon below. But instead of the same glaring, burning sunlight at the surface, the bright, stretched-out cloth drapes filtered it into a bright kaleidoscope of color.

“It’s beautiful,” Ellen said. She stopped at the edge of a rail-lined platform and stared up at the shades.

“It is,” I said. “We’re on a deadline, though. Let’s keep moving.”

“Agreed.” She pulled herself from the railing, and we continued down into the canyon’s depths. At some point, the filtered light mixed together into a murky, dusk-feeling twilight, and a few flights of stairs later, we arrived at the bottom.

So far, we hadn’t seen a single person on our trip down. The reason why quickly became obvious.

A massive arena covered the cavern’s floor. Circles of sand, each outlined by wooden platforms and stone walls, dotted its expanse. Two buildings dominated the arena’s edges. On the far side, a stone structure—the only stone building we’d seen so far, including caves—stretched a hundred yards down-canyon. It was roofed with tile, and no shades hung from its sides. The other building was a wood-walled courtyard and a wide hall beyond it.

The arena was full of people. Or maybe they were monsters. It was hard to tell until we got closer, but they almost exclusively shared Tanat’s slim build and black armor—and the lip-exposing, eye-covering helmet she’d worn, too. Every one of them also carried a sword at their waists, with a handful of exceptions; there were duels on every sandy dueling circle.

At some other time, this would be paradise.

But right now, I had a mission. Ellen needed that core, and she needed someone to help her repair her own with it. The sooner it was done, the sooner she could catch up to me. Hopefully, she’d be ready to make it before the Paragons started hunting—

“You. Outsider.”

I turned, expecting a sword to the face.

I was half right.

Instead of the sword pointing at me, it was aimed at Ellen. She stared down its length at the thin-limbed man holding it. A fire blazed across its length as he poured Mana into it.

“I challenge you.”

Well, obviously, I couldn’t have that.

Ellen was A-Rank. Technically, so was…

Latan: A-Rank

Yeah, technically, so was Latan. It was a fair fight, but at the same time, she had no business fighting if she didn’t have to—and the way she glanced at me and nodded told me everything I needed to know. She felt the same way.

I didn’t waste any time.

The Storm’s Blade came up. It slapped Latan’s flaming blade hard enough to send it flying, and in one smooth movement, I stood between him and Ellen. “She’s not fair game right now,” I said, staring him in the eye.

“According to the rules of our sect, she is. As an A-Ranker on par with the peak of the outer school, she can be challenged,” Latan said.

“And I’m her champion,” I shot back. Ellen rolled her eyes next to me—I couldn’t see it, but I felt it. I couldn’t blame her, either. This was ridiculous.

His eyes narrowed. Fire surged in them. “You, the equivalent of an inner school member, are her champion? Unacceptable. I demand you let me fight her!”

I shook my head. He lunged for his sword, and I let him pick it up. At the same time, I noted a second swordsman closing in from behind. This one’s blade extended like a curving vine, and it seemed to thrash as he swung it, not at me, but at Ellen.

Lightning Dance.

I interposed myself between the incoming blow and Ellen as she spun to dodge it. The attacker’s sword slammed into mine; the shockwave of impact rippled through the woman’s arm. She was A-Rank, too. I smiled, but it didn’t reach my eyes. Those burned with anger. How dare they attack us when we hadn’t even explained why we were here? Were they actually portal monsters? “Step back, Ellen,” I said. “Keep yourself safe, but don’t use your skills or spells unless you have to.”

She retreated to the stairs, and I vanished and reappeared a second time, blocking the path upward to her. It wouldn’t matter against anyone who could fly, and the…

I counted again. Three swords against mine now. The fire swordsman, the plant spellblade, and someone whose sword looked like it was made from feathers.

Fine. One last Lightning Dance brought me in range of the flame swordsman. I shifted to Thunderlord stance as he reacted far too slowly. Lightning cracked. Laminar Blade. The Storm’s Blade was sheathed in a watery layer, and I threw myself into a full-bodied lunge, pulling it at the last second.

The Storm’s Blade hung next to Latan’s head. I flicked it; the flat of the water-covered blade crashed into his unhelmeted head, and he crumpled. “Next?”

Both of the remaining A-Rankers closed in, swords ready. More circled around us to watch, swords at their sides or sheathed and on their hips. Good. At least they weren’t planning on joining in yet.

This wasn’t how I’d hoped this would go. We didn’t have time to fight every A-Ranker with an attitude and something to prove. Wing-sword and plant-sword closed in on me again, this time fully focused on me and ignoring Ellen. One of them yelled something. I ignored it. Parry. Riposte. Turn the blade so the flat made contact instead of the killing edge. I didn’t want them dead. Just stopped. But if this continued, that’d change.

“Enough.” A voice slammed down on the dueling field, and an aura followed it. It hit like a wave of crushing force, overwhelming my retracted one. But when I tried to push my aura out against it, to my surprise, it failed.

That meant three things.

First, there was at least one person here who exceeded my aura’s strength. That meant an SS or SS-Plus-Ranker. I wasn’t surprised that one was here, but I was surprised they were throwing their weight around on this dueling arena.

Second, that information—coupled with what the Crone had said—gave me an idea of what was really going on in the Soaring Saber Sect’s portal world. If there was a monster at SS-Rank or higher, they were also playing the ascension game, and this was a perfect setup for it. The sheer number of students at A-Rank made that obvious. There had to be a hundred. If a tenth of them made S-Rank, they’d be fuel for a surprisingly quick ascension.

And that led me to the third revelation.

Tanat had either passed to S-Rank on returning to the sect, or she was about to.

Retan: SS-Rank Paragon

The sect’s leader—or at least, I assumed he was its leader—stepped through the gate. Physically, he wasn’t bigger or taller than any of the A-Rankers who’d dropped to one knee on the dueling arena. His armor wasn’t any different than theirs, either. He wore the same helmet and leather cuirass. But there were two differences between him and the assembled outer-school swordfighters. His aura loomed over him like a golden-light echo of himself, moving with every move he took. And his sword was tucked into his belt, white light pouring from it to chase the shadows from every corner of the canyon.

I didn’t unsummon the Storm’s Blade or drop to a knee. But I did lower my weapon.

“Are you Noelstra?” he asked, even though he could read my name and rank just fine. He kept a hand on his weapon, and I kept mine at the ready as I nodded, just in case. He returned the nod, a single motion, and moved to the edge of the arena. Standing less than ten feet from me, his aura was even more overwhelming, and the brightness of his aura nearly blinded me.

“Sister Tanat has explained the events of the Lonely Gate to us. Welcome.” Retan’s voice rose loudly enough for the entire dueling pitch to hear it. “This man and his partner are honored guests of the Inner School. Any fighter who wishes to challenge either must do so formally.”

Then his voice dropped again. “Sister Tanat said you’d be coming. She awaits inside the gates.”

As we stepped past the cowed A-Rankers, though, his eyes flicked toward me, and I saw the hunter in them.

The world inside the courtyard, beyond the thick, tall gates, was night and day compared to the one outside.

Ellen was the only person—monster, delver, or whatever they were—below S-Rank. The others were all thin, long-limbed people, but none of them wore armor or helmets. Every one of them carried a sheathed sword at their hip, and every one was an S-Ranker. There were no others at SS-Rank besides Retan, who’d vanished after assigning someone to lead us to Tanat’s quarters.

Our guide led us silently past lush gardens filled with water fountains and ponds of floating lilies. A waterfall poured down from above, cleverly concealed by the cliff and wall so that only those on the inside could see it. Everywhere, the inner school dripped with humidity, moisture, and water; the decadence clashed painfully with the colored sun-screens overhead and the warm, dusty dueling arena, and with the houses tucked against the upper canyon’s walls. Clearly, the world this portal had been ripped from was a place of haves and have-nots.

A stairwell opened in the sandstone, and our guide descended underground. I shrugged. “I guess we follow him.”

Ellen didn’t hesitate. The moment she got into the dark, torch-lit passage, she seemed to stand straighter. When she noticed me noticing her, she shrugged. “I’m not planning on fighting, but this would be the ideal environment for me.”

“You will not have to fight,” a voice said from down the hall. And there, standing in a wide doorway, was Tanat. She wore a simple black robe and sandals that reminded me of flip-flops, and her dark eyes flicked from Ellen to me, then back to Ellen. One hand rested on her sword’s pommel. After a moment, she raised it in greeting. “You are both safe here. I’m glad to see you’ve learned your skills and core more intimately—and that you now understand your relationship with them.”

The guide nodded and left, and for the first time since we’d gotten here, I relaxed. “Tanat, it’s good to see you. I need that core, and I’ve got a different one to trade for it.” I reached into my pocket and pulled the core I’d gotten from Quellat out.

She stared at me, black, iris-less eyes locked onto mine. Then she dropped to a squat. “I’m sorry, Kade Noelstra, but I cannot give it to you. It was the price I paid the sect for ascending to the inner school, and it sits in the treasure hall now.”

Tanat: S-Rank

I grimaced, but Ellen pushed past me, finding a mat on the floor and sitting gracefully. Tanat nodded. “You must be Kade’s partner. It is good to meet you—if he weren’t already claimed, I might have proposed a Dual Skill Progression pairing of my own.”

My cheeks heated up, and I walked to a different mat and joined Ellen. “Is there any way we can get that core?”

The thin-limbed woman found her own place on the floor. She thought for a minute—a long, painful minute. Then she nodded. “The core you want is in the treasure house. However, I may pick a single, non-core item tonight, and the cores and treasures will be open for auction. It is part of the sect’s celebration for an S-Rank ascension.”

I filed that away. The Crone’s entire system was based on gaining as much power as possible, and I was willing to be that this was, too. Retan was playing the ascension game, after all. The treasure house had to be his way to build a surplus of cores. “They’ll be expensive, right?”

“Oh, very,” Tanat said, nodding seriously. “And there is one other problem. You, as outsiders, cannot bid. I could bid for you, but each of us is only allowed a single item to bid on. Can you offer anything in exchange for my bid?”

“Yes,” I said before I could second-guess myself. “I’ll explain it later, but I have access to a weapon you’ll want. If you help me, I’ll give you partial control of it.”

She relaxed. Her shoulders slumped, and her stiff, upright posture folded in on itself. “Very well. I will bid on your shadow core. Do you have something to use as payment? The core you offered me would suffice if it isn’t too heavily bid on.”

“Yes, but that’s not our only problem,” Ellen said. “As you noted, we’re bonded as Dual Skill Progression partners.”

“I did note that,” Tanat said. “I hope your particular pairing proves fruitful, and that your children are as talented as you hope.”

It was Ellen’s turn to blush. She powered through it, though. “Thank you, I think? In any case, my core is damaged, and I need this one to repair it, but I also need a way to perform the—“

“Ah, a break. The sect deals with those frequently as part of the Path’s consequences. The surgical process is painful and tedious, but routine. It would likely not cost you anything further, and several of my class’s more…precise…students could perform it.” Tanat turned to me. “Before we continue, I do wish for the bare minimum of an answer. What do you offer me?”

I took a deep breath. Once I said it, I wouldn’t be able to take it back. But I didn’t have a choice. Tanat had to know, and a part of me hoped I could pull her out of the sect. Staying here, with someone playing the ascension game, was a death sentence. So, after another moment’s hesitation, I said the words.

“The Eye of the Storm.”
