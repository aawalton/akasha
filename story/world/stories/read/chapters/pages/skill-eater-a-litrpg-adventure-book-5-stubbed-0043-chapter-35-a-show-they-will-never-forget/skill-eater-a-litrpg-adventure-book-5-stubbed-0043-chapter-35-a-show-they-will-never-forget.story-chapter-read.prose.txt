Edge got ready to rumble when Belver planted his massive shield and began to advance.

The armor must have been modified to enable the maneuver, because the insanely large slab of steel slid across the stonework as smoothly as if it had been oiled. It felt like he was standing on the tracks playing chicken with a train, chuckling at the ridiculous level of defense the setup provided.

The gladiator was impervious to a frontal assault, and the shield obscured his body. Edge wouldn’t have had any idea what the warrior was doing if it wasn’t for Gaze, adding a psychological component to the giant’s unusual tactic. He wasn’t sure what the man was planning, but he was in for a world of pain if he took the charge head-on. He suspected his opponent had a lethal surprise in store if he tried to circle round and attack from the sides, since it was the obvious thing to do.

He must be able to see me, or that setup would never work. Edge fired a salvo of Blasts while spraying Repel in a high arc, soaking Belver like he was standing in a storm. The wedges of compressed air couldn’t break through, and the water was harmless, but the skills kept the gladiator on his toes and forced him to divide his attention while Edge made his approach.

When glowing dots appeared across the warrior’s body, he realized Assassin’s Boon was pitching in, revealing weak points in the man’s armor and stance. The shield didn’t have any vulnerabilities, but Mel’s aura expanded his options, and it was nice to know Boon didn’t require line of sight to perform its analysis.

Edge assumed a neutral stance while Foebinder stretched to its maximum length, ready to attack from above or reach around the titanic shield if an opening appeared. He entered a trance-like state as Belver crossed the final few feet, trusting his instincts to guide his path.

A split second before he burst into action, his skill senses warned him that this wasn’t a charging attack, and his body moved of its own volition. Instead of trying to run him over, the gladiator’s skill transferred the impetus of his considerable bulk into his Spike Slam skill.

Belver stopped on a dime as his shield came down like an avalanche, clipping Edge’s boot heel as he leapt out of the way. The shockwave rippled through him, dealing enough damage to trip his Organ Guard, but that wasn’t nearly as bad as what followed in its wake. Jagged rocks erupted from the floor, pushing his reflexes to the limit as he danced between the lethal projections, trusting Rejuvenation to restore the damage he’d suffered along the way.

“Fucking regenerators,” Belver grumbled, although the grin on his face told Edge he was having a grand old time.

Foebinder struck a half-second later.

The left chain of oblivion came whipping around in a violent arc with the polearm clasped between its coils. Belver couldn’t move the shield in time and would expose himself to Edge if he tried. Instead, he raised the ball of steel encasing his right fist to block the strike cold, setting the ringing chime of metal against metal echoing across the arena.

By this point, Edge had repositioned, sweeping Opulence low to strike the back of his opponent’s knees. Before the glaive landed, the gladiator was gone.

If he hadn’t done so much sparring with Sakura and Mel and had a Companion who specialized in spatial magic, he wouldn’t have realized what was happening until it was too late. Belver had been hiding a short-range teleport, appearing behind Edge with his shield poised to strike.

Even though he saw it coming, the move almost claimed Edge’s life.

He rolled to one side to dodge the shield slam, ready for shockwaves and spikes alike. He was not, however, prepared for the shield to multiply on the way down, forming an offset copy that was remarkably similar to Double Slash. Foebinder realized what was happening in the nick of time, grabbing onto a gate and pulling him out of harm’s reach with millimeters to spare. Thanks, partner. I owe you one.

Belver had taken control of the momentum of the melee and didn’t intend to return it. He followed in Edge’s wake like an armor-plated elephant, combining shield strikes, stone spikes, and brutal backhands with the wrecking ball encasing his right hand. The gladiator grew more aggressive as their duel wore on and thought he had Edge’s fighting style figured out.

However, the keen tactical insight that came from a career of fighting cored opponents betrayed Belver in that instant. He had no way of knowing his opponent had far more skills than anyone else their stage and could strengthen them through the myriad means unique to Skill-Eater. Edge’s gut told him he could catch the giant off-guard by switching it up, and he struck without hesitation.

The next time the shield came down in a devastating bash, he activated Shadow Play. The attack wasn’t infused with mana this time around, which meant it couldn’t harm his incorporeal form. It cost him a chunk of mana when the metal phased through his legs on its way to the ground, but the move let him materialize inside Belver’s guard with Opulence poised to strike.

He almost won in that moment, but the man was fast and had insane reflexes for someone so strong. The warrior released his shield without hesitation, using a skill that produced a double-speed roundhouse for the first time in the fight.

Edge leaned back fast enough to save his skull, but his nose was shattered in the process. He took a boot to his sternum that shattered several ribs but didn’t abort his attack. He combined Quadra Slash with Elemental Blade, coating the dragon-clad glaive in a nimbus of crackling current.

Four lightning-wreathed blades lashed out as he completed Dragon’s Claw. When Belver blocked, electricity surged through his right arm, delivering a nasty shock to his organs.

“I like you, Edge.” He spat blood as his hair stood on end. “This is the most fun I’ve had in months.”

“The feeling is mutual, but I’m still going to kill you.”

They continued their battle for several thrilling minutes, each doing their best to prevail but not bypassing the other’s guard. While it was a one-on-one duel, they couldn’t ignore the other duo completely. Whenever the shadows grew too thick, Edge and Belver turned away from each other and unleashed powerful attacks, driving the horde back before resuming their contest. When the vines encroached, they repositioned, forcing the plants to spread out instead of coming at them en masse.

Mel and the archer worked surprisingly well for people who’d never teamed up before. She played defense while the woman thinned the horde and tried to snipe the summoner, who had to be running low on mana by this point.

Edge was in the process of setting up a finishing move—soaking Belver with Repel Water and electrocuting him with Devastating Discharge—when danger arrived from another quarter.

“Something’s wrong!” Melenia called out. “The duo seemed frustrated by our arrangement, but their confidence is back.”

“Beware,” Skill-Eater chimed in. “A wide-area attack is imminent. Neither of you will survive.”

Belver and the archer must have sensed the danger too. All four of them turned when the plant-manipulator’s core ignited, emitting a dense flood of mana that could only be the result of a triple combo or ultimate ability. He thought the strangle vines or thorn flingers would be enhanced, or perhaps a new species would appear, but his guess missed the mark.

To the astonishment of everyone watching the fight unfold, the turbulent tide of magic flowed into the flowers instead, causing the colorful blossoms to swell. Edge’s skill senses were screaming that he was in imminent danger, and he’d taken the lord’s warning to heart. Whatever was about to happen, dodging wasn’t an option. Even if he found a way to withstand the attack, there was no way that Mel would survive.

He hadn’t been planning to use Overdrive, as it was imperative for his meeting with the Coalition. But since the ability’s cooldown had been shaved by two and a half days, whipping it out wasn’t a problem anymore. He had no idea what headed his way, but there wasn’t time to second-guess his decision.

“Fuck it,” Edge gathered his will and called upon the magic within. After coming this close, there was no way that he was going to lose this fight without going all out. If the Coalition was watching he would give them a taste of his true strength before their final round of negotiations.

An inferno blossomed behind his ribs as his reactor roared to life—soul thrumming with the reverberations of the great engine Overdrive.

He didn’t have time to sink into an ocean of sensory overload. He rode it out instead, remaining in his body while getting ready to make his move. He flared every power he had, taking advantage of the extra Amplification as he sent a barrage of Blasts the summoner’s way.

It had only been a few seconds since Skill-Eater’s warning, but he was already out of time. The fist-sized flowers bloomed a heartbeat later. Vibrant petals peeled back as the plants released a dense cloud of pollen that wafted toward his side of the arena. He was about to use Maelstrom to blow it away, but then he noticed the woman was guiding the grains with mana, and they were ignoring the brisk autumn breeze.

The shadow beasts were unaffected, but when the substance engulfed the vines, they shriveled and died, shrinking like they’d been severed and left to bake in the sun.

“Ultra-desiccant,” the archer cried out. “We’re fucked!”

In that moment, Edge was overcome with the complete and utter certainty that he would die the instant the cloud broke over him, sucking out every speck of moisture contained within his body, even if he held his breath.

The arena’s barrier flickered to life, protecting the crowd from being caught in the periphery as a plan sprang to life in the theater of his mind’s eye. He concentrated and activated his newest Epic skill, mouthing a silent prayer to any gods who might happen to be listening. The airborne ultradesiccant was barreling toward him, streaking for his position as his magic took hold.

The archer and shield-bearer looked at each other and sprinted in opposite directions so the magic couldn’t target both warriors at once. It bought the duo a few seconds while the pollen split into three pieces, but the archer was cut off when two clouds came from both sides at once. She fired a volley in a final act of defiance, forcing the humanoid shadow to work overtime to deflect her vengeful barrage.

When the cloud engulfed her, the archer started to scream. Her skin sank as the moisture was ripped from her tissue, eliminating her in a flash as an illusion of her mummified corpse struck the stone.

Edge was standing with his back to the wall when the pollen broke over him. He didn’t even flinch. Not because he wasn’t afraid of the potent attack, but because he couldn’t move a muscle. He was rooted to the spot—an ethereal ghost half-phased out of this plane of existence.

At the same time, three humanoid forms went soaring into the air, escaping the wide-area attack with inches to spare. The ash clone went sailing up and out of harm’s way, holding Mel in its arms in a princess carry. The third flying form was the mud sentinel, which the stone clone had flung with incredible force. The rocky construct didn’t have any moisture to steal and strolled through the pollen without a care in the world.

None of this would have been possible before Edge’s evolution. However, the extra forty Amplification from Overdrive provided a massive boost to the sentinels’ attributes. Warlord’s Mantle and Fear the Seasons’ passive aspect had been bolstered too, turbocharging the clones’ stats to give Ash Lilly the strength she needed to fly while bearing Mel’s weight.

This was the first time anyone had seen him use Tri-State Sentinel, including Mel. He had declined to inform her of the constructs’ visages. In part because he was embarrassed, but mostly because he couldn’t wait to see the expression on her face when she realized what was happening.

It might not have been the best idea to surprise her under the circumstances, but the payoff was spectacular. When the crowd noticed the clones had assumed the forms of three beautiful women, including Melenia herself, they went wild. People were cheering, laughing, and wolf-whistling like crazy. Edge could hear Jumo cackling from the stands as tears ran down his face.

Melenia was floored when she caught sight of the mud version of herself—eyes wide with astonishment. Then she started to laugh too. Edge ordered the Mud Mel to harass the summoner’s duo, asked the ash clone to circle the fighting floor, and sent his awareness into his stone construct.

After an abrupt lurching sensation, his consciousness snapped into the stone clone’s body. He could tell right away that the sentinel was considerably faster than before, with comparable upgrades across the board. This skill kicks ass.

He tossed the modified glaive to the mud sentinel, which had fallen after slipping through Foebinder’s coil, then used Soulbound to teleport Opulence into his hands. Edge had been curious about what would happen to the chains of oblivion when he activated Sentinel, since he hadn’t thought to test their interaction the day before. Because they counted as extensions of his body, the chains had become ethereal too.

He still had four and a half minutes left on Overdrive’s clock and nearly five on Sentinel. It wasn’t much time in the grand scheme of things, but it felt like forever during a fight like this one. A hundred-foot range let him maneuver the constructs across a fair fraction of the arena, and his opponents had no idea the limitation existed.

Belver had run out of mana using back-to-back teleports. He called out before the cloud caught him. “Break a few bones for me too…” his words cut off when the pollen engulfed him, mummifying him within seconds.

That left four contenders standing in the ring, or five, if you counted Foebinder. The summoner and plant-manipulator were standing with their backs against the wall, watching in astonishment as Stone Sasha and Mud Mel charged through their army of shadows—glaives lashing out to rend the constructs asunder. Ash Lilly was conducting strafing runs with Melenia, who had convinced the sentinel to hold her by the ankles, letting her launch Force Cleaves from above.

The summoner was burning through the remainder of his reserves, manifesting dozens of ink-black beasts while dodging Mel’s attacks. His partner was out of mana after playing her trump card, forced to battle with spear and shield since her spells were out of play. The pollen dispersed a few seconds later, drying out the mud clone as it faded away.

Meanwhile, Edge carved his way through the center of the fighting floor. The army of shadows converged on his position in a midnight fist, but he kept right on chopping, shrugging off countless fangs and claws that failed to penetrate his rocky exterior.

He bisected two jobos with a horizontal slash, slaughtered a trio of wolves, and tore a pair of hunting cats to shreds without slowing his pace. He felt invincible—stronger, faster, and tougher than ever before. The summoner’s jaw hit the floor as he turned to run, ordering his humanoid shadow to slow Edge down.

The elite construct was a worthy opponent, taking a full minute to dispatch using every trick Beelo had taught him. Meanwhile, Mel pursued the plant specialist from the sky, firing Force Cleaves until her reserves ran dry. At that point, she asked Edge to drop her, landing lightly on her toes to conclude the chase in person.

He ordered Ash Lilly to retrieve the custom polearm, backstabbing the shadow man and pinning it in place while Edge annihilated it with Quadra Slash. He had less than a minute on Overdrive’s duration, but the summoner was running on fumes. With two clones working in concert, the gladiator couldn’t get away. Edge thrust Opulence through the man’s heart as Extinction claimed his partner’s head, bringing the Duo’s Clash to a decisive end.

He canceled Tri-State Sentinel and popped back into his body, grinning as Melenia flung her arm around his shoulders, preventing the spectators from noticing the drawback of his ultimate ability.

Edge’s vision dimmed as the exhaustion took hold, but nothing could diminish the exaltation he felt as the crowd screamed his name. Mel tightened her grip, supporting his weight and holding him close. The fugue state would last for a full five minutes going forward, but he didn’t feel as helpless as before, although using his skills was beyond him.

He shared a grin with the wonderful woman beside him as the magic preventing the announcer’s commentary from reaching his ears faded away.
