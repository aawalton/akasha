A flying, bat-like, overgrown imp shot needle-like darts at Azure as she dodged, most of them pinking off, though a few embedded and stayed stuck just inside the surface of her crystalline skin, with little spiderwebbed cracks made here and there. They stung like hell.

Stupid fucks. Just enough to be a danger, it seems.

The giant imp cackled in glee at her reaction — that was until it was suddenly chomped by the soaring black wyvern form of Deathsting, who literally chewed it to death with crunchy sounds before spitting it out as a bloody mess, sent spiralling toward the ground below.

“A fitting death for a worthless mongrel!” Deathsting declared, incensed as usual by battle with one of the host of things he despised and wanted to kill. These demons definitely fit the bill.

Actually, most everything fits the bill, doesn’t it?

Azure was flying independently, and not at all as fast or maneuverable as her specialty flight spell, which she preferred using, that she couldn’t use in the antimagic field, like most of her favorite battle options.

Damn you, Glae. This is just wrong.

Glancing down at Samantha, Azure saw the glaring light-form of the goddess picking apart an entire group of melee combatants with her conjured matter and gesturing as she used raw telekinesis. Though her expression was impossible to see, by the energy and aura of her motions, and through their perpetual link, Azure could tell her wife was enjoying herself. She was both happy and a bit jealous of that.

“Ow!”

She felt her back pelted painfully by needles, and she spun around to see a smirking imp very pleased with itself.

“You fucking little shit!” Azure cried, quickly pulling a potion out, popping the top, and hurling it at the imp with a snarl.

It dodged the potion easily, soon cackling with taunting glee.

Azure just glared and waited, floating away fast.

The leaking contents of the potion seemed to disappear like fumes. The imp appeared to sniff the air once-

The falling potion exploded like a little firecracker. White fire ignited suddenly in a huge, irregular cloud as the aerosol that propagated went up. When the fire disappeared in a few moments, the imp was a smoking black carcass falling from the sky.

Thanks for the help, Tashome.

They’d prepared for these sorts of situations, so had options that relied on minor alchemical enchantments and mostly raw physical contrivances, rather than emulating spells, which were generally more versatile and powerful. It was something, at least.

Floating Resemblants layered her with heals, making the needles pop out and giving her a wash of relief. They added more wound negations, though these had been overall weak for this battle. By reports all around, every single demon present, from the imps to the bigguns, stripped wound negations on the first attack, and so usually got you on the follow-up. Some also ignored them entirely, but these were rarer, usually coming from weapon-wielders, which everyone had quickly learned to be especially wary of.

She caught sight of a big, reptilian demon swooping for her, its wings looking rather nasty and metal-like, in what she suspected were blades that could sever someone in two. Whether that was feasible in her case was something she didn’t want to test.

Sending a mental command to Deathsting, she floated all-out in his direction, as the speedy demon raced to hit her.

Shit. Might not make it.

An Angel tried to zap the flyer with a laser and missed. Another hit it with an AoE instead, making it disappear briefly in a flash of light. But it was still coming when the flash ended, just smoking and angry for the effort.

Did it just speed up?! Gee, thanks, my ‘guardian angels.’

Azure internally debated on using some of her more precious ability reserves. Deciding against it, she pulled out two divinely-enchanted physical daggers and hurled them at the demon.

Registering it as a threat, it wheeled and swerved smoothly to avoid them, perhaps at least slowing it down somewhat.

But still, it was coming down to the wire, and Deathsting was not quite in line.

Shit-shit-shit, he’s on me!

The demon took a position to sweep a wing into or ‘through’ her, and indeed, it was a sharp metallic blade. Something about its swiftness left her convinced she’d fail if she attempted to dodge, though she might mitigate the wound to some degree.

Deathsting shifted and thrust his own wing toward her just as the blade of the demon’s wing was aiming at her neck. She reached out and grabbed the hook of a wing, and Deathsting rapidly collapsed down into a spear…

Azure swung the spear down in a smashing parry, knocking the wing away and causing the creature to tumble. In the follow-up, she was too far away too quickly, so she threw the spear, guessing the timing and where the tumbling figure would be. A tough thing, but Azure just happened to be an ace at positional guesses.

In its total disorientation, the demon failed to see the spear in time, and Deathsting didn’t need to adjust, as the spear pierced and skewered its body.

As if this wasn’t bad enough, Deathsting transformed from here, ripping the demon apart in a spray of gore.

That’s a bit surprising. He doesn’t manage that very often. It’s some sort of contest he won, basically slaying outright.

“Deceptively fragile, weren’t you?!” Deathsting taunted the ripped apart corpse as it fell.

Azure passed on the information to the gestalt, noting that particular type of demon was strong against energy but not so much against physical attacks.

The flyers continued to be more limited in general, and control of the skies was kept, as everyone picked off imps and other types of demons, gradually figuring out how to overcome different types. Occasionally, Azure was so idle that she swooped down and tossed things ground-side to help out.

Meanwhile, the inner core around the ritual and its conductor primarily had to deal with smaller, sneakier, and faster type demons, as well as those who tried to toss things at Glae through the gaps. The defenders did their job admirably, as seen from the smattering of corpses lying around. There was a singular failure, represented by an arrow sticking right through Glae’s arm, oozing blood. He appeared not to notice it, and the ritual continued, antimagic energy pouring up, down, and out in all directions.

To Azure’s senses, very weird shit was happening globally. Proper thought could only form into a crude analogy for it at best. It was like the fingers of a hand slowly forming, and then wrapping around the world, or reality itself, grabbing something existing, something ancient wrapped around it, and pulling at it. It amused Azure that she could relate this to cleaning cobwebs off a forgotten orb.

Spring cleaning! Hmm. He’s doing more than he said, though he didn’t outright lie. Eh. Whatever.

Azure intermittently watched Samantha help pick apart a larger, super-tough demon, fought off by a dozen fighters and clerics harrying it and avoiding its unwieldy, two-handed maul-like weapon, though the end appeared to be a fiery red eye that was constantly, grotesquely looking around, seemingly in curiosity, even as it smashed into the ground and made craters.

Fortunately, they taunted and infuriated the giant demon enough to take its interest away from the central ritual stones, where, by now, it would’ve probably toppled them all if they were fighting there.

She squinted down when she realized it was something small and annoying that was making it very mad. Then she laughed when she realized what, and then who, it was.

Of course.

A small ‘cherub’ angel — a concept she only knew about after Samantha, because it was some weird Earth thing, and more specifically, from a certain Servitor who occasionally used the form as a jestery joke that annoyed Samantha. She tended to warn him that he better not get close, ‘or that punchable face will attain its promise.’

It really does have a punchable face, doesn’t it?

It was Dart, whom she hadn’t even known was present — but that was pretty much just like him. In any case, he was driving the beast mad with his antics. Though the chaos had injured people, it would’ve been worse if more directed. The demon was a terror that even Samantha had to whittle down.

Finally, a bar of voided white space — the third Spellfyr use — broke through its resistances, angled upward from low, to eclipse its head and avoid any dangerous friendly fire. When the white bar was gone, a fried-clean skull was left behind, and it fell to its knees, and then, with one brief sway, it collapsed to the ground with a resounding thud. A cheer went out as Samantha thrust up her fists.

The eye on the end of the massive maul suddenly popped off and went sliding over the ground at tremendous speed, a gross line of viscera trailing behind it like a tail. It headed not for the ritual, but for a portal, disappearing through it.

At this point, all the portals winked out, and a new one bloomed high in the air. It grew, and grew, and grew to a massive size.

An ominous feeling of gravitas descended, like the day had lost some of its luster, the light some of its shine. Samantha, still in her light form, legitimately dimmed.

A colossal being of black-gray flesh squeezed itself through the portal, floating in the air. I had a head with three eyes on one side, two on another, with a mouth splitting down the middle the wrong way. Massive horns curled up from each side of the head, and its wings were nothing more than burning, smoking tatters and spines. Nonetheless, they slowly flapped. It had four arms and two legs hanging down, all taloned.

As it entered the world, it lifted a hand and popped its sixth eye back in its socket with a loud shlorping sound.

One of the minotaurs whimpered at the sight.

“Babe,” Azure called down in the eerie silence, “I think I’m going to need your help in the sky for this one.”
