Chapter 117: Bygones

After the sim chamber dueling was finished, they all broke their separate ways. Jack grabbed a snack and rested briefly before paying for a private ‘sim grove’ to practice the various things he needed to get sharp on before the match. He’d technically gotten plenty of accuracy practice from remote camera feeds, but it definitely wasn’t where he wanted it to be.

Training in isolation did not greatly alleviate his frustration — a reality he simply had to accept, as it wouldn’t be as good as having eyes. Nothing could make that feed as fast or comprehensive as the naked three-dimensional perceptions of a Non. Vastly better than nothing, though. The information was absolutely invaluable for prediction. He practiced a little more, working on the dual-feed data source of Fieldsense and camera capture, trying to use the latter as supplemental rather than the other way around.

He practiced making really fine wire, with as much strength as he could spare. Using this sort of stuff would make it easier to hit targets remotely. Hedge his bets.

It wasn’t as if he could hardcore focus on one person to begin with, when Defiant was lurking around. Everything he did remotely had to be at least two at once. Namely, Chime and Délight. Hiccup was a wily and chaotic target he’d sooner leave for after the other two. Any given ‘perfect’ attack on her could whiff if she reacted to it in time. Way too much of a gamble compared to the other two.

Délight was a sitting duck, especially. No recourse for defense powers-wise, and not terribly exceptional dodging, either. At least according to the sim predictions, Defiant would protect her first, and that made sense, but if Chime was in trouble enough to draw Defiant, it opened up hitting Délight. It was like pop-a-mole.

We still don’t know the extent of what she can do, either. An unknown equation that would warp everything.

He found himself more or less day-dreaming a dozen times while practicing with the wire. Erratic progress. He could make it easily enough. It didn’t work quite as well for the ‘breaking limbs’ tactic he’d taken on as a counter to Defiant’s bullshit super-reaction protection. He seemed especially good at getting his fingers underneath things that went around someone’s neck, at the last second. Right at the front, where the pressure was most needed.

Augur said he figured it had something to do with lethality improving his ability to counter. Regardless, Jack either needed to move faster or keep going for the ankles.

If he even needed to. Perhaps he could do the blade thing. He’d gotten lucky exactly twice in the sims, sneaking a blade inside a suit. It wasn’t easy. It had to be worth a shot, right? What if-

Jack sighed, realizing he’d drifted in discipline again. His brain was fried.

And tomorrow is another day.

He gave it up and went home, quickly showering and passing out in bed.

Inner Energy has improved to 1.45!

Jack woke up pretty fresh and got an early start on his typical routine, so he could hit up practicing things for the match again with a fresh body and mind. Inkblot said he was going to set up the sim grove to have a reasonable simulation of their starting drill so they could be practiced up leading right up to the match. They’d do it after lunch.

Highfive had a dream that recalled vague memories of a credible story, something about loud siren alarms being an annoyance and disruptor to Chime, especially if it varied the noises in new patterns one after the other. ‘It exists,’ he said. How the hell a dream was reliable, how he knew this… Jack just shrugged that all off and went with it. It couldn’t hurt, right? Augur said it would likely work at least once if he wasn’t used to it by now.

Bo dug into it and found there was an existing system design already, though it wasn’t specifically for countering Chime, instead being some never-used long-range warning system invented by an unknown student. Four-hundred different siren patterns in a row. How was there even that many? Regardless, she got something put together very quickly. They got two noisemakers, one to place on the roof, another for inside.

Recalling Stitcher’s temporary ‘pain mod,’ Jack did the pinch thing to bump it up to the level one version for a while, which was a nagging, persistent pain aching generally at all times, then shooting through the body more as one did physical activity. It was good training for operating when suffering from a minor injury. Mostly, he tested it out on that particular day — after thirty minutes of significant mental drain from the extra effort, he shut it off and took a brief break, knowing he was better off staying sharp and not overdoing it on the day of the match.

Jack ran the gamut of variety training, including creating and entangling with wire, small blades going for the back of the knee or the femoral triangle, branching from spherical bullets, and dealing in general with a simulation, similar to Defiant, that was constantly zipping around and countering attacks. It was essentially all about multitasking, which wasn’t easy when working remotely and with small amounts of mass.

He had a few adjustments to the initial setup. For one, he’d put a larger collection of spheres on the roof to drop down when stealth was over with, or to launch, if necessary. It shouldn’t be a secret that there would be metal up there. According to Augur and the simulations, their enemies were unlikely to try the roof. When they did, they failed really badly, because Jack could remotely keep them off it. Grappling hooks? Forget it. They’d have to be true speedsters to get up those sheer walls before he cut a line, and the instant they put themselves in a precarious and vulnerable climbing position, he punished them severely. Being inside the walls and not mobile was asking for Ferronautian pain.

He also wanted to cram in a little more saturation of raw materials inside the fortress. They’d fought often in there, and the constant seven-minute drilling and improved efficiency from each role had opened up some extra time. ‘What else can I do, chief?’ He always had an answer to that, and after his demonstrations, the team was motivated and eager to set him up as much as possible for the remote fortress defense they were increasingly confident in.

After an early, quick lunch, they jumped into their final drills with a nice simulated fortress that Inkblot had set up. Other than being made of glowing blue, hardened energy, it was a full copy.

They brought all their gear and did a couple of false starts to get accustomed to everyone’s changes, then dove into it. The first try was over the time, but they got it under seven on the second try, and it was smooth sailing from there.

Highfive’s primary work addition, and the biggest reason for their initial try’s lateness, was a separate insulated alcove one room over from the soundproof main area, for Inblot. Whenever they dropped the room wall to spill out, the enemy would be expecting an invisible Inkblot somewhere. They might as well not have him crammed in their space so he could flank more efficiently from the onset, and come from less predictable angles. Jack just had to multitask opening it along with the main room, but it was essentially a closet inside another room, so he could do it sometime before without necessarily exposing Inkblot much.

All too soon, it was time to call it for the final break. They’d next be meeting up at the location of Arena Chamber Bravo to begin the match and put all their hard work to the test. Augur, Ira, and any others allowed would be watching from an observation deck. There would be another for the brass who attended. Whatever the results, news would spread from this particular match-up.

Let’s give ‘em a show, then.

The area chambers were less of a crack-in-the-wall to get to than the sim chamber they’d been using. Jack figured that was due to the fact that bigwigs of the brass would sometimes go there to watch. It used different elevators, larger and more frequented, which went directly to the floor needed. Despite this, special access was tied to that elevator. No one who wasn’t authorized could utilize it.

He met his team at a designated meet-up spot on the general floor of the place, so they could present a united front and go in together, which was the prevailing custom.

Jack was mostly geared up, his armor on to save time, and looking rather conspicuous, but at least he’d been able to have all the bulky gear delivered to their exclusive area. This was tactically essential, for that matter — if Chime saw them and used his sound tricks, he might uncover details that could be used against them, such as the cameras or soundmakers. Thus, they brought no incriminating evidence on their persons, technically owned or not. That included the customized faceplates, whether they were actually useful or not. Instead, they’d brought normal ones as ‘cover.’

The others were geared up similarly to Jack, aside from Inkblot, who wore a simple uniform he’d shed at some point in their exclusive area.

Their entrance was a set of huge double doors that were currently wide-open, which technically opened into the ‘side’ of the area they walked into. It was something like the lobby to a fancy, but small, old hotel, if it had absolutely no seating. Everything was paneled in ornate, polished wood, in between white wallpaper sections with bronze-gold imprints on them, of the mushhushshu in various action positions. There was a central desk with an alcove behind, manned by a prototypical, attractive Memorial clerk. Did they hire only attractive people or something?

There were multiple routes from the lobby, including two sets of stairs and many doors. The actual central door was closed, and more ornate than the others, with a larger, circular symbol of the winged sun of Babylon, in gold. Jack figured it was the VIP entrance.

The enemy team was already there, loitering some distance from the front desk. There really wasn’t much choice with the way the space was conspired.

Despite this, as they were walking up, Highfive painted on a humorless grin and eyed Délight. Highfive leaned his head slightly toward Jack and said, “Ain't no surprise when a viper is lying in wait, eh, bro?”

Délight met Highfive’s gaze evenly. “I’m a snack compared to you, tall drink, but I’m trying to be bigger. Don’t shrink on me now, hmm?”

Highfive frowned at this, unsure how to reply.

Bo replied acerbically, “Don’t pretend this is anything more than some bullshit psyche tactic, hobag. You’re trying to make the boys hesitate.” She scowled sourly, perhaps pretending it was something that had gotten under her skin.

The group had all stopped in front of each other, instinctively making two rows that faced one another off. By chance or design, Jack faced Defiant, who was glaring at him with his arms crossed. Jack grinned and wagged his eyebrows.

Délight had little visual response to Bo’s words, but still met her eyes. “I don’t think they will, nor do I want them to. I’ve already more than proven my talents for manipulation, Bo. The goal that saw me add Defiant to our roster isn’t to prove subtlety.”

Highfive remained a skeptic. “So, you expect us to believe that you want to beat us fair and square all of a sudden? Get real. We’re not buying it.”

“When charm is your bread-and-butter, I suppose all shapes of victory remain jagged and unclean.”

“That’s true for all Linewalkers,” Inkblot said conversationally.

Délight glanced at him. “Is it?” She turned her gaze toward Jack, eyeing him up and down appreciatively. “I beg to differ, I think. This one is as pure as the freshly driven snow.” Her words sounded authentic, the tone both accusative and admiring at once, the final word saturated with wonder.

Hiccup barked a sharp laugh. “Wow. Some things never change, at least.”

Damn you to seven hells, Inkblot. You just had to say that!

Highfive put on a somewhat overdone, cruel grin as he upnodded toward Defiant. “Yo, you like that, pipsqueak? She’s into him. Are you into your girl eyeing another man like a steak dinner?”

Defiant eyed Highfive like a piece of shit on his shoe. “She’s not my girl, you mental infant.”

“Yet you’re with her, aren’t you?”

“So what? I don’t own her and she damn sure doesn’t own me.”

“So, you’re basically a cuckold! You get that, right? Glad I could help clear it up for you.”

Defiant breathed in and out deeply, glaring at Highfive with authentic disdain. “Your name should be High-imbecile. You take the word to startling new heights.”

Jack tried not to cringe. He knew Highfive was deliberately trying to be annoying, but that was pretty awful. Then again, Defiant’s insult wasn’t exactly kind.

Délight raised both her hands. “Can we please drop this subject? I’ve only heard it like a thousand times. We’ll trade terms for what I am; no one will agree, and all will be left unsatisfied. I tried to be normal, okay? It didn’t work.”

Bo scoffed loudly, not showing an ounce of mercy. “Didn’t work? That’s what you call cheating on people over and over? Real rich. You’re just a straight-up trash human being.”

“Yes.” Délight gave the word heavy, long emphasis. She took a breath and shrugged, deliberately looking away from Bo and Highfive. “Now that that’s cleared up…”

“It doesn’t even matter what we talk about,” Jack said evenly, as all eyes turned to him. “This scenario, even this specific place, is simply orchestrated to make us trash-talk and peacock at each other.”

Based on the looks he got, it was the ultimate killjoy, ‘obvious thing you don’t say,’ sort of observation. It was shrugged off.

Except by Délight. She smiled at him and said softly, “See? The purest boy, who comes to my rescue.”

Chime groaned. “At least he isn’t wearing that shitty cologne. Guess it was a liability considering you could smell it in Africa.”

“More like his team banned him from wearing it to save their own noses,” Hiccup added.

Bo opened her mouth, but was suddenly interrupted by an entrance that had them all turning. The nearby VIP entrance door swung open, and two uniformed Nons walked out, pausing briefly as they turned to each other, voiceless but nonetheless with the air of communication. One of them was Stitcher, looking pretty much as she always did.

The other figure was a barrel-chested man in a mask made of silver, wearing his blue longcoat over his shoulders with a white-gold chain holding it there. He was also walking with a black cane that had some sort of complex white-gold top part Jack couldn’t make out, hidden as it was by a thick, black-gloved hand.

Something in his aura and presence made the gut of a military brat like Jack suck in, his back go straighter, and his throat go a bit dry. Well. He’d heard of some unnamed Non general in a mask. Whispers and rumors surrounded him. Was this him? It had to be. That was exciting in and of itself — there weren’t confirmed sightings that he knew of. He hadn’t even thought about that particular military ghost in years, so he hadn’t thought to ask his comrades.

There was something else to the man, too. Something Jack couldn’t quite put a finger on, but made him want to suddenly channel his Memorite to increase his sensitivity. Even from a distance…

The imposing figure had only paused with Stitcher before continuing on without further delay. Stitcher, meanwhile, waited by the door with her arms behind her back, apparently thinking about something — or pretending to.

Everyone instinctively moved out of the man’s path, which was right down the middle of the walkway they were standing in. In addition, everyone instinctually saluted as he passed. Even without a clue who he was, it was obvious he was some big shot. The seriousness of the moment could not be lost. He’d come to watch them, after all. Why else would he be there?

As he came closer, Jack managed to catch sight of the cane top: a white tiger curling up and then down, fangs bared, pouncing. It was breathtakingly lifelike in its craftsmanship.

The closer the man came, the more intimidating his presence grew, like too much mass casting a shadow. It was like Jupiter passing; like an orbital cannon looming. Unlike Stitcher’s presence to him, this was something far more in tune — a closer wavelength of gravity. Jack held his breath, muscles tight, as he tried not to stare.

He completely failed. His eyes just wanted to know more, and he couldn’t stop them.

Though the eyes in the mask were strongly cloaked, Jack saw their glint just as the man was passing by. Those eyes had flashed to his just as the gravity well of power was at its strongest, and so they met.

The coldest of unyielding steel piercing into his soul, a withheld infinitude of violence, a thousand-yard stare multiplied to a million.

Ringing in his ears. His own heart beat in slow-motion.

Q-Loth stirred. Just a little.

The chains of order compacted into a machine exoskeleton. Supremacy of discipline. Mankind’s terror caught in a fist-

Flash!

As soon as it happened, it was over, that overpowering gaze sliding away. Feeling like an insect that got lucky not to get stepped on, Jack struggled not to lose his composure. Not to react, to gasp; not to stumble or back away. Not to let his teeth chatter. Maybe take a seat? No. He held himself rigid like it was his only goal in life.

The ringing gradually faded.

Everyone was silently staring after the man all the way to his walk up the stairs; the only sound was the soft click of his cane, in a steady rhythm.
