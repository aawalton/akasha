Checking how much qi was left, Ferris considered what he could make.

While building the fence, he’d thought up an idea for how to get the Grow seed planted up high. The fence posts were hammered into the ground, with a loop on the end for a chain. Ferris wondered if he could make climbing anchors and hammer them into the side of the dome’s wall.

Ideally, it would be something he could hammer in deep enough to hold his weight, have a hook that he could attach a safety rope to, and somehow be light enough that he could carry a bunch while climbing up a sheer wall.

First, Ferris qi formed a smaller hammer. The handle had a hook he could tie onto his belt. Then, he made what he thought would work as a climbing piton. He’d seen similar designs for sale before, plus he had some experience climbing from his training growing up. It ended up looking like a tent stake with a hole on one side, and he made three of them to test out.

After that, Ferris made a hook and rope that he could tie around his waist to—hopefully—catch himself if he slipped. He could anchor the hook on each new piton that he placed as he climbed.

Interestingly, the bit of safety rope felt different than his qi rope. He suspected this was due to the intent he was using, but this rope cost a lot less qi than the one he used to move the oath statue. Ferris really wanted to experiment more, but he really couldn’t justify the qi. At least, not yet.

At that point, Ferris forced himself to stop crafting. The Qi Pool had seen heavy use that day. He’d made the qi rope to move the oath statue, the fence, and now the climbing gear. It had also been shrinking the last couple of days as he gave the stump of the Core Tree extra qi in addition to the normal watering for the two ability seeds in their pots. He hadn’t been able to harvest the Digest River due to the experiment with Jade’s memory.

At this point, the Qi Pool was down to less than two days’ collection from both the Air Passage and Digest River.

Ferris took his new climbing gear to the slimespace. The Digest River was still slightly flooded, draining at the slower rate he was used to from excessive feeding. He found a good location far on the other side of the dome, between two of the pristine spots where damage would appear, and tested climbing up the wall.

The wall of the dome was mostly vertical. He could see that it curved inwards higher up, but at the base, it went straight up. He was able to easily hammer the piton into the translucent, glowing green material that the wall was made of. When he put his weight on it vertically, the anchor held fine. Unfortunately, the piton failed to stay in place. With the slightest horizontal tug, it slid out of the wall. This design was a failure, especially as he got higher up and started to deal with the dome’s inward curve.

Ferris returned the gear to the soulspace, and then went to check on the Digest River. The area was back to normal, with no signs of the flooding left.

Walking the length of the river, Ferris saw that the clams were still full of qi. It was still strange to him that so many ants could die, and be replenished, without any trackable cost in qi production. There had to be something missing, but at this point, Ferris didn’t think he would figure it out until he could explore underground.

After looking around, Ferris hadn’t noticed anything out of place. He returned to the soulspace to begin his nightly routine of chores. It was liberating to finally harvest the Digest River again, like an itch he hadn’t been able to scratch for three days.

The next morning, Ferris ran out of straw to feed Jade. There were only two days he could work to earn merit points before his next meeting with Serena, and he was at ten of the twelve points needed, so he decided to wait to purchase more animal feed until afterward.

Ferris had grown used to refining spirit gems, but he was grateful Jade was able to rejoin him. He was now extra cautious of any remnant monsters, planning on having Jade stay back until he better understood the threat, but none attacked him that day.

After work, Ferris visited the shops in the second district, looking at their selection of climbing gear. He found a set of pitons that were shaped like screws, with spiraling teeth. They would be harder to hammer in, but he hoped they would hold better than his tent stake design.

If Ferris had an ability to move items into his soulspace, he could’ve just purchased and used them. Unfortunately, he’d have to make his own with qi forming based on the design.

Back at Lily House, Ferris was interested to hear how Brix’s first day of delving had gone, but she wasn’t present for dinner. Cyrus appeared to be worried about her as well, and kept looking to see if she would show up. Compared to the party yesterday, it was a muted affair.

Jade still loved dinner, of course. The slime picked up on some of the mood from Ferris over the bond, but had trouble understanding it. Ferris liked to imagine Jade would have cared about Brix as well, but the slime’s memory problems got in the way.

After the meal, in the soulspace, Ferris set about recreating the spiraled screw piton. He tried to copy the design from the shop as close as possible, and then made three to go test.

Hammering them into the side of the wall was surprisingly easy. They twisted into the wall with each hit. Ferris worried that the translucent green material was too soft, but it appeared to stay together.

The spiral piton held much better than the previous version. After he hammered it in, he gave it a sharp tug. The teeth dug in, locking it in place. He could get it out by pushing the piton back in before unscrewing it, but otherwise it held solidly against his weight.

Back in his soulspace, he made a dozen more pitons. After some thought, he also made a second hook and rope to give himself two anchors on the wall, and then left to try them out.

After a bit of climbing, the fifteen pitons let him get a fair distance up the side of the wall, although nowhere near high enough to plant the Grow seed above the dangerous area. Ferris left the pitons in the wall, planning on adding more once he could afford the spare qi. Until Ferris was able to buy more bulk food, the Digest River wouldn’t generate nearly as much qi for him, after all.

While performing his nightly chores, Ferris had a couple of pleasant surprises. The stump of the Core Tree had sprouted new leaves. Ferris had been giving it double buckets of qi since the scarabs had eaten the tree, and this was the first sign it was recovering. Additionally, the cracks in the oath statue were visibly smaller than before. The combination of new growth and repair gave Ferris a feeling that things were looking up. The statue was in his own image after all, so seeing himself healing was cathartic.

The next day passed as slowly as usual. Ferris refined four more spirit gems without any issues. There was another sandy yellow gem, which Ferris made Jade stay up on the walls while he refined. Thankfully, it didn’t contain any of the scarab monsters.

At the end of the day, Ferris couldn’t help growing excited. He finally had all twelve merit points needed for Acid Resistance. It was a bit too late to purchase it that night, but he could go first thing tomorrow. He planned to study it in the morning, then meet with Serena in the afternoon for assistance.

Actually acquiring the ability would take more merit points and need his Core Tree to recover enough to produce fruit, but he was still happy to take a step forward.

Back at Lily House that night, Brix appeared for dinner.

“I queued up at the Delver’s Hall, and got picked up after waiting about half an hour,” she said, both narrating her first delve and shoving food in her mouth at the same time. She even kept most of it inside.

“It was a Red Tower run, like expected,” she said with a shrug. Ferris remembered she had wanted something else for the resources it could give her, Volcano or something.

“The group was led by a clan member with a bow. I was the only first timer in the group. Not sure how long everyone else had been delving, but they appeared used to it.”

“Was it Devin Noonsong?” Cyrus asked. “He was the group leader my first time, and he used a bow.”

“Yep,” Brix said. “That’s the guy.”

“I wonder if he takes on new delvers often, or if it’s a coincidence,” Cyrus said. “Did he give you the same hour-long safety lecture?”

“Yes, it took forever to just state the obvious. Don’t go off on your own. Don’t touch something you don’t understand. Don’t fall asleep. On and on and on.”

“Well, look at it this way,” Karl said, “you only had to listen to the things that have killed people.”

Brix stopped at that and gave a hesitant nod, acknowledging the point.

Ferris, who had the dubious pleasure of repeating a safety lecture for Jade every morning, could see the value in such things.

“Well, when we finally got inside the rift, he took things slow,” Brix said, waving an arm. “After we jumped into the gate, we were in a field of grass. There was a red tower up on a nearby hill, not that we got anywhere close to it. We fought packs of monsters below it all day.”

“Sounds similar to my first run, and the one I did last week,” Cyrus said.

“It’s frustrating. We walked into the gate, I filled my glass bottles, and then I had to immediately meditate to reset my attack ability. Then we fight two packs of monsters, and half the rest of the team needs to meditate. We could’ve killed three times as many if we didn’t need to stop so much.”

Ferris had heard that many abilities needed to be set up in the soul each time they were used. Making them usable multiple times was one of the first improvements delvers tried to make. Ferris was getting quicker at meditating, but waiting fifteen to thirty minutes between battles sounded like a pain. And dangerous.

“Ha,” Cyrus said, “that matches my experience. But you must admit, the benefits in your soul are worth it.”

“Yes,” Brix looked up with a smile, “I could definitely get used to that.”

Ferris remembered the bounty of qi chunks he’d found in his Air Passage after traveling through a rift. The air itself had more qi in it—especially after a monster died nearby—and qi gathering technique benefited greatly.

“How did the glass bottles work?” Ferris asked. He had been excited about the idea.

Brix took a bit longer to answer than normal, and she lost her smile.

“Cyrus was right, there really is nothing like seeing a monster charging you.” She shuddered a bit. “I forgot to light the fire on my first bottle. Even if it had been lit, the throw missed completely. The group leader, Devin, nailed the monster in the eye after I missed my throw, and then the fight was over.”

The group was quiet for a bit, everyone apparently lost in thought.

“Well,” Brix said, regaining some of her normal cheer, or at least acting like it, “the second fight went better, as well as the third. We won’t talk about the fourth, but I think I got the hang of it by the fifth.”

Cyrus and Brix got into a discussion of the types of monsters they had seen, and what it would be like once they actually got to enter the red tower on Red Tower runs. Karl had little to add, eating silently.

That night, Ferris did his normal chores in the soulspace, but there was much less to do with the oath statue now back at his base and the Digest River not fully producing. The sprouted stump of the Core Tree was visibly growing, with more new shoots since yesterday, and the oath statue had fewer cracks.

He fell asleep dreaming about a red tower.
