Jericho let me lean on his shoulder as he led the two stallions by the reigns, while Calliope (and Nuzzle) rode Allium to guard our rear. We made it to the moat and onto the bridge without incident.

"Is that normal?" I asked. "Having two Unchained sightings near the wall?"

"Back in my day…" Jericho started with a wink, "Unchained never wandered this close to the cities. They do seem to have a nose for hunting down Reapers, but the cities were always a safe haven. These days they seem to stalk the edges, almost as if they know Reapers will be traveling to and from."

"They can't possibly be that intelligent, right?"

"I wouldn't have said so a few months ago, but attacks near the walls and in outlying villages are increasing drastically every day."

We crossed the bridge, the horse's hooves clomping on the cobblestones beside us. Ahead another group of Ambers was heading toward us with their scythes summoned and ready.

They must be coming out to fight the Unchained.

As we approached the gate into the city, the others passed through the threshold from the opposite direction and… disappeared.

"Someone wanna tell me what the heck just happened?"

"Ah, yes. You've probably never noticed this before." Jericho pointed up to the gate opening. "The entryway here is actually a portal to the city. There are four additional portal locations out in the Meadow. Each leads to Meadowvale."

"So those Reapers were exiting at a different location than us?"

"Precisely! The portal to exit will always send you back to the same location you entered."

"It works kind of like the lanterns, then?"

Jericho nodded. "And the taverns."

I hadn't realized the taverns worked that way, but it made sense now that I thought about it. That's how the inside appeared so much bigger than the little shack on the outside. And how The Meridian had so many other Reapers besides the Ambers that roamed the Meadows. It was only a portal.

As I examined the gateway closer, I noticed a slight shimmer to the air around it.

Nuzzle scurried up my leg from behind and plopped onto my shoulder. Calliope rode up alongside us.

"No sign of the Sin. It must have gone a different direction."

"I'll talk to the city guard," Jericho said. "Get the low-down on how things have been going lately. See if I can get some sort of reimbursement on the wagon."

We passed through the portal into Meadowvale. The sensation of passing through a sheet of water flowed over me, except I wasn't wet.

"Ugh," Calliope groaned, hopping down from Allium's saddle. "Does that mean I have to take flower boy shopping?"

"I can take myself shopping, thank you very much."

"Perfect!" Calliope shoved a few coins into my hand. "I expect to be paid back, with interest, of course. Just remember to get a new shirt." She tugged on the giant hole through the center. "I'm getting sick of seeing your five measly chest hairs all the time."

Says the girl who wears nothing but a sports bra.

"Hey, I don't have five chest hairs."

"Is that a brag?" she asked.

Why did chest hair have to be such a sign of masculinity? Of course, Derek had been graced with a full chest of hair, not too much or too little. Probably the exact amount Calliope would have liked. Not that I cared what she would like.

Somehow, even though Derek's parents were nothing special to look at, he'd gotten the best of everything. Meanwhile, I'd never been 'traditionally attractive,' as a bratty girl once told me in high school. I looked down at myself. Well, I was better looking in the Umbral Plane at least.

Luckily, Avery didn't care so much about appearance. It was honestly a miracle she agreed to marry me.

I caught myself in my own thoughts. It was odd how little my life in Vitria crossed my mind while I was here. A pang of guilt weaved through my gut. It's because there's so much new here. I've been either fighting for my life or learning things for the first time ever since I arrived. No other reason. But maybe it'd be easier to partition them than I thought. I didn't know if that was what I wanted though.

It'll be Miles' life that Zephyr fights for.

I had to keep reminding myself of that. Not lose sight of my purpose.

"Bye, flower boy!" Calliope called as she headed off down an alleyway with Allium.

I looked just in time to see Nuzzle waving to me, perched atop Allium's butt, as they disappeared around a corner. I hadn't even noticed the little devil leave my shoulder. Such an indecisive little bastard.

"Don't mind her," Jericho said. "This is just what she does. I'll take you shopping after I speak with Captain Regilas."

"It's fine. I really can take care of myself, believe it or not. And besides we're safe inside the walls now, right? I'll go replace this shirt and relinquish the Will."

"Lovely! Market's down that center road a ways. Feel free to wander though, it does the soul good. I'll meet you outside the colosseum in a few hours. You'll know it when you see it," Jericho said. "But first. He popped an item from his inventory and handed it to me.

A can of orange soda..?

I eyed it suspiciously, then took it.

"It'll heal you right up. Rare drop, but I'm a kind and generous mentor." He bowed.

I popped open the can. It really was just like a soda from the real world… Took a swig and watched my Health skyrocket back up. The splinters in my hands and arms slid out and fell to the cobblestones. The torn flesh of my leg knitted itself back up, leaving hardly a mark.

Then the can dissolved into pieces and drifted away.

I went to thank Jericho, but he was already headed off in another direction, his two skeletal stallions trailing behind.

Alone again.

Not that I minded. Despite the bustle of the city, it was nice to have a few moments of peace without either of them making some wise-crack or me feeling stupid because they neglected to tell me something important. At least here I could just observe and take it all in.

I strolled onward in the direction Jericho indicated, truly relaxing for probably the first time since I arrived. Even Nuzzle wasn't here to interrupt my thoughts with little chirps or licks.

Both Reapers and Penumbrans walked the streets in droves like any real world city, but with much less urgency. No one had their scythes summoned, which was comforting. They simply strolled and browsed shops, chatting and laughing merrily. People here were… happy. I couldn't help but smile as I walked.

The main streets were wide enough for two wagons to traverse side-by-side, though I only noticed one or two. Other roads were shorter, more like alleys leading to buildings that were probably homes rather than shops or services.

For some reason I expected the city to have smaller buildings around the outskirts that gradually got taller and taller toward the center, culminating in some epic tower. But this wasn't like that. The buildings were varying heights and shapes, but—at least in my immediate vicinity—they all appeared to be two or three floors, constructed from a combination of wood and stone. The same dappled gray and brown stone as the wall. There were a couple taller ones in the distance, steeples, watch towers, and the like.

Eventually, the buildings ended, the street opening up into a wide area. In the distance, behind several more rows of buildings, rose a cylindrical stadium, adorned with countless arches. It looked almost exactly like the Roman colosseum would've in its heyday. I'd visited Rome with Avery during college and we'd both been fascinated by the structure. I couldn't wait to get a closer look.

Before me, stretched a large bazaar. Market stalls lined the area in neat rows, some manned by Reapers, but most by Penumbrans. I joined the throng of customers, enjoying the sounds of muffled conversation and venders advertising their wares. Most sold clothing or crafted nick-knacks, but there was also food and drink, even some armorers and weaponsmiths.

I closed my good eye and observed the ocean of people with my Second Sight. The world darkened, like beneath a cloud, but little pearls of sunlit color blossomed in everyones' chest. The vast majority were Amber or purple—I couldn't remember what Calliope had called that rank, but was pretty sure it came after Amber. There were a few higher colors here and there, but not nearly as many.

I was about to open my eye again, when I suddenly realized half the people didn't have Wills. The Penumbrans, the non-Reapers, like Judith at the tavern were Will-less. I'd never thought to look at her with my Second Sight, just expecting she'd be the same as me minus the weapon and cloak.

But I was wrong. Why is that?

Judith had said she was no longer connected to Vitria. Maybe that had something to do with it? Or perhaps that was why they were no longer connected to Vitria. I filed that nugget of interesting information away for later and joined the sea of people in the market.

I browsed the market for a bit. Grabbed a bite to eat. Found a large board with postings for different jobs the Penumbrans were requesting. Most were gather quests, looking for raw materials for whatever trade they practiced. I made note of that for later.

Eventually, I spotted a clothing stall that looked promising.

Avery or my mother typically bought all my clothes and I felt woefully unprepared to select something that would define my first impression with people in a new world.

The items here were all clearly crafted from natural materials. The weave of the fabric was very evident, as well as the imperfection of the dye—more bold in some areas than others.

There was a sign nailed to a post beside the wooden counter. Just like the one at the Meridian tavern it said 'Shepherd's Not Welcome.' I'd noticed it on several other stalls as well.

The man behind the counter finished with another customer and came over to me beaming a large smile beneath a long, curled mustache. "Looking for a shirt, I see," he said, glancing down at the burned hole in my attire. "I've got just the thing."

He came back from the rack of hangars behind him with a shirt that looked exactly the same as my current one—minus the hole.

"Do you have anything with a little more personality?" I asked.

The merchant looked me up and down, his eyes resting on my bluish-colored hair. Still couldn't get used to that. "Hmm," he said. "Yes, I should have something that will do."

He returned with a similar charcoal gray shirt, but this one had a zigzag design down the sleeves in interlaced teal and yellow thread. An emblem was stitched into the front center like a graphic t-shirt, but using the same thread as the sleeves. It looked to be some sort of stylized symbol for water, or air maybe.

"Yeah, sure."

I handed the merchant three of the coins Calliope gave me having no idea if I'd just been ripped off or not.

Well, it's not my money.

The next stall over featured stacks and stacks of books. They have books here! That was good to know. Once I didn't have to rely on Jericho and Calliope for money, I'd have to get a few.

I picked up a leather-bound novel with a ridiculous cover depicting a man in heart boxers and a cat wearing a crown and shooting lasers from her eyes. It was one of those fluffy, flat-faced cats that look like they need to be sitting on the lap of a Bond villain.

"Who the hell comes up with this stuff?" I wondered aloud.
