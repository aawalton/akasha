Cyria was already moving.

She straightened from her cough, wiped the back of one hand across her mouth, and her fingers came up — and Aiven saw the ring.

It was on her index finger, gold, set with a deep red jewel he had never seen her wear before. As she traced a quick figure in the air, two more spectral beasts unfolded around her, larger than the cat had been, longer through the shoulders. Tigers. Translucent, shimmering at the edges, their tails lashing behind them like banners caught in a slow wind.

And there, next to the gold ring, was a second one. Smaller. A pale green stone.

Cyria twitched the green-ringed finger, just slightly.

A swirl of pale green light wound around the tigers. They surged forward at twice the speed they had been moving a heartbeat before, the grass under their paws barely registering the weight.

Daran did not move.

He raised both forearms and crossed them in front of his face, and fire crawled up from his elbows and engulfed them. Then he whipped them outward in a sharp sweeping motion, and the flame leapt off him in a forward-flung sheet — a projectile of fire the width of a wagon, racing across the field at the tigers.

The fire dissipated a pace from them.

Aiven saw it. The swirl of green around the tigers had broken the flame apart in mid-air.

Wind magic. The second ring. Wind layered over the summoning, dispersing fire before it could land.

He filed it.

The tigers closed in.

Aiven charged the Armvil. The cyan etchings flared white along the obsidian, and he angled the palm vent toward Daran.

Daran summoned another pillar of flame around himself. The tigers tore through it. As their jaws came down toward him, Daran swung one armored arm in a flat brutal arc and slammed it across both of them at once, and they shattered into drifting light.

Aiven fired.

The mana bolt streaked across the field and struck Daran full in the face.

Daran's head snapped back. He tumbled.

Aiven was already moving. He hit the grass at a sprint, locked his focus on the soil just behind Daran's heels, and twisted. The ground beneath one of Daran's boots churned sideways. His balance went off.

As Daran staggered to catch himself, Aiven fired again — a clean, wrist-thick beam of pure mana, the kind that had cut through stone in the training fields.

The beam hit.

It did not go through.

It struck Daran's chest and skidded off him at an angle, the impact rocking him sideways and sending him stumbling another three paces along the field before he caught himself. He stamped one boot into the ground and held.

Aiven was already in his face.

The Armvil came up. The fist landed.

CRACK.

The obsidian took Daran across the jaw and dropped him. He hit the grass on his back, and for a half-second he simply lay there.

Cyria was already rushing forward.

Then Daran's whole body lit.

Fire engulfed him head to toe, a roaring sheath of flame around the prone figure, and Aiven leapt backwards on instinct, the heat of it slapping his face. Cyria pulled up short.

Daran pushed himself to his feet.

He stood there inside the fire, unhurried, his red hair lifting in the heat-shimmer, his green eyes finding Aiven through the flame.

"You must be Aiven Roan," he said.

His voice was exactly what Aiven had imagined it would be. Low, ground-down, the words coming out the way millstones turn.

"I have heard of you. It was..." He tilted his head a fraction. "Surprising. To see you have managed to learn so much in only a few days."

Aiven said nothing.

His mind was working. The mana beam should have cut. He had punched through stone columns in training with that beam. Daran had simply deflected off it, the way a thrown stone deflects off a turtle's shell. Either there was a defensive enchantment laid on him before the fight, or one of his articats was doing the work, or, most worryingly, his durability was simply what he was.

Daran rolled his shoulders inside the fire.

"I do not have time for this," he said. "I must return to the Fold quickly. Master's orders."

The Fold.

"...So do I," Aiven whispered.

His chest tightened. Daran had a way back. An artifact, a spell, something. Whatever it was, it was on him, and it was the road to Virelle.

"Aiven."

Cyria's voice, low at his shoulder. He turned his head a fraction toward her without taking his eyes off Daran.

"I can get through his skin," she said. "Just immobilize him for me."

Daran raised both hands above his head.

A fireball bloomed between them, swelling fast, the size of a barrel within a heartbeat. He brought his hands down and hurled it.

Aiven kicked off the ground and flew. Cyria sprang sideways. The fireball roared past between them and kept going, kept going, and struck the line of trees at the far edge of the clearing in a crashing eruption of orange. The trees caught, and it started a forest fire.

Cyria was going to have to deal with that later.

In flight, banking hard, Aiven watched her circle below; distracting Daran with a fresh mix of summons now, tigers and wolves and one great long-bodied lynx, all of them wreathed in the same green wind-haze. Daran's burning arms swept through them one by one, and they unmade themselves against him.

Aiven focused on the soil behind Daran's heels.

The earth answered.

A spear of compressed dirt erupted out of the ground at Daran's back, angled forward, sharpened to a point.

It shattered against his shoulder.

Aiven's jaw tightened. The man's defenses were the best thing about him by some distance.

Gravity, he thought. If he had been able to learn Lucien's gravity in those six days — if he had even one corner of it — he could have crushed Daran flat to the field and ended this. He did not have it. He had wind, lightning, earth, and a single Armvil that could land but could not finish.

The beasts were destroyed. Daran's arms reignited.

And that was the other problem.

The fire was not just an attack. It was a barrier. Cyria could shred a fireball with wind, but wind was defensive — it broke incoming flame. It did not put out a man who was continuously burning. Daran's flames functioned both ways. To get close enough to land a real strike, you had to walk through fire to do it, and the fire did not stop just because you had broken its forward edge.

Cyria was, at this point, only dodging.

Aiven banked tighter and ran the inventory in his head. Lightning. Bolts. Beams. Earth pillars. Mana net. Wind shove. The flight he was currently maintaining.

None of it stopped fire.

Then he thought of the Sunken Fane.

The three-headed lion.

Virelle, hands raised, encasing the mythic-class beast in a clean lavender dome, sealing it away from the air.

He could not replicate it. Not at her scale. Not at her precision.

It did not have to be perfect.

He looked down.

Cyria, mid-dodge, sensed him. Her gaze flicked up to his. He nodded once.

She smiled.

She raised both hands. Another wave of beasts unfolded around her — tigers, wolves, a pair of long-tailed cats — and the green wind-haze coiled around them in a single fluid pass.

"Pointless," Daran said. His voice carried across the field. "Seems like summoning these little critters is all you have, Cyria Amberfang. You are useless in a fight."

"We'll see about that," Cyria murmured.

She charged.

Her claws ignited as she ran — actually ignited, a clean white-gold glow at her fingertips.

Daran fired another fireball.

Cyria did not break stride. She slid beneath it, low to the ground, and as she rose she whipped her tail.

Her tail was wrong.

A long, faceted crystalline structure had grown around it, sheathing the fur in a translucent shell that caught the firelight and refracted it in sharp planes. Yet another artifact Aiven had never seen.

The tail struck.

Daran absorbed it across the chest and rocked back two paces.

There.

Aiven loosed the lightning.

ZRRT.

The white-blue bolt cracked across the gap and struck Daran square between the shoulders. His back arched. His arms locked. For a heartbeat — perhaps two — he could not move.

Aiven seized the moment.

He shaped the dome in his head.

Not Virelle's dome. Not anything close to it. Just a sphere, taller than Daran was tall, broad enough to enclose his stance, dense enough at the skin to hold for at least a few seconds. He did not have her precision. He could not engineer it down to the breath.

He could deny it oxygen. He could close it tight.

Aiven's flight wobbled. Holding the shape and the altitude at once dragged at him — the focus splitting, the air beneath him losing its will to hold him up. He dropped a foot. Two. He gritted his teeth and pulled the dome closed anyway.

A lavender barrier bloomed into being around Daran.

Tight. Sealed. Curved cleanly along its inner surface. Just large enough to cage him and no larger.

The fire around Daran did not go out.

It died down.

It guttered against the inside of the dome, the flames shrinking, starving, the column of heat thinning as the air inside was used up faster than anything could feed it. Daran's coat began to smoke. His shoulders heaved once, twice — the deep dragging breaths of a man whose lungs were finding less than they expected.

It was the most Aiven could do.

The shield was already beginning to flex. He could feel the strain of it down through his arm, into his chest, into the part of him that was trying to fly and seal a dome at the same time and was running out of either.

He did not need it to hold for long.

Cyria was already at the dome.

Her claws still glowed. As she reached it, the green wind-haze whipped around her hand and forearm, coiling tight, sharpening to a fine cutting edge along her fingertips.

Daran's eyes met hers through the lavender.

He rushed forward inside the dome and slammed both fists against the inner wall.

The barrier shattered.

Cyria was already in his face before the lavender had finished scattering.

She slashed.

The wind-edged claws crossed Daran's throat in a single clean motion.

Blood.

The fires went out.

Daran's hands flew up to his neck. His knees hit the ground first. His mouth opened on a sound that did not finish forming, and blood came up between his fingers in a slow, dark pulse.

Cyria did not pause.

She put one knee on his chest and bore him down to the grass. From a small pouch at her belt she produced a slim band — bracelet-thin, dark metal, marked along its length with tight script — and she snapped it around his right wrist in a single practiced motion.

The script lit. Faintly. Settling.

"No more spells for you, Daran," Cyria murmured.

She held him there for one long breath.

The grass under his head darkened.

Aiven let the dome go fully. The last of the lavender drifted apart and vanished. He set himself down on the field, knees soft, the obsidian arm hanging at his side. His lungs hurt. His head hurt. The fire at the treeline was crackling steadily behind them, eating into the canopy.

He looked down at Daran.

Daran's green eyes were wide but unfocused. The blood was still coming, but slower.

Aiven did not feel triumph. He did not feel much of anything yet. There would be time later, perhaps, to put a name on what this had cost.

For now, there was only one thing.

He raised his head.

"Cyria."

She lifted her head from where she still knelt.

Aiven could not quite make his voice carry. The words came out lower than he had meant them to, scraped down to something close to a breath.

"I need to get to the Fold," he said. "Virelle's there."

Cyria looked at him for a long moment.

Then she nodded once.
