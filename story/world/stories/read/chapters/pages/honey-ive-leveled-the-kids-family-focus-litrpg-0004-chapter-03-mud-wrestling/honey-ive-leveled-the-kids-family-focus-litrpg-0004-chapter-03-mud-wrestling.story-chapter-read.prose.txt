The slow part of the game was out of the way. Or at least that’s what Naiad’s dad said when they finally got geared up. The stroll to the mountains was peaceful. Only a single NPC paused them long enough to offer a quest to Triangle. He was a magnet for random quests approaching him. Naiad still looked for her class quest while their dad found his. Triangle just walked around and the NPC came up to talk to him. The NPCs must sense the confidence rolling off him.

“Do you want to do this quest?” Grandma asked him.

He shook his head, having to adjust the hat once he stopped. “I really want to ride a cart. Not gather items.”

“Well then onward we go!” Grandma declared, hammer pointing to the mountains.

“For Shrimp Guild!” Naiad cheered.

“For Shrimp Guild!” Triangle echoed.

Grandma rolled her eyes. “I’ll never understand that hobby.”

“They’re cute and look like Skittles,” Triangle repeated their dad’s words.

They talked about the various shrimp with Grandma, more than she ever cared to know. Naiad noticed her agitation, the same jaw clenching that Mom did. Naiad attempted to prevent Triangle from annoying Grandma, but he persisted in talking excessively about crustaceans, eventually comparing them to lizards, which he deemed superior. After failing to stop him twice, Naiad avoided the conversation and studied their surroundings. The number of NPCs approaching Triangle had slowed. The NPC levels in this part of town were between seven and eighteen. Naiad was only three. They were in a higher-level zone. The buildings were shorter and stockier here. A few buildings suffered from wood rot or broken bricks, looking ready to topple over, all while an NPC stood in the entrance, searching. There were more alleyways with an exorbitant number of shadows and creepy noises as people, NPC and players alike, ran around them.

Her quiver held only thirty arrows, hopefully enough to last whatever fight they went to. The practice they had done with the rats helped her level up, but Triangle couldn't fight yet, so he remained at level two. Grandma was only level one. They looked like easy pickings to any tough mob around the area.

“There’s an entrance!” Triangle declared, as they were at the foot of the mountains.

Unlike the rest of the city, this area did not have any walls. The natural terrain acted as a defense against any other nations. Patrols of guards marched nearby, making sure no creature wandered into the Fanamel. This close to mountains where minor monsters were a threat, the style of buildings favored boarded-up windows and covered blinds. The lack of NPC children playing in the street also made Naiad feel unease.

Triangle only giggled as he ran to the closest cave in the foothills of the mountain. Grandma picked him up and bounded over the rocks to get there faster. If Naiad shouted, it could draw the attention of level five mobs in the area.

“Then we can eat blueberry pie at home!” Grandma shouted so loudly it echoed off the mountain and back down.

“Weeeee!” Triangle joined in.

They would’ve eaten the pie, but Triangle was desperate to get into the game after dinner. The crust alone tempted Naiad to log off now and go eat it.

[Grandma: Do you want to play or walk all day? We’re going to need an archer soon with all this ruckus.]

Naiad pulled her bow out and sprinted around the large rocks that Grandma bounded across on. Her boots sucked into a few mud puddles as she climbed. The old woman made her way past the sign declaring “MINE HAFT CLO ED”, with all the S scratched out.

Pieces of the barricade lay at the entrance, burned to ash. There could be a salamander or a drake hiding in the cave. They were not rats. This zone had higher-level mobs that Grandma was not ready for. The level-one player even entered the cave without a torch.

“Stop!” Naiad shouted in unison with someone else. But she didn’t recognize the scraggly voice.

As Naiad ran into the cave, she pulled out an arrow and aimed at the mob, exactly where her map had a red dot.

The green-skinned creature looked like Dad did when the cat made a loud noise in the night. It was completely unprepared and covered by a loincloth and a bathrobe, with deep dark rings under its wide yellow eyes. Goblins had outie belly buttons, something she never needed to know. What she wanted to know was what product it used to spike its red mohawk. Her bangs constantly got in her eyes, like now when she tried reading the monster’s properties.

It was hard to see in this area of the cryptid cave, but the focus was obvious. The goblin was the source of all light and smells, making him an easy target. A torch fell on the ground beneath its feet. Strapped to the goblin's bony hands was a metal egg shape device with a giant beer cap button on top. In the other, it pointed out a cylinder bottle with a hissing sound similar to her hairspray bottle. The smell was close to it, too. The entrance to the shaft descended further into the mountain, creating mud puddles close to the entrance.

Naiad pulled an arrow back and aimed down and around Grandma. It was a hard shot to aim with how tall Grandma made her character.

“Woah. Calm down!” Grandma had her empty hands up, her new hammer covered in mud, and was a step away from the goblin tinker. Triangle cowered behind her. “Tell us what’s going on?” Grandma asked the goblin.

The mob was red on Naiad’s map. Instructions from Dad were clear: If red, shoot it before it alerts others. If she shot it now, she could loot it and its possible hairspray bottle.

A scream came from down the cave. Multiple soon echoed in with it. This whole cave was a trap of out-leveled goblin mobs. A swarm would be here soon.

“Tell me, what is down the tunnel?” Grandma pointed past the goblin.

“They shall be saved!” The goblin shrieked and clicked the button, and Naiad released her arrow.

The monster went up in a blazing glory, like Dad’s grill when he put too much lighter fluid on it. Dad and the goblin had a similar scream with all the fire happening.

Grandma dived into the goblin as flames covered the creature. The arrow slammed into her shoulder instead. It took a third of her health, and the momentum caused them to fall. Both of them tumbled down the tunnel, splashing mud up as they went.

“Grandma!” Triangle and Naiad panicked.

Triangle remained where he was, as Naiad squeezed past him on the path and chased after her. She summoned one of her two healing potions, knowing that Grandma had no healing potions. Grandma and the goblin slammed to a stop against a wall. Because of the fiery goblin, Grandma's health plummeted to eighteen percent and continued to decline even though the health bar remained green. Mud caked on them like a hippo near a river, slowing down the immolation.

Naiad yanked them apart, burning her hand, and tossed the bottle at Grandma. “Drink it before you go unconscious!”

The next part was dealing with the mob before the swarm showed up. Naiad pulled out her knife, took a breath, and charged at the monster.

Grandma yanked her arm back. Which answered where her remaining two stat points went. Grandma was stronger than Naiad

“Stop! Don’t hurt him. It needs help!” Grandma explained. She took Naiad’s health potion and offered it to the goblin.

“What are you doing? You have less health than it!” Naiad panicked.

Grandma paused and noticed her health bar. “Do you have another? Tristan, get down here and get mud on him until it stops burning.”

A mud fight was a past favorite of Tristan’s. He grabbed globs of it and tossed it at the monster.

Naiad answered the question. “I do, but can you answer my question?”

“Don’t hurt it,” Grandma calmly explained through a chug of health potion. “Gently lob the mud.”

Grandma cackled, “I knew it. A backstory.”
