Chapter 46

December 2011

Cold rain fell heavily over Ikebukuro that night.

Mai, disguised with a dark wig and dressed in a pilot jacket, walked through the streets without hurry. She was dressed slightly differently: a black crop top, leather skirt with fishnet stockings, her makeup making her much paler, with dark masks around her eyes and black lipstick. The sound of her boots on the wet pavement mixed with the city sounds. It was as if the entire world tried to catch her attention, but her eyes were focused solely on her task at hand.

[Everything okay, Mai-san?] asked a voice in her earpiece.

"I'm waiting for the message."

[In position. We await the signal.]

Over the next years, after what had happened in the north, Mai had decided to focus on work. The void left by Miho’s death years earlier in her role as priestess had been quickly filled by two other priestesses, but the legacy could not be easily replaced. Tokyo was a city that forgave neither time nor weakness, and Mai knew it well. Miho had been a pivotal figure in the occult circle, with powers that were truly necessary.

Mai, on the other hand, did not possess Miho’s spiritual capacity but had learned to navigate the city’s murky waters with what she did have: a great talent for finding the lost and taking action when situations demanded.

The other priestesses handled the more spiritual aspects—arcane investigations, cases where mystical powers were necessary to solve complex problems.

But Mai took care of everything else: infiltration, rescuing feys trapped in trafficking networks, recovering items from the black market, hunting those who preferred to operate in the shadows of the city with the help of the Inoue Section or the police.

These were the assignments she handled in her own way. Without magic, relying solely on her cunning and physical prowess, she did not need to invoke great powers or rituals beyond occasionally using some artifacts.

The black market networks in the hidden world were vast in Japan and extended across Asia, and Mai, in some way, had become linked with them.

Her name was whispered in the mouths of those who operated in the shadows. For that reason, she had learned to disguise and camouflage herself within the networks. Some saw her as an ally, like the Inoue Section, others as an obstacle to eliminate, but all agreed on one thing: Mai was someone who could get things done when others dared not. And although her approach was less spiritual, more direct, there was no way to ignore her.

After weeks following the battle in the north, Mai encountered news reports worldwide discussing the unification of Korea. It left her feeling surprised. It had not been her intention, but unbeknownst to the world, that secret battle had shifted the geopolitical landscape of an entire region. Yes, returning to Tokyo and maintaining a low profile, focusing only on problems she could now handle locally, had been a good idea.

And while she had done it out of duty—not just for Miho but also to stop the cult and free the prisoners—it was not as if a mercenary’s pay was enough. Now she had responsibilities, and even as a fey, she had to manage bills and continue working to maintain what Miho had left her. The money Miho had stored had been sent to her, but it was not something that would last forever. That was another reason why some feys around the world avoided integrating into society: taxes.

Fucking taxes.

Thanks to some contacts, she managed to activate a massive barrier around the house, reinforcing the one Miho had left. It had cost a fortune, but at least anyone with ill intentions would not find the house, even if it were right in front of them. In that regard, magic could be quite useful.

Becoming a government worker would have given her stability without risking fieldwork, but she had grown accustomed to this life. The near-total destruction of the cult had revealed clues that, upon returning to Tokyo, she communicated to Section Inoue. Between assignments, she had been investigating on her own.

And in that way, four years had passed.

Four years had been enough. And that night would be another of the many where she was on a mission again with police support and the Inoue section.

That night she was working, and as she walked among the flickering lights and illuminated shop windows, Mai thought about the last job she had completed.

The fey trafficking network she had infiltrated months earlier, using other identities, turned out to be much more extensive than she had thought. She had raided one of the secret auction houses where they sold fey flesh, bones, and essences. She had rescued several, and although some could not be saved in time, at least she had freed those who were still alive.

Yet, one piece of the puzzle remained unresolved: the leader of that network. She knew he was close, but he was not the type of man who could be easily caught—he moved across Japan. He had operated for years in the shadows, traversing the darkest corners of the hidden world. This person had also been one of those supplying captured feys to the north. Now, with the cult exposed and its members scattered around the world, other organizations were attempting to fill the vacuum.

A sound interrupted her thoughts. A soft beep from her phone.

She pulled the small phone from her pocket, and upon seeing the message, she couldn’t help but let a grimace form on her face. It was the final invitation, complete with a location. Someone who knew the major players in the hidden world of the occult had passed her contact to “that person” weeks ago. And she had been following the anonymous messages ever since.

And today was the day.

She didn’t like blind meetings, but this was one of those occasions she couldn’t skip.

With a sigh, Mai pocketed the phone and began walking steadily toward the designated location. The street was quiet, with few people around. The rain had eased slightly, but the air remained cold.

When she arrived at the location—a small café on a corner—Mai entered cautiously. The door opened with a soft chime, and the interior was dimly lit by warm lights. There were barely six people inside, their voices blending with low-volume soft music. At a table in the back, a man sitting in a suit and coat waited. She didn’t know him, but he was the one from the message. The red scarf around his neck was the final signal. Upon seeing her enter, he had slipped his hand into his coat and placed an small yellow plastic envelope on the table.

Yes. It was him.

She approached the table cautiously, her gaze fixed on the man. He was tall, with an ordinary face—one anyone could encounter on the street. There was nothing remarkable about him.

"Finally, we meet, Miss Taneshima," said the man quietly, glancing at her from the corner of his eye.

Mai sat without a word, her eyes coldly observing every movement the stranger made. She knew she shouldn’t trust him but sometimes risks were worth taking. One of the waitresses approached, and Mai took the envelope. She only ordered a coffee and waited for the waitress to leave.

"There it is," the man continued, shifting his eyes toward the envelope.

With the package in her hand, Mai swallowed hard. It felt soft.

At that moment, a man entered, flustered from the cold, and approached the waitress behind the counter to speak. The man sitting across from Mai watched the newcomer.

"Can I see it?" Mai asked.

The man turned his gaze to her and gestured with one hand. "Please."

Mai raised an eyebrow, not breaking her gaze. She swallowed and picked up the slightly bulging envelope. Just by holding it, she felt something liquid moving inside, cold to the touch, as if it contained an ice pack.

She looked around and then inside.

She had to stop herself from vomiting. Among the red of the blood was a liver.

The man leaned closer. "It’s fresh—removed just a few days ago and preserved with a special spell to maintain its properties."

Mai silently thanked her makeup at that moment. With her stomach churning, she closed the package and set it on the table. The waitress brought her coffee, and once she walked away, the man spoke again.

"So… are you interested in continuing your business with me? Expanding your work network?"

"Exactly," Mai said. "I supply a small amount, but certain materials are hard to get. There’s great potential to expand my business if I have reliable suppliers."

The man smiled. "I see. As long as the money is there, I can provide all kinds of materials."

"I’ll send the second half of the payment."

The man nodded, and she picked up her phone. As she sent the final signal, the man approached the table once more.

"Same transaction ID?"

Mai nodded. "Yes, it will be the same."

"Too bad."

Mai looked up.

"I thought, since we’re doing business, there could at least be some trust, don’t you think, Miss Taneshima, or it is Kanzaki? I am confused, honestly,"

Mai stared at him, startled, almost catching her breath.

The man across from her smiled with the calm only someone who had already won before the game even began could have.

In her mind, a single thought twisted like a knife entering her guts: her false identity had been exposed. She had used another for this operation, but somehow they had traced the thread back to the other mask.

Kanzaki.

The word pierced her chest like a bullet.

"So it’s true," said the man, in barely a murmur that resonated more than the bar’s background noise. "You’ve been a tough nut to crack, Kanzaki. You’ve given us more headaches than you realize. But I certainly didn’t expect the rumors about a detective fey assassin to be… so beautiful."

Mai brought a hand to her waist and knocked over the coffee cup.

When it hit the ground, it was like a bell announcing chaos.

The man raised a gun from his coat with the casualness of someone lifting a glass.

And almost simultaneously, another shot came from the doorway, and the man jerked his head just before his brains splattered the wall in a splash-like painting. The one who had fired was the newcomer—the supposed late client.

Mai knew—Inoue Section. Support sent to cover the joint operation. The guy had been trailing her all along, just a few meters behind.

The café erupted into violence.

The patrons, who moments before had seemed like mere background figures, sprang from their seats. Each armed, each part of a carefully choreographed sequence. The Section agent took cover near the entrance as the hail of bullets threatened to turn him into a sieve.

Lights flickered, the air filled with smoke, mixing with the aroma of coffee and low music.

Mai struck the man’s arm, trying to disarm him, but failed. She felt a bullet graze her cheek. Another one of the men was shooting at her. She let go of the man and slid behind an overturned table, firing twice—one shot to the leg, another to the face. For the record, she had tried for the ear, but the guy moved too fast.

Screams from the street, shattering glass.

The dry sound of a body hitting the floor. Another accomplice went down.

And then, something else.

The two waitresses turned toward her. Their faces began to warp as if their skin were wax in the fire. Their mouths opened beyond human limits, revealing rows of sharp, irregular, overlapping teeth.

They were feys. But Mai had no idea what kind.

“Nice costume…” Mai whispered, facing them while reloading her gun.

One of them lunged at her with inhuman speed. Mai twisted her body, fired point-blank, and the creature vanished into the air like torn smoke. A fey vanishing from existence. The other fell from a shot by the Inoue agent, who also went down, hit by a stray burst.

Silence lasted half a second.

Then, the wailing of police sirens.

Several stormed in, blue uniforms, flashlights cutting through the smoke.

Mai barely had time to see her target push a back door and flee inside the building.

The back door, she thought, hoping someone was already there.

She followed, with her pulse racing, and instinct overriding all thought. The man stopped midway and fired a couple of times, but she dodged and pursued him.

Outside, the night smelled of rain and rust. Mai stepped out and was met with a fist to the nose and a crack. A broken nose... that would take a couple of hours to heal.

The alley was narrow, wet, covered in graffiti that seemed to sway in the breeze.

“Still think you can escape your past, Kanzaki? If I could find out who you were, others will too. Just give it time.”

Mai didn’t respond.

The exchange was brief, brutal.

She dodged a blow, fired—missed by inches. The shot grazed her right ear. Mai kicked him into a trash container, then with another strike forced him to drop his gun. She aimed at him, but at that moment, two long arms shot out and tore the weapon from her grip.

“What?!”

A two-meter-long arm loomed over her neck, the shoulder moving toward her, while the other arm that hadn’t released stretched back naturally as its owner’s body approached.

What is he, exactly? Mai thought.

He lunged, pushing her to the ground, and suddenly his hands were at her neck, pressing hard. She kicked him in the groin, but he didn’t flinch. Maybe, in addition to having no scruples, he didn’t have balls either.

The fingers were claws, cold, inhuman.

The man’s face began to melt before her eyes.

His skin changed color, his features stretched and deformed into something… familiar.

It was her own face, her real face, not the one she was using now.

Mai saw herself, panting, reflected in that monster, now smiling with her mouth.

“A shapeshifter…?” she managed to say, more with her gaze than her voice. These had to access genetic material to change. If him killed her here, who knew what he could do with her face? No wonder he had been so hard to capture and approach. He could become anyone.

Something had bothered Mai since she heard the man call her Kanzaki. If he knew it was a trap, why did he go? Could it be that taking her identity was the goal? In the months that followed, agents in various parts of Japan had disappeared. Could it be that this guy killed in this way, using someone's identity for a while to gather information for his investigations and then disappearing?

That could only mean that he now intended to take her identity to infiltrate the Inoue section and find out how far the investigations had progressed.

She had been the target.

Then a shot rang out.

Short. Perfect.

The shapeshifter’s body collapsed onto her, heavy as a wet corpse. She felt the warm blood soaking her along with the rain.

Mai pushed him off, rolling to one side, and the cold air restored her sense of space.

A figure approached from the end of the alley, weapon still smoking.

It was a woman.

Tall, bronze-skinned, dark hair tied in a braid, and amber eyes. She wore a rain cloak with a hood and held a magic pistol in her hand.

“Are you alright?” the woman asked, her voice calm.

Mai raised her gun immediately, her breathing ragged.

The woman lifted a hand. “Miss Izumi, a pleasure. We never met before. My name is Jinat.”

Mai’s eyes widened.

“Do you have a moment to talk?”

The echo of police footsteps drew closer. Flashlights, voices, commands. Mai barely had time to process the name.

Jinat.

That name. She had never seen her in person… until now.

The police entered the alley with weapons raised, eyes shifting from the dead body to Mai and the woman, then back to the body.

“Freeze! Drop your weapon!”

Jinat slowly raised hers and let it fall to the ground.

“I’m just trying to help,” she said, voice steady, yet something in her tone made the officers hesitate for a moment.

“Wait!” Mai said. “She… is with me…”

The officers exchanged confused glances, and Mai looked at Jinat, who remained calm.

“Do you have a moment?” she asked.

Jinat, the new leader of Team Wishper, had contacted her.

Mai had never completely lost touch with the team members, though her relationship with them had always been distant—limited to a few messages and brief meetings since the North Operation.

Jinat, however, had been the key to unlocking the search for answers about what happened in 2006. Mai had never forgotten that the team’s previous leaders had always spoken of her with respect. Jinat had been coordinating operations in India, which was why Mai had never met her. Her presence here was a genuine enigma.

While the police cordoned off the area and explained to civilians that a gang-related altercation had ended in a shooting, Mai stayed to the side of the alley with Jinat, careful not to interfere with the police or the Inoue Section agents gathering evidence. It seemed the café had been a front for illegal occult activities all along—and they had had it right under their noses the entire time. At that moment, the owner who rented the place was being questioned, along with neighboring businesses, to see if they could provide more clues about movements in the area.

The Inoue Section agents moved in and out of the café, carrying briefcases and wearing latex gloves, taking photographs, collecting shell casings, and running scanners over the interior furnishings.

A few meters away, a group of police officers blocked access to the alley with yellow tape as onlookers gathered at the corner.

Mai watched as one of the technicians collected a blood sample from the shapeshifter, whose face was still partially fused—half human, half her own likeness. It was a gruesome sight, but not a new one.

“That was… horrible,” Mai murmured to herself.

Jinat approached after ending a call, holding a plastic folder. Her hood still dripped. Her gaze was calm, almost analytical, as if she had already evaluated the entire scene before arriving.

“Do you smoke, Miss Izumi?” Jinat asked, lighting a cigarette and then offering one to Mai. Mai didn’t usually smoke, and she didn’t like it, but Miho had instilled a habit in her that helped calm the nerves.

“Mai is fine.” Mai took it, and Jinat lit it for her. A mistake. She held back a cough to avoid looking ridiculous. What kind of cigarettes were those? Much stronger than Miho’s. Must be what they call black tobacco. Best not to take it too deep.

“Thanks…” Mai replied, exhaling smoke and watching her out of the corner of her eye. “Thanks for the help, too.”

“You’re welcome. I don’t like wasting bullets,” Jinat said, glancing at the shapeshifter’s body. “This guy was on our radar, but we could never find out who he was, and with good reason. He used multiple identities in Osaka, Yokohama… even Busan. Nobody knew he could replicate entire faces.”

“Now you know,” Mai said. “And you? What were you doing in Tokyo? Don’t tell me you were just passing through.”

Jinat smiled faintly, as if expecting the question. “I came to see you.”

Mai arched an eyebrow. “Me? A warning beforehand would have been better, rather than appearing just as someone were about to kill me.”

“Sorry about that. But as you know, discretion is key in this line of work.”

Mai let out a short laugh. “Maybe...”

Jinat lowered her gaze to the corpse and then looked back at her. “It’s good to finally meet you in person. I’ve heard a lot about you. ‘The Antennae Fey,’ ‘Tokyo’s Moth Detective,’ ‘Moth-Mai.’”

Mai gestured with her hand, she hated that stupid pun someone in Section Inoue had come up with—instead of Mothman, it was Moth-Mai.

“Pick whichever version you like.”

“I like the one that’s still alive,” Jinat replied with a half-smile. “And that’s exactly why I’m here.”

A brief silence settled between them while, behind them, an Inoue technician opened a case with test tubes. Another photographer snapped a picture of the gun on the floor.

“Is this a visit or an offer?” Mai finally asked.

“An offer.” Jinat crossed her arms. “The Wishper team needs capable people. After the Northern incident, the cult disbanded, but its leaders… disappeared. Some believe they’re dead. Others, that they regrouped somewhere in South Asia.”

Mai nodded slowly. “I’ve heard that already.”

“Or it could mean they’re reorganizing,” Jinat said without breaking eye contact. “And not just them. In the past few months, other organizations have emerged. In Istanbul, Marseille, Bangkok. Groups that were dormant are now trying to fill the void. Fey trafficking, artifacts… same business, different flags.”

Mai took another drag and exhaled the smoke to the side. The cigarettes had actually calmed her down, even though she didn't like the taste of tobacco. “I already have enough with the black market here. I don’t need a world tour of problems, not to mention the Dark Events.”

“I know. But that’s exactly why I’m looking for you. We just conduct search, rescue, and infiltration missions. We’re not touching the Dark Events. Other groups are handling that.” Jinat stepped halfway closer, her voice lowered—more serious. “You’re good. Not just in combat, but in adaptability. I saw it in the 2009 reports, in the Falling Stars operation. I was impressed.”

Mai clicked her tongue. “That was a long time ago. And it wasn’t as heroic as it sounds.”

“I don’t care about heroism—I don’t want it on my team,” Jinat replied. “I care about efficiency. And you have that.”

A group of police officers passed by carrying evidence bags. One of them nodded, Mai gave a slight gesture in return. The night already smelled of exhaustion.

“So, what exactly do you want?” Mai finally asked.

“I want you back on the team. Full-time. Wishper needs agents with real experience. Not just theoretical or mystical. People who understand how people move, how the streets works. Not everyone does.”

Mai let out a sigh. “That sounds nice in a report. But I have work here. Tokyo needs me more than you do.”

“Tokyo needs or you need the city?” Jinat asked, calm. Mai stared at her for a moment, not answering. “Don’t get me wrong. I understand you have ties, responsibilities. But for months the government and Section Inoue have been negotiating an agreement with Wishper. If it goes through, part of the Kyoto team working with the Asagiri will relocate here. There will be more agents in Tokyo.”

“Is that official?”

“Not yet, but it will be soon. Three names are already on the transfer list. If you join, you could coordinate local operations when you visit here… but I also want you on bigger missions. Teamwork. Around the world.”

Mai stared at her.

“Yes, I know you’re not good at teamwork and prefer to operate alone. If you accept, you’ll have to learn a few things.”

Mai crushed the cigarette under her boot. “Sounds like a recruitment speech.”

“That’s exactly what it is,” Jinat admitted calmly. “I’ve been watching you for weeks.”

She pulled a small tablet from her coat and turned it on. On the screen were images of Mai coming and going from different locations, including one at the port.

“I wanted to confirm that you’re still the same one I heard rumors about. That you’re still doing the work, even if not much people acknowledges it.”

“And what? You were spying on me?”

“Yes,” Jinat said bluntly. “I don’t like wasting time or talent. And you have both. You could be doing much more than chasing smugglers on your own.”

Mai crossed her arms, taking in the scene around her: the camera flashes, the murmuring cops, the distant sound of an ambulance.

“I don’t know if I’d fit in. Wishper is… structure, protocols, hierarchy… interrogation. I’ve had enough of that.”

“We do what we can—it’s not like we can deal with bastards with candies,” Jinat said with a faint gesture. “We’re few now, and we need people who can think for themselves but also work as a team. I don’t recruit soldiers. I recruit survivors.”

Mai watched her, weighing every word. Jinat was genuine in her tone. Beneath her wig, Mai’s antennas twitched as she assessed her. She was very calm.

“I’ve been there. In your same position, many years ago. That’s why I sought you out. You have the skill, the experience. You’ve seen the worst this system has to offer.” Jinat stared into her eyes. “Don’t stay on a safe island, putting out fires while the whole forest burns.”

“It’s not that simple. Japan needs me. I can do something here.”

“Do you really believe that? Or are you just clinging to what’s familiar?”

“I’m not running away.”

“No, I know. But you’re not moving forward either. You’ve done what you could within a limited framework. I’m not asking you to abandon who you are. I’m asking you to stop settling for comfort. You have a lot of potential, girl.”

“And what guarantees me I won’t end up like them? That I won’t cross that line without realizing it?”

“Nothing. But if you fear becoming a monster so much, it’s because you still have a soul.”Jinat pointed a finger at her chest and smiled. “That too—I want that on my team.”

They stayed locked in gaze for a few seconds until Mai let out a sigh. “I’ll think about it,” she finally said.

“That’s all I ask.” Jinat put the tablet away, stepped back, and pulled up her hood. “You don’t have to decide now. But don’t take too long. The board is moving, and I don’t want to see you alone when the next game starts.”

“What if I don’t like it?”

Jinat just smiled and adjusted her hood. “Wishper isn’t a prison. As our name implies, we move by desire and will. If you’re with us and decide to leave, no one will put up an objection. Just like those two girls who retired—they’re your friends, and they left.”

Mai nodded and gave a faint smile, saying nothing. She wondered where Milena and Sari were at that moment—probably on some lost island in the south.

“Ah!” Jinat added, smiling broadly. “But if you join us and then want to leave, don’t blame us if we try to make you stay.” She extended her hand. Mai accepted it. “Whatever your decision, it’s been an honor to meet you, and thank you for taking care of everyone.”

“Thank you,” Mai said.

Jinat turned and walked toward the alley exit, blending with the glow of the police lights.

Mai watched her go while a technician asked her to sign a witness report. The wind carried the smell of wet asphalt, and a distant thunderclap crossed the sky.

“Wishper… again,” she murmured to herself.

In the distance, Jinat stopped and barely turned her head.

“Don’t think of it as returning,” she said softly. “Think of it as us waiting for you.”

And she left.

Mai stayed staring at the empty street, the lights reflecting in the puddles. For the first time in months, she didn’t know what to think.
