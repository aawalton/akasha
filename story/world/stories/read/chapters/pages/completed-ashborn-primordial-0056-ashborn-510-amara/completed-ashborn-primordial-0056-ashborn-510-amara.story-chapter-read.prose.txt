Maiya groaned. “You have got to be kidding me.”

Random, my foot!

For the opponent facing her was not some random stranger. Not some regular one of the hundred-plus in their batch.

No, it was none other than Princess Mina Hiranya, dressed in far too much gold, who threw Maiya a look of such utter disdain and disregard it made her simultaneously want to laugh… and smash her face in.

Maiya forced herself to take a deep breath. To let go of those feelings.

She’s not the same person, Maiya forced herself to remember. She might be an arrogant prick here, too, but she’s not the same.

Of course, that wasn’t to say that this Mina Hiranya was any better, either. In fact, for all Maiya knew, she could be worse—a true terror she’d be doing the world a favor by ending.

Even so, as much as every fiber of her being wished to slit that chal’s throat, without proof, Maiya couldn’t do anything rash. Even if she did, it wasn’t her place to kill her… or do much of anything to her.

Other than completely and utterly wiping the floor clean with the tears she’d shed after losing so terribly, of course.

Maiya only half-listened as the instructor explained rules she was already familiar with, choosing instead to return the princess’ glare, which only grew more and more intense as the explanation continued.

The princess had dual Water and Lightning Affinities, the same as their universe. That would be annoying to deal with. It was the reason Vir had picked it as his own affinity, after all.

But then, Maiya wasn’t exactly an Ice and Wind mejai anymore, was she?

“Your affinities?” the instructor asked, handing Maiya C Rank Lightning and Water Elemental defense orbs.

“Wind. And Lightning,” Maiya said with a sharp grin, switching her gaze from the proctor to Mina as she spoke.

Was that a flicker of worry in the princess’s eyes? Good.

Lightning mejai were ideally positioned to win bouts such as these. But now that field was even, Maiya had no intention of losing.

“Very well,” the proctor said, handing Mina her Lightning and Wind defense orbs. “As both of you are dual-affinity mejai, this bout will end when either protection orb is fully depleted.”

Both Maiya and Mina nodded after the proctor finished stating the rules, taking up their positions fifteen paces from one another.

Regardless of the outcome, it would be fast. Fights between Lightning mejai always were.

Maiya, however, wasn’t intending to lose.

She surged prana into her Lightning orb an instant before the proctor declared the fight on, shortcutting the start of the fight just a bit. It might have been labeled cheating, but was it really, when her opponent did the same?

Ordinarily, an orb couldn’t endure such a torrent, cracking and splitting under the strain, but Maiya was nothing if not an expert on channeling prana, more so than most mejai. And so, at the very last instant, she restricted the flow, resulting in a pressurized jet of prana that she expertly wove through the inscription, firing her spell with speed that honestly ought to have been impossible.

The prodigy princess, of course, did the same. And she did it a split second sooner.

Maiya had already flung herself to the side in anticipation of that outcome.

People often said that it was impossible to avoid lightning magic, but that was simply untrue. Lightning spells had no secret ability to home in on their foe. If one predicted where their opponent would fire, it was more possible, especially at greater distances. At close ranges like this, it was still doable, albeit a bit more difficult.

And that was exactly what Maiya did.

Her own spell triggered a split second after Mina’s Arc raked the ground where she had just stood. As for why Maiya had taken longer despite being a Prime Mejai at the very pinnacle of magic?

Well, that was obvious. It was no mere C grade Arc spell she was casting after all.

A Lightning Burst easily ten times stronger than Mina’s erupted across the room.

Lightning flashed and thunder cracked, and the last thing Maiya saw before the room went white was Mina’s wide eyes and her expression of dawning shock and horror.

It was a look Maiya would etch into her memory for the rest of her days.

Predictably, Mina’s C Grade protection orb proved entirely insufficient. Not only was it depleted, it cracked in half from the onslaught, leaving Mina screaming in pain as she crumpled to the ground, spasming.

The proctor ran over, cursing under his breath, and announced the match.

“Victor, Maiya! But know that you will be docked for injuring your opponent,” the proctor said, giving her a stern glare.

“Oh, yeah. Oops. My bad,” Maiya said, trying and failing to keep the sarcasm out of her tone. “It’s just hard, you know? Controlling yourself when you’re just that good,” she said with a smirk, watching Mina twitch on the ground.

Well, that was easy, Maiya thought as she walked out of the room, leaving the proctor to tend to the pretentious princess. Who knew? Maybe this loss would finally force her to get her head out of her rear… but who was she kidding? This was Mina, after all.

I wonder what Vir is up to?

Knowing him, he’d have found some devious way of winning his bout even quicker. Even with the handicap of Maiya’s terrible tattoo.

Her face flushed at the thought. That he had to go in crippled to his fight because she couldn’t inscribe the tattoo galled her. Especially since he’d done such a masterful job. On his first attempt, too!

If she’d known learning inscriptions would’ve helped so much, she would have mastered the art long ago!

Maiya let out a long breath and shook her head. There was, of course, no way to know what skills would be useful in advance, and time was a precious thing. She knew she shouldn’t beat herself up over it, and yet, she couldn’t help herself.

Somehow, despite having won so thoroughly, she couldn’t help but feel like she’d failed.

Reaching the stairs, Maiya ascended to the surface, hoping against all hope Vir had trounced his opponent, entirely unaware of the predicament he found himself in.

Vir regarded his assailants like an Ash Wolf might eye a Kin’jal Warrior. Very much prey, but one that could prove annoying to deal with.

Though they had surprised him, this could hardly be called an ambush. In fact, none moved to point their weapons at him, and the woman’s tone seemed almost pleading. Despite not knowing exactly how they’d suddenly appeared, he was certain he could slip away before they activated any of their magics. And of course, he could activate any one of several Ultimate Arts to crush them at any time.

Still, the fact that Prana Vision yielded no information about the woman intrigued him. Only Imperium creations had the ability to block it, and Vir was not aware of any Artifacts that could.

This felt like something else. Like this woman’s fundamental nature was… different, somehow. Between that and her curious choice of word, it was certain there was more to her than met the eye.

“Who are you?” Vir asked the masked woman.

“One who wishes to save this universe,” she said.

Once might have been a coincidence, but twice?

“Interesting choice of words,” Vir said. “You’re the one they call Saint Amara, aren’t you?”

“A name given to me, not one I chose. I am no saint at all,” she said. “Merely a warrior doing what she can.”

Vir found his interest growing by the second. According to the rumor mill, she was supposed to be humanity’s savior.

“People speak well of you,” Vir said

Amara scoffed. “I would not pay heed to the gossip of others. I only hope that through our efforts, this universe might be saved.”

“The way you speak makes it sound like you believe there are other places, not of these realms,” Vir said, dancing around the bush.

“Yes, I am well aware that you are not from around here. That there are forces greater than you or I could possibly imagine, who guide the flow of reality. And so heed me when I say that should you continue to delve into this Vimana, should you tamper with anything related in any way to our plan, you will doom us all. Not just this universe, but yours as well. For I can assure you, you will never leave.”

“Is that a threat?” Vir asked.

But the masked woman shook her head. “Sarvaak of Garga, it is simply the fact of the matter. They will never let you. Make no mistake. We share a common enemy. For both our sakes, please do not interfere.”

Alarm bells rang within Vir’s head. “You know my name,” he said, immediately defensive. “How? Who are you? How could you know these things?”

“I have been exposed to far more than any mortal should ever have to see. Believe me, though it may seem like I know some great secrets, if you had experienced what I had, you would not wish my fate upon anyone.”

The woman spoke in riddles. But it was clear she knew something. In fact, she was the only one he had met in this universe who knew anything at all about the existence of another.

“Why am I unable to see your prana signature?” Vir asked, voicing the question that had been bothering him.

“That is, unfortunately, not for me to explain.”

“And I suppose you were the one, then, who reactivated this Vimana and moved it here?”

Amara nodded.

“Which means you either have a prana key or you can channel Ash Affinity prana yourself.”

The woman said nothing, leaving Vir to continue guessing.

“So then all of this,” Vir said, gesturing to the Vimana around him, “is your plan?”

“Our plan,” she said. “But yes.”

“And am I to assume that you’re in control of the Altani Council?”

The woman laughed bitterly. “There is no council. Not anymore. I am the only one left.”

So she was once part of the council. This grew more and more interesting by the moment.

“How can I help?” he said, cutting to the heart of the matter. “As you say, we share a common enemy. My world’s continued existence relies on my return. However, I wish to help you as much as I can.”

“That’s admirable. Though I’m afraid I cannot let you interfere. Involvement on your part risks bringing their eye upon us.”

Vir wished to counter but bit his lip. He knew that was true. It was why they’d gone to such extreme measures to stay hidden. The insidious nature of their foe was that they were impossible to resist.

There was no great monster to defeat or enemy to thwart. Their plans would simply, inexplicably fail. Most wouldn’t even know there was a foe to begin with at all. It was one of the many reasons the beings who controlled Fate were so terrifying.

“Then let us pass,” Vir muttered. “If you know so much about us, you know we only seek to leave this universe for our own.”

“I’m afraid I cannot allow you to do that either,” Amara said. “Not until our plans have come to fruition.”

“And when might that be?” Vir asked, growing impatient. “We cannot afford to wait half a year. My world dies with every passing moment. Surely you can understand my urgency.”

“I do,” the woman said. “And my heart sympathizes with you. But if you step into that chamber and activate that Gate, as I said, you will have doomed us all.

Even if you manage to step through, you will be torn apart. And whatever hope we have left will be gone.”

“But if your plan succeeds, then we’ll be able to cross?”

Amara nodded. “You have my word. I’ll ensure you get to pass once it’s over.”

“But you won’t tell me what it is. So you expect me to simply wait then? For how long?”

Amara paused. “I cannot say. Please, I beg you—do not interfere. Return to the Academy and do not come back here again.”

Then, without a word, Amara swept her hands across the room.

And just like that, she and everyone else had disappeared, leaving Vir alone.

He gazed up at the top of the Vimana, to where he knew the chamber leading to the other realm to be.

With a heavy heart, he sank into the shadows, returning back to the highest level of the academy.

His scouting mission had gone better than he could have possibly expected. And yet, why did he feel so conflicted inside?

Only after Ekavir departed did Amara dare to breathe, removing her mask and tossing it aside as she stormed into her bedchambers.

“Are you certain this was wise?” the malnourished-looking gray demon sitting atop her bed asked. “Facing him as you did?”

“I cannot know,” Amara replied, sighing. “But what choice do I have? Every future I see ends in disaster. This is the only one in which there is only a sliver of a chance.”

“Still, if he fails to take the bait… If they notice…”

“They haven’t,” Amara said. “Not yet.”

“I suppose you’d know,” the demon said with a dark chuckle.

Amara gave the demon a sad smile. “One of the only perks of being one, I suppose…”
