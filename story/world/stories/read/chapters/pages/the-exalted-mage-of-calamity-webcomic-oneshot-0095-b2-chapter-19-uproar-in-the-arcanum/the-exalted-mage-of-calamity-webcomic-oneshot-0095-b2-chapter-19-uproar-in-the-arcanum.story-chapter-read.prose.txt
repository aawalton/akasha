Across the upper floors of the Arcanum Spire, rumors began to circulate almost immediately. Mages of all ranks and years moved through the halls, while seniors and even a few tower heads hurried between chambers.

“What is happening all of a sudden?” one mage asked, watching several researchers and alchemists rush past.

“You didn’t hear the news?” his friend replied.

He wore gray robes, with a twin-pointed emblem on his chest that marked him as a second-year association mage.

“Hear what?” asked the other, dressed much the same.

“Archmage Ballamus’ sister just woke up. Apparently, they were experimenting with a new cure for her condition, and it worked.”

“Oh?” He looked ahead, watching several tower heads hurry up the steps. “That explains the chaos.” Then he turned back to his friend. “But who discovered the cure?”

“I heard someone say Master Alchemist Kasmeran did.”

“Hm. Makes sense if it was him, then.” The mage frowned slightly. “But I just saw him a minute ago. He didn’t look relieved. If anything, he looked upset.”

“That’s how he always is. Maybe he’s upset about the cost of the materials? The spire did put up quite a significant ingredient request list. You know how much he likes money.”

The man scratched his head. “I guess you’re right.”

♠♠♠

Several containers fell to the floor.

Beakers, bowls, and vials cracked apart, spilling ingredients and liquid concoctions across the stone. A pair of heavy boots stepped over the mess as the fluids pooled together.

A fist slammed onto a workstation.

The impact rattled every glass container that had survived the first sweep. Several measuring crystals rolled across the table, clinking against one another before dropping over the edge and shattering on the floor.

Kasmeran stood over the mess, gritting his teeth.

The door behind him was locked, and the chamber had been completely sealed. Privacy wards glowed along the walls, layered so thickly that not even the tower head could peer inside without tearing half the laboratory apart first. Sound-dampening circles had already been activated. Anti-scrying lines traced the ceiling. Even the ventilation had been closed.

From outside, no one could see him. No one could hear him. Those were the perks of holding the title of Master Alchemist.

“This is impossible,” he hissed.

On the workstation in front of him lay two sets of documents.

The first was his own. Pages upon pages of handwritten notes, reagent combinations, reaction records, failed attempts, and theoretical models. Years of work. Years of refinement. Years of pretending to be concerned while carefully leading Ballamus from one expensive treatment to the next.

The second was a single copied document. A copy of the cure from Ronswick. Just a list of ingredients, with a short explanation of what would happen.

Kasmeran clenched his fist and snatched the page into his hands, reading it once more.

Crushed wyvern claw concentrate.

Sky-ash.

Bitter purple marrow base.

The list went on. And of the twenty ingredients required for the cure, he already knew about half of them. More specifically, his own research had already uncovered half of the ingredients needed. And yet the paper in front of him had the complete recipe.

His fingers tightened around the page.

If the recipe had been completely different, perhaps he could have called it genius. Impressive, thorough, even revolutionary. But it wasn’t different. It was nearly identical. Every single one of his ingredients had been included in the cure, as if all his work had simply been copied over.

“How,” he murmured. Then he clenched his fist and slammed the table. “How?!”

He crossed the laboratory and yanked open a drawer beneath one of the side cabinets. Inside were several bound research journals, each one sealed with his personal insignia. Kasmeran broke the seals one by one, flipping through the pages with increasingly frantic motions.

Extraction method. Failed. Purification catalyst. Failed. Binding agent. Failed.

He dragged his arm across the counter, scattering the journals across the floor.

Four years.

Four years of controlled failures. Four years of carefully measured improvements, each one small enough to seem plausible, but never enough to cure her. Four years of watching Ballamus grow more desperate, more dependent, and more willing to sign away whatever resources Kasmeran requested.

And now?

Now all of it had been taken from him.

“Who?” he whispered.

His mind began moving through names. Through their faces.

Assistants. Reagent handlers. The blooming mages who monitored Lady Frescia’s condition. Perhaps the archival clerks. Could it have been the sweeper?

His thoughts blurred as he tried to remember everyone who had stepped inside his laboratory over the last four years. Anyone who had watched him too closely. Anyone who had asked too many questions.

“Lucian? Thorne? Or was it you, Ballamus?” His voice went quiet.

No. None of that made sense. If it had been any of them, they would have reported him already. It had to be someone else. Someone who had snuck in, gathered his notes, and passed them to that mage from Ronswick.

Kasmeran’s gaze shifted toward the sealed cabinet at the far end of the laboratory.

For several seconds, he only stared at it.

Then he walked over.

He opened one lock. Then another. And another.

Each had a different sigil, requiring a different equation of mana and a different phrase whispered beneath his breath. The cabinet was warded more carefully than anything else in the room. More carefully than his research journals. More carefully than his private reagents.

With the final sigil unlocked, the cabinet opened.

Inside was a narrow black case.

He lifted it out and placed it on the table. His fingers hovered over it for a moment, caught on the question of whether he should open it at all. A few moments later, he did.

Inside was a single vial.

It was mostly empty, save for a small mass of black sludge at the very bottom, less than a single drop of water.

It was the beginning of the past four years.

Having hidden the poison within her food, it had given him a perfect vessel for his experiments. With the nether-grasping disease spreading through the body of an archmage’s sister, he had secured a test subject. More than that, he had secured the dependence of one of the most powerful beings in Vitian.

Every resource had been at his beck and call. Every assistant and mage in the tower had listened to him without question.

And he had worked on the cure with his utmost sincerity.

Because curing an archmage’s sister of an illness that had seemed incurable—it’d bring him prestige. Accolades. Immortality written in records of ink.

Kasmeran’s fingers tightened around the vial.

He could already imagine it.

His name spreading through every spire of the Mage Association. Alchemists studying his formula. Blooming mages citing his work. Nobles begging for private consultations. Archmages who would normally walk past him without a second glance suddenly forced to acknowledge his expertise.

Not as a useful medicinal alchemist. Not as a talented 6th-tier mage who had “done well for himself.” Not someone who simply had the title “Master Alchemist,” but perhaps… something greater. The greatest to be recognized.

He would be untouchable.

And now… it was all ruined.

Kasmeran placed the vial back in the black case and threw it into the trash.

He turned, waving his hand. Every spilled concoction, every fallen ingredient began to move. Vials and bowls lifted, glass resealing back to their original shape. Each one returned to their proper place, as if they had never been broken.

The nether-grasping disease was finished. Even if the cure was not widely distributed, too many people had already seen it work—seen the ingredients—and the recipe. That included Ballamus. Even the royal court knew of their escapades in procuring the treatment.

It no longer held any value to him.

But there was a way to salvage things. To salvage his reputation. To make himself needed once more.

He required something else. Something stronger. Something much older. Something that even the most senior of alchemists would have no record on.

He walked over to another station and opened the lowest drawer of a cabinet. Inside was a thin stone tablet. Its surface had been etched with script so old it could have passed for a child’s drawing in the sand. The text was no longer legible—just stray markings of chaos. Beside it sat several pages of translated fragments, all copied from buried and forgotten archives.

To anyone else, it would have made no sense. But that was on purpose. He had gotten rid of half the translations and memorized them instead. Only he knew what was oeiginally written on the tablet.

“The Hollow Saint,” he whispered quietly.

A man from long eons ago, predating the golden age of the Asvarians. He had cast a plague said to have killed thousands. One so deadly that each person who fell ill to it died within a year. An illness so thorough that the infection only lasted a single month. Within that time, everyone in the ancient city had been infected. And a year later—each one had fallen.

The disease became extinct after only a single year.

And on the tablet was his record of how to replicate that very same disease.

Since Ballamus’ sister was cured, he couldn’t target her again.

No—he needed someone else. Perhaps Ballamus himself or another archmage.

Or maybe… the king.

He had enough aides in the capital. All he needed was the correct timing.

His eyes moved toward the single copied document from Ronswick. The one written by Veronica Everwells.

Before she could rack up more achievements, he needed to move quickly. Because even if he was caught due to some lurking rat, they’d still need him, regardless. No one could beat him in his own field. Not an archmage. And not some greenhorn mage with no credentials.

“You really should have kept your nose out of things, Veronica Everwells. I will expose you as the fraud you are for taking my life’s work.”
