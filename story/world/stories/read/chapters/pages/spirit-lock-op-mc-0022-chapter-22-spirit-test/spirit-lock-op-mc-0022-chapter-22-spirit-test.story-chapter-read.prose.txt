Mayle didn’t flinch when Belia asked if she wanted her as an enemy. “No,” Mayle said. “I’m giving you a simple way to keep Vestra enrolled in this school. If you’re so unreasonable that you’d rather scar your son’s academic record than punish him for his heinous actions, I’ll accept.”

Belia stared coldly as the seconds passed in silence. Then she spoke. “Humans are cruel, selfish creatures, Mayle. We want what’s best for us and our children, and lack empathy for others we hurt. That’s the nature of the world.

You know that; that’s why you cater to rich parents willing to spend anything to make problems disappear. That’s also why you bribe officials, and grease pockets, and bend laws. You’re a criminal—so don’t lecture me on crimes and empathy. I hired you to take in my criminal son and make him legitimate. Instead, my son was beaten, humiliated, and ended up with lifelong disabilities on the first day. Disabilities, Mayle. If he took a beating, I might’ve rejoiced. God knows that boy needed one. But he’ll never talk right again—and the student who did it declared that would happen before the fight.

“That boy maimed my son on purpose. Now, you want me to say sorry…?

“I refuse. Your options are to destroy that kid’s life and make it up to me, or suffer my wrath. And I promise, if you choose the latter, I’ll destroy your enterprise brick-by-brick until there’s nothing left. Choose wisely.”

Belia cut the line, leaving Mayle in a state of suspended animation. She didn’t know what to do. Of all of the people that she didn’t want to become enemies with—Belia Alicibria had to be the worst.

Belia was an “Eternal,” the name for someone who had lived so long and got away with so much that they felt impervious to laws. Eternals were cruel entities, those who lack empathy—and the primary individual that Mayle catered to. The difference between Belia and others was that she was truly above the law, someone who only had to stand up to individuals like Stella Ingsley, who presided over the Golden Belt, the second largest intergalactic empire—or celestial—in the universe. Even then, Belia could mobilize the rift guild industry, and rift breaks were infamous for destroying cities and crop fields. She was someone who stood at the pinnacle of power—an Eternal with no bounds to cruelty.

She was the worst person to make an enemy of.

And yet… Mayle couldn’t get Hayden out of her mind. That “magic.” His secrets. His potential.

Mayle grabbed the emergency bottle of liquor underneath her desk, only to find it empty. She shook it violently and screamed, “Keaden!” Her assistant had poured out her liquor—and she was furious about it.

While Mayle yelled at Keaden for keeping her sober at work, Kayline was pacing back and forth outside the spirit-testing exam, wondering where Hayden was. There were various tests for spirits depending on the spirit’s class; for Hayden, there would be a test to hit as many targets as he could with Gluttony, and another where people hit a barrier as hard as they could with their spirit. It was nerve-wracking that he had to take a spirit test at all; it was worse that he hadn’t shown—and the test was almost over.

No, forget the test. It was terrible that he was at Junolyx at all!

Hayden was in the right during his match with Vestra, but it didn’t change the facts. He horribly disfigured the richest person in the school—on video. Belia surely had a small army working to scrub the internet of the footage. She probably had another team devoted to destroying Hayden by any means necessary. It was terrifying.

Kayline’s link rang. The caller ID read: “Dad.” She accepted, and a handsome man’s face popped up in her mind. “Hey Dad.”

“Kay, I don’t have time to talk, but I need you to listen very carefully. I heard that you asked out a boy named Hayden Reskco.”

Kayline blushed furiously and snapped. “That’s none of your business!”

“That is my business. Because ‘Hayden Reskco’ just got Belia Alicibria’s son expelled from Junolyx. Now, she’s preparing a scorched earth campaign against anyone who even knows the guy. Kay—until we know more about the situation, you can’t talk to this guy. It could get you banned from any guild that employs Alicibria Mining Industries—and that’s half the guilds in the Golden Belt.”

Kayline’s heart thumped. “What? You saw the video, right? Vestra—”

“It doesn’t matter what Vestra did. We see the world for how it is, not how we wish it was. And the simple fact is, Belia’s threatening me and you and Lina. It’s not fair. It’s not just. It’s not righteous—but it is true. So unless you want to suffer immensely, find another crush.” He held up his finger toward someone in the room he was in and then looked back. “I need to go. I love you.”

Kayline wasn’t immature enough to be defiant or to reject his love. Instead, she said, “Thank you for letting me know. I’ll keep that in mind. Love you too.” Then she hung up and stared off into dead space.

Just as she considered going home to prevent seeing Hayden, she heard someone say, “How isn’t that cheating?” and she had a premonition about the subject of discussion.

Kayline turned to the spirit test, where people shot targets. It was packed with spectators, and many were decrying cheating and foul play. The other half were saying, “Shut up! It’s a spirit—it doesn’t matter how it works.”

A white blur suddenly flashed in her vision, and she saw it weaving between moving targets. “Gluttony?” Kayline muttered. She rushed up, and people started yelling at her for pushing forward, but enough people recognized her to let her through. Soon, she was up front, staring down the most spellbinding scene she had ever seen.

Gluttony flew through the range at high speed, biting targets in half. It was like it was playing connect the dots in three dimensions to take down all of the targets. Worse, Gluttony was focusing on targets that Hayden’s competitors were aiming for, depriving them of points. It slowed his movements, but Hayden had eighty points, and the nearest competitor had twelve. The ratio was staggering.

“Go after other targets!” a competitor yelled.

Hayden stared at him apathetically. “This is a competition, yes?”

“Yes. But… actually no. It’s just a showcase to see if we can hit targets.”

“If that’s all…” Hayden whistled, and Gluttony grinned, chomping through full rows at a dizzying speed. The crowd laughed with glee as Hayden’s score counter rapidly increased.

A spectator laughed. “Bro, you should’ve kept it zipped. Now you just look sad.”

There were now no barriers between competitors and the targets, but they were only hitting one every few seconds. Meanwhile, Hayden’s score had already broken two hundred, and the competition was coming to an end.

It was all fun and games until a furious woman shot a fireball at Gluttony.

The crowd gasped when the massive flames engulfed the mouse—and then they watched in amazement as the horix sucked in the flames.

Kayline listened to the puzzled watchers around her.

“Did it just… eat it?”

“It looked that way.”

“Is that even possible?”

“I doubt it. It was probably a trick.”

Or so they thought.

Furious competitors attacked the flying mouse, enveloping it in fire, wind, and electricity, but Gluttony absorbed the attacks without fail. It became so perplexing that the other competitors made it their mission to destroy the spirit beast—but it was futile. Gluttony didn’t even slow down in destroying targets.

Kayline couldn’t help but giggle at Gluttony’s taunts and dramatic bluster. He was like his owner—a complete showoff—and hated by men. Especially the competitors.

“I swear to God,” a competitor said. “Once I—”

A horn rang, breaking the competitors out of their trance. It was only then that they realized that they had been firing nonstop at Gluttony instead of participating. Now, they were all under twenty points, and Hayden stood with a whopping 348. The number was so unbelievable that it sparked an outcry of laughter, mockery, and spite as the losers debated the rules.

Kayline smirked at the salty competitors. She lost sympathy for them the moment that they stopped trying and focused on attacking Gluttony.

Amused and satisfied, Kayline tried to slink off before she ran into Hayden, but she froze when one of the competitors said, “Too bad your horix’s useless in actual combat.”

Kayline passionately yearned to see Hayden use Gluttony for real, and she always enjoyed watching him destroy people. It was a guilty pleasure, so she slunk off to a corner where she could watch the second test.

Hayden must’ve said something savage because the entire crowd oohed in a harmonic chorus before breaking off into laughter and whispers.

It was getting good.

Suddenly, Hayden walked through the crowd, flanked by the other three competitors that stood before the proctor—a B-rank barrier rifter. The man stared at Hayden with strange anticipation.

“Do you want to go first?” Hayden asked, genuinely bored.

The competitor shook his head. “No, go ahead.”

“Okay.” Hayden summoned Gluttony and looked at the proctor. “Just to be clear, the goal is to get past your barrier, right?”

“Yes.”

“And if I hit you?”
