The lights in the auction house began to dim.

Nearly every visible seat was filled, both in the main hall below and across the VIP balconies above. Many of Maftem’s most powerful and wealthy residents had arrived to participate in the Pelotema Grand Auction. Nobles, merchants, mages, guild representatives, and adventurers filled the room, each dressed in their finest attire.

According to the catalogue, a little over three hundred items were being sold tonight.

It was quite an assortment, even for a grand auction.

Of course, not every item would be displayed individually. Some lesser lots would be grouped together, and several would likely pass with little competition. But the important ones—the rare materials, ancient texts, enchanted weapons, high-grade monster cores, and specialty artifacts—would each have their moment onstage.

Veronica sat in her balcony, flipping through the catalogue one final time.

Gwen leaned over from beside her, trying to read the pages. “There are really over three hundred items?”

“Three hundred and twelve,” Veronica said.

“That sounds like we’re going to be here all night.”

Nasha, seated on Veronica’s other side, picked up a small piece of fruit from the table. “At least they gave us snacks.”

“That doesn’t make me feel much better,” Gwen said, staring over at the small plated slices of cake also provided on Nasha's table.

“We won’t be here the entire night,” Veronica said, skimming through the final pages. “The last group of items is extremely expensive, so we won’t be able to afford any of them. The minimum bid increments are all one hundred thousand vix.”

“What?” Gwen gasped. “Then some of those items must be worth millions. Are we going to see people that rich?”

“It is a dukedom, after all. There are probably nobles here who could buy Ronswick City outright. Not to mention commerce groups with treasuries capable of rivaling the kingdom’s,” Nasha said before biting into a slice of cake.

“She’s right,” Veronica said. “Some of the people here conduct business throughout Vitian, including in the capital. I wouldn’t be surprised if they had over a hundred million vix available for this auction alone.”

She had already marked the notable items in the catalogue: starmetal ore, a voidglass shard, drakebone ash, and lunar salt. A few alchemical reagents could also serve as substitutes for opening Avalon if the other materials became too expensive. There were even a few less important items that had caught her attention.

Primarily, ascension materials.

Several items contained enough concentrated destructive power to help her reach the fourth tier. A few plants rich in vitality were also being auctioned, which could assist Gwen’s ascension.

Naturally, weapons were included on the list, some of which would have suited Nasha perfectly. However, those remained a lower priority; Veronica would only consider them if she had funds left over. Weapons could be found anywhere.

A soft chime rang through the auction hall.

The low murmur of conversation gradually faded as the stage brightened. Several guarded doors behind the podium opened, and a tall man in a formal black coat stepped onto the stage. His silver hair was combed neatly back, and a practiced smile settled across his face as he looked over the crowd.

“Honored guests,” he said, his voice carried clearly by a small sound-amplification wisp floating near his face. “Welcome to the Pelotema Grand Auction.”

The room quieted down.

“Tonight, we present a catalogue of rare materials, historical artifacts, enchanted arms, alchemical treasures, spell research, and several specialty lots gathered from across the western territories and beyond. As always, every item has been examined by our appraisers and verified to the highest standards of this house.”

“Looks like things are starting,” Nasha said.

Veronica nodded, her attention fixed on the man below.

“The rules remain the same as always! I will present each item along with its starting bid and minimum bid increment. Simply raise your paddle and announce your bid. If no one outbids you within ten seconds, the lot is yours! Of course, if anyone has a question, the count will pause. All purchased items will be delivered after the auction concludes!”

“This is so exciting,” Gwen said, gripping the railing.

“Since we have so many items this year, let us continue without delay! For our first lot, a twin pair of Evertears!”

The auctioneer snapped his fingers. A faint blue light shimmered behind him. One of the circular altars at the center of the stage lit up with several glowing lines. A second later, two small jewels appeared atop it, both shaped like teardrops. They were pale blue and clear, with a thin line of silver running through the center of each.

Gwen’s eyes widened. “Whoa. What was that?”

“A teleportation array,” Veronica said. “A low-level one. Short distance, probably connected to a storage room behind the stage.”

“They can just do that?”

“For small objects, yes. It’s faster than carrying every item out one at a time.”

Gwen stared down at the glowing altar. “That’s so convenient.”

“It is also very expensive,” Nasha said, taking another bite of cake.

“Lot one!” the auctioneer announced. “A twin pair of Evertears. Low-grade water-aspected gems formed from mineral condensation within old mana springs. Suitable for low-tier enchantments or cooling arrays! Starting bid: one thousand vix! Minimum increment: one hundred!”

A paddle rose from the lower seats almost immediately.

“One thousand one hundred!”

“One thousand two hundred!”

“One thousand four hundred!”

The bids rose quickly, though not fiercely. Most came from the lower hall. A few merchants participated, along with a young mage in blue robes and a woman who appeared more interested in the gems as jewelry than anything else.

Gwen leaned over the railing, following every raised paddle with growing interest. “People are already bidding.”

“It is an auction,” Veronica said.

“I know, but still. It feels different seeing it happen.”

The auctioneer smiled as the bids moved back and forth. “One thousand eight hundred! Do I hear nineteen? Nineteen hundred from the gentleman in section four. Two thousand? Two thousand from the lady in blue. Two thousand one hundred?”

No other paddles rose.

“Two thousand going once.”

He paused. “Twice.”

Another pause. “Sold to the lady in blue for two thousand vix!”

He snapped his fingers again. The glowing altar pulsed, and the pair of Evertears vanished. In their place appeared a black claw that faded to yellow near the tip.

“For lot two, we have a preserved Thunderhawk talon! Suitable for many lightning-based trinkets and capable of imbuing weapons with electricity! Starting bid: one thousand! Minimum increment: one hundred!”

This time, a few adventurers joined the bidding. One man in polished armor raised his paddle confidently, only to be outbid by a mage near the front. The price climbed past two thousand, then three.

Gwen watched with wide eyes.

Nasha seemed more interested in the people. Her gaze moved from balcony to balcony, lingering on guards, servants, and the occasional noble entourage. Veronica noticed but said nothing. Perhaps it was simply the cautious nature of a rogue.

The first few dozen lots passed quickly. Only one or two received any attention from those seated in the VIP balconies.

Nasha leaned over, peering at the catalogue in Veronica’s lap. “The first item you want is coming up, right?”

Veronica looked down. “Yeah. Lot thirty-three.”

“What is it?” Gwen asked, looking back.

“An ascension material.”

The auctioneer snapped his fingers again.

The glowing altar pulsed, swallowing the previous item and replacing it with a small black stone sealed inside a metal frame. The stone itself was jagged and dull, about the size of a fist, with thin red-orange cracks running through it like cooled lava. Every few seconds, one of the cracks brightened faintly before fading again.

“Lot thirty-three!” the auctioneer announced. “A sealed piece of shining emberstone, harvested from the aftermath of a collapsed mana vein in the southern territories! Suitable for explosives, spell talismans, or as a Path of Ruin advancement material!”

Gwen’s eyes flicked from the auctioneer to Veronica. “Well, that makes sense.”

“Starting bid: five thousand! Minimum increment: five hundred!”

Several paddles rose from the lower hall. Unsurprisingly, each bidder appeared to be a mage following the Path of Ruin.

“Six thousand!”

“Six thousand five hundred!”

“Eight thousand!”

Veronica hummed thoughtfully.

The emberstone would be enough to help her reach the fourth tier, especially after absorbing the kassal oil in Greystone. She had also gotten acclimated to her cores after pushing them to the limit during the battle in the Valley of Silence. She was already on the cusp of breaking through. Otherwise, it would take at least a few emberstones or a combination of materials for her to ascend.

Right now, she only needed one final push. “Nine thousand,” she said, raising her paddle.

The paddle had been enchanted with a voice-amplification spell, allowing the entire auditorium to hear her.

Several heads turned to look upward. Few people seated in the VIP balconies had bid so far, so Veronica’s interest in the item drew immediate attention.

Fortunately, none of the other balconies appeared interested.

“Nine thousand from balcony sixty-nine!” the auctioneer called, smiling brightly. “Do I hear nine thousand five hundred?”

Silence followed.

“Oh, hey. I think Lord Vaun finally noticed us,” Gwen said, looking over.

Veronica turned to the left. Sure enough, Lord Vaun was looking toward them from several balconies away. He nodded in greeting.

She returned the gesture.

“Nine thousand going once!”

A man in brown robes seated below raised his paddle after a moment of hesitation. “Nine thousand five hundred!”

“Nine thousand five hundred from the gentleman in—”

“Ten thousand,” Veronica said, raising her paddle immediately.

The auctioneer smoothly turned back toward their section. “Ten thousand from balcony sixty-nine! Going once!”

“That was fast,” Gwen said.

“I need to advance soon. Ten thousand vix to reach the fourth tier isn’t the best deal, but it’s better than what I’ll find through the regular markets and merchants.”

“Ten thousand going twice! Sold!”

The auctioneer snapped his fingers, and the emberstone disappeared, replaced by a pair of steel gauntlets. “For the next lot, we have a pair of battle gauntlets crafted by expert blacksmith Anaran of the Trailing Star Commerce Group!”

“How much do we have left?” Nasha asked. “Ten thousand isn’t a small amount.”

“We had about seventy thousand in usable funds. With the emberstone, we’re down to sixty thousand,” Veronica said, already searching the catalogue for the next item.

“That doesn’t include the profits from the theories you registered, right?”

“No. If I had to guess, I’ll probably make… thirty or forty thousand from the first set of notes. Maybe fifty thousand from the second.”

“Will that be enough to buy everything we need?” Gwen asked, walking back and dropping onto the couch beside Veronica.

“We’ll be cutting it close,” Veronica replied. “Closer than I’d like. In the worst case, we may have to forgo your ascension material. We might find something better in Avalon.”

Gwen hummed. “That’s fine with me. I’m not in a rush to reach the next tier.”

Veronica looked over at her. “You can’t remain at the second tier forever. Honestly, with me as your teacher, you should already be at the third tier and preparing for the fourth. There just hasn’t been an opportunity to focus solely on training you with everything that’s happened.”

Nasha smiled from the side. “Poor Gwen. Being told she’s behind because her teacher is too busy dragging her through disasters like a spirit-crazed demon from hundreds of years ago.”

“Hey, I’m not doing that badly as a teacher,” Veronica said, glancing at her. She turned toward Gwen. “Isn’t that right?”

“Yes, humble master, exalted archmage the second,” Gwen said, mimicking a bow with her hands. “I have learned a great deal from you.”

Veronica rolled her eyes.

Several more lots passed quickly. The steel gauntlets sold for twelve thousand vix to an armored adventurer in the lower hall. A bundle of medicinal herbs went for six thousand. A minor illusion bracelet drew more attention than Veronica expected, mostly from noblewomen who seemed more interested in its fashionable uses than its actual enchantment.

That wasn’t particularly surprising. She had seen the same thing among the nobility in Annesheim. Not everyone with wealth or influence was obsessed with gaining more power.

Then came the drakebone ash.

Veronica had expected it to be somewhat contested. Drake materials always were. They were close relatives of dragons, and their bloodlines were similar enough that most recipes—alchemical or otherwise—could substitute drake components for dragon ones.

The bidding began at eight thousand. Veronica had hoped to claim it for fourteen, but she ultimately won it for eighteen. Four thousand more than she had wanted to spend, but still within reason.

Gwen looked pained when the auctioneer announced the sale. “Every time you raise that paddle, I feel like my soul leaves my body. This is so much more money than I made working as a waitress.”

The lunar salt appeared not long afterward.

Unfortunately, Veronica was unable to acquire it.

The auctioneer had barely finished announcing the lot before three different bidders joined in. Apparently, there had been a poor harvest along the eastern coast, while several shipments from the southern ports had been delayed. What should have been a moderately expensive stabilizing material turned into a small bidding war between alchemists.

The price reached twenty-five thousand vix. Without the poor harvest, the material would have been worth closer to seventeen thousand.

An alchemist from a noble house ultimately claimed it.

The auctioneer was replaced by a woman after the hundredth lot had been sold. She was a beastkin: tall, with short blonde hair and furry ears. A fox, perhaps.

She snapped her fingers. “Ladies and gentlemen, next we have lot one hundred and four!”

Veronica leaned forward. “Here it is,” she murmured.

“Twenty-two kilograms of starmetal ore, harvested directly from Horizon Mountain! A beautiful ore capable of forging weapons and armor that harness the power of moonlight! Difficult to process, but an expert craftsman may possess the skill to refine it if you find them! Starting bid: thirty thousand vix! Minimum increment: one thousand!”
