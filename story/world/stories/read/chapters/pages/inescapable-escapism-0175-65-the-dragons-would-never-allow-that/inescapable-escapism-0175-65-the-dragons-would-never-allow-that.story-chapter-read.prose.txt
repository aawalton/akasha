My gaze flicked back and forth across the page as I devoured the words written before me, allowing myself to be transported to a distant land, where dragons roamed the skies and monsters stalked the earth. They terrorised humans and were kept at bay only by the few brave humans and dragons who had bonded and become responsible for preserving the lives of every other surviving person.

I could almost feel what it was like to stand on the side of that volcano, just like the character in the book I was reading. A sulphuric tang hung heavily in the air and tickled my nostrils as the echo of a roar came from somewhere above me. It wasn’t as screeching as I expected. It had been described as sharp, causing the hairs on the main character’s arms to stand on end, but it didn’t do that to me. It just sent a shiver down my spine.

It was the raw power in that bellow that chilled me. The dragon was so far away that I could barely see it, and yet the sound reached my ears, reminding me of just how important what I was doing was. I could not stop, could not allow myself to slow for any longer than absolutely needed. Otherwise, I’d die, just like the others I’d left behind.

Fear tightened its grip around my heart, and I bent over, bracing my hands on my knees as I fought to catch my breath. I was too close to the crater, though. It made the air dry, and each inhale burned my already raw throat. I felt like I was being cooked from the inside out.

I had to keep moving, I told myself as I took another step. The charred rock crunched beneath my boot, and I almost slipped in the thick layer of ash that coated it, but somehow, I managed to keep my balance. I stumbled onwards, my movement slow but steady.

My gaze flicked towards the summit. I’d been trying not to look at it for hours. I knew that it would discourage me to see how far away I still was, yet I couldn’t help it. I needed to glance upon it, but the sight didn’t elicit the emotion I was concerned about. I wasn’t discouraged. I was just scared.

A thick cloak of ominous, dark smoke covered the mountain ahead of me, and it seemed to be growing. I could almost see the cloud becoming denser before my eyes, and a new concern pulled at me. I didn’t want to think about it, didn’t want to accidentally will it into existence, but it was impossible to ignore.

Was the volcano about to erupt?

It had been years since the last big explosion. Decades, even. Perhaps we were due another one, and that thought made my steps slow again. I caught myself before I could come to a complete stop, forcing my legs to keep moving as my thoughts raced.

As far as I was aware, it had never erupted properly during the trial. There were small ones, obviously. They happened all the time, but they rarely claimed lives. The magma that escapes during those explosions moved slowly, solidifying before it could engulf a person, but a large eruption would be different. They would have no chance of surviving that.

The dragons would never allow that to happen, though, I told myself. They would put a stop to it before the volcano passed the point of no return, or they’d warn us it was coming. They would have passed the message on to their riders, and the trial would have been postponed. That had happened before, and it would happen again if needed.

They needed us. The dragons would allow me and the rest of the people attempting the trial to die because they needed us. They needed riders just as we needed dragons. An unbonded dragon did not have access to its full strength, just as an unbound human did not. We both needed the other to reach our full potential, so I had to believe they would not kill so many of us by allowing the volcano to explode.

My foot slipped on another ash-covered rock, and a plume of dust leapt into the air. I clamped my lips together, holding my breath as I fought the urge to inhale until the ash had settled again. If I did, I would just choke. I knew that. The ash would find its way into my lungs, mixing with any moisture it found along the way, and that would make it impossible to breathe. It would be too late for me. I’d suffocate within minutes.

Somewhere behind me, I heard someone coughing, but I tried desperately to ignore it, pretending the wet, hacking sounds did not reach my ears. I longed to help them, to turn away from the summit and hurry back to wherever they were, but I knew I could not. There was nothing I could do, and I had to be selfish. I needed to only think of myself.

That was what my teachers had drilled into me. They’d repeated it again and again, and I thought they were being ridiculous. I didn’t know selfishness was so necessary for self-preservation. Not until my brother returned, riding on the back of his dragon, his eyes plagued by what he had witnessed in the fight to prove himself to the ancient dragons who decide our fate.

Do not turn back, I thought to myself, continuing to trudge up the almost vertical slope. Just keep going. One foot in front of the other until I either reached the top or collapsed. Those were my only two options, and I was too stubborn to collapse, so I had to make it. I had to get to the top.

My hands pulled at the thick jacket I was wearing, my fingers itching to reach for the zip and pull it down. I wished I could rip it from my body and throw it onto the ground. It would have been so much nicer. I was drenched in sweat, and the thick, stiff fabric chafed against my skin painfully, but I did not dare remove it. I knew if I did, I could die.

It was a gift from my brother, perhaps the last gift he would ever give me, and I did not recognise the material the jacket was made of. I’d never touched anything like it before, but I suspected his dragon had a hand in crafting it. I was sweltering; it felt like I was burning alive, but I assumed I would somehow be even hotter if I were not wearing it.

The thought of my brother and his dragon pushed me onwards. I could feel myself being filled with determination. It coursed through my veins, and my hands clenched into fists as I marched onwards, eager to get to the summit and pass the first trial.

That wouldn’t mean I’d become a dragon rider, though. It wasn’t that simple. More than one trial was required to prove my worth, and although I wasn’t allowed to know any of the details, I’d heard enough to be sure the next few years of my life would consist of endless challenges. It was possible that I could fail any one of them, and that would be it. My life would be over, and I would never see my brother again. He would be alone in the world, with no surviving family.

But at least he had his dragon. The bond between a dragon and their rider was unbreakable. They would be bound together until they both died, and that thought gave me some comfort. At least I knew he wouldn’t be totally alone. Just mostly.

However, if I somehow managed to survive the treacherous climb and the rest of the trials that awaited me, if I managed to show that I was strong enough to withstand the pain and suffering that I would face, I would be just like my brother. I, too, would be a dragon rider. Then, everything would change.

I’d spend my days training with my dragon, learning to fly and fight from its back before being welcomed into the ranks of the elite soldiers who patrolled our land. I’d join them in the fight to eradicate the monsters who hunted the remaining humans for sport and sustenance, and I’d be able to see my brother every day. We would be able to talk freely, openly, like we once did. Everything would be okay.

That was what I’d always dreamt of. Ever since I was a child, even before my parents were murdered, that was the only future I had been able to envision for myself. I knew becoming a dragon rider was the only way to survive in that cruel land, and I wanted to help make the realm safe again. I had to.

What else was I meant to do? I couldn’t ignore the darkness that waited outside the walls of our city, occasionally slipping in through the cracks and laying waste to whatever it could get its hands upon before being neutralised. I wasn’t able to laugh and smile and dance at balls like the others, pushing all thoughts of the war that waged on around us out of my mind. My thoughts always returned to it, and I knew it was inevitable. No matter what I tried, I would never be satisfied, never be able to rest, until the threads of my soul were intertwined with another’s.

I had to keep going. I had to keep fighting onwards and get to the top. It wasn’t much further. Another half an hour, maybe an hour, and I would be there. I would finally look upon the dragons huddled around the crater, unbothered by the heat, and they would decide whether I lived to see another sunrise. All I could do was hope they saw something in me that I wasn’t even sure existed.

Stopping once more, I lifted my bottle to my lips and drained the last of the warm water. There was very little in there, barely enough to wet my parched mouth, but it helped a little. I slipped the now-empty bottle back into place and glanced at the rocks ahead, preparing to continue climbing, when a soft snort sounded from beside me.

My eyes snapped towards the haphazard stack of rocks, and shock warred with fear inside me. The noise that had come from them was not right. It was not the expected sound of stone scraping against itself as the mountain shifted beneath it. It was distinctively alive, but the rock looked as though they’d fallen there rather than been placed deliberately. They must have been there for quite some time, if the amount of ash gathered on top was anything to go by, but…

A realisation slammed into me, and I threw myself out of the world. My room spun around me, and I clutched my book, clinging to it as nausea clawed at my throat. My other hand was balled into a fist and rested on Rex’s side, and fear shot through me as I realised just how tightly I was squeezing my hands into fists, but thankfully, I couldn’t feel any of Rex’s fur between my fingers.

I let my head drop back to rest against the headboard, letting out a deep sigh and noticing how much easier the air was to breathe in this world. I was fine, I told myself. Everything was okay. I was in my room, not climbing the side of an active volcano whilst praying that some terrifying dragons would decide not to eat me if I managed to make it to the top. And that was good. I didn’t want to be there.

Another snuffling noise came from Rex, and I rolled my head to the side to stare down at him. A smile pulled at my lips as my eyes landed on him. He was fast asleep, his back pressed against my leg as he took up much more than half of the bed. I didn’t mind, though.

He’d been there since we got back from his walk. It must have exhausted him because hours had passed, and he’d only woken up a handful of times, generally whenever I got too absorbed in the book and stopped stroking him. He hadn’t noticed that time, though. He must have been too deeply asleep.

I still couldn’t believe that he was actually there, though. It didn’t feel real. I’d been waiting for him for so long, since before I even found him in my garden. I’d always wanted a dog, dreamt of having one, but I didn’t think it would actually happen, and I would have never believed that he could be so perfect or so cute.

Rex was all I needed, I realised. I didn’t need a terrifying and adorable dragon to bond with in order to be protected from monsters. Not in my reality or any other. I had Rex, and he was all I needed. We could keep each other safe. Especially if he really had ripped apart…

My thoughts trailed off, and I glanced at Rex again, my eyes darting towards his claws. I could only just see them beneath his fur, but the urge to reach out and touch them washed over me. I wanted to feel how sharp they were, to know if they could tear through flesh as easily as I was imagining.

I shook my head, trying to push the mental image aside. I was getting caught up in the other worlds, forgetting where I was. I was safe in my reality. There were no monsters or demons that I would have to face. Humanity was not on the brink of extinction. We were not hunted or stalked by shadowy creatures that could slip into a person’s mind and corrupt it beyond recognition, removing their morality and making them a vicious murderer. Those kinds of creatures didn’t exist in my world. They only existed in books.

Letting out another deep breath, I forced myself to look at my book again, trying to continue reading where I left off, but the words made no sense to me. I scanned the page, confusion pulling at me as my gaze landed on an unfamiliar name. I had no idea who that character was, and I was almost certain they had not been mentioned before, but I flicked back a few pages, trying to figure out when they’d been introduced.

It must have been at the beginning of the chapter, I realised as I skimmed the book. I didn’t see their name before it, but I was halfway through the chapter, and I had no recollection of what had happened in it. That concerned me. I knew I’d begun to slip into another world, but I didn’t notice it start to happen. Not at first, anyway. Reading and being in the other world had just kind of blended together.

I began reading again, trying to get back into the story, but within a couple of pages, I could feel my attention starting to wander again. I pushed on desperately, trying to force myself to care about Jourdian and her fight to become the dragon rider that she’d always wanted to be, but I just didn’t.

Disappointment coursed through me. I should have been enjoying the book much more than I was. I’d seen so many people talking about it online, and they’d all seemed to love it. They gushed about it, talking about how good the characters were and how exciting the world was, claiming they’d love to live there themselves.

Even Phoebe had loved it. She was the one who’d told me I had to read it, and I had been liking it at first. I wasn’t far enough in to really assess; I’d only managed to get past the first couple of chapters, but it had been gripping me until I’d gotten distracted by the other world. After that, it just didn’t have the same appeal.

That was my fault, though. The world I’d visited or made up called to me much more than the one in the book. I was painfully aware that it interested me more than it should have, and I longed to learn more about that version of me. I wanted to know how I’d come to be on the side of that volcano, what had happened to my family, and if my brother would be alright.

He would. In time. It would be hard. He’d seen so much, experienced so much. More than he should have for a boy in his early twenties. He was only just a man, not long out of his teens, but he’d been forced to grow up too soon, just as I had. It was impossible not to in that world, especially when our parents were ripped away from us so young. We both had to step up and deal with the responsibilities they’d left behind.

My head snapped up again, and I jerked back. I was doing it again. I could feel myself starting to slip, starting to tumble into another world, and I couldn’t let it happen. There were too many unknowns there, and I couldn’t risk going into another world that scarred me. I had to stick to my worlds, the ones I trusted. That was what I’d decided.

Still, I was tempted by it. The urge to allow myself to be sucked into that place pulled at me, testing my restraint, but there was something stopping me from fully giving in and returning. Something held me back and reminded me that I did not truly want to be there.

As thrilling and exciting as that world was, as tempting as it felt, I knew I would not actually enjoy being there. That life was not one I wanted to live. The world was far too brutal. Pain was too common an experience. I would suffer it daily, and that was not something I wanted.

Memories edged into my mind, flashes of my life in that world, and I couldn’t help but grimace as dread filled me. The trials ahead of that version of me would not be easy. I was well aware of that. My brother had told me far more than he should have, more than he was allowed to, but he knew I would follow in his footsteps, and he hoped the knowledge would help prepare me, that it could save my life.

And it might. I knew more than most, and I had lied to myself, claiming I was ready to do what I had to do, but doubt still pulled at me. How could anyone be prepared for that? It felt impossible. Dragon riders were humanity’s last hope. They were the one barrier that still stood between them and the monsters, but to do that…

I shook my head again, squeezing my hand into a fist once more as images flashed behind my eyes. I couldn’t help but picture the haunted look on my brother’s face as he’d warned me of what trials awaited me.

If I returned to that world, I told myself, I would be forced to watch my friends die. They would be consumed by the horror as I looked on, unable to intervene or even mourn them once they were dead. I would be expected to move on immediately, to acknowledge that they were at fault for what had happened. They were not strong enough to endure what needed to be endured, and I’d be told to be grateful that fact was discovered before it was too late and a dragon was lost to the darkness alongside them.

I couldn’t do that. I knew I couldn’t. There was no way I would be able to stand there, meeting my tutor’s gaze as they told me to continue onwards and forget I’d ever loved my fallen friends. They were to be cut out of my memories, their lives and sacrifices meaningless. It was the only way.

Footsteps sounded on the stairs, and I blinked quickly. Tears stung behind my eyes, and I forced myself not to let them spill. I had to hide them before my mom walked past my door, and I only had a few seconds before that would happen.

Luckily, I was well practised at that. I was used to hiding my emotions and shoving them down deep within me. The urge to cry quickly dissipated, and by the time my mom neared, only a hint of that heartbreak remained.

My gaze did not stray from my book as my mom walked past my door, but from the corner of my eye, I saw her peer in. She didn’t say anything. She just continued walking towards her bedroom, but I was pretty sure she had no reason to be there.

She kept doing that. Every half an hour or so, she’d wander up the stairs and go into her room, glancing in through my door as she did. She’d always emerge with some dirty clothing or something to bring back downstairs, but I was pretty sure that wasn’t the real reason she’d come up.

At first, it had irritated me. I felt like she was keeping an eye on me, like she expected me to be doing something I shouldn’t, but I don’t think that was the actual reason she kept looking into my room or insisted I keep the door open. Instead, it seemed more like she was checking in on Rex, making sure that he hadn’t attacked me or anything.

Not that he would. He was too good to do that.

I waited until my mom had emerged from her room again, a mostly empty cup in her hand, and disappeared back downstairs before letting my head fall against the headboard again. A gentle thud sounded, and Rex sat up suddenly, his eyes opening widely. His gaze found me at once, and a flare of guilt exploded in my stomach.

“Oh, I’m sorry, boy,” I muttered to him, stroking his soft fur again. “It’s okay. It was just me. Go back to sleep.”

He continued to stare at me, his expression intense. It seemed almost like he was waiting for me to say something else, but I had no idea what. I wasn’t even sure if he could understand what I was saying.

Eventually, Rex lowered his head, resting it on the bed before letting out a heavy sigh that made me smile. I watched his body rise and fall as his breathing became even once more, and seconds later, he was snoring as if I’d never woken him up.
