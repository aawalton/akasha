“You’re abandoning me?” Devas asked with so much hurt in his voice, Ophelia actually felt the stupidest of pangs in her heart. There was something seriously wrong with her.

“The Oracle is in trouble,” she replied. She had to shout because she had already abandoned—no, left. She had left that insane Turnalian behind. Not abandoned. What was wrong with her? “I have to make sure she’s safe.”

Devas deftly deflected another spell from the remaining Juren mages while they were all bathed in the apocalyptic light from yet another one of Ryland’s cataclysmic spells. Did that man never run out of mana? “Truly? You’re running to the Oracle of all people? I thought she was as strong as I was!”

So had Ophelia, but something was clearly wrong. “Stop complaining! I killed two of them already. I’m sure you can handle the rest.”

Devas being Devas, Ophelia was distinctly able to make out his grumbling despite being at least thirty armspans away by now.

She cursed herself as she ran. Being so informal with a legendary Turnalian, so downright offensive in all honesty, was awful. She needed to correct her behaviour. The stress of battle and Devas being one of the most powerfully odd entities she had ever encountered were no excuses.

For now, however, she focused on reaching the Oracle. The summons had been urgent. She needed to move faster.

Some of Ophelia’s spells like [Saviour’s Bulwark] actively slowed her down, so she discarded them to run faster. At any other time, she might have considered that a horrible idea. There was not only a battle between powerful mages going on not far behind her, but there was also a far more powerful brawl between Avatars and one insanely strong Incarnation above her too.

But said Incarnation was casting magic that left her quite certain that the Avatars were never going to pose a danger to her. She could see it over her head as she ran. The whole world was soon filled with strange little dots, from which his barriers were now taking over the land. He was literally preventing both his magic and that of the Avatars from reaching the land.

The sheer scale of it was staggering. Ophelia might not have had the time to stop and be actually impressed at how far it stretched, but she had already seen enough. She already knew this was magic only Ryland could ever cast.

Of course, this was mostly to protect his Inscription. Ophelia was no idiot. But that didn’t mean she couldn’t enjoy the benefit too.

There. She was certain she saw the distant spot where the Oracle and the first set of Juren mages were trapped in Ryland’s rifts. The question remained on how Ophelia herself was going to get inside, but she’d deal with that once she got there.

If she was lucky, it was probably one of those spells that was great at keeping things inside but allowed outsiders to break in.

Attempts at contacting the Oracle after the first few messages where she had requested Ophelia to come to her had failed. She wasn’t responding to her Glyph of Communication. Ophelia had been trained not to assume the worst in scenarios such as these, but of course, she couldn’t fully stave off the fear that something had happened to Oracle Vertha.

Just a little longer and—

And the sun descended upon Rhel. Ophelia was so shocked, so overwhelmed by its sudden appearance, that she actually fell to the ground.

There had been a burst of shadows overtaking the land. She had glanced up briefly to see a terrible number of gilt-edged rifts cracking across the atmosphere of Rhel. Ophelia hadn’t been certain what Ryland was about to cause, and though she had expected something quite momentous, a literal sun had not been one of her options.

Staring right up should have blinded her. Should have caused her eyeballs to uproot themselves from her eyes and made her scream in agony as she was scorched to imperceptible ashes.

Nothing of the sort happened.

Oh, so that was why Ryland had gone to such lengths to secure a barrier across Rhel. Not to protect the Inscription against the Avatars. Well, maybe partially.

But more importantly, it was to prevent Rhel itself from being absolutely destroyed by the sun he had brought forth. And then it was gone. Just as suddenly as the sun had appeared, the rifts supporting it collapsed and closed with a snap, leaving behind a recovering atmosphere.

Ophelia blinked out the spots in her eyes. She got to her feet as fast as she could, then got going again. Fast. She needed to be fast. There was no point in being so overwhelmed by what was going on above her. With or without her, that would go on. Her objective was clear. Reach the Oracle in time, hoping and praying that nothing irreversible had already occurred.

She rushed on. Above, she couldn’t tell quite what was going on. The air was doing weird things after being assaulted by an actual sun moments ago. She thought she felt the presence of another incredible power up top, and it really should have concerned her.

But the Oracle came first. Ophelia had already taken too long to get to her. She could feel it in her bones.

So, as the hemisphere constructed from Ryland’s rifts appeared before her, she was already preparing to break through. [Relentless Sentinel] was still active, and [Champion’s Last Stand] would be her main thrust to get in.

She realized she had never properly seen the rear of Ryland’s rifts before. As in, she had often seen the face of the rifts through which stuff emerged from wherever he called them from. But what about the back? Well, Ophelia was getting a first-hand look now, and she had to admit that it wasn’t impressive.

It reminded her of obsidian. Where the front was swirling energy that maintained some sort of barrier between Realms, the back was closed and solid. Unremarkable save for its slightly reflective dark sheen.

Which only emboldened Ophelia. When she was finally close enough, her spells activated. Golden power radiated out of her manasoul and her surging Soul Glyph of Vanguard. Her sword was out, shining with irradiating energy. Just as she reached her target, she swung with all her might and brought her full momentum to bear upon the dark glass.

It cracked and shattered upon impact. The ease with which Ophelia was able to break through actually took her aback, and she gasped as she struggled to stop herself before she collided with anyone.

Fortunately, she did collide with someone. Ophelia and one of the surprised Juren mages went down, the latter shrieking. It wasn’t pain. If anything, it sounded like frustration. What was going on here?

Ophelia tucked herself into a roll and quickly got back to her feet. She swallowed.

The Juren mages had trapped the Oracle in a spell of sorts. All four of the bastards from Jure who were still standing were desperately trying to maintain a magical leash they had flung around Oracle Vertha. As the shattered pieces of Ryland’s rift cage dissolved into smoky particles, Ophelia could only wonder why the Oracle hadn’t broken free yet.

“You sow!” one of the Juren mages screamed. “How dare you interrupt the proceedings!”

“Kill her already!” another one shouted. “[Death Grip]!”

The spell didn’t affect her as severely as it might have, had the Juren mages been powerful. Ophelia’s defensive and warding Inscriptions combined with [Relentless Sentinel] to keep her unharmed, though she felt a strong pressure trying to squeeze her like a constrictor serpent.

That just made her even more concerned why the Oracle was keeping herself restrained by these fools. Had something happened to her powers? Had she lost use of her magic for some reason?

Of course, Ophelia’s training had already kicked in. A rapid assessment of the situation was immediately followed up by a decision.

With a yell, she stomped on the Juren mage she had unwittingly flung to the ground. He had been attempting to get back up, possibly by evading her attention while the rest of his fellows tried to keep her distracted. But it was not to be.

“Stay down, mongrel,” Ophelia growled at the squawking mage. More spells were flung at her from the rest of the fake delegation from Jure, but she had her shield out to block the troublesome magic. Her sword was out too, aimed at the throat of the fallen mage. “Give it up already. Or else I’ll sever your head right where you lie.”

“Such savagery!” he spat. “From a Mage Knight at that.”

She had thought that threatening would end the fight before it could get complicated. That they’d see reason and stop attacking in fear of her killing their comrade.

But it seemed Jure considered its mages disposable.

A hammering of violent spells avalanched upon her location, and she was forced to raise her shield to protect herself. The Juren mage under her foot screamed as he wasn’t spared from the effects of his allies.

“Oracle!” Ophelia shouted through the din of thunderous magic exploding against her defence. “I need your assistance!” There was no response. “Oracle.”

Something was wrong. Ophelia was on her own in this battle. At least the Oracle wasn’t dead, so whatever was going on could hopefully be reversed once she had dealt with the enemy.

But that prospect was proving far harder to bring about into reality.

The enemy mages were changing tactics, swiftly shifting to different classes of spells to try and get through Ophelia’s surefire defence. When the ground turned to mud, she was forced to retreat. But that just brought her closer to the rock golems trying to attack from behind. One of the mages had gotten much closer too, taking potshots from a Glyphtech firearm to debilitate her.

Ophelia fought back against it all. Her manasoul surged as she pulled on more magic than she recalled doing in a long while. “[Unrivalled Call]!”

The spell granted her a bit of reprieve. She was no longer desperately fending off multiple attacks as her opponents were now dealing with her counter. But it wouldn’t last long. She had to leave a lasting blow, or else she’d never gain an advantage.

Ophelia deflected one of the golem’s blows before rushing forward with her defence boosted by [Relentless Sentinel]. The few mages who saw her and attacked failed to stop her.

She grinned as she neared the closest enemy. Yes, she could end—

A weighty thump struck her from behind, breaking her momentum and sending her nearly sprawling to the ground. Surprised as she was, Ophelia didn’t even need to twist her head. The Juren mage she had flung to the ground had thrown himself at her directly. It was his only option now that his spells had failed to penetrate her defences.

Then he chose to explode. The sudden detonation was what caught Ophelia properly off-guard. She caught the strange glow of his body, and while she had figured that the man was preparing some sort of spell or unleashing an Inscription, she failed to predict self-detonation.

The sheer, vaporizing power of the blast threw Ophelia properly to the ground. She couldn’t get back up. Horrific pain had clasped her in what felt like a fatal embrace. This… might just be it for her.

Ophelia sucked in breaths that felt far too wet. Impossible. Were her lungs exposed? The pain was now making her delirious.

Through tear-drenched eyes, she saw that everything in a wide vicinity around her had been incinerated. Smoke and dust cobwebbed through the air, the golems having vapourized away with much of the ground. Unfortunately, it didn’t seem to have affected most of the enemy mages, whose forms and magic swam beyond the haze obscuring her view.

Sounds were muted too. There was something wrong with her ears. Strange fluid was dripping down both canals.

What had that bastard done to her?

Ophelia’s healing Inscription wasn’t working. She could barely control her manasoul to cast any healing spells, let alone actually incant one. Her whole body was terribly weak. For all intents and purposes, she was dead.

Although, shouldn’t she have actually died by now? How had she survived for this long?

The slow clearing of the haze revealed just why. Before Ophelia’s broken form, the Oracle stood like an impassable barrier. A living, breathing obstruction even when her magic was no longer with her.

“Run…” Ophelia tried to croak out, though she failed to properly speak the words. They were faint even to her own ears. “Get…”

The Juren mages were saying much the same thing, strangely enough. They were yelling at the Oracle to get back before she hurt herself irreversibly. The evil mages were intent on finishing Ophelia, but were hesitating since that would harm the Oracle, whom they apparently wished to capture alive.

Ophelia was honestly amazed she even understood all that in her current, pain-delirious state.

Instead of running, the Oracle turned and knelt beside her. Magic poured out of her, landing gently upon Ophelia like soft rainfall. “Fear not.” Even her voice was calm, like a true healer’s, despite the situation they were in. “I will have you back as you were in no time at all.”

Ophelia wanted to protest. That wasn’t what was important. Much as her body was appreciating the healing, much as she was enormously relieved for the pain to no longer be so taxing, the Oracle’s safety was the most important matter here. If the Juren mages were happy to let her go so long as they could finish off Ophelia, then by all means, she should run.

Especially because they were incensed at the Oracle’s refusal to abide by their demands. Ophelia tried to raise a hand, tried to grunt out a warning. Disastrous magic was frothing out of the Juren mages’ hands from the multitude of spells circles shining through the haze still cladding their forms.

“Watch out—”

Ophelia’s renewed voice came too late. The spell circles fired. It appeared the Juren mages’ forbearance regarding the Oracle was really, really limited.

She cried out as she was struck, magic exploding over Ophelia yet again. The despair of true defeat stung even harder than the fact that she was actually dying here. The injuries she saw blooming across the Oracle as her innate defences and Inscriptions simply failed against the spray of volatile magic from their enemies just made Ophelia simply forget her desperation to live.

The Oracle was bloodied, broken, unmoving. Lightning danced across her burned skin. Magical leeches were digging into her ruined flesh as Ophelia felt bloody bile rush up her gullet.

This was… this…

The end. She couldn’t believe it. The puzzle pieces of what happened simply failed to fall into their proper places inside her head. Why and how had the Oracle grown so vulnerable against these bullies?

Ophelia found herself mustering what little of her strength she could summon. If she was dying, then she’d spit in the face of these cowards attacking someone’s back.

A pillar of light hooked her attention far too hard. A presence began descending upon them. A presence so powerful, so breathtaking, everything in the battlefield simply stopped. The Juren mages had frozen, just as Ophelia herself was transfixed by that column of light centred on the Oracle’s wounded, prone form.

That pillar of light was alive somehow.

Ophelia’s breaths quickened in her chest despite the new wounds that had undone what little healing the Oracle had managed to provide before she had been struck. Her heart began hammering in her chest like war drums signalling the start of a campaign.

There was a power among them. A kind of impossible strength she had only ever sensed from one other person during her entire life. A power that could have rivalled Ryland’s own.

She gasped as the Oracle’s body twitched. A moment later, it began moving. Rising. Like a puppet being drawn up with strings now reattached to the controlling marionette. In shocking seconds, the Oracle was standing upright once again, her wounds healed as though she had never been injured to begin with.

Wait. This… this couldn’t have been the Oracle’s plan from the very start, right? Had she aimed, from the very beginning of this entire debacle, to call down an Incarnation upon her very self?
