The sky stretched before Vector like an endless ocean of ink.

Night had deepened fully now—thick, oppressive, and vast. Stars crowded the heavens in dizzying numbers, scattered like shattered diamonds across a blackened veil. Somewhere among them lingered Somira—the moon of Ethereum—almost invisible tonight, reduced to a faint, ghostly contour clinging to the darkness.

It reminded him that tomorrow would be a new moon.

That thought surfaced unbidden.

And with it—

Another truth.

Earth.

Memories that didn't belong to this world.

Vector lowered his gaze.

Before him lay undulating terrain. To the north, Carnatic City emerged from the darkness—its presence hinted at only by scattered pinpricks of lamplight. The land where he stood rose and fell unevenly, shaped by countless low hills no more than two to ten meters high. They formed a landscape of shallow ridges and hollows, each fold breaking lines of sight, each depression swallowing sound.

In the dark, the terrain was treacherous.

Perfect for concealment.

Perfect for ambush.

He lay hidden among the crest with Sebastian beside him, along with Marcus, Tristan, Rhea, and nine others from the Astrum Forces. All but Sebastian were First Stage Warriors—not because stronger hands weren't available. They were. Second Stage Warriors remained behind in Carnatic City, keeping the machinery of the Resistance turning.

Tonight’s operation was only the beginning.

His objective for today was clear.

The weapon consignment.

The Lionheart Empire was funneling firearms and equipment into Carnatic City—modern weapons meant for patrol guards who still carried blades and spears. The same guards who'd stopped him and Sebastian just yesterday.

And also… Sebastian's own investigation had yielded nothing. The Earl's trail had gained nothing useful. So, after meeting with Sebastian and his three other most trusted people—Marcus, Tristan, and Rhea— Vector had made the call. Intercept the shipment, then move on Vantara.

That was why they were here.

Once this was complete, Vector’s group would not return to Carnatic. Under the cover of night, they would depart directly toward Vantara—the land of the Venyar—to prepare for the Lionheart Empire’s covert campaign.

Operation: Break the Fang.

A clandestine campaign to establish a fortress in Vantara.A silent military buildup within the depths of the Naga Forest.A plan to massacre the Venyar people—and launch a surprise rear assault on Ironfang Hill City.

After uncovering the operation, He had left the City Lord’s Estate under a carefully constructed pretext—claiming he was returning to his village within Lionheart territory to tend to his ailing parents.

A lie.

But not a careless one.

He hadn’t simply vanished.

Before leaving, He had arranged for another Resistance operative to take his place within the Estate—someone who would continue monitoring its movements, ensuring the Resistance’s activities carried on without obstruction.

“Young Master… it’s time.”

Sebastian’s voice cut cleanly through Vector’s thoughts.

Vector unslung the bow from his shoulder, fingers curling tight around the worn leather grip as his body shifted into readiness—muscles coiling, breath evening, awareness sharpening to a blade's edge. The words meant only one thing: the convoy bearing the weapon consignment would crest the horizon any moment now, rolling toward them through the darkness like a beast unaware of the teeth waiting in the shadows.

Their position in the undulating terrain was deliberate.

Calculated.

Chosen.

Beneath these rolling hills lay a hidden tunnel leading directly into Carnatic City—more precisely, into the Estate's inner grounds. Vector knew the convoy would take this route to enter the city.

And so, they waited at this particular location.

Shortly after—

From the crest of a distant hill, silhouettes emerged against the starlit sky like shadows peeling away from darkness itself. Torches flickered against the void as a column of soldiers came into view, their flames bobbing and weaving like fireflies drunk on night air. Around thirty men moved in loose formation, their boots crunching over rough earth, two oxen-drawn carts rumbling at their center with the patient, inexorable rhythm of beasts who knew only forward.

As the distance closed, Vector noted their posture—shoulders slack, pace unhurried, conversation drifting on the wind in careless fragments.

Relaxed.

Unwary.

Vulnerable.

A slow, careless amble through terrain. And he knew why—because their destination neared with each labored step, because safety felt close enough to taste, because men always dropped their vigilance when they believed the danger had passed.

So, they had laid down their guard.

Mistake.

Sebastian—the commander of this mission—raised a closed fist.

At once, he and the others present reacted with the precision of a single organism awakening. And Sebastian’s? He was only commanding the loot… means, battle. His senses reached far beyond theirs—wide enough to detect threats before they emerged.

Bows were lifted with whisper-quiet grace.

Strings drawn with controlled tension.

Breathing steadied to the rhythm of distant heartbeats.

They would engage at range first, raining death from the shadows where arrows spoke louder than steel. Then close in for melee, where blood would answer blood.

Vector had just adjusted his grip when a whisper brushed his ear.

A familiar voice.

“Young Master… you’re holding the wrong bow.”

A pause, weighted with unspoken worry.

“That one’s meant for a Warrior.”

Rhea.

He knew her reason for concern and reminder.

As the bow in his hands demanded an immense draw weight—one that would strain even a seasoned First Stage Warrior. Vector hadn’t told them yet that he had broken through—that his strength now rivaled a Second Stage Warrior. Only Sebastian knew.

He offered her a brief smile.

There was no time to explain.

Sebastian had already unfurled his fist. Indicating the start of battle. That. The convoy was within range—four to five hundred yards, close enough to kill, far enough to remain unseen in the void between torchlight and starlight. More than enough for First Stage Warriors to neutralize lightly guarded escorts who believed themselves safe.

Vector released his arrow.

It flew.

Faster than the rest, cutting through night air with a sound like tearing silk.

Clean.

Straight.

Devouring distance like a streak of darkness.

Vector’s arrow reached the enemy first. Outpacing all of others’ arrows.

But it missed.

Passing a hair's breadth from a guard's shoulder, so close the man would feel the wind of its passage kiss his skin, before burying itself into the earth beyond with a soft, disappointed thud.

Not because of hesitation—

Not because of fear—

But because he was still refining his archery, still teaching his body the language of bow and string and flight. He hadn't yet achieved even Basic mastery, that first threshold, where instinct married technique.

In weeks he might achieve it, he estimated with the cold calculation. With the System's guidance… perhaps hours would suffice.

Perhaps less.

The rest of the volley struck true with the finality of judgment rendered.

Arrows punched through necks with wet, choking sounds.

Through foreheads with the crack of breaking pottery.

Through chests with the deep, meaty thud of metal meeting flesh.

Bodies collapsed in awkward position.

Only Vector's first arrow stood alone—quivering uselessly in the dirt like a monument to almost.

"Hanh…"

Sharp gasps rippled through the surroundings around him. He felt the eyes focused on him. And he knew why.

Not because of his miss—

But because of the speed of his shot, velocity that should have been impossible for someone they believed was barely awakened to Blood Energy.

He ignored them, dismissing the whispers and wide eyes with practiced ease.

This was no time for admiration.

No time for questions.

Another arrow snapped from his bow with barely a breath between draws.

This one struck—a guard staggered backward as the shaft punched into his chest with enough force to crack ribs and pierce lung, not the clean headshot Vector had aimed for with Close enough to matter.

A third arrow flew before the second's victim had finished falling.

It struck… lower.

Unintentionally.

Catastrophically.

Vector's eyes flicked to the guard collapsing to the ground, writhing in agony that transcended mere pain, hands clutching desperately between his legs at an injury no man should endure.

Froze.

He stopped firing.

Reconsidered.

This was a battle; he reminded himself with iron discipline. Not a torture session. Not a place for inflicting suffering beyond what victory demanded. He exhaled once, releasing the tension coiled in his chest like a held breath finally freed.

Then sprinted forward.

The others followed in his wake like shadows chasing light.

Some Lionheart soldiers had reacted quickly—diving behind the slopes of the hills with the desperate grace of men who understood that standing meant dying, using the terrain's natural folds to shield themselves from further volleys that would turn flesh to ruin.

Vector's speed tore across the ground, each footfall barely touching earth before launching him forward again, wind snapping at his cloak like living things trying to slow his advance.

Fast.

Among the fastest there, a blur of motion that defied what First Stage Warriors should achieve.

…

Rhea's eyes locked onto Vector's form as he tore across the terrain, his silhouette blurring with motion that defied everything she thought she knew.

Faster than Tristan.

Tristan—who had always been the fastest among them, whose legs had carried messages across battlefields when speed meant the difference between victory and massacre, whose very nickname among the Resistance was "Wind's Shadow."

Yet Vector outpaced him as though Tristan was standing still.

Sebastian followed close behind Vector's wake with that same terrible, effortless grace.

She chose to ignore Sebastian.

Had to.

He was out of their league, existing in a realm of power where comparison became meaningless—a Third Stage Warrior who moved through combat like water through cracks, inevitable and unstoppable.

But Vector—

Vector's performance was something else entirely.

Impossible.

Unprecedented.

His figure had already reached the carts while the rest of them were still closing half the distance, his dark silhouette resolving against the flickering torchlight like a shadow given form and purpose.

The evidence assembled itself in Rhea's mind with the cold precision of someone trained to read battlefields and extract truth from chaos. The previous handling of the bow, the volley of arrows—his shot flying faster than any First-stage Warrior should achieve, the string singing a note too high, too sharp. And now this speed, this explosive sprint that ate ground like he weighed nothing, like gravity itself had loosened its grip on him alone.

It confirmed what she'd begun to suspect.

Vector had broken through.

And not just barely.

The strength he was displaying—the raw, physical capability radiating from every movement—was even faster than her own carefully honed speed, faster than anyone who should rightfully claim the title of First Stage Warrior.

Her breath caught.

As she and the others finally reached the vicinity of the two carts, boots pounding earth in synchronized rhythm, weapons drawn and ready for the brutal intimacy of close combat—

She saw it.

Vector was already engaged.

Already fighting.

A First-stage Warrior from the convoy had emerged from behind one of the carts, blade flashing in torchlight as he met Vector's charge with the desperate ferocity of a man who knew his life hung by threads growing thinner with each heartbeat.
