Soft, small steps echoed through the nighttime alley. Lights shone overhead all across Annesheim. The sound of mechanical carts traversing the streets, along with the swell of people enjoying their nightly strolls, was typical for the capital.

Loud voices filled with laughter, anger, camaraderie, and melancholy could all be heard spilling from one particular building near the west sector.

Two guards patrolled along the sidewalk, making their usual rounds for the night. But then both of them stopped once they heard the sound of small steps running through the nearby alleyway.

As they stared into the dark alley, the steps seemed to stop.

“Hmm… we already know you’re there. Just come out,” one of them said.

For a short moment, there was nothing but the silent ambience of the city around them. Then someone emerged from the shadows.

A young girl wearing a dark cloak stepped out. Her hood was raised, obscuring most of her face.

The guards glanced at one another. It was that kind of knowing expression, one that spoke volumes. They had been through this exact scenario over and over before.

The first man sighed before turning his gaze back to the child. “You know the deal. Watch, but don’t play. And we pretend we never saw you.”

The young girl nodded fervently before dashing down the sidewalk toward the next building over. She pushed open the doors and entered without issue.

Both guards watched her go, then resumed their patrol.

“Say, you don’t think we’ll get in trouble for this, do you?” the second man asked.

The first man shrugged. “We can just say she knows our routes and happens to go in whenever we aren’t here.”

“But what if she eventually gets caught somewhere?”

“First off, she’s sneaking out by herself anyway. She won’t tell anyone. Just let the kid have fun,” the first man replied. He kept his gaze straight as they continued down the street. “And second, there are all sorts of rumors about her. Just consider this us doing her a favor. No need to dig any deeper.”

“Mm… if you say so.”

Inside, there were plenty of men and women alike, all standing around tables. Some gathered in large groups, others in smaller ones. Each person was engrossed in whatever was happening right in front of them. Drinks were carried out by workers dressed in black and white. The smell of alcohol and pipe smoke drifted through the den, even though smoking was restricted to certain areas.

The young girl sped past them all, already knowing exactly where she wanted to go.

As she ran deeper inside, many glanced over and spotted her. However, most of them were regulars. They would recognize her anywhere. Especially since she was the only child who ever came to such a place at night.

“Haha! Four of hearts. That’s a triple hand!” a voice boomed. It was deep, but carried a hearty tone, like an old grandfather sitting by a fireplace.

The man reached forward, scooping his chips back to his side with a grin on his face. He looked to be in his late forties or early fifties. A scar crossed his cheek, partially obscured by a neatly trimmed beard that extended from his jawline to his ears. He was a large man, but not overweight. The type of build reminiscent of a strong farmhand who had spent most of his life doing laborious work.

“Alright. Next game!” said the dealer, a straight-backed, tall man in a gray and black suit with a long, thin mustache. He collected the cards from every player and began shuffling them. “Make your bets!”

Voices around the table spoke up. A woman said two hundred. Another man pushed forward five black, hundred-vix chips.

The man from earlier pushed forward seven hundred. “Seven hundred. Gotta make back my loss,” he said with a grin, leaning forward with his elbows on the playing table.

Just as the dealer began distributing the cards to each player, the man noticed something off to his side. Two small hands gripped the edge of the table, along with a piece of dark cloth poking up between them.

“Heh.”

He reached over and pulled down the girl’s hood. “Came here today, huh, Vee?”

Veronica looked up at Victor and nodded. “I spent all day studying. I wanted to leave the academy.”

Victor gave a half-hearted chuckle. “That’s no good, Vee. Sneaking out like that’ll make you a future troublemaker.”

“They don’t let me out if I don’t sneak out,” Veronica said. She turned her head back toward the table. “Are you winning?”

Victor clicked his tongue, looking at the cards he had just been dealt. “Down a few hundred. Not too bad.”

Veronica hummed, watching the game play out silently.

“Ace of hearts. Ace of spades,” one man called, laying his cards down.

“Double spades,” a woman replied, placing her cards down incredibly slowly as she measured everyone’s expression.

Victor kept his cards close, humming as he saw the rest of the hands.

“One, three, five. Triple jumps,” he said, placing down a one of diamonds, three of diamonds, and a five of spades.

“Already tossing out three cards like that, Victor?” asked the man.

“I don’t believe for a second any of you have anything better, Benton,” Victor replied.

The dealer looked around at each player, waiting for any additional moves. “Round won by Victor!” he announced.

He reached forward, grabbing everyone’s cards and shuffling them back into the deck. He then handed the same number of cards back to everyone, except for Victor. He only received two cards back, since he had won with three. Every other player now had five cards, while he only had four.

“I hope you win,” Veronica said quietly.

Victor glanced down at her and placed his hand on top of her head, ruffling her hair. “I hope I win too, Vee. Work’s been a pain in the ass lately. I’m looking for some good karma to reach me tonight.”

Veronica frowned, reaching up to fix her hair.

Time passed as Victor sat at the table, continuing to play his game of cards. Many of the other players changed games, and new players came by. Even the dealer had a shift change.

Despite staying there for nearly two hours, neither Victor nor Veronica was bored. Victor enjoyed gambling. Even when he lost, his smile never faded, because all he needed was that one good hand. Those consecutive hits to win it big.

And Veronica was mesmerized by the game. How even when Victor lost, he never lost his cheerful spirit.

Eventually, a new man came by, sitting down near the end of the table.

The first few rounds continued like normal. Victor lost one round, but won the next two. But as the second game started, something changed.

As Veronica watched the cards being dealt out, she caught sight of something odd.

It was faint. So faint that she almost missed it.

A tiny ripple of mana brushed against the air near the end of the table. It came and went so quickly that she wasn’t sure if it had truly happened. But Veronica had spent enough time studying mana to recognize when something shifted.

Her eyes slowly moved toward the new man.

He sat with one elbow on the table, fingers resting loosely over his cards. He didn’t look nervous. If anything, he looked bored. His dark hair was slicked back, his clothes clean and expensive enough to fit in, but not fine enough to stand out.

Victor glanced at his cards and hummed. “Not bad.”

The game continued as usual.

A woman placed down two cards. “Double queens.”

“Pair of fives,” another man said, tossing his cards forward with a grimace.

Victor leaned back, tapping the edge of the table with his finger. “Pass.”

“Triple kings.”

The cards landed neatly on the table.

Three black kings.

A low murmur moved around the players. Several people leaned in slightly, while the dealer’s eyes sharpened.

Veronica stared at the cards.

They looked normal. The backs were the same as every other card in the deck. The faces were clean, the edges worn in the same way as the rest. But there was something laced through them. A hint of mana that seemed to permeate from the cards themselves.

Her brows lowered.

She didn’t know what it was—not exactly. After all, she had only been studying magic that could destroy things. She had no knowledge of other spells. It just wasn’t something she could recognize. But it felt deliberate.

The new man reached forward, already preparing to drag the winnings toward himself.

Veronica lifted her hand and pointed at him.

“That man is cheating.”

The table went silent. Several nearby conversations died at once. People turned their heads, looking first at Veronica, then at the man she had pointed at.

Victor looked down at her. “Vee?”

The new man’s smile vanished. “The hell is that brat talking about? I haven’t even done anything.”

Victor’s expression shifted slightly, the warmth in his eyes fading into something more critical. “Why do you think he’s cheating?”

Veronica kept her finger raised. “The cards feel strange.”

The man scoffed. “The cards feel strange? What does that even mean?”

Veronica’s eyes stayed on the three black kings. “I think it might be magic.”

Another murmur spread around the table.

The dealer’s hand froze above the deck.

The new man’s face tightened. “What the hell does a little kid like you know about magic?”

Veronica didn’t answer.

The man scowled harder, then snatched the three cards from the table and shoved them toward the dealer. “Here. Look at them. Go on.”

The dealer took the cards and inspected them carefully. He turned them over, bent them slightly, checked the edges, then held them up beneath the light.

After a few moments, he frowned. “I don’t see anything wrong.”

“See?” the man said, throwing his hand outward. “Nothing. Some stupid child points at me, and suddenly everyone wants to act like I’m cheating?”

The dealer hesitated. “If it’s an accusation of magic, I’ll have to call over an inspector. Just in case.”

The man’s eyes twitched. “An inspector? You’re really going to honor some idiot child’s accusation?”

“It’s house policy,” the dealer replied calmly. “Since there’s been an accusation, it’s my job to make sure it is resolved.”

The man clicked his tongue, brows furrowing. He looked around the table, searching for someone who would agree with him, or at least someone annoyed enough to complain about the game being delayed. While some people did look irritated, no one spoke up. Everyone simply stilled, watching him, curious to see how the situation would end.

A man in a dark blue vest began walking over from the far side of the room. He had a narrow case in one hand and a silver badge pinned to his chest.

The man noticed him and clenched his jaw. “This is ridiculous,” he muttered.

He suddenly stood, chair scraping loudly against the floor. Before anyone could react, he came over, reached down, and grabbed Veronica by the collar of her cloak.

Victor’s chair slammed back as he stood.

The man lifted Veronica into the air, holding her up in front of the table like a badly behaved cat. “You little brat,” he hissed. “You think you can just open your mouth and accuse people? You are coming with me to apologize for wasting everybody’s time.”

Veronica’s hands grabbed at his wrist, feet kicking slightly above the floor.

The table around them erupted in shouts.

“Hey!”

“Put her down!”

“The hell do you think you are doing?”

The man glared back at the rest of them. “What am I doing? Why the hell are you all sticking up for a kid who’s not even supposed to be in a gambling den? Do you all want to be arrested?” He looked back at Veronica, staring directly into her eyes. “I wonder what the guards or your parents will have to say if they see you hiding and sneaking around in here.”

Victor’s hand clamped down on the man’s forearm with enough force to make the man acknowledge him.

“Let her down,” Victor said. His voice was low. Dangerously so.

The man glared at him. “Yeah? This your snot-nosed daughter ruining everybody’s night?”

Victor didn’t answer. Instead, he tightened his grip on the man’s arm.

The man winced, but his anger only grew. He released Veronica with a sharp shove.

She fell to the floor, landing hard on her back. Pain shot through her body. She let out a small sound and curled slightly, arching her back as she tried to roll onto her side.

Victor glanced down.

Veronica was trying not to cry, but a tear had already gathered at the corner of her eye.

For a moment, Victor said nothing. He simply stared back at the man, his arm still caught in his grip.

The man sneered at him. “You should teach your daughter better manners. Because of her, you’re going to get locked up for sneaking a child into a gambling den. Annesheim is pretty strict about its laws, ain’t it?”

The smile that was usually on Victor’s face was gone. He didn’t blink even once as he kept his gaze fixed on the man.

The man noticed too late.

Victor’s fist crashed into his cheek. The sound cracked across the table and echoed through the room.

The man’s head snapped sideways, spit flying from his mouth as his body twisted off balance. He stumbled into the table, knocking chips and cards across the floor as his arm searched for anything to grab onto. He landed hard on his back as chips continued rolling off the table and scattering around him.

For a moment, no one spoke.

Victor looked down at Veronica, crouching beside her. He offered her a hand.

“Vee,” he said, his voice gentle again. “You alright?”

Veronica was about to take Victor’s hand, but from the corner of her vision, she spotted the man slowly getting up.

“You son of a bitch—!” he muttered through gritted teeth.

He reached toward the ground beside him, picked up a black chip, and threw it at Victor.

Victor noticed and raised his arm to block, until he realized it was only a casino chip. He simply stared at the man with his unrelenting gaze as the chip struck him.

But then he felt Veronica’s hand tremble in his. He looked back down at her. “What’s wrong, Vee?”

Veronica’s eyes were wide, staring at his side.

As his gaze followed hers, looking toward the side of his stomach, he finally noticed it.

In those few short moments, the space around the hole shuddered. That was when the real injury appeared. There was a small hole in his tunic. Blood poured down his side where the chip had struck him

“Victor!” Veronica yelled.

Victor clutched his side, fingers touching the blood-stained dagger that had just stabbed him.
