The wind rose like a tide across Helheim.

Ash that had lain still for ages lifted in sheets and drifted over the bone shores. Far out, the corpse ships rocked as if something vast had stirred beneath the black sea. From the edge of her throne room, Hela felt it first. She rose without hurry and crossed to the balcony. Shadow still covered half her face. The other half gave nothing away.

She raised a hand to calm the wind of her own realm. Nothing changed. It shouldn't have been possible.

So she reached for the guardian responsible for the wind. Before she could make contact, a voice found her first, a broken echo, more plea than cry. Hraesvelgr, the great bird that held the winds of all nine realms, was whispering.

"Hel... me... my queen... my mind..."

Then another voice cut in, one Hela had never heard before.

"Shut up. You're mine now. That's what you get for swallowing me."

Hela didn't listen. Not to the plea, and not to the stranger. She should have been wary, should have gone to see what was really happening. Instead she turned back into the dark, drawn to a mirror where her brother and the person she hated most were together.

On the ridge, Fenrir's second howl rang out.

He came over the far hill at full size, iron-dark and vast enough to blot out the gray sky. He could see them both, Loki and Baldr, smaller than ants from where he stood, and he hated stepping on ants. Mid-leap, his body folded in on itself. Muscle and fur compressed with a sound like grinding stone. By the time his paws struck the black rock in front of Loki and the fallen Baldr, he stood only nine feet tall, still a wolf who could look a goddess in the eye and make her measure every step.

Baldr lay where Loki had left him, his golden light reduced to a sickly smear. Fenrir's gaze went to him first.

"You... called me?" The voice was gravel dragged across iron. "How dare you. I am Fenrir of Helheim, brother of Hela, the Vanargand." The last word became a roar, and he let it roll on until it reached Loki too.

He didn't wait for an answer. His jaws closed on Baldr's shoulder and shook him hard. The god of light was thrashed once, twice, then flung across the ridge like a scrap of meat. Baldr hit the stone and didn't get up. What was left of his light flickered once more and went out.

Loki heard it all too clearly, the crunch of teeth through meat and bone. She had known the curse wouldn't kill him. After this, she wasn't so sure.

Only then did Fenrir turn fully to Loki.

She had already backed away several paces. Fighting the beast in front of her was pointless, she knew that, so she let the spell keep weaving behind her back, green threads tightening in silence, ready for the moment she'd have to run.

"Is it really you?" Fenrir asked.

Loki swallowed hard and nodded once.

Fenrir didn't buy it. He wasn't about to be fooled for the hundredth time, so he raised a paw. Debris from the cracked ridge lifted and came down on her in a hard rain of stone and ash. An illusion would have scattered. A clone would have vanished. But Loki was driven to one knee, coughing, and she stayed. Real flesh. Real blood where a rock had opened her cheek.

Fenrir's ears flicked. His eyes went wide. "You're... really here?"

He closed the distance until his muzzle was almost against her face, and sniffed. That was the last proof he needed. His teeth showed. The growl that left him was low enough to rattle Loki's ribs. She finished the last knot of the spell and held it ready, every muscle braced for the same thrashing Baldr had taken.

She held her breath and shut her eyes. Instead, Fenrir stepped back and sat. It was a slow, deliberate movement, almost formal.

"Why are you here?" he asked.

She didn't answer right away. The one who could shake the nine realms and impale Odin himself was sitting in front of her, not attacking, willing to hear her out. It was one of the few things she had never seen coming.

Loki rose carefully, the spell still ready in her hands. A thin thread of fear showed in her eyes, but her voice stayed level.

"I came to free you. Both of you."

Fenrir's lip curled. "Your lies haven't gotten any better. What did that shitty king of kings ask of you this time? Did Odin send you to kill me and Hela? Is he still afraid we'll bring Ragnarok to his doorstep?"

The wind strengthened again, carrying the faint scent of a rising tide.

"I don't follow Odin like I used to," Loki said. "Nobody sent me. You have my word."

Fenrir's eyes ignited.

Loki saw it and backed away quickly. It did no good. The enchantment he had learned at her knee, truth-binding, exact and merciless, snapped into place around her throat like a collar.

"Why did you come?"

Her jaw locked. She fought it for three full seconds, as she had once taught him to fight it when he was small, but Fenrir was stronger than she would ever be.

"No... Fenrir."

"Tell me."

She resisted. Her throat and tongue burned from the inside.

"My... son, please."

"TELL ME!"

In the end, the compulsion won.

"I came for a human! A human named Barry."

The silence that followed landed harder than the debris had.

Fenrir's fur lifted in the rising wind. Rage rolled through him in a visible wave, and his body began swelling back toward its true size.

"Centuries," he said, his voice climbing. "You come back after centuries, and it's not for your daughter. Not for me. For. One. Human."

"Wait, wait, my son, let me expl—"

He cut her off by launching himself at her.

Her blink spell took effect in that same instant. She vanished a heartbeat before his jaws closed and reappeared fifty paces away, on higher ground, where she could watch her own son devour an entire hill just to get at her. One of her eyes went wet. She didn't wipe it. She couldn't.

"My son," she called, her voice carrying cleanly over the wind. "Soon I will be queen of all the realms. And when I am, I'll set you both free from this place. That's my promise to you, Fenrir."

She finally wiped away the tear that had run down her cheek.

Fenrir's body surged outward again, fur and muscle flooding out until he stood at his true height. The ridge groaned beneath him. The realm itself began to crack, straining to contain what he was becoming.

"Don't drag me and my sister into your ambitions!" he roared. "They're yours alone."

He bit down again. The whole hillock where Loki had stood vanished into his mouth in one clean motion, stone and ash together. When he lifted his head, she was already gone.

Loki was far away by then, and her divinity was running low. This wasn't Asgard, where she could jump and blink whenever she pleased. This realm hated her as much as her children did. She turned toward the colossal shape of Hraesvelgr and started flying toward it.

"I will save you both," she said into the wind, "before Ragnarok, before the end. And Barry... you will be the one to fix all of it."

Behind her, Fenrir's growl rolled across the plains like distant thunder. The wind kept rising.

Inside the skull, the pressure changed.

Barry felt the last membrane give way under the claws of the few hunters still alive. He shoved through a curtain of enchanted tissue that made one final attempt to reject him, and stepped out into open space that had no right to exist inside a living creature.

Hraesvelgr's brain wasn't meat, not in any ordinary sense. It was layers of light and slow-moving current, with chambers the size of great halls and runes drifting loose through the air like living script.

He stood on a platform of dense white matter and felt the whole structure notice him, the way a machine registers a new input. One look was enough. This was the power source for everything, the main mechanism that gave air and wind to all nine realms.

Barry smiled, small and sharp. "Jackpot."

The rest of the parasites poured in behind him and scattered at once, antennae tasting the air. A few of the bigger hunters tried to approach the floating runes, and something invisible pushed them back, gently but firmly.

Barry walked forward. Every step sent soft ripples through the light. He could feel the winds of the nine realms moving in time with the slow pulse around him. Hraesvelgr's consciousness was vast, and for the moment it was distracted, focused on the chaos rising outside and on the plea it had already sent.

Barry could see how it all flowed. It worked like any other brain. The only difference was that this one was compiled out of spellcraft, layer on layer, and he had plenty of experience breaking that stuff down and rebuilding it his way.

He raised one clawed hand and pressed it against the nearest current. "Hello, oversized feather duster. I'm the new maintenance department, and you're going to hate me."

A System notification flickered at the edge of his vision.

[Book analysis: 99.99% > 100%]

[Book analysis fully complete. Would you like me to summarize the forbidden ancient series?]

Barry was about to ignore it. Then the word "forbidden" caught his attention.

"I'll give you a shot, System. Let's see how well you read those shitty books Odin tried so hard to hide from everyone."

[Request accepted]

[Forbidden series opened]

Barry's eyes went wide as he read what the blue screen scribbled out.

"Holy shit..."
