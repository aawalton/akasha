Chapter 282

The gigantic shield surrounding the whole inner space erupted from the equatorial Aura Ring, growing into massive hexagons that vaulted upward and downward with leaps of solid energy. At the same time, the data lines coming from the center of the space began to glow with powerful magic, ready to divert as much power as needed to fuel the shield, drawing from the Origin System, from the Domains and from all the infrastructure of the inner space itself.

A second layer of hexagons started to manifest, tinted slightly orange compared to the piercing blue of the first shield. This second shield was slightly further away from the center than the first, and rather than growing from the ring, its hexagons expanded outward from every single data line that plunged into the roiling clouds of chaos outside. As each hexagon grew, the part of the data line that disappeared into the chaos also dimmed, temporarily cut off from the main system.

Michael and Icarus took position near one of the massive hexagons, the closest to the source of the signal from the incoming MIF Garden. Around them floated all sorts of weapon stations, as well as repurposed data lines arranged in a funnel leading directly toward the black hole, cutting off any other escape route into the inner space.

They nodded at each other, and the blue hexagon dimmed and disappeared like burning paper. The orange one outside followed soon after, and they found themselves staring at the hypnotic deep purple of the clouds.

“The signal is getting stronger, Michael,” Icarus said, tensing up like a coiled spring.

More Icaruses were coming from all corners of the space, other variants dressed in different clothes and sporting different strange features.

“We should have contact in three seconds,” the one closest to Michael said.

He saw them tense. The others flew into a formation. Some went to man the many defensive stations they had brought to the shield, while others were summoning massive circles of datascript or commandeering entire pillars of data lines that glowed with magic. This was an all hands on deck situation, it seemed.

“Two…”

The magic intensified, becoming almost solid. Even the empty space thrummed with unspent potential, and the look in the eyes of the many versions of Icarus was of utter, deadly concentration.

“One…”

Michael’s body language was the opposite. He was relaxed, hovering in space with a tranquil look on his face. His eyes drifted over the clouds, as if seeing something inside them rather than being forced to behold the ever-changing purple mists and nothing else.

Next to him, Icarus’s eyes glinted with the light reflected from the two-layered shield. The AI did not look into the clouds, he noticed. Rather, their gaze scanned the empty space right in front of them.

“They are here.”

The ship emerged from the void clouds at great speed. There was no shield surrounding it, and the purple corrupting energies were following it, like smoke still clinging to a person leaving a foggy room. Behind the ship, things gave chase. The creatures were many, screeching and moving like fluid, a mass of appendages and too many eyes.

Icarus opened fire. In a millionth of a second, a monumental amount of magic was directed with surgical precision at the incoming enemies. Lasers targeted the ship, where some of the creatures had managed to latch onto the hull, and they burned the monsters to a crisp. Missiles flew into the void, where explosions rang like great battle drums and lit the clouds from the inside. Bullets rained through the open hole in the shield, together with spears of all colors, pure solid magic, and spells too complex to even comprehend.

Then something big began to emerge from the clouds. The ship was through, and the shield was being raised again, the hexagon-shaped field growing inward from the edges. As the clouds finally parted, out came a giant face with eyes and tentacles and flowing appendages of metal. Magic pelted it, summoned from all sorts of skills under the Origin System, from the artillery stations and space lasers, mass drivers and datascript circles bigger than planets.

Icarus, or rather the many instances of them, were at the center of it all, angels of death and demons of pure battle. Their faces were masks of cold, calculating indifference as they analyzed the threat, found its weaknesses, and fired more and more magic at it. The giant face went from being able to shrug off most of the attacks to having to actually defend itself, sacrificing tentacles and appendages just to make sure that it could reach the closing shield.

It raced toward the shield, but was too slow. The last hexagon snapped close, first the blue one inside and then the orange one outside, cutting off the tip of some of the face’s tentacles. They were bigger than the MIF Garden itself, which was a miles long ship designed to hold the legacy of a whole race.

The face recoiled in pain, its scream not reaching inside the shield but powerful enough to temporarily disperse the clouds of void to reveal a body more massive than a solar system. It bashed its appendages against the shield many times, making several hexagons glow with strain.

“Thank you for opening the shield for us,” came the communication from the ship.

Michael saw that Icarus was not turning their attention away from the shield, not even for a moment. He did, putting the brightening orange hexagons of the second layer of the shield behind him as the massive monster outside kept slamming its enormous tentacles against it.

Nodding at the hologram in front of him, he greeted the pale elf. “Captain Elù-ar, or should I say Void-Alfyr Elù-ar?”

The elf’s eyes widened in surprise. “I am flattered by the honor you bestow upon me, Lord Michael.”

Michael’s lips quirked up when he heard how the space elf had just called him. Just like he had surprised Elù-ar by calling him Void-Alfyr, he too was surprised to be called lord by yet another one of his underlings. Although, lately, he had found himself more and more accepting of the title, especially because it wasn’t something he ever forced upon others, but rather came from them.

“I certainly could not call you Tree-Alfyr like that old codger, could I?” he joked.

“I would have accepted any title,” the elf said. His face, however, told Michael the actual truth.

“So,” Michael began lazily, only for his gaze to sharpen. “What the fuck is that thing that’s still trying to bash its way into my inner space?”

“We were trying to–” the elf began, but then Michael interrupted him.

“One moment,” he said. “Icarus, please make that thing stop. It’s distracting me and it’s drawing a lot of energy.”

“Does it mean I can use the thing?” they replied, their face morphing from deadly focus to the sparkling eyes of a puppy.

Michael sighed. “Yes, you can use the thing.”

“Yay!” they exclaimed.

Before they were even done celebrating, the Aura Ring began to light up. Dozens, hundreds of Icaruses flew to it, and a massive datascript circle began to manifest from where it lay dormant within the circuits of the ring. One line after the other, it reached maximum power. The brightness increased, becoming blinding, until it finally exploded one last time.

Darkness followed. For an instant, the only sources of light in the whole inner space were the two layers of shield and the spiral black hole at its center. Everything else was dim, its power diverted somewhere else.

Then, two beams of unfathomable power shot out from the north and south poles of the black hole. They grew in thickness as they travelled, their power harnessed and redirected by the datascript circle that had summoned them. When they passed by Michael, he felt inside them the concentrated power of all the tiers of magic up to Renegade.

They shot past him, went through the shield, and hit the monster square in the face, swallowing everything in the sharp light of magic, powerful enough to obliterate anything. There was the sound of glass breaking, and a loud roar. Then, when the light finally receded and silence descended all around them, they saw that there was a gaping hole in the clouds of void that was slowly filling back in.

One of the orange hexagons was also missing. The ones close to it were blindingly bright, taxed almost to the point of overloading. Only minutes later did the missing hexagon finally start to repair itself. It was excruciatingly slow, and they watched it as it returned to its normal state, piece by piece.

“Michael,” Icarus said. Their voice was shaking. “Whatever that monster was, it survived the full might of the beam for 1.34 seconds. It even managed to break the shield! What if… what if–”

“It’s dead now,” Michael replied. He gave Icarus a head pat. “Good job.”

“Yes but what if it got in? What if instead of stabbing me, the Renegade had stabbed you instead?”

“What are you talking about?” Michael asked, frowning. Icarus’s whole body was shaking. “Relax, you’re hyperventilating. It’s a panic attack. Hey, listen to my voice. Breathe in… breathe out…” as he spoke, gently hypnotizing Icarus by lacing magic into his voice, he moved close to them and took their hand. “It’s okay, Icarus mine,” he said, stroking their hair with his other hand, sending more of his magic through Icarus’s body and feeling them slowly relax. “The monster is gone. The Renegade is dead and cannot hurt me. You were very cool, you know that?”

There was another pulse of soothing magic. Icarus looked at him with big, emerald eyes. “Was I?” they asked.

“Yes,” he said. “A million of you flying around, destroying monsters with powerful magic? You’re badass.”

“Your badass.” They smiled, their panic momentarily forgotten.

“Go rest, little extreme intelligence. I reckon it was the first time you got scared shitless, wasn’t it?”

Icarus nodded meekly. “How can you be so relaxed, Michael?”

“You just get used to it. Plus,” he winked. “I knew you had it. Nothing can go wrong when we are together, you hear me? Rest now.”

“Can you come with me? I don’t want to be alone,” they said in a tiny voice.

Michael paused. He knew that Icarus was never really alone, given that they always had a billion conversations going on with a billion people all over the world at any given time. For many of these people, they–under the guise of the now ubiquitous Daedalus AI chatbot and assistant–had become closer than a best friend.

“Of course, Icarus mine. Elù-ar, is the ship in good enough condition to wait until tomorrow?”

“It is, Lord Michael. Please do not let our presence distract you.”

“I’m sending information to your AI, Gaia, about where you can find some resources to initiate repairs and restock. Later, if Icarus wills it, an instance of them will come talk to you. Otherwise, I shall speak with you tomorrow.”

“Very well. Thank you, Lord Michael.”
