The open fields stretched before her, tall grass swaying gently in the cool breeze.

Elynara moved with a spring in her step, her basket already half-filled with the herbs the request asked for. And she owed much of that success to the little green fuzzball flitting around her like an overexcited puppy.

The sprite seemed to take to the task with genuine enthusiasm, darting from patch to patch and buzzing happily whenever it found what she was looking for.

It even did a little twirl each time she plucked a batch of herbs, as if to say, ‘Look how good I am!’

Elynara couldn’t help but laugh at its antics.

“You’re really enjoying yourself, aren’t you?”

The sprite bobbed up and down in response, then zipped off to another patch of grass. The way it weaved through the air, skillfully dodging leaves and darting between stems, made her wonder if it was playing a game all on its own.

“Alright, alright. I see it. You’re the best little herb finder there is.”

It buzzed proudly, the green glow of its form flaring up in excitement.

Despite how sore her legs felt, the sprite’s enthusiasm made the task feel much less like work. She couldn’t help but let her mood lift as the basket grew heavier with each passing minute.

Despite all the things her rather holey memory was telling her were off, and even Celestia’s words about the world slowly dying off… Elynara couldn’t help but become more and more enamoured with it, each passing moment its own little wonder.

She smiled.

This place, the sprite, the people… and this world too. It was worth caring for.

And she would enjoy every moment of it she possibly could!

The task itself took less time than she expected, the gathering going smoothly with the sprite’s guidance. It only took her a couple of hours to gather everything she was commissioned for.

The rest of the day was now hers to spend as she pleased.

How lovely!

After finding herself a cozy patch of grass under the shade of a tree, Elynara sat down with a sigh of relief.

Her legs throbbed from the walking and crouching, but the cool breeze against her skin made the soreness feel distant. The town’s walls loomed in the distance, but out here, it felt peaceful.

She sat there for a bit, just resting and enjoying the sights laid out before her, watching the slightly swaying grass in the distance, and the sprite excitedly flying all around.

Letting her thoughts drift with the wind, she absentmindedly stroked the side of her basket.

The past few days had been a whirlwind. From waking up with nothing but fragments of memories and a new name, to arriving at a town full of people who seemed both welcoming and wary.

And then there was the mana. That strange, wonderful force she could feel but hardly understand. The entire world seemed to hum with it, even if only faintly.

Her curiosity burned brighter than any concern. Everything about this world, from the clear decay, the signs of wear and tear on buildings and people’s faces, and even in the face of incredulity that was the Broken Moon – it all felt so secondary to the discovery of mana itself.

She glanced at the sprite, who had settled down on a nearby leaf, bobbing slightly as if it too was resting.

“You really are such an odd little thing.”

Its glow pulsed in response to her soft muttering, though whether it fully understood her or not remained a mystery.

Turning her attention away from it, she once again focused on herself.

There was no sense in wasting time. Might as well keep training while she has the chance.

Taking a deep breath, she called up her status.

Mana: 15/20 (+1.3/h)

Her regeneration rate hadn’t changed much. It still came as 1.0/h from herself but it quickly became clear the environment impacted it too, with just 0.3/h from it here.

And though she only had her gut feeling to go by, because of course she forgot to check, but the town’s mana felt much thinner than even here.

Yet it was still less than what it was on her way to town.

“Maybe it only goes up when I’m closer to something like the altar…”

She shook her head suddenly. There was no point in getting caught up in theories, she had some practice to do!

Shutting her eyes, Elynara focused on trying to feel the mana again. It came to her much easier than before, though she figured it will take her a lot more practice before it becomes her second nature.

She could hardly wait! And her excitement did not break her hold this time either.

Progress!

Reeling in her excitement, she felt around for the mana strands. They were much thinner here than at the altar, but there was still enough to work with.

Drawing them in, she kept on grabbing and filling her core with them, until the familiar sense of discomfort warned her to stop.

Gritting her teeth, she let the mana accumulate beyond what felt comfortable. It was like overfilling a cup, the sensation both thrilling and sickening at once.

She held it. Breathed through the sensation. And when she couldn’t take it anymore, she let the mana flow back out, in a long, slow exhale.

The mana still buzzed faintly within her chest, the aftershocks of that overfill lingering in her limbs.

Taking a deep breath and exhaling slowly, she let the quiet stretch for a bit, simply basking in that warmth for a while longer. It wasn’t long before she let herself settle back into the rhythm once more.

Another round. Another fill.

It took a little longer this time, her core seemed a little slower to accept the flow, but not by much. She didn’t push quite as hard as before, merely enough to reach the edge of discomfort.

Elynara tilted her head slightly, squinting at nothing in particular.

“Huh. Feels like I can cram a little more now.”

She smiled at the thought, unsure whether it was just her imagination or something real, but deciding it didn’t matter either way. Even a hint of progress was enough, and the exercise was becoming increasingly easier to boot!

That little flicker of excitement stirred her memory, bringing back a dozen small ideas she had meant to try since she got here, but never quite got the time!

“The shape and flow. I still haven’t figured out how best to hold it, or what happens if I twist it mid-cast…”

After muttering so, she sat up a little more straight, considering what to start with.

Cupping her hands, she drew out a small amount of mana, barely enough to form a dim sphere between her palms. Shifting its shape, she sort of just let it stretch into a ring, then into a flat disc, then willed it to coil like a loose strand of thread.

Her attempts were all a little wobbly, and the last one felt like it was on the very edge of collapse, but they held!

Satisfied for now, she let the mana dissolve.

Next was an idea she had on her very first night in this wonderful new world. She wondered then if it would be possible to get the fire going using mana alone.

And it was time to find out!

She started by taking a little bit of mana and rubbing it against itself. The image of her frustrated attempt with the stones the other night came to mind, and she couldn’t help the small grin that crept up her face.

“Let’s see if you’re more reliable than flint.”

She pulled together another bit of mana, pressing two small flickers against one another until they met with resistance. Slowly pushing harder, she rotated one strand around the other. The friction sparked faintly at first, then flickered out with a harmless wisp of light.

Still not hot enough, but promising.

Another attempt was about to begin when a blur of green zipped through her focus, scattering the forming strands like leaves caught in the wind.

“Hey!”

The sprite twirled in place, unrepentant, before diving back toward her next conjured shape. It slipped through the forming orb like it was a hoop, then darted away in a zigzag, buzzing with barely contained glee.

Elynara puffed out her cheeks, raising her hand with mock scolding intent.

“You little thief! I was using that.”

The sprite spun in midair, then zipped right up to her nose before darting backward, practically challenging her.

She exhaled a sharp sigh, and then laughed at its antics.

“Fine. You want to play?”

Mana flickered back into her fingers, and she shaped it into a loose arc midair, wide enough for the sprite to fly through.

It didn’t even wait for an invitation, just zipped straight into the curve, spinning like it was part of some invisible obstacle course!

More hoops followed, then a wave-shaped ridge, then a twirling helix.

The sprite darted and dove, weaving through every one like it had been waiting for this all day long. And maybe it had.

Elynara grinned, a lightness blooming in her chest she hadn’t realized she had been missing.

Throwing tiny mana orbs in short arcs, she watched the sprite chase after each one, bumping them midair and sending them spinning off course – only to chase them again!

The little green glow pulsed with delight, circling her head with a musical hum.

It was while she watched it flicker by for the fifth time that the thought struck her.

“I have yet to give you a name, haven’t I?”

The sprite froze in midair, tilting just slightly toward her, as if curious.

She squinted at it thoughtfully.

“I mean, I only just got my name, so it’s not like I’ve had it forever. But still. You’ve been with me from the start.”

She considered it seriously… for about ten seconds.

“Leaf? No. Buzzlet? Ugh. Spiritwig? What am I, a druid?”

The sprite hovered, patiently unimpressed.

Finally, she smirked.

“Fine. You’re a puff. And I met you just after I got here from traveling the stars. So… you shall be Starpuff.”

The green light flared brighter for a moment, then spun in a tight loop and nuzzled her cheek.

She laughed at its behavior, reaching up to gently boop it back.

“Well… I’ll take that as approval.”

Letting the rest of her conjured shapes dissolve, she laid back in the grass, watching Starpuff drift lazily above her.

“I have so many things I want to try. But I’m going to forget half of them if I don’t write them down…”

She turned her head to the side.

“I need a notebook. Or a diary. Or something.”

The breeze stirred her hair. Starpuff nestled against her shoulder, content.

And for the first time in a while, Elynara simply let herself rest.

It was nearly an hour later when she found her thoughts drifting back to her new favorite activity. Lazily playing with her mana again, she remembered to check her status again.

Yep. Nothing has changed. The same level, the same skills. It was actually quite easy to forget it was even there without the blinking dot trying to grab her attention.

It was only when she looked at her mana levels again when she noticed a difference.

Mana: 16/21 (+1.3/h)

Excitement flooded through her, and she was quickly seated once again.

Her exercises have indeed borne fruit!

Without hesitation, she tried again. Filling her core until it felt strained, then letting it trickle out. The process became a rhythm, a strange kind of meditation that left her both drained and exhilarated.

But there was only so much her body could take. By the time her mana capacity increased to 23, she could feel the dull throb of exhaustion settling in. Her head started to ache, and her limbs felt heavy.

“Okay… Maybe that’s enough for now.”

She let her body relax, her breathing slowly returning to normal.

The sprite had been watching her with what looked like curiosity, its glow flickering with every bit of mana she released.

Elynara looked at it curiously, a small smile tugging at her lips.

“You really like mana, don’t you?”

It darted forward, nudging her gently as if to confirm her suspicion.

A soft laugh escaped her lips.

“Alright, fine. Here. But it’s the last bit for today!”

She drew out a small wisp of mana and let it hover in the air. The sprite zipped around it gleefully before lunging forward and snatching it away like a playful predator.

For a while, Elynara just let herself relax from the exercise, before finally getting up. She had tarried long enough, and it was time to get back to town now!

She rose to her feet, dusting off her dress. The town’s walls still loomed in the distance, but the idea of a warm meal and a safe place to rest urged her forward.

“Come on, Starpuff. Let’s go back.”

Trusting it to follow along, she drew one last deep breath, to catch all those lovely but faint flowery scents drifting in the air, before starting her trek back.

And it was about halfway back across the forest border when things went wrong.

A sudden rustle of bushes alerted her. But if she thought it was just some harmless critter at first, the low, guttural snarl of a creature desperate for sustenance said otherwise.

Her head snapped toward the sound, eyes widening as a sickly wolf-like beast lunged from the underbrush a mere dozen meters away, eyes wild and its gaunt frame twitching with hunger.

Panic surged through her limbs, and it was a good thing she was already alert and backpedaling when the snarl came. But upon seeing it jumping out she still stumbled backward, barely keeping her footing.

The basket fell from her hands, herbs scattering over the ground.

“No!”

It charged at her, ignoring her cry entirely.

She saw Starpuff trying to distract the thing, but it was like it wasn’t even there, the wolf-thing simply charging through.

Elynara’s mind went blank, pure instinct taking over.

She drew on every last scrap of mana she had left, forcing it to compress together. To grind against itself, like she had done to make fire.

It barely even worked during her exercise, but she could think of nothing else to use.

And then, with a desperate scream, she hurled the unstable orb of mana at the creature.

The orb exploded on impact, a flash of green light and searing heat forcing the beast to recoil with a yelp, the impact strong enough to almost throw it into the air.

Whimpering, it eyed her one more time before retreating into the brush, leaving her shaking, her breaths coming in ragged gasps.

But whatever that thing was, her quick reaction had kept her alive.

She was thankful it decided the risk was too great to pursue.

Her entire body shook from the lingering adrenaline, her breath coming in shallow, uneven gasps. Starpuff came over trying to cheer her up in its own silly ways, but she was too stressed to respond.

The glowing remnants of her hastily-crafted spell faded from the air, leaving only the quiet rustle of grass and the distant chirping of insects.

Elynara forced herself to stand upright, her legs wobbling unsteadily beneath her. Her mana reserves were nearly drained, the exhaustion settling in like a heavy weight pressing down on her shoulders.

Starpuff floated nervously around her, its usual cheerful buzzing replaced by an anxious, jittery hum.

“Okay… We’re not staying out here any longer.”

Her voice came out like a shaky mumble despite her attempt to sound calm and composed.

Picking up her fallen basket and the scattered herbs with trembling hands, she stumbled back towards the town. Every sound along the way had her nerves on edge, but no other creatures emerged from the bushes.

This time she made sure to keep a greater distance between her and the forest edge, she wasn’t taking any more risks.

By the time she reached the town’s gate, her body felt like lead, but the adrenaline had burned out.

They made it back.

Entering without trouble, the trip through the town was woefully boring, but for once she didn’t mind the lack of excitement.

Boring was good.

She quite liked boring right about then. It didn’t have teeth.

The guild hall was buzzing with life as always, but her entrance drew a few curious glances.

Not that she paid them much mind. Her steps carried her straight to the counter, where Alicia’s eyes narrowed with immediate suspicion.

“You look like you’ve seen a ghost.”

The receptionist remarked, her voice sharp with concern. There was no hiding from her usual unusual clarity.

“More like a hungry monster. A wolf-like beast… I had to fight it off.”

Elynara admitted, trying not to worry Alicia too much.

Alicia’s eyes widened, her demeanor turning from suspicion to alarm, though she didn’t actually raise her voice.

“You went gathering outside the town just like that, and ran into a beast? Are you out of your mind?”

Elynara flinched at the reprimand, her cheeks flushing with embarrassment.

“I–I didn’t have much of a choice…”

“Just because you have some mana abilities doesn’t mean you can waltz around outside town like you own the place, little miss! You don’t even have a staff, or anything for protection.”

Alicia’s voice lowered into more of a whisper, though her irritation remained palpable:

“You could have died out there.”

The words struck her harder than she expected. Her earlier sense of accomplishment now felt hollow, overshadowed by the reminder of how close she had been to losing everything.

Before Alicia could scold her further, a gruff voice cut through the air.

“Ease up, Alicia. Kid’s new, but she made it back alive, right? Besides, everyone’s gotta start somewhere.”

The speaker was a burly man with a thick beard and an easygoing smirk, leaning against the counter with a casual familiarity.

Alicia shot him a glare but relented with a sigh.

“Just… be careful next time, alright? You can’t afford to be that reckless.”

Elynara managed a nod, shooting a thankful glance towards the unfamiliar man.

“I’ll… try to be more careful.”

His piece said and satisfied with her answer, the gruff man returned to sip beer at his seat in the guild’s bar corner.

Her quest was processed without further incident. The small sum of coins she received didn’t feel nearly as satisfying as she had hoped, but it was something. Proof she had accomplished at least part of her goal.

Alicia’s tone was also softer now after working through the familiar procedures.

“Go get some rest. Come back when you’re feeling better.”

“Yeah… I will.”

The walk back to the church felt like trudging through water, her exhaustion only growing worse with each step.

And once the familiar building came into view, its modest structure somehow appeared comforting in the dimming light, and the tension she didn’t know she was holding seemed to leave her body.

Father Alaric welcomed her back with a gentle smile, his eyes noting her fatigue but choosing not to comment. She was suddenly very glad for his laid-back approach.

The evening meal was a quiet affair, the children’s laughter echoing from another room as she forced herself to eat.

The food was warm, but her thoughts felt distant. The encounter with the beast replayed in her mind over and over. How unprepared she had been. How lucky she was to have survived.

As she finally collapsed into her bed, Starpuff nestled against her hair, the fatigue dragging her down faster than she could think.

But even as sleep pulled her under, a stubborn determination burned within her.

She was weak. But she would grow stronger.

No matter how long it took.
