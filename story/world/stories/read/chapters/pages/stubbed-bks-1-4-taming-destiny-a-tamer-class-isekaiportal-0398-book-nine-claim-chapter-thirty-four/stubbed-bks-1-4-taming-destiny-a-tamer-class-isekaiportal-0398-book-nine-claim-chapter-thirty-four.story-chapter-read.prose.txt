The ship’s timbers groan in protest as we press against the iceberg bulwark, the current forcing us forwards into a space that is already occupied.

I hurry to lean over the railing and look in both directions. I can’t see the other group – the ice bergs are in the way – but Garous and Kyrian are both pulling forwards. Good.

And for now, our makeshift bulwark composed of a stiffened roc carcass and two long ice-covered rocky spurs is holding – we’re blocked just outside the Tier five’s mouth rather than already in it.

We’re still under time pressure. Ice is already crumbling at the point where the bulwark meets the edges of the behemoth’s mouth, and the rock won’t be long to follow. And if the Tier five opens its mouth wider while we’re still here, we’re gonners.

What’s Mage Vellus saying? I ask Loran frantically. His shouted instructions pause a brief moment as he replies.

The currents have stagnated. We’re moving, he tells me shortly, then I hear the shouting resume, the winds attempting to suck us further into the Tier five’s mouth whipping away his words.

I breathe a sigh of relief. I know that our plan is working.

For now.

It’s a shame the currents are stagnating rather than being redirected – in our best-case scenarios we’d hoped that the currents might aid us in pulling us past the Tier five’s mouth rather than into it. Still, stagnation is better than before.

Already, I can see that we’re making progress, if slowly.

Mimicking the icebergs disrupting the water currents below, I see Air Mages disrupting the air currents above – otherwise our fliers and sails would be more a hindrance than a help. But air is difficult to make into a solid barrier, and they’re having to combat the control a Tier five has over a substance in its domain when they’re already tired from earlier. I suspect that the only reason they’re succeeding at all is that the air currents are side effects of the Tier five’s presence, and not something it’s doing intentionally.

My gaze flicks indecisively between the blurry barrier in the sky and Bastet who has stopped pacing and is now curled up in a shaking ball on the deck.

My mana channels are still painful, but being in Bastet’s Core space must have counted as Heavy Meditation because I’ve regained a good portion of my mana pool in the last ten minutes.

I could help the Air Mages get us out of here.

Or I could help Bastet deal with the influx of foreign Energy.

I can’t do both.

I clench my jaw in frustration, wishing I had a clone I could deploy. The loud cracks ringing out from the line of icebergs make up my mind for me. They’re a flimsy barrier against the sure death that awaits us in the Tier five’s mouth; helping Bastet is no good if we all die anyway.

“I’m sorry,” I breathe as I step towards the port-side rail again.

We’ll look after her, Ninja promises, Lathani sending her fervent agreement. I nod gratefully at them, stealing a glance up at Kalanthia. Her eyes are faraway and I sense the mana she’s sending down the connections she has with her creations. I don’t need to touch them with earth-magic to know that she’s doing her best to reinforce the rocky spur closest to us. I can only pray that the Earth Mages on the ships on the other side are doing the same.

Then, pushing all else aside, I reach up into the air with my Air-Shaping. My control over it is far weaker than those currently working – Air-Shaping is one of my weakest elemental Skills. But, fortunately, it’s also the one that demands the least magic and the most Willpower, so I’m not too badly off.

I hesitate again between where to concentrate my efforts – I could help reinforce the barrier, or push air into our sails and the alcaorises’ wings to speed us forwards. I could also divide the air before our ship, reducing our air-resistance and even sucking us forwards a little by creating a vacuum.

In the end, my decision is easy. Ultimately, the purpose is to get out of here as quickly as possible. The barrier is currently holding – adding my efforts might extend it longer, but that won’t directly help us get out of here. Reducing our air-resistance and creating a vacuum is probably more than I can manage right now, both mentally and physically. Which leaves the second option as the only possibility.

Noir, Ivor, and Aingeal all send gratitude down the link as their individual endeavours become just a little easier. Our speed picks up a little as we’re not relying only on being towed by Elarion’s ship.

But my mana is draining fast, and we’re running out of time. My heart leaps into my mouth every time a chunk of ice falls – it's becoming increasingly frequent, and the pieces that are falling are bigger each time. We’re still very much in the danger zone, though Kyrian’s former vessel is almost at the edge of the behemoth’s mouth.

Growing my air-wings once more, I take off, grabbing onto Noir’s rope to add my own pull to his, both of us still protected for now by the Air Mages’ flickering shield.

I wrack my brain but can’t think of anything else I can do to affect the outcome. All our mages are currently deployed. Sirocco is our only dedicated flier not currently in the air, but she’s small and light enough that she would be more badly affected by the Tier five’s domain.

My eyes light upon an intact rowboat. I chew at the inside of my cheek for a moment – surely that won’t make much of a difference? But then I exhale and reach out to Loran – can we afford not to do absolutely everything?

Get rowers to take out the rowboat and help.

Confusion is my only reply. I don’t waste time or focus on explaining, but send an image of my idea.

I understand, he replies shortly, then I sense his concentration break away. Soon, I spot Warriors who would otherwise be useless piling into two rowboats that are quickly launched, their ropes tied fast to the ship. Their Class-enhanced muscles straining, they too contribute something to the forward motion of our vessel.

It seems incredible that a small rowboat, even one rowed by a good number of Classers, could affect the speed of a large vessel like this, but in these conditions, any extra forward motion makes us move just that little bit better.

We’re out of the current, Hunter reports. She, along with Catch, the male raptorcats and Artio are all on Garous’ ship. Ostensibly for communication, they’re also there to make sure that no spiteful soul decides to toss off the anchor holding Elarion’s ship to them – and ours by extension. For similar reasons, River, Iandee, and the deris are on Elarion’s ship. I’d like to trust my allies wholeheartedly, but in this situation, I don’t dare.

In reaction to Hunter’s words, relief ripples through our Bond network, though it’s rapidly replaced again by renewed tension. One down, two to go.

I glance behind me. With a better vantage point than earlier, I can now see the other four ships. The sight relieves me – they’re doing better than we are, probably because my ship was the worst-damaged of the fleet. Josiah’s ship is rounding the corner of the behemoth’s mouth, and Sor’az’s ship is already out of sight. Layton’s and Valence’s will be out of the danger zone soon.

A loud crack like a gunshot draws my attention to the barrier closest to me. My stomach drops as I see a chunk of ice that’s twice as big as one of the rowboats slide into the water, leaving the rocky spur below it bare. My throat tightens as I spot the massive crack that’s formed in the rock.

Kalanthia, can you do anything about that? I ask her frantically.

She doesn’t respond in words but with a strong feeling-club of ‘don’t bother me’ that slams back down the Bond. I clench my jaw until it twinges in pain, then beat my wings with ever more urgency.

“The current has changed!” I hear Vellus shout fearfully down below. “A chunk of ice that was blocking it must have broken away too!”

I don’t even need his words – I feel how the progress of our ship has suddenly slowed down, how our stern is being pulled even more strongly against the barrier of rock and ice. I think quickly.

With our mages so pushed to their limits or already exhausted, only one option occurs to me. An expensive option.

Loran, tell Sarran to use the shield device on the iceberg!

There’s a brief pause.

He asks if you’re certain.

Of course I’m certain! I snap back, though knowing Loran doesn’t deserve it. Anyway, I understand why Sarran would want to check – this is something that will hurt to lose. But the truth still stands: no price is worth our lives.

I see a flurry of activity down below. Rapidly, people create a gangway out to the iceberg. Sarran himself plants the enchanted device into the ice and activates it. The metallic ball flashes brightly and then settles into a steady pulsing of a white light. Abruptly the ship is moving faster again as the current that had been pulling it back halts.

I force myself to look forwards, mourning the loss of the expensive device. It’s a portable spherical shield generator that was supposed to be used to set up our first camp when we arrive at the Lost Continent. We may regret using it now, but I’m relieved that at least it’s worked. Its shield is capable of resisting both physical and magical influences, so I was hoping that it would work on a domain. The only downside of it is that, by nature, it cannot be used on something that’s moving. The shifting iceberg will be testing that aspect enough; a ship would definitely be beyond it.

Perhaps Lizil can create another one, I try to console myself. Or maybe Kyrian was given one too and I’ll find it among the spoils I still haven’t finished reviewing.

Our progress seems painfully slow, but I’m starting to see the light at the end of the tunnel. What feels like hours afterwards but is probably only a quarter of an hour, River reaches out to tell me that Elarion’s ship has left the current. It’s still in sight, but won’t be for long – already its prow has disappeared around the corner of the behemoth’s mouth.

When Loran passes on the message to the rest of the crew in order to encourage them, a ragged cheer goes up. We all redouble our efforts as much as we can, gaining our second wind as we see the end in sight.

Personally, I’ve long since run dry on mana – all I can do is keep flying and pulling the ship in the direction of its prow. Those in the rowboats take their second – or third – stamina potions, and several of the mages vomit over the side as they take a cleansing potion just so they can take another mana-replenisher.

Elarion’s ship disappears around the corner and one of our rowboats sets up a cheer, shouting that they can feel they’ve escaped the current. One of them drops a wooden object into the water and plucks it out again, just to prove it.

The tip of our prow emerges past the edge of the Tier five’s mouth and I feel an exhausted exhilaration.

We’re almost there!

And that, of course, is the moment the behemoth loses patience.
