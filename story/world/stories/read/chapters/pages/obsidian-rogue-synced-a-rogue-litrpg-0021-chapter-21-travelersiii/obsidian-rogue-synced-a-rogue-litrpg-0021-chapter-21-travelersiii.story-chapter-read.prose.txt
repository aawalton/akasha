It turned out that learning how to ride wasn’t an easy job. In fact, Levi was almost killed by the mata. It’d repeatedly thrown him off, kicked him with its hooves, and at one point, it’d tried to stomp on the rogue after throwing him off. He’d begun to see the temper that the dealer had talked about—the black mata was out to kill him.

It took more time and hits than he’d have liked, but he finally got a reasonable understanding of how to go about riding the steed. No thanks to Xara. The mage had sat on the steed laughing her lungs out as he got tossed about by the very same mata that was supposed to become his steed. It was a pitiful situation, but luckily for Levi, he was a fast learner, and Xara was a good teacher. So it hadn’t taken him long to grasp the fundamentals, and within a short amount of time, he’d forgotten the scrapes and bruises he’d earned while trying to get on the beast.

“Okay, time to discuss your plans,” Xara said, finally breaking the silence that the two had shared for the better part of the past hour.

“Yeah, I guess it is,” Levi said as he let his eyes wander about, drifting about the dirt path they rode on.

The traffic outside the city was diminished, and the few people he saw seemed to be travelling like them. Most on foot and others on matas. The dirt path was shaded by the hundreds of tall trees that grew in the forest. Birds chirped about, and the warmth of daylight that shone through the canopy warmed his face. The sounds of their steed's hooves hitting the dirt echoed in his ears.

“I don’t have a plan.” He said after a heartbeat had passed. “My only goal right now is to survive long enough to figure something out.”

“That makes two of us.”

“What do you mean by that?”

“I'm in the same boat as you.” She said, tightening her grip on the reins of her steed. ”All my life, I’d been told that I was meant to be the spare. My purpose for breathing, eating, and training was just so I could step in if anything happened to my brother. It was a horrible way to live. On the good days, I wished to die, on the bad days I-I… I wished death for Yuran.”

“Dammn...I can’t imagine what that must’ve felt like.”

“It was hell, but what does that matter to you? After all, we nobles are just pompous, arrogant brats with no feelings of our own, aren't we?” She asked.

She huffed when the rogue refused to respond, but ultimately continued. “My life wasn’t even mine, and my living was as insurance against my brother's death. Of course, if I'd been some kind of prodigy, I could’ve surpassed Yuran in terms of rank even with the disproportionate distribution of the family’s resources. I would’ve called it an impossible task, but it has happened before in noble families .”

“What has?”

“The scenario where a spare overtakes the heir of the house, and eventually swaps roles. It's harsh, but so are the ways of the upper echelon.” She said, before turning to face him. “I think it might interest you to know that the last spare to achieve such a feat was Talron Loern. The former spare of the Loern family, who is now the heir.”

“If what you say about the issues of resources is true, then I must admit that this Talron sounds like quite the prodigy,” Levi said. “However, I’m struggling to see how that should interest me?”

“It’s quite simple, he’s Ghost's brother,” She answered. “Some might even say that he is the person who turned Ghost into the cold-hearted monster that he is. Ghost didn’t take too kindly to the change in the status quo, and he left his family shortly after, only to be disowned. Which certainly didn’t help matters for Ghost.”

“I see,” Levi muttered.

He kept his eyes on the road even as his mata trotted forward. While the things that Xara had just told him didn’t necessarily help his life in any way, he had to accept the fact that they were interesting. The fact that Ghost was of noble birth, downed his spirits a bit. Truth be told, he’d been wowed the first time he’d seen the rogue, plus the way that Ghost had been able to take on the full party who’d been transporting the stone all by himself had raised his opinion of the rogue. Sure, it was the same rogue who was still hot on his heels, but he couldn’t deny that Ghost had been on a league of his own. The ruthlessness that had been displayed had made him think that the rogue was from the gutters, but it turns out it was quite the opposite. The rogue had received noble training and the resources of an entire house.

I guess that's one role model out of the book.

“What of his brother? Do you know what rank he is?”

“Not for sure, but rumor has it that he’s about to break into the A rank, that's if he hasn’t already,” She replied.

“That's crazy.”

“I tell you something even crazier, he’s tipped to become the first S-rank of his family. Everyone expects him to become the obsidian rogue once the time comes.” Xara said. “Which is bad for you, I guess.”

Bad? This is terrible news.

How exactly was he supposed to handle Telron if he was running away from Ghost? For someone like him, there was no difference between the brothers; there was no prodigy or non-prodigy. There was only death ahead if he didn’t get his act together, fast.

“Grakking hell.” He cursed. “Ghost is already more than enough. I can’t deal with his brother as well.”

“Better prepare yourself because you'd have to. Ghost and Telron are just the tip of the iceberg. And until you gain enough power or get a big enough backing to keep the stone for yourself, then you'd better be expecting foes of that scale to come for you … and me.” She said, “Chin up. As it stands, I think Ghost is the only one who should know about your mark.”

“I hope so, I can’t imagine being used for sport by one family,” Levi breathed.

“Hopefully it doesn’t come to that,” she said. “ Have you considered hitting more dungeons? I know that you came out through a dungeon saferoom, but you're only level five, which means that you couldn't possibly have more than two dungeon runs under your belt.”

“I’ve actually only gone on a single run.”

“That checks out. We’ll have to go on more runs.” She said. “I think there should be a couple on the way.”

“Alright. Let's do that.”

“Sure.” She agreed. “ Can I ask a question?”

“Of course”

“Why do you want to find the Black Baron?”

“Someone told me to.” He said.

“Oh, okay,” she said.

The mage had gotten him gear and coin, but his trust was something that couldn’t be bought; it had to be earned, and for someone whom he’d known for less than a complete day, she was getting frighteningly close to earning it. Levi couldn’t afford to slip up again, not to her, not to anyone. He really wanted to trust her with what was going on, but it was too soon.

The less she knows about me, the better. For now, I can’t give her any information that can be used against me at a later date.

His fears about her being a threat to him were still there, sitting buried under his tumultuous emotions. The rogue couldn't rule out anything, most especially when it came to someone he’d known for just a few short hours. Besides, he’d been down this same road before; he’d known Killian for years, and yet the old man had sold him to be butchered.

So Levi felt his lack of trust for anyone was justified; should the mage give him reasons to trust her, he would. Till then, he’d keep her close but yet far away from him. His secrets would remain his, and she could share hers if she wished. A couple of sob stories from the mage wouldn’t be enough to move him.

To be honest, he didn’t care about her story; maybe it was because he’d grown up in the slums where children much younger than them fought tooth and nail for a fraction of what the mage discarded so easily. Those children fought for scraps of clothes, leftover food, and coins. He didn’t think that there was a single child back in Gantus who wouldn’t swap places with Xara. None of those kids would’ve cared about being a spare if it meant that they were well fed, clothed, educated, and had enough coin that they could put some on the side for pickpockets.

The mages' plight was unfortunate, but Levi knew lots of people who would call such a type of life heaven. He knew for sure because he was one of those kids, and so he continued to ride on his steed, focusing more on what he’d heard about House Leorn, rather than the sad tales the mage had told him.

The absurdity of it all. What she calls hell would be considered heaven to others.

That was life in a nutshell. One person's trash could be another's gold. The mage had grown up in wealth and, no doubt, couldn’t see the good of her situation. It didn’t make the mage a bad person; it just seemed off to him, but then again, if he’d grown up as the heir to a noble house, he probably would have thought differently of the whole matter. In the end, perspective was what really mattered, and from where he was standing, her decision was odd.

“Do you have siblings?" Xara asked, breaking the silence and cocking her head to stare at him even as their steeds carried them forward.“I don’t,” He lied. “Grew up alone on the streets.”

“Oh.” She replied and then focused on the road ahead.

The rogue did the same. He had no interest in receiving any pity from the mage. Every bad thing he’d faced in the streets had molded him into the person he’d become today. Sure, he wasn’t in the ideal situation at the moment, but that was life. Levi couldn't change the fact that he’d been born in poverty, but he sure as hell could change his life… change their lives.

Silas, Bree. I hope you guys are okay.

They were his siblings, not by blood but by circumstance. He’d adopted the pair and done his best to keep them away from the streets, and they, in return, had kept him grounded. They’d given him a reason to live. They’d called him big brother, and he’d done the best he could for them. And he hoped his best would be enough because they were now on their own. He wished nothing but the best for the pair.

Levi looked up at the sky, the tall canopy blocked out most of the daylight but he could tell that it beginning to get dark and soon enough it’d be night time and unless the mage had a tent somewhere within that storage ring of theirs, they were going to have to spend the night on the dirt, not that he had a problem with such a thing.

The rogue returned his gaze to the path. The chirps of the birds and the insects soothed his mind, making it easier for him to sort out his jumbled thoughts. He focused on unraveling the thread in his mind that concerned the Black baron. Xara had referred to the person as the Butcher of Talpha, and Levi couldn’t help but wonder what kind of things someone would have to do to earn such a title.
