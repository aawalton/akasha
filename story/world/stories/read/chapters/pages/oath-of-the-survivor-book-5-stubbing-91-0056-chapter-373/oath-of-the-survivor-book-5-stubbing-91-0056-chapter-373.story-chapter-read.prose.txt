Kyle watched the deluge crash forward as Svynar rode the wave like a surfer, axe at the ready.  The entire arena was swamped, except the area right around Duroc.  Any time the water got too close to him, it was pushed away, flowing around him instead.  Svynar rode these waves, taking opportunities to launch deadly strikes with his axe, each of which was narrowly blocked or avoided.  From the outset, it was clear that the other War Chieftain had the advantage.

Still, Kyle frowned as he looked on with Identify.  He had a good sense for Duroc’s overall level of combat prowess, and his friend was showcasing very little of it.  There were many opportunities to land counterattack with the spear or use a shield bash, and yet Duroc seemed content to play the turtle while Svynar kept up his assault.

Svynar, too, was acting strangely.  While the surrounding environment would almost certainly be impacted by a fight between C Grades, Svynar almost seemed to be prioritizing it.  Rather than using focused jets of pressurized water or using his skills to limit Duroc’s movements in subtle ways, he instead kept manipulating the massive wave, despite the bulk of the water not even being helpful in the fight.

The longer the fight went on, the deeper his frown grew.  Despite being a visually impressive display, the two War Chieftains were fighting at a level far below his expectation.  They finished another flashy exchange, and Svynar rose into the air atop a water spout, holding his axe aloft in an exaggerated motion.  Kyle saw something pass between the two orcs, and as Svynar descended, Kyle finally understood.

Duroc rose into the air to meet the charge, spear raised in challenge to the axe.  They passed one another; Duroc picking up a cut on his cheek, as Svynar’s side was grazed by the spear.

“Well, well, well,” Svynar boomed, pausing the maelstrom of water around him.  “It looks like little Duroc might have some fangs after all.  Impressive, weathering my storm.”

“I still have much to learn,” Duroc replied as he floated in the sky, looking down at Svynar.

“I look forward to seeing how you handle the others.  There is no need for me to test you further, you might just have what it takes to stand among us,” Svynar snorted as he rested the axe on his shoulder and allowed the water to drop to the ground.

Kyle could feel the arena shaking along with the cheering from the stands, and had to fight to avoid grinning or shaking his head.  During their last exchange, he finally understood what this was.  Combat theater.

It reminded Kyle of the old videos of professional wrestling from before Earth’s awakening.  It was a spectacle for the masses more than a true test of ability.  Certainly both Duroc and Svynar were fighting at a high level, but neither showed up with intent to do the other serious harm.  While their battleground would have been dangerous for most D Grades, C Grades could handle that level of pressure without great danger.  Moreover, both fought in such a way that they each got to shine in front of their followers.

That, ultimately, was the most important aspect of the Tournament of Ascension.  The citizens of each major village needed to have confidence that their War Chieftain was sufficiently powerful to keep them safe, and this event got to showcase their strength.  Kyle had no doubt that if a War Chieftain was too weak to compete that they’d be thrown out, while any who were strong enough got to put on a show.

It made sense, and it also shed light on some possible motives for Mangalica as well.  The upstart War Chieftain didn’t seem the type to appreciate the finer nuances of leadership, instead leaning heavily into the might-makes-right school of thought.  If he truly felt the other War Chieftains were weak, why wouldn’t he make plans to dominate them?  With the Collective only maintaining a couple of outposts for travel purposes, he could conquer the rest as he saw fit.

Kyle was drawn out of his musings by Duroc’s approach; the large orc walking out of the arena with his head held high.

“Good fight,” Kyle remarked as he fell into step behind the armored orc.

“We did what we had to,” Duroc replied with a small smile.  “While you’re here, do you mind patching up the cut on my cheek?  I don’t see a need to go all the way to the Healers’ area for such a minor injury.”

Kyle quirked an eyebrow, though he was already reaching out a hand to activate Healing Impetus.  “So you ask a C Grade Courier to do it instead?”

Duroc chuckled.  “For what we’re paying you, yes.”

“Fair point,” Kyle agreed as they made their way back to their spire.  Looking back at the arena, Kyle could already see teams of Mages working hard to repair the damage caused by the first match.

Duroc had a small break while the next bout occurred, then was pulled back out for his second fight.  This time his opponent was a Ranger, and Duroc showed his defensive prowess by blocking a storm of arrows, all the while making a slow advance toward the surprisingly mobile archer.  This fight ended as the timer ticked down, the veteran War Chieftain one again commending Duroc on his skills and warning him not to get cocky.

Duroc battled a Lightning Mage next, followed by an ancient-looking orc Fire Mage shortly afterward.  Each time those War Chieftains fought the crowds went absolutely wild, and for good reason.  The spectacle of massive inferno rising to the sky and crashing thunder amidst flashes of electricity were thrilling to watch.  As before however, much of the energy was wasted to create the flashy effects, and in Kyle’s view these were actually the weakest of the War Chieftains by a fair margin.  Not to say that he didn’t still have some injuries to patch up, but their abilities felt far more one-note than the others, lacking in flexibility.

The most impressive fight, at least to Kyle, was against a War Chieftain using a two-weapon fighting style, brandishing a sword and dagger.  The fight was an artful dance in the middle of the arena, both sides showcasing incredible martial skill.  Duroc was forced to demonstrate more of his techniques to keep pace with his opponent as they darted in and out of Duroc’s range to land scoring blows.  The audience, though still excited, was notably more subdued than in many of the other matches, though Kyle made sure C.H.A.D.D. took good recordings of the fight.

Throughout the tournament, Duroc’s administrative staff were even busier than the War Chieftains.  Kyle wasn’t privy to the meetings, though from the bits and pieces he picked up there were gifts exchanged, information swapped, and budding alliances forged.  By the final day of the tournament, they all had a mixed look of exhaustion and satisfaction from jobs well done.

Finally, at the last evening of the Tournament of Ascension, all the War Chieftains gathered together in the arena.  The oldest of the group, the Fire Mage, rose into the air, raising a hand to silence the chatter across the arena.  Rather than the crimson and golden armor that he was wearing during his battles, he was wearing deep red robes over a formal-looking set of leather armor.  As attention was drawn to him, he projected his voice for the audience to hear.

“Friends and honored guests, on behalf of the War Chieftains, we thank you for your attendance.  Despite some… notable absences, the Tournament of Ascension was a success.  Each of the reigning Chieftains put up an admirable showing, as did our newest War Chieftain, Duroc.”

That statement was met by a roar of applause from Duroc’s party, though Kyle could see quite a few glances get thrown toward the empty spire representing Mangalica's village.  Before he could give it much more thought, the Fire Mage continued.

“While he still has much to learn, he has more than proven himself to be a capable individual, worthy of leading his village and guiding them through the challenging times ahead.  It is our honor to welcome his Ascent among our ranks.  Now, we recognize that there have been many… concerns about recent events, but be assured that now, as ever, the military might of our War Chieftains will maintain stability for all of our citizens.  We have no intention of allowing challenges to our ancestral traditions to be taken lightly, and so we have come to a consensus.  We will be forming a committee to discuss these ongoing threats, and to return our world to a semblance of normalcy.”

That was met by more uproarious applause, which he allowed to continue for a minute before raising a hand.

“Now, in the morning, we will all depart these grounds and return to our villages.  We do so with hope for our future, and step into tomorrow with the blessing of the Ancestors.  Be well, walk with confidence, and may you all look back on this tournament with fond memories.”

With that, the assembled War Chieftains fully released their auras for a moment, which washed over the assembled crowd.  There was a moment of silence as people took in the show of power, followed by the loudest cheering of the whole event.  The War Chieftains each returned to their area, with Kyle waiting to greet Duroc.

“Quite a show,” he said, falling in behind the orc.

“It’s good for morale,” Duroc agreed as they made it through the hall to the common area.

The others were already well into their cups, and Kyle gave Duroc a serious look.  “If Mangalica wasn’t going to make a move before, he’s certainly going to make one now.  And tomorrow is his best opportunity.”

“I know,” Duroc said simply.  “You aren’t under any obligation to involve yourself further, you know.  With this, your job is complete.  I’ll have word sent to the Courier’s Guild tonight.”

“I plan to go back to your village before I go home,” Kyle replied guilelessly.  “If I get attacked along the road and need to defend myself and anybody else I happen to be with, that’s nobody’s business but mine.”

Duroc smiled and clapped Kyle on his right shoulder.  “You’re a good man, Kyle Mayhew.  Get some rest, we both know the real fight begins tomorrow.”

Kyle watched the procession of orcs departing, each War Chieftain leading their retinues back to the village.  As the most junior among them, Duroc’s group was the last to depart.  They were only on the road for a couple of hours before C.H.A.D.D. flagged a warning.  Looking at the drone’s map, two C Grade presences were lurking behind a large stone outcropping, with a handful of D Grade and E Grades gathered around them.  One of the C Grades was clearly Mangalica, and the other gave the telltale mana signature of a variant hodag; the most powerful they’d seen so far.

“They’re doing a pretty terrible job of hiding,” Kyle said, alerting Duroc.

The armored orc frowned, looking at the group.  “It’ll be difficult to fight them without casualties.  We’re not too close, I could send the rest of the procession away.  That would leave us with a numbers disadvantage, though.  What do you think?”

“Keep your staff back and out of range.  I’ll take the hodag and the others if you can handle Mangalica.”

“Then it sounds like we have a battle to win.”
