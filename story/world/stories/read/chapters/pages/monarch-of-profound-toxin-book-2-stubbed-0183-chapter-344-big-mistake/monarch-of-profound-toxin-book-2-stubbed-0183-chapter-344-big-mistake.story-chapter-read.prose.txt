In the end, people lined up right there on the street to shake Eik’s hand, touch foreheads, grasp arms, embrace, or whatever other gesture was culturally ingrained in the greeter.

There were a few in there that violated Eik’s personal boundaries rather severely, but it didn’t feel right to say anything in the moment. They were being so sincere.

Aah, nuts… This was exactly the kind of attention he had the hardest time to deal with.

Not to say that he hated it per se. As a matter of fact, he was flattered beyond belief by the attention, but that didn’t make it any less awkward for him in the moment. When it came down to it, that was actually exactly what made it tough.

He didn’t know what to do with himself.

Once they reached a solid thirty minutes of a constant stream of people fighting to be the next one to touch his hand as the gathering of folks itself drew in more and more people who noticed who he was, time became a little tight.

And if that wasn’t a good excuse to dip… “Thank you very much, everybody,” he said, the perpetual smile beginning to show cracks at the edges. “Unfortunately, I do have a few things I have to get to now, so I will take my leave.”

Some people looked disappointed by his departure, but nobody was going to complain to the X-ranked hero for not giving them enough time to worship him. That, however, didn’t mean it didn’t send a pang through Eik’s stomach. “Sorry, but thanks for comin— Or, well, I guess you were all already here, but, you know. Thanks!”

And with that, he grew a sheet of crystal along his shoulders to keep Bin in place as he pulled Goo off Michael’s shoulder before taking both boys under his arms. A single leap brought them high up above the rooftops of Gimleh.

With a bird’s eye view like this, Michael realized that the gathering of people had grown even larger than immediately apparent, extending even to other streets. No wonder Eik had wanted to get out of there.

Even just the number that were here now would probably have taken a couple of hours to get through at an optimistic estimate, and for each person there, another two or three would have been attracted by the chaos.

“That’s… pretty insane, isn’t it?” the healer muttered while he stared down below. All eyes were on them, cheering voices rising up from several streets as the word of who had just left spread like wildfire. “Even just a few weeks ago, we could walk down that exact street and catch maybe, at most, a couple of stares in total. This is completely different.”

Eik sighed. “Yeah, I might have overdone the big speech at the Elixir King event thing just a tad. Man, what the hell was I thinking?”

“Oh, come on, buddy. It’s not so bad to be beloved, is it? In fact, I dare say many would envy the attention you’re now getting.”

“Would you?”

Michael grimaced at the mere thought. “Oh, god, no. Could you imagine? No, I think I’ll let you have all that.”

“Great, thanks for that, mate.”

“No worries. You just enjoy it, my friend.”

“Look, dad!” Bin shouted and pointed down. Neither her nor Goo seemed to have been listening to their father’s pathetic moaning. “I can see Uncle Heathy’s house from all the way up here!”

Eik laughed. “That’s not his house, sweetheart. That’s just his office.”

“Oooh!” she ooh’d.

They went into a sharp dive—a move which, judging by a series of breathless gasps, Michael was not a big fan of. With about thirty meters left before touchdown, Eik conjured up a giant slip’n’slide and hurled Goo down, and then Michael, before hugging Bin tightly and riding together with her.

Silently, Goo altered his body slightly to increase his speed, face scrunched up in concentration. Michael was… loud. And Bin couldn’t have been giggling harder. So long as she was in Eik’s arms, nothing could frighten her.

A small ramp at the bottom threw them off at an angle that landed them right in front of the door to the front room of the workshop where the handmade pieces were sold directly. There were quite a few smiths affiliated with the workshop, so they did distribute to merchants as well, but many buyers, especially those with the money to spend, preferred to go directly to the artisan to make sure that they would get a custom weapon or armor that suited their needs to perfection.

“Hello!” Bin called as she pushed open the door to the shop, a little bell fastened to the frame chiming melodically.

“I’ll be right there!” a voice answered from the back. The doorway to the back was doorless but covered with a thick, segmented hanging carpet which blocked any line of sight into the workshop proper.

The shop consisted of a single large room, glittering works of metal, wood, and leather adorning basically every inch of free wall space, with display cases, racks, and shelves taking up most of the floor in ordered rows.

There was an order to the setup and hand-painted signs hanging from the ceiling made it easy to find what you were looking for quickly. Just a few quick looks at the items on display were enough to clarify just how much further ahead Gimleh was of Earth when it came to pure technique.

It wouldn't be like that forever, but for now, old Ben’s smith back in Forest just couldn’t compare to the kind of work done here.

“Yes, sorry for the wait, folks!” came a familiar voice. “I was just in the middle of cooling off one of my— Oh, Eik, welcome!” It was Kikah, Heath’s disciple brother. The man had already been noticeably larger than Heath but, somehow, he seemed to have gotten even bigger since Eik last saw him, which wasn’t even that long ago.

“Dude, what the hell are they feeding you here? You must be, what, at least two meters and forty centimeters. How did you even squeeze through the door?”

Kikah grinned widely. “Say it louder so Heath can hear you properly.”

“I heard you just fine!” Heath shouted, the sound of his voice muddled. “It’s only a matter of time. Once I get whatever lets Andi grow, your size superiority complex will be history, mate!”

“Uncle Heathy!” Bin squealed. “We’re here to pick you up!”

“Binny! I love that! I’m just finishing up, all right? Why are you guys so late anyway?”

“Eik got swarmed,” Michael called.

“Remember that whole thing where he basically dubbed himself the hero of the Unified Mass who will make sure we get unscathed through the thing with the Lord of the Moon?”

“Yeah, I mean, who wouldn’t?”

“Well, as it turns out, so do a lot of other people as well,” Michael chuckled while Eik cringed. “And apparently large displays with your face plastered on them don’t do much for anonymity.”

Kikah laughed. “Sounds like you’re having a tough time of it, mate.”

“He secretly loves it!” Heath shouted before the brisk bark of his master cut him down to size.

“If you’re gonna scream like that in here, then do it outside!”

“Thanks, Eik, but he’s only seven years old. I think it’s a little early for one of these.”

“A helmet! I’m good at material handling, so I’m hoping to impress my master with alloy-work and layering. Heath is better at enchantments than me, so I have to stand out with my own specialty.”

“We’re cheering you both on,” Michael told him.

Ten minutes later, they walked out of the workshop with Heath.

“What do you need to get, Heath?” Michael asked.

Goo had retaken his seat on the healer’s shoulders while Bin had switched over to Heath’s extra broad frame. According to her, the smith’s mega muscles were way softer than her father’s tough ones. Eik couldn’t help but internally curse his X-ranked body for being too strong to be a comfortable place for his kid.

“I need more magical reagents for my project. I still have a few more things to test before I settle on a final configuration.”

“All right, then. Let’s head to the market.”

“Actually, if you don’t mind, I could really use a bite to eat,” Heath added. “I woke up early to get some things done and I haven’t had the time to get anything.”

“Yeah, that works,” Eik said. “Shall we get some lunch first then?”

“So I cut the bastard’s arm clean off! Fuck if I’m going to let anybody make shitty demands like that!” Eik eyed the group of warrior seated at an outdoor table at a cafe. They had ended up at a little plaza that functioned as a kind of food court, with several eateries huddled together.

They had a shared seating area in the center so customers could sit anywhere regardless of their restaurant or cafe of choice.

It was great, except for… the yapping from the group of brutes. Three men and two women using crass language and talking about some pretty horrible shit. They sounded like the exact types he didn’t want his kids to listen to.

There were plenty of other customers to be sure, but they all looked like they were just trying to get their meals over with so they could leave. Judging by their faces, some even seemed know who the rowdy group was.

Goo and Bin were running around the plaza, playing, while Eik, Michael, and Heath were talking about old TV shows. “Turn down the volume a little, Gooey and Binny!” Eik shouted. “And don’t go too far, all right?”
