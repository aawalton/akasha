Syl Dive Administration - 6 month analysis

As forecast we are still operating at a deficit. Our newly established presence is not yet being recognized as an alternative to those with greater pedigree.

We are seeing a slow growth in returning Combat Pathers, not yet to the point of being deemed regulars but sufficient to point towards future gains. None have yet shown interest in Affiliation, despite the favorable terms.

The lack of a dedicated healer and the cost of ensuring one is present when Divers are in the Depths remains a significant expense and has led to Divers asking whether we intend to employ our own.

~ * ~

Anya glanced around the cavern in near disbelief. They'd cleared it on a previous trip, more than once in fact. They'd figured out the ambushes, the traps, the surprises. The goblin Affinities would change, not drastically but enough to ensure that they couldn't simply predict every step of the encounter, but they'd had a decent idea of what would happen going in.

Nothing had gone to plan. The struggle balancing her control of the goblins with Seb's weakening of them to ensure she didn't get overwhelmed never happened. The momentary stutter when they had to catalog the next set of Affinities to ensure they had a plan? It wasn't brief, it was non-existent.

She could feel Seb's reactions clearly. They were still in the near-meld state that had worried Fay. They'd slipped into it as the battle started and everything had changed. They'd already been working in harmony, trusting each other without having to hesitate. This time they might as well have been that single entity, what one knew the other did at the same time, when one moved the other had already accounted for the subtle shift to the battlefield.

That was- she began.

-we need to test this, Seb answered, not quite completing her thought but expanding on it.

Test?

She felt him pull away, their thoughts separating into their more habitual shape, close but no longer trying to be the same. She thought she knew what he'd meant, she'd felt something, but she wasn't sure.

What's changed, how we slip in and out, what it means, he sent.

A moment later she felt the rest of his meaning, the thoughts and impressions blending into a message she understood, an expression of his point of view that filled in the blanks she'd known were there.

Better, he sent. I couldn't… explain properly? It felt like trying to show myself something I already could see.

First test complete then? she sent as she started to collect Energy crystals.

Apparently, he agreed as he joined her.

It makes sense, she sent. Now. It was right there in the tip of my thoughts, stuck.

Another? This one went fast.

She didn't bother agreeing, he already knew her answer. Their collection sped up as they fell into sync, their awareness blending before they even left the cavern. By the time they had made it to the next encounter they had already reshaped their plans, reworked their approach. They might not be safe pushing extra far down the tunnels, but they'd be able to at least try.

~ * ~

Seb's realization the other night while talking to Fay had made things easier. He hadn't fully understood what they were doing until they'd been caught out at dinner. He'd forced himself to consider it more sharply, to think about the event, especially because of when and how it happened, and all the pieces had started to fit together.

The more he looked, the more the answer made sense. The deeper he went, the smoother everything seemed. The symptoms matched what Fay was worried about, but their Energy Patterns and reactions to one another didn't.

A few days of testing, of paying attention to how and when they started to meld, of pulling apart when it was safe to do so, had continued to confirm what they'd said. The closer their thoughts and intentions aligned, the more smoothly they melded. The more intense those intentions were, the more smoothly it went.

They still had their own thoughts, their own understandings, even when deep in the meld. They shared completely, but their views of what they shared remained distinct. They couldn't always explain perfectly, they had to pull apart to actually show each other elements that didn't make sense.

After nearly a week they were finally ready to take a break from pushing through cavern after cavern. In part they didn't want to push too far too quickly, but they also needed a break, needed time together that wasn't filled with goblins or recovery. So it was time for the one thing they hadn't tested, each other.

Anya was grinning as his attention turned outward. He knew she'd felt him dwelling on their past experiments, on just what they'd figured out and had given him time to make sure he didn't get sidetracked mid spar. He'd never actually claim that he was distracted, but he'd promised her his full attention.

As he set his thoughts aside he felt their awarenesses meld. The feeling was similar to when they faced goblins, yet completely different. Their intents were mirrored, their thoughts aligned. They weren't going to make things easy, they weren't going to hold back.

Seb wasn't sure which of them moved first, wasn't sure if an observer could have answered either. One moment they stood facing each other across their campsite, the next they'd closed the distance and already woven around multiple strikes. Bladesong filled the air, enhancing them both despite their opposing one another.

He didn't want the advantage, he didn't want to lessen the challenge between them simply by manipulating the environment. Her robe seemed to flicker to life, trying to distract him, to make the fight more challenging. It would have worked better had he not been aware, and more importantly were he not able to distinguish her Energy from the robe's.

He tried to pull away, to leverage his focus on mobility and hindrance to gain an advantage. She kept to his side, close enough to prevent him from disengaging, to turn his blade into a hindrance as often as not. He didn't dismiss it, he'd never used it primarily as a weapon despite how well it served now that he knew to enhance it. He stabbed it into the dirt as he rolled away, a pulsing beacon he intended to keep in play.

Anya didn't give him the chance, her attacks coming faster and faster, her form and techniques combining to keep him from shaping the battlefield. Yes he could turn tail, distract her long enough to turn things into a chase, into a different form of play, but that wasn't the point.

They both knew she'd likely win the drawn out close combat, that he'd win given the chance to keep away. Their field was limited, intentionally so. He'd never get enough distance to turn things into a true skirmish, she'd never corner him inside a circle.

The meld grew deeper, feints and counters blurring. Half their battle took place in their mind, leaping one, then two, then countless steps ahead. Despite this they didn't slow, didn't relent in their dance. Reflexes and ingrained motions kept them on their toes, exquisite awareness pushing their fine motor control to the limits as they avoided injury by the slimmest of margins.

At some point they stopped limiting themselves to the field, stopped consciously deciding what could and couldn't come next. They knew where their equipment lay, they wouldn't damage it, they knew what the other was capable of, they'd find the limits that hadn't been pushed.

When finally they faltered it was as one. His manifested energy shattering just shy of slicing her hamstring, the blow landing true and only their shared realization preventing true damage. Her blow to his sternum pulled just enough to leave him gasping for breath and nothing more.

They separated, both drained and exhausted by the extended spar. The meld remained, they might be done but they were still together. Joy, satisfaction and awe filled Seb's thoughts. Whether his or hers didn't matter, they both knew the test had succeeded.

They both knew they'd be sleeping well tonight.
