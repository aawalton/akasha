Several days after the incident at the Scavengers Transport headquarters, news of what happened had spread across town like wildfire. There wasn’t a single corner—not one where even the slyest of rats hid—that hadn’t heard of Talenick’s arrest and the subsequent downfall of his business.

From human trafficking to exploitative slave contracts designed entirely in favor of their masters, the list of offenses was extensive.

The exact details of how the organization had been brought down remained unclear. The most common rumor claimed that a disgruntled mercenary, wronged by Talenick through prejudice or withheld wages, had chosen to burn everything to the ground in a single act of retaliation. A complete scorched-earth approach.

Only a few knew the truth behind the matter.

As for the city itself, many businesses began to be audited.

After the capture of the mercenaries, interrogation tactics were used to identify all the co-conspirators working with them. This included individuals from nearly every known business sector in the city, from food stands to farmers to large merchant houses and alchemists within the mage association.

Each and every case was handled swiftly, backed by the full authority of the City Lord, Viscount Leopold. The guards operated under the direction of his son, Lord Tomas, who also conducted most of the arrests and recorded the information.

“Is this… really alright?” Veronica asked meekly.

She stood inside her inn room, facing a mirror. Beside her, Gwen held up a dress between Veronica and her reflection.

“Leave it all to me,” Gwen said. “We can’t have you going in your travel clothes.”

Veronica’s mind wandered elsewhere as she stood still, letting Gwen inspect the dresses and their matching accessories.

Many of the loose ends in Ronswick had been taken care of. In just a few weeks, she had fought assassins disguised as merchants and even helped take down the business who had been shackling Galland, one that was possibly linked to the largest criminal organization in Vitian.

Lord Tomas was still gathering information about the ‘boots’ that had gone missing. However, most leads ended in dead ends. The disappearances had been carried out professionally, leaving little trace behind. Of course, Galland had been assisting with the effort. Over the past few days, they had found roughly six or seven people who had been taken.

Most of them were in poor condition—starved, injured, and heavily bruised. They were now recovering safely at the town infirmary under the care of specialists.

The two merchants who had staged the goblin attack in the forest had also been executed. Although they had come to Ronswick to forge new identity cards, their list of crimes far exceeded what the prison guards had initially suspected. Not only were they tried for their actions in Ronswick, but they were also wanted in three other cities for far worse offenses.

Luckily, she had been paid for her troubles.

As promised, Lord Tomas provided her with the agreed-upon compensation.

Three kilograms of hartivel vines and five thousand vix.

The vix was useful, but the true reward was the hartivel vines. After speaking with Hugh, Veronica had commissioned a set of three specific potions from the alchemy division—discounted, courtesy of Galland. It would take a few days to prepare them, so she planned to collect them before leaving town.

“Okay… let’s go with this one,” Gwen murmured, looking at the several pieces of clothing laid out across the bed. “…she would probably appreciate this… right?”

Veronica glanced over at the mage for a brief moment before turning back toward the mirror.

The matters with the mage association had also been settled.

Hugh informed her that they had granted her request. Not only was she now a registered mage within the association, but they had also agreed to take responsibility for Rocky and provide protection. For the time being, Tower Master Rarran had assigned several dedicated mages to oversee him.

At first, Veronica had expected them to simply station guards around Rocky. Instead, out of concern for his growing size, they insisted on relocating him to a controlled refuge in the north—the Berdow Highlands—land overseen by the association itself.

Arranging such a transfer would not be simple. Teleportation magic or even an airship would be costly. Still, it seemed the association was more than willing, likely due to the notes Hugh had sent on her behalf. For now, all that remained was to wait for the transportation to be arranged.

Of course, she had also completed her commission at the Adventurer’s Guild. Although she hadn’t killed Rocky, the core matter of the request had been resolved. There had been some pushback at first, given the lack of bodies or clear evidence, but Lord Tomas had stepped in to vouch for her, settling the matter.

With that, the situation with Rocky and Gwen becoming her apprentice had been resolved as well.

The association had even offered her transportation to Annesheim, but Veronica refused.

Although that was her eventual destination, she was nowhere near strong enough to enter on equal footing. She might have the knowledge to stand among professors, but not the power to back it. All it would take was one jealous peer to force the issue, and things could quickly turn dangerous.

There were still a few places she needed to visit first. Especially if she had truly been sent back fifteen years. If her memory was correct, an important event would soon take place on the Fellnight Plains—one she could not afford to miss.

Gwen stepped over and lifted Veronica’s arms, holding another dress up to see how it would look.

“Were you able to meet with Galland earlier?” Veronica asked.

“I was,” Gwen answered. “Oh, this looks great. Let’s go with this.”

Veronica took the dress from her and inspected it. “This one, huh?”

Gwen continued speaking as Veronica stepped aside to change, her voice carrying easily across the room.

“I gave him that dagger, like you asked. Though he was a bit busy at the time to give a proper appraisal,” Gwen said, already sorting through a few accessories laid out on the table.

“Busy?”

“Yeah. He was dealing with some paperwork involving Helm.”

Helm.

Veronica had heard what happened.

He had been badly injured during a prison brawl. It seemed he had been specifically targeted by several other prisoners. Most likely, it was a ploy by the Scavengers to eliminate loose ends. The incident had supposedly occurred only a few hours after her visit to the notary office.

Maybe his injuries were her fault. Nevertheless, accepting one beating as the price for freedom seemed like a straightforward decision.

Veronica returned a moment later, adjusting the sleeves as she stepped back in front of the mirror.

Gwen moved behind her, pulling the dress into place and fastening it.

“It’s a bit heavier than it looks,” Veronica said.

“Deal with it. Trust me. It’s worth it,” Gwen replied, stepping back. “Turn.”

Veronica did, if only slightly.

Gwen circled her once, adjusting the outer layer of the dress.

“I left the dagger with him. He said he’d have more information by tomorrow morning. He does think it’s some kind of ceremonial piece a noble would carry.”

“Good. Hopefully he can trace what family it belongs to. Might be able to get a bit more travel money out of it.”

“How much do we have?”

“After everything—including the vix from Lorence’s room and the guild reward… roughly twelve thousand vix.”

“Mm. Make it fifteen thousand,” Gwen said, patting the sides of the dress.

“Fifteen?”

“I’ve got about three thousand saved up. If we’re going to save the world, then what’s mine is yours, right?”

Veronica smiled. “Of course.”

“Now, turn to the mirror.”

Veronica did as told, giving a small spin, shifting slightly from left to right.

Back in Annesheim, as the Kingdom’s prized prodigy, she had attended countless social events. Most were meant for political networking—building ties with nobility or representatives from other nations. Grand dresses, layered and ornate—she had worn them all.

But they had always been ceremonial. Rooms filled with stiff nobles and careful smiles; sly tongues with buttery words to gain favor. It was exhausting, but a necessary function of her life there.

Even then, her vitality had already been draining from the curse. She had relied on an artifact to maintain her appearance. Without it, despite being in her twenties, she would have looked decades older. By the time she reached her true thirties, the toll had only worsened.

After each event, she would return to her dorm, deactivate the artifact, and resume enduring her condition. It had all been a fragile illusion. A false sense of security.

Now…

She could see herself as she truly was, unmarred by any device.

“So…? How is it?” Gwen chirped, leaning in to study her reflection.

“It’s… very beautiful.”

The dress fit her form closely, a deep black base layered with rich emerald fabric that shifted smoothly with each movement. Silver patterns traced along the bodice and waist, winding toward a green crystal clasp at the front. The sleeves shimmered as they draped along her arms, while the skirt fell in soft, layered folds over a darker underlayer. The design left her shoulders bare, with two long black straps wrapping from the sides up to her neck, secured by one final crystal.

Gwen crossed her arms, clearly pleased. “I’m glad you like it. I think it’s perfect for you. You have the body for it.”

Veronica raised a brow. She had heard that before…

Right. Melda. The clothier from Greystone. Now Gwen was repeating the same words. Though she couldn’t tell if it was genuine or just flattery.

There was also the matter of receiving word from Melda’s friend—the one who worked in Annesheim. Though she hadn’t received a notice or letter yet.

“Where did you get this? It looks expensive.”

Expensive in the way that they only had roughly fifteen thousand vix. The dresses she wore in Annesheim were nearly priceless.

“Ah—it’s a rental.”

“What?”

“I asked Galland if he could find something for you to wear on your date. He got me in contact with a clothier here in town. Everything we take is covered by him since we helped him out. But it’s only a rental, so we’ll need to return it in a couple of days.”

Veronica frowned slightly.

“It’s not a date,” she said. “Nasha just wanted to get dinner with me.”

Gwen tilted her head, a smirk forming as she looked up at her. “Oh, really?”

Veronica pursed her lips. “I told you, she overheard our conversation. She probably just wants to talk about it at dinner.”

“Yeah?” Gwen said, amused. “That’s why she told you to wear something… ‘nice?’”

“She wanted to eat at a higher-class restaurant. It only makes sense to dress properly.”

Gwen sighed. “Oh, boy… I don’t even have a betrothed, but even I can tell something’s going on…”

“What?” Veronica asked, confused.

With one arm folded across her chest, Gwen raised the other, resting her chin against her palm. “I’m just saying, you might want to expect a bit more after this… ‘dinner’ than you think. I don’t know if it’ll get there, but… who knows?”

“What are you talking about?” Veronica said. “I understand what you’re implying, but we’re both women. I’ve heard of performers like Nasha—they like to tease people. That’s probably all it is. Nothing like what you’re imagining.”

She wasn’t unfamiliar with being pursued. In the future, many nobles had tried to approach her at gatherings. Even the King had attempted to arrange marriages for her, though she quickly became too powerful for any alliance to offer meaningful benefit.

The difference was that they had always been men. Of course, there were also women who tried to curry favor with her—their words filled with fake flattery—or there were ones who approached in a way that was more of awe or respect for her position.

At the time, however, such matters had never held much importance when she didn’t know how long she had left before the Medusa’s curse claimed her. There just wasn’t time for such things with death looming overhead.

“Women, huh?” Gwen repeated, now pursing her lips. “I don’t know how things work in the capital, but… it might be a little different out here.”

Veronica simply looked at her, unsure what exactly Gwen was getting at.

“If it’s someone like Nasha…” Gwen murmured. “She’s probably the type to…”

Gwen glanced toward the clothing scattered across the bed.

“Alright. Get out of that dress for a moment. There are a few more things you need to try on.”

“What? I thought you said this was the perfect dress,” Veronica said, lightly lifting one side of the layered fabric.

Gwen looked back with a small smirk. “Oh, it is. That’s definitely the one,” she said. “I’m talking about… the other things you might want to pair with it. Just in case.”
