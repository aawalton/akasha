The Cinderfall Hegemony’s approach to viewing the impending duel was arrogant, theatrical, and hopelessly archaic.

On their side of the valley, combat mages frantically erected massive, enchanted observation crystals on hastily built wooden platforms. They were creating localized viewing screens so King Theron and his nobility could watch the slaughter from the safety of their warded redoubts. The system was primitive, clunky, and highly susceptible to ambient magical interference.

I, on the other hand, had a sports broadcast.

The Oracle, parked comfortably in the stratosphere, fed a crystal-clear, top-down tactical feed directly to the command network. For a more personal touch, a dozen heavily shielded micro-drones hovered around the designated combat arena, providing multiple, high-resolution viewing angles.

I wasn't the only one watching. Across the frontlines, the Aegis officers huddled around holographic projectors set up in the trenches and command bunkers. General Goliath was a legend among the ranks. To the young elves fresh out of the Academy, he was the stoic titan they all aspired to emulate. Knowing this, I authorized a general distribution of extra rations and stimulants. A small morale boost before the show began.

The atmosphere in my household mirrored the front lines, though with significantly more comfortable seating.

We weren't gathered in the cold, sterile CIC. I had moved the command feed to the family room in the New Wighthelm pocket dimension. It was a spacious, sunlit room with plush couches, a massive weirwood coffee table, and a thick, woven rug that currently hosted a dragon-cuddle puddle.

My parents shared a long sofa. My father sat forward, hands resting on his knees, his eyes fixed intensely on the floating holographic display. My mother sat beside him, watching the screen with grim resolve. I sat on the opposite couch, Nyxia close beside me. She was tense, her crimson eyes tracking the Cinderfall troop movements in the background of the main feed.

In the center of the room, Kaelus had assumed a slightly larger form, roughly the size of a draft horse. He lay on his back, eyes half-closed in cosmic bliss, while Lyra and Ela sat on either side of his belly. The two girls were engaged in the serious business of feeding the Dragon Prince. Lyra aggressively shoved a pastry toward his maw, demanding he "eat the mountain," while Ela offered her treats with quiet reverence. Kaelus accepted all offerings without complaint.

On a smaller, separate couch, Patricia finally took a seat. My mother had insisted, practically ordering the head maid to rest her feet. Patricia sat on the very edge of the cushion, her posture rigidly professional, a silver serving tray resting perfectly balanced on her knees.

The seat next to Patricia was occupied by a man who looked like he wanted to phase through the floor and disappear into the ship’s hull.

George.

I had invited Bob’s younger brother to watch the duel with us. I thought it would be a nice gesture, allowing him to see his brother’s triumph from the best seat in the house. Now, watching the young cadet sweat profusely through his pristine black uniform, I realized I might have severely miscalculated.

He was sitting in the private chambers of his Lord Commander, sharing a room with the Duke and Duchess of Wight, a legendary Dragon Prince, and the terrifying head maid of our household. Poor George looked like a rabbit trapped in a cage full of very polite, very deadly lions.

He clutched a cup of tea with white-knuckled hands, his eyes darting nervously between the screen and my father. He’s Bob’s brother. He’s family. He’ll just have to get used to the weirdness.

The sun on the holographic feed reached its zenith. Noon. The designated time.

The movement began.

From the Cinderfall lines, a small procession detached and marched toward the center of no-man's land. Sir Dumaric Farthallow, sporting a newly attached head, rode at the front. He was enclosed in a shimmering, pale blue sphere of defensive magic.

Does he really think I'm going to shoot him again? I mused, leaning back into the couch. The paranoia was almost flattering.

From our side, the sleek, black form of a modified Revenant carrier dropped from the clouds, hovering over the meeting point. General Valen stepped out, dropping to the earth with his usual, theatrical flair.

The two representatives met in the center of the scarred valley. Dumaric looked nervous, his eyes constantly scanning the sky for invisible snipers. Valen looked bored.

"We are here to confirm the terms of the engagement," Valen declared, his voice carrying cleanly over the audio feed.

Dumaric puffed out his chest, trying to regain the swagger he had lost yesterday. "The terms are simple. It is a duel of honor between the chosen champions. No external interference. No hidden archers, no cowardly metal machines firing from the horizon. The combatants stand alone. Only a formally contracted magical beast may accompany their knight."

"Agreed," Valen nodded lazily. "And the conclusion?"

"The duel is to the death," Dumaric stated, a cruel smile twisting his face. "Unless one side yields and begs for their life to be spared by the opposing ruler. Should your champion surrender, he may beg our glorious King Theron for mercy. Perhaps, if he grovels sufficiently, His Majesty will allow your man to live as a slave in the sulfur mines."

My father let out a low, dangerous scoff from the opposite couch.

On the rug, Lyra paused her dragon-feeding operation. She looked up at the screen, then turned to me, her brow furrowed in deep annoyance.

"Brother, this is so boring," she complained, pointing a sticky finger at Dumaric. "They just keep talking and talking. Where are the explosions? I thought you said there would be booms."

My mother smiled gently, reaching over to wipe a smear of frosting from Lyra's cheek. "Patience, my little lioness. The fight hasn't started yet. They are just setting the rules."

Ela ignored the screen entirely, completely absorbed in the task of placing a small, sugary biscuit perfectly in Kaelus's mouth.

Back on the feed, Valen offered a cold, predatory smile. "We accept your terms, Sir Dumaric. A fight to the death. No quarter asked, none given."

Dumaric seemed unsettled by the immediate agreement, but he pressed on, turning his mount back toward the Cinderfall lines. He raised a hand, signaling his heralds.

"For the glory of the Cinderfall Hegemony!" a herald roared, his voice magnified by a cluster of amplification crystals. "For the honor of the Obsidian Throne, our champion steps forth! I present Sir Xeroth, Knight of the First Flame, wielder of the undying fire, and the chosen blade of the Mother Phoenix!"

The introduction was accompanied by a massive, theatrical display of pyrotechnics. A towering pillar of swirling, golden-red fire erupted from the Cinderfall front lines.

From the heart of the inferno, a magnificent phoenix emerged. It was significantly larger than Dumaric’s mount, its feathers burning with a fierce, almost white-hot intensity.

A figure clad in heavy, crimson-enameled armor leaped from the bird's back, landing in the ash with a heavy thud. The phoenix immediately shrank, shifting into its smaller, avian form, and landed gracefully on the knight's shoulder. Its mere presence caused the air around Sir Xeroth to shimmer and warp with immense heat.

The Cinderfall side of the valley erupted into a chaotic, deafening roar of cheers and clashing weapons. It was a desperate attempt to manufacture morale.

I glanced at my father. He leaned forward, his fists clenched tightly on his knees, his knuckles white. I could feel the warrior’s spirit itching beneath his skin. He wanted to be out there. He wanted to stand in the ash, bringing the wrath of Aerthos down on the men who had burned his home.

He knew the strategy. He was the ultimate trump card. If Cinderfall realized Kaelen Wight lived, and that Cygnus had not lost his contractor, the political landscape would shatter. A battle involving two fully unrestrained Tier 9 combatants—my father and the Mother Phoenix—would likely reduce the entire northern half of the continent to a molten wasteland. The Dragon Kings would intervene, a forced ceasefire would be called, and I would be dragged into decades of stagnant, diplomatic maneuvering.

I didn't want diplomacy. I wanted to break them.

My attention returned to the screen as Valen raised his hand to announce our champion.

"For the Aegis Legion," Valen said, his voice smooth and unbothered by the screaming horde across the valley. "We present Baron Robert Millfield. Knight-Commander of House Wight. General of the Steel Tide, and Four-Star Grand Warlord."

There was no pillar of fire. There was no theatrical leap from a flying beast.

I had initially pictured a simple, stoic walk-up for Bob. A quiet, terrifying display of confidence.

Bob didn't disappoint. He began walking forward from our front lines. He wasn't in his massive Mark-M MECH. He wore the sleek, specialized Mark II-G armor I had forged specifically with a Dragon Knight in mind. It was matte black, trimmed in Wight silver, hugging his massive frame perfectly.

As he took his first step, something unexpected happened.

In the trenches behind him, an Aegis commander—I couldn't tell who from the wide angle—raised his gauntleted fist and struck his chest plate in the standard Legion salute.

Thump.

Then, he did it again, slower.

Thump.

A few more officers joined him. Then a squad. Then a battalion. The sound amplified, rolling down the trench lines like a gathering storm.

Thump. Thump. Thump.

It was a slow, rhythmic, terrifying beat.

I hadn't planned this. It was the sound of a war machine coming alive.

I sent a rapid mental command to Tes. Sync the automata. Have them join the rhythm.

Instantly, the half-million unfeeling machines standing in the forward trenches raised their arms. In perfect, mechanical unison, they began striking their armored chests.

THUMP. THUMP. THUMP.

The synchronized impact of half a million steel fists caused a localized geological event. The stones on the valley floor shook. Visible ripples tore through the grey ash.

Bob walked forward, and it looked as if every heavy, deliberate step he took was shaking the earth itself. He was a titan of black steel, walking to the beat of an iron drum.

The chaotic cheering from the Cinderfall lines faltered. It died away entirely, swallowed by the relentless rhythm of the Legion.

Bob stopped a few paces behind Valen, offering a slow, respectful nod.

Sir Dumaric opened his mouth, likely to spout another monologue about bravery and fire, but Valen cut him off.

"And his partner," Valen announced, his voice rising over the rhythmic thumping. "Royal Guard to the King of the Blue Dragons. Aquarius."

A massive whirlpool of water began to form in the dry, ashen air. It swirled upward from the ground, drawing moisture from the atmosphere, before coalescing into a shimmering, muscular form.

Aquarius took his place beside Bob, his watery scales catching the sunlight, a massive, predatory presence radiating a damp, suffocating cold.

The sight of the dragon hit Sir Dumaric like a physical blow. The Cinderfall representative stumbled backward, losing his footing in the ash and falling hard onto his backside, his eyes wide with stark terror.

The realization rippled through the massed Cinderfall forces like a cold wind. They weren't just facing a machine or a dark elf. They were facing a Dragon knight. And not just any Dragon a Water Dragon. The elemental apex predator to their fiery phoenixes.

Valen looked down at the terrified Dumaric, a cruel, mocking smile playing on his lips. He delivered the final, devastating sentence.

"The last Dragon Knight from the old guard of Aerthos."

The words hung in the air. The realization dawned on the Cinderfall commanders watching from their tents. Four years ago, they had purged the north. They thought they had slaughtered every knight, every dragon rider loyal to House Wight.

They had missed one. And now, the ghost of their failure had returned, clad in unbreakable steel, to collect the debt.

Sir Dumaric scrambled backward through the ash. His polished armor clanked undignifiedly until his retainers rushed forward to haul him upright. He pointed a trembling, gauntleted finger at Valen, then at Bob, before turning and fleeing toward the Cinderfall lines without another word.

The parley officially concluded.

Valen offered a mock, sweeping bow to the retreating knight, then turned to Bob.

"General," the elven commander said, his tone shifting from theatrical sarcasm to crisp military respect. "The floor is yours."

Valen engaged his thrusters, ascending back to the waiting Revenant carrier.

Bob stood alone in the center of the valley, Aquarius coiled protectively behind him. Across the expanse of grey ash, Sir Xeroth of the First Flame mirrored his stance. His Tier 8 phoenix perched upon his shoulder, an aura of intense, shimmering heat radiating outward.

The rhythmic thumping of the Aegis Legion ceased abruptly on a silent command from Tes. The sudden absence of noise was deafening.

The midday sun beat down on the valley. The duel began.

Sir Xeroth roared, a sound fueled by the fanaticism of a knight who believed he wielded divine fire. He lunged forward, covering the ground with terrifying speed, his heavy broadsword igniting with a blinding, golden-white flame.

Bob stood perfectly still, his black armor absorbing the harsh sunlight.

Xeroth swung, a massive, two-handed diagonal slash aimed at Bob's neck. The heat from the blade was enough to flash-boil the moisture in the air.

A fraction of a second before impact, Bob raised his left arm. The silver Dragon Knight blade, forged specifically for the elite riders of old Aerthos, flashed from its scabbard.

CLANG.

The impact rang out like a bell. A shockwave of displaced air kicked up a cloud of ash around them. The golden flames of Xeroth's blade washed over Bob, engulfing him in a roaring inferno.

A cheer erupted from the Cinderfall lines. It was premature.

The flames parted, repelled by a localized, shimmering sphere of condensed water magic projected by Aquarius. Bob stood unharmed inside the bubble, his silver blade locked against the burning broadsword, his black armor unmarked.

"You rely too much on the fire," Bob rumbled, his voice amplified by his helmet speakers. "You have forgotten the steel."

With a brutal twist of his wrist, Bob disengaged the blade lock. His superior physical strength—augmented by the Mark II-G's servos—threw Xeroth off balance. Before the Cinderfall knight could recover, Bob drove his armored knee into Xeroth's midsection.

The armor crumpled. Xeroth flew backward, skipping across the ash like a thrown stone.

He bounced back to his feet instantly, enraged. The phoenix on his shoulder shrieked, its golden fire flaring brighter, transferring raw, restorative mana directly into its knight. The dent in Xeroth's breastplate popped back out with a metallic ping.

"You are a relic!" Xeroth screamed, raising his sword high. "The old guard is dead! We are the eternal flame!"

Halting his advance, Xeroth plunged his burning sword directly into the ashen ground.

The earth cracked. A network of glowing, golden fissures raced outward from his blade, heading straight for Bob. It was a high-tier area-of-effect spell, designed to turn the ground beneath an enemy into a miniature volcano.

Bob remained calm, smoothly sheathing his silver blade.

Aquarius, Bob thought, the command flowing seamlessly through their bond.

The Water Dragon reared up. He inhaled deeply, drawing moisture from the very air, before unleashing a torrent of high-pressure, freezing water directly into the approaching fissures.

The collision of extreme heat and absolute cold was catastrophic. A massive explosion of superheated steam erupted, creating an impenetrable, blinding cloud of white fog that swallowed the center of the battlefield.

In the Cinderfall command tents, King Theron leaned forward, his amber eyes narrowed, trying to pierce the fog. On the The Aegis, I watched the screen closely. Tes's thermal and mana-resonance sensors cut right through the steam, displaying the combatants in crisp wireframe detail.

Inside the fog, Xeroth swung blindly through the vapor, his blade meeting only empty air. He relied on his phoenix's magical senses to track the enemy.

Then, a deep, resonant hum vibrated through the mist.

VWOOM.

A blade of pure, azure plasma ignited behind him.

Xeroth spun, bringing his sword up in a desperate parry.

The black-hilted Plasma Katana met the golden broadsword and passed straight through the enchanted metal. The superheated plasma sheared the weapon in two with a sickening hiss, the severed top half of the blade tumbling harmlessly into the ash.

Xeroth stared at the ruined hilt in his hand. His fanaticism finally cracked, replaced by a spike of genuine, mortal terror.

Stepping immediately into Xeroth's guard, Bob moved with a speed that belied his armor's bulk. The plasma katana hummed as he drove it straight through the center of Xeroth's chest plate.

The heat of the blade cauterized the wound instantly. There was no blood.

Xeroth fell to his knees, gasping.

The phoenix on his shoulder shrieked in agony, its connection to its knight suddenly flooded with lethal trauma. It burst into a sphere of blinding golden light, pouring its restorative magic into the dying man.

Xeroth inhaled sharply, color returning to his face. The terrible, searing wound in his chest began to close, the flesh knitting itself back together, the armor reforming.

He looked up at Bob, a manic, triumphant grin spreading across his face. "We are eternal!" he rasped, struggling to his feet. "You cannot kill the First Flame!"

Bob deactivated the plasma katana with a soft click. He stepped back, holstering the weapon on his right hip. He stood tall, crossing his arms over his massive chest.

"I have no intention to," Bob said, his voice dropping to a low, chilling calm.

Xeroth paused, confused.

Bob looked past him, locking eyes with the golden phoenix perched on Xeroth's shoulder.

The bird noticed a shadow falling over it. It looked up.

Hovering silently above the dissipating steam cloud, its massive jaws unhinged, was Aquarius.

The water dragon used the steam as cover to flank the true target.

Before the phoenix could launch itself into the air, Aquarius snapped his jaws shut, swallowing the golden bird whole.

Xeroth let out a strangled, horrified scream, his hand clutching his chest. He felt the echo of his phoenix’s annihilation through their bond—a searing, white-hot pain that burned him from the inside out.

Inside Aquarius's maw, a muffled, golden explosion flashed, momentarily illuminating the dragon's watery scales from within. The phoenix had initiated its resurrection cycles, trying to burst free.

Aquarius simply swallowed harder, channeling an immense surge of freezing, absolute zero magic into his own gullet. The golden light sputtered a few more times, dimmed, and then died completely.

The phoenix was extinguished.

Xeroth collapsed onto his back, his body convulsing as the shock of the severed bond tore through his nervous system. He lay in the ash, gasping, a mortal man stripped of his immortality.

Bob walked over, his heavy boots crunching in the dirt. He stood over the defeated knight.

"You fought bravely," Bob rumbled, a note of genuine respect in his voice for a fellow warrior. "But your king sent you to die for his pride."

Bob drew his silver Dragon Knight blade, the ancient steel glinting in the sunlight. He raised it high.

"For Aerthos," Bob whispered.

The blade fell.

A profound, horrified silence gripped the Cinderfall army.

They had just watched their champion, a Knight of the First Flame, a man considered unkillable, systematically dismantled and executed in under three minutes. They had watched a dragon swallow their sacred fire.

The myth of Cinderfall invincibility died in that ashen valley.
