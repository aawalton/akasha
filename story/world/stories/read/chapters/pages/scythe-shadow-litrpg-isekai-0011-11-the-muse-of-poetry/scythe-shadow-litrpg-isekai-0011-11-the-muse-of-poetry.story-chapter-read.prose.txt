The rhinoceros mutant charged.

A white blur whirred in front of me, blocking my view of the monster, and suddenly I was being dragged along by the hood of my cloak. The tie strangling my neck, something in my back ripping through my muscle and skin. Then I was released, tumbling into the grass.

I rolled into a heap in a cluster of purple flowers.

Before me, a majestic, snow white undead horse reared back on its hind legs. Atop its back, a hooded, Amber-cloaked rider formed a crystalline spear. Moonlight sparkled through the translucent weapon, catching it just right to make a million tiny rainbows across its surface.

I was given little time to admire the weapon, as the rhino stomped its feet and snorted in challenge. My rescuer accepted, urging the horse toward the monster. They jabbed at the rhino's side, but the thick skin flashed like metallic steel around the impact, deflecting the blow. The spear shattered, sending glistening shards into the air that jingled like broken glass.

There was no hesitation. The beast swung it's metal horn, but the horse backpedalled and reared to its hind legs, sending the rider tumbling off. They rolled into a three-point stance.

The rhino looked at me, but was immediately hit in the head by little crystal knives. It turned back to the other Reaper and attacked. They were alone, now weaponless too, with a gigantic beast moments away from trampling them.

I got to my feet, trying to resurrect the courage to go help.

But the Reaper's white horse arrived just in time, storming in from nowhere, lowering its head so the they could grab onto the bridle and swing themselves up to the saddle. They galloped away, putting distance from the rhino, but staying between it and me.

The two stared at one another, each waiting for the other to make a move. The Reaper summoned a new spear and twirled it acrobatically.

Then the horse and rider charged at the rhino, spear outstretched like a fucking jousting competition. As the two foes neared, the spear extended longer, leaking gray fog like a wintry morning’s breath. The monster bellowed.

Then they collided.

The rhino shouldered into the horse, knocking it to the side, but not off its feet. Its horn missed skewering the animal by inches. The rider flipped into the air far more gracefully than I could have imagined possible and landed behind the beast, which stumbled a few feet further, then collapsed. A frosty, blood-soaked crystal spear stuck through its eye and out the back of its skull.

Not crystal. Ice.

I knew that strike all too well.

The girl from the tavern turned around to look at me and threw back her hood, revealing her face. She was smiling wide, her eyes twinkling like her ice spear.

Wonderful. Just fucking wonderful.

So much for escaping her and Jericho. Now, I not only had to deal with her again, but she’d just saved my pathetic life. Of course, it was her fault I ended up back here alone in the first place. She'd killed me, after all.

The girl walked closer, her horse trotting up beside her. Nuzzle peeked out from inside her hood and licked her cheek several times.

Oh, fabulous… And she’d stolen my fucking pet, too.

“Come here to stab me again?” I asked.

She laughed and brushed a lock of blonde and pink hair back over her ear. “Depends. You gonna be a silkie about it?”

“I still don’t know what that means.”

She stalked around me as if trying to evaluate a show dog, then lifted the edge of my cloak and ruffled it. “Silk. As in, soft. Or metal.” She tapped her scythe blade against my chest. “As in, hard. Iron Willed.”

Nuzzle hopped off her shoulder and onto mine. I rubbed a knuckle under his chin.

“Just don’t expect a warm hug. If you’re gonna stab someone in the eye, the least you can do is tell them your name first,” I said.

She blinked, then chuckled. “I never told you my name?”

“You were too busy trying to name me. After a fucking flower, I might add.”

She stepped close, her chest inches from mine, eyes at my chin, looking up. She smelled faintly like strawberries. A finger pressed against my sternum and she pushed away with a twirl. “I’m Calliope,” she said. “And I was wrong. You’re no flower. More like the wind the way you move. Especially with your Aero Shift.”

She’d seen that?

“How long were you watching before you intervened?”

Calliope winked. “Long enough.”

So she’d watched me get completely outmatched by this Sin. That would probably come back to bite me later.

“What now?” I asked.

Calliope shrugged. “There are two Wills here.” She pointed at the rhino and the other Sin it had pummeled on its quest to attack me. A skull had formed over its head, that hadn't been there before the rhino eviscerated it. “One for you. One for me. I’ll take the big boy, if you don’t mind. I did do all the real work anyway.”

I nodded. “Seems only fair.”

“I saw your scythe over there.” She nodded back behind the rhino. “You know how to harvest, right?”

The memory of Zeus stealing the Will of the first monster I defeated returned. I didn't need her knowing about that too.

“I should be able to figure it out.”

I collected my scythe and found the other creature on the slope of the small hill nearby.

It was still alive, pulling itself by one hand up the slope, the other hanging limp at its side. A trail of blood smeared along the grass behind it from the puncture wound in its stomach.

It appeared ant-like, but with a pair of human arms and four human legs. Creepily human eyes blinked away tears on its otherwise insectoid face.

I inspected the creature with my infected eye. What did Zeus call it? Second Sight? The Will in its chest shimmered faintly, then faded out and solidified. The ant-beast ceased its struggle, finally succumbing to the injuries.

At least I wouldn’t have to kill it myself.

I slid my scythe through the exoskeleton and pierced the Will. Veins of light formed across the orb’s circumference, then seeped into my blade, giving it an Amber glow, the same way Zeus’s had after he harvested the first Will I’d earned.

[Will successfully harvested.

Rank: Amber

Affinity: Zeal]

As the Will slipped out of existence, a small object remained in the chest cavity, caged among the ribs, even as the body began its rapid decay. I reached in and grabbed what looked like an engagement ring.

The large diamond in the ring sparkled, set on an intricate silver design. Much more extravagant than the one I'd given Avery.

Avery… I'd seen her. It was only a brief moment. But still… Maybe this wasn't all as hopeless as it seemed. I could go back, though apparently not permanently.

Can I still continue on with my real life, even with all this? Would it be enough to live half a life with her when part of me existed here?

Another text box popped up in my vision.

[Silver Engagement Ring

Effect: Unknown]

[Add to inventory?]

Uhh… Sure, why not?

The exoskeleton on my right arm shimmered, the outer layer liquifying and creeping down my wrist until it encompassed the ring. It pulled the object within and re-solidified.

I expected it to feel weird. To sense an extra weight or presence in my arm. Nothing. As if the object has simply vanished away.

I thought about the ring, picturing the details of the design, the slight hue of the diamond. My carapace rippled, traveling to the tips of my fingers. Little tendrils flaked off and became the ring, just like how the scythe formed.

So weird.

"Get anything good?" Calliope asked, walking over.

"Uh. A ring," I said, as it was slurped back into my inventory. "Do they always leave items behind like that?"

"Yeah. You'll have to bring it to an appraiser to learn what it does though. Or you can just use it during battle without an appraisal if you're desperate, then pray it does something useful in the moment, but that's pretty risky."

Taking items from corpses. Harvesting Wills. This all felt wrong. Killing is supposed to be wrong, right? No matter the justification. But that god had said I'd be doing something good.

These monsters were manifestations of human sin. Corruption I could expunge from the world. It was easy to forget that's what all this was about. That was supposed to make all this easier.

For some reason, it didn't.

I stared at the ant hybrid's body as it disintegrated into ash.

The blood stains remained.
