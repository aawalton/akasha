Chapter II.XIII (2.13)

“Where are the ice drifts?” Holly asked. “Aren’t there supposed to be massive icebergs scattered throughout the sea surrounding this island?”

“That’s what Nick at the bar told us,” Mitsuko replied. “Maybe he hasn’t been home in a while?”

Beside Mitsuko, Holly stood on a crate to get a good look over the gunwales at the island. It was a bleak brown patch of land. Clumps of dirty snow were scattered around the island. They got a long view as The Selcouth Sable wrapped around the island’s coast, approaching from the southside.

“Odd to call this place Porcelain Island when nothing is white,” Mitsuko commented.

“That’s not fair,” Holly said. “If you look in the distance the mountains are covered in snow.”

“Even still, they have stretches of brown smeared across them,” Mitsuko pointed out. “It’s a bleak island. Not a whole lot to look at.”

“There’s a few pine trees over there,” Holly pointed out. “A small forest.”

That was the most exciting and colorful thing they saw along the coast. Everything else was some shade of brown.

“I think Umber Island would be a more appropriate name,” Mitsuko said.

They arrived at the coastal port to no fanfare. The other moored ships bobbed in their wake as the ship’s elementalist carefully brought them into port.

Not a single soul to be seen.

“What happened here?” Mitsuko asked.

But nobody had any response to the query. A somber, depressed mood took the sailors on The Selcouth Sable. There were quiet whispers, but it was as if they were worried they were approaching a haunted port and taking care not to anger perceived spirits.

Mitsuko and Holly were the first two to disembark. Nobody spoke up or stood in their way as they headed into the village.

Half of the dilapidated buildings were half collapsed and the others sagged. Clumps of snow existed solely in the shadows. A small penned area for animals was abandoned with smears of dark brown blood on the brown dirt. Still, nobody approached. The dome above them kept even a breeze from disturbing the village. It reminded Mitsuko of the city on Ashen Island. Utterly devoid of life.

“Nick mentioned an inn,” Mitsuko said. “See anything?”

“Maybe let’s try that house over there?” Holly suggested. “It looks mostly intact. Just a sagging roof.”

Without any other leads, they approached. There was a sign over the door that labeled the building as Snowflake Manor. As Mitsuko knocked, the door creaked open under her touch. The inside did, indeed, look like an inn. An abandoned one in desperate need of refurbishing. Broken chairs and tables were strewn about the main dining area, any bottles on the wine racks had been taken or ransacked, a small mound of ice that reached Mitsuko’s knees was at the center of the room surrounded by a puddle of water, and the wooden ceiling sagged over their heads.

Rounded ears on a furry head twitched up as they approached. Not a sculpt. Not even a species native to the archipelago. The man was unmistakably a bear Kemon. He blinked his big, brown eyes at them and then smiled. His muscular frame sat up and the chair creaked under him.

“Oh, hello! I was just taking a nap.” The Kemon man fumbled for something in his pockets then triumphantly held out a handful of yellow gunk. “Do you want some dried mango? I was snacking on this when I fell asleep. It’s a bit softer than usual because it was in my pocket.”

“No, thank you,” Mitsuko said. She could probably return the dried mango back to a more edible state with a cast of Mend, but her body also no longer needed to eat food so she saw no point.

“I’ll take some!” Holly plucked a strand of the old fruit from his furry hand. Mitsuko half expected the claws to close on her friend’s hand. But no, the gnome successfully obtained the mango.

“I used to have some nuts somewhere as well. But I can’t remember where I put them. How is it?”

“Chewy,” Holly said, her mouth full.

“What is a Kemon doing all the way out here?” Mitsuko asked. “You’re a long way from Tross. And from what I read in the trade ledgers, there’s not a lot coming in or out of here for your nation.”

“I’m a diplomat! Allow me to introduce myself. I am Jay, Shaman and representative of the Aklaq Tribe.”

“Aklaq Tribe,” Mitsuko repeated. “If I remember right, that’s one of the Kemon tribes up on the northern slope of Tross. In the tundra.”

“Correct! With the Tross schism a few years back, we’re currently struggling to keep a hold of our independence. So I got sent out here to gather more allies and political support.”

Mitsuko glanced around the ruined inn. “I can’t imagine you found much.”

“Oh, it wasn’t like this a few days back. Before the dome appeared everything here was spiffy. I was making friends, my kids were off running around with a bunch of snowmen. Granted, sculpts are hardly the most influential worldwide faction, but they could relate to us in a lot of unique ways. I felt some potential here for a strong partnership.”

Holly finally gulped the last of her portion of mango. “You’re children are here?”

“On the island. But they fled to further inland, along with all the surviving sculpts. I offered to stay behind to greet any visitors. Do you want to see some pictures of them? I had some paintings made of them just a week before we last departed Hon! Let me find those.” He started fumbling around in his pockets again. “Oh! I found the nuts! Did you still want some? And spinach too!”

He held out a hand palm up with cashews and a depressingly mashed up green plant.

This time even Holly declined.

“How did the dome appearing change everything around here?” Mitsuko asked.

Jay chuckled. “Look outside. What do you see?”

Mitsuko glanced at the window but didn’t move towards it for a better look. She did note that the window pane was missing from the frame.

“Brown,” she replied.

“And what are you wearing right now?” Jay asked.

“What am I wearing? Normal clothes?” Then it clicked in her head what he was fishing for. “Not winter clothing. I’m not bundled up like I should be on an island perpetually trapped in winter.”

Jay snapped his fingers and pointed. “Exactly.”

“And this all started when the dome dropped?” Holly asked. She tapped a finger to her chin. “I’m not a weather mage, but is it safe to assume that being trapped at high noon on a sunny day did not help this island’s climate?”

“When I first arrived, the days were warm but everything froze at night,” Jay said. “Now though… now it all just melts.”

Mitsuko continued to stare out the window. She’d never stopped to consider how the dome and frozen time might change the archipelago beyond just messing with some peoples’ sleep schedules. But the ecosystem of this island was so fragile that the shift in temperature could alter everything within days.

“You said everyone fled inland?” she asked the bear man.

“Yep. My wife and kids are over there. I can set you on the path if you’d like, but I’ll need to remain here. I offered to stay behind to speak to anyone arriving.”

“Just pointing us in the right direction would be a big help,” Mitsuko said.

Jay stood and only then did Mitsuko realize the Kemon’s full height. He stood over two meters tall and as he stretched, his muscles bulged, seen even under his brown fur and clothing. Not someone to start a fist fight with.

And yet… Mitsuko was tempted to ask him for a spar. She resisted the urge. Maybe something for a future loop though. After all, it looked like good training. The troll was a great opponent and the threat of actual death at his hands kept her on her toes, but fighting a strong opponent who could actually use their brain as well would offer different challenges.

Jay had to duck as he exited the inn.

“Woah. Nice ship you’ve got out here. You brought a full crew of sailors? And here I was assuming you two just hopped between islands in a little skiff or something. Why’d they all stay on board?” Jay scratched the back of his head. “Actually, I forgot to ask. What does bring out all the way out here? Not a lot of folks want to be on this island. And doesn’t look like any of your crew are natives.”

Mitsuko eyed the man and considered what she knew of his background. He was a foreign diplomat from a newly formed breakaway nation in Tross. Where did that leave his allegiances? Pro or anti Hon? It likely depended on his specific clan’s background.

“We’re part of Emperor Sasaki’s royal retinue,” Holly said, completely exposing them. “What’s that look for Mitsuko? No need to give me the stink eye. Was I supposed to lie?”

“You’re supposed to be the politically tactful one,” Mitsuko accused her friend.

“Officials from Hon? Oh yeah, nearly forgot that Emperor Sasaki is supposed to arrive over here in the next week or so. And now, let me guess, you’re investigating the weird dome and time anomaly?" Jay grinned. “That’s excellent! You’ll get along with my wife. She’s also from Hon. We met while on a diplomatic mission in Edgeland over a decade ago. Thankfully long before all their turmoil.”

He continued to chat as he directed them over to a trailhead. The man could certainly yap. Mitsuko was suddenly very grateful he wouldn’t be joining them on their hike up the mountain. Sterling was enough of a nuisance.

He raised a massive paw in farewell. Then his face froze and his eyes widened as if coming to a sudden grim realization. “Wait, don’t leave yet! I totally forgot to show you the pictures!”
