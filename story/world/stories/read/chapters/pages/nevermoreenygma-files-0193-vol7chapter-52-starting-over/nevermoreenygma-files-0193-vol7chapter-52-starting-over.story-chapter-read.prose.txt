Chapter 52

Starting Over

She woke up sweating, surrounded by the sound of cicadas everywhere.

Her vision was blurry, but she tried to focus as best she could.

In front of her, on the wall, a digital calendar indicated the month: August 12, 2048.

Her long, messy hair was dark again.

The familiar wolves, Kara, Kuro and Kiri, sleeping peacefully outside, entered through the walls when they heard the noises and played around her. Then they immediately notified Aika via the ley lines that stretched toward Tokyo.

Mai felt a little dizzy and her throat was dry, as the wolves played and jumped around her. She tried to calm them down with a few pats while they licked her and nudged her from side to side. She let them do it for a moment before trying to get up.

“Stop it, you’re tickling me,” Mai said, trying to calm them down a little. The soft fur threatened to lull her back to sleep.

She had to do it slowly, but she managed it. Yes... she had definitely woken up. She must have been waking up little by little for days, and now she had finally done it.

What she had learned over the years was that the awakening process took several days until her body was finally ready and all her biological systems were functioning properly again. What did strike her, however, was that it had been faster than she had expected.

She had thought she would sleep for at least five or six years.

Well. That was good. Or at least she thought so. She would have to get checked out in the coming days to make sure everything was in order.

Fey didn’t usually get sick, and if they did, it was a one-time thing—they never got sick again. Some exceptions were different types of colds. Thanks to her time in Whisper, it seemed her immune system had become ironclad, rejecting any virus.

Mai looked around while her eyes where getting used.

She checked herself out. Her hair was a little longer but not messy, and her nails were short.

“Thank you, Aika.”

She had the sense that she had dreamed something before wake up, but she had just forgotten it.

The transition from hibernation to wakefulness had been normal, almost like a regular dream. She staggered toward the kitchen, followed by the spiritual wolves, and drank nearly two liters of water. Then she turned on her smartphone, flooded with calls that Aika was making at that moment. There were calls and messages from friends and other acquaintances. Then she turned on the television to catch up on what had happened.

There were still problems in the world, of course. It wasn’t as if she expected the world to have suddenly changed while she slept.

She had just woken up, and that was what she wanted to know? Well, it was a habit she’d picked up when she was at Wishper after all.

After drinking the water, she put a hand to her stomach and ran as fast as she could. She had drunk too quickly and threw up a little.

Behind her, the shikigami watched her with their heads tilted, looking worried.

“I’m fine...”

While learning what was happening, she bathed, dressed, and took the electric scooter from the garage, heading to the nearest 7-Eleven.

She was starving.

The air hitting her face felt completely different.

She wasn't sure she should have driven. But she was really craving something delicious, and the canned goods she had at home were already past their expiration date. After all, she had never stocked up on enough food to have an emergency supply there.

In the months leading up to her hibernation, she had always prepared her own meals. Living for so many decades eating on the road, or on the run, had led her to greatly appreciate the art of cooking fresh food.

But now she wanted to a eat lot.

The owner of the place was a local resident and was surprised to see her. It had been quite a while since he’d seen her. He thought she’d sold the house. Mai greeted him and paid for the ramen portions. The man probably hadn’t realized that Mai had always been there in the house. Well, that meant the barrier set up before hibernation had worked.

She returned home with two full bags.

After two hours, as she was finishing her fifth serving of instant ramen, Aika arrived.

During the time Mai had been in hibernation, her friend had been in charge of watching over her, checking in every two weeks instead of once a month. The visits had been quick, discreet, but enough to ensure Mai was safe and sound.

“Don’t even think about going back to your old ways.”

“I wish it were that easy. But I don’t think so. At least I don’t want to go back to Wishper. Any news? How’s the Section?”

“Everything’s the same in our world. Thankfully. I’m not sure if that’s a good or a bad thing.”

Aika shrugged.

Mai let out a short sigh.

“…I’m thinking about going back to Tokyo.”

“For what?”

“You know what I mean. I want to see everyone, but I think I’ll rest a few more days.”

“Yeah,” Aika said, with a crooked smile as she booped Kiri on the nose. “That’s for the best.”

“Right.”

Aika turned toward the kitchen, inspecting the mess.

“Then let’s start with something more urgent.”

“What?”

Aika lifted one of the empty containers.

“Learning how to eat like a civilized person again. And I am going to clean that mess and preparing something to celebrate.”

Mai made a face. “I’m full for now. I don’t want anything else.”

“Are you sure about that? You don’t want ice cream with beer?”

Mai went quiet for a few seconds.

“Drunken wolf… always leading me down the wrong path. Are you really a priestess?”

“Refusing that is harder than facing a Dark Event.”

“Probably,” Mai admitted.

***

The process of coming out of hibernation had been smooth, but once she opened her eyes, the sense of renewal was overwhelming. She felt as if she had been pulled from the darkness and stillness she had gone to sleep in, as if her body had left behind the traces of fatigue that had marked her for years. Her mind, clear and sharp, seemed to have returned to its starting point, like a completely new person, though carrying the weight of her experience.

During the first week, she focused on resolving small details that had been neglected during her absence. But despite the serenity the place provided, something inside Mai had awakened during that time—a strange desire, one that now resurfaced: she couldn’t remain still. She couldn’t live enclosed in the quiet of routine.

What the hell is happening to me? she wondered. It was almost as if she wanted to work again. Was this the famous Fey impulse to move once awakened from hibernation? She had heard it could be different for many. But did she really want to go back to Wishper?

No, it wasn’t that she necessarily wanted to return. Not at all. She didn’t want conflicts of that kind.

Communications had informed her that around the world, the hidden world was relatively calm, with no major issues beyond the Dark Events and the climate still wreaking havoc. In Japan, there were also problems with agricultural areas and occasional hurricanes, but the implementation of AI and a government plan to encourage a return to rural areas had worked with relative success.

The money Mai had earned during her years in Wishper was more than enough to guarantee her a peaceful life, free from financial worries, for several decades. She didn’t have to do anything, she could enjoy the luxury of rest and retirement, but that wasn’t what she wanted. The exhaustion of so many years in the shadows, dangerous missions, and endless battles had not broken her spirit as she thought it might. Instead, time had focused her gaze on new possibilities, new experiences. But she had no idea whether to go through with what she was thinking.

With renewed energy, Mai decided it was time to return to Tokyo, a place that, despite its immense growth, always had a way of drawing her in. For better or worse, she liked it.

New technologies, advances in artificial intelligence, and the arrival of the Aeon had transformed the city, but the frenetic energy, the sense of controlled chaos, remained the same. It was like an old friend, one who always welcomed her back despite the scars they both shared.

She made the decision without hesitation. Tokyo would be the next step. She needed to find her place again, and discover how to fit into a world that had continued on while she had been away fighting.

Life in Wishper had taught her much about what it meant to make decisions, about the value of perseverance. But now, the true challenge would be living outside that shadow, without the weight of missions or the pain of memories from a past that, although never forgotten, no longer dictated her steps toward the future. Whatever that future might be.

As for living in the shadows… well. That’s a bit of a relative term. But in any case, it was true that her idea involved her being out in public. Maybe she wouldn’t need to hide her ears after all. After all, there had been a trend in recent years of young people getting ear surgery to lengthen their ears. Mai suspected that some group of fey might be behind the trend, but she had no way of proving it. Still, it was one less thing to worry about.

As she boarded the train toward Tokyo, the city remained the same, yet Mai knew it wasn’t. The hidden world was still there. Some things were the same, some things not had changed, but others do—not just in the world, but within herself.

She was ready for that decision.

Why not return to where it all began?
