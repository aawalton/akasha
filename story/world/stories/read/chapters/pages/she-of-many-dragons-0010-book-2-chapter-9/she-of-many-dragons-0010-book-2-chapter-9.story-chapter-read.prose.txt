Throwing that egg in might have been a big mistake.

Alice thought that the fire had been uncontrolled before, but as she and her dragons escaped, the flames grew into a storm, whipping the wind with such ferocity that Alice and her dragons sprinted at a dead run. Hot embers were flung out in all directions. Wherever they landed, they started new fires along the way.

Prim abandoned her position on Alice's shoulder and flew a few feet ahead, warning the group to turn in one direction or another as she saw more patches of illusions ahead. More nests of awaiting Tree Wraiths.

Natura added her knowledge as well and guided them in the direction of healthier forests. Not only was that less likely to have Tree Wraiths, but it would also burn slower.

Meanwhile, Iggy kept behind them, exercising whatever control of the fire he had to keep the worst of the flames back.

It was Numi who found the way to safety—not a new treasure, but a lake that stretched out past the smoky horizon. The stream that they had crossed over before dumped right into it. Like everywhere else, it seemed to be once inhabited but now abandoned.

Numi pointed them to the remains of an old dock, the timbers gray and so far gone that Alice did not dare set a foot on it. But, partially shoved up the bank lay a raft. It was equally old and would not hold her entire weight, but the wood would float if she could clutch onto it.

"The water isn't safe," Prim warned. “What about the eels?"

"I don't know," Natura admitted, "but we killed the eel mother. Plus, there are trees and weeds right up to the bank, see? If this lake were badly infested, the eels would have eaten it all bare."

Alice's breath came out in gasps, her lungs feeling burned from the heat and the smoke. She shook her head, tried to speak, and only coughed. It took her two more tries to get out any other words.

"I have to try. We can't stop here." Unsaid was that she would rather suffer an eel bite than be caught in the fire.

Like Natura had said, the bank was choked with vegetation, too. Most of it was dead debris that had floated in from the surrounding forest and would surely catch fire as soon as the flames arrived. She could not stay here, and she could not run forever.

If only I had wings, she thought, if only one of my dragons could carry me.

But neither was possible. So, she got to work pushing the remains of the raft out into the water.

Natura swam along with Alice, and once they were about forty feet out, Natura exercised her elemental manipulation skill and raised the muddy bank under her.

Very soon, Alice was crouched on a small island. They all piled on, wet and shivering as the chasing flames made it to the bank.

Even from their spot in the lake, the reflected heat was incredible. The sun was starting to set, and the parts of the sky that were not utterly black with smoke were a vivid orange. Against that, a tall gout of dark smoke was rising. Alice squinted.

"Is it my imagination, or are there shapes in that smoke?"

Everyone stared and did not say anything for a few moments.

"It looks like a dragon," Prim said in wonder.

Sure enough, rising from the smoke was the shape of a dark dragon, wings outspread. The details were so sharp, down to visible scales, that Alice felt this couldn’t be an accident. The smoke-dragon stayed in the sky for a few minutes, as if soaking in the last rays of sunlight of the day, before the wind made the shape indistinct.

"I'm afraid we've done a very bad thing by setting fire to the forest," Alice muttered, "even if it was an accident."

"We have not," Natura said firmly. "The forest was very sick, and there were many parts past saving. I very much doubt that those terrible Tree Wraiths will escape this, and the ash may choke the rest of the blood eels in the streams, too," she added with fierce satisfaction. But then she looked around at the flames racing down the bank, stopped only by the water. She sighed, "But there's been so much destruction..."

"It is my fault," Iggy shifted uncomfortably. "I should have been more circumspect with my fire."

Standing alongside him, Spark chirped with sad agreement.

"No," Natura said again, turning to him. "This amount of tinder was unsustainable. If you hadn't done it, then a natural lightning bolt would have. It was only a matter of time."

"Perhaps," Prim said, "but you have been working so hard to enrich the soil as we went along. It's a shame that all your hard work has been for nothing."

Natura drooped a little, and Alice reached out to pat her. But a moment later, the nature dragon straightened up. "It hasn't been for nothing," she said. "I've learned a lot, and all that work has given me experience."

They all went quiet again, lost in their own thoughts.

"Numi, what are you doing?" Alice asked, noting that the little green dragon was digging frantically at their little island of mud.

Numi pushed her snout into the hole she had just made, inhaled noisily, then withdrew with mud all over her muzzle. She looked at Natura. "Oh, hey, could you raise more mud for me?"

“Why?”

"Because look what I found!" She dug her claws in and grasped something, holding it up in triumph. It was an extremely rusted, bent nail. "There's a lot of stuff here, and maybe some good treasure, too!”

Natura looked at Alice, who nodded.

With visible effort, Natura raised more earth from the bottom of the lake and made their little island about half again as big as it had been before. Now Alice could comfortably stretch out her legs.

When it was done, Natura flopped down. "That's enough for now. I can't raise the mud from too deep, and it's exhausting."

Numi was as happy as a clam and occupied herself by hunting around and finding more nails and two more copper coins. As they had been in the mud, they seemed to be protected somehow, and they were imprinted with faces that Alice had never seen. It was all very interesting and kept their attention as the fire burned.

"Aha!" Numi held up a lump—no, it was more than a lump. As Alice looked closer, she realized that something sharp and glittery was peeking out of it. Numi quickly washed it in the lake and held it up again. A red jewel sparkled in the last of the light.

"It's not a gemstone," Numi said, "this is some sort of magical object. I don't know what exactly it’s for because my appraisal skills are too low." As if the system had been listening, she gained another appraisal level. "Oh, never mind," she said, then squinted again at the jewel. "It's some kind of blood magic, but that’s all I know. It's really high level, and I'm still low yet."

But she had done more than just gain a level. Her experience transmitted over to Alice, and she finally leveled up. That meant she had gained another slot for a new dragon.

"Numi!" she grabbed the dragon and squeezed her in a hug. "That was enough to level me, too! Oh, you darling.” Beaming, she looked at all her dragons. “You have all worked so hard. Thank you."

"So you can hatch that creepy egg now?" Natura asked.

"Yes." Alice cast an eye at the still rapidly burning fire. The edge of the flames had moved around the lake, but there was still plenty to burn. "As soon as the fire dies down."

She didn't voice it, but inwardly, she worried that she had made a grave mistake by tossing the egg into the wildfire. It had wanted it to happen, but how could it possibly survive that inferno? And if it did, what business did she have hatching a life that started in the grave of a child and was fed by the flames of such destruction?

She would be tempted to forget about the egg entirely except for one pertinent fact:

Her She of Many Dragons class instincts told her that now the egg had been fed to the flames, it would surely hatch whether she was there or not. The thought of a little creature starting its life alone in a land covered by ash was terrible.

If it did not die of starvation or thirst, which was a thought almost too horrific to bear, what personality would be shaped by such a start? Of being forced to scrape and survive?

No. Her will firmed up. Alice was She of Many Dragons. It was her duty to see that whatever hatched out of this egg would grow up loved.
