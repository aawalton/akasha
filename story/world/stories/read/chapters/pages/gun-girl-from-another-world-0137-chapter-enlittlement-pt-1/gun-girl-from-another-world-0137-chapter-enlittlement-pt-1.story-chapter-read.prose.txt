Enlittlement Pt 1

*Remmi*

I’m laying back on my bed. A real bed, with a mattress and box springs, unlike anything I’ve slept on since coming to Furinshao. Though the summer air outside my double-pane window is warm and humid, the air in here is just cool enough that I’m quite cozy underneath my linen sheets and fluffy comforter.

I’m scrubbed clean from a good, long soak in a bath tub with hot, running water and proper scented soap, shampoo and conditioner. Under the sheets, I’m clad in comfortable pajamas, and despite the late hour, my room is lit, not with candles and a fireplace, but with luminescent ceiling lights and a bedside lamp. The overhead lights are off, but the lamp provides a nice glow without being blinding.

If I tilt my head and listen carefully, I can even hear the gentle blowing of cool air from the floor vent along the wall, the source of that comfortable temperature difference.

No, I’m not back on Earth, though if I close my eyes, it almost feels like I could be. I am, in fact, still in Toleste, in the forest just outside Dabun Village. Through the marriage of Terran residential engineering and Imperial magic artifice, my completed manor has achieved a state of modern comfort.

Dinner was even a pasta casserole made of Essence crops with cheese aged from Essence milk, baked in a temperature-regulated oven. And nobody washed dishes by hand in a sink of boiled water afterward, because, once scrubbed out, they went into a dishwasher. And the leftovers went in the fridge. Like proper, civilized people.

A manor. It’s still hard to think of it in such a grand term like that. To me, it’s still just a large house. Yorin pointed out that a manor was just the main house of an estate, and by such terms, even my previous cabin was technically a manor. When I complained that a manor was supposed to be a massive, grand affair, Ayre had held his hands out in the space between the open-concept kitchen and dining room, spun about and demanded to know what I called this.

Yorin had been more constructive, saying that if I wanted a more traditional layout for a countryside estate like this, I would have spread the structure out instead of stacking it on top of itself. It would have been divided into separate buildings surrounding an internal courtyard, with different accommodations for guests, servants and religious observance. Only in the Imperial City, where horizontal space is limited, are noble houses arranged vertically.

I can understand where she’s coming from, but I can’t help but see that as a huge waste of real estate, a sentiment that set Ayre to cackling at what he declared to be my hypocrisy. I mean, sure, I’ve got the land for it, but it would require clearing a whole bunch more of the forest, and probably extensive relocation of everything else, including the Essence Crops, some of which are whole trees. Explaining that to them didn't drive Ayre to retract his accusations of hypocrisy by that point, but, darn it, at least I’d planned around these things! I should be getting some credit for that!

But, at this particular moment, it’s not really the house that’s occupying my thoughts. As I’m laying in that bed, its frame low to the ground to appease Imperial aesthetics while concealing the box springs from sight, there is an invisible display window open in my field of view, blue with a clear trim, and black text for clear contrast. In that window is a complete list of every spell the Essence System can teach me.

I have a spar with Ayre tomorrow, and it’s been too long since I’ve picked up a flashy, new spell. I’m hoping to remedy that tonight and surprise my best friend with it.

Unfortunately, the bulk of all spells in existence just aren’t very interesting. You wouldn’t think magic could be boring, but the sheer mundanity of some of these makes my brain yawn compulsively. Sure, somebody came up with them because it makes something easier or faster, but there just isn’t much to be said about a spell whose entire purpose is to rearrange furniture.

That doesn’t mean I’m above taking them. I’d be quite the hypocrite if that was how I felt about them. Some of my first spells were for cleaning and starting campfires. I even have one for aging cheese and booze. These aren’t combat spells, and the flashiest they could get is for cringy party tricks. They’re utility spells. Their whole purpose is to take something you could do for yourself, and do it for you in exchange for magic points instead of time and manual labor.

They just aren’t what I’m looking for right now, and many of these are so niche that I don’t think I ever will.

Other spells are just … weird. I’ve seen no fewer than three different spells for turning a wrinkly, old wizard into a beautiful young woman, and two for turning a witch into a wrinkly, old man. And these aren’t curses, either. They’re Target: Self. I really don’t want to think about the implications of these being something mages came up with willingly and intentionally. And you’d be amazed - and probably disgusted - by all of the things you can make come out of a fountain.

And I’m going to look for a Brain Bleach spell for all of them clearly inspired by loneliness. Not to mention all of the fetish stuff. Giving people unlimited magical power and then recording everything they come up with is as bad as browsing the Internet without a filter.

But, finally, finally, I come across something that is just what I’m looking for. In fact, it’s very possible that the System was watching my painful search and took pity on me by slipping this in, because the spell description was clearly written by the sales department.

STATUS SHUFFLE

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Ever stood before an immovable boulder and realized how useless it was to be fast? Have you desperately needed to solve a puzzle box and considered how little good being able to take a punch was doing you? Perhaps you had to outrun something and realized you needed more legs than brains?

It’s a natural consequence of needing to be ready for anything that we just can’t get away with minmaxing as much as we want. You just can’t afford to neglect the rest of your body for hyperperformance in one particular category.

Or, at least, you couldn’t before STATUS SHUFFLE!

Punch that boulder! Take that hit! Lose track of your own metaphors! It’s all possible when you have STATUS SHUFFLE!

Once activated, this spell will reallocate status points from categories you consider expendable to a single category you desire to juice. Be warned, this reallocation will last for the duration of the spell and cannot be undone by additional casting. Do not use STATUS SHUFFLE in rapidly changing situations, while under the influence of intoxicants, or if you are pregnant or may become pregnant.

This spell has the following effects:

Reduces up to two base statistic categories by up to half of their total value

Raises one base statistic category by the total value of the reduction selected

Duration is determined by the Intellect of the caster prior to any reductions or increases

Can only be cast on self

Any sustained multipliers or other bonuses are calculated off of the modified values after recalculation

Any actions taken under STATUS SHUFFLE will be the user's responsibility

My grin spreads across my face, and I do my best not to giggle in my glee as I will the purchase to take place. I can’t wait to knock Ayre’s socks off with this in the morning!

*Ayre*

Remmi is up to something. I don’t know what, exactly, but she’s been far too excitable all morning not to be. I always worry whenever she gets like this. Not like Sacred Yorin worries. She worries like Remmi is going to violate some fundamental tenet of reality and, even worse, everyone’s going to see it.

No, I worry because it usually means trouble. Sometimes minor, and always working itself out somehow to turn out to have been a positive all along, but usually trouble first. When she gets like this, it means she’s about to push boundaries, and she likes pushing boundaries. In fact, I’m fairly certain that the whole reason she gets excitable in the first place is because she knows the potential of what she’s planning to do to cause trouble.

It’s not hard to figure out that whatever she’s excited for has to do with our spar. It’s the only really big thing in our plans today that she’s been looking forward to at all, and said excitement just keeps climbing higher the closer the time comes, like a spring being wound to snapping.

So it’s with no small amount of trepidation that I take my position across the field from her. Since we’re both ranged combatants, we’ve taken to starting at what most would consider rather extreme positions. It would be quite a distance for a melee specialist to close before any combat could actually begin, but for us, it’s at just the distance where we’re not truly outside of each other’s effective range, but we’re starting at distances where our accuracy relative to our opponent’s movements starts to get a little fuzzy.

If Remmi stayed still, I could still shoot one of her Essence Tomatoes out of her hand at this distance, and I have no doubt that she could do the same to me, but neither of us have any intention of remaining a stationary target for the other. As soon as the signal to start goes off, marked by when the bit Sacred Yorin will flip hits the ground, we’ll both immediately start moving. We’ll try to hem each other in with our shots, control each other’s approach, but ultimately, we won’t really be starting the fight until we’re close enough to reliably land shots against our opponent’s ability to evade.

That may sound a lot like the melee quandary, but it’s actually a very short window of time for us, and it’s really more just to prevent Remmi from cheesing her Quick Draw and rendering the whole point of the spar moot.

“Ready, Ayre?” she calls from across the field.

“Ready,” I call back.

Her grin widens, and at a more normal volume I wouldn’t have heard if I wasn’t an elf, she replies, “No, you’re not.” Then she raises her voice again. “Yorin!”

The sacred priestess extends the hand where the coin is cradled against her thumb. “When this imperial coin touches soil, so shall the match begin!”

She sends it shooting up into the sky. Remmi and I both follow it up, but it quickly reaches a point where it’s indistinguishable against the light of the sun. Instead of tracing its path, we’re left waiting for it to come down.

And waiting.

… And waiting.

How hard did she flick the thing?!

From across the way, Remmi goes, “Wow, that’s gonna hit terminal velocity …” I’m not really sure what that means, but I don’t like the combination of a word that references finality with one that means speed.

There’s a shrill, almost painful whistling that hits my ears as I’m finally able to pick out the coin’s descent, and out of the corner of my eye, I can see Remmi tracing it, too. My fingers tighten around my first arrow, my arm increasing tension on the string.

The coin hits in a spray of soil as its spinning form carves a small trench, but I’m not paying it any mind. I’m too busy starting the channeling of Spiral Shot as I swivel toward my friend.

… Who promptly explodes into a pillar of smoke. What …?

Something small and yellow comes blasting out of it. I release my arrow on reflex, but I don’t even have time to process the overshoot before the orange splats of Remmi’s training rounds trace a quick line up my right side, moving from center mass up toward my shoulder.

“Tag, tag, tag!” a little girl’s voice declares at the same time, and my mind immediately assumes Korrigan is doing commentary, despite her being away visiting her grandfather.

The blur passes me on my right side and impacts the ground several body lengths behind me.

This all happens in mere seconds, and most of those seconds were spent staring at smoke. The actual attack could have been measured in fractions.

My mind is still playing catch-up and has only started to realize I’ve been shot when the little girl’s voice speaks again, this time from behind me. From where the yellow projectile landed.

A very smug, “Hehe, looks like my win!”

I start to get a very bad feeling in the pit of my stomach as I slowly force myself to turn toward the impact site.

And it was definitely an impact. There’s a small crater where all of that momentum connected with the grass and dirt. And someone is standing in it.

“Remmi?!”

The yellow hair - the same yellow as the projectile - means it could be no one else in all of Furinshao. But nothing else fits. The figure is barely more than four shaku high, and surely not more than half a dozen kan heavy, maybe if it were soaking wet.

But then she turns toward me in response to me calling her name, and there are those strikingly blue eyes, now seemingly so large in her face, and I realize that her dimensions are like those of a child. This is right about the same time that my mind identifies the gray-blue corona-like flare around the projectile as her clothing flaring about this tiny form like a sailcloth of death.

The same clothing that, no longer being dragged along with her, has largely slumped to the ground. Her dress hangs heavily off of one tiny shoulder as the skirt comes to a stop somewhere past her knees. She’s still standing in her boots, but mostly because she landed in them. One step is going to leave them behind, and the previously tight stockings are pooling atop their rim. Really, most of her clothing is bunched up around the tops of her previously knee-high boots as the last rigid barrier before leaving her completely.

The child before me gives a gasp of shock, holding the now-bulky pistol away from her as the other hand comes up over her mouth. “Ayre, when did you get so big?!”

I barely process the stupidity of her question, my brain already going into panic. I reach down and grab the little girl under her arms, hauling her up into the air and out of her boots and stockings and belt as I spin toward the priestess, the child’s expression looking confused at the sudden change in position.

“Sacred Yorin!” I call out. “Problem!” I try to get my brain to relay to my tongue the no doubt extensive list of precise issues laid out before us, but it stubbornly refuses to do so, and as I’m hurrying toward her, I can only repeat myself, shaking the child for emphasis as if that will explain everything I can’t spit out. “Remmi problem!”
