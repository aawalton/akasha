The entity seemed forlorn at this question. The sky muted and went darker. “It’s fair to say I have no name here, in particular. But I am the one you sought out.”

“But what about-” Samantha waved her hand, and struggled to make her thoughts coherent. “You-... where? This? And the-... I mean, shit… gorgons?!”

The sky took on a new spectrum of patterns and colors. “So that’s what this is about. It’s a complicated thing to explain. The gorgons are in some sense my spawn, though not directly. Can you guess the reasons for that particular form? Stone and serpentine aspects in combination. My aspects, to balance theirs. Perfect, to control the remnants I was charged with redirecting long ago.”

Samantha struggled to understand. “Remnants? Some sort of shapeshifters?”

“Yes. Scattered across the world, digging their claws in for their own selfish profit and ill intent. When it was vulnerable, I cut off the head of their original leader — so to speak — and hunted down each and every servant afterward. Instead of killing them, I made use of them. We did, that is. We broke them down, directing their own evils against them until it effectively destroyed who they were, then I rebuilt them from scratch into a new purpose. A foundation to build on.”

Samantha shivered at the thought. “There is so much to unpack here. Uh…” She ran a hand over her face. “First off, who is ‘we,’ exactly?”

“My mother, your sister. She had to teach me the trick to properly repurpose those entities, but I could eventually do it myself.”

“So, I’m your aunty?”

The sky might’ve shrugged. “These are deific things, not physical hereditary realities. They don’t mean the same things as they do to animals.”

“Right. Anyway, you said you repurposed them? Did that punt their souls, ala what the Dominion does to people?”

“No. They already had that done to them. The way it was done originally has been lost. We were uninterested in learning it properly or seeing it propagate further. These souls are like very convincing constructs, or undead. An artificial spirit baked in, you might say. Due to being the ultimate shapeshifters, you can’t detect it — nor can they.”

“Is their true form even true, then?”

“No. Despite my investiture, I cannot call it the truth. But they do not know this. They see themselves as the children of the First Serpent. Which is true, in a way, but… it is long gone. Because I killed it.”

Samantha squinted, trying to puzzle over this and track just what it meant. “Because my sister had you do it?”

“Because it was justice. And what you speak is like a riddle. To whom is the pleasure served in bed together? We are all of the same union, all aligned in the same syzygy. Our purposes are one, and we are one. Are we not becoming this more and more?”

Samantha blinked. “I… don’t know. Maybe. I guess I get your point.” She took a long moment, looking away, still contemplative. “I… did something, in a place that touched the past. A deal with Serpentkind. I don’t think that I could change everything thereafter, but… something could’ve translated through to her. Made her change things in her strangely more continuous, timeless existence. Change her approach with… serpents.”

The sky swirled into new patterns, frenetic with activity. “That makes some sense. But you underestimate your power here. It is nothing lesser. The present most certainly changed with what you did. Your will likely translated to your twin. How could it not?”

Samantha glared up sharply at that. “I guess I should’ve expected you’d know she’s my twin. Don’t go spreading that around, now.”

The entity seemed a bit amused at that. “Your secrets are mine, Goddess. In any case, what you suggest about Serpentkind seems true. They are wound around you, now, sharing your Fate, and utterly charmed.”

“Does that include you?”

“Very.”

The oily mirage of a sky swirled into curls, fluid-like.

Wait, is this sky flirting with me? No way. Just my imagination. Anyway, I’m a one-sky kind of girl.

Samantha rubbed her neck. “Ehehe. Ahem! I should get this convo back on track to why I’m actually here. Nyomel.”

“You may do with her as you will.”

Samantha blinked. “What?! Hey, wait, no. No, no, no — that makes sense. You know I’m soft! You want me to keep her around since she’s your spy.”

“Her only purpose is to serve you, now. Our wills are aligned. I do not desire her power wasted, but it is your decision to make. You could let her retain her cover as influence over the clans she had been cultivating control over, and keep Deikmorn suitably in hand and motivated. Additionally, you could assure her that she can embrace the serpent aspects in battle, making her that much more powerful as a sorceress. You could also tell her to abandon it all and do literally anything else. Take her as an occasional lover, for instance. She’s quite skilled.”

“Um, no thanks.” Samantha crossed her arms and pondered it all. Finally, she sighed. “I guess the matter is solved, then. You no longer care about her, so it’s whatever.”

“An odd accusation. I simply care more about you and what you require or want than her. You’re a higher priority for Calrenazzod.”

“Okay, what if I said I didn’t want her and you need to take her back?”

“We’d find some use for her.”

The vague and ominous words left Samantha with no doubt that she would absolutely not be releasing her to Endless Templar’s cult. “I see. Well, I suppose I’ll keep her around for the war effort. Give her a slap on the wrist for this supposed punishment she deserves.”

“A wise recourse, I think.”

“While we’re talking about spies, do you have anyone in Matrazere? Or who can infiltrate it?”

“No. Infiltration isn’t necessary.”

“What?”

“You seek to destroy it, no?”

“Yeeeeah, though I don’t know if that is even feasible. It’s more accurate to say I am conquering the town and sieging the fortress. Conquering it? Eventually.”

“You should aspire to turn it into dust. Crush the Dominion’s will in the war and strike fear into their very heart.”

“Again, I don’t know if it’s possible. Supposedly, the fortress is indestructible.”

“Do you not know?” The sky roiled. Oddly, the entire space seemed to compress slightly, as if the world she was on had gotten smaller. More intense. “Nothing is that, Goddess.”

Images were conjured above, of four towers, upon each a complex rune. What made the name of the fortress was the core meaning: unbreakable. In the sky above, they burned like a fiery brand amidst the swirls of colors. They stood out as if more real and perfect than the rest. And then, one of the runes grew something new, like a mutant branch, garish and twisted. The burning light of the rune died as the symbol was made into something nonsensical…

It had been, in essence, destroyed.

The others all met the same fate as if copies, marred and twisted just the same, and then there was a bright flash like a fireball, and the towers were simply gone.

“Undefeatable. Indestructible. Unbreakable. Just words and protestations. If the sun came to swallow its little orbiters, would their unthinkable mass protect them? Mar the primary rune in but the smallest capacity — correct the improper language — and the material will be brought to heel. That will spell the beginning of the end. Then all will fall.”

Tantalized and slightly awed by the demonstration, Samantha nonetheless squinted her eyes as she considered that. “It shouldn’t be possible with how those runes are etched. The meaning bestows the concept on the material, which is carved into the surface, unchangeable. The trade-off was how difficult that general and pure of a meaning was, to execute. The perfection is plainly breathtaking. I believe the words of the historian I read were, ‘An impossible task complete, impossible results followed, fit to persist forever.’ The towers haven’t been so much as scratched, including the runes.”

“Yes. In the physical realm.”

Samantha frowned. “Mar the runes in the spirit world? They’re centered on belief. Something that would take decades, if not centuries, to unravel and have any reflection in the real world. It’s usually outside to inside, but especially in the case of magical artifice. The manufactured runes dictate the spirit, not the other way around.”

A long pause of little movement in the oil painting of a sky. “You and your coven have the power and means. Meditate on it, Goddess, and you will come to know this better.”

“I see. If true, well… Thank you. That would be a literal miracle.”

“The task remains yours to unlock Fate for the rest of us. You are the keyholder of the gateway to the new and final era, of which I am meant to arrive. To see it come to pass, I serve you, and so does all within my control.”

“Happy to hear it.” She had a sudden thought that made her smile impishly. “I think our victory is a gor-gon conclusion!” She thrust out her hands showily. “Huh?! Get it?!”

The sky seemed to do… nothing.

“Humor is a cultural thing,” the sky said politely.

“Yes. I will see you at Matrazere, Samantha. Until then: farewell.”
