This chapter feels weird. I’m not sure what it is. Maybe it’s just me, today’s been an off day. Let me know.

Now that he was here, Ezra found it easier to be distracted, with the peak of Initiate tier and this mysterious possible change looming. As such, through the afternoon's marching, he couldn’t really find his groove with meditation. Instead, he found himself focusing on the aches and pains from the fight, the small wounds he’d sustained as well as his still-aching elbow. Since he couldn’t reasonably expect to be able to speak with Jormun while marching around with everyone else, at least, not without looking insane, he finally decided to use some of the aura in his inner world to strengthen his elbow. It wasn’t much, not compared to what he’d thrown around while he was fighting, but it was more than enough to take the edge off.

“You look tired,” Leah said in her usual blunt way as they walked together.

“Aren’t you?” he asked by way of answer.

“Yeah,” she shrugged. “But I’ll be fine. You?”

“I’ll be fine,” he echoed her. “I’m just… I don’t know. It feels weird. To have killed. To have watched someone die like that.”

“You’ve never seen someone die until today?” Leah asked, surprised.

“Never been there for the last breath,” Ezra clarified. “I’ve seen the usual corpses in the alleys and so on. But never listened to their final words, watched the people who loved them deal with that.”

Leah just nodded, saying nothing. They walked in silence together after that, just the constant sound of their feet on the ground making a sound. Finally, she spoke again, “I think I’m feeling aura, a little. Can you help teach me tonight, when we have a minute not walking? It won’t obey me, so I wonder if I’m just… imagining feeling it.”

Though he just wanted to take care of himself, to focus on refilling his inner world and find out what changed, instead, he nodded. “Sure. I’ll see what I can do for you. I’m no expert, though.”

“But the experts talk to you,” she shrugged. “Thanks.”

Then, she gave him space, and Ezra continued walking in silence. Fortunately, it was only a half day’s march, just long enough to get away from the hundreds of goblinoid corpses that were going to be left to rot. According to the older soldiers, that wasn’t a problem, and that allowing the bodies to decompose like this in a place so far removed from any civilization would be fine, though the Duke would be posting some patrols to make sure nobody came by and got themselves sick by exposing themselves to the decay.

In the evening, Ezra took the time to teach the other recruits in aura collection specifically, though Boyd still couldn’t even feel it. Cord thought he was making progress while Leah didn’t say anything, and finally, Ezra could take the time to just meditate by himself. He was exhausted, and his head kept dipping, but he wouldn’t allow himself any excuses. The words that Captain Riyal and Chloe had exchanged when they first argued about him stuck in his head: that the Duke could come to Count Villy and pay whatever price he wanted to get Ezra on his side. Going off of how Chloe had treated him until now, Ezra had every confidence that the Duke would have a summons for him eventually.

Maybe it was prideful of him to think that a Duke would care to summon him. Maybe it was just stupid. But if his suspicions were right, and he’d learned to trust his gut, then it was only a matter of time before he wasn’t in Villy any longer. Yes, it would be a better place to be, but he knew that he couldn’t just leave himself as a potential slave to the Duke. Even as he got to know more inner city people, Ezra had been reminded today that there were people that would look down on him for any reason, like that man from the Duke’s army. He refused to subject himself to their control, if he could help it, though he had no doubt that, if the Duke came and demanded that Ezra leave Villy for Brayton, that was what he would do.

The only way to guarantee himself some freedom was to get stronger, and fast. After all, an Initiate was just another soldier, while the Adepts were sent alongside one of only two Pseudo-Knights who came on this expedition as guards.

Ironically enough, Ezra’s thoughts about possible futures served to distract him from his mediation, and it wasn’t long before he found himself asleep. Dreams of possibilities flitted through his mind, none staying long. A vision of him as just another soldier in Villy, never progressing. A glimpse of him running away, fleeing to the north to find training among the beastkin of Al Jibahia. Somehow becoming a sorcerer among the naga. A paladin of the Theocracy assigned to an illiar.

Between each brief dream came a sight of him wearing a chainmail shirt over the Duke’s uniform, a sword on his hip and a spear in his hand. And, despite hating the idea of leaving his home and having to listen to some random noble’s commands, this was the only one of the sights that felt right. Instead of the inherent wrongness in every other vision, this one was the only one that felt like Ezra.

Morning came quickly, and for the first time in quite a while, Ezra didn’t wake from the calls of sergeants. Instead, he slowly came to himself, his back and legs aching from where he sat upright on his sleeping roll on the ground. He groaned as he struggled to his feet, stretching and feeling his back and hips pop as he did so. The sounds seemed thunderous, but none of the rest of the recruits in his tent stirred, so, now more rested, Ezra settled back on his sleeping roll to start meditating again, though more focused and well-rested this time.

Now, he could feel the difference in the aura in his inner world. That which he’d pulled directly from the winds outside into his inner world was now just without any affinity, while the stuff he’d transformed himself was still there, clumped into a ball of whirling winds that seemed both to fight against itself as well as to work together. It was a strange contradiction, but Ezra didn’t fight against it. Instead, he pulled the aura of the earth under him into himself, taking the time to pull the stony, stalwart nature of the aura away, leaving it neutral. Then, with a further injection of his own will, he twisted the placid aura into something with more movement, until a thread of wind started to wind into the pocket of wind that swirled inside.

Though he couldn’t quite understand what the difference was, his inner world swelled over time to rival what it once had been. Doing so went more quickly than before, as if he’d already done most of the effort of strengthening his inner world already. Now that he thought about it, that was probably the case, that his inner world already had found its capacity, and now filling it to that point came without effort, as if the space was already prepared. Bit by bit, with a refreshed mind, Ezra was able to fill his inner world with whirling wind attribute aura that, as it grew in density, started to press against itself and the walls of his inner world.

As before, there was a solid limit to how much aura his inner world could support at one time, though now that he filled it exclusively with what it was internally suited for, the discomfort that he felt was less painful and more like the times he’d drunk too much water in an attempt to keep from feeling hungry. He’d called it being “sloshy” as a kid, and he felt like it was still a pretty good way to call it. The aura in his inner world sloshed back and forth, the wind pushing at his limits and demanding more space, to allow it to spread as far as it could. It was being unpleasantly full, with an upset stomach all at the same time.

When he grunted in discomfort and pulled himself out of the meditation, Ezra had to bite back and shout of surprise at Jormun who lurked right in front of him. The ghost asked, What’s different about filling it up with your own created aura instead of natural aura?

Without answering immediately, Ezra stood and strode out of the tent. Since it was still winter, though tending towards spring, the first pinks of sunrise peeked at the horizon, only barely visible over the mountains making the valley they travelled through. The camp was mostly asleep, though patrols remained constant at the edges and the sounds of breakfast being prepared didn’t allow him to think that there was nobody awake to overhear him speaking with his ghostly mentor. There was only so much he could do for that, though, and he strode towards a small clearing between tents where the wind was whipping through the mostly orderly lines.

Finally, where there wasn’t anyone close enough to easily overhear, he settled into a sitting position, half-closed his eyes, and finally answered Jormun’s question.

“My inner world is accepting the aura now. It isn’t just well-suited to being in there, but the aura that I transformed almost seems to be making more of itself. Like,” Ezra tried to explain, “I haven’t meditated nearly enough for my inner world to be as full as it is. So, either there’s something wrong, or, when the inner world is, I don’t know, fed, it fills itself.”

Jormun nodded, his face proud as he explained, That’s one of the main steps needed for people to become able to cross the tier and ascend to Adept.

“One of the main?” Ezra asked. “Is there anything else that I need to do? Or do I need to figure it out myself?”

It’s a hard balance to strike, Jormun repeated for what felt like the fiftieth time, to give instruction without impeding an individual’s path. However, I can repeat what I’ve said before. The ascension to Adept is a qualitative one, and to do so is to grant a more coherent form than what is found while an Initiate is still stumbling through their path. Do you feel like your inner world is ready to become something more coherent? Or is it still just formless?

“No, it’s definitely further along,” Ezra shook his head. “Before, I didn’t even realize why it’s called the inner world, but now I can see it. It’s mostly just a bunch of wind and clouds right now, so I don’t know if that qualifies for ‘ready to become Adept’. But it’s constantly moving, which is why I wasn’t so sure if there was more of the aura when I woke up or not.”

Jormun allowed him to sit in silence for a little while, but as soon as Ezra made even a small movement to draw some more aura into his inner world, the winds whipped around more fiercely than before, seeming to both want more energy and to demand that he give it no more until he expanded the area it could spread through. Before he could decide what he was going to do, Jormun asked a question that had him pause and stare up at the ghost:

Do you think you’re ready to try to ascend?
