Three hours ago. Command tent.

“It’s settled then.” Alaric smiled. “Everyone gather round. We need to make a plan.”

They gathered, and when they were all seated, Alaric continued. “In the spirit of team play, I suggest we revise our opponents and switch them up. The elders suggested that we work as a team, but even they were still individualistic in their thinking.”

“Indivi—” Elias frowned. “What do you mean?”

Seraphine was the one who responded.

“He means that they chose our opponents not for how well it would work as a group, but for how it would benefit us individually.” She gestured to Alaric. “Take Alaric, for instance. Elder Mournwind refused to put him against Aodhán to avoid an...” She made air quotes. “Embarrassment.”

Alaric cleared his throat awkwardly.

“She’s right. But the same goes for her too. She did very well against Sophran Thunderstride in the last battle, and Elder Clearfall practically fought Elder Aspwick to keep her against him.” He sighed. “The point is that our elders are scared—either of losing us or disgracing themselves. If we are to work as a team, we have to make revisions.”

A heavy silence settled over the command tent for a moment before Ariana nodded.

“Okay. How do we do that?”

“Strategically,” Alaric said. “In the last battle, Nymera Vaelthorn killed Kael Varros in less than five minutes, freeing her up to help her comrade, Dravion Ashscar. That is what team play is, and that is what we need to employ.”

He glanced at Butcher, wanting to make sure he understood clearly.

“This doesn’t mean pitting our most powerful against their most powerful. It means pitting our most powerful against their weakest.”

“So, they are killed early.” Lysandra said.

Alaric smiled. “Thus freeing up the strongest to team up with someone else. Two against one. They’ll fall faster.”

Butcher gritted his teeth but said nothing. And after a second or two passed without objections, Alaric asked, “Are we all in agreement?”

“Yes,” Ariana nodded, and Seraphine hesitantly did the same. Butcher took a few seconds before agreeing, but the moment he did, Elias followed suit.

And finally, only Lysandra remained.

Alaric glanced at her. “What do you say?”

Lysandra straightened slightly as all eyes fell on her. She met every gaze, her expression a perfect mask of deep thought and consideration.

Then finally, after a long moment, she said,

“I see the wisdom in your plan, but my matchup against Aodhán Brystion is already perfect. You cannot change it.”

“Lysandra—” Ariana began, but Alaric cut her short.

“No, she’s right. Aodhán is strong, but not so strong that he cannot be defeated. If Lysandra believes she can do it, then there’s no need to change the matchup.” He glanced at the others. “Aodhán isn’t the issue. Nymera and Ashscar are. They are the strongest and the weakest.”

“So I keep Sophran?” Seraphine asked.

Alaric nodded. “If you think you can kill him this time, yes.”

Seraphine considered his words for a moment, then nodded. “I can.”

“Then there are only four of us left.” He glanced at Ariana, his expression somber. “You were set against Nymera. Do you think you can defeat her?”

Ariana opened her mouth to speak, shut it, then shook her head. “I don’t know. I saw the clips of the last battle, I—I’m not sure. I can draw out the fight though.”

“Okay.” Alaric glanced at Butcher. “What about—”

“I can kill her,” Butcher replied immediately, a bloody mist rising from him in waves. “If I’m set against her, I can rip her flesh to pieces, dig her eyes out and eat—”

“Then we’ll set you against Ashscar.”

Alaric cut him off before the bloody mist could grow any thicker, his gaze intent. “He’s the weakest. You should be able to take him out fairly easily.”

Butcher scowled. “And when I’m done?”

“You can join whoever is fighting Nymera against her.” He glanced at Elias. “Do you think you can hold her?”

“No.” Elias shook his head. “Volgrim was stronger than me. I can take one of the space transmigrants.”

“Okay then.” Alaric glanced at Ariana. “It’s just you and me now. Who will face the strongest storm transmigrant seen in centuries? You or me?”

The answer stood before him now, hands reaching for his neck, frost-white hair whipping wildly in the wind.

She was a tempest of chaos and emotion that refused to be tamed—utterly rejecting the very concept of balance or equilibrium.

Power flowed wildly from her, but it wasn’t hers. Not really.

It was the storm’s. And his best efforts to tame it had gone completely unheard.

It had been two hours since this battle began. Two hours of imposing every single thing he knew of serenity upon the world around him. Two hours of fighting tooth and nail against a storm that barely considered him a threat.

But beyond all that, it had been two hours of waiting, praying, and hoping that one of his allies would show up. That Butcher would arrive at any moment. That Ariana would bring her light to this darkness.

But no one was coming.

Not Butcher.

Not Lysandra.

Not Ariana.

Because they were all still busy with their own battles.

And just like him, they were all losing.

Elias Hale was not a man given to independent judgment.

Born in a world where commoners lived, died, and gloried in slavery, he had never quite adjusted to the weight of his newfound power and autonomy.

He deferred to authority.

That was who he had always been. Who he still was.

He had deferred to Volgrim, preferring to take orders rather than give them. He had deferred to Butcher for no reason beyond the simple fact that the man seemed to know what he wanted.

Elias deferred.

He did so until there was no one left to defer to. Until choice itself became unavoidable. Until he was standing alone before an opponent powerful enough to kill him if he didn’t make a choice for himself.

When there was nothing left to rely on—no guidance, no instruction—only then did Elias choose for himself, shedding the persona shaped by slavery and taking on the mantle of his path.

A mantle so heavy that it bent the world into obedience through sheer, immeasurable weight.

The Path of the Gravitic Throne.

It did not merely grant him power. It placed him above. And in doing so, forced everything else beneath.

It made him a living representation of the otherworlders who had oppressed him and his people for hundreds of years.

His domain became his kingdom. And within it, his opponents became slaves to be broken.

Wills that would, in time, yield to the tyranny of his domain.

And despite her fluid command of space, Elias had no doubt that Meredith would eventually do the same.

It was only a matter of time and patience—both of which he had in abundance.

Meredith, however, disagreed.

She screamed against the weight of his domain, a cyclone of fractured space spiraling violently around her. Each shard shimmered with power, layered with an ability he couldn’t recognize.

She was powerful. Far more powerful than he had anticipated when he chose her.

But her strength didn’t matter here.

The world knew it. She just had to accept it.

The shards shot toward him, thousands tearing through space to arrive before him in an instant—only to stop, suspended inches from his body, unable to advance further.

It was not resistance that halted them, nor any visible force. They simply could not continue.

Not within his domain.

Elias raised his gaze, his expression calm, deliberate—echoing something distant, something he had once despised.

“Heel.”

The world responded immediately, and gravity descended in crushing force, slamming into Meredith and driving her toward the ground. She gritted her teeth, her will flaring in defiance as space warped violently around her, but it was not enough.

The earth rose, stone liquefying to encase her form and bury her beneath layers of unyielding mass. She strained against it once, twice, and then—

Space cracked again as she teleported, breaking past gravity to appear behind him. The earth reacted instantly, massive spikes erupting upward in a brutal attempt to skewer her where she floated.

But Meredith was as elusive as she was powerful.

Distance stretched. The spikes elongated, straining forward, yet never reaching her.

She teleported again, slipping through space with fluid ease, and Elias grimaced.

He had done this dance before.

Adjusting his stance, he extended his will outward and commanded the space within his domain.

{MY DOMAIN, MY RULES: COLLAPSE}

Rules were powerful, but backed by his authority and the tyranny of his domain, they became practically absolute.

Space collapsed immediately, distance shattering to—

{MY DOMAIN, MY RULES: SPACE IS BEYOND YOU}

Meredith’s voice cut through the distortion, her will surging outward to match his.

Reality shuddered violently, space knitting itself back together for the briefest moment before fracturing once more beneath the overwhelming pressure of his rule.

But that brief window of stability was all Meredith needed.

She reappeared high above him, so distant she was little more than a speck against the sky.

She thought to escape his domain. To move beyond his reach. But Elias’s domain wasn’t a physical thing.

It wasn’t bound by distance. It was defined by opposition. Wherever she stood, so long as she stood against him, she remained within it.

Going high had only extended his domain, and with a disappointed sigh, Elias clenched his fist.

Gravity answered once more, descending upon her with the force of a mountain, so crushing and absolute that he heard bone snap.

She crashed into the earth a moment later, but instead of cratering it with the force of her descent, the force rebounded through her body, shattering bone and flesh alike beneath the unrelenting weight.

Pain tore through every inch of her being. And still, Meredith refused to break.

Her breath came sharp and uneven, each inhale dragging against ribs that no longer sat where they should.

The weight pressing down on her did not relent. It did not relax or ebb. But she pushed through it regardless, fury kindling her strength as she turned to glare at her opponent.

“You are not a god, Elias,” she spat, pain stabbing into her with every word. “You think you are, but you’re not. You are not all-powerful.”

Elias sighed. “Still. I am more powerful than you.”

Meredith studied him for a moment, then she smiled.

“Maybe,” she gritted, blood spilling through her smile. “but you’re certainly not all-knowing.”

Space cracked in that moment—not before or behind Elias—but directly within the space he occupied. And from the void, a massive claw appeared, then another, then a third…

Elias stumbled backward, eyes wide as a massive spider ripped its way out of chaotic space, claws already charging forward.

And impossibly... it struck, slicing open a crimson line across Elias’s shoulders

Meredith couldn’t believe it. Severance was her familiar, and she had summoned him as a distraction—a desperate gamble to buy herself time to regenerate. She’d never really thought he would succeed.

The gravity pressing down on her vanished as Elias turned his full attention toward her familiar, and Meredith hastily pushed herself to her feet, groaning as blood flowed from her injuries.

The pain was agonizing, but she barely noticed it as she watched Severance slice another claw down Elias’s back. The strike lacked its usual force—diminished outside chaotic space—but it had landed.

It had worked and—

An idea sparked, and Meredith eyes widened.

Without hesitation, she downed a handful of healing potions and forced herself into the air, a slow smile spreading across her face as Elias seized Severance and slammed him into the ground, his domain reasserting absolute control.

But it didn’t matter.

She understood now.

She had been fighting wrong from the very beginning, trying to attack a king from within his own throneroom. Of course it hadn’t worked. But Severance… Severance had proven something else.

Elias could be touched. He could be attacked—just not from within his domain.

And Meredith knew exactly how to do that.

Cultivating the path of the Void-Fractured Boundary, she was a master at bridging reality with chaotic space. This time, however, she wasn’t merely going to bridge them.

She was going to make them indistinguishable.

Smile widening, she stretched her arms outward—beyond reality itself—and seized hold of chaotic space, twisting it the way a sculptor forces shape into unwilling clay.

The void answered. It always did.

And an instant later, the cannons appeared—first as ideas. Then as pressure. Then as things.

They bled into the sky above the battlefield one by one, their bodies remaining in chaotic space—vast, dark, structureless masses that had no right to exist in any sane geometry—but their mouths, their yawning, ringed mouths, she pushed through, forcing the lips of each barrel to breach real space, to hang there, visible, present, like wounds in the air that refused to close.

Five. Then eight. Then a dozen.

Elias looked up then, a scowl on his face as he crushed Severance to dust with his gravity. But the moment his gaze met the sky, that scowl vanished.

His eyes widened at the scale of the weapons she had brought to bear. Then, slowly, he laughed.

“You never learn, do you?” he asked. “Those things can’t hurt me. They exist within my domain.”

Meredith said nothing at first, a cold rage churning within her as Severance returned to her spirit in agony.

Then she smiled. “Like I said earlier, Elias. You are not a god.” Her voice was calm. Certain. “And you are most certainly not all-knowing.”

Then, as one, she fired.

And for the first time, Elias made a choice—and felt his kingdom refuse him.
