Breathe in.

Breathe out.

In…

And out.

After Walter and I had reconciled, there on the upper steps of the Unchained’s bridge to the surface, my closest remaining confidants within the Herztalian expedition had closed ranks behind me. Sylvia, Walter, Maria, Renauld…these were the people whom I personally believed I could trust the most.

Nyx was dead, and I had to accept that. The onyx Sculpted woman had been one of the most efficient, steadfast commanders I had ever known, but she had been an unfortunate casualty of the fighting in Rhoscara. She would be missed, but she wouldn’t want us to just lay down and die.

Marcel, too, was another of our dead. He was going to be…less missed, I would admit, but he had still been our primary link to the Gnolls that had chosen to accompany us north. After all, the Ashen Bride had been their vessel in the first place, and it had long since been reduced to ash. We were lucky that Sylvia had managed to retrieve the mobile Ward Stone of the Astray that had been installed upon. That, to me, was the most important part of it.

But, beyond them…

There were still the remaining Captains of the expedition to consider.

I hadn’t seen hide or hair of Gustave in weeks. Most people hadn’t, apparently. The young Army Captain had survived the fighting, but seemed to have gone to ground in the aftermath of it. I wasn’t quite sure why…but I could hazard a guess.

Among the expedition, not only had the crew of his Steel Resolve been among the most supportive of Marcel’s bid to supplant me before his death, but they had suffered some of the worst casualties in the fighting after. It had hurt, hearing how people that I considered countrymen these days had so little faith in me, but I’d deal with it when the time came. I suspect I knew why Gustave had avoided me, but I could nip that in the bud easily enough.

Bella, though…

I had seen her, off and on, during our retreat from Rhoscara here to this caldera. Not personally, in truth. More like in the distance. The crew of the Thorny Reef, Bella’s ship, had survived like true pirates in the end, doggedly hanging on to the very end. We had even acknowledged each other, in the rare moments my clone hadn’t been busy forging. I could count her, I feel, but we weren’t really friends anymore.

Merely colleagues.

And then we came to the last of my Captains.

Olag.

For all of his drunken fury and sorrow, I needed him. That Dwarf might as well be one of the last surviving links I had to Dwarvendom right now, and he was a noble to boot. Even if he was Velancian, I’d need his legitimacy within the Holds. He was one of the people I hoped to reach the most with the coming show. And I was sure he was going to come, considering I had specifically asked Sylvia to whisper in the ears of people guarding the downtrodden, segregated Dwarves of the Raven’s Beak that he was moping in.

I wanted him here.

Alveron, though…there was no telling what was going on with him. The Elves had rallied around their leader and, apparently at his request, sequestered him away from the world. Nobody was getting in and out of their camp.

Fine then. It was disappointing, but I could understand just why Anguis’s words might have dealt him a blow. All I could do was hope that he remembered his responsibilities before the end.

But the first step to rallying the expedition…was rallying it’s leaders.

And I was sure I could pull this off.

All these thoughts and more swirled through my mind as I stood upon the very top step in that plaza. I was still with my eyes closed, breathing evenly, as I felt the space all around us begin to fill up. Both with the expedition and with others. This settlement hadn’t exactly been deserted before Bleddyn, and I had dropped over a dozen thousand refugees upon its doorstep. The entire bulk of the apparatus that the Unchained had spent the last year building was here.

They, too, were part of my calculations.

I tracked it all closely with my Aetherial senses, as the press of bodies drawn in through either duty or pure curiosity reached a critical mass…

And I opened my eyes.

It was time.

What greeted my gaze, sprawling out before me on the floor of the caldera, was by far the largest gathering of people I had ever seen in one place. The plaza wasn’t exactly small after all, considering this had, once upon a dark age, been where the guards of Addersfield had directed all of their ill-gotten gains into silos.

Now it was filled to the brim with thousands and thousands of idly chatting people. Humans, Dwarves, Gnolls, Sculpted, and even some Elves had come to watch, drawn by the pressing mass of people. Many of them were from the expedition, I could tell immediately…but many of them weren’t.

That was fine. They were still people of Vereden. I was still satisfied, for multiple reasons.

A quick glance told me everyone whom I needed to reach was in attendance.

Probably.

Including Bleddyn. Hello, old friend. No need to look so confused. I know you had never seen me like this. I’m guessing those are your commanders by your side, looking mildly affronted at whatever the Hell I was pulling off.

Just wait.

I took a deep, deep breath and cast a familiar Spell.

I can’t even imagine what public speakers from Earth would do to be able to amplify the reach of their voices like I could at a whim. Still, considering the sheer breadth of the plaza before me…

I pumped just a bit more Mana into the matrix than usual.

And then I slammed the butt of my staff onto the solidly shaped stone of the stairs beneath me. The sound of the impact rolled out over the stone of both the plaza and the caldera itself like a thundercrack. It was, ah…actually much louder than I had even intended. The natural acoustics of the caldera amplified my already magically enhanced blow to the point I saw many people closest to the steps actually wince and clutch their ears in pain.

In particular, with his werewolf-level senses, I could see Bleddyn glaring up at me with narrowed eyes. I restrained a wince, even as I felt the people at my back shuffle in place.

I…kind of felt it too. I’m pretty sure every single person in the caldera had heard that, much less the settlement.

I pretended I was letting the moment build as all eyes in the plaza zeroed in on me, and the conversation died, but really, I was just giving myself enough time for my ears to stop ringing.

Still…the mood I was looking to set hadn’t been broken, and I allowed myself to fall back into it.

Contemplative, focused, and defiant.

I broke the expectant silence in a way I’m not sure the crowd was expecting.

With a question.

“What does the sky mean to you?” I let the words linger in the air, just long enough for some people in the crowd to grow confused. “What does it mean to gaze up into the bright blue yonder and wonder, just what is beyond that horizon? How does it make you feel to stare up into the darkened heavens and contemplate the stars? We know there are other worlds out there, but are there more? How many of them bear planets not unlike our own Vereden? How many of them bear foreign, alien souls with wants and needs and fears just like ours? What does the sky mean to you, whether it be dark or light, or a painted shade of the evening? Because I’ll tell you this, my friends…”

“It means quite a bit to me.”

Slowly, I broke from my rigid posture to start pacing upon the top stair of the lift. Back and forth, slowly, enough that the people had to follow me with their eyes, I paced, the sound of my staff the only thing that reached the people as I did.

Thump

Thump

Thump

“And yet it’s been stolen from us,” I said, cocking my head as I paced, eyes fixed on the crowd. “Months and months and months, the sky that so many people have known all their lives has been sickly and grey. No more blue mornings, with a radiant sun to shine down upon us. No more bright, moonlit nights to gather and dance beneath, reveling in the fact that we are alive. We are here. We are denied even the soothing grey of a rainy day, as the water of life swells in the clouds and falls upon our crops. All of those colors that mean so much to so many people have been stolen from us…by one man.”

I very purposely did not say Dwarf. There was no need for even the slightest bit of division here.

I sought unity instead.

I abruptly changed subjects. “Many of you know who I am, and rightfully so. After all, for months now, I’ve been your commander as we braved first the seas, and then the savage Aether of the world that has been forced upon us. However…we are guests here, in your home, o’ warriors of the broken chain. It is only right that I introduce myself.”

I stopped my pacing and fully faced the crowd, squaring my shoulders…

And sweeping into a theatrical, and yet, earnestly respectful bow.

“I, am Marshal Nathaniel Hart of the Order of the Polaris Reach,” I smiled slightly, a gesture I’m sure not many of the startlingly silent crowd could see. But it was still important. “Late of Herztal, from whence we came. It was mine and my forces who have come to your doorstep, wounded and frightened.” I turned my gaze around slowly, panning it over the crowd. “I in particular was both. In the fighting that occurred at the city of Rhoscara, I was wounded near unto death. Through great effort and long patience, I am returned. And I. Am. Angry.”

The smile fell off my lips then.

“I am angry at a great many things, my friends and comrades. I am angry at the battle that was lost. I am angry at the comrades who were stolen from us. I am furious at the innocents who were cut down in the streets of a city that did not need to be razed. But most of all, good people…I have grown to hate he who is ultimately at fault for all of this.”

“Anguis, of House Savoy.”

A sudden intake of breath swept through the plaza, as a great many people, Unchained or expedition, were suddenly baited with the knowledge of something many had sought for months.

What was happening to our planet?

I intended to give it to them.

“No doubt there have been rumors about just what could have stolen our beloved sky from us, and replaced it with the beloathed Skyfall. I’m sure the Mad God has featured in those rumors. After all, who or what else could have the strength needed to cloud the skies of all of Vereden? Here I tell you, that is only the partial truth. The Mad God, he who was once known as Fynneas of Freedom, God of the Elves, is the puppet of none other than the Serpent himself. From the lips of Anguis did these truths spill, as he gloated to me, seated upon the back of another. Through foul means has Anguis seized control of the Mad God and wrought the once Divinity’s power for his own means. With it, he had conjured into being the Skyfall, as he seeks to usurp the throne of Divinity from Fynneas.”

Stunned silence. Even individual breaths sounded deafening in the quiet that had fallen over the plaza.

And I didn’t blame them.

These were crazy things to say. I’m sure many didn’t know either how to take it or even how to parse it. What did I mean, when I said a Dwarven Prince was trying to steal godhood?

I…had decided to be mostly truthful in what I told in this speech. I didn’t want to mislead everyone. People deserved to know what had fallen upon Vereden.

The specifics of the mechanics would only confuse them.

Still, though, this was enough. I wanted to reach not only my people…but the Unchained too.

After all, I had no doubt Bleddyn was going to marshal his forces to help us. I wanted them to want to help us as well.

I didn’t want soldier slaves.

“All of this, everything we have suffered since the skies lost their life and a cage was conjured overhead, is because of the foul ambitions of one man,” I leaned forward on my staff, staring out at the crowd with a piercing gaze. “But men can be broken…as can their ambitions. There exists hope, my friends. Always, always, the light of hope shines from where you might least suspect it to.”

I started pacing once more.

Thump.

Thump.

Thump.

“We traveled north seeking the truth of the Skyfall, and a method to end it. Anguis ventured forth to stop us in our tracks, because he feared the might of the populace united. He cowered at the very thought of unity within those who ever fight for freedom. And so he struck out at the city of Rhoscara, our gathering point. We…were not prepared for the depths of his depravity, and so I…wish to apologize.”

I stopped and bowed deeply at the waist.

This time, there was no theatricality in the movement.

Only sincerity.

“I take full responsibility for the loss of Rhoscara, in the absence of my friend, Her Highness the Prince Elysael. I failed both the city of Rhoscara and my people by not anticipating Anguis’s frightening actions. No doubt, even now, he retreats to the safety of the divinely powerful, nigh impenetrable wards that have fallen over the Elven capital of Smaragd, smug in the knowledge he has won. But he has not!”

My shout echoed out over the plaza, not quite as loud as my blow from earlier, but certainly enough to cause many people in the crowd to jump from the suddenness.

I bared my teeth to the crowd. “I tell you this now, he is not safe! Not while I draw breath! Not while the expedition still lives! And it is my hope that he will never know another moment’s rest while the warriors of the Unchained still sharpen their blades. There is a way through those wards, in the most unlikely of places. Far to the north, within the Mountain Holds of those Dwarves who disdained to bow their heads to Velancia, there exists a chink in the armor that is the Smaragd wards. A weapon that can bring them down. It is held within the grip of an ally who has quietly hidden themselves for a very long time indeed, just waiting for someone to come along and take it.”

I closed my eyes and took a long, deep breath before gradually letting it out.

I opened my eyes. “Soldiers of the expedition…warriors of the Unchained. I intend to venture north, through the Long Road, and take the hand that is outstretched to us. This weapon will allow us the means to strike back at not only Anguis, so he can answer for his crimes, but Fynneas as well. If we can but reach out for it, we can grasp victory. It only waits for those who are bold enough to risk it all…and I believe you are all bold enough indeed. I ask of you, all those gathered here now…will you fight with me?”

I braced myself for an answer…

And I was oddly overjoyed by the response I received.

“NO!”
