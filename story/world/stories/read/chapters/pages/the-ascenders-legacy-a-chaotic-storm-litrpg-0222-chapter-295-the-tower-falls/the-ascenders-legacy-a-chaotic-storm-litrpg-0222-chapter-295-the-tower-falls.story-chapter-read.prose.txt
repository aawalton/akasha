{Tyrant of Boundless Empathy}: You have refined your understanding of the concept of empathy in a deviant direction, emphasizing control and persuasion over the gentleness of empathy. You have now gained a passive area of influence. Upon activation of this skill, anyone within your area of influence gradually attunes to your emotions, thoughts, and desires. This manifests as a near-absolute synchronization of emotion, forging an instinctive unity of will. This effect does not impose mind control but aligns those affected through sheer empathic tyranny.

Two hours had passed since Aldric discovered the graft within the ziggurat—two hours that had turned the air itself electric.

In that time, the Alliance had grafted nearly a million soldiers into its ranks. Mythics and Champions from every corner of Lutia filled the skies, their auras simmering with barely contained anticipation. Telepaths to one side, priests to the other. Death awakened to the left, and life awakened to the right—each group vibrating with their own unique hum of power.

Still, they kept coming, more and more, until the ziggurat itself seemed to tremble beneath their collective presence. Bloodlust hung thick and tangible, staining the sky in a deep, ghostly crimson. Anticipation, excitement, and dread swirled through the air, a storm of emotion that pressed against Aldric's empathic senses, washing through him like waves against glass.

"Are you ready?" Tyrus asked quietly beside him.

Aldric nodded. "I'm more than ready."

The Alliance was finally ready to enter the order—hundreds of thousands of soldiers arranged by tier, with the strongest leading the charge and the weakest bracing the rear. Unsurprisingly, Aldric and Tyrus had been stationed toward the end of their ranks—mere sparks among the blazing suns of Mythics and Champions.

But Aldric didn't mind at all. What mattered to him was finding his brothers, wherever they were, and bringing them home. As long as he was able to do that, he cared very little for where he was placed.

That resolve steadied him as the command horn sounded—a low, resonant call rippling from the front lines like a death knell. The note hung in the air for a heartbeat, heavy with finality.

And then the great army began to move.

The ground trembled beneath thousands upon thousands of boots—a rhythmic thunder that built into a continuous roar. Despite their numbers, though, it took only a few minutes before Aldric was before the graft again. But this time, it had changed. The once-invisible seal had opened into a swirling portal of crimson energy that twisted and crackled like living flame.

With determination hardening his heart, Aldric took in a deep breath, tightened his grip on the sword he had been given, and stepped into the portal.

The world blurred immediately, colors bleeding as the portal hummed and spat him out into a steaming cauldron of chaos, battle, and noise.

So much noise.

Sound hit him like a physical blow—a wall of screaming metal, shrieking magic, and dying men that threatened to shatter his eardrums. Mythics and Champions clashed with their enemies in the sky above, their collisions sending shockwaves that rippled down to crack the earth itself.

Explosions bloomed in the air like flowers of destruction—violet, gold, crimson, and green—each detonation so deafening it drowned out thought, erasing everything but his primal instincts to fight and protect himself.

The sky was ablaze with death, and yet people took to it without hesitation, joining the slaughterhouse with cries and shouts of righteous fury.

Energy beams crisscrossed in every direction—some as thin as needles, others wide enough to swallow buildings whole. They collided mid-air in cascading explosions, painting the clouds in streaks of fire, energy, and blood.

That blood splattered down to the earth like rain—droplets, streams, and chunks of flesh falling to drench the battlefield below in a constant, warm drizzle of death.

But the ground itself was no less chaotic.

All around Aldric, people screamed and fought, skills and weapons clashing to split the air and rock the earth. The scent of blood, ozone, and fire hung thickly in the air, undercut by something even sharper—the scent of fear and desperation.

Bodies—some whole, most not—littered the earth in piles. Mud, formerly brown, was now red, viscous with blood, viscera, and worse things.

The war had only begun a few minutes ago, but already thousands were dead, their bodies carpeting the earth in grotesque layers. Hundreds more died with each passing second—a constant hemorrhage of life that showed no signs of slowing.

Beside him, Tyrus was a pyre of azure soul flames, burning with righteous fury as he unleashed his hatred on the cultists around them. Black and red robes went up in flames before their wearers could even scream, bodies shriveling as souls went up in flames, defenseless against Tyrus’s attacks despite any differences in tier there might have been.

But the impressiveness of Tyrus’s flames was the farthest thing from Aldric’s mind as he dodged a beam of searing light, only to stumble into a wave of jagged ice spikes. Tyrus blocked the attack with a wall of white flames before it could hit, shouting. “Stay behind me, Aldric. I’ll protect you.”

But he had forgotten that Aldric was a Brystion just like Aodhán. He didn't need protection. He was the Tyrant of Boundless Empathy, and it was time he showed the world what he was truly capable of.

For the first time since he gained the deviant skill, Aldric channeled more than a thread of quintessence into it. Heat erupted within him like an inferno, and he shuddered—not from fear, but from the sheer volume of emotions that pressed against his mind in that moment.

His senses sharpened—every heartbeat a drumbeat against his skull, every scream of pain a stab in his heart. But with the discomfort of his heightened senses came control and strength—a power so ethically gray he'd abstained from it ever since he'd gained it.

But not anymore. Not against cultists.

His irises shone a deep purple, not with light but with connection, as invisible threads of control bloomed from his body like roots through the battlefield, piercing into every cultist they could like ethereal spears.

Aldric multiplied the threads until the connection to so many people began to overwhelm his senses. Only then did he stop.

But by then, he'd already subdued over two dozen cultists—their minds and hearts beating in absolute empathic synchronization.

Their emotions washed against his mind like water against glass—seeking entry, seeking mutual connection—but Aldric destroyed them with a thought, enforcing a numb sense of devotion and absolute loyalty on his victims.

In an instant, twenty-seven different cultists of varying affinities and strengths turned toward him, gazes vacant yet expressions fierce as they converged around him in perfect synchrony.

Tyrus stumbled backward, his eyes widening in fear as he prepared to defend himself and Aldric against overwhelming numbers. He reached out a hand to conjure a wall of searing flames, but before he could, Aldric spoke.

"Easy, Tyrus. You wouldn't want to injure our new guards, now would you?"

Tyrus stuttered to a halt, and then his gaze widened even further. "Did… are you responsible for this?"

Aldric barely heard him through the noise and chaos all around them, but he understood the confusion and shock surging through Tyrus in that moment and smiled.

Chuckling, he walked to stand before his nearest victim and asked, "Where are they keeping all the kidnapped individuals?"

The cultist turned and pointed a hand toward a black tower in the distance, surrounded by a battle so intense reality rippled from the force of it all.

"They are inside that building?" Tyrus asked, and this time the cultist spoke, its tone inflectionless.

"They are inside Abyssos, which is inside the building."

Aldric grimaced and opened his mouth to speak, but before he could, a stray beam of death essence shot toward him, ripping through the fabric of space like burning coal.

Tyrus moved to protect him, but the thralls beat him to it, chillingly efficient as they formed a human shield around the both of them while one of them shot toward the attack, a bubble of water erupting out of her in defiance.

But the attack was too powerful for her, and it simply tore through her bubble to burn her to ash—her body bursting apart to drench them all in blood and ash. Another thrall took her place in an instant; a searing beam of light pierced straight through the explosion to disperse it.

It worked.

The beam detonated—ripping the second thrall also to pieces to drench them all in another shower of blood and viscera.

Aldric grimaced at the sight, wiping blood from his face, eyes, and mouth in disgust.

"Can't they die without bursting apart?" Tyrus growled beside him, his disgust more than apparent.

"I do not control how they die, Tyrus. I simply control what they do, and right now," Aldric glanced at the black tower in the distance, "they are to lead us there."

The thralls obeyed even without his direct command and began fighting their way through the battle, ripping through their own comrades without hesitance or fear for their own lives. Like machines they fought, uncaring for their own safety, only Aldric's. Safety was a concept they no longer understood, and none hesitated to ram themselves into a burning spear just to kill its wielder.

It was a disturbing sight, seeing the full extent of what his deviant skill could do to a person, but Aldric squashed any uncomfortable emotion before it could form. These people had kidnapped his two brothers and a host of other people to do despicable things to them.

There was no reason to empathize with them. What he was doing may not be right, but they deserved every bit of it for all the lives they'd taken for their fanatical cause.

His heartbeat steadied, his expression hardened, and with a determined look, Aldric pushed every hint of empathy away. The world dulled immediately—the emotions of the battlefield subsiding to simmer at the back of his mind.

And with the emotions no longer clamoring for his attention, Aldric was finally able to focus fully on his thralls. More boundless threads bloomed out of him, spearing enemy cultists to add to his growing army.

When one died, their threads sought out another target—always spearing, always subduing—and before long the initial number of twenty-seven had doubled.

Tyrus followed warily beside him, now rendered completely useless with more than fifty thralls guarding them. His emotions ranged from awe to disgust to fear—not for, but of Aldric—but Aldric spared no attention to his emotions or that of anyone else for that matter.

Instead, his gaze was fixed on the black tower as his thralls cut their way toward it. Somewhere inside that building, Aodhán and Daruk were trapped.

But it was also the area where the battle was most chaotic. With his strength, there was no way he was getting through it on his own. Even with his thralls, he needed help.

An explosion of spatial essence boomed in the sky above him, and he raised his head to see Artemis flitting through the sky in a blur of purple. The man disappeared before he could call to him, the bodies of a dozen cultists raining down on them, limbs and heads scattering.

Tyrus raised a hand to incinerate the falling bodies, but an array of red vines speared through them before he could. Roots bloomed within those decimated body parts, connecting them, and an instant later, the parts rose to their feet—an amalgamation of plant and flesh, wooden and bloody.

Tyrus grimaced at the sight, disgusted by what seemed to be plant necromancy. He glanced at the thrall that had used the skill, and whatever sympathy he'd had for them dried up instantly.

He turned to whisper something to Aldric, but before he could, a flash of reflection nearly blinded him. Shielding his gaze, he looked up, searching for the reflection that had nearly blinded him.

And then he saw it—a blur of reflection, then another, and another until there were hundreds of them, each emanating the immense aura of a Mythic. They sliced through the battlefield like spectral dancers, cutting down cultists before vanishing into reflective surfaces like smoke on the wind.

"Champion Matharantha," Tyrus breathed in amazement, and beside him Aldric smiled, pride and awe flickering in his chest. He opened his mouth to call out to her—but a voice brushed against his thoughts before he could.

"Look to the horizon, Empath."

He did. And there he saw an army of telepaths—an ocean of mental energy billowing out from their cores in waves of light and shimmering static.

"What do you want?" he asked out loud, and the voice came again.

"You are heading for the black tower. We too. Perhaps we can help each other."

Aldric glanced at the battle happening around the tower itself, taking note of the number of enemy Mythics in the area alone. He had been looking for help, and here it was presented to him on a platter of gold.

He would have to work with telepaths, but unlike Aodhán and Daruk, Aldric had no fear or disdain for his psionic siblings. He glanced back at the horizon and smiled widely. "Perhaps we can."

While the war raged—reality itself trembling from the clash of power—the telepaths set themselves apart to play god.

There were only a dozen of them, half in the Mythic class, and the others standing so close to ascension they could rival any Champion in terms of power. But telepaths never stained their hands in blood.

They were above that.

Or at least they should have been.

Their plan had been to enter the order, cast their mental net, find Mirith, teach the cultists a few lessons, and get out. But upon entry, they had met their match—enemy telepaths intent on thwarting their mission at all costs.

Their leader, Razan al-Mithra, floated at the center of their circle, his brow furrowed in rare frustration. Then he turned to his right hand, his mental voice low. "This will not do. Mirith is somewhere in that black tower, and we must extract her before the tide turns."

Beside him, his second-in-command, Kira Valeh, nodded, releasing a pulse of mental energy to direct a host of the Alliance army to the northern ridge where a new wave of cultists was approaching. The soldiers shifted formation immediately, guided by what they most certainly would call battle instincts, and she immediately turned her attention to the next area that needed tweaking, all while responding.

"Perhaps it'll be a good idea if we split up. I'll take half our forces to the tower, while you and the remaining half deal with our enemies."

Razan's eyes narrowed, then softened. "So be it." He glanced down at the battle raging below and added, "And take Aldric Brystion with you. I hear he was very useful in finding this place. Perhaps he'll be just as useful in finding this Abyssos."

Kira inclined her head, sending out the mental command to her unit even as her consciousness stretched downward, threading through the chaos until it brushed against a sharp, alien tide of thought—Aldric's mind—so absolute and boundless it nearly bent her will under its sheer force.

The contact startled her, and she yanked her consciousness back as if she had just touched something scalding. Aldric's psyche burned with layered emotion—empathy refined into something deeper, heavier. Stronger than what anyone of his tier and class should have been capable of.

Kira smiled.

"Yes," she thought to herself. He’ll do nicely.

While all this happened, a particular Mythic was having the time of her life.

She hadn't come here with any particular mission in mind, save for a chance to use her abilities to the absolute max and bask in the fervor and intensity of war.

Excitement hummed like fire in her veins as she mirrored attacks, amplified them, and pulled enemies she couldn't defeat into the glass plane to be dealt with by her duplicates.

With a wide smile, Matharantha danced to the rhythm of war, flitting through the air too fast for her enemies to follow, hundreds of copies creating a disorienting display around her.

Each kill she made pumped excitement into her spirit. Each strike she mirrored—absolute bliss.

It was intoxicating, and she soon lost herself in the cacophony of it all, her consciousness spread over hundreds of iterations, darting in and out of reality like phantoms. Each motion she made split into a dozen echoed versions that tore across the battlefield, dancing between fire, shadow, and everything in-between.

Cultists screamed all around her as duplicates of her blades appeared in their chests before she even swung. Duality sang within her, warping the world into contradiction—light bleeding into darkness, sound into silence, and life decaying to death in flickers of impossible rhythm.

A spear of pure obsidian flew toward her from an enemy Mythic; she didn't dodge. She raised a hand, and in one heartbeat her copy was the one struck, not her. In the next, the copy bled into the real, and the spear rebounded—turning midair and impaling its master through the throat.

More attacks came, but their wielders all met the same fate. But with each kill, Matharantha only became more excited.

The air solidified into glass everywhere her presence lingered too long—the mirrorverse bleeding into reality to steal away a part of it into itself. Each iteration born from those surfaces became a possibility, a lie, a truth. Duality was everywhere. It was in everything. And with an evil cackle, she pointed a finger toward an army of cultists—their foreheads beaded with desperate sweat—and smiled.

Divide.

It wasn't even a rule, and yet the world obeyed.

An army of copies burst out from her core, cutting through the ranks like shards of silver rain. Their formation broke instantly—confusion and terror feeding her aspect until the line between self and echo blurred completely.

For a few terrifying seconds, even she couldn't tell if she was real or one of her duplicates.

And then—she saw it.

A black tower—surrounded by an army of cultists so numerous they numbered in the thousands.

It loomed in the distance, an impossible monolith that seemed to pierce the clouds themselves, blacker than night, swallowing all light that touched it. Matharantha didn't need anyone to tell her that the structure was important to the cultists. She could see how eager they were to fight and die to protect it.

It was a beacon to them. Their anchor. Their heart.

Matharantha wanted to tear it down with her bare hands.

Instead, she settled for something far nastier.

Her breathing steadied. The world tilted, and as one, hundreds of her selves stretched their hands toward the tower, and with one voice—eerie and righteous—they spoke.

SHATTER!

Energy and willpower burst out of her core in a wave so mighty it would have drained her dry back in the advanced class. The mirrors all around her rippled violently, bending inward toward the tower. Space folded, the very air screamed, and a duplicate of the tower shimmered into existence beside the real one—perfect, identical, and then—

—It broke.

Reality folded in on itself instantly like shattered glass.

The false tower collapsed first, and its ruin tore the real one apart. Black Stone screamed as it twisted into itself, folding in infinite directions, turning inside out until there was nothing left but a yawning void, a floating box the size of a mansion, and three individuals radiating power on a scale she hadn't felt in a while.

Her eyes widened in fear as she recognized them—not their persons, but the distinct sense of danger and oblivion that radiated from them.

Calamities. Three of them.

And the Alliance had none.

Matharantha had never fled from a battle faster in her life, but she'd barely vanished into her mirror portal before she felt time suddenly freeze around her—coming to a halt so profound even the air in her lungs stopped moving.

The entire world came to an absolute standstill.

And then, with a groan rumbling from the very fabric of existence, it began to rewind, erasing all the damage she had caused.

The calamities had arrived late to the party, but with so much power at their disposal, why not simply go back to the beginning?
