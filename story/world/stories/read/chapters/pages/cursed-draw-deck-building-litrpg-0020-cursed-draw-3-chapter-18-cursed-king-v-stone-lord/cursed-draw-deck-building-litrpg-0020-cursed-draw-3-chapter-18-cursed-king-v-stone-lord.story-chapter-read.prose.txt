​

Alley hadn’t been surprised when Vern sacrificed his second Crystaline Barrier to negate his attack with Necrotic Fiend. Obviously, the older man was going to do what he could to defend his Terracotta warriors while they multiplied out of control.

Without using his Discard option, Alley ended his turn and allowed Vern to begin. The white-bearded man considered his options for a few seconds before activating a Spell.

“Hall Of The Underlord”

​

​

As a Spell, Alley had an opportunity to read the text on the Card Image, and he immediately noticed that while the spell could generate a lot of potential Power, it wouldn’t be of any use until Vern’s next turn.  That didn’t make its Manifestation any less impressive, however.

After the Card was played, Vern’s side of the field instantly began to transform.  The lightly snowed upon earth of the arena beneath the bearded man was suddenly replaced with carved stone tiles, a few seconds later a set of ornate walls rose on three sides, bearing what appeared to be pelts of huge monsters Alley had never seen, and a fireplace that was the size of Alley’s room back on Cursed Isle at the far end of the other side of the middle barrier. A roof above appeared, but so too did several  balconies and what appeared to be the beginning of levels high up in the air. It looked like someone had cut a tower in half and then placed it on Vern’s half of the field.

The spell cost the other man a Vial, but he had plenty left to play with.

“Why do none of my Land Manipulation Cards ever produce anything stylish? Look at this thing, and do you remember the snake temple that guy with the hissing titans Deck had?” Alley muttered to Denderra.

“What do I have? A shanty town for zombies.”

The pale ghost let out a little giggle.

“My brother has an actual castle, and it can fly. So I guess he didn't feel the need to show off with temporary structures.”

Alley tilted his head as he considered the reply. “I guess that's true. Though I don't see myself getting a castle any time soon.”

Denerra shrugged.

“No shame in being poor.”

“There is a bit of a gap between being poor and owning a castle.”

“Is there? I've only ever been a princess, so I'm not really versed in the degrees of not having money.”

Alley let out a snort and turned his attention back to his opponent. Vern had Manifested another Terracotta Warrior from his hand and redrew at the start of his War Phase, before sending one of his Clay Soldiers to attack Alley’s Cursed Gravedigger.

“Terracotta Warrior, Forward March!”

The Terracotta Warrior at the center front of the square formation  turned towards the small skeleton and began to stiffly march towards its target.

Despite the clay warrior’s movements lacking anything that could be described as smoothness, they were not slow by any means. Alley wasn't overly surprised, after all, the Power of a creature wasn't simply representative of its strength, but could be a measure of speed and magical ability also. He did however, find the movement of the clay man oddly unsettling; even Mechkin had a greater fluidity to their movements than the statue-man.

Just before the Terracotta Warrior was about to cross the blue barrier that separated the two sides of the field, Alley activated his play-any-time spell.

“Soul Exchange!”

​

Selecting Cursed Gravedigger as the sacrifice, Alley actively chose a different Terracotta Warrior to the one that was attacking to target with the spell. It didn’t really make a significant difference, but knowing that the warrior, which now lacked a target, would have its attack negated was satisfying.

The spell activated, and both the Cursed Gravedigger and the chosen Terracotta Warrior exploded into blue light. The blue energy flowed across the field into each other and then dissipated.  As predicted, the rapidly advancing Terracotta Warrior paused just as it was passing through the middle barrier. After a few seconds, it spun about and returned to its position amongst the square formation.

Alley had been right; it was satisfying to see the enemy Creature have its attack negated by a technicality.

More importantly than how it felt was the fact that the spell triggered the Cursed Gravedigger’s effect and allowed Alley to activate the ability from Necrotic Fiend. As always, the sound of shuffling Cards filled the air as five Card images were sent from Alley's Library to his Crypt. First went another copy of Cursed Gravedigger, next was Grimoire of the Doomed Scholar. That almost drew a little hiss from Alley, who would have liked that particular draw. The third Card sent from Alley’s deck was a Unique called Erastus the Grave Caller. Fourth was the spell Bone Shield. And finally, the fifth Card sent by Cursed Gravedigger to the Crypt was Resurrected Manticore. Before Vern could declare anything further, Alley activated his Necrotic Fiend's ability and discarded Phoenix Urn Phylactery. The Relic was a strong Card and one Alley intended to make frequent use of, but he couldn’t justify its cost in this match. Not just the Vial Cost, which was already a resource that Alley was running low on. The phylactery also reduced his Hand size so long as it remained in play.

Speaking of Handsize Alley, he was momentarily down to only three Cards since he had played Soul Exchange in his opponent's turn, and then activated Necrotic Fiend.

The spell that the demonic-looking monster pulled into Alley’s Hand with its purple coloured magics made Alley smile. This was what he had been looking for, the Spell Card that would not just break the stalemate between him and Vern, but utterly shatter it in Alley’s favor. It wasn’t a moment too soon, even with Alley having slain a Terracotta Warrior and reduced the clay soldier’s power by three hundred, they would soon be strong enough to overwhelm Necrotic Fiend.

Vern narrowed his eyes and looked closely enough  at the back images of Alley’s Cards as though if he focused hard enough, he could tell what the Cards were. The white-bearded man was clearly suspicious of what Alley was doing.

“I end my turn.” Declared Vern without playing anything further. Once again, the Shard of the Living Mountain activated and spawned another Crystaline Barrier, which in turn activated the swarm of Terracotta Warriors. A mere few seconds later, Alley was looking at sixteen of the warriors standing in frozen unison. On top of that, they each had a whopping fifty-five hundred Power.

The clay soldiers still couldn’t threaten the Brutish Zombie, but combined with the ability of the Hall of the Underlord, they were on the precipice of ripping Necrotic Fiend apart and being a serious threat to Alley himself.

Fortunately, the small army wasn’t going to get the chance, not with what Alley had just drawn.

“What is that doing in your Library?” Asked Denderra with a concerned look that only Alley could see.

“It's a strong Card, and I got it approved by the officials before the cutoff this time.”

The Oblivion Maiden made a frustrated noise.

“I can see that it's a strong Card, but you don’t even have a single one of the Resonances that Card needs. Not only is it going to cost more than it should, but something that powerful is going to make you very sick. This is almost as stupid a thing to do as when you purposely activated my brother’s cloak.”

“It's one Card, being played once.” Alley shot back. “I’ll be fine.”

“Resonance sickness isn't a joke, Alley, neither is bleeding out. Why are you so obsessed with winning? These are just tournament matches!”

Alley had to admit that was true; it wasn’t like the matches were life and death. He could, in fact, afford to lose. So why was it that Alley was so determined never to lose outside of a practice match? It ran counter to what he always told himself about it only being a game, and he didn’t really have an answer to the question. He supposed it could be a result of the weird fusion that had taken place with Anaten. That had certainly played havoc with Alley’s moods and personality, though he felt like he was mostly returning to normal after the temporary creation of that thing that had inhabited his body.

No…this was something else, something that had been slowly growing ever since he left home, or even a little before that. Something that had begun with the only real Challenge Alley had ever lost.

“Soren.” He said to himself so quietly that even Denderra didn’t hear him.
