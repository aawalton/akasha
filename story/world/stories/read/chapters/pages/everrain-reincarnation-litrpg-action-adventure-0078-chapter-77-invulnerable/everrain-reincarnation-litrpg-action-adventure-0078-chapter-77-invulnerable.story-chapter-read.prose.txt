Day 373, 10:00 AM

The passage leading from floor one hundred to the answer to Everrain’s mysteries is no different from any other stairway connecting dungeon floors. One major difference is immediately apparent, the light at the bottom is countless times brighter.

“Stand behind me.” Edna pauses without protest and falls behind me three steps later.

I reach the bottom step, and a blinding, golden light assaults me. I blink, and my eyes adjust, the forty points invested in physique doing their job.

Before me, at the center of the room, hangs a massive, foot-wide, golden crack, splitting the air itself. The light is pure magic gushing into the world, and beyond the torrent of light, I can see the looming darkness.

Twisted appendages, fangs, and eyes lurk beyond, glaring menace at me. Undisguised contempt washes over me, and yet all of that takes the backseat before the real shock.

“Are you Hadriuse?” I stare at the man, his features familiar from so many portraits scattered around all over the mansion I have haunted for decades if not centuries.

The years haven’t been kind to him. Golden cracks mar his skin and radiate from within his eyes.

“We are not Hadriuse.” Two voices escape the mage’s throat, grotesquely impossible as his eyes roll. “You may die now.”

The thing waves its hand at me, and a golden crescent of pure magic slashes towards me like a blade. I jump to the ground and roll under the attack. Faced with an unreasonable opponent beyond my comprehension, I do the only thing I can — I rush towards him.

Hadriuse doesn’t react immediately, his senses seem to be disjoined from his body. He makes another attack, but it lands ahead of me, and it’s even easier to dodge than the first one.

I jump over the golden crescent just as it sinks into the ground without a sound, melting it and infesting it with a golden aura. He waves his hand again, but the spell’s pattern changes. Instead of a slashing weapon it evolves into a swarm of nut-sized orbs, which fly towards me like a swarm of angry bees. My mind hastened to the limits of what hyper-stimulator can do for me. I read their patterns and jump to the side, dodging all but two of them.

Batsy weaves through the air, catching the weightless motes of gold. The contact produces no jolt or any other physical reaction. Striking the spell stopped it from hitting me, but felt no different from waving Batsy through the air.

While the golden orbs offered no resistance, that does not mean they had no effect. Batsy’s two sections turn to gold, then start warping. Spikes and twisted gnarled growths sprout from the shiny sections, the inanimate weapon somehow mutating under the golden light’s influence.

An oval crack appears in my staff, then splits along the middle, a golden eye staring at me.

Its dead gaze is nerve wracking, or it would have been, had I not spent years in Hadriuse’s basement, faced with much weirder things than a winking staff.

Hadriuse unleashes one more barrage of golden orbs, these ones more scattered. Too scattered, in fact. I jump and twist through the gap between them, roll on the ground, and continue running.

The golden light suffusing Batsy flickers, its hold over my weapon vanishing, but not before another oval appears, this time spawning a fang-filled maw. The light disappears, and the mouth and the eye freeze, but if I have to block another wave of golden light, I’m throwing away my staff. There ain't no way in hell, I’m letting myself get bitten by a stick.

Hadriuse stares blankly for another two seconds, processing everything that’s happening. Then his body twitches, as if torn in two different directions, as if trying to perform two different actions at the same time.

The dichotomy of thoughts and actions paralyzes him, and buys me enough time to reach him. I swing my staff at his head, intending to brain him, but Batsy strikes him like she would a rock. My arms sting and teeth clatter. Worse, the golden light spreads from his flesh, infesting Batsy. The eye moves, the jaw opening with malice as the staff twists to let it bite me, but I send the damn thing flying straight at Hadriuse’s face before it can finish its chomp.

The Batsy ring loops itself around the abomination’s head, squeezing and constricting, but achieves nothing. I have no spare weapons, and even if I did a lot of good they would do against a steel statue.

It’s probably stupid, but I slam a fist straight into Hadriuse’s gut, aiming for the section with no golden veins. The punch does nothing save bruise my hand, but I unleashed devolve in the moment of contact.

A patch of Hadriuse’s stomach rots and flakes away, but then the golden blood oozes out of the wounds, mending the damaged flesh and converting it into golden tint. The attack seemed pointless, but Hadriuse’s face twisted in pain for a moment, his eyes regaining a spark of intelligence before it disappeared once more.

I move a third of my remaining mana, funneling it into the spell and envelop Hadriuse in a cocoon which eats away at his flesh, but leaves the golden sections untouched.

“You must destroy me and the crack!” The man exclaims in agony, his voice completely different from the few words he spoke when I arrived. “It once used all Guide users to link itself with the world, but with me gone…”

The spell ends, the outer layer of Hadriuse’s flesh turns completely to the gold matching the crack, and he becomes a statue of light. The glowing golem punches, unleashing a fist-thick ray of golden light at me.

I move to block the arm, but even with my strength, instead of moving the arm I end up moving myself. I tumble to the floor, but at least I dodged the death ray.

A glint of gold catches my attention. My sleeve is changing and mutating, so I tear it off as hook-like appendages grow from it, trying to dig into my flesh and afflict me with the same curse which has taken over Hadriuse.

The golden mannequin looks down on me, and I have to admit, the intimidation factor of something akin to a golden statue glaring down at me is way beyond what the abominations have managed.

I will my mana into a new spell — something which can pierce Hadriuse’s body, to purge or destroy the raw magic consuming him.

The effort draws all my remaining mana, the burst slamming into his chest and staggering him back a step. The golden plating falls. Like shattered plaster, it rains from Hadriuse’s chest, dissolving into nothingness as it strikes the floor.

The monster topples over, but the gold is already gathering to close the damaged patch. He has infinite energy, while I’m spent. I have lost. My mind wanders towards the thoughts of the next redo and what I can do better when Edna appears next to me.

Golden streaks mar her face and torso. The barrages I managed to dodge struck her and infested her body. She’s moving like a puppet with half its strings severed, her face contorted in pain. I can tell she’s struggling against the eldritch monsters’ control, but her delicate willpower is just that - delicate.

I step away from her, ready to dodge the hand glowing with mana gathered in it, when Hadriuse’s golden hand closes over my ankle, gripping me like a vise.

“You can die now, puny demigod,” Edna and Hadriuse say at the same time in identical voices.

A wave of fire strikes me, boiling my flesh and struggling to disintegrate me as the spell resistance offered by my inhuman attributes works to keep me alive. The first wave of fire leaves me alive and in agony. The pain is so intense, almost like that time ants ate me alive.

Without proper medical attention, I am certain to die, but there’s no need to think about that. Mana gathers in Edna’s hand once more and surges straight at me.

***

“What happened?” A voice asked in the dark as the golden crack disappeared.

“I don’t know, I guess he had some trump card which activated when his life was in danger and he somehow sealed the doorway.”

They inspected the piece of void in which their potential meal once stood. Years passed as they struggled to reopen the rift, but each attempt failed, mortals needed to open the gate, not because the task was difficult, but because it was forbidden.

“I guess we just have to be patient and start over.”

A hundred and sixty Everrain’s years later, the gate reopened.

Two vast wills poured into it, and found their original vessel undamaged.

They roared in laughter.

“He turned back time!”

“We won’t make the same mistake twice. We will either force his trump early, or restrain him, should our attempts fail.”
