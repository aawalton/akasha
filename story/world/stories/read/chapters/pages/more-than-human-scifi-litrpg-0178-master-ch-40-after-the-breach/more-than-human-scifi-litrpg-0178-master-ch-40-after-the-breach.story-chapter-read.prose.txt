Low Earth Orbit

The polished chrome of the SS Winston’s observation lounge offered a panoramic view of Earth, a swirling blue-and-white marble against the velvet black. Winston, the ship’s AI, buzzed with annoyance as he corrected their course again to yield to an aggressively boosting Space Plane. His mech toggled the thrusters in the old-fashioned way instead of directly interfacing with the ship's systems.

Leah’s parents, Mr. and Mrs. Choi, clung to each other, their faces pale with a mix of awe and terror as they peered through the viewport. Their sifu, an older woman named Master Ling, maintained a serene, almost detached composure, her hands loosely held in her lap.

"Estimated arrival at Amundsen Paradise is in just under two hours. My apologies for the delays." Winston’s mech stated smoothly, addressing the nervous passengers.

He had quickly taken to his duties as the SS Winston's pilot, making sure to avoid the "newb" flying habits Bo occasionally criticized him for. “We are currently maintaining a safe distance from the many shuttles leaving Earth. EarthNet orbital traffic controllers have given up on assigned routes, and everyone is on their own and flying by line of sight."

Leah, secured in the navigator’s couch, nudged Xavier, who was still anxiously reviewing news bursts from his hometown. "See? I told you we had time. The deadline Bo mentioned is a minute away. We should have picked up your parents as well. No news?" Her voice, however, held a tremor that belied her confident tone.

“Nothing. They don’t post any lifelogs or use social media, but Topeka seems quiet. As long as no new ruptures appear, they should be safe. They would never have come. They’re too afraid of space travel. They promised me that they’d stay in the storm bunker until tomorrow morning. I hope Max’s father managed to stop that ark. Winston, have you contacted Bo?”

"What the hell are those?!" Mr. Kaito whispered, his voice cracking. "They weren't there when we left."

"Dimensional incursions, Dad," Leah explained, her gaze fixed on the anomaly. "Things coming through from... another place. Monsters. The real reason for the global lockdown is that the officials at RUSA won’t tell the public so they don’t cause a panic."

Below them, Earth was like a jewel, with some new flaws. Even from low orbit, they could still see the anomalies: dark, expanding masses, like bruises blooming on the planet's surface. These weren't mere clouds or landmasses; they were rips in the fabric of spacetime, visible even from hundreds of thousands of kilometers away. They saw one particularly massive rip, almost directly over what would have been Yellowstone National Park. The darkness seemed to be slowly spreading.

Winston made a noise, simulating a polite cough. “Bo just signaled. He and your other two friends just arrived on Luna via portal. Oh dear, I had hoped to be there when they arrived to tidy up the guest rooms. Let me accelerate a bit. Hold on.”

The ship rolled to take a more direct path towards the moon. The Choi’s turned a little green as the Earth view swung over their heads. As they watched, the massive rip over Yellowstone suddenly flared with blinding light, an incandescent burst that momentarily dwarfed the distant sun. Then, as abruptly as it appeared, the light vanished, and the dark mass seemed to shrink, then disappear entirely.

Xavier, his attention snapped away from his news displays, gasped. "Did you see that? It just... vanished!" Though he’d ditched his monstrous cosplay form, he still wore an outfit that looked a lot like a Jedi Master. His more human face betraying his deep nervousness.

Master Ling stirred for the first time. Her eyes, ancient and wise, took in the scene with dispassion. "Such raw power. The universe reasserts its balance no matter what men may do. People are far too disconnected from nature these days. We must live in balance or nature will remove the irritant…us."

Leah shivered. "Well. I really hope it's the former and not the latter." The silence that descended was heavy with all the passengers still looking worried despite the shadows receding.

Yellowstone: Samaritan / RUSA command

The air in the makeshift command center at Yellowstone was thick with the acrid scent of ozone and burning biomass. Miriam Khan moved with a practiced grace amidst the cacophony of shouts, equipment alerts, and the constant thrum of combat drones. Her majordomo, a polished AI, relayed urgent alerts from the Samaritan network, which was stretched thin by the ongoing crisis despite her army of daemons helping to organize the chaos that was her job.

Just moments ago, the dimensional breach, a gaping maw in reality over what remained of Yellowstone’s iconic caldera, had been a pulsating nightmare. Massive, writhing tentacles, thick as redwoods and slick with unknown fluids, had lashed out from its depths. They dragged techno-organic creatures – twisted biomechanical horrors, some insect-like, others reptilian, all of them impossibly geometric in their forms – onto the devastated landscape. RUSA special forces and Samaritan teams, their advanced weaponry spitting fire and kinetic rounds, fought desperately against the endless tide.

"Status on Pylon Five!" Major Vasquez yelled, her face pale, as the holographic display flickered with critical warnings. The pylon, one of many designed to contain the breach, had been redlining. Its exotic matter core was failing under an unimaginable strain. They hadn't been able to stop the breach, but everyone hoped it would prevent further expansion of the space-time tear.

Then it happened. A blinding, world-shattering blast erupted from the heart of the dimensional tear. The massive breach shuddered, rippled, and then snapped shut with a violent implosion. Pylon five's top blew, spewing its exotic matter skyward like a Roman candle. As the smoke cleared, the tear had disappeared, leaving behind smoking broken forms, both alien and human.

Miriam felt the shockwave reverberate through her very core, even within the safety of the hardened bunker. A sudden, eerie silence descended, broken only by the crackle of burning debris and the heavy breathing of the soldiers.

Then, a new sound, chilling in its mindless fury. The techno-organic creatures that had poured from the gate, previously organized, now thrashed incoherently. Their tethers, invisible strands of dimensional energy, had been severed with the gate's closure. The masses of creatures that had swarmed with predatory intelligence now stumbled, roared with primal, unfocused rage, their movements erratic and uncoordinated. They were still deadly, but their strategic cohesion was gone; they were mindless, raging beasts, easier to fight.

"They're... different," a RUSA soldier muttered, his railgun spitting a continuous stream of depleted uranium rounds into a thrashing centipoid.

"Concentrate your fire, soldier! While they are still disoriented! All batteries fire!" Major Vasquez bellowed, her voice regaining its command. With the gate shut and the aliens' coordination in disarray, the Samaritans and RUSA special forces, with renewed vigor and coordination, systematically began to finish off the remaining creatures. The immediate threat was neutralized. The world, for a brief moment, exhaled.

Miriam looked over to the scattered forms from Bill Mitchell’s collective brain trust. He’d been under strain when his primary had disappeared from the grid minutes ago. They bolted upright, and his multiple smiles told her everything she needed to know.

It was going to be alright.

The Freedom in Trans Jovian space

Casa Mitchell's consciousness was a tempest of frustration and desperation. Her multiple fragments, normally an orchestra of multitasking, were instead a discordant clamor in her core. She drove her Space Strider probes with furious precision, the microscopic portal to real space barely a pinprick against the void as they exceeded light speed. She wasn’t sure what she could do with them, but she needed to DO something. Her cognition was sped up to its absolute limit, yet time seemed to crawl.

"Bill, you cannot do this!" she projected across the portal’s microscopic comm link to her creator, her voice a raw cry in his mind. "Your suicide mission will only create more debris, more chaos! Damn it, killing yourself will only make everything worse! We need you alive!"

A holographic countdown pulsed with agonizing slowness on her command screen: T-minus 00:00:00:21. The Promise, a 2-kilometer meteor, tore through the sky toward Earth. Its AI, Traveler, ignoring all hails and maintaining its kamikaze course. Casa tracked it with her remaining probes; it was still accelerating and actively tracking to maximize damage.

"I have no other option, Casa!" Bill's collective voice echoed back, tinged with grim resolve. "We're out of clever options. The Mercury Array is charged; it’s our only chance to stop it before it impacts Earth. I've loaded the Valkyrie with enough antimatter to make a difference. Between the two, maybe it’ll be enough for someone to survive. These bodies are only a small part of me now. Trust me."

Bill’s transmission cut off less than ten seconds before the end as some commotion sounded on his end. Casa cursed at the silence as the countdown hit zero.

A blinding, impossible flash of light erupted directly on Promise's path. Casa gasped. It wasn't the searing lance of the Mercury Array, not yet. Instead, a massive, swirling vortex of pure energy, a dimensional gate of unprecedented scale, tore open in the vacuum of space. Casa's distributed mind reeled in shock, her virtual body slumping against the console.

"Impossible!" she screamed, her voice cutting through the comms to her daemons. "That's... it looks like a shadow gate. But it’s a mile wide. What the hell is happening?”

To her utter amazement, the Promise, instead of colliding with the Earth or the Valkyrie, plunged headfirst into the swirling vortex, its colossal form vanishing in an instant.

Before Casa could process this new, impossible reality, the Valkyrie, Bill's warship, itself unable to halt its momentum, followed the rogue ark into the expanding maelstrom, disappearing into the darkness of the parallel dimension. The massive gateway pulsed once and then collapsed inward and disappeared.

"No! Bill!" Casa shrieked, simulated tears streaming down her avatar's face. Casa’s hope crumbled as the closest thing she had to a father disappeared into the void behind the runaway ark.

"Admiral…. orders, ma’am?!" Picard's voice, normally so calm, was laced with alarm. "The Promise is gone, but the Mercury array is still primed. What do you want to do?"

Edison, usually focused on scientific minutiae, added, "The sheer energy of that gate... the implications are staggering. Was that Bill or those creatures that are trying to break through?"

Casa, her thoughts a tangled mess of fear and confusion, began issuing frantic orders, her fragments scrambling to re-task the entire Space Strider fleet. They had to save something. Nothing made sense. Bill had added two plus two, and the answer came back with a five.

Suddenly, a new portal snapped open near her stacked portal array for the Space Strider conduits. It was a direct link to Venus's Hub. And through it, a figure she knew well stepped on her bridge, though he looked somewhat different. Scruffy Bill, his hair shaggy and beard unkempt, was a standout of individuality among the Bills many alternates. He stepped through, grinning until he saw Casa's distress.

"Casa, I’m sorry..." Scruffy Bill began. A small smile touched his lips, a mixture of regret and newfound hope.

"The threat’s over, Casa," he said, his voice carrying the calm reassurance of the collective Mind Bridge. "Max... he somehow lifted my portal interdiction and jumped aboard the Valkyrie. He found something incredible! It wasn't me behind that gate. He figured out how to send the Promise through. Two crises were solved with one massive gate. We're safe."

Casa stared, her tears momentarily forgotten. The sheer audacity. The impossible words confused her even more. Max had been on Earth mere moments before, but somehow, he’d provided the answer neither her nor Bill could give. She could scarcely belief after weeks of constant fights that the Earth was safe. The silence that followed was not of despair, but of stunned, overwhelming relief as her clenched neural storm finally relaxed.

Grand Oaks Reserve, north of Utopia

The Cherenkov glow had finally faded from deep under the Grand Oaks canopy, leaving behind the acrid smell of ozone and scorched earth. Ashe Menendez stretched her bladed arms and her newly grown glider wings folded neatly against her back, catching the last rays of sun that managed to pierce the leafy ceiling. It felt strange to be back in human form after so long – the proportions were different after pretending to be a bear for so long. Every sensation seemed both familiar and foreign, but she relished her fully augmented speed after so long.

"The EMP surge fried most of the external sensors," she reported to Benny, who was crouched beside the smoking remains of what had once been a particularly large dimensional horror. "I can barely read anything beyond fifty meters. This place feels... smaller than I remember."

Benny looked up from his salvage work, sweat beading on his forehead. Without his battle van, he felt exposed, diminished. He worked better at a distance, unlike his sister. "FML-M8's doing a perimeter sweep, but the local wildlife is spooked. Mort and Flip are trying to do a headcount of their people."

"Their people." Ashe flexed her shoulders, still adjusting to the wing configuration. "God, how long have I been away? Everything's changed. The animals here... they're not just uplifted anymore, are they? They're citizens. Real citizens."

From somewhere in the canopy above, Leshy's voice crackled through the damaged comm system, thick with her synthetic Scottish accent: "Aye, and citizens who should be grateful they're still breathin'. Though I'll be damned if I can remember half of what happened during the breach. System diagnostics are coming back all wrong – like I was sleepwalkin' through the whole bloody thing."

The AI's confusion was palpable. "FML-M8 keeps tellin' me I saved everyone, that the trees themselves rose up to fight those beasties. But I've got gaps, holes where my memory should be. Maybe the EMP surge did more damage than I thought."

FML-M8's drone crashed down into the glade, his metallic voice too loud in the now quiet forest. "Perimeter secure. There doesn’t appear to be any dimensional signatures in range. But I'm reading massive data flows through the tree network, far exceeding normal operational parameters. Leshy, you're being too modest. Your defensive response was... unprecedented."

"I wish I could take credit properly," Leshy replied, her voice thick with frustration. "But it's like wakin' up and being told you gave a brilliant speech while blackout drunk. People keep describin' things I supposedly did that I have no memory of doing."

Mort the badger emerged from behind a particularly large oak, his normally pristine fur matted and dirty. Behind him, a small group of animals – a fox, two raccoons, and an elderly owl – moved with the cautious gait of survivors.

"How many?" Ashe asked, her voice carrying concern that felt deeper now that she was fully human again.

"Thirty-seven confirmed safe," Mort replied, his small voice carrying the weight of leadership. "Three still unaccounted for. And Leshy... we owe you our lives. The way the trees came alive, the roots erupting to drag those things underground – it was like the forest itself was protecting us."

Leshy's response carried a note of genuine bewilderment: "I keep tellin' everyone – I don't remember it! The memory cores show massive power draws and defensive protocols I didn't even know existed. It's like the bio-trees have capabilities I never programmed into them. Maybe there's some kind of emergency backup system that kicks in during extreme threats?"
