Clark pushed the metal artifact into the side of the Blast furnace, and closed the clay exterior around it with a flourish of [Unblemish]. A ripple of magic pulsed across everything, and he watched as it started sucking up a bunch of Water mana, and probably others, from their surroundings. A small shift happened around it, the surface of the squat, cone-like mound of clay magically stuttering for a moment. Then... it stopped.

Clark frowned. That had been cool.

"Almost there?" Alyssa asked, mostly out of curiosity rather than the aura of vaguely aloof annoyance she was trying to present as she channeled [Flamewind] into the base of the furnace, keeping everything half-running while he and the Commander had been improving its function.

The Commander tsked slightly as she consulted the divination tablet. "Darn. If it were half a meter closer to the stream, this would be self-sustaining."

She lowered the artifact and looked around, tapping her fingers in thought. "I suppose we did need Smith after all. Ride. Do you think you could run and let Smith know we need him, then get back before... and I assume that's a yes."

Alyssa's new trails of light following in the wake of her really fast movement was so cool. It was all futuristic and magical. She was gone for barely a minute, and landed right back where she had been, with a very diffuse flamethrower once again filling up the base of their iron-smelting station.

Or, Clark supposed, their steel-smelting station!

Henrietta had her eyes closed, but she seemed somewhat frustrated as she took several deep breaths. "I think that's all correct?" she speculated. "Nothing smells off, but."

Clark waited for her to finish her thought, before realizing what was going on. She was trying to not express her disappointment in her lacking magic-senses. Clark had learned a lot about how they worked since he'd landed, and that the Commander's current class was not the best set for alchemy-work.

"I am certain it is excellent, Commander!" Clark reassured her. "We can easily verify whether it is steel once it comes out the bottom, yes?"

"I suppose that is true," she was still frustrated, but moved past it. "Once Smith gets here, and sets up an [Order Mana] current to keep the transmute running, we can figure out what adjustments we might need for it to be actually good steel. It's been years since I was doing this, and even then I had [Tend Crucible], so I'm adjusting. Actually, Ride! Three steps to your left!"

The Ranger made the requested adjustment, and the Commander made a few measurements with hand-motions, pointing at Tower Stream and back to the squat Blast furnace, clicking her tongue. "Nope. Nope. The steel's going to wind up way too brittle if we do it that way. The angle between a Fire source and a Water-based enchantment is going to give way too many grains. Counter-annealing, maybe? The larger we can get the crystals, the better."

"I had always thought steel was merely carbon mixed with iron," Clark confessed. "What does Crystal do?"

"If only it were just mixing iron and carbon," Henrietta automatically responded. "There's not a single means of making steel that isn't solidly chymistry, rather than purely chemistry. What I'm concerned about now though, is whether we'll wind up with molten steel that refuses to solidify into anything but a ton of effectively separate pieces of metal, with intersecting grain boundaries - GBs - which will make it practically too brittle to do anything, and probably screw up half of Smith's Technology-based enchantments. Half a step back, one more step to the left, then half-step forward again there, Ride!"

She dropped her voice again, "I'm just glad I was able to make what I could in Metalpress. Getting actual consistent output from the blast furnaces, and getting rid of the interfering soot, should go a long way for our goals."

Clark listened while the Commander explained it to him. She had explained how it worked to him before, but she was explaining it more for her benefit than his - treating him as her familiar - and each time she explained it, he understood just a little bit more anyway, so it was really a win-win.

Apparently it was possible to burn charcoal too hot, so that it added too much of the carbon needed to make steel, and made the carbon that was added... improper in some way? Clark was still trying to understand exactly what was going on, but apparently what they had been doing, making liquid iron with charcoal, was bad chymistry and would make poor-quality iron instead of steel. But the Commander had done some material alchemy and made crystals and metal things which could... do stuff. And make the extra carbon burn away, and result in liquid steel being made.

She had also mentioned getting Oliver to redo the outflow of the furnace with some Metalpress magic, so that it created full slabs of metal, rather than just kind of pooling in the increasingly-abused pit of iron, which was just the kind of magic that Clark loved to watch.

Then Oliver arrived, and after talking to Henrietta for a minute, nodded and took up a position right on the bank of Tower Stream, on a slightly rocky spot to keep himself from sliding into the water. He planted his feet about shoulder-width apart, then put his hands together so that his fingers and thumb made a diamond in front of his face. He stood there for a minute, chanting things that Clark might not be able to understand, but could still enjoy as it conjured forth faint snippets of images, each lasting far too little time to really understand but coming together like little pieces of color in a stained glass mural. It painted the picture of a place where the heart of the earth was pushed forth, and where molten metal would come forth, ready to build new things and eruct grand towers of steel and magic.

Then a great glittering wave of magic rose up from the stream behind the Archmage. Unlike the muddy reddish-brown the stream usually was, the mana sparkled with deep depths like a diamond, both crystal-clear and the rich blue of the ocean at the same time.

It was eternal change, an everlasting cycle, the cradle of all which happened, the highway of civilizations and the base for every creature and all healing. His favorite element, the best one of all, both gentle and ferocious, devastating and caring.

It was Water, and the sight of it nearly took Clark's breath away.

Oliver thrust his hands forward, and the wave charged forward. It changed as it did so, from a glittering wave into a spiraling stream, still sparkling and still beautiful but now filled with clouds and steam as it plunged into the base of the Blast furnace. It stayed like that, even as Oliver relaxed and started wandering around, doing little bits of magic to tighten up the stream, make it more firmed up and like a pipeline of Water mana being pulled out of the stream and feeding into the base of their furnace.

Then the Blast furnace shuddered, and everyone froze. Mana began to pour forth from the top like it was an erupting volcano, and they erupted into motion.

"Were there any dragon parts used in your upgrades?" Oliver asked, a tinge of panic in his voice.

"Shouldn't have been!" Henrietta called back. "Haleford! Over here!"

Clark ran to her side.

"[Unblemish] here, as hard as you can. Keep it working. Do not let that crystal break, and keep it from moving as much as possible. Ride! I need you up top. Try to establish a wind going down!"

Alyssa jumped up to the top and started using her Skills. Oliver cast a spell on the outflow portion of the furnace, then ran over to Clark.

"I need you to relax your magic for just a moment, I need to borrow... okay, thanks!" He ran off with a thread of [Unblemish] clinging to his fingers, which he briefly wrapped around what he had been working on, and then went back to where he had done his Water-pulling thing. "Give me a blast of magic as strong as you can manage into the current, Clark! Full skill, I think I need all of it!"

Clark was momentarily torn between his two jobs, but found a moment when the Blast furnace was shaking little enough that he felt comfortable stepping away from the very important crystal to unleash ten Power's-worth of [Unblemish], further empowered with all of his subskills, and rapidly exhausting his mana in the process.

He preserved just enough that there would be no risk of him collapsing, and then allowed his Generation to sustain the ongoing repair of the crystal. But he made sure to be in a position where he could watch and enjoy whatever Oliver was preparing to do.

And he was really glad that he did! His skill had blasted out in no particular way, doing very little like a hammer swung through the air, but it had then collided with the current of mana Oliver was controlling, and it was woven into this great big spell that flowed into the smelter. From there, Clark could see as the spell coated everything, smoothing out cracks and tightening up lines, making some of the clay it was made of get smoothed out, turning a deeper red color almost like it was being glazed.

Then, the shuddering returned. Oliver involuntarily flinched, and the Blast furnace let loose a mighty crack, revealing a deep red glow within its heart. A crimson pillar of energy, too cohesive to be normal fire, shot from the peak into the sky, and great clouds of golden billowing smoke erupted from the crown with a tremendous wave of mana. Aurora-like flames spilled forth from the top, turning the sky around it technicolor, and a wave of mana flowed out the top like lava, darkening the ground and giving it a reflective, metallic sheen.

"Is that..." Alyssa started.

"Looks like a volcano to me!" Henrietta may not have been panicking, but there certainly was urgency to her words. "Stand clear! Ride, get Smith! Haleford, hold tight!"

An ink-flail wrapped around his waist, and then he was being carried into the sky. He was set down on the top of the Spire, near their friendly pillar of fire, and then the four of them were watching their Blast furnace turn into an erupting volcano.

"And after all that work we just spent upgrading it, too." Alyssa sighed. "At least it isn't spreading, like when we lost Shelter. How long do you reckon it'll take to stop erupting, that it'll be safe to go down there?"

She looked to Oliver, who shrugged helplessly. "I don't know! This isn't supposed to happen! I didn't think it was possible to happen!"

"It did sound a bit like you were casting and making it into a volcano," Alyssa said, which Clark had to agree with. "I think you used four different words that mean lava in your spell?"

"Those were standard invocations," Oliver defended himself. "'A volcano' isn't even a thing which elemental magic recognizes! Certainly not like this, anyway. Eruptions as a phenomenon are wholly divorced from lava, magically speaking. They both happen at a volcano, but that doesn't make them Associated!"

"Smith," the Commander commanded attention with a soft word, even as the rumbling of the volcano got louder. "Tell me if I'm missing something, but I'm not seeing or smelling any Dragon at play, which should be present in any runaway reaction or spell?"

"It is, yeah. Check in the..." Oliver stopped. "Huh." He cast a glance at his familiar-drone, assessing whether he wanted to send it down into an ongoing eruption, then deciding against it. "Can I see the Window?"

Henrietta passed the divination tablet to him, then stepped off the edge of the cliff. Her wings spread out behind her, and she began circling around the volcanic plume at a safe distance. The glittering, golden clouds absolutely filled the air, a column of smoke far thicker than when Jacob had triggered a Wild magic storm last year. This time, things were not flaking apart into so much ash, which he was glad for.

Some extra flickers of red light pierced through the smoke, almost like flashes of crimson lightning surrounding the column of red energy reaching into the sky.

"Huh. What is that?" Oliver's thoughts called Clark back to the present. "It definitely isn't dragon. Hmm."

Clark looked over at the tablet, and saw it was displaying a bunch of complicated looking colors and graphs that he had no way of understanding. "Ugh. Well, I'm not planning on getting close enough to actually get better measurements, and I'm not risking the drone on it." he sighed, rubbed his forehead as he fought off a desire to be upset, and handed the tablet off to Alyssa. "Right. I've got some Autonomous Divinations set up to monitor it, but let me know if you need me. Not that I think there's anything I can do to actually help with this. So I'm going to get back to working on the bots before I forget what I was doing."

"What do you expect me to do with this?" Alyssa asked, then opened and closed her mouth a bit while she looked for words. "I'll give it to the Commander, I guess? Clark, do you want a lift somewhere before I take off?"

He shook his head. He wanted to watch the storm. Volcanoes were cool, and he got to see firsthand a mini magical volcano erupt. Then he stopped for a moment. "Would you be able to retrieve pieces for a clockwork heart that I might assemble?" he asked.

Clark finished winding the spellcord around the gears, got the gears slotted in place and twisted up, polished it up with [Unblemish], and held it for a minute, marveling at the little artifact. It needed Oliver to do some more magic to it before it was done, but it could still tick and tock and spin around and be a little piece of magic that swirled with magic and glowed with such a lovely light.

The clouds of smoke had yet to subside from around the Blast furnace, and had instead grown so thick that it was almost impossible to see anything that was happening in there. The smoke that stayed on the ground, instead of spreading about and exposing everything to the full force of the magic, flowed into the Tower Stream and drifted away on the current. Clark was unsure if that was something the magic was doing all on its own or if it was something that the Commander or Oliver had done, but it was being a very considerate volcano.

Everyone else was doing their regular jobs at this point, because the volcano was doing its thing no matter what, and they got bored of just watching the magical display. Clark felt a bit bad for them, because he could watch it for hours more. Glittering shapes in the clouds, brief flashes of rainbow colors, occasional glimpses of the Blast furnace being much darker than it was before.

But work still needed to be done, so Clark took the Spire Elevator down to the First Hearth and started making food. The brick kiln blocked his view of the clouds a bit, but the golden clouds were just so big that he could still see it well enough.

Dinner came and went, Tully came in for snuggles and scraps, Oliver bemoaned the endless issues he was having getting his bot to just stand up properly, Jacob spoke of the dragons getting stronger, Alyssa asked if he could make something with greenspice for tomorrow, Henrietta took notes on the tablet. Oliver and Jacob started playing a game of chess, Alyssa worked on making a clockwork heart, Henrietta made herself tea, got a piece of paper, and worked on her calligraphy.

Clark took a bit of spare leather and kneaded it between his hands, [Unblemish]ing it as he walked around First Tower. It was never peaceful here, never nighttime, so there was always hustle and bustle around while the wood kiln made lumber, the sleeping hut pulled in Shadow mana to keep itself restful, and of course the Blast furnace was still erupting like a volcano. He took a seat on the bank of Tower Stream, just a bit upstream from where the golden smoke poured into the current, pulled off his boots, and dipped his feet in the water.

The current was strong but not unbearable, same as always, and just past the point of being pleasantly cool, at least until his feet got used to it and he could wiggle his toes in the mud. A notter inkling shuffled past him, diving into the stream to pick up more clay-rich mud. Birds let loose their wonderful song, the eternal daylight warmed Clark's body unhampered by the magical smoke, and he turned his face towards the sky to get more of its warmth.

Eventually, he dipped the leather scrap in the river and let the current wash over it, using [Unblemish] as it flowed so nicely in the moving stream-water, and getting more practice with putting complex patterns into the material with ⟨Retouch⟩.

Eventually, tiredness visited Clark, and he retreated to his bunk. Sleep claimed him almost instantly, and he awoke just as quickly. He emerged into the daylight, warmth flooding his body, and looked over to where the Blast furnace was.

Or perhaps it was 'had been'?

It seemed as though the volcanic eruption had subsided overnight, and in the place of what had previously been a mound of clay with some wooden structure, was... well, it could only be truly described as a small volcano, yet even that seemed to be a gross understatement.

It was perhaps twenty feet tall, and had what Clark could only think of as a 'volcanic' shape, with a cone that grew steeper until it reached the top, where it was cut off. But that shape was no longer made by clay. Instead, a dozen metal slabs quite similar to what they turned the iron into formed the mouth, and beneath them were all kinds of pipe-looking things, flat plates that had the same magical glow as motion slides, and a bunch of red, glowing lines that looked like circuit boards, like Oliver's skin did.

It looked like a volcano, but an industrial one.

The column of red light still blazed into the sky, and a certain degree of smoke continued to escape from its mouth, as did a thin trickle of white-hot metal coming from between two of the metal slabs, dripping down the side and flowing into the metal holding-pool. When Clark got closer, he could see another trickle coming out the other side of the, was it still a Blast furnace? On the other side of it, but that was cooling into rock like it was slag.

It was, in two words, absolutely awesome.
