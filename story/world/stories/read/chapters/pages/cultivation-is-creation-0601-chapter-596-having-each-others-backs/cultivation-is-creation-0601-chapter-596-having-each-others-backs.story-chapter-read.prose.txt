Just as I was about to channel qi, a wall of ice erupted between us, giving me the fraction of a second I needed to roll out of the way. Not a moment later, Di Heng’s fist shattered through it.

“Thank you,” I managed to gasp out as Xie Ling materialized beside me.

She didn’t bother to respond or even look at me, her focus was on the mountain of a man that was turning to face us.

“Your fight is with me now.”

With that announcement, she attacked with one ice technique flowing into another.

I could tell by the way she was fighting that she wasn’t trying to win, right now, she was just trying to buy me the time I needed to recover. Usually, I preferred to face threats by myself, but a teammate like her was one of the greatest advantages of working in a group.

As Xie Ling slowly drew the mountain away from me, Li Na appeared by my side, her formation already active. As the healing energy fed my body, the cracked ribs stabilised enough that breathing no longer felt like knives in my chest.

“Thanks.”

“No need to thank me,” she replied softly, “we have each other’s backs. That's what you said, right?"

With a slow nod, my focus shifted to how the others were getting on.

Tian engaged Ren Sheng directly. His sword work was flawless. Each strike was typical of Wu Kangming: precise, economical, and perfect. But one way or another, Ren Sheng matched him move for move.

"You're good," Ren Sheng murmured, blocking a thrust that should have pierced his heart while using the momentum to shift into a stance that I had never seen before. "Really good. But you should know that I’ve fought swordsmen better than you. Let me show you what I learned from them."

Ren Sheng flowed through high-level techniques that had clearly been copied from ancient swords masters. These were styles that had been lost for thousands of years and had belonged to cultivators who had reached this step and fallen. It wasn’t a surprise that Tian was forced backward, unable to attack because every opening was a trap constructed from centuries of accumulated knowledge, all he could do was focus on pure defence.

This was the first time I had seen someone get the better of Wu Kangming in pure sword skills.

My gaze shifted back to Xie Ling. The ice princess wasn't doing much better against Di Heng. Whenever her attacks landed, they weren’t enough to penetrate his body. The difference between a Half-Step Lucid Lawbearer and a genuine Lucid Lawbearer was being made clear.

As for Song Wei and Shen Bolin, they were fully engaged with Tian Feng. The offensive specialist was giving them everything they could handle. His cleaver moved faster than physics should allow. His Law made every edge cut deeper; even grazing hits left serious wounds that bled more than they should.

I couldn’t believe that they were struggling against Tian Feng despite their overwhelming cultivation advantages. That shouldn't be possible. Two Peak Lucid Lawbearers against one Peak Lucid Lawbearer should be decisive in most cases. And it looked like things would only get worse, if I didn’t do something, someone might die.

Now that Li Na's healing formation had done its work, I could end this.

All I needed to do was land one sneak attack with my full power.

Wrap my red sun energy and qi with dream qi to hide the signature, pick one of the Three Calamities while they’re distracted, and land a sneak at full power. That would break the Eternal Triangle permanently.

But who to pick?

Probably, Di Heng. The defensive specialist was still engaged with Xie Ling, so his back was already exposed. And he was the slowest among them. One strike with everything I had should be able to pierce through his immovable defense before he can react.

With the decision made, I gathered the combination of energies, causing vines to form along my arm.

It was time to end this.

"Now!" Shen Bolin's voice cut through the chaos making me pause.

Li Na stood up, her hands moving in complex patterns I'd never seen before, causing spiritual energy to explode from her body.

"What…what are you doing?" I called out.

"Doing what I need to end this," she whispered, the expression on her face making my chest tighten with sudden dread.

Golden light erupted from her position.

A formation array spread across the entire step, intricate beyond anything I'd seen her create before, connecting to all three of the Calamities simultaneously. They all froze as golden threads wrapped around their bodies and sank into their spiritual foundations, cutting their techniques off mid-activation.

"What—" Ren Sheng started to say, but blood poured from his mouth before he could finish.

The golden threads were draining their existence within the trial by targeting the core of what made them manifestations, then it tore it apart.

“Worthy opponents,” Tian Feng murmured, his cleaver falling from his hands as his body began to dissolve from the feet up. "You fought well. Better than most who reach this step." Before he could say more, his form disintegrated completely.

"The mountain stands until it doesn't," Di Heng rumbled with a simple nod. "Such is the way of all things." Then he too dissolved into motes of light.

"I would have liked to see what you really were and what that energy is," Ren Sheng sighed, ignoring Tian to look at me directly with a sad smile on his face. "Maybe in another life, we could have trained together instead of—" His words cut off as the formation finished its work.

The number in the sky changed from 10 to 9, but no one celebrated because Li Na collapsed, hitting the ground hard.

The damage the technique had done to her was clear. The girl’s left arm was bent at an impossible angle, clearly broken in multiple places. Blood ran from her mouth, her nose, her ears, and even her eyes. Her chest rose and fell in shallow, desperate gasps.

Before her head could hit the stone a second time, Shen Bolin was there to catch it.

“You did well,” he said softly, cradling her against his chest as one hand stroked her head with surprising gentleness. "You did so well."

No one knew what to say. We just stood around them in silence. What could we say? The girl had sacrificed herself so that we could go deeper into the trial. Unfortunately, this was the way of the world. People died so others could advance. But if I had another second to act, maybe I could have turned the tables, maybe she wouldn’t have needed to use a sacrificial technique. Dammit.

"Do it," Shen Bolin said quietly.

“No.” Li Na's eyes widened. "That's not right. It isn't—"

"You need to get the lotus," Shen Bolin interrupted. "This is a sacrifice worth making. You have the ability, so use it."

"But he trusted us," Li Na whispered, trying to shake her head but failing. "We said we'd have each other's backs."

"And this is how you will." Shen Bolin's hand continued stroking her head. "Do it now, before it kills you."

The rest of us looked around in confusion, having no idea what they were talking about.

Li Na closed her eyes as tears ran down her cheeks, mixing with the blood. Her lips moved, forming words in a language I didn't recognize. Ancient syllables that carried power far beyond what her Oneiric Sovereign cultivation should allow.

What happened next made my eyes widen in shock.

The girl’s broken arm straightened, bones knitting together with audible clicks. The blood flow from her nose, ears, and eyes stopped, then reversed, the red trails disappearing back into her body. Her chest rose and fell with easier breaths as color returned to her face.

The wave of relief seeing that couldn’t be put into words. Tian’s shoulders relaxed slightly, Song Wei nodded his approval, and even Xie Ling had a whisper of a smile on her face. I was about to laugh from the sheer happiness running through me when agonising pain drove me to my knees.

“Ah,” I cried out as my ribs shattered in multiple places.

My vision turned red as blood poured out of my eyes, nose, ears, and mouth. With a nasty crack, my left arm twisted at an angle that shouldn't be possible. This…this was the same pattern of injuries that had been inflicted on Li Na.

"I apologize," Shen Bolin said, and he actually sounded sincere. "But this was necessary."

I tried to speak but only managed to cough up more blood.

"Li Na's bloodline ability allows her to transfer her injuries to someone else," Shen Bolin continued. "Every wound, every broken bone, every drop of spilled blood can be transferred, but like any other technique, it has its limitations. The target must be a real person, and they must be close enough to touch with spiritual sense."

The pain was worse than anything I'd experienced in this palace, worse than the Drowned Vestibule's pressure, and worse than fighting the manifestations. This was the accumulated damage from sacrificing a life to kill 3 Peak Lucid Lawbearers, no one was equipped to handle something like that.

"I'm sorry," Li Na whispered, walking over to me with no trace of blood on her face while I lay there dying. "I know this isn't fair. I know you trusted me, but..." She paused. "Think of it as you having my back one final time."

I tried to respond and tell her that wasn't what that phrase meant. That having someone's back meant protecting them. It definitely didn’t mean using them as a sacrifice. This was pure betrayal. But I couldn’t say anything. The pain made it impossible to form coherent thoughts, and blood filled my mouth faster than I could spit it out.

A sharp and angry voice, cut through the haze.

"How…how could you do this?" Tian demanded as he stood between me and the others.

"It was necessary," Shen Bolin said. “You need to calm down. These things happen.”

"You stabbed him in the back!"

"How can we trust him? He’s been hiding his abilities. Anyone can see that." Shen Bolin argued. "But Li Na is someone who would die for the team. Since I had to pick between the two, the choice was obvious."

"The choice was betrayal!" Tian's spiritual pressure flared, forcing the others to take a step back. "You made him trust you, then sacrificed him!"

I had never seen Wu Kangming this angry before, but I couldn’t hear the rest of the conversation. The pain was too much. Their voices faded as my consciousness began to slip. There was no doubt about it, after everything we'd been through, I was going to die on step ninety.

Honestly, I had expected Song Wei or Shen Bolin to betray me. Hell, I'd prepared for it. But Li Na? The quiet one who'd always stayed in the background, who'd seemed so genuine when she'd said those words about having each other's backs?

That made it worse somehow. I had been paranoid, but clearly not paranoid enough.

The black stone beneath me felt distant, like I was floating above my own body. The sounds of argument faded to nothing. The pain began to recede as I left everything behind. Then everything went black.
