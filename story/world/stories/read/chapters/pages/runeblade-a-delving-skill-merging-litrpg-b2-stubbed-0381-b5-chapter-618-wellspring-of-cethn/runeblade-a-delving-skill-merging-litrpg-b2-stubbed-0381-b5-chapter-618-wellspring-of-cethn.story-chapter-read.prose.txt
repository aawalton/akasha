It refracted, contracting and shifting like organic netting over the surface of the sweeping wave and current lines. It made him feel like he was standing in a raging river that had been frozen in amber.

Despite those artful carvings, he was still unsure whether the tunnel had been carved into the bed rock, or if it was a natural cavern that had been expanded. The path was smooth, but the gently curving footprint of an ancient stream had been worn into the stone. The ceiling and walls were no help either. Stalactites hung like the fangs of some great beast, and while the surfaces were oddly straight, there were only naturally jagged cracks absent of tool marks — aside from the artful swirls that had been carved deep into its surface.

He stopped craning his neck when he realised that some of his companions were far less enraptured by the splendor. Julian and his men trotted ahead, utterly focused on the depths of the tunnel. Their enthusiasm was understandable — he needed time. The last few battles had drawn deep from his spells and resources.

“Julian, wait!” he called.

Kaius cocked his brow, drawing a resigned scowl out of the noble.

Thank all the hells that the lord had seen reason. He wasn’t exactly worried that they would run into something, but he’d had a few too many nasty surprises in the last few years to ever be comfortable risking it.

A moment later, his backline took a seat themselves, chatting with Kanmost in a low volume — Ianmus, it seemed, wanted to know more about the original legends of this place.

“How long do you need?” Julian said, pulling his attention away.
