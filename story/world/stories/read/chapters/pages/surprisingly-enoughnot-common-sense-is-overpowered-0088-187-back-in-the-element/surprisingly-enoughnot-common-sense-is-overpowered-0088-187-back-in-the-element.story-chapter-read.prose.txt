That reaction was about what Ning expected. A Foundation Building alchemist already possessed considerable influence within the sect, and Ling Xin was hardly some obscure cultivator hiding in a forgotten corner of Alchemy Peak.

Ning wasn't trying to throw his master's name around just to bully someone, but there was also no reason to pretend his circumstances didn't matter.

"I'm younger than you, I'm an alchemist's disciple, and I'm learning alchemy myself. I can make pills, buy pills, trade pills, trade favors for pills..." Ning shrugged. "And cultivation takes a very long time."

He allowed the silence to linger for a few breaths.

"You'll take missions outside the sect eventually. You'll travel through cities, enter secret realms, buy medicine from people you don't know, make deals with other cultivators..." Ning's smile remained perfectly pleasant. "Are you really sure you want to spend the next few decades occasionally wondering whether Ji Ning still remembers you?"

Liu stared at him.

The amusing thing was that Ning hadn't actually planned anything.

He didn't need to.

His previous world had provided him with more than enough examples of how people behaved when they knew something unpleasant might happen but had no idea what, when, or how. Uncertainty was much harder to deal with because the other person would happily imagine possibilities all by themselves.

And cultivators had a much wider selection of horrible possibilities to imagine.

"You wouldn't dare do anything inside the sect," Liu finally said.

"...Probably not."

"...Probably?"

Ning tilted his head. "I don't know what future me is like."

Liu's eye twitched.

"Look, I'm genuinely offering you the reasonable option here. Tell me who sent you and why. I take your points, you leave, and I stop caring about you."

Liu remained silent.

Ning waited several breaths, then raised one hand.

Golden light gathered around his fingers.

[Golden Finger]

Liu's gaze immediately dropped toward it.

Ning looked at his glowing hand as well. "You know, I really don't want to torture anyone."

Liu's face tightened.

"It sounds exhausting, for one thing. Messy too. And I'm fairly certain the tower would throw you out the moment I did anything genuinely dangerous." Ning considered that for a moment, then glanced back at Liu. "Unfortunately for you, there's probably quite a lot of room between completely unharmed and the tower deciding your life is in danger."

Liu swallowed.

"Cold, heat, itching powder..." Ning counted casually on his fingers before suddenly pausing.

"Actually, I've been wondering about something. The tower teleports you outside once you take enough damage, right?"

Liu stared at him warily. "What about it?"

"If I strip you first, do your clothes get teleported with you, or do you appear outside naked?"

Liu's expression froze.

Ning glanced down at him, genuinely curious now. "I've never tested it. Want to find out?"

Silence stretched between them.

Then Liu's shoulders slowly sagged. "Fine."

The golden light disappeared immediately.

"See? Talking is much easier."

"Don't act like you're the reasonable one here," Liu couldn't help but retort.

"I gave you the reasonable option first."

Liu glared at him for several seconds, apparently finding no satisfactory response to that, before finally exhaling.

"There is a bounty placed on you and Xiao Fan by Lei Dong. He's offering five thousand spirit stones to whoever captures either of you and brings you to his faction."

Ning blinked.

"...Dafuq?"
