Chapter 115

Joint Guild Exercise, Part II (Wolf and Shark)

August 2nd

1 day, 13 hours, and 06 minutes until Phase 2 of the Gods Game commences…

AEA FACILITY — DMG OH-NE-003 JOINT BRIEFING

The next day, we have a scheduled meeting with the AEA, Pegasus Guild, and the Gateborn Delegates (which I’m assuming means Yomhalde). Harvest Guild meets early and rides together—Labonte insists on optics, which means suits, black cars, and the kind of controlled arrival that makes you feel like you’re in a political thriller instead of an apocalypse-adjacent comedy of errors.

We’re in the back of an all-black limo. Labonte is in dressed in his standard suit, though has opted for darker colors than usual. Shades of purple and magenta blending together in various layers. Jerome is beside him, also in his standard suit. He’s got a tablet in his hands, reviewing several documents relating to our joint Guild exercise. At Labonte’s insistence, I’ve also squeezed myself into a navy suit of mine which fits a little more snug than I remember.

“Remind me to get you an appointment with my tailor,” Labonte comments, eyeing the hem of my pantleg, which sits uncomfortably high above my ankle. “We’ll need to get you proper fitting clothes.”

Jerome glances up from his tablet. “Are we sure we’re comfortable with the media rights in the AEA’s contract?”

Labonte grins. “There’s no prying AEA out of Wolf’s jaws. Probably the best terms we’ll get. And I’m shrewd, but not cruel. We’ve already hosed them on all other financial terms. I think we’re making out better than Pegasus. But does it include the provision we discussed last night?”

Jerome nods. “Yes, we have review privileges and the covenant requiring that Harvest have top billing over Pegasus.”

“I’m sure that pissed them off!”

“Probably.”

“Er—media rights?...” I hesitantly chime in.

“Gate Stream Media Group has a right of first refusal on the official activities of the AEA,” Jerome says. “They will be filming this joint exercise and will be releasing coverage of it. Potentially including a livestream, but that will depend on a number of factors, including safety concerns and potential sensitivities around confidentiality.”

Gate Stream Media Group. I’ve watched more than one of their event streams. Were they the ones who produced the coverage of the goblin horde attack in Chicago? The goblins had attempted to steal the giant bean and bring it back through the Gate. While that may have been a harmless endeavor, the monsters also vandalized local businesses and posed a huge safety concern.

“So, people will be watching us?”

“Indeed,” says Jerome, looking over a couple more things on his tablet.

I swallow, a lump forming in my throat. Suddenly, I feel a clamminess settle over my skin. I wasn’t expecting to be blasted to peoples’ devices around the world. Large enough monster outbreaks garnered a lot of media attention, analysis and online discussion. I’m not sure how I feel about being thrown to the public and potentially torn apart if things don’t go as well as we plan. Hell, we still have no idea exactly what we’re getting ourselves into. I suspect Labonte and Jerome have more details than they’ve shared with me but are letting me get the latest and greatest during the AEA’s briefing.

By the time we pull up to the AEA-operated facility, my stomach is already a jumbled mess. If it wasn’t for my nerves at this mission being broadcasted, it’s the fact that Pegasus Guild is going to be at this briefing too. Just like us, they’re lending two members from their Premier Team. Which means there’s a good chance I’m intimately familiar with at least one of the members.

Sarah.

Hypothetical scenarios of coming face-to-face with my ex-fiancé have been playing in loop in my mind, like a tooth I can’t stop worrying at. What will I say? What will I do?

The closest I’ve gotten to seeing her since the breakup is her face on a screen. A Guild promo, a news segment. A smiling image of a woman who broke my heart and tore me down, and now’s one of the faces of the most popular and largest Guild in the United States.

I’m so anxious I almost want to vomit.

I can actually feel bile in my throat when we pass through the first security checkpoint.

The facility is secured like a military base, rather than a magically reinforced warehouse serving as a temporary point of convenience for purposes of this mission. It’s not far from the lake and the edge of downtown, tucked away near the old airport. Concrete barriers a and fences manned by cameras make up the perimeter. AEA agents with earpieces and the calm, dead-eyed vigilance of people who were professionally dangerous are posted at each point of entry.

We’re escorted inside by three of these agents, two more agents staying back with the car and driver. The inside of the space is large and empty. I imagine it’s a repurposed warehouse, or perhaps a hangar. There are a couple of modular officers tucked into the corners of the space. Large windows on the third story let in a lot of natural light, in addition to the industrial lights fixed into the rafters. There’s something about the space—about how it was modified—that reminds me of places I’ve been before. That’s when it hits me. It’s a Containment Area. The reinforced walls hum with the ambient vibrations of runes embedded in the structure. The modular offices are fixed with thick viewing glass along the sides facing the open space making up most of the room. It looks a lot like the indoor combat areas used for Guild assessments and high-level sparring. Probably a precautionary measure, but the realization doesn’t do anything to help my nerves that are absolutely buzzing.

Agents Alvarez and Keene are there to meet us. They nod as we approach, professional and unreadable. Labonte starts talking immediately—questions, demands, comments about timelines and “asset deployment,” as if he’s discussing quarterly earnings and not a mission involving a significant breach of a DMG.

My eyes keep flicking to the far entrance on the other side of the room, and keeping my ears open to sounds coming from the entrance we used to enter the warehouse, which is now at our backs. Where’s the Pegasus Guild? I think.

“Where are the representatives from Pegasus Guild?” Jerome asks during a moment when Labonte needs to catch a breath.

“They should be arriving any moment,” says Agent Keene.

I sense them before I can see them. A couple minutes later two figures enter the warehouse through the far entrance, accompanied by three more AEA agents. And the first thing my brain registers is relief so strong it makes my knees almost wobble.

Not Sarah! I let go of a breath I didn’t realize I’d been holding this entire time.

The first figure is a short Indian woman who’s older, maybe around my parents’ age. She’s practically miniscule, standing at a smidge over five feet tall, if I’m being generous. She has laugh lines that soften her face, and dark hair threaded with gray in a way that looks intentional and elegant. She’s dressed comfortably in whites and beiges, with an oversized cardigan that makes her look like she could be someone’s aunt at a family holiday dinner. Her hands are buried casually in the deep pockets of her cream colored cardigan. There’s a weight to her presence that doesn’t need aura to be felt.

I recognize her instantly.

Reshma Murmu. The Sainted Blade. That’s the title Pegasus Guild granted her. But I think her class is something else… A Bone Cleric? That sounds right.

She smiles warmly at the room, like she’s walking into a book club meeting.

Labonte visibly restrains himself from saying something. Jerome just stands there, politely waiting for the newcomers to join the fold.

The second figure is… a dude. My brain stutters because this guy looks so… vanilla? Just an absolutely unremarkable man. He has average height, an average build accentuated by the fairly form fitting street clothes. He’s got pale skin and dull, gray eyes. He looks to be about my age, maybe a couple years younger. I think I know his name too. I rack my brain and it comes to me.

Matt Bruck. Or the Silent Specter. I can’t remember his Class, though.

I remember him from early Pegasus coverage. He looks a bit different now, though still as unremarkable. His hair—once a buzz cut—is now a messy mophead. His eyes are ringed with dark bags like he hasn’t slept in a week. And there’s a thin scar running from ear to ear across his neck, which I would have missed if it hadn’t been for my [Perception]. It’s like someone once tried to remove his head and failed—or succeeded and he got better. Either way, it’s a hell of a healing job.

I realize that’s what’s different about him. Combat has touched him. Marked him. When I last saw him on T.V., he was a random joe who’d hit the jackpot and fallen into celebrity in the new world with the System. Now? He’d seen the tradeoff first hand. He walks in quietly, gaze scanning the room without lingering on anyone too long.

And as he steps fully into the light, I realize the most unsettling part: He doesn’t look like a hero. He looks like someone who has survived being one. He looks as excited to be there as I am.

Labonte straightens, smile sharpening.

Jerome’s eyes narrow slightly, just for a second, analyzing Murmu and Bruck.

Agent Keene steps forward to greet them.

And I stand there, heartbeat steadying, stomach unclenching, thinking one thing with absolute clarity:

Thank God it’s not Sarah.

Then another thought follows right behind it, cold and heavy:

Sometimes it’s better to deal with the devil you know.

Reshma smiles, taking a hand out of one pocket to wave to us. I smile and awkwardly give a little wave back. Matt doesn’t acknowledge us.

Agent Keene claps her hands once, addressing the five of us.

“Let’s formalize introductions,” she says.

She gestures first to Labonte and Jerome, then to me, then to the Pegasus pair.

“Harvest Guild. Guildmaster Amos Labonte. He will not be a part of the joint task force. Vice Captain Jerome Williams, who will be leading this operation. Premier Team Operative Joseph Sullivan.”

Hearing Premier Team Operative attached to my name is… weird.

Keene turns to the other side.

“Pegasus Guild. Premier Team Operative Reshma Murmu. Premier Team Operative Matthew Bruck.”

Reshma steps forward with a warm smile and offers her hand like we’re meeting at a brunch, not prepping for an inter-realm threat.

“Hello,” she says, genuinely pleasant. “I looked into the two of you before arriving today. You certainly have interesting Classes. And I’ve heard you did quite well in Columbus at the recent assessment hosted by Chase Guild, Mr. Sullivan.”

I blink. “Uh. Thanks.”

Her grip is firm. Her eyes are kind. But there’s something in her—something steady and sharp—that reminds me of a knife wrapped in velvet. Something unbreakable and dangerous, no matter how sincere her exterior may be.

Matt Bruck nods to us next.

Not in a rude, or hostile sense, I realize. Just… tired. His expression says I don’t want to be here, but his body language says I’ve been ordered, so I’m here.

He shakes Labonte’s hand like he’s touching a damp towel. Shakes Jerome’s like he’s scrolling through a website’s terms and conditions. Then shakes mine like I’m a TSA agent checking his passport at airport security.

“Matt,” he says, voice flat.

“Joseph,” I reply, trying to keep it neutral.

“Pleasure,” he says.

“It is.”

“You…”

“I’m what?”

“You can let go of my hand now.”

“Oh! Er—right! Sorry, dude!”

I let go and his gray eyes flick over me for half a second—taking inventory—then dart away. Like he’s already done the math and decided whatever I am isn’t worth his attention now that the niceties are over.

Labonte’s smile stays plastered on anyway, because Labonte’s smile and the confidence it exudes are a weapon.

The group’s attention all snap to the doors behind us, which fly open.

Three more people enter.

Two men walk in first—average height, average build, plain clothes, faces that should be memorable in a “I definitely saw you at Target last week” kind of way. The moment I try to focus on them, my mind… slides. It’s like my brain can’t pin them down. Like there’s a fog between my perception and their features. I look right at one and immediately forget the shape of his nose, or the color of his eyes. I glance at the other and my mind refuses to commit his hair color to memory.

I frown, unsettled.

And then she steps in.

The woman is tall, well over six feet. Her face is all sharp angles. Her blood red hair cut into a bob, with razor sharp bangs. Sunglasses sit on the bridge of her hawkish nose. She’s wearing a navy suit jacket and matching skirt, with a patterned dress shirt and a crimson pair of heels.

Labonte leans toward me and whispers, reverent and giddy, “That’s Stephanie Wolf.”

I blink. “Who?”

Labonte’s eyes go wide like I just asked who Beyoncé is. “Gate Stream Media Group!” he hisses.

Stephanie Wolf doesn’t wait for permission. She steps into the center of the space like the warehouse belongs to her now and claps her hands together, excited.

“Okay!” she says brightly. “I’m thrilled. Truly. This is a huge opportunity.”

She looks around at all of us like she’s selecting pieces on a chessboard.

“This Quest is going to do numbers.”

My brain catches on the phrase like a snag. I raise a hand slightly. “Uh…” I say. “Do numbers?”

Wolf turns her head toward me. Even with sunglasses on, I can feel the weight of her stare. “For television,” she says, like it’s obvious. “And streaming.”

She opens her arms wide with a flourish. “This will be a new special: DEFENDING THE DMG! Can you imagine?!”

Labonte practically vibrates. I’m sure he’s imagining it right now. He looks like he’s about to foam at the mouth in excitement, which is deeply unsettling.

Wolf continues, voice smooth and confident. “My last special on Pegasus Guild reached eighty million concurrent households.”

Eighty million. That’s a lot… I think… I actually have no idea.

“Cool,” I say lamely. I make a small sound in my throat that might have been a cough.

“This will definitely do better,” Wolf says, like she’s discussing expected revenue numbers.

That’s when I remember seeing her name, floating on a credits screen.

I blurt, “Oh… Fire vs Ice? You made that?”

Wolf’s smile sharpens. “Yes.”

My stomach flips.

Fire vs Ice. It was a highly streamed event on the west coast. A fire beast had escaped a Gate and triggered wildfires so massive they looked like the end of the world. Sarah had headlined that team. My parents didn’t watch in protest and a show of solidarity with me, but I actually watched parts of it myself. Late one night, a couple of days after the live event. I hated myself for watching it. I hated myself more because it was actually… good.

Sarah—with aura-formed crystalline wings extending from her shoulder blades—freezing the monster’s flames in an instant. The editing and music had actually made the moment pretty epic, which I, again, hated to admit. The chaos after had made up most of the show. Flame-spawned monsters had poured out of the main beast’s body. Guild combatants chased them and either snuffed them out or contained them, helicopters dumping retardant, the whole city watching like it was a live sports event.

It was spectacle. It was terrifying. It was… entertaining.

Wolf smiles wide, revealing too-sharp teeth.

“We’ll be filming everything,” she announces brightly. “Starting now!”

The two nondescript men behind her shift. Their bodies ripple. Metallic seams appear along their arms and necks. Their torsos bulge and unfold like origami turning into machinery. Camera lenses sprout from shoulders, chest plates, foreheads. Microphones extend like antennae.

“What the hell!” I gasp.

“Interesting,” Jerome says.

An excited, shrill giggle rips itself from Labonte.

Reshma and Matt don’t react at all, clearly having seen this before.

“My film crew golems,” says Wolf. “You won’t even notice they’re here.”

That’s when I realize. The difficulty to focus on their features. These men… These golems… They’re enchanted to be forgettable, to draw my attention away from their presence.

And Stephanie Wolf?

I reach out with my [Aura Sense], which immediately confirms my suspicions.

She’s not just a producer. She’s a System User. And a damned powerful one at that.

The camera golems pivot toward us with little mechanical whirs, lenses clicking, irises dilating like hungry eyes.

One of them swings its torso slightly and a tiny red light blinks on. Recording.

Agent Keene clears her throat, and the room’s vibe shifts again—away from Wolf’s gleeful “this will do numbers” energy and back to business. I wonder why the AEA would allow this, as it seems grossly inappropriate for a quasi-governmental entity. But I remember that the AEA was not affiliated with the government of the United States. They simply were the joint-governing body formed for overseeing Guild and System User activity. I think about how much they be profiting from the exploitation of Gate maintenance that will be required whether it’s filmed or not.

“Mission overview,” Keene says. She pulls an entire table from thin air. It pops into place between the group, and I nearly jump backward.  Was that entire thing in her Inventory?

Alvarez toys around with a couple of dials on the table. A holographic map blooms above the table. Lake Erie appears in cool blue tones, Cleveland’s shoreline marked with clean grid lines. A small pulsing dot hovers out over open water.

“DMG OH-NE-003 is located twelve miles off the coast,” Keene continues, voice steady. “In Lake Erie itself.”

I blink. “In the lake?”

Labonte makes a pleased sound.

Keene taps the hologram. The dot expands into a circular icon with layered rings—security perimeters, patrol routes, something labeled AEA PLATFORM. There are several white dots surrounding the red dot designating the Gate.

“What are those?” I ask.

“AEA-affiliated water vehicles,” says Alvarez.

“We are expecting a breach,” Keene continues. “A massive breach. A hostile force from the Realm beyond is preparing to push through the Gate.”

The room quiets. Even Wolf’s grin tightens slightly.

Keene continues, clinical. “Our Gateborne allies have warned us. We have corroborated their intelligence through monitoring operations within the Gate.”

I feel my skin prickle. Monitoring operations within the Gate. It seems like I’m not the only one spending extended periods of time in other Realms.

“The hostile force is relatively small,” Keene says. “Estimated at approximately twelve combatants, all with equivalent power levels of Level 45 System Users.”

A small exhale runs through the room. Not the army I feared we’d be fighting. Twelve sounds manageable, even if each is as strong as a Level 45 fighter. But after what I’ve been through, I’m confident I can fight above my System-assigned weight class. As I’m sure the other three can.

“All, except one that is,” says Keene. “They are led by a monster with an equivalent power level of Level 130.”

Her words smack me in the face like a brick. I feel my mouth go slightly dry. Did she say Level 130?

The others, to their credit, do a much better job at schooling themselves. Reshma’s expression doesn’t change, but I sense the air around her tighten. Matt’s eyes narrow a fraction. He looks mildly annoyed, like someone just told him his day will be inconvenient. Labonte’s face… brightens, actually. Of course it does. Like he just heard the words historic opportunity. Jerome stays calm. But I notice his fingers tap once against the table—an unconscious tick of calculation, perhaps?

Keene keeps going, unfazed by our subtle reactions to the information.

She rattles off logistics: AEA platform deployment, containment reinforcement in the form of AEA agents. Evacuation protocols for the inhabitants of the City and other shoreline areas. Fail safes and other big picture details. Media perimeter and aerial zone. Wolf doesn’t look too happy at that last one.

“You’re telling me my golems can’t get any closer than that?”

“No, that is restriction does not apply to constructs,” says Alvarez. “Just remember that liability for loss rests solely with you.”

Wolf shrugs. “Fine with me. Go on.”

“Expected breach timing is the evening of August 4th,” Keene says. “We will convene on tomorrow to review specific strategy and battle tactics based on what we learn from our Gateborne today.”

Wolf raises a hand without asking. “Let’s make sure we get some really nice training footage tomorrow… I’m looking at you Muscles.” She tilts her sunglasses down, waggling her eyebrows at me suggestively.

I shudder.

Keene doesn’t even glance at her. “That is noted.” She addresses the four task team members. “Tomorrow will include training exercises to ensure your teams can work effectively together.”

A single day of training seems far from sufficient enough, but I bite my tongue.

That’s when the big door to the warehouse opens again.

Two AEA agents enter, escorting a very familiar, tentacle-covered face.

Yomhalde steps into the room. He scans the room, eyes sharp, then finds me.

Behind him walks another cephalopod-humanoid monster. This one has a similar build, similar tentacled beard, but taller by a head, and his skin is more bluish, like deep ocean water under moonlight. He carries himself with quiet authority, posture straight, gaze steady, hands behind his back.

Agent Alvarez steps forward. “Gateborne delegation,” she announces. “This is Yomhalde of the Deep, and this is Challo, Elder of the Deep.”

Challo inclines his head slightly, acknowledging the room without flinching at the cameras.

Yomhalde ignores everyone else and marches directly to me.

He reaches out—not for a handshake, but for a forearm clasp. Not knowing what else to do, I step forward and meet him. His grip is strong. Strong enough that if I weren’t me, it would hurt. His tentacles sway slightly, and his eyes—those surprisingly intelligent and perceptive gray-blue eyes—are alive with something like pride.

“Joseph Sullivan,” he says, voice booming. “I am glad a warrior such as you has joined our cause.”

“Yomhalde,” I reply, grin tugging at my mouth. “I didn’t expect to be seeing you again, if I’m being honest.”

He leans in slightly. “I received a strange vision,” he says. “In a dream.”

My grin falters. “A… vision?”

Yomhalde nods solemnly. “I saw you. In battle. Beside me. Against a strange masked figure. It was peculiar. And certainly an auspicious dream, indeed! I imagine it was my ancestors giving me guidance from the Great Beyond. With you at our side, our victory is but guaranteed!”

“A… dream, you say?” My brain instantly flashes to Hollowroot Bastion and the fight against the reanimated Russian guy. To the moment I summoned a facsimile of Yomhalde—and watched him die.

I bark out a laugh, forcing it casual. “Ha. Yeah. Dreams are weird.”

Yomhalde’s eyes narrow like he knows I’m deflecting but accepts it anyway. “Indeed. But I take omens seriously, Human Joseph.”

I nod, still smiling, while inside I’m quietly thinking: That wasn’t a dream, buddy. That was my spell. And somehow… you felt it. I make a mental note that I definitely need to explore the mechanics of my Summon Interplanar Ally spell further when I get a good opportunity.

Challo has barely said a word since entering, but the way Yomhalde shifts around him tells me everything I need to know. Yomhalde is a hero among his people. He told me that himself with enough pride to power a small generator. But this Challo character carries authority like gravity.

He finally speaks. And when he does, it feels like the floor vibrates. His voice is extremely deep and heavily accented. Each word is slow and weighted, like it had to push through water before it reached air.

“We show you now,” Challo rumbles.

He reaches to his belt and produces a small metallic sphere. It’s a dull silver, etched with faint lines. He drops it onto the surface of the table. It lands with a loud thud, before rolling to a fitted groove in the table’s surface.

Clink.

The sphere locks into place, then the top of it unfolds with a soft mechanical whir. A lattice of light expands outward, forming a holographic projection over the table.

And standing there in shimmering, cold-blue light is a human shape made wrong. A gigantic shark-man, easily five times the height of a normal human in the projection, with broad shoulders and torso as thick as a California redwood. Its arms are covered in slabs of thick muscle, ending in clawed hands. The head is pure predator: sleek snout, jagged teeth visible even with the mouth closed, black eyes that look like they don’t reflect light so much as swallow it. Its skin is scarred and plated in places, like it’s worn armor and grown into it in place. Over its head the System-style tag glows faintly—though the details blur, like the System is attempting to identify that monster, but it’s being distorted by the fact that this is simply a projection.

Challo gestures toward the hologram.

“Nereus,” he says. “The Trench King.”

Yomhalde’s tentacles twitch in restrained fury, his tentacled bear bristling.

Challo continues, voice steady, brutal. “He leads enemy tribe. They took control of our territory… where the Gate to your world is.” His gaze sweeps the room like he’s making sure we understand the stakes. “Yomhalde and I… we infiltrated… and were able to enter the Gate. Trapped here now.”

He points again, and even in hologram form the shark-man’s presence feels oppressive.

“Nereus seeks only conquest. Destruction.” Challo’s voice drops lower somehow. “To reclaim Gate… we must defeat him. He seeks the resources of this world, and to hurt your world.”

“Just to spite our people,” adds Yomhalde. “A cruel beast, Nereus.”

The camera golems shift immediately, lenses whirring, bodies angling for dramatic shots like they’re predators too, just a different kind. Stephanie Wolf practically glows with excitement.

“Oh, yes,” she says, placing a hand on the shoulder of one golem. “That’s perfect.” She points. “You—get me some close-ups. I want that thing to look absolutely menacing. This will make for an incredible dramatic build up for the livestream intro.” One of the golems moves closer, lenses telescoping out with a soft zzzt like it’s sniffing prey.

I stare at the hologram. Then, I glance at Wolf. A shark and a wolf. Both predators. Different ecosystems, same instincts. I wonder which one I should be more careful about.

POV: The Imp King

He could feel the city starting to notice him. Not people, of course—most people were cows, milling through their little routines, mooing at headlines and chewing on whatever outrage the day served them. But there were also the disgusting insects. Those who dared use the System. Seeking to hungrily gnaw at his flesh. Their eyes were turning, slowly, but surely. His time in this city was nearly over. He would need to move again.

How annoying. The anger boiled within his gut, making him want to scream. To lash out and bite back at those gnawing pests. It was getting harder to keep that righteous fury tamed. But he had to control it. For his lord… His lord who had chosen him to be the inheritor of this filthy world. For the next sacrifice…

This next sacrifice would not be like the others. The others had been… easier tests. Practice for a sacrifice such as this. Necessary steps to refine technique and sharpen the blade. He’d never dealt with a sacrifice as powerful as this one before.

She would resist. But he would break her all the same.

After all a struggle, when you did things properly, was part of the artistry. It was the difference between a crude butcher and a priest such as himself. He smiled to himself in the dimness, savoring the thought.

He summoned his Status window and let it hang in the air before him like a mirror.

LEVEL: 50

He admired the number the way a connoisseur admired a vintage. His lord’s gift had allowed him to grow quite strong. Each soul confluence compounding the strength he drew from each sacrifice.

And, of course, there was the new Trait his lord bestowed upon him.

Chernobog had witnessed his work and smiled. He was special. Chosen. A finger pointed through the veil and pressed into his soul. A mark that could not be scrubbed away.

He focused on the glowing line of text.

Trait: [Chosen Priest of Chernobog]

Description: You have proven yourself worthy of Chernobog’s Gifts. Your [Soul-Touched] Trait has been enhanced. Your [Soul Hunter] Skill has been upgraded from (Legendary) to (Immaculate). You have gained the Gifts of Chernobog. See your [Skill] Menu for additional Skills.

Trait: [Soul-Touched] (Enhanced)

Description: You have been Soul-Touched. One hundred Participants entering their Bronze Gate have been Soul-Touched by the threads of Chernobog. As one of the Soul-Touched you gain the following benefits: (1) Increased Experience Upon Level Up, (2) Increased Stat Growth Upon Level Up, and (3) Access to Soul Confluence.

Enhanced: Any Divine Boon received by the User will be doubled. Additional details shared about remaining Soul-Touched.

SOUL-TOUCHED REMAINING: 15

Soul-Touched Eliminated within Bronze Gate Selection: 25

Soul-Touched Sacrificed: 60

Priests of Chernobog Remaining: 3

SKILL: [Soul Hunter] (Immaculate)

Description: You are capable of identifying other Soul-Touched within a 5-mile radius. Once you’ve identified a Soul-Touched, their location will appear in your [Map] Menu at all times. You are able to mark another Soul-Touched. A marked Soul-Touched will be unable to move more than 25 yards from you. You may only mark a single Soul-Touched at a time. A Soul-Touched may be marked by more than one Priest at a time.

There were only fifteen Soul-touched remaining in the Game. Once there had been a hundred, though a fourth of those lambs selected for the slaughter hadn’t even made it out of their Bronze Gates. A thinning of the herd.

Of the fifteen, twelve had no special designation.

And then there were three. Three priests of Chernobog.

He and the two would be his left and right hand in the new world order. He let the number sit on his tongue like sweet wine. Three. A holy count. A small, perfect circle. A trinity of purpose.

He examined his new Skills… The Gifts given to him by his lord.

Skill: [Master of Dominion] (Immaculate)

This Skill gave him greater control over his Dominion and the imps contained therein.

Skill: [Sleep in Shadow] (Immaculate)

This Skill allowed his imps to travel through shadows.

Skill: [Blood Rage] (Immaculate)

This Skill improved all of his attributes if he needed. All that was required was to tap into that sleeping anger beneath his skin.

Skill: [Slaughter Charm] (Immaculate)

A glamour Skill that mentally and emotionally disarmed his sacrifices, making them less suspicious… Less afraid of their fated destiny.

Finally, his attention slid to the one that made his blood hum. A Skill that could only be triggered when he himself was close to death.

Skill: [Become Avatar: Chernobog]

Description: The User is capable of manifesting the power of Chernobog. This Skill may only be activated when the User’s Health is at 0 and the User is sufficiently bloodied. When activated, the User may temporarily become an Avatar of the Lord Chernobog.

Warning: Additional effects triggered upon death while in Avatar state.

He re-read the description slowly, savoring each word as though it had been etched into stone. Manifest a god’s power? Not merely imitate. Manifest. He smiled, wide and private, like a man enjoying a secret joke at a funeral.

A soft ping of real-world news intruded the fantasies playing through his mind. He looked at his phone. A notice from the AEA. Evacuation instructions. Official activity taking place in Lake Erie. Warnings of an anticipated monster outbreak requiring all citizens to leave downtown on the 4th.

That meant people will likely start the evacuation on the evening of the 3rd. He almost laughed. The timing was exquisite. The government, in its clumsy attempt at safety, had gifted him a city in motion. Streets congested, people distracted. Quieter, more private pockets of space in the absence of those who had already fled. Law enforcement stretched thin helping prepare for the monster outbreak. Attention turned outward, toward Lake Erie.

The perfect stage for a sacrifice.

Damien.

A pleasant man. A harmless man, in fact. The kind of man who smiled and pretended he cared about the lives of these animals who lived in this wretched world he was meant to rule.

Veronica Sampietro.
