“Thank you.”

The words came out strangled, rough, from a throat that had no right to work after Palini’s neck had been snapped. Xavier, unable to reach his friend, clenched his left fist and pulled at the remaining chain.

Still wouldn’t budge.

“What the hell do you mean, thank you? Look at this mess!” Xavier relaxed his hand and slumped down, backside hitting the cold stone. No observation crystals were attached to the walls, but he was sure they were being watched.

“My family.” Palini coughed. Spat dark, congealed blood. “You showed me… they’re safe.”

Xavier shut his eyes. His brain was fogged. The rooms in his mind were locked. The needle-sharp pain still covered every inch of his head, inside and out.

Roln, he whispered the thought. Roln, are you there?

He opened his eyes and glanced around the room. No one else was there. No words whispered back for only him to hear.

Xavier touched a hand to his still-raw head.

“Xavier?” Palini choked out. His healing should have kicked in by now. It hadn’t. “Do you… have a plan?”

Xavier remained silent. Closing his eyes, he searched inside. He’d done this already. From the second he’d woken he’d been searching. This search was no different from the last.

His Denizen cores, even his Reality Core, were locked. His energy channels too. He wasn’t sure how. Without his spiritual sense functioning, he couldn’t examine what had been done to him. What he did know was that they’d been… deactivated. Paralysed.

Not damaged or broken, but inaccessible.

A spell… which means it might wear off.

But there was another core inside his body that remained untouched. A core that functioned entirely differently to the others at his disposal.

His Void Core.

What the hell am I supposed to do with that?

“Xavier?”

Xavier blinked. Palini stared at him with quiet desperation.

“I don’t have a plan,” Xavier replied. Not yet. “But don’t worry.” He glanced at the stone-slab ceiling a few hundred feet up. “We won’t be stuck here long.” He turned and wrapped the fingers of his right hand around the chains still holding his left wrist, gathering all the strength of his body without any energy or spells able to enhance it.

Arrack may have locked down his cores and energy channels, but the man couldn’t know how strong his body truly was even without them. And his right hand, reinforced by refined Celestial Energy to take the weight of the power he’d had to bear to kill the World Destroyer, was the strongest part of him.

Xavier pulled.

The chain broke.

Xavier made for Palini. He was about to take the man’s hand but stopped. A touch in the wrong place would cause more pain. “Your cores? Energy channels?”

“Inactive.” Palini coughed harshly.

Xavier gently rested a hand on the man’s shoulder. He searched for words of reassurance but anything he found fell short, so he fell back into silence.

“Think. You’ll—” Another cough. “See us through.”

Xavier pulled away and stood. He stretched his arms and wings, expanding himself after being in that contracted position, then stepped over to the wall and ran his palms across it as though looking for a seam. He doubted he’d find one. He just needed to move. And as Palini had said, to think.

He quickly fell into a meditative state even as his fingers trailed over the cold, smooth steel.

His thoughts were in shambles. Not just foggy, but fragmented, jerky and slow at the same time. Finding that central room in his mind took what felt like an age, and when he arrived everything was fuzzy, as though a haze had settled over his eyes.

Roln? he called, his mental apparition standing alone. The dead Wanderer would know what spell had caused his predicament and if it was possible to counter. Are you there?

No answer.

The meditative state deepened as his body made its way around the circular room, palms brushing along every inch of the wall he could reach without flying. He took in the state of his cores and energy channels yet again.

Nothing flowed through them. They were stagnant.

His pores were locked down, making it impossible for him to draw in Celestial Energy, else he could at least use it to manifest spell patterns in the space between his cores.

All he had left was the pure strength of his body…

And the Void Core.

The Void Core held a small amount of C Grade Void Energy. It was something that he and Xavier Prime had yet to experiment with. Harnessing Void Energy was on their agenda, but other things had proved more important.

Xavier stopped running his hands along the wall—there was one more core he hadn’t accessed. A core that most didn’t consider a core.

His Soul Core.

He’d depleted a portion of his Soul Energy in an attempt to bolster his power right before Arrack had attacked him. Not enough to cause him soul sickness, but there wasn’t an abundance remaining before he risked that.

Xavier craned his neck backward and stared at the stone-slab ceiling. His wings snapped to their fullest and he leapt, flying upward in a slow spiral. Worries about Earth, Adranial, and all those he cared about invaded as his wings pushed through the air.

Had he saved the Silver River sector only for it to be used as a bargaining tool by Arrack and others like him?

Had he really fallen into this trap?

I knew if I left that portal open, you’d slip straight through it.

Xavier hadn’t broken the seal on his soul the System had placed on it when he’d been integrated. The danger was too high. None of his drones had lived to survive the process. All the refined Celestial Energy that had reinforced his body and channels had been farmed from those now-dead drones.

Besides, breaking the seal without the ability to manifest spell patterns was impossible.

But there must be something I could use this Soul Energy for…

“You broke the chains.”

The voice came from below. Xavier flinched at the sudden sound.

Arrack stood in the centre of the room, hands by his side, peering at the chains lying on the stone floor.

Xavier flew downward. A swift strike. Claw out his throat. End it fast. His fists clenched as he alighted on the stone a few feet from his captor. Still, the man didn’t look at him. Rip through his stomach, disembowel him, make him feel the pain—make him suffer like he makes Palini suffer.

Arrack’s gaze rose lazily. “Try it.” He touched thumb and middle finger together. “I would enjoy the diversion.”

Xavier glanced at Palini—his limbs broken at joints, his neck turned at an impossible angle, the soft wheeze of his breathing and the agony in his eyes.

Heavy Telekinesis. He locked eyes with Arrack. The finger-clicking’s just for show.

“Have you had time to think?” Arrack dropped his hand by his side. “This doesn’t have to be a bad experience for you.” He smiled. It wasn’t warm. “Once you give me your secrets, I could let you live. Your home planet, too. Your sector. They could be placed into my protection.” He lowered his head. “Or…”

Arrack clicked his fingers.

Xavier’s left ankle snapped. His leg struggled to bear his weight.

Click, click, click.

Another ankle. Both wrists.

He remained standing.

Click. Click.

His knees jerked, calves yanked to rotate one-hundred-eighty degrees outward while his thighs were held in place.

Even as his limbs shook, even as the pain hit, Xavier didn’t allow himself to drop to the stone. By sheer force of will he stood, staring defiantly at the sadist across from him.

Xavier felt his body try to heal. He’d grown back his hand after it had blown up, broken bones would slow him, but they shouldn’t stop him. But something was actively blocking it. Without full access to his spiritual sense, he wasn’t sure what it was, but there was a familiarity to it.

The toxic energy—whatever blocks my channels and cores.

Arrack frowned. He raised a hand, then pushed down. Though he wasn’t touching Xavier, a heavy weight—heavier than that stone slab of a ceiling—fell onto his head and shoved.

With broken ankles and kneecaps Xavier couldn’t brace against the floor. He tried to hold himself up with his wings. That slowed his descent until Arrack’s forehead creased more deeply and the weight became heavier.

Xavier hit the ground.

He lay there, the weight now pressing against every part of his body, holding him there, making it impossible for him to move. Arrack stepped up to him, leaning over his body to stare down at Xavier, a smile cold as the steel walls on his lips.

“You are a man used to winning. I wonder how it feels to lose. This is only the beginning, Xavier Collins. I told you—you’re broken. Now, I’m going to teach you, one step at a time, just how broken you are. And when you’re ready for the torment to end…” He swung his gaze toward Palini. “It can end in one of two ways.” The cold smile broadened. “I’ll show you one of them now.”

“No!”

Xavier tried to cast Time Alteration. Tried to manifest a spell pattern. Tried to launch to his feet.

He failed.

Arrack held his hand open and slowly closed it. The skin around Palini’s head compressed, eyes bulged, skull cracking.

Xavier reached for the only thing he had left other than his Soul Energy—he reached for his Void Core.

Dangerous. Too dangerous.

He didn’t care.

He pulled the dark energy out, every drop he had, and thrust it through the air toward Arrack. Arrack’s eyes widened.

But time did not stop.

The time mage isn’t here. He’s alone.

Arrack formed a glowing blue shield in front of him. The Void Energy phased through it and struck him in the chest. The A Grade’s movements became sluggish. He wasn’t paralysed, but he was slow.

The blue shield disappeared and the pressure compressing Palini’s skull eased. The world cleared. Xavier’s energy channels, his cores, unlocked.

Every one of Arrack’s spells had been interrupted.

Arrack’s eyes widened, fear pulsing as Void Energy dug through his channels.

The Locked Down status effect has been lifted.

Your energy channels, cores, spells, spiritual sense, ability for higher thought, and System notifications are no longer blocked.

As a lingering effect, all your spells have been cooled down.

Xavier’s body healed rapidly even as he manifested spell patterns. The first spell pattern he manifested was Time Alteration, but as fast as he was, he couldn’t cast it fast enough.

Arrack lurched forward with a jerky movement, falling to the floor to latch onto Xavier, gripping his forearm, fingernails digging through his skin. The Void Energy Xavier had thrust at Arrack had been C Grade. Its effect wasn’t powerful enough to stop him in his tracks.

A time dilation field snapped around Xavier as he struggled to wrench the man’s grip off his arm. Palini, still dazed, his own wounds healing, was trapped outside it. Arrack’s fear rose, waves pulsing harder. The Void Energy was eating through his energy channels, becoming stronger as it feasted.

Xavier manifested spell after spell. Energy coalesced in the air. A telekinetic push, resisted. Purple energy seeped from his pores to invade Arrack’s head—resisted, the man’s mental shield tougher than the layered steel walls around them. Thousands of soul apparitions appeared in the air. Terrifying monsters and powerful-looking Denizens, washing over Arrack.

That made him flinch.

Harmed but not hurt, Arrack grew stronger as each fraction of a second passed, his grip on Xavier immovable. The time dilation field was useless while they touched, blocking any chance of reinforcements but nothing else.

Xavier stabbed at Arrack’s body with his spiritual sense. The Void Energy had cut through the man’s veil, weakening it but not breaking it. Even so, Xavier could see enough of the man’s energy channels and the Void Energy eating through them.

The Void Energy was moving backward.

Arrack was pushing against it, able to resist the inferior C Grade Void Energy in a way Xavier had never been able to resist the A Grade Void Energy that had attacked him.

His Void Core had been the only play he had. It worked. Xavier was back to full strength.

This should have turned the tide.

“You’re weak.” Arrack pushed the last of the Void Energy out of his body, somehow dispersing it in the air, making Xavier unable to reclaim it. The A Grade’s fear diminished. “Toying with things you don’t understand.” A double-ended spear appeared in the man’s hand.

The blade pierced Xavier’s heart, toxic energy pumping straight through his bloodstream and channels. His vision dimmed. But it didn’t interrupt his time dilation field—that wasn’t a spell he’d cast, but a spell pattern initiated.

Arrack, his blade still deep inside Xavier’s chest, frowned at the active time distortion. He peered intently at Xavier, perhaps probing with his own spiritual sense. Xavier’s veil was down, but he thought he felt the man’s awareness wash over him.

“Interesting.” Arrack released Xavier’s forearm but kept his blade inside his chest. Unlike the last time the toxic energy had spread through him his world did not dim. Arrack, gaining his feet, hauled Xavier into the air at the end of his spear.

Xavier hissed through his teeth—he wouldn’t let the man hear him scream.

Still encompassed by Xavier’s time dilation field, Arrack walked over to where Palini lay on the ground. “I’m not sure how your spell remains active, but I do believe I was trying to show you something important.” He placed his boot on Palini’s head.

Palini entered the time dilation field long enough to have his skull crushed as Arrack stomped downward.
