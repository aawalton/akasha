Arthur woke shivering in the crevice, his body stiff and aching. Hunger gnawed at his insides. His tongue felt like sandpaper, and he didn’t dare check how many ribs were now visible under his skin.

But more pressing than thirst or hunger was the thought that the chimera might still be outside. So, he didn’t move toward the light.

Instead, something… pulled at him. A subtle, persistent tug, like a whisper brushing against his thoughts. Spy instinct or suicidal curiosity. Hard to say.

He stood and stumbled deeper into the crevice, away from the outside world. A little sunlight slanted in through cracks in the rock, but the path quickly darkened. Still, he moved forward, one foot in front of the other, scanning every inch. The walls narrowed. The air cooled. Moss glowed faintly along the stone. Just enough to see, just enough to raise questions.

When the light on the walls turned greenish-blue, Arthur crouched and scraped some off. He sniffed it. Dirt and algae. Perfect. Then he tossed it into his mouth.

It tasted like something scraped off a sewer wall, but it was moist. Moisture meant survival. He chewed more.

The path ahead twisted like a serpent in a death roll. Arthur advanced slowly, his spy instincts on high alert, ears trained for sound, eyes adjusting to the dim light. Every step was measured. Every breath controlled. He mentally mapped each twist and turn. Getting out would be tricky but not impossible. His memory was razor-sharp. He remembered every twist and turn of the tunnel with perfect clarity, even in this broken body.

Eventually, the passage opened up into a cavern with smooth, glistening walls. He paused. Waited. Watched.

No sounds.

No signs of movement.

No feces. No bones. No scent of prey or predator.

A dead zone.

His instincts, oddly, told him to keep going. An insistent, invisible tug drew him deeper. He followed, past offshoot tunnels and collapsed ridges, past silent stone and patches of glowing moss, until the path finally widened into a circular chamber so vast he couldn’t see the ceiling.

And that’s when he saw it.

At the heart of the cavern sat a creature unlike anything he had seen. Cat-like in form, but massive. Easily thirty feet long. Its fur shimmered faintly with a mix of pale blue and deep brown patches, and it pulsed - not like breathing, but like… flickering mana.

It was also clearly dying.

Parts of its body were raw and discolored, marred by the brown patches that ate into its vibrant coat. It sat with regal posture, but the weight of exhaustion hung off its frame like a leaden robe.

Arthur took one look and did what any sane, underfed spy would do. He turned around and prepared to quietly exit.

Then a voice, not heard but felt, echoed in his head.

"Arthur. If I wanted you dead, you wouldn’t be here."

He froze.

Not just because of the voice.

But because it said his name.

The voice was calm, feminine, ancient and disturbingly intimate.

"Yes," it said. "I nudged you here. You didn’t stumble onto me by accident. I read your thoughts. I know who you are. Both who you were, and who you pretend to be now."

Arthur turned slowly, his spy-trained mind racing. He studied the creature’s posture, its eyes. It wasn’t posturing. Wasn’t bluffing.

And it was right.

If it wanted him dead, he’d already be paste on the cavern wall.

He straightened and did what came naturally: asked the right question.

"Why?"

The creature lowered its head slightly, eyes glowing. "I heard your screams last night. Your grief. I’m sorry for your loss. Even if your parents weren’t."

Arthur blinked. That... wasn’t expected. But he kept his face calm. Tactical calm.

"Still doesn’t answer my question," he said.

The creature’s eyes crinkled, almost amused. "You’re bold. Good. You’re also right. I need something from you. As much as you need something from me."

It shifted slightly, revealing two large, glowing eggs behind it. Encased in a protective cocoon of shimmering energy. "I am what your people call a Mana Nexus Beast. Our kind lived at the edge of the known universe, near mana wells so dense, most life couldn’t survive. We could manipulate mana at will. We were feared, respected… until the Corruptor came."

Arthur raised a brow. "Corruptor?"

"A being. No - a blight. Something that poisons mana itself. We were hunted. I am the last. These eggs are the last." She paused. "And I am dying. Slowly. The low mana in this world keeps the corruption at bay. But not for long."

Arthur studied her carefully. She didn’t seem to be lying. But he was trained to know that even truth can be used as a weapon.

"And you want me to… babysit your eggs?"

"Not just babysit. Hatch them. Raise them to be powerful. Protect them until they can protect themselves."

Arthur folded his arms.

"And in return?"

"My soul. I will sacrifice it willingly to grant you its power."

"And the catch?"

The creature actually smiled. "Arthur, I like you. Most would’ve begged for the pact by now. You? You’re still suspicious. Good. There are two catches."

"First, if anyone powerful learns you carry my offspring, they will hunt you. No warning. No mercy. You’ll be enemy number one."

"Second, you must raise them to A-rank within one thousand years. Fail and your soul is forfeit. No afterlife. No reincarnation. Just oblivion."

Arthur sighed. "So: assassins, ticking clock, and soul death."

He paused. Arthur stared at the eggs. At the ring. At the beast. "I’ve never even raised a goldfish."

"Then you’ll learn. I’ve already given you the knowledge."

It stepped forward in a blur, touching his forehead. Arthur staggered as a flood of information buried itself in his brain - feeding schedules, mana regulation, danger signs, behavioral patterns. The instruction manual to raise the beasts.

"Fine," Arthur said. "Let’s do this."

"There’s a spatial ring beneath me. Take it. It will hide the eggs until they reach your size. After that… they’re on their own, unless you’ve grown stronger."

Arthur approached, careful not to touch the corrupted patches on her skin. She nudged a ring toward him - silver, etched with glowing runes.

"Last step," she said. "There’s a spike behind you. Use it. Stab me in the corrupted spot. The System will mark you as my killer."

Arthur tilted his head. "System?"

The creature sighed, visibly wilting.

"I… used the last of my strength to transfer the knowledge of raising my children to you. I don’t have enough left to explain what the System is. Only that it will give you the power you need to keep them and yourself alive."

Arthur hesitated. His old self would have done this in a heartbeat.

Now?

Now it felt like ending a pact before it had truly begun.

But there was no other path.

With steady hands he took the stone.

"You’ve already given me peace," the creature said softly. "Now take my strength. And don’t waste it."

Arthur struck.

There was a flash. Not light from the moss, not fire or lightning.

It was a flash of something deeper - a raw, blinding energy that burst outward without heat or flame. Just raw force, like the mountain itself had exhaled through his soul.

Then...

A voice, loud as thought, crisp as crystal, spoke across his mind like a HUD booting up.

> Congratulations! You have unlocked the System.

And then, the pain.

Not physical but something more.

Something deeper.

Arthur’s world went white.
