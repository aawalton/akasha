Room by room, hall by hall, Xavier and the Denizens he’d freed made their way through the base a hundred galaxies away from the Orin sector.

The agents, even those he was sure were more powerful than him, all took his orders. Xavier didn’t know if it was a change in loyalty, pure convenience, or because they thought they owed him a debt—maybe it was all three and a hell of a lot of other things in mix on top.

He’d stealthed into this base and freed them. Because of that, he was their natural leader, and these were people who’d grown used to following orders—and they were very good at it.

Not knowing how large the base was, it was difficult to know how much they’ve managed to cover. Having Alica’s memories, he was at least aware that he’d covered more than she’d ever known of this place existed.

One-by-one, more of the agents were turned. They covered the entirety of the Social Rooms and all the agents’ quarters. As far as they’d found, four-hundred-and-seventy-two agents were currently on base, and every single one was now on Xavier’s side. They weren’t all in the same place. That many agents walking through the base at his back would take up far too much room. The walls might have blocked his Communication Stones, The All-Seeing Eye, and even his spiritual sense, but Xavier was able to keep in contact with the different groups of agents by leaving a drone with them—the telepathic bond he shared with his drones didn’t use his spiritual sense to function, and that was one thing the walls didn’t block.

Xavier split his mind, putting separate parts into the different drones so he could lead the groups of agents through the base more effectively. Each drone had a time dilation field. Each field was synced to his own. Each drone was able to erase contracts and trick the observation crystals.

Not a single agent or even staff member of the base resisted after they’d had their contracts erased. As far as base takeovers went, it was all going far more smoothly than he could have imagined. Not a single drop of blood had been spilled.

Too easy.

He kept waiting for the other shoe to drop.

As Xavier had converted more and more agents, the wheels turned in his mind and his plan expanded. The base staff turned out to be just as clueless as the agents, which meant despite now having over seven hundred Denizens on his side, he still didn’t know where they were or who these people’s bosses were.

He certainly didn’t know who Management was.

What he did know, however, was where the memories were being kept.

With the help of the converted staff, they’d mapped out more and more of the base, finding only a few heavily restricted areas they weren’t sure what contained.

The Memory Room was on the opposite side of the base from the Mission Room. The Mission Room was somewhere they hadn’t been to yet. Whenever it was mentioned by agents or base staff, fear instantly started wafting off them. Xavier felt hints of that fear in his own mind—it wasn’t his own fear; it was the layer of Alica’s memories he’d placed over his consciousness.

No one wanted to face Striker.

Xavier wasn’t yet sure if the fear was rational or not, but it was deeply embedded in their psyche. He didn’t believe the fear was warranted now that he had at least one hundred A Grade Denizens with erased contracts itching to get their old lives back and willing to do anything to achieve that goal. He couldn’t see how Striker would be any match for them.

But Striker had a direct link to Management. If anyone in this base had the ability to alert the outside world of what was happening here, it would be him. The Mission Room was also the most heavily guarded and protected room in the entire base.

The second most heavily guarded room in the base was the Memory Room.

That was where they were headed right now, still inside time dilation fields, still creating simulations to trick all the observation crystals he came across.

“So, who are you really?” Dybal asked him as they were one hall away from the Memory Room. “The mission brief said you were C Grade, but warned you were dangerous. That you’d taken out B Grades before. The way they’d laid out the trap we didn’t think there was any chance you’d survive.”

“Alica wanted to toy with you,” Lydad said. “She powered down her time dilation field, making it weak enough that you’d be faster.” The mage paused. “She wanted to see a glimpse of what you were made of.” He shut his eyes. “Guess she got more than she bargained for.”

“Erasing contracts, tricking those crystals, those drones—the notification said you were dead,” Dybal said, almost spitting the last word. “Yet here you are, walking through the base like you own it.”

Xavier’s lips quirked upward at one side. Like you own it. That was exactly what his plan had expanded toward. After killing the World Destroyer and making that declaration to the Old Monsters, he couldn’t let whoever had sent these agents to kill him off easy. A hundred sectors away from Orin wasn’t anywhere near the inners. If he didn’t make an example here, he would look vulnerable.

He was starting to wonder if making that declaration had been the smartest thing he’d ever done.

“Let’s just say I’m more than meets the eye,” Xavier replied. “But if you’re willing to follow me beyond this base… well, I can tell you a hell of a lot more then.” He raised his chin. “Not just tell you, either, but teach you.”

Dybal scoffed. “A C Grade teach us?” There was scorn in his voice, but something else, too, as he seemed to deflate after he said the words.

“Erasing System contracts is unheard of. It should be impossible,” Arrack said. “Anyone capable of that is worth listening to.” The big man inclined his head. “Whether I get my memories back or not, I’ll follow you, Breaker.”

Xavier blinked. “Breaker?”

Arrack straightened. “Breaker of contracts, breaker of chains. You have unbound us from our slavery.” The man shrugged. “If you don’t like the name—”

“Breaker…” Xavier tasted the word, then smiled. “No. I like it.”

Arrack smiled back, a hint of pride in the many-thousand-year-old Denizen.

They continued to the next hall. Xavier used a concealment spell to hide him from the observation crystals and tricked them with a simulation before having the others follow around the corner. This was a practiced manoeuvre now, after how many times they’d effectively executed it.

At the end of the hall was a heavily reinforced door. Like the walls, it was made of steel. And like the walls, it was layerd with runes. Powerful protective runes.

There was no guard in front of it. Why would there need to be, when everyone inside the base was contracted to obey? But just because there was no guard didn’t mean it would be easy to break through.

So far, Xavier had made his way through the base without having to break anything. He hadn’t tried to alter the runes in the walls. He hadn’t needed to get through a locked door. He’d simply moved, time dilation fields wrapped around him and the others, from one hall or room to another.

Examining the runes, he became instantly aware that there would be no way to alter them or break through that door without activating an alert. What he wasn’t sure of was how wide that alert would be.

Activating such an alert wouldn’t stop him from breaking through. He needed to get to the memory stores on the other side. This was half of everything he’d offered the Denizens he was trying to liberate.

But more than that, he needed to restore Palini’s memories.

The man had a wife. A family that stretched down generations. A life worth remembering—worth protecting.

Xavier wasn’t going to leave this place until his friend’s memories were restored. He was the whole reason Palini was in this mess to begin with. Management had taken Palini because of him. They’d kidnapped his family because of him.

They’d wiped his memories because of him.

Time to fix this.

Xavier walked straight up to the door. Palini, Dybal, Lydad, Arrack and twenty-one others were at his back, with hundreds more spread through the base ready to reinforce them if needed. He didn’t want to put all his forces—which was how he was thinking about them now—in one place if something went horribly wrong, but they would filter through here on his command.

“Need help?” Arrack asked with a white-knuckled grip around his staff and a hard stare at the reinforced door.

Xavier shook his head. He stepped up to the door and placed a hand on it. Closing his eyes, he pushed his spiritual sense as far as it could go, reading the many different runes. Eyes still closed, he smiled. Thin lines of Celestial Energy seeped from the pores in his fingers and pushed through the layers of steel in the door, mingling with the runes inside.

In the past, he would have had to make such alterations with his Strike Out spell and his soul bound weapon in the form of a stylus. Ever since he’d been able to manifest spell patterns inside his body, however, he’d started experimenting with other ways to create runes outside his body.

The stylus was nothing more than a tool to concentrate energy. It turned out that if he harnessed his focus and strengthened his control over the Celestial Energy that left his body, using a stylus became absolutely unnecessary.

Xavier altered one rune after another until he heard several loud clicks through the layered steel.

At the same time, he felt the runes he couldn’t reach on the far side of the door activate—runes that he wasn’t able to manipulate because of the protections in the way. The runes were able to sense when the other runes were manipulated or changed. When they activated, they set off an alert.

Even with that alert set off, Xavier still had the advantage of being inside a time dilation field.

He opened his eyes and released a sigh as the heavy door swung forward on silent-smooth hinges.

His eyes widened as he saw what lay on the other side.

Hundreds of different shelves with orbs of white light resting on them—orbs he’d seen and even created before. Those orbs were essentially balls of memories.

And they were all stored here.

But there was another problem. The Memory Room—and room was selling the place a little short, as it was the size of a large warehouse with a ceiling that stretched at least fifty-feet high—wasn’t deserted. There were Denizens inside.

Guards. At least a hundred of them.

Xavier had known about the guards. He’d known about the guns on the walls, too.

The staff he’d converted had told him about it, as some of them had set eyes on the inside of the room in the past.

What he hadn’t known was what happened when that alert was activated.

As the door had swung wide, a time dilation field had formed over the entire room. He’d felt it coalesce.

And it was stronger than his own.

Which meant those magic-infused guns weren’t frozen in time like they were supposed to be, and neither were the guards. The time mage responsible for the time dilation field stood at the very back of the massive Memory Room. Xavier rapidly probed the man—a demonkin with short, stubby red horns and purple robes—and found out how the time dilation field could be more powerful than his own.

They were A Grade.

Every single one of the guards in the room was.

And at the far end of the room, behind the time mage, another door opened, one he hadn’t known was there. Xavier saw what lay on the other side.

Reinforcements.
