As he got closer to the mill, he sensed auras and soon spotted several manned outposts.

Crouched in scrub, Rex glanced down at his gloved hands, realizing that they did indeed have a faint pattern over them that matched his surroundings.

Huh, my Ambusher Feat is actually pretty sick.

It was less easy to tell how much it helped dampen his aura, but considering the lookouts appeared completely oblivious to his presence, while he could easily see them, was reassuring.

They were just standing there, open targets ready to be struck. But he restrained himself. Attacking now could alarm the camp, so he skirted around, scoping out each of the lookouts and patrol paths.

Gathered around the river, there were several large buildings, but more interestingly, a wooden palisade that joined them all together. But instead of being used as a wall around the buildings, the palisades created a massive courtyard between them.

He kept going, and by the time he circled the entire camp, he counted a dozen guards between lookouts and patrols, which was honestly more than he had expected, especially since he had already seen several people around the buildings. Not only that, but he had yet to spot any of the hunters he recognized.

But that didn’t stop him from concocting attack plans, as utterly insane as that was. He had to remind himself that he was still only level 7, and if he did end up getting into a fight, he needed to make sure that it was very much on his terms.

Concentrating on restraining his aura further, Rex crept closer. He wanted to try to get an accurate count before he decided how to go about things.

As he got closer to the mill, he spotted a group of people unloading wagons and carrying stuff into what looked like a large barn beside the river.

That’s a sack of corn. And that guy is carrying tomatoes. So, that barn must be where they’re storing their food. Good to know.

A moment later, he spotted someone coming out of the distillery to order the recruits around.

Wait, that’s Freyja!

Unfortunately, he didn’t spot his bow on her. But that didn’t mean it had been destroyed. She didn’t appear to be an archer, so she had no reason to lug it around.

Interesting. It seems that, for whatever reason, Freyja was hiding her true identity; she’s given up on that now. That’s one of Mulvaney’s men, but where are the rest of them? He thought as he spotted one of the remaining hunters walking beside Freyja.

As he watched her, Freyja turned her gaze directly at him. It didn’t seem like she had spotted him, but it freaked him the fuck out.

Carefully, but quickly, he backstepped into the brush behind him, and the moment he had some distance, he picked up his pace. He had felt something probing him, as if trying to detect his location.

Whatever her deal is, she’s sure got a knack for sensing auras.

Rex wished he had gotten more time to scope their base out, but he had a pretty good idea of how many there were and the layout of the base. However, he really wanted to know what was behind the palisades.

But he had to do it safely.

On his way back, he walked around the palisade but didn’t notice any obvious opportunities. He could have used a tree to climb over, but doing so would put him in a riskier situation, and he didn’t want to use a plan that might result in abandoning Slimer inside.

Rex had learned from his previous battle at the farm. Even though things had worked out for him, the reality was that he had taken an unnecessary risk. If he kept doing things like that, sooner or later, they would catch up with him. He couldn’t make a habit of running into burning buildings to save Slimer.

For now, he decided to head back to Andy and Ernie.

“So, did you come up with a plan?” Andy asked.

“It’s probably more accurate to say that I’m coming up with a plan,” Rex smiled.

“You know, we don’t have to attack,” Ernie said, but he sounded weak. “We can just keep walking.”

“Bad idea. Especially in your current state.”

“So, what do we do?” Andy asked.

Rex remembered his backup plan for his meeting with Freyja. He could do something similar, building traps throughout the forest. But there was a problem. The more mana threads he held onto, the greater the mana drain would be. And he’d need a lot of traps to turn the odds in their favor, considering how badly outnumbered they were.

Not to mention how long it would take, and the risk of someone spotting him while he worked.

He wished there were monsters around like the goblin camp. That had been a good trick, but he couldn’t sense anything, and luring something from so far away was not practical.

“Unfortunately, there’s no easy way to go about this. We could take out their lookouts, but with a bigger plan, I don’t think that’s a good idea. Sooner or later, they would realize what’s happening and do something about it. And the last thing we need is for them to be on even higher alert. They’re already clearly worried about something, but hopefully, they think we’re dead.”

“So?” Andy said.

“Well, I do have one idea I’ve been throwing around. I noticed that they were storing all of their food in the same building. It’d be risky, but if I could get close to it, I could set it ablaze with Slimer. Without food, they'd be forced to hunt sooner or later. And out in the forest, it would be a lot easier to pick them off.”

“Man, you really are insane,” Andy said. “You want to hunt them all down?”

Rex shrugged. “I mean, I don’t see any other options. Besides, Ernie doesn’t seem to be getting better. We need some medicine, and there’s nothing to loot at the farm. Unless you know herbs…”

Andy slumped.

“And with my new trusty jar, bringing Slimer with me is easy.”

“Oww, man. I don’t like this,” Andy sighed. “But I suppose it could work. What about Slimer, though? You want to risk him again?”

Ernie had fallen asleep again and was breathing heavily.

“He’s survived okay so far. And if the plan works out, he’ll get plenty of food to fill himself up with. Besides, look at him. He isn’t good. And it’s not like I’ll be able to creep through the forest holding a torch.”

“Fine. For Ernie, then. Go give them the hells, Rex,” Andy said, patting him on the shoulder.

He made his way back around to the storage barn and waited until the auras he sensed had moved away, and then closed in the last bit of distance.

Rex prepared himself, grabbing a rock and marking it with his spellcircle. He had already summoned a new Mystic Arrow, but wanted to keep that for backup.

Then, he made his way up to the barn and shook the jar atop a boulder. The elevation meant that Slimer was closer to the thatch roofing, and he quickly ran back, taking cover behind a tree before Slimer could roll out of position.

“Forgive me!” He muttered and threw the rock.

He saw the light flashing outward as he rolled back behind the tree, and felt the heat and explosive force burst out even as he took cover.

The moment the fire had passed, he sprang out, running toward the scattered bits of slime. Spotting a wiggling bit, he scooped Slimer up into the jar and got running.

It wasn’t until he had created some distance that he even looked back to verify the damage. As he suspected, the hard timber walls took the blast quite well, but the flames were already quickly spreading across the thatch eaves, and he could see lines of fire spreading across the roof.

Looks like it worked.

Shouting was already filling the air, and when he crept around, he spotted several recruits taking buckets from the river.

But that wasn’t all. Two fires were apparently too much of a coincidence, and he spotted a couple of patrols charging out into the surrounding forest in search of whoever caused it.

Good luck, bozos.

They could try, but under the cover of brush, he was hard to spot. And he was already moving away from them, easily able to sense the patrols and manoeuvre around them.

He kept his head low and only walked when the path was clear. And a moment later, an explosion at the distillery wrestled back their attention.

“Shit! Forget this! If the benefactor returns and everything is destroyed, we’ll be to blame!”

Several patrols quickly turned around and headed back to fight the fire before it burned everything.

Maybe I do need a fire spell. Causing pyromaniac mayhem is kinda fun.

Andy was on high alert when he got back.

“That you, Rex? I can feel their auras from here.”

“Yeah, they’re pissed,” Rex said with a massive grin.

“So, it worked?”

“Yeah, it looked like the fire was spreading, too.”

“Whew,” Andy wiped his brow. “That is a relief.”

“Yeah. I think it’s best we move camp a little further back, and then in the morning, I’ll go assess.”

Morning came around; Ernie was looking as pale as ever, seemed delirious, and was breathing heavily.

“We need to do something, Rex,” Andy said as he tried to feed Ernie.

“Yeah, I know. Keep him safe. I’ll do what I can.”

Rex felt a little guilty not prioritizing medicine for Ernie, but considering their situation, they didn’t have much choice. With Freyja around, sneaking too close to the mill was dangerous. At least the fire should have caused a good deal of chaos, chaos he could exploit.

And he didn’t have to go far before he found an opportunity. Patrols were out, no doubt looking for whoever did the damage. But they weren’t stupid. They kept to a formation, each within line of sight of one another. He could easily set up a trap and take one or two out, but others would see, and he only had a single arrow.

But Rex was patient. His near-death incidents had taught him to be. And he carefully spied and crept around, following them as they moved through the woods.

And soon, they made a mistake. Two men veered away from the others, disappearing down a shallow gully as they searched a second part of the forest.

Perfect targets.

Even better, one of them had a bow, and more importantly, a full quiver.

Still, he had to be careful. With only one arrow, he needed to perfectly plan his ambush, especially since Slimer needed to feed before he could be used again.

Of course, he could use  [ Transference ] with Andy, and perhaps he would later. But they couldn’t leave Ernie alone in his current state.

Adding to the difficulty, the men weren’t keeping to a predictable path. They kind of meandered all over, pushing bushes with their machetes and kicking at the ground.

Rex decided that the most reliable way to catch them in his trap was to simply create a line of [ Corrosive Touch ] spellcircles along the ground. He couldn’t guarantee they would step on any individual spellcircle, but with enough, it would work.

Also, it didn’t actually use too much mana to create a spellcircle he didn’t activate, and as long as he didn’t hold onto the mana threads for too long, their drain would be minimal.

It didn’t take long for one of them to step on a spellcircle, and Rex activated his mana link immediately. The man shouted in agony as acid melted the soles of his boots. As the man bounced on one foot, gripping the other, an arrow whizzed past, catching his companion in the face.

They didn’t stand a chance. The man was still gripping the remnants of his boot and partially melted foot as Rex came rushing through the brush. The man looked up, and his eyes widened as his hand reached out, palm pressing against his face, and [ Corrosive Touch ] activated.

The man fell to the ground, and Rex immediately rushed to the corpses, searching for medicine and grabbing the quiver and arrows.

The shouting hadn’t been ideal, and he could already feel a few auras nearby spark up. They had no doubt heard the commotion and wouldn’t be far away.

Shit, no medicine.

He quickly grabbed anything of value and sped away, vanishing back into the forest, and taking steadying breaths as he fought to strain his aura.

Experience rewarded for killing multiple people.

Borshan, the God of Murder, smiles upon you!

Great, Borshan again.

He could already tell that the patrols didn’t know where he was, as they aimlessly thrashed through the forest.

Scattered over a small distance, he could sense six of them. If they were all weak, he figured he might be able to take them. But there was no need to rush. They didn’t know where he was, and he could safely recover his spent mana.

And as he crept around, trying to decide on his next target, he spotted a few more.

Wait, aren’t they recruits?

Rex paused. This complicated things. He felt no empathy for killing the Credo’s thugs, but what if the recruits were like him or Andy?
