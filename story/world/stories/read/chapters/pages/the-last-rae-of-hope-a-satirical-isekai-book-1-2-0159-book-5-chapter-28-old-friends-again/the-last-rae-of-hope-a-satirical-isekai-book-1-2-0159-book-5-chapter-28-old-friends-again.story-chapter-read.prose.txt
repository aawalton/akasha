Before I could find Aurum to blunder through an impromptu, self-led cavalry lesson, Nora, Tetora, and Vernie returned with an extra member.

“Vulture?” I blurted the moment Vernie’s ex-husband appeared. My gaze also met hers, only to force her to turn away from me.

Probably not her choice? Nora’s, then?

The tall, lanky man bobbed his head in acknowledgment, his long, dark bangs falling over his eyes. “Captain.”

He set down a heavy canvas bag decorated with tiny pockets.

“What’s—”

“Gemstones,” he replied. “More are on the way.”

Gemstones?

Tetora caught sight of my recent experiment. “A saber?”

“Ahh…” I replied, fumbling with Will a bit. “Turns out the sword can change shape, too! It’s just that um, the sheath’s still just a sheath so—”

Will changed back with a shimmer and tugged himself toward the sheath, as if his demonstration were over.

“Ah. Well, I guess I’ll just have to hold off on mounted saber work…” I murmured as I returned Will to my side, though my pleading eyes were still on Tetora.

Tetora’s ears went flat. “I can help with the saber part, but you’ll need a different teacher for mounted combat.” He looked at the floor for a moment before he continued. “I can think of a few people who might be able to help—if we can find them.”

“Oh… Speaking of finding someone,” I said, turning to Nora. “Where’s Prince Mito right now?”

Nora blinked, gesturing at Vulture, who had moved on to staring wide-eyed at the bastion’s glowing runes. “Aren’t you going to ask what we were up to?”

“Well, yes, but… I think he’s in trouble. King Saulus passed on, and it was Prince Viktor who was riding through Amantia on Aurum’s back.”

Nora whistled, whipped out her phone, and began to swipe. “I need gemstones to counteract those ereborite jewels—I think, anyway. Vulture’s got the supply chain locked down on that, so…” She paused, then tapped the screen a few times. “Castle Amantia.”

“Huh?”

“That’s where Prince Mito is,” Nora insisted, as if I had known she suddenly switched context.

“No… The crowd said he’s missing, and Prince Viktor is to be crowned king in his absence.”

Vernie cleared her throat, finally speaking toward me. “Probably in the secret dungeon.”

“Secret dungeon?”

“The one under the normal one.” She put her hand to her hip. “Sub-basement.”

I started to chew my lip. “Any chance there’s an exit that doesn’t involve—”

“No. You’d have to get into the castle proper first.”

I sighed, realizing I had put the entire city on alert with my horse-thieving shenanigans. “Maybe it’s best to leave him be for now?”

Nora, however, continued to swipe and tap. “I don’t think so. I’m sure this is all a trap, but I think it’s a time-limited one.”

Vernie nodded. “He’s only alive to bait you in, but when Viktor becomes king, I’m sure he won’t want any loose ends alive.”

I scowled. “Can Galenus just drop us in?”

“No,” Nora replied. “No interior drops. Too imprecise. I don’t want to be embedded in a wall.”

“We’re going to need more information…” I muttered. “I don’t want to go in blind.”

“I think…” Nora said slowly, finally lowering the phone. “There’s a group preparing to rescue him. There’s a whole bunch of our Ecclesian acquaintances gathering up on the northeast side of the city.”

“Noble’s district,” Vernie added, then her eyes widened. “Cybill?!”

Nora blinked, then tapped vigorously. “Yes, her too! Priority flag!” She scowled. “It only lights up when you tap the person specifically. I wish I could enable notifications like this!”

“Add it to our list of corporate grievances,” I muttered, shifting my sword and shield. “Let’s go see what they’re up to.” I glanced at Vernie cautiously. “Girls… night out?”

Vernie looked at me for a moment, her lip quivering, before she turned to Nora. “Disguise pins?”

“I have three,” Nora said with a nod.

I blinked. “I thought we only had two left?”

Nora cleared her throat. “Tarek dropped a few off, along with some demons and dark mages. Recruitment’s going well among the displaced dark mage tower staff.”

I sat with that for a second, realizing there was more going on than I could pay attention to. “That’s Headmaster Nora for you.”

“After a few run-ins with angels, Tarek’s standard offer to join us has been going smoother,” she replied a bit evasively. “He hasn’t gotten any of the priests to join him yet, but there’s always hope they’ll see the light.”

He’s trying to recruit priests? Talk about ambitious!

“Let’s hope we can get Prince Mito to see it, too,” Vernie remarked. “I’m sure he’s in nothing but darkness now.”

***

Galenus deposited us at the edge of the nobles’ district with his customary efficiency, not even bothering to ask why we were going there. The cobblestone streets were quiet, lit only by the amber glow of the lamps that were provided to security to keep the peace. We walked without hurrying, passing by several armed guards who gave us only cursory glances. Vernie set the pace, a step ahead of us, her hands clasped behind her back. Every once in a while, I saw her fingers wiggle, and it seemed enough to tell the few people watching us we were supposed to be there.

“Why didn’t you tell him you were leaving?” Nora asked suddenly, in a voice that I felt should have been quieter. “It’s not like you to bypass a check-in.”

I kept quiet as I considered the fact that the small talk was probably the best disguise of all.

“He’ll figure it out.”

Vernie paused, then turned, looking over her shoulder. “You should talk to him sooner rather than later about what you’re mad about. Otherwise, it’ll just fester and grow the rift wider.”

I tripped over one of the cobblestones. “You… Of all people! That’s your advice?!”

Rich, coming from you.

Vernie huffed, waving her arms as she subtly passed on yet another sign that seemed to be accepted by a few watchers on the street. “Nora told me about what happened after… I left. I still think he’s…” She trailed off several times, struggling to start her next sentence. “B-but don’t end up like Vulture and me. Talk it out… Rae-Rae. Even if he doesn’t deserve it, you do.”

Okay, you may know something.

I glanced sideways at one of the lamp-laden private soldiers, who couldn’t have looked more disinterested in our conversation about relationships.

“Later,” I eventually muttered in reply, knowing she was right about this one.

Vernie continued down a few streets, each one narrowing slightly. The last one turned out to be the main path to Cybill’s mansion. A small but stylish wrought-iron gate waited for us, along with two guards who instantly recognized Vernie the moment she pulled off her pin. Within moments, we were ushered into the dark stone building inside as the passphrase “crow’s feet” passed across several servants’ lips.

“There are so many better phrases that old hag could have used to describe me,” Vernie muttered darkly.

A bright lamp poked out around a hallway corner. “I knew that one would be most memorable,” Cybill declared, holding out the light, her heavy wool dress whirling as she turned. “Follow me.”

The central room she led us to was awash with candlelight. Several familiar people were already there, poring over a map of the castle that lay on a large, round table.

Prince Mito’s theatre troupe! They’re all here!

“Well, which two red monkeys did you bring with you?” Cybill asked. “I don’t recognize either.”

Vernie laughed and gestured for us to remove our pins. Several gasps greeted us as we shed our disguises, but Cybill herself seemed unsurprised.

“You caused quite a stir stealing back that horse, you know,” Cybill advised, a frown creasing her brow. “You’re going to need to be considerably more subtle when you go to retrieve Prince Mito from the dungeon.”

“You saw that?” I asked mildly, running my hand through my hair.

“Half the city saw that,” she replied crisply before pausing. “I’ll admit it, though. It did lift my spirits a bit.”

“How’d you know Prince Mito was in trouble?” Nora asked.

“I’ve been harboring a criminal of fair repute.” Cybill pointed, and a large harumph came from a dark corner of the room.

“Upstaged again.”

“Emmy!” I cried, recognizing my doppleganger. “You’re alright!”

“Of course I’m alright! Even the chaos in Chairo couldn’t kill me!” she declared, even as she rushed in for a hug. “But your hair! It’s… It’s…”

She was leaving a lot out of the conversation, but she probably had her reasons.

“You don’t have to try so hard. I know it’s different.”

She hugged me fiercely. “It suits you, but not me.” She then shook her head. “I’ve decided I’m no longer going to try to fill your role.” She coughed. “You… You will get him back for me, though?”

“I’ll give it my all,” I promised.

“Then you’d better pick your dancing partner,” Cybill declared. “Prince Viktor’s coronation ball tomorrow night is your best way in. I’m sure you’ll find your way out.”

“My dancing partner?!”

Nora and Vernie both gave me a wicked look.

“Oh, we know just who to send her off with,” Nora replied knowingly. “Stealing her horse back was just practice for our upcoming royal robbery.”

I bristled. “...Absolutely not.”

“You just said you’d give it your all,” Vernie reminded me pleasantly.

“To Emmy! I said that to Emmy!”

Emmy, for her part, looked away and began studying the map with greatly feigned interest.

“I’ll work on your gown,” Cybill said with a dreadful smile, as if everything had already been decided. “Do be a dear and swing by to grab it on your way tomorrow.”
