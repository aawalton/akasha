“I don’t think I like this plan.” I couldn’t help but lament.

“Why?” Cerise asked.

“Because I am a damn chauffeur. Why is this guy driving so slow? Move, move.”

—At least you ain’t got it as bad as me. Complained Toby through the intercom.

The sun had already set, and the city bristled with neon. Our plan to infiltrate Phoenix Guild was in full action.

Cerise would attend the party, try to get near the Guildmaster, and hack his phone—which she couldn’t do remotely because it was isolated—to get access to the special floors.

Meanwhile, I, disguised as a chauffeur, would get into the waiting area, sneak out and open the hatch for Toby, who was sneaking in through the sewer.

Why?

Because Cerise said you needed to secure an escape route before infiltrating somewhere.

Which I totally agreed with, but Toby disliked it for the obvious reason.

After that me and Toby would go to the hidden floor, get Tina—who we hoped was there—and escape back through the sewer.

“You guys are complaining too much.”

Cerise rubbed her lips together before closing her pocket mirror. She was dressed in a beautiful red dress with bracelets and a necklace, her hair cascaded down her back. She looked like an elegant lady from a rich household, which she actually was but had never once given the vibe of.

Except today.

“Just do your job properly. We go in, grab Tina, and get out. Then it’s clearing and closing the Rifts back to back.”

“Easy for you to say,” I mumbled. “You’re just gonna stay inside and drink some wine while we do all the work.”

“I would very much like to switch places with you. Sneaking past the guards sounds more fun than being surrounded by men who want to court me just to get to the Guild, while I have to control myself so I don’t punch them in the face and antagonize Father.”

“Are you actually afraid of angering your father?” I made a joke out of it. “I thought your anger was the same for everyone.”

I looked at her through the rear-view mirror.

Cerise’s face went through a myriad of expressions in a second. She was angry, sad, anxious, afraid, happy, nostalgic.

She wasn’t good at hiding her expressions.

She kicked the back of my seat.

“Focus on the road, chauffeur.”

“Damn, Cerise. Don’t kick.”

“Call me ma’am, or people will get suspicious.”

“I am not calling you ma’am.”

“Do you want us to fail? Remember, it was you who sent Tina there.”

Guilt-tripping me into this is not fair.

“We’re here.”

I stopped the car when we reached the front of the Phoenix Guild.

“The door, chauffeur.”

Damn her.

I got out of the car, walked to the back, and opened the door for her.

She stepped out, her handbag dangling in her hand.

Everyone present was stunned by her.

“Cerise, I did not expect to see you here today.”

A man in a white suit who had also come to attend the party and was just about to head inside came back to greet Cerise.

I recognised him as Palik, a Silver-rank Walker of the Raven Guild and Rank 88 on the Top 100 Silver Walkers list in Walker Magazine.

“Hmm, not even a greeting or something?”

Cerise stared at him but didn’t say a word.

“Chauffeur, park the car. Then wait for me.”

Instead, she looked at me, excited to finally hear the word from my mouth.

“Yes… ma’am.”

Delighted, she turned and walked toward the door, her heels clacking against the pristine marble.

I will totally get back at her for this.

I turned around and pulled open the driver’s-side door. Suddenly, it became harder to move and slammed back shut.

I noticed a hand with white sleeves resting on top of the door.

“Hey,” Palik said. “Tell me where you picked Cerise up from, and I will pay you handsomely.”

So that’s what this was about.

“Her house,” I answered bluntly and pulled the door again.

It shut.

“Smart,” he said, raising his eyebrows tauntingly. “Where is her house?”

“On the ground.”

I pulled the door again.

It shut.

“Don’t try acting smart with me. Just tell me where she lives and get paid. Otherwise, I have other ways to get that information out of you, and you wouldn’t like any of them.”

Hah. Haven’t I heard that line before?

“Why don’t you ask her yourself? Oh, wait. You can’t, because she talks more to me than she does to you.”

“You—”

A long, loud horn interrupted whatever Palik was about to say.

He finally noticed my hand on the steering wheel.

“Do you want to make a drama here? Everyone’s looking.”

Palik looked around. Everyone’s attention was on us because of the honk.

“You,” Palik snarled. “You’d better hope I don’t see that stupid mug of yours again, chauffeur.”

He shut the door once again and walked inside with heavy steps.

You better not show me your mug, Rank 88.

“Who does this car belong to?” the guard asked.

“Miss Cerise Truman,” I answered.

He typed something into his tablet. A second later, he looked up.

“Did she come separately from her parents?”

“Yeah. You know how she is if you see the news at all.”

“Oh, yeah, I have. The articles about how her father doesn’t let her into any of the diving squads because he dotes on her so much.”

“I don’t think that is the case here.”

“Ohh, some insider news. Tell me. I’m all for the news.”

A chance to get back at her. Hehe.

“Promise you won’t tell anyone.”

“Even Oran won’t be able to get it out of me.”

Oran was a Gold-rank Walker with some kind of Skillcard that let him know what people were thinking.

Well, that was the rumor, at least.

I chuckled.

“Come closer.”

The guard brought his ear a little closer.

“She lives with her boyfriend, and her father is against it.”

“Whoa. How interesting. Do you know who her boyfriend is? Must be some popular Walker.”

“Sorry, but I do not know.”

The guard’s face visibly saddened at the lack of tea.

“What’s taking so long?” someone from behind my car shouted. There was a line to get into the parking lot and then go chilling in the waiting room.

“Just a second!” the guard shouted.

“Hey, do tell me if you find something else on this.”

“Will do.”

“Go on ahead, then.”

He hit the top of the car twice with his knuckles, then backed away.

I gave him a slight nod and drove further in.

I locked the car and stretched my back.

“Time to go to that waiting room and stuff myself with those snacks.”

—Hey, where are you? I am almost here. Toby said through the intercom.

Ah, man.

“Will be there before you,” I answered.

“Cerise.”

—Yeah?

Her voice was muffled. She must have been at the corner of a hall, hiding her mouth to talk with me right now.

“Take care of the cameras, please.”

—You ready? Only one chance and five minutes.

“Yeah.”

I channeled my Magicka and focused on Shadowstride.

—The cameras are down.

I vanished.
