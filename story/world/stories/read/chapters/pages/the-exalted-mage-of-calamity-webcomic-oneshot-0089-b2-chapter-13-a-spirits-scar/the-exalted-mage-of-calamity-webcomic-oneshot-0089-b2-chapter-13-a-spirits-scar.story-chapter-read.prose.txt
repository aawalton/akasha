The nightmare Veronica had entered was nothing like what she expected. Unlike hers or Gwen’s, Dusk’s was far more… lonely. It wasn’t despair so much as melancholy. Even as a spectator, she couldn’t help but feel for the boy.

“How much longer until I see Selene?” Dusk asked.

He sat inside a metal cage hidden beneath heavy cloth coverings on a wagon, transported like valuable cargo. He wasn’t allowed sunlight, and the only food given to him were small pieces of stale bread. A bucket had been provided for waste, though it did not take long for the men organizing his transport to realize it was unnecessary.

While Dusk looked human, he wasn’t one.

“Yeah, yeah. We’ll be there soon,” one of the men outside replied from the coach seat. “Just wait quietly. The more you talk, the longer it takes to get there.”

It was a ridiculous excuse. The man claimed it was part of human culture, that talking during journeys slowed travel.

Naive as he was, Dusk believed every word. He couldn’t risk doing anything that might stop him from meeting Selene.

The transport group consisted of five people.

Three rode alongside the wagon on horseback, all dressed in dark rogue equipment. The wagon itself was mana-powered and required no horses to pull it. Sitting at the front were two others: a merchant who was in control of the operation, and a dangerous-looking man.

He was large with a bulky build. His muscles resembled those of a bear. A trimmed beard covered his jaw, and one hand constantly rested near the greatsword at his side.

From what Veronica could gather, four of them belonged to a mercenary company hired to guard the caravan. The merchant himself wasn’t connected to them, but he was overseeing Dusk’s transport and eventual sale to whoever the buyers were.

Veronica sat atop the wagon roof directly above where Dusk was being held. Her body remained ethereal, capable of phasing through anything she wished. No matter how much she tried interacting with the world around her, nothing happened. She was nothing more than a ghost forced to watch events unfold.

Still… something about all of this felt strange.

If everything she saw was part of Dusk’s memory, then how was she witnessing any of this? Dusk himself was locked inside a cage beneath heavy tarps. When Veronica phased inside earlier to check on him, she had been greeted by near-total darkness. The only visible light came from the faint glow of her own translucent body.

And yet, even now, she could float high above the caravan and clearly see every member of the group.

The range of the dream was enormous too. Roughly half a kilometer in every direction. She could fly through the air and observe everything within that massive space.

That shouldn’t have been possible.

Unless Dusk possessed some way of perceiving the world beyond normal sight.

A spiritual sense, perhaps?

Fortunately, Sage informed her that time inside the nightmare moved significantly slower than outside. Hours within the dream translated to only minutes in reality. Sage couldn’t determine an exact conversion, however, since it seemed to fluctuate based on Dusk’s mental state.

[Memory Fragment found. Initiating warp.]

Veronica sat up slightly, floating higher into the air above the caravan.

Each time Sage found a more recent memory fragment, it linked her to that point in Dusk’s memory. Eventually, she would catch up to his consciousness and reach the nightmare itself.

“The world really did look quite different a thousand years ago,” Veronica murmured.

From everything she had gathered, this memory dreamscape took place roughly a thousand years ago. Long before the Valley of Silence had been mysteriously destroyed and given its current name.

Dusk was being transported somewhere far north, well beyond the valley’s boundary and nearing Vitian’s northern border. Using contextual clues from their speech and the environment around her, Veronica concluded that they had been transporting Dusk out of the valley for a little over five months. They were traveling along a lengthy dirt road in the middle of nowhere.

The world around her flashed white as the scenery changed.

As the memory settled, Veronica immediately realized where they were.

“This…”

It was true. They really were bringing him north.

Veronica looked around. The wagon was still there, but all of the mercenaries, including the merchant, were gathered farther ahead, speaking to another group.

Then her eyes widened as she focused on them.

That was impossible. No one in her time had seen one before. And yet, she was staring at eight of them.

“As…varians?” she whispered, shocked.

They were an ancient civilization that existed only in some of the oldest history books. Far older than any known kingdom or nation still surviving in the world. They were masters of using and manipulating different energies, which earned them the title of the magic empire. Their civilization had created many known foundations of magic by harnessing the world’s natural energies and studying the powers of other creatures and mythical beasts.

The only reason Veronica recognized them was because Vitian’s magic had been derived from many of the records they left behind. Records and logs older than Vitian’s earliest founding king.

Aside from that, their appearance was immediately recognizable.

Asvarians wore gold-plated clothing. Their iconic armor featured sharp shoulder plates that pointed outward. Each Asvarian also wore the necklace of their nation, a requirement to be considered a citizen: an eleven-pointed star of black and gold.

“But… why would Asvarians want Dusk? Unless…”

She frowned.

While they were considered her magical ancestors, masters of magic worthy of reverence, they were also a ruthless people. Anything that caught their eye was immediately coveted. If they didn’t understand something, they took it back to study.

That included live experimentation.

She had heard old stories and fables that Nidhogg had once been one of their targets millennia ago, but due to his role in the world, they chose to spare him. After all, without Nidhogg, the world would be destroyed by the overabundance of life provided by Yggdrasil.

A destroyed world was no place for further research.

Veronica hovered lower to listen to their conversation.

“is kœr nàsh zeǁ g’œ hàzzy?”

“Yes. He is inside a cage,” the merchant replied after a moment.

He turned around, peeking toward the wagon. It seemed as if he was judging whether he was far enough away from Dusk’s ears.

However, considering Veronica could hear him clearly, she knew that wasn’t the case at all.

“He still thinks we are bringing him to meet Selene.”

“ẓœk sheg’” the Asvarian said.

The merchant furrowed his brows, frowning. “I apologize, but I don’t know what that means. I’m not entirely caught up on your language yet.”

The Asvarian man’s expression didn’t change. Instead, he simply lifted one hand and called forth a woman at his side.

Another Asvarian stepped forward, one with pale golden hair tied behind her head and pinned with several golden ornaments. Unlike the man, this woman spoke in the common tongue, though each word carried a strange accent.

“He said, good. Has there been any trouble?” she translated.

The merchant’s frown shifted into a smile upon hearing her words. “Ah, of course not. He has been cooperative so far. No resistance or trouble. He is quite eager to meet that spirit, Selene.”

“Yes. Those two shall be… reunited,” she said slowly.

Her gaze shifted past the merchant and toward the covered wagon. Her eyes narrowed slightly, as if she could see through the layers of cloth and metal.

“Where is the second?”

The merchant paused and stiffened.

Veronica’s own gaze sharpened as she stood beside them, listening.

“You mentioned there were two spirits. A brother and sister,” the woman continued. “You have one.”

The merchant laughed nervously, rubbing the back of his neck. “The boy mentioned he had a sister. However, we waited at the sanctuary for an additional month. According to the boy, he told her specifically to stay behind and not come out.”

“Why did you not have him return and bring her along?”

He shook his head. “He said he can’t traverse realms that easily. He was hoping to find Selene, and that she would be the one to take them back.”

The Asvarian woman’s eyes thinned. She then turned and spoke to the previous Asvarian man. It was clear now that he was some kind of representative, or at least someone with significant standing within their group.

“Ah!” the merchant said quickly. “We are keeping an eye on the place, just in case she does come through. If that happens, we’ll have another transport ready.”

The merchant turned toward the bulky mercenary leader. “Bring me the communication crystal.”

After a moment, the man returned holding a white crystal orb. It seemed much older than any communication crystal Veronica had seen before. An earlier design, perhaps.

“Our communication crystals don’t hold much power, and they lose their charge during travel. I was hoping you could provide some mana to charge it. We can ask the others if she has appeared yet.”

The Asvarian woman translated the merchant’s words to the man. A moment later, he raised his hand, lifting the communication crystal from the merchant’s grasp into his own.

He poured mana into it, causing the crystal to shine a bright white. Then he sent the crystal back to the merchant.

“Thank you, Sir Treejk.” The merchant activated the crystal. After waiting roughly ten seconds, the link connected.

“This is Raune,” the merchant announced. “Has there been any news of—”

“Raune! I’ve been trying to contact you for nearly a month now!” the other voice interrupted.

It sounded like someone in his late twenties, his voice edged with panic.

“What?” Raune replied. “What happened?”

“That other spirit. The one the boy mentioned. She descended.”

Raune’s eyes lit up. The Asvarian woman turned at the same moment, translating for their leader.

“That’s great! Have you already prepared the transport to—”

“No! This isn’t great!” the man shouted, cutting him off. “Everybody’s dead! The Valley is gone!”

The merchant’s elated expression immediately collapsed. The Asvarian woman also stopped translating midway through, staring back at the communication crystal.

“Dead? What do you mean, dead?” Raune asked.

“I mean dead!” the man snapped. His breathing came through harshly over the crystal. “The mercenaries are gone. The watchers are gone. The entire village is gone. The valley itself is… I don’t even know how to explain it.”

Raune’s face paled. “Slow down. What happened?”

“I don’t know! She came out just like the boy said she would. A little girl with blonde hair. We followed your instructions. We tried to lure her in gently. She was asking for the boy.”

Raune’s gaze flicked toward the wagon.

Inside, the cage shifted.

Dusk had heard.

The man on the other end continued, his voice shaking. “But she wouldn’t believe us. I think she overheard one of the men talking about handing them over to those strange Asvarians. Then something happened near the sanctuary. The whole place lit up. There was this… this light. Not fire. Not mana. The entire valley shook, and then everything started collapsing. The trees withered in front of us. The ground split. People were screaming. I wasn’t at the sanctuary when it happened, but from the valley’s edge, I saw everything break apart like it was made of sand.”

What? This is how the Valley was destroyed? Not some fight between two powerhouses?

Veronica noticed the expression on everyone present change. Even the Asvarians were perturbed.

A loud shudder reverberated through the space. Veronica looked up and saw a large crack forming across the sky. Immediately, she looked toward the others, but no one seemed to notice it.

It was a crack in the memory.

“What happened to the girl?” Raune asked.

There was a brief pause from the other side. When the voice returned, it was lower. “I… don’t know.”

The merchant gritted his teeth. “What do you think happened?” he said, voice edged with annoyance.

“I think… after learning that her brother and that Goddess Selene had been taken away to be studied and used as materials, she realized we were lying. She didn’t trust us from the moment she descended. And after overhearing one of the mercenaries…”

His voice trembled. “She detonated.”

Veronica’s senses focused on the wagon. From within, she heard Dusk’s voice come out small and strained.

“Dawn…?”

“Detonated?” Raune repeated. “What nonsense are you speaking? How could a puny spirit child destroy an entire sanctuary?”

“It wasn’t just the sanctuary. The entire valley is gone. Even the village is in ruins. There’s nothing alive in there anymore.”

“A spirit can carry that much power,” the Asvarian woman said. “If she held even a fraction of Selene’s power, it is possible.”

Raune looked over at her.

“But if the girl is dead, then that is a shame,” she continued. “Having two specimens would have been preferable.”

Another crack split across the sky. Veronica looked around, noticing the world trembling around her.

Then the wagon exploded. The blast hammered the air with shards of debris, broken planks, and twisted metal.

Everyone turned toward Dusk. He floated slightly above the ground, his expression grim and hollowed by grief.

“What?!” Raune shouted. “You brat—” He spun toward the mercenaries. “What are you doing?! Grab him!”

All of the mercenaries grabbed their weapons. The other Asvarians also twitched, preparing to move.

However, the space around Dusk exploded in blinding light, kicking up debris and dust from the paved road.

Dusk’s form turned ethereal. Through the dust, a storm began to build, spiraling into a tornado. From within its eye, a bright light flashed, before streaks of purple began mixing in.

In that moment, the world changed and turned white.

Dusk was teleporting again, just as he had done before to cross boundaries.

[I have found Dusk’s consciousness. I am warping you to that fragment now.]

As the world reformed around her, Veronica found herself back at the sanctuary.

Except… things looked much closer to how they appeared in the physical world. There were no longer lush trees at the base, nor bright stone and flowers along the mountains of the valley.

No.

Everything was dead. Ashen. Decayed and decrepit.

It wasn’t as extreme as what she had seen in the present, but the similarity was obvious. In a thousand years, she could see how it would become that way.

Her eyes landed on the statue of Selene.

The statue was broken now. Earlier, when Dusk descended, it had still been whole and unblemished.

That wasn’t the case anymore.

And kneeling before the statue… was Dusk.

Veronica walked toward him. She had noticed it the moment the world loaded in around her.

The cracks were still in the sky.

Dusk was hurt. And his tears—they wouldn’t stop flowing.
