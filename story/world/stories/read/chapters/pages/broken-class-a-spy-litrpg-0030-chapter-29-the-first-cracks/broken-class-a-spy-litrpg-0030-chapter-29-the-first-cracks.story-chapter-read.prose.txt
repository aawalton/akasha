There were five of us in Rowler’s squad.

Tannis was our support mage. Sharp-eyed, sharper-tongued, and always two seconds away from rolling her eyes.

Brigg was the tank. Big, slow, and steady, like a mountain with a grudge.

Then came Kell, our loudmouth warrior with a scar down his jaw and more bravado than sense.

And me, Hark. The other blade in the mix. Quieter than Kell, but faster.

Rowler, of course, was the anchor. Our leader. Hard as stone, and twice as stubborn.

We had made camp just after dusk. The fire was already out, burned low while we talked, then smothered before full dark.

No fire now. Too risky. The forest around us was quiet, but not the kind of quiet you trust.

Kell volunteered for the first watch.

“Try not to fall asleep drooling on your blade again,” I muttered as I lay back.

“Please,” he snorted. “That was one time. And it was tactical drooling. Monster thought I was already dead.”

Tannis let out a dry chuckle. “You’d have better luck scaring monsters with your breath than your sword.”

Brigg grunted as he lay down, already half asleep. “Some of us need rest, not a comedy show.”

Kell just grinned and took up position near the base of the massive redwood we were camped beneath, half-hidden in its sprawling roots. He might joke like a fool, but when it counted, he knew how to hold a line.

We settled in. Sleep came slowly, the kind where every sound felt like a threat. But after a few minutes, the forest’s rhythms began to hum again. Leaves rustling. Insects chirping.

Then...

A low, guttural screech cut through the night.

“Up!” Kell’s voice cracked like a whip. “Monster incoming!”

We were trained. Tired or not, we moved.

Brigg grabbed his shield and snapped into a wall formation before his eyes were even fully open. I flanked left, Tannis behind us, already muttering a chant. Rowler barked something I couldn’t make out over the thudding of footsteps. But it was instinct by now.

Not even an hour of sleep, and something was already on us.
