Cigar smoke filled the air as I sat opposite Philpott.  The warlock was, for once, sensibly clothed in a tweed suit.  I assumed he was freeballing underneath the trousers as was his want, but my mind was largely focused on what an epic waste of time the last week had been.

All piled up on the shelves, it had looked like a lot, but when I had a moment to take a breath and check my storage space, I had been severely disappointed.  All that wealth amounted to less than a third of my current net worth.

Sitting in the comfort of the club room in the Bank, I was surrounded by opulence.  All the furniture was made from deep, rich coloured wood, velvet covers and fancy tapestries.  The handful of other people present were all dapper-looking and well-to-do.  In some ways, the Club was egalitarian.  Nobility didn’t count for anything; money talked, bloodlines walked.  And of all of them, I was the richest.

Thinking of myself as someone who had a net worth was a very strange thing.  That was my dad, back on Earth.  I was the lacklustre child who never amounted to anything.  Not that I’d had much of a chance, dying in my mid-twenties.  Now, though, I was one of the wealthiest beings in the world, as it should be, and raiding that bank had resulted in negligible gains after I split the loot as we’d agreed.

“A hundred and fifty million wasn’t worth my time, Philpott.  There’s a war against an extra-universal entity going on.  Said entity plans to rip this universe from reality and drop it into the Outside so it can be devoured by the nightmares that lurk there.”

He took a long pull on his cigar.  “You’re thinking short term, Bob,” he said as a cloud of smoke hid his face.  The leather chair creaked as he leaned back.  “You’ve basically won the civil war with that theft.”

“I’m not a thief.  That gold was mine to claim by right of being a dragon.”

“Potato tomato.  Lucratis has zero liquidity due to your admirable thoroughness in claiming other people's money because it’s obviously really your own.”

“Are you taking the piss?”  I gave him a grade-five glare.  I’d been neglecting my glare game recently, and it washed over him with no discernible effect.

“Not at all.  You turned three hundred million and change into a steaming pile of dragon poop.  That’s what’s been in all the papers in the city, by the way, an excellent fuck you to the authorities.  You made them look weak.  They can’t protect what’s theirs.”

“If we’d hung around any longer, they’d have thrown enough mages and immortals at me to kill me.  It wasn’t really a win. I…I ran away.”  That hurt to admit, my draconic pride was stung, but it was the truth.

“Not in the eyes of the public.  However close you think it was, you came, you saw, and you went away with all their money.  They couldn’t stop you, and now Lucratis can’t pay out on deposits.  You caused the single largest bank run in the Empire's history.”

“Which are historically not good for the common man and woman.  What you’re bragging about is that I just stole the life savings of thousands of peasants.”  I reached for my glass on the side table and took a long swig.  I’d stolen from my own people.  The draconic part of me had already decided everyone in the empire belonged to me.

While I had no objection to taxation, as long as it was being paid to me and I was exempt, it was only too easy to imagine how I’d feel if some bad actor, or Morris, got into my hoard room and made off with my precious shinies.

“You think the average citizen keeps their savings in the bank?  They have hidey holes or bags of silver under their mattresses.  The only people you really hurt were the nobles who don’t like you.  All the ones who do like you bank with us anyway.”  He smiled, something evil in the expression.  Nyal flicked from his left eye to his right, then did a wiggly little dance in Philpott’s sclera.

“I didn’t just shaft the common folk?” I asked.  Not that it bothered me.  Nuh uh.  I was a ruthless dragon.

“Only the rich pricks who already hated you.”  He shrugged.  “It couldn’t have gone better.  You’ve got the thieves guild in your pocket now.”

“Yeah, them,” I growled.

“They’re not bad eggs, Bob.  Sure, they steal stuff, which is against your scaly morals unless you do it.  You can see the hypocrisy, right?”

A wave of my aura washed out, causing the other residents to shift uncomfortably.  None of them looked over at us, even though they must have known it came from me.

“It’s different when I do it.”

“Of course it is.”

I chose to ignore the sarcasm.  “So it all worked out.  We nicked a pittance and nearly lost half the leadership of the Guild.  Now the people hate me, and I’m even further from uniting the empire so we can fight the Changer properly.”

“Hardly!  The people love you for it.  None of them likes the nobility.  Most of them are power-mad weirdos who abuse their position for personal… gratification.”

“Says Mr never-wears-underpants,” I snapped.

“I can’t buzz my wings to cool off in the mammal costume.  Surely you know what it’s like?” he objected.

“Not an issue for me.”

“You were a mammal before, though.  I was an insect, and we don’t adapt well to glands.”

“Or having a brain larger than a pinhead.”

“I can see you’re having a bad day—”

“The Changer broke out while we were dicking about robbing that bank.  He steamrolled the eastern flank of the knife ears and is now pushing towards the Empire.”

“You’ve noticed how many more elves are in the dungeon since you got back?” Philpott asked slyly.

“Yeah.  They’re running away rather than fighting, which is a big part of the problem.”

“Think of it as consolidation.  They're concentrating their forces, mustering their strength so that it's on hand when you’ve united the empire and the other races.  The advance has slowed since yesterday,” he pointed out, tapping the ash off his cigar.

“Only because—”

“Because it can’t be everywhere at once.  I’ve read the briefings from Kat, and I’m assuming you haven’t.”

“I skimmed them,” I said guiltily.

“Skimmed.  As bad as it might seem, we’ve got some time before we need to panic.  There was no way we could contain the thing forever.  The breakout was inevitable.”

“I haven’t even spoken to the giants yet.”

“Ask the stunties.  They can put you in touch.”

“Really?”

“Of course.  The mountain peoples all know each other, even if they don’t like the rest of them.”

“So what’s this plan of Denarios’?  You dragged me away from my shinies and Esme.  It had better be good.”

“Come on board as a director at the bank.  Invest more of your hoard, and we’ll control the imperial economy within a month.”  He licked his lips nervously.  Now that he’d gotten to his reason for the meeting, he seemed less demonic and much more nervous.

“No foreplay, huh?  Nope.  You’ve gotten as much as you’re going to get out of me.”

“But Booooobbbb…”

“You got two hundred million or something?  Almost as much as we stole from Lucratis.  And you’ve got your share of the half the Guild is getting from that haul.”

“It’s a good bump, but that half is being split a lot of ways.  There are bylaws for how we distribute that kind of score.  Urchins to pay off, infrastructure.  You saw the wardrobe webway, you think that’s cheap to maintain?”

“I think it’s insane, but par for the course for Helstat.  You could just get portals.  I’d do you a good price.”  I was lying about the last part, naturally.

“It’s a tradition.  Bertiflex the Arch Relocator set it up nearly a thousand years ago,” Philpott muttered.  “The point is I’m only going to get a few million, maybe ten if I’m lucky, as my share.  It’s not enough to give the Bank the oomph we need to ruin the House of Lucratis.”

“Considering you did bugger all to contribute, you’re getting out with more than you deserve.  Have you considered hitting them again?”

“What?”

“If you’ve got them on the ground, put the boot in, don’t piss on them.  That isn’t their only establishment; they used to have one in Fidler’s Mill.  Rob the rest.”

He blinked slowly, then scowled.  “Fine, I know that’s what you were saying from the start, Nyal.”  He focused on me.  “We could use the portal network.  Outside of Ankmapak, they won’t be able to respond as quickly, if they even notice.  We portal in, sneak into town and rob them blind, then disappear.  The on-hand deposits will be much smaller, but it won’t cost us anything, and the risks are much lower as well,” he said, stroking his chin with one hand.

“Have a word with Mrs Sanderson, tell her I’m authorising you to take along a minion with a bunch of portal stones.  You could hit half a dozen towns a night and be back here by dawn.  I’ll need further assurances, though.”

“That would—dammit Nyal!  Fine!”

A portal to hell ripped open next to his head, and the handful of others in the room all decided they suddenly had some urgent business elsewhere.

“Other people are so limiting.  They think things about you, form opinions, tie you down to a perception of what you ought to be, in their eternally humble opinion.”

“Nails down chalkboards are easier to listen to, Nyal.  I appreciate what you do, and question your sanity for binding yourself to this bug—”

“Oi!” Philpott objected.

“No offence.” I continued, ignoring the lesser member of the alliance, “But I have to live among the mortals.  I’m not a ruler in hell, I’m a draconic emperor on Earth.  Helstat,” I hastily corrected.

“Not yet, Bob.  But soon, if you listen to me.”

“I appreciate the heavenly and demonic assistance, for all the good it’s done me.”  I couldn’t hide the bitterness from my voice.  “Alright, spill it.  What stupid crap do you want me to do next?”

“You mean after you split the gold with the Guild and secure their allegiance for as long as you live?” Nyal asked.

“Yes.”  I physically hurt to say it.

“And after you’ve manufactured a bunch more immortality injections?”

“There’re a couple of dozen on standby already.  You cannot seriously expect me to part with them for less than half a billion each?  And that’s insanely discounted.  The more we make, the lower the price becomes.  No alchemist ever had a near-endless supply of dragon’s blood and unicorn horn before, so we’re breaking new ground nd smashing glass ceilings.”

“You’ll need to use them.”

“Piss of Nyal.  I’d rather die than piss away that much gold.”

“If you do use them, and keep making more, you’ll be Emperor of the world for a thousand years.”

“And I’d get all the taxes?”  Greed perked up in my mind.

“Yes.”

“And I’d be in charge of everything?”

“Yes.”

“And all I have to do is give up billions of gold worth of goods for the cause?”

“Yes—no wait!”

“Get wrecked, Nyal.  I’ll use those potions as I see fit.  By which I mean I’ll auction them off.  Lots of desperate rich bastards must be looking for the chance to have a second life in case things go sideways.  I smell profit, and taste gold.  The Thieves will get their share, Philpott.  But you idiots are not getting me to waste my immortality injections.”

“Was it a waste when you gave one to me?”  Esme asked from behind me.
