Within the first relentless minute of them fighting the two eagles, Henry noted a key difference from the last time he had faced a big powerful bird.

“Stay still you damn, over-sized… chicken!” Henry hissed between gritted teeth, barely dodging the bird of prey as he dipped below the sparse canopy. Then he pointed a finger and let out another Toxic Ray toward its back, only for the bird to easily barrel roll out of the way and smoothly transition into yet another swoop, screeching ferociously as it jutted its claws out, ready to skewer Henry. Then it blurred, and Henry immediately Blinked away, having already experienced the creature’s mind-boggling speed. Only the near instantaneous reaction saved him from having to heal whatever chunks the talons would have torn away. He’d already lost a head and a shoulder. He wasn’t too interested in starting over.

Good thing my main’s body’s tiny, he thought as he glared at the bird. Unlike the seagulls, the birds of prey weren’t just content to blast them from afar. In fact, if the Stormgulls had been hybrids that attacked from range then dove in to finish the job, the eagles exclusively—so far—prioritized savaging their targets with their talons. Henry’s bird never gave him more than a second or two to aim before it came back for another attempt. Suffice it to say, practicing his ranged attack wasn’t going so great for him. Not when the bird refused to give him space. The Toxic Rays needed a couple seconds to prepare and aim, especially with him tweaking the aperture of the ray. Rupture Casting could let him bypass that requirement with raw strength, but that wasn’t the point. He wanted the skill to grow in potency, and that needed time. Time that Henry could hardly afford without cheating, and at this point, using the full strength of his Avatars did feel like cheating. Which was why he had been using them sparingly. But now, he was seriously reconsidering that, and as he continued the song and dance with the A-rank, he peered through his Avatars at the other fight.

A couple hundred yards away, Maurice was faring relatively better. It probably helped that Maurice didn’t have any self-imposed restriction and well, the crab was cut above the average B-rank.

It definitely showed.

Maurice was almost as relentless as the bird, alternating between swiping with his massive, summoned claws, and creating more suspended imploding bubbles all around him, turning his surroundings into a three-dimensional minefield. Maurice made the weaker bird pay with its feathers for every one of its charges, even when it nimbly flew between them. Because he would then Blink out of the way, and the bird would suddenly find itself face-to-face with a dark bubble ready to rip its feathers out.

Well, at least he’s doing well, Henry thought as he had one of his Octominds pop in the path of his bird as it came by for another swoop. The Octominds appeared with its arms outstretched, ready to wrap around the bird’s head, but the white and blue-spattered eagle slipped just out of reach of the Apostle’s arm, slipping under the Lifeblood arms as it jutted its talons out, and Henry had been expecting that. He’d refrained from using his Avatars heavily, but this was getting old. If this damn thing refused to let him get a good shot at it, then Henry would make it stay still.

In quick succession, two other Apostles shed their invisibility and dropped from where they’d been observing, and the eagle would have likely dodged them just as easily as it had the first, if it weren’t for four other arcane Apostles summoning a Telekinetic wall just in front of its beak. The eagle screeched as it slammed into the magic, and that small moment of chaos was enough for the other three Octominds to wrap their arms around its wings and body. It was still a damn fight as the bird was not happy at being roughly handled, and it savaged one of its captors with its beak, still hovering in the air under the effect of its own magic. But Henry had his opening.

While the four arcane Apostles nursed their heads from the backlash of opposing the A-rank with Telekinesis, Henry channeled a torrent of mana into the custom Toxic Ray nexus he was fiddling with while making sure it didn’t get out of control, then pointed his index finger at the bird, its tip already glowing yellow.

Then Henry took the shot. Instantly, a pale, finger-thick yellow bright line shot out and skewered both the bird and the cloud far above it. With the skill having been infused with a little bit of Predation essence, the power was tremendous, though it was still a bit weaker than what he’d used against the chameleon. But it was enough to pierce through the bird’s feathers. Because now that he was feeling them through his Apostles, he was pretty sure that even metal would have issues cutting through the damn things.

Wind suddenly exploded out of the bird, shredding Henry’s Apostles into fine red mist and just in case, Henry blinked a good distance away, but the cutting maelstrom the bird had summoned never got too far from it. It spread its wings once more, glaring at him and if Henry’s paralytic had any effect, he couldn’t see it. But that wasn’t an issue. His toxins would grow stronger soon.

“We could stop if you want. You’re the one that attacked us, I’ll remind you,” Henry shouted at it, but in response, the bird screeched and shot up in the sky.

“You’re not running, are you,” he muttered, looking through the eyes of his remaining projections. He had five Apostles left, which he didn’t feel like losing, but there were plenty of Avatars. A couple of hundreds in total, and around thirty in his immediate vicinity. At this point, Henry was confident he could kill the bird whenever he felt like it, so long as he used Unfettered Echoes. But he still wanted to practice his skills against an actual opponent. And it turns out, he would still have the opportunities as the bird suddenly dove back toward him. Just like the fighter-jet footage he’d seen in his past life, Henry watched as the bird broke the sound barrier in a heartbeat, intent on skewering him with its beak. Somehow, somehow, Henry met the bird’s eyes as it closed in on him. Its cold gray gaze almost as sharp as its beak and talons. It was the gaze a predator would fix on its prey.

Right then and there, Henry’s mind flashed to Attal’s underground compound, and how the fabric of reality had torn. How that… thing had looked down on them. That hadn’t been the gaze of a normal predator. Nothing like this eagle. No… that was the look of another kind of predator. The kind that looked at you as a plaything for it to twist and break.

Henry blinked out of the way while the ground below him exploded as the bird slammed into the ground like a mortar, sending shattered rocks and wood spraying up. It really was like a detonation had gone off, but while there was no smoke nor fire, there was still a cloud of dust and debris, which rippled, then burst outwardly as the bird flew up.

Letting out a slow breath, Henry rolled his shoulders and called forth the nearby Avatars to assist. Training was good and all, but he shouldn’t be wasting time.

“Rookie. Did you see that?”

Hammond pursed his lip for a second, the loosened them. He didn’t appreciate being called a rookie, especially at his rank. But it wasn’t like he could mouth off to the man getting piss drunk behind him.

“See what?” he asked after a moment as he looked about. The clearing was empty. They’d met nothing worth hunting in their patrol and now, it seemed like they might have to spend another night in the goddamn forest because his senior wanted to take a break. Hammond would have liked to spend the night at an inn, but it looked like he won’t be getting to. Especially if old Omar decided to take them on a goose chase. True, Hammond wanted a good fight, but he also wasn’t dumb enough to get too comfortable around here. Omar, on the other hand, treated the area like it was his backyard.

“White light,” Omar said.

Now Hammond had to turn and look at the man. He couldn’t be this drunk. He was A-rank, for god’s sake. They both were.

Omar was frowning, looking up toward the horizon. Then his eyes widened and his arm shot up. “There! Did you see it?”

Hammond looked toward the direction he was pointing, staring in the air for a five seconds. Then ten. Then thirty.

Was the man seeing things?

Hammond swallowed and did not let the frustration show on his face. He did not want to deal with this. He wanted a bath, and a meal. He’d been patrolling for the last goddamn week and he was sick—

“Up. Let’s go see what it is. Might be a light creature. Could be worth a pretty penny,” Omar said, stretching his back with a wide grin on his face and before Hammond could manage an argument against that, the man leapt up in the air, landed on branch, then hopped again.

“Hurry!” the shout came a moment later, and Hammond bit his tongue. There was plenty he could say. Plenty he could complain about. And plenty of ways he could see this endeavor fail and be a massive waste of his time. But the rules were the rules, and he had agreed to them. Hammond hadn’t gotten to A-rank by shirking from what needed to be done. So without a word, he put one foot in front of the other, spear in hand, and followed.

“This better be worth it”, he still muttered.
