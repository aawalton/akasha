Chapter 17

“I showed the architect where to place the rose window. He thanked me for my eye for beauty. I did not tell him that beauty had nothing to do with it. The window is positioned to channel a frequency that will not exist for another eight hundred years.” - Codex of the Long Watch, Field Observation, 13th Century, France

Dawn came without colour.

The violet sky lightened from a deep bruise to pale lavender, but the sun, if it still existed behind the system fence, produced no warmth and none of the familiar gradient of a morning.

Arthur woke to the pull. The pull towards Greenwich had sharpened overnight. He could feel the direction behind his eyes, steady and fixed, a compass needle that had stopped searching and locked onto target, less than a mile away.

Steve was sitting against the low brick parapet, the billhook resting across his knees. He looked tired but his gaze was sharp as he studied the open guidebook beside him. Donna had finished repacking before either of them noticed. Her axe was strapped, her pack was closed, and she was chewing the last of a protein bar with the joyless focus of fuelling a machine.

Arthur's shoulder had improved overnight. The joint still carried the memory of the damage, a faint catch in the rotation that his brain classified as reduced range, but the grinding pain was almost gone. The Ether-driven healing had been working on his frame while he slept.

Steve tapped the guidebook. "The Royal Observatory sits at the top of the hill. The south-facing slope is open ground with the park wall on three sides. The Prime Meridian line runs north-south through the courtyard, a brass strip set into the concrete."

"What about the approach?" Donna asked. She'd stood without wincing. Her back had settled overnight and seemed functional if not comfortable. She was scanning the hill with the same assessment she gave every approach: terrain, cover, sight lines, threats.

"Open parkland. Maybe three hundred metres of grass between the gate and the observatory. No cover except a few trees."

"And anything in the park has had all night to settle in. The Ether density near nodes attracts creatures. We learned that at Bloomberg," Donna said.

"Local Ether density within the Greenwich node proximity radius is elevated. Standard Zone transitioning to Sanctum-adjacent. Enhanced mutation characteristics. Increased aggression potential." Archie added.

"Aggression potential. Right," Donna repeated, giving a single, tight nod.

They descended the stairwell and crossed the road into Greenwich. The park wall appeared ahead, Victorian brick topped with iron railings. The main wrought-iron gate stood wide open and beyond it, the hill rose through grounds that had already started to change.

The grass was wrong. It was still growing, but the colour had changed from green to a dark, blue-tinged emerald, and the blades were longer than they should have been for early spring. The trees lining the path seemed to have put out new growth, their leaves thick and possessing a faint, violet sheen beneath the morning light.

"The vegetation is responding to the Ether density," Steve said quietly. "Same principle as the creature mutations but slower. Give it a few weeks and this park won't look like London anymore."

“So barking then?” Arthur said.

They moved up the hill in formation and the pull behind Arthur's eyes intensified with every step, the pressure humming towards something that felt more like a harmonica than a headache.

After two hundred metres or so, Donna stopped dead in her tracks, her hand snapping out to signal a halt as her gaze locked onto the distance.

Arthur saw what had made her stop a second later. Movement within the dense tree line to their left. Something heavy and deliberate was moving through the blue-tinged brush, with the measured tread of an animal that was not actively hunting but patrolling a boundary.

A massive shape detached itself from the deep shadows between two ancient oaks. Then a second. Then a third.

They had been red deer pre-system, the kind that had roamed Greenwich Park for centuries as a protected tourist attraction. Their antlers had thickened into ridged, bone-plated structures that caught the violet light like crowns of chitin. Their bodies were heavier, denser, the muscle visible beneath hides that had darkened to slate grey. Their eyes had the same faint blue glow as the foxes, but it was steadier, colder, with a focus that suggested something more mature than just animal instinct.

They stood there with their heads held high looking at the party. Almost daring them.

"Three. Spread formation. They're watching us," Donna breathed.

Arthur read them like he would a building. A high centre of gravity and those antlers would be devastating weapons. But they were also top-heavy with legs built for sustained movement, not explosive acceleration. A completely different threat profile from the guardian bull in the Mithraeum.

Donna's grip tightened on her axe and Arthur felt Steve tense behind him, his billhook rising. The deer did not move. They maintained their loose arc, six glowing blue eyes tracking the team, and simply waited.

Arthur's instinct said fight. His experience said the same. Everything they'd encountered since the sky changed had attacked. The foxes had attacked. The bull had attacked. The alley spider had attacked. Every creature, every time.

But these weren't attacking. And the pull behind his eyes was doing something strange. It was not spiking the way it had before the bull emerged. It was neutral and felt tuned towards the node, discarding the deer altogether.

"Do not engage," Arthur said, keeping his voice low and steady.

Donna flicked a sharp glance toward him. "Arthur—"

"They're not hunting. Look at the spacing. If they were going to charge, they'd have funnelled us between the trees. They're positioned to observe, not to intercept."

"Or they're positioned to surround," Donna said.

"I don’t think so. It feels like they're holding a perimeter."

Donna held his gaze for two seconds. Then she lowered her axe fractionally, keeping it cocked at a height she could swing from if she had to.

"If you're wrong, I'll kill them, then you."

"Fair enough."

Then they took a leap of faith and started walking. The deer paced them along the tree line, maintaining a constant distance of thirty metres. Their eyes watching, assessing. Arthur felt the Ether pressure from them, a dull background warmth like standing near a radiator. These creatures were soaked in the energy.

They reached the observatory courtyard without incident. The deer stopped at the tree line and didn't follow onto the paved ground. Arthur paused and looked back at the three pairs of cold blue eyes watching them from the tree line, ancient, patient, and motionless at the boundary of their domain.

"Archie, the deer. Were they the guardian?"

Archie's geometric lines shifted into an arrangement Arthur had not seen before, expanding outward in an intricate lattice, as if processing something that required more than the usual resource allocation.

"Not all evaluations are combat-based," Archie said. "The candidate's threat-response profile is a tracked variable. Restraint is data."

Steve let out a long, slow breath, lowering his billhook.

The brass strip was in front of them. Set into the courtyard concrete, running precisely north-south. The Prime Meridian. Zero degrees longitude. The line where the turning spheres divide.

"There are two of these, though," Steve said, out of the blue. "Two zeroes. This brass line is the old one, sighted through a telescope in 1850 off a dish of mercury. The satnav zero sits about a hundred metres that way, past the railings." He pointed east.

Donna turned on Steve. "And you didn’t think this was relevant information to know before we walked in here?"

"It just occurred to me, besides, both the zero marks are here, so we were always coming to Greenwich."

Arthur did not have to think about it. The pull sat on the brass line. "It is this one. The old one."

Arthur walked over, dropped to one knee, and placed his palm flat against the cold metal.

The activation was nothing like the London Stone. The Stone had been a capillary. This was an organ. Blue light erupted along the meridian, racing north and south simultaneously as though the metal were a fuse and the Ether were fire. The light spread outward across the courtyard in complex geometric patterns, forming precise circles, sweeping arcs, and converging triangles. It illuminated intricate channels carved into the stone that had been invisible a moment before. The entire courtyard was a circuit board, and Arthur had just switched it on.

The pressure behind his eyes peaked and then broke open and for a few seconds, Arthur wasn't on the courtyard. He was somewhere else. Somewhere vast.

He felt the Grid. Not the whole of it, not the planetary network he knew must exist intellectually. But the London fragment. The London Stone, humming gently to the northwest. The Mithraeum, a deeper hum below the City. And he saw the glowing pathways between them, conduits running beneath the streets like subterranean rivers of refined Ether, connecting node to node in a web that he could perceive clearly.

Beyond the immediate London cluster, there was only vast, overwhelming darkness.

The Grid extended outward in every direction, immense, ancient, and almost completely silent. He felt major lines tracing south toward the English Channel, east toward the coast, and north into the heart of the country. All of them were cold. All of them were dark. All of them were waiting. He was standing at the edge of a planetary network, and virtually none of it was powered on.

The scale hit him like vertigo and for a second his mind failed. Nothing in his profession or experience had any framework to explain what he was seeing. He was a man kneeling on a brass strip in a London courtyard, and the thing beneath his hand was older than language, larger than continents, and he had no way to think about it that didn’t collapse under the sheer weight and scope.

The vision ended and Arthur jerked his hand back from the brass. His knees were on the concrete. His heart was hammering and Steve was gripping his shoulder.

"Arthur! Arthur, look at me. Are you alright?"

"I'm—yeah I’m fine," he lied, his hands still shaking. "I saw it, Steve. The Grid. The London nodes. They're all connected, right underneath us. But beyond London it's….Dark. Almost everything is dark."

Steve's face went still.

Blue text assembled in Arthur's vision.

NODE ACTIVATION: GREENWICH OBSERVATORY

Classification: Major

Grid Status: ONLINE

ETHER ABSORBED: +65

INTEGRATION: 120 / 119

LEVEL UP: LEVEL 3

INTEGRATION: 1 / 218

ATTRIBUTE POINTS AVAILABLE: 3

Level three. The node itself had pushed them past the threshold. But the number felt small against what he'd just seen. Donna was watching the courtyard. The bright geometric patterns carved into the concrete were slowly dimming, but directly above the Prime Meridian strip, where the light had been the brightest, lines of glowing blue script were assembling in the air.

Depart the Zero, where the stars were bound,

To the hidden nave where the walls are round.

Where two share a saddle, find the Red-Cross gate,

Where the boy king's guardian bears the weight.

Steve read the verse three times, pulling his notebook from his pocket and rapidly copying the text into the margin.

"The first two lines are straightforward, thank god," Steve said, pointing his pencil at the page. "'Depart the Zero, where the stars were bound.' That refers to zero degrees longitude right here at Greenwich. Our business at this node is concluded."

He shifted his pencil to the second line. "'To the hidden nave where the walls are round.'"

"A nave means a church, but it also mentions round walls which means a round church. And there's only one in London that matters. The Temple Church. Built by the Knights Templar in the twelfth century and consecrated in 1185. It's in the Inns of Court, between Fleet Street and the Thames."

He paused. “There are two other possibilities. The Templars had an earlier headquarters at Holborn called the Old Temple, and excavations show that it had a round nave as well. Then there’s the Order of St John at Clerkenwell, headquarters of the Knights Hospitaller, the Templars’ rivals. Also round.” He shook his head. “Though, both have been rubble for centuries.”

“So, the one on Fleet Street is the only surviving one with a round nave?” Arthur asked.

“I think so.”

“And if it is pointing to the Templars, then the red cross would be the one on their tabard, yes?”

"Yes. The Templar cross. Red cross on white. That's their symbol. So 'find the Red-Cross gate' means find the entrance to a Templar site. It's reinforcing line two. Temple Church."

"But the first part of line three says something else," Donna said, reading over Steve's shoulder. "'Where two share a saddle.' What? Two people on a horse?"

Steve paused, his pencil hovering over the paper. "That's... you're right. That's too specific. Two riders, one horse. I've seen that somewhere. On a seal or a coin..." He rubbed his forehead. "It's at the edge of my memory, but I cannot quite pull the exact reference."

"And the last line, ‘Where the boy king’s guardian bears the weight.’ Who is that?" Arthur asked.

Steve stared at the words in his guidebook. "I do not know," he admitted, clearly bothered. "A child king and someone who guarded him, and this weight. I can state with confidence that our next target is Temple Church. The last two lines have more in them, and I cannot place all of it yet."

"You'll work it out on the walk," Donna said. It was an assessment, not a reassurance. Steve's brain plus time plus distance equaled answers. She'd seen it before.

"We need to move regardless," Arthur said. The courtyard's light had faded, but the deer were still standing motionless at the tree line watching the courtyard. The pull behind his eyes had moved somewhere northwest, though it was very faint.

He stood and adjusted his pack. Level three. Three unallocated points, a half-solved riddle and the memory of a dark Grid that stretched across the planet. As they turned toward the park gate, Archie's floating form drifted alongside them.

"The optimal route passes through an area of elevated Ether density. A former industrial site, approximately one point three kilometres southwest of the London Bridge approach."

"Are you warning us?" Donna said.

"I am providing navigational data."

"With an emphasis on danger."

"Emphasis is a subjective interpretation of objective information delivery."

Donna looked at Arthur. "He's warning us."

Arthur tightened his grip on the Bullguard Shield and set a steady pace down the hill. The afterimage of the dark planetary grid was still burning behind his eyelids, driving him toward the round church. And between them and it something existed that had Archie worried.
