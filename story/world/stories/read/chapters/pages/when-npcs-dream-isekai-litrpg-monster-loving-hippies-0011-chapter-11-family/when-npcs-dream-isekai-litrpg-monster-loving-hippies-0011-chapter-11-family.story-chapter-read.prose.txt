We woke up to morning sun and roosters shouting kukuriku! and I just knew this was going to be an awesome day.

Some part of my brain reminded me that I was probably two tones too happy and optimistic because of Bob’s venom in my blood. And okay, yeah—maybe being this cheerful and calm wasn’t the most optimal state of mind from a survival perspective. But on the other hand, being paranoid and terrified all the time wasn’t optimal either. So we were probably okay. And also—this was a lot more fun.

I woke Bob up, because he was such a sleepyhead. He startled upright, twitching and looking around like he forgot where he was. Then he relaxed and blinked at me sleepily.

Adorable.

“Come on!” Juliette called, fully energized. “I can’t wait to meet the tribe!”

She grabbed some of the food we’d saved and packed it in a cloth bag. I picked up Bob and tried to nudge him into a second cloth bag.

“Don’t be a baby,” I said as he squirmed in protest. “It’s just until we get out of town. You know we can’t let anyone see you.”

He understood enough of what I said to grumble, stop resisting, and crawl inside.

We walked in silence for a while, Once we far enough out of town, I pulled him out and let him sit on my shoulder, stroking his fur as we walked. The three of us, off to the forest again—but this time, not as lost, terrified strangers.

This time, we were guests.

I was happy, and so I started singing The Teddy Bears’ Picnic, not caring if the French version translated weirdly.

♪ If you go down in the woods today, you're sure of a big surprise... ♪♪ If you go down in the woods today, you'd better go in disguise... ♪♪ For every bear that ever there was will gather there for certain because— ♪♪ Today's the day the teddy bears have their picnic... ♪

I trailed off. I... didn’t actually remember the rest.“Your singing is...” Juliette said, trying to find the words. “Abominable. Yeah, I’ll go with that.”

“Oh really? Think you can do better?”

She did. Somehow, it translated weirdly but still sounded beautiful. I was struck.

“Your singing is... Amazing!” I said, grinning. “Yeah, I’ll go with that.”

When we got close to the forest, I placed my hand on Bob’s fur and let him guide us. His soft vibrations translated into images and directions in my mind. It was like having a GPS—but made of fluff.

Bob showed me what a huge mistake it had been to enter from the front last time. Apparently, we’d walked right through White Stripe territory, killed one of their scouts, and got spotted by one of their care bear patrols. Just really bad luck.

This time, Bob guided us about a mile to the right before we entered the forest. A much safer route.And it was like we were seeing the forest for the first time.

It was beautiful.

We passed strange, fascinating monsters, and Bob pointed each one out, explaining what they were, how they behaved, and how dangerous they might be.

There was a mouse-dragonfly—not aggressive, but it could spit acid, so definitely not worth hunting.

A massive, scaled hippo with three horns—we gave that one a very wide berth. Herbivore, sure, but Bob said it was territorial, fast and deadly.

We even saw a giant spider. Legit—eight legs, web-weaving and everything. It was the first creature that looked exactly like a classic fantasy monster from Earth. I’ll have to see if there’s more out there like it.

We also spotted a large scaled rat with horns. Similar in body shape to the Care Bears, but smaller. Tough, but technically prey.

Apparently, the Scorpion Surikata were omnivores. Bob even pointed out a weird purple fruit that was edible. For them, at least. I wasn’t about to risk it.

Finally, we arrived.

Two Care Bear guards stood at attention at the tribe’s entrance. They immediately looked alarmed when they saw two humans—but relaxed when they spotted Bob on my shoulder.

“Part of the family,” Bob conveyed with a simple fuzzy vibration.

We slipped past dense shrubbery and into a hidden cave—low but wide and deep. Inside, there were piles of leaves, bundles of cloth, and what looked like a hoard of shiny trash—glass shards, maybe jewelry. There was even some old human stuff. Garbage turned treasure.

At first, we didn’t see anyone else.

Then Bob let out a loud screech—and slowly, from the dark corners of the cave, they emerged.

About ten Scorpion Surikata stepped forward. Most were small—half Bob’s size. A few were adults.

The kids were hesitant, but one brave little guy stepped forward. He approached me, sniffing my pants and shoes.

Cutest thing ever.

I bent down and petted it. It vibrated and began ‘talking’—showing me an image of me falling over because I had only two legs. It was curious how I stayed upright.

So I lifted one leg, balanced on the other. They all stopped moving, visibly impressed.

Another came forward—slightly different fur color. Maybe female? She touched me and showed me an image of herself being held in the air.

She wanted me to pick her up.

So I did.

And suddenly, we were swarmed. Me and Juliette, neck-deep in Surikata scorpion kittens. Lifting, holding, playing—it was so much fun. I could’ve stayed there all day.

Then, a larger female approached—slightly bigger than Bob. Probably his sister - the tribe leader.

She gave a low screech. All the little ones scattered instantly.

She wanted to talk.

I placed my hand on her back respectfully.

First, she showed me and Juliette surrounded by Surikatas rubbing against our legs.That meant gratitude. Deep thanks.

Then she showed a row of ten Armadillo Care Bears standing upright, holding spears.

She believed they could be armed.

That was... incredible. If she was right, it would change everything. A tribe of trained, armed Care Bears was Terrifying and awesome.

Then she showed us walking back to the town, carrying a bunch of spears we’d picked up there. “We will get the spears,” I said. “It’ll take time. Two weeks.”

She didn’t understand ‘two weeks’ exactly, but she got the idea—it would be hard, but we would do it.

Finally, she showed the scene from a moment ago—me playing with the kids—and then herself rubbing against me.

I understood the message.

You are family.Then she turned and walked away. She was smart. I liked her.I reached out, took the food bag, and spilled its contents—some not-so-fresh bread, apples, yams, and cabbage.The kids were thrilled. They swarmed it like it was candy—except the cabbage. That was a solid no from everyone.

We played with them a bit longer, until they started getting sleepy.

It was time to go.

We had to bring Bob with us—both for safety and guidance. Without him, the forest was still deadly, and we didn’t know the way back on our own.

But soon, we would. I was determined to learn. Every walk with Bob was a lesson. A treasure trove of knowledge.

On the way back alone, we spotted another ten new monster species. Bob explained their habits, dangers, and even just how delicious their meat was.He really did know everything about this forest.

When we returned to Juliette’s room at the mansion, Bob curled up on his favorite pillow and passed out immediately. Adorable fluffball.

But I had work to do.

We needed money—and fast. No one knew when the White Stripe Tribe might strike again. We needed those weapons, gear and supplies.

“Hey Juliette,” I asked, “how does the job thing work around here?”

“It’s stupid simple,” she said. “Just find an NPC that looks like he runs something and ask him. Wait—why don’t you go to the blacksmith? That’s where... you know... Ramon used to work.”

Yeah. That was a good idea. Working there might even build some muscle. Which might come in handy if, say, I was going to war for example...

I was also curious how the system managed industry. Would it just take three hits with a hammer on a metal bar and magically turn it into a perfect sword?I knew enough about this place not to get my hopes up. From what I’d seen so far, this world was maybe 95% mundane and 5% magical. Still, 5% magical was better than Earth.The blacksmith’s shop was run by Beran—your classic dwarfy human. Short, bearded, muscular. Stood behind the counter like a good Elder Scrolls NPC just waiting for the player.

“Good day, lad,” he said in a deep, gravelly voice. “What brings ye here?”

“I’m an adventurer,” I said. “Looking for an honest day’s work for an honest day’s pay.” Probably heard that one on TV.

Beran grunted and looked me over. “I do need an apprentice. But this ain’t easy work. Got no room fer laziness. Are ye up to the task, boy?”

“Sure,” I said.

“Ye start tomorrow. Sunrise. Don’t ye dare be late.”

And just like that, he turned and ignored me.

Shortest job interview ever.

My first job! Not just in this world—ever. Yeah, in my 16 long years, I didn’t lift much of a finger.

Metalworking apprentice. Yeah, I was even kind of excited.

I mean—how hard could it be, really?
