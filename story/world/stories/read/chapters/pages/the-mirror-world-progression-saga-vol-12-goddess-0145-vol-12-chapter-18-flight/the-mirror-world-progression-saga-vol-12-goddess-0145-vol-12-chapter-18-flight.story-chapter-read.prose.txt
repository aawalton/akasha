Sweeping by on the back of Deathsting, Azure unleashed her sixth destructive AoE spell into the ranks, using her now most powerful go-to attack for the purpose: Lake of Mayhem. Indeed, when the mass of shifting, twisting spaces erupted in the ranks, mayhem ensued.

She’d dropped several still persisting. It made a shifting zone of spatial distortion, moving isolated ‘streaks’ of space to other spaces, sometimes including whole people and sometimes… partial people. Few had managed to resist it below, that she’d seen. The enemy had not expected such magic to work due to teleportation restrictions.

Azure bypassed all of those now, however. She didn’t truly do it like anyone else, moving along an axis only she had access to.

The spell lasted only thirty seconds, but living beings — which she was ruthlessly targeting, specifically — could not bring themselves to hold their ground, if they managed to avoid death. They fled the space, creating a vacuum like nothing else when the duration ended.

Dax was paying attention to where she made those pockets, and taking advantage by filling in the gap with their own troops. Those troops could then fire magic horizontally to avoid the range restrictions.

Meanwhile, a veritable horde of dragons was breathing numerous varieties of breath attacks on the enemies below. Obviously, the enemy had been prepared for fire and endured it well in most cases. Acid breath was doing just mediocre, often thanks to secondarily ruining weapons and armor.

The highest elite Faerie Dragons such as Dart and Athos could breathe Pneuma fire, though, and this was reaping vast destruction. Ironically, the best mid-tier breath seemed to be the prismatic one. It was successfully dazing enemies on the frontline, allowing others to take advantage. Many of the shapeshifting Servitors had been changing to the base dragon type that used that attack, adjusting as ordered.

One dragon had been paradoxically doing the best with fire, burning soldiers to a crisp, whatever their resistances. As Azure flicked her gaze over, she saw the enemy’s spellcraft finally take it out by dispelling it. They’d had trouble doing so, but eventually their waves of magic succeeded by focusing on it.

It had been under illusory disguise to appear as just another Servitor, but it was actually Samantha’s summon. The fire it breathed was like a real ancient dragon’s breath, more potent than the emulative Faerie Dragon stuff. Resistance did nothing.

I’m sure she’s disappointed they finally cracked it, but she has to be happy with the damage dealt. It did more than any other three Faerie Dragon elites.

Though there was a smattering of other kinds of flyers, the majority were held off in reserve for now, including Palthennius, who was not technically less deadly on the ground anyway. He was better in the air as an interceptor or duelist, and there was neither thing in the air at the moment.

Though the battle in progress was all-in-all very chaotic and casualties had accrued on both sides, the enemy ranks were far more broken up and pockmarked by the enterprise, while the alliance forces were maintaining cohesion with extended, slowly filling in fat ‘necks.’

Like clockwork, we’re advancing, and I think we’re dishing significantly more damage than they are.

It had been only minutes of this, but the enemy’s efforts were not up to stuff in countering any of it, including Azure. She was too fast for single-target blasts; the AoE was mostly countered by the Counter Crew, though in some cases she was even too fast for them.

She got reprimanded for ‘going too far’ because of that. In any case, she discovered she and Deathsting could easily tank the efforts of random magi.

Besides, any time there was concentrated fire from a pocket, that pocket got erased — a storm of destructive magic descended on them, with a potency so overwhelming there was usually only dust and smolders left in the wake of it. None resisted it — absolutely not a single soul on the frontlines. It did make one wonder what waited on the backlines, but it was still rather mindblowing to see.

Sometimes, Azure could even catch a glimpse of the lightning-fast figure dishing it out directly in the process, but that was only because she could always sense exactly where she was.

Other times, the figure paused deliberately, as if boldly wanting to be seen, and posing rather dramatically and dominantly with her staff held somehow both menacingly and protectively.

And that was her, really. Iconically, this was her energy.

Regardless of which situation was seen, when Azure beheld that figure, she was usually like a silhouette, and multiple silhouettes like copies followed her, their hair and battle dresses billowing in the sky like banners, and her resplendent crown glowing and gleaming in the light.

Samantha, you are so fucking hot. I wonder if we can sneak in an after-victory session, before the end of the world, or whatever? Damn. Probably not…

The closer tower fortresses had put up more of a fight, resisting her temporarily, or simply persisting while weaker targets were focused on. Nonetheless, strike teams had blown up several. Otherwise, the towers had done minimal damage, as the magic that was slung downrange got prioritized for countering.

Ultimately, they were being neutralized more or less in sequence with the general advance, systematically as it continued.

As Azure sped through the air on her mount, looking for another good target, she became aware of activity from all of the towers at once. Considering they peppered the horizon visibly from the air, that was hundreds.

Oh, that ain’t good.

Response was immediate as protections bubbled up over everyone in the air. At the same time, the towers accrued a build-up of tremendous entropic energy, and a moment later, unleashed it in synchronicity. Blasts fired into the air and the world went cold and dark.

As soon as Azure tasted the first notes of what was happening, she bailed, collapsing Deathsting into a knife and twisting herself through mirror spaces to another spot far away, behind the protective lines. She was the only one that could, and they’d used it only sparingly in great secret, to keep the knowledge from the enemy… but she knew she needed to use it, right then.

Morphing up through a mirror, Azure cast out her greater senses in worry for Samantha — but she was fine, having speedily dodged the intense core of it and taken minimal wounds.

Visually, the sky was still turned as black as a void as the entropic magic saturated the air with hundreds of layers of destructive spellcraft, of such an intensity unheard of and unexpected. It shredded defenses, resisted countermagic, and ripped into the aerial ranks of the dragons.

It lasted only seconds, and then the blackness faded and was gone, leaving a terrible aftermath. Carcasses of dragons fell from the sky, dissipating even as they did, most of them being Servitors that had just been banished.

“We lost Athos,” came a sober call in the battle gestalt.

We’re barely begun, and we lost one of our best?

“Pull back the aerial forces,” came Samantha’s cold command. “Priority for all elites is to form teams and strike at the towers one-by-one. We’ll need vets in support in each case to move through these ranks on the ground. Casualties will be expected. Dax can make the call about how deep we have to go. The Brain Trust in general will assign targets. I’m guessing we need to destroy at least two-thirds of these, if we distribute our destruction correctly. Most towers did that mega-blast, but not all.”

“You’re not going to use the crown?” Ash asked.

“No. It’s too early. I’ll reinforce the army with the cloud wreath in a moment, but no further special resources.”

“Good Goddess,” was all Dax said, in clear approval.

“Are those towers really only dangerous with line of sight?” General Carlisle asked.

“No,” Samantha replied, “but that spell was. It’s something special. They can’t repeat that even at a hundredth the potency on the ground, not with just dozens of towers able to concentrate in an area. My guess is they’ll save the energy.”

“At least it’s bloody finite, then.”

“I don’t want to test how finite. We can’t lose hundreds more flyers by any means.”

“This was a good strategy on their part,” General Linnore mused. “Covers their weakness without wasting what flyers they did have, and forces a longer attrition game as we have to stretch and contort our lines rather chaotically.”

“I’d expect no less from them. A bunch of uber defense-plus-offense wizard towers is right on point for them.”

“The potency is absurd,” Zephyr said in complaint. “They shouldn’t have this level of entropic magic to sling around in bulk.”

“Welcome to the art of war,” Dax said in faux-cheer. “If you don’t expect the unexpected, you’ve failed as a strategist. In any case, the Dominion is going to have to make adjustments themselves, even imminently. They’re not doing well at all on the other side. They underestimated the minotaurs and are paying for it. They’re on an epic tear as we speak.”

“I’d like to be excused from the aerial restrictions,” Azure said, making sure her mental voice wasn’t too flippant. “They can’t hit me, as I just proved.”

“That sounds fucking dangerous,” Samantha said with a faint note of panic in her voice. “Your uses are limited with that.”

“We’re at war, dear. What isn’t? But I promise I won’t expend them all.”

Dax scoffed. “If the enemy is stupid enough to target lil’ ole her with that, let them waste their time. I think this is too good to pass up. She’ll operate with almost as much impunity as you, Goddess.”

Samantha sighed testily. “Fine. Everyone get to work!”

Smirking a bit to herself, Azure spun Deathsting in her fingers. “Ready to get to work, then, my pointy-tailed murder buddy?”

Deathsting gave her a ferocious snarl. “There was never a time I was not, Wielder. Hurry it up! And this time, let me kill more!”

Laughing, Azure leapt into the air as Deathsting transformed, and they flew above, bold and alone, unrelenting in their punishment of the enemy.
