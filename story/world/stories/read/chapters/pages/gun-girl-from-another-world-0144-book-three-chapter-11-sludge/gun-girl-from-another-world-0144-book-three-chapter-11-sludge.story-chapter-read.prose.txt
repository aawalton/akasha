Chapter 11

Sludge

Ayre is staring at me like he’s second-guessing the vote of confidence he’s just given.

“You want us to teach someone we can’t communicate with an entire language in under half a day?”

I do my best to return the expression. “Well, yeah, Ayre. If you could already communicate with her, there wouldn’t be much of a point, would there?”

“... And how do you propose we do that?”

I break my imitation of my best friend’s stare in favor of a beaming smile. “I suggest starting with the alphabet!”

Ayre’s gaze gets even flatter, which drives my wattage even higher. Finally, he gives me the only appropriate response, and cracks me over the forehead again with his bow.

I rub the spot with my hand for a moment. “Ah, it’s the timetable you’re worried about? No problem! Of course, I have a solution in mind!”

“Remmi,” Korrigan whines, “nobody trusts you when you act like an overconfident mad alchemist!”

Even Kyuuga is giving me a judgmental stare like he finds me unbelievable.

I keep my nose up in the air as I dig around in my storage. “Oh ye all of so little faith! If I say I have a solution, have I ever failed to follow through?” I pull out a little glass jar with a victorious flourish, holding it out toward my friends’ faces. “Tada!”

Four sets of eyes - Angel, Ayre, Korrigan, and Kyuuga - all lean in close to it to examine the thick, syrupy concoction behind the glass as if it would reveal its nature to them.

“It’s … purple?” Angel half asks, half states.

“It looks like liquefied bruise,” Korrigan contributes.

Ayre tilts his head to peer past the bottle at me. “You didn’t come up with some crazy idea to bottle Corruption, did you?”

“Of course not!” My expression is hurt and aghast in equal measure as I retract the jar as if to protect it from hasty hostility. “This is made entirely from Essence crops! It’s just purple because it’s got beets and blueberries in it!”

I won’t deny that the benign explanation for the color causes open expressions of relief to pour across the faces of my party.

“So it’s something you cooked up in the kitchen,” Ayre summarizes. “But that still doesn’t look like anything edible. What else did you put in it?”

I put on my salesgirl smile as I hold the jar up next to my face. “It’s a blend of produce chosen for their amplifying effects on brain function, reduced to concentrate it into its purest nutritional state!”

Ayre’s gaze is actually narrowing again. “So you didn’t pick them for taste. Spit it out and answer the question, Remmi. What else is in there?”

“Vitamins, minerals, omega 3’s, amino acids, complex carbohydrates, gotta have that energy!”

Ayre takes a threatening step toward me. “The bottle, Remmi. What. Did. You. Put. In it?!”

I put on my best pouting face. “A lot, okay? You can’t expect me to remember it all off the top of my head!”

“You have four hundred Intellect, Remmi! I absolutely can expect it!”

I give a defeated sigh. “Fine, but you’re going to be very disappointed when you realize you’re making this great, big deal out of nothing!”

He crosses his arms and settles his glare on me, unwilling to let it drop, so I begin counting off on my fingers.

“Egg, milk, almond milk, bacopa, lion’s mane, ginkgo biloba, collard greens, broccoli, carrots, tomatoes, squash, turmeric, habaneros, moringa, and a pinch of lemon salt to keep the oils from separating. It’s basically a super-healthy Intellect smoothie!”

Ayre’s eyes go wide, but everyone else looks confused.

“What’s habaneros?” Korrigan is the one to ask.

“A nightmarish torture food from Remmi’s homeland,” the elf rats on me. “It literally only exists to set your mouth on fire.”

I scowl back at him. “That’s not true! The breeds made for that are way hotter! Habaneros are a naturally occurring monstrosity!”

“The fact that even you can’t deny it’s a foul demon plant does not engender confidence in your concoction, you madwoman!”

I make a silly face and tilt my upper body back and forth. “Oh, look at me, I’m Ayre ThiccThighs, and I pulled out my big thesaurus today!” I cross my arms back at him. “You should know by now that I wouldn’t give you guys anything too dangerous!”

“You made coffee that gives two levels of caffeine overdose and sold it to the Empire!”

“Rude,” I come back. “First off, it was confiscated by the Empire, that’s entirely different from selling it to them, and second, I said I wouldn’t give you anything too dangerous!”

“That it needed to be confiscated is exactly the kind of problem I’m talking about!”

“The only reason it needed to be confiscated is because Yorin declared it a Heavenly Artifact!”

“Or cursed demonic aberration.”

“Okay, that’s, like, three completely unrelated categories!”

Ayre takes a deep breath and calms himself back down by force. “Okay, stop, let’s back up. This can all be solved very easily with a single question: What is it called?”

My smile leaps back into being. “Remmitastic Think Tank Juice!”

He’s not buying it. “I didn’t mean what you called it, you psychopath. What does the System call it?”

Aaaand my smile melts. “... Purple sludge.” I immediately perk back up. “BUT! It does confirm all of the benefits I was aiming for!”

He’s not biting. “Show us the window, Remmi. I’m not letting you put that stuff in even a monster’s mouth without knowing for sure it’s not just poison with an Intellect buff.”

I sigh, but oblige him. I cast Identify on the bottle and then spin the screen toward the lot of them.

Purple Sludge

-----------------

A concentrated concoction of collards and unholy confidence engineered to maximize the intellectual benefits of Essence crops ... at the expense of all other considerations. Despite looking like the scrapings off the bottom of an alchemist’s still, it’s actually an extremely well-considered and brutally healthy nutritional infusion almost fit for human consumption.

Consuming this ... beverage grants the following benefits:

+300% Intellect for 2 hours

+300% Point Gain toward Intellect-based Skills for 2 hours

Increased intestinal distress

May require high Toughness to consume successfully

Consult an alchemist if experiencing increased brain size lasting for more than four hours

“Remmi … no.”

I motion to the window. “It says it’s healthy and fit for human consumption!”

“That's a willful disregard for the ‘almost’ part!” He motions to it, too. “And when was the last time you heard of something being described as brutally healthy?!”

“I … think there was a diet exercise regimen I read about somewhere …” I’m quick to double down. “And it calls it a well-considered blend!”

“It requires high toughness just to keep it down!”

“It might require it! Now who’s willfully disregarding key words?!”

Ayre takes another look at the window. “... It says it only lasts for two hours.”

But I wave the implied question off. “Oh, that’s not a problem! It takes so little to make a serving that I accidentally made a whole stockpile!”

He doesn’t look as impressed as I think he should. “... You intend to force-feed a child half a dozen of these things?!”

“You don’t need to say it like it’s bad,” I retort. “You know the System likes to exaggerate for laughs!”

Suddenly, I feel in danger from the look he’s giving me. “You know what, Remmi? That’s a very good point. You have enough to spare. You should drink one first. It will help you with your operational plans.”

I fidget with the jar, rolling it between my fingertips. “Oh, gee, I don’t know, Ayre. What would I even do with twelve hundred Intellect?”

“Reflect on your decisions.” He crosses his arms. “I’m serious, Remmi. I’m not letting you give that stuff to anyone else until you down one yourself. So if you’re so sure it’s safe and the only way we’re going to get this done in time, you’d better stop waffling and start chugging.”

All eyes are on me, so I look around at them, definitely not looking for a route of escape, and swallow. “Alright, fine, I’ll do it!”

Slowly, and absolutely not like I’m uncorking something radioactive, I undo the lid and turn the jar at an angle to stare down at it. The concoction is slow to shift to the new bottom, but fluid enough that it does so without trailing residue. Once I throw this thing back, it’s all coming out, like one angry, toxic slug.

… Oh, gee, I really could have done without that mental image …

Reflexively, I swallow again, and I remind myself that it’s just fruits and veggies in milk with an egg. I resist the urge to cast Empower to pump up my Toughness as I take the plunge and down it like a shot.

There really isn’t much lemon salt, so you wouldn’t expect it to hit first, or be particularly noticeable, but clearly a little bit goes a long way, because its sour bitterness is the first thing to impact my tongue. Then there’s a sensation of … swamp, like I’m taking a mouthful of muddy root. It’s earthy and green, but in an aggressive, heavy way, like that root is an anvil sitting on my taste buds. And then the heat sets in, like horseradish and hot sauce. Because the lemon salt kept all of the different oils from separating, it doesn’t just land on my tongue. It rolls across it and snowboards at terminal velocity into my throat and sinuses as I feel the burning around the back of my skull. Despite the presence of beets, carrots, squash, and blueberries, there is no sweetness. It’s lost in everything else, leaving behind a dry, bitter cloy of tannins to fill the wake of old pennies.

I cough and blink to try to squeeze the water out of my eyes. “Yummy.”

“You liar!”

“Hey, it could be a lot worse!” I tilt the jar around thoughtfully. “In fact, if I dehydrated it entirely into a powder, I could mold it into hard tablets, and you could take it like a multivitamin. The taste wouldn’t actually be far off from that.”

Ayre leans in close to better examine me. “You’re going flush, but not pale.”

“That’ll be the niacin rush.”

He ignores me. “You look steady. Your eyes are dilated, but you’re not tipsy.”

“That’s the spice.”

Finally, he looks at me directly. “How’s your heart rate?”

“Elevated,” I reply, “but that’s also going to be because of the spice. It’s …” I give my head a quick shake to clear the horseradish out. “Woo, it’s a kick in the head.”

Ayre frowns like he’s trying to consider if my statement should be evaluated as a warning of a literal blow to the skull. “And, in your compassionate opinion based on kindness and empathy, would you give this to a child?”

I tilt my head as I give that serious consideration. “Honestly? Yeah. It’s nasty, and you’re going to want to be sitting upwind of me if I burp, but it’s only, like, medicine nasty, or liver and onions nasty. Completely survivable, with a low risk of psychological scarring.”

The elf’s scowl makes me wonder if I should have specified that the last bit was a joke. “Alright, fine, Angel can take it, but you’re taking it every two hours, too! If this is such an essential moment, then you need to be going at full gallop, too!”

I snap a salute with a hand that’s suddenly bristling with three more jars. “Aye, Capitan!”

Ayre gives a dramatic roll of the eyes before snatching the jar from me and making his way over to Angel. “Come on, Korrigan,” he calls as he goes. “We’d better get started if we’re going to be setting a national record for fastest language course.”

I set the extra jars on a nearby table and clear a spot to begin unloading sheaves of paper. “Have fun, you three! Mama’s got an AI-powered 3D printer to reprogram!”
