Day 372, 2:00 PM

After a bit more than fifteen days of grinding abominations flooding the tunnel from various side passages, we finally reach the other end. We’re at the first floor of a dungeon, the rain drumming outside. However, the dungeon is completely different from the others I have seen so far.

“What is that?” Edna points at the pulsating mass of flesh and ichor covering ninety percent of the floor, stretching all the way to the ceiling.

“It’s eighty-two percent green mana, twelve percent blue, and six percent black, with multiple base components. The dominant component is… humans.”

The pulsating mass chooses that moment to slither and gently spew a man-sized leathery egg. Something stirs within, and a lobster-like creature, weaker than lesser abominations, tears open the shell. It’s white and pudgy, like a giant silkworm, but I recognize it as one of the things which have been attacking us for the last seven hours.

I touch the hatchery, and it disintegrates as it devolves and magic leaves it. That turned out to be a mistake, and Gila loses her lunch as the thing falls apart into base components. Even Edna’s face turns an unhealthy waxen yellow as blood leaves it. I, on the other hand, have seen and studied the horrors in Hadriuse’s basement, along with the research journals.

I don’t think there’s anything left in this world which could disgust me. Then again, Edna probably believed that up until half a minute ago.

“Gila, you can stay here with the tree soldiers and purge the filth from the room.”

She nods, and starts disintegrating the remains while the thirty-seven remaining tree soldiers block the entrances.

“Edna, are you well enough to continue?”

Wordlessly, she turns to the stairway, and we head down.

“Do you think all the floors are like that?” She asks once we’re out of Gila’s earshot.

“I don’t know. They could be, or they could be regular floors. The exit leading to the tunnel is much wider than the ones leading into the jungle, but the path between the floors is still the standard width, meaning, they reared the abominations on this floor.”

The talk is pointless, we will know in a minute, but I can tell Edna is afraid, trying to escape the silence and everything that is to come. I can’t blame her, she has every right to be afraid. The only reason I’m not terrified is because I’m numb to death, and because I’m hoping I will figure out a way to solve this mess without dying. As for the vision, I can fake everything so that Gila believes her words to be true while I’m off on a tropical island enjoying a well-deserved rest.

Our rotten luck, just as bad as I feared, interrupts my sunny fantasy. From the entrance of the second level, we see another hatchery. A lobster-like thing is drying its wet armor next to the empty shell, and the hatchery is spreading a cluster of tendrils around the egg, consuming it.

“Do you want to do the honors?”

Edna unleashes a firewave, singing and gesturing properly, the way I explained to her on the way over. I whack the abomination with Batsy, snuffing out that minute problem before it gets a chance to grow.

Edna is quiet and grim as we descend through thirty floors of hatcheries. She doesn’t destroy them completely, rather she unleashes a single firewave at the most critical section, and the whole thing collapses all on its own. Thanks to her going back to singing and gesturing, her mana regeneration can keep up with the consumption.

Then, we’re in for another shock. The twenty-first floor is empty.

“There’s nothing here, not even vegetation,” Edna states the obvious, and I have a hypothesis.

“Biomass. They used anything that spawned inside the dungeon as fuel for the hatcheries. Growing that many minor abominations on the top floors is impossible without an abundant source of food.”

I run the numbers in my head. The first floor probably spawned a fraction of the energy needed to form a minor abomination, while the fiftieth was a fraction short. Assuming a linear distribution, the twenty-first floor had a sliver over forty percent of the energy needed to form a minor abomination, and when coupled with the fiftieth floor, they in average gathered seventy percent of what was needed to spawn a minor abomination per floor. Thirty floors in two weeks created feed for twenty-one minor abominations.

As for the higher floors, they probably converted them into hatcheries because they deemed them inefficient. But this means the church was producing alarming numbers of abominations.

And the only thing they could do with the abominations is release them into the wilderness, but to what purpose? It suddenly hit me. The number of abominations outside kept growing, even though these creatures could not reproduce as such. And given that they were dying as they hunted and ate each other they should have exterminated themselves long ago without a wormlord to produce new ones.

Assuming the final wormlord is alive…

“Edna, I’m sorry I’m repeating myself, but I need to confirm something. Nobody ever saw the last wormlord, right?”

Edna nods. “I told you before, the last wormlord never made an appearance. Why are you asking about that now?”

“What if, I know my idea is insane, but hear me out. What if the church had one or more facilities like this in the corrupted lands, spawning abominations for centuries, until they had enough to make the wave of monsters resembling a wormlord’s attack?”

Edna touches her chin as she considers my words in silence, then shakes her head. “Making something like this is beyond an inquisitor’s ability.”

“Could you make it? It’s beyond my ability, just so you know,” I tell her bluntly. “I’m not sure an archmage walking the path of life with centuries’ worth of experience could fabricate a horror like this, which means the eldritch monsters beyond this world are behind what we’re seeing here.”

“Why didn’t they just kill all the mages and consume the world already if they can do something like this?”

A valid question, one I don’t know an answer to. This whole setup looks like more work than just conquering the world. And if the eldritch things really found me a nuisance, they should have struck me already, assuming they have the power to do anything other than text me through the Guide. But here I am, alive and mostly well.

“What if they can only control dungeon denizens? Or the space inside the dungeon?” Not the most probable conclusion, but certainly one within the realm of possibility.

Edna graces me another doubtful look, one I guess I deserve.

“Whatever the truth, the answer lies at the bottom of this dungeon.”

“And how do you plan on reaching the bottom?”

I shrug. “I reached the floors beyond ninety near Hadriuse’s abode, I can do the same here. That’s why I’m conserving mana.”

A silence descends as we head for the next stairway. We’ve been at this for a bit over ten hours now, with some ten more to go, assuming the next fifty floors are empty, and I have a feeling they aren’t.

“What did you find there?” Edna breaks the silence with surprising interest, but considering our circumstances, what is beyond the ninetieth floor might be relevant.

“Abominable creatures, similar to the hatcheries above, yet…”

I describe in detail the creatures I have encountered and how my evolutionary dispel completely annihilated them. I conclude my telling lamely, explaining how I had stopped my descent when I ran out of mana. She can’t believe I could handle three floors back then, and with unrefined mana at that. My capacity right now is more or less what it was back then, but the refined mana should be enough to see us to floor one hundred, but what awaits beyond is anyone’s guess.

The next floor is empty, as is the next, and the next. I answer all Edna’s questions as truthfully as possible as we walk down the abandoned floors, but we tense up with the fifty-first floor ahead. If the church has left the minor abominations up to their own devices for decades, they should have already evolved into elder abominations, or something relatively close in terms of power.

The fifty-first floor is pitch black, and a sweep of scanner confirms it’s empty. I nearly laugh at the irrational relief that comes out of nowhere. I could have wiped out the monsters without breaking a sweat, but even the thought of wasting refined mana before the wormlord fight hurts my soul.

“Did they feed the minor abominations to the hatcheries as well?” Edna’s words drag my mind back to the empty floor.

Her guess is reasonable. Having that much useless energy, matter, and mana stuck down here is an utter waste of resources. If I were in charge of the process, I certainly wouldn’t leave that much building material down here.

“That’s certainly possible. But we should stay on our guard, in case an abomination ambushes us from someplace.”
