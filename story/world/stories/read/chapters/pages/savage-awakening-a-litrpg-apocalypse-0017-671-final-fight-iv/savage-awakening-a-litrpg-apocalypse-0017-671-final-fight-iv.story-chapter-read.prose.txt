Malzareth screeched a curse at the disintegrating Mirror Dragon, which Zane ignored.

He just turned on his next target. With a step he was gone.

Stegar was Malzareth's third-strongest Prime on paper—it wasn't a match for the Primes he'd just taken down. And more, it was only a fraction of itself. It only had its single remaining body.

He didn't anticipate that this would be close. He did anticipate it'd have tricks it'd use to survive.

He arrived in front of it just as it activated its Supreme—the distance between them had simply vanished.

He was on it in an instant; he felt its essence surging as it tried to hyper-jump—a familiar Skill by now. Arcane Titan's step canceled the Skill and threw it head over heels.

At this point, in terms of pure speed and reaction speed alike, he seemed a step above anything else on the battlefield—and that included Malzareth, which was unexpected.

It was a wise choice by Stegar to enter its Supreme right away. It'd essentially been forced to.

It was a very rarely used Supreme, perhaps the Supreme Dragonspire knew least about. Stegar had wielded it exactly twice in the past eight weeks.

All the tubes on its body flooded with an immense amount of essence; it super-charged all its functions.

At the same time, he noticed a thick line in the Astral Plane establishing—a soulsilver line between Stegar and its Throne.

Before he could make sense of it Malzareth flooded the air with more spears. He stepped easily out of the way.

Another slight point of surprise—Malzareth hadn't shown many kinds of attacks so far in the fight. He'd expected a little more—a few more attacks on the scale of the ones it'd hurled at the Barbarian Sage, for instance. Especially since he was now closing in on its second Prime.

Was the dragon more injured than it let on? Or was it something else?

In fairness those spear waves were quite formidable; any Dragonspire elite would've had to take them seriously. He was taking them without issue now—he was hardly slowed by them—but a single well-placed wave likely would've downed the Zane of just a few weeks ago.

He still kept a close eye on Malzareth as he moved against its final Prime.

His Axe missed Stegar by hardly an inch. It'd righted itself mid-air, moved out of the way even as his Axe arched toward it, and the next few exchanges were slightly confusing, because it felt like Stegar always started moving exactly as he did, or even a few moments before. It was just enough for it to get out of the way.

Even with Stegar in its Supreme form, the sheer power and speed differential should've made this impossible.

But for those first few exchanges it somehow managed it, and he realized the connection to its Throne flared each time its body moved. And it wasn't essence that moved through that connection, but some kind of soul-material.

At first he figured it'd gained some kind of ability to read Fate, or some kind of future sight. He quickly realized—after seeing it move away the instant he'd formed the intent to strike—that the explanation was rather simpler.

It was either mind-reading or pure prediction.

With its skillset, that meant it was likely simply pure compute.

It had a model of him in its mind, a model that was apparently near perfect, and in whatever Supreme state this was it was simply able to perfectly predict him. Its throne just fed it calculations, and it reacted with impossible efficiency.

There were a few ways to solve this issue, to his mind, but he went with the simplest. He'd realized something else—this was a throne room, which meant it was quite a limited realm. He didn't need to be precise, especially with the differentials at play here.

All he did was spike his Pure Size to its absolute maximum.

And then he simply unloaded slashes. 8-packs of Star-Crushing Slashes, over and over again, in Stegar's general direction—slashes that essentially struck the entire plane all at once.

It felt a little crude, like he was cheating the fight... but then again, this wasn't, in his mind, an issue that required a complex solution.

It truly didn't matter, at some level, what he used.

If he had infinity at his fingertips, he figured he might as well wield it.

The only slightly tricky thing was that he had to interpose himself between Stegar and its Master occasionally, so it couldn't simply hide under Malzareth's shields. The dragon would shield itself, but not its Prime.

Zane's solution had the added benefit of clearing away Malzareth's two remaining Prime Summons. Summons that he'd realized pretty quickly were nonfactors in this fight.

If the differentials between him and the rest of the Primes were like this... he could almost ignore the Summoned Primes.

Malzareth had evidently realized this too; rather than continually shield them from his realm-wide blows, it'd simply de-summoned them.

It also decisively speared Stegar, finishing its own Prime. But a fair fraction of the Prime’s soul still flowed into Zane.

A Prime Endbringer has been slain.

It was a chase that, in the end, did little more than expend his time.

Even still—by the end of it his Supreme bar sat at around 60 seconds' worth of time.

And then there was a single enemy left.

Malzareth didn't re-summon its Prime Summons.

He also noticed two thin golden lines of blood on its neck—wounds he didn't even remember causing... then again, he must've slung out nearly a hundred Star-Crushing Slashes there, and at speed. He supposed it wasn't surprising that one got through.

I believed I could stall you, said Malzareth. Then it made a shattered, discordant sound—a laugh, he realized with surprise. Tell me—what am I supposed to do against this?

He frowned slightly. He couldn't get a read on its attitude; he wasn't sure what he'd expected its reaction to be, but it wasn't this.

He'd thought it'd seem a little more desperate, maybe. Instead it felt strangely resigned.

He approached it slightly more cautiously than he otherwise would—keeping his senses very tuned to his environment.

Slightly more cautiously didn't mean cautiously, though.

He was also quite aware he had less than a minute to execute this.

He stepped, and in the next instant he stood before Malzareth.

In the instant after that, he slashed—and reality warped.

It seemed the closer reality was to Malzareth, the easier it was to warp, since that warping happened nearly instantaneously.

His spear missed.

But it was a localized space-warp, and he was on the dragon again instantly. It halted him for a second with six spears—his Health bar jumped down, then instantly back up. Then he attacked it once again, and it warped once again.

He could catch up to it, but it simply warped too fast. The moment he made to attack it was gone.

His brow furrowed as he considered how it was managing to do this.

It was just reality-warping. No void-walking, no spatial tunneling tricks—without a dedicated portal or an established spatial path, those couldn’t be done here. It was why you couldn't simply void-walk into the Superdungeon, or out of it—its construction didn't allow it. It was meant to be a prison, after all.

Even degraded as it was in this stage of the Chaos Cycle it still maintained that capacity. Its void-walking denial functioned something like Dragonspire's linked Safe Zones in that way.

Malzareth relied simply on its pure reality-warping Skills.

And it managed to take a solid twelve seconds off Zane's Supreme this way.

In that time he didn't land a single strike.

Twelve seconds which felt quite confusing—he felt it shouldn't have been able to do this—until he realized what was happening and turned to its Throne.

Then he annihilated it.

Malzareth tried rather furiously to stop him. It summoned well over a hundred spears in the effort, a dozen of which could've matched the spears that'd taken down the Barbarian Sage.

He'd chosen to tank the lighter ones as he slashed.

In the end the throne was fixed in space, which made it decidedly difficult to defend—especially against someone like Zane, who could get pretty much any angle of attack he wanted on it, and unleash a flood of slashes in a blink.

Three full eight-slash combinations, and he'd cleaved the throne into a dozen or so unequal blocks. It lost its luster in the Astral Plane.

When he turned back to Malzareth, it could still warp quickly. It wasn't instantaneous anymore.

With every engagement, he could now land two or three clean blows.

Now golden blood started flowing in earnest.

As the exchanges piled on, Malzareth began trying Skills it’d never shown before.

Reality hardened around Zane, darkening, as a Corruption-laced sphere started shimmering into existence around him, a sphere the size of a small moon—

Universe Expansion, hissed Malzareth. Nyx Mundi—

Six slashes and he'd carved through it before it could even come into being, and it let out a piercing cry.

It attempted five different forms of soul attacks, as far as he could tell, the bulk of which simply failed against him—they struck his Ironclad Great Sage Mind like hail against a cliff.

It spread a field of curses in front of it that Zane simply side-stepped. It then attempted to land three curses that Zane felt instinctively he'd have to take quite seriously if they struck, but it wasn't anywhere close with them.

It threw Skills at him that he recognized—Skills that belonged to the Mirror Dragon, the Carved Saint's freezing touches, just adapted to its claws, even pillars like Noughtfire's—Skills that'd surprised him, but they were uniformly less successful than its spears. It seemed to perform them simply because it could.

I’m not meant to win. It seemed stunned. It laughed its discordant laugh—Do you understand the absurdity of this? You face a Summoner and a Mental Mage. These are my strongest Skillsets—one you nullify by default. The other empowers you! You see what it is that I am made to resort to?

What is this farce of a Fate?

Zane ignored it. He kept striking.

His Supreme's bar kept diminishing... but at this point it wasn't a worry at all.

He still had two-thirds of it left.

And at every point of contact, his three clean blows only seemed to hamper it more, building upon the body of work, the strikes he'd already put on it as he stalked it across the realm—a series of violent, brilliant teleports flying across the space—

Its physical defenses weren't as robust as the Mirror Dragon's. Which made sense, given its skillset.

You understand, Malzareth said abruptly—even as a new Slash laced into one of its necks—I cannot win. Even if I win, I cannot win.

Zane kept ignoring it.

I was nearly slain by a backwater local, it said. Not even the Universe-class Titled one, at that! And Father's Corps, turned to little more than dust in my hands—it seems Fate wishes to grant me a ritual humiliation. How could I return to Court?

It still seemed far too calm, even as he carved the life out of it.

Perhaps I shall enter exile.I t seemed amused at the thought. Perhaps another Universe is in my future.

He didn't believe it was because it'd lost its mind—he didn't let himself believe that for a second.

You are confident that you have this, it continued. Of course you are. You do not respect me.

Its words came slightly more haltingly now; it winced between some of them.

It ceased fighting as hard as it'd done.

And why should you? All you have seen is uselessness... how could you believe that I constitute a talent that, in another Galaxy, might've brought the Universe to its knees? Does that not seem a joke?

He carved one of its wings clean through.

I had not wished to admit it, it said. But you have the greater gifts, Zane Walker. I shall give you that much credit.

It sighed, seemingly uncaring. By now the realm was lit up gold with its blood; it'd splashed all over the broken thrones, and lines of it were scattered throughout.

It glanced down at its newly wrecked form.

It seems this battle has entered its twilight... already, it has entered its twilight. Let us conclude this farce... Even this is a humiliation. The lengths to which I must go to kill a True God.

Zane's eyes narrowed.

Let me offer you a coin flip in Fate. It still spoke in that slightly detached way. Survive this, and I can do little more than congratulate you...perhaps a coin flip is inaccurate. It is a weighted coin... it is a coin I can only flip once.

He knew it wasn't bluffing.

He didn't quite understand why it was telling him this—unless it was just that certain of itself.

It'd seen what he could do, and it was still that certain.

What is it, it said, eyes gleaming. That you think my Title means?

Its eyes went shining silver.

Zane knew its Supreme. The Skill that'd instantly frozen the entirety of Dragonspire's elite—rendered them essentially helpless. But that was a soul Skill.

But this was a different Skill.

If this does not kill you, it said. Then the task is impossible. And I might find myself at peace, knowing that—there was simply nothing more to be done.

For the next few seconds, nothing changed.

It wasn't some illusion—truly, nothing changed in that floor—but he couldn't have sensed what was coming. The scope of the attack went far beyond that floor.

The folk on Earth saw it, as they saw the Corruption scars on their planet—still lingering from the Monsters' attack—disappear.

The Steelheart disciples at the edges of the Galaxy, working on damaged instant teleporters, saw it—saw the Corruption loaded deep within pulled away by some unseen force.

The Scryers in Command saw it as widely as anyone—could see the scale of it, across the entirety of Dragonspire...

"It's been slain?"

"The signature's still there."

"Then why...?"

Then, gasps—"The Superdungeon—it's scryable!"

And Reina's True Body saw it, standing outside that base floor. Saw the Corruption stains vanishing from the length of the Superdungeon, moving from top to bottom, revealing the original colors of the floors.

"Gods' sake, Lu—he's taking on Malzareth by himself!" said Thalia, teeth gritted. "We'll make it. If he falls in there alone, how am I ever supposed to face Reina?"

The two of them moved through a score of Class II Greater Endbringers with such speed they could've been fighting Ascendants.

Thalia tapped her charm—"We're closing in on your floor, can you be our eyes?"

She was speaking to Reina's avatar—the one higher up in the tower, embedded with the upper-floor units.

But it was silent.

"There!" The Huntress reacted first. She threw out her hands, and four orbs of lightning connected around them, making a cage-shield.

But it wasn't an attack. It passed right through them.

They watched as all the Corruption on the floor wiped away—like a line of System color was crossing the realm at once...

"Shit," whispered the Huntress.

She showed Thalia her scrying glass.

Striker and the Carved Saint had just entered the Mirror Dragon's Lair when it happened.

They felt it behind them and stepped out of the way—and an ocean of pure Corruption rushed past them. Like the Huntress, they set up shields, but it didn't even touch them.

Striker tapped his communication charm, and his eyes narrowed.

He checked his scrying glass and tabbed through, floor by floor.

Where it'd only shown maps of the essence signatures of the lowest few floors before—the best the Scryer's Guild could achieve, which was a slight disappointment—now crystal-clear images were flickering into view.

He saw himself standing in the Mirror Dragon's Lair, a slightly surreal experience.

All this Corruption was drawn, apparently, from all over the realm, though it was difficult to know precisely given the sheer activity on the communication channels.

He saw it all flood through the Lairs, passing easily through the broken floors...

Flooding straight for the final floor.

And for the first time, he—and the rest of Dragonspire—saw.

Saw the image of Zane, standing in the throne room, Corruption wiped off the throne room itself.

As an entire Galaxy's worth of it collapsed on a single man.

And there, in the Throne Room, Malzareth executed its final attack.
