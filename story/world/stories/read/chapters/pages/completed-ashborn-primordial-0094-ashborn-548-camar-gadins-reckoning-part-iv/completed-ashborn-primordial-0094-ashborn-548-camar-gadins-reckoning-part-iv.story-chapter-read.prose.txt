Camar Cadin might have survived, but never had victory felt like such a terrible loss. Not with so many dead.

Not with the rupture in Vir’s chest widening by the second. His prana stopped cycling, and his body no longer responded to his commands.

Vir’s legs failed him. Iteration IV fell away, and Balancer of Scales sputtered out.

The ground rushed up, faster and faster, until he struck the rubble of Camar Gadin, bones breaking as he tumbled to a halt.

He barely noticed it. Not because of any shortage of it, but because every ounce of his attention was focused on the core. On preventing it from outright exploding.

Every second dragged into an hour. Every minute an eternal battle. A battle he was losing as the pain continued to fight his concentration.

Vir attempted everything he knew. From restarting his prana cycling to venting the excess prana as best he could, all to no avail.

Yet just when he thought all was lost, the core abated. Just barely enough for Vir to shunt some prana through his ruined Yuma’s Embrace.

The tattoo functioned at a fraction of its capacity, but it healed him well enough to focus his attention on the core again.

It was a delicate dance. The moment he became slightly distracted, the core flared up, threatening to explode. Vir was caught in an unending war he had no idea how to fix.

Vir finally managed to force himself to his feet, his right arm still ruined and hanging useless at his side.

It was only then that he got a good look at the devastation that surrounded him.

The lance that had hit this district hadn’t left much. Towers had been reduced to slag and the streets were simply gone.

Then there were the dead. They were everywhere, struck down where they had stood. The rest, he knew, had been vaporized. Nothing remained of their bodies.

So many souls. So many who hadn’t made it.

He’d told Thaman to evacuate. Had sent every resource he had to help, and even then, this was the result.

To think he’d promised the Rajas a new era of prosperity. To think he’d told them the worst horrors were behind them. That humans and demons would coexist in peace from now on.

Looking at the carnage and devastation, he had to wonder if any of that was true.

If his words had been a lie, and whether they’d simply traded one evil for another.

No, he thought. I refuse to accept this.

How could he? Every fiber of who he was rejected this reality. Not after all he’d sacrificed to bring about this new world.

Not here. Not like this.

To let so many Bairans die in the streets of their own capital…

And yet what could he do? He could hardly even stand, let alone help rescuers treat the injured.

The tiniest lapse of attention was all it took.

He felt the prana leave him a split second before he understood what had happened.

Thick, liquid prana arced from his body like a snake. Dozens of tendrils, each branching out across the rubble, and Vir realized with horror that he had no control over it whatsoever.

With all his might, he tried desperately to pull it back, but it was useless. The tendrils had a mind of their own, for the prana was no longer his.

Ignoring his will, they speared out into the rubble, and Vir could only gawk as the nearest one speared into a dead Bairan’s chest.

And then Vir lost control of his prana.

From Aspect of the Demon God to Yuma's Embrace, every Ultimate Art inscribed into his skin blazed brighter than they ever had, drawing on the ruptured core in a way Saunak had never intended. Broken as they were, the activation only melted them further, rupturing his very body from within.

Vir dropped to his knees, wailing in agony like a child.

HIs vision began to dim. The inscriptions ruptured entirely, twisting and morphing from their original forms.

And through his blurring sight, he witnessed an impossibility.

For the dead Bairan…

Who’d been a lifeless corpse only moments prior…

Moved once again.

Roshan felt like he hadn’t stopped running for a single moment ever since the evacuation order came down.

He was the Raja's son, after all, and that meant something when said Raja was Thaman Baira. While his father might be best served commanding from the castle, standing idly by while the city was evacuated was not something Roshan could afford.

Where his father had the burden of leadership, he did not, which left him free to make the most impact he possibly could.

Whether that be ending riots among Bairans desperate to beat each other onto an Asuran skyship or creating alternate evacuation routes—often by force.

Several women and children would have been trampled had Roshan not rescued them.

None of it was even close to enough. For every demon he helped, a hundred more clogged the streets, and the death toll climbed ever higher.

Even still, it was something. As his father had taught him since he’d been just a boy, a Raja always rose to the challenge, no matter how daunting.

So Roshan kept moving, forcing himself to focus on the task at hand and not the light show in the sky, where the Akh Nara singlehandedly held off the greatest weapon of the Gods.

Forcing himself not to envy a being with such incredible power. Power that could truly make a difference. While Roshan was busy saving a few dozen souls, he was saving the whole city.

Roshan had just finished punching through the outer wall to open up a new exit path when the hair on his back stood on end.

He felt the air change around him—a wrongness that set every instinct he had on edge.

He looked up just in time to see that the Vimana's great weapon had gone dark, its devastating beam already fading.

All around him, cheers erupted. Roshan stared in disbelief.

“The Akh Nara prevailed!”

Cheers went out one after another, swiftly turning into a victory chant.

Roshan’s worry melted away and his back slumped.

He was halfway through his sigh of relief when a burst of light erupted from the bottom of the Vimana, a dozen slivers of white firing in every direction.

He watched on, wondering whether this was the Vimana’s last gasp.

The Bairans’ chants quickly turned into screams of fear.

“Calm, everyone! Calm!” Roshan cried. “The Akh Nara will protect us!”

His words quelled the panicking mass, but only briefly.

For the Akh Nara was nowhere to be found in the skies above.

It was only then that he began to worry that something had gone terribly wrong. He wasn’t alone.

He tried consoling everyone, saying that the slivers were unlikely to land upon them.

And yet, somewhere deep down, he knew.

That it would land among the demons he’d toiled to save. That the result would be devastating and that there was nothing Roshan could do to change it.

And yet Roshan couldn’t bring himself  to believe that the Akh Nara would ever let such an attack harm them.

It was perhaps why Roshan felt no fear as the light became blinding, consuming all that he saw.

It was why he didn’t panic, even when his flesh began to boil, and it was only when every part of him erupted in agony that he realized the shocking truth.

That Roshan, raised and educated for a long life of service to clan Baira as its future Raja, was about to die. That the few families he’d worked so hard to reunited and the demons he’d saved mattered not one bit. That they were all about to be cruelly erased from the face of the realm.

He’d failed them. He’d failed his father, who was watching from the castle and would see this district vanish. He had failed every demon in Baira, and he wouldn’t ever have the chance to tell them he was sorry.

That he wished he could have done more.

Roshan felt the end coming, and yet he did not pray for himself.

Please, he begged with everything he had to every god he knew. Save them. Take my life, but please, let them live.

His prayer never left his lips. The world darkened and his heart stopped.

His breath halted and the pain disappeared.

The light took him… and Roshan ceased to be.

He’d ceased to be, and yet he could see again. He could move once more.

It was the afterlife, Roshan concluded. His soul being ferried into the great cycle of reincarnation.

And yet, he had to wonder why the afterlife looked so much like a destroyed Camar Gadin, down to the rubble poking at his back and the sun in the sky.

But his chest did not rise or fall as it should.

That’s… impossible.

He sat up, staring at his hands in disbelief.

He touched his chest, finding no trace of injury. No gaping wound nor scar. No beating heart or vibration of any kind.

Only the sensation of alien prana. Prana that was stronger than he had ever known, coursing through every vein in his body, empowering it.

The very same prana that Ash Beasts possessed.

Ash Beasts… and one other.

His prayer, it would seem, was answered.

But Roshan did not dwell on the moment. Did not revel in this new life he’d been given.

Instead, he scrambled to his feet and immediately began searching the wreckage for the others. The families and the others he’d been trying to help.

It was not his own life he had prayed for. If there truly was a god capable of working such a miracle, if his desperate plea had been answered, then there was yet hope.

Hope that they had been granted the same miracle.

And so, despite wishing for it, despite praying for it with all that he was, Roshan stood stricken as he watched them rise.

Not just one or a dozen, but every single one.

Across the ruins of the eastern district, demons punched through rubble, rising like ancestral spirits from the ground. Patting their own bodies and calling out names. Embracing one another in tear-filled hugs.

Roshan knew this not because he saw all of it—that was impossible from where he was standing. No, he knew because he sensed them. A bond that connected him to every other who’d arisen.

To the old red demon woman he’d saved from trampling, staring blankly at her hands, refusing to believe what she saw.

The family he’d reunited, together once again, the mother wrapping her arms around her three children.

But how? Why? And what was to become of him now?

Questions for which he had no answer.

Roshan’s legs went weak. He collapsed to his knees, and his whole frame shook as he wept.

He wept like he did when he’d just been a young boy.

He wept in relief. In awe. In gratitude and a million other things, overwhelmed in every way.

Tears flowed down his face in a raging stream, and they did not stop.

It was why he failed to notice one other thread, not until he regained his wits a long while later.

A tendril of pitch-black prana extending out of his chest, thicker than the others. Running not to the fallen souls, but a figure who knelt on broken rubble, one arm hanging ruined at his side.

Roshan knew him at a glance, even without the pranic thread connecting them.

How could he not?

Every demon in the realm knew the Akh Nara.

But he now realized he had prayed to the wrong gods.

Roshan had prayed to Yuma. To Badrak and Adinat, and even to Janak.

But there was a god who walked among them already, was there not?

One that worked miracles as a matter of course.

For only a god could bring the dead back to life.

Roshan's knees struck the ground once again, but this time it was of his own volition.

He slammed his head against the dirt, his words thundering with confidence even as his body trembled before this great being.

“All hail the Akh Nara! Lord, God, and Savior!”

He was not the only one. The name spread across the rubble like Ashen fire.

Demon after demon turned toward him, chanting his name.

“All hail the Akh Nara! Lord, God, and Savior!”

For the first time in an Age, a deity had blessed them with a true miracle.

And the dead he had given life rose to honor him.
