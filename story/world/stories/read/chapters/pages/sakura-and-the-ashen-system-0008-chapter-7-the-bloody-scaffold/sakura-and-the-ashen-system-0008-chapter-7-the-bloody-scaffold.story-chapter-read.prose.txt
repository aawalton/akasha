Lir did not get the chance to say anything else. One of the guards slammed the shaft of his spear into his chest, not striking him for real, but so hard and without hesitation that the boy staggered back several steps and nearly fell when his heel caught on the uneven stone of the street.

“Hey!” he snapped at once, already trying to lunge forward again. “What the hell are you doing?!”

“I said step back,” the senior guard repeated coldly, without anger. “One more step and you’ll be detained for obstructing the city guard.”

“Go, Lir! Go home!” Sakura managed to say.

She was still standing where she was, feeling the fingers under her cloak go unpleasantly numb. One guard had a hard grip on her elbow, while the second stood so close that the spearhead hung in front of her eyes like a silent warning not to do anything foolish. Lir jerked forward again, but not as confidently this time. He had understood now that this was not random roughness in the street and not an attempt to scare her. This had already been decided in advance, and his only role was to stand aside and stay out of the way.

“She didn’t do anything,” he said now, no longer outraged, almost confused instead. “You didn’t even ask...”

“The one who should will do the asking,” the guard cut him off.

Sakura glanced at Lir. His face had gone almost boyishly helpless. It looked like he had not expected Mebrel to greet them like this either. For one moment she almost felt sorrier for him than for herself: he had come here feeling like he was leading a strange girl into the city, doing one small good thing, and it had all turned out to be far more complicated than that.

“It’s fine,” Sakura said quickly, before he could say anything stupid. “Don’t argue.”

She was not sure it was fine, but right now it mattered more that Lir did not do anything reckless. No one was going to listen to him anyway. And if he started acting the hero in front of armed guards, Brein really might carry out at least half the threats he had made.

Lir clenched his teeth.

“This is bullshit.”

The guards did not let them exchange another word. They led Sakura away at once, quickly and in silence, down a different street, toward the opposite side of where she had assumed the center of the city would be.

Up close, Mebrel looked even grimmer than it had on the first streets. People turned to look, then immediately looked away again. The sight of a woman being marched under guard clearly did not surprise anyone here. Or maybe it happened often enough that it had stopped meaning anything.

A few minutes later the road sloped downward, into an older part of the city where the wooden houses gave way to heavy stone buildings. Sakura smelled the damp before she even saw where they were taking her. The inner yard sat low and dark, as if it had been denied proper light forever. In the middle of it stood the scaffold.

It was not as large as she might have imagined, but it was no less horrifying for that. Wet dark wood, blackened by years of rain, with stains sunk so deeply into it that they looked far too much like old blood for her to pretend otherwise. A heavy beam jutted out at the edge, with a thick rope hanging from it, twisted slightly. On the boards were not only dark reddish-brown smears, but other marks too, yellow, gray, as if everything a person could leave behind before dying had ended up on that wood. At the sight of it, something inside Sakura drew tight into a knot. She could end up on that platform.

Beneath the scaffold was the dungeon: a low stone level with iron doors and barred cells, where almost no light reached. They forced Sakura down the short staircase before she could properly look around. The air below was heavy, damp, and sour, and Sakura grimaced as nausea rose in her throat.

They led her to a solitary cell, opened the door roughly, and one of the guards shoved her inside without wasting words. Sakura stumbled, hit her palm against the wet wall, and jerked away at once because the stone was cold and sticky. The door clanged shut behind her.

“You won’t be bored for long,” the same guard said almost indifferently. “They’ll come for you soon.”

Sakura turned sharply.

“Who?”

She got no answer. The guard was already walking farther down the passage. His footsteps faded above, and then the dungeon filled with other sounds: nasty snickering.

Her cell was solitary, but not isolated. Across the narrow corridor, beyond the bars, were other prisoners. A lot of them. The cell opposite was packed so tightly it looked as if people had simply been dumped into it like old sacks. Men of different ages and faces, some overgrown, some toothless, some bruised. The stench coming from there was so bad that at first she wanted to turn away, and then stop breathing altogether.

“Oooh,” someone rasped. “Look at the little bird.”

“Where’d they drag down one that pretty from?”

“A gift from the holy springs, obviously.”

“Hey, girl, what are you in for? Poison your husband?”

Sakura sat down on the edge of the narrow bench against the wall and said nothing. Her heart was still beating too fast, but her mind had already started doing the thing it did best in moments when panic was useless: analyzing. Cell. Thick bars. No window, just a small vent near the ceiling. Iron door. Narrow corridor. At least two guards above, maybe more. The city outside. Nothing in reach except her own cloak and a body that had only just begun to recover from everything it had been through since yesterday. Chance of escape? Almost zero. But not completely. If they moved her somewhere else, if they opened the door, if they left her alone in the wrong place... No. It was too early to think about escape yet. First she had to understand what they wanted from her.

“Why so quiet, pretty thing?” someone behind the bars kept at it. “Saving that tongue for rich folk?”

“Look at how she wrinkles her nose. Means she’s not one of us.”

“Maybe she’s a demoness. There are plenty of those now.”

At the word demoness, Sakura slowly raised her head. She had held back up to that exact moment because she knew better than to answer that kind of thing. If she reacted, they would never shut up. But right now she felt too disgusted, and far too tired, to put up with strangers trying to entertain themselves at her expense.

“Shut up,” she said quietly.

It did not work the way she expected. Or rather, it barely worked at all.

“Oh,” the same voice drawled. “The little bird’s got a sharp beak too.”

Sakura rose slowly, walked up to the bars of her cell, and looked directly toward the voices.

“I said shut up,” she repeated, harder this time. “Or do I need to explain it in words even you can understand? If I’m a demoness, it won’t take me much to curse you.”

This time the answer was not more mocking laughter, but a chuckle. Low and calm. Nothing like the voices that had been taunting her just a moment ago. Sakura turned her head and only then noticed a man in the far corner of that overcrowded cell, one who had seemed not to exist before. He sat against the wall, slightly apart from the others, in a dark cloak with a deep hood, so still and quiet that until that moment he had simply blended into the shadow. He did not look like part of the rabble.

“Strong character,” he said with a faint smirk. “Sharp. I like that.”

Then he lazily turned his head toward the others.

“Leave the girl alone.”

And they obeyed. It happened too fast, without any real protest. The ones who had been baring their teeth and throwing disgusting jokes a second earlier went silent all at once.

Sakura narrowed her eyes.

“Who are you?”

The man shifted his wrists slightly, and only then did she notice the bracelets on them. White, like bone, or something close to it. Thin dark runes had been carved across their smooth surfaces, and they were clearly more than decoration. Sakura did not look at them for long, but something inside her had already tightened unpleasantly.

“A mage,” he answered simply.

Then he lifted his head and slowly pulled down the hood. His face was narrow and pale, with dark attentive eyes. His hair was white, a little longer than soldiers or craftsmen usually wore it, the color making his pale face look even sharper. It was hard to place his age at once. Not an old man, but not young either. The kind of man who might have been thirty-five.

He looked at Sakura more closely, let his gaze linger on her a little longer than it should have, and gave her a cryptic smile.

“You’re a very interesting case.”

Sakura frowned.

“What does that mean?”

But in the next second she understood on her own that it was a stupid question. He was not going to discuss something like that here, in this stink and damp and in front of a dozen listening ears. And she would not have wanted that either.

The man inclined his head slightly, as if agreeing with the thought she had not said out loud.

“Gilbert,” he said. “You can call me that.”

Sakura hesitated for a second.

“Sakura.”

“Very beautiful name.”

A brief silence followed. The scum near the mage pretended to have lost all interest in her, but Sakura could still feel their eyes on her. She let her gaze move cautiously over Gilbert and tried to do what needed to become a habit in this brutal world: determine his level.

Nothing. Or rather, almost nothing. A faint question mark flickered in the air before her eyes for just a moment, then disappeared.

Sakura blinked. So no. You could not read the people around you that easily. Or not all of them. That knowledge left her both disappointed and even more wary. She tried to check the level of some of the others in the rabble too, but nothing happened. No level appeared.

“You think very loudly,” Gilbert observed almost lazily.

“Then close your ears,” she answered dryly.

His smile widened a little. After that he said nothing more, and Sakura decided not to continue either. Time in the dungeon passed slowly, measured only by distant footsteps, dripping water, and the ache in Sakura’s back. The stink grew weaker and stronger by turns. Somewhere above, footsteps scraped.

Sakura sat in silence for a long time, watching giant centipedes creep slowly along a crack in the wall. They were nothing like the ones she might have imagined in the old world. Too large, too bright. One shone a deep blue, like wet metal, the other greenish with a disgusting oily sheen. They moved carefully, testing the stone with their thin legs, and then one suddenly lunged at the other, and it all happened too fast: a snap, twisting bodies locked together, a crisp rustling sound, writhing little bodies. Sakura watched one devour the other and thought that this world, apparently, had taught even its insects the same simple rule: eat, or be eaten.

Sometimes she glanced at Gilbert. He sat almost motionless. Only once did he open his eyes and catch her looking. Sakura turned away at once.

When they finally came for her, she almost felt relief. Even this was better than endless waiting in a cage beneath the gallows. Two guards opened the door, told her to come out and not waste their time. Sakura obeyed without arguing. Objecting now would have been pointless, even stupid. As she passed the neighboring cell, she glanced at Gilbert. He was no longer smiling, only watching her with that same unnervingly calm look.

Up above, after the dungeon, the air felt almost clean. The scaffold still dominated the yard. They led her by a different route, through inner streets, past storehouses and a well, to an unremarkable building that looked little different from the others on the outside. No luxury and no flags, just gray stone, narrow windows, and a heavy door. But the closer they got, the more strongly Sakura could feel that the place mattered.

Inside it was dry and quiet. They led her down a short corridor, stopped at a door, one of the guards knocked, then, without waiting for an answer, pulled it open and shoved Sakura inside.

The office was more spacious than she had expected. Behind the desk sat a man, and one glance at him was enough for Sakura to understand: the head of the city. He was fat, but not in a soft, prosperous way. More like a man who loved power and food too much. Thick fingers, fleshy cheeks, a heavy chin, small eyes. He wore an expensive but not refined dark coat that showed wealth. And then there was the smile. That was the most unpleasant thing on his whole face, greasy, as if the man was already entertaining himself any time someone ended up at his mercy.

He looked Sakura over from head to toe, leaned back slightly in his chair, and spoke first.

“So then, my little fish, should I have you executed, or give you a chance to explain yourself?” he asked, and let out a nasty little giggle.
