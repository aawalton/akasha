Ferris awoke, gasping for breath and clawing at his neck. It took a couple seconds for him to calm down and realize he was back at Lily House, on the floor in his room. Well, he had never truly left, but his mental body had died and thrown his consciousness back into the real world.

He spent a moment deciding that strangulation was a worse death than being eaten alive, although he did admit to a heavy dose of recent experience biasing him against the faded memory of his first death. At least the first time had ended quicker, instead of lingering as he slowly lost the ability to fight back…

Ferris shook himself and checked on Jade. The slime was awake and moving around, although bubbling unhappily. He got the impression over the bond that the slime had regained consciousness a while ago, but then hadn’t been able to wake him.

Losing a mental body usually knocked someone out for hours, but Jade had recovered faster than Ferris. He had a moment of panic realizing he had been unconscious next to Jade, and then tried to remember the last time he reviewed the safety instructions. It was now early morning, judging by the light cracking through the shuttered window. At least the slime would only have access to his hand, although Ferris would’ve probably died from blood loss while unconscious... He shuddered as he reminded the slime of the rules, attempting not to think about what the demon’s head melting had looked like.

It was odd, Ferris was beginning to rely on Jade intuitively—trust forged through battle. Yet at the same time, he needed to constantly remind himself to be careful. How much of his trust was forced on him by the bond, and how much was his natural feelings for someone that had fought to protect his life?

Jade had died first, after all. Ferris didn’t know how much the slime really understood about life, death, and the soulspace, but Jade hadn’t shown any hesitation to give their life in his defense.

In the end, Ferris had died before recovering the oath statue. He wouldn’t be able to cultivate again for two days… yet he reached for a glimmer of hope.

While he wanted to recover the statue, his more important need was to prevent it from being destroyed. He wasn’t sure, but he had a theory that the tall, lanky demon had been the driving force attempting to break it.

The regular demons had acted simpler: roaming in packs and attacking on sight. They only behaved differently around the fort, and he’d seen the tall demon both giving orders and personally attacking the oath statue.

When Ferris was surrounded, he’d decided to go all in on this theory. It wasn’t a large risk, as he couldn’t escape anyway. Not much to lose. He’d believed since first seeing the chain demons that he would win in a one-on-one fight against any of them.

Well, two on one, counting Jade. The lanky demon had been stronger than the smaller demons, but Jade had tipped the odds in his favor.

If only the chains hadn’t tied him down, Ferris might’ve even gotten away alive. The previous day, that was why he’d let the first chain demon steal his spear, rather than get entangled.

His hope was that without the tall demon giving orders, the others would return to their more basic behavior, leaving the statue alone. It wasn’t the greatest plan, but it was all he had. Besides, his soulspace was both shaped by and reflected his feelings and intent. If I believe it, would it become reality? He wondered.

This is a good thing, he forcefully told himself, the current location of the oath statue is actually perfect. He wanted to keep it from laying down roots, and the wooden frame it was hanging on did that wonderfully. It even had a strong fort protecting it for him. Yes, he resolutely told himself, I won, and the statue is now exactly where I want it to be, safe and protected.

Distracting himself from the nagging doubt, Ferris turned to Jade and maintained his stance.

“We did it!”

“We did?” Jade swirled a bit.

“Yes! Why don’t we go get a snack to celebrate?” Ferris stood.

Jade immediately rippled in pleasure. “Yes!”

Ferris untied Jade’s chain from his dresser, wrapped it back around the core, and then carried the slime out to the table in the common room. It was a bit early for breakfast, but Mother Lily brought out some tea and crackers for them while they waited. Jade was back to the rating game, somehow still remembering it. The slime had even given Ferris a rating for each demon corpse it ate. Apparently, the frogs were much better than either the chain demons or the imps. Although Jade liked that the fatter chain demons had more quantity, the slime said it didn’t change the score. The lanky demon had the highest rating of them all, although Ferris paled a bit at the reminder.

Ferris decided to hang out in the common room that day. He considered a plan for if his oath statue was destroyed. He had felt a connection form when it was created, he expected to feel it severed if it was broken. His best bet would be to immediately seek out Elder Harper and attempt to explain himself. It wasn’t great, but without Uncle Winton around he had no way of escaping the bastion.

After making the brief plan, he decided to actively not think about failure. He had won, after all. There was no need.

Until his mental body recovered, there wasn’t much Ferris could do. He couldn’t go to his job assignment like this. Thankfully once he recovered in two days, he would still have three days left this week to make his quota. He had an appointment with Serena the following day, and no way to contact her to cancel, so he would just have to show up. He could try to think up some experiments related to Jade’s self-healing that didn’t require Ferris to access the slimespace and suggest them instead, hopefully not wasting her time.

He could visit the library. Ferris wouldn’t be able to study the jade slip on crossbows, but there were mundane books available that didn’t cost merit points. At some point, he also wanted to explore the other districts of Clan Hollowfield. Learning their locations and specialties would help him in the future.

Instead of all that, Ferris decided to stay at Lily House for the day. Recently, he had grown more upset and worried about Jade’s memory. Ferris had tried for two days to track how long the slime could remember something, only to be distracted both times. Today, he would stay near the clock to get a better answer.

It had the added benefit of letting him rest. Death in the soulspace was an ordeal. Taking a day to sit around and recover would help. A bit guiltily, Ferris hadn’t given Jade a day off after their mental body died, although he did carry the slime around almost everywhere.

Ferris noted the time and gave Jade more names: two dozen famous delvers from Clan Bluereef. Well, he thought, if not famous then at least they were locally well-known inside the clan. Often on rest days, people would gather and tell heroic or comedic stories of the clan’s past and present warriors. He knew many of the tales by heart and smiled as he pictured some of them. Seeing the larger Scalefort Bastion had shifted some of his perspective, but the delvers from home were still famous to him.

He decided to check every half hour starting immediately, as Jade’s memory seemed to be shorter, or perhaps more random, than he’d originally thought.

The day passed easily. Ferris played a couple impromptu games with Jade, took a nap, went for a walk, ate a small picnic in a nearby park, practiced some of his breathing exercises, and did some light physical training. Jade mimicked the exercises as best as a slime could, wanting to be included.

Best of all, he made it all day without feeling the oath statue breaking. This definitely meant his theory was correct and he had won. Definitive proof, no need to consider or worry about it anymore.

It would have been an idyllic day, if not for the confusing results of Jade’s memory test. It’d been twelve hours, and the slime had perfectly remembered every name.

Sitting at dinner, Ferris was lost in thought of what it could mean. He’d originally believed he had the times wrong, as he hadn’t always had a clock handy to confirm. He now had two days of confirmed memory loss before nine and eleven hours, and one day of retention up to twelve hours. Plus, the confusing memory of the slime ranch lasting much longer. He had checked, Jade still remembered being in the pit with all the other slimes.

Jade was happily eating, oblivious to the conundrum. Brix had taken to feeding the slime, making a game out of tossing pieces of food and watching the ripples. Karl had not shown up for dinner, likely deep in cultivation. Cyrus, recently abandoned by Brix to feed Jade, turned to Ferris and started a conversation.

“Something on your mind?” Cyrus asked, likely due to the lost look on Ferris’ face.

Ferris looked at him. He didn’t want anyone to know about Jade’s memory problems, but he could use some advice…

“Yeah. Hey Cyrus,” he began, considering how to discuss the problem. “You’re a research assistant, right?”

“Research lackey, copying forms and doing grunt work,” answered Brix, still focused on Jade, but not enough to miss an opportunity to poke fun at Cyrus.

“Yes,” Cyrus answered, ignoring Brix, and sitting a bit straighter, “I was recruited by Elder Clarke for my superior insight.”

Ferris considered the glint of light that had appeared in Cyrus’ eyes when he’d introduced Jade. His ‘superior insight’ was likely a useful ability, perhaps useful enough to hire him on as a retainer.

“What do you do when your information contradicts itself?” Ferris asked. That was probably safe enough, although he would likely get generic advice. That was the price of secrecy.

“It could be many things. For example, you could have bad methodology, a false assumption, an unknown bias, too small a sample size, mistakes in collecting information, or even sabotage by another.” Cyrus shot a look at Brix when he said that last possibility. There is a story there, Ferris thought.

“What would a bad methodology look like?” Ferris asked.

“It can be any flaw in your procedures. For instance, once I was asked to measure the temperature of a liquid every ten minutes, but I wasn’t told where to measure it. Due to the nature of the liquid, the temperature was vastly different in the middle than on the outside, giving potentially skewed data. Naturally,” somehow Cyrus sat even straighter, “I noticed the flaw and pointed it out. The procedure was instantly modified to have a consistent location for the test.”

Ferris considered Cyrus’ example. It made him immediately thankful he wasn’t a research assistant. Hemon had proposed Ferris get involved in Hollowfield’s research, but Cyrus made it sound tedious and pedantic. Hopefully there was more to it than that.

As for problems with his methodology, perhaps it was due to the difference in what Jade was remembering? Ferris had picked groups of names he thought he could remember, but maybe he should start testing the same thing every day? Would that cause other problems? What if Jade started to remember them longer due to the same names given every day? It would be hard to keep thinking of new names each time, but maybe he could use numbers instead… Ferris didn’t even trust himself to remember a set of numbers all day. It might still be worth trying, but he would need a way to record them. I can probably afford paper, he thought with a smile.

“A false assumption,” Cyrus continued to explain, despite Ferris not asking beyond the first possibility, “usually means you assumed or were told something, and you believed it without checking.”

That… might be possible, Ferris thought. Foreman Carter had told him that slimes would forget any training the next day, and Ferris had then tried to narrow the ‘next day’ down to a shorter time frame. He had never questioned if it was actually time that made Jade forget. Even the phrase, short term memory, implied that he needed to test duration.

Ferris groaned at this. It meant he needed to start over from scratch, recording anything he could think of that Jade did or had done to them. Then, once he had a couple days of data, he could start looking for patterns. He definitely needed paper. Ferris also eyed Jade, casually rolling around on the table in front of Brix. He’d believed the slime was safe with her due to the time since he had given the safety lecture.

Cyrus smiled, as if he’d won something, but then continued ignoring Ferris’ groan.

“An unknown bias is like a false assumption; except you don’t know you have it. You believe something so inherently that you don’t think to question it.”

Ferris had no idea how he would question what he couldn’t think to question. He hoped it wasn’t this.

“A small sample size giving contradictory data usually means there is a high variation, and you simply haven’t tested enough to see the pattern.” Cyrus waved a hand in the air.

Ferris had only checked three days. Maybe the memory loss was not consistent, but was based on time, and he just needed to keep gathering data to see the bigger picture.

“To err is to be human, sometimes people write down the wrong number. A good research procedure will account for mistakes.” Cyrus nodded sagely.

Ferris doubted this was his problem. You can’t record it wrong if you don’t write it down at all! He thought a bit petulantly. Although realistically, with only three data points, this one isn’t likely.

“Finally,” Cyrus turned to face Brix, “sabotage is when someone relabels your bottles.” He started throwing pieces of food at her with each word. “They. Think. It. Is. Funny. When. It. Is. Not.”

Brix, holding a hand up to block the incoming food, started giggling. “Still worth it,” she said, uncharacteristically not fighting back, “you had blue hair for a week.”

Jade, thinking this was a new game, was more than happy to clean up the food. Brix even picked up and used them as a shield, the slime easily able to consume incoming projectiles while staying firm enough to be held. Ferris wondered if he could someday stop arrows that way.

Overall, Ferris had a lot of ways his test could have been wrong. He needed to try again, with a better approach. Cyrus hadn’t needed specifics to poke a ton of holes in his experiment. At least he was no longer confused why his results didn’t add up…

After dinner, he went to bed early. Jade was secured to the dresser, out of reach. Ferris attempted to cultivate, but as expected he was still unable to enter the soulspace.

It was still nice to meditate. It allowed him to organize his thoughts, and better consider Cyrus’ advice.

If Ferris didn’t understand what he was doing wrong, he just needed to take a step back and reevaluate. Keep collecting information, and look for new ways to test.

It was only a matter of time.
