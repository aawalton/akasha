I watched from the corner of the wagon, exhausted, as Renauld turned one of my finished clavicles over and over in his furred hands.

The last five days…had been a bit of an ordeal for me.

I had never maintained a clone for as long as I had this one. More than that, I had never inhabited a clone body for as long as I had this one.

It was, frankly, a nightmare.

I don’t know what I had been expecting, but it wasn’t the reality of the experience. Perhaps I’d expected it to be a grand old nothing, in the end. A clone didn’t really have muscles that could grow exhausted. A clone didn’t really have a stomach that demanded sustenance. And it certainly didn’t have any lower organ systems that needed to be maintained. Without all the banalities of regular Human existence, even an Awakened one, it should have been a very mild, possibly even boring time in this clone.

It hadn’t been.

It had started setting in on the second day I had spent on this project, when I had settled in to fully enchant my new bones. A quiet sense of unease had begun to roll over me, like there was something wrong with me. A feeling on the back of my neck that something was about to happen, something was out of place, that I was being hunted.

An almost paranoia, I would call it. I wasn’t…totally unfamiliar with the sensation, so I had done my best to ignore it, hoping it would go away.

It hadn’t. In fact, it had begun to grow worse and worse over time. It reached the point where I was only seconds away from a panic attack with full-on hyper-ventilation, and nothing that any of the others on this wagon with me could do anything to stop it. The entire time I had been working in this wagon, Sylvia had been driving it from up on the driver's bench, with Renauld at her side. Fade, of course, was in here. The Spirit Wolf had been napping at the time, only to somehow sense my panic over the bond. He had woken up, and when he couldn’t really calm me down, he had started barking to catch the attention of the others. Renauld had shimmied his way back into the cabin while I was working, while Sylvia manned the reins, all while the wagon was in motion.

He had found me wild-eyed and breathing heavily, staring at him like I was about to lunge and tear out his throat with my teeth.

My fight or flight instinct, you see, tended toward fight.

He’d done his best to calm me down, even asking for help from Sylvia. My lover hadn’t been able to calm me just like Fade had, and, as a last-ditch effort to stop me before I did anything I’d regret…

My Familiar had lunged forward and popped my clone on his lone remaining antler.

The very moment my Outer Lattice had reappeared within my body, startling Core and Middle from their meditations, the panic had stopped.

Just like that, I had been able to think once more.

This was…an odd situation, but I was starting to become an expert at untangling odd situations. After catching Middle and Core up on things, I had started to brainstorm with my other Lattices just what the Hell was going on.

My best theory…had to do with my soul.

Whenever I transferred a single Lattice to clone body, it’s not like I was transferring my soul as well. Only a single third of my Mind with a capital M was being shuffled over. Meanwhile, my Soul with a capital S was remaining within my actual flesh body.

From all of the Aetherial Theory I had learned, a Mind was…not really meant to exist for long, without direct contact with a Soul. Especially not a fraction of a Mind.

I don’t think I had damaged my Outer Lattice being separated from my Soul for the few days I had spent. But it might have been close, and the paranoia feeling that something was wrong, that I was about to die, might have been an early warning system that I was nearing the damage point.

Curiously, this explanation might have been the solution to exactly why my Skills functioned and interacted the way that they did. I had wondered just why The Lattice of Heaven and Earth didn’t allow me to spin up more Lattices like Outer, Middle, and Core, instead supplying me with simple programmable ‘dummy’ minds to shuffle off to Sprite bodies. I hypothesized that it was because those simpler minds weren’t in danger of this almost…desynchronization with my Soul. They weren’t in danger of coming apart, as they didn’t possess the higher mental capabilities of my three main Lattices.

It was possible, even probable, that the System could design each of the abilities that allowed me to create these clones with all of that in mind.

Impressive.

The rest of all this, though, was that I had agreed with the other Lattices that we had a small weakness in our clone Skill. A single Lattice could not exist within a clone body for longer than forty-eight hours before the connection to the Soul started to erode, and then the Lattice needed to return back to the body.

Not a huge flaw, but an inconvenient one.

With that settled, I had created another clone and transferred the Outer Lattice back to it within half an hour after Fade had popped the old clone. I’d needed to provide a hasty explanation to the wary Spirit Wolf and concerned Renauld about what I had realized, and then…

By order of both Renauld and Sylvia, I had needed to start taking regular breaks back to my main body.

But, with all of that said…

I had done it, and the proof now rested in the furry palms of Renauld.

“It looks about right,” The Gnoll eventually said.

I restrained the urge to sigh. “Thank you for the glowing review,” I said sarcastically. “I know it looks right, but will it do the job?”

I nearly had a heart attack when Renauld casually tossed the hard-forged pseudo-bone over to me in response. If I had a heart right now, I’m sure it would have been pounding as I carefully caught it out of mid-air. I fixed Renauld with a caustic glare as I almost cradled the fruits of the last near week of labor close to my chest. It was never in any danger of being hurt, of course. This thing was forged from the strongest metal I had ever seen outside of Primordium, and layered with enough Enchantments to choke a donkey. The rickety floorboards of a wagon weren’t going to damage it.

But it was the principle of the matter.

“Yup,” Renauld popped the word smugly. “They’ll do. Now that you’ve done the easy part-”

I barely restrained the urge to strangle him.

“-we just need to recruit whatever Preceptor that Bleddyn has at their base, set up a clean room if he doesn’t have one already, and then we can get started on the actually difficult part. The surgery. In case you hadn’t already realized that.”

I openly scowled at the Gnoll’s faux-helpful tone. “I loathe you.”

Renauld just winked back at me.

Contrary to what I had said, though…

I was actually a bit relieved. Renauld seemed, to my eyes at least, to be feeling better after everything that had happened with Marcel. After knowing him for so long, I had come to realize that when Renauld was feeling down, he was moody, angry, and a bit alcoholic. But when he was in better spirits?

He was sarcastic, blunt, and yet, oddly playful. The Healer seemed to have settled into a middle-ground between both extremes and appeared to be coping with his ‘loss’, such as it was.

Good. Even beyond the upcoming surgery, I was going to need his help in the near future.

Our little back and forth was cut short when a banging sound rang out from the front of the cabin, caused by a metallic fist. “Finish up in there!” Sylvia hollered over the clatter of the wheels. “We’re nearly there! I can see the front of the procession already starting to slow!”

Sylvia’s words were like a bolt of adrenaline down my non-existent spine. I exchanged a glance with Reanuld, spared another toward the napping form of Fade, and then made a decision.

Slipping my precious, precious bones into an inner pocket of my repaired coat, I approached the side door of the wagon, opened it up, and then stuck my head out the side.

Today was the day that we were going to be reaching Addersfield. Not even today, really.

We were only a few minutes out, and I had only truly finished the ‘bones’ an hour ago. Renauld had shuffled into the back of the wagon to render his judgment, something I had appreciated. It let me focus more on him being a shit, instead of my apprehension of returning to Addersfield.

Even if it was going to be underneath it.

Sylvia was right, though. Our position in the procession was far back enough that I couldn’t see our destination, over the seemingly endless train of carts and wagons ahead of us. But I could see a gradual slowing in the traffic in the distance. This refugee train was far, far bigger than the Herztalian expedition had ever been alone. At our peak, I would have put us at maybe two-thousand strong.

This, though?

Easily three times that.

For as much as I might have failed to save the city of Rhoscara itself…I had managed to jump-start the saving of much of its populace. That felt good, I didn’t mind admitting.

It was an incalculable loss to lose a city as ancient and storied as Rhoscara. But a city wasn’t just a place.

It was a people. If we had managed to save enough of the Rhoscarans, one day…one day they could rebuild.

Hopefully.

We would just have to ensure that they had a living planet to do so on in the first place.

I clambered out the door, edging along the railing of the wagon until I reached the steps leading up to the driver’s bench. As I reached it, I heard Renauld follow after me. I climbed the steps and settled on the bench next to Sylvia, leaning over and pressing a brief kiss to her cheek as I did. The Sculpted woman accepted it tolerantly before handing me the reins to the horses trotting along in front of us. I took them without complaint as Sylvia relaxed to my left, and Renauld flopped down to my right.

There in the middle of the driver’s bench, I directed our wagon down the stone-tiled path of the Long Road. Gradually, the slowdown of the procession reached us as well, and I eased the horses back. Before long, we had come to a complete stop, smack dab in the middle of the road, surrounded on all sides by other wagons. Looking around, I noticed we seem to have stopped in the middle of an Elven contingent.

I’m not sure I liked the way the chamels were eying our horses. The horses didn’t either, from how they were cantering.

Luckily, we didn’t have to wait for long. Members of the Unchained had started to thread through the ranks of the procession, and they seemed to be directing individual carts and wagons to where they should go. The line was starting to move in front of us, and I was…a bit startled to see who had come to get us.

The eternally shirtless form of Bleddyn…carrying what looked to be a clipboard.

My friend had climbed the steps on the side where Sylvia sat and just shrugged unrepentantly at our combined stare. “Just cause I’m the leader doesn’t mean I don’t carry my weight.”

I rolled my eyes at him. “Alright, whatever. Where are we going, o’director?”

…………………………………

It took another few hours before the procession was settled, and then…we were finally in the hidden main base of the Unchained.

I couldn’t help but outright gape at what it turned out to be.

“Anyone else getting deja vu?” Renauld muttered apprehensively to my side. Sylvia furrowed her brow at him, before it rose in surprise at my equally shocked expression.

She didn’t understand. She hadn’t been there.

This…was the single largest cavern I had seen…

Since our trip into the belly of the depths of Mt. Goryuen.
