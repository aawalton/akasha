“I really wish I could make some kind of emergency healing artifact for Cora,” I said after Stephen and I parted from the Arkenesis couple. “I’m not too worried about Mike since Cora can heal him if he gets injured, but if something takes out Cora…”

“You’re worried about the Night Stalkers, aren’t you?” Stephen asked, reaching out for my hand.

“A little bit,” I admitted, leaning against his muscular chest. “I get that they tend to stick to specific areas of the floor, but the Night Stalker we encountered at the beginning of our delve proves that they don’t always stick to specific territories.”

“You should have more faith in Mike and Cora. They’re both experienced enough to stay safe.”

He wasn’t wrong.

Mike had magic-based armor on top of physical armor, and Cora had exceptional stealth skills she could use to avoid notice. Her Shadow Clone also served as a good decoy, so I really wasn’t overly worried.

Still, it seemed a little imbalanced that the Arkenton dungeon leaned so heavily toward stealth-based ambush predators. Having at least one per floor seemed a bit excessive. It might even explain why the city was smaller than a silver-ranked dungeon should normally warrant.

I couldn’t help but want to ensure my friends were safe when they inevitably delved without us. But I’d already tried recreating my emergency healing talismans since evolving, and no matter how hard I tried, I could not get the enchantments to take.

Even trying to layer my intentions and the effects wasn’t enough to get the enchantment to work. Ultimately, I had to admit that I just didn’t have the mental strength necessary to create such a complicated effect.

Leaning back so I’d look up at him, Stephen asked, “Do you think we’ll actually get to see a demonic incursion this week?”

“Maybe,” I said without much feeling. “They supposedly breach within the monitoring area once or twice a week, so it seems likely.”

“With such wide coverage, it’s really surprising the cities in this region have to worry about attacks at all,” Stephen mused.

I headed over to the table and started pulling plates covered with food from my storage.

“Yeah, but it’s not like they catch everything. And even if they do notice an incursion, unless there is a Porter on call who can reach that particular location…”

“I get it. There’s only so much they can do,” he said, taking a seat.

Truthfully, I suspected the goddess would interfere if an Incursion Response Team became too effective at repelling demonic incursions. While I could certainly be wrong, I got the feeling that the goddess was using the threat of demons to motivate her people to progress and eventually ascend.

Nothing else really made sense. I mean, it wasn’t like she couldn’t reinforce the dimensional barrier protecting this realm from incursions. She was a goddess, after all.

The very fact that incursions were happening in the first place was a bit suspicious. The fact that Dia had used demonic incursions as a pretext for regressing the realm’s technological advances was also questionable, leading me to think the goddess wanted demons to attack her realm.

To the immense frustration of the instructors managing our class (and many of the students), not a single demonic incursion occurred within the areas being monitored during the entire week we were on standby.

That is not to say no incursions or demonic attacks occurred within the realm. The realm was entirely too large for such a thing not to happen. However, any attacks that did occur happened outside of the area being monitored, so the IRT was unable to respond.

Ultimately, the week and the class ended with little fanfare. Everyone, including Stephen and me, was added as temporary ‘shadows’ to different Incursion Response Teams based out of Portal.

There was no requirement that we respond when our assigned team was on standby or responding to an incursion, but we would be informed and allowed to accompany them if we were able to make it to the rendezvous point fast enough.

Eventually, we would be assigned to permanent teams based out of Sorenguard. Or, well, most of the class would be. I somehow doubted that either Stephen or I would be included, considering our lower levels.

The fact that we planned to relocate closer to the city of Portal didn’t even factor into the matter, given our ability to teleport on a moment’s notice. Whether a city away or half the world apart, as long as we had a waypoint, it was all the same.

“What an anti-climactic end to the training,” Stephen said with a sigh as he plopped onto the couch in our apartment.

We were only planning to remain there for a few more weeks before officially relocating to Arkenton to be near Mike and Cora now that our training was done.

“I was really hoping to at least get to see what an incursion looked like,” Stephen added.

“Me too,” I agreed. “I almost expected they’d take those of us with porter skills out to establish a few waypoints while we waited.”

The prospect had been mentioned quietly by one of the instructors, but nothing had come of it. I could only assume that someone higher up the chain had rejected the suggestion, though I couldn’t really imagine why.

It wasn’t like any of us would attempt to combat an incursion on our own or anything.

“I’m surprised they didn’t, honestly. It seems like a bit of a waste.” He picked up the information tablet on the table. “Maybe we should explore the area around Sorenguard a bit and try to find a few strategic observation points of our own.”

“Like the ones the Porters mentioned they had throughout their monitoring area?” I asked, sitting down next to him.

The instructors had mostly described elevated locations that provided a clear line of sight across huge distances. Ideally, those locations would be under the protective aura of a dungeon to prevent any breaches from opening on top of them, though even that didn’t ensure they were necessarily safe.

“Exactly,” Stephen said, turning on the tablet. “We could travel around on our next day off and see what kinds of places we can find. Based on what we learned in class, we should be able to figure out the most likely incursion locations, so if we can find a few spots overlooking those places, we’ll be ahead of the game when the local IRT becomes active.”

“You wouldn’t rather wait until the people in charge figure all of that out?” I asked.

Shrugging, Stephen replied, “I mean, we can if you want, but I thought it might be a fun excursion for the two of us. I can fly in my mirror falcon form, and you can do that chain teleportation thing you do sometimes. As long as we’re both using stealth, it should be pretty safe.”

Truth be told, the thought of exploring more of the world outside of established channels sounded like fun. Sure, it was a bit risky, but no more than anything else we might do.

And though it had been tedious at the time, I often thought back on my time spent scouting my old realm with fondness. There was something uniquely appealing about exploring new places without a guide.

“We can do that, I guess,” I said. “You want to go tomorrow?”

Though we normally delved every weekend with Mike and Cora, because our schedule had gotten a bit messed up due to the class, we’d decided to take this weekend off to relax instead.

“Really?” the shifter said, looking unusually excited. “I was thinking you’d probably want to wait until next week, but tomorrow would be perfect! Let me pull up some maps and see if I can find likely incursion locations. We can aim for locations with nearby mountain ranges to start.”

I chuckled as Stephen dove into the new task. I could understand why he was so excited about the idea. Truth be told, I felt the same way.

Exploring the realm had always been one of my goals. The thought of paving our own path and discovering new or little-known locations was immensely appealing. It would also expand our Porter network, which was something the local branch had asked us to do.

With that thought in mind, I suggested we stop by the Porter Guild before our excursion to accept the quest to expand the local network. That way, our efforts would be rewarded while also ensuring the guild had an idea of where we were in case something unexpected happened.

Once the paperwork was complete, Stephen and I set off, eager to explore more of our new world.

B1 Epilogue

“Are you really going to allow the new Space Magi to take part in IRT activities?” Mathias asked the Guild Leader. “It seems too risky to me.”

“She is only supposed to be involved in dungeon relocation activities,” Solan replied before adding, “At least, for now.”

“And what about her partner? The report I read indicated that he had been trained for actual breach closure. For someone who has barely reached bronze rank… Are you trying to get him killed?”

The Guild Leader shook his head and shot a wounded look at his closest friend.

“Of course not. But I can’t say that I disagree with having him trained as a closer. He’s got the right skills to make the role work.”

“But he’s bronze-rank,” Mathias repeated.

“And that’s why he won’t be taking part in any active missions outside of his role as oversight until he gets another evolution or two under his belt – just like his counterpart.”

“I didn’t see any mention of Emie being trained in how to manually close breaches,” Mathias commented, flipping through the most recent report.

“That’s because she hasn’t been trained in such. I might be old, but I’m not stupid. The last thing we need is for another Space Magi to go missing.”

“On that topic, there’s still no word from Daver, though we’ve had IRT members send message capsules through the last few breaches before they closed, just in case he actually did get pulled into one and managed to survive. We’re not sure if he’d even be able to get the messages, though, since we couldn’t get the Enchanter Guild to put their special enchantments on the artifacts.”

“That’s no surprise,” the Guild Leader said. “We both know they have strict rules about how they can use their knowledge. Short of some kind of divine intervention, I don’t see that changing.”

The men grew silent as they pondered the limitations the goddess had imposed upon the realm after the arrival of the demons.

Solan’s maternal grandparents had actually lived during the time before the demons had started invading. They’d been quite old when they’d had his mom, and she’d had Solan after reaching silver rank and deciding to give up the delver lifestyle.

Solan himself was quite old – old enough to clearly remember the stories his grandparents had told him about Vasillios prior to the demons’ arrival.

That was how he knew about the existence of Divine Enchanting – a technique that used to be the predominant form of enchanting until records of it had all but been destroyed during the early years of the incursions.

The city of Portal itself was a testament to what was possible using the ancient method, and only a dozen or so Enchanters still existed who were capable of repairing or recreating such feats.

That had, apparently, been by design. At least, that’s what his grandfather had always told him. And since his grandfather had been one of the very few Enchanters capable of using Divine Enchanting, Solan was inclined to believe him.

In fact, the existence of Divine Enchanting itself was something of a secret, even if many of the older platinum and mithril-rankers were well aware of it.

“Perhaps I can talk to the Enchanter Guild again—”

“There’s no need,” Solan interrupted. “Their decision won’t change. They’d never risk the goddess’s wrath for a single person, especially when there is no certainty that Daver is even still alive.”

“I’m sure he is,” Mathias countered. “We both know that Daver is too competent to have died at the hands of a single incursion force.”

“Unless they had a champion,” Solan pointed out.

“If they had a champion, I highly doubt Daver would have gotten anywhere close without a full team as backup.”

“We cannot discount the possibility that they managed to catch him by surprise. As much as I want to have faith in the man, we need to start accepting the fact that he might never return.”

The pair grew silent as the thought of their friend’s potential demise weighed on them both.
