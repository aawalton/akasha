[You think the Apostle is actually the Abyss itself, Master?]

“Yes,” Viktor said as he dropped heavily into the nearest chair.

He had just made it back to the Core Room from the fourth floor, drenched in the stench of blood and scorched flesh, his sword arm throbbing with every heartbeat, aching from somewhere in the shoulder and pulsing all the way down to the five fingertips. He drew a long breath, then let it out slow.

“If he were, then many strange things about him would suddenly make sense. His knowledge, for example. The man seems to know the God of Death and his followers a bit too well. I already suspected he knew as much as Nephra or the Eternal Flame. Now, if he really is the Abyss, an Ascendant who has walked this world for millennia, then such knowledge is only to be expected, isn’t it? And then, there’s the matter of that power...”

[You mean what he used to stop me teleporting Khenemhotep away?]

“Yes.”

He had been puzzled by it at the time, because it had absolutely nothing to do with all the flesh-weaving and bone-armoring and tentacle-sprouting the Apostle had been so fond of. But now, he saw the truth. That was not the power of the puppet, the human Reliquary. No, it came from the puppeteer himself.

The Abyss was, after all, a Dungeon Core. And an extremely powerful one at that. So it was not a stretch to suppose that such a being could exert control over other dungeons to an extent, the same way a king might walk into a lesser lord’s castle and give orders to the people living inside it.

Fortunately, it seemed he could only do so to the immediate surroundings of his puppet, and only for a very limited time. A small mercy in an otherwise deeply unsettling revelation. And the puppet in question had been destroyed, so with any luck he could no longer pull that trick here again. Probably.

Viktor sighed.

So this is the power of an ancient Ascendant, huh? The man had merely used a fraction of his might, and he had come within a hair’s breadth of wiping out everything in the dungeon single-handedly. Viktor didn’t want to imagine what his full strength would look like.

[I wonder why he chose to intervene so directly.]

A very good question. If the Abyss could simply reach out and do this sort of thing whenever the mood struck him, why had he waited until now? If he had come, say, six months ago, then Viktor would have been completely powerless to stop him. Or, for that matter, he could just come back right this moment, wearing a different man’s face. There would be no way for the dungeon to fend off the attack.

Perhaps, there was something that held him back, some sort of restriction that kept him from doing things the way he wanted.

“That we have to ask Nephra.” Viktor snorted. “Pretty sure she’s the one who set the whole thing up.”

[I do wonder what their relationship is like.]

“Both are Ascendants who have been around for thousands of years, so I guess they’re equal in power. And they both serve Nakhran. But I don’t think that makes them friends. More like two people who happen to want the same thing for the time being, and are willing to work together for as long as that lasts.”

But of course, he had no idea what either of them was truly after, nor had he anything on which to base a guess.

Well, actually—

“I believe the Mastermind who orchestrated my death three hundred years ago also wants me to reclaim my Thaumaturgy. I don’t know why, but that’s what they want. Hence, until I reach that point, they will continue to ‘help’ me get stronger. However, they won’t hand power to me on a silver plate. No, they expect me to work for it. Bleed for it. That’s why they’re going to lay down a series of trials for me, challenges that are nasty enough to kill me outright if I put a foot wrong.”

[And that is where the Abyss comes in.]

“Exactly. The guy has a perfectly good reason to want me dead. Because I could become a genuine threat to him in the future. Every step I take down my Path is a step toward being a problem he will have to deal with sooner or later, so he’d rather do it now, before I have the teeth to bite back. On the other hand, it’s exactly because I can’t resist him on my own at the moment that the Mastermind will need to put a leash on him so that I can have a chance to win.”

What a stupid game. He wanted no part in any of this, though it didn’t look like they cared to ask for his opinion.

[Which means... since we have defeated him, we won’t have to fight him anymore for the time being. Am I correct, Master?]

“Yes. Let’s hope so.”

This whole competition was Nephra’s idea to begin with, so he doubted that she would break her own rules. Six members to a team, and that was that. The Abyss couldn’t just barge in again wearing a new set of borrowed skin. And, out of the six Iskora brought, five had perished. Which left exactly one remaining.

And so, Viktor closed his eyes and sent his vision to the Great Hallway once more, where he found the battle between Galatea and Xander had wound down toward its inevitable conclusion.

The bastard was no longer blinking around. Instead, he was forced to stand his ground, trading blow for blow with the armored construct. It seemed his winged boots had a limit to their use, and they had finally run dry. And without that trick, the bastard couldn’t run anymore. Dead meat. Good.

Xander was breathing hard, shoulders rising and falling with each ragged pull of air. His sword-hand trembled, the flaming blade unsteady in his grip. Even though he hadn’t suffered any serious injury yet, Viktor could tell this fight might not last longer than five more exchanges.

[Master, Galatea is asking what she should do next.]

Huh?

Viktor frowned. What kind of question was that? Was there anything else to do other than finishing off the enemy?

But then, he realized what she meant. Galatea knew Xander was the one responsible for what had been done to Claire, so she was asking whether he wanted the pleasure of putting down the mad dog himself.

Viktor chuckled. That woman always went out of her way to do the most unnecessary thing, didn’t she?

Well, the answer was a resounding yes, but... Unfortunately, there was a complication. Something considerably more pressing and dangerous than any revenge he might fancy.

Khalid had told Xander: “...You’re going to meet our superiors tomorrow. They... they’ll teach you the Forbidden Words. In the worst-case scenario, use them.”

The Forbidden Words, yes, one of the two weapons the Children of the Abyss employed for this invasion. The other was the Apostle, whose terrifying power Viktor had already experienced firsthand. And this one was an even greater mystery, since the Eternal Flame didn’t know anything about it at all.

He let out a sigh. There was a good chance this battle was merely halfway done.

Naturally, the best course of action was to kill Xander before he could use his secret weapon. But what if the attempt failed? Viktor had no intention of getting anywhere near the bastard before he knew with absolute certainty what exactly he was dealing with.

“Tell her to finish him off. As fast as she can. As surely as she can. Don’t think about anything else.”

[Understood.]

The next second, Galatea’s hammer struck Xander square in the chest. The bastard went flying, crashing hard against the ramp, his sword spinning out of his grip and clattering across the stone.

Before he could scramble to his feet, a volley of arrows peppered his body, sinking deep into his shoulders, his chest, his thighs. At the same time, silver-armored soldiers and hulking tomb guards converged from all sides, eager to hack the bastard to mincemeat. Galatea lunged as well, her hammer raised high, throwing the full weight of her towering construct behind a strike meant to crush his skull and put an end to this whole sorry business.

“Sva...” Xander rasped, his voice barely a whisper, cracked and half-swallowed by the blood pooling in his mouth.

The suit of armor that housed Galatea’s skull froze mid-lunge. The hammer was still held above her head, but she wasn’t moving at all.

“...rog.”

“Are you insane?” Galatea shouted. There was an edge to her voice Viktor had never once heard from her before, something that could only be described as dread. “Do you have any idea what you’ve just done?”

What? She knew what it was?

The next instant, before Viktor could pursue that thought any further, Xander’s flaming sword exploded. The blast threw everyone nearby off their feet, Galatea and the Templars and the tomb guards, all sent crashing into the surrounding walls. Everyone, that was, except the bastard closest to the explosion. Xander himself didn’t so much as sway. Instead, he caught the fire pouring off his destroyed weapon, his whole body going up in flame.

And the fire kept growing. Bigger, hungrier. The heat was so severe the stone touching it went soft and ran like wax. The molten rock was then drawn into what had once been the spike-haired bastard, and the whole mass—fire and magma together—swelled and swelled with every breath.

“Celeste. Get everyone out. Now.”

[Yes, Master.]

The mass kept expanding before Viktor’s eyes, filling more of his vision with each heartbeat. At this rate, it would soon consume the entire Great Hallway. And what would happen after that? Was it going to eat through the tomb itself from the inside out?

What the hell was going on? All of this, the whole cascading catastrophe, had happened just because Xander spoke that.

Sva—

As Viktor attempted to recall the word, his own thoughts flinched away from it like a hand yanked back from a flame. Something in him was shouting to stop. Screaming that this was a road with nothing good waiting at the end of it. And that partial word itself, that mere fragment, incomplete and half-thought, had nearly drowned him. His senses had been overwhelmed, flooded with something too vast to process. Terror and wonder both at once, tangled up so tight he couldn’t tell where one ended and the other began.

And, he remembered what Haku had once told him.

Is that, Viktor thought, the true name of a god?
