“What do you mean, ‘killed’?” Veronica asked. “Who killed them?”

Perri slowly turned, her eyes locking onto the castle in the distance. “The Last Gold.”

Veronica and Gwen glanced at each other briefly before returning their attention to Perri.

“Who is the Last Gold?” Veronica asked.

Perri faced her. “That is what we call the last survivor of the gold ones. The Last Gold.”

Veronica raised a brow, her question not really answered. “Why did he kill the other Asvarians?”

“In the past, when they all arrived, they wreaked havoc on the island. Tore the place apart, collecting every resource they could find.” She crossed her arms, floating a little higher. “Killed a lot of us, too. That’s when Morgan decided to seal off the island. Avalon became a prison for them. Over the next several decades, they became… crazy.”

“I’d probably become crazy too if I was stuck on an island forever,” Theo added.

Perri nodded. “One of them, however, was even crazier than the others. He killed the rest and did something with their bodies. I don’t know exactly what, but afterward, that man began to look younger, while all the other bodies shriveled up.”

Everyone’s eyes widened slightly.

“He… became younger after killing them?” Gwen asked slowly. She turned toward Veronica. “Hey… are vampires also…?”

Veronica shook her head. “I don’t know much about the Asvarians, and I know even less about vampires. As far as I’m concerned, they’re myths.”

“I’m sure a lot of people thought unicorns were myths, too. But we saw plenty on the island when we came through the barrier,” Nasha said.

“Right… that’s a good point,” Veronica admitted after a moment.

Vampires were mainly creatures of story. Humanoid beasts that feasted on blood, often described as pale and unusually handsome, with several traits associated with bats. However, no one had ever seen a vampire in modern history. The only accounts came from ancient records and fairy tales.

“If it’s the Asvarians, they’re super old and crazy obsessed with magic and stuff, right?” Gwen asked. “What if they actually are vampires? That might explain why they know so much. I’m sure being immortal would help with that.”

Orban groaned. “Seriously? I’ve been havin’ a hard enough time keepin’ up, but now you’re all suggestin’ that not only are those gold-plated bastards here, but vampires might be real too?”

“Gold-plated bastards?” Nasha asked with a raised brow. “You know about them?”

Orban crossed his arms with a huff. “Know ’em? Hah. Every dwarf despises them. In the past, our ancestors were forced to create their armor for ’em. We don’t do that anymore for obvious reasons.”

Veronica turned back to Perri. “So… the Last Gold killed the rest of the Asvarians and somehow stole their life force?”

Perri nodded. “I was too young to remember much at the time. But according to my mother, after he killed the other gold ones, several of Avalon’s guardians tried to lure him inside the castle. Morgan had given them one last mission. If all of the gold ones entered the castle, Phillus would activate the second seal she left behind.”

She paused.

“Since the Last Gold had already killed the others, Phillus and several more guardians lured him inside instead. Phillus managed to escape and activated the seal, trapping the Last Gold inside the castle. But in doing so… he was also forced to trap several others who stayed behind to keep him there.”

“Phillus?” Veronica repeated. “Who is that?”

“The Great Stag. He is the current guardian of the Lightless Spring.”

Veronica narrowed her eyes.

The Lightless Spring… it sounded familiar. What exactly was—

Sage conveniently opened her status screen, displaying its description.

According to Morgan’s book, there existed a place called the Lightless Spring several kilometers east of the castle. It was where she had treated the legendary warrior after he arrived on Avalon near death. According to her, the spring’s water possessed powerful revitalizing properties capable of healing even otherwise fatal injuries.

Veronica thought that over for a moment.

Not only was something like that immensely valuable, but it could prove even more useful to Gwen if the water could serve as an ascension material later.

“Then the reason you want us to reach the castle…” Gwen said softly.

“My dad was one of the guardians of Faewood,” Perri said. “He stayed behind with the others to lure the Last Gold into the castle. I want to check on him and everyone else who was trapped inside. And I want to know whether the Last Gold has died or not.”

Everyone stayed silent for several long moments.

Their expedition had changed substantially from what most of them imagined.

A few traps, maybe a fight against a monster or two, and some mysterious puzzle. That was what most of them had expected from a trip to an ancient island like Avalon. But now, if they wanted to break into the castle and reach the treasures and forge within, they might have to face an ancient Asvarian who may or may not still be alive.

If the legends surrounding vampires were somehow true, all of them would be severely outclassed in a direct fight.

Veronica pursed her lips. “If the Asvarian is still alive… he might at least be in a weakened state. If he’s been trapped inside the castle for hundreds of years without anything else to absorb life from, then he’s either already dead or probably too frail to even move.”

“I wouldn’t underestimate those psychos, girlie,” Orban grunted. “Far as I know, they’ll do anything in the name of greed. Even at death’s door, they might have some trick to keep themselves alive. I wouldn’t be surprised if the one in the castle learned necromancy and turned his own body into a lich while waitin’ for the day he could escape.”

“Can Asvarians even become liches?” Theo asked.

Orban shrugged. “Hell if I know. That ain’t the point.”

Veronica looked toward the distant castle again. “Do you know how strong he is? The Last Gold?”

Perri shook her head. “No. It was a long time ago.”

Veronica frowned. “How long have they been imprisoned?”

Perri tilted her head and hummed. “I was still very young around the time, but… hm… I think it’s been about four hundred years now?”

“What?” Gwen blurted. “You’re over four hundred years old?”

Perri stared at her. “Yeah. What about it?”

Gwen’s mouth remained open, speechless.

“Wait, then you’re saying the Asvarians have been stuck on Avalon for over four hundred years?” Veronica asked.

“I think so.”

Nasha looked over at Veronica. “Asvarians are humans, right? People can’t live that long.”

“I’m… not sure,” Veronica admitted. “They were an ancient civilization. There’s definitely evidence suggesting at least some of them were human, but they could have been another race entirely. There’s plenty of surviving history about their magic and achievements, but not much about the people themselves.”

“But Perri said those guys went crazy after a few decades, right?” Theo added. “That has to mean they’re human. Dwarves and elves live longer than humans, so fifty years or so shouldn’t be that bad, right?”

“Ain’t that simple,” Orban grunted. “Yeah, we dwarves can live up to four hundred years. But that don’t mean fifty years of absolutely nothin’ wouldn’t drive me insane. You wanna try spendin’ fifty years trapped on one island?”

Theo rubbed the back of his head. “I guess… you got a point there, Master.”

Veronica turned back toward Perri. “You said this Phillus—this Great Stag—helped trap the Last Gold?”

The fairy nodded.

“Then he probably knows more about the Last Gold than anyone else still living on Avalon, right? Maybe even more about the Asvarians themselves?”

“Probably.”

“And he’s still alive?”

Perri nodded again.

Before Veronica could continue, the fairy hesitated.

“If… you want to know more about the gold ones, he has one of them.”

Veronica blinked. “What?”

“One of the dead golds. He has one of their bodies. He took it after the Last Gold killed them.”

That immediately caught everyone’s attention.

“He kept one of their corpses?” Nasha asked.

Perri nodded. “He thought it might be important sometime in the future.”

Gwen turned toward Veronica. “If you have the body, you can analyze it, right? Maybe figure out what the Last Gold actually did to them. Or at least whether the Asvarians were human.”

“Could still be a lich,” Orban cut in. “Those crazy bastards would do anything for knowledge, even if it meant givin’ up their own bodies.”

Veronica crossed her arms for several moments as everyone continued talking.

If the corpse had been preserved well enough, Sage might be able to determine what species the Asvarian had been, along with whether anything unusual remained inside its body. More importantly, they could potentially learn how the Last Gold had killed him in the first place.

And Phillus himself had actually been there.

Morgan’s journal could tell Veronica what happened before she left Avalon. Phillus could tell them what happened afterward.

Opening the castle blindly when they had an opportunity to learn more about whatever might still be trapped inside would be needlessly reckless.

Veronica turned to everyone. “Let’s go to the Lightless Spring. We’ll talk to Phillus and inspect the body before we even think about opening that castle.”

Everyone stopped talking and looked at her.

No one had any complaints.

Veronica turned to Perri. “Will you guide us there?”

The fairy flew a little higher. “Alright. Anything to help find my father.”

The path out of Merrywind Grove was far less welcoming than the path leading in.

Perri flew several paces ahead, leading them along a narrow trail between dense trees. The white trunks and drifting petals of light had long since disappeared behind them, replaced by a path of darker trunks and thicker roots.

“Hey, Perri,” Gwen called.

Perri slowed down to match her pace but continued flying. “Yes?”

“It’s probably obvious, but we aren’t from here. Why are you helping us take things from your island? Isn’t this place your home?”

Perri tilted her head. “Taking things from the island isn’t bad. We take things from the island all the time.”

“So taking all those pillipilli fruits—and the springwater, once we get there—you don’t consider that stealing or anything?”

The fairy shook her head. “No. It’s what nature gives us. As long as you don’t take too much or step into the wrong territories.”

“Territories?”

“Different parts of the island are managed by different species. And among those species are tribes or pack leaders,” Perri explained.

“Then Faewood is controlled by fairies?”

Perri nodded. “Although we don’t really have a leader or anything. But no one messes with us much since they can get lost inside the forest.”

“What about Merrywind Grove or the Lightless Spring?” Gwen asked. “You said Phillus is the guardian of the spring, right?”

“Guardians aren’t really leaders, but more like elders. My father was an elder of Faewood, so he’s a guardian. He can tell other people what to do, but that doesn’t mean you have to listen.”

“What determines something being a guardian?” Veronica asked. “Just being old enough?”

The fairy shook her head. “No. Not every elder is a guardian. The main thing you need is strength. Though the older you are, the stronger you are.”

Veronica raised a brow. “That’s it?”

“Yep.”

Orban grunted. “Finally. Somethin’ simple and not convoluted.”

They continued following Perri through the forest. The trees gradually grew denser, their branches interlocking overhead until the sunlight faded into a dim haze. Before long, Veronica heard the faint sound of running water ahead.

Finally, the trees opened into a wide clearing. At its center was a large crevice in the earth. Upon closer inspection, however, they could see a thin line of distortion near its edge.

It was filled with water. Water so clear, it appeared almost invisible.

“Is… this it?” Gwen asked slowly, stepping closer to the pond.

Veronica’s ear twitched. Her eyes flicked toward the opposite end of the clearing, past the trees.

Sage’s passive barrier immediately formed around her. That, however, wasn’t enough. Just as quickly, Veronica raised her palm and formed a larger barrier around everyone.

A moment later, a massive polychromatic beam of energy burst through the trees, roughly the width of a wine barrel. It slammed into her outer barrier and exploded into a shower of colors. Everything turned bright as the attack blotted out the clearing in a dazzling flash.

Gwen stumbled backward while Nasha drew her bow. Orban immediately pulled the shield from his back, and Theo took cover behind him.

Only a minor crack formed across Veronica’s barrier before the beam finally lost its power.

Once the light disappeared and the world returned to view, Veronica didn’t lower her barrier.

“W-what the hell was that?” Theo shouted.

Veronica didn’t answer. Instead, she kept her attention fixed ahead. Her ears picked up something heavy moving toward them from the depths of the trees.

Branches shifted apart as an enormous figure on four legs stepped out of the forest. Two massive dark antlers branched from its head, together stretching wider than Mirabelle’s deck. It towered several meters above them, its head tall enough to reach the upper canopy. Iridescent markings ran across its body; each trail seemed more a part of its skin, rather than etched or tattooed, shimmering with the same strange colors as the beam that just attacked them.

Perri flew ahead, passing through Veronica’s barrier.

“Phillus.”

The Great Stag glared back at her. Surprisingly, it could speak, and its voice was deep enough to reverberate through the clearing.

“Perripippa.”
