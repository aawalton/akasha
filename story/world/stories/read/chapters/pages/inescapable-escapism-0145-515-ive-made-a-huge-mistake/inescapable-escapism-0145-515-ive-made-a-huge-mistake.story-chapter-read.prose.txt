“Man, that was so fun,” Seth said as we walked through the corridor towards the canteen again. “I can’t wait until we have the scuba diving intensive.”

“Oh, me either!” I agreed enthusiastically as I tucked my wet hair behind my ear.

The thought of diving again made me kind of anxious. It reminded me too much of what had happened last time, but seeing the class messing around in the depths of the pool during our swimming class had reminded me of how much I’d enjoyed it.

“How often do you reckon we’ll have to do dives in the pool?” Seth asked. “I meant to ask Lucian, but I completely forgot.”

My eyebrows pulled together as I searched my memories. I was sure someone had mentioned it before, but I couldn’t remember who or when.

“I think… we have to do it during the refreshers we have every six months,” I said. “But I might be wrong.”

“Oh, that sounds familiar,” Seth agreed. “You don’t think we’ll just have dives in the pool during the refreshers, do you? I know it’s like ten metres deep or whatever, but that can’t be the same as being in the ocean, right? There’s no fish or anything! Surely, that makes things harder. Or maybe easier? I don’t know.”

“Yeah, I’m not sure either,” I said. “I don’t really remember seeing too many fish when I went diving before.”

The words slipped out before I could stop them, and the moment I realised what I’d said, I felt my eyes widen.

“You’ve been before?” Seth asked. “What was it like?”

Panic began to flutter in my chest. I had dove before, but not in that world. It hadn’t happened in my reality either. It had been in Mitch’s world, and my brain turned empty as I tried to think of a plausible explanation to give Seth.

A school trip? Maybe he’d buy that, but I was pretty sure most schools didn’t go on scuba diving trips. That seemed too fancy for the school I’d gone to, and although Seth hadn’t attended my school, it worried me.

I just needed to say something that was close to the truth. Lies were most believable when they were barely a lie. That’s what we’d been taught, and I just needed to put it into practice. How hard could that be?

“Yeah, I have,” I said with a shrug. “But only a couple of times. It was when I was on holiday with a friend.”

That wasn’t too bad, I told myself. I hadn’t offered too many unnecessary details, and I’d been fairly vague. That was good. It was harder to get caught out in a lie when there was nothing concrete to use.

“Oh, awesome! What was it like?” Seth asked again.

Crap. I’d completely forgotten to answer that when he’d asked before. I’d been too distracted by trying to think of an excuse, and I knew it was going to be more difficult to answer that question.

“It was fun!” I said before adding. “Well, kind of. I was pretty nervous, so I don’t think I took in too much.”

I laughed, trying to make it sound as if I was embarrassed by how I’d felt at the time. It came out a little too high-pitched, but Seth joined in.

“Oh, that’s fair. It does sound kind of nerve-wracking, to be honest,” he told me. “But I’m sure it’ll be less scary when we have classes here. I mean, all of the teachers have been super nice so far, so I bet whoever teaches diving will be too.”

“Yeah, I’m sure they will. I think we’ll have to wait a while to find out,” I replied, eager to shift the topic of the conversation away from me a little. “Rodgers said the intensives won’t start until everyone’s out of the induction wing, so they don’t need to stagger the introductory classes too much.”

There was a pause as Seth considered that.

“Okay,” he muttered. “Well, I don’t think we’ll have to wait too long for that. I didn’t really pay that much attention to everyone else when I was in the induction wing, just Scott, Abbie and Katie, but it seemed like everyone was doing pretty well?”

I chewed on my lip as I hesitated, trying to work out whether I wanted to tell Seth the rest of what Rodgers had said.

“Yeah…”

Seth glanced across at me, confusion slowly appearing on his face.

“What?” he asked, sounding worried.

“Nothing… it’s just…”

I trailed off again.

“It’s just?” Seth prompted.

A grimace twisted my features as I glanced at him out of the corner of my eye, but luckily, I was spared from answering for a few seconds as someone hurtled past us.

“Sorry!” they shouted to us as they almost collided with the doorway of the canteen.

“Everyone here is so polite,” Seth muttered more to himself than to me.

“Hey! Careful,” one of the tutors called after them. “People are carrying hot food about.”

I could feel Seth’s eyes on me as I watched the girl come to a sudden stop and wince.

“Sorry,” she told Alice.

“It’s alright. You in a rush to get to archery?”

The girl laughed as she walked towards the table where the tutor was sitting.

“How did you know? I only have twenty minutes before my shift in the GSOD starts, and I wanted to squeeze in some practice.”

“That’s fair. You can use one of the lanes in the shooting range after your shift instead if you want,” Alice offered. “Or in the morning. Taking your time to fuel your body is more important. You know that.”

“I know,” the girl said with a sigh. “Thanks, Alice.”

“That’s alright. Go grab your lunch,” she replied, nodding towards the buffet.

The girl smiled at the tutor before turning away, and we followed her towards the food.

“Thanks,” I said as Seth handed me a plate before looking around at the steaming trays.

I hadn’t realised just how hungry I was until that exact moment, but the smells made my stomach grumble so loudly that I was almost certain my mom must have heard it in the other world. I was almost tempted to check, but I couldn’t bear to tear myself away from the Academy at that moment. Not when there were so many delicious things I could eat there.

The food at the Academy was always so good. That was the problem. I was filled with indecision as I walked along the length of the buffet, wanting to try everything. Well, everything that didn’t contain meat.

There were too many vegetarian options, though. I wasn’t used to that. In real life, there were normally only a few to choose from, and I generally didn’t like a couple of the offerings, so that made the decision easier. However, at the Academy, there were so many options that I knew I’d like.

I piled my plate high with scoops of pasta and quinoa salads that were filled with vegetables and beans before topping it with crumbled feta and moving along to the next section. My mouth watered as I stared down at the rice noodle soup, but I forced myself to keep walking. Although that did smell delicious, I felt like I already had too much on my plate to have soup as well, and I could always have it another day. They tended to repeat some of the options a couple of times a week.

“Is that good?” Seth asked, making me jump.

We’d split up to get our food, and I hadn’t even noticed him approaching again.

“The tofu?” I asked, glancing at the tray of baked tofu nuggets in front of me.

I already had a small mountain of them on my plate.

“Yeah. I don’t know that I’ve ever had it before,” he said with a shrug.

“Oh, that’s fair. I think they’re pretty good!”

Seth cocked his head to the side before nodding.

“Cool. I’ll give them a try.”

I smiled to myself as I stepped aside, watching as he scooped a couple of the nuggets onto his plate before moving towards the dips. The tofu was good, but it was even better when dunked in ranch. I hadn’t had that much before I’d gotten to the Academy; it wasn’t really a popular dip in the UK, but I loved it.

“You got everything?” I asked as Seth joined me again.

“Almost,” he said as he squirted ketchup onto his plate. “Now, I have.”

I returned his grin before looking up at the tables.

“Where do you want to sit?”

“Mmmm… I don’t mind. How about over there?”

I followed the direction of his nod to an empty table at the back of the room.

“Sure!” I replied before starting to make my way towards it.

There was something so nice about having someone I knew in the main building of the Academy. I’d felt the same way during breakfast, but the feeling had only grown stronger since then. Before Seth had joined me, meals had made me anxious. I didn’t like walking into the giant canteen and having to sit by myself.

It wasn’t the eating alone part that worried me. That wasn’t too bad. It was the constant awareness that someone might notice the empty seats around me and decide to fill them. That had happened a few times, and I appreciated their kindness, but I also hated it at the same time. It just made me more uncomfortable because I always felt like I had to make constant conversation with someone I didn’t know, and I never knew what to say to them.

Granted, I didn’t always know what to say to Seth, but it was easier. Even when there were gaps in our conversation, it wasn’t quite as stressful as when that happened with a stranger. Plus, that was only a temporary thing. Once Katie, Abbie and Scott got out of the induction wing, they’d sit with us too, and they were better at keeping the conversation going than I was.

“So…” Seth said as we sat down. “What was it you didn’t want to tell me out there?”

My eyes snapped to him, and I couldn’t help but laugh.

“I was kind of hoping you’d forget about that.”

Seth smiled.

“How bad is it?” he asked. “Did Rodgers say they weren’t going to be for a few months?”

“Yeah… kind of?”

Seth’s smile faltered.

“More?”

I hesitated, putting off answering by spearing a tofu nugget and taking my time to chew it slowly before swallowing. Seth’s eyes didn’t leave my face as he waited for me to reply, and I wasn’t sure why I was dreading it so much, but I didn’t want to be the one to give him the bad news. He’d sounded so excited about diving, and that made me feel guilty. It wasn’t my fault that it was still going to be a while before we went, but I still felt bad.

“Rodgers said the intensives will probably start around the end of the year,” I blurted out.

“What?”

“Or the beginning of next…”

Seth’s mouth fell open, and he stared at me in shock.

“But that’s…” he started before trailing off. “It’s only… It’s August!”

“I know. Apparently, most people should be out of the induction wing by then.”

“But not all?”

I shrugged.

“I don’t know. That’s just what Rodgers said, but I guess it might be sooner?”

Seth let out a sigh as he stabbed a piece of chicken and ate it, a morose expression on his face.

“Well, that sucks,” he said.

“I know,” I agreed, trying to ignore the guilt that threatened to put me off my food.

We ate in silence for a few seconds before the disappointment slipped from Seth’s face, and he perked up a bit.

“I guess… it probably won’t feel like that long, actually,” he said. “I mean, the rest of the classes here are so interesting, so I bet the time passes really quickly.”

That drove away some of the emotion swirling in my stomach, and I felt a flicker of excitement return.

“Yeah, for sure.”

“I mean, if it were back home, it would feel like an eternity, but here, it probably won’t be too bad.”

“Mmm,” I agreed as I reached for the water jug and cups in the middle of the table. “Want some?”

“Sure!”

I grabbed two cups from the top of the stack and flipped them over before filling them both up. Seth smiled gratefully as I pushed one across the table towards him before lifting my own glass to my lips and draining it immediately.

One of the pasta salads was much spicier than I expected when I’d first seen it. I’d assumed the slivers of red in it were tomato or red pepper, but they must have been chilli. My mouth was on fire. I couldn’t help but pour another cup of water in a desperate attempt to extinguish it, but it did very little.

“You okay?” Seth asked, concern pulling at his expression.

“Spicy,” I managed to get out between gulps of water.

He laughed.

“Oh no, really? How bad is it?”

“It tastes like fire.”

Seth chuckled again.

“Really? Which one was it?” he asked, looking down at my plate. “I kind of want to try some.”

“That one,” I said, pointing at it with one hand as I filled up my cup again. “Help yourself. I’m not having any more.”

“Are you sure?” he checked.

“Yeah,” I told him as I turned the plate so the pasta was closest to him.

He started to reach across the table towards it before pausing, his fork just centimetres from the edge.

“Oh wait,” he muttered before looking up at me. “I’ve used this on meat.”

Confusion washed over me, and I stared at him.

“Yeah?” I said slowly, trying to work out why he’d mentioned that.

“You’re veggie, right?”

“I am…”

He leant back again.

“Do you just want to drop some onto my plate then? I wouldn’t want to risk touching any of your food with my fork and contaminating it for you,” he suggested with a smile as he pushed his plate towards me.

My heart clenched painfully, and I nodded, unable to hold his gaze. I was so touched by his consideration, and it had taken me completely by surprise. I’d shared food with other people before, and I knew it was silly, but the thought of their cutlery touching my food after it had been used on meat always made me uncomfortable. I tried to ignore it every time, but I normally ended up avoiding the parts they’d eaten from.

“How much do you want?” I asked as I lifted my plate.

“Mmmm, I don’t know. Just a few pieces?”

I pushed a couple of pieces over the edge, watching as they landed on Seth’s plate before looking up at him again.

“That enough?”

“Sure!”

He speared one of the pieces with his fork before lifting it and examining it closely. It looked as though he was trying to work out just how spicy it really was, but after a few seconds, he gave up and took a bite.

I watched him carefully, trying to work out what he thought of it. Part of me was almost worried that he was going to say it wasn’t too bad, and I knew that didn’t really matter, but it would have made me seem pathetic.

Luckily, I didn’t have to worry about that. Within a couple of seconds, Seth’s eyes widened, and he met my gaze.

“Fucking hell,” he remarked as he reached for his water.

A laugh slipped from my mouth, half motivated by relief and half from enjoyment, as I watched the pain on Seth’s face as he chugged his drink.

“I told you it was bad!” I cried, barely able to hold back my laughter.

“This isn’t bad!” he shot back. “It’s hellish! Why would anyone do this?”

“I don’t know,” I chuckled as he filled up his cup again.

“There’s not even a taste! I’d understand it more if there was, but it just burns!”

I had to cover my mouth to stop myself from laughing again. He seemed genuinely in pain, and I did feel a little bad, but it was mostly just funny.

“I know,” I agreed. “Are you okay?”

“I think that’s done permanent damage to my taste buds,” Seth lamented as he wiped his watering eyes. “What if I can never taste anything again?”

“It would be a tragedy,” I said before taking a bite of a tofu nugget dipped in ranch. The creaminess helped soothe some of the residual fire. “Don’t worry, I can still taste this, so you’ll probably be fine?”

“I hope so,” Seth replied before glancing towards the buffet again. “Man, that was horrible, but… I’m almost tempted to have more. Is that wrong?”

“Yes! Is your mouth not still burning?”

It was so bad. I had no idea how he could possibly be considering having any more of that pasta.

“It is…” he said slowly, eyeing the remaining pieces that I’d put on his plate unsurely. “But there’s something weirdly moreish about it, you know?”

“No! I don’t know!” I shot back with a laugh as I watched his fork toy with them.

“Like, don’t get me wrong, it hurt, but… I don’t know,” he said before seeming to come to a realisation. “Wait a minute…”

“What?”

I had no idea where he was going with that, but I wanted to know.

“Is this why people get tattoos?” he asked.

Confusion slammed into me. I could not see a single connection between tattoos and the spicy thing we’d just eaten.

“What?” I said again after a few seconds.

“You know… it hurts, but there’s something almost… addictive about it?” he explained with a laugh.

I joined in, shaking my head.

“I mean… maybe? I don’t know. I’ve never gotten a tattoo, and I’m not tempted to have any more of that pasta.”

“Hmm,” Seth replied thoughtfully. “Maybe you just have more willpower than me.”

“Maybe.”

“I mean, you are vegetarian, and I assume that takes a fair bit of restraint. You know what? I’m going to have some more.”

“What?” I cried.

“Just the rest of this,” he said, gesturing towards the two remaining pieces on his plate. “Then, if I want more, I’m going back up to the buffet. That’s allowed out here too, right?”

“Yeah, the tutors don’t care if you have seconds, but… are you sure that’s a good idea? I mean, we’ve not got that much water left.”

Seth eyed the jug in the centre of the table. It had been full when we sat down, but there was only about a third still in there.

“Ah, I’m sure it’ll be fine,” he decided with a shrug. “I mean, it can’t be as bad this time, right?”

“I don’t know…”

I felt conflicted as I watched him stab the pasta again. I was torn between knowing how spicy it was and that it would hurt him whilst still wanting to see him eat it again because of how funny it had been the first time. It wasn’t that I liked to see him in pain. Obviously, I would have hated it if it was real pain, but something about his reaction had been hilarious. It reminded me so much of Duncan.

“Time to find out,” he said with a grin before shoving the pasta into his mouth.

Immediately, it was clear Seth had his answer. A flush began creeping up his neck before spreading onto his face. His expression became panicked as he chewed quickly, already reaching for his cup.

“It’s just as bad?” I guessed.

“Oh no,” he gasped between gulps. “Worse. Much, much worse.”

“How?” I laughed.

He drained his cup, emptying the last of the water into it before lifting it to his lips again.

“I’ve made a huge mistake. It’s a grower,” he spluttered. “How is it still getting worse?”

“Do you want me to go fill that up?” I asked, gesturing towards the now-empty jug.

Seth shook his head, looking around the canteen frantically. The taps were on the other side of the room, next to the buffet, but there were jugs on the other tables around us. I saw Seth glance at one, seemingly considering running towards the nearest one and pouring the contents directly into his mouth.

“No, I might die before then,” he said dramatically before turning back to me. “Do they leave the milk in the fridges all day?”

I glanced towards the glass-fronted fridges next to the buffet, but I couldn’t tell if there was anything in them from where we were sitting. I knew they were always well stocked at breakfast time, but I wasn’t sure if someone moved the remaining bottles to another fridge in the kitchen once we’d finished that meal.

“I’m not sure,” I told Seth with a grimace.

“Me either,” he muttered. “Fuck it. I’m going to go find out. If not… I’ll just knock on the kitchen door and beg whoever’s inside for something.”

I found myself grinning as I watched him push himself away from the table and hurry towards the buffet at a pace just under a run, his cup clutched in his hand. As he drew clear, someone else leapt out of their seat and sprinted towards the fridges. She threw the door open before ripping the lid off a bottle and pouring it directly into her mouth.

“Use a glass!” one of the tutors called.
