“How is it that this is the second time I’ve met somebody important today by drawing my weapon?” Ezra asked as he stood up and stepped away from Hera. He shifted his axe into his left hand and offered a hand to help her up, which she took. She continued smiling, cocking her head to the side as she rose to her feet.

She moved smoothly, with practice. Evidently, the training that had done little more than frustrate Adonis was something that Hera excelled at.

“You’re not hiding your body at all?” Hera asked, continuing to leer at Ezra’s chest and arms.

“By your own words, I know that there’s nothing I should feel the need to hide, why would I?” Ezra shrugged. “You’re not the first person to see me shirtless, and you wouldn’t be the first person to see me naked if I wasn’t wearing pants. For now, I’m here to make sure there’s nothing that needs my attention because that’s my job.”

The teasing drained away from the young noblewoman’s face at that, becoming more… normal, for lack of a better way to say it. She started to open her mouth, but seemed to think better of her knee-jerk question. Then, a quiet that was quickly becoming an uncomfortable silence was broken.

“So, Hera,” Adonis interrupted whatever his older sister was about to say, “now that you’re done ogling, is there a reason why you’re here tonight? You weren’t at dinner, which, although mother didn’t say anything about it to me, we both know you’ll be getting more than an earful for it.”

“I know,” Hera waved off the concern, whether it came from a place of schadenfreude or something more genuine. “I was busy.”

“That’s not much of an explanation,” Adonis scoffed.

“Beat me in a fight and I’ll tell you.”

“If you come into my room and start making challenges, then I can guarantee whatever you want me to do for you isn’t going to happen.”

“Come on, Donnie!” Hera’s tone immediately went from imperious to wheedling. “I didn’t mean to step on your fragile ego.”

The two continued bickering back and forth, the argument quickly going from friendly to gaining more teeth and anger as it went on. The stiffly put together young man that Adonis had appeared to be for the past several hours melted away into a boy who argued fiercely and didn’t pull back from dealing low blows as Ezra snuck briefly into his room to dry off and pull a tunic back on. When he returned to the room, the argument was reaching a crescendo.

“—and that’s why nobody wants to marry a ruffian who’s more bandit than noble,” Adonis sneered at his sister. The way that she recoiled from that meant it had been a pretty good hit. However, the way that she’d dressed him down for his inability to make friends a moment before had landed just as hard.

Just as Hera was about to make another response, Ezra cleared his throat loudly. Fortunately, both turned towards the interruption, and Ezra took that as an opportunity to hopefully stop the argument.

“I’d say you’ve both given as good as you’ve gotten. If you really want to fight, go ahead and continue, but we still don’t know why you’re here, Hera. Maybe we’d all prefer it if you talk about that instead.”

She seemed to fight against herself, to refuse to leave the fight with Adonis getting the final hit. Then, she got control over herself, nodded, and walked to the side of the room. Another chair was there, and she threw herself onto it, her legs over the arm as she looked at Adonis.

“What do you know about the leadership in Al Jibahia?” she asked, sighing.

“It’s ruled by the leopardkin, claiming direct descent from Jibahia the Beast,” he answered immediately. “But that’s basic stuff. I’m gonna need more information about why you’re here asking me about it, because I’m sure you already knew that.”

“I was getting to that,” Hera granted. “I was talking more about the actual politics of the country.”

Adonis waited, looking at her and wanting more information. She didn’t give it, and he shook his head.

“That doesn’t mean anything. ‘The actual politics of it’? There’s religion, there’s backstabbing, there’s families with power and families who are losing power. There’s a million different things that are happening, and you just said, ‘tell me about the politics.’ Do you hear how stupid of a question that is? Obviously, you don’t want to tell me exactly what you’re trying to learn about, but if you want to keep what you’re researching completely secret, then you actually have to conduct the research and get behind a book.” The more he talked, the more animated Adonis got until finally he threw his hands up and shrugged as he continued talking.

“So either tell me more specific things or get to the library and hope you can remember how to read long enough to find what you’re looking for.”

“That’s the last time you’re insulting me tonight,” Hera warned, and Adonis nodded his head, giving a quick apology as the two settled into comfortable positions. Then, after a few short moments of hemming and hawing, Hera seemed to come to a conclusion.

“I know that the emirate part of the country’s name ‘Emirate of Al Jibahia’ means that there’s a lot of princes, different leaders of the different cities and territories. I also know that each one can basically make their own laws, with some exceptions. The ruler of the country, the one who controls everything in the grand scheme of things, is called the Beastling. At least, in our language. So, onto my question, how is the Beastling selected over all of the princes? How are they kicked out from the position? I know it isn’t a hereditary post of leadership, so how does it work?”

Adonis nodded, understanding a bit better what she wanted to know. “It’s a position that’s voted on. The only ones who can vote are all of the princes, and their reasons for voting for any individual vary from one prince to the next. One might value the strength of an individual, another the strength of their armies. A third might come to a deal through agreements on taxes, border adjustments, easements, direct bribery, or more. I can’t tell you what exactly an individual prince’s priorities would be, I don’t know them.

“As for how the Beastling might be deposed? Primarily, only through violence, and considering they take a seat in the capital city of Jibahia and gain command over the main army, that’s only happened successfully twice, so far as I know. I know there’s some method of calling a replacement vote early, but it’s almost never invoked, as far as I know. Something to do with it calling in question the divine right of rule of the Beastling specifically and the leopardkin in general? I’d have to look deeper into it, because I don’t know. Usually, the elected Beastling serves for twenty years or until they die. Historically, if the Beastling reigns for longer than two decades, they’re reelected upon the culmination of their term.”

Adonis provided all this information without hesitation, his gaze even, only flicking away from Hera for a moment as he thought about how the princes of a foreign country could vote for a new leader. Other than that, all of the information seemed to be immediately available to his recollection, and he provided it without a second thought.

“Any other questions?”

“Nothing else about the facts, but I wouldn’t mind your opinion,” Hera said, looking around the room. Her eyes kept flicking back and forth, looking for something she couldn’t find in the room, and Adonis sighed. He looked at Ezra and seemed to go from confident and competent to embarrassed.

“Would you pull that rope in the corner of the room for me?”

Adonis pointed at a rope that hung a single pace away from where Ezra stood. The guard stepped, pulled, and heard a faint tinkling sound. Starting to understand what was happening, Ezra smiled and asked, “Am I allowed to ask for a snack too?”

“I would prefer that you do,” Adonis smiled, some of the tension leaving his shoulders as he said it. “Mikhail refused to accept any such amenities, and it made me even more uncomfortable around him, to have him looming in the corner and watching me as I indulged.”

“The man’s a prick, you can say it,” Hera grunted. “I’m surprised he was able to make it as long as he did.”

“I prefer not to speak in such an uncouth manner,” Adonis said, looking at his sister in a way that was definitely chastising. “Suffice to say, however, I hold no fondness towards him.”

The three devolved into a conversation regarding what kind of snacks they wanted to request for a few short minutes, and by the time the servant, an older man with silver hair and a thin mustache, arrived, Adonis had the order prepared. He gave it with a smile and happy greeting of the man, and the servant walked out. The conversation remained inane for several minutes after that, until the servant returned with a tray that he brought and placed on the table in the center of the room.

“Thank you, Thomas,” Adonis said with a smile. “How’s your granddaughter?”

“She’s recovered, thanks to you. Again, my sincerest gratitude for your assistance.” The old servant, Thomas, lit up as he spoke of his granddaughter.

“I’m glad that what little pharmacological knowledge I’ve gained was of assistance to her,” Adonis’s smile grew wider as he heard the good news. “After all, her birthday is… I’m sorry, forgive me. A part of me is saying that her birthday is this week, but now that I think about it, I think my memory’s off.”

“In this case, it is, my Lord,” Thomas agreed, bowing his head. “There remain a few months yet to her birthday. However, hearing that you were thinking of it will delight her to no end. There are a few things more mystical to a six-year-old than their birthday and nobility, in that order.”

Adonis laughed, stood up, and patted Thomas on the shoulder as he escorted the thin, kindly man out the door. The two continued to converse, stopping at the door for another minute or so as Ezra and Hera waited for Adonis’s return. Hera still had her question she was going to ask him, but she didn’t wait to dig into the food. She took slices of ham and cheese between her fingers, placing them directly into her mouth without any utensil or concern for propriety.

Despite himself, Ezra found himself watching the way she sucked her fingers, his attention and thoughts more than was polite. She was pretty, though in a more tomboyish way, like Chloe, than Tilly. Her face was tanned and her body thin but strong. As she took another bite of the food, putting it in her mouth and then sucking the last bit of moisture from her finger, Ezra couldn’t help but remember the feeling of her body pressed under his.

“He’s always like this,” Hera said, thankfully pulling Ezra from his imagination that continued to spin down uncomfortable paths. She gestured for Ezra to help himself as she continued, “There’s very little that can escape Adonis’s attention. That, combined with the fact that he refuses to treat servants as anything less than equals means that any conversation he actually engages with can’t help but endear him to the common man.”

“Seems like he could be a great leader, if you ask me,” Ezra said, as he took a small handful of salted nuts. He tossed a few into his mouth, surprised by how tasty they were. Adonis had insisted on bringing them, and Ezra hadn’t ever really eaten nuts. Maybe, now that he had, if he could afford them, he’d eat them more frequently.

“Maybe,” Hera shrugged. “I don’t know if he’d be able to make the difficult decisions that need to be made as the leader of many positions needs to. I think he’d be happier as an administrator.”

“I wouldn’t mind being an accountant or minister of the treasury,” Adonis said, coming back into the room. He saw the dish of nuts that had been already dug into and glared at his sister. She raised both hands in innocence and pointed at Ezra, who offered over his fistful of the snacks. Adonis shook his head, smiling as he saw. Ezra was enjoying his own preferred snack.

“So what was your other question?” Adonis asked, turning back towards his sister.

“What’re the chances I’m able to start a conversation with one of the princes to start negotiations on helping them ascend to the throne?” She was casual with how she said it, even as she suggested embroiling herself in the succession of another country.

“I’m not the person to ask,” Adonis dismissed her. “I don’t have connections or authority.”

Hera grumbled against that, but it wasn’t long before she turned and left from the window again. Before she leapt down the banister, Ezra said, “Please don’t come through the window again. If you really need to, leave a letter or something. Let me know what’s coming, please?”

“But the surprise is half the fun!” she protested, not giving him an answer before she left.

Ezra was left on the balcony, and then, as he and Adonis finished the snacks, he finally got to rest in his new room for the first time. Maybe he’d actually be able to get some good sleep tonight?
