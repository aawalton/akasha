Far away from the Storm Sect, in the ashen reaches where the Soul sect was located, High Elder Severian stood at the center of a ritual formation, energy and soul essence undulating wildly around him.

The ritual had begun at midnight, at the exact stroke of twelve.

Severian had chosen the hour deliberately, as the soul was quietest at the peak of night.

More people were asleep. More souls, slumbering, thus making it easier to find a soul fragment amidst the chaos.

Or at least, it should have been.

But four hours had passed since the ritual formation was first activated. It had been activated a dozen times after that, violet light erupting from the ritual circle to bathe the room in thick, nearly viscous soul essence. Yet, none had succeeded in finding the soul fragment of Ascendant Edrick Ghaltris.

This was the fourteenth activation.

Yet as the violet runes along the floor began to flicker, and the whooshing sound of static began echoing within the room, Severian knew once more that it would fail.

His jaw ticked in irritation as the winds picked up, the whooshing of static rising to crescendo before—very suddenly—plummeting.

The violet light vanished. The runes dimmed. And silence returned to the hall, only for a moment before Malverin spoke, her voice echoing from a corner of the room.

“I think that’s enough tries for today, brother,” she said quietly, the weariness in her voice evident. “It’s obvious it’s not working.”

Silence reigned for a moment longer, and when he didn’t respond, his youngest sister, Emily, spoke.

“Perhaps, Edrick Ghaltris left no soul fragment in his legacy. Maybe that’s why you can’t find it.”

“No,” Severian shook his head, knowing for certain that that wasn’t the case. The soul of an Ascendant wasn’t something that could be hidden easily. Not even by the system itself.

Ascendants were gods—immortals just beginning their journey toward eternality.

A fragment of their soul shouldn’t have been this hard to find. Not with his affinity. Not with his path.

In the past, he’d been certain the limitation was as a result of his tier and class. But he was a Calamity now.

A god in the making.

The soul fragment of an Ascendant should have been a beacon to his senses. And yet, it only teased the edges of his mind, flickering just barely out of reach.

There was no more doubt about it.

This was the system’s handiwork. It knew what he was planning, what he wanted to use that soul fragment for, and it wanted to frustrate him.

“Severian,” Malverin called to him again, and with a deep exhale, he looked up, violet eyes gleaming with resignation.

“We cannot continue the search from here,” he said quietly, a flicker of emotion gleaming in his eyes. “The system won’t let us.”

“So, it is as they say then?” Emily asked. “Fate itself chooses those who stumble onto legacy seeds?”

Severian grimaced, a scowl pulling at his lips.

“Fate is a partial judge. A fraud.” He nearly spat. “She never gives to those who truly deserve it.”

His gaze hardened. “Your daughter deserves it. We deserve it. But fate would rather give it to some bumbling idiot who doesn’t.” He shook his head. “To leave one’s destiny to fate is to kneel before a master who has never loved you. No, we must seize our fate.”

“And how do we do that?” Malverin asked, her eyes narrowing in agreement.

Severian glanced at her, a small smile pulling at his lips.

“Come,” he said, leading them to a table at the edge of the room where a map lay. He gestured toward the whole of Gordon as his sisters joined him, a shapeless slab of earth thousands of miles across.

And then he circled out a small portion, only a few hundred miles away from the storm sect.

“This is Alcatraz,” he smiled. “One of the largest mundane cities in all of Gordon."

"I remember it," Malverin frowned. "Wasn't it the one that was nearly destroyed by a rogue fire cultivator about fifteen years ago?"

"It's the one," Severian nodded. "I have narrowed the location of the legacy to the city and its immediate surroundings. The system won’t let me narrow it any further.”

“But why?” Emily asked. “Why would Edrick Ghaltris leave a legacy in a place like this?”

“Because he wants some mundane to find it,” Malverin scowled, then glanced at Severian. “We have to act before that happens. If we can no longer narrow the search from here, then we have to visit the city itself.”

Severian’s smile widened to a grin. “Indeed. I say we go this instant, beat both the Storm Sect and Fate at their own devices.”

Malverin nodded, determination flashing in her eyes.

This was what she had been looking for these past few months. A purpose. A drive. A reason to get back on her feet and move.

She glanced at her brother, holding his violet gaze for a moment before nodding.

“Let’s do it. Let’s steal from Fate.”

Severian’s grin widened and he caressed her face gently. Malverin leaned into it, feeling a sense of safety wrap itself around her.

Nothing could reach her here. Not while her brother was alive.

Then she glanced at Emily and smiled. “Let’s do this. Let’s get your daughter a legacy.”

Emily nodded in agreement, determination flashing in her eyes, and Severian smiled.

He was happy to have them both on board. To have them face against the world as a family once more, just as they had done in their early days after their parents’ death.

But as much as he was doing this for them, he was also doing it for himself. For his own advancement.

And he would be damned if he let Fate steal something else from him just because it was playing favorites.

Back in the Storm Sect, within the elder hall, the High Elders sat before one of the most renowned Calamities in the sect, a subtle air of uneasiness flowing around them as the man bowed.

“High Elders,” he greeted. “I fear I do not come bearing good news.”

Almost as one, the High Elders deflated, the motion so subtle very few people would have noticed.

Amoz, however, did.

He grimaced. “I apologize for this disappointment, High Elders.”

“Oh, no. Do not apologize, Amoz.” The First Seat waved aside his apology. “We knew already that this wouldn’t be easy. Remember we still haven’t found Ascendant MountainFist’s legacy, and he told us exactly where he would leave it.”

“I still maintain that he lied,” High Elder Mystral snorted. “The man was a habitual purveyor of falsehoods.”

“Whatever the case may be,” the First Seat continued, eying Mystral lightly. “The fact remains that finding legacy seeds is a notoriously arduous task. We knew it would be difficult.”

Calamity Amoz bowed slightly. “I appreciate the consideration. The system is fighting very hard against me on this.”

“How bad is it?” High Elder Tharn asked.

“Very bad,” Amoz shook his head. “I have only been able to narrow the legacy’s location to the city of Alcatraz. Any further and my search skills stop functioning. The system will not let me narrow it further.”

“Oh, that is bad,” Tharn grimaced. “You will have to continue the search on site.”

Zarxan shook his head. “That’s a lot of land to cover without your abilities, Amoz.”

Amoz nodded. “I’m aware, High Elder Zarxan. But I don’t think we have a choice if we intend to find this seed.”

A beat of silence descended over the hall as the High Elders contemplated his words, slight frowns blooming on their faces.

The system was always cagey with legacy seeds, but they had still found a few in the last few centuries. Especially since they knew where the Ascendants would leave them.

It was a common practice in the sect to foster a greater generation of disciples.

Despite knowing the location of these legacies, however, finding many of them had been very hard.

Nevertheless, this was the first time the system was reacting so strongly towards protecting the legacy seed.

Shifting carefully on his throne, High Elder Kaelthorn asked, “Do you know why the system is reacting this strongly? It has never clouded your skills before.”

Amoz hesitated a moment, then shook his head. “I’m not sure. Perhaps, it is because this seed will not merely create an inheritor but a double inheritor.” He frowned. “It makes one question why no one has found a triple inheritor yet. Has no double inheritor ever ascended? Have they never left a legacy behind?”

The frowns of the High Elders deepened, and after another moment of silence, the First Seat spoke.

“That is something that has always baffled us.”

He shook his head and leaned back into his throne with a sigh. “You said you had two suspicions. What is the second?”

Amoz hesitated once more, this time long enough to give the High Elders themselves pause.

“Amoz?” the First Seat prodded, and the Calamity sighed heavily.

“I do not want to create unnecessary panic. But if the system is reacting this strongly, it could also mean we are the only ones searching for the seed.”

His words caused every High Elder to sit up immediately, their eyes narrowing at the sign of a threat. Even if still intangible.

“I take it you do not mean mundanes,” High Elder Velora said softly, and Amoz nodded.

“I suspect, perhaps, that the system is protecting the legacy, concealing it in a way that makes it difficult for anyone to consciously find using skills or rituals.”

This time the silence that descended was longer. Thicker.

“This is bad,” High Elder Mystral stated a moment later. “We have relaxed and now, our enemies gather, silently plotting. What would they do with a legacy seed of storm should they find it?”

“Infiltrate,” Zarxan and Velora responded together, their eyes meeting from across the others.

Zarxan shook his head. “If that is the case, then we must act very fast. Even if it isn’t true. We cannot afford to risk it.”

The First Seat nodded, his gaze hard. “Zarxan is right. Whether this is true or not, we do not have the time to waste.” He glanced at Amoz. “Go to Alcatraz immediately. Take as many people as you need and begin your search.”

“No,” High Elder Kaelthorn shook his head. “That’s too risky. If our enemies are searching too, then speed alone won't be enough.” He grimaced. “We need an advantage. Amoz said skills and rituals are not working. What about innate abilities? Have we tried those?”

Amoz blinked. “I… we have no one with a search ability, High Elder Kaelthorn.”

“I beg to differ,” Kaelthorn shook his head. “Innate abilities are rare enough that they might work where skills fail. And we have one person with a search ability.” His gaze strayed to Zarxan. “We have the Crown of Ruining Storms and his core sensing ability.”

The High Elders stilled immediately.

Zarxan didn’t.

“No,” he replied hastily, glaring at Kaelthorn. “We do not know that abilities will work. If our enemies are going to be there, sending Aodhán will mean putting him in harm’s way.”

“I will be there,” Calamity Amoz replied, a hint of hope in his eyes. “And if necessary, I can take a few more calamities along.” He looked to Zarxan. “If Aodhán can help, we will be remiss not to use him.”

The First Seat paused, then slowly nodded. “Amoz is right, Zarxan. If Aodhán can help, we have to use him.”

Zarxan growled, about to counter, but the First Seat glared at him. “I understand your concerns for his safety, but we cannot keep Aodhán in a bubble. He is a transmigrant, not decoration. We will ask him, and if he agrees to help us, then that will be the end of it.”

Zarxan scowled, but said nothing else, aware enough of himself to see that he was thinking too emotionally.

Still, the thought of putting Aodhán at risk didn't sit well with him at all.

“He will be fine, Zarxan,” Velora spoke a moment later. “He fought in the Balance War and survived. He’ll come back from this alive too.”

Zarxan glanced at her, hesitating a moment longer before eventually sighing.

“Fine.” He glanced at Amoz. “But I’m leaving him in your care, Amoz. Make sure he comes back alive.”

Amoz bowed. “I will.”

“Then get Aodhán,” the First Seat commanded. “Tell him we need him.”

Calamity Amoz bowed deeply and left the room, leaving the High Elders far more uneasy than he’d met them.

As the door shut behind him, the First Seat grimaced, feeling a bitterness on his tongue.

It seemed another storm was gathering. And this time, it would break over the mundane city of Alcatraz.

But while the storm gathered in Gordon, another was already brewing in Lutia—barely visible—its rumblings, barely perceptible, except to the most perceptive.

Geneva Rhyntharion would never have counted herself among such an elite group of people.

But as she stood in the bloodstained battlefield, gazing out at the destruction they had wrought on the monster horde, she felt a shudder of great unease move through her.

It wasn’t aversion to the stench of blood and death all around her. Nor was it disgust at the thousands of innocent creatures she and the Brystion brothers had just killed.

It wasn’t even the unfamiliarity of some of the creatures she had just put down, though that contributed too.

No, the uneasiness came from the unnerving fact that even negligence couldn’t have created a horde this size.

Natural hordes were never this big. Never this uniform in tier.

Something was terribly wrong.

And as the thought lingered in her mind, that unease deepened into dread.

Inhaling slightly, she glanced toward the Brystion brothers, both of whom had wrought nearly as much destruction as she had in the last three hours. They weren’t done yet.

Hundreds of creatures were still scattered across the battlefield, some in groups, others alone. But their numbers weren’t quite so overwhelming anymore.

The local military could take care of the rest, while they—she glanced toward the city wall, and grimaced—dealt with the city itself.

Lifting a hand to the sky, she unleashed a wave of purification runes, and sighed under her breath as a cleansing light of pure white descended over the battlefield, so bright it nearly eclipsed the sun itself.

The light engulfed the entire battlefield, cleansing it of everything dead and crimson.

It shone over her and she shuddered again, this time in relief as the blood, guts, and viscera caking her skin were burned away.

The Brystion brothers returned to her side then, and a moment later after the light had dimmed, the three of them returned to the city wall, the echoes of snarling monsters still behind them.

When they arrived on the wall, they were met by the awed, almost reverent gazes of the soldiers who had watched them fight for the last three hours.

This time, however, the sight brought Geneva no joy.

She met the gaze of the captain firmly. “I believe you can take care of the rest?”

The captain bowed.

“Yes, champion, but perhaps you would like to stay and see us fight,” he glanced up at her hopefully. “We would be honored if you did.”

Geneva shook her head. “I’m sorry, Captain. But there is a matter I must see to immediately.” Her eyes hardened slightly. “Take me to the Viscount. He needs to give me a report on how a horde like this grew without his noticing.”

Almost immediately, the soldiers stilled.

Aldric, on the other hand, twitched slightly, following the sharpest scent of fear to a diminutive soldier at the end of the line.

He didn’t turn, though. Instead, he fixed his gaze on the captain as the man frowned.

“I don’t think that will be possible today, Champion,” he responded genuinely. “I just received news an hour ago that the Viscount has left the city for a meeting.”

“While his city was under siege?” Geneva asked, her expression twisting into a scowl of disbelief.

“I…” the captain stuttered. “That was the information I received, Champion Rhyntharion. I would not lie to you.”

Geneva glanced at Aldric and he nodded.

“He is telling the truth, or at least what he believes to be true,” Aldric said with a grimace, then slowly turned to the diminutive soldier at the back of the line.

Their gazes locked, but before the man could panic, Aldric pointed to him.

“How about he gives us a tour, then,” he smiled. “This is our first time in Ironfel. Perhaps, we could look around?”

Geneva glanced at him suspiciously, then turned to the captain who looked very confused.

“If… if it’s a tour you want, I can set up a tour guide for you.”

“We do not want a tour guide,” Daruk spoke for the first time, his voice as cold as ice. “We want the soldier. We promise to have him back in an hour too.”

Geneva nodded, playing along. And after a moment of hesitation, the captain nodded. “If you insist, champions.” He turned to look at the now terrified man and smiled. “Don’t worry, Alan. You get to lead the witch of Selia through the great city of Ironfel. That’s a great honor.”

Many of the soldiers laughed and Alan forced a smile. “Yes, right. The greatest of honors.”

“Good,” Geneva smiled, her voice firm as she walked toward him. “Let’s begin with the nearest bar. We could definitely use some food after that last battle.”

Alan nodded shakily, and a few minutes later, they arrived before a small scanty bar only a stone’s throw from the city wall. As they stepped into the building, however, Aldric gestured toward a group table and spoke.

“After you, Alan.”

Alan slid into the booth, his eyes slightly glazed as he turned to face them.

It was the effect of Aldric’s pseudo-mind control skill. The very same skill he had once used to manipulate Makeba during the Blackwell Mansion heist.

He slid into the booth after Alan with a gentle smile.

“Now, tell me the truth, Alan,” he spoke. “Where is the Viscount?”

Alan looked up, opened his mouth to speak, and promptly exploded, spraying the entire bar in blood and viscera.

For a single heartbeat, stunned silence reigned over the bar.

And then—

The bartender screamed.
