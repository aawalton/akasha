To speak of Rhel, one must speak of its history. It is commonly known that Rhel was formed as a settler Realm near the end of the Realmbreaker Wars. The world arose as a refuge for those looking to get away from the ravages of endless military aggression.

Yet, ironically, the nation failed to get away from it all. For there were many who refused to recognize it. Many who came raiding. Many who assaulted and ripped away its resources, leaving the world barren and the people distraught.

They were forced to become warlike once more.

In the last stretches of the Realmbreaker Wars, Rhel was the only Realm that fought defensively. The only world where the whole planet contained the single nation called Rhel as well. The only kingdom with one, seemingly eternal king seeing to the stability of an entire planet’s worth of people.

Naturally, they would have fallen against the might of the various Realms without proper assistance. They never gained much help. But the main assistance they could count on from the very beginning of the existence of Rhel was one to be ferociously reckoned with…

The Early Years

History of Rhel

X-739

When Ryland had worked on the spell circles to establish inter-Realm travel, he hadn’t actually meant for them to facilitate civilian use. Oh no. Back then, they were for war.

When Androvia had been turning the tide of the Realmbreaker Wars and becoming a force to be reckoned with, all because Ryland had started using his rifts for all sorts of military advantages, Inscribing spell circles for rifting had been one of his first ideas. This way, the army could travel quickly and efficiently between bases, no matter where they were located.

This was especially vital for military operations on other Realms, where Androvia really became a force to be reckoned with, all because of the element of surprise.

However, one of the key establishments for signing the Accords was Androvia’s surrender of all such teleportal spell circles. It was something Ryland himself had pointed out that would be one of the key points of contention.

Eventually, they had all agreed to create a special taskforce to oversee the spell circles. An entire organization, small but highly important, who were dedicated to managing the spell circles.

Of course, Ryland himself hadn’t created such a group. One of the old mages of that era he had been close with had asked him for some of the guiding principles and had referred to him for a lot of the early decisions. But even then, Ryland had long lost his drive to be directly associated with such mundane things that others could already handle.

Even then, he was already focusing on how to reclaim the lost pieces of himself.

It was a testament to that organization that the spell circle was so familiar and stable, as it had been for centuries now. The mana intensifying was powerful, as it needed to be, but it was also smooth. Not gentle, exactly, but one that knew its place. A step away from the virulent intelligence afflicting all Living Mana.

The world shifted. Colours bloomed, threads of gold on the verge of fracturing running through it all. A sudden rush of air gusted through them for just a moment.

And then everything faded. Ryland blinked a few times to get the brilliant colours to stop dancing on his eyelids.

They were all now on Rhel.

“Welcome,” Yulkaviel said. “To the newly-constructed Hall of Travel!”

It wasn’t a much bigger affair than the one they had used near Arcoryx Academy. The enormous hallway housing the teleportation circle was lined with tiled pillars, the tops of which were decorated with powerful Inscriptions for various purposes.

They emerged out of the hall in single file to carry out their arrival processing, while a smaller group of Rhellians went in to experience the wonders of magical advancement.

Ryland noted quite a few curious looks thrown their way.

“Marionys Ryland,” the golemite officer said when it was Ryland’s turn for processing entry. It was standard procedure when travelling between Realms, and often when travelling between nations within a Realm too, so he didn’t mind much.

The golemite man frowned up at Ryland, his lava-filled eyes slowly squinting up at him from the name on the card that the academy had issued.

“You really are Marionys Ryland?” he asked.

“Indeed,” Ryland said. “Are you entertaining doubts about my identity?”

“I am… although, I have no room to ask for evidence or anything of the sort. After all, I must accept the reasoning provided by the High Adjutant himself.”

Sint poked her head around Ryland’s back. “Why are you doubting him? What’s Marionys Ryland to you?”

The golemite looked at her squarely in the eyes. “Marionys Ryland is a name woven through the layers of history that have settled upon Rhel. All those who are interested in our great Realm’s past are well aware of what that name means. Of what it stands for. As such, finding someone else walking about with it is… minorly shocking to say the least.”

When they were done processing and had moved on out of the Hall of Travel, Sint was once again squinting at him.

“I’m not surprised by anything I heard,” she said. At least she had lowered her voice so that no one else would overhear. “I can’t smell the traces of this world on you, likely because you’re using a different body from back then. But it makes sense you’ve been around. A lot.”

“Rhel is just one of the Realms I’ve been to and left my mark on,” Ryland said. His eyes crinkled just a bit at his smile. “After all, wouldn’t you say I’ve left my mark on Androvia on my return?”

Sint grumbled but conceded the point.

No one else mentioned anything about the little holdup, likely because they were all too busy gawking and staring around at everything Rhel had to show.

One of the most magical things about the Realm was how much of it was essentially reclaimed land. Rhel was originally a wasteland, a desert where almost nothing grew, a Realm devoid of any of the necessities that would allow life to flourish.

Yet, through the clever application of magic, it had been settled and tamed and remade.

Where once this exact location had been nothing but a sea of sandy dunes, they were currently walking along a wide boulevard that had all the modern Glyphtech other Realms boasted. Self-driving carriages, boats floating down slim canals, paths lined with gold-and-green leaved trees, tall buildings built with polished sandstone and alabaster and abalone.

As they walked, the High Adjutant himself pointed out different buildings off in the distance and other landmarks. The walk was clearly purposeful, the route well planned in advance.

They went by a park filled with statues in warlike poses, past a museum where they could see many weapons and armaments were stored, through a quarter of the city where many of the faithful of the local religion stayed and greeted the newcomers with friendly waves.

“Is it me,” Linsby said, pushing his glasses up with his hand. “Or does Rhel really value their history a great deal?”

Sint confirmed it with a nod. “I’ve noticed it too. The statues. The museum. Even that temple we passed was of Setharas, the Great God of war.” It was almost imperceptible, but Ryland didn’t fail to catch her sidelong glance in his direction. “You wouldn’t happen to know anything about it, would you?”

“Hmm,” Ryland said. “I’m certainly aware that they are very particular about their history on Rhel. They consider it a vital part of their identity.”

“I think that makes sense too, honestly.”

Ryland wasn’t quite certain what she meant by that, but he was certain that he didn’t want to explore it just then. As he had mentioned to Alaric, he had a certain history on Rhel. He just needed to be a little wary. There was no point in dragging down the rest of the exchange semester through any complications his presence might cause.

“We’ll be nearing the Rhellian Institute of Arcane Arts soon,” the High Adjutant said over the light chatter. “So far, we have been a little mum on what exactly you might expect there because I’ve wanted to leave that to our esteemed Council of High Academs when the time is right. That said, I’m sure you’re all dying to know how your semester will go, yes?”

There was a chorus of resounding yeses from most of the students in the group. Everyone who hadn’t replied vocally, including Ryland, were still looking intently at Yulkaviel.

“Well, rest assured that you will not lack for opportunities to learn,” he said with a bright smile. “For opportunities to grow. For opportunities to showcase what exact kind of mage you already are. And, of course, for opportunities to enjoy yourselves!”

There was an appreciative round of cheers at that. As they clapped, a flock of birds shot through the sky above. Sparkling golden feathers rained down from the sky, shimmering and glinting in the sunlight. Several oohs and has followed.

Yulkaviel continued leading them to the Rhellian academy, which would be the main location of their semester. He mentioned all the different courses they’d be allowed to participate in, depending on what they had already covered. There would be almost no restrictions. The students were very excited to hear about that.

As long as they were capable of absorbing the topics, as long as they possessed the right prerequisites, they could enroll into whichever class they wished.

And of course, if the Academs from Arcoryx Academy approved. There were undoubtedly certain differences in Rhel’s academy compared to Arcoryx. Something Yulkaviel promised would be discussed further with just the Academs later on.

Aside from just courses, there would be workshops, field trips, and even seminars for those really interested. To ensure that the foreign students got as much as they deserved, the Institute for Arcane Arts had invited a great number of important and experienced speakers to give talks on various subjects.

“And of course,” Yulkaviel said at the very end, lips quirking up into a grin that appeared almost mischievous. “We will have a nice little tournament during our adventures together.”

Several more excited gasps and chatter followed that little announcement. A great many students all exchanged quick, challenging looks, like they wouldn’t mind facing off against and beating students from their own schools if it was needed.

“Do you think they’ll have a tournament for the graduate students?” Sint asked.

“If they do,” Ryland said. “I certainly hope I don’t have to participate.”

“What do you have against wiping the floor with every single person who might dare to stand against you?”

Ryland looked over at her because of the odd phrasing. Sadly, he found nothing in her expression that suggested she was joking. To be fair, he couldn’t actually recall if Sint had ever joked about anything.

“It removes the purpose of the competition,” Ryland said.

“What do you mean?” She considered for a moment, uttering another question before he could start answering the first one. “Isn’t the point to find the best? Aren’t you the best?”

“Not in this sense.”

“No?”

“No. A tournament conducted by the Rhellian Institute of Arcane Arts would be to determine who among the students are the best. Who among their peers, their cohort, those who can be reasonably categorized as being at the same level.”

“None of which you are.”

“Yes. Quite.”

Sint scoffed, her whiskers shivering. She bared her carnivorous teeth challengingly. “You know, what you did a month or so ago didn’t go unnoticed.”

Ryland stilled for just a moment before continuing on. Up ahead, Yulkaviel was pointing out interesting facts about the lake of green water they were passing by. “How are those related?”

“I’m getting to it. I don’t understand what exactly happened because the rumours are wild, but I still had one question.”

“You wish to know the truth?”

Sint’s challenging look faded for a moment. “No. Not really. My question is, for all that you did, did you actually reveal the full extent of what you’re capable of?”

Ryland had to take a moment to think about it. He remembered the Calamity, recalled its extensive power, recollected just what he had needed to do to finally end it once and for all. “No, I don’t believe I did.”

“Good.” Sint huffed, before her challenging look returned once more. “Then I didn’t miss anything.”

Ryland laughed, shaking his head a little.

“Maybe you can do that one of these days,” Sint said. “Just let loose. Unleash it all. How much could you really bottle up in there, Ryland? Don’t you have any wish to meet someone who can actually bring out every single thing you’re capable of? Don’t you want to face off against anyone who can push you past your limits?”

Ryland arched an eyebrow at her as he tried and failed to keep the corner of his mouth from curling up. “What limits?”

Sint stared at him. Before she could reply to that, they had arrived. The Rhellian Institute of Arcane Arts welcomed them with wide open gates.

The whole place was tremendously different from Arcoryx Academy. And entirely unfamiliar for Ryland too. He hadn’t been here when they had started construction.

It was built more like a fortress, surrounded by walls that were nearly fifty armspans high. Every corner of the wall sported a tower, from within which magic glowed out bright as starlight. Beyond the walls themselves, only the tallest of the institute’s halls peeked out to give them a hint of what lay within.

It was almost like they were afraid of being attacked.

Considering the Flourinus Order was supposed to be on Rhel somewhere, that didn’t seem like an impossibility.

But it wasn’t like they had to wonder for long. Within a few minutes, the great gates of the institute rose up through specialized Glyphtech that left it entirely noiseless, and soon, they were in the field right after the gate, taking in everything. Even Ryland joined in on the staring.

Just as the walls suggested, the interior of the academy was very utilitarian. Where Arcoryx had boasted a smooth mix of old and new halls, all built artfully for the specific purpose of creating an academic environment where students could be fully immersed in the idea of learning magic, the Rhellian Institute was far more… respectful of Rhel’s history.

“Is it me,” Linsby said, clearly looking like he was resisting the urge to take off and wipe his glasses. “Or does every building look like they uprooted a castle cliff and just plopped it in here?”

Sint had to stifle her snicker.

“Dearest students, teachers, and other personnel from Arcoryx and Androvia,” Yulkaviel said, spreading his hands. “I welcome you all to Rhel and to the greatest academy for learning magic on Rhel, the Rhellian Institute of Arcane Arts.”

At his announcement, the doors to the first hall opened wide. A small army of students marched out, dressed in the institute uniform and taking an honour guard position on either side.

Even the uniform was militaristic. Long coat with metal gauntlets and a breastplate, plus combat boots. The classic dress of the War Mage. The students’ faces were forcefully neutral, eyes severe, postures straighter than that of a plank. Well-drilled was an understatement.

“We will have a final small screening,” Yulkaviel said with a tiny note of apology in his voice. “A final formality, for safety’s sake, since we have a great deal of students here, from all over you understand?”

He looked at them all with such a sweet expression for an old man, one who had so kindly entertained them all along their long walk, that they couldn’t refuse his wish.

While the students of the institute herded their Arcoryx compatriots to a different area, a small group of older guards headed straight for Ryland’s little group of sub-Academs. He thought that maybe there was a separate process for the graduate students, maybe even the Mage Knights and the lone Sentinel accompanying them, but that turned out to not be the case.

Instead, they came straight for Ryland himself.

“Marionys Ryland?” the dragonkin guard said, his helmet shaped to accommodate his reptilian head, “You are to come with us.”
