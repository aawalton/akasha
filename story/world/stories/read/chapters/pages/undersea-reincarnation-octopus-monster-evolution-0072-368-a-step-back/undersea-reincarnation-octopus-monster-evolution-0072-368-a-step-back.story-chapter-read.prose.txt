Toying with the Clydemar had felt good, Ash concluded as she slid behind him and tore his tendons. It would do. While it might not be enough to satiate the anger and resentment she still had for the bloodline, seeing the realization that she wasn’t taking him seriously dawn on his dumb, smug face was like a balm on her soul. Especially with how much shit-talking he’d done at the earlier bits of the fight. Ash would even go so far as say that the experience had been fun.

Up to now.

“Yeah,” he rasped, bloodied teeth grinning at her. “I know who you are. Ashlyn. Daughter of no one. I know. And they’ll know by the end of the day. How many do you think even remember your last name?”

Ash stood over him, her face frozen as the words rang in her ears. The small victory she’d tasted turned to ash in her mouth, and a numbness was spreading down her limbs. But even through it, every nerve, every muscle, every bone in her body was itching to step forward and plant her dagger through his eye. Even Velistraine’s warnings and plans were being drowned by the buzz in her ears and somehow, her defeated opponent’s voice still managed to pierce through everything and find her.

“Once they know, what do you think they’ll do? Snuff you out to hide their shame? Finish the job so no one could come for them? My family wasn’t the only one who benefited. Do you have a dagger for all of ‘em? Do you think they’ll just wait for you to grow stronger?” Lucian rocked back and forth as his words slipped through her thoughts, leaving a trail of poison whenever they passed. Ash gripped the dagger tighter. She could even feel someone trying to speak to her through telepathy, but that only made the buzzing in her mind louder.

Ash took a step forward. Her senses could only make out three things; the cold blade in her hand, the taunting voice in her ear, and the bile in the back of her throat.

She saw Lucian’s mouth open for another volley as he tried to get up, but her hand moved by itself. Before Ash could decide what to do, she hammered his mouth down with the fist still wrapped around the dagger and this time, she held nothing back. She punched down as hard as she would if she’d been sparring with Maurice or Henry.

Her knuckles crunched through bone, and the sand exploded upwards.

Right as the gasps of the crowd reached her ears, she watched the Clydemar’s blood fly as he rolled back, the momentum hard enough for him to roll head over heels, kicking up sand as he went. It was as if she was floating above her own body. And in a heartbeat, she knew that if she didn’t catch herself, she would have followed him to the ground and pommeled him until everything above the shoulders was reduced to paste. But something in the back of her mind caught her.

Ash stood there, heart hammering in her ears while she watched him squirm like a stepped-on worm. She felt air enter her lung, and clear out some of the fog in her mind.

Had she been holding her breath all this time?

Lucian groaned with pain, and the groan slowly turned to wheezing then laughter. Ash looked down at the blood dripping down the short dagger. It’d be so easy to step forward and silence him. And while she couldn’t say if ending him would quench whatever was wrong with her, the urge was there. The urge to do it and think about it later.

To just quiet him.

Glancing past him, she saw the cold, furious face she’d rather have fought instead. His father.

She’d rather get him. And like a piece of raft surfacing after a storm, Velistraine’s words rang in her mind.

Do you trust me?

Ash’s breath hitched, then she tore her eyes away from the father and back to the son. She had won. If she could even call it that. And she mostly, mostly, had stuck to the plan. So it was time to go.

The first step away was the hardest. Her foot refused to move. As if the only movement her body could conceive of was one that would take it closer to her target. But Ash hadn’t survived what had been thrown her way to have her decision be dictated by instincts.

The first step was the hardest. But once her leg moved, and once her foot hit the ground, the buzzing quieted, and the sound of her environment came back in her ears. The crowd was roaring, having been thoroughly entertained. The nobility were giving her a mix of glare and calculating gaze. Sparing a look toward the central box, Ash saw the king eyeing her. Still looking bored. But then he gestured, and a man in a steward uniform approached and bowed.

Maybe things did go off-plan, a bit, she thought, biting her lower lip. She was supposed to keep the fight a little even, but things slipped out of control near the end. A flare of anger lit up in her chest. At herself for allowing petty words to throw her off-course like that. But before she could wallow in self-recrimination, a cheery voice reached her.

“[Ash! Are you okay? Velistraine says to wave if you’re okay. And if not… uh… Lift the dagger? Yeah, lift the dagger if you’re not okay!]”

The voice was like a bucket of cold water. And taking a deep breath, Ash used the clarity provided to her for a few moments to shove her worries aside for a bit. Yes, she was annoyed at herself. Yes, she had let herself be goaded. But that can be dealt with later. At least as of today, she gave the Clydemar’s prestige a bloody nose.

The first of many.

A smile tugged on her lips at Maurice’s voice, and as much as she was tempted to look down on Lucian, she didn’t want to be riled up even further so raising a hand, she waved and began walking toward the exit.

In case Lucian’s words were true, she had to be ready in case someone tried anything. And for that, she’d better stay around her friends. She could sort herself out later. Right now, she craved the company of those she trusted.

First of which: recruitment.

Beating what was considered a “powerful” noble senseless did wonders for Ash’s prospects, and by the time they made it back to the ship—because that’s where Ash wanted to be—there was already a handful of agents waiting for them, eager to have a chat with Ash, to inquire about her current position on Red Reaver, and to if she’d be interested in exploring different options.
