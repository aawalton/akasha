“Explain yourself…”

The deep voice rang out, solemn and grave.

Luke turned to Riann and saw the concern etched across the man’s weathered face. Losing nearly all the officials in the imperial court had been devastating—but the claim that the noble families backing the kingdom had also perished? That was catastrophic.

There was little time for lengthy explanations—not with an army from Lhair marching toward them.

But Luke knew at a glance that the towering Master of Arms wouldn’t accept silence as an answer. He needed Riann’s support, and so, he chose to speak openly.

“This was their plan all along,” Luke said. “They never intended to rule through the Second Prince. Their goal was to destroy us completely.”

The words hung in the air, drawing varied reactions from the men around him. The tension was suffocating as each of them grappled with the dire implications.

By now, no one needed to ask who they were. It was obvious.

“It was all a ruse,” Luke continued, “meant to bait the traitors into eliminating anyone capable within the imperial court. That’s why they’re sending the army to Ralis. With no one left to organize a defense...”

At the mention of the encroaching force, Richard’s jaw tightened. His fists clenched at his sides.

“Luke, how sure are you?” he asked. “We never actually saw the enemy...”

Riann cut in before Luke could answer.

“Assuming you're right, Viscount Drakon,” he said, voice low and measured, “why would Lhair want to wipe us out completely? If they’ve already killed our court and noble families, we’re no longer a threat. Attacking Ralis would be senseless—a costly siege for no real gain.”

Luke nodded slowly, absorbing the logic. It was the part that didn’t add up.

While Ralis was the capital of the Marxx Kingdom, it wasn’t rich in resources. A few trade routes, merchant houses, maybe—but hardly enough to justify a prolonged assault and capture.

Tactically, it wasn’t a stronghold either. Nestled in the southern reaches of the Kingdom, the city lacked natural defenses and held no strategic value on the broader map.

“I don’t know,” Luke admitted, shaking his head. “There’s no real advantage to capturing Ralis—aside from the fact that it’s the capital.”

Of course he didn’t mention the grudge that the Bishop held towards him, not because Luke didn’t find it relevant—just that he didn’t believe the man was stupid enough to throw men away for such a pointless endeavor.

Riann’s brow furrowed in response.

“Yet you still believe they plan to attack?”

“I can’t be certain,” Luke replied. “But we can’t afford to ignore the possibility.”

A heavy silence followed—until Kayson stepped forward.

“So what now?” he asked grimly. “Everyone’s dead. The nobles in the city. The king. The entire imperial court. Someone needs to take charge of the city’s defense—or we’ll be sitting ducks when Lhair arrives.”

“We need to have the Second Prince ascend the throne,” Richard said firmly. “If he appoints Luke as one of his ministers, we’ll have the authority to mobilize the troops inside the city.”

“I agree,” Riann added. “The sooner Prince Lyon is crowned, the sooner we can organize a defense.”

He turned to Luke, his gaze steady.

“Where is he now?”

“Sebastian is keeping him safe in the central district for now. Kayson, bring them both here. As for the noble district…”

Luke’s voice trailed off as a system notification flickered across his vision.

[Your subordinate Christian has been gravely wounded]

“Fuck!” Luke cursed, eyes widening.

“Go to the noble district! NOW!”

Startled by the sudden outburst, all three men froze, exchanging uncertain looks. But it was Kayson who reacted first. He spat a curse of his own, spun on his heel, and sprinted toward the palace walls.

With a powerful leap, he vaulted the high stone barrier as if it were no more than a fence—his superhuman strength and speed flashing in full display.

“What happened?” Riann asked sharply.

“Our men are under attack in the noble district,” Luke replied, tension gnawing at him. “We can’t afford to lose anyone else.”

Though Christian was only labeled an Uncommon Subordinate, his strength was still bolstered by the system. For him to be gravely injured meant one of two things: it was either a sneak attack… or their opponent was a Holy Knight.

I hope it’s the former. If it’s a Holy Knight…

He didn’t allow the thought to finish before the unmistakable sound of blades being drawn snapped him back to the present. The steel-on-steel rasp echoed through the hall as both Richard and Riann raised their weapons—greatsword and longsword gleaming—as they turned toward a figure emerging from the haze.

A man in a tattered robe stumbled toward them, his face obscured by the lingering smoke from the thunder bombs. With the wide frames of the two Boyd warriors blocking his view, Luke craned his neck, narrowing his eyes as he tried to make out the figure.

But as the smoke thinned, recognition struck—and relief followed like a wave.

“Stand down. It’s Tristan,” Luke said, placing a calming hand on Richard’s shoulder.

Sure enough, the weary figure stepped into clearer view. Tristan’s trademark wry grin appeared beneath dirt-smeared cheeks as he glanced at the bodies strewn across the stone floor.

“Looks like I was a little late.”

“It’s good to see you, my friend.”

Luke was the first to step forward, embracing the man. Though Tristan’s frame was lean, it felt like hugging a statue carved from marble. Thankfully, the man didn’t squeeze back.

“Is this all who survived?” Tristan asked grimly, his tone sobering.

“Apart from a few low-level officials who are still unconscious—yes,” Riann answered.

“And the noble families?”

Luke’s expression darkened. He gave Tristan a swift rundown of everything that had happened so far. At the mention of Christian’s injuries, Tristan’s expression flickered—his confident demeanor slipping.

“Will young Kayson make it in time?” he asked, a hint of worry in his voice.

Just as Luke opened his mouth to answer, a familiar chime echoed in his mind. A new notification window unfurled before his eyes.

[DING]

[Quest: Muddied Waters has been completed]

[Please claim your reward in the Quest interface]

What!?

Instead of relief, the unexpected quest completion filled Luke with unease.

Tristan noticed his shift in expression immediately.

“What is it, Luke?” he asked.

Luke held up a hand to silence him, focusing inward as he navigated to the Quest tab in his system window—his heart pounding in his chest.

Main Quest: Muddied Waters

Details: The Marxx Kingdom has been infiltrated by the serpent, its minions spread throughout the realm. But what is a mere snake in front of the Dragon?

Task: Reclaim Ralis and ascend to power [Completed]

Reward: System function [Claim Reward]

Failure: Defeat

He reread the quest details, confusion mounting. It had clearly stated that he needed to not only reclaim Ralis but also ascend to power. Without placing the Second Prince on the throne and being appointed as a minister, the second condition shouldn’t have been fulfilled.

Yet… the system had marked it as complete.

With no further explanation provided, he couldn't make sense of it.

I’ll just accept the rewards for now, Luke thought. Maybe the new system function will help us.

He didn’t dwell on the mystery for long. With a mental command, he accepted the quest rewards—prompting a new notification to appear:

[You have received the System Function: Chat]

Chat? Luke’s eyes lit up with intrigue.

He quickly navigated to the new function, indicated by a glowing red marker in the system interface. As he opened it, a list populated before him.

Two menus appeared:

One labeled Subordinates,

Another for Allies, which included individuals like Master Boyd and others with whom he shared a strong bond.

Some names were greyed out—likely indicating they were currently unreachable for one reason or another. It was rather intuitive, like many of the online games he’d played in the past.

Swallowing down his excitement, Luke’s gaze moved through the Subordinates list and paused when he saw Christian’s name greyed out. A grim reminder of the alert he’d received earlier. But the others remained available.

Subordinates:

Sebastian Black

Tristan Gale

Kayson Diego

Zion Mason

Jason Jacks

Blake Adams

Christian Hays

Victoria Rose

Luke’s eyes lingered on the final name.

Victoria Rose…

Her name wasn’t greyed out. That meant she was alive—and reachable.

Ever since hearing she’d been gravely wounded, a sense of dread had weighed heavy on his heart. He hesitated for a moment, knowing this wasn’t the ideal time to check in—but guilt flared as he tried to scroll past her name.

I’ll just check quickly—to make sure she’s okay.

He selected her name.

There was no dial tone. No ringing. Just silence.

Then—

Victoria: “L-Luke!? Is that you?”

The familiar voice echoed in his mind, soft and fragile—tinged with disbelief and emotion. Hearing her like this made his chest tighten with guilt.

Luke: “It’s me, Victoria. Are you okay? Where are you now?”

He was momentarily startled. He hadn’t spoken aloud. The words had been transmitted telepathically.

His Scholarly Pursuit trait flickered in the background of his mind, already analyzing the implications of this form of communication. But logic was drowned out by the pounding of his heart.

Victoria: “Luke… You’re alive?”

Her voice was filled with tremor and raw vulnerability.

But his relief was short-lived.

Luke’s brows furrowed. From her reaction, it was clear she had believed—truly believed—he was dead. And knowing Victoria, she wouldn’t have come to such a conclusion on her own… not without proof.

Unless… someone told her.

Luke: “Are you in Ralis City?”

It was the only place he’d allowed the false report of his and the Second Prince’s deaths to spread. If she’d heard it—and believed it—it had to be from inside the capital.

She didn’t reply right away.

But in the silence, he heard her quiet sobs.

Soft. Choked. Unfiltered.

Not broken—but something closer to relieved.

And that made Luke’s heart ache all the more.

After a while, her breathing steadied.

Victoria took a slow breath and spoke softly.

Victoria: “I’m sorry, Luke… your men—”

Before she could finish, another voice cut in.

“Victoria!? What the hell are you doing?”

Luke stiffened. He recognized Kayson instantly—the very man he had just sent to the noble district. His Scholarly Pursuit skill whirred to life, assembling fragments of information with terrifying speed.

His heart dropped.

Luke: “Victoria… go back to the palace with Kayson. We’ll talk about everything when you get here.”

He kept his voice steady, but the tremor still slipped through.

Victoria: “Urgh!”

Her scream tore through his mind.

Panic exploded in his chest.

Luke: “Victoria!?”

He called her name again and again, but she didn’t answer.

Only strained groans of pain echoed back.

A notification burst into his vision.

[This participant is no longer contactable]

[Your subordinate Victoria Rose is gravely injured]

Luke’s eyes widened in horror.

He swiped the alerts aside and snapped to Kayson’s name in the chat list.

Luke: “Bring Victoria to me. Now.”

Kayson: “W‑What!? Luke?”

Luke: “There’s no time—just hurry!”

Kayson: “It’s… it’s a mess here. I think Victoria was fighting our men and—”

Luke’s breath caught in his throat.

He forced the words out.

Luke: “Just bring her back.”

Only after Kayson’s confirmation did Luke move to the next name.

Luke: “Zion, report. What’s your status?”

A brief pause—then Zion’s voice returned, unsteady.

Zion: “General… it’s bad. Christian’s bleeding a lot.”

Luke: “Apply pressure to the wound. I’m sending help.”

Luke clenched his teeth.

Whatever had happened in the noble district, it sounded like Victoria had been the one to injure Christian. It was the last thing he had expected—and the worst possible timing.

He moved to the final name.

Luke: “Sebastian. It’s Luke. Don’t ask how I’m contacting you. Christian is bleeding out in the noble district. Go to him. Bring the Second Prince if you have to.”

Sebastian: “Luke?”

The pause that followed was too long.

Too heavy.

Sebastian: “I have… bad news. The Second Prince—”

A chill crept up Luke’s spine.

Deep down he already knew.

Swallowing the dread clawing at his chest, he forced himself to ask.

Luke: “What is it…”

Silence.

Then—

Sebastian: “He’s dead.”

The words shattered him.

It felt as if the world collapsed inward, crushing everything he had been holding together. His iron resolve—strained for so long—finally broke.

Luke’s knees gave out.

He sank to the floor, the crushing weight of the moment pressing down on him as if the heavens themselves had fallen.

It all made sense now.

The reason the system had marked the quest as complete.

With the death of the Second Prince, the requirement to be appointed as a minister no longer applied—there was no one left to appoint him. No royal to serve under. No crown to legitimize authority.

He was the only one left.

No rivals. No claimants. No throne to inherit—only power to seize.

Luke Drakon was now the sole capable ruler remaining in Ralis.

A sudden shout pulled him from his thoughts.

“Luke! What is it?”

Richard stepped forward, grabbing him firmly by the shoulders.

Luke slowly raised his gaze.

He looked into the eyes of the man who had sworn his life to the Second Prince—a knight through and through. The grief waiting behind Richard’s expression was already threatening to spill forward.

But there was no time for softness. No time for ceremony or grief.

“The Second Prince is dead,” Luke said, his voice steady despite the weight of it.

He drew in a breath.

“I’m sorry, Richard.”
