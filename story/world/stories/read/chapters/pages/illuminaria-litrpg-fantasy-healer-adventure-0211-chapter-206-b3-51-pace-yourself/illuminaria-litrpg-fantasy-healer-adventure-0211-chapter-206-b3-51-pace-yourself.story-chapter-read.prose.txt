Chapter 206 / B3-51: Pace Yourself

Far too soon after the sun began to light the sky the next morning, Kenda squeezed herself against Joe and mashed a fierce kiss to his lips before launching herself from the bed.

“I gotta go!  Sorry!  The guard wants to make the first excursion into the valley tomorrow.  I have so much prep work to get done,” she bubbled in a joyous frenzy.  Her smile stretched from ear to ear as she summoned clothes from her spatial bracelet.  She yanked the outfit on, not even bothering to gather up the clothes he had helped her shed the night before.  Joe received one more exuberant kiss before she was out the door, twisting her hair up as she bounded down the steps.

“Good lord,” Joe sighed, sending a force paw to close the door.  He flopped back down onto his pillow and felt the feathery weight of the ghost pack settling around him.  Joe hoped Mojo was still being good, but he wasn't too worried.  The shug-pup adored the elderly Catstaffs and Mahq; he wouldn’t ruin his chances to keep visiting them by misbehaving.

Sure that all was well enough with the world without him for just an hour more, Joe let himself slip back into a doze.   In a few minutes, his dreams carried him to an odd location.

At first, he thought it was Donnelly, the huge dormitory that he lived in during his first year at college, but its hallways twisted and intersected rooms in odd ways. Sometimes, hallways were the classic institutional white walkways, but after a while, he found himself strolling past colonial wooden paneling, with Oriental-pattern carpeting underfoot.  Stairways led off the endless hall, some dull and boring, others elegant and sweeping.

The air around him hummed with quiet conversations, laughter, and the occasional echo of someone calling a name that might be his, but he was never sure if it was. As he wandered, people greeted him warmly, acting as though he was expected, inviting him into some very odd conversations. Eventually, he headed off again, weaving his way through the bizarre labyrinth.

He wasn’t nervous about any of this strangeness.  Often, he felt baffled and driven by an urge to get somewhere or that he should be doing something.  The deeper he went, the more the dormitory felt like an endless living organism, full of possibilities.

When he finally broke out of his slumber into wakefulness, he could recall the concept and feeling of the dream vividly, though the many specific details were just fuzzy impressions.  For a moment, he was a bit bummed to have awoken, kind of wanting to go back and explore the place some more, but its clarity faded away with every second that passed.

Shaking away the lingering traces of his oddly poignant dream, Joe looked to his table, debating if he just wanted to go straight to work.

“Not yet.  Let’s stretch out the kinks first.  Who wants to go for a walk?” he asked his trio of watchful companions.  That phrase was somehow universal to all three worlds, Earth, Illuminaria, and the Feylands.  Ranu rocketed off the bed, screeching to a halt at the door to the atrium.  Bayu began to shout her head off.  Big Baru gave Joe a dirty look that clearly stated, ‘Look what you’ve done.’

Bayu’s cacophony summoned Mojo and Mahq, who were keen to join in on the run.  The druid’s form flowed effortlessly into a dog larger than Mo but only about two-thirds as tall as Bayu and Ranu. Since Joe could tell that everyone felt like pushing themselves, as soon as they made it across the Rock Walk bridge, the group turned south, heading away from Cliffside.  Instead, they galloped down Banu Leaf Lane towards the farmlands.   Mojo’s [Slipstream] skill allowed him to keep up for small sprints; the rest of the time, he rode on Joe’s shoulder or Mahq’s back.

The plan originally was to run a big loop and come back to the South Wall district gate.  Yet as the runners reached roughly the midway point, Joe could see a gathering of animals and people that had congregated onto the path that connected their farm to the lane they were jogging.  They all stood at the front gate, under a sign that announced the farm’s name as the Sweet Cane Farmstead.  Both the people and the herd animals were covered in welts that burned red in Joe’s woundsight.

He turned to intersect with the group, calling out, “Hey!  I’m a healer with the Adventurer’s Guild.  Who needs me the most?”

They all turned towards him, and he could see relief bloom on their faces.  The farmers were mostly human, with one large, ogreish fomori standing a whole head and shoulders taller than the others.  The children were brought to him first.  The poor kids had clearly been stung several times each.  At the center of the scarlet welts, a barbed stinger was lodged into their skin.  These were not the tiny hooks Joe had seen from honey bees.  Each barb was as big as a large thorn.

“What did this to you guys?” he inquired as he used [Deaden Flesh] to numb the spot before his [Casting Claw] plucked out the first stinger.

“Got us a nest of Grumble Bees in the barn,” a woman identified as Winnie Carlyle huffed.  “We heard the horses hollering this morning and all o’ us just got ourselves stung ta bits gettin’ them outta there.”

“Ahh, sorry. I’m a bit new to Fort Coral.  What’s a grumble bee?”

“Mean buggers.  Bout as big as little Terrie’s fist,” she replied, pointing to the preteen youth Joe was working on. “Not only do they regenerate them stingers, their buzzing will just about drive ya mad.  Sounds like a table of bitchy bitties grousing about every darn little thing: weather, food, family … you name it.  Makes it so folk lose their train of thought and get turned around.  And that just gets them stung all the more.”

“Ok.  Let’s keep everyone away from the barn for a bit while I heal these stings.  Then I’ll look into clearing out the nest,” Joe announced as he dropped two [Halefires] far enough apart to cover all the farmers and the stung stock they had brought from the barn.

As Joe began to work on the people, he noticed Mahq, back in his human shape, moving to the suffering animals.  Joe merely had to throw a look at Baru, and she, along with the other two hounds, moved to guard the young man.  Mojo also joined them, looking to see if he could help his buddy.

Joe left them to work on the beasts while he patched up the farmfolk.  His process was to numb the spot, pluck the stinger, use [Dispel Noxa] to push out the bee venom, and then let the [Halefire] soothe and heal.  With the first welt, he noticed the expelled venom was more acidic than he expected.  It burned the surrounding skin further as it was pushed out of the wounds.  Thankfully, his satchel had an endless supply of bandages.  Joe began to eject the caustic fluid directly onto the gauze, throwing away the melting scrap before it could touch the patient again.

Eventually, he found a stinger too deep for his [Casting Claw] to reach.  Once again, the satchel came to the rescue.  Joe withdrew a tiny scalpel-like knife along with a set of tweezers from his healer’s bag.  With these, he was able to pull the barb out of the numbed flesh, causing as little damage as possible.

Curious as to how Mahq was dealing with the stingers, he looked over at an unexpected sight.  Mojo was perched on the back of a large dray horse, his tail hanging down over the beast’s haunches.  Hanging onto the shug-pup’s long prehensile tail was a small bird that looked like a nuthatch.  With a quick flick of its head, the pointed beak flashed into the wound and plucked out the embedded stinger.  The Mahq-hatch spat out the barb and then placed its forehead against the wound.  Green tendrils of magic flowed around the inflamed wound.  As the angry red aura faded away in Joe’s woundsight, the pair repositioned themselves to reach the next welt.

Together, the two healers fixed everyone up in fifteen minutes or so.  Joe looked at his experience gains and was grateful to see that these non-life-threatening heals had only added a tiny bit of experience.  He was in no danger of leveling too soon.  He still had a few [Level-Lock] potions, but he doubted he would need them unless these bees were much higher leveled than Joe was expecting.

Joe asked Mahq to stay with the farmers and left Baru to take care of any bees that made their way to the group.  Even though they were mostly spectral, Joe had found they could solidify their jaws.  More than once, he had seen Baru latch onto Mojo’s tail before the shug-pup was about to raid the kitchen cabinets.  Her gentle grip held the changeling firmly.   He had no doubt that she could bite if she needed to.

As Joe, Mojo, Bayu, and Ranu reached a fenced paddock attached to the barn, he was able to get a good look at their adversaries.  The bees were massive bumblebee-shaped apian insects, each one as large as a tennis ball.  At least a hundred of the yellow-striped bugs ambled in odd loops through the sky around the red barn.

Grumble Bee Scout: Level 3: Vermin: Skirmisher: Vigor

Joe kept his distance for a minute and just watched.  At their level Joe was not too worried that they could do much to him, but there were a lot of them.  If they had any sort of cumulative effect like the dirge wights had, he might be in for a rude surprise.

As he studied them, he noted they flew in awkward, blundering paths.  It was almost as if the bees were unable to fly in straight lines.   That might help if Joe had to run away, but it also might make them harder to hit.

At this distance, he could hear the weird, annoyed-sounding buzz of the bees.  It really did sound like a room full of pissy Karen’s complaining about their service.  Joe could see the two ghost hounds shaking their heads, and Mojo kept swiping his hands across his ears.  It seemed to have no effect on  Joe thanks to [Iron Mind]’s major resistance to the confusion effect.

Pulling a hunk of obsidian from his [Bullet Bag], he realized he’d have to be careful where he tossed his rock sprays since he didn’t want to damage the family's barn any more than was absolutely necessary.  He wouldn’t be much of a net-positive help if he rid them of the muttering monsters yet ended up trashing their livelihood in the process.

“Ok, Mo.  I want you to keep your distance.  You are my artillery support. Got it?”  A quiet ruff was his affirming answer.  “You two stay back too and protect, Mo.  Only come to my rescue if I fall down.  Even if I get fully swarmed, stay here. Got it?”

The spirit hounds of Annwn dipped their heads, for once Bayu refrained from a verbal reply.

He needed to be able to see the bees and so opted not to go with a hide-covered [Grimoiriguard].  Instead, he let the pelt cover most of his body.  The wolfhead settled onto his crown, shading his eyes like a baseball cap. Only his face, hands, and feet were unprotected by the [Hide of the Night Hunter].  He knew he was going to be sweating heavily in a few seconds, but the thick, shaggy fur, with its median physical damage resistance, should shield him from the majority of the stingers.

He dismissed the one [Casting Claw] he always had active, and he resummoned a pair of the amber paws using [Dual Cast].

“Okay.  Here we go,” Joe declared, moving toward the swarmed structure, while [Grit Razor] chewed into the chunk of volcanic stone in his fist.
