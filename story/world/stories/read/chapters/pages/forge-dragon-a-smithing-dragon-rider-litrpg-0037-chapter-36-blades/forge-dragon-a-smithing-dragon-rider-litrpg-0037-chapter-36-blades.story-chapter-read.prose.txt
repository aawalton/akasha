The two ran on together and Caleb kept up his barrage.

Behind him he sensed the movement as a contingent of troglodytes and Frigidi moved to stop him, but Caleb sent four pila flying back at them. The spears took out two and discouraged the rest. For the first time since Caleb had gained his magic, he stood in a field littered with steel, and the power he felt seemed limitless. So far he’d fought with only what he brought with him, but standing in a field of magical spears, he felt like nothing could touch him.

Another Frigidi grew brave and ran at Caleb. He let the man get within the range of his Imperium before suddenly lifting three pila off the ground in his path to become palisade. He ran into the spears and he momentum was enough to see him dead.

The remaining soldier took in the sheer amount of pila around them and backed up well out of Caleb’s range, leaving their leader to fend for himself.

Caleb moved in closer to the battle to aid his brother and Bog who had by then engaged the giant. Bog had already bitten into the foot of the giant only to be through back a dozen meters. Grisim stood opposite the faster initialized and landed blows when the opportunity presented itself. Grisim was by far the lesser threat of the two, but as such he was disregarded by the giant.

Caleb halting his barrage until he got closer, only starting up again when he was confident he wouldn’t Grisim or their empire allies. Bog kept up repeated charges, and each tim he reached the giant he was kicked back. The giant saw the scorching hot dragon as the number one threat and as such made sure to get rid of him every change he had. Despite the apparent futility of his methods Bog was doing the most work. The kicks to the dragon—even if Bog never landed an opening blow—left damage to the foot of the giant which he had to replenish with snow from the battlefield and the other two attackers always landed blows on the armor when the focus was on Bog.

Finally the giant had gotten enough of Grisim, and swung his sword wide pushing the collared warrior back. In the opening, he reached out for Grisim with he offhand and snatched his spear, breaking the haft in half.

Without a weapon, Grisim fell back, but the giant didn’t let him.

Caleb broke off his barrage. He’d covered one side of the giant with pilum but they weren’t slowing him down and the action of the battle kept knocking them off. He ran to intercept the attack on his brother and threw his hammer in front of him to strike the reaching arm. The hammer struck the armor, but instead of cracking a part off as it had with the elementals, it sank into the soft snow and then fell to the ground. The force of the blow was enough to distract the man into taking Caleb on as a new threat, allowing Grisim to pick up a blade of his own.

Distantly Caleb heard sounds of protest from the direction of the legion at the action and Grisim himself did a double take at the weapon he held before charging the giant once more.

Once more bereft of a weapon, Caleb looked to the battlefield to provide and provide it did. To his metal sense, he stood on a mountain of steel. And, since he didn’t actually stand on a mountain of steel, that meant one thing. He stood in a field of magical weapons.

All around him lay the dead legionaries, and their bodies were covered in magical steel. He summoned a spatha from the ground to his hand and ran to aid his brother.

The giant came at Caleb first, and he dove to the side, pushing himself against a pile of armored bodies. He flew away easily, slashing at the outstretched arm as he went. The blade cut cleanly and easily through the arm. Easily enough that Caleb had to check the weapon through his interface.

Blade of the Legion (soul bound)

Uh oh, he thought. He definitely wasn’t supposed to be holding that. Thankfully the one he’d taken was not of a dead soldier, and he’d avoided being conscripted to the legion from the single act.

Grism got in behind the giant and landed two slashes against his calf, taking chunks of snow armor with it.

The giant donkey kicked Grisim sending him flying back. He crashed in a heap, not dead but no longer in the fight.

Caleb tried to see where the other initialized warrior was but couldn’t see him or sense his movement. The blow to create space for Grisim must have landed and Caleb and Bog were in this alone.

Bog? Caleb questioned through the Bond.

The dragon was limping back to the battle in a lot of pain. Caleb told him to wait.

Free of any allies he had to avoid harming, Caleb looked for more weapons.

The battlefield was littered with blades, and Caleb ran around avoiding the blows of the giant collecting them. He didn’t risk picking up any with his hands, but as he’d learned, hands were such a crude tool for wielding steel.

He brought them up and spun them around him, creating a storm of spinning steel. The giant tried to reach in but lost a snow finger when a spatha came spinning around at it. the spatha in question lost its momentum and fell to the ground and Caleb had to expend a lot of metal effort to isolate that single blade and picking it back up.

Controlling the storm of blades spinning around him was relatively easy compared to the work he’d been doing with his magic. He leaned on the technique he used for controlling the wind. For the wind, he didn’t control it on a micro level but on a macro level. He visualized the particles of the air as a single thing, and with that mindset could move large quantities at once. With steel he’d focused on the details, controlling down to the crystalline level to achieve his desires. But in this he found he could blend the techniques. If he thought of the blades as one, he could spin them around him just as he did the air.

It was only when a single blade required his attention that he needed to split his focus to lift it up.

The giant gave up trying to grab at Caleb and brought his own sword to bear.

Forced to dodge, Caleb jumped up, losing his control of his blade storm as he flew upwards. He sent a single blade—the one he’d been correcting—at the giant and it drove deep into its side.

Caleb landed in the wake of the blade sweep, and conjured the storm of blades again just in time to fend off a kick from the giant. Caleb ran in close, inside the reach of the long sword, and allowed his spinning storm of death to shave a layer off the armor.

Unable to avoid any damage, the giant risked it and punched through the storm at Caleb.

Once more Caleb dropped his control of the storm as he had to rely on a skill. Immutable Metal activated and the force of the strike which struck Caleb in the head, was distributed through his whole body, turning the death blow that would have broken his neck into a punt that sent him sailing.

He landed hard in the snow, and quested out around him for more weapons. He found more, and worked to get them up into a storm, but stopped.

Instead, he activated Vortex, and then pushed it further. Instead of using his Imperium Ventorum to enhance the skill, he leaned on his Imperium Metallorum. The blades on the ground around him had stirred in the wind, but once he activated this Imperium it was like the wind was a load stone. Slowly the blades began to drag around him in a circle. He pushed more of his Imperium into the skill and the blades began to move faster. More and they left the ground. More and Caleb felt the strain, but the blades lifted fully into the air and joined into the vortex in full.

The effort was great, but it was easier than it had been. Now the spinning blades were partially taken up by his skill, and he could divert more of his attention to focusing on the individuals that needed his aid.

He ran around the giant, dodging blows with his Imperium aided leaps. He circled the giant, keeping the great sword out of his reach. Caleb never had to attack to land blows, all he had to do was stay close. Slowly the giant shrank as the blades shredded the suit of armor. The snow would replenish itself, but slower each time.

Caleb’s massive mana pool began to drain, but another of the ritualists’ potions saw to that. Caleb was sure this giant has potions on him, but it didn’t seem that he could take them encapsulated as he was.

In the end it became a battle of attrition, and Caleb had the upper hand. Slowly the giant shrank, his sword shrinking with him. He landed blows on Caleb, but always was Caleb able to get a blade in between him and the attack, allowing his Immutable Metal to keep the blow from being lethal. At other times he used his power attack to jump roughly out of the way.

And then the white spray of snow that was a constant effect of Caleb’s attacks was suddenly red, and Caleb was looking at half of a man.

The back half, for the front portion had been shredded away.
