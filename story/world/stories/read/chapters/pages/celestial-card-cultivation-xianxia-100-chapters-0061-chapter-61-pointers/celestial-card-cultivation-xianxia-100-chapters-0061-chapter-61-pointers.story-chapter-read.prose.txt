Despite all his obligations, Jianyi had found some time for the library. There were two topics he wanted to research.

The first was the Wild Chrysanthemum he had found during their recent mission. He sat on a small table with the large tome that Elder Qin Ye had recommended as the reference book on herbs in front of him.

There were half a dozen Wild Chrysanthemums in the book—and it only covered Qi Gathering and Qi Condensation Realm natural treasures!

After reading up on all of them, he was reasonably sure that the plant he had so crudely harvested was a Nourishing Chrysanthemum, a Qi Gathering variant with a Wood affinity. Its leaves were used by alchemists for some restorative potions.

He was pretty sure that the attendants at the gathering hall would have told him the same thing, but now he knew, and he had learned a few things along the way.

Setting the topic aside until he could go and sell the plant to the sect, he focused on the second topic.

When the concept of the spirit needle had been introduced in class, he had promised himself to look into the method with which a cultivator could do the same work without the artifact. That had been before Elder Sun had gifted him one of the implements that allowed you to extract cards from cultivators, beasts, and natural treasures, but it still interested him, and by now, he had internalized Elder Sun’s idea that it was helpful to choose the hard way as one progressed along the way.

It turned out that extracting cards without the help of the artifact required a cultivator to control four threads of qi in parallel, something that Jianyi was still far away from. He was unable to control three threads at the same time and assumed that going up to four would be a magnitude harder still.

Nonetheless, he took notes on how the technique was supposed to work—it was freely available in the library, as it was not something that had any demand—and stored those in one of his rings. Maybe it could be a training tool once he had advanced to that level.

Satisfied to have these two tasks completed, Jianyi returned the books and left the library.

Once at home, he found a message from Lu Shan; it was the time for the third and final duel.

He only had a few days to get ready.

Minghao clapped him on the back and sent him off.

Beyond his regular training with group Amber, Jianyi had spent quite a bit of time with his teacher and the gauntlet artifact that forced precision under pressure.

Not that it was directly helpful to the duel, but he had also learned Eternal Sun and Eternal Moon, the two next movements of the Eternal Spear Meditation.

What could potentially help during the fight was his daily practice on activating techniques without physical indication, and he had even managed to activate Force of the Forest while in the gauntlet. Once.

As he was walking down the path from the inner sect to the training field of the outer sect, he was still unsure whether he would help or hinder himself if he tried to activate a technique during the duel.

He would have to decide in the moment.

Shuhua, Miyao, and the cousins were waiting for him at the entrance to the outer sect. He smiled at them and even appreciated their support, but was glad that they did not try to engage him in a conversation. There was so much going on in his head that he needed to sort before he could step into the ring.

Front and center were the words that Elder Sun had said to him as he had brought him breakfast this morning.

“My young student, in the olden days when one was challenged to a duel, it was called to ‘exchange pointers’, after which the protagonists continued to slaughter each other, which seems pretty pointless to me, if you excuse a little humor. But the idea was not a bad one.”

That had been all, and the elder had waved him out of his study.

Before the last duel, the elder had given him a hint that had helped him make the duel much closer than it would have been otherwise, and he was sure that the elder’s words had not been thoughtless babble—but how it was supposed to help him beat Lu Shan was still not clear to him.

And he wanted to beat Lu Shan. Badly.

It wasn’t even that he hated the boy. After his first despicable act of attempted theft against Miyao, he had not acted dishonorably. Just deranged.

No, it was almost pity, more than hate, that he felt.

But he had felt the looks of his fellow disciples in the aftermath of having been beaten twice. He knew that it shouldn’t matter to him, but to beat the noble who had been trained by the best tutors while Jianyi had practiced in his small backyard would be a validation of the hard work he had invested ever since he had joined the sect and before.

There was also, he had to be honest with himself, apprehension of what techniques the other boy had available to him. The one he had used in the first duel had been enough to beat him, and he was sure there were more.

Lastly, he was pretty sure that the smart move was not to show a fire technique. While he trusted his companions—or friends rather—there was still no reason to advertise that he had two affinities. Or more…

He took a deep breath; there was nothing to do about it now. He would just have to show everything that he had learned and see whether it was enough.

The number of spectators had grown once again. It felt like almost everyone who had joined the sect at the same time as Jianyi was present. And a few select others: in one corner sat two of the inner disciples who trained him in group Amber, and he could even see the inner disciple who had accused him of being a troublemaker and then apologized, sitting on the stairs overlooking the fields, who nodded when their eyes met.

Cai Fangyin stood alone at a ring, waiting for the contestants. Lu Shan had apparently not yet shown up.

Jianyi went over to the weapon rack right away and selected a blunted weapon. Only then did he join Fangyin and greeted her with a slight bow. “Senior sister, I hope this… spectacle isn’t interfering too much with your duties.”

Fangyin smiled and shook her head. “It doesn’t—and if I understood Lu Shan correctly, this is the last in the series?”

“I sincerely hope so,” Jianyi said with a chuckle. The short exchange helped him calm down a bit.

He was alerted to Lu Shan’s arrival by the increased noise from the audience. He saw a few people, including some of the group that had caused him trouble in group Amber, shouting encouraging messages to the disciple who looked serene as he joined them.

After greetings were exchanged, Lu Shan got a spear for himself and took position in the ring.

“You know the rules. The fight ends with first blood, when one yields, or I call it. No lethal attacks.” Fangyin looked to both of them, making sure they acknowledged her statement.

“Begin!”

Jianyi hadn’t seen a reason to deviate from his relatively successful strategy in the second fight: relentless attack. Right from the beginning, he struck at his opponent from all angles. Lu Shan, however, was not surprised and defended and countered effortlessly.

His movements were still incredibly efficient and elegant. But, to Jianyi’s slight surprise, he could keep up. He blocked an attack with Unyielding Wall and seamlessly moved into a Flowing Current that Lu Shan blocked himself, their spears clashing at shoulder height and, with a loud crack, Lu Shan’s spear broke, the tip flying by Jianyi, narrowly missing his neck.

Lu Shan stood still, his mouth open, but Jianyi stepped back, reminded that in a previous duel he had almost picked a spear with a crack, and indicated for Lu Shan to go get another spear.

Cai Fangyin made a motion for Lu Shan to go ahead and grab the spear, though she looked at Jianyi with a raised eyebrow. Belatedly, he realized that in a duel, he probably wasn’t obliged to extend that act of fairness to his opponent, but he thought it was the right thing anyway. Anything else did not seem dignified to him.

As Jianyi stood in the ring, the shouting and laughter of the audience in the background, he thought of Elder Sun’s words. And they resonated with him. Exchanging pointers was about learning, getting better, getting stronger, so that you could eventually defeat the real enemy. The message was clear: fight to the very best of your abilities so that you could learn from the experience. Fear of losing was the enemy of focused learning.

He twirled his spear in newfound joy and took position to start the fight anew.

Lu Shan cleared his throat as he reentered the circle. “Your honorable behavior does not make up for your weakness, but it shall count against the humiliation you deserve,” he declared loudly.

It didn’t matter to Jianyi. He wanted to fight against an opponent who had been significantly stronger than him so far and see how far he had come.

The fight got more frantic.

Piercing Fang. Blazing Arc. Flowing Current.

Met: Unyielding Wall. Retreat. Sink.

On both sides.

Then, two cards appeared before Lu Shan without the exaggerated inhalation that had given it away the last time they had fought.

Still, Jianyi intensified his attacks, trying to interrupt the activation of the technique—but Lu Shan remained focused, keeping his defense up while waving at one card to rush towards Jianyi, and grinned triumphantly.

Before the technique could take effect, Jianyi decided to take a gamble and also drew on the techniques in his upper dantian.

Two cards appeared before him, and to his relief, Force of the Forest was one of them.

Force of the Forest

Technique

Common

1x Wood

Swift

Cultivator's strength is enhanced, lasts for five breaths

He guided his qi through his Celestial Core Card, into the technique that activated immediately, as it was a swift card.

He felt the ground below him grow muddy and start to suck at his feet with every movement he made—the same technique Lu Shan had used in their first duel.

But, at the same time, he felt the strength of the forest, the strength that made it resist a storm or crush stone with its roots, weave its way through his body. There was no visible change, but Jianyi could feel it with every fiber of his being.

And he used that strength to catapult himself forward, out of the mud, led by his spear, towards Lu Shan.

The boy reacted, but too slowly. Jianyi’s spear’s blunted tip dug deep into Lu Shan’s shoulder, and pushed him back with enough force to make him fly out of the ring, landing with a crash, surprise still etched in his face as he tried to gather his breath.

For a brief moment, there was silence from the audience as they recovered from their surprise, but Cai Fangyin stepped forward decisively. “Zhao Jianyi is the winner.”

A mix of loud applause, surprised murmuring, and celebratory shouts from Jun came from the audience as Lu Shan got up and stepped back into the ring. His face still incredulous, but he did bow to Fangyin and, with some hesitation, to Jianyi, who returned the gesture.

“I will meditate on this outcome, but I do suspect that your previous humiliations in combination with your honorable conduct today balance the scales of karma.”

With that, Lu Shan turned and left the training field.

Jianyi smiled. Smiled for having won the duel. Smiled for having shown what he could do. But mostly, smiled for having closed the gap to some of the best of his fellow disciples—and thereby was one small step closer to being able to reach his goals.
