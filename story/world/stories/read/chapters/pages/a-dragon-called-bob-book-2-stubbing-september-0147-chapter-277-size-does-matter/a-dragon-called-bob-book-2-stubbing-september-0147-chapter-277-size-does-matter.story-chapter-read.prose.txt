A shiny golden coin rolled back and forth from claw to claw.  The hoard nestled against my tummy gave me more solace than anything else I could imagine, and I burrowed down deeper into the monetary embrace.  My eyes followed that auric disc back and forth.

A tiny nudge was all it needed.  A microscopic force resulting in so much beauty as the light caught the edges.  I brought a palm down on the coin and sent a glare at Kat.

“I’m pretty sure this is bad for my spleen,” I grumbled.

“You don’t have a spleen anymore.  Your immune system is… different.”

She stood before the end of my snout, defiant and proud.  As much as I wished I could just eat her, I knew it was a wasted effort.  She’d pushed and pulled, nudged me, all to drive me to where she needed me to be.  The fact that an entire universe apparently rested on the back of her decisions wasn’t important to me.

“The stress, Kat.  I’m pretty sure I could eat the hoard, then crap it out, and I’d be fine, albeit a little sore.”

“I figured you learned your cloaca had to obey the laws of physics after you gave birth to Morris’s egg, or what you went through with the Dreadchef,” she replied.  She was currently attired as an extra from a beach party version of Final Fantasy VIII.  It was distracting.  Couldn’t the system just roll her a costume that was staid and professional?

“Oh, I learned what a pain in the ass felt like.  You need to level with me, princess.  We’re screwed.  The Changer is spreading.”

She pursed her lips like she was sucking on a lemon.  “I know, Bob.”

“I’m failing.”

“Hey.  You weren’t expecting this,” she said gently.

“I’m… You’re meant to help me.  You knew what was coming.”

“I’ve done what I can.”

“You’re a thousand years old!  And all you’ve done is poke me when it suited you and make bloody movies!  You’re like nipples on a breastplate.”

“I’m older than that.  This isn’t my first gig.”

“What?” I snapped, my eyes leaving the disc of gold and locking onto the pixie.

“You’re my fifty-fourth job.  There are others who’ve been in the game as long, but I’m pushing six thousand years of actual lived experience.”

“How… Fine, so you’re positively geriatric.  You drive the market in stair lifts and Zimmer frames.  You’ve saved fifty-four universes?”

“Twenty-three.”

“Half?  You’ve destroyed a couple of dozen worlds?”

“I didn’t destroy anything!” she snapped angrily.  She turned and began to pace back and forth, “I just failed.”

“I’m noticing a pattern.”

“Don’t be a dick.”

“What do you want me to be, Kat?  You lied to me.  It’s my first time.  You’re a special case.  It was all bullshit.”

“I’m sorry about that.”

“Bollocks,” I sneered.  “What happened to you after thirty universes died?”

“I can’t say for sure.  I might have gone back into storage between missions?  For me, I just appear in my boss’s office after no time at all.  It could be centuries between jobs.  I have no idea how long it’s been.”  She sagged, dropping to sit cross-legged, facing away from me.

Her profile was the same as usual, bar the bikini.  It was close to her original costume, just less armoured-looking.  The eye that I could see was misting up.

“So you get off scot-free?  No consequences for Kat if a world dies.”

“I remember them all, Bob.  I spent decades, hundreds of years in some cases.  You’re young.  You can’t understand what it’s like to lose something after so much time.  It becomes… normal.  Comfortable.  Like, you forget it can all go away at any time.”

“So the minion of the gods sent to help me has PTSD.”  I almost flinched at my own tone of voice, but I would see this through.

“Yes, dammit.  More of a baby than you can imagine.”

“You ever seen what happens on a world where a changer gets loose?” I asked more gently.

“Unless IMPS uses a failsafe, it’s pulled Outside.  You wouldn’t like being Outside, Bob.”

“I do prefer AC and, well, air.  What the hell is the Outside?”

“It’s hard to describe…”

“Try,” I growled.

“Formless.  A full vacuum?  It’s a contradiction heaped upon madness. The things that live there, that make it up, aren’t alive in any way we understand.  They’re more like ideas.  Strings of concepts constantly consuming each other, melding, merging, forming new constructs that can last… There is no time there, so I guess they last forever?”

“So definitely not a copyrighted concept associated with supersoliders and demons.  The Changer is one of these demons?”

“Part of one?  It acts like a bridge.  Most of it is in this world, but a thread crosses the divide.”  She rose to her feet and dusted herself off, despite the immaculate cleanliness of my treasury.  “All we need to do is snip that thread.”

“By we, you mean me.”

“Yes,” she said in a small voice.

“And how does one circumcise this interdimensional being of infinite time and space?”

“It changes every time.”

I snorted and then caught the coins that rolled away on the gust.  Shinies, Esme, my…friends?  My business interests.  The movie business was making big bucks, sequels, franchises, and merch were all in the works.  Nothing was going to take away what was mine.  Motivation was always the crux of solving any problem.

“Why do they want to destroy this world?” I asked, continuing to roll a gold coin back and forth, but watching Kat out of the corner of my eye.

She took a breath.  “IMPS and the Outside have always been at war with each other.  Whatever IMPS builds, they try to tear down.”

“Because IMPS stole something from them?” I asked, remembering the devil-furbies words from the volcano.

“NO!  No.  It’s not safe to talk about the ixnay on systemay.”  She mugged furiously at me, rolling her eyes at the ceiling.

“Are you epileptic?”

She ran a hand through her hair and let an angry screech.  “I thought I’d made it clear that bad mouthing the system was, how should I put it?  Not very bright?”

“Asking questions is somehow bad mouthing?  Is IMPS a teenage girl?”

She flinched, then zipped up and booped me on the snoot.  Not the gentle kind one might do to a beloved pet.  The kind you do to try and break someone's scaly nose.

“You aren’t in any position to be punching me at the moment, Kat.”

“Someone's got to!  You need to get your head in the game!  Unite the Empire!  Marshall all the human forces to support the knife-ears.”

“Philpott’s plan is going to take a few weeks to play out.  The old guard is rallying around and doing what they can to handle the sudden loss of liquidity.”  Shiny, sweet-smelling liquidity.

“Then you should send the dwarves.”

“Can’t.  They’re still uniting as well.  If only someone who knew what was coming had told me to skip going to the Fallimpiad?”

“You didn’t trigger it, Bob,” she said gently, “it’s not your fault.  That would have happened if you were there or not.”

“But me being there made it worse,” I ground out between clenched fangs.

“No!  The Changer was met with Champion on his arrival.  They don’t feel fear or anything.  As long as that umbilical cord to the outside is available, they can just fall back.  You being there made him cautious.  It’s why he’s expanded so slowly.”

“That’s slow?”  It was my turn to screech.  “It’s taken three quarters of the bloody elves' bloody homeland!”

“It’s being cautious.  Because it was met with you.”

“But I ran away.”  Wrath shrank in my mind, and Greed sobbed quietly in a corner, filled with worry about all the shinies I had on the line.

“You can’t face it on your own.  That’s what the Primal Empire is for.  It’s why there’s a prophecy.”

“What we’ve got can’t hold him back.  We’re sending bunnyborgs fresh off the production line straight through a portal and into the war.  The monsters Vik has been churning out are the same.  Off to battle as soon as they stop dripping.”

“They’ve stopped singing,” she said.

I blinked slowly.  “Music’s influence is failing?  I’m being abandoned by a god when a pair of them promised me full support.”

“No no.”  She held up both hands.  “They aren’t singing, they’re grunting.  Angry, mean, guttural and evil-sounding grunts.  When they hee-hee it sounds like a death threat.”

“MJ went death metal?  That’s something at least.”

“If everything we’ve got at the moment isn’t working, we need something new.”

“They’re all at sea.  The fighters, anyway.  Another few weeks, and they’ll be hitting Ankmapak unless it’s already in our hands.”

The ones who survived went back and divided, or whatever it was they did with their ovipositors and… other parts.  The numbers were bulking out nicely, but even they couldn’t match the reproduction rates of the Outsider.

“Giants on Helstat are a bit weird.”

“Colour me surprised.”

“Stoner giants all about free love and peace?”

She smiled then sighed.  “Pretty much.”

“I need soldiers, not peaceniks, Kat!”

“They can fight.”

“In between bong hits and drum circles?” I snapped.

“Yeah.”

“They actually do that?”

“Wanna know who the god of the giants is on this world?”

Gold shifted as I rolled onto my side, feathered wing propped out behind me so I didn’t crush it.

“Nah.  I’ll save that one.  I need to level and go grab an angry dwarf, don’t I?”

“Yep.  Crack on scale-brain!”
