The Samanthian war machine churned onward up the river to Matrazere, and the brain machine behind it worked tirelessly to gain vital intel to stop the Dominion’s mad plot.

Dominion skirmishing was nearly suspended a day after obtaining Palth, but gradually amped back up after that, to keep the attrition going. They avoided any outskirts defenders as much as possible, as this was saturated with Servitors and difficult-to-kill, fully-supported modular teams of elites. Against the latter, when battle was waged, it tended simply to draw and waste resources on both sides.

Thanks to wizards, long-range magic bombs could hit troops, and the sheer numbers made it difficult to protect them all. Sometimes, a given target had an Antimage or Samantha herself to counter; sometimes it had lesser protections, and there were casualties. Samantha and her team worked to tighten it up as they went along. Most of the time, they could intercept, but occasionally the attempted terrorizing paid off.

Psychologically, it was effective, like endless artillery raining down march after march, day after day. This wore the soul down, and there wasn’t much helping it. The Dominion refused to stop, even as they lost more valuable troops and resources in the process. They just had it spare.

The further upriver they got, the more supply lines were targeted. They were prepared for this with response teams, and utilized other means in support, such as mirror-walking critical supplies, or via Samantha making runs herself, cramming loaded-down people into a Mirror Maze, and then transporting them to camp. Even so, there was no avoiding the movement of bulk supplies downriver, for both the armies and for their held forts.

To help combat some of these difficulties, Samantha set up a large support camp by moving people and supplies rapidly from the Oboors site to the Eleks Obelisk site, easily crossed via the Bridge of Edges. The Eleks location wasn’t exactly close, as it was at best a third point of a triangle nearly equidistant from Rivermount and Matrazere, but it was also far less harassed if used as a route to the army, which was getting a little closer each day.

In addition, the forests were perfect for hunters and gatherers to score additional food supplies, which were getting more and more valuable. The entire civilized world’s resources had seemingly been sucked in toward the war and its literal millions of the directly affected (whether soldier, camp aide, refugee, etc.), including a staggering percentage of nearly every grain store north and south. Food was becoming like gold very fast, with many opportunistic merchants shifting their focus to get in on this profit. With the war so deep inland now, it wasn’t cheap to do, either.

As taxed as her transport network was directly for the war, she still obtained a lot of benefits from flying supplies, such as over the mountains south, instead of letting caravans charge a premium for the same shit in Rivermount. Burnstire became another such place of relief to sell grain and preserved meat to them, in addition to entirely handling their own army. They’d been resisting selling until recently — ironically, not for profit, which they were now going to bathe in, but for wartime caution. Samantha could do transportation even faster herself, but this stole her from other things. Thankfully, Azure had begun to figure out how to move between the points on her own through mirror-mimicry abilities, so she was lightening the load.

The advancing hordes passed multiple small, abandoned communities along the way. They took another irrelevant fortress, and this time, it didn’t even explode.

Spies poked for intel, but the investigation gathered little of substance, and the nature of the spell waiting eluded them. It had to be bound up in the fortress. Matrazere exuded a saturating aura that changed people who lived there. It imbued them with fear, apathy, and darker things, entrapped them if they didn’t leave in time. Few seemed to think they could leave even if they wanted to. In most other places, the young men who grew up there inevitably moved away. Not Matrazere. The generations, almost to the man and woman, stayed. Suffered. Locals filled the town and the fortress.

Hopelessness. That was what soaked into and tainted the citizenry and soldiers who stayed long enough. This was certainly a factor in the spell, but it was merely a component. It gave Samantha nothing to work with, and there was no counter other than to conquer the place. Those tainted citizens served as anchors, reinforcing and echoing that aura.

Days passed with too little understood.

A much more potent scry-blocking swept through the town outside Matrazere, blocking passive mirror access.

Spies went dark, missing their communication windows. One-by-one, it was confirmed. None were strongly connected to her organization, much less Followers — as none of that could be risked — so she was temporarily blind in the town.

Samantha felt sick. Only the worst could be expected. She knew she’d gotten all those spies all killed or worse.

And deeper worries plagued them.

General Rallante was feeling tired yet pleased, as he sauntered through the dark, drafty halls of Matrazere, cold all year round. His travel had been sudden, unexpected, and blisteringly swift. He had not expected to be back in the dreary locale he’d been stationed once before so soon, nor would he typically relish it. In this case, he’d thought it for demotion at first, thanks to the failures in the Borderlands, but he’d been pleasantly surprised to find he’d actually been promoted to a higher station.

No longer a technical merc! A fully-honored Dominion Brass General! Head of the main armies, even, which the formerly illustrious golden boy, Palthennius, had previously claimed.

Your loss, ya sadsacked dipshit.

Apparently, Palthennius had been infiltrated, influenced, and corrupted by the enemy, going off and blathering about curses this, zombies that, as he ineffectually and comically attempted a coup in Matrazere.

A coup! In Matrazere! Rallante would be laughing about that one for a while. He supposed delusions of grandeur should be expected after the insanities in the Borderlands. Anyone who understood Matrazere, though, would know the two scenarios to be day versus night.

It was pointedly ridiculous that a Heaven Knight of that man’s stature and stock had fallen so low. He had everything: pedigree, looks, accomplishment, seeming invincibility, and a fast track to the tippity-top of the Dominion’s martial world. And then, one encounter with the witches had turned him inside out. Maybe it was just Mr. Perfection suffering his first real loss, which opened that door.

Maybe there’s something to be said for those of us who’ve had it rougher. We bend and don’t break! Well, I damn sure deserve this honor. I’ve put my fucking time in. More than my time, and forever loyal, whatever the labels necessary for ‘expendable’ men sent to the hinterlands.

His only regret was that he wouldn’t get to stab that bastard, Carlisle, through the heart, finally. Actually, he took that back: two regrets. He wouldn’t get to stab Lucky, either.

Well, let’s not speak too soon! Defending Matrazere is the surest task in the land. After this is done, might get swung back from whence I came with a newer, bigger army fit to crush those stupid ass cats into the dirt. Hell, I might even have the honor of taking that cute little witchy upjumped servant girl in my bed. If she’s half as fair and spirited as reported… Ah, but would that be a fine reward for hard work!

Rallante had to cut short such scintillating ruminations as he arrived at the primary ritual chamber area, where a short ceremony was to be conducted to swear him into his new role. He’d never been there, only heard of it in whispers. It was a high honor just to be received there.

He was led by a robed wizard from the smaller antechamber into the massive ritual proper smack-dab in the middle of the fortress. Instantly, he felt magic in the air with such thickness that his hair stood up, and his eyes watered from the sting of it.

Even as they did, his mouth went dry in seeing what awaited him.

Hundreds of wizards were arranged in descending circular rows that overlooked a pit he was walking down into via obsidian stairs. Each standing spot held a rune… not quite glowing. The runes were black, the magic crackling and stewing from underneath. Every wizard was robed in black and had on a hood — this was shocking, as they were illegal. Apparently not so much in secret rituals.

Rallante caught up to the wizard to whisper, “What is this for?!”

The wizard replied placidly, “Just what we said: your true coronation and anointing. A powerful enchantment to protect you from death. Necessary for our chosen champion, who’ll keep the heathens from our sacred halls. Not once sullied by an invader, maintained pure for our purposes, for aeons and aeons. Forever — with your help, General.”

Rallante swallowed as he looked around. “It certainly looks like death.”

“To a layman, yes. Such is our way, and how that vicious hand will be withheld from you. You wouldn't expect our methods to cheat death to be an opposing energy, would you?” A dismissive, mocking tone.

Rallante licked his dry lips. “No.” He felt a strange kind of excitement, he had to admit. Something buried deep was being brought to the surface, resonating with that place, and the wizards holding it in their power. Something old and powerful, growing stronger and stronger, as he approached the focal point, which was now pulling him. “I… I feel it, now.”

“So soon? How encouraging. Yes. A resonance, an embrace from an old friend. Do not resist. Receive it greedily, let it suffuse you, and it will keep you. Glorify you. You will become… unstoppable. Unkillable. You, truly one of ours, fit for Matrazere’s darkest hour, its toughest challenge…”

Rallante passed some threshold where the pull was so undeniably strong, he’d never care to turn from it. Wouldn’t. His mind fogged over, filled with ramping, intense energy, tuning in and connecting to something greater. His feet marched to the glory awaiting him without restraint. “It’s… truly glorious!” he cried, voice breaking.

“Indeed,” replied the wizard, smiling. “So will be your transformation, and so it will echo, far and wide. Far, wide… and deep into the bones of who and what we are. So it will be known again, through you. May this soft-hearted era finally die, may a time of proper fear proliferate. Take the high honor, General, as champion of this. Take it beyond…”

They neared the bottom, and a wonderful, musical bass filled the air, as the wizards raised their hands and began to chant in harmony, a magic ritual saturating the air with so much magic that a man could choke on it.

Especially because it was necromantic magic.

Rallante fell to his knees when he finally reached the center bottom, directly on the last rune to be occupied. He couldn’t keep standing. He sobbed because what was happening was so hopelessly glorious and beautiful. The collapse and end of all things. It would stop his heart soon, if-

The wizard shoved a ritual dagger right into his heart. Rallante tried to take a breath, and couldn’t.

“Take it beyond, General Rallante,” the wizard whispered in his ear as everything began to fade, “and leave us this offering. You’ve served us well. We thank you.”

As his life flashed before his eyes, Rallante felt regret. Not for those he’d killed, but for those he never got to.

Fuckers! I’ll get you in the next one, I swear, so help me-
