After dropping off Maurice and Velistraine back in Thalis and in preparation for their travel, Henry retracted the network he’d extended to the Sanctuary. But he didn’t dismiss them, as that would be a waste. Instead, he had the furthest clones find second furthest ones, then had one of them send the spare clone into his Maw before starting the process all over again. It was like tidying up a bunch of Matryoshka dolls, and while tedious, Henry had Octominds that didn’t whine nor complain, so he had them take care of it. It would take a few hours, maybe even a couple of days, but it would be done, and then Henry would be able to create a new network from Thalis to wherever they were going.

With that process well in hand, Henry switched places with a clone near Rocky’s old territory.

Camouflaging himself out of habit, Henry swam down and made his way toward the crevice that would led down into the ruins of the lobster. It wasn’t too long of a swim, barely an hour’s worth, and when the submerged city came into view, Henry popped out fifty of his already collected clones and turned toward the navigator and the scouting Octominds.

“Spread them out. Look through every nook and cranny for anything valuable or shiny. Texts, gold, pearls, and most importantly, anything that might look or be related to the metallic orbs. If you find plates with text on them that you don’t understand, store them. Go.”

The two projections of the Octominds saluted and disappeared right as all of his clones turned invisible and swam down. Leaving them to their tasks, Henry turned his attention to the tunnel that lead into the lobster’s chamber but before he dove in there, he looked around

Just like how he’d left it, a city lay under him. Though to be fair, it might have been abandoned by its original creator, but it sure became a home for all sorts of wildlife ever since. A few homes and towers here and there were broken, but in general, the buildings still stood strong, their construction somehow preventing the growth of algae along the walls. Though whatever the enchantments or materials that had been used, it wasn’t always effective as here and there, the flora bloomed, undeterred. It grew out of plenty of spots. It managed to grow out of the patches of soil or out of the walls of the cavern, some behaving like trellis, stretching down toward the roofs of the buildings below.

Everything looks the same, mostly, Henry thought as he slowly approached. Though he wondered. Who had lived here, and why had they left? Considering the state of things and the lacks of remains, it looked abandoned. As if one day, everyone just packed up and left. How long ago, he couldn’t tell. With magic muddying things, it could have been a dozen years or a couple of centuries.

Henry finally arrived in front of the tunnel and paused to take the situation in.

First of all, let’s tryto keep it to a spar instead of hunt, he reminded himself. This wasn't about killing the lobster. He wasn't even sure he could. This was about testing himself, seeing how far he'd come since his evolution. Could he survive an all-out fight with an A-rank? Could he push it, hurt it, maybe even make it retreat? That's what mattered. The core would be nice, but he couldn’t forget that he was the one being the aggressor here. While the lobster might end being an unwilling sparring partner, he’d rather not kill it if it’s possible. But he wasn’t going to hold back. He didn’t think he could even afford to.

I got plenty of healing stocked up, orbs of healing, mana is full, Aspects are… mostly full, he told himself. It was a little scary, facing an A-rank.

Actually, was it even still there?

Henry peered through the clones he’d sent scouting and quickly realized that none had approached this particular tunnel, so he summoned another out of his Maw and sent it forward. Through its eyes, Henry saw into the chamber that held the lobster and just like the city, it was the same as the last time he’d left it. It was like a hangar of some sorts. Many large depressions were carved into the walls and front and center, there was a wide, gateway that led out into the water. Glancing around through the eyes of his clone, Henry soon had eyes on the lobster. It was still in the same spot he’d seen it the last time, but while it was mostly still and potentially even asleep, it didn’t seem very… happy.

Lightning was already crackling across its shell. Had it sensed him already?

Henry observed for a few moments, but as the seconds ticked by, it didn’t seem to be reacting to the clone. It wasn’t looking back toward his real body either.

Was it having a nightmare or something?

Henry pushed the thought away and let out a slow breath. Alright, let’s see, he thought as he activated Identify, and just to make sure it didn’t fail, he fed it some of Thaumaturgy’s conceptual essence. If this thing was in the 30s or 40s or anything higher, he was getting the hell away. But if it were under level 20, then he was taking the shot.

The spell activated, and the lobster’s antennas shuddered as the crackling electricity grew more agitated. It knew he was here, but hiding had never been his intent. And thankfully, his investigation effort worked.

[Stormclaw Bruiser of Tide and Thunder (A) - Lvl ?]

That puts it under level 25, Henry deduced as the lobster’s presence fell on him. He swallowed, a shiver coursing down his arms. If it was on the upper limit of that range, Henry was in danger. With every ten levels in A-rank, the jump in power was significant, as Arisia had drilled into him. If the lobster was in the first few levels of A-rank, then he was safe. He’d only know which situation he was in once he started clashing with the creature.

Of course, he could have asked Zerathstra for details, but the guardian wouldn’t always be around to hold his hand. Henry had to make his own judgments and reap their rewards or suffer their consequences.

Those are thoughts for another day, Henry told himself while the creature’s antenna twitched about for a few moment, then like a compass finding its north, the antennas swung and pointed toward Henry’s real body, at which point the crackling grew wilder. Bolts flew out of its back, ionizing the water around it, and Henry knew he had its interest.

Excitement welled in his stomach.

This was the scariest and most vivid part of the fight. The calm before all hell broke loose. The lobster might be a little stronger than the limit he’d set himself, but if he didn’t push his own limits a little, how was he supposed to survive what was to come? How would he adapt to whatever the System was preparing? He had to take some risks. He had to force himself out of his comfort zone. After all, what was life without a little spice?

With that in mind, Henry decided to go and greet his sparring partner. Switching places with the clone right outside the chamber, Henry swam into the chamber, waving his giant arm at the lobster as he made his own lightning crackle across his limbs. It looked much weaker as lightning wasn’t his specialty, but the spirit of the challenge was there. Henry had also considered growing large enough to dominate the A-rank, but he also didn’t want to give it too much surface to lay into him, so he opted for being just twice its size.

“[Hey. Come out and play,]” Henry sent the lobster as he floated near the massive exit while he did his utmost not let his arms shake. He couldn’t tell if it was anxiety, excitement, or both. But that didn’t matter. The dice was cast. Best case scenario, Henry would prove—to himself—that he can go toe-to-toe with a low A-rank and walk away with his head held high, which should net him a bunch of Aspect levels. Worst case? He’d have to make himself scarce. But so long as he could put up a struggle, he should still get some growth for his Aspects. Worse than that would be death, but Henry didn’t see a scenario in which he couldn’t get himself away.

Escape was relatively easy with all of the clones around.

Still, as Henry floated in front of the exit, the deep blue sea stretching infinitely behind him while the lobster stared him down, he made sure that he had all of his defensive abilities were working at a 100%, and unlike the fight against Velistraine, Henry wouldn’t be taking any risks. He was going to generously dip into his reserves of essence. Though as he watched the creature lumber out of the hanger, lightning crackling out of the glowing pattern that appeared over its shell, Henry swam away and out of the ruins.

The fight’s nice and all, but I still gotta make some space for the clones to work, he thought. Henry was ready. And he was about to send another word to the lobster, only for the truck-sized monster to suddenly disappear in a flash of light. If it hadn’t been for Henry’s enhanced cognition, he wouldn’t have seen the monster blur toward him. But still, by the time he managed to react, the glowing blue claw of the lobster was all that he could see.

It was already inches from his face.

The violence, the impact of the claw-punch was nothing Henry had ever experienced. It was like a strapped bomb had gone off right against his face. Instantly, Henry lost his sight, and he could feel the burn of salt water reaching places it shouldn’t. But before he could recover and react, the impact was followed up by a pulse of muscle-seizing, nerve-frying and mind-melting discharge of electricity that nearly winked out his consciousness then and there. Or it would have if Henry hadn’t recently evolved. Or if his abilities haven’t recently upgraded.

Actually, all things considered… it’s not that bad, he thought as he let the momentum propel him back, and just for good measure, he Blinked a few more times away to create some distance while Life Surge knit his face back, though he still prioritized one of his eyes.

In the meantime, he looked through his clones.

The lobster was staring at him, now standing where he’d been a moment ago. The creature’s presence pressed down on him, but Henry’s own Aspects pushed back strongly enough for the pressure to be only a little stifling. Still, his face still hurt like hell. He could ride the pain, but he definitely couldn’t eat too many of those punches. His nerves were still screaming and his flesh was still twitching and spasming from the residual electricity, but Henry had dealt with worse while much weaker.

Well, this is definitely going to be challenge. But it didn’t one-shot me, so there might be a chance, Henry thought as the floating Octominds began crackling with mana, one of which latched on to him and helped healing his burnt skin. He’d pulled them away from network-duty for the fight, and he was glad he had. With a flex, Henry summoning a couple dozens of clones and as the lobster glared, pincer clacking, eight leviathan teeth appeared on the tips of all eight of his limbs.

He could feel it now. The lobster was nowhere near Velistraine, and he should be able to put up a good fight. But he still had to be wary.

“[My turn,]” Henry said as his form crackled before he blurred forward himself with an activation of Lightning Dash and as the world lit up around him, a stray thought managed to pull through the chaos.

Henry wondered if this lobster was the originator of the that skill.
