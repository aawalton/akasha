Power swirled around Bortim, and Keyla gasped. Elion grabbed her by the arm, trying to support her as she staggered. Her body slumped, and she leaned limply against Elion.

“What did you do to her?” Elion demanded.

“Kneel,” Bortim ordered. Keyla’s legs bent, and she squeaked as she knelt to the floor.

“A simple strengthening of her attachment to this rag,” Bortim said, waving the handkerchief carelessly. “She’ll feel compelled to obey whoever possesses it.”

“You can’t—” Elion protested.

“But I can,” Bortim said. “I already have, and I’m about to do it to you. The beauty of it is, I’m not even really compelling you. She’s free to sever her connection with this rag whenever she wants.” He crouched down beside Keyla, dangling the handkerchief in front of her. Keyla snatched at it, but Bortim plucked away too quickly.

“Sit here, quietly,” he said. “Do not attempt to retrieve this bandanna.” Keyla folded her arms in her lap.

Bortim turned to Elion. “Like I said. She can disobey me, but it would just mean choosing to sever her connection with this Bondhold. But that would mean letting go of everything that led her to form that connection in the first place, so I don’t think she will.”

As if in response, Keyla sat quietly, tears dripping down her cheeks.

“Let me guess. It belonged to a family member? A sister? A mother? Ah…” He watched Keyla’s face. “Your mother’s. Is she dead then? You can go free, but to break this bond you’ll have to give up your memories of her.”

Keyla did not move.

Elion surged forward, reaching for the handkerchief-turned-Bondhold.

Bortim danced out of range, and Elion snatched at empty air. The chain binding Elion to Keyla jerked up short, pulling Keyla’s hands. She folded them calmly back in her lap.

Elion shook, his face flushed. He clenched his fists, preparing to charge the man, even if he had to drag Keyla across the floor to do it.

Bortim gestured, and A strong force pulled Elion backwards, against the cinderblock wall. His back and arms pressed up against the wall as if pulled by gravity. He struggled, resisting as he pushed off the wall. The binding weakened, and Elion’s confidence surged. He fought harder against it.

“Now it’s your turn,” Bortim said. He turned his red third eye to examine Elion. Elion tore his right arm from the wall, his connection to it melting away. He pulled his other arm free, like pushing through water, and tried to lean forward, unsticking himself from the cinderblocks.

“Ah, a strong, strong connection to something… else. Something not here,” Bortim said. “I wonder what it is. That would make a powerful Bondhold. Shame you’ve hidden it. Maybe Kendry will want to know about it and go dig it up.”

Elion glanced at the rifles in the middle of the room. He would have to pull Keyla halfway across the floor, but if he jumped suddenly, he might be able to get to one before the spry old man could stop him.

Bortim held up the Starholder pendant. “This will work just fine, though,” he said.

Elion lunged for the rifle, the last of the wall binding falling away from him. Red chains whirled, crackling with power in the air. Gasping, Elion tumbled to the ground. The room faded.

Liora stood before him, 7 years old, showing off the necklace Mom had given to her. Elion hadn’t wanted a necklace, but he still felt left out.

Liora, older now, walked down the front drive, high heels clicking on the walk. Elion frowned at the way she showed off her muscular legs, flashing through a slit in her dress that ran practically to the top of her hip bone. The low cut of the dress showed off a broad swath of flesh, Starholder pendant perfectly framed by her collarbones.

She needed him to be there for her.

Elion picked the pendant from Snicker’s mouth, panic and anguish washing over him as he realized Zev wouldn’t be able to find Liora by tracking this pendant.

The pendant dangled from its broken chain, as Elion slipped a paperclip through the loops to repair it, placing it over his own neck.

“Liora,” Elion gasped. His eyes focused on the ground, the dirty floor of Bortim’s cell, where the Red Judge tortured them with their past. Elion had not made it to the rifles. He crouched, hunched over, panting on his hands and knees. Keyla rocked softly behind him, chain taut between them.

Elion struggled to his feet, aiming to wrestle his pendant from Bortim’s hands. “Give that back to me!” he snarled.

“Stop,” Bortim commanded, and Elion froze. “Go kneel by her.”

Elion tried to resist the command, but his heart ached, and his soul told him that to disobey Bortim was to abandon Liora.

If he disobeyed, Liora would be torn from his mind.

He couldn’t do it. He walked over and knelt beside Keyla.

“Good,” Bortim said. “Glad we can now deal like civilized people. No need for violence around here.”

Elion watched the door, feeling like he could just get up and walk away. He tried to tell himself he was being manipulated, controlled, but the strong binding he felt to his Starholder pendant kept him pinned. Disobeying Bortim would be betraying Liora.

*I know it’s a lie,* Elion thought. *Somehow it still holds me.*

If he severed his bond with that pendant, would he lose all memories of her? Or was that another lie from Bortim? He couldn’t risk that.

“I’m going to take off your chains,” Bortim said. “I want you both to remain seated, and calm. Don’t even think about escaping, running, or fighting.

Elion relaxed, letting all thoughts of fighting back slip away from him.

Bortim walked over to them and, producing a key from a pouch at his belt, and unlocked their handcuffs. The chains fell to the ground. Elion and Keyla sat quietly, staring at their captor. Keyla’s eyes flashed defiantly.

“Tell me about these guns,” Bortim said, walking over to the stack of belongings and picking up a rifle. “I haven’t seen one like this in a long time.”

“I made them,” Keyla said.

“You made them?” Bortim raised an eyebrow. “I don’t think so. These are the work of a skilled Artificer. I knew the man who designed them, once, many years ago. Davin Aerden, a master craftsman. The designs were lost…” He touched the rifle as he spoke, lovingly, caressing its curves and edges.

“But you can’t lie to me. Tell me the truth. Where did you get these guns?”

“I made them,” Keyla said again.

“You must have found them somewhere,” Bortim said. “Fixed them up. That’s what you mean by made them, isn’t it?”

Keyla shook her head, but the man was not watching her.

“It doesn’t matter,” Bortim said. “They’re nice weapons, but I have no need for one. Prator protects me, and I help him keep order in New Kairn Tol. Kendry can have them, though I doubt she’ll be able to figure out how to recharge the energy packs.”

He tossed the gun down. It clanged against Snicker’s cage, and the cat mewed loudly from inside.

“Before I turn you over to Kendry,” Bortim said. “I have a few questions for you. I order you to answer quickly and honestly. What were you doing in the ruins?”

“We were coming to New Kairn Tol,” Elion said, and Keyla nodded.

“Hmm… So not scouting for the army? What can you tell me about them?”

“Nothing,” Keyla and Elion said, simultaneously. “I don’t know about that,” Elion continued.

“Are you assassins? Are you here to kill Prator?”

“No,” Elion said. “Is someone trying to kill him?”

“Someone did try to kill him,” Bortim said. “But we’ve got the situation under control. The assassin is proving hard to understand, and all I want to know is who helped her. She had to have help to get so close… But that doesn’t matter. You don’t know. I just had to ask.”

Elion stared silently back at Bortim.

“What do you know about the army, out on the Ater plain?” Bortim asked.

“I don’t know anything about an army,” Elion said. “I already told you that.”

“Nothing,” Keyla said again. “Please let us go.”

“I order you to stay silent unless directly addressed, and then you will speak only to answer the question,” Bortim said, his voice imperious.

“If you don’t know anything about the army, where did you come from? What are you doing here?”

Elion’s mind whirled. He felt compelled to speak, to answer the question. Like Liora needed him to. He knew it was all a lie. His mouth opened, words that would betray all his secrets on the tip of his lips. *I can still lie. Just reveal a part of the truth.*

“We’re running away,” Keyla said.

“We’re looking for someone,” Elion said.

Bortim looked between them, an eyebrow cocked curiously.

“Which is it?”

“Both,” Elion said. “We came here to look for someone.”

“Who?”

“My mother,” Keyla said.

“Why do you think she’s here?”

“Just our best guess. She used to live here,” Keyla said.

“And what were you running from?” Bortim asked.

“Warlocks,” Elion blurted, even as he tried to bite his tongue. “They chased us into the sewers.”

“Warlocks, huh?” Bortim said. “Are you the ones who got them all excited then? Their camp has been practically buzzing with energy for the last day or so.”

Elion shrugged. *Someone else could have got them riled up.* “It was me,” he said. “I’m the reason they’re here.”

“Cocky little punk, aren’t you?” Bortim said, half smiling. “They’re here because they care about Thrandicite shipments.” He scratched his balding head. “And I know it wasn’t you. Couldn’t have been. Some Aurelian out there took down their skyskimmer, and they’re mad about it.”

Elion felt a tinge of defensiveness. Why did Bortim assume that Elion couldn’t have destroyed a skyskimmer?

“Why do you assume it wasn’t me?”

Bortim just laughed and moved on. “Well, that’s about it. More labor for the Acid Pools I suppose, you don’t seem that useful for anything else.”

He watched Elion and Keyla for a moment, like an artist considering his work.

“Stay here and don’t touch any of this stuff,” he said, gesturing toward their belongings and the rifles. Then he walked out of the room, leaving the door open.

Elion immediately stood and tried to pick up one of the rifles, but as his fingers neared the gun, he felt a powerful sense of duty and longing. It wasn’t worth it to abandon Liora. His fingers hovered in the air near the rifle.

*I hate this. I know I’m being manipulated. But if I touch this rifle… I feel as though I’m abandoning Liora forever. I can’t forget about her.*

He left the laserarm on the floor, and tried the door. Again, the sensation of betrayal ate at him, and he couldn’t bring himself to leave the room.

“What did he do to us?” Elion grumbled.

“I’ve heard about Bondholds,” Keyla said. “I didn’t believe they were real. Red Judges can increase or decrease the connection between two people. That’s why, in all the stories, true lovers go to a red judge to strengthen their oaths of loyalty.”

“So they can just… force us to do things against our will?”

Keyla shook her head. “I don’t think they can.”

“He attached me to that wall somehow,” Elion said.

“But it melted away quickly. Red Judges can’t make you love someone you hate, or hate someone you love. They focus on strengthening or weakening existing bonds. When they try to bind you to something else, it falls apart quickly.

“I heard a story once of a man who hated living on Kylios. He went to a Red Judge, who worked with the man to weaken his bond to the planet until he floated away into the sky.”

“That’s stupid,” Elion said. “I didn’t consent to his mind control.”

“He’s not mind controlling us,” Keyla said. “He strengthened an existing bond, a connection that we long to be enhanced.”

Elion thought about his relationship with Liora. He did wish it was better.

Keyla touched the place where her bandana had been.

“Was that your mother’s bandana?” Elion asked.

“It was,” Keyla said. “I miss her so much. I know I could disobey him, but that would require weakening my connection with her and I can’t bring myself to do that.”

Elion ran his fingers through his hair. “One of us needs to do it,” he said. “We have to get out of here. Prator is working for Venya, and it’s only a matter of time before they find out about us. Bortim might be going to tell them right now.”

“Just walk out of the room,” Keyla said. “Disobey the order, and you’ll be free. But your heart can’t let go of that connection, so… we’re trapped in this room.”

Elion still stood in the doorway. Sweat trickled down his spine. He didn’t leave.

“Who is it for you?” she asked. “Who gave that pendant to you?”

“It’s my sister’s,” Elion said. “She lost it when Dorian kidnapped her.”

“Ah,” Keyla nodded. “And that’s why you’re here. If you broke that bond, you might lose your will to live.”

“Why do the objects even matter?” Elion asked. “That bandana, it’s not your mother.”

“It reminds me of her,” she said. “I feel closer to her when I have it. It’s symbolic.”

Elion thought about all the t-shirts he had in his closet, souvenirs purchased for him by his parents on vacations they’d taken as a family. Most of the shirts were too small for him now, but he couldn’t bring himself to get rid of them.

Groaning, Elion turned away from the door. “What can we do?”

“I don’t know,” Keyla said, wiping tears from her eyes. “If we don’t choose to break off the connection, then I guess we just wait for them to come back.”

*I’m not waiting around to be manipulated more.*

Snickers yowled inside his box again, and Elion had an idea.

He picked up the chains that Bortim had taken off of them. “He said don’t touch that stuff,” Elion said, pointing to their belongings. “He didn’t say we couldn’t touch these…”

Keyla bent to reach toward the chain, but withdrew her hand sharply before touching them. “I thought he did,” she said.

“I thought he meant 'our stuff’ specifically,” Elion said, holding the chains in his hands. Keyla reached for the chains again, but couldn’t bring herself to touch them.

*This is good to know. Individual interpretation matters.*

Elion carried the chains over to Snicker’s cage. A simple latch on the outside prevented the box from opening. Gripping two chain links, one in either hand, Elion reached for the latch.

“He said not to touch that!” Keyla warned.

“I’m not going to,” Elion said. “I’m touching the chains, and they’re going to touch the box.”

*Kind of pedantic, but I don’t see why it wouldn’t work.*

Moving carefully, Elion manipulated the latch with the chains and popped the box open.

Snickers sprang out, then climbed atop the backpack, mewing and pawing at the fabric.

“He’s hungry again,” Keyla said, smiling. “You did warn me that he never stops eating.”

“Doesn’t help us much,” Elion said, “But it’s helpful to know that technicalities still apply here.”

Snickers freed the package of meat from the backpack, and looked over Elion and Keyla appraisingly. Then he slipped from the room.

“Well, at least he isn’t going to share our fate,” Elion said.
