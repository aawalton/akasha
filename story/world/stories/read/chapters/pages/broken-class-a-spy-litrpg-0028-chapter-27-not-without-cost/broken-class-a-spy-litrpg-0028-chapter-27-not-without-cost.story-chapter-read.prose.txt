Now that Ysara had answered all of Arthur’s questions, he decided it was time to let Yudursh out of the storage ring and allow her to heal him. But he needed assurance first.

Ysara’s breath caught in her throat as she followed his gaze to the artifact ring. "You're going to let him out?"

Arthur crouched beside her, eyes level and unflinching. "Eventually. But not before I get some guarantees."

She tensed, her silence loaded with apprehension.

"When I let him out," Arthur continued, his tone even but ironclad, "you'll order Yudursh to stand down. No sudden moves, no heroics. And whatever pact he swore to you, I want it extended to me."

Her face tightened with emotion. A storm of guilt and uncertainty brewed behind her eyes. This wasn’t just a request, it was a tearing at the foundation of her oath. "That pact is sacred. It’s not meant to be shared."

"And neither is my patience," Arthur replied. "He gets to live. You get to heal him. I get to stay breathing. Fair trade, isn’t it?"

Silence stretched. Her jaw worked slightly, like she was chewing the weight of the decision.

Then, at last, she gave a stiff nod. "I’ll do it."

Arthur stood, tightening his grip on the ring already in his hand. "Good. Let’s see if your big protector knows how to play nice."

He activated the ring.

There was no flash, no swirl of energy, just a faint ripple in the air, and then Yudursh’s battered body materialized on the ground beside them, limp but unmistakably alive. Arthur moved swiftly, stepping in and prying the blades from Yudursh’s weakened hands before he could fully react, storing them back into the artifact ring without a word.

The air thickened with tension.

Ysara scrambled to Yudursh’s side, hands glowing faintly as she began to heal him. Her mana wove quickly, expertly, closing wounds and easing the tightness in Yudursh’s chest.

A groan escaped his lips as he stirred, then flinched upright, eyes locking on Arthur.

His hands twitched toward his blades, but they weren’t there. His last memory was the blur of motion - pushing off the ground, twisting his torso, and lashing out at a threat his instincts had screamed was near. Steel had met resistance. But not the kind he expected.

Then everything had gone white.

Now, the blades were gone. And as his gaze locked on the man before him, the tension from that final strike still coiled through his limbs like a spring.

"Easy," Ysara said, her voice firm. "He’s not the enemy."

Yudursh didn’t look convinced. He recognized the man instantly, the same one who had unleashed terrifying power against the rogue. His breath caught, dread crawling up his spine. Last he remembered, his blade had found its mark. Now, he was powerless. Exposed. Instinct flared before reason caught up, and he shifted, planting himself between Arthur and Ysara.

Ysara reached out quickly, her voice calm but urgent. "Yudursh, stand down. You're injured, disoriented, and he's not drawing a weapon. Read the situation."

His eyes darted between her and Arthur, and for a moment, it looked like he wouldn’t budge.

She stepped closer. "I know this is sudden, and I know you're still hurting. But listen to me. He isn’t our enemy. I’ll explain when the moment is right."

He stayed rooted, tension still clinging to his frame. His jaw tightened, and when he spoke, it was low but respectful. "Then please, Princess... help me understand."

"And I will," she said gently, her eyes never leaving his. "But not here, not while your wounds are still fresh and everything feels like a threat. Just know this: we need him, and he needs to know you won’t strike him down. That pact you swore to me... it must include him too. This isn't a request I make lightly. And I promise - when the time is right, you’ll understand why it had to be this way."

Yudursh's brow furrowed deeply. "With respect, Princess... that is not the way such pacts are meant to be shared."

"I know," she said, lowering her voice further. "But nothing about this is normal. You deserve answers. But not here, not now. And right now, we don’t have the luxury to debate the sacred traditions. We need to adapt."

A long pause followed. Then, with visible reluctance, The drop of Yudursh’s shoulders wasn’t surrender, it was sacrifice. Pride chipped away by necessity.

Yudursh drew in a breath and placed one hand over his heart, bowing his head toward Arthur. His voice dropped to a low, grounded timbre - less ritual and more personal. But every word carried the gravity of a vow hardened by battle and years of service.

"By blade and breath, I swear. To shield you from harm, in shadow or light. To heed your command when voice or steel calls it. To guard your path until my limbs fail or the pact is severed by mutual will. May the old forces mark this vow, and may this pact, once made, not be broken lightly."

A ripple passed through the air, unseen but felt, like pressure dropped for the span of a heartbeat. Ysara watched him closely, recognizing the solemnity of Yudursh’s words.

Arthur said nothing, but the weight of the vow pressed faintly against his chest, a silent mark of the new pact that now bound them. Only then did Yudursh rise and meet Arthur’s eyes. He was still wary, but bound.

"It’s done," he said quietly.

At first, he seemed steady, but only for a moment. Then a sharp breath escaped him, and his hand shot to his chest. His knees buckled as the strain caught up all at once. Ysara lurched forward on instinct, catching his arm before he hit the ground. She guided him down gently, steadying his weight with care.

Arthur remained still, watching with careful detachment.

His brow furrowed, and he spoke silently into the quiet of his thoughts. Drogan. What’s happening to him?

Tch. Of course you'd ask now. Drogan’s voice crackled through Arthur’s mind, sharp with irritation. You see that twitch in his chest? That’s not just exhaustion. That’s the system extracting its pound of flesh. Soul pacts aren't free, Arthur. They cost energy, stability, resilience. Most people can barely handle one. Two is stretching it. Three? You’d be lucky to stay upright.

And before you ask your next brilliant question, he added with a venomous sneer, just remember, I only answer because I’m bound to. Keep poking me like some spoiled noble and I might start finding loopholes sharp enough to bleed you. So do us both a favor and think before tossing every stray thought at me like I’m your personal tutor."

Arthur’s eyes narrowed slightly, but he didn’t speak.

"You, of course," Drogan went on, "are an exception. Three pacts and not even a flinch. Most people would be on the ground by now. That man? He’s carrying two, and it’s already grinding his nerves raw. Stack a third, and you might as well start digging him a nice, shallow grave."

Once he was settled, Ysara returned to her healing, her hands steady now. Within minutes, his breathing eased, color returned to his face, and the worst of the damage faded.

Ysara stood and turned to Arthur. "What now?"

Arthur didn’t answer immediately. His gaze flicked briefly to Yudursh, still seated but more alert, before settling on her. "Turn around."

Ysara stiffened. "He’s not ready to…"

"I won’t harm him," Arthur said, tone even. "But I need to be sure."

Yudursh met Ysara’s eyes, then gave a faint nod. Bracing a hand on the ground, he pushed himself upright with effort and slowly turned his back to Arthur. She followed a heartbeat later, tension sharp in her shoulders.

Arthur stepped forward, placing a hand on each of their shoulders.

There was no chant, no flash. Only a subtle shift in the air, like space folding in on itself.

With a silent command, both Ysara and Yudursh vanished into storage once more.

Arthur exhaled, the weight of silence pressing in now that they were gone. He needed time to think. More importantly, he had to verify her claims. Ysara had said the nearest city was a two-day hike from the ambush site.

Time to find out if she’d been telling the truth.
