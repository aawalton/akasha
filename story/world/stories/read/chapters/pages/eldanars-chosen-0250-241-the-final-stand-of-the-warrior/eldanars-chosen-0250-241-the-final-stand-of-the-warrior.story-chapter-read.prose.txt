A six-foot gap of open air was entirely too large to be called a crack, but when framed against the colossal, thirty-foot-wide archway of the Ashenfall gates, it felt like a suffocating needle's eye.

To the terrified militiamen watching from the safety of the inner staging area, the sliver of pale light visible between the heavy oak doors was a beacon of hope. To Josh and Torin, standing shoulder to shoulder on the cobblestones directly outside the threshold, that narrow gap was a meat grinder they had to feed with their own bodies.

The brilliance of the chokepoint was undeniable. The infinite, crushing numbers of the undead horde were instantly rendered irrelevant. The massive ocean of rotting meat violently funnelled itself towards the opening, but the physical reality of solid matter dictated that only a handful of monsters could step through the gap at any given second.

"Thrusts and overhead chops only!" Torin roared, his gravelly voice echoing loudly against the heavy timber doors. "Do not swing wide, or you will take my head off!"

"Understood!" Josh bellowed back, planting his heavy steel boots firmly into the slick cobblestones.

He raised his shield, angling it slightly to cover his left flank, completely trusting the heavily scarred veteran to cover his right. The first wave of the horde crashed into the opening, shrieking in mindless, ravenous fury.

They were rotting villagers and low-tier Crypt Ghouls, entirely devoid of armour or martial skill. They threw themselves blindly at the gap, desperate to reach the living prey.

Josh did not need to step forward. He simply braced his massive frame and let the monsters impale themselves on his guard. He drove the heavy steel blade of his longsword straight forward, executing a flawless, brutal thrust that punched cleanly through the chest of a bloated ghoul. He ripped the blade free, instantly raising his shield to brutally bash the next leaping zombie back into the crushing ranks behind it.

Beside him, Torin was a machine of relentless, measured violence. The veteran fighter kept his heavy iron mace close to his chest, snapping it out in rapid, devastating overhead strikes. Every time the heavy iron head descended, a skull shattered like a brittle clay pot.

For the first five minutes, the defensive line held perfectly.

The two men settled into a grim, efficient rhythm. The narrow chokepoint prevented them from being flanked, and the monsters were too fragile to break through their raised shields. They were effectively plugging a bursting dam with iron and steel.

However, a horrifying, entirely physical problem quickly presented itself.

The monsters they were killing did not simply vanish. While some of the older, heavily corrupted dungeon beasts dissolved into golden dust upon death, the vast majority of the freshly raised villagers simply collapsed into regular, rotting corpses.

The bodies began to pile up directly in front of the narrow gap. Within ten minutes, a massive ramp of severed limbs, shattered ribcages, and black sludge had formed on the cobblestones.

The advancing horde no longer had to run at them on flat ground. The zombies were now scrambling up the mound of their own dead, leaping down at Josh and Torin from a higher elevation.

"Kick them back!" Torin yelled, stepping forward to violently shove a heavy, lifeless corpse out of the opening with his iron-plated boot. "If this pile gets any higher, they will spill right over our shields!"

Josh gritted his teeth, his lungs burning with a fierce, metallic agony. He kicked a severed torso away, his heavy boot slipping dangerously on the slick gore coating the stones. His mana core was virtually dry, reducing the blinding blue lightning of his Stormforged Adamantite to a pathetic, intermittent spark that crawled weakly along the fuller of his blade. He was relying entirely on his passive Living Bulwark skill to keep his muscles from collapsing under the immense strain.

Then, the chaotic, mindless shrieking of the horde suddenly shifted.

The disorganised frenzy of the lesser zombies seemed to part, forcefully shoved aside by a much heavier, significantly more disciplined presence.

"Armour!" Torin barked, his voice tight with sudden alarm.

Marching up the grotesque ramp of corpses, stepping smoothly over the ruined bodies of the ghouls, came the Wight elite guard.

The blackened steel knights had returned to the front lines. They did not leap or thrash. They moved with terrifying, cold military precision, their heavy tower shields raised and their greatswords drawn. The piercing blue soul-fire burning within their dark visors locked onto the two exhausted defenders blocking the gates.

"Hold the line, Josh!" Torin roared, raising his mace high.

The first two Wights reached the top of the corpse mound and stepped simultaneously into the six-foot gap.

The clash of steel was deafening. Josh brought his heater shield up just in time to deflect a punishing, two-handed downward cleave from the Wight on the left. The kinetic force was staggering, rattling his teeth and sending a wave of severe, spectral cold washing up his arm.

Josh instantly triggered Galvanic Riposte, hoping the parry would send a high-voltage shock back into the monster.

A weak, sputtering arc of static electricity travelled up the Wight's blade, but it lacked the raw amperage to paralyse the elite knight. The monster did not even flinch. It smoothly redirected the deflected energy, stepping into Josh’s guard and thrusting the heavy steel pommel of its greatsword directly into the centre of Josh’s breastplate.

The impact forced all the air from Josh's lungs. He stumbled backward half a step, his boots sliding on the wet cobblestones.

Before he could recover his footing, the Wight thrust its blade forward. Josh twisted his torso violently, but the narrow confines of the chokepoint severely limited his mobility. The jagged, freezing steel of the greatsword slipped past the rim of his shield, biting deeply into the unarmoured gap behind his left knee.

Josh let out a sharp, ragged roar of pain as the necrotic cold flooded his veins. His leg buckled instantly, forcing him down onto his right knee.

"Get up, lad!" Torin bellowed.

The veteran fighter abandoned his own duel, swinging his heavy iron mace in a brutal, horizontal backhand that completely shattered the side of the Wight’s knee joint. As the creature staggered, Torin brought the weapon back around, caving in the blackened steel helmet and dropping the monster into the sludge.

But Torin’s intervention left his own flank entirely exposed.

The second Wight exploited the opening with ruthless efficiency. The knight thrust its heavy greatsword forward, driving the blade deeply through the overlapping iron plates protecting Torin’s right side.

Torin let out a harsh, wet cough, blood instantly welling up behind the visor of his helmet. The veteran did not fall. Driven by pure survival instinct, he reached out with his left gauntlet, grabbing the blade of the greatsword to prevent the Wight from ripping it free. He pulled the monster closer and drove the heavy spike of his mace directly through the creature's visor, extinguishing the blue soul-fire instantly.

Josh pushed himself painfully back to his feet, ignoring the screaming agony in his leg. He stood shoulder to shoulder with the bleeding veteran, completely filling the gap once more.

"How bad is it?" Josh gasped, his voice raspy and weak.

"I’ve had worse hangovers!" Torin lied, his breathing reduced to a shallow, bubbling wheeze. "Just keep swinging!"

But the situation was rapidly deteriorating from a desperate hold into a complete impossibility.

The ground beneath their boots began to violently tremble. The rhythmic, thudding vibration was not caused by marching knights or scrambling zombies. It felt as though a massive battering ram was being repeatedly dropped onto the frozen plains.

Looming out of the swirling winter fog, towering a full fifteen feet above the sea of rotting meat, came a pair of Bone Goliaths.

The massive, fused monstrosities of bone and dark magic were marching directly towards the gates, wielding massive, jagged clubs ripped from the trunks of dead oak trees.

"Gods have mercy," Josh whispered, his storm-grey eyes widening in sheer horror. The six-foot gap was an excellent defence against infantry, but a Bone Goliath could simply reach through the opening and crush them both into paste with a single swing.

Before the towering monsters could close the final twenty yards, the sky above the gates suddenly ignited.

The defenders on the upper timber walls, having desperately rotated their casters, finally unleashed a coordinated volley.

A massive, roaring sphere of concentrated orange flame plummeted from the ramparts, striking the leading Bone Goliath directly in the chest. The explosion was deafening, washing the chokepoint in a wave of blistering, foul-smelling heat. A second later, a half-dozen jagged spears of pure, crystalline ice slammed into the monster’s thick skull, shattering the bone plating.

The combined magical bombardment was devastating. The leading Goliath staggered backward, its fused ribcage glowing with residual heat and its skull heavily fractured. It let out a deafening roar of pain, blindly swinging its massive oak club into the horde surrounding it, crushing dozens of its own lesser allies.

"The wall is holding!" Josh yelled, a brief surge of hope cutting through the despair.

But the hope was violently short-lived.

The magical barrage had severely wounded the Goliaths, but it had not destroyed them. The second behemoth stepped smoothly around its thrashing comrade. It raised its massive, heavy timber club high into the freezing air and brought it crashing down towards the narrow opening in the gates.

"Brace!" Torin roared, throwing his entire weight against the heavy oak door to his right.

Josh mirrored the action, pressing his back against the solid timber of the left door.

The Goliath’s massive club did not strike the defenders directly. The sheer width of the weapon prevented it from passing cleanly through the six-foot gap. Instead, the heavy timber smashed violently against the exterior faces of the two locked oak doors simultaneously.

The concussive shockwave was apocalyptic.

The twenty-foot-tall wooden doors groaned in sheer agony, the thick iron bands protesting loudly against the immense kinetic force. The heavy iron dropping bar wedged into the cobblestones jumped violently, showering sparks into the air.

The impact transferred directly through the solid timber, hitting Josh's backplate with the force of a falling boulder. The air was violently expelled from his lungs. His vision swam with dark, pulsating spots, and he felt the sickening crack of a rib fracturing beneath his heavy armour.

Across the gap, Torin collapsed onto his hands and knees, spitting a large, dark wad of blood onto the cobblestones. The veteran’s iron plates were severely dented, and his breathing was incredibly shallow.

The Goliath raised its massive club, preparing for a second, devastating strike. The heavy oak doors had survived one blow, but they would undoubtedly splinter and fail under a sustained assault, crushing the two defenders beneath tons of shattered timber.

They could not hold the line. The overwhelming quality of the enemy vanguard had finally broken their defensive perimeter.

The Wights were already scrambling over the massive corpse mound, their greatswords raised, eager to exploit the momentary incapacitation of the two fighters.

Torin slowly pushed himself up from the bloody cobblestones, leaning heavily on the haft of his iron mace. The scarred veteran looked at the advancing Wights, then looked up at the towering Bone Goliath preparing its next strike. Finally, Torin turned his head, his dark, weary eyes locking onto Josh.

"Get ready to close it," Torin commanded, his voice a harsh, blood-choked rasp that carried absolute, unquestionable authority.

Josh blinked, shaking the dark spots from his vision. He gripped his heavy shield, confused by the order. The dropping bar was wedged firmly into the stone. The right door was incredibly heavy. They needed both of them pulling with all their strength just to move the massive timber.

"Close it?" Josh repeated, his voice filled with frantic confusion. "Torin, what is the plan? We need to retreat together!"

Torin did not answer the question. He did not explain his reasoning, and he offered absolutely no time for debate.

The scarred veteran lunged across the narrow, three-foot gap separating them. He did not swing his mace. He slammed both of his heavy, iron-plated gauntlets into the centre of Josh’s breastplate.

The shove was completely unexpected, and fuelled by a massive, desperate surge of pure adrenaline.

Josh, already entirely off balance and physically exhausted, was violently launched backward. His heavy steel boots lost their grip on the slick cobblestones, and he stumbled blindly through the narrow gap, crossing the threshold and falling heavily onto his back inside the safety of the inner staging area.

"Close the bloody gate!" Torin screamed over his shoulder, his voice echoing fiercely through the stone archway.

Josh scrambled to sit up, his heart hammering a frantic, terrified rhythm against his broken rib. He looked back through the narrow opening, his storm-grey eyes widening in absolute, profound horror as he realised exactly what the stubborn man was doing.

Torin had not stepped back with him. The veteran had stepped forward, placing himself entirely on the outside of the heavy timber doors.

Torin planted his boots firmly in the bloody mud, standing alone in the six-foot gap. He faced the advancing Wights and the towering Bone Goliath, raising his heavy iron mace high into the freezing air.

"Torin, no!" Josh roared, scrambling desperately to his feet, his hand reaching out towards the gap.

But the veteran did not look back.

A sudden, blindingly bright, pure white light erupted from the seams of Torin’s dented iron armour. It was the terrifying, unmistakable glow of a fighter intentionally and violently overcharging their own mana core past the point of physical containment. Torin was burning his own life force to create a massive, unstable magical bomb.

Torin let out a final, deafening, battle-crazed roar that drowned out the shrieking of the horde. He charged forward, throwing himself directly into the suffocating mass of blackened steel and rotting meat.

"Push!" Jax yelled from the shadows of the staging area.

The dual-wielding rogue, aided by three burly city guardsmen, threw their combined weight against the heavy timber of the right-hand door. Without the horde actively pressing against the interior edge, the massive oak door swung smoothly and rapidly across the cobblestones.

Josh lunged forward, desperate to grab the closing edge of the timber, desperate to force his way back outside to save his friend.

He was a fraction of a second too late.

The heavy door slammed violently against the locked left door with a deafening, final boom that shook dust from the stone archway above. The six-foot gap completely vanished, instantly cutting off the blinding white light of Torin’s final charge and the horrific shrieking of the undead.

The heavy iron dropping bar on the right door was instantly slammed down into the floor, and massive timber braces were quickly wedged against the wood by the frantic guardsmen.

The city gates of Ashenfall were entirely sealed.

Josh stood in the dim, silent light of the inner staging area, his hands pressed flat against the rough, cold bark of the massive oak doors. He stared blankly at the solid timber, his breathing ragged and entirely shallow.

A heavy, muffled thud echoed against the outside of the wood, followed instantly by a massive, concussive boom that rattled the heavy iron hinges. The white light of Torin’s core detonation briefly illuminated the cracks in the wood, followed by absolute, profound silence from the other side of the barrier.

The veteran had bought them the time they needed. The gates were closed, and the city was safe… for now.

Josh slowly slid down the rough timber, his heavy armour clanking loudly against the stone, until he was sitting on the cold cobblestones. He buried his face in his blood-stained gauntlets, the suffocating, crushing weight of the true cost of survival finally settling heavily upon his shoulders.
