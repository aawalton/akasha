The only sound was rustling as corn stalks clawed at our pants. It hung on us, pulling us back with every step as if to embrace us. Our already slow crawl turned into a painful creeping pace.

Our care did allow us to avoid patrols of those monsters, and eventually, I saw one of the many telephone pole wires break off on the other side of the road and travel deeper into the corn.

In a low whisper, I called to Nate. "Is that where we're going?"

He followed the direction of my finger, his face darkening into a scowl. "No. In fact we want to avoid that place"

Questions burned on my tongue, but I suppressed them for now. Time and place.

We walked for a while after that. I had no way of keeping track of time, but when we finally arrived, my feet ached, and the adrenaline had long since worn off. My back was stiff and painfully ground with resistance anytime I moved my shoulders, as the gaps in my torn muscles prodded one another. If I had to guess, it had been about an hour since I saw the powerline going across the street. But the walking was done. We had arrived.

There was a distinct gap between the fields of corn and the Greenhouse, which was undoubtedly our destination—like a lawn mower had cleared the land. Long dead roots and the bases of stalks peeked out of the ground. I squinted at them and realized they weren't stalks at all. Well, kind of. The stalks still existed, but in the center of each was a thin, sharpened stake of wood.

Beyond the barrier of essentially caltrops was the Greenhouse. Its walls had a white opaque coloration, making it hard to peer through, but besides that, it was essentially just a building-sized tent from the outside. The only other notable feature was a large flag pole in the center that went high into the air. At the top was a red triangle flapping in the wind.

Yeah. Fuck that.

I'd make sure every word I was given was true before I set foot in that Greenhouse.

"I'll wait here," I nodded towards Nate, "go get your friend."

Nate stared at me with a frown, which quickly shifted into a look of frustration and then confusion, before they slowly nodded and settled on an easy-going smile. "Okay. Just be careful, we set up some traps around here."

With that, Nate walked up to the edge of the field of corn and hopped over the line of caltrops and entered through one of the tent flaps.

It wasn't that I didn't trust him. Okay, no, it was exactly that. It was nothing against Nate inherently, but I also wasn't about to blindly put myself in a compromising position. Outnumbered in an environment I didn't know. A million things could go wrong. People usually gave trust freely. In the real world, you trusted a bus driver to navigate around a city, you trusted your professors to teach, and a babysitter to watch a child—essentially, you trusted people to do their jobs. That trust and understanding that you could walk around and not be unduly harmed was the basis of a social contract.

Right now, I wasn't in society. I was in The Endless Dream. I couldn't even trust that the ground wouldn't collapse under me. So I didn't trust Nate, and I didn't trust his companion I hadn't seen.

It was ten minutes before Nate came back out from the Greenhouse with someone else in tow. To my surprise, it was an older woman, likely in her mid-fifties. Her face was twisted into a scowl, and she moved with a slight limp on her left side. Graying hair mixed with blond, which she had tied up in simple braids. In truth, it made sense. Why would Visitors be limited to younger individuals after all, but I still hadn't been expecting it.

Seeing the two of them, I hopped over the spike traps and gave them a wave.

Nate spoke first, "Sorry that took so long Dorian. It took some convincing to get Maggie here to agree to help."

The woman glared at me before turning back to Nate, "It's a waste of a charge."

A sheepish smile crossed the other man's face, and he shook his head, lowering his voice to a whisper, forcing me to strain my ears to hear. "You already agreed, Mags."

She grunted and took a few steps forward, holding out her hand towards me. "Hand."

The woman rubbed me the wrong way, but I also wasn't going to turn down the healing Nate had promised me, and so I let the crone take my hand.

Then the pain was gone. I frowned, twisting around to look at my back, and to my surprise, the wounds were gone. There were no fancy sparks, lights, chanting, or even a process of my skin painfully knitting itself back together. The wounds were there, and then they weren't. Simple as that.

I struggled to process it, as if my mind had a discontinuity, even though I remembered every moment. It should have hurt. It felt like someone just erased the time it would've taken my body to heal. It made a cold shiver crawl up my spine. Somehow, that was more unnatural than anything else.

Nate was talking, but I didn't catch what he said. By the time I had turned around to look at him, he had staked open two large flaps leading into the Greenhouse. The insides were pretty bare, with lines of empty potted plants and a section near the center where several chairs had been arranged.

"-neither here nor there. I bet you have a lot of questions. Come on in. We'll answer as best as we can," is the tail end of what I heard Nate saying before I followed the two of them inside and took a seat.

Maggie lingered by a nearby table, seemingly refusing to join Nate and I—which I found annoying, but ultimately said nothing.

"So. Since you're a newbie, I bet you have all sorts of questions. Lay 'em on me," Nate spoke, his voice seemingly carefree as he kicked his feet up on the table.

I had to be careful about my questions. If I learned too much, the System would likely just erase my memories, but what did it consider as too much? The only time I caught it erasing memories was the other Instance I kept trying to enter. Hmm. What to do.

"Do you perhaps have a sheet of paper and something to write on?" I finally decided on a course of action that would work, and the fact that I was still remembering things now this much should be fine.

Nate gave me a strange look, and Maggie mean-mugged me from her spot to the side.

"I mean sure," Nate started, "why though?"

Uh. Actually, that was a good point. If the System was stopping me from learning things, it would also stop other Visitors from learning them too, so nothing the pair could tell me should be considered knowledge forbidden by the System.

I suppose that they could have more Presence than me and, as such, have access to a wider knowledge base. Hmm.

"Well I guess, first. How much Presence do both of you have?"

Now both people gave me shocked looks. Ah. I said something wrong.

Nate shook his head, chuckling slightly. "You really are new. Dorian, that's not just a question you ask." There wasn't any indignation in his voice; he wasn't angry, just surprised and a little amused. Like I'd just asked what his net worth was randomly.

Well, that was annoying. Probably a good reason for it, but still. How was anyone supposed to know how much information could be passed between each other? Whatever, back to plan A. Lying. Even if the questions I asked now were fine, who's to say I wouldn't learn something later, and the System just wipe everything after I had completed the Instance? Better to have a paper trail.

"Oh okay. Sorry I didn't know that. As for the paper, it's one of my Restrictions. It's… really annoying to explain"

I saw Maggie opening her mouth from the corner of my eye, so I bulldozed through an additional explanation. "I'll tell you all about it after the Instance, but for now just know it shouldn't affect much besides this."

Maggie closed her mouth, and Nate shrugged. After a few seconds, Nate retrieved some loose-leaf papers and a pen and placed them across from me, and I began asking my questions.

"First things first. What in the hell is The Endless Dream?"

Nate grimaced, moving his feet from the table to the dirt-laden floor. "Can't start with an easy one, huh?" An eerie wind blew at that moment, causing the Greenhouse to make a disgusting creaking noise. "No one really knows, to be honest. Everyone has their little pet theories. As for hard details. For lack of a better term, it's a Nexus."

I felt one of my brows raise. Seeing that I wasn't going to interject, Nate continued. "It's bits and pieces from all of our worlds overlaid and overlapped, and at the places where it overlaps the most. That's where Instances live. Sometimes." He tapped his temple, "As for why? Some people think the Dream is trying to stitch itself together, others think it's sorting realities, some even think it's searching for something."

Too much at once. That piece of information alone essentially shattered all my preconceived notions about The Endless Dream. So many things fell into place while simultaneously the rug was pulled out from under me.

"Wait. Wait. Hold on. What? Are you saying every Instance is another world?"

Nate paused, struggling to find the words while doing a 'so-so' gesture with his hands. "Eh. Kinda. It's like. It's. Uh. Maggie, want to help me out on this one?"

Maggie gave Nate a dirty look, but at this point, I thought that she might just be looking, and that's how her face was stuck.

"Instances can be worlds, rooms, a single trap even. Worlds can have multiple Instances too. It's like" she hesitated for a moment before continuing, "a square can be a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square."

I furrowed my brow. That explanation didn't make any fucking sense whatsoever. But I think I understood the gist. An Instance could be a world, or it could just be a self-contained Instance without anyone living in it. Which would mean–

"So Visitors can come from worlds or Instances?"

Nate nodded, "Yep. If one of the monsters escaped from the instances, even they could be a Visitor. They're a real bitch and a half to deal with."

I scribbled furiously on the paper, using a shorthand so I could keep up with the conversation.

"Okay yeah, but why the hell would anyone want to become a Visitor?"

The pair gave me another funny look that I was already starting to get tired of. Yes, I didn't know anything. That was established. Please, for the love of god, just answer my fucking questions. No. That wasn't fair. They were. Probably just had never met someone as clueless as me.

"Er. Dorian. Did you not choose to become a Visitor?"

"No!" I hissed out, "Why on earth would I choose this!" I gestured wildly around the room. If I had the choice … If I had the choice – knowing everything, good and bad, that awaited me – maybe I would've done it anyways, but I had no way of knowing that. That choice was taken from me.

A heavy silence fell over the room before, to my surprise, Maggie spoke. Her expression, usually terse and full of vitriol, softened considerably. "Boy. Did you truly not choose this path?"

I shook my head, "No! I just fell asleep one day, and I was in the Dream." Learning that there was meant to be a choice filled me with anger, but what made me feel even worse was that they'd both chosen this. They had signed up for death puzzles, invisible monsters, and for what?

The elderly woman started pacing back and forth, seemingly deep in thought. Nate continued the conversation in her stead. "Being forced into The Endless Dream is very rare. So rare that I've never even met someone who's met someone that it's happened to. Generally, anyone dragged into the Dream is completely consumed by an Instance."

I put my head into my hands. Okay. This was too much to digest right now. I'd need to come back later and process it all. Wait…

"Consumption. I heard about this during my tutorial," The System had told me back when I met The Spinner, but I never asked what that specifically meant.

"Dorian…" Nate grimaced as I saw him searching for the right words. "You need to understand. Presence is everything. It allows for a lot of strange powers, whole societies are built around it, but when that number hits zero. It's not just death."

Nate clenched his teeth, but continued to stare at me, as if he was trying to impart the most important information in the world. "The Dream takes the scraps left after zero. It wears you and eats you from the inside out. It's the worst way to die.

Another pause, and I could tell he was reluctant to continue.

"The Dream is cruel, often vicious without reason. And its favorite bit of malevolence? It pretending to be the person it devoured."
