Chapter 368

Eik wandered down the same main street he had walked down on his first visit.

The fracture specialists of Eik’s Great Group of Guys were so awesome that they had opened that original portal almost on top of the actual official Gohkamorian fractures and dropped him smack dab in the middle of the city.

That meant he now knew exactly where he was going.

And as he walked, for some strange reason, people flocked around him. Well, “flock” might be a bit of a strong word to describe what was happening. They were certainly keeping their distances, but they were following him.

Funnily enough, his slowly growing entourage consisted almost entirely of children, and at least some of the few adults there seemed to only be there in order to wrangle back their kids. He chuckled. So, he was such a terrible existence that only children dared approach.

At the great plaza from which he had delivered his messages, he stopped and looked around. Yes, this place would do just fine for what he had planned this time as well. And that huge building—the seat of the Gohkamorian Ministry of Economic Development—that he had completely demolished on his first visit was still nothing but a pile of rubble, to his joy. Although clean up work did seem to have been started.

For a couple of handfuls of seconds, Eik stared up at the sky as he tried to calculate how to proceed with what he had in mind. For the thousandth time, he found himself wishing that higher power rank also granted a boost to his intelligence and ability to think. Oh, man, if only…

Finally deciding on something in his mind, he began the construction work. Sure, it could have been completed in a single second, but there was more to it than simple efficiency. For this, he wanted to make a proper show of it.

If it was too quick, the many people he wanted to see what was happening wouldn’t be able to follow along. No, it had to happen at a proper pace.

First to appear was a massive column of glittering blue crystal that rose into the sky, climbing at a rate of about ten meters per second. His keen ears heard gasps from the audience watching from what they mistakenly hoped were good hiding spots. Unfortunately, so long as they still remained on Gohkamoria, there was no such thing as a good hiding place. He would always find them.

Once it reached about two hundred meters, it stopped rising and instead widened at the top, growing outward into a wide T-shape.

Something struck Eik in the back of the head.

Curious, he glanced back to find a Gohkamorian youth staring daggers at him. Judging by his furious facial expression, he was just about ready to explode. In his left were three rocks, and his right was extended out in front of him in a dead giveaway that it had just thrown the projectile that had hit Eik’s head.

“Uuh, okay?” Eik muttered in puzzlement. Well, he had a pretty good guess what this little interruption might be.

The youth spat loudly on the ground and hurled another rock. Eik didn’t bother to avoid it. He barely felt it on his jaw.

The boy looked to be somewhere in his mid teens. Maybe fourteen or fifteen, but Gohkamorians were one of those races that were extra hard to place. The small facial plates along their foreheads, their gray and leathery skin, along with the natural creases in their features made it near impossible to get a good read on their ages.

But, still, Eik was pretty sure this guy was a young one, going by his size if nothing else. Nearly all Gohkamorian men were straight up giants, and this guy was not even as big as Eik. And if that hadn’t been enough, the voice that came out of the kid when he spoke gave it away for sure.

“I’m going to kill you!” the boy shouted. Damn, if he wasn’t brave, though. Eik had to give him that. Or was it stupidity? Yeah, maybe that one. Eik knew a lot about doing stupid stuff and this was up there with some of the things Eik had done in his time.

Admirable.

The youth faced Eik alone at the plaza, only a single other boy—one of his friends, presumably—risking a couple of steps out from the safety of the shadow of a building to hiss frantically for the rock thrower to get his dumb ass back there right now.

“I mean, you’re certainly willing to give it the old college try, or however that saying goes. You know, I’ve never really understood what that means. What does college have to do with trying stuff anyway? I’ve never really been able to figure it out. I mean, sure, in college you try many things, not just academically but socially as well, but I can think of plenty of other places where trying is a big part of it. Like a cooking class, for example. You go there because you want to try to get better at cooking, right? And yet I have never heard anybody say “let’s give it the good old cooking class tr—”

“I don’t give a shit about any of that!” the boy spat and drew a thick sword from a sheath strapped to his back. It looked pretty cool, but it seemed unwieldy as hell and the kid struggled for a couple of seconds to pull the blade free properly. Eik could probably have murdered him a thousand times in the time it took him to get his weapon ready.

“Well, then tell me what you give a shit about already. I don’t have all day, you know.”

“You took my father away!”

“Oh… I see,” Eik muttered. Of course it would be something like that. It wasn’t like bad guys couldn’t have families that loved them at home.

“If you don’t give him back to me, I’m going to split you open from neck to groin!”

The corners of his lips twitched as he suppressed a smile. Damn, this kid was a tough cookie. “Who’s you father, then?”

The boy’s fingers tightened around the handle of his sword as he spoke through gritted teeth. “Fleet Major Jarahukh.”

“A military man, huh?” Eik mumbled as he tried to recall a person with that name. Yeah, it did ring a bell, but he couldn’t exactly put a face to it.

“A proud soldier and commander in the great Gohkamorian Armada, yes!” the youth shouted with fervor. “He deserves to come home!”

Eik sighed. “I am sorry to be the bearer of bad news here, but your father had a direct hand in the systematic terror and mass murder of the people on my planet when we were first becoming a part of the Unified Mass. I have confirmed this myself. He will not come home again.”

“If my father killed your people, then you must have done something to deserve it! It was your own fault, so give him back!”

Briefly, rage flared up inside Eik’s chest, but he quickly got it under control. This kid had every right to feel the way he was feeling. He was young and had just lost his father, after all. But alas, don’t eat the pie if you don’t want to die, as they say. And Fleet Major Jarahukh had had a few slices of that pie.

“Your father is going to die,” Eik told him matter-of-factly.

With a howl of fury, the boy leaped forward. It was clear that he had only just Awakened and while his movements were practiced, he had not yet adjusted for his new and improved physical power.

His lunge started off skillfully, but he underestimated the strength of his own legs and overshot his leap by a good bit, tumbling through Eik’s sidestep with little control. Catching himself, he pivoted on his heel and managed to turn around and come back for a slash over the shoulder at Eik.

The youth's wrist was caught in a steel grip and the X-ranker arrested all motion until he hung helplessly in the air, kicking and snarling to free himself.

“Pathetic!” Eik declared and hurled the boy away bodily, sending him skidding across the ground and ripping up his skin and clothes. He didn’t mind being the arch villain of the Gohkmorian history books. Not if it got his point across. “You think you can do anything with such pathetic prowess? You are a thousand years too early to even try, boy!” Too corny?

Not ready to give up, the boy charged back in with a throaty growl, but before he could make contact again, a Gohkamorian woman appeared between him and Eik. A precise discharge of aura sent the boy rolling backward with a breathless yelp.

She was enormous. Not as big as the men, but still significantly larger and more muscular than Eik. A three meter long greatsword was held steadily in two hands. Her stance alone told Eik that she was a formidable warrior.

“Welcome,” he said with a light smile.

“You will let my son go.”

“That young man?” Eik asked, gesturing to the kid who was only just getting back on his feet, visibly shaken by the forceful burst of aura.

“I would sooner pull you onto my blade along with my own body than let you touch him.”

“You can take him.”

She stared for a moment, seemingly unable to comprehend what he was telling her. “What?”

“I’m saying you can take the boy and go. You’re his mother, correct? The wife of this Fleet Major Jarahukh?”

“Yes…” she replied with hesitation.

“Well, then I’ve already checked you and your family. You had no direct hand in the decade-long invasion of my home, as far as I can tell. Therefore, you are free to go. And for the record, your son came at me, not the other way around.”

“I will claim your head!” the youth shouted, quickly regaining his energy and rushing in past his mother.

“Enough.” Eik’s voice was like a gravitational well pulling all other matter except his words into an inescapable void. From his body rose the formidable figure of a four hundred meter tall avatar of the Monarch along with all the aural pressure that followed.

In an instant, both mother and son were flattened into the ground by the spiritual pressure, unable to move a muscle. Holding them there, Eik gazed up at his gigantic crystalline structure which had begun to expand again, the horizontal line of the T-shape widening even further.

When that finally stopped, little protrusions began to spring up atop that horizontal beam of crystal, numbering about a thousand.

To those lacking sharp eyes, it might have been difficult to make out exactly what those protrusions were, but for everybody else, it was clear.

Eik had just prepared more than a thousand crystal crosses.
