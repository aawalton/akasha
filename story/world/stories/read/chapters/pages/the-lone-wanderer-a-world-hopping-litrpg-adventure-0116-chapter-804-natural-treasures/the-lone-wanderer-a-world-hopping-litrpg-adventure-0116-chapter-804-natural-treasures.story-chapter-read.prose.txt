Percy didn’t dare to relax until he saw the giant eel’s soul begin to dissipate, as he knew that the creature was more than smart enough to play dead to lure him in. Fortunately, it only took a couple of minutes for the silver flame within its frame to scatter beyond the point of no return, as the beast didn’t seem to have any strength left for traps nor tricks.

The sea was a lot calmer and dimmer without the eel’s tampering. The whirlpool still spun above the carcass, but it did so more slowly without the monster’s willpower to accelerate the currents. The water was rich with ruby dust as always, but there was far less leaking out of the motionless body, allowing the abyss to darken slightly.

However, the eel’s scales still shone in a deep red light even in death, its serpentine body looking like a cursed mountain range jutting out of the ocean floor.

The battle hadn’t been very dangerous for Percy, since he could have tried again the day after if he had failed to kill the beast. At worst, he would have lost the element of surprise or been forced to target a different eel, though his time was too limited and precious to be wasted on mistakes.

Unfortunately, he wasn’t the only one who had registered the monster’s demise.

The eel’s spawn finally found the courage to emerge from their lifelong prison and began fighting over their sire’s flesh. There was more than enough meat for them all, yet only one core, and an unknown but finite number of ruby glands. Besides, their chaotic battle was about so much more than just nutrients – the creatures had to eliminate their rivals early, lest they suffered each other’s existence for years to come.

The Orange and Yellow eels wisely chose to remain hidden in the safety of their claustrophobic cavern, but the Green ones boldly gnawed at the quasi-divine beast’s scales in a race to the juiciest chunks of flesh. The smartest among them knew better than to waste their time breaking their tiny fangs against their progenitor’s tough exterior. They seemed to realize that they could skip several hours of pointless effort by sneaking into the demigod’s open maw and chomping a path through softer tissue.

As if the eels’ infighting hadn’t been messy enough, they were soon joined by scores of Green creatures from outside the whirlpool. Some had been dragged into the battlefield inadvertently by the current, but Percy was certain that the majority had willingly swum into the chaos to seize the opportunities that the tyrant’s demise had created.

The invaders’ efforts to steal the monster’s proverbial crown from its rightful heirs were probably doomed to fail, since Percy hadn’t seen any quasi-divine beasts of a different species in the ocean. However, the fact that these creatures were even bothering to compete against the eel’s descendants made him feel more confident in his plan to deliver the resources to Nephthys.

Five minutes after Percy’s narrow victory, the colossal carcass was covered in schools of opportunistic scavengers from head to tail tip, making it appear twice as thick as it was.

‘Piss off!’ he demanded, scaring all but the stupidest and bravest of creatures away with a powerful burst of willpower. He hadn’t gone through so much trouble just to lose his prize to a bunch of fish. ‘Go kill your own demigod, bastards!’

Shaping his domain into a drill, he began digging his own path to the most valuable body parts. Strictly speaking, the whole thing was downright priceless, since its hardened scales and enormous fangs could probably be turned into enchanted armour and weapons suitable for Blues and Violets.

Percy was skilled enough in magiscript to personally equip his entire army, though he didn’t think that that would be a good use of his time. His human subordinates were too heavily outnumbered by Machaon’s forces, and the strongest among them would quickly outgrow the tools anyway. Meanwhile, the Starry Commanders had no use for external objects, and Percy had no intention of labouring for years to repurpose and enchant the entire carcass.

Instead, he focused solely on the most important organs.

The eel’s mana core was relatively difficult to locate. It was as tiny as a sapient’s, since mana cores didn’t grow with a beast’s evolutions. Pinpointing the creature’s equivalent of a “sternum” was a bit of a challenge, and Percy had trouble perceiving Clear mana, since he wasn’t a true demigod despite possessing strength on par with one. When he finally found and extracted the spherical crystal from the monster’s body with the help of his sentries, he took a moment to appreciate how flawless it was. It was so transparent that he would have doubted that he was holding it if he couldn’t feel it pinched by his willpower.

‘To think that the eel’s entire body was powered for several hours by this little thing…’ Percy mused.

‘No wonder gods have to create literal worlds and compress space itself to fit those worlds inside their bodies,’ Micky added. ‘It’s the only way to pack even more power, since mana can’t get any denser than this.’

Unfortunately, the eel had drained its mana reserves dry before succumbing to its hunger, but the empty, strawberry-sized marble would still make for a rather nourishing meal for either Nephthys or Micky. Even Percy’s Orange host briefly stirred from its catatonic state, instinctively wishing to consume the round object.

After squashing the creature’s pitiful resistance, Percy stored the spherical organ in his seal. A few faint cracks had appeared on its surface, yet he knew that mana cores grew more resilient with each advancement, so a core of the highest grade would probably last until he returned to Remior.

Locating the eel’s special glands wasn’t any easier. Unlike the core, the organs that generated ruby powder weren’t very mana dense, which ironically made them equally difficult to perceive with Mana Sense.

The good news was that the glands were downright massive. Percy found them by following the branching tubes through which the ruby powder used to flow to the monster’s pores. The bad news was that there were at least twelve of them, scattered in six pairs across the eel’s serpentine body, each barely fitting inside Percy’s spatial seal.

They looked like giant chunks of luminescent ruby, glowing far more brightly than the creature’s scales. Their size ranged between seven and eight metres across, and Percy suspected that there were several smaller ones that he hadn’t discovered.

‘What are the odds that one will be enough to upgrade Nephthys’ mutation?’ Micky wondered.

‘I don’t know, but let’s not risk it,’ Percy replied.

He didn’t mind leaving a couple of crystals behind to give the smaller eels a chance to rise and stabilize the ocean’s ecosystem a little sooner, but he intended to bring most of the organs to his familiar.

His main body emptied out his own spatial seal before sending it through the cord. Upon receiving the second quarter-fragment on Ruby Heart, he pressed them together until they fused into a cube-shaped storage space with double the length and eight times the volume.

Swimming through the narrow tunnels that he had previously carved into the eel’s flesh, Percy severed each gland from the surrounding tissue with his domain and touched the crystals to store them into the expanded seal.

His host was just a patchwork of mangled organs and loose bits of soul held together by mana, spectral threads, healing potions and wishes, so he knew that he had to hurry up. Getting kicked out of his temporary body right before the finish line and wasting all his previous efforts would be as hilarious as it would be tragic.

It was only after he had safely stored away the eight largest glands that he allowed himself to leisurely exit and scan the carcass one final time from above. He wasn’t just examining the dead eel out of curiosity, but also to give himself some time to channel Micky’s spiritual blood back through the cord. The Huehuan could replenish the resource if necessary, but retrieving it was faster.

With Percy’s permission, the swarm of ravenous Green creatures rushed back to the carcass to resume their frenzied feast.

The sight of the colossal beast being devoured caused him to sigh internally. He didn’t regret what he had done, but the relative ease by which he had defeated the eel was somewhat disconcerting. If he could beat a quasi-divine beast with minimal preparation and using nothing more than a broken Orange body, the enemy nobles would have a field day slaughtering his Starry Commanders, despite their numerical disadvantage.

‘I suppose that’s why beasts across the universe tend to only find success after discarding their original affinity and developing sapience. No amount of physical strength can make up for the element’s shortcomings,’ Percy lamented.

Upon sending the final droplet of midnight blue mana back, he finally left his host’s battered body behind, allowing the cord to pull him back to Remior. If nothing else, his bittersweet victory had only confirmed the importance of strengthening his familiar and her subordinates as soon as possible.
