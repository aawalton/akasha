The alchemical pitch trenches at the base of the Ashenfall timber walls did not merely catch fire. They detonated.

Brett stood at the absolute edge of the upper wooden parapet, directly above the main iron portcullis, as the lower-tier casters hurled their ignition spells downward. A blinding, roaring wall of brilliant orange and noxious yellow flame erupted instantly, shooting twenty feet into the freezing morning air. The sudden, catastrophic wave of heat hit the defenders on the wall like a physical hammer blow, singeing eyebrows and forcing the heavy infantry to raise their steel shields simply to protect their faces.

Brett did not flinch, nor did he raise his hands to shield his eyes.

The elemental mage stood perfectly still, his long fingers resting lightly on the rough wooden rail. The intense, rippling thermal updraft washed over him, but his newly acquired crimson Ember-silk cloak immediately went to work. The magical fabric shimmered brightly, absorbing the ambient, roasting heat and funnelling it directly into his core as raw, usable mana. Instead of feeling suffocated, Brett felt an incredible, surging high of unlimited energy.

He pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, his pale eyes narrowing as he looked down into the inferno.

The charging tide of the undead vanguard did not hesitate for a single fraction of a second. Thousands of reanimated zombies, rotting villagers, and disfigured Crypt Ghouls threw themselves directly into the roaring flames. They did not scream in pain, for they felt none. They simply waded into the liquid fire, their bruised, purple flesh instantly blackening and blistering.

"They are trying to smother the pitch with their own bodies!" Captain Aris roared from a nearby watchtower, his voice cracking over the deafening roar of the fire.

Brett knew the captain was right. The sheer, impossible volume of rotting meat pouring into the trench was actively choking the alchemical flames. The air was instantly flooded with the thick, oily, nauseating stench of burning hair, roasted rot, and boiling stagnant blood.

It was time to go to work.

Brett did not unleash a massive, apocalyptic area-of-effect firestorm. He was a scholar of the arcane, and he understood the brutal arithmetic of a siege. This was a battle of pure attrition, a calculated attempt by the Bone General to drain the defenders' magical reserves on the cheapest, most expendable troops available. If Brett burned his core completely dry in the first ten minutes, the heavily armoured skeletal knights waiting patiently in the distance would slaughter them all.

Instead, Brett raised his hands, his long, elegant fingers splayed wide. He drew upon his vast mana pool, compressing the thermal energy until it shifted from standard orange fire into blinding, searing white plasma.

He aimed at the thickest cluster of ghouls attempting to cross the burning trench, and released a tight, concentrated beam of living heat.

The plasma lance struck the charging zombies with surgical precision. It did not explode. It simply melted everything it touched. The white-hot beam sliced cleanly through five rotting torsos in a single, sustained sweep, instantly vaporising the dark magic animating them and reducing their brittle bones to glowing white ash.

Brett released the spell, took a measured breath, selected a new target, and fired again. It was a rhythmic, mechanical process. Aim, melt, release.

All along the timber walls, the chaotic symphony of the defence was in full swing. Heavy iron-tipped arrows rained down in continuous, darkening volleys. Boulders and loose cobblestones were hurled over the parapets by the heavy infantry. Dozens of magical spells illuminated the pale morning sky, balls of fire and jagged shards of ice tearing into the charging ranks.

But as Brett paused his casting to let his core briefly cycle, he looked down the line of defenders, and a cold, sharp knot of dread formed in his stomach.

The battle had barely begun. It had been perhaps fifteen minutes since the first ghoul threw itself into the pitch trench. Yet, the fragile, conscripted line was already showing terrifying signs of breaking.

Thirty yards to his left, a young human mage wearing the blue robes of a novice academy student was on his knees. The boy was gasping for air, his face entirely drained of colour, a thick stream of dark crimson blood pouring from his nose. He had panicked, dumping his entire magical reserve into a series of massive, uncontrolled fireballs, and was now suffering severe, crippling mana sickness.

Further down, the civilian militia members drafted as archers were faring no better. Drawing a heavy war bow required immense back and shoulder strength. The bakers and merchants were already shaking violently. Their draw speed had slowed to a crawl, their arms trembling so badly they could barely nock their arrows, let alone aim them accurately into the swirling smoke below.

They were exhausting themselves at a terrifying rate, and the undead simply kept coming.

Brett turned his attention back to the ground directly below the portcullis, and the true, horrific reality of the Bone General's meat shield strategy revealed itself.

The magical and physical bombardment was undeniably devastating, but the results were a chaotic, unbalanced mess. When an arrow or a plasma bolt killed a true dungeon spawn, the creature shrieked, and its body instantly dissolved into a shimmering pile of fine, golden dust. The dust fell harmlessly into the burning pitch.

But a massive percentage of this horde was composed of natural corpses, twisted and raised by the ambient necrotic fog. When these creatures were struck, they did not dissolve. If a zombie took an arrow to the chest, it kept walking. If it lost an arm to a falling rock, it simply dragged itself forward with the other. Even when they were completely destroyed, their ruined, physical bodies remained.

And the dead were piling up.

The burning pitch trench directly in front of the main gates was now completely full, choked entirely by a solid mass of charred, smoking corpses. The flames were smothered beneath the sheer weight of rotting meat.

The following waves of zombies did not even break their stride. They used the ruined bodies of their comrades as a solid, fleshy bridge, marching directly over the smoking trench and throwing themselves against the base of the high timber walls.

"They are climbing!" Torin bellowed from a few yards away, leaning dangerously far over the parapet to watch the enemies approach.

Brett looked down, his stomach heaving in sheer, unadulterated revulsion.

The zombies were literally piling up against the solid oak logs of the wall. Hundreds of them were crushing themselves against the timber, allowing the endless ranks behind them to scramble wildly over their shoulders and heads. They were constructing several, massive, writhing ramps of bruised, purple flesh and jagged bone, intent on simply walking directly over the high parapets.

The golden dust of the destroyed dungeon beasts mixed sickeningly with the dark mud and the blackened blood, creating a horrific, glittering slurry that coated the climbing monsters. Every time a defender managed to kill a zombie near the top of the pile, the corpse would slide down, and two more shrieking horrors would instantly scramble over it to take its place.

The highest point of the writhing meat ramp was rising at a terrifying pace. It was already halfway up the thirty-foot wall, entirely focused on the reinforced platform where Josh, Brett, and Carcan were stationed.

"Melee fighters, on the wall!" Captain Aris roared, drawing a heavy broadsword. "Drop the masonry! Crush their foundation!"

Josh did not need the order. The human Guardian had already unclasped his heavy steel shield, resting it against the wooden railing. He was currently standing over a massive pile of heavy, jagged limestone blocks and shattered paving stones that the labourers had dragged up the stairs earlier that morning.

Josh bent his knees, ignoring the screaming protest of his body. He wrapped his thick, steel-clad gauntlets around a massive chunk of limestone easily the size of a beer barrel. He hauled the tremendous weight up to his chest, the raw, latent magical energy of his class heavily reinforcing his dense musculature.

He stepped to the edge of the parapet, holding the boulder high above his head, and hurled it straight down into the writhing mass of climbing ghouls.

The impact was sickeningly loud. The massive stone struck the centre of the fleshy ramp, instantly crushing a half-dozen zombies into an unrecognisable paste of shattered bone and dark blood. The concussive force dislodged a dozen more, sending them tumbling backward into the mud below.

"Keep dropping them!" Bhel yelled enthusiastically. The dwarven fighter could not lift the massive boulders with his heavily bandaged chest, but he was currently hurling heavy, iron anvils over the side with his good arm, laughing a harsh, battle-crazed laugh every time he heard the satisfying crunch of bone.

But the sheer numbers were entirely overwhelming the physical defence. The gaps created by the falling masonry were filled almost instantaneously by the relentless, shrieking tide pressing from behind. The ramp of bodies simply absorbed the heavy stones, incorporating the debris into their horrific structure, and continued to rise steadily towards the wooden walkway.

"Josh, save your strength!" Brett shouted, grabbing his friend by the thick, padded shoulder of his gambeson. "You are just adding structural foundations to their ramp! We cannot fight them at the top! We have to break the base!"

Brett stepped fully up to the wooden railing, leaning perilously out over the sheer drop.

His brilliant, analytical mind completely bypassed the terrifying visual horror of the writhing zombies. He stopped looking at them as monsters and began viewing them as a purely structural engineering problem. The ramp was essentially a triangle of stacked weight. If he simply blasted the top layer, he was just trimming the hedges. He needed to remove the load-bearing pillars at the absolute bottom of the pile.

Brett closed his pale eyes for a fraction of a second, drawing an immense, humming surge of mana from his core. He tapped into the ambient thermal energy funnelling through his Ember-silk cloak, completely ignoring the sharp, warning ache building in his temples.

He opened his hands, angling them straight down toward the very base of the timber wall, where the lowest layers of zombies were being crushed against the wood by the weight above them.

He did not use short, precise lances this time. He unleashed a massive, sustained, highly compressed torrent of roaring white plasma.

The heat was absolute, blinding agony. The air warped and shimmered wildly around him. He swept the continuous beam of liquid fire in a slow, deliberate horizontal arc directly across the foundation of the fleshy ramp.

The effect was instantaneous and utterly devastating.

The concentrated plasma did not just burn the lower-tier zombies; it completely and violently disintegrated them. The intense thermal shock vaporised flesh, bone, and rusted iron simultaneously, turning the load-bearing corpses into clouds of glowing white ash.

With its entire foundational layer abruptly and cleanly removed, the massive, twenty-foot ramp of writhing bodies instantly suffered a catastrophic structural collapse.

A deafening, wet, tearing sound echoed over the noise of the battle as the physics of gravity reasserted dominance. Hundreds of screaming, climbing zombies suddenly lost their footing. The entire massive pile sheared away from the timber wall, collapsing backward upon itself in a horrific, tumbling avalanche of rotting meat and flailing limbs.

They crashed heavily onto the dark mud and the smothered pitch trenches below, crushing hundreds of their own advancing reinforcements beneath the sudden, immense falling weight.

A ragged, breathless cheer erupted from the militiamen and the city guards stationed near the portcullis. They had been mere minutes away from having to draw their short swords and fight the horrors in desperate, close-quarters combat on the narrow walkway.

Brett immediately cut the plasma stream, dropping his smoking hands to his sides. He stumbled backward from the edge of the parapet, his chest heaving with deep, ragged gasps. A thin sheen of cold sweat coated his pale face, and his hands were trembling visibly from the severe magical exertion.

Instantly, a warm, soothing golden light enveloped him.

Carcan stepped up from behind the heavy fighters, her amber-topped staff pulsing brightly. She did not cast a major restoration spell, conserving her energy, but she wove a quick, delicate thread of healing magic directly into Brett's chest. The holy energy instantly cleared the dizzying ache from his temples and steadied the trembling in his elegant fingers.

"Pace yourself, Brett," Carcan advised softly, her melodic voice a stark, calming contrast to the howling chaos of the ramparts. Her luminous silver eyes were filled with deep concern. "You cannot melt the entire army alone. You will burn your core to ash."

"I know," Brett wheezed, offering the elven healer a weak, highly appreciative smile. He leaned heavily against the wooden railing, looking back down into the valley.

The collapse of the ramp had bought them a precious, fleeting moment of relief, but the victory was entirely illusory.

The writhing, chaotic ocean of the lesser infantry did not retreat. The sea of bruised, purple flesh simply absorbed the fallen bodies, scrambled wildly over the fresh pile of corpses, and immediately began surging back towards the timber walls to begin the horrific climbing process all over again.

And far in the distance, standing perfectly still in the pale morning light, the thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights and the towering Bone Goliaths had not taken a single step forward. The General was patiently waiting, watching the defenders of Ashenfall slowly, inevitably bleed their stamina dry.

Brett's pale eyes tracked the relentless, endless tide of rotting meat washing against the base of the wall. He wiped a smear of black soot from his cheek, letting out a long, slow breath that plumed white in the freezing air.

He looked over at Josh, who was already reaching for another massive chunk of heavy limestone.

Brett offered a dry, completely hollow chuckle, forcing a brief spark of his usual sarcastic wit into the grim morning.

"Well," Brett whistled softly, leaning his smoking forearms against the wooden parapet. "It is going to be a rather long day, Josh. And an even longer night."
