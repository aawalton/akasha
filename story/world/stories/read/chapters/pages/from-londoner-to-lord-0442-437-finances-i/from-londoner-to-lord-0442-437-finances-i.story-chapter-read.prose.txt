Later in the afternoon, Syrene and Gorsazo had gone back to their own work. The teacher had headed for the longhouse blocks to hold the evening classes, while the researcher had gone for a walk through the manor grounds to think over everything she had learned about Earth today. Duvas had joined them near the end of the conversation and had confirmed that Kivamus truly carried memories from Earth, though Syrene had not really doubted it by then.

It would take her some time to fully realise what she could do with that kind of knowledge in this world, but for now she had more than enough to think about.

Kivamus looked at the majordomo. “Well? Do you have the numbers ready?”

Duvas nodded, though he looked anxious. “I do. I was just talking with Madam Nerida to confirm a few things, but I have it ready. But mind you, these are still estimates. We keep moving workers from one project to another, and new refugees arrive every week, which will change the calculations. And are you sure about increasing wages for the captain and some of the others to 25 coppers a day? And you want to keep paying the same rates to the master craftsmen?”

“Yes,” Kivamus said. “That only affects around a dozen people at most, but getting good wages will make them less likely to leave the village. I’m not worried about Hudan, but the carpenters and blacksmith need to stay satisfied. It will also help in bringing in more skilled craftsmen here, including Isuha and anyone else we can persuade to move to Tiranat.”

“Alright,” Duvas said. “That’s how I've made the calculations too, but I should warn you in advance. The news isn’t good.”

Kivamus let out a breath. “I expected that. I've made some mental estimates of the number already. But I still want to know exactly how far in the red we are.”

Duvas adjusted the ledger in his hand. “These numbers are based on our latest population estimate of around 650 people in the village. That number may be lower or higher by at most a dozen people, but it's close enough.”

"Firstly, I'll mention our revenue estimates. From Pydaso, we should get 605 gold on average every month, since a single trip of his lasts around three weeks. Another 306 gold by selling coal to Cinran based on our current monthly sales of around 30 wagon loads. Entry taxes from visiting traders, along with sales taxes from local merchants, should give us around 40 gold every month once the village economy starts moving properly in a few weeks. However, we are not charging any entry or exit duties or any taxes from Trevalo and Pydaso, because like you said, we need to keep them favorable to us, and charging just two more merchants will only add a negligible amount of revenue anyway. Also, Trevalo should return within a week, and once he does, he should be able to sell an extra 27 wagon loads of coal to Ulriga based on our current production levels of 57 wagon loads.”

Kivamus frowned. “Why are we producing so little coal when our population has increased this much? The population of Tiranat has nearly doubled from what I see.”

The majordomo shrugged. "Last year around 100 miners were producing 40 wagon loads of coal every month, but while the population has increased, a lot of our workers are busy elsewhere these days. We now have around 350 laborers fit to work, but many of them are tied up in other jobs. Some are farming, some are cutting the new road and canal through the hills, some are building the longhouse block, and others are still digging clay for regular village use like making pottery or even new furnaces. Then there are workers at the sawmills, paper presses, furnaces, and the smokehouse. Some have also gone back to repairing and reopening shops now that coin payments are about to begin, or to help out their family members who already own such a shop. Around 30 are apprenticed to the various craftsmen in the village—the carpenters, the blacksmith, the fletcher, the potters, the wainwright and so on. So only around 220 people can actually go to mine coal each day at the maximum. Of course 57 refugees have just arrived yesterday, and haven't been to the mine even once yet. My estimate is that around 45 of them are going to work as coal miners—and again, we don't have enough mining tools to give them—while others will join as apprentices of various craftsmen. So as of now, just 175 miners have been working at the mines, and their expected coal output is around 70 wagon loads a month, based on last year's production numbers.”

Duvas looked at his ledger. “Once the latest batch of refugees starts going to the mines, their expected output will rise to around 90 wagon loads a month. However, we’re only getting 57 right now. That's because the new miners lack experience inside mineshafts, and there is a persistent shortage of mining tools like pickaxes and shovels, as well as wheelbarrows, wagons and carts for transportation within the mineshafts and from the mine to the village. Trevalo’s wagons helped a lot, but he’ll take them away within a week. The three new wagons from Torhan’s clay mine will help though, and the wainwright should finish his first wagon soon. I expect that within a couple of months, our coal output should rise to the expected 90 wagon loads a month as the new miners learn from the experienced ones and as the ever-present shortage of tools eases. The output from the craftsmen will keep increasing with the new forge and the triphammer working at the eastern stream for the blacksmith, and the sawmills helping the carpenters.”

He glanced at Kivamus. “Our population is also increasing by around 30 new people coming here every month on average, although some of them are kids and others too old to do mining work. The number of new workers available to work is also reduced somewhat by our continuous guard recruitment, including the 20 new guards you ordered the captain to hire this time. Either way, coal output should rise continuously in the coming months, and these calculations will keep changing as well. So our revenue should keep increasing in the future, at least until the northern road is blocked again for the winter.”

Kivamus nodded. “That makes sense. Go on then. How much can Trevalo bring us?"

“Trevalo’s transport cost will be higher on the longer northwestern route compared to the traders just taking the coal to Cinran, so he can’t offer us a great price,” Duvas said. “But we will provide the protection on the northern road in the forests, with those guards doubling as wagon drivers, so we should still be able to negotiate with him to get at least 10 gold per wagon, compared to the 10 gold and 4 to 5 silver coins the Cinran traders offer us. For the current surplus of 27 wagon loads of coal, that will add another 270 gold of income each month to what I listed earlier. It will increase in the future as our coal output increases, since there is basically no upper limit to how much coal Trevalo can sell in Ulriga, as long as he can transport it from here. So for the coming month, our total revenue should be somewhere around 1,220 gold a month.”

Kivamus’s eyebrows rose. “That is a lot... Until last autumn we earned just around 490 gold in total every month.”

“It has indeed increased by a lot,” Duvas admitted with a smile. “For comparison, the tax collector Ustaimo said Cinran earns around 5,000 gold in revenue each month. That’s still much higher than us, but it’s no longer ten times as much. Obviously, none of this would be possible without the medicine trade, which is our single biggest source of revenue right now.”

Kivamus grinned. “Keep watching. Soon we’ll earn more than Cinran! I'll make sure of it.”

Duvas gave a tired smile. “I’m looking forward to that day, and at this point, I have no doubt it will happen eventually. But while our income has increased by a lot, our expenses have risen even more.”

“I already expected that,” Kivamus said. “How much will we be spending once we start the coin payments?”

“I’ll start with the expenses for the manor residents,” Duvas said, “including the guards.”

He looked down at the figures again. “We now have 100 guards on wages, with men and women paid the same. I know you have ordered Hudan to recruit 20 more guards, but I had already done the calculations for the manor so I haven't updated it with those numbers."

Kivamus shrugged. "That's okay. Your calculations already include paying them as laborers, so the numbers will not change by much because we will only have to pay the difference in the wages once they are hired as guards. You can ignore it for now. Carry on."

Duvas nodded. "Any new recruits start at 11 coppers a day. That's what most of our guards earn. Those who are still fairly new but already showing better performance in training, like Tesyb, Yufim, Torig, Hyola, Isomi, Savomi, and Levalas, are at 12 coppers a day. More experienced guards like Calubo and Nurobo get 14 to 16 coppers. Those with leadership experience or specialist value, like Kerel—who's in charge when the captain isn't here—the ex-mercenary Feroy, and the instructor Nesser, are all at 20 coppers. The captain is now at 25 coppers a day, since you wanted him to be paid more than the others." He lifted his eyes from the ledger. “That puts guard wages at 375 gold a month."

Kivamus winced at that number, but stayed quiet.

“By the way, we let go of the older villagers we had been using to help man the watchtowers,” Duvas said. “We have enough female guards now to serve there, so that'll save some coin."

The majordomo continued, "The regular manor staff is next. For servants, maids, and grooms, most are on 9 to 10 coppers a day depending on skill and experience. That is compared to the usual 8 coppers a day for laborers and miners, which is why getting a job in the manor has always been highly sought after. The more experienced ones here can even get up to 12—such as Leah, who can sew well and handles most of the leather armor repairs. This group comes to 96 gold per month in total.”

He moved his finger to the next line on the ledger. “For me as majordomo, Madam Hilda as head cook, Madam Nerida as head maid, Gorsazo as head teacher, and Syrene as head researcher, it is 25 coppers a day each. That comes to 37.5 gold a month.”

He tapped the page. “So total wages for manor residents and guards come to 509 gold a month.”

Kivamus grimaced. The expenses kept increasing like a rising tide. “Go on..."
