Chapter 378

“Ah, nuts, man…” Eik groaned and let himself fall backward toward the hard stone floor.

Even with an X-ranked body, falling flatly on a hard floor was not particularly comfortable, so just before he made contact, a large pillow of springy Profound Toxin squelched into existence between his back and the stones.

He was alone in the laboratory. The professional alchemists had worked their asses off for a long time, and with the completion of the recipes for the four grade 7 potion, their roles had been fulfilled and Eik had paid them just as handsomely as he had promised them he would.

At this point, he had a pretty good routine going with the X-ranked potions. In fact, in a small crate on a table pushed up against a wall on the opposite side of the laboratory he had collected ten vials of each of the three main types.

And with that, he had felt confident enough in the process to move on to attempting a Legendary Mystery Medicine variant of the Potion of Mighty Strength. That was, a pill which, rather than a powerful, short-lived effect, would grant a less intense version of the same effect. Permanently.

Once he had a handle on the regular recipe, there usually wasn’t much trouble in converting it to Legendary Mystery Medicine. It was mostly a matter of infusing Profound Toxin in the correct way, correct dose, and with the correct timing.

So why the hell had he already failed sixteen times in a row?

Because the god damned active ingredient in each of the recipes were parts harvested from him, and those parts already contained Profound Toxin. So it just wasn’t reacting like it normally would.

He buried his face in his hands with another groan. “What the hell am I supposed to do here?” he asked himself out loud. It was always calming, albeit only slightly, to speak out loud when he was worried.

With a gasp, he flew back to his feet, only narrowly avoiding carving out a groove in the ceiling as he misjudged his own strength. Usually he managed to control it well enough, but there was the occasional slip. At least he hadn’t killed anybody by accident. Yet

“Hot dog, I got it! I think I got it!” In a near literal instant, he collected the ingredients for another attempt, moving just slowly enough to not destroy the space.

He had been following the wrong logic. Until now, he had been trying to solve the mismatch by adjusting parameters such as timing and dose, but that didn’t change the fact that he was trying to alter the recipe with a property it already possessed.

Even Visage of Death and Panacean Quintessence he had tried, but that had proven fruitless. No, it had to be the original Profound Toxin. But different. And different he could do.

Standing in front of the waiting mixture, Eik reached deeply. This was something he usually didn’t bother to use. Either the power wasn’t necessary, or the opposition was so strong that sticking with normal Profound Toxin was safer than spending the extra time working with this.

On the tip of his finger, a droplet of bright, unimaginably glittering azure bubbled up. The same stuff that he had first discovered in the stone basin at the peak of the mountain in Toxin. The same stuff he had fallen into during one of his evolutions.

It was Profound Toxin, but it was also something different. Like the spring from which the rest of it originated. It was potent, yes, but it was difficult to harness compared to the common stuff, even now. But as the spring, it was the perfect agent to cause the change in the mixture that he needed. It was the obvious answer he had been missing.

It was immediately obvious that he was onto the right thing here. What he had needed was not to intensify or supplement the Profound Toxin already there, but to alter it with its progenitor. And it was working.

Two more times he screwed it up, but on the third attempt since employing the new method, success was finally within his grasp.

When the mixture rose up and out of the cauldron before coalescing into a small chromatic orb, he knew he’d got it. Catching it in his palm, Eik felt the power flow through the elixir—familiar but far more powerful than any that had come before it.

Without a second of hesitation, he threw his head back and swallowed the pill whole, feeling the effects melt into his system as it traveled down his esophagus.

He took a deep, calming breath, heart racing away like a galloping horse. Images of his fight with the Lord of the Moon invaded his mind as fear warred with a desperate reach for confidence.

“Daddy’s home!” Eik howled through the house and stomped his boots on the mat. Written out in the coarse bristle, the words “live, laugh, love” were spelled out in a neatly swirling font. Ihasu had immediately gotten one made on custom order when she heard about the concept.

She just didn’t grasp the cultural significance the same way he did. How could he have the boys over with that thing lying there in plain view? Ugh. How about something more manly, like “beer, sharks, denim vests” instead? Was that too much to ask?

When he had brought up that alternative idea, he had been informed in no uncertain terms, that, yes, it was in fact too much to ask.

His little son barreled into his chest before the door had even clicked shut behind him. “Dad!” he shouted into his shirt.

“Hey, kiddo,” Eik said and ruffled Goo’s hair, his ears listening for sounds of other people in the house. He had promised not to employ his hyper enhanced senses at home. Actual dangers would be picked up by the hundreds of high-ranked Living Manifestation hidden everywhere before they became a problem. “Say, are you home alone, buddy?” he asked with concern.

“No.”

“Who’s here?”

“Bin,” the boy said, pointing to toward the back garden.

“Phew, okay, good. Is she alone?”

“No.”

Suddenly his stomach was a knot. “Who’s she with?”

“Phillip,” he said. When his father didn’t answer, he looked up and saw the tension on Eik’s face. “Don’t worry. Mom said no kissing.”

Eik nearly choked on his own saliva then and there. “Kis— Kissing? Why in the world did she feel the need to tell them that?”

The boy just shrugged and reached his hands up toward Eik, which usually meant he either wanted to be picked up or wanted snacks.

“No,” he drawled when his father grasped him under the arms. So, snacks, then.

Pacified by a handful of assorted cookies, Goo climbed onto Eik's shoulders as he made his way out back. Bin and Philip were talking and laughing in the hammock. Okay, good. No signs of kissing. Briefly tempted to get reports of events of the past few hours from Living Manifestations, Eik thought better of it. He wasn’t sure he was emotionally prepared to hear it.

“You guys want juice or something?” he called.

“Hey, dad! Yeah, and cake!”

“Mr. Magnasen, thank you for having me!” Philip said with a smile. He had grown comfortable with their family. Maybe that was all right, after all.

“Yeah, no worries.” He had Goo deliver the treats to them, grabbing an extra for himself on the way. Eik didn’t want to get too close, just in case they were holding hands. “Where’s mom?” he asked his son when he returned.

The boy pointed toward the front of the house, where Ihasu was just coming home.

Inside they sat down together, Goo on dad’s lap, munching away on his cookies.

“How’s it going?” Ihasu asked him.

"I don't know, honestly," he admitted with a sigh. "Apparently, my daughter is winding up for her first... kiss." He could barely get the words past his lips without crying.

She punched him in the shoulder. "I was talking about that significantly more important thing that concerns the continued existence of all life in the Unified Mass."

“Pretty good,” he said, lips pursed in thought. “I completed the Legendary Mystery Elixir yesterday. I’ve already taken most of them. I’m leaving some for Chop as well, of course.”

“You’re not sharing them fifty-fifty?”

“I considered it, but now that my Perfect Consumption has been elevated to Supreme Vessel, I reckon I get at least twice the effect compared to other Awakened. Probably more, but it’s difficult to say for sure since I got it as I was taking my first potion at X-rank.”

“Why give him any at all, then? Wouldn’t it be safer to take them all yourself?”

Eik shrugged. “Maybe, but the two of us together stand a better chance of surviving, and the Lord of the Moon is too powerful right now.”

“He’s that strong?” she asked, concerned.

“Honestly, yeah. And I’m pretty sure he’s growing stronger at a crazy rate. After all, he’s not actually climbing to new heights so much as taking a fast elevator to the floor he was on eons ago,” he said but pivoted the subject. “Anyway, do you know what I did thirty times today?”

She frowned. “No, what?”

“Cut off my own god damned ass. Can you believe it?”

Taken completely by surprise, Ihasu couldn’t help but burst into laughter. Eik drank up the jingle of her voice like a thirsty man in a dessert. It had been a good idea to come home, if just for a little bit.

“What are the other parts they need?”

“Bone marrow from my femur for Metal Body and nerves from my brain stem for Magical Empowerment. Kiq’s Temper is gall bladder, but I’m saving that for later.”

“Brain stem?” she gasped. “How’s that safe?”

He slapped his chest. “X-rank, baby. It would only kill me if I didn’t have the energy to regenerate, but damn, if it doesn’t suck regardless. It might be the most uncomfortable thing I’ve ever experienced, and I once stepped barefoot on a dead toad in the dark when going to take a leak in the middle of the night.”

“The brain stem thing sounds worse.”

“Yeah, probably,” Eik agreed with a chuckle.

For a minute, they sat in silence, both lost in thought. Only the sound of Goo’s chewing sounded in the living room. Eik caught Ihasu’s eye. She looked afraid, so he shot her a beaming smile. “You’re pretty cute, you know that?”

She snickered. “Yeah, I knew.”

“Of course you did. I say it all the time.”

“When do you think the Lord of the Moon will show up?”

For a few moments, Eik chewed on his answer but could think of nothing. “I don’t know. Anytime? Never? I am endlessly confused by him.”

“Do you think there is a chance that he might let us be?”

Eik sighed. “No, I don’t. Our continued existence goes against his goals. At least if I’ve understood him even a little. And through my communion with Profound Toxin, I’d like to think I do.”

“What does he want?”

Eik’s eyes were grave. “A cosmos reborn in his own image. Torn to the ground and rebuilt with bricks of his own design.”

“So, what, he wants to be a god, then?”

“Painfully unoriginal, isn’t it? Of course, that is just what Profound Toxin thinks, and if there were ever a time to recognize a biased opinion…” A pencil-thin tendril slapped him on the wrist in admonishment.

“And Profound Toxin doesn’t desire godhood?” she asked, the notion suddenly striking her as a possiblity.

Eik snorted. “We’re too lazy and unambitious. Not to mention that we already are god on Toxin, and it’s more fun if it’s a little unique. Ruling the whole universe would be such a drag. I’ve got enough on my hands with the company, and that Philip kid.”

“You have got to drop that,” she said with an eye roll that completely wiped the concern for his deific aspirations. “They’re just friends.”

“Yeah… for now,” he grumbled.

Soon, her face changed again. “Eik, I’m scared.”

“I know.”

“What’s going to happen to us?”

He reached out and put his hand on hers, squeezing it. “With these new potions, I’m confident that Chop and I can win this in the end,” he lied.
