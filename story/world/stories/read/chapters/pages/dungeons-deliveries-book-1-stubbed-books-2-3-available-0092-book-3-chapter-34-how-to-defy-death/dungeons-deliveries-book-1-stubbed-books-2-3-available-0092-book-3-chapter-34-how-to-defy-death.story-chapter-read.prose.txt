The specter of death stalked any mind that fathomed its existence. It could lurk slow and persistent, or strike in a sudden leap. There was no form it could not take, and no matter what, in the end it caught everyone. Well, most everyone. Not him though. Not anymore.

That should have quelled his fear, but death was a trickster and simply turned into a new kind of shadowy companion.

“Petal.” Petunia, his wife, said while squeezing his shoulder. “It’s time.”

“Just a moment.” Petal Gravewhistle answered softly.

Petunia waited with him, which was convenient, as the slumbering man Emilio had unceremoniously dumped at the northern outpost required her tinctures. Her presence also soothed Petal’s anxiety. He didn’t understand why their king had brought the man along. There was something deeply unsettling about his state, and he wished his other wife, Marzipan, could be there too, but she lay hidden close by.

He needed to prepare, as something truly grand stirred.

From behind a bush of wild blueberries, Petal could not see the gathering around the dragon. But he did not need to see what he could feel.

Drums made from the skin of enemies shuddered the air under their beatings. Porcelain bodies danced barefoot upon the earth. The Soil Mind, so thoughtfully cultivated throughout the outpost by the gentlest of their number, cast back the night with its orange fungal glow. Gnomes from every ilk moved or waited in anticipation. The scavengers, the infirm, the barely held together, the builders, the caregivers, the nurses, even the children. Seven of his own, marked by their potent hearts, rustled about with the rest. Even the non-gnomish cores he felt. [Runs with Wind] watched from the edge, his Dungeon monsters twinkling in the periphery. Friends too, old and new, had come. Their participation was a great blessing for the Ritual. Gaia would be well pleased with the variety of life present.

“Peeeeetal.” Petunia urged.

“My dear dewed bloom, be patient.” He whispered, casting a wider net out, only to find exactly the source of his fear.

The horrid, lurking abominations who thought themselves clever didn’t scare him because they were dangerous, fetid, unnatural twistings of life that shouldn’t exist. He was the [Eternal Gnome], blessed by Gaia and Emilio alike. His body may melt or break, but so long as he burdened the torment, he would stand again. Rebuilt by Gaia with the strongest materials around. Infused and blended until every bit of his previous form was nothing but a speck. But as he was reformed, unkillable and exempt from death’s embrace, his mind, his soul, and his purpose remained untilled.

The burden of pain he welcomed. But the specter of death welcomed him anew, waving instead at the gnomes after sneering at him.

Where he could be rebuilt, they were but porcelain. Chippable, crackable, their bodies could be shattered. Many were powerful or sharp of wit. But so many lived partially broken. Their scars did not seal over. When damage chipped them away piece by piece, their burden increased permanently, making the next conflict that much more difficult.

Such was the plight of the enlightened garden gnome.

The children were so hard to form and were terribly dangerous on the mother. Only half lived through the great crack of the stomach. And Rituals to heal happened only at solstice, and maybe for one if luck was present perhaps two.

“She’s waiting, Petal.” Petunia pressed up from behind him, her own healed stomach meeting his back. “If she’s ought to be our queen, we shouldn’t have her upset.”

This time, Petal didn’t answer her. He filled what little remained of the hollowness within him, lingering on the decision forced upon him.

There was no other option than victory. For the gnomes to rebuild their number, their enemy must fully fall. Much like their fungus Soil Mind, the gnomes must expand, contract, and spread the spores of their kind far. Out into the vastness of it all. For they existed in a forest, but one full of darkness. Stick your head out and announce that you’re there, and another would come and test your mettle. Or, you could go to them and become a beautifully brutal weed.

The gnomes had opted for both, utilizing the full breadth of scope nature encompassed. They would weed out their enemy and burn the roots. But they would also trap the confidently aggressive who attacked the guise of gnomish weakness.

Petal smiled sadly, thinking on how the tactics the gnomes employed were akin to nature. The battalion of monsters thought to terrorize their Ritual with a large-scale attack this very night. Many gnomes would fall, even though they were ready. It was inevitable.

But like Gaia herself, the gnomes would use every available bit. If the Beast of Blightwood meant to break their spirit, the gnomes would change the path of root.

He opened his eyes, turning to meet Petunia’s, and worry etched upon her face.

“Your eyes, Petal.” She said. “They’re not blue. They’re brown. What’s happened to your eyes?”

Petal looked down at his body, smiling. Gone was his paleness. He’d battled against mighty foes, taking the worst of the punishment. His flesh had melted, shattered, and grinded away. As the [Eternal Gnome], it was his duty to lead the charges and bear the hurt where others could not. Now his body was rock and metal, mud and hardwood, twisted throughout with oxidized copper green.

“I’m still me.” Petal answered, sticking out his chest. “I’ll always be me.”

Petunia laughed, the worry disappearing. “Oh, aye. It’s still you, alright. Ready?”

Emilio had not led the gnomes astray. His king wanted a queen. She waited on Thumb Rock, mud brown and majestic. The most spectacular dragon Petal had ever seen. To be formed in compliment to Gaia was astounding enough, but her ability to fly along earth like a gust?

She would do well as queen. Now they just needed to convince her with their hearts, and of course, a wonderous gift.

Gaia called, and he was elated to feel her happiness. Petal stuck his chest out further.

“It’s time.”

Petunia snarled in that way he loved, shoot out both hands to grasp his nipples and give them a mighty twist. She yanked, and twisted harder, releasing them as Petal gasped.

Thwack, Petunia slapped him across the cheek, hard.

Thwack, She slapped him again.

Thwack, Another slap for good measure.

And then her lips were on his. Petunia pulled away after a moment, nodding.

“Well, we’re ready.” She said. “But is Boanne?”

He felt out for her heart, standing atop Thumb Rock. She was ready. He turned and walked toward the blueberry bush.

Petal Gravewhistle, Steward of Sun and Shade, Ritualist of the Allen Gardens Gnomes, Father of The Seven Stirred upon Thumb Rock, reached for the very first skill granted to him upon his awakening. [Rite of Rootbound Chorus].

No two Enchantments or Rituals were the same. You could not recreate this kind of magic with measured beakers and perfect ratios. It was only possible by feel.

And Petal Gravewhistle, once a lifeless gnome, felt greater than all. He pushed through the bush and bellowed the beginnings of his Ritual call.

“GNOMES—HOW DO WE STAND?”

“ROOTED IN SOIL!” Every gnome roared back, turning to Petal with a hungry look.

He stood tall, turning his gaze upon them all. Taking his time, Petal took in their eager eyes. Scared he might be for their survival, but not a single gnome thought of running away.

His voice timbered out, and Gaia in turn joined the Ritual.

“We welcome Boanne, of mud, of stone, of soil, of sky,

Let our roots hold her, may her winds bear high,

With Sun granting fury, and Shade granting might,

Our grandest gift is marching to live through plight,

Yet, we gnomes present you, Boanne, a gift to bring you only delight.”

Silence settled amongst the clearing. The gnomes had worked tirelessly through the day to prepare the gift. Petal hoped the dragon would find it pleasing. But from her look, the way she stared out eagerly, he had no doubt that she would.

And in the peak of her pleasure, Emilio would come to greet her.

And in the peak of their meeting, death would greet the Ritual. Only, the gnomes were expecting this guest.

“BRING THE GIFT!” Petal gave his signal.
