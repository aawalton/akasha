Peng Li ran through the open arena, the giant skeleton following his every move. The reaper was right after him, fighting with more eagerness than before.

The giant skeleton had formed a while ago. It looked like a giant mutant of sorts, with three limbs and a large tail that acted as a foot and hand at the same time. When it attacked, it would change from standing on it to jumping off of it and swiping with it. Peng Li had almost died on a few occasions from getting surprised by it, but survived each time thanks to his quick reflexes. There weren’t any obvious weaknesses Peng Li could exploit, sadly.

The reaper, on the other hand, was watching everything attentively, eyeing the beady crystals that formed the giant skeleton’s eyes.

When it had formed, there were still some skeletons left on the ground, and they all rose to form a decently sized army that was not dangerous to him, but that would make it harder for him to fight freely.

The sword gripped in his hand was filled to the brim with Reaper Qi, but he’d made a regrettable discovery.

Reaper Qi didn’t really work if the target was already dead, or if it had no flesh and anything to poison.

He cursed his luck once again as he ran away and chopped off the small skeletons, only for them to reform a few seconds later.

It had gotten to the point where he’d found the entire ordeal annoying, but he was unable to leave as well. He didn’t know any techniques that would allow him to deal with the skeleton. All that he’d ever known was the Reaper Qi.

Without it, there were very few, if any, techniques he could use.

It wasn’t because he hadn’t learned any; he did, but they weren’t all that high level. The issue was that he’d only, to any degree of proficiency, trained with the techniques of his sect.

His basic jabs, kicks, slashes, and thrusts were useless, and since all of his Qi was Reaper Qi, rather than the usual, normal Qi, it had little effect on his strength.

He wanted to cry, but no tears came, and he could only blame himself.

Gritting his teeth, Peng Li’s brain worked quickly and realised that if there was any sort of weakness on that thing, then that would be the eyes.

He could reach it just fine, even though the thing stood at a dozen or more feet tall. Enhancing his body with Qi was no problem.

The problem arose when attacking, since he had little strength against it. Hell, he was even struggling to deal with the small skeletons, and the worst part was that the reaper figure he had relied on for so long wasn’t doing anything either!

He couldn’t speak to the thing either, although he was pretty sure it had some sort of sentience. The only thing he could do was run and try to think of a plan.

His bag of holding held many treasures, but it wasn’t like he could simply take them out to test. He had to think about something, a solution.

There were no extra weapons, but he did have pills. He even had some poisons, but nothing that would work against an undead.

The only thing he could think of that might have an effect was corrosion, but not the Reaper Qi corrosion, the other, more natural, acidic sort.

But he didn’t have any form of acid either, and there was no way he could concoct some even if he knew how.

The skeleton roared all of a sudden, its maw opening toward the sky. He wasn’t sure why, but it sounded… anguished.

Even the skeletons stopped.

Peng Li didn’t have much time to think about why it was acting like that, and decided to take advantage of the opportunity to gouge its eyes out.

He ran like the wind, using the Reaper Sect techniques to get closer in a few steps, and jumped up and onto the thing’s face.

He held on for dear life by the bone, afraid to step into its mouth as well.

The volume of the anguished howl was deafening, but he kept going, fighting the urge to throw up.

With his sword raised, he planted it under the skeleton’s eye, pushed it as deep as he could, then grabbed it with both hands and used all of his weight and strength to try and pry it open.

He… couldn’t, or at least not immediately. He was struggling to gouge them out because of how firmly they were planted inside the socket.

Unexpectedly though, the reaper figure of all things decided to give him a helping hand.

It used a lot of strength to bend the sword by the hilt, and the two of them managed to extract the first crystal.

It fell down with a loud thump, finally awakening the skeleton, who stared at them with newfound anger.

Peng Li gulped and hurriedly jumped down, dodging the powerful strikes from the skeleton. Each time it punched or kicked, the ground shook, leaving a deep imprint on the solid surface.

That was clearly different from before, when it wasn’t as aggressive.

The skeletons on the ground also seemed to have gained a new intelligence and did a far better job at getting in his way.

If things kept going as they were, Peng Li would surely die.

He hadn’t noticed in that moment that the reaper had floated above the crystal and lightly touched it.

When the figure’s bony fingers did so, the crystal disappeared, and it reappeared in a small version inside its own empty eye socket.

In that moment, both the giant skeleton and the regular skeletons stopped to stare at it and, feeling the power it was currently exuding, shivered.

Then, as if to mock Peng Li, all of them, giant skeleton included, went to one knee and bowed!

The young man gulped, unsure what the figure he’d summoned and used at his own leisure would do.

It turned to face him, a creepy smile spreading on its bony lips, and nodded.

Peng Li wasn’t sure what the nod had meant, but he’d felt the power to control the skeletons be given over to him.

Yet, at the same time, he knew that it could easily be taken away in the blink of an eye.

What is this thing plotting?!

Left with no choice but to play along, he stood up and nodded toward the thing. There was an eerie quiet in the area, but it soon disappeared as a swirling vortex appeared behind Peng Li.

He had a feeling that he knew exactly where it was going to lead, and after a bit of hesitation and calculations, he walked through it.

So what if he was to be enslaved to the reaper for the time being? Once they were out of the realm, his father would find a way to get rid of it if he hadn’t already by then.

The skeletal army joined him in as well, following shortly behind with the reaper at the tail.

◈◈◈

When Peng Li came out of the portal, the first thing he did was look around. Greeted by the sight of a normal jungle, with no predators, giant worms, sand rats, or anything else around, he finally let out a sigh of relief.

Looking around, it looked like he was the first to arrive at the center. He knew that it was the center because a giant, and truly giant, stone stele was standing in the middle of a stone pedestal.

Next to it was also a giant stone board, but he couldn’t fathom why it would be there.

Without waiting on ceremony, he walked forward, the skeletons waiting at the entrance of the portal. The reaper walked with him, just as it had before, but a glimmer of intelligence shone in its emerald eye.

Doing his best to ignore the creepy feeling, he made his way forward and touched the stone stele upon reaching it.

There was no grand ceremony to what happened next. His forehead burned with an extreme heat for a short moment before all of his points moved away from him and into the stele.

The crystals inside the stone stele began filling up with a red light, and it was only then that Peng Li noticed that there was also another set of tinier crystals next to it.

Ten, to be exact.

Next to each crystal, be it small or big, there were numbers.

The first big crystal was one hundred, the second big crystal was a thousand, the third big crystal was ten thousand, and so on and so forth until the ninth crystal.

The maximum number of points was… What the hell is that number? Ten… ten billion?

He looked at that number with shock. How could it be ten billion? He couldn’t help but hope that the number of points he had was nearing one billion, but even he dared not be that arrogant.

Next to each of the large crystals, the ten small crystals were accompanied by a multiplicative value, from times one to times ten.

So this means that if I were to have one hundred, it would show up as one hundred times one, and two hundred, one hundred times two. It rounds up to the nearest whole…

Peng Li could imagine a poor bastard with ten points too few to qualify for the next threshold. Although it didn't look like it was going to show all the points on hand. It would just show the total whole.

With that newfound knowledge, Peng Li looked at the rising number with anxiety.

It raced past the hundreds and thousands, not even slowing down in the tens of thousands or hundreds of thousands.

It only slowed down when he’d reached the million mark, and finally stopped on the ninth small crystal after the million mark.

He had nine million points.

So close…

The red dots stopped pouring in, and when they did so, Peng Li let go of the stele.

Taking a step back, he noticed that the stele glowed with a strong light, and the red light within began to vanish.

He was scared for a moment, but soon relaxed upon seeing that it had turned into a streak of light that headed toward the giant stone banner.

There, his name, a name he hadn’t given upon entering, was marked

[1. Peng Li: 9 000 000 Points]

He couldn’t lie to himself; he did feel some sort of pride upon seeing that number. However, an extreme feeling of shame arose within him when he remembered that it wasn’t him who had done all the killing, but the reaper figure behind him.

Looking at it, he saw that it had no expression, as if those matters didn’t matter to it at all. Peng Li couldn’t help but get a bad feeling in his gut, but he’d decided to fight it.

He’d made the mess; he was going to fix it.

◈◈◈

Over the course of the next few hours, more and more disciples showed up at the stone stele. All of them were in horrible condition, wounded, exhausted, and some even within a blink of their life.

Those that were healthiest had a lot of opportunities to kill those that barely walked on their two legs, but didn’t. They all understood that this realm wasn’t one where blood had to be shed, and one could only blame their own lack of ability for ranking last.

Not to mention this was only the first of four trials, so they weren’t at their wit’s end yet.

Many of them did look with extreme apprehension at Peng Li and his army of undead, and also avoided him like the plague.

Other than that, things went on as usual. The first of the arrivals who had already sat down and recuperated now got up and walked toward the stele.

They all saw Peng Li’s name on there, and were all in awe of it and the number it was displayed next to.

The first one who’d built up the courage to get their red dots counted was Mei Ba Do, a fellow sect member of Peng Li.

His forehead looked impressive, but not as impressive as Peng Li’s was.

The points transferred from his forehead to the stele, and quickly rose up to the tens of thousands and hundreds of thousands, until it reached the million mark, and stopped at the three multiplier.

Three million points.

A good number by any means of the imagination, he thought, but he couldn’t quite be sure of it either. The only other point of comparison was that monster, Peng Li, and it was hard to compare him to anyone in the sect.

Especially not with that army of undead following him.

The points turned into a streak of light and became text on the blackboard.

[2. Mei Ba Do: 3 000 000 Points.]

Many followed after him, and the rankings changed as more names were added. In the end, ten out of the twelve who had entered had put their names on the ranking. Peng Li remained at the top, of course, and a conflicted feeling of disgust and pride fought within him.

A common message appeared in their heads as soon as the last one entered their name.

There were only thirty minutes left.

None of them were anxious. Even the people of the Rock clan, who wanted to get close to Fa Xiaolan, were not worried. Whether or not it was because they had faith in her abilities, or because they didn’t care that much about her, it didn’t matter.

As time passed, they’d felt that it was weird that the strange Hwei Badai hadn’t arrived yet.

Just as they’d begun speaking amongst themselves, though, a figure crawled his way out of the forest, his body scarred from top to bottom, clothing torn and blood hardening around his wounds.

When they saw him, they all let out a collective gasp of shock and stared at him with widened eyes.

He didn’t look at any of them, and his only goal was to desperately get to the stone stele and put his points in.

Blood dripped down his legs, staining the ground, but he kept going and finally smashed his palm on the stele.

His forehead burned with an intense heat, and the red dots flew out of him like a river. They all rushed through the stele, climbing the steps at an impressive speed.

It easily passed the ten thousand and hundred thousand ranks, climbing up into the millions, which it then rushed through until…

The eight multiplier.

He’d gotten somewhere between eight and nine million points, but clearly on the lower end of the scale.

So as a result, he could only be satisfied with second place.

[2. Hwei Badai: 8 000 000 Points.]

Seeing that, he let out a bitter laugh and collapsed.

Some helpful people dragged him away, covering themselves in blood as well, but it was better than letting it dirty the entire platform.

Some of them questioned if he would even live. But since he was breathing and didn’t bleed… Well, he did bleed a lot, but for a cultivator it wasn’t quite enough to do him in.

Resting under the trees, everyone waited patiently, or impatiently for some, for time to tick down. None of them had any hope that Fa Xiaolan would make it in time.

“That monstrous woman probably got herself killed being too arrogant.” Qi Rian said, biting down on meat. It was one of the clan members of the Rock Point Clan.

“Oh, shut up, you’re just afraid of her.” Lian Shu shook his head and looked toward the horizon, his eyes still having some hope.

“So what? Did you see how easily she trampled on everyone? With that kind of strength, it’s obvious she’s going to get first place if she survives!”

“Will you two keep it quiet? Some of us are preparing for the next round.” Shuzhai Shu of the Scholar Domination Sect said, half-opening an eye and awakening from his meditation.

“Sorry, sorry.” Qi Rian apologised and kept munching hungrily on the meat, his appetite seemingly unquenchable.

The realm announced that there were less than five minutes left for all contestants to submit their scores.

As time passed, more and more people forgot about the idea of Fa Xiaolan making it. Many even got ready to leave for the second realm slightly ahead of time.

Yet, even as they called it a day, a disturbance in the forest awakened even those that were deep in meditation.

Through the thick bushes, one could see a figure slowly making its way forward, with what looked to be deep wounds leaving a thick trail of blood behind.

As the figure stepped into the light, it was clear that she was the person everyone was thinking about.

The monstrous woman, Fa Xiaolan!
