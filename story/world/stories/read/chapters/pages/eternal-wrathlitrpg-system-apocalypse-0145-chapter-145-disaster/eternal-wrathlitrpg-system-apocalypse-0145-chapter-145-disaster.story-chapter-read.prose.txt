“Huh? James! That town is on fire!” Kouki shouted as he flew with his dragon. He and James sped up, landing before the gate guard.

“What happened?” James dismounted before the gate guard. His mind raced toward Andrew and his team, who were assigned here. How could Andrew let this happen?

“Someone made a mistake and lit the granary on fire two days ago. The fire spread, displacing about fifty houses. The fire was extinguished two hours ago, but to be honest, we are fucked.”

Horror spread across Kouki’s face. Losing the granary was the absolute worst situation this town could get into. They still had one more week before the next moon came. And three days after that. Alas, James, who was detached from the dungeon’s food source, only gave them a glance.

“Was everything ruined?” Kouki asked.

“Most of the grain was burned, but the meat and perishables inside the icehouse were safe. We lost about half of the food supply.”

“Can you survive until the next cycle? There is only a week left.”

“We don’t know,” the soldier said. “Anyway, please come in.”

Kouki and James went inside. Unlike Maruk’s village, the atmosphere was more depressing than a graveyard. People walked around with miserable expressions. The tension was palpable; even James could feel it would soon boil over.

There, Andrew sat lifeless in the corner of a street, like a rich CEO who had lost all of his assets in a fire. James remembered that Andrew wasn’t registered with the census, so he was refused every service. However, unlike James, who didn’t have any friends, Andrew should have been able to stay with one of his henchmen like Sanders or Anderson.

“Andrew, you look like shit,” James greeted him.

“James. Yeah, I’ve seen better days.” Andrew sighed. “Did you see the smoke from outside? Someone brought a lantern into the granary, and it ignited when it hit the fumes from the oil stored inside. The flames raged, and we only managed to put it out a few moments ago.” Andrew looked at him, desperation clouding his face, hoping James could somehow grant him salvation.

“Someone decided overtime was a good option, huh?”

“It’s rather hard to miss such a spectacle. So, who fucked up? Did you find the culprit?”

“Yeah, quite a bit, actually. Anderson was one of them. But he couldn’t be the culprit because he was further away, yet the council didn’t believe him.”

“So, how fucked are you? I heard from the guard that you lost half your food supply.”

“By our estimates, half of the population won’t get anything to eat for the rest of the journey, until the next cycle. Bartholomew’s scribe told us just minutes ago.”

“Well, you’ve got that Four-Eyes skill, right? He can buy food from the shop.” James referred to Anderson as Four-Eyes.

“Ugh… There must be a way to help you. Is there anything we can help you with?” Kouki said.

“Well, the problem is Anderson is one of the people who are going to be executed. He was on the scene with ten other people. They will all be executed come tomorrow, even though he was not the one who made the mistake. Damn it,” Andrew cursed.

“That’s unfair! If Anderson was not at fault, why was he punished?”

“None of the people were willing to acknowledge the mistake, so the council decided to punish everyone,” Andrew said. “I fought hard, but they were unmoved in their decision. Unless someone comes forward and admits their mistake, Anderson’s punishment will not be any lighter.”

“Damn…” James cursed along with him. He thought hard with Andrew. He actually had a plan—a draconian one, sure—but a plan nevertheless.

“If all you need is a culprit, can’t you ask someone to be one? Were all the culprits natives? Or is there anyone who is a member of your team?” James asked. It was rough, but the needs of the many outweighed the needs of the few. That was why James refused to cooperate with others. Sometimes, he would be the one left out.

“Shit! Is that the only choice?” Andrew cursed.

“We will find another way! If not, we can always help Anderson flee the town,” Kouki said.

“Hm… Unlike me, Anderson couldn’t survive on monsters alone. I can store biomass with my skills. Unless he has a way to get coins, he is going to starve. And the only reliable ways to obtain coins in this dungeon are by taking quests, right?” James frowned.

“Yes… I have to persuade at least one guy to give up his life. Fucking hell! Who should I choose? Silas, Hain, or Robert? No, I couldn’t possibly ask this. None of them are the type to willingly surrender their lives, even to save others,” Andrew cursed. This was different from risking your life battling monsters. He was asking people to go on a suicide mission—taking the blame for something that wasn’t even theirs.

“Well, I think Anderson could just live in the wilds, you know,” James said.

“Huh? What do you mean? If you go outside during a full moon, you will die.”

“But the moon only lasts three days. He was a soldier, right? Surely he received training to survive in the wilds. Then, for the last three days, he could just sleep on Maruk Town’s streets like me, while bringing something like smoked meat for rations,” James said.

“…Anderson was a marine, not a soldier. Well… he should have survival skills.” Andrew turned to James. “Yeah. I will try. Let’s go to the Census Bureau. That’s where they keep the prisoners.”

James looked over at the Census Bureau. This building and the one in Maruk’s town functioned the same, but this one was luxurious. The walls were adorned with marble, its floor with granite. The building practically gleamed under the red moonlight.

James would have admired the majesty of this building, but since the moon was red, it gleamed under a sinister light. The building looked as if it had been painted with fresh blood.

Under normal conditions, James didn’t want to have anything to do with this building. But this time, the fight was on Andrew. Andrew went to the receptionist, preparing to meet one of the prisoners.

“Huh? I couldn’t meet with the prisoners? Why? They were supposed to have their last rites! It is their right!” Andrew exclaimed.

“The council has decided to hasten the execution. It will not happen under the moonlight, but inside the dungeon tomorrow,” the receptionist said.

“Why? Why kill all of them? They might be innocent!” Andrew said.

“It was Lord Bartholomew and his advisor’s decision, not mine. Anyway, all meetings with the prisoners will be denied.”

“This is bullshit! You violated the law you created yourself!” Andrew said. His accusation fell on deaf ears. Even when he reached higher up the management chain, the answer was the same. He couldn’t meet with the prisoners no matter what.

“No. We couldn’t lose Anderson right now. I have to save him, even if I have to break him out of prison.” Andrew cradled his head. Even if Andrew were to register right now, he wouldn’t get many jobs and would still suffer from hunger.

Humans could survive without eating for ten days, but that would leave them helpless. They would essentially be in a coma. Andrew knew this, so he was visibly distressed.

“Andrew, don’t be rash. There must be something we can do.”

“No, Anderson is vital. If everyone pooled their coins for him, Anderson could buy food for all of them. No matter what, Anderson must survive,” Andrew said. “In the worst case, I could migrate all five hundred people to Maruk on the last day before the moonlight. Then ask everyone to transfer coins to him and buy food for the last three days.”

“Alright. I am coming with you,” James said.

“Really?”

“I always hated the government. Might as well turn into a terrorist once in a while.”

“…Thanks, James. I owe you one.”

“You owe me more than once, Andrew. Remember that. Get Sanders here. Let’s attack the building now.”

“No. It would be better to do it alone. That way, there would be fewer victims,” Andrew said, his expression grave.

“Victims?” James asked. It was rare for Andrew to want to do things alone. James thought Andrew was a team player. His power lay in commanding a team.

“Sorry. I mean criminals… What we will do was essentially be a crime. James and Kouki are outsiders, so the law shouldn’t apply to you. But I will essentially be joining Anderson to migrate to Maruk’s city,” Andrew said.

“Hmm? But you and Sanders were unregistered as well, right?” James followed Andrew’s logic.

“I expect us to be denied entry to this town ever again. Just because we are outsiders and unregistered doesn’t mean there is no law for us. I will tell Sanders to register himself in this city,” Andrew said, thinking out loud.

“Ah… you want to leave Sanders as a spy in this city.” James realized Andrew’s goal.

“Hahaha. You are putting my words in a bad way, James. But yes. I need men to manage things here while we are gone.”

“There are over 500 people in the city… Do you need Sanders?” James said.

“Those 500 people will be joining us in Maruk for temporary shelter, remember? But I will leave Sanders here. If I ask Anderson to buy several MRE, he should be able to survive here in the upcoming week.”

James had a bad feeling about this. But there was no better plan, so he agreed.

Even though there was only the moon hanging in the sky, there was still night and day in this dungeon. The intensity of the light emanating from the moon was different during that time. But despite all the intricacies, there was no difference. James found no difference in the behavior of the locals whether it was day or night.

“Huh? Andrew, are you crazy? You are attacking from the front door?”

“I don’t know another way. When I talked to Anderson last time, we met there, in a room in the basement.” Andrew pointed toward the Census Bureau.

“Wow. You are more reckless than I thought. I like it.” James dismounted from his raptor and shouldered his axe, sword, and bow. “Let’s begin. Time for mind magic to do the trick.”

“Must have gotten that from you, James. Let’s go,” Andrew said, his face resolute as he brought his spear and charged the front gate.

“Let’s sneak right in, James. I am counting on you,” Andrew said.
