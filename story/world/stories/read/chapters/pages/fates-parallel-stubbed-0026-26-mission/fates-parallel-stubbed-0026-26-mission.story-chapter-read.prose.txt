Lee Jia slowly stirred awake to the sound of Ienaga’s authoritative voice. Her head was propped up on something soft and a pleasant warmth was spreading throughout her body.

“—techniques are coming along nicely, but you’re all too focused on single combat. Kim, I know habits are hard to break, but you’re training here as a martial artist—you rely too heavily on your magic. When cornered by a close combat specialist, your talismans are not the first thing you should be going to.

“Seong, I know you can get by on mana sense, but completely abandoning an entire sense is foolhardy in a battle—stop fighting with your eyes closed. I know you consider your ancestral technique to be off limits, but either get it under control or get over it.

“Takeda, your combination of illusions and telekinesis is clever, but you’ve got a long way to go before your doubles are convincing. It’s a good goal, but for now work on something simpler to pull off than full-on doppelgangers.

“An, you’ve come a long way, but I don’t think that technique is combat ready yet. As powerful as they are, four attacks is too few before you’re forced to back down. Even with your healing, the recovery period is too long for too small of an impact on a fight.”

“Yes Master Ienaga!”

As the addressed students chorused their acknowledgement, Jia realized that her head was in Eui’s lap, and the warm feeling was her healing. She had only just started learning how to heal others, but her limited mastery was enough for Jia’s limited injuries. As terrifying as it was to think, Hayakawa had been holding back. Eui had noticed her waking up and poked Jia in the cheek with her tail.

“Welcome back to the land of the living, Jia. Sorry I went down so fast, I don’t think she wanted to let me charge one of my attacks.”

Jia sighed as she sat up, her head was throbbing painfully as she rubbed it.

“Eh...we always knew Hayakawa was going to ruin us. I think we did ok, given the circumstances.”

Jia heard Miyata scoffing as he overheard her, but he went down before anyone else did by throwing himself in the way of a predictable feint aimed at Hayakawa. She didn’t think he had much cause to be prideful.

“You all fought well, but as I already said you’re too focused on individuals. Especially you, Hayakawa. Now that Lee is awake, further critiques can wait, though. I have an announcement to make.”

Ienaga glanced around to make sure she had the full attention of the ten students present. Once the group had grown to ten, it had stopped there. Even after Dae had awakened, he found himself in a separate group—apparently Ienaga liked to keep her classes small.

“There’s an opportunity to gain some practical experience coming up—a mission within the greater academy grounds that needs to be seen to. In theory, it would be trivially easy for me or the other instructors to resolve it, but we agreed that it was an excellent chance to test your progress and give you some real-world experience.

“There will be a full announcement tomorrow at the central courtyard with details. For now, just know that this is not an open invitation. Each instructor will be nominating a handful of students who will then be given the chance to accept or decline. As my first advanced group, while you’re not all my best students, you are my best group. Teamwork and cohesion is just as important as individual strength, so I’ll be nominating you all.”

Lee Jia looked around at the group present—There were the Yamato students, Hayakawa, Miyata, Satou, Kasai, and Takeda. Representing her homeland of Goryeo were Eui, Eunae, Kim, and herself. And finally, the single Qin martial artist in the class—perhaps in the entire school—was Guan Yi.

It was an odd grouping, with Lee Jia and Takeda Rika’s friendship bridging the two distinct social circles together. Kim Yongsun was a bit of an odd man out, and something of a loner. He didn’t talk much and kept to himself, but participated as enthusiastically as anyone else in the training and sparring matches.

Still, even with the slightly standoffish students like Kim, Hayakawa, and Miyata, Lee Jia considered this group to be her true peers in the academy. Qin kept her isolated from his other students like he was ashamed of her—which he probably was—and Hwang’s lessons were so large that it felt far too impersonal. To this day, the only friend she’d made from Hwang’s classes was Dae.

Ienaga’s voice snapped Lee Jia out of her reverie.

“For now, consider yourselves dismissed. If you have extracurricular plans, I suggest you cancel them and go home to rest. We’ll meet tomorrow at noon in the central courtyard. If you have classes scheduled, those are cancelled.”

Lee Jia wondered what this mysterious mission would entail. Between the regular sparring sessions and the continuing novelty of the world of cultivation, it wasn’t like she lacked for excitement. Still, she was positively vibrating with anticipation for a welcome break from routine.

Jia, Eui, Takeda, and Eunae were gathered together for tea in the latter pair’s larger house. It had taken some convincing, but Eui had finally been convinced to join them, though she sat between Jia and Takeda while studiously ignoring Eunae. Paradoxically, Eui and Takeda had bonded somewhat over the time Eui stabbed her, and they were even on a given name basis.

“Any idea what this ‘mission’ is going to be?”

It was an obvious topic, but Jia was excited about it and wanted to hear the others’ thoughts on it. Eunae was the first to offer a response.

“I’ve heard a few people talking about it. I think Qin and Hwang have already made their nominations as well. I can’t say anything certain, but it should take place outside the walls, yet within the formation—most likely towards the mountain to the north. It will be a group expedition, so we should start thinking about allies now.”

As always, Eunae’s information network was impressive. Despite her claims of loneliness and a lack of friends, Eunae probably knew more individuals by name than anyone else in the entire school. She kept her metaphorical ear to the ground.

Jia put a finger on her chin and tilted her head in thought.

“Hmm, well Dae was probably nominated by Hwang…”

“He was.”

Jia nodded at Eunae’s confirmation and continued.

“I can probably get him on our side.”

Eui scoffed.

“Tch, of course you can. Puppy boy is hopelessly in puppy love.”

Jia flushed bright red as she protested.

“He is not! We’re just friends, I keep telling you that!”

Eunae shook her head and smiled ruefully.

“I hate to say it, Jia, but Eui’s right. Hyeong Daesung’s infatuation with you is pretty much common knowledge. Among those who keep track of such things, you’re considered to be one of his biggest weaknesses.”

Takeda nodded along in agreement with Eunae’s analysis. Traitors! Jia was practically glowing as she buried her face in her hands. She desperately tried to change the topic, cutting through Eui’s malicious cackling.

“Anyway! If Xin Wei was nominated we should try to get him as well. I don’t like him, but he knows me and Guan Yi goes where he does. The fact that we’re friends with Guan is another point in our favor. He’d never admit it but Xin is pretty easily influenced by Guan’s opinions.”

Eunae nodded appreciatively.

“Good. I think we can count on Kim Yongsung’s support as well. Our families know each other and are allies. We’re both black sheep in our families and he’s a bit odd, but I think he’ll be inclined to conform to our family politics.”

“What about you, Takeda? Any friends from home you can rope in?”

Takeda scratched her cheek and grimaced slightly in embarrassment.

“Eh...no. Probably not. Hayakawa’s like a mini-Ienaga. She’s the daughter of Shogun Ienaga’s chief rival. People compare her to Master Ienaga a lot, and favorably. She’s more talented now than Master Ienaga was in her youth, and people think that when the current Shogunate falls, it will be to the Hayakawa clan. Long story short, her status is through the roof, and anyone from my country with half a brain is going to be following her.”

Eui snickered and poked Takeda in the side.

“What does that say about you, Rika?”

“Shut up!”

Rika slapped Eui in the shoulder and Jia felt a small twinge of jealousy at the casual display of affection. She ignored it and moved on with the conversation.

“Is there anything else we can do to prepare? Eunae, are you sure you don’t want one of my manuals? Takeda’s done pretty well with Valley of Illusory Mists.”

Eunae shook her head, declining Jia’s offer as usual.

“It’s kind of you to offer, but I have no way to repay the favor. Besides, my newfound connection to my qi has led me to make a few insights towards my ancestral technique and I’ve had a breakthrough on it recently. My powers aren’t appropriate for friendly sparring matches, but against beasts and spirits I may finally have a chance to show you what I’m made of!”

Jia mentally noted that Eui’s powers weren’t too friendly either, but knew better than to pick at Eunae’s insecurities.

“I guess we’ll find out tomorrow!”

The girls spent the rest of the evening making small talk and speculating excitedly about what was in store for them.

The central courtyard was busy in a way that reminded Lee Jia of the initiation ceremony, though the crowd was much smaller. Rather than the two hundred or so students that had crowded the courtyard back then, today there were only around thirty. The extra elbow room made the delineation of the various cliques a lot more obvious.

Predictably, the students from each nation generally favored their own countrymen. As Takeda had predicted, most of the Yamato students had flocked around Hayakawa Kaeda, including Miyata, Kasai, and Satou, as well as a few unfamiliar faces that Lee Jia could only assume were nominated by Hwang. And every single Qin student—nearly half of everyone gathered here—were standing together in a loose formation with Yan Zhihao at the fore.

The remaining group wasn’t much of a group at all. Jia, Eui, Eunae, and Takeda had all arrived together, having walked as a group from the dorms. Aside from them, the only remaining members of the gathering were Kim Yongsun and Hyeong Daesung, who stood at an awkward remove from both the major cliques and each other. Dae’s eyes lit up as he spotted Lee Jia’s group approaching.

“Ah! Miss Lee, I thought I’d find you here. Good to see you—er, and uh, the rest of you as well, hehe.”

Dae blushed and scratched the back of his head as he awkwardly greeted the group of girls. Lee Jia noticed Kim Yongsun quietly edging closer to the group and noted wryly that, aside from Takeda, they had essentially formed their own Goryeon clique.

“Hey Dae, good to see you too. Is this everyone? I would have expected more mages from Hwang’s group.”

Dae looked around and shrugged.

“Oh, he nominated mages. Just most of them have already fallen in with their countrymen. If you’re subtly trying to imply half-spirits, then I was the only one nominated by Magus Hwang.”

Lee Jia blushed. She hadn't consciously meant to imply that, but belatedly realized she had been thinking it.

“That’s kind of surprising, to be honest. Not that Ienaga was particularly nationalistic with her picks either just…”

“Qin obviously was, yes.”

Dae nodded in understanding.

“Though I should mention that it’s not unusual that I’d be the only pure mage nominated. Mages are typically rather hopeless in combat until the second stage. Your duel against Yan Zhihao was something of an outlier, and even then you made judicial use of your martial arts.”

Lee Jia raised an eyebrow at Dae curiously.

“Pure mage? I thought you were fully awakened already.”

Dae sighed and shook his head ruefully.

“Nominally, yes. I’m pretty terrible at martial arts so far, and I’ve yet to make any progress on that spiritual technique you loaned me.”

Lee Jia frowned at that. She had lent him the Domain of the Earthen Realm technique at his request and he had given it back after transcribing it. Was he going to be ok? She couldn’t imagine trying to fight with only magic. Their conversation was interrupted by the appearance of the instructors commanding everyone’s attention.

“Welcome, everyone! You’ve each been selected from among your peers as the most advanced and capable students in order to take part in an extracurricular opportunity to gain some practical experience!”

Magus Hwang addressed the group with his usual exuberance.

“As promised, I will now explain the details. As some of you may know, the shield formation protecting our campus also doubles as a mana collector. This, in addition to our location on a natural convergence of leylines, means that the campus and its surroundings have a significantly higher than normal density of mana.

“This was done to improve the efficiency of cultivation in all three disciplines, but it has some side effects. The increased mana density leads to a greater incidence of magical beasts and elementals, and also attracts spirits—which the formation is unable to keep out due to its nature as a mana collector.”

There was a brief susurration in the crowd, but it was quickly silenced by Elder Qin.

“Silence! Pay attention. All this is to say that the population of beasts has grown too much, and must be culled.”

Magus Hwang nodded appreciatively at his colleague and continued.

“Indeed! If left unchecked the beasts may grow to stage three or above. Culling them will be a good opportunity for you all to gain practical combat experience and resources. Additionally, something seems to be attracting the beasts toward the northern mountain, where the population has become especially dense.

“There could be any number of reasons for this, from a spirit trying to enthrall them to some undiscovered ruin within the mountains housing a powerful artifact. This leyline nexus has existed for millenia, and we’re surely not the first people to build on it. Your secondary objective will be to discover what is causing them to concentrate in the north.”

Magus Hwang stepped back, apparently finished speaking, and was quickly replaced by Ienaga, who smoothly took over.

“You’re being given complete discretion over how you conduct this task. However, it is recommended that you work in groups. Who you decide to work with and how your group functions is entirely up to you, but going solo is not recommended. Beasts are dangerous and they will not hesitate to kill you. Your safety is absolutely not guaranteed, and participation is strictly optional.”

Ienaga finished speaking before stepping aside to make way for Elder Qin.

“We do not believe that any elementals or beasts exceeding the third rank will be present. A third rank beast is a dangerous but manageable threat for cultivators of your caliber. They are strong, but mindless. Elementals are more dangerous, should you encounter one in the third rank, take caution—they are much smarter than their beast counterparts.

“Finally, should you encounter a creature that you believe to be in the xiantian ranks, or any spirit, you should retreat with all haste and report back to us. Any spirit you encounter may have arrived from outside the formation, and there is no way to estimate their potential threat. Xiantian creatures are unlikely, but may have been laying dormant before the formation was erected. In either case, you would court death by engaging them at your level.”

Finally, with Elder Qin having said his piece, Hwang stepped up once more.

“You have one week to thin the population as much as you can and complete the secondary objective. In addition to any resources you find, there will be a special reward for whichever group defeats the most monsters and another for the first to discover what is attracting them toward the mountain. Good luck!”

With their announcement complete, the instructors left as abruptly as they had appeared, leaving the students to figure out the rest on their own. As the students began to chatter excitedly among themselves, Lee Jia scanned the crowd intently before spotting her target.

Guan Yi was hard to miss, standing a head and shoulders above the disciples around him, and of course Xin Wei was there right next to him. Yan Zhihao already had the largest group here by far. Surely it wouldn’t be unfair to poach one or two allies? Lee Jia put on her sweetest smile as she approached what were technically her oldest acquaintances in the entire academy.

It was time to form a team.
