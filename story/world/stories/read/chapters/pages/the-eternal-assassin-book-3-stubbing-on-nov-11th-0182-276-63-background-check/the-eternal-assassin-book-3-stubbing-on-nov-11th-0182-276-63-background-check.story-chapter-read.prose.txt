Kroult hadn’t moved so much as a single undead muscle in the time the four of them came to a decision. The lich was still kneeling by the fire, his own flaming eyes dim as he stared at seemingly nothing at all. His bony fingers were still resting on the side of the soulsteel sword thrust through his chest, but he made no move to remove the blade. Asher half wondered if he’d somehow died for real, before the green flames of his eyes flickered slightly as they all walked back into view.

“I’ll keep this simple,” Asher said, crouching down in front of the weary lich as he chose his words carefully. “My team and I are currently hunting down the Council of Death, and we believe you would be a valuable asset in doing so. Even if your vengeance has burned out as you claim, we still want your magic on our side. In exchange, once we’ve finished them off, I will grant you the eternal rest you so desire. I have a skill that makes my attacks permanent. I’ve already killed two liches with it, so I know that it works. You help us finish off the people who sealed you in that tomb for the last six hundred years, and I will help you pass on, regardless of where your phylactery is. Deal?”

Kroult looked up, his surprise somehow coming through despite his blank face as he returned Asher’s stare. “...You have a skill so powerful? You are not lying?”

“You’ll see it in action for yourself before long,” Moxy said, grinning down at the lich. “I think it’s kind of pathetic that you want to die, but yeah, the Runt can help.”

“Moxy,” Rulfar frowned, shaking his head before turning to the lich. “Yes, we can all attest to the power of Asher’s skill. And we really could use your magic to aid us in our fight.”

“My magic is not like that of the other liches',” Kroult explained, his head lowering a tad as if afraid they would rescind the offer of bringing him the peace he desired once they heard the truth. “I told you, it was my master who was talented, not me. I do not even understand the ritual that transformed me into what I am today. What I learned I only achieved through decades of hard work and study. I have a number of minor boons I am capable of casting, but none of them are very powerful.”

“Still, that’s an edge we could use in our upcoming battles,” Asher said. “Come on, I know you said you’re tired, but surely there’s at least a little bit of that spark left you felt upon me freeing you. You really don’t want to see those bastards dealt with for what they put you through?”

Kroult sat there on the ground, silent as he thought. His frail, undead fingers slowly trailed along his chest, gently brushing over the countless holes that were still left in his flesh from the hundreds of spikes that had been embedded into him to prevent him from using his magic. Asher didn’t know how exactly an undead went about healing their dead flesh, but he had a feeling it wasn’t going to be a simple process.

To Asher’s surprise, it was Samantha who crouched down next. The hunter moved as if to lay a gentle hand on the lich’s shoulder before hesitating and thinking better of it. Even so, her eyes had none of their former hatred or desire to see Kroult destroyed for what he was. Instead, she looked upon the crestfallen lich with sadness.

“I was the first one to argue for your destruction,” Samantha admitted, causing the fires of Kroult’s eyes to wink out for a moment as he turned to look at her. “The Council of Death took almost everything from me. Other than my brother and Asher, I lost everything, and I want to see them all suffer for what they did. However… it sounds like they took everything from you as well,” she admitted, giving Kroult a small, pained smile. “They took your master and the one item you treasured above all else, and then they took your freedom. I’ve been trying to wrap my head around the concept of being trapped within a metal coffin hidden deep beneath the earth, unable to so much as twitch for century after century. To be honest, I’m struggling to comprehend just how horrible that had to be, or how you remained sane after all that time. You have more reason to hate the Council than any of us. I know you’re tired, but it sounds like before the Council sealed you away, you lived your life by wandering the world and helping people with your magic. Well. Now you have the chance to do that one last time before your eternal rest. We need your help, Kroult, and you need ours. A deal for a deal, just like what you used to do. What do you say?”

Something about Samantha’s offer seemed to spark new life into the undead lich as the fires blazed within his eyes at the reminder of who he used to be. Ever so slowly, he nodded, his voice growing a bit stronger with every syllable as he spoke.

“Yes… A deal for a deal… Some of my magic in exchange for your assistance, just like the old days. I think I would like to do something like that one last time before truly passing on. If only to honor the way my master taught me how to live.”

Shaking slightly, the lich pushed himself off the ground, forcing his withered body to its feet as he stood tall before the four of them. Despite the soulsteel blade still stabbed through his chest and the horrific state his body was in, he struck a strangely powerful visage as he looked between all of them.

“I will help you take down the Council of Death. No one else should have to suffer as I, or as you, have been made to. Though once we are done… I will have you complete your end of the bargain. Do we have a deal?”

“The moment the last member of the Council falls, I’ll help you pass on,” Asher nodded, finding it more than a little strange that he was currently agreeing to kill someone in order to obtain their assistance. “Though all of this is contingent on us verifying that what you’ve told us is true. No offence, but we can’t just take your word at face value. Seeing as you’re a lich and all.”

“Do what you must. I have told no lies,” Kroult said, wobbling slightly in place and nearly tipping over before Rulfar reached out and caught him.

“You know, if we’re seriously going to be working with our own bag of bones, we should probably take that sword out of his chest and let him patch himself up a bit,” Moxy pointed out. “He’s not looking so hot. Even for a dead guy.”

“Verification first, then we worry about that,” Asher said, grabbing Kroult by the shoulder and pulling him back into his rift. At the same time, he pushed a sliver of energy into the lich as he placed an Eternal Mark on him, ensuring that even if this was all some incredibly elaborate ruse and the lich managed to give them the slip, Asher would be able to find him again. Nothing short of a Void element could remove one of his marks, and the only person in the entire world with such a power was currently hiding within the void realm itself.

Getting to Zygrul and finishing it off wasn’t going to be easy, but at least they had plenty of other liches to worry about in the meantime.

With their decision made, Asher pulled the rest of the team into his rift as well before focusing on another one of his Eternal Marks and triggering Recall. The world shifted around him as he teleported hundreds of miles in an instant, and suddenly, he was standing within his and Samantha’s bedroom in the royal palace.

The room was just as opulent and tidy as they’d left it, and Asher shook his head at the absurdity of the fact that he was functionally living like a king when he wasn't busy running around killing bad people. No matter how many times he returned to the royal palace, the fact still shocked him just as much as it did the first time Donvath offered him a room within its walls.

After letting the rest of the team out of his rift to rest up from their hours spent battling against the undead roaming the Deadlands, Asher went straight to Donvath, spotting a familiar face standing firm outside the High Prince’s office.

“Hey, Lily,” he smiled, waving at the royal guard. Lily had been the one to find him after his first victory against one of the greater demons. While he would have recovered on his own from being torn in half eventually, it had been her powerful healing skill that had patched him up in moments. “It’s pretty late. Is Donvath still up?”

“He tends to work late into the night,” Lily nodded, barely sparing him a glance as her eyes continued sweeping the corridor, as if an assassin lying in wait had been biding their time for this exact moment. Asher thought it a touch ironic, both since he was probably the deadliest assassin she’d ever laid eyes on, and because Donvath was functionally immortal. While the High Prince could technically be killed, his Timeline skill had gained an automatic trigger upon being evolved to the third tier. If he died for any reason, time would rewind itself to ensure that never happened.

The Time element was ridiculous.

“I need to chat with him,” Asher said with a shrug. “Time sensitive, can’t wait until morning.”

“Just try not to keep him up too late. He needs to get some actual rest every once in a while,” Lily said, opening the door for him and motioning him on through.

As per usual, the High Prince’s office was a cluttered mess. Stacks of papers filled with minuscule script threatened to fall off the desk at the slightest of touches, and an empty potion bottle lay discarded on the floor, no doubt having once contained this world’s version of alchemical super coffee. Donvath barely even glanced up from his scribbling as Asher walked in, grunting as he went right back to work.

“Asher. Good work on the Deadlands. Exciting stuff.”

“You were able to see that?” Asher asked, surprised at the revelation. Donvath had made it quite clear that events involving liches, greater demons, and Asher himself were always hazy and difficult to make out. Most of the time Donvath didn’t have any idea what had happened until Asher told him about it after the fact.

“Nope,” Donvath chuckled, finally putting a paper aside as he rubbed at his eyes and grinned. “The fall of the Deadlands swept through the royal circuits like wildfire. Every noble across the continent with a sending stone is already aware that the Deadlands are no more. And the real question on everyone’s mind is, who is responsible?”

“They’ll know soon enough,” Asher sighed. “I like to keep a low profile, but in order to claim Nunait’s five-million-shard bounty they had in place for the Deadlands’ eradication, I have to go public. Anyone who doesn’t already know about The Reaper is about to.”

“Eh, I can think of worse things to be known for,” Donvath said, pointing his quill at him. “This will be good for your reputation. Before now, everyone who’s heard of The Reaper only thought of you as a master assassin. Nothing like reclaiming thousands of square miles of workable farmland to bolster your reputation with the common man.”

“I suppose,” Asher said, deciding to get straight to his reason for coming. “Donvath… I need a favor. I didn’t just manage to kill another one of the liches making up the Council. I found a third. Only, he’s not like the others. He didn’t even want to become a lich in the first place, and he spent the last few centuries sealed within a metal coffin, buried deep underground. He’s agreed to help us take down the rest of the Council, but I need your help verifying what he told us. Or more accurately, I need to borrow one of your truthseekers.”

“Wow. That’s one hell of a claim,” Donvath said, not looking all that surprised as he started gnawing on the end of his quill. “Still, the enemy of my enemy and all that. Can’t say I’m against the idea. I’d certainly be pretty pissed after escaping such a prison. Alright, I’ll help you make sure you’re not being played. Though what are you going to do once you don’t need his help anymore? I assume you’ve thought that far ahead.”

“He actually wants to die,” Asher admitted. “Once the Council is no more, I’ll help him with that.”

“Simple and sweet,” Donvath grinned, tossing his chewed-up quill onto his desk as he got to his feet. “Good enough for me. You look absolutely awful, by the way. Go get some rest. And a shower. Leave the lich with me and I’ll put him through the most rigorous background check of his unlife.”

“You sure you don’t want my help?” Asher asked. “He might be dangerous.”

“Asher, you just gave me a reason to avoid doing paperwork for a few hours,” Donvath said, giving him a threatening look. “If you try and take this away from me, I’ll order the servants to put a severed fish head under your covers and tell Samantha it was your idea.”
