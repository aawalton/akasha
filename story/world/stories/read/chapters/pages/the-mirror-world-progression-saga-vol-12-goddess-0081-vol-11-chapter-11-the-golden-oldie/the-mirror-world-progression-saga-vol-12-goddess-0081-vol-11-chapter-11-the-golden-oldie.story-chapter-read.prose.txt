As Samantha waited without speaking, Azure glanced uncertainly between the serpentine figure on top of the dais and her lover, before clearing her throat and stepping forward slightly. “Yes, um, hello, Goldie. Thank you for helping with that other thing, as well. Samantha pulled me out of a situation I suppose I would’ve gotten… stuck in.” Goldie only smiled and nodded down patiently to this, so Azure continued, “I was told to come here when I’d come to the end of my explorations into my past, but still had holes in exactly what happened.” She paused uncomfortably. “Were… you the one to have me broken out? How were you involved in what happened to me?”

Goldie maintained her usual, mild smile as she studied Azure. “My specialty is normally of the mind, to which has delivered me a swan song of running and fading ignobly, to avoid a worse fate for creation, which I knew I would play a part even toward the end, whether bitter, sweet, or in between. The Sage of Man hunted us down like no other, you see? Which should explain much about how clandestine we’ve needed to be in our dealings.”

Azure’s eyes widened. “Mind specialist… that’s right…” Of course! This is perfect! She suddenly gestured toward Samantha. “Can you help her, then? She had extensive damage caused by-”

“Azure, please,” Samantha interrupted with a yell-whisper through her teeth, brows rising insistently. “This is about you!”

Goldie suddenly laughed and touched her cheeks with two hands, turning them side to side as she made pleased noises. “Ohh, but how adorable you two are! How blessed am I to behold something so heartwarming as the selflessness of lovers to each other! I will cherish this moment. Ahem!” She lowered her hands and looked more seriously from Azure to Samantha. “The Goddess is how she wants to be, and tremendously stable. I will not interfere. I fear I’d not be able to handle her case, as a blurry-eyed old potter might not be confident in putting back together a shattered vase. My skills, you see… they’ve atrophied. She’s done a better job in her adaptation than could possibly be hoped for in restorative efforts.” Goldie looked to Azure, and her face became one of sad sympathy. “I’m sorry, dear.”

Azure was frowning and nodding reluctantly when a realization dawned. Eyes slowly widening, she stared up at the nagi for many long moments, processing all of those details, as Goldie waited, a knowing expression painted on her countenance. “You… you put… me back together?” Her voice cracked to her own ears. “It has to be true, right? Your specialty. I-I was…” She trailed off, swallowing a lump in her throat.

I was all but annihilated. Aeons of degradation… reduced to nothing. I know it already. I feel it in my bones.

Goldie’s face was solemn now, as she made two prayer signs with four arms, and bowed her head slightly. “Not much comes back to me, but this I know: you were brought to my doorstep as little more than a depleted husk, deprived and starving of identity. Under the blight of the Sage’s destruction, one cannot be simply preserved and expect to retain memory. This is much worse for one tied to physicality, as opposed to a pure spirit. Active efforts are required to reinforce elements of one’s persona and history, etching it into the structure of the mind. Moreover, I think you were drained and experimented upon even further, effectively the opposites of support, reinforcement, and preservation. If I had to guess, he tested the limits of his existing spellcraft on a strong will, and the various ways to help or hinder it.”

Goldie sighed and seemed to deflate down into her cushion. “I’m left with little more than vague principles to explain to you directly. I see the concepts and reasonings, feel through my intuitions, and little else. I retain only scarce flashes of our interactions, as my own depletion is accelerated and nearly complete. Again, I’m sorry.”

Azure hung on every word, and each terrified her more than the last. Somewhere deep within her soul, hooks were caught, pulling, tearing at the hollows of truth within. Though the memory wasn’t there, she knew these weren’t mere serpentine lies. Taking a deep breath, Azure opened her mouth. “So, wh-”

“But!” Goldie declared with an echo, a bejeweled hand thrust out flat. “I made a provision.” She thrust out another hand, which appeared to have a dark, gleaming coin within it. “Tricky, but I managed to sequester memories and impressions and other things in a place, by… er, well, somehow.” She looked over at the coin in her hand as if surprised to see it. “And this token will take you there.” The words sounded somehow both certain and uncertain at once. Goldie blinked, shrugged, and then smiled down at them as she offered her hand. “Approach, both of you! Take it.”

Exchanging a glance with Samantha, they both began up the pyramid, not stepping but simply floating in the manner of the spirit world they were familiar with. This was a specific realm, but the rules seemed to hold for them.

The nagi was still looming over them as they approached closely. Azure gravitated toward the token, which Goldie offered to her freely. It was something like obsidian, with a very basic imprinted image of a cairn or tomb entrance on it.

Azure hesitated before taking it, glancing at the fairly giant entity before her with a measure of wonder. “Regardless of what you’ve forgotten, you likely… fashioned me, correct?”

Goldie tapped a finger on her lip and pondered this. “Rebuilt, dear. My principles err toward a free, second chance in such a case as yours. I don’t dictate, I unpollute! Make way for the possibility, at least, of clarity and purity. As for your physical self, some part of you determined that. You must know you’re a shapechanger.”

Azure blinked. “I… don’t really have extremes of such capabilities.”

Goldie bowed her head as if at an obvious truth. “So you appear to have chosen, dear. That much, I can tell you, is of your own free will.”Azure blinked again. What shapeshifter would give up a power so many would literally kill for?

But she knew the answer even as she asked it within herself. More than anything, she’d longed for identity her entire life. Her prior one had been one to escape from it only into the destructive trap the Changers had waiting. The trap had given her the ability to be anyone, but it had led precisely nowhere in the end. The Monster in the Mirror had been nothing but cursed for it. She’d thought herself preserved from the worst the Changers had within their tower-temple: to obliterate identity entirely, kicking the soul out to make a blank vessel. But she’d actually been the worst, the remnant of one almost but not quite obliterated, carrying mountains of trauma back into the world. Their supreme failure and consequence for arts forbidden with good reason.

She could not bear that power again, not fully. She’d chosen another way.

Azure looked down at the token in Goldie’s hand. With a deep breath, she took it. “Thank you, Goldie.”

“Of course!” Goldie exclaimed with a bright smile. “A shame we couldn’t have met under better circumstances. I feel affection for you! I’ve watched you from afar, hoping to see you make good choices, hoping to see you blossom, feeling that it would be the culmination of my finest, final work, my magnum opus, even as I am unable to fully interface with it. I’ve not been disappointed.”

Azure returned the smile sadly. “I’m glad. In another life, perhaps we’d have been closer.”

Goldie winked. “Perhaps!”

“I have questions, too, Goldie,” Samantha said, and held up her hand, displaying the ring. “Firstly, is this ring, the Ring of the Cheater… is it you? It acts sentient at times. Like right now, maybe. Go ahead, ring: do something.” The figure in the ring did not so much as twitch. Samantha frowned. “Hey, what gives?! Are you shy here, for some ridiculous reason?!” Nothing. Samantha cleared her throat and turned back to Goldie. “So, yeah, um, are you controlling it?”

Goldie had a sober, wise expression on her face as she looked from the ring to Samantha, to the ring, and back one more time. “No.”

Samantha’s mouth fell open. “No? Is that all?”

“Yes.”

“Seriously? You can’t tell me anything more?”

“No.”

Samantha sighed and dropped her hand. “Fine, then. I see oracles can be stubborn about some things.”

“As if you aren’t, Goddess?”

Samantha chuckled faintly. “Touché.”

Goldie smiled, bowed her head, and raised her brows. “Your other question?”

“The last time I was here, you said our cause was fairly hopeless. You said I’d be inevitably defeated by the Sage. Have things changed? Improved? What are our chances now?”

Goldie held her eyes, and her smile grew very wide. “Ohh, you already know, dear! As if you are the same unproven child who came here before? As if you haven’t rejiggered reality to your whims, as if I couldn’t feel all that you’ve done as it shook the foundations of creation? You’re going to float there, staring at me with that glittering, hopeful third eye like a little spirit cat waiting for a treat?! Mmmnf!” She uttered a high-pitched squeal and suddenly bounced up and over on her hindparts, grabbing both Azure and Samantha in her arms and squeezing them into a hug, right into her ample bosom.

The two lovers made muffled, slightly strangled noises.

Goldie squeezed and rocked them side to side, calling, “You two, I just can’t handle it, you’re so humble, so darling, so cuuuute!”

“Please, Goldie!” Samantha managed. “This is a bit- ach! Much!”

“Hey, we’re not- uck!” Azure muttered ineffectually.
