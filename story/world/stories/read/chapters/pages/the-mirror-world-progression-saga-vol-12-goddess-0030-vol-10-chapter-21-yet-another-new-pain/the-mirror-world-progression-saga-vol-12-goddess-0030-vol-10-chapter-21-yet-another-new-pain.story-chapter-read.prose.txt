The lich gestured with his staff at Samantha once more, and this time, it flipped to the last page. The room seemed to lose a little more of its sparse light as a sense of dread permeated. The baleful red eyes behind the shadow seemed to seethe. He hated this, she realized — hated resorting to using it. But not as much as he hated her.

Something unseen and barely felt ignored the immunity field, as there was nothing physical to it whatsoever. It ignored the wound negations just as it ignored armor. It ripped directly at her spirit, like a monstrous, decrepit claw, trying to shred it apart. It was obscenely strong and fueled with absolute hatred, like the last curse of a witch burning at the stake.

Samantha was out of [Alter Fate] uses and hadn’t recharged them. Only the obscene bonus from [Timeless Coalescent] put her in range of surviving this heinous thing. There weren't exactly many other boosts against it. She didn’t know how bad it would be, but she soaked it all the same…

It was pretty bad.

You have been dealt a Spiritual Mortal Wound. Reduced to a Critical Wound due to [Timeless Coalescent]. You are at -2 Effective Levels, or -20% to Effective levels (whichever is worse), for as long as the wound persists.

You are stunned for 10 seconds.

You have blacked out.

The pain stabbed into and gored her essential being; it was among the worst things she’d felt, perhaps even the worst. It was hard to say, considering the things she’d been through. But it was so bad she got out nothing more than a choked-off scream before she went unconscious.

You have been healed completely by User Azure. Resonant impairment and ghost pain give -1 Effective Levels to all actions for the next minute.

She was on the floor, Azure’s face above her, looking down wide-eyed with her hand on Samantha’s chest. It was strange to feel nothing, yet recall the agony she’d just been afflicted with. Her spirit remembered it well. It created a hollow feeling, as if the damage must belong there, and she was in a mirage where it wasn’t. There was expectation and terror to feel it again.

Samantha pushed the nonsense away and forced herself up, floating and flying immediately.

The lich was very near, wielding his staff like… a staff. It was wreathed in black flames, and the book was entirely closed. Just glancing at it, she felt a supreme sense of finality to that. Without spells of its own to cast, the lich was simply swinging its weapon at Deathsting, who’d ignored it thanks to the protective field still being up. Snarling in a frenzy, he was ripping the lich’s shadow-stuff apart with his claws.

“The field is going down now!” Gabriel warned, even as the field could be seen dissipating.

Samantha spun her head around quickly to take stock. Deathsting had already crippled the ape-zombie, which was crawling on the ground. He’d also decapitated the other zombie giant, the big lizard one, at some point when Samantha either wasn’t paying attention or had been unconscious.

The lich wasn’t even paying attention to her, focused on its mortal melee combat with the dark wyvern, who was quickly adjusting to be more defensive and shy away from the nasty-looking staff. Samantha fired three mirror-lasers from her array into the lich’s already damaged shadow of the torso. The first hit and blew more away, and the other two hit his staff-wielding arm and then his leg.

Azure leapt into action, though she could add only two daggers in that span due to her preoccupation and limited speed. She hit once, to rip away everything on the lich’s torso and knock him back slightly. The second dagger was blocked with the staff.

When the lich saw Samantha, his red eyes widened, and he made a furious noise.

Samantha zoomed up almost point-blank to the lich and held out her palm. “Time to retire for good, Consolidator.”

With her Haste action, she blasted him from the neck down with [Spellfyr: Unleash] yet again — but this time, his shadowy protections were gone, leaving his torso unprotected.

When the white bar of annihilation cleared an instant later, there was empty space where the lich’s torso should be, as well as his other arm and one leg. A head, a hand wielding the staff, and a leg dropped to the floor. The shadow completely dissipated, the staff lost its flame and went dead, and the red glow in the Consolidator’s eyes faded away.

Deathsting coughed a contemptuous laugh. “Ha! Good riddance. An honor, to put down such an unnatural beacon, such a cretin.”

“Mmn.” Samantha floated just above the floor, looming right over the remains, her eyes scanning for any sign of animation, not yet capable of believing it was over. She shivered from the resonant ghost pain of the spiritual wound, like claw marks were still there.

She almost jumped at Azure’s comforting hand at the small of her back. “Are you alright?” Azure asked, sympathy and worry painting her features.

Samantha nodded quickly. “Yeah, I-”

There was a long, low moan. They looked over to see the ape zombie, which was staring in forlorn horror at the corpse of the Consolidator.

Deathsting snarled in contempt and rushed over, grabbing the head in one claw and severing the neck with his blade-like tail. The zombie did not resist, or even so much as look at Deathsting. It just kept staring at its former master’s scant remains until its head was severed and tossed aside to roll around on the floor.

“Perhaps open the force wall,” Deathsting suggested, gazing over at the blocked hallway, where another giant undead could still be seen beating on the other side, pressed against it. “We could get rid of the rest of them.”

“I think not,” Samantha said, looking back down at the lich’s remains. “That’s a waste of time. We have what we came for.” She glanced at Azure and back to look at the Consolidator’s head. “It’s interesting… The pain was horrible, but so fleeting. I can’t fully remember it, just the echo. Did he design that in life? If so, he was more merciful than he could’ve been.”

Azure’s eyes flickered. She frowned slightly and met Samantha’s gaze, but shook her head and looked away.

She thinks it's a silly observation. I guess it is.

Samantha’s eyes were next drawn to the staff. Golden, with the closed book at the top. She Identified it on pure instinct.

A Staff

The book is closed.

Samantha made a deadpan face. Oooof course it's not gonna give anything up that easy.

It was still quite the magical artifact, with a strong aura. She also knew it was cursed, and potentially a bad idea to simply pick up willy-nilly.

Samantha cleared her throat. “Azure, can you… shunt all this shit into a dimensional space safely? Like quarantine? The staff is cursed. I want ownership avoided. Might as well do the same with the head and the rest of his remains.”

Azure grinned and shrugged smugly. “Consider it done, my love.” She looked over at the remains on the floor and slowly swept her hand across the space between them. Each item went shimmery, distorted, and seemed to fall into an imploded space, something like a slice in reality.

Samantha watched, fascinated. She’d only seen this done here and there. “You know, you ought to try that on enemies.”

Azure grunted. “It’s too slow. Maybe as a prepared trap. But the description says it can be resisted. They’ll pass right through the collapsing space like passing through a water current.”

“It might throw them off balance or trip them up, though. And be extremely uncomfortable.”

“Hmm. Good point. You might be right? I think I could train to do that much quicker.”

Samantha flashed a smile, which got one in return as if it were contagious. She floated over through the room, inspecting things. Too late, she saw the stupid last rogue black orb still floating in the air start moving toward her. It had never detonated because the lich had been too near it.

Well. She wasn’t ‘too late’ to escape it, because she was fast. She zipped across the room, pulling it toward her. When it was far enough away from everyone, she moved a mirror through it, and it detonated harmlessly.

The acid sludge from the orbs had eaten through the floor in numerous places. Samantha floated over in the direction of the sarcophagus and stopped along the way, as she noted the item the lich had before, lying on the stone floor: the stylus. When his off-hand arm had been incinerated, it had fallen.

She carefully inspected it and found that it was not cursed in any way, but very magical. She Identified it.

Stylus of Carving

This arcane artifact can easily indent any normal material (clay, stone, metal, etc.) to carve symbols or other designs. The indents can be no deeper from the uniform surface than the stylus tip.

If used to form magical script, glyphs, or runes, improve their potency by 2 Effective Levels, whether as Item Level or Caster Level.

Ah. Cool. This can be a house item in Itha Ranon, for enchanters and such to use. I know about a dozen people off the top of my head who would want it. Perhaps a time rental system?

Musing about it, she picked the item up and twirled it in her fingers as she flew over to the dais and the sarcophagus.

There wasn’t anything noteworthy within it. Inspecting it magically, as well as in the spirit world, she sensed it was a potent magical focus and construct, a key to the whole, awful place. Something the Sage had done, from here, branching upward through a vast catacombs. She also knew the object was durable as hell. Even her Spellfyr wouldn’t take it down. At least, not in one shot.

But… she was going to destroy it. It was not indestructible, and she would not leave such a vile thing here. She had a duty not to. She needed to destroy it.

The voice in her head spoke quietly, as if to herself. ‘Like Creation, Destruction is essential. We must have the right; we must have the means. Without it, what dignity can exist? What iota is left uncorrupted?’

I understand.
