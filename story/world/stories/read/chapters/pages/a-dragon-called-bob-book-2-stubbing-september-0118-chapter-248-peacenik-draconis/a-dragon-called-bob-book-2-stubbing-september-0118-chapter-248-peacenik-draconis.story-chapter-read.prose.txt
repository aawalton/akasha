The very pinnacle of Mount Bob was spiky in a way that suggested erosion and weathering did not apply to it.  I curled my draconic bulk around the spire and surveyed my “empire”.

To the north lay the orlic wastes, rapidly emptying of my pet tribe as they headed south to engage in perpetual war against an unkillable, shapeshifting demon.  Nothing could possibly go wrong with that arrangement.

East and west were frontier human settlements, hardscrabble farmsteads and villages completely dependent on the larger towns for protection.  The ones in my line of sight were all dependent on the guard that Salnia had trained up in Fidler’s Mill.  They were mine, whether they knew it or not, and I would fight to protect what was mine.

I roared, raising my head to the cloudless sky, basking in what I’d achieved.  Mostly by accident, I couldn’t deny, but it was mine.  To the south…

The Mill had changed.  The old walls the dwarves had built had been surrounded by new streets and houses, and more walls had been built outside of the new limits. It was slowly but surely becoming a proper city.  Still small, to be sure, but the original couple of thousand was now pushing ten thousand souls, and it would only keep growing.

Beyond it lay the constant warzone of orlic and flesh-demon, which carefully steered clear of the road network.  It was working surprisingly well.  No monster or bandit attacks were happening to the south of Fidler’s Mill.  Another win I could add to my tally, and any of those idiots that tried just ended up as fodder for the orlic or food for Viktarlpth, making my forces stronger.

The cold wind didn’t bother me.  It snatched at my feathered wings, making me want to stretch them out and soar higher into the crystal blue sky, but I fought down the urge.  I had two more meetings to go, and this would arguably be the most important.

“Dad!”

Morris slammed into me, nearly breaking me free from my perch.  A pair of tails snaked out to grab him and hold him close to the cliff face so he could latch on.

“You’re bloody older than me.”

“Not on this world.  So what’s the plan?  I’ve got some ideas.  You should sue the Madson for missing loot.”

“I don’t think the legal system is that advanced on Helstat.  It’s still more of a might makes right kind of setup.”

He scurried higher and mirrored my pose in miniature, curling his neck up from the other side of the pinnacle.  Morris was still small, for a dragon.  Perhaps twelve feet from nose to solitary tail.  His scales gleamed in the sunlight.

“You’re growing fast.”

“Not all of us get cockrings that let us skip the first ten evolutions.  Mum keeps me well fed.”

“You know I licked that damn thing before I knew what it was?”

“Ha.  Yeah.  Mum told me.  Look, Bob, I’m not a kid, even though a big part of my mind thinks I am.  When are you going to let me fly?”

“It’s dangerous, Morris.  Even for a dragon, and especially now we’re kind of taking over the empire.”  I looked away and caught sight of the others spilling from their aeries, half a mile further down my mountain.  “How are you getting on with the others?”

“Pete’s a good guy, just a bit twitchy.  I knew a guy like him, quiet as shit, but when things kicked off, he was the one going in there with the knives.”

“I thought you were a… merchant of sorts, not a gangster?”

“You see some shit in London.”  He shrugged.

“I can imagine.  Banterbungler!  PULL THE FUCK UP!”

Bargleblaster had been aiming to swoop in and land at the very peak.  Setting aside his bulk, he’d clearly gotten a couple of Increase Mass rolls in his last set of evolutions; he wouldn’t fit and had no respect for individual space.  I swatted him away with a tail, and he flew down a few dozen metres before landing.

“Rude!” he grumbled, shaking out his wings.  “It’s nice up here.”

“It was… You’ve grown.”

“Nearly thirty metres long now!”

“It’s the motion of the ocean, not the size of the… dragon that counts.”

“Piss off Bob.”

“Why does Bob need to piss off?  He pulled us off watching those mammals building walls at Baginton.”  Jace slammed into the cliff beneath us and swarmed upwards, claws clicking as they bit into the rock, until his head was just below Bargleblaster’s tail.

“I really don’t.  That mountain you’re clinging to?  It’s got my name on it.”

“And your poop.”

“Hi, Lille.  How’s the wing doing?”  I was regretting calling this meeting.  I should have just done it down on the grass, but when only dragons can get to a place, it adds a certain cachet to host your meetings there.

“My wing still hurts a bit.”

“I’m sorry about that.  You fought really well.”

“Did you see her impale the last golem on her tail and use it like a club?”  Jace asked.  “She was like a Barnatocus, slamming it around like a club!”

“That isn’t a real monster,” I muttered.

“It bloody is!”

“Don’t care.”  The rest of the dragons circled in and landed slightly lower down.

“It’s cold,” Chi complained.

“It’s not that bad,” Pete offered in his choir-boy voice.

“I’m an old lady.  Don’t you backtalk your elders, child!”

“How old were you on Earth?” snapped Lille.

“I… I was twenty-one when I died.”

There was a collective intake of breath.

“Shit.  What happened?”

“I saw a young boy crossing the street.  Normally, I’d just have ignored it, none of my business after all,  but there was a truck coming.”

“So you dashed out and got hit by truck-kun instead?” Pete asked softly.

“No.  It didn’t quite happen that way.  I don’t want to talk about it,” Chi replied.

“I mean, c’mon! You know how I died,” Jace objected.

“Trying to bomb an innocent planet of civilians from orbit,” Chi replied somewhat smugly.

“Those fuckers had it coming!”

“Did they though?” asked Bargleblaster.

“Shove it, human.  You dehydrated due to playing video games!”

“HUMAN?  I’m a goddamn dragon!”

“CHILDREN!” Chi bellowed.  “Behave, or you know what will happen.”

I didn’t know what would happen, but I was willing to let that slide.  The dragons I’d thought of as kids, well, it turned out most of them were older than the one I’d thought was a grandma.  People lying about their age was strange to me.  Her bellow had had the desired effect, and I could get a word in edgewise.

“The point is what comes next,” I said quickly.

“Usually your mum,” sniggered Bumperburger.

“Shut up.  We are at what is known as a pivotal moment.  We, but mostly me, have a chance to shape the future of this world.  I want you to be a part of that bright future, but again, it’s mostly my choice.”  Vanity and wrath were kind of running my mouth at this point, if I’m honest.

“You just want nukes and A10s, Bob.  What’s in it for me?”

“Banglebanger, you are really getting on my scales.  If you’ve got something to say, spit it out.  If you’ve got something you want to do, stop being a pussy ass keyboard warrior and DO IT!”

He blinked his big green eyes at me, then his wings tucked in against his back.

“I want a bigger cut,” he said, like Oliver Twist asking for more slop.

“Fuck you, and no.  But you’ll get out what you put in.  Life is not a zero-sum game.  You’ve all done well since you flew west with me, haven’t you?  You’ve grown strong, and none of you has died!”

As a selling point, none of you has died would be fine, but there was an implied yet that I couldn’t shake.  But they had done well under my admittedly lax tutelage.  They had all grown stronger, and as I’d circled up around Mount Me, phrasing, I’d been unable not to notice the extent of their hordes stashed away in their private aeries.

“Not having died is hardly a selling point.”  Bloody Chi was always pissing in my cereal,

“You’re dragons!  The mightiest beasts to ever stalk the land and sky!  And I—I need you.  I can’t do this on my own.  Sure, humans are ok, orlics are arguably better but grosser, and dwarves are just ambulatory liver assassins, but none of them can do what we can do.  We are the apex predator, but that doesn’t mean we have to be a terror.  We can be a force for good in this world.  And incidentally make our hoards fats and shiny.”

I knew I’d hooked them.  The word shiny was like dangling a set of keys in front of a kitten, but I needed to set the hook.

“I’m going to be the emperor.  Not because I want to.  It sounds like a shit load of work that I’ll be palming off on Kat and Mrs Sanderson.  I’m going to be emperor for a very simple reason:  I hate being taxed.  Fuck anyone who thinks they can rob another being of their hard-earned income… except for me.  So here’s the rub, I’ll offer you guys a solid five percent off on all your taxes.  You’ll be the richest beings in the world!”

“And you won’t be paying any taxes at all?” asked Bargleblaster.

“Well… no.  I’m the emperor.  But all my funds will be put back into the economy to drive growth and long-term profitability,” I lied.  Like hell would I be ploughing my shinies back into the mammal world.

“So what’s in it for us?” asked Jace.

“A shit load of shinies?  That you’ll earn by killing things.  Then you get the biomass for eating what's left.”  I am always a very clever dragon.  They didn’t seem too impressed by my solution.

“So we get gold from our kills, and we get to eat what we kill.  I’m wondering why we need you?”

“Et tu, Chi?  Sure, you guys could go solo.  Fly an independent flag and then get swarmed by the Hunters' Guild.  I’ve killed their heavy hitter, but a pack of dogs can bring down a lion.  You don’t want to be the scaly lion, do you?”

“Sounds good to me,” said Jace.

“Yup,” added Bargleblaster.

“Wait!”  Their wings were spread, and I was fairly sure they weren’t bluffing.  I needed to keep them by my side.  Not too close, for sure, but close enough I could throw them at blobs of infantry when I needed to.  “What else do you need?”

“Dad, fuck these shmucks.”

“Language, son.”

“I’m ten years older than you.  If they’re too dumb to see which way the wind is blowing, screw them.  More loot for us, more biomass for me.”

“You aren’t stealing my biomass, you little turd!  I’m the big dragon here!” Bargleblaster snapped.

“Pfft.  Big.  How long have you been on this asshole of a world?  A few years now?  I’ve been here a couple of months, and I’m a third as big as you are, dipstick.  I bet without Dad you’d still be bothering yetis in the hills to the east, wouldn’t you?”

“I never touched the yetis—”

“Consensually?” Morris sneered.

“No! I mean yes!  I mean shut the hell up, you little prick!”

“Is that what the yetis told you when they told you to leave the money on the dresser?”

My head was ping ponging back and forth like a drunk at Wimbledon.  It seemed like the kid was winning?  The others were all looking pissed off, but their anger seemed to be directed at Bargleblaster, not me.

“Enough!  I want you all with me.  You guys will be getting cushy gigs.  It will mostly involve doing whatever you want, with occasional bouts of ultraviolence.  But we’re dragons!  What does it matter to us if we need to recycle a bunch of mammals every now and then!  You—we can be as gods!  Together, nothing short of the Shadow Flight could stand against us.”

“So why don’t we just go burn down Ankmapak?” Pete asked quietly.

The wind blew through my feathers, and it felt like the sky darkened.  It didn’t, I wasn’t in an anime.  Or was I?  What the hell was this world, really?

“That’s complicated, but the short answer is no.”
