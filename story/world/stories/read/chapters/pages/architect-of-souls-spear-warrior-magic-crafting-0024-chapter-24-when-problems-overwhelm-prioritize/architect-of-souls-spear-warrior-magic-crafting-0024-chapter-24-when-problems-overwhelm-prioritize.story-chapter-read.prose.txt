The trail the chain demons had left as they dragged the statue away was still present. Ferris wondered once again if it was natural for changes like this to persist in the soulspace, or maybe it stayed because he wanted it to.

Ferris called Jade over, then gathered his equipment: axe, new lightstone, quiver, and crossbow. He wanted to create a new spear, but first he needed a demon corpse. There was only enough qi left for a single new spear, after all. He wasn’t as practiced with an axe, but it was better than no melee weapon.

Ferris considered preloading the crossbow, but he worried it wouldn’t fire correctly. In the real world it would be bad to keep at tension for long, and he had no idea if that would translate to the soul.

Setting out, Jade rolled along beside him. The slime was… not fast, and he had to slow down quite a bit for Jade to keep up. He usually either carried Jade or walked slower with his crutch, so hadn’t been an issue. In the future, he might need to find a way to carry the slime without his hands while hunting in the soulspace. Maybe a slime-backpack?

The chain demons are also slow, Ferris thought, but are they slower than Jade? Ferris’ plan was to run away, reload, shoot, and repeat. If Jade couldn’t keep up, should he carry them? Ferris wanted to know if Jade could take a chain demon down alone, or if the chains could pull out the core. Unfortunately, there was no safe way to test it.

About ten minutes down the trail, Ferris heard chains clinking. He quickly cocked and loaded the crossbow, preparing for a fight. He called Jade over and waited for the demons to approach.

There were two chain demons, but they were unexpectedly accompanied by a blue frog demon. Ferris paled when he saw it jumping around next to them. He hadn’t planned for this. He should have considered the possibility, but he’d never seen two different types of demons work together.

The frog would be faster than him, giving no time to reload the crossbow. He had to take it out first.

Ferris, glad he’d already loaded the weapon, focused on the frog. He placed his feet in the stance the book had instructed, took a deep inhale, lined up his shot, and gently squeezed the trigger as he exhaled. The bolt… missed completely.

All three demons let out a screech after the bolt flew by, then charged at him. The chain demons were as awkward and slow as ever, but the frog was on him almost immediately.

He had no time to draw the axe from his back. Ferris slammed the butt of the stock into the frog, catching it midjump. Luckily, he landed a solid blow that threw the demon down to the ground.

Before the frog could recover, Jade rolled on top of it. The thing jerked around, trying to escape. Quickly, Ferris stepped on its leg. Pinning it in place.

Ferris watched in a bit of horrified fascination as the frog demon began to dissolve. He’d seen Jade eat a lot of food, but never a creature with body parts and a skeleton. Despite the demon being a mental construct, it looked real enough.

While Ferris was distracted, the other demons had approached close enough to attack. A chain came down from overhead, but Ferris was able to jump backwards just in time. He continued to back up, telling Jade to follow as fast as they could.

He watched closely, seeing if he needed to charge back in. If Jade wasn’t fast enough, hopefully he could provide a distraction.

Thankfully, Jade was able to just barely keep away from the demons, leading them on a somewhat silly chase. It would’ve been humorous… if Ferris hadn’t just almost gotten killed.

Taking a deep breath, he cocked and loaded another bolt in the crossbow. The stock’s butt had deformed a bit where he hit the frog, but it didn’t affect the weapon’s ability to fire. The basic qi construct really couldn’t take much damage.

Lining up another shot, Ferris aimed for one of the chain demons’ heads. He missed again, but at least hit the thing’s arm. Despite the crossbow’s basic construction, the bolt did penetrate the demon’s skin. Four shots later, he landed a hit in an eye, and it dropped to the ground. The last demon took four more bolts to die.

Ferris smiled, a bit manically. The plan had worked. If he fought the chain demons alone—and didn’t run out of bolts—he had an unassailable advantage. He missed a lot, but he would get better over time. There are certainly plenty of targets to practice on.

Ferris had fired ten of his twelve bolts. After recovering them, four looked unusable. Jade had completely eaten the blue frog demon, but there were still the two chain demon corpses.

The frog demons had been small enough Ferris was able to tie three onto his sack and carry them, but these fat chain demons were much larger, standing almost as tall as Ferris’ waist. He couldn’t determine any way to carry both, so reluctantly he let Jade eat one. It would likely disappear by the time he could return anyway, and Jade was excited to consume it. Ferris still had no idea what the consequences of that would be.

Jade’s Consume Pool ability would make a copy of the corpse in the slimespace. Both the original and copy would dissolve together, but then what? Would the fish eat it, and become more powerful? Would it help or hurt the clams? The previous corpse Jade had eaten hadn’t had any noticeable effect. Jade was helping him fight, so some demons would get consumed mid-battle either way.

Hefting the last corpse, Ferris and Jade headed back to his Heartfire. Thankfully, nothing attacked them on the way back, and he dropped the heavy corpse near what little remained of his Qi Pool. Exhausted, Ferris had to take a couple minutes to recover.

The crossbow had worked, and Jade had done better than he hoped, but he had two new problems.

First, the chain demons were not alone. He needed a plan to fight combinations of more blue frogs, red imps, and potentially entirely new types of demons. With the stress of the last few days, Ferris wouldn’t be surprised if new heart demons had taken root.

Second, he had broken four of his bolts fighting just two chain demons, and three in practice. He only had eight remaining. His aim might improve, but he’d seen dozens of demons around the fort. There were likely many more unseen. He would run out of ammo long before he could recover the oath statue.

For the first problem—fighting multiple types of demons—Ferris wanted his spear back. Jade had proven capable of supporting him better than he’d expected. While the slime would likely die facing any of the demons alone, Jade could quickly take out a knocked down enemy. The blue demons in particular weren’t able to defend themselves in a grapple. When armed with a spear, he believed he could reliably provide the opportunity Jade needed against the quick demons.

Beyond his spear, Ferris considered what else he could do. After the first chain demon had stolen his spear, he had wanted to make a smaller backup weapon. Also, the book on armor at the library made Ferris want to experiment with crafting protection. Neither would have helped against the chain demons, but they could help a lot against the frogs. Only… he didn’t think he could afford the qi.

This lack of qi was tied to the second problem. Ferris needed either more bolts, or stronger ones that didn’t break as often. Preferably both.

To acquire more qi, he could harvest his Air Passage to gather what had accumulated today, but that may not be enough. The acid river in the slimespace had plenty, but he had no way to get it.

For stronger bolts, Ferris decided to try the demon corpse qi reinforcement technique. The bolts were a bit like small spears, so it should work.

After his break, Ferris half-filled his cooking pot with liquid qi. This left pathetically little remaining in the pool, although he could probably get a couple bolts. He took his axe to the chain demon’s corpse, adding chunks to the pot until it was full. After cooking the mixture over his Heartfire, Ferris used the cooled liquid to form a new demon reinforced spear.

Ferris was still worried about side effects of Jade eating the demons, so he took his familiar over to the slimespace to check. It wouldn’t hurt to see if he could somehow gather the qi from the Digest River. Who knows, Ferris thought, maybe there’s a pile of qi magically already collected and waiting for me.

He grinned. Not likely.

The chain demon and blue frog Jade had eaten were nowhere to be seen, and nothing appeared different. Even though the total size of the chain demon was larger than any previous meal, the acid level hadn’t visibly changed. Odd, as a normal real-life meal had raised the river. More proof that the copy is only enlarged when it’s eaten by Jade’s real body, Ferris thought.

He looked longingly at the plentiful clams, each with a qi chunk display proudly along the bottom of the river. Ferris wanted them. The solution to his ammo supply problem was just out of reach.

His best idea was to use a qi construct or reinforced demon qi to retrieve them. Make something like a fishing pole, have Jade hook the chunk, and then reel it in. The chunks of qi didn’t dissolve, so maybe something created from qi would be resistant.

To test the theory, Ferris had brought one of his failed qi construct triggers and the demon reinforced axe. He tossed the trigger into the green river, only to watch as it fizzled and melted away to nothing. Well, that didn’t work, he thought with a sigh.

Next, Ferris dipped the end of the axe’s handle into the river. Like the trigger, it bubbled and dissolved away. He pulled the axe back, examining the slightly shorter handle. It would still be usable, but the experiments had ended in failure.

With no other ideas, Ferris and Jade returned to his soulspace. Clan Hollowfield’s library probably had a solution, but the minimum price for cultivation books was six merit points, so that wasn’t an immediate option.

Since he didn’t have access to the qi in the slimespace, all Ferris had left was the Air Passage. Armed again with a spear, Ferris and Jade headed off to gather what he could. This time he strapped the crossbow to his back, carrying his melee weapon at the ready. He didn’t want to get caught swinging the crossbow up close again, it would be easy to break. He should have plenty of time to swap when he ran into another chain demon.

Arriving at the Air Passage, Ferris discovered a different problem: standing right above the trench was an entirely new type of demon.

He quickly covered his light, examining the demon in the soft glow from qi chunks in the Air Passage.

It was covered completely with a dirty, ragged cloak. Two golden horns poked out of the top, but the cloak had no other holes. It was larger than the red imp and blue frog demons, about as tall as the chain demons.

The demon was alone, standing ominously above his Air Passage. Ferris didn’t think the thing had seen him, as it hadn’t attacked yet. It had no visible eyes, but he doubted it was blind. His light should’ve been blatantly obvious as he approached. Everything else here attacks on sight, he thought. Is this one different?

Beyond the demon, he could see more qi chunks scattered about than usual. Ferris had not gathered them the day before, so there were two days’ worth accumulated.

Watching the new cloaked demon, Ferris had to make a choice. He could attack, but the first battle with a new type always carried extra risk. If the demon had immediately noticed him, he would have no choice; but as it was, he could still leave. Yet, if he just left then he couldn’t create more ammo.

The cloaked demon wasn’t moving, so Ferris settled down to watch. Perhaps he would learn something.

After less than a minute, the demon started slowly skulking along the upper ridge of the Air Passage. As it moved, its cloak shifted with the outline of what looked like limbs. It was hard to tell for sure. The two golden horns poking up from its head glittered in the Air Passage’s glow, a stark contrast to its drab tattered cloak.

Ferris was preparing to follow, but then something changed. The demon let out a high-pitched screech, then leaped down into the Air Passage. Faster than Ferris thought possible, it grabbed one of the qi chunks and turned around. With another leap, it was up and out. Without stopping, the thing bolted off into the darkness.

The demon, and the chunk of qi, were gone.

Ferris was a bit shocked; it’d moved much faster than any other demon. There was no way he could have outrun the thing. His surprise quickly turned to anger. On top of everything else, now he had to deal with thieves infesting his Air Passage.

The good news was that the demon had run off, leaving most of the qi chunks, with no others in sight. Cautiously, Ferris and Jade climbed down into the Air Passage and began collecting. The cloaked demon was yet another problem for another day.

Following the length of the Air Passage, they ran into three more of the new demons. They were always alone, and each time they either ignored or failed to notice Ferris and Jade. After spending a few minutes skulking around outside the Air Passage, the demon would leap down, grab a chunk of qi, then run off with it. Ferris wasn’t sure how much of his qi the demons were stealing, but at this pace they would clear out the Air Passage before he could each day. Frankly, it felt like they couldn’t have been around for long, or else there would barely be any qi chunks left. Maybe there was a limit to how much they would steal?

After making an entire loop, Ferris checked his bag. He’d collected more than on a typical day, but not as much as he would’ve expected from two days accumulation. It was better than nothing, and he’d been able to avoid a risky fight.

Back at the Heartfire, Ferris dumped the qi chunks into the Qi Pool. He wanted to create more bolts, but it would take a while for the chunks to turn liquid. There was now more solid qi than liquid in what remained of his pool.

While waiting, Ferris practiced more with the crossbow. When first testing the weapon, he’d mostly just stood in one spot. In the actual fight, Ferris was sprinting around between each shot… which he hadn’t practiced at all. Before, he’d hit a limit where he wasn’t noticing large gains from short practice, but now that he added movement between each shot, he was quickly improving again. It may have looked a bit ridiculous, but at least Jade was the only witness. He did get better, but it cost him three more broken bolts, leaving just five remaining.

About an hour later, he checked the Qi Pool again. Some of the qi chunks hadn’t liquified, but he had enough to craft some bolts. He moved most of the remaining liquid into the cooking pot, about what he had used to form the spear. More of the chopped demon’s corpse went in the pot, equal in volume to qi, and then Ferris cooked it over his Heartfire. Once it had cooled, he was able to make fifteen new bolts from the mix.

These demon reinforced bolts felt more rigid than the simple qi crafted ones. Ferris’ aim suffered a bit, but he soon adapted to the differences.

Ferris looked back at the trail leading off into the darkness. With a deep breath, he prepared himself. He was going hunting once more.

And this time, he intended to assault the chain demon’s fort.
