A meranthrall was sent flying across the forest ground before crashing down and gouging a short path through the dirt.

Nasha landed on one leg, her other still extended from a high kick.

“Looks like you’re back in top condition,” Veronica said, walking up beside her.

Nasha turned. “The healer did wonders. I don’t feel sore anymore. Especially with Gwen’s magic stimulating my muscles.”

“Good.” Veronica lifted her arm, aiming her palm at another meranthrall.

They were short creatures, about the size of an adult goblin, but with bodies more like ogres or trolls. They had three fingers, gray skin, a black horn that curved left from what appeared to be a pig’s head, and an X-shaped cross mark across their chests. Like goblins, they were tribal monsters that lived in camps and possessed some semblance of community and reasoning. The key distinction was their connection to the natural elements.

The meranthrall growled, huffing smoke from its nostrils. It reared its head back, and the scar-gouged cross mark across its chest began to glow. A second later, a cross-slash of fire exploded forward, shooting at Veronica like a blast from a cannon.

She fired her spell the moment she saw the element of the attack.

A thin but violent tornado formed, drilling through the center of the blast. The fire was dragged into the vortex, coating the whirlwind and further strengthening it.

Veronica’s spell continued forward and pierced through the meranthrall’s chest before dissipating into the air several meters behind it. The fire it carried singed the creature’s skin, while the wider rear end of the tornado gouged a spiraling burn wound across the rest of its body.

The beast staggered for a moment, unable to breathe. Then the remaining markings of the cross on its chest glowed brightly, rising in intensity. It tried to clutch at its chest, but it was too late.

Unable to regulate its elemental power anymore, the meranthrall detonated.

Body parts flew in every direction, along with blood and viscera. Its horn shot through the air and sliced off a nearby branch before lodging itself into the bark of a tree. Its legs went in two opposite directions, while the rest of its body parts caught aflame as they barreled through the air.

Any debris that flew toward Veronica collided with her barrier and sank to the ground. Nasha, on the other hand, simply dashed backward and avoided it altogether.

Veronica turned to glance at her. “Say, are you better as an up-close rogue, or a long-ranged one?”

Nasha dashed back beside Veronica, moving only on the tips of her boots. “I’m a better shot with a bow than I am fighting up close. It’s just that I don’t have a good enough bow that suits me.”

“What about the viscount’s bow you chose?”

“It’s not bad. The bow itself is quite good. But the only redeeming quality is the charged aura shots from the enchantment. In most cases, it’s just a normal bow.”

“Hm. I see.”

Several other meranthralls in front of them began to screech and growl. Roughly five more of them, all furious that two of their own had died, and that their murderers had enough time to stand around and converse in the middle of battle.

Veronica turned around. “Gwen! Are you ready?”

Hiding behind a tree, Gwen had her eyes closed, channeling mana through her staff. “N-not yet!”

Veronica faced the meranthralls once more. “Say, how close are you to your own breakthrough?”

Nasha rolled her shoulders while the meranthralls charged up another attack. “Ah, actually, I already broke through.”

Veronica glanced back at her. “What? Already?”

The woods lit up as three separate cross-shaped attacks came at them, all of different elements: fire, lightning, and wind.

Selene’s Tear glowed around Nasha’s neck as she twisted her body, winding one foot behind her and carving a line through the dirt. As the three elemental attacks approached at the same time, her leg was suddenly coated in a thin layer of red energy.

Nasha kicked forward, sending out a powerful gust of wind that blew away the nearby leaves. Her foot connected with all three attacks in a single kick, dispersing them with a loud popping sound that echoed through the woods.

The air crackled with electricity, sizzled with dispersing embers, and howled with wind.

Her kick swept all the way around, twisting her body in a full rotation before she landed back in a neutral stance. Without missing a beat, Nasha charged forward, now coating both legs in aura. She blitzed toward the nearest meranthrall in the blink of an eye and punched it.

Her spiritual-energy-infused fist caved in the monster’s snout and blew it off completely. Blood and wind rushed backward in a cone as the body dropped, utterly beheaded.

The two other meranthralls shrieked, watching another of their own die. But Nasha didn’t let up. She immediately moved and targeted them, delivering quick jabs straight into the center of their cores.

In under a minute, she eliminated all of the monsters except for one.

“Hey! Leave that one for Gwen!” Veronica yelled, cupping her hands around her mouth.

Nasha listened and stopped another kick mere inches from the monster’s face.

Veronica turned back toward Gwen. “You ready?”

Gwen, who now had her eyes open, body tense and lips pressed firmly together, nodded. “I—I think so.”

With a flash from her staff, the crystal gem at its tip glowed bright red, and a small flame appeared above it. The flame wasn’t normal. It began to flicker violently, its shape changing like bubbling water left to boil. Soon enough, it started to resemble something with wings, though hazy and unstable.

Gwen thrust her staff forward, and the bead of flame, shaped like a small bird, flew ahead. Nasha moved out of the way just as the flame reached the last meranthrall. It crashed against the creature’s face, burning its skin and causing the poor monster to shriek in pain. It slapped a hand across its snout, patting out the fire burning there.

Veronica watched with her arms crossed, eyes fixed closely on the monster’s face.

Alright, Gwen. Prove to me you have the skill to learn this.

As she watched, the air subtly shifted around her. It wasn’t something a normal person could sense. Not even a mage would notice unless they were specifically paying attention to it. But Veronica had always been sensitive to mana around her. Even when she was young, she had sensed that despicable man using veil magic to cheat his hand while gambling with Victor.

She could almost imagine his face meeting Victor’s fist for the first time.

A small smile crept onto her face as she reminisced.

The meranthrall eventually doused the fire burning across its face, leaving nothing but small scorch marks. But just as the embers were snuffed out, something strange happened. The blackened marks on its face suddenly began to glow. First white, then orange.

And all at once, the flames regrew.

The monster screeched again, slapping its hands against its face despite the burns forming across its palms.

However, the fire didn’t stop. The meranthrall kept slapping and thrashing its head around, but the flames simply coalesced and danced around its strikes. For a split second, the fire reformed into the shape of a bird perched upon its nose, only to be patted out again.

After a painfully long minute, the meranthrall finally collapsed to its knees, most of its head burned to a crisp. Once its body fully fell over, the flames stopped reforming.

“Not bad for a first attempt,” Veronica murmured.

She turned around to look at Gwen.

Gwen was heaving, one hand braced on her knee while the other held her staff forward, slightly drooped. Sweat poured down her face as her head swayed from side to side.

“Holy… shit,” she muttered. “That… sucked.”

Veronica chuckled, seeing how tired she was after a single spell.

Phoenix Ember. A Tier-2 spell created by a mage named Fia Twindale, a 9th-tier archmage who had passed away nearly thirty years ago. She followed the Path of Blooming and the Path of Splitting. Although she specialized in summoning, the principles of her magic could be applied to other pathways with certain modifications. It might be considered plagiarism, but Gwen was a Path of Longevity mage. The work needed to reform the spells was not simple, but it could be done. She merely needed to follow her own path, using the resources left behind by the prior generation.

Veronica had a feeling Gwen could pull it off, though she was pleasantly surprised the girl managed it on her first try. But perhaps it was to be expected. After all, Gwen had a natural affinity for converting ambient mana. By cycling the mana in the air, it was much easier for someone like her to renew the flame. Not as a summoning, but as a lingering effect that continually revived itself.

The original spell simply conjured a small flame resembling a bird. It was alive with the caster’s mana. It could think and attack on its own, but it could also die and dissipate. Fia managed the spell so that the phoenix, using its own mana system, would continually revive itself. But because of that unique effect, the spell was quite mana-heavy.

However, Gwen’s pathway and her innate ability to use the surrounding mana were invaluable. Despite the spell not being a summon, she could imitate the legendary bird’s immortality by continually healing it. It wasn’t alive by any means, but alive was subjective. It was all based on the caster’s interpretation.

Instead of something that revived itself anew through flames of rebirth, Gwen became the trigger for it. An offensive spell that she could heal and support in combat.

Truly, it earned the name Phoenix.

“Alright, you two!” Veronica shouted. “Training for today is done! Let’s collect the ears and head back!”

By the time they returned to the Adventurer’s Guild, it was already noon in Maftem.

The process of turning in the commission was simple enough.

They deposited the seven silverwolf ears first, followed by the meranthrall horns they had collected. Although the horns were not part of their original commission, they were valuable resources that could be used to craft gear or ground down into alchemical reagents.

Nasha had known how to harvest the horns cleanly, as expected of a rogue with years of experience.

What surprised Veronica the most was Gwen. Despite her usual hesitation, Gwen handled the task of severing the ears from the silverwolves cleanly and without complaint. Each cut was neat, with minimal damage to the ears themselves. She also didn’t seem bothered by the blood.

Only after watching her finish did Veronica ask about it.

It turned out that before being captured by Lorence, Gwen had taken commissions from the guild regularly, a fact Veronica had forgotten. It was mostly herb gathering, the exact task that led her to be ambushed by the man in the first place. But she had also hunted horned rabbits and other small monsters before, and knew how to safely and effectively harvest the important materials.

The revelation mage, the clerk Veronica had spoken to last time, was the one who confirmed the proof of their commission. Their payment was processed without much trouble. Fourteen hundred vix for the silverwolves, and an additional six hundred for the meranthrall horns. It wasn’t an impressive amount by any means, but it was enough to make the trip worthwhile, especially since they were there for training first and foremost.

Once everything was finished, the three of them left the guild.

Gwen stretched her arms over her head the moment they stepped onto the busy street, out of view from the Adventurer’s Guild. “Finally doneeee… I’m starving.”

“Did you two want to go out to eat? We did spend several hours out there today,” Veronica said. She patted the sack attached to her hip. “We have enough funds for that at least.”

“Hell yeah,” Gwen said immediately, clutching her fists in front of her. “Some people at the library gave me recommendations while I was studying yesterday. Apparently, there’s a really good noodle place on the south side of the city. Something about a special sweet peanut sauce.”

Veronica raised an eyebrow. She wasn’t a stranger to trying new foods anymore. Especially if they were sweet.

She turned to Nasha. “Nasha?”

“Sounds like a plan.”

“Great,” Gwen said, already smiling. “I’ve been thinking about it since yesterday.”

The three of them passed by a busy street. Many people were out with their families, most of the foot traffic moving in and out of various shops, restaurants, and other businesses like bookstores or private apothecaries.

Many lampposts along the street were beginning to light up, each one powered by mana. Almost no places here used torches any longer, and most carriages that drove along the street were powered by mana as well, or pulled by wooden or stone constructs resembling horses. A few—their entire carriages were summoned constructs themselves.

As they walked along a particularly crowded sidewalk, someone accidentally bumped into Nasha’s shoulder.

A man in a brown coat stumbled back with a young boy clinging to his sleeve. The child looked no older than eight or nine, with messy dark hair and a half-eaten glazed pastry in one hand.

“Ah, sorry, miss,” the man said quickly, dipping his head in apology. “Crowd’s thick around this time.”

Nasha turned toward him, raising a hand. “It’s fine,” she replied with a warm smile.

The boy peeked around the man’s coat and mumbled, “Sorry, miss.”

Nasha crouched down and patted his head. “Don’t worry about it. No harm done.”

The man gave one more apologetic nod before guiding the boy past them, disappearing back into the crowd as if nothing had happened.

Veronica glanced after him for a moment, then looked back at Nasha. “Are you alright?”

“Mm?” Nasha looked over her shoulder. “Yeah. He barely touched me.”

“People should watch where they’re going,” Gwen muttered, though her attention quickly returned to the idea of food. “Anyway, the noodle shop should be a little south of here. I think we can take the main road, then turn after we see the building that looks like a boot.”

Nasha nodded along, but her fingers slipped briefly into the fold of her pocket.

Her smile didn’t change. She kept walking as if nothing had happened.

Veronica didn’t notice.

Gwen didn’t either.

For a few more steps, Nasha walked with them, but then she slowed. “Actually,” she said, touching a hand to her chin as if remembering something, “I just realized I have to stop somewhere first.”

Veronica turned. “Hm?”

“That place I auditioned for,” Nasha said. “The owner wanted me to come back and sing one more time before fully booking me. I forgot they asked me to stop by this evening.”

Gwen blinked. “Right now?”

“Unfortunately.” Nasha gave an apologetic smile. “It probably won’t take too long, but you two shouldn’t wait on me. Go eat first.”

Veronica studied her for a moment. “Are you sure?”

“Yeah. I’ll meet you back at the inn afterward.”

Gwen looked between them, then frowned. “But you’re going to miss the cold noodles.”

Nasha shrugged. “I’ll treat you two if I land the spot, then. It’s a middle-class entertainment plaza, so the food is going to be better than any regular restaurant here.”

Gwen crossed her arms. “Well… alright. But I’m ordering extra today since you’re gone.”

“We’ll go ahead, then. Don’t take too long. We still have to discuss what we’re going to do tomorrow.”

Nasha smiled at her. “I’ll try not to.”

And just like that, Nasha parted from them, giving a small wave before turning down a side street.

The moment she rounded the corner, the smile on her face faded.

A few minutes later, once she was far enough away, she reached into her pocket. She pulled out a small string, the one the man had slipped inside.

There was nothing magical about it. No special effect. No poison or hidden mechanism. It was a simple thread, likely pulled from an embroidered blanket.

The only thing unique about it was its color.

Red.

A vibrant vermillion.
