The sky felt charged. The hairs on my skin stood on end. Chett had been compromised somehow, as I’d feared he might be the moment I’d seen his attack, but I hadn’t anticipated such a swift betrayal.

I felt static in the air. Something gathering. I trembled, hoping it was all in my head, but not believing it for a minute.

“Another one is coming! Get down!” I shouted.

I was right. Moments later, blinding light surged once again, and I turned away from the crack of light.

Unlike the last time, the explosion didn’t cause an eruption. The wall didn’t shake. Tentatively, I opened one blurry eye to see a man standing with his hand raised high.

My vision was still blurry, but I quickly recognized him as Ekren. He and Burnom had gotten along immediately, since both of them were originally from Rechin Amut. He was a massive, burly man who looked ill-suited to the Tacurian Army uniform, and even more ill-suited to his profession. He was a mage who looked like a blacksmith. I’d liked him immediately.

Minor Bolt Ward

Effect 1: Lightning attacks directed at the wielder are absorbed by the orb, and can be redirected to different targets.

“Oh shit!” I cried, before covering my eyes again. I did so not a moment too soon, as the Rechin man thrust the stone forward, and lightning cracked out of the end, blasting horizontally across the plain in an instantaneous burst.

The explosion was deafening, but when I dared to open my eyes again, I saw a crater where a chunk of the enemy earth mages had been.

“Take that ye’ mercenary fucks,” the man said lowly. “Oy! Thanks for this! Saved my noggin!”

I could barely hear him, but I shook my head.

“S-sure! Any time!” I said.

The attack had certainly startled the Eschal advance, but it hadn’t slowed them. On foot they came, their ranks spread wide. Still outside of crossbow range.

I heard the sharp whump of heavy weights moving far away before another barrage of boulders launched into the air. They whistled, audible even from this distance, filling me with dread. Earth Mages boosted them once more, trying to propel them faster than I could make them explode.

Hastily, I coated the one that looked like it might be heading for my spot on the wall, trying not to look at the massive gap where at least five people had been standing.

“Order the soldiers to remain calm, and await the order to fire. They will soon be in range!” Neogon shouted.

I nodded, using the Public Speaking skill as best I could. With my ears ringing, I could barely hear my own voice. I hoped people farther from the lightning could hear better than I could.

My mana was down to 1,241, and in a split second, I decided I could handle three of them. If they did enough damage to the enemy by exploding over their heads, they might stop trying.

My mana plummeted to 912, and two of the massive boulders exploded. Debris scattered everywhere, killing some soldiers and being blocked by the quick reactions and shields of others. I had underestimated the mana I’d needed for the third and unfortunately, set it aflame without destroying it.

‘No!’ I thought, realizing that I’d done nothing wrong. I just hadn’t prepared for them to adapt. It was reinforced by Earth Magic! They’d shielded it!

A fourth rock missed the wall and slammed into a tree stump ten feet short.

The fifth smashed into the wall far up the line north toward the river, and I screamed as I saw a body fly backward off the wall, moments before impact.

I was situated roughly near the middle of the wall, so my voice could be heard all the way down both sides. I winced as screams echoed from there, and cringed again when the burning rock also found its target north of me, but thankfully didn’t hit with enough impact to shatter the logs. Merely shake them. They did catch flames, though.

A water mage immediately began dousing the flames before they could spread.

“If this keeps up, they might not need to invade. We might all just die standing here on this death trap!” shouted someone to my left.

“Hold steady! This is nothing. Mere opening salvos!” Neogon called. “Get me whoever the hell is blowing up their rocks and tell them to stop wasting mana!”

“Don’t know, sir!” came the voice of Sergeant Vovel as he landed nearby. A dragoon as well.  “Probably not one of ours! I’ve never seen fire magic used like that!”

The wall was holding exceptionally well. Even when entire chunks had been knocked out of it, the rest was holding firm. Still, those rocks were enormous, and each one landed with its own sonic boom and dust cloud. The only one that had hit the top of the wall had surely killed as many people as the lightning bolt had.

They were outside our range, but they could hurl rocks at us all day. Still, they were overly confident in their assault. The infantry weren’t advancing into our crossbow range. They were waiting for us to be softened.

I let oily fire mana fill the back end of my cannon. I was lucky the thing hadn’t been hit by lightning. Even if it hadn’t, it might’ve fallen off the wall, rendering it useless. I didn’t know the range or how to aim it. Its barrel was propped up between the tops of two logs, aimed roughly in the direction of the enemy army. It was aimed slightly above them.

It might be a waste of ammo and mana, but I had to try.

“Get away from the cannon!” I shouted. The people around me didn’t seem to realize what I was saying, so I physically pushed one of them. Eyes focused forward, they barely noticed.

No one was standing to the right of my newest invention. It was too close to the gap, so once I’d gotten everyone at least a few feet away, I felt safe enough to use it.

“Get down! I’m firing!” I screamed, holding my ears, before setting off the oiling mana. The explosion was quieter than the lightning, but louder than the rocks. Soldiers around me screamed in shock, and jumped away from my cannon when a gout of flame and rock shot out of the end.

The massive barrel slid back, but was stopped by a metal bar I’d insisted be there. The last thing I wanted was for the cannon to blow itself backward off the wall.

I frantically looked out to the field. Barriers of wind and earth had lifted out of the ground in front of some of the soldiers but they’d been reactionary. Attempts to stop a second cannon shot, but they’d done nothing to stop the first.

I could almost see a crater formed out of the bodies of the men my cannon’s grapeshot had slain, instead of sundered earth. Blood coated the stumps, and men screamed, adding to the cacophony.

We had a weapon that could stop them, but the end of the barrel looked frayed and red-hot. I tried to add more of the tiny stones into the barrel, but it was hard to even get my hands near the thing.

“Here!” someone shouted, and I turned to find a fire mage using their magic to pull heat from the front of the barrel. I hadn’t even known I could do that!

“What in the heavens is this thing!?” he asked.

“It’s called a cannon!” I shouted. Everyone was shouting. Unfortunately, almost everyone on the wall was practically useless until they came into crossbow range. A few people had fired bolts, but they’d all fallen short of the enemies.

“Fire that thing again!” shouted Neogon as he approached.

“I’m trying!” I yelled.

“Get out of the way! Let stronger men load it! How many times can you fire it?”

“More times than we have ammo!” I shouted, hoping I was right, as I stepped away and let two soldiers begin scooping up the rocks that the cannon used and dropping them into the front of the barrel.

All of us flinched when another lightning bolt slammed into the far end of the wall near the beach to the south. Chett. The bastard wasn’t attacking near where the lightning ward was anymore.

Far away, I saw the Eschal Army begin to advance up the rocks and sand near the beach. It was too far away for me to see the damage, but Eschal’s decision to charge worried me. The grapeshot should’ve frightened them, not galvanized them.

Ekren suddenly dashed that way, leaping across the gap the lightning bolt had caused. Sergeant Vovel leaped over the gap and the running soldier before grappling him.

“No!” I heard him shout. “You protect the girl’s weapon! You stay here!”

“They aren’t going to attack the center again! I need to be where the lightning is!” he cried. “Else what fucking good am I?”

“Already more good than we’ve had any right to! Chett’s betrayed us, and I don’t care how fast you can run, you can’t predict where the bolts will fall! Defend the general and the girl’s weapon!”

“You daft, sergeant? Chett’s been teeterin’ on the edge of going Hysteric for ages now. This ain’t betrayal!” shouted Ekren.

“I’d be inclined to believe that, if the bolts weren’t all falling on our damned wall!” the sergeant replied. “Hysterics would be hitting houses, random ground, everything around!”

I didn’t have time to listen to the rest of the conversation as the two privates finished loading the cannon. I sank another 40 mana into the barrel.

“Firing! Hold your ears!” I shouted again.

The soldiers had been frightened the first time, but they were professionals. The second explosion didn’t have nearly the same effect. Even nearby townsfolk didn’t jump.

The cannon hit more targets. More men and women fell to their deaths, but it wasn’t enough. The moment the dust cleared, the enemy commander ordered a full charge, and the soldiers began to run.

The lines of fighters pouring from the treeline seemed endless. If we were faring this poorly here, how must the trenches to the north and east be doing? There were so many. The fire mage used mana to pull the heat out of the end of the barrel again, but the cannon’s front end had fully cracked now. I didn’t think I’d be able to get more than one last shot, and the enemy commander also seemed to realize that it couldn’t be turned, so his soldiers were spreading out and away from the blast zone of the cannon.

There were so fucking many.

“Crossbows! Fire!” Neogon shouted.

A cheer went up from the soldiers and the people of Pemolar! I screamed right along with them, but I didn’t have my crossbow anymore. I was dirty, covered in soot, and everything tasted like smoke. Below me, Eschal soldiers were being slaughtered, but they just kept coming. It was hard to imagine what ten thousand soldiers looked like until they were barreling straight towards us.

Tears were streaming down my cheeks as I watched crossbow bolts find chests, stomachs, eyes. Men and women screamed, all of it strangely muted by the familiar burning sensation of burst eardrums.

Another lightning bolt struck on the north end of the wall. Another. Another. Screams began to reach my ears, but the bolts never came near me.

Suddenly, the air right above me seemed to… harden.

“Get down!” I screamed, diving to the floor.

Too late.

The air solidified into solid stone before exploding in a shockwave that sent crystallized air blasting in every direction.

Ekran died instantly as the hardened air smashed into his chest. I gasped in shock as he toppled off the back of the wall straight through flimsy guard rails that were no longer strong enough to hold his limp body. The Minor Bolt Ward fell with him.

Others in plate armor fared a little better, but the floor itself was torn to shreds. The air had punched holes in the wood planks, tripping some of the soldiers who’d been lucky enough to be wearing metal armor. They fell through, tumbling, only to be caught on the scaffolding below.

By some miracle, I thought I was unharmed. My heart was pounding so fucking fast I could barely breathe.

“That's my fucking sword!” I hissed, enraged, but a treacherous groan made me freeze.

The cannon was heavy. So heavy. I felt the slight creak in the floor moments before it collapsed. Below me, the man who’d been loading the cannon screamed as it sank through the ground in a crunch of splintering wood. I followed right behind as the floor gave way.

I scrabbled, desperately trying to catch something while it felt like the whole world was falling on top of me. Other bodies fell too, but I couldn’t see. I managed to catch hold of a scaffolding beam, but I wasn’t strong enough to hold myself with just one arm, and my hand was yanked free. I toppled, then screamed as I landed on top of the boiling cannon barrel.

I jumped, but I couldn’t move as wooden planks fell like rain. I covered my eyes, my back searing against the nearly molten metal.

Desperate, I reached out with my fledgling fire magic, trying to use it to pull heat away, but I didn’t know how. I screamed and scrambled desperately. A hand grasped my arm and yanked me free of the pile of timber. I tumbled forward, out of the crumbling ruin, only to meet Normuran’s worried eyes.

“Gods, girl. Can’t you stay uninjured for ten minutes!?” he shouted.

A wave of healing magic washed over me, and blessed relief filled my lungs. I was panting like a dog as I stared up at the old man who’d probably saved my life ten times in the past four months.

I hugged him, crying. Sobbing.

“It wasn’t enough… I’m sorry. I’m so sorry. I…”

His hands fell gently on my back. The healing light never slowed as I cried. What more could I do?

“It’s not over yet, Lady El,” he said. “If we all pull together, we may yet see this through.”

Distant lightning struck the wall again.

Slowly, I pulled away from him. My ears were feeling better. I knew he would stop healing me soon, needing to conserve his mana, but it had given me enough relief from the pain to register his words.

As my breathing slowed just a little, I had a thought. The idea I’d almost grasped a while ago, that had slipped through my fingers, suddenly solidified.

"If we... all pull together..." I murmured.

For the moment, I was safe behind the wall. Alive… But I didn’t think that would be the case for long. The army had wind magic, and just before I’d fallen, I’d seen the enemy mages raise wind barriers, protecting the army from our onslaught of crossbow bolts.

If something wasn’t done, we really would all die. Pemolar’s Hill would be erased, just like Mitoras. Just like Lindolen.

But I had an idea.

Opening my menu, I stared at my remaining free points in dismay. Just 31 left. It would have to do.

“Neogon was right,” I thought bitterly, as I emptied my free point pool into Public Speaking.
