Though Evening Clouds disciples couldn’t go up the mountains unless in specific situations, Inner Disciples were free to roam as they wished. Even though they focused on nurturing Outer Disciple’s strength and cultivation to break through into Qidense realm, they also were the main force for raw fighting power. The mountains sowed the seeds of the Seven Clouds, helping disciples break through their bottleneck and gain comprehension, but the Evening Clouds taught everything when it came to battles, and trained the Cloud Knights.

The perimeters of Evening Clouds that surrounded the Seven Clouds Sect was not small by any means; its sheer size was the largest out of any force or faction in Cloud City. And this did not even include the Cloud Knight Garrisons, scattered throughout the province. As Shuren descended the mountains on his flying nimbus, he looked towards the first place he had gone to, almost a year ago. The examination stage, at the most south western corner of the Evening Clouds.

And true to its name, from a distance he saw the mountainous colours of matte grey clash with the mixture of orange and crimson painted buildings and halls built across the Evening Clouds.

I need to keep my emotions in check.

Shuren took a deep breath through his nose, slowly exhaling out as the sharp winds swirled around him during his descent. A breathing technique he had once discerned from Dan Zi’s Scriptures, it was known to submerge the burning heart within the cool air.

Within a dozen metres before landing, Shuren’s metal wristband vibrated, dismissing the flying nimbus with but a thought. And with another thought, the cloudmulet gave him the cold sensation of information. Like icy snakes slithering at the back of his head, giving him the faint recollection of the Evening Clouds map.

“It’s midday, so none should be at their dorms. I’ll head way to the cafeteria,” Shuren said to himself as his feet softly landed on the dirt. Once, the same fall would have caused him to dull the bones in his legs. Now, it was a landing he could easily soften with the mastery of his Qi.

There was no need to interrupt anyone’s training or classes. After walking around he found his way into the public cafeteria, a simple building of a small height, where the smell of soup and tender slow-cooked meat carried across the air. In Evening Clouds, everyone ate together the same meals, and all meals were free as well, which helped most of the outer disciples that didn’t come well off.

There were a few groups of people, including a rather rotund boy that ate on his own, having rather large servings. Shuren went to one of the groups, bowing and clasping his hands in respect. Even though he didn’t need to as an Inner Disciple, he had to be courteous to gather information.

“Fellow disciples, it is good to see you.” The table had instinctively stopped eating to pay their attention to their senior. They all clasped back in synchronicity, not daring to disrespect an inner disciple.

It wasn’t hard to tell that Shuren was one. Aside from his clothes, that didn’t follow the standard one tone dark orange, they could instinctively tell as cultivators that he was a higher level above them.

“Senior Brother, it is good to see you as well.” The oldest of the bunch, Luo said. “My name is Luo. Do you remember me from that expedition to the Dezuan Forest?”

“Ah, it is you. You did well back then. Then Luo, you know of my cousin Liu Wei? I’m here to ask some questions.” Shuren ensured he lowered his voice and held a stern face. Most of the people in the group instinctively looked away. Even the other tables of people eating lunch had stopped talking, the ambience of chatter having long gone.

Luo held a strained face, as if recalling the incident he knew of. “Yes, I presume you’re here to ask about what happened to him.”

Shuren nodded. “He’s my younger cousin. I’d like to know the situation around him.”

Everyone shuffled in their seats, waiting for their leader, Luo, to speak.

He put on a sombre expression. “It’s a bit complicated. Wei had a fight with another Inner Disciple. They had both recently graduated at the same time, and were known rivals. Wei has a relationship with an outer disciple girl, so he still frequents the Evening Clouds. Unfortunately…” Luo had scrunched up his face, clearly uneasy to recall what happened next.

Shuren slightly narrowed his eyes, intently listening.

“His rival had unexpectedly made a move on his woman, forcing himself onto her. To protect her honour, he challenged that scumbag to a duel.” Luo continued, but his face contorted from disgust, even raising a clenched fist on his table. “But that human waste refused, instead lunging to fight him outside the dorms. And when we found the state of my dear brother Wei… He warned all of us to stay away to be safe, and he would go call for help.” Luo almost began tearing up at how his friend had been wronged.

Shuren’s jaw clenched the further he talked, and he took a deep and long breath. Now was not the time to react.

Shuren asked with a cold voice, “Who is this rival of his?”

“You should know him very well, senior brother. After all, he was the one who ran away like a coward on that night.”

Shuren narrowed his eyes, with a corded neck. “Tez…”

Calming himself down, Shuren looked at the group on the table. “If any of you is also a witness, then come give me more information.”

They all shook their heads, instead retelling incidents of how Tez was the biggest piece of trash they’d ever seen. How he always had a lecherous look, and had a significantly terrible reputation.

Shuren went to the other tables of people eating. All of them had said similar things.

“That guy Tez, he thinks just because he’s an orphan he gets the right to do what he wants. The sect gave him a scholarship only because of that. Just because he’s been born without a family doesn’t mean shit. Everyone else here also has something they go through.”

“That guy would always break weapons in the training hall, frustrating everyone else. We don’t have an unlimited supply of training weapons, stupid kid.”

“Tez, he-”

“All of you guys shut up! Tez wouldn’t do something like that!”

Shuren looked at the direction of the voice.

A large man with a bulbous belly started tearing as he yelled. “You guys know nothing!”

Dozens of eyes had drilled their sight into the lone man. He shivered.

A bang could be heard on the table. “What do you know, Fatty Chen? You weren’t even there as a witness, just your feelings aren’t enough to overturn the facts. Just because he gives you his leftover meals means nothing!” Luo’s face soured, reminiscing the terrible incident.

Shuren walked to the table where Fatty Chen sat, maintaining a stern expression.

“Chen, what I need to know is what happened on the day of the incident. Don’t use your relationship with him to defend him without proof.” Shuren scolded him, giving him an opportunity to speak. Fatty Chen lowered his head staring at the table, with tears in his eyes.

Shuren sighed. The situation was far messier than he thought. “Luo, where can I find Tez.”

It was best to simply ask the source himself.

Before anyone else would say a word, Luo spoke. “At night, he’s always training right outside of these dorms, near the forest. If you come by tonight, you can handle him.”

Shuren understood the meanings behind his words.

I couldn’t get any more information on the nature of the incident. Everyone’s being cagey around me.

Shuren grew irritated when he had to consider his face. Afterall, his own cousin had potentially been crippled, and he decided to enact the same punishment on the person who did it.

But he doesn’t have the qualification to do that in the Seven Clouds Sect, not when there’s already an organisation that handles the affairs of grievances and punishments between disciples. Shuren, an Inner Disciple, didn’t have the power nor influence to do as he wished. In fact, he doubted Core Disciple’s could do it anymore.

He would be insulting the Seven Clouds Sect for not going to them on this matter. He would be insulting his reputation for fighting someone clearly below his level. And he would be chastised for resorting to underhanded tactics, facing someone away from others rather than holding a public duel. Worst of all, he would implicate the Liu Clan, as someone who decided to join a sect far from home.

The last thing he absolutely needed was for his political opponents to gain a chokehold on his status to continue to wall him down, unable to become patriarch.

When he walked past a training hall, a young girl who had been running bumped into him. Naturally, he didn’t budge, but the girl had tumbled.

“S-sorry. I’m so sorry.” Her unkempt black hair was endowed with a branch. A silvent. Why were all silvents he ended up meeting at the sect so awkward? Shuren wondered.

When she saw he wore his own unique robe, she shuddered. “I’m so sorry, Senior brother, please forgive me for dirtying your clothes.”

He sighed. That combined with his stoic expression made her grow into another panic. It was ridiculous how this girl would start stuttering and panicking over such a useless matter.

“You’re Arene. The one on that expedition. No matter. I want to ask you a question.”

“It is good to see you again, senior brother!” She bowed down while saying with vigour.

Shuren could only frown at the subservience of the girl.

It felt far too overplayed.

“Do you know of Liu Wei? He’s my younger cousin. I’m here to ask if you’ve been a witness to any strange incidents recently.”

Her eyes shuddered. “The- The great Liu Clan!” She knelt down. “Junior sister sends her blessings to the Liu Clan.”

Shuren groaned.

“Enough! Get up and stop making a mockery out of yourself. This is the Seven Clouds Sect, not the Liu Clan,” Shuren commanded. “I came here to ask for questions, not to be treated like some piddling child that needs the attention of others!”

This isn’t normal. Is she doing this on purpose to irritate me or what?

“Do you know anything about what happened to Wei?” Shuren was already getting tired. He decided this was today’s last interaction.

She looked down. “Yes, he’s…a prodigy of the Liu Clan. He…” By this point, a few of the other disciples had gathered nearby after leaving the training hall. A few of the girls waited around, looking at her.

Shuren would’ve preferred to have a one on one conversation in private, but it wasn’t really possible in a public place like the sect. It wouldn’t be appropriate for him to ask her to meet him in a secluded place as well.

“Speak your mind. I want nothing but the truth.” Those words bore on her mind heavily.

She gritted her teeth. “He’s a victim. He tr-tried to protect the girl he liked but Tez hurt him,” the girl answered with a strange tone. The same group of girls still loitered around, looking at her back.

Shuren massaged on the ridge of his nose with his fingers.

Something’s off.
