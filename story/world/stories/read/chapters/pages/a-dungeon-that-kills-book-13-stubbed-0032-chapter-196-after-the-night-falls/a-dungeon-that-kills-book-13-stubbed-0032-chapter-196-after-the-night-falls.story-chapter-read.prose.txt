“I’d heard what happened to her,” Cedric said with a heavy sigh as the door swung shut behind them. “Even so, I wasn’t prepared for this. Seeing it with my own eyes is something else entirely.”

“Same here,” Viktor said. “I could barely recognize her when I came back to this place. It had been only a week, just one week, and she...”

Cedric said nothing. He didn’t ask why Viktor hadn’t been at his sister’s side from the very beginning. He didn’t ask what had kept him away during that one week. In fact, he didn’t react at all. Clearly, he had heard the story, either from Rhea or someone else.

Out in the corridor stood the remaining members of the party. Unlike Cedric, they looked much as they always did: Lucian with his long face and big nose, Noi’ri with his mottled fur and hunched posture. As for Fiora, well, Viktor had seen her just recently at Mandragora’s place, so it was hardly a surprise her appearance was exactly as he remembered.

All four of them had come to the Sanctuary to visit Claire, but of course, getting too many people into her room at once would have disturbed her rest. So only Cedric had actually gone inside with him, while the other three stayed here in the corridor.

“I still can’t believe things could’ve turned out this way,” Lucian murmured.

Viktor shrugged. “Sometimes bad stuff just happen. We don’t have a say in that matter; the only thing we can do is make the best of the cards we’ve been dealt.”

“You’re right,” Cedric said. “We will save her, and we will make that bastard pay. Just leave it to us.”

Viktor arched a brow. “What do you mean?”

“It means,” Fiora said, “that our party will be the ones representing Daelin in the competition.”

Viktor chuckled, glancing at Cedric. “Your mother is allowing that?”

“She didn’t like the idea at all when I told her,” Cedric said. “But I stood my ground. It’s not as though there’s a better party in Daelin to put forward anyway. Well, maybe Ekon’s. But they don’t seem particularly interested in this contest, and they’re not at their full strength either. So it falls to us. In the end, she agreed, though I wouldn’t say she did so with any great enthusiasm.”

“You know, none of you are obliged to put yourselves in harm’s way over this.”

“I’ve heard about your theory from Rhea,” Cedric said with a slow nod. “And I find it plausible enough. Still, it doesn’t sit right with me to stake everything on an unknown entity like the dark spirit. I want to do everything within my abilities. I want victory to come from my own power.”

Viktor studied the young man for a moment. “Well,” he said. “If you’re determined, I won’t try to stop you. But be careful, and remember that there’s no need to throw yourselves into unnecessary danger. If the dark spirit does show up and start attacking the Iskora team, then it’s their fight, not yours. The moment that happens, put as much distance as possible between your party and the battle. Just pull back and wait for the dust to settle before you make your next move.”

“I know. I won’t do anything reckless.”

Still, Viktor thought, this might introduce some complications to the plan.

He had intended to drop a big rock across the entrance to the great tomb and call it a day, but now, he might need to come up with a more sophisticated solution to keep the Daelin team from interfering with his fight against Xander and his men.

Well, he would think about it later. Today he had something else that needed to be done, and it was just about time.

“The sun is setting soon,” he said. “I have to head home before the curfew begins. The last thing I want is to run into trouble with those bastards.”

“You’re not staying here tonight?” Fiora asked.

“I have something I need to take care of,” Viktor said. “Jeanne and Rhea will look after Claire for me.”

“If you’re going back now, then we can walk together,” Cedric said.

And so he left the Sanctuary with them. They parted ways at the town square, where he bought a meatwrap from the Southern man’s shop. Then he made his way home, quickly ate his dinner, and turned his mind to what came next.

“Celeste, teleport me to the dungeon.”

[Understood.]

His surroundings rearranged themselves around him, and in an instant, he found himself standing beside his Dungeon Core, her pale light casting long shadows across the stone walls of the Core Room.

This visit was going to be brief, though.

Without a word, he walked to a nearby table, where the items he needed had already been laid out and waiting. He grabbed the ink pot and the golden ringed cross, putting them into his pockets.

“Teleport me back.”

[Yes, Master. Please, be careful.]

“I will,” he said with a smile. Even though this should not involve any risk at all, it never hurt to be prudent.

After returning to his room, he moved to the window and pushed it open. Outside, the sun had slipped behind the rooftops, and the curtain of night had fully settled upon Daelin. The town was draped in deep blue shadow, broken only by the occasional flicker of a lantern in a doorway below.

He activated his wind gems, and the familiar pressure built under his feet as he rose into the sky. He paused there for a moment, slowly turning to orient himself against the darkened skyline. Then, he flew north.

This part of Daelin was what passed for a “middle-class” district—meaning it was poor by the standards of everywhere else. The Adventurer’s Guild was here, so the streets were thick with cheap inns that catered to the adventurers. In these places, there was no pretense of comfort or privacy: eight to ten people might be packed into a single room on a busy night, sleeping in shifts on straw pallets or simply on the floor. Other than the inns, there were also the houses of small vendors, Guild employees, and caravan station clerks, people who had just enough to keep a roof over their heads.

And it was to the home of one such “clerk” that he was now making his way.

He slowed as he reached it, hovering silently above for a moment before he activated “Dark Veil” and let the shadows wrap themselves around him. Then he descended, drifting downward to the window on the upper floor. He had been here several times before, though only to deliver letters that carried his instructions. But tonight was different. Tonight, he was going to enter.

He reached out, touched the windowpane, and pulled it open. She had left it unlatched, as he had told her to. Slipping inside, he drew the window shut behind him.

The room was dark, with no lamp or candle burning. The only light here was a thin, pale shaft of moonlight that cut through the darkness, just enough to sketch the outlines of a table, a wardrobe, and a narrow bed. He drifted to the deepest corner of the room, and sat down.

Then, he waited.

It wasn’t long before he heard the sound of the front door opening downstairs, then the low murmur of voices floating up through the floorboards, then the footsteps on the stairs, unhurried and close together. Finally, the door to this room swung open, and two silhouettes walked inside. A man and a woman.

“It’s dark here,” the man said. His voice was young, almost boyish. No wonder he had fallen for the oldest trick in the book.

“I could light the lamp,” the woman said, her voice carrying a warmth that could melt iron. “But... should I? What we’re here to do doesn’t require any light. Unless...” A pause, perfectly timed. “...you’d prefer a proper look while you’re at it.”

The man swallowed; the sound of it was audible across the room.

“I... I... Is this real? You’re not... you’re not pulling some kind of prank on me, right?”

I promise you, Viktor thought from his corner, this is considerably worse than a prank.

“You’re so funny. But that’s exactly why I like you.” The woman giggled. “I’m glad that you came.”

“Of course I came,” the man said. “When you said to meet you here, I thought... I assumed that you needed my help with something, something you couldn’t discuss at the station with other people around. But then, you told me to come upstairs, and I—”

“Shh.” The woman’s silhouette stepped closer, the moonlight tracing her curves as she pressed herself against the man. She raised her hand and pressed a single finger to his lips. “You think too much. You’re always so serious.”

“I... I...” The man’s hands came up and hovered there, uncertain where they were allowed to land. “I still can’t believe this is actually happening. You know, I’ve always been thinking about... about you.”

“I know you have,” the woman murmured, reaching up to toy idly with a lock of his hair. “I’ve seen the way you look at me.”

The man laughed nervously. “I just... I didn’t want to presume. You’re the most beautiful woman I’ve ever met. You’re so way out of my league, and I thought—”

“You’re so tense,” the woman said, cutting him off. She rose on her tiptoes and brushed her lips to his cheek. “Let me help you relax.”

And the sound the man made in response was somewhere between a groan and a whimper.

Alright, Viktor thought. Enough of this. So he activated “Change Voice” and let the words come out low and flat.

“Leave us.”

He could do it the instant they entered the room, of course, but Yvonne’s performance had been so effortlessly convincing that a curious part of him had wanted to see exactly how far she would take it. Nevertheless... yes, this was enough. There was no reason to go any further.

“What—!?”

The man flinched as though he had been struck. He spun toward the corner, his eyes searching the dark for the source of the voice.

Yvonne, on the other hand, didn’t waste even a single breath. She had already moved away from the man, crossing to the door in a few soundless steps, slipping through it, and drawing it shut behind her with a soft click. The entire sequence took less than three seconds from start to finish.

Professional, indeed. Viktor chuckled. Very professional.

Well, she had played her part beautifully. Now it was his turn.

And so, he launched himself forward, a sphere of water already conjured above his palm.
