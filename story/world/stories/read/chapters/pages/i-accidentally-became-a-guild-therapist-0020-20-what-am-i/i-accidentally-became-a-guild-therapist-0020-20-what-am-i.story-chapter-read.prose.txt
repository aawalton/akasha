I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 20 – What Am I?

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS: Arc II closes quietly. Livia writes. The guild is asleep. The questions that have been accumulating since Chapter 11 are not answered tonight— but they are named. And Miles comes to sit with her. Not to fix anything. Just because she is there.

It was late.

Not dramatically late—not the kind of late that means something is wrong. Just the ordinary late of a guild that had spent three days recovering from a wedding and was now, collectively, asleep or close to it. The main hall was empty. The guild garden was dark. Glimmer was presumably wherever Glimmer went at night, which Livia had learned not to question because the answers tended to be either mundane or metaphysically complicated.

Livia was at the small table in the planning room, because the planning room had the best lamp and the door had a latch and nobody had a reason to come looking for her.

She had her notepad open to a fresh page.

She had been sitting with the fresh page for twenty minutes.

[System Note: You are awake. The guild is asleep. You have not written anything yet. The system is not going to ask why. The system is simply noting that the page is there when you are ready.]

Thank you, system.

Very helpful.

She picked up her pen.

She did not write notes. She did not write case summaries or progress updates or the careful clinical observations she usually committed to paper after sessions.

She just wrote.

I have been here for eight months.

I know what I do here. I sit with people while they figure out what they feel. I give things names. I notice the gap between what someone says and what they mean and I wait in it with them until they close the gap themselves. I am good at this. I was not good at it when I arrived. I became good at it here, in this world, with these people.

I do not know what I am.

Not in the way that people mean when they ask the question as philosophy. I mean it literally. The subreddit analysis thread said I don’t fit any known entity classification. The deleted comment said the same thing. T.S. said I am not supposed to be here. There is a classification review pending from an external party that nobody has identified. Aldous checked the registry and found no match. I am a Mental Supporter, level 7, in a game called Aethelgard, and I do not have a player ID and I am not in the NPC registry and nobody on this server knows what I am.

I know what happened before. I was in the real world. I was a psychology student who was failing her thesis. I was walking home from a bakery. There was a motorcycle. And then there was this.

The gap between the motorcycle and this is where I do not know.

I have tried to remember it. There is something there— not a blackout, a gap. A specific nothing that feels like it should be something. Like a word you almost know.

[System Note: Memory access restricted. Reason: classified. The system acknowledges it has said this before. The system acknowledges it is not helpful. The system does not have clearance to say more. The system is sorry.]

The system is sorry.

The system has clearance levels.

There is someone above the system.

I have been inside this system for eight months and I am only now understanding that.

She turned to a new page. Wrote at the top: What I know.

T.S. sent a message to the quest board three days before I arrived. They said I am not supposed to be here and neither are they. They have been trying to reach me since before I arrived. They have not tried again since the first message, which was deleted in four seconds by an automated system faster than standard moderation.

There are boot prints in Whispering Cavern that don’t match any registered player or NPC. The same tread pattern was at Aldous’ quest board six weeks before I arrived. Elder Tobren saw them in his garden path. Whoever made them has been moving through this server without being logged.

My server classification is under external review by an unknown party. This was triggered when I was classified as Server-Wide Asset. Miles said “that’s not how this works.” The review has not resolved.

A comment on the subreddit said I don’t have a player ID and don’t fit known entity classification, and that people outside this server have noticed and some of them are not friendly. It was deleted in four seconds. Same deletion speed as the T.S. message.

Elder Tobren described the watching as protective, not predatory. He was specific about the distinction.

Alondra— the healer who left one month before I arrived— is watching from outside and wants to talk. She left for reasons she thought were good. She is not sure they were. She watched the wedding.

She turned to another new page. Wrote: What I don’t know.

Who is T.S.

What the gap is between the motorcycle and this.

Who the external party is that requested the classification review.

Who made the boot prints and why they have never been logged.

What “outside this server” means and who is there.

Whether T.S. is the same person as the unlogged boot prints.

Whether any of this is connected to what I am.

She looked at the two lists for a long time.

The first list is getting longer.

The second list is not getting shorter.

These are related.

[Achievement Unlocked: Named the Things. +0 EXP.]

[The system notes: naming something is not the same as resolving it. The system notes: it is still more than not naming it. The system suggests sleep. The system knows you will not sleep yet.]

She turned to a third page.

This one she did not head with a question. She just started writing.

I did not come here on purpose. I did not choose this guild or this role or this world. I arrived with zero stats and a notepad and a job description that said: listen to adventurer woes, offer comfort, log outputs.

Bron cried on my bench on day two. I gave him a breathing exercise I half-remembered from a lecture I had mostly slept through. He tanked a wyvern the next morning and felt something. That was the whole thing. That was how it started.

Now I know that Bron’s fear is not immortality— it’s erasure. He is afraid that if nothing can mark him, nothing will remember him. He talks to his plushie about it when he thinks nobody is listening. He is getting better at saying it out loud.

Phina spent eighteen months building a case for love because she needed to be certain before she asked. She got married to a slime today. The dungeon was delighted. I was, too.

Nyx went into a dark floor alone, cleared it, turned off his flashlight for four seconds, turned it back on. He said “thanks for coming” into the gap between a door and a frame while already halfway through it. I need a new category for him and I still don’t have one. I think the category is just: person I trust.

Alaric bets on better things now. Miles said so. Miles was right.

Miles made coffee. He said the meeting was good. He said three years without saying three years. Progress: 15%. For Miles, 15% is a long way.

I do not know what I am in this world’s taxonomy. I know what I am to them. That might be enough to work with.

[System Note: You have been awake for three hours writing. The system notes the three pages. The system notes this is the first time you have written about yourself rather than about your patients. The system considers this progress. +800 EXP.]

[Mental Supporter Lv. 7: 6,100 / 7,000 EXP.]

I got EXP for writing in my own journal.

The system is very consistent about what it counts.

I am almost not surprised anymore.

The door opened.

Not loudly. Not urgently. The specific quality of a door opened by someone who had checked whether the lamp was on before deciding to knock, and had then decided not to knock.

Miles came in and stood in the doorway for a moment, taking in the three pages of notes and the cold coffee and the late hour.

“You’re awake,” he said.

“So are you,” Livia said.

He came in. Sat down across from her, not in the chair he had used for the strategy meeting but the one beside it, the one that did not have a table directly between them. He did not ask what she was writing. He looked at the lamp instead, which was a thing he did when he was giving her space to decide how much to say.

He checked the lamp before coming in.

He has done this before. The lamp thing.

He checks whether I’m there before he decides whether to come find me.

I do not think he knows I have noticed this.

Filed in the section marked: things I am not ready to say out loud yet.

She closed the notepad. Not to hide it—just because she was done for the night.

“I was writing about the questions,” she said.

“The T.S. questions,” he said. Not surprised. He had known she was carrying them.

“Those. And the others.”

He was quiet for a moment.

“Have you figured anything out?”

“Not tonight.” She looked at the closed notepad. “I just needed to name them all in the same place.”

“Does that help?”

“Usually,” she said.

He nodded, in the way that meant he was accepting this as a real answer rather than a deflection.

They sat for a moment in the quiet of late night in a guild hall, the particular peace of a building that is full of people who are asleep.

Then Livia said: “Alondra contacted me.”

The quality of Miles’ stillness changed. Not tense. Just precise.

“She’s ready to talk,” Livia said. “She watched the wedding broadcast. She said she was outside the door when Phina first talked about Glimmer. She said she didn’t think she’d be watching from outside when it actually happened.”

Silence.

“She used her real name,” Livia added. “She has been anonymous for three weeks. She used her name.”

Miles looked at the lamp for a long time.

“How is she?” he said, finally. Not what did she say or what does she want. How is she.

How is she.

He asked how she is.

He has been angry at her for seven months.

The first thing he asked is how she is.

“I don’t know yet,” Livia said, carefully. “She hasn’t told me much. She said she left for reasons she thought were good reasons and she’s not sure they were.”

He processed this.

“You’re going to talk to her,” he said.

“Yes.”

“And then?”

Livia looked at him.

“That depends on a lot of things I don’t know yet,” she said. “Including things she’ll need to tell me. And things that are going to be your decision eventually, not mine.”

He was quiet for a while.

“She was the healer,” he said. To the lamp again..

“I know.”

“She was good at it.”

“I know that too.”

Another silence.

“This is going to be complicated,” he said.

“Yes,” Livia agreed.

“But you’re going to do it anyway.”

It was not a question.

“Yes.”

He looked at her directly. Not evaluating. Just present.

“Okay,” he said.

One word. Complete sentence.

He said okay.

He did not say: don’t. Or: be careful. Or: I’m not sure about this.

He said okay.

He is trusting me with this.

That is not a small thing.

That is the largest thing.

He did not leave right away.

She did not ask him to. They sat in the planning room in the late-night quiet, and at some point Livia made fresh coffee because the previous mugs had gone cold, and Miles accepted his without comment, and they talked about nothing in particular for a while—the dungeon contract renewal, the quest board volume, whether Glimmer was going to continue claiming territory in the guild garden or had reached its natural boundary (current assessment: unclear).

Normal things. Guild things. The kind of conversation that exists not because of its content but because of what it means to have it: we are here, and it is late, and that is fine.

Eventually Livia said: “I should sleep.”

“You should have slept three hours ago,” Miles said.

“Correct.”

He stood. She stood. At the door, before leaving, he said:

“The questions. The T.S. thing. The classification review.” He paused. “When you’re ready to talk through it, I’m here.”

When you’re ready.

Not: I need to know. Not: tell me everything.

When you’re ready.

“I know,” she said.

He nodded once. Left.

Livia stood at the door for a moment.

I know what I am to them.

That might be enough to work with.

I think it is.

She turned off the lamp and went to bed.
