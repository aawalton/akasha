It’s your greed that fuels you, Sys. And it’s your greed that’s gonna bury you.

Ethan, Fauna, Klax and Tara faced Sys in the center of the swirling vortex of Argwyll’s ocean. Each of them was, at this point, more than ready to see the bastard parasite angel be destroyed, and understood Ethan’s plan.

But it had been a sudden, and quite unexpected turn of events.

Tara had screamed as the world died around her. Klax had held her close as he saw the Tialax guide perish in the green fire that erupted from Keadmon’s Cradle.

And yet, Fauna had been strangely calm. Because ever since she’d shared that kiss with Ethan, she’d come to understand what he’d been trying to communicate to her back in her room. And possibly even before that.

She’d come away from him with a gift, and a promise that lay at its heart:

[Hidden Skill]

Skill Graft (Grade S)

A creation of the Architect, bestowed to Archon Ethan as his part of their bargain.

Effect: All Skills Duplicated and transferred to [One] other User on touch.

She’d felt him apply the Skill to her in the moment their lips met, and with a little spark of shock, she’d felt the energies of all his Hosts – all his Skills – flow into her body and consume her.

But she’d managed to stand. Because beyond all that raw power – power that shouldn’t be wielded by anyone as he said – she knew he trusted her to be the one to carry it through.

In that moment, she’d understood his intention without him even having to communicate anything to her.

“It’s not himself he was ever planning to destroy,” she’d told her companions as they balked against her summoning up the scintillating hexagonal shields of [Repulsor Shield] combined with [Shield of Aegis] to nullify the damage from Sys’s rebirth. “It was Sys. It was always Sys.”

Tara and Klax had both balked at this.

“The System?” Tara asked through the storm.

“It’s been playing us all from the beginning,” Fauna replied, keeping her staff and protective skills steady, trying to keep her body from being overwhelmed. “I don’t know how. I don’t know why. But it wanted Ethan to seize power – it wanted him to overcome Keadmon not so that he could make a better world, but so that there were no more obstacles in its way. It’s probably been waiting for this chance since it was bound to Karfangg.”

“How – how did he know?” Klax grunted. “And why did he not tell us?”

The varicose green beams of Sys’s expanding power sought out the life within the collapsing Umbral Monastery. Fauna kept her shields up, but even with their protection she knew the three of them could not withstand the sheer might of a new God’s strength. So, gritting her teeth, she activated [Balm of Krea] and began regenerating any and all damage that managed to slip through the cracks.

As the restorative powers of the enemy angel flowed through her hybrid veins, she answered Klax’s question as best she could:

“It would give the game away,” she said over the din. “Think about it: Sys has been the one person who’s listened to every conversation, observed every facet of Argwyll and us, all through Ethan’s eyes and ears. I don’t know how Ethan managed to get this Skill past it, and I don’t know if Sys knows he just gave me his cloned Skillset. I can only think that, hungry for power as it is, it simply wasn’t that interested in our moment together.”

“Being a third wheel and all that – I get it!”

The Umbral tower twisted and fell away, revealing the swirling vortex that the sea was becoming.

“But – what the hell can we do?” Tara screamed from Fauna’s back. “Even with all of Ethan’s Skills, can we take Sys down?”

“We don’t have to,” Klax smiled from beside her.

At Tara’s confused look, Fauna shot her a focused glance.

“Remember why Ethan first came to Camoran?” she asked her Minxit sister.

Tara had looked at both of them and known, then, that she’d been a fool to ever doubt her Archon.

“That sly son of a…but couldn’t he have at least given us a warning or something? I mean – all that theatricality, all that saying he needed to die –“

Fauna’s eyes grew cold. Tara – for the first time ever – was silenced by the Hopla girl’s stare alone.

“That hasn’t changed,” Fauna said. “Ethan sacrificed everything to draw Sys out – guessing that dangling ultimate power before it would coax it to make its move. There’s only one surefire way to beat something so mindlessly obsessed with raw power like that.”

Tara and Klax both exchanged knowing looks.

“…he’s got the Skill,” Tara said.

“And the will to use it,” Klax nodded solemnly.

“But can – can you really let him go?”

Fauna said nothing to Tara’s question.

Because right now, there was simply no time left.

She prepared a [Summon Illusion] S-Grade spell and told her companions of the last stage of their plan.

Holding both their hands, she asked if they were with Ethan to the very end – where he’d always wanted them to be.

And their reply was without hesitation.

Now, they stood with Ethan before the rage-fueled Sys, and reaffirmed the promise they’d made long, long ago:

“This is what we’re meant to do,” Klax said.

“Because it’s what we’ve chosen,” Tara nodded.

“Our Paths – everyone’s paths – has been leading us to this outcome from all the choices we’ve made. Us, Jun’Ei, Kaedmon, the Greycloaks, Artorious, and Ethan – we’ve all only ever had one enemy. And this victory against you belongs to all of us.”

Sys’s eyes flicked between all of them.

Then, even through his suspended state, he managed to coax out a chuckle.

How you’ve fed the hubris of these animals, he said. They really do take after you now, don’t they?

Ethan shook his hatty head.

You’re done, Sys, Ethan said. You could at least admit it and go out with some dignity.

I’m done, am I, Ethan? Not with only twenty seconds on the clock of your Time Stop.

That much was true – twenty seconds were ticking down. They had exactly that much time to silence the parasite once and for all.

You think to know my weaknesses, but you forget that I was part of you for long enough to know yours. You’re still clinging to your precious image of yourself as a hero, even now. But nothing – no Skill you have – can destroy me in the time you’ve got left after your little speech.

Ethan’s thready smile never slipped. And nor did those on his teams’ faces.

It’s not a Skill that belongs to me, that’s true, Ethan said. But it is the Skill that will end this, Sys. And once again, it’s the Skill you’d be most likely to overlook.

Fauna’s hands glowed with sudden vibrant power. Ethan closed his eye as she applied [Skill Graft] – granting him the copy of all the Skills Sys had taken away again.

Then the crimson eye of the Demon Hat opened again, and he was whole once more.

Only then did Sys see what Ethan’s ultimate gambit had been all along:

Soul Purge (DIVINE SKILL)

Effect: Complete System shutdown on contact with target. All Skills are wiped from the target’s mind. The User’s consciousness is also cleared.

You…Sys’s mind fumbled as Fauna brought the hat closer and closer to his face. You would really…

End myself to destroy you? Ethan replied. You better believe it. That’s exactly what you hated me for doing, after all. Giving up all the power in the world. It’s just not something you’d ever be able to understand, is it?

The tenth second of [Time Stop] ticked by. For a brief moment, he sounds of rushing waves, his Host’s beating heart, and the sounds of Ethan’s breathing in his own mind snapped away into silence.

He could do nothing but look on as the Demon Hat was inched towards him.

Nothing, that is, except try to keep from smiling.

Perhaps I am more…forgetful these days than I used to be.

From all around them, the Hybrids suddenly realized that they were being watched.

As the fifth last second of [Time Stop] ticked, five dark eyes suddenly appeared from outside the walls of the cyclone.

But you’ve forgotten something, too, Sys said.

Ethan’s eye bulged. He urged Fauna forward, but by then, it was well and truly too late.

…Time Stop has an effective radius.

Fauna surged forward, hands glowing with the last remnants of the [Skill Graft] she’d used on him, her silvery eyes blazing with that stubborn, righteous light that Sys would never understand. She thrust Ethan toward the parasite’s face—toward the one simple win condition in this entire ridiculous battle:

Touch him.

Wipe him.

End him.

But something moved in the storm.

Not a body. Not a wing. Not a spell-beam.

A shadow.

It slid between Ethan and Sys with a wet, impossible grace, like ink poured through air. For a half-second it was only a smear of darkness, and then it had shape: a ribbed silhouette, too thin to be alive, too dense to be smoke, its mouth the only feature that made sense—an open, hungry crescent filled with lambent green light.

He should have seen it - should have been able to sense Sys activate the ability in the moment he'd gotten out of his grip. But the irony was palpable: it was exactly the trick he'd played on the Greycloak Assassin back in the Southern mountains.

Ethan’s crimson iris widened.

And before he could look at the wry, alien grin that had spread across Sys's face, the darkwraith struck.
