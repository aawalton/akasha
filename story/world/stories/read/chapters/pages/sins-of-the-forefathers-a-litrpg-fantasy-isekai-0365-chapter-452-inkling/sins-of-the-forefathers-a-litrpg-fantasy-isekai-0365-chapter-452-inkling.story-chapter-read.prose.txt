I took a deep, deep breath, filling my artificial lungs to their absolute capacity…and held it for a moment.

None of it was strictly necessary. I technically didn’t even need to breathe, as a clone. None of the copied organs involved in the process was anything more than Aetherial constructs. No oxygen particles were being processed and circulated throughout my bloodstream within me. There was no blood period.

But the gesture sure as Hell helped me process just what Hermus was telling me.

Almost involuntarily, my eyes flickered over to Rafael, standing patiently off to the side. I don’t know just what the Lich saw on my face, but it was enough for him to slightly incline his head my way.

Ah.

So…what I was being told was nominally the truth. There was likely more to it, I’m sure. But…if what he was saying was true…

Then Hermus might have been one of, if not the primary backer of the god’s rebellion, thievery, and Ascension into Divinity.

My right hand twitched toward the, coincidentally, Netherim-manufactured laser pistol sitting innocuously at my hip. I very rarely used the thing, considering the scarcity of Flux Cores I had available for my use. I’d been holding back on it for so long because I wanted all of my shots to be used for the direct fight against Fynneas.

But for this?

I might make an exception.

Rafael likely didn’t recognize the weapon at my waist, but he likely did recognize a hostile movement when he saw one. The man had been a high-level, and high Level, Classer for literal centuries before his death, after all.

The look in his flaming eyes took on a warning tinge.

Alright. Fine.

I’d hear Hermus out. I’d table the sudden escalation into extreme violence.

For now.

Instead, I let out the deep breath I’d been holding and fixed the massive mole skull with a frown. “You’re telling me that you were the person who mentored the gods, before they were the gods. That you gave them not only the skills and knowledge of how to steal their Ascension from the System-as-it-was, turning it into the crippled System-that-is. But that you also gave them succor and a place to do it from, here in your own home. Do you…do you even understand the magnitude of what you did?”

I only realized just how loud my voice was getting, there at the end, until I was done and the echoes of my words were bouncing back at me. I wasn’t quite shouting at the former Great Spirit of Death…

But it was close.

Hermus was quiet for a moment. ‘Yes,’ He eventually replied, a note of quiet, sad acceptance in his voice. ‘I do.’

That only infuriated me more. “I’m not sure you do,” I hissed, my hands balling into fists. “The System is dying.”

My anger was…loosening my tongue, to the point that I just didn’t care about keeping secrets. This was not something I had shared with either Sylvia or Fade. As someone who wasn’t even connected to the System, the look on my Familiar’s face was a simple case of pure bafflement.

But Sylvia…

I didn’t care for the look of pure dread that had suddenly taken over her fair, Mithril features.

Nor the confused, almost betrayed cast to them.

I’d…deal with that later. For now, I wasn’t finished with this fucking rat.

“Because of whatever it is that your apparent students stole from the System, it ripped a bleeding hole into it, and it’s been slowly draining for millennia,” I breathed heavily, my anger still high in me. “And when they did that, they conquered entire planets in a bloody wave of ambition. Their rule was built on the deaths of countless innocents, not to mention their own people. Where are the Netherim, Hermus? Their absence points to the fact that they slaughtered them all, so your ‘students’ could rule as false gods! This isn’t even counting the events of the War in Heaven! And here you are, telling me that you might as well be directly responsible for all of it?! Is that what you’re saying?!”

Silence filled the chamber, then. The only noise that pierced it was my heavy, enraged breathing.

I grit my teeth when no answer was forthcoming. “Well?! Answer me!”

Quiet for only a few moments more. And then…

‘Like all things…’ Hermus began quietly. Enough so that, even with his strange method of communication, his words almost didn’t reach me. ‘None truly think themselves as invirtuous. At the end of all things, if one’s deeds result in catastrophic results…they did not arise from a place of malice. No…those who would claim the mantle of Divinity did so from a place of ambition. And it was not an ambition from which the purpose was genocide.’

I shook my head, looking away from Hermus’s once corporeal form. “Let me guess,” I said bitterly. “They wanted power.”

Power, power, power. It’s what it always seemed to come down to, in the end. Not just on Vereden, but even on Earth. People wanted power. Either over others or within themselves, the overly ambitious wanted the strength to assert themselves as mighty. To wield their strength to ends that placed themselves over others.

They were strong. If others were weak, it was their own fault.

Power, in the minds of the malign, was the ultimate expression of morality.

I was so tired of it.

I was surprised when instead of sadly agreeing with me…

Hermus chuckled. Maybe a bit forlorn. But the former Great Spirit audibly chuckled. ‘No, actually. Tell me, young Nathaniel Hart. What do you truly know of the society that the Netherim lived under?’

I paused, thrown off by the question.

The answer was…not a whole lot, honestly. The Netherim repository that I had been gifted by Travers had included only practical, and not cultural knowledge. Travers had deliberately removed everything within the library about who the Netherim had been, and not what they had made. What I did know had always struck me as a bit strange.

The Netherim were…from somewhere? Vereden, or maybe one of the other planets? I think some of the implications I had seen down in the bunker had been that they had been on some of the other planets. Was Vereden their homeworld? Was it one of the other planets? Possibly, but who knows?

But for the ones on Vereden, they had disdained the surface. While they were the ‘Netherim’, everyone else upon the planet was apparently the ‘Genirim’. Whatever the Hell that meant, I had no clue. But some things Aveline had told me in the past implied that it had nearly been forbidden for the Netherim to venture to the surface from their underground and hidden bunkers. They were not supposed to mingle or interact with the dirty, dirty Genirim, I suppose.

Why? Why, why, why, why? Questions like that had haunted me for a while. What was the purpose of the Netherim living as they had? Because they’d had one, I was sure of it. It wasn’t just a strange cultural tradition to live in hardened underground bunkers like they had either feared something…

Or they had to.

I didn’t buy for a moment that the entire reason the Netherim lived underground had been because of the Aetherial density. Aveline had told me ages ago about how Netherim children needed ‘adjusting’ to the Aether of Vereden, if they wanted to survive on it. It was the entire reason that every single Netherim seemed to have had the same emerald eyes, so eerily similar in shade to my own. If it were that easy to bypass that restriction, they would have conquered the surface in no time.

In the period of time that the Netherim had seemed to exist, the ‘Genirim’ upon the surface would have been little more advanced than tribal savages. With their advanced Aetherological science and literal laser weaponry, they could have conquered the planet in no time.

None of these considerations were new to me. Plenty of late nights had been spent contemplating the problem of the Netherim. Because it mattered to me. Not only for historical ones about the planet I now call home, but for practical ones.

I was one hundred percent certain that the mystery of the Netherim had something to do with the System. With the mystery of the Precursors. And about what had truly brought me to this planet.

I ached for that knowledge, sometimes. Even with how I had adjusted to life upon Vereden, even with how I had no desire to return to Earth at this point…

I just wanted to know why.

So, no. I didn’t really know much about the Netherim culturally, and I told Hermus that.

‘I’m not surprised,’ Hermus said evenly. ‘They were insular, even in my time. The Concordian Hierarchy had quite literally no contact with the Netherim in that age, before I descended into a physical form. There was…a vague understanding, on both sides I later learned, that we must each exist. It’s the Aether, you see. The simple presence of them under the surface of Vereden was enough to transfer across realms, carrying hints to their presence, and us vice versa.’

I nodded slowly to show I understood, before pausing. Something had just occurred to me, brought on by the presence of Rafael lingering nearby, listening to our conversation with open, rapt attention. “Wait a second. If you’re the father of Necromancy, then did you ever train a Netherim man by the name of Jonathon Travers? He, ah….” I gestured helplessly with my hands before giving up. “He would have been a kind of Healer called a ‘Doctor’?”

A very perceptible sense of confusion and bafflement filled the air, then. I think I had really stunned the former Great Spirit with that. ‘How could you possibly know that name?’  Hermus finally answered slowly, confusion in every otherworldly syllable.

“Ah…” I considered how to possibly put this before deciding blunt was the best solution. “I found the bunker beneath Goryu-I mean, Vereden’s tallest mountain. Inside, he was…still kind of around, as a Lich. A…lot happened, he set off the reactor, blew up the mountain, and…here I am?”

Stunned silence. Both from Hermus, and Rafael.

“Just what have you been up to?” The other Lich muttered to himself in confusion.

‘To think Jonathon had lingered, after all this time…’ Hermus whispered to himself, seemingly forgetting about the rest of us. ‘What could have driven such a thing…?’

I kept my silence on that. The existence of Aveline was not something that I felt like spreading around.

Hermus eventually broke out of his shock. ‘To answer your question, though we are jumping ahead somewhat…yes, I knew him. As I said, in those days, my disciples and I created the foundations of Necromancy. It had been several centuries since I began my work when Jonathan sought out these caverns and my instruction. He was an apt pupil, more interested in the possible medical applications of the art. I welcomed his inclinations. It is a noble thing to look upon Death and wonder how it might assist the living. Still, his research bore limited fruit in the years he spent here. I…had no idea he’d picked up enough upon the necessities of Lichdom before he left. Thank you for helping him move on. I hope that he has found peace at last.’

“If you’re really on the other side, you…could go find him, maybe?” I said leadingly.

To my disappointment, Hermus just chuckled, immediately seeing through my ruse. ‘It does not work that way, and that is all I shall say. But to continue, those whom you knew as the gods…they were the last generation of my students before the fall of the Netherim. Fourteen in all, they came to me during a particularly fractious time in Netherim society. The younger generations were brushing up against the walls set in place by the Netherim Elders, those who ruled their underground societies. They chafed at the restrictions. Study with my enclave was one of the few options allowed to those who wished to be out from under the thumb of the Elders, and yet still compliant with the edicts that forbade interaction with the surface and the Genirim. And so, those fourteen came to me, and they were rowdy indeed. Each had their own quirks, and each their own talents. In time, as the years passed, they…began to question matters more. What, exactly, was the point of it all?’

“The point of what?” I leaned forward hungrily. Was I finally about to get some answers?

‘That’s just the thing,’ Hermus laughed helplessly. ‘They didn’t know. None of the Netherim people knew or understood their grand Purpose but the Elders. It was a very authoritarian society, you must understand. As a people, the Netherim had a unified Purpose that they were, through all of their endeavors, working toward. Every hidden city, their creches, were assigned to one facet of the Purpose. Only their Council of Elders truly understood the whole…”

“But by meeting up here…” I said slowly, putting the pieces together. “Individual members of different creches were able to get some kind of idea.”

‘Exactly so,’ Hermus said with satisfaction. ‘Not the whole truth. Not even a true fraction of it. But an inkling, and that was enough. By the time of my last apprentices, word had spread among the Netherim populace, and discontent arose. The Elders had their own master, so to speak. Only…it was some kind of otherworldly force. Something that called itself…’

‘The System.’
