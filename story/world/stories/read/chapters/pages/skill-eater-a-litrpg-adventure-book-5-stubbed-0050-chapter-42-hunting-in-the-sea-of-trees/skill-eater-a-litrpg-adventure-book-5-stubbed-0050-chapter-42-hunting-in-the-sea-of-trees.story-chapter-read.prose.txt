Well before the sun rose, Edge found himself crossing the Lakelands while heading for the Sea of Trees.

With his Speed bolstered by Fear the Seasons and Warlord’s Mantle, his shadow body devoured the distance—effortlessly gliding across rough terrain without having to worry about corporal concerns like protruding roots or looping vines. He hoped to reach the borderlands in another few hours, which gave him plenty of time to ponder recent events en route.

The crew had debated their options until midnight, concocting strategies to determine the Legendary lens’s location and reveal the Ancient’s identity. They hadn’t come up with anything particularly promising, but Sakura would continue her efforts to hack the auction’s database, while Trapper would consult with information brokers to identify potential buyers.

While it was an important endeavor, it wasn’t their only pressing concern. The second and presumably final meeting with King would take place in four days, and Edge needed to maximize his gains before that fateful hour arrived.

Now that he’d hit stage three, he could start cycling up again. He would receive a bonus until he hit cycle seven, thanks to the experience he’d accrued while at the peak of stage two. Even more critically, it was time to add more skills to his collection. Skill-Eater had manifested two slots early, leaving him with seven to fill. Not to mention his Triple-Merger Token, which could free up two additional sockets if he came up with a promising way to use it.

He was also dead broke after restocking his supplies and refilling his Organ-Guard. Edge could always borrow Credits from his friends, but he hoped to return with enough parts and resources to cover basic expenses. As the horizon lightened to a murky predawn blue, he pondered his goals for this solo outing—a two-day trip to hunt as many creatures as he could.

He had chosen the Sea of Trees in part because it was filled with powerful beasts that weren’t hidden below the waves, but also because he’d grown fond of Glide. He planned to kick off his skill-stealing spree by adding it to his permanent collection, freeing up an auxiliary slot to pick up any random power that seemed useful at the time.

His myriad dilemmas did nothing to stem the excitement welling within him as he pondered the possibilities his evolution afforded. After all, Edge had been eagerly awaiting this moment for months.

He had spent long hours contemplating which powers he wanted to add to his collection while combing through the societies’ libraries. He had paid close attention to every spell the monsters, beasts, hunters, and gladiators he’d encountered had cast—spoken to everyone he trusted about the advantages and drawbacks of various skills.

He didn’t know exactly what he wanted to Extract, but he had come up with some guidelines that would help him decide once he saw what options were on the table.

He had already determined that unless it was flat-out amazing, enabled insanely powerful combos, or let him overcome defenses that were problematic at present, there was no point in picking up more active attack skills. He would consider something that let him perform stealthy assassinations if he came across it, but he was more interested in powers that could control the battlefield over direct damage.

His mobility was in good shape, and he didn’t plan to add any movement skills unless he found a teleport like Blink, Swap, or Dart. He was happy with his physical defenses too, as Shadow Play and Harden could counter a wide range of attacks, and Rejuvenation could erase any damage he took from incidental fire.

On the antimagic side of the equation, he had Aegis to nullify elemental energy, his scythe for countering incoming spells, and Magebane for direct skill suppression. That being said, a Disruption-based area-of-effect skill like the Disruption Pulse Warren employed would be a welcome addition to his collection, as would a barrier like Sakura’s Disruption shield.

That brought Edge’s train of thought to enhancement skills.

He definitely wanted to steal another aura or two. Warlord’s Mantle was his primary aura, though Aegis and Mental Fortress could be used that way as well. Running multiple auras at once counted as a combo, but with his current Generation, he could handle stacking two auras for short spurts. Better still, he could use them all at once while Overdrive was active, which would be even more amazing now that the ultimate’s Amplification bonus had tripled and its duration had expanded to five minutes.

More passives were always welcome, since they never counted as combos and the drain was inconsequential by this point. But what kind of passives would be best? His top priority was skills that augmented his attributes. The only one he had right now was Fear the Seasons’ passive component. It boosted his Speed at the moment but would switch to Disruption when winter arrived.

As far as active, non-combat powers went, his short-range detection was in a good place thanks to All-Seeing Gaze, and Shadow Play was sufficient for stealth. He wouldn’t mind a long-range sensory skill like Sakura’s scrying magic or Riller’s drone, and some type of communication magic would be nice too.

However, as enticing as they were, those weren’t the only options on the table. Edge had focused on acquiring powers to help him survive throughout most of his adventures. But now that he had so many slots to work with, it was time to consider skills that didn’t offer direct combat utility but would enhance his development in other ways.

He could grab a learning skill to facilitate his growth in target areas. Maybe one that makes mastering the glaive easier. He could also pick up some harvesting skills to increase his income or some social skills that would let him sense emotions and detect lies.

Hell, I could even try out a crafting skill or two. I don’t have time to learn something complex like armor or weapon smithing, but some basic alchemy to let me craft consumables in the field might come in handy, especially since I can carry delicate equipment and volatile reagents safely inside my rune. I could even do the crafting in my vault to take advantage of the time dilation or get some assistance from my avatars.

He wasn’t sure where to pick up some of those skills without stealing them from a person, which he was loath to do to anyone who wasn’t a mortal enemy. However, that was a problem for another day. There were plenty of juicy powers on the menu in the extreme-threat biome ahead. He already knew where to acquire Glide and had spotted several promising skills while passing through the biome on his way to the free city.

Since he had plenty of open sockets, he planned to try out a range of powers before deciding on a final selection, and he was so eager to get started that he wanted to dance. Edge was happy to leave his troubles in the rearview mirror for a few days and focus on something fun, savoring the experiences that only life on a magical world could provide. He enjoyed breakfast while perched atop a boulder, watching the glory of the rising sun paint the heavens in neon warmth that was reflected in the water below.

It was a beautiful morning. He basked in the biome’s beauty while searching for promising skills. Along the way, he watched for signs that he was being followed via traditional means—relieved that he no longer had to worry about being tracked with spells. He didn’t come across anything interesting just yet and arrived at the borderlands in record time, staring at the titanic trunks looming a bit further ahead.

He rematerialized and used Foebinder to pull him into the branches, following the living highway formed by the trees’ broad limbs. From the Sea’s middle stratum, he had a good view of the forest floor, along with everything living in the branches and an occasional glimpse of beasts dwelling in the canopy or flying through the cerulean skies above.

He spotted several skills that were worth considering, writing down where he’d observed the beasts along with the details of their magic. Along the way, he gathered random resources, grateful that Dragon’s Greed made what would normally be a long and arduous process a trivial task.

A little later in the day, he came across one of the beasts he’d come here to hunt.

The giant squirrels were curious, cute, and friendly, but that didn’t stop him from taking one down. Edge was a beast hunter of Ord. While most of the creatures he killed were vicious predators, sometimes his targets were far less menacing. Even so, he wanted to make this quick and painless so the critter didn’t suffer. He had a vial of paralytic poison sitting in his vault, and now was a perfect time to break it out.

He hadn’t been using his chakram much lately, as the killing tool Ander had crafted wasn’t designed to damage high-stage opponents, but the bone-and-brass ring was an ideal delivery system this time around. Foebinder wanted to become more skilled at using weapons, so instead of throwing the chakram himself, he reviewed his plan for engaging with the left chain while using a special rag to apply the alchemical concoction.

With that out of the way, he handed the ring to the living links and turned into shadow form, following the man-sized beast until it was distracted by harvesting plump red berries from a sprawling bush. He came to a stop on a nearby branch, rematerialized, and told Foebinder to take its best shot. The black chain reached back and sent the chakram flying with impressive precision, biting into the squirrel’s shoulder with a bright spray of blood.

The creature took off in a flash, but it was already too late. Less than a minute later, it came to a staggering stop before crashing to the ground. Edge walked over and knocked the squirrel out with his iceblade, leaving it vulnerable to Extraction.

He activated his ultimate ability and followed the twin chains into the creature’s core. Unsurprisingly, the beast’s inner world featured one of the sea’s towering trees. A banquet of fruit and nuts was growing within easy reach, and there was a comfortable den inside a hollow portion of the trunk. He perused the avatars long enough to make sure there wasn’t anything unexpected, then ordered the chains to attack.

They took off in a flash, chasing down a skill that took the form of a furry coat with wide flaps of skin that let the creature ride the breeze. They trapped the avatar in their coils, yanked the skill out of the squirrel’s core, and popped it into his mouth.

It didn’t fight back and was quickly subdued, spreading the flavor of freedom across his palate along with an image of charting a course between endless branches.

You have extracted the skill: Glide (Uncommon, Rank 3).

Digestion is complete.

Would you like to slot Glide now?

Edge confirmed the prompt and waited for the avatar to bond with its socket, transforming into a manmade wingsuit in the process. Next, he Absorbed the copy of Glide in his auxiliary slot and fed the experience to the fully functional version living in his kingdom. When he stole another skill and added it too, it was sufficient to take Glide to rank two then and there.

While he was here, he decided to grab the Common skill Climb, which he placed in his auxiliary slot until he came across something better. With Extraction’s new fifteen-hour cooldown, he planned to grab three more skills before calling it a night and would use the rest of the daylight to find his next target.

There was no way the squirrel could survive in the extreme-threat biome without its movement skills. Rather than let it live in fear until it was eaten by a local predator, he touched his iceblade to the beast’s head. The subzero chill froze its brain within a matter of seconds, killing the creature humanely while leaving its meat and hide in pristine condition.

He skinned and butchered the carcass, fed part of the meat and organs to Rue, and stored the rest in his vault to sell when he returned. With that out of the way, it was time to upgrade Glide. Edge stepped into his central chamber and pulled up his Guide to consider his options.

The skill Glide has advanced to Rank 2.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Please choose between one of the following options:

Warning: If no option is selected, your skill will remain unchanged and gain a slight bonus to mana efficiency.

Before he made his decision, Edge considered what role he hoped Glide would play as time went on.

The first option was solid. Scouting from above was a natural use for the skill, and being able to see and hear more clearly from further away would improve his aerial reconnaissance. It would also boost his agility, making Glide better-suited to combat and more useful indoors or when navigating obstructed spaces.

Moving on, the stealth route would help him avoid dangerous enemies, although it was too specialized for his needs, particularly since it wouldn’t work against predators positioned higher than he was.

By the time he reached the third choice, he knew it was the one for him. Achieving some form of flight was the reason he’d wanted to add Glide to his core collection. With any luck, that upgrade combined with Maelstrom will do the trick. Filled with a burgeoning tide of excitement, he said, “I’ll take option number three.”

When Edge uttered those words, the avatar began to change. An airfoil extended from the bottom of the wingsuit, allowing it to remain airborne indefinitely with a little help from Sea. He spent some time welcoming his newest power, then popped back into his body, hoping this next part would work out the way he intended.
