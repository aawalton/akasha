I stared out the window as we pulled onto the street our house was on, my head still spinning. I couldn’t wrap my head around what had happened. How had so much time passed whilst I’d been in the other world? It was half three the last time I’d checked, but suddenly, it was almost seven thirty. Four hours had gone by without me even realising.

It had been longer in the other world, though. I’d spent pretty much the entire school day there, gone to all of my classes and paid attention in all of them. I hadn’t really been tempted to leave the world or anything, but that still didn’t feel right. Even when I’d spent that much time in the Academy before, much less time had passed in reality.

But it wasn’t always the same. Time moved differently in each of the worlds I’d visited, and it was never consistent, so maybe I shouldn’t have been so taken aback. I should have been used to not really knowing how many hours I’d missed when I returned to reality, but I wasn’t.

It worried me too. I think that was part of why my brain was so stuck on the four hours I’d missed. We’d stopped to get snacks and drinks and to use the bathroom. Mom had woken me up a couple of times when I’d fallen asleep and snapped at me, arguing that I wouldn’t be able to sleep that night. I could remember it all despite not actually being there when it had happened, but none of that had pulled me back to reality. I’d just missed it all.

How much more could I miss? Sometimes, if something bad or scary was happening, I’d feel it. It didn’t matter what was going on in my other world; I’d be forced to return to deal with whatever it was, but that hadn’t happened. Sure, nothing particularly awful had happened during the last few hours of the journey. It wasn’t anything that I actually needed to be there for, but that didn’t stop my brain from spiralling.

Next time, I couldn’t help but think, it could be worse. Mom could crash. Someone could pull out in front of her or swerve directly into the side of the car, and I might not even notice. Perhaps I would just wake up in a hospital and have no clue how I’d even got there.

That would definitely make me think I was in the wrong world, I realised. If I’d just been in the car with Mom before I left, and when I returned, I was suddenly lying in a hospital bed with a doctor fussing over me, I knew my initial impulse would be that I’d made a mistake. I’d assume something had happened, and I wasn’t really in reality.

It would probably take me a while to believe that I was, and that could be dangerous. The doctors might think there was something wrong with me if I ignored their questions and instead searched through the dizzinesses for my reality.

But that wouldn’t happen, I told myself, trying to push the anxiety away. It was so unlikely. I was always pulled back home when something really bad happened, and that would continue. I’d just missed the journey because it was boring. I didn’t want to spend hours trapped in the car with Mom, so the other world had helped out. It had allowed me to stay there until I got home. That was it, and I didn’t need to worry about it for any longer.

Still, a nibbling sense of unease continued to pull at the back of my mind, but I refused to pay any attention to it. It was pointless. There was nothing I could do about it, and I didn’t really want to anyway. I was too excited to finally get home and see Dad again. It had been too long, and I missed him so much. I always did when we were in Scotland during the summer.

A grin appeared on my face as we turned onto the drive, and almost immediately, I saw the curtain in the lounge twitch. Somehow, Dad always knew when we got home. I wasn’t sure if Mom had told him roughly what time we’d be back or if he just spent the whole time sitting there waiting for the car to pull onto the gravel, but he never missed it.

Before Mom had even switched off the engine, Dad threw the front door open. I grabbed for my seat belt, glancing down quickly as I scrabbled to undo it, and leapt out of the car. The stones crunched under my feet as I ran towards him.

“Gracie!” he cried, pulling me into a hug.

“Dad!”

Happiness washed over me as I wrapped my arms around him.

“I missed you so much, sweetheart.”

“I missed you too,” I replied, my face smushed into his chest.

He pulled back, his hands resting on my shoulders. His eyes seemed to flick back and forth as he took me in, a warm smile on his lips, and I couldn’t help but notice how much better he looked than the last time I saw him. It wasn’t just the smile on his face; he looked healthier.

There were no bags beneath his eyes, I realised. I’d always just assumed they were permanent, like mine, and I’d gotten so used to seeing them that it was weird to see him without them. It looked almost wrong, even though I knew it wasn’t. It was a good thing that he didn’t look quite so exhausted all the time.

He’d probably been able to sleep better without Mom and me at home. He could go to bed whenever he wanted and probably slept in at the weekend, and that must have been why he looked so healthy.

Plus, he was more tanned than normal. His skin had taken on a golden hue that always made me kind of jealous. I wished I’d inherited that from him, but instead, I just went red whenever I’d been in the sun for too long.

He must have done something to the garden, I realised with a flare of excitement. Normally, when we were away, he did something. I wasn’t sure why. Maybe he got bored being by himself all the time, or perhaps he just always wanted to do it but didn’t have the time with us there, but whatever the reason, there was always something.

Last time, he planted a flower garden. It didn’t last long once we got back, and I hadn’t heard Mom say anything about it, but I’d loved them. I’d tried desperately to keep them alive, going out to water them almost every day, but every single one of the once-bright plants died within the month, and that broke my heart.

Dad said it was to be expected, and there was no need to apologise. Apparently, flowers didn’t last for long once they bloom, and he was just happy that I’d gotten to see them, but I’d still cried that night once I shut myself away in my room.

He just deserved better.

“You’ve grown again, haven’t you?” Dad asked, his eyes narrowing.

I couldn’t help but look down at myself. I doubted it. I was pretty sure I hadn’t gotten any taller in over a year, but I had no real way of knowing without measuring myself, and I didn’t have a tape measure.

“I don’t think so?” I said.

Dad cocked his head to the side.

“Mmmm, no. I’m certain,” he decided. “You’re taller. Grace, what did I tell you about that?”

His tone was playful, and I grinned as I ducked my head, pretending to be apologetic.

“I know.”

“You’re not allowed to do it,” he told me.

“I’m sorry. I’ll try harder this time.”

Dad sighed and shook his head.

“If you keep going at this rate, you’ll be even taller than me by the end of the year, and then what? You’ll have to start ducking through doors! Oh no,” he gasped, a mock concerned look appearing on his face. “We’re going to have to move. The ceilings are just too low, and we can’t have you scraping your head on them all the time!”

I snorted. There was no way that would ever happen. I was barely five foot as it was, and I truly doubted I’d shot up in the last month like he was pretending.

“Such a shame,” I sighed, mimicking his tone. “I really like our house, but I guess there’s no way around it.”

Dad’s smile grew into a grin, and he opened his mouth to respond. Before he could speak, the car door slammed, and he looked away.

“I should go give your mom a hug,” he told me, squeezing my shoulders before letting go.

Mom was looking pointedly at her phone as Dad made his way across the gravel towards her. She must have known he was approaching. It would have been impossible to miss the crunch of his footsteps, but she didn’t look up until he was almost right in front of her.

“Welcome home,” he said.

“Thank you,” Mom replied as she reluctantly accepted a hug from him.

Her posture was tight, and even I could see how loosely she was holding him. Her hands were barely resting on his back.

I looked away awkwardly, wishing I’d brought my phone with me, but I’d left it in the car when I’d gotten out to greet my dad. Instead, I just stared at the house, trying to spot whatever it was that I knew Dad must have done to it during the last month. As far as I could tell, it looked the same, though.

“I missed you,” I heard Dad say.

No response came from Mom. She didn’t tell him she’d missed him too, I realised, and I glanced back just as she started to pull away.

“Ugh,” she grumbled, rubbing her cheek with a disgusted expression. “When was the last time you shaved?”

Dad’s hand came up to touch the stubble on his chin. It didn’t look particularly long, not longer than normal, anyway.

“This morning,” he said, sounding unsure. “Sorry, love.”

“It’s fine.”

There was an awkward pause, and I chewed on my lower lip. I wanted to say something, but at the same time, I didn’t. It felt like I’d be intruding on their conversation, and that felt weird. Mom would definitely get annoyed at me for that, even though she didn’t really seem to want to talk to my dad.

“How was the journey?” Dad asked after a couple of seconds.

“Fine. The roads were full of idiots, though.”

Dad frowned sympathetically.

“Ah, I’m sorry.”

“Why?” she snapped. “You weren’t one of them.”

“I know, but still.”

“Mmm,” was all Mom said as she looked away.

Another pause stretched out between us, and the urge to break it thrashed in my chest, but luckily, Dad spoke again before too long.

“Are you hungry?” he asked, looking between Mom and me. “I can rustle something up or order something if you’d like?”

Mom’s lip curled slightly at the suggestion of Dad cooking, and I hoped he didn’t notice it. He wasn’t a particularly bad chef, no worse than Mom was anyway, but Mom always found something to complain about when he did make anything.

“No, thank you,” she sneered. “We ate along the way.”

“Are you sure?” Dad asked before glancing at me again. “What about you, Gracie? Do you want anything?”

I hesitated for a moment, trying to work out whether I was hungry or not. Mom was right. We had stopped and grabbed snacks during the journey, but it wasn’t exactly a full meal. It was just a bag of crisps or a sandwich, and part of me longed for something proper. I wanted something hearty and full of vegetables, which I was sure I hadn’t had all day, but then I caught sight of Mom staring at me.

“I’m not too hungry,” I said.

That was definitely what Mom wanted me to say because she looked away again, glancing down at her phone, but Dad’s eyebrows pulled together.

“Well, if you do get hungry, just let me know,” he told me.

“I will,” I lied.

It wasn’t that I didn’t want to tell him. I just knew it would create an argument if I did. He’d make me something or order a takeaway, and Mom would get annoyed because she’d decided that I’d already eaten enough and didn’t need anything else. She’d accuse him of not caring about my health or trying to fatten me up, and he’d try to say that it wasn’t that; he just didn’t want me to be hungry, but that wouldn’t be enough of an excuse for Mom. I knew that. I’d heard the argument too many times.

“Well, you two must be exhausted after driving all day,” Dad said. “Go in and relax. I’ll unpack the car.”

Mom didn’t even hesitate. She immediately breezed past him without even saying thank you.

“I’m going to take a shower,” was all she said.

I hesitated, though. As much as I did want to go inside and lock myself away in my room, I wanted to spend more time with Dad. Plus, I still hadn’t worked out what to say to Phoebe, and I knew that I needed to do that. I hadn’t responded to her texts for hours, and I needed an excuse because there was no way I could tell her the truth, but I felt bad lying to her.

“I’ll help,” I decided.

“Don’t be silly,” Mom snapped from the doorway. “Stephen can manage it by himself.”

“I know, but I don’t mind helping,” I replied, keeping my smile pleasant as Mom continued to stare at me.

For some reason, that seemed to annoy my mom. She’d clearly wanted me to just let my dad empty out the car by himself, but there was no need for that. It wouldn’t take long for us to do it together, and I had nothing else to do with my evening.

After a few seconds, Mom scoffed and spun on her heel, storming into the house. The door slammed behind her, and Dad chuckled softly.

“You don’t need to help, kiddo,” he said as he crossed to the back of the car and opened the boot before glancing towards the door. “You can go head up to your room or grab some food whilst your mom is in the shower if you want. I refilled the snack cupboard the other day, and I don’t think your mom is going to be too happy with some of the things I bought, so you might want to grab what you can now.”

I grinned at him as I joined him at the boot.

“She’ll still be in the shower by the time we’re done,” I said.

“She will,” Dad agreed as he started pulling suitcases out. “So, how was Scotland?”

That was the question I dreaded every year. Dad always asked it, and no matter what I said, he always managed to see straight through my answer. Still, I couldn’t bring myself to tell him the truth.

“It was nice,” I lied, trying to inject as much enthusiasm as I could.

A wince flitted across his face, and he sighed.

“Ah, I’m sorry.”

“It’s not your fault,” I told him. “And it really wasn’t that bad.”

Dad didn’t seem to believe that either. One corner of his mouth pulled up in a slight smile, but there was no happiness in his expression.

“I’m glad,” he said. “Here, take this one. It’s not too heavy.”

I took the suitcase from him and carried it into the house, leaving it in the hallway. I was never really sure what to do with Mom’s bags because every year without fail, she’d either get annoyed at my dad for going through her luggage or for not starting to put her dirty clothes in the wash. Sometimes, she’d get annoyed with him for both at the same time, which was pretty impressive to me in the worst way possible. There was no way to win, but Dad tried regardless.

We were silent as we continued unloading the car, but unlike before, it felt comfortable. I didn’t feel the desperate urge to say anything just to break it. It was just… nice. Dad and I could just exist without awkwardness simmering between us, and I liked that. I wished things were simpler, that we could have that all the time, but I knew it wasn’t possible.

“This is the last one,” Dad said, lifting a suitcase out of the boot. “But I think your backpack is still in the front.”

Coming to a stop, I peered in through the window, immediately spotting the bag in the foot well and my phone, which I’d left on the seat.

“Oh, yeah,” I replied, pulling the door open as he walked past me into the house.

I swung my backpack onto my back and slipped my phone into my pocket, starting to straighten before freezing again. There was so much trash in the car, I realised. I didn’t even remember eating much during the drive home, but empty food packets had been shoved into the pocket in the door, and I hesitated for just a moment before starting to grab them.

“Don’t worry about that, kiddo,” Dad called as he pulled the driver’s side door open, and his eyebrows rose. “Well, I was going to say I’ll grab it, but… maybe I’ll just grab a bin bag.”

A smile pulled at my lips again.

“Yeah, that’s probably a good idea,” I told him as my gaze travelled over the centre console, finding even more empty packets shoved into the space there around the discarded coffee cups.

“I think so,” Dad agreed. “But leave that side. I’ll sort it out.”

Guilt simmered in the pit of my stomach. I hated it when anyone cleaned up after me. It was my mess. I’d eaten that food and left the rubbish in there, so I should have been the one to empty it out. It would only take him a few seconds, if that, but it still didn’t feel fair to leave him to do it.

“It’s okay,” I said. “I don’t mind.”

Dad looked at me from the doorway, his expression firm.

“I mean it. Go on. Take your bags upstairs and start unpacking.”

I shuffled slightly as I tried to work out what to do, and my guilt grew more intense.

“I don’t…” I started before trailing off as Dad pressed his lips together. “Alright.”

The serious look disappeared from his face as quickly as it had appeared, and he beamed at me.

“Good. Leave all that on the seat, and put your dirty clothes in the hamper. I should be able to get started on them tonight.” He paused, glancing back at Mom’s many bags. “Or maybe tomorrow morning. Do you need anything washed urgently?”

“Uhh… no. I don’t think so,” I said as I let go of the rubbish I’d pulled out of the door pocket.

I definitely hadn’t brought all of my clothes to Scotland with me, so I probably had something I could wear to bed.

“Okay, great,” Dad replied with a nod, holding the door open for me before lowering his voice and saying, “And don’t forget to grab some snacks. You might want to do that before you unpack.”

My eyes flicked towards the ceiling. I could hear the shower going in Mom and Dad’s room, and there was no way to judge how much longer Mom would be in there. Sometimes, it seemed like she took hours. Other times, she’d be done in a few minutes. I was pretty sure she’d have a long shower, though. She probably wanted some time to herself after being around me all day, but it was best not to risk it.

“Good call.”

Dad smiled as we walked across the hall, and he paused just before entering the kitchen.

“Do you need a hand carrying those up the stairs?” he asked.

“No, I should be okay.”

His lips pressed together again, but that time, he didn’t appear angry. His face was scrunched up, his eyebrows drawn together as he shot me a sappy expression.

“You’re so grown up.”

I couldn’t help but laugh at that.

“Because I can carry some bags up the stairs?” I asked as I grabbed the handles of the cases and hefted them into the air.

“Yeah,” he replied, his expression unchanged. “I remember when you couldn’t even lift one, and now look at you!”

I laughed again and shook my head. I didn’t think there was anything particularly impressive about what I’d been doing, but his tone was so genuine. He seemed lost in his thoughts as I looked away and began dragging the bags upstairs.

About halfway up, I found myself wishing that I’d taken him up on his offer. Neither of the bags were particularly heavy, but together, and with my backpack on, it was difficult. I managed it, though. My arms were beginning to ache as I reached the top step, but I pushed on, refusing to let go of either handle until I reached my room.

A happy sigh slipped out of my lips as I stepped through the door. It felt like it had been so long since I’d set foot in my room, and I’d missed it. I’d missed sleeping in my bed. It would be so peaceful without the hundreds of pictures of half-naked people staring down at me.

My eyes landed on my bed, and a grin pulled at my lips. I dropped the bags immediately and hurried towards it. Dad had gotten me new bedding. I couldn’t help but run my hand over the blue and cream striped duvet cover, marvelling at how soft it was, but that wasn’t all. More presents awaited me.

Two stacks of books rested side by side on top of the duvet, and my gaze roamed the spines, drinking in the titles as my smile grew even wider. Dad always gave me money to buy books. He never hesitated to do so, but there were a few that I wanted the physical versions of. Either I’d read the ebook before and loved it, or, for some reason, I just wanted to read the actual book of it, and Dad had asked me about them a while ago, but I thought he was just making conversation. I didn’t think he’d actually remember them.

There were so many, though. He must have remembered every single book I’d said I wanted over the last few months, and he’d gotten them all. There were a few others too. Some of the books in the stacks, I couldn’t recall ever mentioning to him, but they looked good. I did want to read them.

I picked up the top book, the first in a new series by an author I loved, and cracked it open. My eyes devoured the first line before I could stop myself. Immediately, I was hooked. I wanted nothing more than to curl up in bed and lose myself in the world she’d written, but I forced myself to place the book back on top of the pile and turned away from it slowly. I needed to go thank Dad before I allowed myself to read any more.
