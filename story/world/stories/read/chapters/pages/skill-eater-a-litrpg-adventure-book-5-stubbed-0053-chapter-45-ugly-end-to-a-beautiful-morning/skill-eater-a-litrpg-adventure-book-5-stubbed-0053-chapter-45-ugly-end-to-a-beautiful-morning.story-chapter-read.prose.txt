Edge emerged from the Sea of Trees accompanied by the radiant glory of the rising sun.

The heavens were a refulgent wash of yellow, orange, and red—the canvas for nature’s latest masterpiece. The jaw-dropping display was mirrored by the myriad waterways comprising the Lakelands, forming a luminescent panorama that took his breath away. He sat down on a boulder, basking in Ord’s splendor while savoring the satisfaction of a successful hunt. These are the moments that make life worth living.

His morning took a decided turn for the worse when his view was obstructed by a screaming vortex. Wind Ward snatched a projectile out of the air, shredding it to smithereens in the blink of an eye.

At that point, several notable events occurred at the same time.

Edge had been working with his avatars on emergency-response scenarios. The instant Ward triggered, Harden activated itself and covered his body in a layer of diamond scales. Meanwhile, Gaze switched from plant penetration to its combined ocular mode, revealing a trio of transparent blades streaking for his throat. They were infused with mana, so Play held itself back—poised to defend against purely physical attacks.

There wasn’t time to get out of the way. He flared Harden and Mantle, sending shards flying as crystal met crystal with incredible force. His skill senses filled in the gaps as he sought the origin of the strikes, which were some kind of armor-piercing projectiles.

He unleashed a volley of Vacuum Blasts along the reverse trajectory, asking the avatar to use its grenade mode to flush out his assailant. A stream of profanity filled the air as a stealth field wavered out of existence, revealing not a monster or beast, but a man wielding a polearm Edge hadn’t seen before. I’m pretty sure it’s called a war scythe.

He kept the Blasts coming, adding water bullets to the mix to throw the warrior off-balance until Ward’s cooldown expired, which would provide some insurance when he was ready to close the distance.

“What the fuck is your problem?” He yelled, mostly to buy time, since it was obvious the man hadn’t attacked him by mistake. “I don’t have anything of value other than a few potions and my armor. Nothing worth risking your life to acquire or claiming mine.”

“That’s rich,” his assailant replied, “considering you’re holding the most expensive polearm I’ve ever seen.”

The comment made Edge realize he’d seen the man before, though he had never faced him in the ring. He was a gladiator who was close to breaking into the top one hundred on the polearm ladder, which meant he was deep into stage three.

“Are you stupid? Opulence is Soulbound. If you kill me, it will crumble to dust, and you’ll walk away with nothing for your trouble besides the grievous bodily harm I’m about to inflict.”

“That rumor is bullshit,” the warrior retorted as the men exchanged fire. “Even if it’s not, I’m happy to eliminate the competition. After your performance in the Duos’ Clash, I’d rather not face you in the Carnage Cup.”

There were rumors that some of the gladiators were stone-cold killers, willing to murder to climb the ladders, even after the anomaly. Edge suspected there was more to the story, but in the end, the man’s motivation didn’t really matter. He wasn’t about to let someone walk away after trying to assassinate him, and it was clear that the murderous bastard had no intention of backing off regardless.

Fighting another skilled warrior was incredibly dangerous, particularly someone whose cycles and combat experience surpassed his own. If he let the man dictate the momentum of the battle or land his strongest attacks, Edge would lose his life despite his unique advantages.

My best bet is to surprise him and score a killing blow before he has time to revise his strategy. Since this attack was premeditated, he will expect me to use the skills I’ve revealed in the arena. I can use that to establish control before going in for the kill.

Edge summoned Foebinder and manifested a flameblade within its coils, which couldn’t be blocked or parried like a physical weapon. The crystal hilt erupted in a searing plume of plasma, prompting the gladiator to take a hasty step back. At the same time, Edge expanded his truesteel buckler, transforming it into a round shield three feet in diameter.

The armor obstructed his opponent’s view while shielding him from the crystal blades, reducing the warrior’s options. Edge tried a few taunts as a distraction, but the man was done talking. He managed to draw things out long enough for Ward to recover without tipping his hand in the process.

That’s one problem solved. It’s time to get up close and personal.

He conveyed his intentions to Ward’s avatar while getting ready to make his move. When he was sure the skill understood what he wanted, he reached for his bandolier, using his left hand to slide a mid-grade Speed and Power potion free in a single motion. He pulled the stoppers with his teeth and slammed them back-to-back, exploding into motion when the alchemical concoctions took hold.

As a crystalline barrage shattered against his shield, Edge charged, surging forth so fast his world smeared. He spread his jaws and let out an Intimidating Roar, sending Foebinder high into the air to attack from above.

When the warrior reached for a sheath attached to his belt, Edge knew something dangerous was coming. Since gladiators’ skills were common knowledge, it made sense to supplement them with equipment designed to catch opponents off-guard.

His assailant’s attacks had been linear up to this point, but the throwing knife he flung was far more mobile than Edge’s chakram and considerably more lethal. The weapon flew fast as thought, curving up and over the shield’s rim to perforate the base of his brain. The blade was so fast that he couldn’t dodge or activate any skills, even though he could see it coming.

Wind Ward, however, was perfectly capable of activating itself, and the skill specialized in countering threats of this nature. A tornado came screaming into existence, slamming into the supersonic knife like a wrecking ball mounted to a bullet train. An intense battle ensued within a fraction of a second, as the avatar tried to destroy the high-grade weapon before it passed through.

Ward couldn’t finish the job, but it warped the knife’s blade and cast it aside, glinting as it skidded across the dirt. Meanwhile, Edge shrank his shield and brought Opulence whipping around, switching on Quadra Slash and Elemental Blade while flaring Aegis of Nullification. Three offset copies appeared, billowing with frost as he drew deeply from the magic within and activated Devastating Discharge.

Four torrents of subzero ice shot out from the blades, along with a new twist he hadn’t seen before. Apparently, Blade’s avatar had been experimenting with tweaking the spell’s shape, shaping its wintry wrath into jagged spikes rather than simple blocks.

The gladiator had amazing reflexes, activating multiple skills to bolster his defense while throwing himself out of the way. Only one Discharge caught him head-on, although a second kissed his boots in passing.

He’d mitigated the damage but hadn’t escaped unscathed. The ice had penetrated his armor in several places, although nothing looked too deep. Even so, the cold will sap his strength, and he doesn’t know I can’t use it again.

They exchanged a brutal series of strikes, trying to break through the other’s guard and end the fight in a flash. As they traded blows, Edge built a profile of the warrior’s skillset and fighting style. The gladiator’s crystal blades could strike from range, but most of his magic enhanced his melee attacks. The war scythe is nasty. He keeps trying to catch my limbs. I’m pretty sure neither my armor nor scales will stop it.

The fighter’s form was impeccable, and his proficiency with his polearm exceeded Edge’s own. He was incredibly fast and had a ton of Durability and Disruption, letting him shrug off all but the strongest skills. Most troublesome of all, he was using a power that let him track incoming attacks, although it felt completely different from Enhance Senses or Gaze.

On the plus side, Foebinder was doing a fantastic job of interfering with the warrior’s rhythm. He couldn’t block the flameblade and had to dodge it instead. Every time he tried to catch the chain with his scythe, the living links went limp and slid out of the way, refusing to offer the resistance the weapon needed to cut through.

Rue was whining from inside his den, asking to join the fight. “No,” Edge replied through their bond. “He’s too strong for you to tackle, but don’t worry, we’ve got this. It’s time to play some cards he’s never seen before.”

With that, Edge activated Tidal Stream, using the gravitational current to shove his opponent back and fuck up his timing. Even though the man’s Disruption mitigated the force the aura exerted, it didn’t do anything to prevent Edge’s magic from affecting the ground beneath his boots.

The would-be assassin swore in surprise when a patch of loose dirt gave way, sending him staggering to regain his balance. Edge excluded the man from the effect, then switched it on again, causing him to lose his balance and hit the ground hard. Whatever skill he’s using to avoid my strikes only works on direct attacks. He definitely didn’t see that one coming.

Edge had created an opening, and it was time to bring their brawl to its blood-drenched finale. The gladiator had proven to be remarkably tenacious and was already rising to his feet. His reactor roared to life, signaling the activation of an ultimate ability. The warrior moved like greased lightning, reaching out with the war scythe to catch Edge’s bicep.

While his attention was divided, Foebinder came up and around. It released the flameblade, entering the gladiator’s blind spot as the heavy weapon sheared Edge’s right arm off at the shoulder. The bastard thought the fight was over, but he was about to learn the error of his ways.

Foebinder counted as an extension of Edge’s body, which meant Soulbound could teleport the glaive into its coils. He flared every skill he had as the left chain swung Opulence at the gladiator’s back, comboing Quadra Slash with Elemental Blade along the way. He kept right on casting, stacking skills while asking his avatars to lend him their strength.

The warrior didn’t sense the attack coming until the last possible moment, because there wasn’t an attack until the glaive appeared. However, his reflexes were insane—the legacy of countless battles with his life on the line.

With only a fraction of a second at his disposal, he brought his polearm to bear, planning to block and counter with an attack that would end Edge’s life. However, that wasn’t how events played out.

Instead of stopping when it met the war scythe’s shaft, Opulence severed the high-grade wood with no appreciable resistance. The Greed-bolstered glaive carved through weapon, armor, and flesh in turn before emerging into the light, heralding the geyser of blood that followed half a heartbeat later.
