Rain still pummeled against the windows of the Fable Train, despite nearly a day having passed since the storm began. Even after the true undead beast had been killed, it had still taken the rest of the night to clear the remainder of the attacking army. That confirmed for them that there must be a second out there somewhere, though it appeared that the one that escaped had only used its remaining forces as a distraction.

Ethan and the army were back on the move now, as the storm had progressed to a phase of primarily rain, although Tomo warned that another burst of wind and lightning would usually come in a few days. Ethan, Apollo, Selina, and Tomo now sat around a table in the main train car, discussing what they’d faced.

“There’s some good news,” Apollo said. “While we did lose more than half our army, the majority of those were Dusk and Dawn rank, and allowing the remaining forces to devour the rest means we haven’t lost as much corruption as I’d feared.”

“Any sense of how much we retained?”

Apollo considered. “We probably have two thirds of what we started with?”

“Still a considerable loss,” Selina said. “If we encounter the undead again, we’ll need to be prepared.”

“Agreed,” Ethan said. “The solution isn’t exactly palatable, but it’s effective.”

“I don’t think there’s much to prepare,” Apollo said. “It’s not like we’re training the army; I control them. I’ll keep the Twilights as a perimeter, and command them to attack and devour on sight.”

“That’s probably all we can hope for,” Ethan agreed. “So we have a plan for the future, but we still have to deal with the losses we’ve incurred. We’ve lost weeks of work.”

“Weeks we have,” Selina said. “It’s frustrating, but we don’t have a strict deadline for all this.”

“That…may not be entirely true,” Ethan said.

“What do you mean?” Selina asked.

Ethan sighed. “While I was out there searching for what had originally killed the scouts, I found something else.” He took a deep breath. “There was a dead Follower in that valley, recently killed by the undead.”

“What?” Selina said in shock.

“I think they found a way to follow us through,” Ethan said. “It’s the only way I can think to explain their presence here.”

“But why would they come?” Apollo asked. “Just to disrupt things for us?”

Strategus appeared behind them. He was tied to the Fable Train like Apollo now, and could appear and disappear at will. That almost exclusively meant disappear in his case, but he looked seriously concerned now.

“The Throne,” he said ominously.

They all stared at him, and Ethan hated hearing his fears said aloud. “You think they might be after it?”

“I don’t know how they found out,” Strategus said, “but there’s nothing stopping them from taking it. They’d need exactly the same thing we do–a sufficient quantity of Dusk’s power. If they can gather it before us…”

“Then the Followers could take Dusk’s place,” Ethan finished. “That’s an unimaginable worst-case-scenario.”

“Are we sure about this?” Apollo asked.

“We have to assume the worst,” Ethan said. “But we also need to confirm. I think the best thing to do is for me to go find them. The Follower was to the West, and there’s nothing but deadly sea to the east, so I should be able to find them if they’re out there.”

“That could take weeks,” Apollo said.

“We have to know,” Selina said. “We can’t just keep going as if nothing is wrong, if we’re actually in some kind of race.”

“She’s right. If they really are collecting Dusk’s power, we need to know how they’re doing it, and at what pace.”

“What are the odds they know where the Throne is?” Strategus asked.

“Again, assume the worst,” Ethan said. “Even if they don’t, they’ll have to figure it out eventually.”

“What do we do if all this is true?” Apollo asked. “It’s not like we were going at a relaxed pace so far.”

“Sabotage,” Ethan said immediately. “I’ll scout them out, being very careful. I’ll get a sense for what they’re doing, and if they are going after the Throne, I’ll see if I can find ways to slow them down. It might be the best role for me anyway.”

“That’s debatable,” Apollo said. “We still need someone rounding up Twilights for us, especially if we suddenly have time pressure to deal with.”

“Work on that while I’m gone,” Ethan said. “If it does come down to a race, it might be that we can’t win by continuing as we are. We need to be prepared for the possibility that you spend the rest of this adventure without me, while I do everything in my power to make things difficult for them.”

“We’re getting ahead of ourselves,” Selina said. “Let’s start with what we know. There are Followers on Potentia, is anyone going to argue that we keep moving forward without investigating them?”

Silence greeted the question.

“Then at least our next step is clear,” Ethan said, “I’ll get some supplies together, then head out later today. If I can keep up with the storm, it might be safer traveling.”

“Wise,” Tomo said. “Most demons will be in burrows or caves to avoid the rain and lightning. It will not be easy to track a Follower, however, especially after a storm.”

Ethan shrugged. “The one I found had the robes they wear at Twilight rank, which is also when they start flying, so it’s not like I’ll be following footprints.”

“Then how do you plan to track it at all?”

“We’re coming up into the equivalent of the southern United States,” Ethan said. “I’ll follow the coastline to the west, and use Deevees to scout. We can cover a considerable area that way, and there should be clues if they’re building a massive army, like we are.”

“That’s a lot of country to cover,” Apollo said warily.

“I’ll come back if I haven’t found anything in a month,” Ethan said. “And with my Spirit here, you should be able to call me back fairly easily. We can’t communicate through it, but if you hit me with a weak spiritual attack, I’ll feel it, and rift right back.”

The others considered. “I suppose that’s the best we can hope for,” Apollo said.

“Be careful out there, Ethan,” Selina added.

“I’ll do my best, you do the same.” He paused. “Oh, and don’t get too close to the sea.”

Selina rode through the dense jungle on Walter’s broad back, holding onto something she thought might be an antenna rising from the creature’s insectoid head. Walter looked like a mix between a crab and a beetle, and he was roughly the size of a small house. Navigating through the jungle was really more a matter of Walter’s enormous pincers ripping trees from the ground, and tossing them aside.

Behind them, a pack of Dusk rank demonic wolves loped easily along, sniffing at the air for prey. The discovery that Dusk rank demons would follow a Twilight without any real guidance from Apollo was a useful boon. Finding a way to trust a Twilight demon out on its own was a more difficult prospect, but in the two weeks since Ethan left, they’d been hard at work.

“How’s it going?” Apollo asked.

Selina moved to the center of Walter's shell, examining the steel spear covered in golden runes, which was driven a short way into the demon. “Everything looks good so far. Spear isn’t moving, runes are holding, and Walter still seems to be following your commands. Or else he’s very sneaky.”

“That’s amazing,” Apollo said. “It feels as if he’s right beside me. How far out have you made it?”

“It has to be nearly ten miles,” Selina reported, looking down at a survey map she’d created with drones. Michael Larkin had built the interface for her, then Fable had integrated the technology, and learned to create the drones at will. So far they’d been using the system to map out Potentia as they went. Unfortunately the signal had limited range, and nothing on this planet seemed to work exactly as expected.

Selina had placed a small receiver on Walter’s back next to the control spear, and it was at least allowing her to control the drones for nearly a mile in any direction.

“Well, I think we can call the first test a success,” Apollo said. “Are you ready for the second?”

Selina removed an arrow and quiver from her inventory, looking at them doubtfully. “Cara taught me a bit about shooting a bow,” she said, “but this still makes me nervous.”

“Aim shouldn’t be that important,” Apollo said. “Like we talked about, as long as you’re strong enough to get one of the arrows stuck in the target, it should all be fine.”

“I’m still not sure that’s the case,” she said.

“I forged those arrowheads with Twilight power, Selina; they’ll penetrate. Just try to aim somewhere it won’t easily get knocked out again.”

“See? You’re already making things harder!” Selina insisted.

“I believe in you,” Apollo said, and she could hear the smile in his voice. “Let me know when it’s time.”

It was a while before “time”. Walter was one of the larger and more powerful Twilight demons they had, and for good reason. If they were going to catch another Twilight, and couldn’t kite it the way Ethan did, the best bet was something like Walter physically dragging the target back to Apollo, who still traveled with the army.

Unfortunately, that put them into a bit of a tricky situation. On Potentia, smaller, weaker creatures didn’t attack stronger ones without some kind of advantage. Walter was so large that few other Twilights would dare make the attempt. Selina also knew that he had some disturbingly powerful attacks hidden within his shell, beyond his raw titanic strength.

That meant that they traveled safely, which Selina appreciated, but that wasn’t particularly useful when hunting other demons. The solution was the small pack of Dusk wolves, although that wasn’t without challenges itself. Apollo couldn’t direct the demons from so far away. The control spear maintained his connection to Walter, but Apollo couldn’t guide the wolves, he could only release them.

He did so now, letting several of the smaller creatures rush out into the jungle, live bait for the second test. When nothing came of it for nearly a half hour, Apollo released another three.

“Only five left,” Selina reported after two more rounds of failure. “I don’t think the trees are getting them. There aren’t that many in this jungle that are carnivorous.”

“They’re probably just escaping,” Apollo said. “We knew this was a longshot as we haven’t found a reliable way to locate demons to bait out.”

“Why not send the final five at once, then we’ll head back,” Selina suggested.

“Are you sure? Only a couple more hours until dark. Maybe Walter knows a good place to dance the night away.”

“Walter is a perfect gentleman,” Selina said. “He knows I need to be home before sundown.”

Apollo chuckled, then released the wolves as asked. After another forty-five minutes, Selina admitted they needed a better plan, and Walter obligingly turned and began moving rapidly back toward the rest of the army. They were nearly out of the jungle when Selina turned at a sound, surprised to find two of the wolves rushing right toward them.

She readied her bow, signaling Apollo to tell Walter to halt. The wolves rushed by, giving Walter a wide berth, and the massive crustacean-insect clicked its pincers in response.

When the attack came, Selina wasn’t prepared for it. She had her arrow nocked and waiting, pointed in the direction the wolves had run from. Unfortunately, the demon came from the sky.

Selina leapt from Walter’s shell, landing on a disk of light halfway to the ground, and gliding to safety. Looking back she saw something like an enormous bat, with a horned head and hairy, sinewy wings. It was less than half Walter’s size, but clearly understood it held the advantage in the air.

To make matters worse, poor Walter didn’t know what to do. Its body was under attack, a short beak and long talons trying to burrow through thick shell, yet it felt that it needed to await Apollo’s orders. Orders that wouldn’t come.

Selina raised her bow again, and fired at the bat creature. The arrow went embarrassingly wide. She shot again, and this one deflected off a rib bone. Again, and the arrow actually passed right through the membranous flesh of one wing.

“Come on!” Selina berated herself. Walter was trumpeting in pain, and counting on her. She brought the bow up one more time, then sent her disk of light directly toward the bat. She waited until she had nearly crashed into the Twilight flyer, then released

The arrow made a satisfying thunk as it dug into the demon’s flesh. Though it was so small the creature barely noticed it lost in the thick muscle of its wing joint, it was exactly where it needed to be.

“Arrow on target,” she told Apollo. “Now!”

There was no clear indicator that anything had changed. Not from the control spear, and not from the arrow. Walter, however, made it clear that he’d heard the command. Apollo couldn’t direct the fight personally, but he could point Walter at the arrow.

Two enormous pincers reached up, snapping wildly at the flying creature. The bat made to gain altitude, but Walter’s shell split open, releasing a dozen red tentacles which shot after its prey. They were easily severed, but very strong for their size, and did their job well–wrapping around furry wings, and making the demon plummet to the ground.

It never completed the trip, as waiting pincers caught the bat holding it in place hard enough to snap bone.

Selina touched down lightly on Walter’s back. “We have it!” she told Apollo. “Test two was a complete success! Looks like this army is growing again.”
