After the brief encounter with the rogue named Nasha—alongside a quick magic lesson—Veronica and Gwen scavenged what remained of the goblin site.

As the merchants had claimed, they found a small sack containing crushed hartivel vines. This reward was the main reason Veronica had accepted the commission in the first place. The extra thousand vix was nice, but it paled in comparison to the powder’s potential uses.

Hartivel vines were a high-demand alchemical ingredient with numerous applications: healing elixirs, mana recovery potions, and various enhancement tonics.

While Veronica wasn’t an alchemist herself, she knew of many potions she would need in the future. These included core awakening potions, or even elixirs that cured demonic corruption in its early stages.

Of course, those required several other rare ingredients, placing them far out of reach for now. Still, the vines alone could fetch a hefty price. With two kilograms as the promised reward, she’d expected to make nearly ten thousand vix.

The only issue was... the hartivel vines weren’t authentic.

“Those bastards really did set the whole thing up,” Veronica muttered.

Gwen leaned over her shoulder and peered into the small brown bag. At the top sat a thin layer of crushed hartivel vines. Beneath it was another thin pouch acting as a divider. Below that: dirt and twigs. The contents were ninety percent soil, ten percent vines.

Veronica frowned and stashed the bag into her pack. For now, she could make do with the small amount that remained. But once she returned, the merchants would have hell to pay.

The rest of the wagon’s goods weren’t worth much. Just simple grain and shattered pottery.

The only notable discovery was a hidden compartment beneath the wagon bed. From above, there was no latch; it was seamless without a handle. It was, however, accessible from underneath. That was most likely how the two men had ambushed Nasha. Once she inspected the cargo, they opened the hatch, splashed paralysis powder, and stabbed her before she could retreat.

Veronica still wasn’t sure how they had managed to coordinate it all without alerting the goblins.

Gwen searched the two bodies for valuables. The daggers were coated in an unfamiliar poison. Fortunately, they had proper sheaths, so the girls took them.

They also found identification cards.

Both fakes.

Gwen was fairly certain the man who’d been pierced with an arrow wasn’t actually seven and a half feet tall. The facial structures didn’t even match. They were similar, but only at a glance.

Veronica cast a spell, splitting the earth open. Together, they dragged the bodies into the pit before she sealed it shut, forming a crude grave.

It wasn’t out of respect.

It was to keep the corpses out of sight, away from scavengers or anyone else who might tamper with the scene.

She couldn’t drag two bodies all the way back to Ronswick. Burying them here would serve as a marker. A guild member or city watch officer could verify her report later.

For now, she took the fake IDs, the hartivel vines, and the twin daggers.

Then she and Gwen headed back toward Ronswick.

Up ahead, there was a small line of people at the front gates. Anytime someone left, they had to verify themselves before being allowed back in. This applied to adventurers on commissions, traveling merchants, and even nobles—unless they were important enough.

Veronica and Gwen walked along the path toward the end of the line.

“Huh? Uh—Veronica?” Gwen started, seeing Veronica continue past it. She hurried to catch up behind her. “Hey, we have to wait in line,” she whispered.

“Relax,” Veronica said. “We’re fine.”

And just like that, the comments began from those patiently waiting.

“Hey! No cutting in line!” called a large, bulky man with a sheathed greatsword across his back.

“Miss, it’s rude to cut,” said another man more gently.

Others glanced over, their voices low with snide remarks. No one stepped forward to physically stop her.

They likely expected her to be turned away at the front.

“Halt,” an armored guard said. His armor bore a clean sheen, fully plated and far more refined than the rough-cut plate from Greystone.

Veronica stopped as prompted.

“Apologies, but are you from one of the greater houses here? If not, we must ask you to return to the end of the line and wait your turn,” he said. He looked to be in his early twenties—firm but not rude, simply doing his job.

“No, I’m not,” Veronica replied. “But I have something better.”

She reached into the small pouch tied to her thigh and produced the map—the same one Leopold’s guard from the envoy had given her.

She handed it over.

The guard leaned his spear against the stone wall behind him and unfolded the map. He inspected it for several moments, brows furrowed, unsure of what he was seeing. Then he flipped it over, and his expression shifted immediately.

“Ah—I see. You are free to enter,” he said, handing the map with the viscount’s seal back to her.

She took it and nodded. “Thank you.”

Just like that, they bypassed the line and passed through the gates. Gwen stayed close at her side.

“What was that?” she asked. “Are you actually a noble like I thought?”

They stepped onto the stone-paved streets beyond the entrance.

“No. I’m not a noble. But I am a guest of Viscount Leopold while I’m in the city. I saved his life, actually. Got a few neat perks from it.”

Gwen’s eyes widened briefly. “Ah… I see. That makes sense.”

They continued walking side by side until they reached the first intersection.

“So?” Veronica asked. Her gaze drifted to the sky, judging the sun’s position.

It was early evening now.

She turned back to Gwen. “Do you want some time alone to think about my offer? We could also go to the mage tower and see if they’ll hear my request about Rocky.”

Gwen puckered her lower lip, thinking. “Hm… I think… I might need some time alone to think about it. A lot has happened today. And I’m…” Her eyes drooped. A certain fragility began to show through the cracks. “It’s a bit unreal being back in the city by my own choice. After two years with… him. I don't have to worry about showing up to the cave. Or always looking behind my back in case he's watching.”

Veronica furrowed her brows.

“Are you alright? He didn’t do anything to you—did he? Take the time you need to rest.”

Gwen shook her head quickly, her gaze falling to the ground. “No. Besides using the binding ring on me a few times, he mostly just kept me imprisoned in that cave with Rocky. He rarely stayed long, even though he visited almost every day.”

She looked back up at her. “I’ll give you an answer tomorrow. Is that alright? Or actually… how about in two days? At the Crimson Court that Nasha mentioned?”

Veronica nodded. “Of course. That’s fine with me. It’s a big commitment, after all. I’m sure you might want to tell your parents about it.”

“Ah. My parents don’t live here. They’re in Filgrove. I stay here with my aunt.”

“Well, I’m sure your aunt would want to know, then.”

Gwen nodded and began to turn away. Then she paused, remembering something. She quickly turned back.

“Hey—um… this whole student thing… is it… paid?”

Veronica raised an eyebrow and stared at her. Then she chuckled.

“Yes. It’s a paid position—for as long as you’re my private disciple during my travels, at least until we reach Annesheim. I’ll cover your costs and needs. But don’t expect a steady salary.”

Gwen nodded. “Alright. Thank you.”

She turned again, but paused once more and looked back.

“And… thank you for saving me. I mean it.”

Veronica smiled.

Gwen walked off, leaving her alone.

“Well—today was eventful,” Veronica said quietly.

She looked down at her palm and sighed. “I’ll have to wash this off. Wish she could’ve just told me, instead of writing on my hand.”

The Crimson Court was apparently an entertainment house, known for its theater and performances—singers, bards, jesters, full play productions. From what Gwen told her earlier in the forest, Nasha was a fairly popular singer there. That's how she recognized her.

It was Wednesday now. In two days, perhaps she could spare the time to attend. Especially since Gwen had seemed particularly eager, choosing it as their meeting place and all.

Veronica lowered her hand and started walking.

Today, however, wasn’t over. There was still one matter to settle.

She turned the corner and headed toward City Hall.

Two merchants still needed to learn a lesson.

The dining chamber of the Ronswick estate was pleasantly warm, accompanied by the gentle clink of silverware.

Viscount Leopold sat at the head of the table, coat removed, his posture finally less rigid than it had been all day. There had been quite a panic when he returned; people had noticed the bandages lining his body. The entire estate had been in an uproar trying to uncover what had happened.

Eventually, things settled. His family, however, still carried their worry.

To his right sat his wife, Lady Arianne. Claire sat between them, feet swinging beneath her chair despite being told to act dignified during family dinners.

“And then,” Claire said with dramatic seriousness, lifting her fork like a miniature rapier, “I was about to strike Instructor Henry, but he blocked it and knocked my weapon to the ground.”

Leopold blinked once. “Oh? Normally he would dodge.”

“That means I’m getting better, right?” she asked eagerly.

Arianne hid a smile behind her glass. “It means you’re learning. Perhaps you’ll be able to hit him soon.”

Claire beamed.

For a moment, it felt like any other evening: a simple dinner after a long day’s work.

Leopold’s eldest son had been one of the few pressing him for details about what had occurred in Greystone. Tonight, however, he was occupied at the magistrate’s residence handling paperwork and could not attend dinner.

Claire finished her last slice of meat, carefully dabbing her mouth with a napkin.

“I’m done,” she announced, pushing her plate forward. “May I be excused?”

Arianne nodded. “Of course.”

She turned slightly. “Marceline?”

The maid stepped forward from the wall.

“Walk with her in the gardens,” Arianne said gently. “Nothing strenuous. Afterward, take her to the study.”

“Yes, my lady.”

Claire slid from her chair and paused beside Leopold.

“I love you, Daddy.”

Leopold leaned down and hugged her. “I love you too, sweetie.”

Claire then moved to her mother.

“I love you, Mommy.”

Arianne embraced her. “I love you too. Try not to trouble Marceline too much.”

Claire frowned. “I don’t trouble her.”

Marceline smiled softly from behind her. “She’s no trouble at all.”

With that, she led Claire from the room, leaving Leopold and Arianne seated together. A few guards and servants remained stationed along the walls.

For a moment, the chamber felt… empty.

Arianne’s gaze drifted to her husband’s shoulder, where faint bandages were still hidden beneath his attire.

“The physician says the scar will remain.”

“I’ve earned worse,” Leopold replied evenly.

“You almost died,” she said quietly, reaching across the table to take his hand.

Leopold set his fork down. “The situation in Greystone was unprecedented. Something I never could have imagined.”

“Imagined,” Arianne repeated. “A demon manifested inside Baron Welterman’s county. Within our own territory. A cult comprised of commoners—and some of our own servants. Conspiring to summon demons. How could anyone have foreseen that?”

She lifted her glass to her lips but did not drink. Her voice echoed faintly against the rim. “Word will spread soon. Rumors will reach the other houses.”

“It’s inevitable,” Leopold said. “An entire mining town attacked for a ritual conspiracy. Too many bodies lie dead. We will have to interrogate the rest of our household as well.”

The servants standing along the walls did not move, but a subtle tension filled the room.

“Will you inform Duke Manevich?” she asked after finally taking a sip.

“I’ve already drafted the letter,” Leopold replied. “It’s better he hears it from me than through rumor.”

He dabbed his lips and took a sip of a smooth, blue-colored drink. Sweet, and easy to swallow.

Arianne inclined her head. “How much will you include?”

He stared down at the table, focused on nothing in particular.

“Everything.”
