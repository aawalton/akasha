Laughter echoed through Aodhán's living room as he finally moved to sit down, a glass of wine in one hand and a box of biscuits in the other.

It was what remained of his dinner from the night before, and everyone dug in eagerly the moment he placed it on the table.

"Hey, is this what you've been eating?" Ashscar asked, picking up the last biscuit with a deep frown. "What happened to actual food?"

"Don't even ask," Aodhán winced slightly as he bit into his own biscuit. "Just wash it down with the wine. I promise it tastes divine."

Nymera grimaced after taking a bite. "It tastes like crap."

"That's why you wash it down."

No one argued with that logic.

Aodhán pulled another bottle of wine from his spatial storage, and everyone immediately stretched their cups toward him for a refill, expressions twisted in mutual disgust.

Chuckling, Aodhán obliged them, feeling slightly guilty himself for serving his guests stale biscuits and cheap wine.

But what was he supposed to do?

They had arrived far earlier than expected.

After filling everyone's glass to the brim, he finally sat back down and sighed happily. "It's better with the wine, right?"

"It is," Sophran admitted hesitantly. "I'm just not sure it's healthy."

Ashscar snorted and promptly dropped his biscuit back into the box. "I'm sure you all have enough vitality to survive this poison, but I'd rather starve than eat that."

Aodhán sighed dramatically. "Alright, I'm sorry. I spent the last four days meditating. I didn't exactly have time to cook."

"Or clean, by the looks of this room," Nymera added.

Aodhán paused mid-bite and slowly glanced around the perfectly spotless living room, visibly offended.

"What's wrong with my room?"

"Uhm… everything?" Ashscar pointed toward the overgrown plant in the corner. "Is that a pile of weeds?"

"It's the effect of—" Aodhán stopped abruptly, narrowing his eyes at all of them before laughing. "No. Absolutely not. I refuse to let you all gang up on me like this. The house is literally self-cleaning. I do not need to clean it."

"Or maintain the plants, apparently," Ashscar shot back with a grin, and everyone burst into laughter.

Aodhán glanced around at all his friends, mouth hanging open in betrayed disbelief.

"Get out of my house, all of you," Aodhán laughed. "I missed you people for four days and this is how you treat me?"

Everyone burst into laughter again, and Aodhán shook his head in amusement as warmth spread quietly through his chest.

"I'll pay you all back for this," he promised. "And next time, I won't be so gentle."

"Seamus maybe," Nymera chuckled. "He's the biggest slob here."

Seamus, who had been reaching into the box for Ashscar's abandoned biscuit, immediately looked up in offense. "Hey! I'm not a slob. Have you seen Sophran's house?"

"What?" Sophran jerked his gaze toward Seamus with dramatic annoyance. "My house is a palace of cleanliness. If anyone here is a slob, it's either you or Nymera."

Nymera straightened immediately in offense. "My house is squeaky clean."

"Oh really?" Aodhán raised a brow. "So you wouldn't mind if we all headed over there right now?"

Nymera stilled immediately, her eyes darting from one person to another before she slowly sank back into her chair.

"I don't know," she said cautiously. "I haven't been home in four days. It may have gathered some… dust."

Aodhán stared at her in disbelief. "It's literally self-cleaning."

Nymera opened her mouth as though to argue, paused, then quietly reached for her wine instead.

The room erupted into laughter once more, and Aodhán could only shake his head helplessly.

"I hate you all."

More laughter followed his declaration, warm and easy as they all relaxed deeper into the couches, exhausted from the twisted effect.

Aodhán smiled.

He had expanded the living room to nearly double its previous size, giving the familiars enough room to run around without bumping into anyone.

The underlings had also left the living room to hang out on the terrace above, leaving them to simply enjoy each other's presence.

"Ah," Nymera sighed, placing her legs on the table. "It feels so good to finally leave those chambers. This has to be the most brutal twisted effect I've experienced in a while."

"Me too," Sophran nodded in agreement alongside everyone else. "It felt deeper this time. More violent."

"Indeed," Ashscar frowned thoughtfully. "I believe it had something to do with the ascension. Insight farming and all that. But then again, this isn't my first time insight farming, and the last time wasn't nearly this brutal."

A contemplative silence settled over the room for a moment before Darien broke it, glancing toward Aodhán with a smile. "You are pretty lucky to have been spared from it."

Aodhán grimaced slightly. "I'm not sure lucky is the right word. Staying here alone for the past four days wasn't exactly fun either."

"Yeah," Seamus frowned, absentmindedly running his fingers through Skip's fur. "But yours was voluntary. You could've gone out anytime."

"I…" Aodhán opened his mouth to argue, paused, then shrugged helplessly. "I guess I could have."

Nymera slowly intertwined her fingers with his, amusement dancing in her eyes as a small smile tugged at her lips. "Did you confine yourself to the house out of solidarity for us?"

"No. I—" Aodhán stuttered before shaking his head. "Maybe. But that wasn't the main reason. After testing my abilities in the rift, I wanted to consolidate my gains and explore my time aspect a bit."

"Ugh," Ashscar groaned, pinching the bridge of his nose. "I still have a ton of skills I haven't properly tested yet."

"Me too," Nymera admitted, biting her lip worriedly. "And I still have some fragmented inspirations floating around in my head. I really need to sit down and meditate."

"We all need to sit down and meditate," Darien chuckled.

"Or sleep," Seamus added, sinking deeper into his chair and earning a round of tired chuckles from everyone else.

They talked for several more minutes after that, catching up on the past four days and discussing some of the insights they had gleaned from the ascension. But as time passed, the room gradually settled into quieter conversation, everyone too mentally exhausted to maintain the earlier energy.

By noon, nearly all of them had fallen asleep, some even snoring softly.

Aodhán smiled faintly at the sight and gently patted Nymera's hand before getting up to clear the biscuit box and empty wine glasses from the table.

After that, he went upstairs to check on the underlings, chuckling when he found them all also fast asleep, most of them already sprawled across chairs or resting against the walls in exhaustion. Rathis Cloudrend was the only one still awake, quietly meditating in a corner.

However, from how exhausted the man looked, it was obvious it wouldn't be long before he joined the others.

Aodhán shook his head in amusement, glad he no longer had to suffer through the twisted effect.

Hopefully, though, he would understand it properly soon enough.

He stepped out of the house a few minutes later, and looked up to find Varéc, Caelion, Levi, and Giselle soaring across the skies while their more grounded companions—Skip and Halo—watched longingly from the rooftop below.

Chuckling softly, he made his way toward the market at a leisurely pace, mentally listing everything he still needed to buy as he walked. Aside from food, he needed a few new clothes for himself, some household supplies, and a decent amount of ingredients to restock the kitchen.

Since he already knew what he wanted, he spent very little time at the market before making his way to Elder Gumi's workshop, a small smile tugging at his lips as blistering heat from dozens of active forges engulfed him the moment he stepped inside.

Despite all the strength he had gained in the past few months, the heat of the workshop had yet to become comfortable.

It had, however, become familiar, and he bowed respectfully as the gazes of dozens of Mythics turned toward him.

This time, every single one returned the greeting respectfully, with some even bowing in return, forcing Aodhán to bow deeper just to maintain the sect's hierarchy.

It was always amusing whenever that happened, though he couldn't exactly blame them.

In fact, he was glad for it.

He had earned that respect. There was no need to feign humility about it.

After greeting several of them personally and answering a few curious questions about witnessing the ascension, Aodhán finally made his way to the back room and knocked, sweat pooling lightly on his forehead from the oppressive heat.

For a moment, nothing happened. And then a peephole slid open to reveal a familiar face.

"Elder Kobi," Aodhán greeted politely, and the man immediately broke into a bright smile.

Several bolts clanged loudly as locks disengaged one after another, and a moment later the heavy door creaked open.

"Well, if it isn't our little prodigy," Elder Kobi chuckled, stepping aside to wave him in. "How are you doing?"

Cooler air immediately washed over Aodhán the moment he stepped into the room, and a subtle sigh of relief escaped him.

"I'm doing fine," he grinned. "Much better now, at least."

Elder Kobi laughed knowingly, and Aodhán's gaze drifted around the room, quickly noticing the absence of Elder Sedgwick and Elder Summer.

He frowned slightly, about to ask where they were, when a loud clang suddenly echoed from Elder Gumi's office, immediately pulling his attention toward it.

She stepped out of the office an instant later, a deep frown on her face and a pile of parchment tucked beneath one arm.

Aodhán bowed immediately. "Good morning, Elder Gumi."

"Good morning," she replied absentmindedly, her brows still furrowed in concentration. "Kobi, come look at this."

Elder Kobi hurried forward, shutting the heavy door softly behind him while Aodhán followed after out of curiosity.

However, the moment he drew closer to the forge table, his gaze caught a pile of electric-green ores stacked neatly beside it. The ores crackled faintly with golden lightning, casting an emerald glow across the metal surface, and Aodhán's eyes widened instantly in recognition.

"Are those voltrite ores?" he asked, his voice finally drawing Elder Gumi from her thoughts.

She looked up then, her eyes widening slightly as though only just realizing he was there.

"Aodhán," she said, some of the tension easing from her expression. "How nice to see you."

"You too," Aodhán smiled crookedly, though his gaze kept drifting back toward the glowing ores. "Uh… are those really voltrite ores?"

"Yes," Elder Kobi answered absently as he skimmed through the parchments Elder Gumi had handed him. "They're for a client."

"It's for High Elder Velora's great-grandchild," Elder Gumi corrected. "He awakened last night. This is intended as a gift."

What?

Impossibly, Aodhán's eyes widened even further as he stared at the small fortune sitting atop the forge table.

This was being handed to a tier one cultivator?

Clearing his throat, he finally asked, "Wha… what exactly are you making with it?"

"Bracelets," Elder Kobi replied, placing one of the parchments down. "And what appears to be some sort of shoe—"

"An awakened boot," Elder Gumi corrected, and Kobi immediately nodded.

"Right. An awakened boot." He turned the parchment toward Aodhán. "We're trying to use the inside-carving method we learned from your pendant, but we're not entirely sure how that's supposed to work with a shape like… this."

Aodhán lowered his gaze toward the parchment, his eyebrows slowly climbing as he took in the bizarre knee-high construct of streamlined voltrite and layered plating.

The thing looked less like footwear and more like a weapon someone had decided to strap to their leg.

"How exactly do you plan on carving this?" he asked, and Elder Gumi grimaced.

"I have a few ideas," she admitted, "but none I particularly trust yet. The angles of the boot make proper inside-carving almost impossible."

Aodhán nodded slowly, already beginning to see the issue himself.

"Why not simply carve the runes onto the exterior?" he suggested. "That would be much easier."

"It would," Elder Gumi agreed with a nod. "It would also be significantly weaker, and High Elder Velora could not have been more specific about what she wanted."

She glanced back down at the parchment and massaged her temples tiredly. "Any ideas, Kobi?"

Elder Kobi glanced at the design, then slowly shook his head.

"None. I don't think we can inside-carve this boot without breaking the circuit somewhere along the way. The angles are too sharp."

Elder Gumi pursed her lips before finally turning toward Aodhán. "What about you, Aodhán? Any ideas?"

Aodhán blinked, momentarily caught off guard by the question before shaking his head. "None. I'm not sure I see how this could work either." He glanced toward Elder Kobi. "Maybe Elder Sedgwick might have an idea?"

"Sedgwick and Summer are still in confinement," Elder Kobi replied. "It's just me and Elder Gumi today."

"And you, Aodhán, if you're willing to stay," Elder Gumi added, glancing at him.

"I will," he replied without hesitation. "How can I help?"

Elder Gumi lowered her gaze back to the parchment, and for a brief moment Aodhán thought he saw the beginnings of an idea flicker behind her eyes.

"Well," she said slowly after a few seconds, "you can start by making chaos strips while I think more on the design."

She tapped lightly against the parchment. "We may have to create some sort of hybrid weave between inside- and outside-carving to stabilize the sharper angles." She sighed softly. "Unfortunately, it seems beauty is also of the essence for this project."

She stared at the parchment a few moments longer before abruptly nodding to herself. "Alright. Let's start with the chaos strips first. Kobi, begin smelting the voltrite while I work through this design."

She disappeared back into her office immediately afterward, and Elder Kobi turned toward Aodhán with a grin.

"Still remember how to make chaos strips?"

"I do," Aodhán nodded before pausing. Then he slowly glanced back toward the glowing pile of ore. "Wait… aren't voltrite ores insanely expensive?"

Elder Kobi raised a brow. "Yeah. This little pile alone is worth over twenty million sect points."

Aodhán's jaw dropped instantly, and he turned back toward the ores with entirely new eyes.

He had always known Calamities and High Elders were absurdly wealthy, but spending twenty million sect points on such a small pile of ore was simply outrageous.

It also made him realize just how tiny his request for a hundred thousand sect points had truly been all those months ago.

That definitely wouldn't be happening again.

If he ever discovered a cure for the twisted effect, he was absolutely charging a far more substantial price.

Shaking his head, Aodhán picked up the tray of chaos treasures resting beside the voltrite ores and made his way toward the paradox still to begin refining the chaos strips.

The motions returned to him almost instinctively, as though it had only been days since he'd last done this rather than months.

Smiling faintly, he placed the treasures into the containment vat and rested a hand against the still to channel lightning into it. Before he could begin, however, Elder Kobi spoke.

"So," Elder Kobi asked casually as he moved toward the forge to begin smelting the voltrite ores, "I hear you're immune to the twisted effect."

Aodhán glanced at him in surprise before slowly nodding. "I am. Uh… how did you know that?"

Elder Kobi shrugged lightly. "High Elder Velora mentioned it while she was giving us the commission. She wondered whether Elder Gumi might have some idea how such a thing is possible."

Aodhán stilled slightly at that, curiosity immediately stirring within him.

"Did she?"

Elder Kobi shook his head. "No." He glanced back toward Aodhán just as the forge burst to life behind him, pressurized fingers of blue flame erupting outward with a sharp hiss. "Do you have any suspicions?"

"I have a few," Aodhán admitted with a shrug. "But none of them really make sense."

Elder Kobi chuckled softly. "That's alright. Even Calamities barely understand the twisted effect after all."

Aodhán snorted quietly and lowered his gaze toward the still beneath his palms as he began channeling lightning into it. The still hummed to life immediately, and the chaos treasures he'd placed into it suddenly began unraveling into thick black sludge, clumps of impurities rapidly revealing themselves like floating clots.

This time, however, he had both speed and experience on his side.

Twirling the chaos fork between his fingers, he picked away the impurities with remarkable precision, his movements smooth and practiced as he worked before the sludge could solidify.

By the time he finished, the sludge had solidified into a thin iridescent strip that resembled polished metal more than condensed chaos.

"Impressive," Elder Kobi called out from beside the forge, a faint smile tugging at his lips. "It seems I taught you well after all."

Aodhán chuckled. But before he could respond, Elder Gumi suddenly burst out of her office with a smile on her face.

"I've got it, Kobi," Elder Gumi announced, raising a parchment for both of them to see. "I found the perfect solution."

"Really?" Elder Kobi raised a brow and immediately crossed over to her, the tray of voltrite ores still in hand. Aodhán followed after him as Elder Gumi spread the parchment across the forge table, excitement gleaming in her eyes.

"The problem isn't the angles," she muttered, tapping several points along the sketch. "It's the continuity. The elder runes are too large to survive this many directional shifts without destabilizing. Every time the circuit passes through one of these bends, the energy flow begins to unravel."

Elder Kobi nodded slowly. "Of course. Then how do we maintain continuity without resorting to lesser runes?"

Elder Gumi's smile widened slightly. "We don't."

Aodhán blinked, understanding very little of what they were discussing. Elder Kobi, however, stared at her as though she had suddenly lost her mind.

"We don't," he repeated flatly, the disbelief in his voice unmistakable, but Elder Gumi barely seemed to notice.

"We don't," she repeated excitedly, tapping against a new layer she had sketched into the design. "Instead, we divide the internal pathways into smaller circuit segments and stabilize each section independently."

Elder Kobi frowned. "But the angles—"

"Will be bridged externally using chaos strips as ornamental seams."

Aodhán's eyes widened. "You want to weave inside-carving and outside-carving together?"

Elder Gumi grinned. "Exactly. The internal circuits maintain the elder resonance while the external seams preserve continuity through the sharp transitions. The chaos strips should stabilize the flow long enough for the entire structure to harmonize."

Silence settled briefly over the forge table.

Then Aodhán exchanged a glance with Elder Kobi, who still looked unconvinced. He grimaced. "It… wouldn't hurt to try."

"No," Elder Gumi nodded, already turning back toward the parchment. "No, it wouldn't."

She glanced at Elder Kobi. "Get on the forge, Kobi. I need those voltrite ores melted before sundown." Then she turned toward Aodhán. "And you—I need at least a dozen chaos strips. Immediately."

Aodhán nodded, grinning despite himself. It seemed he was about to witness a monumental achievement in runecraft firsthand.

And he couldn't wait to begin.
