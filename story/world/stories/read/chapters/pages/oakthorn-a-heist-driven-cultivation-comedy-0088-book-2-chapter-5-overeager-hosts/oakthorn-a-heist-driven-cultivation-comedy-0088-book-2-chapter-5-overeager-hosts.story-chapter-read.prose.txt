The air in Jameson’s reception room crackled with unspoken threats and centuries-old power plays.

Cade’s gaze flicked between Bernard and Jameson, mapping the invisible currents of alliance and animosity flowing through the room. The draugr crime lord sat at the head of the polished ebony table with the casual confidence of someone who’d survived a millennium of backstabbing. Beside him, Astrid’s perfect posture betrayed nothing, but the slight twitch at the corner of her mouth told Cade she was enjoying this far too much.

“Let me be absolutely clear,” Jameson said, steepling his gray fingers. “This isn’t a contest. It’s an open contract with... peculiar circumstances.”

Bernard snorted, the sound jarring against the opulence surrounding them.

Bernard’s crew spread out behind him like a living shadow—five distinctive figures who radiated danger in different frequencies. Cade cataloged them with cool efficiency: a bulky woman in mismatched armor, a dark elf with an unceasing smile, a mountain of a man whose neck disappeared into his shoulders, a thin man with a glint in his eyes as he assessed Cade’s female companions, and finally, Rufus, whose scarred face twisted into a sneer as he stared at Gavin.

Jameson ignored the Stone Britches’ leader and instead turned to Cade’s crew. “My daughter insisted on recruiting outside talent—specifically, your crew. She said that despite your many shortcomings, you showed great potential during the Elysian razing.”

“While Father thought more… seasoned… expertise would be more appropriate,” Astrid added, her voice honey-sweet but eyes sharp as flint. “Hence, the Stone Britches.”

“I see you’ve collected quite the menagerie, Stormhollow,” Bernard commented, his gaze lingering on Chubbs, who was currently attempting to braid Elena’s hair with sticky fingers. “A dragonling, a toddler... I see you and your lover have come up in the world since our last encounter.”

Bernard stared at Orro with a congenial smile. Everyone in Cade’s crew turned to the two of them.

“Not. A. Damned. Word,” Orro hissed through his teeth, arms crossed tightly over his chest.

“Gav, who exactly are these lovely companions of Bernard?” Cade inquired privately as he smiled warmly at the human crime lord.

“Bernard, gold-ranked earth dominion. Elizabeth is the ex-Arbiter with a grudge. Not sure of her dominion. Just know she’s powerful and very unhinged. The big guy is Augustus, silver-ranked demolitions. Then there’s Nash with the creepy eyes and Glenn, the ever-smiling demon of the Stone Britches. And Rufus...” Gavin’s mental voice faltered. “Pugilist. And a complete sadist.”

Jameson cleared his throat. “As I was saying, this isn’t a competition. It’s a matter of... proxy.” His undead eyes glittered. “Clover Clan politics are complex. Ancient. This particular dispute requires resolution, and the recovery of a certain artifact will settle it.”

“And if one crew happens to succeed where the other fails,” Astrid added, “the matter resolves accordingly.”

“So we’re fighting your family feud,” Cade translated, not bothering to hide his displeasure. “Fabulous. Always wanted to be a pawn in an undead power play.”

“You misunderstand your value, Stormhollow,” Jameson replied calmly. “Pawns are expendable. You’re more... specialized pieces.”

Orro’s voice slipped into Cade’s mind. “We need that map regardless of their politics. Do we cut and run?”

“Not yet,” Cade responded. “I’d like to know what we’d be walking away from.”

Bernard leaned forward, his massive fists resting on the table. “Let’s cut to the chase. What’s the job, and what’s the payment?”

“Recovery of an ancient artifact,” Jameson explained, placing the wooden box on the table with deliberate care. “This contains the map to its location.”

“And the payment?” Bernard pressed.

“For you?” Jameson smiled thinly. “Territory rights in the Lower Warren. For Stormhollow’s crew...” He glanced at Astrid.

“Information,” she supplied smoothly. “And the highest membership into the Clover Clan. That means our transportation routes, our name as protection, and our resources to help you get where you need to go. Oh, and plenty of more job opportunities that won’t need any half-wit broker to skim off the top of your hard-earned loot.”

Cade felt the others’ attention sharpen through their bond. His mind replayed the past few days. He hated to admit it, but they wouldn’t survive long enough to find Destruction’s domain, much less get there in one piece.

They needed this.

Rufus suddenly barked a laugh, harsh and grating. “You’re trusting these amateurs with a job this critical?” His scarred face twisted as he jabbed a finger toward Gavin. “This one got his entire last crew killed. Ran like a coward when the Lifekeepers came.”

The accusation hung in the air like poison. Gavin’s face went completely blank—the kind of blank that came from practicing how not to react.

“You don’t know what you’re talking about,” Gavin said quietly, but Cade caught the slight tremor in his voice.

“Don’t I?” Rufus pressed, a cruel smile spreading across his burned face. “I know exactly what happened in Thornbridge. Everyone does.”

“Gav?” Rayka’s concerned voice touched their minds.

“Later,” was Gavin’s only response, his mental walls slamming up.

Chubbs chose that moment to toddle away from Elena, making a beeline for the gleaming artifacts displayed on pedestals around the room. Rayka smoothly intercepted her, swinging the giggling girl back into her arms. Their foreheads rested against one another.

“No touchie,” Rayka murmured, returning to her seat as Chubbs pouted magnificently.

“But the shiny!” the divine toddler protested, reaching toward a particularly ornate dagger.

“Interesting company you keep,” Bernard commented, his calculating gaze fixed on Chubbs. “A little girl kidnapped by city-destroying thieves. The gods must be getting drunk in their own hubris if they let this one slide.”

“Careful,” Nora warned, her voice soft but carrying an edge sharp enough to draw blood. “Some words can’t be taken back.”

Bernard’s smile widened, his immaculate black mustache accentuating the smug curve of his lips. “Just making conversation.”

“Is that what you call it?” Elena snorted. “Sounds more like you’re compensating for something.”

Bernard’s underlings—Nash and Elizabeth—shifted forward in perfect unison, hands drifting to their blades. Augustus, the mountain of muscle, cracked his knuckles with a sound like breaking branches.

“Fear magic is weak,” Augustus rumbled, his voice like gravel being crushed. “Scary shadows come. We fight them with fists. You all die soon.”

Everyone stared at him in confused silence.

“Was that... supposed to be a haiku?” Jer whispered, loud enough for all to hear.

“Been working on them,” Augustus grunted defensively, crossing massive arms over his chest.

Jameson sighed, tapping the table with one long finger. “If you’re quite finished with the posturing, I have an empire to run.” He opened the wooden box, revealing an aged parchment within. “The item in question is hidden in a location of considerable danger. Recovery will require... specialized skills.”

Cade caught Orro’s subtle shift as the assassin angled himself for a better view of the map.

“Getting anything?” Cade asked.

“Partial. Looks like somewhere in the marshlands to the east,” Orro replied.

Bernard reached for the map, but Jameson closed the box before his fingers made contact.

“Details after we’ve settled terms,” the draugr said smoothly. “I assume both parties are interested?”

“Absolutely,” Cade replied before Bernard could speak. “Though the crew distribution seems a bit... lopsided.” He nodded toward Bernard’s five against their effective three—Nora would be protecting Evie, Rayka had Chubbs, leaving Cade, Orro, and Gavin as their offensive line, with Jer and Elena in support roles.

“A fair point,” Bernard conceded with mock graciousness. He turned to Gavin with a predatory smile. “Perhaps we could even the numbers. After all, we do have an outstanding debt to settle, don’t we, wolf?”

The temperature in the room seemed to drop several degrees as Gavin stared back, silent and unreadable.

Rufus’s burn-scarred face split in a grin. “Time to pay up, pup.”

The silence stretched like a bowstring pulled to breaking.

Gavin stared at the ornate carpet, shoulders hunched in a way Cade had never seen before. Gone was the swaggering womanizer with the sharp tongue and sharper fashion sense. In his place sat someone haunted, cornered.

“Gav?” Cade prompted through their mental link, but met only silence.

Rufus circled the table like a predator scenting blood. His burn scars twisted as he smiled, the puckered flesh pulling his mouth into something more snarl than grin. “Nothing to say, wolf? No clever comebacks? How unusual.”

“This isn’t the time or place,” Gavin managed, his voice stripped of its usual flair.

“On the contrary,” Rufus countered, planting his hands on the table and leaning in. “I think your new friends deserve to know exactly who they’ve invited into their little family.”

Jameson observed with the detached interest of someone watching insects beneath glass, while Astrid’s eyes narrowed with calculation.

“Thornbridge,” Rufus continued, savoring the word like fine wine. “Lovely little town. Or it was, before a certain werewolf repainted it red.”

Gavin’s knuckles turned white against the polished wood. Through their mental bond, Cade felt a surge of shame so potent it tasted like copper in the back of his throat.

“What’s he talking about?” Jer asked, glancing between Gavin and Rufus with growing unease.

“Ancient history,” Gavin said tightly.

“Eight months isn’t ancient,” Bernard interjected, his deep voice resonating through the chamber. “The bodies are barely cold.”

Elena’s hand drifted to her whip blade. “I’m getting really tired of the cryptic threats.”

“Not threats,” Rufus corrected, eyes never leaving Gavin. “Just facts. Your friend here was meant to be our telepath on a job in Thornbridge. Simple extraction—get in, grab the target, get out.”

“Gav, if you want me to shut him up, just say the word,” Cade offered privately.

Gavin’s response was barely a whisper in their minds: “He’s not lying.”

The admission hit like a physical blow.

Rufus, sensing his advantage, pressed on. “We were supposed to extract a noblewoman’s wayward son from a cult. Straightforward. But our wolf here...” He tapped the table near Gavin’s hand. “He neglected to mention it was the full moon.”

The pieces clicked into place with sickening clarity. Nora’s face hardened while Evie’s eyes widened with understanding.

“It wasn’t his fault,” Rayka said fiercely, clutching Chubbs closer. “Lycanthropy isn’t something you can just—”

“Control?” Rufus cut her off. “That’s precisely the point. He knew the risks. He went anyway. And when the change came...” His burn-scarred face twisted. “Tell them what happened next, wolf.”

Gavin finally looked up, his eyes flat and empty. “I lost control.”

The simple confession seemed to drain something vital from him. Cade had never seen Gavin like this—not the cocky, flirtatious rogue who deflected every serious moment with humor, but a man hollowed out by guilt.

“Lost control,” Rufus repeated mockingly. “Such a polite way of saying you ripped apart three of our crew, alerted every cultist in the compound, and got the rest of us trapped when the Lifekeepers arrived.”

Bernard nodded grimly. “Eight of us went in. Three died by his claws. Two more fell to the cultists after our cover was blown. The rest of us barely escaped the Lifekeepers.”

Gavin’s jaw worked. “I tried to warn you. I told you the timing was wrong.”

“Not hard enough,” Rufus snapped.

“Enough,” Jameson interrupted, tapping one gray finger against the wooden box. “Fascinating as this melodrama is, it’s irrelevant to the task at hand.”

“I disagree,” Bernard countered smoothly. “Reliability is absolutely relevant. And Gavin here has proven himself...” He paused, “...unreliable at best. Catastrophic at worst.”

Chubbs squirmed in Rayka’s lap, her cherubic face scrunched in concentration. “Wolfie sad,” she announced with toddler certainty. “Mustache man is mean.”

Rufus’s scarred face darkened. “Keep your little brat out of this.”

“Watch your tongue,” Nora warned, her hand dropping to her sword hilt. The shadows around her deepened, tendrils of her fear magic seeping out despite Jameson’s earlier warnings.

“Or what?” Rufus challenged, stepping closer to Gavin. “You’ll unleash the dog on me again? Or perhaps the toddler will smite me with those golden eyes of hers?” He leaned in, his voice dropping to a hiss. “You owe us blood, wolf. Three lives. And debts always come due.”

Gavin remained perfectly still, neither confirming nor denying the accusation. The weight of it hung in the air between them, heavy with unspoken truths.

“So here’s my proposal,” Bernard said, breaking the tension with practiced ease. “To balance our teams, Gavin returns to his original crew to settle his debt. An elegant solution to our numerical discrepancy.”

“Not happening,” Cade said flatly.

Bernard smiled thinly. “I wasn’t asking you, Stormhollow. Stay out of what doesn’t concern you.”

“Oh, it concerns me, you remarkably talented potter. Gav’s with us now,” Cade insisted, catching Gavin’s eye. “Whatever happened before, he’s one of ours.”

Something flickered across Gavin’s face—surprise, relief, or perhaps both. For the first time since Rufus had started speaking, the telepath’s mental walls lowered slightly, allowing a trickle of gratitude to seep through their bond.

Rufus scoffed. “Loyalty to a monster? How quaint.”

“We all have our shadows,” Nora said quietly, her gaze steady on Rufus. “Some are just more visible than others.”

“Poetic,” Bernard commented dryly. “But impractical. We need balanced teams for this job, and I’m simply suggesting the most obvious solution.”

“Your definition of ‘balanced’ is interesting,” Orro observed, speaking for the first time. His dark eyes catalogued each member of the Stone Britches with methodical precision. “A gold-ranker leading silver and bronze cores against a barely-awakened destructive core and a rag-tag support crew. Numbers don’t mean shit to someone like you. Just power.”

Rufus’s face flushed beneath his scars. “We earned our ranks.”

“As have we,” Orro countered softly. “Though perhaps not in ways that cowards like you would recognize.”

Rufus took another step toward Gavin, close enough now that their knees nearly touched. The scarred man leaned down, voice pitched low but carrying in the tense silence. “You can play pretend with your new friends, wolf, but we both know what you really are. You’re the coward who runs when things get difficult. You’re the monster who can’t control himself. ”

Gavin’s eyes flashed yellow for the briefest moment, then subsided.

“I’ve changed,” he said quietly.

Rufus laughed, the sound like grinding glass. “Monsters don’t change their nature. They just find new victims.”

Time slowed as Cade registered the dagger in Rufus’s hand—a thin blade designed for silent killing, angled perfectly for a strike beneath Gavin’s ribs that would puncture his lung. The scarred man’s fingers tightened around the hilt, muscles tensing for the thrust.

“Weapon!” Cade broadcast to the others, even as he was already moving.

Before Rufus could complete his strike, Cade’s hand shot out, fingers closing around the man’s forearm with devastating precision. For a heartbeat, nothing happened—just Cade’s grip, firm but not yet painful.

“You know what I hate the most?” Cade asked, his voice dropping to a register that silenced the room.

Gone was the flippant thief, the charming rogue. This was Cade Stormhollow, Son of Ruin—the man who had faced down gods and monsters and lived to make jokes about it. His eyes, normally bright with mischief, had become cold as winter midnight.

“What?” Rufus snarled, trying to wrench his arm free.

Cade’s grip tightened. “People who backstab my crew. It’s rare that it happens so literally, but my response is always the same.”

Then he unleashed his power.

Flames spiraled up from where his fingers met Rufus’s skin—not wild, uncontrolled destruction, but precise, targeted heat. The smell of burning flesh filled the room as Cade methodically seared through the layers of Rufus’s skin, his expression never changing.

Rufus screamed, a sound of pure animal pain. The dagger clattered to the table. Bernard and his crew surged forward, but Orro was already on his feet, twin blades appearing in his hands from seemingly nowhere. Nora’s massive sword cleared its sheath with a sound like thunder.

Through it all, Cade maintained his hold, his voice conversational.

“Gavin is many things,” he said, watching the flames lick higher around Rufus’s struggling arm. “Annoying, certainly. Overdressed, absolutely. A shameless flirt, without question.” He twisted his wrist slightly, eliciting another howl from Rufus. “But he’s ours. And we don’t abandon our own.”

“Stormhollow, enough!” Bernard’s voice boomed across the chamber as golden-brown energy swirled around him—the manifestation of his earth dominion.

“Not quite,” Cade replied, still eerily calm. “I want to be very clear about something.” He leaned in closer to Rufus, whose face had gone chalky white with pain. “The next time you reach for a weapon in the presence of my crew, I won’t stop at your arm. I’ll burn you from the inside out, starting with your lungs so you can’t even scream. Do we understand each other?”

Rufus managed a jerky nod, sweat pouring down his scarred face.

Cade released him, and the man collapsed back, cradling his severely burned arm against his chest. The skin was charred black in places, angry red in others—a precise pattern of destruction that mirrored exactly where Cade’s fingers had gripped.

“You’ll pay for that,” Bernard growled, earth magic swirling more intensely around his massive frame. The floor beneath them trembled, marble cracking as his power resonated with the stone.

Cade rose to meet him, wind and flame intermingling around his form—the dual aspects of his destruction dominion manifesting in tandem. “I’ve been hearing that a lot lately. Funny how I’m still standing.”

The two dominion users faced each other across the ruined table, power against power. Bernard’s gold-ranked earth magic wrapped around him like armor, crystals forming at his knuckles and along his forearms. By comparison, Cade’s newly awakened core should have been hopelessly outmatched—yet his destruction magic pulsed with frightening intensity, the winds it generated sharp enough to slice through the heavy velvet drapes behind him.

“Enough!” The word cracked like a whip as Jameson rose from his seat.

The draugr crime lord slammed his palm onto the table, activating a complex sigil that had been hidden in the wood grain. Light burst outward in geometric patterns, spiraling across the floor and up the walls in a lattice of magical constraint.

Cade felt his connection to his core vanish—not suppressed, but temporarily severed. The winds died instantly, leaving him feeling hollow and exposed. Across from him, Bernard staggered as his earth armor crumbled to dust.

“Containment array,” Jameson explained, straightening his impeccable suit. His undead eyes swept the room. “No dominion magic will function within this chamber until I deactivate the array. Do not test my patience again.”

Even Nora’s shadows had retreated, her fear magic neutralized by the powerful ward. Only Chubbs seemed unaffected—the divine toddler giggled and clapped her hands as if she’d just witnessed an entertaining show.

“Pretty lights!” she exclaimed, reaching toward the fading sigil patterns.

Rufus clutched his ruined arms, hatred burning in his eyes as he glared at Cade. Glenn and Nash had positioned themselves protectively around their injured comrade, while the massive Augustus looked ready to tear someone apart with his bare hands.

“I will not tolerate this behavior in my home,” Jameson continued, his voice arctic cold. “You are all professionals. Act like it.”

Cade noted that Jameson also didn’t send for any sort of healer. He assessed the Clover Clan’s leader with fresh eyes. Maybe it was embarrassment that his chosen crew had started the violence, or perhaps he really didn’t care that his potential tools got damaged.

Astrid, who had observed the entire confrontation with calculated interest, finally spoke. “Perhaps this... passion... indicates we’ve chosen the right crews for the job.” She smiled thinly. “Nothing motivates quite like animosity.”

“Speaking of the job,” Jameson said, pointedly returning to business. “As I was attempting to explain before this childish display, the item in question is located in a very specific region.”

He opened the wooden box again, revealing the map inside. This time, he spread it on the table for all to see.

It depicted a vast swampland, with a particular area circled in red ink. Notes in an ancient script marked various points throughout the region, with warning symbols clustered heavily around the circled location.

“Well, damn it all to the four hells,” Cade muttered when he recognized the location.

It was the one place in the world he really, really, didn’t wish to go. He’d almost prefer to go back and steal from Scorn again than go there.

Jameson traced a gray finger along the map’s edge. “The Blightmarshes. Specifically, the ruins of Cathedral Point, and the crypt beneath it.”

A heavy silence fell over both crews.

“You’ve got to be joking,” Nora stated flatly. “Honor has the entire region quarantined and patrolled by his Arbiters. Nothing survives there.”

“For good reason,” Bernard added, his earlier anger temporarily set aside in the face of this new information. “It’s said that the old gods dumped their worst nightmares there, not to mention the vile Progenitors and their spawn.

Jameson nodded, though a dark look briefly flickered over his chilly gaze. “Indeed. Which is why I require specialists. The item is hidden within the deepest level of the crypt, guarded by the gods only know what kind of traps and wards.”

“Wait. You seriously don’t know more than that? We’re going in blind?” Elena questioned sharply.

“We know that the creatures that caused the quarantine in the first place still live there,” Jameson clarified. “Ancient horrors that have made the swamp their home for centuries.”

Cade let out a low whistle. “Lovely vacation spot. Corpse-filled crypts, monster-infested swamps. You really know how to show a crew a good time.”

“The compensation reflects the risk,” Jameson replied smoothly.

“What exactly is this ‘item’ we’re risking our lives for?” Orro asked, his dark eyes never leaving the map.

Jameson exchanged a glance with Astrid before answering. “An artifact known as the Soulkeeper’s Crown. It was forged by a long-dead god during the Culling War.”

“And what does it do?” Cade pressed.

“That,” Jameson said with finality, “is clan business.”

Cade caught the subtle shift in Astrid’s expression—a flicker of something that might have been doubt, or perhaps concern. Whatever this fancy hat was, it wasn’t just “clan business.”

It was something more.

But he didn’t have time or an interest in absorbing another divine artifact, thank you very much. He’d get it, hand it off, and be on his way.

“So you want us to navigate a deadly swamp, break into an ancient crypt, face horror-story monsters, and retrieve a shiny crown,” Cade summarized. “All while competing with another crew who’d happily slit our throats.”

“That’s the essence of it, yes,” Jameson confirmed. “Though, in fairness, I’m not sure of the condition of the Soulkeeper’s Crown. It might not ‘shine,’ as you aptly put it.”

“That’s really not the clarification we need right now,” Elena muttered as she shook her head at the map.

Cade glanced at Bernard, who was also staring at the map with intense focus. Despite their animosity, he could see the gold-ranker calculating the same variables he was—the route, the obstacles, the likelihood of survival.

“This is a terrible idea,” Nora sent silently.

“We still need that map to Destruction’s domain,” Cade reminded them. “And now we need to be first, or Bernard gets it all. Friends, we can’t survive out there without the Clan’s protection. I wish there was another way, but I can’t see it right now.”

Bernard’s eyes narrowed as he studied Cade. “Having second thoughts, Stormhollow? Perhaps this job requires a more... experienced crew.”

“Just warming up,” Cade replied with a razor-sharp smile that didn’t reach his eyes. He turned back to Jameson. “One question: what’s stopping us from killing each other the moment we’re out of your sight?”

“Professional courtesy?” Jameson suggested dryly.

Cade snorted. “Try again.”

“Self-interest,” Astrid interjected. “The swamp is vast, the dangers numerous. Two crews have a better chance of navigating its perils than one alone.”

“We’ll be fine on our own, though I don’t mind if this conflagration of failures softens up the beasties for us,” Bernard quickly rebutted.

Gavin finally spoke, his voice quiet but steady, with some of his old fire returning. “That’s fine, Bernard. I like our odds. We have something you don’t.”

“Which is?” Bernard asked with a raised manicured brow.

Cade leaned back in his chair, forced nonchalance masking the anticipation he felt pounding in his chest. “His point is we’re about to go monster hunting in the world’s deadliest game preserve.”

The Son of Ruin grinned, though it was all teeth and no humor. “And you seem short a few monsters.”
