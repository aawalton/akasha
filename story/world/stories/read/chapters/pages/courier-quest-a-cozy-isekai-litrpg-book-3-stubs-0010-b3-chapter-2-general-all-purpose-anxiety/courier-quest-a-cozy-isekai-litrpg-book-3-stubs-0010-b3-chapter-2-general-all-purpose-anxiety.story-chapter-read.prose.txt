“How are you feeling, champ?” Wayne asked, slapping Trevor on the back.

“About how one would expect,” he replied with a small smile.

The two were in a tent just outside of where the wedding was to be held. It was a fantastic late autumn day, not too warm or too cold, and the sound of the nearby river soothed the nerves of anyone who needed a little white noise. Trevor didn’t generally consider himself one of those people, but it was helping today.

They had opted for an outdoor wedding. There was a perfect place less than a quarter mile away from Tosa where the river that went through the middle of town split to create an island before it merged again. Serna Island had become a great place for picnics and other events since it was far from any of the dungeons. A couple of buildings had even been built on it, though they were meant for recreation and not habitation.

Trevor adjusted his black suit and checked his cuff links for what had to have been the tenth time in the last three minutes before reaching up to check his dark green tie, which matched his vest. Wayne was quick to slap his hand away.

“The knot’s perfect,” he chastised. “Don’t you go messing it up just because you’re nervous.”

“Alright, alright, I hear you,” Trevor said, holding his hand up and away so the dwarf knew he was leaving it alone. Walking around the older Summoned, he checked the flap of the tent.

Klar and Thimbleden, his best man and groomsman, were showing guests to their seats. Both were wearing black suits that matched Trevor’s own. It was slightly more of a hassle for the minotaur, who as a whole preferred more loose clothing, but he was so ecstatic about taking part in an Earth-style wedding that he hadn’t complained yet.

Several rows of seats had been set out in front of the gazebo that had been built on Serna Island years ago. Wayne had gone out of his way to give it a fresh coat of paint a few weeks back, and the bright structure looked brand new. The event was small, but almost every seat was already full. There were more minotaurs than he remembered inviting, though.

“Klar didn’t tell me his family was so big,” Trevor muttered to himself.

The merchant prince had asked if he could bring some of his closer relatives to the event to show off Earth culture. He was one of the few anti-isolationists from the Isle of Horns, and he relished in meeting new Summoned. Not just to go into business with them, though he would if he could, but because he was genuinely curious about worlds outside of his own.

Now, Trevor couldn’t see the front of his section of seating because there were so many minotaurs in the back. Not that he was complaining; he had been asked and his consent was given. He just realized he should have asked some follow-up questions instead of blindly agreeing.

One of life’s great lessons, that.

“Shouldn’t you be going soon?” Trevor asked as he turned around. “We’ll be starting soon, and you’ve got to walk Adabelle down the aisle.”

Wayne was standing in for the demonkin’s father, which made sense considering he fancied himself the team dad of the Tosa Summoned, and he had gladly agreed to it when asked. There might have been some tears involved, not that he would ever admit it.

“There’s a whole procession that needs to go ahead before we do, so I’m golden,” he said with a shrug. “Just didn’t want to leave you alone. You don’t sit still very well.”

“I’m perfectly calm,” Trevor replied.

“And you’ve got good company,” Wayne chuckled. He grabbed a glass of juice from the table next to him and toasted to the courier before taking a sip. “Plus, I’m not going to hang around outside Addy’s tent while those ladies are fussing over her. They got Rashie to be the muscle, making sure no one bothers them, and she’s taking her job seriously. Sent her own father away, if you could believe it.”

“I sure can,” the courier laughed as he reached up to check his tie again. The dwarf gave him the stink eye, and he put his hands back down. Trevor smiled. “Really, I’m okay. There’s some general, all-purpose anxiety because, you know, this is my wedding day, but it’s no worse than usual. I’m ready for this. I’m just glad Adabelle hasn’t realized just how out of my league she is. Count myself lucky every day.”

“I’ll agree that you’re a lucky guy, but not for that. You two are good for each other,” Wayne said with a dismissive wave. “You’ve both got some troubles, sure. Everybody does. Y’all balance each other out and that’s something I think a lot of people back where we came from get mixed up on. They think it’s supposed to be about highs all the time, but nobody’s riding planes that don’t come back down again. She keeps you out of your head, and you keep her out of the muck of work. Balance.”

“Thanks,” Trevor said, his smile widening. “It’s good to hear that.”

A bell rang out across Serna Island, and Wayne finished his juice before setting it back down. “That’s my cue. Better get back before that third bell rings. Don’t want my little girl yelling at me.”

“Go, go, go, it’s all good.”

“Don’t mess with your tie, it’s perfect,” Wayne reminded him as he left the tent backwards.

“I heard you the first time, old timer.”

“Then don’t fidget with it, cinnamon roll.”

With that, the dwarf left Trevor all alone. The courier picked up and drank from his own glass. His hand unconsciously went for his tie, but he stopped mid-action.

“I’m really not very good at being still,” he said to himself. Taking a breath, he set the glass back down and waited.

Lailah arrived just as the second bell rang. She was wearing a snappy black suit with white undershirt and black tie. There was a clipboard full of paper in her hands, and she looked him up and down before nodding, apparently satisfied with his attire.

“You ready to go, messenger boy?” the elf asked as she slipped in through the tent’s flap.

“If you’re the one taking me to the altar, then wouldn’t that make you the messenger?” he asked in return.

“Don’t you get silly with me,” Lailah warned, though it was in good nature. She waved the clipboard towards the tent’s flap. “Come on, let’s get up there.”

“Lead the way.”

Fiddling with his cuff links so he wouldn’t mess with his tie, Trevor followed the elf. They passed by rows of people, and he smiled and greeted them as appropriate. A small minotaur with barely any horns raised his hand and waved it back and forth, prompting his father to quickly rein him in. The courier returned the gesture, though not nearly as enthusiastic, and a big grin blossomed on the kid’s face.

Drelik, at the front, stood up to greet Trevor. He had become something of a grandfather to the courier in his time here, and wasted no time giving him a hug. Clay emerged from the dwarf’s pocket to nuzzle their master’s cheek. The two made an unlikely pair, but the familiar had come to enjoy the gallery, both the paintings and the sculptures. They enjoyed seeing new and exotic things.

“I’m so happy for you, my boy,” Drelik said quietly. “Marriage is one of the greatest steps a man can take, and I’m glad you found someone like Adabelle to share your life with.”

“Thank you,” Trevor said, patting him on the back. “And thank you for your advice. You helped me get here.”

“Any time, Trevor. Any time.” They pulled away, all smiles, and Clay hesitated.

“C’mon,” Lailah said, holding out a hand for the familiar. “You can have a front row seat with me if you promise to behave.”

Clay nodded enthusiastically before jumping onto her hand. The groom followed Lailah up onto the gazebo. There was already a podium waiting for her and she took her position behind it with the familiar laying down flat on one side. Trevor stood to her right. His posture was ramrod straight as he smiled at the crowd.

The majority of it, minus the family of minotaurs near the back, were Tosan natives or those who lived close by. Several of the business owners Trevor had worked for were here, including Corrin, who made the wedding party’s outfits, and Grunder, who was sitting farther back so he wouldn’t obstruct too many people’s view.

Most of city hall was here, too. That included Bocoro, the halfling official who was here on behalf of King Eskretet, who was invited on principle even though no one expected him to appear in person. He was also going to be the temporary mayor while Adabelle was away.

The Queen of the Feywood and her son, Prince, had also been invited to come. They had declined for their own reasons, unfortunately. Everyone was pretty sure the capricious fey still had her vagabond child confined to house arrest.

That meant the only denizen of the Feywood who was in attendance was Glyrphizith, the dragon. She was absolutely massive and there was no appropriate chair for someone her size. Her green-scaled body stretched over one of the rivers, which still allowed it to flow without obstacle underneath, and one of her wings provided shade for those seated.

Dragons, as Trevor learned, had a fairly good reputation in this world despite their intimidating size. Glyrphizith made conversation with everyone who approached, and delighted in speaking with the few children who were brave enough to greet her.

Melvirn was fidgeting in his seat. Trevor had extended an invitation to the absentminded wizard under the assumption that he was going to forget when it was happening, but he had come. Dory and Greer were beside him, which explained his presence neatly. Not that the courier had hoped he wouldn’t show up; he was just pleasantly surprised.

Gabin Roux sat with Reed, Beth, and their son Dill. The lord of Vilderchtif mingled jovially with the proprietors of the Halffle Inn, whose other two children were a part of the procession. It was good to see people brought together like this.

The third bell rang as Trevor’s gaze met Drelik’s again. He hazarded a small wave, which was returned, before he looked down the aisle.

There was only one groomsman and bridesmaid in addition to the best man and maid of honor. Adabelle had wanted a small ceremony, though it had certainly grown a little bigger than that, but the wedding party still reflected that wish.

Thimbleden and Rashie were the first to come, arm in arm with each other. He wore a black outfit with a vest the same green as Trevor’s tie, while she wore a light blue dress. The courier could immediately tell that the shark girl was nervous based on the way her tail slapped the ground, but she put one foot in front of the other. Perhaps a little too quickly because her gnomish escort had to hustle to keep up.

When they reached the gazebo, Thimbleden delivered Rashie to her place, giving the girl a soft smile before heading to his own spot. He nudged Trevor as he walked passed in solidarity, and the courier turned his attention to the next pair.

Klarkartar walked down the aisle arm in arm with Griffin, the three-tailed kitsune. Like Lailah, her wife, she had also adventured with Adabelle before she started training to become Tosa’s mayor. They were a mismatched pair considering how tall he was compared to her, but they moved with grace and dignity. The merchant prince, ever the gentleman, bowed as he delivered the maid of honor before moving to his place behind Trevor.

But not before giving the groom two thumbs up out of excitement, throwing decorum out of the window for a quick moment. Still, Trevor couldn’t fault him. He was giddy, too.

The ring bearer, Rupert, walked down the aisle. A pair of wedding bands laid together on the silver pillow he carried, which had his utmost attention. The look on his face was one of solemn determination. He had worried about tripping, or having them slide off, or that something was going to go wrong and he was incredibly careful.

His relief was palpable as he handed off the rings to Klar, who pocketed them. The minotaur winked at the halfling boy, who beamed before running off, putting all of that nervous energy to good use now that he was done.

His sister, Jewel, was already halfway to the gazebo. She twirled as she threw white rose petals from her basket with reckless abandon. While her older brother had been very serious, she was milking this moment for all it was worth. It was clear she was enjoying herself, so much so that the halfling girl ran out of petals before reaching the first two rows.

She looked up with panic, eyes set on Lailah. The elf just smiled and beckoned her forward and to the side where her brother was. Jewel curtsied on her way by, apologizing to the front rows in a quick, quiet tone that they wouldn’t have any flower petals flung at them.

There was silence after that as everyone turned. A fourth bell rang out, as clear as day, and Wayne stepped around the corner. His arm was looped around the bride’s.

Trevor swallowed, and then his heart skipped a beat. A part of his brain fizzled as he got his first look at Adabelle in her wedding dress.
