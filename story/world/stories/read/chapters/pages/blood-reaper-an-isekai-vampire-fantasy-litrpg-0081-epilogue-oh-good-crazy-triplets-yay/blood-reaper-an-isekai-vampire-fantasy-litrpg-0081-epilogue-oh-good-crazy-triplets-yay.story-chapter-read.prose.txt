New prey.

How utterly delightful.

Lysandra Ravenhart stood before the hidden observation window that looked out over her throne room. Her silver-white hair flowed like liquid moonlight down her back. From this concealed alcove high above, she studied the newcomers entering the Crucible her long-lost lover had created.

A Crucible that was undeniably hers, now.

Part of her wanted to speak with these strangers. Deep down, she craved their stories. To know what had brought them here. To hear of their battles, their wars, the blood they had shed to make it all this way.

But six centuries of existence had taught her patience.

Still as death, she simply watched as they reached the foot of the stairs leading to her platform. Her two sisters stood on either side of her, all of them silent as ghosts as they observed the first signs of outside life they had witnessed in over a decade.

“They survived Raven,” Vivienne remarked, her voice carrying a childlike wonder that belied the ancient hunger in her crimson eyes. Her white gown glowed against her perfect alabaster skin, creating an illusion of purity that made her predatory smile all the more jarring. “What a delightful surprise.”

“Beastly,” Octavia agreed. The middle triplet leaned closer to the window, her amber eyes dilating with interest. The blue-black veining beneath her pale skin pulsed visibly, a remnant of the countless monster essences she had absorbed during her centuries of evolution. “Which do you think is the monster among them? There’s always one.”

“The big one,” Vivienne answered with a wicked little smile. “A handsome devil, perhaps, but a monster nonetheless. Look at the way he carries himself.”

“I think it’s the female with the dark hair,” Octavia countered, her mismatched claws tapping against the waist-high wall that separated them from a steep drop to the throne room below. “Her movements remind me of the Ashfire Elementals. Look at how she moves. The way she watches the others when they’re not looking. You can tell she already sees death as art. I’d say she’s halfway to becoming like us already.”

“On the contrary,” Vivienne interjected. “She’s one of mine. A deadly beauty, like a nightshade. She will make a lovely addition to my collection.”

As her sisters debated, Lysandra remained silent. Her focus never left the tall man in the center of the small group’s formation. His broad shoulders and dark features reminded her of a few lovers she’d had before, but he carried himself in a wholly unique way.

Perhaps Octavia was right.

Perhaps he was the monster among the others.

The ancient musk of the timeless, moss-drenched stone permeated her hidden chamber, along with the distinctive emptiness of Shadow Realm energy that always lingered on her skin. The air shimmered with displaced reality, betraying the small stretch of air where the three sisters had bent the world to create their private viewing parlor.

From here, they could see everything—every beautiful nook and cranny of their altered landscape.

“A healer,” she finally said, her voice carrying the cold echoes of a thousand assassinations. “When was the last time we saw one of those, Octavia? Three hundred years?”

“Longer,” her middle sister answered. “Much longer.”

“Hmm.” Lysandra tapped one finger against her cheek, and her crimson lips parted to reveal her perfect fangs. “It’s been so long since we’ve had toys to play with.”

“It has indeed,” Vivienne replied, though her eyes never left the newcomers.

The four vampires below stepped cautiously across the polished floor, their weapons ready and their eyes scanning for threats. Lysandra and her sisters watched them with calculated interest.

Vivienne giggled. “I want the telepath. Breaking his will is going to be such delicious fun.”

Lysandra kept her focus on the healer who moved protectively near the leader. The crimson mist that always accompanied them swirled more rapidly around her feet, responding to her interest.

Healers never lasted long in the System’s world.

Perhaps this one was worth collecting. It had been so long since Lysandra had claimed a new toy of her own.

“A team.” Octavia scoffed, her lip curling to reveal teeth that had evolved far beyond what any vampire should possess. “Loyalty is such a tedious quality. It never lasts in this place.”

“And yet,” Lysandra replied, “it makes the moment when they finally turn on each other so much more satisfying.”

With a subtle wave of her hand, she adjusted their viewing angle, focusing more closely on the leader. As his features became clearer, Lysandra felt a jolt of recognition so powerful it momentarily stole her breath. The mist around her darkened and swirled more rapidly, betraying her emotional state despite her carefully maintained composure.

A Dusk Emperor.

Here, of all places.

“You’re intrigued by this one,” Vivienne observed, her tone suggesting she’d noticed more than Lysandra wished to reveal. “Why?”

Lysandra composed herself and simply shook her head in answer.

Her sisters didn’t need to know the danger they were in. This man, strong as he might’ve been, was still a baby vampire—and that meant he didn’t yet understand the true scope of his potential.

No, her sisters could remain blissfully ignorant. She needed them to focus on their challenges, after all, to preserve this growing realm of hers—a land beyond either the System or the Shadow Realm’s control.

This man was someone Lysandra would take care of on her own.
