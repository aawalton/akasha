Adam’s feet sunk into the deep layer of ash. Burning fragments rained down like snow and his surroundings lay in ruin. A haze of heat and smog curled through the air around him.

Even though his father hadn’t been a part of the Trials, he had still been given an island, or, more likely, this ruined ash-filled place was the same regardless of which dead person he travelled to using the visiting stone.

He instinctively knew not to travel to any of the players’ dimensions in this universe, since they were infected with the Flayed Lady’s power, but he needed to speak with Fal, so this had been the only way to find her that he could think of.

Adam trotted through the deep ash, going north towards the player house. Above him, the dark clouds drifted lazily by, while crimson lightning swam through them like leviathan monsters.

His relics were gone and his newly acquired boosts to his stats were likewise suppressed, but he wasn’t afraid of this place like he’d been last time, because he knew the power that Fal’s soul bargains represented. Especially to someone like him, who needed to take risks to reach his goal.

The smog cleared briefly to reveal a silhouette in front of him. Unlike Luvicidix and Alivida, she already looked humanoid.

Fal turned to meet his eyes, the fire in her irises standing in stark contrast to her grey hair and dark moth wings. “You returned,” she said evenly.

“I need your help,” Adam told her. “The Flayed Lady has pitted every single player against me in this world.”

“That explains the sense of wrongness I feel about this universe,” Fal remarked.

“Luvicidix said the same thing,” Adam replied.

“Would you like to peruse my bargains of the soul?” Fal asked.

“Before that, I have something I need to give you,” Adam said, handing her the vanity mirror.

Fal accepted the hand mirror, gripping its black-veined handle as she gazed at her own reflection.

“Alivida crafted this. I can feel her soul in it, though the magic is borrowed,” she said and the mirror vanished, though no visible change came over her.

“Do you feel any different?” Adam asked, unsure if he’d made the right decision or not. His hope was that she would be more open with the bargains and what they did, if he first freed her from this place. It was a shame he wouldn’t be able to use whatever weapon she might offer him, but that was besides the point anyway.

Fal looked at Parvillo who floated next to Adam. He hadn’t even noticed his companion come along to this ruined place, but then again, he was far more powerful than the cubes.

Her eyes returned to Adam. “I have gained a modicum of autonomy it would seem.”

With a wave of her hand, ash and fire washed across the ruined island, before they returned to Adam’s dimension, appearing behind the lighthouse on the northern part of the island where a break in the trees on the edge led to a steep drop into the clouds below.

Ash and fire floated around them in the same way that the other three moths used their scales to stop time, but unlike their scales, the ash slowly gathered on the grass where they stood.

“The air here is much less oppressive,” Fal noted with a tone of satisfaction in her voice. “Thank you, Adam. While I cannot meet my sisters, I can feel them nearby. That is a consolation.”

She reached out a smouldering hand to him and a piece of ash landed on her palm. Then she closed her fingers over it and turned her arm to drop it onto Adam’s awaiting hand.

What landed on his palm was not ash, but instead a small stylised dark-grey metallic hand with long nails gripping a gem with fire swirling around inside it, moving about as though it was a living thing.

< < Unique Relic Obtained > >

< Ash and Fire Brooch (Divine) — The funeral brings ash from the fire, and from the ash sprouts new life >

Divine??

I thought she’d give me a weapon, not a relic. Do all the moths give relics instead of weapons, perhaps?

But what does that even do?

Adam equipped the relic and it just attached itself to his milky-white slime body, on the left side of where his chest would normally have been.

“Thank you, but how does it work?” he asked

“You must turn the dead to ash to release their soul,” she replied, cryptically.

I guess I’ll try and use it on a corpse and see what happens.

Maybe it creates something or heals me, when I use it. Or maybe the ash it produces has special properties?

Fal moved her hands through the air, trailing embers. “But this is why you came to me, is it not? Remember, all power comes with a price.”

< < Fal’s Soul Bargains > >

There were a lot less options this time, and two he hadn’t seen before, but which had demerits too negative for him to use regardless. The one he’d been specifically seeking was missing though.

“Where’s the Lonesome Spire upgrade?” Adam asked.

“It is not yet available,” Fal said.

[It would be a good choice for you,] Parvillo remarked.

Fal looked at the icosahedron, but then turned her gaze back to Adam. “Perhaps it would be better for you to wait until I have the bargain you seek. You cannot accept more than one bargain, after all.”

Adam nodded. He had hoped to get the Lonesome Spire immediately, since it would double his stats in exchange for making it impossible to leave any stage if his teammates were still alive. Normally, it would have been a foolish pick, since the demerit could not be switched off, but given the hostility of this universe, it was literally the perfect choice for him.

“When will it be available?”

“While I am allowed to explain my bargains in more detail to you thanks to the vanity, I cannot answer that question,” Fal said.

[It will appear after Stage Four,] Parvillo answered, which surprised Adam.

“Aren’t you breaking the System’s rules by telling me?” Adam asked him.

[The rules state fairness is mandated. Given your situation, it is fair to inform you properly, given the opposition you are facing.]

That’s some bizarre logic, but it works in my favour, so I will take it.

“The nameless upgrade, what does it do?” Adam asked Fal.

“While wearing the ‘Hood of Namelessness’, you become able to turn invisible at will,” Fal answered.

That sounds insanely powerful, but the demerit is awful.

“And the invading stone is just like the visiting stone, but I get to hurt the player I invade?” Adam asked.

“Correct, and like the visiting stone, it only functions while on Interim Island,” Fal said.

“What about the other bargains you had last time? The Soul Dial and the Creation Spark. How do they work exactly?”

“I cannot answer those questions since the upgrades are not currently available to you,” Fal replied.

Adam looked to Parvillo, but he remained silent, so it was likely a hard rule he could not or would not break, especially since the answer didn’t help with the current situation.

“Are you able to go wherever you wish on the island?” Adam asked her, changing the subject.

“Yes, I may go where I wish, but I cannot be seen by anyone except you.”

“You should visit the garden,” Adam told her. “Meilo is there, and inside the tent, Alivida appears at night.”

Fal looked to Parvillo, like she was silently asking for permission.

When he didn’t say anything, she looked back to Adam and said, “I just might.”

She turned her gaze towards the clouds above. Unlike in the dimension where he’d found her, the clouds here were fluffy and calm, illuminated and dyed by the amber light of the setting sun.

What was more, in the clouds above was where Luvicidix resided.

I wonder if she’s looking for her sister.

What might happen if I gift all of the moths the vanity upgrade?

Without a word of goodbye, the ash falling down around them just vanished, taking Fal with it.

The ash that had already fallen remained where it coated the grass.

Some had landed on Adam’s body, but his slime skin had absorbed it without him noticing.

Although Adam didn’t regret giving Charlie the ability to leave the tavern, which was haunted by what the Flayed Lady had done, the realisation that the moths might give him relics instead of weapons in return for the vanities was something that completely changed their value to him this loop.

I’ll give my last one to Meilo, Adam decided and headed for the garden.

Maybe I should spend my shards to get a fourth vanity. I won’t be unlocking the third sheath this time regardless, and the unique relics, if indeed each of the moths give one, might make a big difference.

As he walked past the lighthouse, he wondered if its boon would inadvertently end up protecting the hostile players he’d be grouped with from stage six and onward.

I just hope she hasn’t used the same strategy as Nwetrou did in my last loop, making it so all the biggest threats have blocked me in the tavern to stay hidden.

Although, I suppose she could just keep them out of the tavern so I wouldn’t be able to target them…

As Adam neared the garden, he saw small bits of ash dotted the road here and there, until the buildings opened up to show the garden proper, revealing Meilo who was in the process of pruning a small apple tree. Nearby, though unseen by her, was a pile of ash that slowly grew on its own.

Even though she cannot be seen, Fal is leaving a trace of her presence.

“Hello Adam,” she greeted him. “Have you finally realised my worth?”

She really took that comment personally…

It didn’t seem like she sensed Fal’s presence nearby, even though the ash should be an obvious indicator.

“I’ve brought you a vanity mirror,” Adam said, handing it to her.

Meilo stared into it curiously, until suddenly the golden-orange light inside her expanded and made her blinding to look at.

Once the intense light passed, what remained was a humanoid hawk-moth made of transparent glass. Her eyes glowed with the same light that pinged around inside her body, and she had a summer dress of triangular glass shards covering her. The two wings hanging down her back looked more like a cape now, and she had short hair with each strand like a thin glass tube, as well as relatively short antennae. Despite the transformation, she still did not look ‘alive’.

Meilo flexed her wings. “Thank you for this gift. I may be able to answer your questions more candidly now.”

“Well, in that case,” Adam started.

“Before you ask, I must give you this,” she interrupted him. She put her hands together, and the golden-orange firefly within her body flew into her cupped fingers, before returning to her chest.

When she lifted her right hand, a small glass shape lay atop her left palm. She offered it to Adam.
