The air in the western valley tasted of churned earth and nervous sweat. Dalgor tightened his grip on his iron-tipped spear, his knuckles white against the dark wood.

He stood near the vanguard of a massive, disorganized sea of humanity. The army of peasants, conscripts, and minor loyalists had swollen from a few thousand to nearly three million as they marched through the heart of Aerthos. They had moved under the protective, shimmering umbrellas of House Black’s Mage Towers, avoiding the worst of the Cinderfall patrols, gathering momentum like a slow-moving avalanche.

But now, the march was over. They had arrived at the front lines.

Every man and woman in the sprawling formation braced for the slaughter. They expected a bloodbath, a desperate, final stand where they would sell their lives dearly to buy the young Lord Wight even a fraction of an hour.

The reality, however, broke their grim anticipation.

As Dalgor’s column crested the final ridge, they were met by rows of massive, pristine combat tents and sprawling, immaculate medical pavilions. Figures clad in sleek, dark armor moved efficiently among the arriving troops. They were a mix of humans—faces Dalgor recognized from the old Wight retainers—and silver-haired Dark Elves.

They were distributing hot food in sealed silver pouches and organizing triage for those wounded on the march.

Dalgor pushed through the confused ranks, anxious to see the battlefield. He walked past the medical tents, his boots crunching on the frozen ash, until he reached the edge of the true front line.

He stopped, his breath catching in his throat.

To the east, the towering, jagged peaks of the dwarven mountains stood like a wall. Behind him to the west, the Mage Towers of House Black pulsed with dark, potent energy. The Cinderfall army was massing in the valley to the south.

But they were not attacking.

Every time a Cinderfall unit attempted to advance, or even peeked a head above their hastily dug trenches, the ground would erupt, or a streak of blue light would snap from the horizon, vaporizing the threat instantly. The war had stalled into a brutal, one-sided containment.

A hand grasped Dalgor’s shoulder.

He jumped, spinning around, his spear half-raised.

An elf stood there, his face unreadable behind the visor of his black helmet. "Stunning, isn't it?" the elf said, his voice amplified by the suit's speakers. "It's all thanks to our Lord Commander. The weapons he invented might as well have changed warfare forever."

The elf gestured toward the distant, humming turrets. "The soldiers tell me you're their leader. The Lord Commander wishes to speak with you."

Dalgor stiffened, a surge of inadequacy washing over him. "You flatter me," he replied quickly, his voice rough. "I am no leader. I am just a man who stepped up in a time of need and confusion. I hold no rank."

The elf chuckled, a low, metallic sound. "That is precisely what a leader is. It’s nice to meet you. My name is Erovos. I am part of the Operations and Logistics Corps under General Mirelle."

"I am Dalgor," he muttered, acutely aware of his mud-stained tunic and rusted breastplate.

He felt a sudden, crushing sense of shame. He was about to meet the Lord of House Wight, the architect of this impossible, terrifying army, looking like a beggar.

Erovos led him through the camp. The anxiety buzzing in Dalgor’s veins was almost overwhelmed by the sheer, surreal efficiency of the place. It looked less like a military encampment and more like a massive, finely tuned clockwork mechanism. Everyone moved with a specific, undeniable purpose. The quiet hum of absolute organization replaced the usual shouting and chaos of a war camp.

They arrived at a large, heavily warded communications station. Erovos snapped a crisp salute to a massive figure standing near the console. The man wore the crest of House Wight on his black armor. General Goliath.

Dalgor stopped. He was standing in front of a solid, blank wall of black glass. He opened his mouth to ask where the Lord was, but the wall suddenly shifted color.

The glass hummed. A life-sized silhouette of a man materialized. The image resolved, revealing a young man with silver hair and sharp, sapphire eyes. Dalgor recognized the regal, elegant features of the Lady of House Wight, but the fierce, striking intensity in those eyes belonged entirely to the late Duke Kaelen.

Alarion Wight spoke.

"I thank you, and everyone who has marched forward to aid me. I shall remember this favor you have shown me. For now, rest and recuperate."

The voice was calm, holding an absolute authority that demanded obedience.

"You all shall act as the last line of defense. If the enemy manages to break through our blockade, your troops shall remain to the west as a reserve force. You will work under General Goliath directly. Also, if you or your people need anything—rations, medical supplies, or anything else—coordinate with our logistics core. We shall aid you to the best of our ability."

Dalgor gripped his spear tight. "But, my Lord," he argued, his voice trembling slightly. "I am but a mere peasant. There are others more suited to lead these forces. There are several noble houses and lords among the assembled army. They will not answer to a commoner. I did what I did due to convenience and circumstance. I am not fit to lead."

Alarion offered a small, sharp smile.

"In my army, merit is valued above birth and lineage. If it is the nobles you fear, I will solve that."

Alarion’s image stood taller. "I, Alarion Wight, Acting Duke of House Wight, knight thee as Sir Dalgor, Knight of House Wight."

General Goliath stepped forward, nudging Dalgor from the side.

Every man who had ever lived in Aerthos knew what to do next. It was a moment every peasant boy dreamed of. To be knighted by House Wight meant the right to command. It offered the opportunity, however slim, to visit the Blue Dragon territory and attempt to bond with a dragon.

Goliath held out a long, heavy blade. It was a signature weapon, slightly different from the traditional Dragon Knight blade, forged from a dark, humming alloy.

Dalgor didn't hesitate. He dropped to one knee, resting his rusted spear on the ground, and took the blade. He bowed his head, swearing the oath of a knight of House Wight in a voice thick with emotion.

He didn't know it, but Alarion had been watching him for days. The Shadow Order’s regular reports highlighted Dalgor’s actions. The man was the only thing holding the chaotic, makeshift army of three million together. He was an unpolished talent with the spark of a true political leader or military general. Alarion needed to secure him, to legitimize his command before the nobles tore the army apart with their bickering.

Goliath, having been briefed beforehand, simply nodded as Dalgor rose, a new weight settling on the former peasant's shoulders.

The communication feed cut out, plunging the CIC back into its usual, sterile silence.

I rubbed my eyes, a dull ache throbbing behind my temples. That was exhausting. I needed to return to my chambers. If I missed another meal, my mother would likely commandeer the CIC and turn it into our new family dining room. I had been meticulously hiding the brutal, grinding reality of the ongoing war from Lyra, and the last thing I needed was to feed her explosive tendencies by exposing her to live combat feeds. She enjoyed the spectacle of the explosions far more than she understood the lives they reaped.

I walked down the quiet, obsidian corridors toward the residential wing, the soft hum of the ship’s engines a comforting vibration beneath my boots.

As I approached my quarters, I slowed.

Sitting on the floor outside the heavy weirwood doors was Ela.

Patricia sat quietly beside her, hands folded neatly in her lap.

Ela was clutching a large plush toy, almost half her size. It was a dragon, styled suspiciously like Kaelus, but there was a glaring difference. Instead of starlit scales, the plushy was a vibrant, deep crimson. Nyxia had apparently sewn it herself for the girl. Ela had declared that she liked the color of Nyxia’s eyes and wanted her dragon to match.

The crimson dragon had quickly become Ela’s favorite, mirroring how Lyra carried her miniature Reaper armor plushy everywhere.

As soon as she saw me, Ela jumped up, her face lighting up with excitement. Then, almost instantly, she hesitated, her shoulders curling inward as a familiar, shy apprehension took over.

Patricia stood up, smoothing her uniform. "The young lady has been visiting your door for the past few days, my Lord," she explained quietly. "She was hoping to speak with you, but she insisted on not disturbing your work."

I knelt, bringing myself down to Ela’s eye level. I reached out and gently ruffled her silver hair. "You should never have to worry about that, Ela. I always have time for family. Now, what is it I can do for my sister?"

Ela leaned into the head pat, a small, tentative smile touching her lips. "I... I have been visiting the lady who helped me," she said, her voice a meek, reedy whisper. "The one from Oakhaven."

She paused, her sapphire eyes darting to Patricia to check if she was saying something wrong. Reassured by the maid's neutral expression, she continued.

"She recently told me I can no longer visit her," Ela said, clutching the crimson dragon tighter. "Because she will be going back to work soon."

Ela took a deep breath, biting her lower lip as she mustered her courage.

"Brother," she asked, her voice trembling slightly. "Can you not send her to work so soon? She just recovered from those injuries. She... she should rest for a bit more."

I looked into her eyes. They were swimming with tears she was desperately trying to hold back. The guilt was radiating off her in waves. She clearly blamed herself for what had happened to Varuna, for the brutal beating the operative had taken to protect her in the mud.

I stood up, keeping my expression neutral, and looked at Patricia for an explanation.

"Operative Varuna has returned to The Aegis for rest and recuperation, my Lord," Patricia reported, her voice carrying the crisp efficiency of the Shadow Prime. "She has recently passed her physical and psychological assessments and has been deemed ready for active service. She has filed a request for a new assignment."

This was complicated. Varuna had requested the reassignment herself.

I looked back at Patricia, an idea forming. "Does she have formal maid training? And how fond is she of Ela and Lyra?"

I had recently combined the attendants for Ela and Lyra into a unified team. They would split up if the girls went in different directions, but since the two were inseparable ninety percent of the time, a unified guard detail was the most efficient logistical choice.

"Operative Varuna has completed comprehensive training as a maid, my Lord," Patricia confirmed, a hint of understanding in her eyes. "It is a standard part of her espionage infiltration training. And she has grown quite fond of the young ladies during her recovery."

"Okay then," I said, making the decision. "Have her assigned to their detail. That is her new assignment."

Ela looked utterly confused. She stared at me, trying to parse the military jargon.

"It means she will stay here," I explained gently, crouching back down. "She will stay with you and play with you, just like your other attendants do."

The confusion on Ela’s face vanished, replaced by an expression of pure, unadulterated relief. She didn't say thank you. She just launched herself forward and hugged my neck tightly, burying her face in my uniform.
