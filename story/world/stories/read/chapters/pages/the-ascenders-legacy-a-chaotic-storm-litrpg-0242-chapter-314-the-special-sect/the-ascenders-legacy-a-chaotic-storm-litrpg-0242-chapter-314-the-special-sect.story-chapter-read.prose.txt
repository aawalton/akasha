After the proclamation, Aodhán wasn't allowed to join the other initiates. Instead, elder ThunderWind took him, urged him onto a platform of stormy wind, and immediately flew out of the area, muttering something about getting out while they still could.

"The other Specials should distract them a bit," he muttered, his gaze darting worriedly toward the gathered crowd even as his platform zipped through the air faster than any bullet Aodhán could imagine.

“Am I in danger?” Aodhán asked jokingly, but Elder ThunderWind didn’t smile.

“If we remain in the outer sect for much longer, then yes, you are in danger.” He smiled then, shaking his head. "You may not realize this, but your actions today have sparked the hearts of many. You are an inspiration, and I bet you've earned yourself more than a few fans in the last hour alone. Myself included."

Aodhán smiled awkwardly. "I'm glad to hear that."

Elder ThunderWind chuckled. "Being a Special has its downsides, and after so many years as a judge, I've learned that for Specials like you, it's always better to get out as quickly as possible."

"Else we'll be swarmed," Aodhán surmised, grimacing as memories of reporters in Lutia flashed across his mind. He'd always hated people fawning over him like that, but where Lutian reporters had been intent on understanding his power and finding faults in his words, Aodhán doubted the disciples cared about any of that. More likely, they would all challenge him to a duel to experience his strength for themselves.

Regardless, he was glad he could avoid it.

With all that had happened, he hadn’t gotten the chance to see how well the others had done. And as the Mountain of Tempest disappeared behind them, he asked. “What about the other initiates? How well did they do? How well did Crixis do?”

ThunderWind smiled. "I believe your friend Crixis made it all the way to the core sect—an exceptional feat for someone with no inheritance or bloodline. But then again, with parents already in the sect, he must have been training his spirit ever since he could walk."

Aodhán smiled at that, not too surprised by how far Crixis had climbed. For all intents and purposes, he had seen it coming.

They soon reached the outer sect proper, and Elder ThunderWind asked. “Is there anything you need to take from the visitors’ quarters?”

Aodhán shook his head. “No, I’ve taken it all.”

"Good, because I doubt you'll be coming back down here for a while. Many never even bother to. So, if you enjoyed your stay in the outer sect, this is your chance to soak it all in."

Aodhán glanced down at the outer sect from their position in the sky and sighed, taking in all its rugged magnificence and delicate beauty. Several buildings rose into the air, none quite as spectacular as the initiation center itself, yet bearing a sense of age that made them look more like monuments rather than mere buildings.

But as beautiful as the sect was, the truth was that Aodhán hadn’t formed any attachment to it or its people. If he left now, the only people he would miss would be Rhetta, Yerxa, and Zarion. And even then, it wouldn’t be a crushing emotion.

Smiling, he glanced down at the residential quarters. He'd hoped to give Rhetta Gorvak's sword, but that didn't seem quite possible any longer. Perhaps, he could send it to her after settling down in the Special Sect.

Inhaling deeply, his gaze lingered on the sect for a moment before straying upward, past the inner and core sects’ mountains, to linger on the sect shimmering just above the clouds.

His gaze hardened with determination, and he nodded. "I'm ready."

The construct shot toward the special sect immediately, and a minute later, they passed through a shimmering barrier of energy that would have been impassable had Aodhán been flying alone.

“The barriers prevent disciples from going and coming as they like,” Elder ThunderWind explained with a smile. “Especially those from the lower sects.”

“But those from higher sects can go down?” Aodhán asked.

Elder ThunderWind nodded. “As much as they wish.” He raised a finger to Aodhán, showing off a glinting obsidian ring. “This provides them access. You’ll receive the same access when you get yours.”

They burst through the clouds and into the Special Sect a moment later, and Aodhán opened his eyes to find himself in what he could only describe as a city of refined storm. It was vastly smaller than the outer sect, barely a quarter of its size, but it was so astonishingly beautiful it was like he'd stepped into a new dimension entirely.

The buildings here were less ancient, yet what they lacked in age, they more than made up for in grandeur. Mansions of polished black stone rose, and special metals dotted the sect, each one peculiar and radiating an aura of utmost power.

Runes shimmered everywhere—on the buildings, on the ground, even on the trees—lighting the entire sect in an array of jade and gold.

But beyond all that beauty was the density of storm essence in the air—so saturated it was practically alive. Each breath tasted electric, like ozone after a lightning strike. Aodhán could feel it humming against his skin, seeping into his pathways with each inhale—so powerfully that he had to fight the urge to drop to his knees and begin cultivating immediately.

Elder ThunderWind smiled proudly at him. "Special disciple Aodhán Brystion, welcome to the Special sect. Your new home."

Aodhán just gaped at the sect, lost for words. The sect was not just beautiful. It was the perfect cultivation spot, and he’d just been handed that on a platter.

“This is…” Aodhán finally whispered, shaking his head. Yet he found no words to qualify the sight before him.

Beside him, elder ThunderWind smiled proudly. "I think 'perfect' is the word you're looking for."

Aodhán shook his head. “I fear perfect doesn’t quite cut it.”

Elder ThunderWind chuckled. “Why not let’s settle for it while you figure out a better term.”

Aodhán nodded, still awestruck as he took in every detail of the sect below him. There were only a few dozen buildings in the entire sect, and seven of them were residential—their architecture seemingly tailored to their occupants.

The first rose in a sweeping serpentine arc, its black stone walls rippling like scales caught in perpetual motion. The mansion was huge, easily the biggest among the ten, and from its sides, pressurized streams of water cascaded down with deliberate force, creating an illusion of the building rising from watery depths.

The second was just as unique, with towers stretching skyward in defiant lines as sharp as talons. Gold and crimson lightning arced between each spire in controlled bursts, never quite touching but always threatening to. The roof swept back like unfurled wings, each tile etched with feather patterns that shimmered when lightning struck nearby.

The other buildings had no animalistic theme, and the moment his gaze landed on the seventh mansion, Aodhán realized why.

The seventh mansion stood apart from the others—not in distance, but in presence—its walls still humming with fresh power as if it hadn't quite decided whether it wanted to be solid yet. And just like the other two, it had an animalistic theme: draconic majesty blending almost seamlessly with chaotic unpredictability.

Its midnight walls rippled like scales etched with lightning-shaped runes—violet, red, and gold crackling in short bursts of energy. But rather than a roof like the others had, this mansion was shielded by a stretch of solid storm, shaped into draconic wings.

It would have been comedic if not for the intense aura of power radiating from it—a power his spirit completely resonated with.

Exhaling in amazement, he whispered. "I take it that one's mine."

Elder ThunderWind nodded. "It was completed only a few hours ago. The architects had help from High Elder Zarxan to incorporate your familiar into the design.”

Aodhán smiled. “They got it right.”

He glanced at the first two mansions, then put the pieces together. It seemed that only specials with familiars got animalistic-themed buildings. A dragon-themed building for him and Varéc, a thunderbird-themed building for the second mansion, and a—

He frowned at the theme of the first building, not quite sure he understood what theme the architects had been going for. Frown deepening in stumped thoughts, he glanced at elder ThunderWind and asked. “Is that a whale?”

Elder ThunderWind laughed. “No, it most certainly is not. You are close though.”

Aodhán glanced back at the mansion, trying to glean some knowledge from its watery theme—a myriad of sea creatures rushing through his mind. But none quite seemed to fit the grandeur and subtle terror the mansion emanated.

Except, well, a whale.

When nothing else came to mind, he asked. "What is it?"

"A Leviathan," Elder ThunderWind replied proudly, his expression morphing into a wide smile when Aodhán’s eyes widened in shock. “It belongs to Special Disciple Nymera Vaelthorn, the most powerful transmigrant the sect has seen in centuries.” He smirked at Aodhán. “Though I believe you just contested that authority.”

Aodhán glanced back at elder ThunderWind, not quite sure how to respond to such a statement. Indirectly, Elder ThunderWind had just named him the strongest disciple the sect had seen in centuries, and he wasn’t sure how he felt about that.

All his life in Lutia, he’d stood out. He’d been the strongest disciple of his set in the 5th academy, and he wasn’t sure he wanted a repeat of experiences that came with that.

So instead, he glanced at his mansion and asked. “Can I take a look inside?”

“Of course,” Elder ThunderWind cleared his throat awkwardly. “Let’s get you acquainted before the other specials arrive. They’ll be here very soon.”

Aodhán nodded, and together they floated down toward the dragon-themed building whose aura seemed to pulse as they drew close. Aodhán regarded it for a moment.

“It’s not a normal building, is it?” he asked, and Elder ThunderWind shook his head.

“No, it is not. None of the buildings you see in the Special Sect are. They are all Mythic-ranked items.”

“What?” Aodhán glanced sharply at him in shock. The Mythic rank was the rank above the Epic+ rank, and in all his time in Lutia, Aodhán couldn’t remember ever seeing one. Yet, somehow every building in the special sect was apparently Mythic?

It seemed he’d grossly underestimated the wealth of the Storm Sect.

Glancing at the buildings with renewed appreciation, he asked. “Are they soul-bound?”

“Of course,” Elder ThunderWind frowned at him. “All Mythic items are soul-bound. They will be bonded to you like your familiar and cannot truly be destroyed as long as you remain alive.”

“That… that’s amazing.” Aodhán smiled, his mind racing with possibilities. “Will it grow alongside me? Or will I outgrow it when I get to the Mythic class?”

Elder ThunderWind nodded. “That’s usually the problem with soul-bound Mythic items. But this one will grow with you. If you meet all its requirements for growth, you can upgrade it all the way to the Blessed Rank.”

Aodhán shook his head, unable to believe that he’d gotten it all for free. Of course, he was paying for it with his presence here as a transmigrant, but still…

He shook his head again, and Elder ThunderWind chuckled. “You are a special disciple, Aodhán Brystion. This is only the first of many gifts you will receive. You need to acquaint yourself with being treated specially.”

Aodhán chuckled shakily, letting Elder ThunderWind's words sink in. He understood his significance within the sect well, but it seemed he had to keep reminding himself that this wasn't Lutia, but Gordon.

Inhaling deeply, Aodhán nodded. “I’ll try.”

“And that’s all anyone can ask for.” Elder ThunderWind smiled. “Come on. Let’s bind you to the house.”

They descended to the ground, and slowly, almost reverently, Aodhán placed a hand on the house and ran his palm against the shimmering scales that covered the wall. The house hummed in response, and with a swift activation of Merge, he cut his palm with a claw and placed it on the wall.

The blood sank into the scales without a trace, vanishing as though it had never existed, and for a heartbeat, nothing happened.

Then the house flashed with gold-white light. A subtle warmth flowed up his arm, not invasive, not demanding—simply present. The humming deepened, settling into a steady rhythm that matched his own heartbeat, and somewhere within the structure, locks disengaged one after another with soft, distant clicks.

Aodhán felt it then—a new presence within his spirit, anchoring itself beside Varéc. Varéc purred in fascination within his spirit as he revolved around the building, poking it with his claws to see if it would budge.

It didn’t.

Smiling, Aodhán pulled his palms away from the house and turned to see if any changes had been made to the house. Before he could, though, a stream of notifications pinged in his mind, and several lines of text appeared across his vision.

Congratulations! You have bonded a blessed item—Dragon Mansion: [CLICK TO VIEW STATS]

Aodhán clicked on the notification and was surprised to be met with another.

View summarized stats / view extended stats.

"View the summarized stats." Elder ThunderWind chuckled. "You can view the extended version in your free time, and when I say free time, I mean free time, because you'll need a lot of it."

Aodhán nodded and clicked on the first option.

Immediately, a screen appeared before him.

***

[STORM-FURY MANSION]

Rank: Mythic (Soul-Bound)Type: Residential Cultivation MansionBonded Owner: Aodhán Ashoka-BrystionTier Restriction: None (scales with owner)Durability: Self-Repairing

Aodhán’s eyes widened as he read through the summarized stats, marveling at the tier restriction and durability of the mansion. His expression, however, faltered when he came across the number of rooms, and he quickly looked up at Elder ThunderWind.

"Eighteen rooms? What am I supposed to use so much space for?"

Elder ThunderWind scoffed. "Only about seven of those rooms are residential. The others are specialized rooms."

Aodhán glanced back at the mansion. "Specialized for what?”

Elder ThunderWind chuckled. “For cultivation, of course. Everything you might ever need is in that mansion.”

Aodhán took a moment to process that before shaking his head. “Still, seven is a lot."

"Oh, I'm sure you'll be thinking otherwise the moment your underlings show up."

"Underlings?" Aodhán asked in confusion.

"Yes, underlings." Elder ThunderWind smiled widely. "As a special disciple, you are required to appoint at least one disciple from the lower sects to help you through your journey. They will be your partner and most loyal supporter.” His eyes narrowed. “Have you decided who you might appoint?"

Aodhán smiled at that, his expression brightening significantly as he wondered just how Crixis, Rhetta, Yerxa, and Zarion might react to his offer.

He shook his head. “I haven’t decided yet. But I have a few people in mind.”

He had been a bit nervous about navigating the special sect alone, but it seemed he wouldn't be alone after all. If he was obligated to pick people, then it was better that it was people he already liked.
