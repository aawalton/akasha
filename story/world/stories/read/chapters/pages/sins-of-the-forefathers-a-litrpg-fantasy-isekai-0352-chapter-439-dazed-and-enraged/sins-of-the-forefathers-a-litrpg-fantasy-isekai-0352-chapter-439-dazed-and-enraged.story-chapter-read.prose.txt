I could tell that Sylvia was both still a bit displeased by my words earlier and slightly confused by them. I rarely spoke about myself in a manner as I had, and I attributed that to my multi-part mind. When you could approach a conversation through three different thought lines, each of which could tell you if you were about to say something stupid or combative, you tended to pick your words a bit better.

But I was alone, right now. I just the Outer, stuck in a clone body, while the Core and Middle Lattices tended to my real one. I was more likely to be frank, to be honest.

Less careful.

Still, I had meant every word of what I had told both Walter and Sylvia. I was perfectly content to let Sylvia mull over my words, as she led me to a particular area of the encampment. A particularly well-guarded place, and for good reason.

This…was where the Unchained were keeping the Dwarves that were suspected might just be under the control of Anguis.

Bleddyn and the Unchained weren’t stupid, after all. They had made the connection that somebody must be controlling the minds of the Rhoscaran forces, if they were outright culling their own people. It wasn’t a particularly hard leap of logic to assume it might be the notorious trickster that was literally known as the Serpent. It also wasn’t hard to tell that it was only Dwarves that seemed to be under this control.

Bleddyn hadn’t been particularly surprised when I told him that it was Anguis behind it all. I…kept the source of it to myself, for now, the revelation that he was wielding part of Fynneas’s divinity to manipulate the collective soul of Dwarvendom. All I’d been willing to say was that the Prince of Vittolia had admitted to being a mind mage.

In response, Bleddyn had confirmed to me that they had been segregating every Dwarf who had been acting oddly during the evacuation, and every Dwarf that was likely to have been in close contact with Anguis during the battle. I was told that reports indicated that the Serpent himself had wandered almost blindly through the districts of the city, as the slaughter had been underway.

I withheld a sigh as I witnessed what could only be called a small concentration camp within the bounds of the encampment. Mobile palisades had been set up to contain the several hundred, miserable-looking Dwarves sitting despondently around campfires. While every single guard posted to the camp, either from the Unchained or from the expedition, was visibly wary of the detainees, they weren’t being mistreated per se. They had tents of their own, food, and supplies to the same extent as the rest of the refugees, but they seemed to be aware of their possible state as potential mind-dominated slaves. Downcast eyes, weeping, and outbursts of senseless, frustrated anger were far more common within the bounds than it had been within the rest of the camp.

As I watched, withholding a wince, I saw two Dwarves get into a fist-fight over what seemed to be a bowl of stew. Only for a pair of Human guards to intervene and literally carry them away, flailing as they did.

It was a depressing sight, and I felt for them. I really did.

And I wasn’t just here to gawk at their suffering. There was someone in particular I had to speak to, within that camp. Grimacing, Bleddyn had told me that they were inside.

They might just be the only person I could ask about certain…things.

Quietly, I approached the checkpoint of the camp. Judging by the unfriendly look I was getting from one of the guards, I was betting this guy was one of the Unchained and didn’t recognize me.

Luckily, the other guard was a member of the expedition, and visibly brightened up at the sight of me. I actually knew the name of this one too. To the best of my knowledge, he had been one of the members of the Polaris Reach who had been recruited from among the homeless population of Blutstein.

Thaddeus, I believe his name was.

The reedy-looking man smiled widely at me as I came to a stop before him. “Marshal! I hadn’t heard ya were awake! Are…are ya okay?”

I smiled easily at him, touched by his concern despite myself. I also couldn’t help but notice the re-assessing look I was now getting from the other guard. “In a manner of speaking,” I said vaguely, to both of their confusion. “But I’m glad to see you alright, Thaddeus. Now, I’d love to stay and chat, but I’m afraid I’m here on business. Bleddyn told me that someone in particular was being held within, and I wanted to speak to him. Do you happen to know-”

“Oh! Yeah, I know where ‘e is!” Thaddeus said excitedly. He turned and clapped the other guard on his back, causing him to stagger from the force of it. Thaddeus had been a former soldier, to the best of my knowledge. I imagined he had decently high Strength, from what I knew of Herztalian Army training standards. “Jim, ya mind watching the gate while I guide the Marshal about?”

The guard, ‘Jim’ apparently, rubbed his neck and shrugged. “Aye, if Bleddyn is fine with this,” He said reluctantly. “But don’t be gone too long, yeah? Make me nervous with all these stunties at me back. Like one of ‘em could jump up and shank me at any time.”

Thaddeus rolled his eyes as he opened the palisade gate and stepped through. “Yer too jumpy, Jim. That’s yer new name. ‘Jumpy Jim’. Be back in a mo’, Jumpy.”

The last thing I heard before the gate shut behind Sylvia and me, as we followed after Thaddeus, was a, “The hells it is-”.

I stifled a small laugh as I followed the former pikeman into the camp itself.

Thaddeus had come quite a way since he had joined the Polaris Reach. He wasn’t quite the deeply depressed tramp he had once been.

I was proud of him.

The feeling faded, though, as I was led through the camp of suspected mind slaves. This really was the best solution we had for them. The most humane one. Nobody among either the expedition or the Unchained was going to be willing to kill them outright. Not considering our motives for existing as organizations in the first place. But neither could we be certain they were safe to be around, so the best thing that could be done…was sectioning them off.

Still, the atmosphere was even more miserable in here than it was in the rest of the encampment. Misery, fear, and anger hung heavy in the air, and I received more than one foul look as Thaddeus led me further in. I…didn’t really want to spend more time within these walls than I had to.

The necessity of it made me uncomfortable.

Eventually, Thaddeus led me to what seemed like any other tent. There was nothing special about it, but the man seemed to instantly recognize it. No guards, no loiterers. In fact, it seemed like most of the other refugees were avoiding this area. It was startlingly absent of other Dwarves, in comparison to the rest of this camp.

Thaddeus stopped in front of it and turned to face me. I was unsurprised to find that his previous grin had fallen away to be replaced by a grimace. “He’s inside,” the former soldier said, jerking his head towards the covered entrance. “Just…be careful, yeah? He’s been actin’ oddlike. Even odder than ‘e used to, and that’s sayin’ somethin’.”

I exchanged a glance with Sylvia before turning back to Thaddeus and nodding. “I’ll be careful,” I promised. “If worse comes to worse…I’m sure we can subdue him.”

Hopefully, it wouldn’t come to that. I wasn’t confident in my chances against the person in that tent, as a weakened clone. I’d be relying on Sylvia if things went south.

Returning my nod, Thaddeus stepped to the side, and I took the invitation. Brushing aside the tent flap, I stepped inside…

And was immediately struck by the scent of alcohol.

Strong alcohol. Pure spirits, it smelled like, brutal and harsh. It was almost antiseptic in its intensity.

The interior was visibly unkempt, as well. Dirty and uncared for, stained sheets and wooden bowls of food littered the ground, while rumpled clothing was piled in one corner. Numerous bottles of pure spirit alcohol lay discarded across the floor, too, their wide mouths showing just how empty they were. It was…almost impressive how dirty it was in here, considering this encampment was only a few days old. A stopgap after a mad dash from the conquered city, to better collect our forces.

For a moment, I didn’t see the owner of the tent. Then what I had thought was a pile of dirty clothes in the corner shifted…and I had to duck as a pale arm tossed another empty bottle of booze right at me.

The bottle sailed right over my head and through the flap of the tent. I heard Thaddeus yelp in surprise as it shattered on the stone ground of the Long Road outside.

Looking back to who had launched it, I was unsurprised to see a (relatively) huge, familiar figure start to rise from the pile of dirty linens. Despite the time that had passed while I had languished in my coma, they still seemed to be dressed in their bloodstained, cracked armor from the battle of Rhoscara. My nose wrinkled from the new smell that assaulted my nose, something strong enough to cut through the ripe scent of booze. Old sweat, dried blood…

And fear.

The unkempt form of Olag of House Florens, bloodied, dirty, and visibly drunk off his ass, glared blearily at me from where he sat on his mound of stinking cloth. He mumbled something, seemingly unable to properly focus through all the alcohol pulsing through his veins.

I’m…pretty sure he didn’t actually recognize me.

I couldn’t myself from turning to Sylvia with a raised, disbelieving eyebrow. I didn’t even need to say anything for her to understand me; I just needed to gesture to him with one hand.

Sylvia sighed, seemingly putting aside her displeasure with me. “Yes, that’s really him,” She said tiredly. “As you’ll recall, the Raven’s Beak and the Captain-General were chosen as the vanguard force, meant to be closest to the harbor when matters with the Savoy turned to outright battle. As such, when they did, Olag’s company were among the very first to be caught in Anguis’s control. There were…quite a few clashes between the Unchained and the expedition, against Raven’s Beak members as we were evacuating. The Serpent seemed to delight in throwing our own former comrades against our fleeing backs. Among them…was Olag.”

I bowed my head, shaking it in dismay. “So he was under Anguis’s control?”

“He still is,” Sylvia corrected me gently. “As best as we can tell. At the sight of him, we sallied out to try and at least rescue one person of import from the enchantment. It was…quite difficult to subdue him non-lethally, but we managed. He only ‘broke’ from visible control when we had retreated from Rhoscara for several dozen miles.”

I didn’t blame Sylvia for the skeptical air quotes she did then. I myself had trouble believing Anguis’s control could be discarded at all.

“Since then, he’s been like…this.”

I turned to stare at the pitiful form of Olag, then, contemplating him. “He must be aware of what happened to him,” I observed quietly. “Of what he might have done under Anguis’s control.”

“And thus the drink,” Sylvia confirmed.

“Thus the drink,” I sighed. I shook my head and firmed my will. “Still. I need some answers, and I need him sober for that.”

Approaching Olag, I reached for my Mana, a tad dismayed at just how much weaker it was in this level-halved clone body. Still, it was enough for the Spell I was weaving. I seized the completely insensate form of Olag by the jaw and ignored his weak, inebriated protests.

And shocked him with one of the few distinct Healing Spells I knew in my repertoire. A sobering Spell.

Immediately, Olag seized in my grip, and I skipped back a step. Just in time to dodge a much more coordinated swipe from the Florens Dwarf. That would have been enough to pop this clone, I was sure of it.

Now forcibly ensobered, Olag drew in a shuddering breath and glared at me with bloodshot eyes. “You. Why?”

Not…quite what I would have expected, from the normally well-spoken Dwarf.

I nodded a greeting to him. “Olag. I…hope you’re doing well, despite the circumstances. Know that we, and I in particular, fully intend to see what can be done to-”

“Shut up,” Olag cut me off, outright snarling. The twisting of his face only made his tattoos stand out sharper. “Get to the point.”

I narrowed my eyes at him. “Alright. Where is Elysael?”

It was entirely possible that Olag was the only person who would know what had happened to the Prince of Rhoscara. Neither Bleddyn nor anyone else I had spoken to yet knew anything about what had happened to my friend. She hadn’t been spotted since before the Savoy scourging of the city had begun, and it was imperative that we find her. Even if she was currently mind-controlled, she was still the linchpin of our efforts in Dwarven lands. If either the Unchained or the expedition ever sought to maintain even a hint of legitimacy within the Principality after we won, then we needed her.

Olag’s next words shattered that hope.

“She’s dead,” He said with a rough sneer, before turning his back on me deliberately. “Now leave me be.”

My heart stuttered within my chest, and I did something very foolish then.

I lurched across the room and grabbed Olag’s shoulder in disbelieving command.

Mistake.

In my weakened state, I didn’t even see the next moves. I was only able to parse them after the fact.

Olag spun to face me with a murderous snarl on his face, hands outstretched to tear my clone’s throat out with his bare hands.

Only to still when he felt the point of a deep blue short-sword touch itself against his throat, held by Sylvia. My lover didn’t need to say a word to stop him. Her icy gaze was proof enough of what would happen, if the Dwarf carried through on his movement.

That didn’t deter him much.

“Do it,” Olag hissed to Sylvia, ignoring me completely. “Kill me. Slice my throat and let me bleed out. Remove my head if you need to. Anything but allowing me to live. Free me from the dishonor I was made to participate in. There is no point to my existence if my beloved city has been destroyed. That I helped cull. Kill. Me.”

Sylvia…barely reacted to Olag’s desperate plea. “No.”

I shuddered from how close that had been, even though I knew this was just a clone. Even if he had popped it, I could have had another clone of myself in this tent within the hour. But it was a really bad idea to become complacent about imminent threats of violence.

That’s how you lost your survival instinct.

“You’re sure?” I asked Olag roughly. “You saw Elysael’s body? You know for a fact that Anguis killed her?”

Seeing that Sylvia wasn’t going to kill him, Olag turned his gaze away from her to sneer at me once more. “No, but it doesn’t matter,” He said viciously. “The Serpent did worse than kill my dear cousin. He murdered our legitimacy. Even if Elysael lives, she’s nothing but a stateless politician now. Rhoscara has been razed, our forces either murdered or dominated. The Florens dies with us. We are of no further use to you, human.”

I couldn’t help but breathe a sigh of relief, to Olag’s confusion.

All of that might be true. But…

“She’s still my friend,” I said quietly, meeting Olag’s eyes to convey my seriousness. “And if she’s still alive, there’s hope for her…and you. I’ll get her back somehow, Olag. For now…” I stood up from the aborted lunge I had made for Olag. “You should focus on getting yourself together. You’re of no use to anyone, like this.”

Olag’s sneer…faded, and in its place remained only bitterness and exhaustion. “Just…leave me be, Hart,” He said in a defeated tone. “Leave me to regrets and my failures. I cannot withstand your platitudes right now. Just…go.”

Exchanging a glance with Sylvia, I nodded. She sheathed her blade, and with one last warning glance at the despondent Captain-General, we left the tent.

And one of the last surviving Florens alone.

Everyone deserved space to grieve.
