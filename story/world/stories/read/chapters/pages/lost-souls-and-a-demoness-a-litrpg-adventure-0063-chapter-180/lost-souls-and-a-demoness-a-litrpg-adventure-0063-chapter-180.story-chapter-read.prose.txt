"What was that?" Jade coughed, rubbing her leg where the flaming whip had scoured her flesh. The skin looked whole and unblemished, but an echo of the blazing pain still prickled like an electric current. "Where did he- they… come from?"

She took Luis's hand, allowing the larger man to pull her back to her feet, and shot a glance at the newcomer. The demon woman, the interface identified her as a lugren named Elyx, was lying on the rust-colored sand, stirring weakly. Jade had nearly been incapacitated by a small brush with the pain demon's flames, and that woman had taken a full blast of them head-on. She groaned weakly, and Jade winced in sympathy. Even if they were enemies, she didn't deserve that. And, on that note, were they enemies? She was a demon, natural born, if the Labyrinth was correct, and yet her surprise attack was the only reason they'd emerged from this confrontation without casualties.

"I dunno." Luis grunted, hunching over with his hands on his knees. "All I care about is that there aren't any more of them."

Jade swept the area with her senses again. Nothing, not even a hint of any unfamiliar auras within at least a quarter mile. The surrounding desert, darkened by the thick shroud billowing from the top of the plateau beside them, was barren and empty. She couldn't rule out all hidden threats, this ambush was proof enough of that, but it was the best she could do.

"Maybe we should ask our new friend." Jade suggested, pointing at the incapacitated demon. "Is everyone else alright?"

The question received a chorus of affirmatives, some more enthusiastic than others. None of them had escaped the battle unscathed, unless you counted Seris, who hadn't left the airship with them, but it didn't look like anyone was seriously injured. Siora was helping Tara up, soothing her burns with more of her healing magic, and Naomi's musical aura filled the air around them. The restorative notes eased their aches and pains, but it couldn't help with the mental trauma such extreme pain had inflicted. This wasn't an experience any of them would soon forget.

"Thanks, Tara." Jade said, helping Siora pull her up. "That was some nice timing. I owe you one."

"Only one?" Tara asked, brushing a strand of sweat-matted hair out of her face. She managed a half smirk, though it didn't reach her eyes.

"Yeah, well, you can cash them in when we get back to the Labyrinth."

"Hmm… I'll have to think of something good."

Jade figured she would be happy to do just about anything for the tek'kalla once they returned, mostly because that would mean they'd survived.

The demon on the ground, the one with her head still attached to her body, groaned again and shifted.

"I think she's coming to." Luis said.

"I'm healing her, too." Naomi said, still plucking out a calming tune. "Should I stop?"

"No." Jade and Fenrin spoke up at the same time. Jade gestured for him to continue.

"We should talk to her. Find out why she helped us, and if there are any more of them."

"She might know more about what's up there, too." Jade added, pointing towards the top of the tall plateau. They couldn't see much of the towering palace from such a steep angle, but the cloud of liquid darkness flowing into the sky was sign enough that they were in the right place. Still, more information could only help.

When nobody voiced their disagreement, Jade crouched down beside the wounded demon and shook her shoulder.

"Hey. You awake? Can you talk?"

Other than her crimson skin and curling horns, the demon looked mostly human. Her eyes changed that. They fluttered open, revealing bright red orbs that seemed to glow from within.

"I-I think so." She coughed, moving to sit up. Jade didn't stop her, although she stayed alert for any sudden moves. She needn't have worried, the demon looked about ready to slip back into unconciousness.

While they waited for their unexpected ally to regain her wits Jade suggested they move back into their invisible airship. While its enchantments clearly weren't foolproof, there was at least a chance that the brief conflict hadn't been detected by anyone in the palace above them. Even if they had, having this conversation inside the ship had to be better than standing around in plain view at the base of the plateau. Most of them gathered in the passenger bay while Fenrin and Seris remained on the lookout for signs of more trouble. Jade stayed in the rear of the group, letting Tara and Luis take the lead in questioning their unknown guest. She'd burned through too much of her mana already and couldn't waste any more on a charm spell, but her empathic senses were free. As long as she stayed focused, they'd do as a makeshift lie detector.

Over the course of the next half hour, Elyx explained several things to the mysterious group that had appeared out of nowhere, in the heart of their most sacred lands. She was pretty sure that just speaking with them constituted treason, but since she'd already been condemned by the outcome of the ritual she'd overseen, not to mention her role in killing Zertorlk, why bother sticking to the rules now?

Really, siding with the newcomers hadn't been much of a decision. Choice one: Wait for Zertrolk to kill them and then go right back to her own imminent execution at the hands of Lady Ira. Choice two: Help the Wayfarers defeat Zertrolk, and then… well, Elyx wasn't sure what happened after that, but an uncertain fate was better than the alternative. That conviction had already been shaken by the full blast of Zertrolk's soul fire she'd just been subjected to. The demon's body still trembled with phantom twinges from overloaded nerves. Death may have been preferable to that experience after all. Still, she'd made it this far, and Elyx reasoned that she may as well see where this went.

Her new… captors? Saviors? Whoever they were, had many questions. Elyx assumed that they had come to somehow stop Lord Vastrozun from holding open the gateways to the Labyrinth, she couldn't think of another reason for them to sneak so close to his sacred home, so she refrained from mentioning that she was the architect of the ritual that had partially succeeded in linking their two worlds. That admission probably wouldn't win her any points, and she'd experienced enough pain for a lifetime in this day alone. Instead, she simply explained that she was one of the assistant magical researchers that had been tasked with figuring out how the Labyrinth worked while the rest of the demon forces prepared the main ritual. Which had been true, up until the point Zertrolk disposed of her predecessor and appointed her as a replacement, at least.

From there, she went on to explain that the ritual hadn't worked correctly, and that Zertrolk had essentially brought her as a stand-in sacrifice when he had been summoned to report on the situation by Lady Ira.

"Who's Lady Ira?" The largest of the men, Luis, asked, when she paused to take a breath.

Elyx blinked, surprised. But of course, she'd never spoken to anyone outside of demon society before. You'd be hard pressed to find a demon who didn't instinctively shudder upon hearing the name of Lord Vastrozun's right-hand woman, but how was anyone from the rest of the cosmos supposed to know of her? She'd been isolated with their people for as long as any of them. Longer, really, since she'd been here from the very beginning.

"Lady Ira is… well, you could call her Lord Vastrozun's second-in-command." Elyx said, trying to think of how best to explain. At this point she was pretty sure these people weren't going to kill her, a sense that she'd grown adept at weilding during her time with the commander. With that in mind, she felt no reason to hold anything back. "She has always been one of his most powerful supporters, and it's said that he trusts her judgement above almost anyone else's.  I only know the stories, I've never met her myself. Which I very much hope stays true, since many who do don't survive the experience. And I doubt she'd be happy with me for having this conversation with you, so…"

"You don't seem to mind telling us everything we want to know." The tek'kalla woman said, eyeing her suspiciously. "You do know we're trying to seal your people away again, right?"

"Tara, if she didn't know that you shouldn't have told her."

Tara gave Luis a flat look.

"Oh, like she'd think there was some other reason we came all the way here in a state-of-the-art tek'kalla stealth ship."

So that's what this vessel was? Fascinating, she'd always wanted to poke through tek'kalla magitech, she had so many-

No. she chided herself. Survival first, research second.

Elyx had always struggled with priorities. Which was, of course, how she'd ended up in this mess to begin with.

"Anyway." Tara said, pulling the conversation back on topic. "What do you get out of this?"

"Not dying would be a great start?" Elyx offered hopefully. Luis chuckled.

"We usually don't kill people who've helped us as much as you have. That fight was looking pretty bad before you stepped in."

"Yeah." A woman with an unfamiliar instrument slung over her shoulder said with a shudder. "That fire was… I'm never going to forget that feeling for as long as I live."

"Soul fire." Elyx explained. "It's unique to pain demons. All greater demons were granted special powers by Lord Vastrozun when he elevated them. It falls to the rest of us to serve them."

"I take it you're not a greater demon, then?" Luis asked. Elyx shook her head.

"No. Just a normal Lugren. For most of my life, I took care of the stasis chambers with the rest of my clan."

"Stasis chambers?" asked Tara.

Elyx gestured towards the hatch, and the barren wasteland beyond.

"Most of our world is as lifeless as this place. There are few places where food can be grown in any meaningful quantity. Because of how much sustenance they need, the greater demons were placed in stasis centuries ago. For generations, my clan have been the caretakers of our enclave's sleeping population. Now that our people have a way out, I imagine the order to wake them will come soon."

"Not if we can help it." Luis said, crossing his arms.

"Right, you're here to close the gates. Since the ritual wasn't completed properly, Lord Vastrozun's power is probably the only thing keeping them open and stable. Are… you here to defeat him?"

Elyx cast a critical eye over the rather diverse group arrayed before her. They fought well, anyone who didn't wouldn't have lasted a moment against Zertrolk, but if a single greater demon posed that much of a challenge to them…

"We aren't going to share our plan." The succubus spoke for the first time. Elyx flinched instinctively. Even knowing that this was a Wayfarer, not a natural born demon, she was too accustomed to the pain such creatures met out on a whim to suppress the reaction. "We may be grateful for your help, but we don't trust you."

That was sensible, especially considering it hadn't even been an hour since the last time she'd betrayed someone.

"It had better be a really good one…" was all she could think to say.

"You're awfully calm for someone surrounded by a group of heavily armed, mysterious invaders." Tara observed.

Elyx blinked once.

"I've been around people who could easily kill me for most of my life. You seem more rational than any of them ever were."

"Your society doesn't sound all that great." Luis commented.

"If it was, I probably wouldn't have helped you kill my commander just now."

"Are you always so flippant?" Tara asked, exasperated.

Elyx thought back to her many interactions with Zertrolk, and her absolute inability to show the greater demon the respect she'd been supposed to.

"You know what? I think I am. Honestly, it's something of a miracle that I've made it this far."

"I like her." Luis laughed.

"You would." The succubus snorted, but Elyx saw her posture relax ever so slightly. Oh? A succubus with a sense of humor? This day was full of surprises.

"Anyway," the bard spoke again, cutting off whatever Tara had been about to say. "Back to what you said earlier. This Lady Ira you mentioned, is she here?"

"That's what the commander told me." Elyx nodded. "We were on our way to meet her."

"Damn." Luis frowned. "We were told… we were hoping your emperor would be alone."

"He usually is, other than her. These lands are forbidden, not even the greater demons venture here uninvited."

"We'll deal with her if we have to." The succubus said, fingering the clasp of a pouch hanging from her waist. She looked troubled. "At least there shouldn't be anyone else, then. You mentioned she was powerful. Do you know what exactly her powers are?"

Elyx shook her head.

"Only that they are not like any that demons possess."

"Why is that?" Luis asked.

"Did I not say?" Elyx kept having to remind herself that these people knew next to nothing of demon society. "Lady Ira is an angel. The only celestial to defy the gods' will and fight against our imprisonment during the great war. The holy fire at her command is something that all demons fear, from the greatest war chief to the lowest caretaker."

All eyes turned to the succubus.

"Amazing." She sighed. "I was really hoping we'd never have to do this again."
