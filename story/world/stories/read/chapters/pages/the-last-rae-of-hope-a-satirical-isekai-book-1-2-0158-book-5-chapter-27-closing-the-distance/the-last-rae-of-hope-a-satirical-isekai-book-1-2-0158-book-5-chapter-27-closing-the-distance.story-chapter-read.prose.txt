After I semi-avoided thinking about a certain demon king and his cunning use of connotations, along with the inevitable confrontation we were absolutely going to have later, I got a good night’s sleep. Credit goes to Chester, who joined me sometime in the middle of the night.

“You put Aurum to bed properly, right?” I mumbled. “Stable, I mean, stable.”

Two green eyes flashed as he settled next to my pillow.

I stretched and pulled him close despite his protests. “That’s not even close to the weirdest question I ever asked you, and you know it.”

The next day started a little lonely. With Nora, Tetora, and Vernie still otherwise occupied on the surface, I decided it would be best to take Galenus’s advice to heart and begin preparing for the second fight on my list.

The smooth, stone-walled training chamber held the familiar runes and buzzing lines of the experimental lab I had been dropped into during my first visit to the maze, only this version was much larger. Galenus was there with me, tracing several magic circles into the air, poking and prodding at them to change their shape.

“Looks pretty complicated…” I observed aloud as I adjusted my armor.

He paused, his ears perking forward in amusement. “I make sure of it.”

Nora was definitely rubbing off on him.

“It begs the question, though… what does all that mystical stuff actually do?”

“I am building you exactly what you asked for.”

“Is that so?”

I had requested a training dummy to simulate General Ragnerus, to which the usually composed goat demon had bleated loudly in raucous amusement.

“Observe.”

Dark mist began to swirl from the floor, and the General’s form started to assemble within its center. His hulking, crimson frame came first, then his jagged greatsword burning with midnight lava, and finally his smoldering eye.

Galenus exploded into laughter as he delivered his first-ever joke. “Behold! Your training dummy!”

“Ahaha. I see what you did there.” I agreed. “Very impressive. I see he even has an idle animation.”

Galenus gave me a quizzical look but didn’t press me any further.

“So to start fighting, do I just—”

The chamber rattled.

“That’s nothing like him,” Bodhil shouted as she phased into the room.

“It is structurally accurate,” Galenus retorted, his hair bristling.

“Incomplete.” She pointed sharply at the General’s head as Beigaldi’s form coalesced beside her. “He’ll be free when he fights, and there is no future in which he does not reclaim his second eye beforehand.” She then paused, her flames dancing nervously. “Not that you stand a chance either way.”

“He will be raging in Animus Overload,” Beigaldi added, folding his arms as he gave me a side eye. “Not standing there like a dolt.”

Galenus frowned and adjusted several runes. In response, Ragnerus opened his second eye and bellowed as he raised his sword, summoning several arcs of lightning.

I rolled my shoulders and reset my stance, but Bodhil stepped directly between us.

“He’s not even at half strength,” she scoffed. “That goat’s coddling you.”

“How would you know?” I asked.

Her flaming eyes narrowed into molten slits. “Do not question my skill! That shadow is weak.”

Galenus gnashed his teeth as he swiped his hand to the right. Black and crimson mist roiled off the General’s shoulders.

Then Ragnerus lunged straight for me, skipping the customary blustering roar.

The first cleave came down hard enough to crack the rune-laden stones beneath my boots. Faith rang out after she caught it, my pink aura infusing her silver sparkle and negating the force lightning accompanying the strike. I pivoted into the counter I’d drilled with Tetora back when he played the General the first time, angling Will toward his exposed flank. The sword’s tip, however, never made contact, as all I could hit was the air in front of him.

Ragnerus never paused. He twisted through his follow-up, reversing his greatsword into a backhand sweep. Without time to block it properly, I dodged, ducking and rolling. By the time I scrambled to my feet, he had switched stances, lightning once again coiling around his blade.

He surged forward again, dissolving into crimson mist crackling with lightning. I failed to dodge, the mist passing straight through me, every hair on my arms lifting with the worst static shock imaginable. With time enough only to turn and raise my shield, he struck at me again.

Two-eyed Ragnerus was just too much!

I stepped in on his next thrust, aiming to intercept and redirect, but he pivoted just beyond my reach. My strike found nothing but animus-blackened air.

I can’t get in close enough!

He shadow-stepped on my next attempt, because of course he did.

I managed to avoid his lightning cloud surge the second time, though my hair thoroughly fuzzed up.

Just as the cloud started to buzz, I thrust forward with Will, hoping to catch him as he formed. Once again, his greatsword crashed against Faith the moment it apparated, driving me back before I could score a hit.

Bodhil laughed from the sidelines. “Pathetic human. You don’t know when to stop, do you?”

In retrospect, she was correct, but I hadn’t quite realized it was time to regroup. I ground my teeth and re-engaged, testing different angles, trying to bait him into overextension, but even when he committed heavily to exchanging blows, my return strikes grazed or missed where they should have landed solidly. The static cloud shifting was annoying, to be sure, but my choice to go pink seemed quite insulating against it. At one point, I had moved too close to Beigaldi, who seemed to take damage from its electrification even at a bit of distance.

“You’re too short, human!” Bodhil eventually thundered, punching one of the runes Galenus was maintaining. General Ragnerus faded from existence, and the house lights came up.

I whirled, my teeth bared and weapons still active. “What the hell do you think you’re doing? I was getting the hang of it!”

“Even if he honors the rules of combat, you’re too short. Do you understand now? You’ll never defeat him this way.”

Short? Me? I’m not short at all!

Galenus snorted, summoning an idle Ragnerus once again. I walked over carefully, sizing myself up just in front of him.

It wasn’t just that he was taller. It was his greatsword.

“I think I get the problem now,” I admitted, lowering Faith and Will. “Let’s take a break.”

Ragnerus faded as Bodhil regarded me with open contempt. “Figure the rest out with someone else,” she said, disappearing before I could retort.

Beigaldi lingered behind. “She was being intentional,” he said with a sigh. “Do not misunderstand her.”

“This is her way of helping me, isn’t it?” I muttered, only half-grateful.

Beigaldi started to disappear. “I refuse to answer on the grounds that I don’t relish being immolated.”

I glanced at Galenus, feeling the weight of Will increase in my hand. “Um… Maybe just a wooden dummy for now while I take a few steps back, okay?”

“As you wish—”

Galenus flinched just as a glorified stump of a training partner appeared. “Lady Nora beckons. Please excuse me.”

Now alone, I took a seat on the floor, pulling Will into my lap. “It’s not your fault, you know. You can’t help how you were forged.”

He was cold with dread.

Dread?

I can feel his emotions, too?

So I listened, running my fingertips along his hilt while intentionally avoiding the snake mark.

Fear of being cast aside.

Dreading failure. Only able to dull, never able to change for the better.

Jealous that Faith can shift, even though they were forged from the same meteorite.

Fascinated, I gingerly set him aside after a reassuring pat and pulled Faith into my lap instead. “A quick check-in with you now.”

She trilled a flute-like melody in surprise, then flashed.

Sadness. Sadness that her other half can’t understand her music.

Lacking connection and communication.

Impressed by his ability to return to my hand when dropped.

I hesitated.

It couldn’t be that simple, right?

But then again, these two colleagues were on a team that never really had the chance to talk in person. As the team leader, I should coordinate their communication more effectively.

I placed one hand on each, then closed my eyes. In my mind’s eye, I saw them as two spirits standing face to face at a distance. Faith was a woman with white hair and wings, her shield-form held before her. Will gleamed in his armor, flame lighting up his blade. They were staring at each other, like two awkward teens on a first date.

“I’ll stay over here,” I shouted in my mind. “You two should take this time to get to know each other!”

Faith beckoned to me, seemingly panicked.

I shook my head. “You two can work this out, I'm sure of it!”

Faith sighed, then nodded, and Will eventually copied the movement. They talked for a little while, slowly at first, but they seemed to relax, gesturing more and more as they spoke out of earshot. Every once in a while, however, they gave me a rather pointed look.

“I believe in you both!”

Faith held out her free hand at one point, and Will hesitated. Faith extended it again with emphasis, and Will took a deep breath, then grabbed it.

There was a flash of brightness so great between them that I had to cut off my connection. When I opened my eyes, we were all glowing the same pastel pink.

“Um… What did you guys agree on, exactly?”

Faith rang out with an orchestra of triumphant brass and soaring strings. Underneath it all was just a slight bit of sass.

“Well, congratulations, I guess,” I murmured, rising to my feet. “But just to be clear, no one’s getting replaced. We’re a team, okay?”

I bent over to pick them up. Faith shot upward, slapping herself into the palm of my hand.

“How?!”

The rest of my words disappeared as my fingers closed around Will. He grew heavy, his form shifting tenously in my grip. His straight blade curved as his guard reshaped itself into an elegant basket around my hand.

“What the?!”

Talk about skill sharing!

“I… Don’t know much about sabers… and switching sword styles at this point might be hard…”

His saber shape wasn’t really any longer than his longsword form, either.

Never mind the fact that he would no longer fit in his sheath.

I didn’t want to reject his idea outright, however. The element of a surprise-shaped weapon had merit.

“I’ll talk to Tetora about some stances and strikes we could try—”

Faith peeled out in a sudden rendition of the William Tell Overture, the driving rhythm and galloping tempo shaking me to the very core.

“Mounted combat!” I shouted, finally getting the whole picture. “Why didn’t I think of that?!”

Faith answered with three crisp trumpet blasts and a rolling drum cadence that unmistakably translated to ‘took you long enough.’
