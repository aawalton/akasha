Dreamlight - Carlson

Carlson did not like Dorian, but recognized power when he saw it. The young man acted more like a vagabond than a vicious noble. Despite what some thought, even in his own little group, the vagabond was always more dangerous than the noble. A noble would hide behind money, soldiers, and corrupted laws. They were masters at using the tools at their disposal and wringing every resource they could for more than it was worth, but they usually had little personal power and hid behind the laws. When faced with a Paladin who outranked them and could act as they wished, many a nobleman had put themselves in cuffs rather than play the game of cat and mouse where they'd try to hide their transgressions, and Carlson would find the trail regardless.

The vagabond, in contrast, knew the law was not on their side. They hid and skulked and grew in violence. Moved and risked only what they wanted when they wanted, they would not just roll over when someone outranked them because everyone outranked them.

As far as Carlson knew, though Dorian was not a vagabond, he merely acted like one. Even if the young man had committed some manner of crime, what could Carlson do? They were not from the same world; hell, they weren't even in Carlson's world currently. Any judgment he could pass would be purely the arbitrary flailings of a single man who wanted things done their way. He was a man of unyielding belief in rooting out all seeds of corruption that would harm the masses. Here, though, in the Dream, he was still figuring out what that meant. They could not truly die here, not unless they went into an Instance and were drained of Presence, but he could still not abide by it. The closest comparison he could make was where a murdered person was resurrected in the necessary time frame for the magic to take hold, in which case the offender would be jailed for five years of labor or two and a half if they agreed to be hanged and resurrected. It was close, close enough for comfort, yet there were more items like it about the Dream. He needed to be careful with his decisions, especially with the fate of his entire world on the line.

He would not morally beggar himself for survival. Some actions were too heinous to commit even when risking an entire world. Volunteers for a great quest he could understand and approve of, blindly reaching out and taking one or two away a year, he could sympathize if not disparage wholeheartedly. This though… Barrow knew more about Instances and this Dream than him, but nothing that would forcibly drag millions to their death could be benevolent. The ends did not justify the means, no matter what anyone said. The scales would never be balanced. That's not to say Carlson's hands were bloodless. He had made judgment calls. Many nobles and vagabonds were felled by his blade, and more that he hadn't killed directly had been condemned to death by a court, but he did what he could with what he had. He couldn't wax poetically his way out of that, nor did he want to. He accepted the contradiction.

"Does here work?" the young man, Dorian, gestured at a block of buildings away from the main camp. The construction was mostly hardened liquid stone along with some bricks. A strange sight, too boxy for Carlson's tastes, but the Paladin still nodded.

"Good as any. I admit I don't know how your powers work. Will the area here be safe without your supervision or will the Dream try to return it to the current state?"

Dorian paused at that for a moment, "Dunno. I can certainly make some things permanent, but I'm limited by Presence, it would cost more than I'm willing to spend."

"It will stay," the third and last of their group, Barrow, confirmed. "The Dream does not care what is changed outside of Instances."

Carlson didn't frown, didn't even twitch a muscle, but he made a mental note to corner his friend later. The man knew too much about the Dream. More than Carlson himself, which was surprising since Barrow insisted he was no one of note. Obviously, that was not the case. For not the first or even third time that day, Carlson felt like he sat on a powder keg, one that kept getting kicked by flint-laden shoes.

"Then this spot is fine," Carlson reaffirmed before anyone could ratchet up the tension. "Barrow, go find Minerva, help her plan some expeditions into nearby Instances. I'll help Dorian with the layout here."

Barrow opened their mouth to argue, and Carlson just shook his head and raised a hand. "This is the best of both of our talents. Go," and Barrow listened, albeit begrudgingly.

They both watched Barrow leave. Only when the other man was out of sight did Dorian speak up, still watching the streets. "I know when someone wants to have an awkward conversation. Let's not play games."

Carlson nodded, "I do not care if you are a Dreamspawn," the Paladin started, "and I do not know what I plan to do yet. Someone stole my world and gave it a task. I do not trust it. I do not trust you either, but I can speak to you face to face." The man paused to let all that sink in and to gauge Dorian's rather surprised expression, which faded into one of suspicion.

"I assume you have a reason for telling me this?" the young man asked in turn.

"Yes, I want to know the why before I commit to anything. So, I'll ask you directly. Do you know anything about this and are you the one, or working for the one who ordered this?"

The young man laughed and shook his head, a small smile touching his lips. "No, no one I know did this, but I have some guesses."

"And what you would want for the information?"

"The Dream is apparently made up of layers. I'm looking for someone in the higher layers. It'll be faster with people to help me scout. You will help with that. You and Minerva."

The Paladin grimaced, "I do not know if we'll be alive for that long. We many be immortal here, but our bodies still exist in our world."

"Tell me," Dorian said, his grin just growing wider. Carlson felt his heart begin to hammer, the atmosphere in the space changing suddenly to that of damning deals. "You are a righteous man no? Would you do anything to protect the people of your world?"

"No." Carlson spoke resolutely, responding instantly and without a shred of doubt. The response took the air right out of Dorian's sails as he looked surprised.

"No?"

"No," Carlson reaffirmed. "I would do much, but the ends do not justify the means."

Dorian nodded, discarding the act he had begun putting on. "I see. Well, I'll explain bluntly. There's a way to disconnect yourself from your world. You'll be a Resident of the Dream until another world takes you in. You won't be at risk of dying anymore, but you can not go home."

"Could I not just reconnect to my world after all of this is done?" Carlson asked.

Dorian paused, seemingly not having considered that before nodding slowly. "I don't know. I don't see why not, besides you needing to scout the higher layers for me."

Carlson got a lot out of the brief exchange, including a way to safe his people. Even now, some would vanish into thin air, having been killed in their world and turned into beasts. With this, he could save quite a few of them. It was valuable, precious knowledge he would use, but it was not the agreement he had wanted to make. If anything, it just gave him more time to find what he was truly searching for. "Alright, if you help me find out who and why gave this quest and help my people disconnect from our world, I'll help you scout out the Dream. We can work out the details another time or while we work." Carlson gestured at the block of buildings they had chosen for housing and held out his hand.

"Deal," Dorian took the Paladin's hand with a smile that made Carlson's skin crawl. He still did not like the young man, certainly didn't trust him, but this was the hand he had been given, and he would make the best of it.

Meanwhile, Dorian internally was a wreck. It had gone better than he could have imagined. He thought that maybe the Paladin would attack him, or relentlessly question him, but that hadn't happened. Everything was going smoothly for once, and that made Dorian very nervous. Nothing ever went smoothly in the Dream. He didn't want to wait for the other shoe to drop. He quickly went about imagining the barracks setup described to him and shaping the Dream to his will. It was tiring work, and many walls were slightly warped or bowed due to his negligence and distracted thoughts, but he was going for speed. The other shoe was going to drop, and he wanted to find out whatever it was before it did.

Moonlight - Joey

The eyes of the people worried him. They didn't hold any malice toward him; they didn't look at him strangely or with any worry or any other negative emotion. Instead, they looked through him with dull, lifeless stares. When the children had first approached him, his heart had nearly jumped out of his chest. He thought for sure they were zombies or some hive mind, but when they got close, light had come to them. They smiled, and light hit them as they looked over the car with curiosity that only children possessed. He had thought nothing of it, and then he had walked away with Maerin, and when he glanced back at all of the people surrounding the car, their lightless eyes had returned.

Something about it unnerved him greatly.
