I had a dream.

A long, long dream.

One that I desired to forget.

A dream, a wretched dream. An abyss ever radiant.

But I know deep in the depths of my heart, that was not me.

It wouldn’t be. It shouldn’t be. It couldn’t be. But in the end, it was...

For on the day where the scent of sea salt drifted in the winds, and waves hit the shores.

I had killed someone.

“Come here, boy!” A middle-aged man sat on the floor, with two bowls of food prepared with a meal. He wore a simple linen shirt and skirted pants.

He had tanned skin, with messy dark blue hair.

Just like I did.

“Coming dad!” I yelled back, running from the back of the house to meet him, seeing the food he’d made.

There were just the two of us left in this house now.

My father had been in a jolly mood.

It was rare.

I sat down in front of him, seeing the mild and salty fish porridge.

It was actually quite delicious, which I didn’t expect.

“Let’s eat.” My dad commanded.

And so we ate.

It didn’t take long for me to finish my bowl.

I sighed with relief, enjoying it.

“How was the porridge I cooked, my son?” My father asked.

I gave the most brilliant and cutest smile a filial son could give.

“I loved it dad! You’re the best cook!”

“Grab me my sake from the cupboard.” He commanded.

I did as he said. As a filial son.

And I poured him a small cup of his favourite wine.

I once more sat down in front of him, watching him enjoy his drink.

At one point, I thought a tear came out of his eye.

Or maybe it was the wine when he was drinking?

It was then he asked me a question.

A simple question every father asked his child.

“Son. Do you want to become a cultivator?”

His eyes focused on me. On my answer.

So I gave the most obvious answer.

One any child would give.

“Of course dad! I want to become the strongest cultivator ever!”

Any father would be happy to see their son be enthusiastic about becoming a cultivator, right?

“I see.” He looked to the ceiling, though I couldn’t see his expression.

And he talked.

“You once hated eating the fish porridge we’d make. You’d beg me and your mother to buy spices and meat. You could stomach the fish, but you struggled to finish the porridge.”

“That was my fault. I should’ve focused on you back then.” He refused to look me in the eye as he admitted all of this.

“When I pushed you to be a cultivator, trying to be better than all the other kids, when I used to scold you all the time to become one, you grew distant from me. I knew at one point you hated that word down to your depths. I didn’t give you that space to live a child’s life. Because of that, not once did you admit to my face you wanted to become one. Even though I would hear you talking to yourself in the well about your lofty goals. I wasn’t good enough to hear them from you. I’m sorry.”

For some reason, I felt his words were distant.

“And you used to be far more rowdy as a result. You weren’t filial, listening to my every command and order like a father wished for. You grew tired of me, wanting to live outside the house.”

I felt metal rest on my neck, to the point blood formed.

And once more, my father looked at me.

It was as if he wasn’t looking at his own son, but a monstrous demon.

“You aren’t my son.”

Ah.

I should’ve realised.

This was the love of a father.

“Where is Al-Tezar? The child who my wife named in honour of her ancestor?”

And what was on his face wasn’t the loving gaze of a father.

But the despair of a man knowing his son had been replaced by a monster.

The blade on my neck trembled.

“WHERE IS MY SON?!”

I stood inside a wooden house, watching the first years of my life here flash by.

“This again…” I was forced to relive this every night.

I couldn’t grow numb to it.

That was the least I could do.

“There’s always a reason for everything. Perhaps today, you may gleam another meaning behind it,” a young man cut in, with radiating white hair and sharp steel eyes. He wore a simple and unblemished white robe, with a silver sash that flew amongst an invisible breeze.

“Oh I know the reason. This is simply my punishment. So, who are you?” I instinctively placed an arm on my hip, scrutinising the character before me. Was I finally developing a new illness, or was he really not a mere figment of my imagination?

But I couldn’t deny but think of a single word when I saw this man.

Radiance.

“Think of me as an observer, just like you.” He swatted away my question while holding a hand to his chin, taking in his surroundings.

And with that, an old memory continued to move, like the shutter of an old film reel.

Inside a quaint little home at the northern edge of an island, Where we gathered rainbow coloured resin from the trees to sell, salvaged salt oil from the shores, and hoped to produce a prodigy to stand out amongst its neighbours; a person who could cultivate.

A young boy sat, eating a porridge, while his parents chatted about the mundane matters of their life.

“Al-Tezar, has there been anyone at Old Zenbu’s school that understands Qi yet?” His father asked with urgency, slamming the cup of water on the table.

“Dear, that question again? Aren’t you a bit too obsessed?” His mother asked, tired of the same topic every day.

“We haven’t had a cultivator from our village in multiple generations, except your great-grandfather, who left long ago. Whoever becomes a cultivator will either leave to join those lofty places in the sky unaffected by the oceans, or become the next chief to lead us all.” The father took a bite off their fried fish with soup, waiting for a response.

“F-father. There is none who has learnt to use Qi, but Teacher Zenbu has praised the village chief’s granddaughter the most for her performance,” The young Al-Tezar stammered, looking down to focus on his food. He hated the soup made from the leaves of the trees that grew around here; it was far too sweet and salty.

And he especially hated his father’s incessant scolding every day about him becoming a cultivator.

“Hmm, as expected, the progeny of the chief is well learned.” But irritation grew in his father’s face as he muttered under his breath. “But it is we who should be the chief, we are the ones with an Immortal in our lineage, and I run the village’s company of salvagers. His entire lineage has not even a speck of the Architect’s Grace flowing throu-”

“Dear! Why are you so obsessed? You always bring this up all the time, you don’t eve-”

A loud knock on their door interrupted them, with the father ordering his son to open it. “Al-Tezar, go.”

The young Altezar groaned, slouching.

“Tez. Listen to your father, okay?” His mother said.

And only then did he listen.

Al-Tezar scrambled out of his chair to open it, only to be greeted by an old man with vigour and healthy shape, with a soft smile on his face. “Forgive me, I wasn’t aware that you were eating dinner.”

His father removed all notions of anger, giving a beaming smile as he stood from his chair. “It’s alright, chief. I’ll come talk to you outside.”

The young Al-Tezar slowly sipped his soup, having already eaten up his fish. He began fidgeting his fork with his spoon, a tell tale sign of his desire to leave.

His mother sighed. “It’s ok Tez, you don’t have to finish it. Tomorrow I’ll ask your father to buy some spices and meat, okay? You can go play.”

Young Al-Tezar dropped off his chair, running to give his mother a hug. With a playful stride he left through the back entrance, going to the old well cavern that belonged to his family.

“Interesting. The way you look at your own self, it’s as if you’re seeing a stranger,” the radiant observer noted, taking in the sensations. I watched him put his hand through the door, but it went through affecting nothing, as if he were an apparition.

I held a wry smile on my face, at the absurdity of the situation. “Heh, it’s something I can’t help. When you look to the past, it’s like seeing a swarm of regrets, cutting at you from the inside.”

The radiant observer ended up seeing the matter at hand.

“Yet, it does not seem like they are even yours.”

I breathed heavily through my nose. Great, now I’ve picked up this habit of snorting.

This man had scary intuition, whatever he was.

It seems he didn’t see the first nightmare in the beginning.

The film reel continued to move.

Al-Tezar’s father held his head in his arms as he sat on the table, ruminating on the village chief’s words.

“He’s too insistent, wanting to purchase not only our house but even our land. I can’t even deny it, the village chief is even offering a fair price. But if he does, I’ll lose more of my influence.” His father questioned, seeing the half empty bowl. “Al-Tezar, that kid is too quiet, he’s still going out to play on his own?”

His wife sheepishly smiled, cleaning up the table. “Come eat, dear. As for our land… My great-grandfather left it. If you ask your men of salvagers to expand the cavern, we might get closer to its purpose.”

I laughed with ridicule hearing her words. Indeed, her great-grandfather had left this place for them.

In the end it was an absurd joke.

“This is your obsession. Until you sever these ties, you won’t ever be able to ascend. For the past is like spilled milk is unable to be returned to its jug. Let alone a jug that was never in your grasp,” the radiant observer revealed, seeing through my heart like he was peeling an onion’s layer bit by bit.

Al-Tezar’s father drew a long sigh, rubbing his eyes with worry. “I would’ve done so years ago, but it’s too risky. The moment news of this leaks out, the rest of the village will definitely want to get themselves involved, especially that chief. In Fact, he’s already suspecting it, trying to buy it off me.”

“Al-Tezar is already ten. It’s time he starts to work with me. Once he learns with me to become a salt oil salvager, he’ll understand the dynamics of this island, and he’ll help me gain more reputation. Old Zenbu hasn’t taken anyone yet as a personal disciple, and I can’t keep waiting,” he announced to his wife, changing his plans.

He washed down his woes with the sweet spicy soup that was left.

The reel continued.

The scene changed, as a young Al-Tezar played in the cavern deep in the well, with a pond nearby. He used the ancient carvings on the wall as fuel for his imagination, seeing himself as a legendary hero. With a stick in hand, he caressed the wall, with the yonder of a child. And when the radiant observer noted those carvings, he narrowed his eyes.

In here at least, he could think about the unachievable goals he had. Even if he grew tired of his father pushing him to do it.

“If I can understand the Breath of the Architect, like Teacher Zenbu said, then I can be a cultivator! Father, mother, just wait! I won’t let you down!”

“Very good, child. My granddaughter needs to face ambitious youth such as yourself.” A firm hand clasped on the boy, with a sharp pain pricking the back of his neck.

His eyes drooped with tiredness.

The village chief patted him, walking him towards the well. “Your father is a smart man. I hope he understands what is best for his son.” He walked towards the cavern, feeling it with his hands. “Indeed, the secret behind this place has hidden itself within the shadows of his lineage.”

And the village chief left the cavern well, his steps the only sound in this silent abode, as he headed towards the northern shore.

On that night, a large shadow of water engulfed the cavern well, flooding everything within and causing a cave in.

The lanterns on the island flickered, with the desperation of a father leading a team. “Hurry! My son is still in there! There’s been a cave-in, so take the utmost care when looking for him!”

In desperation, he ran to grab his own gear as well, to dive right in.

“Wait here, the rest of them will find him. If you accidently break a rock because you can’t focus, you’ll end up burying your own son. Pray to the Architect, and we will succeed. First, tell me what happened,” an old man with a red thin string on his wrist patted the father, and a strange calmness overcame him.

“Old Zenbu, you’re also here?! I’ll… I’ll wait, I can trust my men. If you have any wisdom, we would all take what we can get.”

But soon, when one of his workers came out, with a young body riddled with bruises and destroyed bones, Al-Tezar’s father madly ran to see for himself.

He cradled his own son in his own hands, his skin pale and blue, and the movement of blood dull, he could only hang his head down in shame.

On that night, Al-Tezar had died.

He despaired, wailing in misery at the loss of his only child.

Until his son had woken up in the morning.

Completely changed.

“He died. Yet, here you are, living. You did not defy death, rather, you usurped it,” I heard the radiant observer say, his white hair floating with a life of its own.

He came face to face with me, examining my very existence. In mockery.

“On that day, he died. Even his soul could not make it to the Genesis River. It was destroyed and in the end, devoured by you, so you could take place in that hollow body. What a fantastical coincidence,” he continued, and with each word, I felt the world around me fall apart.

The film reel reversed back, spinning for a long time.

Until all that was left was me and him. And an endless beach, with the boundless ocean. Across the horizon, skyscrapers stood, with the international railway lines extending to outer space, having conquered the world’s firmament.

An old memory, of an old life.

This was my domain.

A nostalgic place.

And for the first time, I had another being observe it.

What did it seem to his eyes? To that man who could pierce through even my heart to read my thoughts.

The radiant observer saw the landscape, and his eyes slightly widened with contemplation. He saw the reflection on the surface of the ocean, examining himself. Even he could tell that the nature of this place was completely different to the one from before.

“So? Isn’t it courtesy for a guest like you to state his name?” I faced him, gazing at his brilliant steel eyes.

I could even get an inkling of the type of person he was. There was a saying in where I came from, that eyes were the window to the soul.

“You are a monster. But if a monster like you wants to play the game of courtesy, then the owner of the land must state his name first,” he retorted back with a light smile. As if he was watching an extremely interesting show.

I decided to speak the truth. My truth.

“My name, huh? Well, you can call me Tez.”

He laughed with ridicule at my answer, almost like he couldn’t believe it.

“Kid, or if you even are one, tell me this,” he asked me, though I’m not sure why he was confident I would listen to his questioning.

“You cheated death, beginning a second life, escaping the gaze of the heavens, defied the Testament of the Architect, and with all that wisdom you still amounted to nothing. How did it feel when you took over the body of a dead child, taking his name for yourself? And…”

He stepped forward towards me, and the ocean became turbulent. Storms of water rose, tornadic waterspouts formed, and clouds embroiled over the sky.

Shit! I let my guard down, shouldn’t this just be a dream?! But my senses are telling me that I’m screwed if I stay put!

I stepped back, my hair flapping against the whistling winds to the point I could barely keep my eyes open.

“How did it feel to usurp that power?”

Then I felt it.

A tip of a sword ripped through my skin, forming out of my arm.

SYSTEM WARNING

Unable to comprehend Path of Reverend Sword!

Unable to contain Path of Reverend Sword!

Warning: Soul Host Compromised!

I faced the blare of sounds in my head, and for the first time, an immense fear of the unknown came upon me.

There was only one time I felt this in my life.

When I had first met the realm beyond death.

“I am the one who once walked the peak of Zhenren’s Throat. I am the one who discovered the kiln at the bottom, and roamed beyond Radiant Abyss. I am ambition.”

His voice became distant, and only warnings sounded within my own head.

DO NOT LISTEN TO HIS NAME.

“My name is-”

SYSTEM ABORT

And with the cosmic darkness that shattered in the sky, the System had finally shown itself.

The radiant observer stared at it, this time with glee.

”Found you.”
