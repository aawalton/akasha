Valanor moved at a steady pace through the wilds of Potentia. This was the first time since arriving that he’d found any real dense vegetation that wasn’t actively trying to devour him, and he tried to enjoy the relative peace. He knew it couldn’t last, after all.

The forest grew in a valley between two mountains, and was surprisingly lush. He occasionally needed to seek out new paths that his large, armored body could fit through, as it felt like a shame to simply knock the rare trees down. They were all a collage of reds and browns, making the woods practically invisible against the rust-colored mountains.

Still, nowhere on Potentia was truly safe, or truly isolated. Valanor had been attacked twice, and even ambushed a small part of demons once. They’d all been Dusk rank, so no major threat, but he refused to become complacent, or leave enemies at his back. He’d already learned the hard way that any demon left alive could end up stalking him for miles.

But he felt confident. His Bonds and his body had recovered from his endless battle with the mysterious white-horned demon, and he’d found that traveling alone attracted far less attention than traveling with a group. That only added to his regrets at failing his team, and much of his time was spent in a spiral of self-recrimination, despite knowing it achieved nothing.

Valanor kept moving forward, only vaguely certain that he was going in the right direction. He’d lost patience once, and used Twilight power to begin leaping up a mountain, but he’d quickly been taught the danger of such a move. Three flying demons had emerged from hidden lairs, looking like enormous birds with hooked beaks, and they’d been on him in minutes.

Another time he’d actually tried riding Syph, his ice wolf, but ran into similar problems when a small stampede of many-legged insects burst from the ground to chase them. Ultimately Syph had needed to backtrack entirely out of their territory just to end the pursuit, and it had cost Valanor more than a day in slow, ponderous travel.

The forest around him was growing thick again, and he found himself stepping sideways between the narrowly spaced trees. When he at last emerged into a small clearing, too rocky for anything to grow, he found another “present” waiting for him, and chuckled softly.

The husks of a half-dozen dead demons were slowly dissolving into magic before him. They’d likely been some kind of large felines, though it was difficult to tell given the state of their ravaged bodies. Valanor moved quickly past them, knowing that few things could attract demons as quickly as unprotected meat.

This was far from the first time he’d encountered this kind of oddity. The last three weeks had brought many signs that he was never truly alone, and there was little doubt that it was his unseen shadow at work.

At first there’d simply been glances of something from the corner of his eye. Vague impressions that he wasn’t truly alone. At night, when he rested, trying to find some semblance of safety, he could feel a gaze, watching him from the darkness.

Slowly, the signs had become harder to ignore. One could never travel for long on Potentia without encountering demons, yet Valanor met nothing that could truly test his limits, or make him fear for his life. The packs of Dusks he encountered were small enough to pose no threat, and twice he spotted Twilights from a distance, moving in the opposite direction, as if running from him.

More recently, it seemed his unseen “friend” wasn’t even trying to hide his presence. Valanor would find slaughtered demons waiting in his path, often disturbingly fresh. Twice he’d faced Twilight opponents that had obviously just recently been in battle, sporting terrible wounds, and proving to be easy victories.

Valanor decided that tonight he would finally try to take things farther on his end. There was no telling how long he could simply benefit from the current arrangement, and he felt that his unexpected ally deserved an extended hand.

He already knew where to do it, having spotted a suitable location from higher in the mountains.

The sun had already set by the time Valanor reached the narrow stone gap he’d been searching for. It was like a small wound on the mountainside, and the high rock walls provided a rare opportunity to build a fire. He still shaded it with small stones on one side to keep the light from shining out like a beacon, but eventually he was resting comfortably with his back against a wall.

Syph slumbered lazily next to him, her massive, furry body a source of comfort and heat, despite her identity as a frost wolf. Her massive, barrel chest rumbled so loudly with snores that he occasionally worried it would attract attention, but nighttime in Potentia was always a cacophony of sounds as countless creatures went out on the hunt.

Valanor sipped at a cup of tea as he waited, staring out past the fire with a mix of hope and trepidation. He’d preserved some demon meat with simple magics, and stacked it high. It was the best offering he could make, and only time would tell whether it was accepted.

As the hours rolled by, he let his mind wander. He thought of Ethan, and Prince Calevaro. It had been so long since he’d seen either. Part of him still thought like the First Shield of Viridus, a constant pull of duty making him feel like he should be with the prince.

He had similar feelings about Ethan, despite the tragedy of the boy’s death. Valanor had at times felt like an older brother, or possibly even a father to that strange lad from another world. No amount of logic could convince him that it wasn’t his fault the Chosen had fallen. It always felt like a failure, somehow, no matter that Valanor’s shield couldn’t possibly have held back the disaster that buried most of the city of Sanctustella, now Ethan’s grave.

Valanor was in the middle of a frustratingly familiar line of thought, one that always devolved into a mix of self-recrimination and self-pity, when he finally noticed that he wasn’t alone.

The white-horned demon watched him from a crouch across the fire. It slowly brought large chunks of meat to its mouth, unblinking eyes studying Valanor.

As Valanor looked back into that intelligent gaze, he once again found himself wondering if it was actually a transformed Nator looking back at him. One of Selina’s people, not so far gone as they’d all assumed. But whatever this creature was, it had not only walked away from their battle those weeks ago, but had been actively helping him since. Valanor let himself hope.

“Hello there, friend,” he whispered.

Gallow hovered above his growing army, enjoying the feeling of the wind flowing around his body. He hadn’t realized how much he’d missed this feeling, the simple joy of being outside, floating on a cushion of power. He’d been a simpler being once, not yet an Emperor, and had gone into battle with his own host. Those days were as old as their most ancient histories now, but Gallow still thought of them fondly.

He looked down at the several dozen Twilight demons that his kin had gathered, and that Conquest had leashed. They were of various types, but that meant nothing to Gallow. They were nothing but walking vessels of power to him, and he only thought of them as a measure of the progress they were making.

It was too slow.

Reluctantly, he lowered himself to the ground, feeling a frustrating stab of pain from the wound in his chest that refused to heal. He forced himself not to touch the gaping hole, poorly hidden behind a fold hold of a robe he’d donned.

He moved toward Bek, who–as usual–walked next to Conquest, who in turn was riding his enormous bear. They were connected by thick lines of dark flesh to each of the demons, which marched around them like a protective circle. Bek was looking down at his devices, making notes and calculations that no other being was likely able to understand.

“What news?” Gallow demanded.

Bek didn’t look up. “I continue to work on the final challenge, my Emperor. I’m convinced there is a way to bind you to the army, but I require additional time.”

“I need that connection, Bek,” Gallow said, knowing full well he stated the obvious. “This tale will have a miserable ending if only this creature can take up Dusk’s power.”

Bek tapped a dismissal of the worry with his fingers. “I will not allow that to happen, Emperor. Already I believe we have the elements required. Your Familiars are connected, the energy simply does not flow. Right now, the power of Dusk lies within the demons of this world, and I believe gathering them is sufficient for our purpose.”

“But the power needs to be collected in one being,” Gallow supplied.

“Correct, my Emperor. When the time comes, we will need to force that energy into you. We will also need you to be close enough to Dusk’s empty throne for you to claim it before the corruption affects your mind. Given the energy required, I suspect this will be a narrow window.”

Gallow made a displeased sound. None of this was entirely new, but he’d been hoping Bek might have solved something.

“What about the kin?” he asked. “The demons have been arriving more slowly, I think.”

Bek did look up then. “Massun is dead,” he said. “He disappeared when scouting to the west. We know he reached the ocean, but his implants went quiet shortly after.”

Gallow tapped his fingers in a gesture of mourning. So few left…

“And Ethan Bishop?”

“We’ve discovered his location, and it is as we suspected. He raises his own army, currently larger than our own, but growing more slowly. It is made up by a greater number of weaker demons, and is vulnerable to sabotage, when you decide the time is right.”

Gallow found that to be comforting, at least. “If the armies clashed, you suspect we’d win?”

Bek hesitated. “There is some kind of construct at the center of his forces, covered in Rune Circles I do not recognize. There are several oddities to it, and I will share my report with further details. But the simple truth is that we do not know its capabilities. There are reports that this construct grows in size, and is disturbingly well-armed.”

That soured Gallow’s mood. “We cannot allow the Chosen to continue unopposed. The time to strike may not be now, but I wish for a list of possible methods of disruption. Keep one of the kin near his forces.”

“Of course, my Emperor,” Bek said with a small bow.

Gallow rose back into the air, not finding it as relaxing or freeing as minutes ago. This world, this power would be his. Even if he had to tear through Ethan Bishop to take it.
