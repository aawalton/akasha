“What are you talking about?”

“You know who burned the granary, right? Yet somehow, you refused to tell. Who did this?” James accused him. James didn’t want to find the culprit. That wasn’t what bugged him. He didn’t care who burned the granary, but why it happened. He wanted to know whether the system allowed this to happen, or if this was a scripted event.

If the dungeon allowed this, it was merely an act of freedom with no deeper meaning—just like when you kill an NPC in a dungeon. But if this was a scripted event, that meant something bigger was on the way. James couldn’t put it into words, so he used the way he knew best: threat.

“As I said before, it was because a lit candle fell.”

“Damn it, Silas. Why didn’t you tell them in court? You could have saved a lot of people!” Andrew said. Almost ten men had been executed here.

“I was the one who put the candles there. It was outside of my calculations! If I named the culprit, they and I would be executed. But if I framed it as an accident, I hoped we would get clemency. That granary was important, right?” Silas said.

“I don’t care about that. I want to know how the candle got there and who made it fall. Were they survivors, or were they natives?” James asked. His question made Andrew tilt his head.

“Bernard, Clement, and Nafghar were the ones in the room. It could be any one of them.”

“Are they all survivors?”

“No. Only Bernard was,” Andrew said. He then turned to Silas, asking for more information. “Where is Bernard?”

James was mildly amazed. Had Andrew already memorized all the survivors’ names under his command? All five hundred of them?

“Dead six hours ago. You know how the ones they took never came back, right? I figured we would be executed early to reduce food consumption.”

“It’s worse than that,” James said. “Andrew, let’s get going. Let’s chase the running soldier.”

“But what about them?”

“You can take them with us or leave them here. But there isn’t much information we can get from them. Let’s chase him.”

“…Understood,” Andrew said. “Here. I will release you.”

After releasing Anderson and Silas, James and Andrew ran along the hallway. Anderson followed suit, but Silas didn’t. At the end of the corridor, there was a door. When James and Andrew opened it, horror couldn’t even begin to describe what they saw.

A pungent smell of ammonia assaulted their nostrils, like someone had pissed all over the place. The smell wasn’t the worst thing. It was soon kicked into the back seat when James saw what was inside. Rows of human flesh were strewn across the room, eaten by some sort of white larvae. The larvae wiggled, and at the torso of each victim lay a flower. Its fruit pulsed like a beating heart. There, on the other side of the room, the soldier they had chased lay dead. A sword was pierced through his heart by a man in silver armor. His gallant armor betrayed the atrocity he had just committed. Sensing the fresh blood, the larvae rushed to feast on him.

“What the hell…” Andrew stared at the scene, agape. “Count Bartholomew, what’s the meaning of this? Did you know about this?”

“Hm… farming, huh?” James pondered. He thought they couldn’t possibly farm here. This world didn’t have sunlight, and the law was different as well. But thinking back, couldn’t mushroom grow in the dark? Moreover, all they needed was dirt.

The Count was doing the same thing, but with monsters. James didn’t know why they were doing it now with human lives instead of other meat, but alas, the Count had really gone mad.

“Answer me!” Andrew threw his spear at the Count. The spear glowed golden, approaching at mach speed. Bartholomew merely raised his hand; a golden pentagram shone from it. The golden meteor crashed into it and stopped midair. Afterward, the spear dropped to the ground. The Count didn’t attack. He began a soliloquy.

“This monster was a forbidden seed of Yggdrasil. Once upon a time, there lay a great city in the north. The city prospered. Tens of thousands of people lived in that city. However, the city had one problem. It had too many mouths to feed. Just hunting wasn’t enough for the residents to sustain themselves.

“But because they had this tree, they survived. But they had to pay the price. Each month, they would sacrifice a handful of their people to the great tree, and in return, the tree would grant them food—one soul for a hundred portions of food. Don’t you think it was a splendid offer?”

James suddenly had an inkling of the city the Count was talking about. He then compared the tree and the flower stalks that grew on the corpses, wondering whether they would grow as big as that tree.

“Well…” Andrew was stunned.

“That city was destroyed, Andrew. I have been there. There is a catch to that offer,” James snorted at the Count’s words. “At the very least, he was no cultist like before.”

“Ah. I see. So you have been there. That means you have seen the Black Plague and survived? Amazing. Anyway, I digress. I suppose I must now kill you.”

James brandished his sword. He knew he wouldn’t let him out alive. “I feel stupid for thinking that you would let us go.”

“Just let us go. You can sacrifice your own people. There are others you can sacrifice, right? Why us?” Anderson said. “We survivors only arrived here a month ago, and you are willing to kill us like pigs? I thought we were all equal here.”

“You are all equal. I also sacrificed my own people. You simply were convenient black sheep. I mean, this is all technically your fault, right? The granary burned because of you, so you need to take responsibility.”

“I didn’t burn it! I just happened to work in the same place!”

“Same either way. I need about twenty souls to sacrifice to Yggdrasil before it can be harvested. And then, I need about twenty more to survive until the red moon. Your souls alone won’t suffice.”

“You are going to destroy us all,” James said. “That cockroach was dangerous.”

“Cockroach?” Andrew tilted his head.

“The thing he called the Black Plague. It was millions of cockroaches, devouring anything on sight.”

“Can we stop them?” Andrew asked. But James only shrugged.

“It doesn’t seem the Count is willing to let us go. Besides, are you okay with sacrificing people just to eat? I thought you hated this kind of thing—sacrificing others for your own goals. He is basically sacrificing humans and feeding us monster fruit, you know. I don’t even know if it’s edible. There might be parasites inside…” James said.

“My apologies, but I can’t allow you to leave here alive. Cultivating these plants is the highest taboo. It directly violates an ancient treaty. If word about what we are doing were to get out, Maruk would come here with his army, and my people wouldn’t even support me,” the Count answered. He then brandished his sword. “I suppose this marks the end of our talk. Pity, Anderson. I had high hopes for your achievements.”

“He’s coming.” James activated his skill, Lunar Counter, as the Count leaped toward him with his lunge sword skill.

As both golden glows met, the skill teleported James behind him, ready to finish him off. But the Count slashed backward, and both sword skills clashed again. James was bewildered. This was the first time his Lunar Counter had ever been stopped. Their swords slowly pushed against each other. When James knew he wouldn’t win the skill clash, he activated his jet thruster, leaping away as the Count slashed empty air before him three more times.

“Shit. This guy is strong,” James cursed.

He followed up with several lightning bolts at the Count. The Count didn’t block them with his armor, but with his sword. With each swing, a lightning bolt was cut down. Andrew leaped to his spear, while Anderson tried to gather mana for his ice magic.

James fired his mind magic, trying to delude his opponent. But his mana eyes saw the magic projectiles bounce off the Count’s sword. It shouldn’t have been possible. The magic should have passed through any armor or weapon.

“So the sword also has special abilities, huh? Well, mine has special abilities as well.”

James then swung his sword from a distance, activating his Wind Slash. Andrew followed up from the rear, thrusting his spear right at him. The Count deflected all of the strikes with the precision of a surgeon. When Andrew used his skill, James followed suit with a lunge attack, striking at the Count’s flank. The Count also used a sword skill. James felt a shiver as he recognized it. It was one of his sword skills—Lunar Counter.

“Fuck! Andrew!” James roared as the Count shifted beside him, trying to cut Andrew down. But the Count didn’t follow through with his strike. Instead, he redirected it to his side, where Anderson lunged at him with his dagger skill.

Anderson was thrown back. A pool of blood gushed from his chest. Smelling the scent of blood, the voracious larvae turned toward Anderson. They slithered toward him.

The Count’s sword skill didn’t end there. He still had one more hit reserved for James’ lunging attack. His expertise with the sword was terrifying. But James had several tricks up his sleeve. He doused the Count with fire breath when their skills clashed.

James expected the Count to cry out in pain as the flames engulfed him. But he didn’t. His sword continued unimpeded as he deflected James’ strike and lacerated James’ chest. His armor shone golden as the familiar pentagram glowed beneath his feet, enveloping him in a protective aura.

The fresh blood agitated the larvae. When they came closer, James used his ice magic to freeze them. The Count’s eyes widened as James mercilessly stomped the approaching larvae under his boots.

“Stop it!” The Count leaped toward James, his sword glowing golden. James leaped away with his jet thruster, dodging the strike.

“Oh… I should have known to use such a trick.” James smirked when he finally found the Count’s weakness. “Didn’t think you would care so much for these bugs.”

Time for round two.
