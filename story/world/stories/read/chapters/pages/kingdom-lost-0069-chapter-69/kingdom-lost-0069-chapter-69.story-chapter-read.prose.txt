Chapter 69

The Great Meeting Room of Clawborn Citadel lay wrapped in the bruised light of dusk. Torch flames hissed along the walls, throwing long shadows across black stone carved with claw motifs that seemed to shift when no one looked directly at them. The room smelled of cold iron, old smoke, and the faint metallic bite of frost that had begun to seep even here, far from the northern borders.

A long obsidian table dominated the center, polished so darkly it drank the light. Advisors stood along its edges like statues, rigid in their formal robes and armor, faces set in practiced neutrality. No one spoke above a murmur. The silence pressed against the ears.

Octavia sat at the head, armored in dark plate etched with silver lines that caught the torchlight like veins of moonlight. No mask today. Her face bare in the dim light, no veil of silver to soften the steel in her expression. She had summoned them on short notice. Urgency overrode ceremony. Her gauntleted hands rested on the arms of the throne-like chair, one finger tapping once, twice, then stopping as though she had caught herself. Outwardly she was still, the picture of imperial composure.

She kept her gaze fixed on the double doors at the far end of the room. They had not moved in hours. She had waited longer than she ever waited for anyone. Edrin Kavos had been given resources, freedom to roam the borders, and explicit orders to report. Yet he delayed and every day of delay brought fresh reports she could not ignore.

Frost crept into northern villages that had never known winter this early. Clawborn tribute caravans arrived half-empty or not at all. Drivers muttered of empty roads and shadows that moved against the wind. Unexplained portal flickers appeared in the south, brief tears in the air that left behind glittering residue no mage could fully explain. Residue that smelled of cold stone and something older.

She shifted once. Leather creaked beneath the plate. One advisor glanced toward her, then quickly away. Another cleared his throat. The sound was small and swallowed by the room.

Tal-Vesh stepped forward from the shadows near the table. Older than most in her inner circle, his face was lined from years of carrying bad news. His voice came low, meant for her ears alone.

“He approaches, Empress.”

Octavia’s finger stilled. She drew one measured breath, then gave a single nod.

“Let him enter,” she said. “No fanfare.”

The words carried just far enough. Guards at the doors straightened. Metal scraped as they pulled the heavy iron rings. A draft of colder air slipped through the widening gap, carrying the scent of pine and frozen earth. The torches shrank back for a heartbeat, then steadied.

Octavia did not rise. She waited, gaze steady on the doorway, while the room held its breath.

The double doors parted slowly, hinges groaning like distant ice cracking. A sharp draft rushed in ahead of the figure beyond them, carrying the clean, biting scent of pine and frozen earth. Torch flames leaned away, dimming low before flaring again.

Edrin Kavos stepped through the threshold. Tall and lean, his frame looked almost too spare beneath robes of deep gray wool edged in frost-silver threading. His skin was pale, nearly translucent at the temples, and his eyes were the colour of thin ice stretched over deep water, calm yet unreadable. In his right hand rested a slender staff of dark wood, its surface polished to a dull sheen. At the top, a small crystal glowed with steady blue light, pulsing once as he crossed the threshold.

He offered no bow. Only a slight inclination of the head, polite but unhurried.

Guards along the walls tensed. Hands drifted toward sword hilts and spear shafts, fingers curling but not closing. No one drew. The room stayed silent except for the soft scrape of boots on stone as Edrin advanced a single step.

Octavia rose from her chair. The dark plate armor shifted with a metallic sigh. She regarded him without blinking.

“You kept me waiting, Kavos,” she said.

Edrin’s voice came calm, carrying just far enough to reach her without effort.

“The north does not keep to southern schedules, Empress. Nor do the doors I tend.”

He took another measured step forward. The air around him cooled noticeably. Tal-Vesh shifted his weight, shoulders tightening. The others followed suit. The torches burned a shade dimmer, as though the room itself had drawn back from the man who had finally arrived.

Octavia gestured toward the obsidian table with a sharp flick of her gauntleted hand. The motion was precise, commanding without flourish. Edrin approached, staff tapping once against the stone floor with a soft, resonant note. He stopped at the edge of the table but made no move to sit. The crystal atop his staff pulsed once, a slow bloom of blue light that faded as quickly as it came.

She fixed him with a steady stare. “Reports of portal activity multiply. Unauthorized openings. Residue traced to places it should not be. Explain.”

Edrin regarded the table for a moment as though reading something in its polished surface. Then he lifted his gaze to meet hers. His voice remained calm, measured, carrying the quiet weight of someone accustomed to being heard without raising his tone.

“The fabric between places is thinning. Not from force, but from hunger. Something pulls at the threads.”

Octavia’s eyes narrowed. She leaned forward, elbows resting on the table’s edge, armor creaking.

“What comes through? Slaves? Weapons? Or something worse?”

“Echoes, mostly.” Edrin’s fingers tightened on the staff, knuckles pale against the dark wood. “The openings are not all mine.”

The words hung in the air. A low murmur stirred among the advisors, quickly stifled. Tal-Vesh shifted again, his lined face tightening.

Octavia’s voice sharpened. “You are the only one in the realm capable of such magic. If not you, then who?”

Edrin tilted his head a fraction, the gesture thoughtful. “Some doors open by others, where they are entered not exited.”

He paused, letting the silence stretch. Then he continued, voice soft but carrying a new edge of precision.

“The residue patterns match an ancient awakening.”

Octavia studied him. No warmth entered her expression. She straightened, the chair creaking as she settled back.

“You delay your return. You speak in riddles. I gave you resources, freedom to roam the borders. I expect answers, not nonsense.”

Edrin met her gaze without flinching. The crystal in his staff pulsed again, slower this time, as though echoing the rhythm of his breath.

“Answers without context are dangerous, Empress. The north does not yield them easily and some truths are best learned in time. These truths answer to no one.”

The room grew quieter still. Shadows stretched farther along the claw-carved walls as the torches burned lower. Octavia’s fingers curled once on the arm of her chair, then stilled. When she spoke, her tone held the flat certainty of command.

“Then speak the pieces you have. And speak them plainly.”

Edrin let the silence settle a moment longer. Then he spoke, voice low but carrying a new direction.

“The disturbances are not confined to the south, Empress. The north requires attention.”

Octavia narrowed her eyes. The torchlight caught the sharp edge of her gaze.

“Speak plainly.”

Edrin’s fingers shifted on the staff. The crystal pulsed once more, dimmer this time.

“ Frost moves where it has not moved in centuries. Villages further north near the great divide empty from weather shifting and crops being destroyed. These are signs of the coming. You don't need my council on what you already know.”

Octavia studied him. Her posture remained rigid.

“So the portals?”

Edrin’s tone stayed even. “The portals are symptoms, not the disease.”

The crystal in his staff dimmed. The chill deepened in response. Tal-Vesh drew his robe tighter across his chest. The torches flickered, flames pulling inward before steadying once more.

Octavia straightened. Her voice turned to steel, cutting through the cold.

“Then we will not wait for symptoms to become plague.”

She turned to Tal-Vesh and addressed the room at large.

“The lion’s share of the army marches as we speak. The largest Legion, left the northern gates at first light. They carry my banner north to secure the borders, and meet this threat head on, this will remind this realm the power of the Clawborn Dynasty.”

Edrin’s jaw tightened. His eyes narrowed and he made no protest. The silence around him felt heavier than before.

Octavia fixed him with a steady look.

“You will join them, Kavos. Or you will explain why the realm’s greatest portal mage prefers to watch from the shadows.”

"And you will do yourself a favour and remember who serves who in mage…" said Tal-Vesh.

Tal-Vesh was unable to continue speaking as he began to choke and gasp, clawing at his throat, then dropped to the floor.

"Release him… now" commanded Octavia.

“As you command, Empress.”

His voice dropped softer, almost to himself, barely carrying beyond the space between them.

“Marching legions may only feed what waits,” said Edrin.

Octavia’s gaze hardened as Edrin took his leave and Tal-Vesh slowly climbed to his feet.
