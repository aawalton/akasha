Rowan watched the three demons with a wary expression. The change from triumph to alertness would have been jarring if not for [Iron Will] keeping his thoughts steady, but even still, he glanced towards the staircase.

Crap, Rowan grimaced.

It wasn’t there. And not just that, but the Mist around the chamber swirled. Shapes formed. Walls rose from the ground with chittering lesser demons climbing over them. Rowan felt his heartbeat quicken, trepidation warring with excitement.

Stay calm, he told himself. Assess the situation and the threat.

The Demon was an illusionist and power-stealer, a deadly combination that made him a formidable opponent. So far, it showed its tier-three affinity, a Mist-based movement technique, a psychic attack, and a close-range thrust.

Out of all its abilities, what worried Rowan the most was the movement technique combined with its illusions. If he focused on a feint instead of the real threat, getting taken out by a surprise attack wasn’t out of the question—the same way it had tried to do three times already.

In a straight duel, he had no doubt he’d be able to hold off the demon's attacks. Yet for some odd reason, Rowan felt this fight wouldn’t be anywhere close to fair. But then again, they rarely were.

The terrain around him changed again. Walls grew and shrank, pitfalls formed in the ground, and false mirrors with distorted reflections turned at an uneven pace. Through it all, the demon stood on the opposite side of the chamber, an ugly grin on all three of its faces.

Rowan tried not to get disoriented, but even with [Iron Will] working to the fullest, it was proving harder than he’d hoped.

The lesser demons tried to draw his attention, chittering and snarling as they vaulted over imaginary obstacles and climbed over fake walls. Rowan didn’t let his focus waver, keeping his gaze firmly set on the three demons looking at him with malicious eyes.

“I know they’re not real,” Rowan called out, moving his [Blazing Chains] to disturb the demon’s illusions. “So if your tactic was to scare me, it didn’t really work.”

The demons didn’t respond.

Instead, they breathed him in.

Three sets of glowing eyes fluttered closed as their slit-like nostrils flared, drawing in slow, deliberate breaths. Their tongues—too long, too thin—flicked out, tasting the air as though sampling a fine wine.

One of them shuddered, a pleased hum vibrating in its throat. Another licked its lips, gold eyes gleaming with hunger.

“Ah,” the first one whispered, exhaling like a man drinking deep from a goblet. “There it is.”

“The flicker of doubt.”

“The ember of frustration.”

“The heat of anger, simmering beneath all that lovely control.”

They spoke in unison, voices warping and overlapping, as if they weren’t just sensing his emotions, but feeding on them.

One demon tilted its head, pupils narrowing. “But where is the fear, mage?”

Another inhaled again, slower this time, letting the taste linger on its tongue. Then it bared its serrated fangs in something that might have been a grin.

“Ah… I see,” it muttered, savoring the words. “You truly think you can win.”

The others chuckled, their laughter vibrating through the chamber like the rustling of dead leaves.

“How delightful,” all three said in unison.

Rowan swung his chain at an approaching lesser demon, his Intent overpowering the illusion with relative ease, leaving behind nothing but Mist.

“Of course I do,” he replied, his heart beating rapidly as the walls started closing in. “You think because you’re a higher level than me that I’m at a disadvantage?”

Returning its grin, Rowan pulled deeply from his Core. Yet instead of the raging intensity of Fire, a smooth, flowing Wind answered. It raced through his channels, tracing a path to his palm. For a heartbeat, the intensity of his chains and shield flickered, but Rowan simply sharpened his focus.

Keeping this many spells active at the same time was a feat few Yellow-Core mages could manage, especially considering their power. And here he was, doing it while not even halfway through advancing his Core through Orange.

The demon’s eyes narrowed as a swirling vortex appeared in Rowan’s palm.

“And what exactly—”

He didn’t let it finish.

The [Gust] he cast was unlike any Wind spell he’d cast before. It erupted from his hand in a thick column of twisting force, blowing away the Mist and dissipating the illusions.

A lesser demon snarled as it jumped towards him from a nearby boulder, and Rowan simply pointed his arm towards it. The three demons startled back in surprise as their illusion dissolved, jumping closer to the Rift at the back of the chamber with a hiss. Rowan ignored them. His eyes scanned the room, exchanging one of his chains for another [Gust], forcing more of the Mist back.

The demon's true body was somewhere in this room, and Rowan felt it was a fair bet it wasn’t one of the three. With the ability to cloak its position, it didn’t make sense to announce its presence before going for the finishing blow.

It can talk, but it’s still a monster, Rowan thought, sweeping his spells across the chamber, battling against its Intent.

He felt something resist harder as the [Gust] reached the right corner, a surprised grunt following quickly after. Turning his other hand towards the demon, Rowan forced more mana through his channels, empowering his spell to an even greater height.

Forceful Current, he intoned, twisting the circuit into a new configuration.

Congratulations! You have advanced [Gust] into the Murmur-level spell, [Wind Blast]!

The System notification only pushed that further.

[Storm Touched] made itself known once more, making the two spells Rowan sent the demons way effectively Chant’s.

Crystal rapidly formed at the demon's clawed feet, moving up its body and encasing it in a suit of armor. Considering the distance, Rowan didn’t manage to shatter it like last time, but his opponent was still forced to erect a shield spell in addition to its armor. He tested his own against it, feeling the Crystal give. Not only that, but the Mist retreated, completely shattering its illusion.

Rowan’s smile widened.

He had more mana than the creature.

The only reason it would withdraw its makeshift Domain was if it had to. That was effectively its most powerful tool, and without it, the Demon was forced to truly engage. Three Crystal shells stood at the far end of the room, the Rift bleeding off ethereal sparks behind them, its three forms revealed.

Without the Mist supporting them, they crumpled like brittle glass.

The demon hissed angrily as Rowan battered against its defenses, “How!?” It screamed, all pretense of calm gone. “It should be impossible for someone of your level to see through my illusions!”

The wall cracked.

“You wanted to make this fun!” Rowan shouted back, a wide smile on his face, the thrill coursing through his veins. “Well I’m having a fucking blast!”

One of his [Gust]’s dropped.

Sealed Pyre, he intoned.

Something tugged at his circuit, trying to shift it. To add another loop.

Rowan’s focus struggled to encompass it. Without hesitation, he dropped his last chain, quickly followed by his shield. The only thing he kept up was the [Gust], knowing he needed to keep the demon pinned down.

Yet even still, it wasn’t enough.

The orb of flame struggled in his grip, growing hotter by the second.

Rowan made a decision.

He forced the circuit back into the familiar pathways for [Fireball], coming to the sound conclusion that trying to create his first Hymn-level spell during a fight wasn’t a particularly good idea. Especially when it involved this amount of power.

Just the fact that it seemed possible filled Rowan with a boundless sense of anticipation, only fueling his determination to win this fight. There was so much he wanted to accomplish. That he needed to accomplish. And if he was mastering Hymns at half a year, those goals might not be as far away as Rowan first thought.

He was growing stronger. Fast.

This demon won’t be what ends my Path, Rowan vowed.

In a flash, a barely perceptible strain rippled through him, a subtle surge so fleeting it almost went unnoticed. For that heartbeat, the sensation was all there was—a delicate spark of power that vanished as quickly as it arrived.

It seemed to add another layer to his spell, pushing it to the brink of what Rowan could handle.

He flicked his wrist and dropped [Gust], launching the [Fireball].

As soon as he did, Rowan flared [Iron Will], diverting two strands of mana to cast his shield. The strain eased the moment the [Fireball] left his hand—a small mercy that allowed him to react. But even still, he wasn’t quick enough.

The flaming projectile crossed the chamber in moments, impacting against the demon's Crystal barrier. There were no shouts of surprise this time, just the deafening roar of an explosion.

The chamber shattered with a single, deafening blast. A shockwave tore outward, hurling shards of crystal and searing embers in every direction. Rowan caught the brunt of it, sent skidding across the stone floor as heat crashed over him in a choking wave.

His semi-formed shield held, pulsing steadily in front of him as it soaked the worst of the impact. Even so, his hair flew back and sweat beaded on his brow, the air around him rippling with lingering heat.

He should have probably felt at least the slightest bit of trepidation, yet the only emotion surging through him was an eager giddiness.

This was what he was capable of. What his magic could do.

As the explosion passed, Rowan let out a drawn-out breath, making his shield smaller and moving it above his shoulder.

The walls around him were charred black, but his eyes focused on the Crystal dust covering the spot where the demon stood.

“Impressive,” Rowan called out, his limbs starting to feel heavy. “I thought for sure that’d kill you.”

Rowan felt at his Core, a look of surprise flashing across his face.

He’d expected to be at less than a third after that last spell, but his reserves hovered around half.

He frowned. That’s… weird.

Not that he was going to complain about having more mana, but there was something weird going on. Something he didn’t quite understand.

Taking a step forward, Rowan focused.

Later, he thought, walking cautiously towards his seemingly downed opponent. This fight isn’t over just yet.

If the Crystal was still manifested, that meant there was something still alive under there.

Let’s hope for grievously injured.

Casting two [Blazing Chains] came harder than he’d expected, the spells feeling sluggish as Rowan curled them protectively in front of him. There was a very slim chance this was another ploy, and if it was, he didn’t plan on falling for it.

He stopped when his chains got into range.

“You promised me answers,” he said, the anger he’d been suppressing coloring his tone. “Talk, and maybe I won’t make you suffer.”

His chain moved to hover above it, a fierce red glow illuminating the Crystal dust.

It was a gratifying feeling to have a Demon at his mercy—a catharsis he didn’t know he was chasing. His mind tried to flash back to that fateful day, but Rowan didn’t let it. Replaying that memory felt like a well-worn groove at this point, the sorrow and rage fading into something softer.

A low whimper answered him.

“Please… mercy,” the Demon whispered from beneath its shattered barrier, the extent of its injuries still hidden from view. “I… I’m just a pawn… following orders.”

Rowan lowered his chain.

“Whose orders?” he growled, his body tensing. “Why are you here? What are you planning?”

The Crystal shifted. For a moment, there was silence. Then, a soft voice whispered. “...Conquest.”

Mist erupted in a swirling vortex.

Rowan’s spell was already moving, slamming down against the Crystal mound with all the force he could muster. But once again, he was too slow.

The Mist didn’t manage to spread out far, yet that wasn’t the intention.

Utilizing it, the Demon transported itself out of the way of Rowan’s chain, reappearing a few feet away.

Its body was charred beyond recognition, a smoldering stump held against its chest. The expression on its face was one of pure terror, a delicious contrast to the self-satisfied one it wore earlier.

They locked eyes, and then, with surprising quickness considering its state, the creature ran.

Not towards him. It knew better than that now. But towards the Rift still steadily pulsing at the far end of the room.

Rowan’s eyes narrowed, a sense of unease suddenly flaring up.

He didn’t know why it wanted to reach the Rift, but what he did know was that if your opponent wanted something, you denied them.

So he bent his knees and ran after it.
