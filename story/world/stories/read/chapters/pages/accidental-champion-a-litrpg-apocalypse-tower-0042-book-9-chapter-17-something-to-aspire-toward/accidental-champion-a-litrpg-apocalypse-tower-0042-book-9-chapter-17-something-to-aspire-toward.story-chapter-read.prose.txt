Xavier and Adranial spent two weeks working on the design for their home.

He hadn’t known what he was getting himself into.

Xavier had a few ideas about the type of house he saw himself living in, but almost all those ideas were based on his life on Earth and the things he’d seen on TV and in movies—that and his obsession with fantasy novels. So the two types of houses he had in mind were modern billionaire mansions or massive, sprawling castles.

Adranial, however, was the descendant of the most powerful Denizen in this entire universe. She’d been born billions of years after his rule began, and if anyone could be said to be used to the finer things in life it would be her.

Apparently her knowledge of architecture was just about as good as her knowledge of the Tower of Champions.

After he’d learnt just how passionate she was, he stepped back and decided it wasn’t worth fighting for full control—not that she completely took over. The house they ended up designing was a true collaboration between the two of them, and in the end, it did turn out to look like something from his daydreams.

It wasn’t quite a massive mansion or a sprawling castle, it was smaller and more intimate than that, but it had the option to expand and their lives expanded—just as the Seed Sanctuary and the Roving Seed Base had been expanded over time.

The outside looked castle-like, with solid stone walls and a small tower.

A tower that happened to have shelves lining every wall fully stacked with books. The library had several levels to it, the books stretching all the way to the top of the tower form the very bottom, with a staircase that clung close to the shelves and walkways for those who couldn’t simply fly. Though as a compromise the tower had a hollow middle that Xavier could traverse with a flap or two of his wings, making it to any part of the library he wished.

The tower library was far and away his favourite part of the house, and as he collected more books, he could keep extending the tower ever more upward. His imagination went a little wild with that, wondering if the tower would one day be as tall as The Collector’s castle in the Ventorin sector—that throne room was in space, for god sakes.

Now that’s something to aspire toward.

After they’d designed that room, and finally realised it in reality, walking through it, he’d had to stop and look over at Adranial.

“What is it?” she asked.

He dropped his head, sighed. “Just… I love everything about what we’re doing.” He ran a finger along one of the spines. “This house, you, I’ve dreamt about things like this.”

“You feel guilty,” Adranial whispered, taking his hand. “Because you’re enjoying yourself.”

He nodded.

She ran a hand through his hair. “It’s okay to stop once in a while, especially inside this field. You deserve this. You’re not…”

“Letting anyone down?”

She hadn’t known what to say after that. But after a little while, he’d managed to sink into it again, to enjoy the process.

The main rooms were rather conservative, considering Adranial’s upbringing. A living room large enough for several couches whenever they had company. A separate dining room, because even though the two of them didn’t need to eat at their stage of advancement, it was something they both enjoyed.

The bedroom was large enough to fit what was essentially a super-king-sized bed. After that room had been built, well… needless to say, they took full advantage of it.

Things needed to be tested, after all.

When they were finally done, walking through the space, he was surprised at how perfect it felt. He’d thought it would take time for it to feel like home, but the instant they had the furniture all in place, and their things out everywhere, it just felt…

Right.

Like this was where he’d always meant to live.

When they stood outside, looking in, Adranial rested her head on his shoulder.

“I’m surprised you didn’t want it… bigger. You know, given what you’re used to,” Xavier said.

“I do want it to be bigger, one day. But right now, it would feel a little too empty, wouldn’t it?” Adranial sighed. “Imagine if it was five times the size, walking through echoing halls and bare rooms.” She shook her head. “No, I don’t think I could stand that. Especially when you’re away.”

Xavier grunted. “I suppose I hadn’t thought of it that way.”

“I don’t… have a lot of memories, from when I was growing up.”

“I could—”

She stopped him. “Nothing has changed. I don’t want to reintegrate them, Xavier. Not yet. Maybe…” She shook her head. “The memories I do have, I remember walking through a vast tower, larger than anything on this planet—larger than anything in this sector, I’d wager—and so many of those floors and rooms were empty. Generations and generations of my family, each with their own floor, but so many of them weren’t there. They’d gone off, ruling their own planets, or expanding in far-off sectors. I don’t have the warm memories from my childhood. I have those cold, empty ones.” She raised her chin, staring at what they’d built together. “I don’t want this place to be hollow like the one I grew up in was.”

Xavier felt the weight of her words. He had all his memories from when he grew up. His life was a very different one to hers. But even with all his memories intact, he couldn’t point to many good ones—except for the ones where he was lost in a book, or figuring something out he thought was cool.

By himself.

So unlike Adranial he didn’t much mind the idea of empty rooms and echoing halls, but he also didn’t need the place to be any bigger than it was. It had everything he needed—not just a library for all his books, but an office where he could write, something he’d had complete free rein over designing.

He just wished he could take full advantage of it.

“It’s time for you do go, isn’t it?” Adranial asked.

Xavier tilted his head to the side. “I think it’s a little past due. Not that I haven’t loved every moment of this.”

Adranial leant deeper into him. “Can I come with you?”

“I’m not even sure where I’m going yet.” He paused. “Wherever it is, it won’t be safe.”

“I can look after myself, you know.”

He smiled. “I know. But—”

“I understand.” She sighed. “You need to do this on your own. Classic Xavier, that is.”

“I need to push forward.”

“As far as the rest of the universe sees it, you only just defeated the World Destroyer.”

“That’s not the point.”

“I know, just… You’ll contact him if you need to, won’t you?”

Xavier frowned. “Xavier Prime?”

She nodded. “I know you want to do this alone, but you don’t have to do it alone. That other version of you can take care of himself too.”

Xavier conceded the point. “I’ll bring him along if I think it’s necessary. And I won’t hesitate to call on the Spirit of Time, either.”

“First… you should return to the Tower of Champions.”

From the universe’s time stream, I haven’t even been gone from the tower a full day…

Xavier frowned. “I can’t take Xavier Prime there. Not to our tower.”

“I know, but…” Adranial bit her lip. “There’s a particular floor—” She sighed. “The System is stopping me from telling you anymore.”

He glanced up at the sky. The rules of the tower had changed significantly because of a decision he’d made. Any Denizen could enter any of the one-thousand different floors, and they could initiate a break from the tower whenever they wished—as long as they’d cleared ten floors while they were there.

Any ten floors.

The rules around how long they could spend away from the tower had also changed. As there was so much flexibility inside the tower itself, the System had decided no Denizen—no matter how far along in the tower they were—could spend more than a year away from it.

But they could return whenever they wished.

It was something he hadn’t taken advantage of. With all that had been going on, returning to the tower hadn’t crossed his mind. What if Jhanku sent more agents after the people he cared about?

What if he sent them after Adranial?

The fix to that was simple enough, he supposed. If he was really worried about that happening, he could take her to the tower with him…

But he couldn’t ask everyone he cared about to return to the Tower of Champions.

If he was really going to go back there, to possibly leave the two sectors he ruled vulnerable… he needed to make sure they would be safe without him.

And how the hell do I manage something like that?

Xavier didn’t have an answer. Not yet. But, then again, maybe he didn’t need an answer—maybe the answer was already there, staring him in the face.

The Silver River and Ventorin sectors were united. In his absence, the Ventorin sector had been integrated to the same degree—meaning all of them had access to the two Defender spells Territory Defence and Unity Defence.

The spells he’d used to take advantage of that, to funnel all that energy into his arm and one-punch the World Destroyer, weren’t something just anyone in his rule could take advantage of, but that didn’t mean he couldn’t teach some of them to.

Standing outside their new home, Xavier tapped his foot in the snow, thinking things through.

The agents lured me out of my sectors for that trap. It’s true that they might make their way into my sectors—there might be infiltrators inside Silver River right now, even if they can’t make it to Earth—but my domain is where my people are most powerful and protected. They all have abilities these agents aren’t able to access…

Still, none of his citizens were a match for an A Grade.

But with their powers combined, they most certainly were. At least, they’d be a match for an A Grade like Arrack.

He just needed to make sure every citizen in Silver River and Ventorin were aware of the threat.

And he needed to make sure that if he was gone, the people closest to him would have the power to protect his sectors. Because even with a Communication Stone that worked from any universe, he wasn’t sure the System would allow him to use it on a tower floor.

He’d be completely incommunicado.

“All right,” Xavier said. “I’ll return to the Tower of Champions.” He raised a finger. “But there’s something I need to do first.”
