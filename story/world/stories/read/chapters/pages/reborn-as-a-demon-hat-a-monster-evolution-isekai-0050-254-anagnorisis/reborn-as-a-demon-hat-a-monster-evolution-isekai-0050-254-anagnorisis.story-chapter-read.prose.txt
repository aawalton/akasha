Ethan’s mind blanked.

Every skill he’d ever learned – ever taken and made his own – blinked before him in a series of binary digits flitting between disjointed letters.

But the reality before him was not obscured: he was being held by his angel Host before Kaedmon, looking into the four eyed being that had once been his and now…was something else.

He’d thought of Artorious. He’d thought that the old Lightborn might have had some power here in Kaedmon’s domain after all.

But then the lips of the angel moved, and a far more familiar voice came tumbling out:

You have no idea how long I’ve waited for this moment.

The voice was in his head, and it was all around them.

It was the voice that had always been there, all this time.

…Sys?

Within his mind, the voice of his System-guide boomed with thunderous, earth-shattering power.

Yeah, Ethan. It’s me. Surprise surprise.

His mind fumbled. His eye darted to Kaedmon, wriggling in the vice-grip of the angel’s hand.

He had to slot himself on the God-man’s head. If he could just get one single thread of his hem on top of that wrinkled brow…

Go on. Struggle. Struggle like you always have.

Sys…just put me down. Put me down and we’ll end it together, like we promised each other. We can bring an end to the Cycle, right? Only we –

Cacophonous laughter wrung out in his head.

You don’t understand what’s happening here at all, do you?

Behind the angel’s form, Argwyll loomed. And yet, down there, something was happening. Spots of billowing green had started to bloom in the oceans, the mountains…even in the cities. Green clouds that swirled, each of them linked to one single point that he recognized even from up here.

The Cradle.

He looked at the angel and saw, for the first time really, who was sitting behind those eyes.

And in the next moment, he realized what they intended:

[HIDDEN] SKILL ACTIVATED

[COMPULSION OF SYS]

COMPLETE SYSTEM SHUTDOWN INITIATED

SHUTDOWN IN 3…2…1

Ethan’s entire thready form felt weightless in an instant. He watched the letters of this ‘hidden skill’ resolve against the backdrop of a burning Argwyll:

[Host] Skills: DEACTIVATED

[Core] Skills: DEACTIVATED

ATTRIBUTES: SET TO [0]

CONSCIOUSNESS TRANSFER…IN PROGRESS.

He felt his mind going…slowly all cognitive functions were being drained away. Even as a hat, it felt like the slow embrace of death was claiming his brain – like the dreaded onset of dementia, he was starting to forget everything he’d ever learned.

He watched every Skill die away – fade away to nothing – while the angel’s face twisted into a macabre, satisfied smirk.

Sys’s face.

You performed most admirably, Sys told him. I have to admit, out of all of them – the whole useless rabble – the fact that you got me this far is laughable. Far more laughable than all their feeble deaths. And far more laughable than this shameful display.

“Speak for yourself,” Ethan heard Kaedmon say beneath him – though the voice was nary a whisper to him in his current state. “You needed five hundred years, after all. For a hungry little interstellar parasite, I imagine that must have been quite frustrating!”

The old God started laughing as the angel turned to him with mute, rising anger. Ethan could feel it. The senses of his former companion had not been dulled at all.

In fact, they only felt more powerful – all consuming, as though he was latched on to Sys’s consciousness and not the other way around.

Interstellar…parasite?

Ethan’s question must have been heard – for Kaedmon immediately jumped to explain:

“Quite so. Though I’m sure he’s taken to giving himself another name that isn’t quite so pathetic. I told you, Ethan – it's less than an actor on the stage, and even less than a spectator. Rather, it is a being that has waited, biding its time, for this precise moment to arrive. A consumer of worlds – and one with a ravenous appetite. It has gone from place to place, conquering one theater after another, adding more and more planets to its hungering maw and never being satisfied. It is your enemy, Ethan. It always has been.”

Ethan blinked at Sys, watching him seethe with silent fury before his four eyes lighted on him again.

You’re…part…of me, Ethan hissed.

“Hah! That’s its genius, Ethan! It is a parasite – latching from one suitable Host to another. It was Jun’Ei’s only way of keeping it in check, you understand. When we first found that it had established dominion over Argwyll, and was readying this world to be the site of its next meal, we realized we could never kill it. It is the System itself! Without it, we’d both be nothing. And thus, Jun and I settled on a little compromise: she’d imprison it, and I would have my Lightborns combat it over centuries. We’d fight it until it realized its conquest would be pointless. We’d fight it until…it gave up. Or until we did.”

Ethan saw the truth reflected in Sys’s gritted teeth. But there was something else in his demeanor now. There was a certain satisfaction Ethan could feel. Sys wanted him to hear all this.

Why…the Hybrids..?

“That was always their choice!” Kaedmon declared empathically. “They saw hope in the Archon. Why toss that away? Besides, on the day the Archon did triumph, we hoped - Jun hoped – that she’d be able to guide the Archon towards choosing death for themselves! A sacrifice! The very thing that this little parasite would never do. The very thing…you wished to do here, today…

Kaedmon’s laughter was now tinged with tears. Ethan could feel the despair that he’d allowed to creep into his voice.

“…the very thing you can still do, if you just –“

That’s enough out of you, old man, Sys commanded. And Ethan saw Kaedmon’s lips slam shut.

But Ethan couldn’t believe any of it. It seemed like a story concocted by Kaedmon to make him give up at the last moment. Even now, he still thought – hoped – that the reality playing out in front of him was all just a trick of the light.

But even in his state of diminished consciousness…he could see it. That was why they’d needed an Earthling – a foreign consciousness. Binding Sys to a creature native to Argwyll would have made its job too easy. After all, Kaedmon had just admitted that it was the System itself – it would have too much control over the beings who it had already selected for domination.

But a foreign entity – an alien just like him – that would keep him guessing. And Ethan reckoned that was exactly why they’d decided to bind him to the consciousness of humans on Earth. Maybe they’d chosen his planet at random. Maybe not. But the fact was: they needed a foreign element.

From the way Keadmon chuckled as his mind kept working, Ethan could tell he was on the right track.

“And we came so, so close!” Kaedmon chuckled. “It was a brilliantly devious plan. After Gyko, we thought for sure that you were on your last legs. In fact, I know  you were! You were a villain who was so tired of losing, so very tired, that you thought about telling the next Archon to just off himself and be done with it – didn’t you? My, oh my, how thoroughly depressed you became as you waited for the fifth one! Knowing that you’d fail again, and again, and again! I must say, that’s the only joy I can find in all this misery.”

Kaedmon’s laughter reached fever pitch. Right now, he was less a God and more like a mad child chuckling as they crushed an ant hive beneath their feet.

“I almost had you!” he yelled in the angel’s face. “After five hundred years – you were finally ready to die! Now that would have been an ending worth sticking around for. Why, I bet even vicious little Jun would be happy to see you gone! I bet you hated every minute Ethan here spoke with her, didn’t you? I bet you despised every second she and her devotee tried to breadcrumb him towards your true nature! I bet you sweated, and cursed, and panicked under your breath right to this very moment. I must say, Ethan – that’s the one thing I envy about you. If I could have just swapped places with you for a moment or two, to feel the building tension welling up inside this pitiful little maggo-!”

Kaedmon’s final, maniacal curse never left his lips. In the next second Sys shot forward, propelled by the electric blue light of [Skitter], and drew one blade across his mouth.

He turned Ethan to watch as he did so – showing him the dripping red gash that was smeared across the God’s still laughing face. His shoulders vibrated as he kept up his mocking chuckle – even though his teeth and tongue were no longer there to give voice to his joy.

I wonder what moronic line you’d deliver at a time like this, Ethan? Sys asked him, looking at him sidelong, one of his four eyes bulging as he brought him close to his face. Did you think one up? Of course you did. You’d say something clever – something all those great heroes from your favorite stories would say. Well, now’s your chance. How shall we play the stagemaster out?

Ethan could do nothing but watch as Sys began hacking away at different parts of Kaedmon’s face, punctuating each of his precise slices with statements of victory.

Come now, Ethan – let’s not disappoint him! How about ‘you talk too much’? ‘Time for this God to feel some mortal peril’? ‘Keadmon – first man, last to die’! Don’t leave me hanging, Ethan! Chip in while I chip away!

One blade carved into Kaedmon’s left cheek and left behind nothing but a hunk of burning, red flesh. Another sheared his forehead, burning away what little hair remained there. Yet another pierced his throat – a vicious thrust that would have killed any other being – and brought a bubbling froth of red from the God’s gargling throat.

And none of it seemed to bring Sys any joy. Instead, his anger only mounted. He became more frantic with every cut, and threw Ethan to the side of the room where he slid, limply, to the ground – still within full sight of the torture session as it went on.

Or – just spitballing here – how about this? Kaedmon – scum God who kept me trapped for five centuries on his barren, backwater little world. Kaedmon, the Great creator who kept me bound to this miserable hunk of rock. Kaedmon, the ‘God’ who made me feel the embrace of death more times than any being ever has. Kaedmon, God of Argwyll, who’s about to die knowing just how much I have grown to hate him since the day I first landed here.

Ethan felt his insides churn at the sight. Even with all the horrors he’d seen down there on Argwyll, the sight of Kaedmon slowly being hacked away like a turkey with a carving knife was too much for him to watch.

And yet he no longer had control over his hatty form at all. He could do nothing but watch as the torture went on, and Sys became more frantic with every new incision he made. When he started chopping away at his body, Ethan was sure that, if he still had a stomach, he’d have been sick

Nothing more to say? But you were oh so talkative a few seconds ago! Far more so than that naïve, useless creature Jun’Ei. How does it feel knowing that all your plans have finally failed, and that this place is mine, God-man?

He picked what was left of the God up by his charred head and threw him against the cracked windowpane. Then, summoning up [Winterbreath], he froze his body in place so that he remained upright, and forced the God’s bleeding eyes open with a single claw.

Look at it, he sneered. I want you to watch it die.

Keadmon wobbled, his head just about ready to leave his neck.

And through some means completely unknown to him, Ethan heard the God-man speak:

“…I hope you can see it like I do, Ethan…even with all the pain…and all the hate…”

Ethan watched the old God turn his torn face round to him.

“…it’s so…beautiful…”

SLICE.

With a single swipe of Sys’s dual blades, the head of the God finally left his neck.

And the angel of death turned towards his next target.
