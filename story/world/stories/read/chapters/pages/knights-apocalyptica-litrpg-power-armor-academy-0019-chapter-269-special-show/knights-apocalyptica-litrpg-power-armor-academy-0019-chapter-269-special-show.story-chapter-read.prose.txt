Garin fiddled with his gun while Munchy nibbled on a roasted carrot on his knee. The smell of the searing meat made Erec’s stomach clench in hunger. Which was odd, since he’d thought the swelling anxiety meant he wasn’t eating at all tonight; he shuffled in the back seat of Enide’s car, getting a look at the sizzling roast. Fido howled as the pup stared at the meat, the stars above glittering on the glossy, fat-coated surface.

For the first time since the day began, Erec was, temporarily, thinking about something other than his friend’s dumb decision.

Garin coughed.

Erec winced, and his thoughts went from hunger back to the situation at hand.

“We’re three hundred miles out now,” Enide said, breaking the silence. "I was tracking the odometer. So hey, officially getting into the ‘pretty far’ territory.”

“When is far enough?” Garin asked.

Never would be best. Erec shivered and then sat up; it was time for the conversation then. His eyes lingered on his best friend, who spun the meat. For a second, Erec paused. If he’d been forced back into the Academy, watching Enide, Garin, and Colin head on an expedition, what would he have done? Could he really say with a straight face that he’d have accepted that answer from Boldwick? That he wouldn’t have been thrown into a rage and done something dumb?

No. He’d also have been reckless.

Garin stopped spinning the meat and looked at him, hopeful that Erec had all the answers.

I don’t. Erec cleared his throat. "It's a gamble whenever you do it; the longer the stretch of road we get, the less likely they’re going to be willing to send you back. Really, the more we get out, the better your chances of not being sent back and having to choose to abandon the Knighthood.” That is IF the Order doesn’t dismiss you as an initiate for this.

“I keep… It’s hard to keep the animals quiet in the trunk. What if I get caught?”

“Then it looks bad, but we’d be further out,” Erec admitted.

“Then Boldwick will kill me.”

“Listen, this already all looks bad to Boldwick. At this point, I’m not sure you will make it better. I’d love to say you should go talk to him today and clear the air, but if we’re being realistic, none of this is going to end well. So, we have to make the best of our bad situation.” Erec flopped away from looking at his friend, fully back onto the car seat, and covered his eyes with an arm.

Despite the wonderful smell, his stomach was bubbling again with anxiety.

Tomorrow, he’d be assigned a night patrol; that left Garin and Enide out here alone; he just thanked the Goddess that most of the Knights knew about his and Enide’s relationship, and were permitting them this little bit of privacy to make camp. Right now, then, he wanted nothing more than to look at Garin.

He kept turning over the problem in his head. Weighing if they should just go to Boldwick and rip the bandage off, trying to picture how that conversation would go, and if there was anything he could say to save his friend from the wrath of a Master Knight. So far, a day into that horrible thought exercise, he’d yet to come up with any solution other than delay further. But that wasn’t a plan that would work out.

It was made even worse by the fact that on this expedition, Boldwick had been so damn busy. Adding even another complication to an already complex operation was going to really piss him off.

“I’m going to tell him,” Garin said, and Erec went quiet. There it was. He sat up—looking at his friend, seeing if he was serious.

Fido let off a soft whine.

Poor guy is starving.

“What the fuck is that?” Enide said.

There was a bang.

Erec sat up instantly; his axe found his hand. Electricity rang through his veins; it wasn’t just the bang that jolted him. No. The earth itself churned; the sky burned with power—his eyes jumped, torn to the place where he felt the Round Table connecting with this realm. All of the hairs on the back of his neck stood, and before he realized it, he was grinning.

Kay, Bedwyr—

He squinted.

In the distance, he saw a moving stone giant, which took a column of electricity to the face. The two of them were fighting one another; they’d issued a challenge. And both, as a result, had embraced their mantles.

About time.

“Get in the trunk, Garin. Enide, we’re driving over there now.” Erec moved quickly, slicing into the roasting meat with his axe, then tossing a chunk of it into Enide’s trunk. Fido leaped in, as did Munchy, the two trying to brawl over the meat. With that, the animals were sorted. Garin was still asking too many questions, but Erec ignored it, grabbing his friend by the shoulder, then shoving him away.

Enide gave a quick, “Hell yeah,”, hopped in the car, then revved the engine.

In under fifteen seconds, her car was rushing across the wasteland, shuddering violently.

“Can you go faster?” he asked.

"Damn, hero, you're excited," she said, but did as he asked, slamming her foot down on the pedal. The violent shaking turned into the whole frame struggling to keep together as she pushed her car to the brink.

All the while, Erec watched the approaching fight; eyes glued to the giant and bolt of electricity going at it. How no one else felt it, he’d never understand. The connection to the Round Table was widening; the fight and the glory it brought were an almost tangible thing. Too long had it been since he’d seen Bedwyr and Kay go at it, and he’d be damned if he missed it now.

Tonight, it turned out, was going to have a very special show.

Colin hovered in front of the stone colossus as a shard of lightning crashed down from above, and with the gentle guiding of the many glyphs he'd wrapped around his hand, flooded through his arm, then pulsed into a ball in his hand.

He raised the ball, and it pulsed out in a beam.

CRACK.

It tore across the side of the rocks, vaporizing some of them and exploding others as the water inside the stone evaporated and released with pressure, cascading against the shell as huge portions tumbled off the stone giant to crash into the ground below.

A spear flew at him; far too slow.

Colin lurched his body and twisted in the air, catching the second trick that Bedwyr was trying to pull. Magic on the ground being cast simultaneously to the spear thrust, a counter-offensive that would come up and bite him in the ass if he didn’t notice.

Clever.

Colin stretched out his hand, using his own earth glyph to disrupt the brewing spell below, and wrested away the mana from the area before Bedwyr could finish and send a massive stone pillar at him.

A spike erupted from the ground and splintered as the land broke apart into two separate directions, torn between two spells.

Same tricks as you always do. Hide in your shell, play games with magic. They don’t work.

“Do better, Bedwyr,” Colin shouted, using magic to amplify his voice.

Then he paused.

Always do?

Since when had he fought this thing before? He’d seen it only once in the Cavern Seven fight, but he’d never fought this. Yet there it was. Memories of facing off like this… how?

Colin squinted, hesitating, the electricity that was flowing through him like a river, channeled from all the air around him, came to a brief halt. It was as if the act of scrutinizing it had stopped it in its tracks. His eyes widened; his control over it had increased. It was damn near natural. But the shock of full control broke him, for just a brief second.

Bedwyr followed through by spinning his spear around, the speed suddenly violently increasing as gravity glyphs activated. The massive weapon moved far quicker than any car and crashed right into his side. Taking advantage of that gap in disorientation.

Colin flipped head over heels, spells gone as his concentration was torn away to deal with the pain. His Armor jolted as it crashed into the ground, digging him into a small crater in the wasteland. Energy crackled from him, more spells loosening. So many. Far more than he could normally handle, in fact, his fractured mind didn’t even recall knowing half those spells he’d used.

His vision doubled.

In one of those sights, he saw the approaching point of a pillar of stone, stabbing down with a force that would shatter a mountain, held by a Knight he knew very well. Erec's brother. Bedwyr.

In the other vision, he saw a silver chair beneath him. A round table in front of him. Two different realities, and oddly, both as real as one another. The contrast, the sensation of being in both places at once, made his spine curl; this was madness.

Was Bedwyr intending to kill him? How could he handle an attack like that—

Why are we in denial?

Voltage snapped through him, every muscle brought to a new tension. The synapses in his brain were firing with a burst of electricity that defied the biological nature of his brain. With the increased electricity, his thoughts sped, his soul reached out, welling with it, crackling thunder into his body, forcing it to work, making him adapt. He could handle an attack like this because he was not merely a Knight Initiate who’d lucked into being a Knight Errant.

He was a Knight of the Round Table. He saw the table; felt its weight. It was power, wasn’t it?

Colin vanished with a spark; Bedwyr’s massive stone pillar collided into where he’d been a split second later. The land cratered, kicking under his feet as his body sped away. A wave of dust shot up from the impact, a BANG sounding out miles away, but Colin had evaded it. And even better, Colin was preparing for his counterstrike, his muscles twitching faster than possible, using small microglyphs to send pulses through his muscles to force the reaction speed. Three spells came at once, two of which were lightning swords conjured to his hand.

It was a spell he’d created, and one which should have taken seconds, yet here he’d brought them to bear in a heartbeat.

The third spell broke his momentum with another massive charge of electricity and shot him like a bullet right back at the stone giant. His eyes closed as he rushed through the wave of debris, his armor taking on several dents as he broke through, then ran up the weapon; one blade sank below, its tip tearing into the stone spear as he ran up its vertical wall, rushing several stories high as his spell deconstructed the weapon beneath him.

Bedwyr wasn’t passive. Magic channeled through the weapon, forcing Colin to navigate a maze of sharp, jutting stone that tried to spear through him.

The air burned with ozone as he moved, his Armor taking several scrapes. Fresh blood spilled down his side as one of the rocks broke through the metal and stabbed him.

But he cleared the weapon; in return for the new tear in his side, he'd reached the giant's face.

His swords converged above his head; a storm cloud crackled in the heavens above. Glyphs swam around him in ribbons, their golden light crackling with electricity, the spells he’d layered on his weapons maturing to completion. When did I…? No, it’s there. I did do that.

It was… it was like there were several of him. Working in conjunction, controlling different spells in order to manage the mental workload and fatigue. Lightning coursed through his mind, letting it split and operate with independent goals. But more, those parts of him had more; they had a history that he could barely grasp. Just the thought of how he’d gotten here gave him a split headache; it was a second, allowing his weapons to reach their apex, but in that second, he’d had to pause. To finally think. And it wasn’t making sense.

The swords came together and then slammed into the head of the stone colossus, driven like a nail into a board of Bedwyr’s precious defense.

Colin was filled with an overwhelming sense of deja vu, swearing he’d done this same thing before; he remembered sitting at a round table he’d never been at, boasting to the other Knights about it.

Just as it had then, it did now. A shockwave split the air. Another boom, which split the earth itself, sounded from the heavens above as a column of lightning crashed into where he’d planted the sword, snapping into the rocky defenses of the Knight, vaporizing stone while sending chunks the size of people flying into the surrounding landscape.

There were few barriers strong enough to withstand that attack; it was far better to try their hand at evading, which is what made it so particularly effective against a big, bulky target.

Colin launched himself off with another burst of lightning to not get caught up in the residual effects; the river of electricity in him came quicker now. The memories… so many memories of places he’d never been, fights he’d never had. Books he’d never read; he swore he could feel vellum pages beneath his fingertips, stained with ink as he stared out a tower window while breathing in the dusty scent of old secrets.

Colin gripped his head as he fell several stories; his spells coming undone, the confusion too much to bear.

The giant shook. As he fell, he saw mounds of rock coming from the earth below, flowing up the massive legs like water; its head was already missing, and the column of lightning was tearing into its chest, headed right for where Bedwyr liked to hide himself in the middle of it. The stone reached the battle in the shoulders, trying to stop the lightning from drilling down.

But it was useless.

They’d done this before. Bedwyr should know better.

Kay smiled.

“Bedwyr, you never did best me; today will be no different. The glory is mine as always,” Kay said; no…Colin…

The chest of the behemoth stone golem exploded. Colin’s eyes widened even as the ground approached rapidly. What did I do? The implications of that explosion rocked him, fighting past the strange torrent of memories that didn’t belong to him, and thoughts crowding in from… whatever Kay was. What had he just done?

This was… It was a spar. Sir Able and Sir Helvek had told them to go at it, to really let go.

That was one thing. This was another—he’d just unleashed an attack that you would only use on a monster who’d broken through the Kingdom walls. On a beast that he was the sole protector, stopping it from consuming thousands. Very much not the type of attack a Knight launched against a fellow Knight in the middle of a field expedition. By the Goddess, he’d probably just killed Bedwyr.

As it was…

Another boom as more lightning surged from above, the final blast, shattering the core of the giant stone Knight. The arms crashed into the ground. The legs tumbled. Cracks ran on every available surface as the last of the lightning discharged. Colin landed nearby, tossing himself away from the exploding giant so as not to be buried by falling stone.

He’d just murdered Erec’s brother.

Horror swelled in his chest, but the deeper part of him, that current of electricity, rebuked the feeling of killing another.

It spoke.

No. What a foolish assumption.

“No?” Colin said, the thought had entered his head unprompted, like all of the visions that were spilling in. On one hand, the panic rising in his chest, the shame of what he’d done was overpowering. On the other… Why had another sword made of lightning appeared in his hand again?

The ground burst. A hand made of steel grabbed him by the leg and cracked him into the ground. Several smaller spikes of metal stabbed into his armor, drawing blood. Bedwyr rose from the wasteland, sand streaming from his face, a mad grin on his lips, and when he spoke, his voice held the weight of worlds.

“Sloppy, Kay. I seem to recall your endless mirth when that worked last time. It’s too prideful of you to assume I wouldn’t come up with a counter. Or were you hoping I was lazy?”

Kay.

There it was.

Kay.

The table was in front of him; the physical world around him vanished. He saw the same Bedwyr there, a Knight at the Round Table. Reclined in his chair. It was a simple thing, not elegant like his silver throne, but rather a relic of power made of stone, but Kay knew from experience that said throne had a weight to it that made it so not even Arthur himself could lift it. Only the densest of the earth belonged to that seat.

Bedwyr stared at him from across the table; one hand tapping the beautiful, round surface, his eyes judging him.

The memories.

He belonged here. This… this was him. His mantle. His place at the table—as did Bedwyr. His brother in arms. Lives flooded through him, a collection of lives that were too much, washing over him like a river, but not holding. The connection to the mantle wasn’t strong enough, yet. He tried to form several glyphs, tried to grasp the important magics and history that came in that torrent of memories, tried to steal from himself the things that would escalate his own power. Like he always did. But at this point, he knew it was futile. Still, one must always try.

Bedwyr snorted.

“Ever the same, Kay.”

“As are you,” Kay replied. Colin rubbed his head; the memories still split, still raw to handle as they collided.

“It must be difficult for you when you reconcile your past lives with your current. It’s your first time seeing the table so clearly this time, right?” Bedwyr asked, the room around them flickering. The moment of fighting paused outside of it. Frozen in a bubble of time as the Knights were pulled to the table, their heavy souls brought to this place.

The weight in the room settled on Kay. Its glory running through him, his pride flaring. But then, that wasn’t him. Not completely.

Damn it. Colin slammed a hand on the table. “I am not Kay.”

“You are. But you aren’t. As I am not Bedwyr, yet I am. As Erec is Erec, yet not, as Arthur is evermore, out of reach, yet not the same." Bedwyr tilted back his head and took in a deep breath. "I fear we come together once more, if so many of us awaken at once, so close, that there will be great trouble for us to handle."

More memories. Quests. Fights. Wars that had shattered planets; Colin shivered in his seat, trying to reconcile what was happening. What this even was. The more the memories came, the more comfort they brought. At first, he was afraid that it would steal who he was away. But the Table wasn’t like that. He would remain Colin, as he would be Kay when the time came.

The trembling of his hands stopped.

Bedwyr smiled. "Thank you for this fight. It will be truly a wonderful thing to best you in arms, Kay."

"Presumptuous," Colin said, the room shaking. Kay was his, an earned title. A pride to bear. One of his many. A pride that would not allow a Knight like Bedwyr to get one over on him.

His vision doubled.

“Arrogant as always.” The voice came from two places—the table… and…

The world snapped into focus again, stone spearing towards his face. Colin fired off several spells, lightning cracking off his skin and snapping into each of the stone spears. It would be the first real blood, the one that said, 'I could have killed you, but I held back.' A single one of those would guarantee his victory in the bout.

Bedwyr would not win. Golden electricity ran through him, the pride of his status as a Knight. The knowledge that he was better. Smarter—Colin didn’t understand everything that was happening, but the fight brought more of him out, let more of that live electric current dominate through him. He let himself embrace the torrent of history, pulling from it spells he’d never imagined, and control he couldn’t have done as he was before.

More stone spikes appeared, at all angles, and a spear tip made of steel tried to slip in. Colin repelled it with a field of magnetism using a clever little twist of electricity. He used that same trick to yank at Bedwyr’s arm, throwing the Knight off balance—he broke free of the grasp around his ankle, rising into the air, his sword held high over his head, like a god about to bestow a smiting upon a disbeliever.

Bedwyr grinned up at him, stone cobbling together on his body, threatening to once more become the unstoppable colossus of stone that could shatter Kingdoms, or in one case, ruin a world.

“It has been too long,” Colin said, embracing it. The shock of his power spilling through him. He’d never felt so alive, never felt so like him. All the years under his father, he was always questioning his own actions. Now? Against his fellow brother of the Round Table? He could let loose and let free what he was always meant to be.

The power collected, the stone on Bedwyr grew, and to the east, he sensed the approaching wave of fire that signified yet another of the Round Table. Erec would be joining them. Knowing the bloodlust of that particular member, perhaps the fight would escalate into a three-way battle. A wonderful reunion.

As he measured how far their brother was, he paused. Two Knights watched on the sidelines. From where the camp was, there came more Knights to witness their glory… Sir Helvek, Sir Able. In the flow of the fight itself, he’d scarcely paid attention to the two. But they were watching, half a step away from intervening. As if they could, between two Knights such as he and Bedwyr.

And…

What in the hell was that?

Shadows burst out of the ground; things the size of cars with chests the shape of boulders. They moved in a mass of shadow, insects. Colin gave a deep shudder, and Kay comforted him, a spark trying to ease the fear of the disgusting many limbs. Their maggot bodies squirming, accelerating as they crawled out from the ground.

Worse, they became a mass of black, ripping into each other as they tore across the waste, a pile of black, maggoty beasts, screaming to reach the Knights.

Colin’s fingers shook; though Kay had no fear of beasts such as these… well, he couldn’t get over the horrible wriggling mass. They needed to die. Now.

Colin discharged his lightning with a giant bolt, and Bedwyr paused, following his gaze.

“Ah. That’s rather foolish of them, is it not?” Bedwyr asked.

“Those disgusting beasts must be put down; we can resume our fight afterward.”

“You knew I was going to win,” Bedwyr said, spinning his spear; the rock around him breaking, revealing some odd complex of intricate metal bands which hovered, like armor, but not quite. That was new.

Colin frowned at that. Had Bedwyr been playing with new spells?

"Don't be full of yourself. We defeat them, then I will prove to you how wrong you were," Colin said. "Though, unless we intend to kill one another, I don't know if we should continue. Our bodies are not what we're used to. Better to spend ourselves on a foe worthy of death.”

"A fair point. Perhaps, instead, we change the game. Which of us, brother, can kill more, faster? That will be the true contest," Bedwyr proposed.

Colin hummed. “That suits me fine. Do not think you can best me; if this is how we shall determine the victor, then so be it.” The other Knights were moving, cars were approaching, but truly, Colin, Kay, or whatever amalgamation he’d become in the moment, didn’t care. Joy spread through him the way a charge went through a wire; here he was, fighting with his brothers in arms again. Even now, he was sure Erec would join them for the fight too… even if they were facing off against such horrible creatures.

Though he knew, now, he’d changed. That current of lightning, that centuries of history… a door had been opened, which never would be closed.
