Before the woman who had to be The Lost Lady could get any closer, Asher tried to slip into the astral to buy himself time to think. But when his skill failed to activate, he cursed, hopping back and attempting to slow down time with Comprehend’s second-tier ability. When that failed to work, he started to panic. It wasn’t until he raised the shovel he was still holding in his hands and prepared to fight that he realized The Lost Lady was no longer walking toward him.

She was walking toward a noticeably younger Ulma, who was frowning and standing next to a large, muscular man who shared some traits with the two children he’d seen earlier.

“Are you lost?” the younger version of Ulma asked. Her frown deepened as The Lost Lady ignored her and continued moving forward, her eyes glued directly onto who Asher could only assume was Grant’s face. Looking between Grant’s confused face and the admittedly attractive woman approaching them, Ulma clicked her tongue as she raised her shovel threateningly and stepped forward. “Lady, you can take your naked butt and—”

Asher blinked as he almost missed what happened. Even with Comprehend helping him keep track of her, The Lost Lady shot forward, moving so quickly she was little more than a blur of white as her long hair trailed along behind her. One second, she’d been almost two dozen feet away. The next, she was standing directly between the two farmers, her hand clasped tightly around Grant’s throat as she effortlessly lifted him into the air and the man began choking.

What is going on? Asher thought as he watched Ulma scream and drive her shovel straight through The Lost Lady’s back. Just as she’d told them, her shovel slid through the woman’s flesh without issue, but there wasn’t so much as a drop of blood. When it was pulled back out, the flesh sealed practically faster than the shovel could be withdrawn, leaving not even the faintest of scars. As Grant began driving his trowel into the lich’s chest with just as much effectiveness, Asher blinked as he looked down and realized he was still holding Ulma’s shovel in his hands.

“Some sort of vision of the past… tied to the shovel,” he muttered, figuring that much out easily enough as his voice warbled like it did in the astral realm. There wasn’t anything else it could be, seeing as events were playing out just as Ulma had told them. Excitement bubbled up inside his chest as a hunch formed around what was happening, but Asher put that on hold for the moment. If this truly was an accurate vision of the past, then he needed to absorb every detail he could while it lasted.

Ignoring their strikes, The Lost Lady turned and began walking back the direction she’d come, easily holding the large man up by the throat as though she weren’t lifting what had to be over two hundred pounds of pure muscle up by one arm. At this point, Grant’s face was turning purple, and his trowel had fallen from his hand as his fingers went limp. Blades of grass seemed to reach up from the ground and try to wrap around the lich’s feet as the man triggered some sort of skill, but the lich ignored it entirely, snapping them easily as she kept walking. She didn’t even hesitate until Ulma screamed and charged at her back, moving to drive the shovel through the back of her knee.

The attack came so quickly Asher barely even caught it.

Without the lich even turning around, a lengthy lock of white hair snapped out faster than any whip, cracking against Ulma’s head and sending the woman flying backwards. Landing in a heap in the dirt, blood began pooling out of her head as she laid there, somehow having managed to keep a hold onto her shovel the entire time. The lich continued walking away with Grant’s now lifeless body, never looking back or making so much as a sound as she walked off. Asher moved to follow her, before sensing a strange tugging sensation from the shovel in his hands. Somehow, he understood that the shovel couldn’t be moved farther than a certain distance from this past event, and he wasn’t able to let it go. Before he could think too much on that, the world twisted and rippled again, and he was back in the present.

Dropping the shovel at his feet, Asher staggered back, holding a hand against his head as he clenched his eyes shut. Despite the fact that he’d moved around while witnessing the minute-long attack of The Lost Lady on Ulma and her husband, he could somehow tell that in reality, he’d spent the entire vision simply standing there, unmoving.

“Asher?!” Samantha shouted, snapping him out of his daze. Only then did he realize her hands were locked on his shoulders, and that she was holding him steady as he wobbled. “What happened?! You just zoned out and wouldn’t respond to anything we said!”

“If my hunch is right…” he muttered, slowly opening his eyes and grinning as he spotted the first of his long-awaited notifications. “…I just got my first blended skill.”

Eternal Knowledge (active) - lvl 1 - Relive impactful events that are tied to objects of personal importance.

Eternal Knowledge - lvl 2

A quick glance at his interface was all he needed to confirm that Eternal Knowledge was the blended skill to come from his Knowledge and Eternity elements, as if it wasn’t obvious enough from the name.

“Are you serious?” Samantha gasped, her concern quickly shifting over to excitement to match his own. “That’s incredible! What does it do?”

“It’s a real trip. In more ways than one,” he chuckled. “I just saw the exact events Ulma was telling me about. How The Lost Lady attacked them and stole her husband away. I saw the whole thing as if I’d been there and personally witnessed it. I’ve never felt anything like it.”

“Now that sounds like a useful skill,” Rulfar said, scratching his stubble as he thought. “Especially for going up against a bunch of ancient liches who have lived realms know how long. Did you see anything that would help us? Any weaknesses we can exploit?”

“I think so. But before that…” Turning back to Ulma and the chief, Asher bowed his head in apology as he picked up the shovel. “Sorry about that. And for dropping the shovel. One of my skills just reacted in a way I wasn’t expecting, but I’m okay now. If it makes you feel any better, I think you may have just given us a giant hint toward how to best take down The Lost Lady.”

“That does make me feel a bit better,” Ulma grunted, taking her shovel back from him. “As much as I’d love to be the one to put that spirit down, I guess I won’t mind if you five manage to do it for me.”

Thanking her one more time for her help, and the chief for introducing them, Asher took his team and led them off to the side of the village. Once they were alone, he threw up a Spatial Lock to be safe as he shared with them what he saw. Just as he’d hoped, he was able to share his memories of living through the past event with his teammates using Incorporate’s second-tier effect, which was a fantastic synergy between his skills.

“She definitely has a Flesh element, Zygrul was right about that,” Rulfar muttered. “That’s probably how she maintains her body so that she doesn't look like a walking corpse. But what was with the hair?”

“Hair element. I’ve seen it once before,” Moxy grunted. “An elite who used to work for Loratta had the element. Went by the name Hairslay. As terrible as the name was, she was no joke. Still couldn’t hold a candle to me in a fight, but she was one of the better ones, no question there. And I don’t think she trained a day in her life.”

“She moves almost as fast as Smiles did,” Samantha pointed out, looking more than a little concerned. “And she doesn’t rely on the realm of ages to do it like the greater demons do. How are we supposed to keep up with that kind of speed?”

“I’m not sure, but we do have one ace in the hole,” Asher said, grinning as he held up a dagger. “You all saw how she fights. She takes the attacks head on, ignoring them and letting her Flesh element heal herself up. If we’re lucky and she isn’t aware of Sever the Thread just yet, I should be able to kill her in a single blow.”

“Zygrul did mention that she was isolated compared to the other liches, so there’s a good chance she won’t know,” Rulfar added. “However, if you don’t finish her in that single strike, we might be in trouble. You told us before that people have been able to get around your skill by cutting out their wounded body parts and replacing them with new ones. I’m willing to bet she can do that in an instant with her Flesh element.”

“Not to mention there are her minions to worry about,” Moxy pointed out. “She didn’t just drag this Grant guy back to her caves for no reason. If you reveal your skill taking them down, that might tip our hand as well.”

“All good points,” Asher admitted. “But what matters is the unknowns we’re going up against are getting smaller and smaller. We know she has the Flesh and Hair elements, so that just leaves one wildcard, as well as whatever spells she might throw at us. But we’ve got our own wizard for that!”

“Hedge wizard,” Kroult said quietly, shaking his head as he pulled his hood back to look at them. “Again, I remind you that I will be no help in actually putting any of these liches down. Though I will provide support where I can.”

“Fantastic! We’ll be able to destroy any and all hedges that stand in our way!” Moxy laughed, clapping the frail lich on the back and nearly sending him flying forward before Rulfar caught him. “I think that’s enough talking and dreaming about shovels. Where’s this cave entrance? I’m ready to kill a lich!”

“Just make sure Asher is the one who does the actual killing,” Rulfar reminded her as he brushed off Kroult’s shoulders and made sure he was alright. “We need Sever the Thread to make the final blow, or this whole thing is pointless.”

“Yeah, yeah, come on,” Moxy said, spotting the caves off in the distance and taking off in a jog toward them. Seeing no point in delaying any longer, the rest of the team raced after her. It wasn’t long before the five of them stood at the yawning mouth of a dark cave, and Asher handed out potions of Darkvision to Moxy, Samantha, and even Rulfar. While the bloodhound had a skill to allow him to see with almost no light, if the caves were as deep as they suspected, there wouldn’t be any light soon enough. After switching his Eternal Mark from the room the package had been placed in to the entrance to the caves, Asher nodded.

“Remember, safety over speed. The last thing we need is for one of us to get lost or snatched up by one of The Lost Lady’s minions while we’re exploring the cave system. And you all know what that means.”

“Only so long as we’re in there,” Moxy grumbled, even as she held out a hand. “I hate the idea of you knowing where I am whenever you want.”

“I’ll take them off the moment we’re done,” Asher promised, going around and placing Eternal Marks on Rulfar, Moxy, and Kroult. He already had one on Samantha, but now he’d be able to keep tabs on the full team as well. It was unfortunate that he had to remove so many of his old marks in order to do this, but there was no avoiding it. Ensuring the safety of their whole team was far more important than a few hours of Samantha flying him back to a city he used to have a mark in to replace it.

Now, beyond the four marks on his teammates and the one at the cave entrance, he only had marks on Brian, their room in the royal palace, Scritchems, Rosh, and Helpha’s store in Dormaul. It was wild to him that Eternal Mark was up to ten total marks compared to the one he’d originally started with. Ten sounded like so many, and yet here he was, wishing he had three times that number.

Once his team was marked, Rulfar took the lead, the bloodhound unsheathing his axe and taking a deep breath of air in through his nose before letting it out slowly.

“Alright,” he said, motioning for them to follow him as they descended into the massive cave system nobody had ever returned from. “Let’s find us a lich.”
