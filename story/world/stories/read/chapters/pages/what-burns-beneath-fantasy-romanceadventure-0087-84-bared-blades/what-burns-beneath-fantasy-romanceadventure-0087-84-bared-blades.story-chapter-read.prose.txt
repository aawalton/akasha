A wave of dread carried me to the Watch barracks. Hands wringing, eyes darting, heart pounding.

There were people being tortured here. Tortured.

My gorge rose at the thought. My mind spun.

Would such cruelty save lives? Would it glean information? Could it? If their pacts made it physically impossible to speak of things forbidden, surely torment would yield nothing but more senseless bloodshed.

I entered the building behind my companions, feeling thoroughly sick.

There were no immediate horrors. All that lay within the main room was a single parchment-stacked desk, weapon racks, armor stands, grinding stones, a stairway leading up, and a long hallway leading off with dozens of closed doors on either side. It seemed that this barracks was mostly a space for the Sedgewick Vale Watch to rest and recoup between shifts.

We passed through the empty space quickly. Once near the back door, I heard a muffled thump from down the hallway. My feet froze. I waited for whatever it was to get louder, harsher, for it to crescendo into a cry of pain-

And then strong hands closed over my shoulders. Renner pushed me out the door. The look on his face was grim, unapologetic, and told me in no uncertain terms that I was not walking back inside.

I turned away, pulling my cloak tight. Chilly afternoon air nipped my nose.

The training ground before us was a flat, wide circle of tamped brown dirt surrounded by a short, thick fence. Several brightly painted archery targets and stuffed training dummies were positioned around the ring. Weapon racks with wooden swords, spears, axes, hammers, bows, and shields of all shapes and sizes sat propped along the fence.

Renner and Jareth didn’t use the training weapons. The Thalessian bared his long, two-handed witchwood sword and Renner drew his shorter broadsword and black-hilted dagger. Jareth wore a green linen tunic over thick trousers, and all Renner had for protection was his dark jerkin.

This made me nervous at first, then downright terrified when their swordplay began. Teela and I spent the first stretch of their sparring match squeaking and squealing in fright, which earned several eye-rolls and chuckles from each combatant.

They never hit each other. Whenever a blade sliced close the defendant would parry, kick, or grab his opponent and the two would wrestle and wrench themselves away.

It was clear they’d done it all before. They could’ve announced that this was the thousandth such sparring match and I wouldn’t have been surprised in the least. They knew each other. Jareth predicted every quick, lethal move from the smaller man and Renner danced with practiced ease from the larger’s infrequent, heavy onslaughts. For what seemed like ages, metal rang in a teeth-grinding whine but neither man managed to land a hit.

I wonder what it would be like watching them fight. Not as opponents, but actually working together. I bet it would be amazing.

And terrifying.

“You ready?” Jareth was red-faced and panting on one end of the arena, yet somehow the very picture of smug. One side of his mouth curled up.

Renner prowled some distance away. The tip of his longer sword hissed a trail through the dirt. “For?”

“For me to win.” The smile widened.

He snorted. “I’ve been running circles around you. Like usual.”

“Well, I didn’t want to embarrass you too badly in front of the ladies.”

“Uh-huh. Shame you don’t have that shiny breastplate on. I wanted to leave the first dent.”

Jareth laughed. Then, with a wink towards me and Teela and a heavy breath, he… changed.

It was a subtle thing. A little bulge to the muscles of his neck, a blush of white on his lower lip as his teeth bit down. And then, so fast and so strange I surely must have imagined it… a shimmer of pale light in those striking blue eyes.

He crossed the arena in a heartbeat. Jareth didn’t so much lunge at Renner as he… appeared before him. All that was left in the space he’d abandoned was a spray of dirt. Renner shouted an oath as the witchwood sword flashed in, much too fast to be dodged but positioned to be parried.

My ears rang with the impact, but it wasn’t the sharp ting I’d typically heard from clashing blades. No, there was hideous force behind this blow, like a hammer striking a nail, and Renner’s sword went flying. Copper eyes widened and he pivoted away, diving for the blade, but Jareth was already there with another spray of dirt and a wicked smile. Metal rang as he batted the stray sword straight from the air, spiking it to the ground.

Renner spun to lash low with the dagger. Jareth slipped behind him with all the casual grace of a shadow. He caught the other man by the back of his leather collar and sent him careening off-balance. Then he was just there again, covering the distance with inhuman speed. A bone-cracking thud resounded as his pommel slammed into the smaller man’s chest.

Renner dropped flat on his back in a cloud of dust. The witchwood tip flicked a divot into his dark jerkin.

“Told you.” Jareth winked down at his stunned opponent.

Renner groaned.

“Okay. Saints and… salt and… that was… incredible,” Teela breathed, elbowing me. “Suddenly I’m way less worried about a bunch of Fae showing up tomorrow. Salt, we can probably go have drinks while the Order handles everything!”

I silently mirrored the sentiment. Then, quite abruptly, Jareth turned. Both nostrils flared and his chin rose, reminding me of a hound catching wind of an uncanny scent.

He looked at Teela. Very strangely, as though he’d only just realized she was there, and this obvious surprise was followed by a flash of something that made my breath catch.

I’d never seen such naked, shameless desire before. I was assuredly not the recipient of that look, but even witnessing it flooded my veins with heat.

Just as suddenly as it appeared, the expression vanished. He blinked, face smoothing, and rubbed one thumb along his lower lip. “Uh. Heh. Got my blood pumping pretty good.” He stooped to haul Renner upright. “Sorry. Probably could’ve pulled that a bit more.”

“You think?” the smaller man gagged. One hand rubbed his heaving chest and he jerked away from the attempted aid. “Salt, they blessed you? You’re a paladin? When? Why?! You’re too young! You can’t-”

“Told you, brother. Times are changing.” Jareth held the gilded sword high. “And about… three weeks ago? We had wraiths at Woodwine, an actual vampire holed up near the White Glade, some ice witch terrorizing the outskirts of Tithe, and messengers from Lighthold said something big was in the woods. All at the same time. Can you believe that? Order got stretched thin, didn’t have anyone blessed to lead the charge in Woodwine, so… well, I volunteered. Heh. A lot. Emphatically.”

My head spun. All of that? All of that, recently, all across the realms? We thought this area was bad, but… ashes, Fae haven’t just been coming through here. It really is everywhere.

Veldian’s solemn warning crawled through the back of my mind. ‘History is about to repeat itself…’

“And they let you?!” Renner‘s voice was hoarse with fury. “Who?! Where was Joselia?! Where was anyone with a shred of sense?!”

The larger man drew back. His easy expression was quickly becoming a grimace. “She was out helping. Unlike some people. Salt, Renner, I thought you’d be happy for me. I’m the youngest paladin in decades. I’m a… what?” Jareth inhaled, voice a curious mix of polite and puzzled. He turned and looked directly at me.

I blinked at him. “What?”

“You said… something?”

“Me? No.”

“You did.” He rubbed one hand along his clenched jaw. “I heard you. You said…” he swallowed. Then, gaze flicking to meet mine, gave his head a little shake. “Perhaps I… my apologies, lady Brin.”

“Are you alright?”

He rumbled a little laugh. Sweat shone along his upper lip. “Yes. Heh. Better than alright. There’s… I hope you’ll reconsider. Think about rejoining.” He gave Renner a rueful grin. “There’s nothing like this, brother. Salt, I feel… unstoppable’s the only word for it. Ready for an army of monsters. I’ll face them head-on, won’t even break a sweat.” He stepped forward, voice lowering. “And if anyone’s got a taste for blood, well-”

“I don’t need Thalessa’s blessing to kill things.” Renner’s face was white. The sheer, seething vitriol in his tone made me step back without even thinking about it.

“Right.” Jareth’s smile tightened at the corners. “No, guess you don’t, huh?”

Brittle silence settled between the two men.

There’s a lot I’m missing here. An ocean’s worth. I glanced uncomfortably between them. Just moments ago they’d been laughing with each other, and now… Renner looks furious. Or… no. No, I don’t think it’s anger. Not… exactly. I studied the man’s face. Lips a bloodless line and every muscle rigid. One boot shifting back, as though he was bracing himself against the very earth.

He almost looks… afraid.

Teela raised a hand. “Um… I’m lost. Jareth was a templar, but then a few weeks ago he became paladin? Right? Isn’t that a good thing?”

“Not when he’s too young.” Renner looked the other man up and down. “You have any dreams yet? Wake up gnawing your own fingers off?”

I blanched. “What?”

“Wives’ tales.” Jareth waved the other man away. “Old horror stories. I’m perfectly fine.”

“Uh-huh. ‘Perfectly fine.’ I’ll carve that right onto your tombstone.”

“How were you… how does one become a paladin, exactly?” I piped in. “Does it involve magic of some kind?” It must. No human could move that fast. It must be something magical.

In a way, how Jareth had moved reminded me of Velidan. Speed so blinding that it seemed as though he simply appeared in another place. Strength as impressive as it was horrifying.

Blue eyes sparkled, pulling me from my thoughts. “Thalessa’s blessing. Templar reaches thirty winters-”

“Which you didn’t,” Renner snarled.

“And gets brought before the tree.” Jareth’s face shone and his shoulders drew back. Broad, bold, and proud. “There’s some prayers, an offering, a cup of some truly disgusting wine, and… well, here I am. Faster, stronger, better than belief.” He spread both hands wide, chuckling with obvious pleasure. Then, as if to prove the point even further, he very casually reached for a wooden training sword propped nearby. His knuckles turned white, his corded arms twitched, and with a resounding crack the weapon snapped in two.

“The… saints, you… the tree? What tree?” I managed, gaping at the splintered wood.

He dusted his hands. “Ah, I suppose I should be more respectful. Thalessa. The first witchwood.”

The first… huh. The first witchwood. It made logical sense, of course, even though my forehead scrunched. Of course there was a first witchwood. There’s a first everything, isn’t there? And it makes sense the tree would be up in Pinnacle, where the Thalessian Order has its roots. Heh. Roots. I bit down on a smile, then said earnestly, “I bet the tree’s beautiful. I’d love to see it.”

“No, you wouldn’t,” Renner muttered darkly.

“This one’s scared of her. Imagine that? Scared of a witchwood.” Jareth elbowed the smaller man, who glowered in response. “She’s magnificent. Huge. The entire Order is built around her, and I mean that literally. The branches are taller than anything else in Pinnacle.”

I goggled at the thought.

The paladin’s grin returned and he shifted, facing Renner fully. “Hey, you can be mad. Worried. But a deal’s a deal, and now I get to show you something.”

Gloves hands clenched. “Right. Not like this was a fair fight.”

Jareth boomed a laugh. “Aw, c’mon. You should’ve seen the look on your… ahem. It’ll cheer you up.”

Renner looked like he was contemplating murder. “Doubt it.”

“It will. And, here… because that wasn’t a fair fight.” One broad hand delved into a boot and he pulled out a short, narrow dagger. Familiar glassy silver was woven in with the steel in an intricate, razor-edged design. Strips of night-black leaves were wound gracefully around the hilt. He held the blade out. “No hard feelings, right? Not nearly as good as the sword, but it’ll still get the job done.”

Renner accepted the weapon without comment. He still looked markedly unhappy, but the edges of his frown softened.

Jareth cast a sly glance towards me and Teela. “If we weren’t in polite company, there’s a few good jokes I could make about manhood right now.”

“Yeah?” Renner tapped the man’s much larger blade with his new knife. “Do any of them involve over-compensating?”

The afternoon air rang with booming laughter and quiet giggles from me and Teela. And with it, some of the tension seemed to fade.

Eventually Jareth’s shoulders settled and his chortle died. “So you know that, uh, thing…” The paladin’s baritone was smug. He paced a short distance away, sword held horizontally before him, and slid one boot to the side. Taking a wide, ready stance. “That we’ve… practiced?”

Renner eyed the other man warily. “Uh-huh…”

The smile turned vicious. “Go ahead.”

He frowned. One gloved hand trailed up, fingers playing around the hilt of a sheathed knife.

Jareth rolled one shoulder. “I count six on you. I can do six. Heh. I could do a thousand, now.”

“A thousand what?” Teela hissed. Her gaze darted between the two of them.

My own brow furrowed as I examined Renner. It wasn’t hard to guess what he currently carried six- if not more- of, but surely Jareth couldn’t mean…

Metal rasped against leather as a dagger was bared. “You sure?”

“Yep. C’mon. You know you want to. Let off a little steam.”

Copper eyes narrowed.

Jareth just grinned. Deep blue flashed with a flicker of that bright, pale light. “C’mon. All six.”

I clasped a hand to my throat. “Ashes, don’t-”

Both men exploded into movement. Gloved hands tore dark hilts from their sheaths. Silver flickered through the space between both men, each knife moving nearly too fast to follow.

But the witchwood sword was faster. Impossibly fast, made so by the impossible speed of its wielder. Jareth twitched the gilded steel six times, earning six harsh rings and six sprays of sparks.

And six daggers were sent spinning away.

It was over in a heartbeat. Both men stood calmly, regarding the fallen blades.

Jareth dipped into a dramatic bow. “C’mon. You have to admit that was-”

“Yeah.” Renner looked begrudgingly impressed. He dragged a hand through his hair. The scowl had twisted into something less angry and more… resigned. “Alright. If I get to whip knives at your face, this salt’s… a little more tolerable.”

Deep laughter rang through the chilly air. “Knew you’d like that! Want to do it again?”

To my shock, Renner chuckled and nodded, then went about collecting his fallen daggers. I gave Teela a look that clearly said, ‘can you believe them?’ She returned the expression with a snicker.

“What was… that?” Jareth turned towards me again. A vaguely puzzled expression twisted his handsome face.

I mirrored the look. “I didn’t say anything.”

He went very still, knuckles white around the pommel of his sword, looking for all the world like he didn’t believe me.
