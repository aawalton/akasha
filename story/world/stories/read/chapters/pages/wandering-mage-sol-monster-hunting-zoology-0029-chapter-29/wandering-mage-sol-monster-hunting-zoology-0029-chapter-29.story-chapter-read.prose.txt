Tressa stared at the dwarf who stood with a knife in his hand, staring her down with hate. Her eyes lowered to where her Dotrin was smashed under his boot.

Tressa's mouth opened as she let out a cry, falling forward onto her knees to mourn her fallen pastry, completely ignoring the dwarf and the knife.

“What have you done! You fool!” Tressa cried. She reached a shaky hand towards it and then, in Elvish, whined, “My poor, poor fallen friend. I will avenge you! You deserved a far kinder fate than this!”

“Huh? What’s that? You threatening me, Elf?” the dwarf yelled.

Tressa shot up to her feet, her face full of anger and annoyance. “You’re the one who fell into me, and now you threaten me with a knife? And worst of all, you crushed my poor Dotrin pastry! I will avenge it!”

He looked down, startled, and found that there was indeed a pastry smashed under his boot.

“Now pay!” Tressa demanded, holding her hand out.

He sneered. “How dare you speak to me, you murderin’ elf. Give me one fucking reason I don’t kill you right where you stand.”

Liltin walked up behind her, folding his arms. “Because I would stop you before you took one step towards her. That, and I think her two hunter friends would make sure no harm comes to her.”

“Hunters?” the dwarf said, looking left and right to see Lisha snatching up her halberd and coming over, and Braidon not even bothering to grab his club as he put one massive hand into the other, cracking his knuckles with loud pops.

“Ah…” the dwarf gasped, taking a step back and adding a berry-smeared footprint to the floor just short of the flattened remains of Tressa’s dessert.

Tressa stepped towards him, her hand outstretched. “Pay.”

He looked left and right over his shoulder, spotted a few of his friends with mean expressions as they observed the confrontation, then looked back to her. “Pay? Ha! Why would I pay a damn murderer?”

“I am no murderer.”

“You’re a High Elf, based on yer looks, and that means you’re a murderer.”

Tressa’s face soured. “I had nothing to do with the war. I was a child when the Mad King was killed, and that has nothing to do with you smashing my Dotrin! You pay, mini man!”

“Mini man!?” The dwarf sneered, and a collective gasp rang through the room. Tressa blinked, looking around at how people who had remained uninvolved now looked at her with disgust.

“Tressa! Don’t call a dwarf that!” Lisha hissed at her.

“What I do?” Tressa blinked.

“You see!?” the dwarf barked, taking advantage of her slip-up. “A typical High Elf, looking down on all of us!”

Tressa glanced around once more, baffled by the sudden hostility.

“I never looked down on you!” she protested. “I only teased you for your short height!”

Another collective gasp swept through the tavern.

Lisha slapped a clawed hand over her face.

Chairs scraped as more people got up from their seats. Particularly, every dwarf in the tavern was now looking to get involved.

“You damn High Elf! Killing our ancestors wasn’t enough for you, was it!?” someone yelled.

“You have no place here!” yelled another.

“We should drag her halfway across the bridge and toss her in the damn lake!”

“P-Please!” Tressa waved her hands, panicked. “I never insult. It a mid-utter-standing!” she said, messing up the words.

“Enough, everyone!” Liltin yelled, stepping in front of her and pounding a fist to his chest, making it clear he was defending her. “This girl ain't harming no one, and she clearly doesn’t speak the common tongue well. It was a mistake and she obviously met nothin’ by it! Get a damn hold of yourselves!” he yelled, his voice carrying through the entire bar.

Tressa let out a breath of relief, having a dwarf stand up for her going a long way, especially one who was as clearly respected as Liltin.

“But Liltin, she’s a High Elf!”

“And so what? The war ended two hundred years ago. I’d wager nine out of ten of ya’ don’t even remember it.” Then he shouted even louder, “Well, I do, and I know better than anyone most High Elves were oppressed under the same bastard that caused everything. Think about it, who killed the Mad King in the end? It was the High Elves themselves, you damn fools.”

Tressa would have gladly pointed out her own father was part of the coup that ended him, but she, of course, wouldn’t risk slipping that she’s nobility, especially not in this situation.

“So lets all be sensible,” Liltin said, calming his tone. “This is a dispute between two people. I’ll be damned if ye’ start tearing my bar apart like a pack of raging goblins. Now Tressa, apologize for what ye’ said, girl.”

Tressa bowed to the dwarf who had started all this, though she hated having to apologize to him. “I truly regret what I had said, and I am sorry.”

“And you, Balm,” Liltin said, pointing at the perplex dwarf still holding his dagger. “Put that thing away this second, and pay up for destroying her pastry. It was the last one I had for the night, at that.”

“Last one…” Tressa whined quietly, regretting her apology now.

Balm clicked his teeth. “What do I care? Her people took way more from us than a damn Dotrin. I ain't paying back no damn High Elf!” he spat, though he at least put his dagger away.

“If it bothers ye’ that much then pay me back at least,” Liltin said. “Cause I won’t charge her for something she didn’t get to finish.”

Balm growled, not accepting that either. But as he looked Tressa up and down, he seemed to have an idea. “How bout this, little elf. I’ll pay you back if you can outdrink me!”

Tressa stopped. “Sorry?” She also thought it a bit unfair that he could call her little but not the other way around.

“If I cannot fight ye’ then we’ll settle this by drink,” Balm said.

Tressa smiled. “With pressure.”

“Pleasure,” Lisha corrected.

“Pleassure.” Tressa cleared her throat. “I show you I not killer, and if I win you pay me back for the Dotrin and apologize for you threats!”

“Fine, fine,” the dwarf sneered. “But if I win, you kiss my boots and pay me two silver.”

Tressa glared at him. “You mean to make me grovel?”

“Yes, all the way down on your hands and knees, and you give me boot a nice kiss ye’ damn elf.”

Her face turned red with anger at his terrible words. “No. I will make no such deal.”

“Oh so ye’ scared huh?”

“No, it is wrong to make a deal like that,” Tressa insisted. “I would never make you do it. So the deal is pay me back double for my pastry, and if I lose I pay you two silver, which is still far more.”

“She’s given ye’ better terms,” Liltin added. “It’d be cowardice to not accept it as it is.”

“Gah! Fine,” Balm growled.

Liltin nodded. “Though I’ll warn you, Miss Tressa, he has won more battles than any in drink. And I don’t know if you realize how we dwarves are.”

“Then I must put my skills to the test,” Tressa assured.

She quite enjoyed ale, but the truth of the matter was that it paled in comparison to Everwine. The strength of the two drinks was hardly comparable, and from her observations on her travels, despite the reputation of dwarves and their drinking ability, she had a feeling she was better.

After all, she routinely made herself hallucinate many times over the years with poisons, and alcohol was little compared to that. And she had seen other elves get drunk faster than she had before, so she suspected she had an abnormally high tolerance.

The tavern was now alight with a different kind of excitement. The people who had been glaring at Tressa at best, and ready to mob her at worst, were now collectively grabbing tables to clear out space in the middle of the bar, leaving only one table behind in the center of it all for the battle to take place.

Tressa took her seat across from Balm as instructed, and she could see already how confident this dwarf was.

He also had already drinking more than me tonight, so I am at an advantage either way, Tressa thought. But he must be quite confident to make this challenge in spite of that. Maybe I shouldn’t take that for granted.

“Alright, we will use half pints for this,” Liltin said, then nodded down to the cat girl waitress who scampered over and placed a sloshing mug of ale in front of Tressa. But across the table, an identical furry hand plopped down another mug, and Tressa blinked, looking back and forth.

“There are two of you!” Tressa said in shock as the two small cat girls moved around the table to stand side by side.

They both bowed. “Did you not realize, Miss Tressa?” one who had a 50% chance of being Siri said with a chuckle.

“Wouldn’t be the first time,” her sister said.

“I’m Siri,” said the first.

“And I’m Poppy,” said the second.

The tavern let out a collective laugh at Tressa’s baffled expression, because she wasn’t the first one to make that mistake.

“I thought you were just really fast!” Tressa exclaimed.

They tilted their heads towards each other, and their tails flicked as they spoke in unison. “We get that a lot!”

“Come on!” Balm grumbled from across the table. “Are we drinking or not!?”

“Yes!” Tressa said, grabbing her mug.

Many gathered around the table, only leaving room for the cat girls to make it back and forth to the bar while Liltin worked on filling mugs and taking bets. Though some still looked hesitantly at Tressa, most seemed far more interested in the joy of watching the battle and began placing bets.

Tressa grinned as she saw Briadon walk up to the counter to make a bet himself, but her mouth fell open in shock as he cleared his throat and set down a silver coin. “Fifty copper on Balm.”

“Briadon!” Lisha said in horror.

“You’re betting against me!?” Tressa yelled at him from the table.

“Sorry, but it’s a dwarf,” Briadon said with a shrug. “When it comes to drink, they never lose, so this will be an easy win.”

Suddenly, even more people were rushing the counter to place their bets, having seen the High Elf’s own traveling companion bet against her. Tressa felt betrayed and insulted! She thought Briadon, of all people, knew how well she could handle her liquor and would have more confidence in her! She could even keep up with the giant minotaur himself most nights!

“Well, I for one, won’t bet against my friend,” Lisha said, and Tressa's mouth practically unhinged in further shock as she put a gold coin down. “I’ll bet half of this, fifty silver.”

Wait a second…

Tressa grinned as she caught the subtle smirk pass between Lisha and Briadon. She had to hide it to not let anyone realize the little scam they just pulled. The betting odds were so heavily against Tressa now that if she won, Lisha would receive far more than double her money, maybe four or five times more!

Haha, leave it to those two to never miss an opportunity, Tressa thought smugly.

Though as she stared at the mug in front of her, she just hoped she wouldn’t let them down. Losing fifty silver would be quite a hit to their funds.

She and Balm both lifted their mugs up to each other in challenge, and it was easy to forget this all started with him threatening her with a dagger. Then they both tossed back the drinks, gulping deeply.

The dwarf drained his mug considerably faster, slamming the empty glass down when Tressa still had a good few swallows left. By the time she was done hers, Siri and Poppy had already placed fresh mugs, and Balm had already started on his second.

Tressa slammed down her empty glass and grabbed the new one, gulping it down as well. It was clear she wouldn’t be able to beat him in speed, but that’s okay; that’s not what would matter here. The challenge was on who could drink more it wasn’t a race. Still, she wouldn’t let him get too far ahead of her if she could help it.

After finishing her second and starting on her third, she noticed Siri and Poppy, aside from bringing out fresh mugs, had a board they were tallying the number of drinks on.

After seven drinks, Balm sat back confidently as Tressa worked on gulping down her sixth, having fallen a whole drink behind. “Having trouble there, Elf?” he jeered from across the table, then he let out a long and loud belch that practically echoed over the excited chatter of the crowd.

“Nasty,” Tressa said, as she finished her drink and wiped off her mouth. “You are a nasty, nasty dwarf.”

“Haha! Says the smelly elf!”

“I do not smell! I will make you pay me back for the dortin!”

“Yer just upset you know you’re going down, elf! Shame you didn’t agree to kiss my boot!” he laughed. “But I bet you’ll be down under the table one way or another when we are done.”

“No, I will not lose!” Tressa said, instantly grabbing her seventh mug to try to catch up.

Behind her, more hands exchange coins, perhaps making their own side bets.

Tressa finished her seventh drink and felt herself a little wobbly as she slammed it down on the table. “You drunk yet, dwarf?”

“No, elf, but seems you are starting to get bleary-eyed!”

“Ha! As if!”

They each grabbed their eighth, Tressa hardly taking a break after catching up.

They continued on, and after another ten minutes they had drunk twelve pints each, Balm no longer managing to outpace her as he had been, but that did little to waver his drunken confidence.

“Hahahahaha! Bo’s I make!” A massive belch left his throat that sent a sour smell all the way across the table. “I win dis’ boys!” he said and his dwarf friends cheered him on.

Tressa looked down at one hand, her vision moving slightly, drifting from whatever place she focused on. But she grabbed the fresh mug as Siri or Poppy sat it down in front of her and started drinking.

Balm belched and quickly grabbed his, as she was technically a few swallows ahead of him now and his pride wouldn’t let him fall behind.

“I can’t believe this,” said a human.

“Right, the elf might actually win this.”

“Balm has never lost before. His highest drink is twenty-one half pints.”

“What are they at now?”

“I think they at twenty.”

“Come on, Tressa! You got this!” Lisha cheered from the sidelines.

“Oh no! I might actually lose!” Briadon groaned, laying it on thick.

They finished their twentieth and slammed them down in unison, the cat girls already providing the 21st on the now littered table.

“Chug! Chug! Chug! Chug!” the whole bar cheered as they drank.

“Go elf! You can do it!” someone yelled, several patrons coming to Tressa’s side as the night wore on. Some just wanted to see the legendary drunk, Balm, loose for once, as many had been humiliated by his antics in the past.

Tressa smiled as she set her mug down. She put down an elbow to prop herself up and pointed a shaky finger at him. “Ya ready ta give up, dwarf?” she slurred.

“Ha na Balm don't—hick—don’t give up never!” He jabbed a waving finger at her. “I’ll drink ye’ under the table, elf! Under it! Then I drink the damn table too!”

“Alright! This is twenty-two!” Liltin called as the girls set what very well may be the final round of the competition. “If either finishes this, it’ll be a new in-house record!”

Everybody in the bar cheered at that.

Balm lifted his drink, entire mouthfuls sloshing over the brim, “Hahaha no petty elf will bea’ meee!” he slurred as he began gulping it down.

Tressa had to use both hands to keep her own mug steady, but she buried her face in it and started drinking, slowly tilting back more and more as she drained the glass.

Her head was spinning, and she wasn’t sure she’d ever been this drunk, but she thought it wasn’t as bad as the time she tried a certain mushroom to attempt to counteract the Blue Mimic toxins, only to find the mushroom actually amplified it. It was quite a different experience, as trees had grown faces and she laughed as she sang along with them, but suffice it to say, this spinning delirium the ale put her in was nothing compared to that.

But when she was only halfway done with the drink, she heard a crash that brought her eyes to the other side of the table, where she did not see her opponent anymore. She looked to the floor as everyone cheered and saw Balm passed out under the table. Some people groaned in disappointment, including Briadon, who loudly proclaimed, “No! I should have known better than to bet against that elf!”

Tressa could only smile as she knew she won but decided to take the record. That, she thought, would be an even greater victory of Balm than licking someone's boots!

She tossed back the last of the drink, then stood from her chair. “Wooo! To all the goooood peeeeople!” she cheered, raising the empty glass high in the air and then downing the last of it.

“That’s a new record everybody!” Liltin called over the excited commotion, but he didn’t need to tell anyone as the whole bar was alight with cheers.

“Oooooy!” many cheered, crowding over to her to pat her on the back and congratulate the elf who downed the unstoppable Balm.

Next thing she knew, she was being lifted up and set on the table on shaky legs as she laughed.

Then she started tapping her foot and singing, the bar quieting to allow the sound of her voice.

“Long live the trees, the willow it weeps! Let its branches embrace your soul!

Listen to its word! Turn your head to the wind! Together the willooowww siiings!”

Many laughed cheerily while some who knew the song joined in as it was a very old song known across the continent.

Soon it seemed the whole bar was singing along with her.

“Together the willow sings!

The branches and birds will guide your way, let the willow embrace your soul.

Then as the willow sings, the wind will guide theeee, and tomorrow you will seeee the liiight!

Let its warmth hold your souls as the willow weeps your wooooess!”

Then, as she continued, many stopped singing and only softly clapped along as her voice carried through the tavern, a sorrowful hum coming to her throat as she let it lead into a set of oooos as each octave changed in ways no human nor dwarf could match.

The crowd quieted, tears appearing in many eyes as she continued.

“Oh willow, oh willow, I weep for thee, I give you my soul as sorrow does sing

As tomorrow comes I give ye my grief. Take away the cries of mine.

Take it to the river upon the wind you cry into.

Let me feel the soft touch of your leaves. Oh willow, oh willow, it weeps for me!”

Though it was a sad song, the cheers and whistles continued as she finished, even as many of the older patrons were dabbing at their eyes.

Another drink found its way into Tressa’s hands, and she was lifted off her feet once again as she was passed through the bar, laughing and then downing the drink that spilled out and got more on her face than down her throat.

Several cries of joy came through the cheers at the elf’s song.

“That elf can really sing!”

Soon many began to clap, and another song took up this one, singing about a drunken dwarf who found a bath full of ale. He drank to where he found his shoe and soon began to eat the hard leather.

At some point, instruments came out, Tressa, losing track of the night, at one moment sitting on a table with Lisha and Braidon on either side, another fuzzy memory of scratching Siri or Poppy’s ears as they allowed her to do so, she was pretty sure she was sitting on Braidon’s shoulder at one point, singing one again but a much more cheerful tune and waving the mug of ale that she had completely lost count of.

Everybody was having fun, even Lisha and Braidon were getting quite drunk, having plenty of funds from Lisha’s gambling win to support the night of partying.

Lisha looked at her two friends, seeing that they were both beyond drunk and that Tressa was slowly starting to give in to the amount of alcohol she had consumed. Lisha stood as Braidon walked her over and set her down, the small elf girl weaving on her feet and smiling.

“We hav’ fun hahahaha. I won! He goonna have Doortimm,” she giggled then let out a burp.

“Come on, Tressa, let's get you to bed,” Lisha said, and took the drink from her hand and helped her across the bar, the girl swaying on her feet with every step. She accepted high fives and pats on her shoulder and back as Lisha escorted her out. She was now like a local celebrity for how the bar’s patrons regarded her.

Tressa let out another loud hiccup but then stopped walking.

“Tressa?” Lisha asked.

Tressa ran, looking for something but ended up collapsing on her hands and knees as she began to vomit.

“Oooooo,” anyone who saw cried.

“Is that suppooose ta happ’n?” Tressa slurred, her face full of confusion as she stared at the vomit on the floor.

“Yes, that's what happens when you drink past your limit. I'm honestly surprised you're even standing,” Lisha said as she helped Tressa wobble back up to her feet.

“I really don’ feel goo’,” she said as Lisha grabbed her arm to steady her. “Why the roo’ moovin so muc’?”

“Cause you are drunk.”

“Hmmm.”

“Come on, let's get you to bed. Liltin mind if I loot a bucket off ya?”

“Not at all. I agree it's amazing she is still up.”

“I'm sorry about the—”

“Worry not. She has made some good friends this night,” the bartender said with a wink. “I never thought I’d live to see the day a room with so many dwarves singing along with a High Elf. I have a feeling this is going to be talked about for years to come.”

“Thank you,” Lisha said, giving him a toothy smile.

“Worry not, lass; I'll take care of this. Get your friends to bed. And ill have Yort bring you some water and the bucket.”

“Thank you again.”
