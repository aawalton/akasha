Few fables speak of the Overrealm. It is not simply another Realm for mortals to tread and leave their fallible marks on. No, as all the great faiths decree, the Overrealm is the Plane of the Great Gods. Where divinities beyond the Great Gods exist in forms we can barely comprehend.

So, let me tell you of their greatness.

Praise the divinities, for it is through their blessing that this whole universe came to be. For if they didn’t feed the Wellspring, then magic as we knew it would never have come about.

Praise the Great Gods, for if they didn’t stand mighty against the Hordes of Indomitus, then the cosmos we have come to inhabit would never have been ours to do with as we willed.

Praise them, for none wish to be like the ancient Archmagi Confluence, whose daring ritual to bring down the very Great Gods themselves only cast the world in terrible shadow for nearly an aeon before the divinities’ mercy returned, blessing the worlds with their vital energies to allow life to flourish once more.

Praise be the Great Gods. Praise be the Ones Above All. Praise be the Immortal Lights.

Praise be.

A Little Faith.

Fororias E.

“But why is she in the Overrealm of all places, though?” Viren asked.

Ryland felt like he had mentioned the specifics already, but since he was doing nothing more than infusing more and more mana into the Inscription covering the wide field, he was happy to answer.

“The Flourinus Order has trapped Acrypha there so that they can use the Great Gods to prevent me from getting to them,” he said.

“Yes, sir, I remember that,” Viren said. “But I think you also mentioned something else they might be planning.”

Oh, right. Ryland had never expanded on that. Not even for himself, now that he thought about it.

Ryland started using his Deific Prints from the reward of his last battle to enormously empower several of the Glyphs in his Inscription. “I don’t know their exact plans, but the core tenet remains true. They adore their Calamities and will do anything they can to bring them about.”

“But they’re in the Overrealm, sir. Will the Great Gods bring about Calamities somehow?”

“Doubtful, since the Great Gods can only have very limited expressions of their power through rigorously selected candidates, like the Oracle. However, it is true that Calamities and the Great Gods are connected.”

That made Viren start. “Connected? How exactly, sir?”

“Well, think about what Calamities are. You remember your lessons, yes? Incarnations and Calamities are Pure Manifestations of manasouls, which can only exist outside of a body when they’re in that empowered state. However, where Incarnations have successfully bound a Concept to their manifested manasoul, Calamities fail to do so successfully.”

“And is it that failure that causes them to go berserk and destroy everything?”

Ryland smiled. “Good catch. And yes, you’re correct. Calamities lack control over their Pure Manifestation because as they have failed to bind their Concept, they are in a perpetual limbo of eternally attempting to bind Concepts.”

Viren looked like he greatly missed having notes and a quill to write things down. “I see. But how does that connect to the Great Gods?”

“The Great Gods are ascended beings who have bound themselves to Overarching Concepts.” Ryland pointed to the Glyphs he had Inscribed on the ground. “See, the thing with Concepts is that some are Lesser and some are Greater. Some have sub-Concepts as part of their definitions and whatnot. Great Gods are the gods of things like war, love, death, and so on.”

“Are the Incarnations different in how they are bound to Lesser Concepts?” Viren frowned. “Like, is there an Incarnation of Blood? Or Swords? Or Heartbreak?”

“Essentially, yes. An Incarnation cannot take over a Concept overseen by a Great God.”

Ryland didn’t need to look down at Viren to see the boy’s eyes screwing up as he considered the implications of all the information Ryland had dropped. Whatever classes he took in the academy, Ryland was quite certain they didn’t make him learn anything truly religious. That just wasn’t needed for Arcoryx’s curriculum.

“So then,” Viren said. “There is no Great God of Sorcery, is there?”

Smart. “No, there isn’t. Although, there does exist a Great God of Mana.”

Viren’s eyes widened. “Do you know that Great God? Are you on good terms or does that god hate you too?”

“It’s… complicated.”

Thankfully, Viren had no further questions for Ryland. While he wasn’t opposed to answering more, he also wanted to focus on completing the ritual so they could go retrieve Acrypha.

“The problem with Concepts,” Ryland said as the ending bit to their impromptu lesson. “Is that Overarching Concepts can affect Lesser related Concepts. For instance, an Incarnation of Faith will need to answer to the Great God of Trust.”

“Trust?”

Ryland understood Viren’s confusion. Things weren’t always clear cut when it came to Incarnations and Great Gods. “Suffice it to say that the Great Gods can assert a certain level of influence through Incarnations related to them and thereby affect the world at large. Of course, most Incarnations don’t want this, which is why they stay away from civilized locations.”

“Most,” Viren said with a note of suspicion. “But not you.”

Ryland smiled. “Not me. The Great God of Mana has never deemed it worthy to pursue me and make my life miserable, so I’ve been blessed.”

Viren clearly had questions about that too, but he wisely remained silent for the time being. According to him, he had distracted Ryland enough. For now, Ryland focused on finishing up a good chunk of the massive Inscription before they got going.

In truth, Ryland eventually realized he hadn’t answered Viren’s most pressing question of what Acrypha was even doing in the Overrealm? What sort of plans did the Flourinus Order have for it? All because Ryland himself didn’t fully understand their intentions.

It was worrying all the same. First Mavar. Then the Great Gods. The deals the Order was forming and the leverage they were using had to be quite significant.

Ryland finished up the Inscriptions, and it was soon time to depart. While Ryland had been asserting the right Glyphs and using enough mana to do so, Viren had gone around and scouted the local area. This whole operation was going to take several days, so he’d need accommodations, somewhere to store his food and belongings, and so on.

Unfortunately, most of the infrastructure of Rhel had been destroyed entirely by the destruction Ryland and Mavar had caused. Even the last vestiges of the Rhellian Institute still left standing were far too unstable for habitation.

It wasn’t a major problem. Ryland had offered to take Viren to the same Realm where he himself tended to rest, but the boy had refused.

Instead, he had decided to make camp on Rhel. With the aid of Glyphtech he had bought and borrowed, he was able to set up quite a nifty little area to keep everything he needed magically stored away for later use. It wasn’t ideal, but it wasn’t entirely horrible either. Ryland was quite impressed by Viren’s resourcefulness.

“So, we’re going to meet a dragon, sir?” Viren asked when it was time to leave. “A Planetary Archdragon?”

He sounded awed and he hadn’t even seen the dragon yet.

Ryland smiled. “Indeed. We require the Archdragon’s assistance. If we can acquire it, then our path forward will become a whole lot easier. But I’m sure its help will not come cheap.”

Viren nodded. “I’m sure you’ll be able to handle it, sir.”

“Me?” Ryland threw him another little smile. “Not you too?”

“Well… it’s an Archdragon. What am I supposed to do against something like that?”

“Oh, I think you might be surprised. After all, I wouldn’t be taking you with me if I didn’t have a role for you to play.”

Viren was a little surprised by that, but only for a second. Then he nodded with determination. “I’ll do whatever I can, sir!”

“Good. Let’s go. [Oneself World]. [Translocate].”

As a stable pocket of atmosphere clad them both, a gold-edged rift opened up and swallowed both Ryland and Viren, soon spitting them out into the cosmos. It had been a while since Ryland had met the Archdragon in question, though it hadn’t been difficult to locate his target. Just a few minutes of searching with [Omniscience] had revealed its location.

And now, they were both in front of a dragon that could have bitten off chunks of the entire planet of Rhel.

“You have got to be…”

Viren’s words failed him as he gawked at the creature heading straight in their direction. Ryland understood his reaction entirely. The first time he had seen Vorkorax, he had been taken aback by the sheer scale of the creature. To think there was a dragon big enough to dwarf entire planets and maintain its own atmosphere was frankly impossible to believe.

Yet it was right there. Right in front of them and getting ever closer. Its “tiny” scales were each a mountain taller than those that existed in the Accorded Realms, its craggy tail long enough to wrap around a moon. Its set of four, ginormous wings didn’t beat, but still sailed through the void of the cosmos. Clouds wrapped around its neck and body, water turning into fathomless oceans all across its wings and back.

Vorkorax was so enormous, even Ryland simply took his time to appreciate the sheer living enormity.

“How…?” Viren couldn’t even find the words to express himself.

“Are you questioning how a dragon can grow so big?” Ryland asked. “Or are you asking how a dragon can support a civilization on its scales?”

“That thing has a civilization?” Viren said faintly. “No, of course it has a civilization on its back.”

Perhaps he was a little too stupefied with disbelief to quite understand how a dragon grew to such massive proportions. Maybe he would better enjoy that education at some other time. His next words kind of proved that. He was still stuck on the scale of the creature.

“And here I thought those Archdrakes you fought were big, sir,” Viren said. His eyes were as wide as coins. “They were big enough to cover up all of Arcoryx Academy by themselves. But this thing…”

He just shook his head like he would wake up from whatever dream he had sunk himself into.

“Would you like to say hello, Viren?” Ryland asked.

“Um…”

Ryland thought Viren was still too amazed by meeting such an impossibly large being, but that turned out to be false. The boy’s face turned a terrified pale, and Ryland turned to face Vorkorax as well.

Where the approaching dragon had opened its gargantuan maw, which was glowing brighter and brighter like the core of a planet.

A glow that foretold a blast of dragon fire.

It was only a second later that the enormous gout of flames emerged from the maw of the Archdragon, shooting at Ryland and Viren with the speed of a meteor and with enough burning force to devastate planets in one strike. The deluge of energy was so massive, it beggared belief.

Good thing Ryland had practice defending himself against eradicating heat from when he had crucified Mavar into the sun.

Viren’s scream lasted merely a second before the roaring flames swallowed them both, so bright with heat energy that the flaming stream was practically white, outshining all the nearby stars.

Thankfully, they were unhurt. Mostly. Ryland had already cast [Absolute Timefort] on both himself and Viren to protect themselves against that raging, species-ending inferno. He would have tried [Awakened Avatar] and [Animus Aegis], but both those spells would only work on him. As such, [Absolute Timefort] was a much better choice.

Still. It took Viren perhaps a whole minute before he realized he wasn’t being incinerated to nothing. He wasn’t even finding it hard to breathe. Thanks to another cast of [Absolute Timefort] taking place atop the original cast of [Oneself World], they still had breathable air.

“Sir!” Viren said in terrible alarm.

“It’s alright, Viren,” Ryland said. “Wait one moment, and this will all pass.”

It did. The planet-sized fiery stream ended, not because the Archdragon lacked energy to keep it up, but because it had thought whatever it had attacked surely had to be reduced to nothing at all by now.

“Impossible!”

The Archdragon’s telepathic voice ripped into Ryland’s mind like an avalanche of jagged, overheated glass. Viren winced at just the singular word landing in his mind.

“Greetings, Vorkorax,” Ryland said normally. The Archdragon possessed powerful Inscriptions that would allow it to communicate with anyone at any level needed. They didn’t need to perform anything ridiculous, like yell into cavernous ears that could hide entire nations. “I see your chosen method of greeting hasn’t changed over the centuries.”

“You… Ryland?” The Archdragon was clearly surprised to see him back. Notably, he was not at all regretful about having blasted Ryland with enough flames to drown entire worlds in heat.

“The very same. Care to discuss a small matter?”

“What do you want this time?”

The Archdragon hadn’t stopped flying through the cosmos at them, so now Viren, who had almost fainted in relief after not being burned to a crisp, was now panicking once more. One was liable to panic with a planet-sized dragon headed in their direction.

Ryland, of course, didn’t move at all. Viren was rapidly tugging on his robes, pointing at the approaching enormity that was slowly starting to give the boy vertigo.

“I want you to help me break into the Overrealm,” Ryland said.

That made even the enormous dragon stop short.
