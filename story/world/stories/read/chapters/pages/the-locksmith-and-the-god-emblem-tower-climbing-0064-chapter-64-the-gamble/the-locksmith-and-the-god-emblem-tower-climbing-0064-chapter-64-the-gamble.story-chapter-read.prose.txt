"Who..." The man said in a trembling voice, wide eyed. "What are you..."

"Relax." Goldman said first, his peaceful instinct coming into play. "We won't hurt you."

You can't know that. Matthews thought to himself, but remained quiet. Even the skeleton couldn't take his eye off of the figure.

Harry, still shaken with the diary pages, stared at the man who was visibly frightened. Seeing how he reacted, along with his attire, it all felt so... normal. The majestic and mythological idea of the Astronomer was coated with desperation and bad-slept nights. Harry focused on the scars on his cheek, along with the wrinkles and folds in his face, almost as if he was broken both internally and externally. But then the young thief saw the multitude of emotions swimming in his eyes. An eye whose color was tainted by a force unknown; an ocean polluted. In the outer layers of the organ, a few strands of a special color seemed to stop at the pupil like a black hole. The man who escaped the universe, a traveler amidst travelers; an escapee. And here he was, a prophet whose words were disturbed.

Why did he understood the man so much? From this distance, it seemed as all of his secrets were spilled. All his words revealed.

"How are you... here?" The man asked, once again, taking a step back as soon as Goldman moved. "I'm not understanding."

"We climbed your Tower." Harry said. "The Logos Tower."

"Logos Tower!?" He appeared distressed. "I—Don't take another step forward! Just... don't!"

"Okay." The young thief replied, taking a step back. "We won't move."

He glanced at each member of the group, assuring them to maintain their positions. Madeline then shook her head and frowned. Harry gave her a clueless shrug.

The source of the sound, probably coming from the telescope, was non-existent now. The rumble which it also made wasn't there as well. What was weird, though, was how the space appeared unnecessarily large. The shelves themselves possessed weird proportions and the books were all just copies of a few pieces. Looking up, Harry saw three empty rows on every shelf.

The Tower didn't made sense from the beggining, of course; loops on every floor; a bunch of misplaced structures and reality-bending islands floating in the ocean. But even in that fever dream of a place, it all felt connected, somehow. The strange and weirdness of it all, maybe it had a reason. This floor is meant to hold the answers... right? Harry thought to himself, but held very little hope after seeing the legendary Astronomer; sapped and fearful, like a dog after a heavy rain.

"Why are you here and who are you." The man asked again, and if it wasn't for the emphasis on knowing the group's identity, Harry and the others would've thought the Astronomer was senile for asking the same question.

The group, including Goldman, all stared at Harry, for some reason. Really? He's been doing the talk since we got here... Well, it's once in a lifetime, I suppose. The young thief sighed deeply.

"My name's Harry Bockart." He began as he pointed to every member. "This is Goldman, our... pirate." The Locksmith omitted the fact that he's also the leader, afraid it might damage his image since he's the one talking.

Harry continued introducing the members as the Astronomer narrowed his eyes, keenly staring the group.

"I-is this... everyone?" He regained his composure.

"Yes." Harry said.

"Alright..." The Astronomer sighed. "You said you climbed a Tower, is that right? What Tower is this and why?"

"...What do you mean?" Harry gave him a confused look. "We climbed your Tower, to meet you. You're the Astronomer. You're the one who created this place; a Tower hidden beyond the facets of reality and unshakable by even the strongest of weapons. The place who defies entropy. After leaving this legend behind, a few people even found the clues you left behind for entering the Tower!"

"This doesn't make any sense." The man replied, coming closer to a blackboard as he looked around. "They weren't meant to be clues, kid. And this isn't a Tower; it's a prison."

"Are you joking with us?" Harry said, raising his voice. "Because if you are, this isn't funny. The Logos Tower is real. Everyone that's been banished to this place knows it. The outside showed us."

"No." The Astronomer replied as he scratched his left eye. "I-It isn't, it can't be. I trapped It here with me because that was the right thing to do. As such, the moon won't crash and the Earth won't shatter. At least the Emblems live and die with me."

Who's It? Harry added inwardly. Moon? He thinks the Emblems aren't out there?

In a swift movement, Harry then conjured a door right in front of the Astronomer. Creating a passage in front of them, in two simple steps, the man was right in front of them, disoriented by what had happened.

"...How did you do that!?" The man asked then took a step back, stumbling on a few books. Before falling, however, Goldman pulled him, saving him from the fall. "What is..."

The Astronomer then looked at the members of the mysterious group of travelers. One of them pulled out his hood and mask, which revealed a skull. On the left, a woman held a brush that emanated a green substance while a child was shrouded in darkness, his shadow missing.

"The Emblem Theory." Harry simply stated. "That's your work, isn't it?"

"...So it is out there." He said after a moment. "At least they work as intended."

"...For how long have you been here?" Harry asked, a surge of sympathy overcoming him. "In this... place, I mean. This study room."

"After It consumed my..." The Astronomer paused for a second as he got up. "I was forced to imprison It, here. I-I didn't know what could've happened... The Emblem was suppose to have worked properly, I can't understand how there are still breaches... It hasn't been more than an hour after the incident."

An hour!? The Locksmith, lost in his explanation, tried to find a logical reason for this. The higher you climb, the faster time passes? That's why there are less hours in each loop; it's compensating the 24 hours in its limited time. But even so...

"What about the loops?" Harry asked. "Down here, beneath this telescope, there's a library. How come you never found the way to the lower floors?"

"What loops?" The answer, masqueraded as a question, made the group tremble like they were embodied strings in an out-of-tune guitar.

"...Are you telling me you don't remember the time loops?" Madeline continued the interrogation when a still shaken Harry couldn't. "You don't know about the fights in the Colosseum, nor the floating island on the 4th floor? The two mountains clashing together? Anything?"

"Two mountains..." The man's eyes danced among his thoughts, unpredictable as raindrops in a city rain. "Oh, God, I... I made a mistake. I shouldn't have showed Logos those drawings. I shouldn't have used the Emblem. It has created a Tower, an amalgamation of creative freedom, one turned real by the their distorted power. This isn't a prison; it's a home."

And before Harry could question him about the nature of the Emblems and ask for a more detailed explanation, he felt every hair on his body stand on end. He could feel his pupil dilating, taking over his eye as if it had always been there. His blood boiled and froze so quickly that the young thief thought, for a moment, that he would have a thermal shock. In a second, he felt his nerve endings pointing to... something. To the left, the telescope seemed to tremble.

This explosion of sensations only intensified when the whole group looked outside, exactly where the telescope was pointed.

"Greetings." He said, his voice slightly muffled by the mask. "The Locksmith, Cardley."

"...Raven?" Harry mustered all courage left in him and continued. "What are you doing?"

"You know this guy?" Goldman asked without taking his eyes off the figure..

"We've... met." Harry simply nodded, unsure what to do next.

Raven was still wearing his unnecessarily detailed mask. It had small bumps and waves that appeared and disappeared in the mask's material, similar to overgrown roots of an ancient tree. Or squiggling worms festering on its host. Either representation was valid.

The figure hovered in front of the telescope lenses, just as it had appeared to Harry during the 4th floor encounter. Raven raised its frail, elongated limbs in a gesture of greeting, only stopping when he noticed an old woman squinting her eyes while also glancing at an empty piece of paper. She seemed confused.

"Curious." The masked man simply stated.

"It's you!" The astronomer, Cardley Logos, stepped forward and pointed his finger at the figure intrusively. "You stole my son! You deserve to die, you—"

"I didn't steal anything." Raven's voice circled the room a few times and reverberated through the vaulted ceiling above. "It was your fault."

Harry felt a hand on his shoulder and turned quickly, only then realizing how jumpy he was. Goldman gave him a determined but still worried smile as he seemed to prepare for a fight. Realizing the final moment was approaching, still mixed with emotions stored up over the years, the Locksmith held back and took a step forward.

Madeline, still frightened, gripped her paintbrush like an eagle holding onto its prey as Matthews twisted his neck sharply. Brun, still trying to understand the entity, was frustrated to realize her Emblem wasn't working properly.

"I don't want to interrupt you two," Harry began, drawing attention to himself. "But what the hell is going on here? Why doesn't the Astronomer remember the past loops? Is this the final floor of the Tower? What do we need to do to get the God Emblem?"

"You're not interrupting, for this is not a conversation nor a discussion. Right, Cardley Logos?" As the Astronomer was ready to reply, Raven continued. "Well, Harry Bockart, you've proven yourself to be very valuable, and I appreciate your fine climbing until now. But it wasn't without a slight help, was it not? Either way, you'll have your answers and I'll have my freedom. That's what we agreed upon, right?"

"Agreed!?" Cardley bellowed and turned to the Locksmith. "You made a deal with the devil? Are you insane? He's not to be trusted, he's the reason I—"

"Shut it." A simple order zipped the Astronomer's mouth as his heartbeat doubled.

"Look," Harry added as he glanced at the group, for some reason now shy. "I don't care about the Tower, okay? I don't need to know the answers, I just want to reach the end. We all do; we're tired."

"Yes, yes..." Raven said, almost dismissively. "But even the laziest student must study to understand a test. You either understand what's at stake, or you don't go home. There isn't an in-between. You don't go back on this, do you understand? "

Harry met the gazes of each member of the group. He saw himself reflected in their eyes and realized he would have to make a decision, one that would impact his future and that of the Tower. He saw Cardley Logos's cracked face and immediately began to ruminate on sacrifice and how far he was prepared to go. The path of the Tower had led him to this, a conclusion where no decision would be wise.

Matthews took a step forward, placing himself in Harry's line of sight. He saw his leather-rimmed glasses glued to his skeletal face. He can't even cry. He remembered his struggle with his brother and what had been taken from his life, an irreparable mistake. He remembered the warmth and embrace of his mother, and for a brief moment, he realized what mattered most in his life.

"I do." The Locksmith said. "Let's end this."
