Bart broke the first seal. Inside was not the lost biography of a legendary elven scholar. It was a completely ordinary personnel record.

ATTRIBUTION ROOT I NAME: EDITH VETCH

Field: Pomological Studies

Recorded position: Orchard Keeper

Faculty standing: NONE

Contribution: Orchard yield trials and fruit culture stabilization

Hester Crowl stared at the slip of parchment. Her golden academic robes suddenly looked very heavy.

"Edith Vetch?" Hester murmured.

Bart looked at her. "You know her?"

Hester frowned, a look of profound cognitive dissonance crossing her face. "Every first year pomologist knows the Vetch pruning tables. They are foundational to modern orchard management. But she wasn't a scholar, she was municipal staff."

"She was also one of Veridian's registered contributors," Bart said.

He didn't wait for Hester to process the shock but methodically broke the wax seals on the remaining six slips, peeling back the blank spell paper and laying the true provenance records across the desk.

ATTRIBUTION ROOT II NAME: BRAMWELL EDDEN

Field: Fermentation Sciences

Recorded position: Brewery Cellar Supervisor

Conrad Tiller let out a sharp, dismissive breath. "A brewer?"

Gregor immediately held up his hand. "Careful. I've met some genius bakers."

Bart moved to the third slip.

ATTRIBUTION ROOT III NAME: MERRICK ROOK

Field: Bryology

Recorded position: Junior Research Assistant

"Rook," Maud Brack said quietly. "The Rook Cultivation Method. He was an academic, at least, but far too junior to claim foundational authorship."

Bart broke the fourth seal.

ATTRIBUTION ROOT IV NAME: AGNES DORR

Field: Mycology

Recorded position: Independent Field Naturalist

Location: Rimewater Marshes

Bart tapped the location. "Rimewater."

Gregor looked up, his eyes brightening. "The three week horse."

"Ah Yes!," Bart said.

He uncovered the final three records in quick succession.

ATTRIBUTION ROOT V NAME: CRISPIN ASHCOMBE

Field: Soil Alchemy

Recorded position: Laboratory Technician

Conrad’s massive hands gripped the edge of the desk. "A technician designed the substrate reaction theory?"

Bart didn't answer, he just laid out the sixth and seventh slips.

ATTRIBUTION ROOT VI NAME: NELLA NOLL

Field: Medicinal Cultivation

Recorded position: Village Herbalist

ATTRIBUTION ROOT VII NAME: HOLLIS TARN

Field: Agricultural Thaumaturgy

Recorded position: Farm Ward Engineer

Bart laid all seven files side by side.

Elian looked back and forth across the spread of parchment. "None of them are Aldous Veridian."

"No," Bart said.

"None of them are even Aldous."

"Not necessarily."

Bart pulled the secondary tracking logs back across the desk and began matching them against the seven records, reconstructing the "impossible" timeline that had baffled them only an hour ago.

Suddenly, it wasn't impossible at all, It seemed completely mundane even.

"Year 412, Spring," Bart read. "The orchard trials. Edith Vetch."

He moved his pen down. "That same week, the western fermentation study. Bramwell Edden."

"Two days later, the fungal field survey in Rimewater. Agnes Dorr."

He moved to the next entry. "The medicinal cultivation lecture notes filed here in Ravespire. Nella Noll."

Bart tapped the last report. "And the Soil Alchemy work from the southern conservatories. Crispin Ashcombe."

Gregor looked over the now perfectly ordinary timeline. "So no horse?"

"No horse," Bart confirmed.

Gregor looked mildly disappointed.

Elian wasn't looking at the timeline. He was looking at the names.

Vetch. Edden. Rook. Dorr. Ashcombe. Noll. Tarn.

His eyes moved back to the archival tag.

Then back to the names.

"Oh."

Bart looked up.

COLLECTIVE RESEARCH DESIGNATION: V.E.R.D.A.N.T.

"Their surnames," Elian said.

Bart followed the sequence. "Verdant."

Gregor frowned. "Greenhollow scholars made a plant word out of their own names?"

Elian shrugged. "We have a theme."

"But what were they actually doing?" Maud asked, her voice losing its usual academic detachment. "These are seven entirely different fields. An orchard keeper, a brewer, a village herbalist, a farm engineer... why would they submit a collective registration?"

Bart flipped through the packet until he found an old correspondence summary tucked near the back. He scanned the neat, archaic handwriting.

JOINT RESEARCH FINDING Independent observations indicate common thaumaturgical behavior across seven biological systems. Living biological systems demonstrate the capacity to stabilize, transmit, and transform magical energy more efficiently than purely artificial alchemical structures. Recommendation: Unified publication.

Maud read the text over his shoulder. She went very still.

"They weren't working on seven subjects," Maud said quietly.

Bart looked at her. "Precisely!"

Conrad finished the thought, his rumbling voice unusually subdued. "They were working on one."

They had all discovered different facets of the exact same phenomenon. The orchard keeper's fruit cultures, the brewer's yeast, the herbalist's roots, the technician's reactive soil were all seeing the same underlying process. It was a unified theory of living magic.

Bart turned to the collective publication petition.

"If the research was this groundbreaking," Elian said, "why didn't they just publish it under their real names? Why invent a publication identity?"

Bart pointed to a rigid, stamped block of text on the application's rejection history.

GREENHOLLOW FOUNDATIONAL PUBLICATION STANDARD - HISTORICAL A work seeking Foundational Scholarly Status must be registered beneath a single recognized authorial identity. Principal authorial identity must demonstrate:

- Chartered academic standing

- Recognized disciplinary sponsorship

- Senior scholarly status

INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTORS MEETING FOUNDATIONAL AUTHORSHIP

STANDARD: 0 OF 7 Initial submission: REJECTED

Reason: INSUFFICIENT SCHOLARLY STANDING

Elian frowned. "Without Foundational Status, Greenhollow wouldn't accept the unified theory into its certification system. They could publish observations, but the seven fields would continue treating them as separate research."

Bart looked at the seven records. "So they could publish the pieces."

"But not the conclusion," Maud said.

Conrad frowned heavily. "Then they couldn't publish it."

"They did publish it," Bart said.

Hester pointed at the ledger. "Under Veridian."

Bart flipped to the next page of the petition. Old Greenhollow law contained an obscure exception. A collective could petition the Rooted Quill to register an independent publication identity. Once certified, the identity not any individual contributor could serve as the recognized authorial entity for interdisciplinary work.

COLLECTIVE ATTRIBUTION EXCEPTION:

For interdisciplinary work, a publication identity certified by the Order of the Rooted Quill may satisfy the authorial standing requirement

These 7 people had used it.

COLLECTIVE PUBLICATION IDENTITY PETITION

Contributors represented: 7

Proposed publication identity: ALDOUS VERIDIAN

Rooted Quill provenance deposit: REQUIRED

Public byline attribution: WAIVED

Petition status: APPROVED

Gregor stared at the approval stamp. "So they found a loophole."

Bart adjusted his glasses. "They followed the publication regulations."

"That's what I said."

Elian was staring at the requested name. "Aldous Veridian." He gave a small, sudden laugh.

Bart looked at him. "What?"

"It's an old elven dialect," Elian said, shaking his head. "Roughly translated? It means 'Venerable Green.' Perhaps 'Noble Green.'"

Gregor stared at him. "So they invented a respectable old elf called Old Green?"

Elian smiled faintly. "That seems to be what everyone eventually decided."

Bart looked closer at the original Rooted Quill registration. He checked the demographic fields.

Birth date: - Species: - Age: - Personal history: -

Bart realized exactly what he was looking at. "They never claimed Veridian was a person."

The vault was quiet.

"Of course they did," Hester said immediately.

Bart turned the ledger toward her. "Show me where."

Hester looked at the blank fields. Her eyes darted across the parchment, searching for a biography, a faculty appointment, a physical description but there was nothing.

Bart gestured to the secondary logs he had pulled earlier. Everything in the original documentation said submitted under Veridian, registered to Veridian, or publication identity Veridian.

It was only later, decades or centuries later that the phrasing subtly shifted in the departmental texts. Veridian's study became Veridian traveled to Rimewater to conduct the study.

"The original attribution wasn't false," Bart said.

Hester frowned, looking at the mountain of paperwork that had just dismantled her entire academic worldview. "Then what was?"

Bart looked at the centuries of secondary records, the portraits.

"The biography."

The pattern was finally clear. Seven people had produced the research. Greenhollow's rules had refused to recognize their unified work under their own names, so they created a publication identity the rules would recognize.

The book became famous.Somewhere in the centuries that followed, the paperwork became a biography. Bart looked at the tarnished brass emblem of the Order of the Rooted Quill. He finally understood their purpose.

Their job wasn't to decide whether using a publication identity was good or bad. Their job was simply to ensure the truth survived the bureaucracy.

Stamped at the bottom of the petition was a single line of heavily embossed text.

A QUILL MAY WRITE ONE NAME. ITS ROOTS MUST REMAIN TRACEABLE.

Bart read the motto. "That's very annoyingly sensible."

"But why did they stay hidden?" Maud asked, tracing the edge of Merrick Rook's slip. "Once the work was accepted, why not reveal themselves?"

Bart checked the next section.

PUBLIC CONTRIBUTOR DISCLOSURE: AVAILABLE UPON CONSENT Disclosure election: DECLINED

Requested by: ALL SEVEN CONTRIBUTORS

"They could have," Bart said.

Maud frowned. "And they chose not to?"

"All seven of them."

"Why?" Hester asked.

Bart studied the record. "It doesn't say."

He studied the signatures again. Whatever their reasons, one thing was clear. No institution had secretly removed their names from the original filing. All seven had signed the request themselves. The erasure had come later, when everyone forgot that Veridian had never been a man.

Bart reached the final section of the archival packet.

HISTORICAL ATTRIBUTION ROOTS: 7

CURRENT SUCCESSOR CLAIMANTS: 3

DISCIPLINARY CONSOLIDATION RECORD: ATTACHED

All three professors immediately moved closer to the desk.

"Successor claimants?" Hester asked, her voice tightening.

"Three," Conrad said, his eyes narrowing.

Maud looked at the other two. The fragile, stunned truce that had settled over them began to crack.

Gregor slowly backed away from the table.

"Boss," Gregor whispered.

"Yes?"

"I think the turf war is coming back."

Bart opened the heavy consolidation record. He looked at the chart that traced exactly how the seven original fields had been absorbed into the three modern departments over the centuries.

He looked at the chart then he looked at all the three professors.

None of them appeared to like his expression.

"What?" Hester demanded.

Bart adjusted his glasses.

"You are all going to be disappointed."
