I had always believed Aurum to be more than just a horse. There’s an intelligence in his deep, soulful eyes that speaks volumes without a single word. So when I caught him staring at the spot Olethros had previously occupied, I couldn’t help but wonder what profound insights had been shared between them.

Maybe Aurum had been somehow hostile?

“Just to be clear, demons aren’t the bad guys now, you know,” I muttered softly, running a brush through Aurum’s mane. He snorted lightly, gently nudging against my hand. “I mean, as a group. I’m not saying individuals aren’t subject to variation or anything. But Olethros is on our side, okay?”

He tossed his head just as I finished another pass.

“Hmm…”

Olethros’s sudden departure still unsettled me. It felt like there was something more to it, something lurking beneath the surface that I couldn’t quite grasp.

“… You’re not jealous of him, are you?”

Aurum flicked his ears back and forth, his tail swishing as he fixed me with a pointed stare.

“Sheesh, it was just a question. No need to sass me about it.”

“Mrowl?”

I turned just in time to see Chester saunter through a mirror portal, eyeing Aurum with a calculating glint.

“Uhm, Aurum, this is Chester. He’s… well, you can probably tell. And Chester, this is Aurum, my horse. I really hope you two can get along.”

Chester made his way between my boots and sat, his ears rigid as he stared up at the horse.

“Er… I think he’ll need proper accommodations. I’m not sure if we have any place for a stable, though… Or if that’s even possible? I don’t… um…”

Nora had tried to explain the inner workings of Aziza’s Bastion to me, but I lost the thread about three sentences into her dissertation. I did know that more occupants somehow increased their available resources and specializations, which ran directly counter to every scientific principle I could poorly cite. After the sixth or seventh attempt, Nora finally said its magical capacity operated on an algorithm not unlike my own, and that I should stop worrying about it.

Chester glanced up at me, making sure to catch my eye before flicking his tail. Then he rose, stretched with a theatrical-like slowness, and deliberately brushed against my leg before stepping away.

“Well, pardon me,” I muttered, wondering if it was something I should ask Galenus about instead.

As I pondered, the cat padded toward Aurum and leaped effortlessly, landing squarely between Aurum’s shoulders.

“Ah! That’s not a good idea—Chester?”

He circled once, twice, and settled, exaggerating every biscuit he made.

Aurum, sedate as he could be, lifted his head as a mirror, wider than others, bloomed in front of them.

“Hold on,” I said quickly as Chester mewled. “Absolutely not!”

He’s my horse!

Aurum flicked his tail as he trotted through the mirror.

“Wait!”

Chester glanced back just as he passed through, his eyes half-lidded in satisfaction.

The portal narrowed the instant Aurum’s tail vanished, shrinking to a thin vertical seam before vanishing completely. The brush I had been using also disappeared, as if it knew I no longer had any use for it.

“You’ve got to be kidding me.”

Amos stood before a functional, full-length mirror, carefully inspecting his hair. I leaned against the adjacent wall, hoping to pull off a level of casualness that wasn’t too transparent.

“How’s it going?” I asked.

He didn’t look over, and his shoulders tensed a bit. “Every time I think I have it mastered, Lady Nora corrects me.”

I gave him a wry smile as I scratched my cheek. “She’s the best subject-matter expert out there, though.”

Amos paused, finally turning his gaze. “Even over you?”

“Especially over me,” I said without a second thought. “She has a perspective I could never see, truth be told.”

He let out a small, strained chuckle. It was an admission that he already knew why I was here.

“…May I speak with him?” I asked, barely louder than a whisper.

He straightened and folded his arms tightly behind him. “His Majesty has requested that he not be disturbed,” Amos replied cautiously, “unless it is an emergency.”

“No. It’s not an emergency.”

Overthinking something probably doesn’t count.

If I were being honest with myself, it was about here that I realized it probably wasn’t something Aurum had “said” at all.

It was something I had.

“I think I hurt him, somehow,” I half-confessed, my eyes finding the floor.

“He needs time to reconcile his thoughts,” Amos replied with an empathetic calm.

“But then... How do I fix this?” I burst out.

“I am not at liberty to discuss the matter.” Then, as if relenting, he added, “You likely did nothing wrong in the first place.”

“If that’s true, why would he be hiding from me?”

He studied me through his monocle. “Doing everything correctly does not guarantee a perfect outcome,” he said after a long moment. “Sometimes, it doesn’t even result in significant change. But that doesn’t mean we can’t try again.”

Except if the outcome is so bad that you don’t get the chance to try once more.

“…Fine. I’ll give him space. When he’s ready to talk about it, he knows where to find me.”

In the meantime, I’ll need to plan for contingencies in case I fail big time.

I have to make sure life and beyond go on for those still here.

“Miss Rachel?”

“Ah… Sorry. I was just thinking, er… I ought to check on Kiko and the other children.”

“No,” he said immediately, his robes fluttering in panic. “Th-they’re doing just fine.”

Pfft!

“…Just make sure Kiko and Edric don’t traumatize him any further,” I murmured, a dry grin tugging at my lips.

His eyebrows rose slightly. “Most would fear for the children’s safety, not His Majesty’s emotional well-being.”

I laughed before I could stop myself, but the sound died quickly. “I like to think I know him better than most—Oh! But not you. He’s… really lucky to have you watching over him,” I said, sincerity infused in every word.

“I am his elder,” Amos admitted as he shifted back and forth on his feet. “Under my watch, someday he may even fully mature.”

I stared at him for a moment.

He flinched. “Our conversation is private, of course?”

I bobbed my head, then leaned in, leaving it lowered.

“W-what are you doing?” he asked, his tone elevated with warning.

“Receiving affirmation?”

“Do not,” he said urgently, “ever pat or allow any demon other than His Majesty to pat your head.”

I frowned upon straightening. “Why? I thought that meant I trust you.”

“It does… and then significantly more,” he muttered. “This is just like him to only half-explain.”

“What’s the half he left out?”

He looked everywhere but at me. “It signifies… potential core-mate acknowledgment.”

Heat crept up my neck, spreading to my cheeks. “Oh.”

Oh.

Oh.

Oh fucking fuck.

We stared at opposite walls as we both squirmed, the silence oppressive.

“Well,” I said, squeaking at the highest register, “perhaps I should go check in with Galenus.”

“Yes,” Amos agreed at once, summoning a mirror portal for me. “That would be most wise.”

Galenus, as almost always, was in central command, surrounded by a myriad of holographic runes. They flickered constantly, updating as if they were some sort of magical readout.

“Do you ever get a chance to rest?” I asked.

“I do, at times,” he replied, concentrating on a pulsing topographical map. “Worry not of my ability to undertake my assigned tasks.”

“Well… It’s great you can handle them, but it’s important to have a good work-life balance, so I hear.”

Galneus looked up over the rims of his glasses. “Completing my tasks is my life. It is what makes me feel the most alive. The execution of informed action. To be able to process data and react as a result of its analysis. To have my lords depend on my judgment and ability.”

Lords?

“How many bosses do you have?” I asked.

Galneus turned slightly transparent. “Aziza is my Master. I serve Olethros in a temporary, contractual capacity. My connection with Ambrose is mutually beneficial; I have not felt the need to define our working hierarchy. And Lady Nora…”

“Is the bossiest of them all right now?”

Galenus quivered. “I… She…”

I patted his shoulder, making sure I didn’t come anywhere close to his head. “I’m sure you’re meeting all her expectations.”

He glanced at me guiltily. “I… apologize for what I did to her… and you… in the Maze. And… For studying you.”

“With the emotional torture and all?”

He actually shrank a little. “In retrospect… yes. I can see my tasks then were driven by personal bias and prejudice.”

“Me too,” I admitted. “Me too.”

Project was certainly a term one could use… I guess.

“And… if she still refuses?”

He stiffened, his eyes hardening. “Then I will not allow her entry here. I will… Terminate my allegiance to her.”

“I hope it doesn’t come to that,” I murmured.

The map he was looking at flickered, and I saw three familiar figures, two humans and a tiger, flash repeatedly over Fort Turri.

What are those guys up to anyway?

Galenus was probably sworn to secrecy.

“And what of General Ragnerus? What do you think will change his mind?”

Galenus scowled, his hands reaching for his goat horns. “There is only one type of data he accepts. You came close to convincing him once. As the tiger clan would say, thrash him again.”

Will gleamed with anticipatory delight.

“Isn’t there any other—”

“I’ve already prepared a training ground for you,” Galenus said, tapping a few runes in the air. “Once Tetora comes back, I’m sure you will be…” He grinned, exposing his fangs. “Put to task.”
