Elarion and I hurry back to the settlement as fast as we can. I offered to carry him while I fly, with Catch and Pride sheltering in my tattoo, but he turned down the offer. When I see how he creates and uses vines of slippery ice to essentially skate across the ground as fast as I can fly, I understand why.

The scene that meets our eyes when we reach the square where Layton directed us is one of only slightly controlled chaos. The person causing all the problems has been restrained, but continues to attempt to lunge at anyone nearby, whether that may be the guards pinning her arms behind her back or any passersby. The white robes of the healers are already on site, treating what looks like bite and scratch wounds on a surprisingly large group of people. The crowds, some of whom are only just now coming out of their hiding places, are beginning to gather again, concerned murmurs washing over me as I fly in.

I'll need to be careful how I handle this. I don't want people worrying that slaves or anyone else are suddenly going to go feral or getting the idea that taking out frustrations on those around is acceptable.

For a moment I allow myself to feel my own frustration – can’t anything go right? And then I take a deep breath and push it down along with my fear of failing these people who are trusting me to lead them. They won’t do anyone any good.

Deciding that the healers have it under control for now and that further decisions will have to wait until I know exactly what happened, I land beside where the woman with a silver collar around her neck is being held. Narrowing my eyes, I realise I vaguely recognise her – she's one of the slaves taken after the battle with Kyrian and his allies. I can't immediately bring her crimes to mind – there was so much happening at the time – but a quick review of her contract would tell me. What I can say is that she can't have shown violent impulses before now, otherwise I would have obliged her to Bond, as I did with a few others of her cohort who had been sentenced for assault, murder, or rape.

Looking into her fevered eyes, it's clear she's not in her right mind – as if the way she's growling quietly under her breath and her tendency to snap at anyone within range weren't indication enough. I sigh. I'm not going to punish someone for being sick.

"Take her to the healing rooms," I tell the guards quietly. "Make sure she's bound so she's no danger to herself or others, and keep a close eye on her until I come."

The guards dip their heads – the gesture of respect limited by the fact that they need to keep hold of their prisoner – and then, expending considerably more effort than should really be necessary for a non-Classer, haul her away.

My peripheral vision catches someone moving closer to me, and I turn to see Layton approaching, a worried frown creasing his brow.

"What happened?" I ask quietly, my eyes flickering toward the discontented crowd, whose murmurs have only grown since the prisoner was taken away.

Layton is silent for a moment, then sighs.

"I don't know exactly. I was training a group of guards when a messenger came pelting in, gasping about a fight in the marketplace. I made my way here quickly, only to find that someone had gone mad – had foregone any weapons in favour of using her teeth and nails to bite and rip at anyone she could catch. When I saw the silver collar around her neck, I knew she was one of yours. That's when I called you." He pauses. "Fortunately, the guards I'd been training with had followed close behind and were able to subdue her and calm the crowds."

"Then you have no idea what caused it?"

Layton shakes his head regretfully.

"Truth be told, Markus," he murmurs, drawing closer so those around are less likely to overhear, "I'm not entirely surprised that someone has cracked. This is a difficult situation for all of us."

I make a quiet sound of agreement as I consider what we’ve faced in the last few weeks alone.

"And the slaves didn't choose to be here," I point out. "In some ways they may be more vulnerable to a breakdown."

Layton neither agrees nor disagrees.

"Either way, it's happened. What do you intend to do about it?"

"First, I need to find out why. She didn't look rebellious to me – she looked sick." I sigh. "Based on what I find, I'll decide what needs to happen after that. It may mean interviewing all of the slaves to check whether any others are approaching a psychological edge."

I don't finish that thought. I don't want to consider it further until I know exactly what's going on, because the truth is that with everything so close to the line right now, we don't have the resources to deal with multiple people having a breakdown. But, as this has just shown, we can't afford to ignore it either.

"Let's get eyewitness accounts," I say, returning to the immediate situation. "Maybe something triggered her. We also need to interview those who work with her – they might have noticed something beforehand that were clear signs of a psychological break. Or it may be due to something else like eating the wrong kind of food. If we can isolate the reasons, we might be able to help her better – and prevent it from happening again."

Layton nods, a grim look in his eyes, and we separate to do exactly that. I reach out to Maxen to see if he's available to help, and he agrees to bring a small group of his most trustworthy soldiers. Layton, with the same idea, begins organising the remaining guards to spread out and take accounts.

Heading into the crowd myself, I field all sorts of questions and concerns from my shaken people. They expected this place to be safe from the dangers outside and who are now disconcerted to find there may also be dangers from within. It’s been months since the trial where saboteurs were identified, months of working together and presenting a united front against the world that’s trying to destroy us.

My words of reassurance turn hollow as I keep repeating them. But there's little else I can do right now since I don't yet know the cause, or whether this is a one-off or something likely to happen to others. The last thing I want is for everyone to be looking at each other in suspicion, wondering who is going to crack next.

I can only hope it isn't a result of the experimental processed food. We believe there's little Energy in it, but for all I know there could be other toxins building up. The lead people once used in pipes contributed to lead toxicity in the body, often affecting the brain and causing hallucinations, psychosis, and aggression. I find myself wondering whether something similar could be happening here. If so, we need to find it and solve it quickly.

As the crowds begin to clear – either heading home or returning to the normal flow of the marketplace, albeit far more subdued than usual – I spot Elarion leaning over the well at the centre of the square, peering down into its depths.

"Dealing with crowds is thirsty work," I say as I approach him, with a wavering attempt at humour. A smile flutters at the corner of my mouth, then fades. I'm honestly not feeling very amused right now.

Elarion shakes his head, his expression intent.

"I’m all but certain that something is down there," he murmurs quietly, "and yet all of my skills indicate it is nothing but water."

A cold chill runs down my spine as I remember my own uneasy feeling about the well in District five. That one is still closed, since none of us have had the time to inspect it in enough detail for my satisfaction. But this is District two.

Closing my eyes, I send my earth-magic through the wall of the well, then transform it into water mana to inspect the water itself. When that returns nothing unexpected, I run a normal Inspect, then an Inspect Item. None of it reveals anything of concern, though I have to admit my normal Inspect is slightly less detailed than I'd expect for a non-magical well. But perhaps that's precisely why it's less detailed; there simply isn't that much to say about it. Then, recalling my concerns earlier about lead poisoning, I use Metal-Shaping to search for unusual concentrations of any metals. Nothing out of the ordinary.

"I'm not getting anything," I admit. "But you felt something?"

Elarion hesitates.

"I didn't feel something, exactly," he begins slowly. "However, as a Class heavily linked to nature and the life within it, some of my senses are very attuned to living things. There is a shadow of something that is not there."

I turn to look at him, confused.

"A shadow of something that's not there. How can something that doesn't exist cast a shadow?"

Elarion meets my eyes, serious concern in his own.

"That is precisely what I'm wondering."

I swallow.

"Right," I say after a moment. "I'm going to close off this well too."

I place my hand to the wall to activate the emergency closure. Elarion watches with concern.

"Is that wise? District five is already closed. If we close this one, it will put considerably more pressure on the other three and mean that many people have to walk much further to fetch water, increasing their burden and reducing their productivity."

I hold his gaze steadily.

"You just told me there is a shadow being cast by something that doesn't exist. To me, that means either something exists that we can't detect, or some other effect is at work that we don't yet understand. Right now, I'm not willing to take any chances. Hopefully we'll get to the bottom of it sooner rather than later." I pause. "In fact, would you be willing to work with some of the other mages to try to figure out what exactly is triggering your senses – something the rest of us haven't been able to detect so far? And then check District five as well?"

"You wish me to prioritise this over the farming?" Elarion asks doubtfully.

I hesitate, then nod.

"So far you're the only one who's detected anything. I had a sense that something was wrong with the well in District five, but I don't feel the same here. And since our only water source is the wells, due to our distance from the river and the danger the forest poses right now, it's paramount that they remain safe to drink from. We'll be in crisis even faster from contaminated water than from the food situation. At least we have fish coming in for that." I pause. "Surely you can delegate some of your farmers to run initial experiments in your absence? You'll need a handful of mages to run tests on the wells, but not all of the mages you normally work with."

Elarion nods slowly.

"Delegation should be possible, though I would have preferred to be present at the start of any experiment to ensure it's set up correctly."

"Then perhaps spend a mark or two with the farmers first, and then return to the wells as soon as you can," I suggest.

"Wells – plural." Elarion repeats. "Are you speaking only of this one and District five?"

I shake my head.

"Given that I thought there was only a potential problem with District five and yet you've found something here too, I think it's worth you inspecting all of the wells to see if this shadow appears elsewhere. And looking for any other anomalies while you're at it."

Elarion considers that, then gives a brisk nod.

"Very well. That does seem logical. If you organise the mages to join me, I will speak to my farming team and delegate what I can. Ask the mages to meet me here in a mark – that should give me enough time to do what I must with the farming group."

Relief goes through me. Perhaps we'll finally be able to get to the bottom of whatever may be wrong with the wells.

"Good. While you do that, I'm going to go through the eyewitness accounts, organise the interviews of those close to her, and inspect the woman who went mad – to see if I can work out exactly what triggered her."

Elarion nods, though I can see his mind is already elsewhere, no doubt moving onto the experiments he intends to run. With a quick and somewhat absent-minded farewell, we separate – him heading off to meet with his farmers, me to collect the accounts that have been recorded.

We need to get to the bottom of this, I say to myself grimly as I head for the healing room with parchment in hand. I don’t know how much more the settlement – or I – can take.
