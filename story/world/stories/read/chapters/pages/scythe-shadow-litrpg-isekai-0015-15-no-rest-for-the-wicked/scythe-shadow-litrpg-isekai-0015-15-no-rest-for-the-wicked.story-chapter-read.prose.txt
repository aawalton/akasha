“Anyway,” Calliope said as she hopped to her feet. “You're taking all this rather well. I honestly wasn't sure what to expect. Maybe you're not a silkie after all." She winked at me then turned to head back toward the front of the tavern.

"What now?" I asked, standing up to follow, Nuzzle still latched onto my leg like a leech.

"Your Volition is full, so there's not really much else you need to do at the moment. If you want, I can show you how to phase back to Vitria. You're probably nearing time to wake there anyway. I know I am.”

My heart jumped. I'd only been back for a few moments before. Since all this began I'd barely had time to think about it. Derek and Avery were there waiting for me. Everything's gonna be okay.

I could go home. I could… Could I stay there? Just choose never to come back to Penumbra. Not have to deal with traveling to the Spire and earn my freedom at all?

I knew it wouldn't be that easy I already had my suspicions that this place would haunt my dreams for the foreseeable future, but I had to ask anyway.

"Calliope, do I have to return here? What's stopping me from staying in Vitria?"

She turned her head to me, but kept walking. "Sorry, Zephyr. There's nothing you can do. Every time you sleep you'll come back. Whether you like it or not. No rest for the wicked, am I right?" She grinned. "At least you'll have top notch company while you're here. And there's always the Spire to strive towards, like you said before."

My heart sank, suspicions confirmed. Leave it to her to shatter my hopes before they could even fully form. They were foolish hopes anyway, though.

But if that were the case, it meant I would never get to rest—mentally. In Penumbra while asleep, in Vitria while awake.

No rest for the wicked is right. What an understatement.

“How do you do it?” I asked as we walked. “Live your real life on top of this, I mean?”

Calliope shrugged. Nuzzle had made his way to her shoulder now, without me even noticing. Indecisive bastard.

“Honestly, I don’t know which is more real for me anymore. There's a part of me that can be free here, not constrained by societal expectations and all that, you know? Not just trying to fill a role I feel I'm supposed to fill. Sometimes it feels like I’ve spent more of my life in Penumbra than the ‘real world,’ as you call it.”

I shuddered at that prospect. “How long have you been doing this?”

“Fifteen years,” she said casually. “Got pulled in when I was ten.”

“Fuck…” I gaped at her.

She laughed, some life coming back into her cheeks. “That’s nothing. Jericho’s been going for thirty-two years. Best thing you can do, I figure, is separate who you are in Vitria from Penumbra.” She held up one hand, then the next. Nuzzle jumped into her right hand. “Two lives. One there. One here. Partition your mind and don’t think about the other until you’re there. When I'm here, Vitria doesn't exist. When I'm there, I've never seen Penumbra.”

“Easier said than done,” I grumbled. That would require some wild mental gymnastics.

“Isn’t everything?”

Calliope stopped by the post holding a lantern and the tavern’s sign. She flicked a fingernail on one of the glass panes with a tink.

“There are lanterns like this stationed all over the Umbral Plane,” Calliope said. “They act as phase points—essentially respawn locations. Whatever lantern you touch last, that’s where you’ll phase in next time you return.”

Nuzzle flattened his face against the glass. Calliope pried him away, his eyes popping away like suction cups. Then she rubbed his head, earning a gentle coo.

“And because I hadn’t touched a lantern before you so generously skewered my brain, I was sent back to where I originally arrived.”

“Exactly! You’re catching on.”

“So let’s say, theoretically, I didn’t want to rely on your icicle lobotomy to get back to Vitria.” I stepped forward and tapped the lantern blatantly with my middle finger so Calliope could see.

She grinned. “It’d be so fun not to tell you, but Jericho would eventually teach you anyway, so I guess I’ll cave and share the secret. It’s easy really. All you have to do is close your eyes and tap your heels together three times, then say ‘There’s no place like home.’”

I snatched Nuzzle from her hand and placed him on my shoulder. He licked my cheek with his scratchy tongue and chirped happily. “Nice try. How about the version that doesn’t require ruby slippers and a yellow brick road?”

“Okay, fine…” She turned so her back faced the lantern, then pointed to the ground. “Just stand so your shadow is on the ground in front of you. Doesn’t have to be by a lantern. Could be in the middle of the forest, in a dungeon, literally anywhere as long as you're casting a shadow. Close your eyes and imagine your body separating from your Will. Then fall forward into your shadow. No catching yourself though, or it won’t work.”

I raised my eyebrows skeptically. She clearly wanted me to fall flat on my face and embarrass myself. But the whole ritual was so obscure. There was no way she could’ve come up with that on the spot.

“Thanks,” I said. Instead of following her instructions, I waited, trying to gauge her reaction.

Her eyes lit up playfully. “You don’t believe me.”

“Haven’t decided yet.”

“If it will make you feel better, I can go inside while you break your nose—I mean—phase out to Vitria.” She grinned wickedly.

“You truly are the worst, aren’t you?”

“It’s a matter of perspective.”

I shook my head and sighed, gesturing toward the tavern door.

Her mouth opened, then shut. “Wow, you’re actually gonna send me in?”

“Yup.”

“No faith…”

“Zero.”

Calliope headed to the door, waving as she went. She opened it and placed one foot beyond the threshold, then called back. “Just remember, face first. And don’t try to catch yourself, otherwise you’ll be out here looking like a fool!”

She started to close the door but stopped. “I truly am sorry, Zephyr… That you were pulled into all this. Enjoy your time back home.”

The door clunked shut, and she disappeared.

I shook my head and looked at the orange salamander on my shoulder. “What do you think, Nuzzle? Am I being hazed?”

He rolled onto his hip and started licking his own crotch.

“You always know just what to say…”

With any spectators gone, I turned so my shadow rested on the ground before me. Supposedly, my ticket back home, at least temporarily. But I couldn’t help but dwell on something Calliope said. ‘Partition your mind.’ To not think about Penumbra while I was in Vitria, and vice versa. That’s not something I could do. I had only just arrived here, and it was already a part of my soul.

Zephyr was Miles. Miles was Zephyr.

Both were me.

And I was a killer.

Sure, they were monsters. And there was maybe some holier-than-thou reason to end them if what that golden god being told me had any truth to it. But still… It was my hand—my scythe—that took their lives, whatever they were.

Affinity: Death.

Was there a reason for that? For me of all people to have that affiliation?

I shuddered. Death was something I'd tried to cut from my thoughts. Innumerable hours of therapy hadn't prepared me for this though. Confronting it head-on. Confronting what I'd…

Nuzzle licked my cheek again, sensing my distress.

No. I pushed the thoughts from my mind. Keep pushing forward. No looking back.

I stepped up to the lantern. My reflection shone on the glass in the fire's light. A face… My own, yet not. Familiar and foreign.

I'd do what needed to be done to survive. And then I'd find a way out. I'd make it to the Spire.

My shadow waited below. Through that portal, my fiancé, my brother, my real life.

The draw of that world pulled me forward.

If they want to make me a killer, then it's a killer they'll get.

I closed my eyes and fell forward.

The world spun rapidly around me, then I was gone.
