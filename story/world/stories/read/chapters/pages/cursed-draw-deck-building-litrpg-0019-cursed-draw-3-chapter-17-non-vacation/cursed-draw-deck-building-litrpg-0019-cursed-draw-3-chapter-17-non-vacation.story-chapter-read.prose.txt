Gwenia Creed had essentially been demoted. Within the space of a few days, she had gone from being the first minister and de facto ruler of the entire Final Peaks alliance and its population in the hundreds of thousands to leading an expedition of around two hundred and fifty people.

Normally, such an enormous drop in her authority would have been a devastating blow for someone as driven as she was. Instead, the return of Soren and assignment to her current task had come as not only a relief but an opportunity that left Gwenia’s heart racing with excitement.

Soren’s overinflated Deck Sprite had pinpointed the location of a new continent that was on the verge of surfacing. It had been more than a hundred years since the island that had become the Yellow Shores nation had arisen in the western Turquoise Sea, and that had been a small enough landmass that mapping it out and finding its Chaos Lock had barely taken a few days. This newly arriving land was predicted to be almost as large as the Mucklands.

Gwenia had taken on the role of first minister as she both believed in Soren's vision for the Final Peaks and in her own abilities as an administrator. It had nothing to do with where her passions lay. What Gwenia saw herself as was a scholar and a scientist. It was a significant portion of why she had backed Soren years ago when he'd initiated his coup. After all, she couldn't be the one conducting the experiments if she were the test subject. It also went without saying that with so few God-Ranked people left in the world, she’d be giving up a rare opportunity to observe one if she ceased to support Soren.

While the final five ranks had never been exactly common, as the number of Resonances that could be harnessed had gone up over the centuries, the frequency of people achieving a God-Rank had dropped drastically. At first glance, that seemed almost paradoxical, but if one considered it, the issue began to make sense.

Just as Resonance flowed from the leader of a sect, nation, or any other organization into those who placed their faith in that person. So too did it flow upwards from the believers into the head of the organization. It was a phenomenon known all over the world by many names, including Resonant Authority or Faith. That was all well and good if everyone in a group made use of the same or similar Resonance, like how the Void Summoner used Void, the Heaven Seekers Divinity, or the Gorret Brotherhood Fire.

In the modern era, that wasn’t really how things worked, though. Most people used whatever Deck they could get their hands on, and with all the different types out there, it became far too risky to build a connection with those under you. That was all well and good if you were simply going to rule your nation from an aloof seat away from the average Deck wielder.

While this was a fine system, it made ‘touching the pillar of divinity’ all but impossible for most rulers, since they needed to draw on the Resonance systems of thousands of people to get beyond rank six. Soren on the other hand, had been able to cheat his way through the mortal ranks because his body had the unique ability to convert Resonance of any type into Life Essence. Rather than have to win matches or evolve Cards, the most powerful of the Creeds had simply fed upon the Resonance sent up the chain of authority by his followers until his Resonance system had swollen to the point where it had no choice but to Rank up.

It was a fascinating process to study, and one Gwenia had devoted many hours of thought and research into attempting to replicate. Sadly, as best she could tell, the only way to create another Soren would be to collect children and send them through the ‘gate to the heavens’ at the top of the former Heaven Seekers guild and hope for the best. Considering Gwenia and the rest of the Creed children had all been sent through the gate dozens of times without changing in the way Soren had, it wasn’t a truly viable path to replicating the god.

That was especially true given the other ways in which the children had been affected. While it was true that the Creeds tended to have talents or abilities not possessed by regular Deck wielders, they also tended to be so narcissistic that it bordered on lunacy.

It had hurt Gwenia’s pride to abandon that particular research project. If there was anyone in the world who should have been able to come up with a solution for that problem, it was her. As a matter of fact, that was true of all mysteries of the natural and unnatural world. She was Gwenia, and while she was the fourth or fifth most powerful Creed, her mind was second to none among her siblings or anyone else, for that matter.

It had been Gwenia who kept the Final Peaks united and serving Soren whilst he had gone off to shirk his responsibilities for the better part of two years. Granted, the winged boy had returned more powerful than ever before and had showered the majority of the Creeds with Cards that no one else could have created, but he hadn't understood the precarious nature of the situation he'd left.

By insisting all of the positions of major authority go to a Creed, Soren had created an upper class that was made up entirely of narcissists who each individually believed they knew better than anyone else. Preventing the Final Peaks from devolving into a dozen factions vying for supremacy while Soren was away had been a nightmarish amount of work.

Leading this expedition to explore a new land wouldn’t be easy, but compared to what she had been doing, it should prove both simpler and far more exciting. Especially knowing that Gwenia was supported by two other Creeds,  thirty paladins, ten priests, five professional scholars, virtually unlimited funds, and roughly a hundred mundane sailors and soldiers. It was hardly an invasion force like the one Savat, Ellie, and Fyera had been sent south with, but it also didn’t need to be.

What it needed to be was a group that could both move quickly enough to seize the Chaos Lock of the new continent first, whilst also being able to create new Cards and otherwise study what the newly arisen land had to offer.

That was why Gwenia had taken her expedition north into the Cerren Empire and requested a galley from the Cerens. The empire ran for hundreds of miles north, controlling everything from the northern edge of the Final Peaks to the tribal lands of the Thorn Aegis. In terms of territory and population, the empire was drastically greater than the Final Peaks, something that had never been the defining factor in the relationship of the two nations.

Even before the rise of Soren and the other Creeds, the Final Peaks alliance had consistently produced far more powerful Decks and Deck wielders than their neighbors. So much so, in fact, that the Cerren Empire was functionally a vassal state of the Peaks, who could easily obliterate the empire’s ruling family at any moment without having to commit to a true war.

Knowing this, the Cerren’s had not objected to Gwenia’s request for a ship and a force to crew it. It was a good thing too as while the Final Peaks produced minds and players of great ability, they did not have a navy nor the expertise to build and make use of one.

The expedition that Gwenia was leading had only left the Cerren port city of Gastapon a day and a half ago. Given the Cerren expertise with boats and the complete lack of it from the Peakers, Gwenia’s force didn’t have a lot to do but stay out of the way and enjoy the sights of the Jade Sea. Like the Silver Sea and the Golden Sea, the Jade Sea practically shone in the light of the morning sun. It turned the water almost entirely see-through for dozens of feet, and allowed  one to witness the schools of fish and other aquatic life that populated the second-oldest body of salt water in the world.

It would eventually be a problem as the force she had brought slowly got bored and restless with the trip, but Gwenia herself wasn’t worried about her own enjoyment. Upon boarding the galley, she had a small pavilion set up in the center of the main deck. Obviously if the ship had to deal with storms or whatever other dangers the sea could provide she would go below deck to the cabin that had been set aside for her, but for now it gave her a place from which she could watch the dozens of rowers work the oars, or observe the beautiful sees, but most importantly it meant she could recline in the sunlight of the open pavilion, or in the shade of the great sails while she worked on her journals and written ideas for future experiments.

The plan had never been to treat this trip like a vacation, and once they drew near the new continent, she would have to focus intensely, for now, though Gwenia intended to treat the trip as restfully as she could.

The leader of the expedition had finished sketching an idea she’d had for a winch system that combined Earth and Pressure crystals, and was napping in a reclining chair with a futon atop it in the mid-afternoon sunlight when she was awoken by Crusader Avartin, who commanded her paladins.

Yawning, the fire-eyed woman turned her gaze on the paladin, who cut a striking dark-haired figure while he was out of his bronze armor.

“Lady Minister,” he said seriously. “I’m sorry to wake you, but we are being pursued by some sort of sea-chariot pulled by Mechkin dolphins.”

Gwenia let out a small groan and pulled herself to her feet. To the best of her knowledge, there was only one person who used such a method of conveyance, and he should be south, leading the invasion force amongst the dunes.

“Inform the captain to rest the rowers and let the chariot catch up with us.”
