"You lied to me." Ash stared. The words had come out sharper than I had intended, and yet not sharper than they deserved to. The chamber was empty, and the doors had sealed behind the Inker's retreating back. It was just the two of us now.

"What?" Ash's voice was careful. Her hand had extended towards me, and it still hovered there. The confusion on her face was plain enough. She was hurt. I could see it in the set of her jaw, in the way the warmth left her eyes. Good. That meant she was listening.

I held her gaze. "You are deliberately holding yourself back. Did you think that I would not notice? Do you consider me a fool?"

"Lys, I don't understand what you're-"

"Your core filled at the same time as mine." Ash went still. "My mana core," I continued, "is many times the size of yours. You should have filled yours long before I filled mine. The only way they finished at the same time is if you slowed yourself down. Deliberately." I folded my arms. "As for this business of forming an Essence Core -it is not that complex. You said you did not understand the method that man explained. I think too highly of you to believe you could not have figured it out. You are the slayer of the Demon Queen. You are not a fool. Do not take me for one either."

The silence stretched between us.

Slowly, Ash's hand fell down to the side. Her lips quivered, and she looked past me, at the far wall. She looked smaller than she had just a few moments ago. The Hero kept her gaze there for a long time. "Yes, you're right," she said eventually. "I slowed down."

"Why?" I pressed.

She did not answer. She did not answer for so long that I pressed again. "Was the pain too much? I find that difficult to believe, given what you must have endured when you-"

"It wasn't the pain." Her voice was low.

"Then what was it?"

Ash was quiet for a long time. I waited. Heroes were famously stubborn. Even more than Demon Queens. Still, I would have this answer from her. If that meant we stayed like this for hours, then so be it. Eventually, Ash spoke. "I didn't want to be alone."

I stared at her. "What?"

Color rose from under her collar. She still would not look at me. "I just…wanted to do it with you. I wanted to do it at the same time. You know…like…together." Her voice grew lower the more she spoke, and by the end, it was barely more than a murmur.  Her hand moved to the back of her neck and stayed there.

Anger rose in me and much of it. Enough that it drowned out anything else I might have felt. "Do you think I am that weak?" I demanded. "Do you think I need your pity?" My voice had never been sharper. "Who do you think-"

Ash's head snapped toward me. "It wasn't pity!"

I glared. "Then what was it?"

"I just told you what it was!" Her voice rose, and for one moment, the Hero's composure broke entirely. She met my gaze, and her eyes were wide. "I wanted to do it with you. That's all. That's all it was. It wasn't pity."

I rose. I rose to my full height and strode towards her. Ash stared up at me as I drew close. Her brow was furrowed and her cheeks were flushed. I leaned down over her and put both hands on her shoulders. Ash was very still, and very warm. I had not expected the latter, though I could not have said what I had expected instead.

"What- what do you think you're doing?"

I shook her. Once. Not hard enough to hurt, but hard enough to make her head rock back until her hair covered her eyes.

"You are going to listen to me," I said. "I am going to say this once. I will never repeat myself. So, you will listen, and you will remember." She stared up at me. My hands tightened on her shoulders.

"You will reach your full potential. You will never hold yourself back on my account. That is an insult to me. It implies you can reach a height I cannot, and there is no such height that I will not surpass." I paused. "If you are presently ahead, then cherish it. It will not last."

This was beneath me. I had shown too much. Ash's lips had parted. Still, the Hero did not say a thing.

I looked away from her, at the far wall, just as she had done moments prior. When I spoke, my voice came out softer than it usually did. "Ash, in my time, it was always I who left others behind. I left behind every general I had and every rival I might have had." I paused, but only because the next words were hard to say. A lump in my throat that I had to force out. "This feeling -of being the one left behind- is not unpleasant. But do not misunderstand me. I will master this. Under no circumstances will you ever hold yourself back again on my account."

I met her gaze again. My red eyes bored into her blue ones. "Or I will never forgive you."

Ash stared at me. She stayed exactly where I had left her for two breaths longer than the moment required. Ash nodded. "Okay," she said.

I held the Hero's gaze. Finally, I released my grip on her shoulders and took a step back. Something about the distance between us felt different.

I sat cross-legged on the cool stone and closed my eyes. The marks hummed on my arms -Requiem on the right, Cradle on the left. I breathed and fed the smallest threads of both Essences into it.

I used too little of each power for it to result in what had happened before. I did not know what the Inker had done to stop me before, but I did not think I or Ash could replicate it now.

The pain came, and the carving began. It lasted all of ten seconds before the two annihilated each other. It happened in the exact same way as before. The Requiem's carving tore through the Cradle's, and the Cradle's tore through the Requiem's. I adjusted my approach. Perhaps the trick lay in alternating the two. I fed my core the Requiem first, then the Cradle a few moments later. They annihilated each other.

I tried feeding them to opposite sides of the core, keeping them as far apart as I mentally could. They found each other anyway and destroyed what the other had made. My jaw clenched. The pain from each failure was one thing, but the frustration burned brighter still. It died a few moments later. That was new.

Ash spoke from in front of me. Her voice was quiet. "This is the first time."

I opened one eye. "The first time for what?"

"The first time someone hasn't minded being left behind."

I opened the other eye. Ash was staring down at her own hands. There was nothing out of the ordinary to see. "It happened before?" I asked.

"It has happened many times." She turned one hand over, studying the palm. "All of my friends when I was growing up. The other candidates, before I got the Hero class. Every party they ever paired me with, before the final one." She paused. "They all resented me for it. Maybe not at first, but eventually they did. Always. So I learned to be…a little less."

I looked at the purple Spark on the Hero's right arm, one that stayed a Spark, even as her other mark had become a Line. The System had dubbed it with the name 'Veil'. A veil, for a Hero who had already spent one lifetime hiding.

Fools had looked at this woman and made her believe that she should be lesser than she was. That she should shine less brightly than she did. In that moment, I wished I could find every one of those fools, and give them the end I had given to so many others. They were more deserving than most.

I spoke, and my voice was hard. "That is foolish. Even if the entire world resented you, you should never think of such a thing again. There is no point in concerning yourself with the opinions of the weak and the simple."

Ash glanced at me. There was something almost fragile in her expression.

"How am I supposed to just stop? It's been…it's been my whole life, Lys."

"Because I will never resent you for being excellent," I said. "Do not forget who you are. You are the slayer of Lysanthia, the Strongest who has ever lived. If that does not earn you the right to stand at your full height, then nothing ever will."

Ash stared at me. The fragile thing in her expression shifted into something else. "You definitely let me win," she murmured.

"That final attack made me nervous," I admitted. Once, I would have never been able to. "It was quite something. You did destroy my castle with it, I would imagine."

"I think I did." She grinned. It was small and crooked and entirely unlike a Hero's smile. I found my own lips twitching in answer. The silence that followed was one of the warmer ones.

"Well?" I said. "Do it, then. There should be nothing stopping you now."

Ash closed her eyes, and the air around her changed. I could see the mana moving with my eyes. The air around her was distorted, flowing into her in thick streams. The chamber walls around us all flickered, and I felt the taste of Essence from her. The taste of Separating. And of Hiding. Ash was truly going to carve her core then.

I watched Ash, but I also continued my own work, because a Queen did not simply sit idle while her retainer labored.  I drew in Essence from the Requiem and the Cradle. The result was the same. When they met in my core, each destroyed what the other built before either could carve anything. I tried different approaches. I tried to use more of the Requiem and less of the Cradle. I tried to use more of the Cradle and less of the Requiem. There was only pain.

Ash's lips quivered, and then her face twisted. The expression was impossible not to notice from where I sat. Then her shoulders tightened, and her hands that had been flat on her knees curled into fists.

I had stopped trying to draw in Essence altogether. My attention shifted to Ash instead, and my own failed efforts mattered little and less. The currents of mana around Ash were growing thicker and more numerous by the second.

Then the Hero let out a small groan. Her entire body trembled. I knew that the currents  raging around her would have flattened a lesser person. I moved through them. The mana currents pressed against me and I did not feel them. I knelt beside her and hesitated. What was I doing?

Ash groaned again, and this time it was softer. It sounded no less pained. I reached out and put a hand on her shoulder. Ash winced. My grip was too tight, and I loosened it. I was one of the Ten Fools. Whatever pain she was going through, a clumsy touch would not-

Ash settled beneath my touch. The trembling eased. She did not open her eyes. I stayed.

Time passed and I did not track it. The egg pulsed against my hip. It had been pulsing for some time. I tried one last time. One last time, I reached inside my core with a thread of Requiem and Cradle Essence. The result was no different than all the others. I stopped. The mana in the chamber had thinned around us. That was why I could not focus. It had nothing to do with the sound Ash had made.

Near the end of the carving, I saw Ash do something. Her left hand rose and she pressed it flat against her own chest. For a moment, the black mark of her Sever seemed to shine. She drew her hand down in a single motion.

That was Parting. It had to be. She had…turned it inward, but why? This boundary is an added limitation. That was what the Inker had said, and his words came back to me now. Had Ash…? Surely not.

All I could do was stare at the Hero. The precision of what she had done was no small thing. No, it was not just that. It was also the willingness to turn your own power against yourself, to cut yourself open from the inside. Just for a chance at greater power. It was either the bravest or the most reckless thing I had ever seen a person do, and I had done both in ample measure.

There was a mistake the weak always made when it came to power. They were always drawn to power that came easily. That came safely, without risk. There is no power in this world worth having that comes without risk. The world is made of only two types of people: those who can see this and those who are fools. I smiled.

Eventually, the currents slowed until I could no longer see them. The pressure in the chamber eased until the air was just air again.

Ash slumped forward, and her breathing came in ragged gasps. Sweat ran down from her temples and onto the floor below. Her hands were pressed flat against the ground, and her arms were shaking. "Did it work?" I asked. I knew the answer.

She nodded, slowly. A small smile played across her lips. "I…I think so." Her voice was raw. "I can feel the mana inside me changing. It's…" She swallowed. "The Inker wasn't wrong. It really does feel like something alive crawling through you. Don't think there's a better way to describe it." She looked at me. At my hands, still at my sides.

Her small smile died. She hunched in on herself, just slightly. She was shrinking again, as she had done once before. I liked the sight of it the second time even less.

Both marks hummed on my arms. The Requiem and the Cradle, still incompatible, still destroying what the other tried to build. The mountain sat before me, still unclimbed. "Show me," I said.

Ash blinked. "What?"

"Your Essence. Flare it like you would mana. It should work the same. Show me what you've made."

Ash hesitated. She looked at me. "Are you sure? I don't have much of it yet, but still…"

"I am sure."

Ash closed her eyes, and I felt it. Every instinct I had tensed at once. For a moment, Ash might as well have been a hundred-foot-tall giant, and I the most diminutive ant. Something inside of me seized. It took all my regal bearing not to simply fall over. My heart pounded in my chest. This was my instinct telling me that the Hero was dangerous, and that I, in comparison, was not.

Ash stopped and her presence vanished. She was on her hands and knees in an instant, crawling toward me, her face stricken. "I'm sorry! I'm sorry, I didn't mean to- are you-"

I smirked up at her. "What are you talking about, you fool?" Ash froze. I held her gaze.

The smirk did not waver. "That is exactly what I'd expect from my retainer."
