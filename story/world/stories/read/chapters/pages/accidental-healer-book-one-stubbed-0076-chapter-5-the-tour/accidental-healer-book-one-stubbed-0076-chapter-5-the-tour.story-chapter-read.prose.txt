“A mission!” I said while simultaneously opening the message on the status screen.

Order of Tranquility

Members: Durkil (squire), Norso (squire)

Acclaim: 0

Objectives: A nearby faction has requested aid to resolve a level 45 anomaly that will soon open. Rewards: Acclaim: 5, 10,000 UBCs.

“Huh...”

Jared studied my face. “What is it?”

I scratched my chin.

“Nothing, it's just not that exciting of an objective. 10,000 UBCs and five acclaim.”

“What did you expect? It’s your first objective.”

Jared was, of course, right. This was our first mission and honestly it arrived faster than I’d expected it to. But 10,000 UBCs? That was basically nothing. Was it even worth going?

Again, of course it would be. Even if the rewards weren’t great, the faction who’d requested help was asking for a reason. Plus, my gut told me that the real reward would be the acclaim that would be earned.

It would also be great to start meeting some of the other factions in the region.

“Yeah alright, fair point.” I conceded.

“Norso.”

Although I hadn’t seen him yet this morning, I knew that he’d be nearby.

Gravel crunched behind me.

“Sir.”

“Were you able to see the new objective for the order of Tranquility?” I asked the dark elf.

“I saw there was one, however I couldn’t see the details.”

That made sense. Most likely he’d need a different title in the order to see or accept missions.

“It’s a simple level 45 anomaly that will open soon. Do you think you and Durkil could take care of it?”

Normally I’d jump at an opportunity to go and meet another faction but with so much happening here I wasn’t ready to go just yet. Norso was already at level 47 and Durkil was level 55. Combine that with the fact that they were powered by layers of titles that boosted their stats, and you had two extremely powerful elites.

A level 45 anomaly would be childs play for those two.

“Consider it done.” And before I could add anything else, he was already headed off presumably to locate Durkil.

I quickly re-opened the screen for the order, and opened the screen with the objective. There was a prompt that read:

Assign members of your order to this objective?

Below the prompt were Durkil and Norso’s names. I selected them both.

Members assigned. Location and objective details have been provided.

I nodded, satisfied. That was easy enough.

“That dark elf really is eager, isn’t he?” Jared observed, following where Norso ran off.

“Eager is an understatement.” I agreed. “But I like him.”

“Do you think they can do it alone? We could always send them with someone.” Jared asked, hands on hips.

I waved my hand dismissively.

“They’ll be fine. If the faction their helping can’t even handle a level 45 anomaly, they aren’t dangerous, plus they haven’t been overrun yet so no one around them is going to be a threat either.”

Jared grunted his agreement.

***

A short walk later led us to my parents.

We entered the open pavilion of their teleporter. The space reminded me of our own, but I found it interesting that they weren’t identical. We’d both earned blueprints from the system.

Theoretically, they should have been the same.

In my opinion, the one in my faction was more aesthetic. Wide arches, tile mosaics, pillars, and large plazas created a Greco-Roman theme in Layton-Mischief Township. New Boise's felt more functional, still beautiful, but it was more hard dark lines and less open.

Martin was surrounded by a group of about twenty or so people, my mom being one of them. His voice echoed through the empty chamber.

“I can’t imagine a situation where travel between territories wouldn’t be completely open.”

My feet echoed through the chambers, and he looked up to see Jared and I waiting.

“But why don’t we just ask them?” He said, gesturing for us to join him.

The group turned their attention to us. With an awkward shuffle, a lane parted and we stepped through it, eyes watching every step.

“Layton, you’re looking at the city council for New Boise. These fine people have all kinds of questions, like for example, will there be free travel between territories?”

I joined my dad and he grinned at patted me on the shoulder.

“Why wouldn’t there be? Were all one faction now, aren’t we?”

A plump middle-aged man in overalls spoke up.

“What about migration?”

“Migration?” I repeated with a glance toward my dad.

“Apparently there are quite a few residents of New Boise interested in moving their residence into your territory.”

“Why?” I wondered genuinely confused. “Have any of them even seen our territory?”

“Not your territory, no. But they’ve seen your faction members.”

“People want to feel safe.” A woman in the front in a yellow flowery dress added.

“You are safe.”

“Because of your factions' strength.” The plump man pointed out.

“Well, I mean, I don’t care where you all choose to settle in, but it's not like we're not going to leave this territory without protection. If you want to move over to one of our other settlements, though be my guest.”

The plump man seemed to like the answer, and he folded his arms with a nod.

“But you should know, it’s just as dangerous through that portal as it is here. Maybe more.”

He didn’t seem to care.

“If you do decide that you want to move, you will need to talk with Jared here. He’ll coordinate everything. What other questions do you have?”

“Are all the species in your faction friendly?” A younger man with bright blonde hair and green eyes asked hesitantly.

I laughed. “They’re friendly to our faction.”

“Yes, they are friendly.” Jared cut in. “Some might look fairly intimidating though.”

“Maybe steer clear of the catkin. They aren’t dangerous perse....you know what. We have the city council here, why not just pop on through and get the tour?”

There were murmurs of excitement throughout the group.

“I think we’d all enjoy that very much.” Martin said, beaming.

***

I stepped into the portal and felt the familiar warm water sensation washing over my skin as New Boise fell away, and my boots found purchase on the marbled floors of LM townships portal.

Soft buzzing sounded and I stepped forward clearing space for the others following behind me. I walked backwards with my hands up, gesturing at the white marble.

“Welcome, to LM Township.”

Wide eyes and slack jaws searched the bright room filled with dazzling shards.

“It’s beautiful.” Claire said, in a faraway dreamlike voice.

“Jared and his guys really did a wonder on this place.” I agreed and turned to wander out of the towering archways. “Come on, there's more to see.”

The entryway opened onto the sprawling plaza with a wide reflection pond and hedged paths.

“We might not have your fancy magic rotary mower, but we keep things pretty tight over here.” I informed my dad, hands on hips.

He whistled, and we kept moving, the group shuffling to keep up.

The cobbled paths lead us to LM Mainstreet.

It was a hive of activity. People entering and exiting shops and chatting and laughing in small groups as they went about completing the work of the day. Guildians, Dark Elves, Wolves, Blue Men, Goblins, Elder Gnomes and even some Catkin all filed past our small group.

One of the wolves wandered nearby, and the group all tightened closer to me.

“Sir.” The wolf said with a crisp nod and continued his business.

Hands wrap around my arm. “Layton....did that werewolf just nod at you?”

I looked down to see my mom watching the wolf closely.

“He’s technically just a wolf, I think. And yeah. Everyone in our faction can talk. It's a little weird but you get used to it.”

“What about those?” She pointed at one of the Catkin who just happened to be standing on a three-story building.

I grimaced remembering the time all three hundred of the little furballs all tried talking to me telepathically at once.

“Yes, they can talk also. But not verbally. At least not yet. So, if you have a weird random thought in a voice you don’t recognize, just ignore it.”

“They’re like Mischief then?” Martin asked.

“He wouldn’t love the comparison, but in the sense that they speak telepathically, yes.”

“Aren’t they cats like he is?” He asked as we passed Damon’s forge.

If it was just my parents with me, I would’ve stopped to make introductions but with the group we just moved on from the large building.

Damon was now a Guild Master, and the perks included more advanced objectives with better experience rewards, higher quality equipment available for purchase in the interface shop and better, if only very minimal, prices when selling equipment back to the system.

His forge was nearly getting too large for where it was situated in town. Jared told me that there were already plans to convert the old forge into an armory and move the actual forge nearer the ore mine.

“They aren’t cats. Well, I guess maybe they used to be cats. They were mana spawns that Mischief somehow tamed. They’re still learning table manners though.”

“Those are mana spawn?!” The plump man coughed.

“They were mana spawn. Now they’re Catkin. One of them was actually in Mischiefs litter, his name is Bracken. I’m trying to convince him to join the order of Tranquility but he’s not very tranquil yet. Good fighter though.”

My mom looped her arm through mine. “I’m happy to see how inclusive your faction is. It’s surprising that so many were willing to join you. Most non-human factions we met just wanted to fight.”

We wound our way through the wide cobbled city streets.

“It wasn’t easy.” I admitted. “Most we met wanted to fight also. But we always tried to give them the choice when possible.”

She pulled my arm tighter.

“You must’ve some great parents.”

I did.

After a little while I let Jared take over the tour, and I walked near the back of the group with my parents.

“It’s so beautiful.” Mom said. “I can’t believe how stunning it is.”

“She’s right, Layton. What you’ve accomplished here is incredible.”

“It didn’t start out that way.” I said with a chuckle. “You should’ve seen my first shelter.”

My mom patted me on the back. “I’m sure it wasn’t that bad.”

“It Was!” Jared shouted from the front of the group.

Martin raised an eyebrow and I nodded in mock shame.

As if on cue, our little group arrived at the open clearing where Mischief and I first met and completed our trial together. The clearing itself had been through its fair share of abuse after acting as the staging area to the many raids we fought.

Now though, it was essentially restored to its natural state. There were small differences though.

Pebble stone paths wound through the drifting tall grass fitted with sturdy wooden benches accompanied by delicate looking arches.

“This is where I met Mischief.” I informed my parents. “It's also where we fought together in our trial. Jared wants to maintain it as like a memorial or something, but I think it's silly.”

My parents and I lagged behind the group.

“You fought it, just the two of you...” My dad walked down the small slope through the pine trees and into the amber light of the sun. Mom followed.

“You must’ve been so scared.” She said and I could already see the water forming in the corner of her eyes.

We weren’t all that from the spot that the first wave of chaos spawn piled onto my body. Unconsciously my hand moved to my hip where one of the little demons tore off a chunk of flesh.

“It really wasn’t too bad.” I reassured her. “Besides, now look at me?”

I flexed a bicep then raised it to my lips to kiss it. We all shared a laugh. Despite literally everyone else experiencing clear changes to their physique, I remained nearly the exact same. Even my dad was packing on noticeable lean muscle, and he’d clearly grown several inches.

We passed through the meadow and crossed the wide bridge leading to Jessie’s fields. The trees were all cleared, and acres of fields sprawled before us. By now this section of the city was its own village. Homes and crept along the sloping mountain along with more crop storage.

My dad reached up and wiped his brow. “It's hot, like summertime hot, over here.”

“That’s because Jared spent a small fortune purchasing some kind of weather apparatus thing that's powered by mana shards.” I pointed to the grey cloudy heaven above. “It’s like a greenhouse, meaning we can grow crops year-round.”

“What happens when you run out of mana shards?” Mom wondered.

I laughed. “Yeah that ‘aint happening. We have a basically bottomless shard mine here and we’re probably going to upgrade it soon.”

“A bottomless shard mine?!” Martin scoffed. “We’ve been forced to either use chaos shards or drop piles of UBCs.”

I gave him a sheepish grin. “Well not anymore. You’ll have to work out the rights with Jared, but I know he wants to improve excavation efforts on both of our mines.”

“Both?” He asked, raising an eyebrow.

“We also have a bottomless mineral deposit. Oh, and a marble quarry. I guess that’s one of the benefits to living up here in the mountains.”

“Your faction really does have an embarrassment of riches.” He mused.

“Our faction.” I corrected.

He rested a hand on my shoulder. “Our faction.”

We spent a few minutes walking around the farm, eating some bread, and searching for Vixey the little fox. She was nowhere to be found, so we gave up and decided to split off from the group and just wander through the city.

Completed rustic homes, shops, and food stalls blended into the natural scenery of trees with stone paved paths that zig zagged softly up the mountainside. Birds chirped in the trees mingling with the peaceful conversation of people just living their lives.

Eventually, we reached the walls, taking the stone steps up the ramparts.

I breathed out a sigh.

“I know it's ugly right now. The chaos spawn weren’t easy on the forest.” I said, scratching my head. “I was kind of hoping the system would just fix it. But we need to expand anyway so I guess it's not so bad.”

“How many chaos does it take to do this?” My dad asked, eyes wide.

“I’d wager somewhere in the millions.” I said with a shrug. “Not all at once of course, they came in waves.”

“It looks like a nuke went off.”

In a way, it did. Surrounding the entire city walls was a barren space that stretched for at least a couple miles.

Not all of it could be blamed on the chaos spawn either. Spells and skills weren’t gentle and after so long all that was left was a baren wasteland.

We weren’t wasteful though. Trees that weren’t trampled too thoroughly were used to build or craft.

“We’ll restore it. Pretty soon it will be back to its natural beauty.”

“I’m starting to see how your people got as strong as they did.”

My dad's voice seemed distant. “Not just your people. You.”

I looked over at him, and I saw a look I knew all too well. His fists were balled and face was stone.

“I know that look.” Mom said in a worried tone. “You’ve got that crazed look in your eyes.”

It was the same look I saw in my dad anytime he’d made up his mind about anything. It was a look that I respected more than anything else in my father. When he made up his mind, there would be nothing in the world that would keep him from accomplishing it.

“I’m so proud of you, Layton.” His fists unclenched. “And it's about time I start carrying my own weight around here.”
