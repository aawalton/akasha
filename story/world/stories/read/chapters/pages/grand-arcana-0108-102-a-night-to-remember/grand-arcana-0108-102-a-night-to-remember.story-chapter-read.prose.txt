No one knew the rules merchants followed. They always had to sell and buy—unless a participant had a card preventing it—and could never take back an offer. This merchant had confirmed that he could sell Minor Arcana cards, so deity or not, there was no way for him to refuse.

Price was always a contentious topic, but as a rule, Minor Arcana cards always got cheaper the more the trials progressed. At the start, a single card was worth a fortune, only obtainable through prizes or the gambling tables. Now, once they were officially on the market, the average price was about a gold coin, give or take.

“No.” The merchant’s eyes narrowed, much to Mercury’s amusement.

“I can afford them,” I said in a firm tone, both my Charm special and my Lover’s card.

“Gaining this many cards early on will put you at an unfair advantage.”

Mentally I froze. He wasn’t wrong. But out of the many ways he could have phrased that, why did he choose this one? The trials were more than amusement, more than a way for the deities to pick favorites. Could it be that it was all a tournament?

“Then I’d like to buy them in installments,” I said. “Just like my pain debt. I buy all of them now, but only get them when you deem fit.”

You’re really not good at this. That’s what happened when a non-trading deity stepped into the shoes of a merchant. Based on his general behavior, the deity gave the impression of being linked to combat rather than crafting, nature, or some emotional state.

I could see the flashes of anger in his eyes, sparkling like bolts of light within his golden irises.

“Agreed,” he said at last. “You can only claim one House. Which will it be?”

The temptation or request a House we didn’t know about was huge. However, the Grand Arcana was ripe with stories of participants who pushed their luck too far. Asking for all the Minor Arcana was one way to impose a staggering debt that would remove me from the game and place me into a trial-world until it was paid.

So far, I had managed to obtain roughly a third of all Minor Arcana types. Chalice and Swords held seven and six, and Chains and Rings—four. The practical thing was to pick a house in which I had fewer cards. That way, I was guaranteed a far greater choice. Alternatively, I could max out my Swords. Most of the attack skills were there, so it was also a good move. Yet, something in the back of my mind told me to consider Chalice.

There would be no reward for collecting all the cards of a House. That much I knew from my past run. Still, there was this urge to get that particular set.

I bet you wouldn’t have any issues with such a choice, Marcus, I thought. Intuition aside, he had probably already devised several plans to abuse the system. The sixth trial was the perfect opportunity for him to do just that.

“Chalice,” I said.

Sparta arched a brow. Her lips trembled, as if about to ask whether I was sure. Mercury managed to catch himself on time, though, stifling any potential sound that would come out.

“Chalice.” The merchant mused. “And after?”

“Chains, Swords, Rings,” I said without hesitation. “And I’d like one more thing.”

“That much was obvious.”

Normally, this would be the point at which a proper merchant would start showing his wares, recommending all sorts of crap to gauge the participants’ skills. Nothing of the sort took place. The golden-eyed man just stood there, waiting for me to tell him exactly what I was searching for. In a way, he slightly reminded me of Carl.

“I want a drain protection ring.” I focused all my attention on the merchant’s expression. “As a rib ring.”

“Rib rings are expensive. You’re still paying for the last ones.”

My debt isn’t just for that. “I can handle more pain.” I played the role of an idiot.

“No. You might not live to pay it back. The ring and your cards are more than the lynx is worth.”

“I’ll buy the cards with gold. Will that be enough?”

Beside me, Sparta crossed her arms. No one knew the exact value of an albino creature. The only thing for certain was that it wasn’t negligible. The golden-eyed merchant could say any price and I would have no way of knowing. Being too harsh, though, guaranteed that fewer participants would focus on capturing them.

Albino creatures didn’t fight, for the most part, they didn’t pose any danger whatsoever. The only thing they did was waste time. The creatures were always hidden, out of the way, and preferably had to be captured alive. The only way to stumble upon them was through absurd luck or divine assistance. The deities and merchants valued them enough to encourage participants to find them.

“One rib ring.” The merchant raised a finger in the air. “And the cards in installments.”

I nodded.

“Alright.”

The merchant vanished without explanation. He didn’t give me anything. He didn’t even pretend to try. It was as if he had finished the minimal requirement of a job and checked out.

Immediately, I checked my inventory.

RING OF DRAIN PROTECTION

(Item – Rare)

A rib-ring that reduced the effects of life, magic, and stamina draining by 90%.

So it was there. I let out a mental sigh of relief. Looking at it, the ring didn’t seem all that special. It didn’t completely negate the effects, yet for my current trial, it was a major boon. Having it, ensured that I could spend hours in the Crystal Fields instead of minutes.

The cards were also there. Seven new types had appeared, all of chalice, granting me a set of interesting new skills. Most of them were combat-oriented, as was standard for the early Houses. I now had the ability to deal with the effects of poison, bleeding and fire. The most useful of the cards by far was the Thirteen of Chalice, which granted me the ability to perform force thrusts similar to Eleonor’s attack of choice. Being able to corrode and infect also were rather useful. I also had, interestingly enough, the ability to reduce the impact of an attack when blocking with a shield.

What trash, I thought.

Now, I saw why I never took the effort to remember the cards. With the exception of a few very specific skills, most of the other ones were pretty much useless. Major and Grand Arcana cards duplicated a lot, while granting a whole lot more. After the tenth trial, I’d probably feed them to my divine blades, or incorporate them into Serp or something else.

“Don’t worry about him,” Mercury said. “It’s the way he is.”

I remained silent, unsure how to react.

“You really are a curious anomaly,” the deity continued, fully aware that I knew its nature. “Many don’t know what to make of you. Several factions are testing the waters, including my own.”

Sparta smiled, revealing just enough of her bust to imply that Mercury’s faction was the better choice. Sometimes, the deities really considered us to be idiots. Sadly, nine times out of ten, they were correct. Not this time, though. Despite my feelings for Sparta, I had no intention of trusting a deity.

“So, did you decide?” she asked.

“No,” I replied.

“It’s a mistake to think Hypnos could be trusted. Those that deal in illusions have a hard time offering real rewards.”

They’ve given me more than enough tangible goods. “I still plan to assist when possible,” I ventured.

When dealing with Mercury, I had to be very careful of what I said. If there were ten deities that knew about the Divine Page card, he was likely one of them. Openly posing the question, however, was a certain way to ensure that I would never get it without a pact.

“It’s all part of the Grand Arcana,” I added.

“Ah.” The woman let out a chuckle. “It’s always amusing when participants things they know what’s going on. You’re an interesting one to watch, I’ll grant you that. And you did save my follower more than once. Just remember that our patience isn’t eternal.” The tone suddenly shifted.

“The local participant,” I quickly said. The last thing I wanted was for him to end his possession of Sparta on a negative note. “Was he one of yours?”

“Local participant?” the deity asked, genuinely intrigued.

“There’s a local lord in the city nearby who’s a banished participant,” I elaborated. “He said he was sent here by his deity. Is he one of your faction?”

“An interesting question,” Mercury said, indicating that the answer was no. “Keep entertaining us, anomaly. Everyone is watching.”

The gold vanished from Spart’s eyes. The moment it did, the woman began her collapse to the ground. Instantly, I stepped in, catching her before she fully fell.

“Sparta,” I said. Leave it to a deity to take possession of a follower while she was still under the effects of the Emperor card. “Sparta.”

I saw her eyes focus on my face. I half expected her to push away, indicating that she was back to her normal self. She didn’t, choosing to rest in my arms longer. The sad part was that I had no way of knowing whether she was still playing the recruitment game or not.

“Can you stand?” I asked.

“Only one way to find out.”

As I expected, she didn’t have any issue whatsoever. Almost gracefully, she stepped away, pretending to be slightly off balance, just enough to make it believable.

“Did you catch it?” She looked around. Several unconscious lynxes remained on the ground near us.

“Caught and traded.” I nodded.

“I guess your work for the evening is done.”

Images from the past flashed before my eyes. There was a lot less at stake this time around, but I still had a lot to lose. The chances of dying in the trial were close to zero, but there were other ways I could be harmed as well.

“You can say that.”

You really should start thinking with your head, old man! Serp grumbled, only to be ignored.

The rest of the night continued the way Sparta and I had pretty much envisaged it. It was obvious that she was doing a lot to please me, though that could also be attributed to youth. After all, both of us were ten years younger, brimming with hormones and adrenaline. It would be years before either of us could mellow out.

No creatures approached while we gave in to our primal urges. None of them even noticed, all the time, Sparta’s Thief card remained in effect. Just in case, I cast a few magic circles to surround us with a wide ring of stone walls.

Shortly before dawn, we stopped. Both of us had too much energy to sleep, so we just lay there, looking at the forest leaves above.

“Different from the inn,” Sparta said.

“Not that much,” I replied with a shrug. “It was so dirty I couldn’t tell the difference.”

She shoved me with a chuckle. For a moment, I felt a speck of bliss: not the sex, but the sliver of normalcy I was experiencing just now.

“What was it before for you?” I asked. “Before the Arcana.”

“Different.” She gave a measured response. “Though probably a bit easier than for you.”

“You knew it would happen?” I turned, looking at the side of her face.

“You didn’t?” She turned as well, looking just as surprised as I was.

“You thought I knew?” I let out a laugh. “I was having a fun weekend with my family when I was suddenly dragged into this. I’ve been playing it by ear since then.”

With the majority of my initial group dead, there was no way she could claim otherwise. The deities remained an unknown factor, but I strongly doubted they would have been keeping an eye on me from this early on. If they had, this was the perfect opportunity to find out.

“That’s not what I heard.” She smiled at me. “Supposedly, you were head and shoulders above everyone else. Even the pact holders.”

“Oh?” I semi-sat up. “And who said that?”

The odds of her giving me an honest reply were close to none. Before she could even attempt a lie, I heard the sound of an arrow being released. Thanks to the Listener card, I knew precisely where the shot had come from and where it was heading.

A giant magic circle formed beneath us, erecting an even taller wall just beyond the existing one.

We were being hunted.
