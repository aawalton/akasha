Alex, in the swamp…

No. Just no.

I did not want some tail.

Not at all.

Waste of a manifestation. Couldn’t I have gotten something like… well, anything else?

Though, thinking back, the creature didn’t have a ton going for it without the moss. Its defense was too low to really compete, and it didn’t really fight besides shooting more moss things out of its mouth. Or had that been the moss the entire time?

I didn’t know, and I didn’t really want to find out.

I leaped out of the water and back into the massive tree with the kid who had hid. He was thankfully gone, along with the other one. The two flying people were also gone.

As soon as I landed in the tree, the scaley birds descended on the Mankal.

I shivered and fled in the direction Lenna had gone. After a few moments, the chirps and tearing sounds from the feast vanished, and I found the trail she had left behind.

Five dots appeared on the edge of my radar, and I slowed down, switching to being quiet, fast, and hidden.

“Is your partner okay?” asked a hushed voice. “We shouldn’t stay out here much longer in the dark. Soon, the waters will come alive.”

“She will be here soon. The stars have spoken,” said Lenna, her voice in that ghost sort of tone. It kind of freaked me out, but it was a part of her, and I trusted her. It was still creepy, though.

I paused to take a deep breath, before peeking around the tree trunk.

The two adults huddled near the kids and kept glancing at the sky. Neither of them wore armor. Neither of them looked like they were doing very well, either. One used a stick to lean on, while the other had an arm wrapped around his center.

I shifted to my normal form, just coated in armor, and stepped out from around the tree.

Hall gasped, his eyes going wide.

All the attention turned to me.

“We should move, those birds are feasting,” I said with a frown. I’d survive a flock attack of the flying scavengers, but I wasn’t sure about the rest of them.

The one with the stick nodded. “We need to hurry. They aren’t the dangerous part of the night.”

The adults led us through the swamp on foot.

Lenna shot me a glance, but I didn’t know what she was trying to ask.

The rustle of leaves and branches creaking filled the air, along with a musty scent. The heat dissipated slowly, but the heaviness in the air remained.

Lights flickered in the distance, but not in the direction we’d been headed. The two leading us through the swamp picked up the pace when a light flashed between two far off trees.

No one spoke.

They headed directly to one of the mountain cliffs off to the right side, instead of heading down the valley to the waterfall.

Finally, they paused near an outcropping of the stone wall.

The one with the staff leaned in close and their wings appeared.

The kids tensed up, along with the one still clutching his midsection.

A soft buzzing rose from his wings, causing an outline to form on the stone.

More lights flickered in the distance, all heading in this direction.

A doorway cracked open, and the one who was still wingless shoved the kids inside, before rushing after them. The one with the wings motioned for us to hurry.

I barely stepped inside before he rushed after us.

The lights rose through the air, drawing my attention, but I didn’t step out of the shadows.

Then, the door snapped shut.

“We made it,” huffed the one with the staff. He slumped against the wall, sweating.

“Who needs to be healed?” I asked, right as Hin opened his mouth. He snapped it shut.

“Heal the one holding his stomach,” said Lenna. “I think it's infected.”
