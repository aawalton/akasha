One hour ago. Ten kilometers north of Ronswick.

“Fine! If you want to die, be my guest!”

A massive explosion tore through the forest. Trees were obliterated in a blazing fireball, their trunks reduced to splinters as dark smoke billowed upward in thick, rolling plumes. The blast rang out like overlapping thunder. Wind rushed past her as the impact carved a crater into the ground.

As the flames spread outward, Veronica swept her arm across the air. A torrent of wind surged forth, scattering the smoke and ash.

When the haze cleared, only scorched earth remained: blackened, fractured, and hollowed from the blast. The destruction mirrored the aftermath of her battle with the demon in Greystone.

Her gaze settled on the Scavenger before her.

The man lay at the center of the crater, his body charred black, faint embers still clinging to his frame. He wouldn’t be able to speak or confess anymore; he was now a corpse.

Veronica clicked her tongue as the mana within her system gradually settled.

Originally, she had intended to kill one and incapacitate the other.

Things didn’t go how she wanted.

The battle had been far from easy. Both opponents were a full tier above her, and they fought accordingly. She had managed to take down the woman first: a concentrated spear of light smashed directly into the woman’s arm as they tried to block. The resulting explosion tore the limb off completely.

Seeing her grunt in pain, Veronica made the decision to spare her life; she was no longer in a condition to fight.

But then the scavenger had the audacity to swallow a pill—a lethal one—designed for quick deaths. She had taken her own life once she realized what Veronica’s plan had been.

That was when Veronica realized that the other man was the same. They weren’t just simple mercenaries—not employees of an escort company sent to assassinate her. They were actual assassins. The kind that wouldn’t hesitate to end their own lives if it meant avoiding capture.

After that, Veronica stopped holding back. It wasn’t as if she had any spells meant to detain.

Eventually, her frustration boiled over.

She exhaled sharply and glanced down, lightly gripping her left shoulder. Blood ran down her arm, dripping from her fingertips.

“Damn Scavengers…” she muttered. “What kind of organization hides assassins among its own ranks?”

The moment the fight had begun, they noticed her injury. What should have been a clean engagement turned messy almost immediately. Both of them targeted her arm without hesitation.

In the end, she broke her arm again.

Gwen’s magic, though dissipating, was still helping to ease the pain, alongside Sage numbing her senses. Though it wasn’t anything compared to when her body was petrified by Medusa’s curse. Still, she’d need to be healed all over again.

Veronica let out a sigh. “Not like I needed a confession from them anyway,” she muttered.

It would’ve been nice, but Galland’s plan didn’t necessarily need them. Still, there was more for her to do now that both were down. Things were veering far from what she had originally planned.

“This timeline is so screwed,” Veronica muttered under her breath as she began walking back toward town.

As she moved, another thought surfaced. Her frown deepened, her gaze drifting across the scorched forest.

Unlike fighting demons who were enemies of humanity, she had simply fought two ordinary people. They were assassins, sure—but that was irrelevant.

The destruction she had left behind… it was quite excessive. Lord Tomas had given her permission, knowing exactly what kind of mage she was. Even so…

“They’re definitely going to fine me for this…”

One hour later. Meeting room, City Hall.

The clatter of armor echoed faintly through the transmission link.

Then came a laugh. Talenick’s voice carried through, amused. “You don’t understand the hell they’re about to unleash.”

Silence followed, broken only by the distant noise of clatter bleeding through the crystal.

“Well… that was ominous,” Nasha said at last, leaning against a nearby bookshelf.

“Who is… they?” Gwen asked. “Is there another group involved?”

“It’s possible,” Galland replied. “Operations like this don’t exist in isolation. If mana crystals are being smuggled and people trafficked, there are bound to be others with power involved.”

“Quite so,” Lord Tomas said quietly. “His words suggest there are still loose ends. Ones we’ll need to deal with.”

Veronica tapped the crystal lightly with her uninjured hand, cutting the connection. Her other arm rested across the table. Gwen, seated beside her, had been tending to it the entire time.

The battle she fought against the two scavengers in the forest was tougher than she thought; a broken arm wasn’t on the list of things she expected to leave the fight with.

“So… what now?” Gwen asked. She didn’t shift her gaze; her hands remained hovering above Veronica’s arm, continuing her work diligently.

“For now, we secure their base and confiscate anything of importance,” Tomas said. “That includes the notary office. After that, I’ll begin the process of releasing the… ‘boots,’ as he called them.”

“There’s also the matter of those already sold into the underground,” Galland added. “We may find a trail, but tracking each of them will be difficult—even with confessions from Talenick or Dain.”

“Hm…” Gwen murmured.

Veronica glanced at her. “What is it?”

Gwen shook her head slightly. “Something he said… it stuck with me.”

“Talenick?”

“Yeah,” she said. “He mentioned someone. A name… Vance? Vark?”

“Varce,” Nasha corrected.

Everyone turned to look at her.

Galland and Lord Tomas both knew of Nasha—but only as a popular singer in town. Neither had ever seen her like this. Clad in stealthy black rogue gear, armed with daggers and other blades.

“You know him?” Veronica asked.

Nasha turned toward her, a faint smile forming.

“Varce is famous in the underground,” she said. “Deals in everything from narcotics, trafficking, assassin guilds, and contraband. And when I say famous… I don’t mean locally.”

“What do you mean?” Gwen asked.

“He’s known across all of Vitian,” Nasha replied. “Normal people won’t know his name. But if you’ve dealt in the underground for a while, his name appears as a legend. If Talenick’s tied to him, it’s probably through intermediaries. I doubt someone like Varce operates in a place like this.”

Her expression grew a touch more serious.

“He’s the kind of person even the imperial court treats as a threat.”

“That… complicates things,” Lord Tomas said slowly. “If people were trafficked to him, tracking them down will be extremely difficult if it’s someone even the royal family cannot locate…”

Sage. You know anything about this Varce?

[Varce is the leader of Vermillion, a deeply rooted criminal organization operating throughout Vitian.]

The hell? How come I’ve never heard of him?

[Vermillion operates in secrecy and avoids public attention. Their strength lies not in overt power, but in influence, deception, and evasion.]

What happens to Varce in the future? Do you know his whereabouts?

[There are no records of his capture or death up to the date the demons attacked. Some documents may hint that he is operating somewhere northwest or far east, possibly near the border. All sources are unconfirmed and are only testimonies.]

No records… so that means he probably wasn’t caught. Not before the demon invasion, at least.

[He most likely died during the demon invasion.]

That made sense.

After all, Sage only had memories of documents recorded from Martin’s database. As humanity perished, there was little left to report. Criminal activity was of lesser concern than that of surviving or saving others from demon attacks.

“Veronica?”

“Huh?” she said, turning toward Lord Tomas.

“You alright? You seemed… distracted.”

“Ah, no. I’m fine. Just thinking about something.”

“I see,” he said. He brought his arms forward, holding the documents in hand.

“I would like to ask you something, Veronica. Where—no… how did you know those documents were inside the notary office?”

“How?” she echoed.

“More specifically,” he continued, “how you knew where to direct Miss Nasha to find them.”

He glanced down at the papers, flipping through them slowly. “It took her no more than a few minutes to find them. Only because you told her exactly what to look for.”

His gaze sharpened. “I know you mentioned in our last meeting you were confident in where they were, but… How? How did you know?”

He was suspicious.

Veronica couldn’t blame him. It was suspicious—knowing exactly what to look for.

“My intelligence network.”

Galland’s lips pressed together slightly at her answer.

“Your intelligence network?” Lord Tomas repeated.

“I have people who gather information for me,” Veronica said evenly. “That’s how I knew what to look for.”

It was the perfect excuse. It wasn’t something they could easily verify or deny.

“I see.”

Lord Tomas rose to his feet, addressing everyone in the room. “Thank you all for your assistance. There is still much to be done, but this marks the first major step toward resolving the issues plaguing Ronswick.”

The others followed, standing up as well.

Veronica kept her arm lifted carefully, allowing Gwen to continue her work.

“I will need to visit the Scavengers’ headquarters and the notary office to secure both locations,” he said. “As for the rest of you, I recommend taking the remainder of the day to rest. If you notice anything suspicious, report it to me, my father, or the clerk in the lobby immediately.”

Everyone nodded in agreement.

“You will all be properly compensated once this matter is concluded,” he added. “There may be further questions, so I may call upon some of you again. A formal trial will also be held for those involved.”

With that, the meeting came to an end, with everyone filing out of the room.

As Veronica stepped out into the hall with Gwen beside her, a voice called out from behind.

“Miss Veronica. Do you have a moment?”

She turned. Galland stood a short distance away, hands folded behind his back. Like she’d seen him before, he walked with his shoulders hunched forward.

“What is it?” Veronica said.

“If you have a moment… would you mind having a chat with me?”

Veronica studied him for a moment. She glanced at Gwen, then back at him.

“Can she come?” she asked, tilting her head toward Gwen.

He nodded. “Of course.”

“Alright. We can have a talk,” she said.

Behind them, Nasha was the last to leave the room. She lingered near the door, watching them.

She said nothing. Instead, she waited until they had left completely before turning and walking down the opposite corridor.

They were in a modest eatery. A restaurant in the northeastern part of town, a quiet place tucked along the street. The three of them sat at a corner table near the wall. Gwen remained beside her as they sat across from Galland. They both ordered some ordinary snacks.

Veronica—she ordered a small slice of cake.

Veronica’s arm was still healing, but Gwen no longer needed to constantly re-apply her magic.

For a time, they ate in silence. That was until Galland became the first to speak.

“That contract you offered me,” he said. “The one involving the Mage Tower.”

Veronica set her fork down. “Are you considering it now?”

Galland by this point was no longer an indentured servant to High Crates or the Scavengers. His excuse several days ago had been that he couldn’t take her contract because of his debt. With all of that cleared up, it was the perfect time to sponsor and form a relationship with him.

“I considered it heavily from the first time we met,” he said. “However…”

Veronica’s face sharpened.

However?

“Your explanation,” he continued slowly. “About how you found me. About how you knew where the documents were. All of it… was a lie.”

Her brows creased.

He hadn’t asked it as a question. No, he stated it plainly.

“That contract you showed me—I would be more than willing to accept it. In truth, the terms favor me so heavily that I would almost feel guilty taking it when others have not been given the same opportunity.”

He paused. “But before I do… I need to understand. Why?”

“Why?” Veronica echoed.

“Why me?” he said. “I consider myself a capable merchant, even with my junior standing at High Crates. But I’ve also spent the past few years quietly investigating Dain and the rest of them. When you approached me… I thought I had been discovered. That I had pushed too far.”

Veronica gave a small hum in acknowledgment.

“But that didn’t happen. Because during our conversation, I felt sincerity in your words. An actual want of you trying to recruit me. I knew you weren’t a part of the scavengers or the other merchants.”

Gwen slowly glanced over at Veronica.

“That’s why I must ask… after all this time of me laying low. Or me working behind the scenes with Lord Tomas and Helm—how exactly did you find out about me? Why select me as the sponsored in that contract?”

He paused again.

“You also knew the whereabouts of the documents inside of the notary office. It’s one thing to know or guess in which section they might be in—but you provided Nasha with the exact details of what the documents contained and even looked like.”

Gwen’s chewing slowed to a stop.

Galland’s brows drew together as he studied her.

“How do you know so much?” he asked quietly. “Why are you so familiar with me? And… what is your goal in all of this?”

A brief silence settled over the table.

“Why did you help take down the Scavengers?”

Veronica remained still for a long moment.

Was it wise to tell him?

Across from her, Galland didn’t look away. Gwen waited beside her, equally as curious. Veronica had explained how important Galland was to her. But she also never told her why.

Perhaps… it was a good opportunity to tell them both here. If they were to be allies in the future—there was no point in keeping secrets from them.

“You two… “Veronica said slowly. “You wouldn’t believe me even if I told you.”
