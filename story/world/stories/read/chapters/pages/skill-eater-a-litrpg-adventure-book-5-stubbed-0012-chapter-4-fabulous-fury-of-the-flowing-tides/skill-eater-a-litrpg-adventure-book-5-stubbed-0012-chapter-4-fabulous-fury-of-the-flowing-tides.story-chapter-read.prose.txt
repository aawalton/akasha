The crew slept in late the next morning.

They ate a big breakfast and helped Edge steal two skills from an unfortunate beast before resuming their journey to World’s End, considerably stronger than when they’d passed the other way. He added the experience he Absorbed to Rejuvenation, which he had decided to prioritize after taking heavy damage during the last fight despite his myriad defenses.

He was hoping to turn its healing magic into an aura, but even if that wasn’t in the cards, advancing a skill that let him deal with everything from noxious substances to foreign mana to grievous bodily harm would be invaluable during the trying days to come. As a bonus, the chains of oblivion found an avatar they wanted to devour, taking them one step closer to ranking up.

The team was almost back when he felt a familiar sensation emanating from his core. Whenever Edge Extracted a skill and added it to his collection, the avatar was broken down and reborn inside him, changing its appearance to align with his nature. For most powers, the process only took a few seconds, but Rare skills assumed an egglike shape for two days before their transformation was complete and Epics took a full two weeks.

When he popped into his inner world, his avatars were gathered in front of two eggs resting in a corner of his central chamber, eagerly awaiting the big moment when a new friend would join the team.

The Rare egg was the same shade of burgundy as the stingray’s skin, and the Epic matched the electric green hue of the lobster’s shell. That observation reminded him that he had a big chunk of keratin sitting in his vault, which he’d grabbed when Rue cut the dimensional anchor free. He was certain it was a high-grade crafting material, but his speculations on how he could use it were cut short when a crack rang out. It was followed by the maroon egg shattering, revealing the avatar inside.

To his surprise, it didn’t look like flowing water or an abstract representation of kinetic energy, which was what being on the receiving end had felt like. Instead, the skill took the shape of the full moon shining in the sky. Its appearance made a lot more sense when his Guide appeared and flattened to form a screen.

You have extracted the skill: Tidal Stream (Rare, Rank 5).

Digestion is complete.

Would you like to slot in Tidal Stream now?

When he acknowledged the prompt, the silver moon floated over to the pillars Skill-Eater had manifested, binding itself to the socket mounted to the top. During the process, the skill was reset to rank one, just a fraction of a percent away from reaching rank two. It was the most experience a new skill could start with, a detail Edge had come to value as he grew more accustomed to the powers provided by his Unique core.

While it would offer immediate strength if the skills retained their original properties, this way, he could pick every upgrade himself, taking them in a direction that would synergize with the other powers in his collection and be more useful over the long haul. That being said, stealing a single skill would be sufficient to rank it up right away, and he was eager to find out what options were on the table.

He had saved one charge of Extraction to do just that, but before he went on a quick hunt to obtain the experience he needed, it was time to introduce himself to the newest member of the team.

“Hello.” He came to a stop while staring up at the moon. “I’m pleased to meet you. My name is Edge, and we will be working together for the foreseeable future, along with the rest of the gang.” He gestured to include Rue and the avatars who were waiting to greet their new neighbor.

“It will take a little while before we know each other well, but please let me know if there is anything I can do to make your stay here more pleasant. Feel free to pick a plot for yourself in our village and decorate it however you please.”

The moon bobbed in the air, regarding him in turn. Through their newly forged bond, he had the sense that the avatar was friendly but aloof, not used to interacting in such a direct manner. With that, he turned the floor over to Chibime and Sea, who would help Stream feel at home inside his core.

As the avatars gathered round to welcome the radiant orb, he pulled up his profile to read the skill’s description, supplementing the brief vision he’d experienced when it was bound to his skill socket.

Tidal Stream <domain, gravity> (Rare, Rank 1).

Deploy a gravity-based domain that mimics the effect of king-tide currents, immersing everything it encompasses within an endless flow of force. Once a direction has been set, it may not be changed without manifesting Tidal Stream from scratch. The mana drain of the domain is proportional to the mass it is affecting at any given moment.

Awesome. Edge had suspected the skill was a domain, given its wide area and constant effect, but he had pegged it as type of kinetic magic rather than gravity, which he knew next to nothing about. He suspected there was considerable overlap given the forces involved, but he would have to ask Sakura to learn more of the details, whose understanding of physics and magic far surpassed his own.

He was thrilled to have picked up a second domain, and excited to try combining two at once, although his instincts were telling him the combo penalty for doing so would be substantial. That reminded him it was almost winter. Soon, Fear the Season’s elemental domain would shift from wind to ice, which would synergize with his runic Frostfury Medallion.

He stepped back into his body, let his team know what had happened, then found a tasty looking beast passing nearby. He stunned it with Intimidating Roar, stole a skill to feed to Tidal Stream, and put the creature down. He removed the entrails, drained the blood, then skinned and butchered the carcass so the crew would have some fresh meat for dinner.

After washing up, Edge entered his central chamber, where Stream’s avatar was glowing considerably brighter than before.

The skill Tidal Stream has advanced to Rank 2.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Please choose between one of the following options:

Warning: If no option is selected, your skill will remain unchanged and gain a slight bonus to mana efficiency.

All three options sounded good at face value, but since Edge hunted with his crew a fair fraction of the time, the second offering was the best fit. Reversing the flow would be a neat trick to catch his opponents off-balance, but it wasn’t anywhere close to as useful as being able to exclude his allies from the domain’s affect.

Random objects and my own body too for that matter. When you took into consideration the fact that the cost was proportional to the mass affected, it was also a significant boost to the skill’s mana efficiency. A bigger radius would be awesome in general, but without being able to choose the domain’s targets, it would dramatically increase its drain to have more objects in range.

“I’ll take the second option, thanks.” He voiced his decision as his Guide turned into a mote of light and sank into his chest.

When Stream’s transformation was complete, Edge’s eyes widened in surprise. Instead of growing bigger, the manifestation now had a grinning face along one side of its cratered surface. It would have been downright terrifying if it was moving, but even frozen in place, the silver moon’s smiling visage was imposing to behold.

He congratulated the avatar on its rapid advancement, spent some time bonding with the team, and returned to the outside world—ready to take his newest power for a spin.

“Does anyone want to help me test my new domain?” he asked. “It’s the gravity magic the stingray used during the fight, so I don’t think there’s any risk involved.”

“Sure thing.” Jumo grinned and grabbed his spear from the wagon.

“I’m in,” Melenia added as everyone else gave them some space while watching on with interest. “We might as well get some sparring in while we’re at it.”

Just like Maelstrom, it took some time and effort to prime Tidal Stream by pumping gravity mana into the environment, which felt strange compared to the elemental frequencies he’d been using regularly over his hunting career.

He wondered if it would be more difficult to counter this domain by introducing opposing energy, since gravity didn’t have an oppositional force as far as he knew. I suppose antigravity would do it, but running into an enemy that uses it doesn’t seem terribly likely.

When he was ready, Edge gave the signal and raised Opulence in a defensive stance. Jumo and Melenia took off in a flash, circling round from opposite sides before closing the distance.

He had only intended to test Tidal Stream, but he decided Mel was right. A two-versus-one duel sounded like a perfect way to blow off some steam. He wasn’t planning to use offensive skills, which meant the odds of hurting experienced warriors were negligible, and he could heal any damage he took as needed.

After a moment of consideration, he activated Warlord’s Mantle for all three of them. They would benefit equally from the bonus attributes, and the ghostly cloaks’ defensive properties would provide an additional layer of insurance.

He was at a severe disadvantage, and they were probably going to win, but that didn’t mean he was going to make this easy. As his friends crossed the final few feet, he gathered his will and activated Tidal Stream for the first time. While it was only rank two, Stream was a Rare skill and Edge had a ridiculous amount of Amplification for someone his stage, and what happened next caught everyone by surprise.

All three of them stumbled when a tsunami of gravity washed over them, relentlessly driving them in the direction he had visualized before activating the domain. It felt like he was submerged in a river whose water wouldn’t bear his weight. Mel laughed as her boots slid back, along with the loose dirt she was standing on. Jumo jumped to free himself from the shifting soil, which helped although the current was still dragging him back.

If Edge didn’t have so much experience using Gaze’s combined mode, which generated a three-dimensional model of everything within its range, he would have had a hard time adjusting to Tidal Stream. It didn’t provide the same kind of information, but it still gave him a sense of everything within the domain’s radius, which felt a bit like running his fingertips over a complex carving. It let him identify what he wanted to include or exclude from the gravitational effect, even when he wasn’t looking that way.

He visualized what he wanted and regained his footing as his body and the ground beneath his boots stopped moving, leaving only his opponents fighting against the flow. The mana drain immediately dropped from extreme to something he could maintain for quite a while, revealing just how heavy all the earth and rocks he’d been shoving were.

“This is awesome.” Jumo cackled while Dashing forward, getting close enough to trade a rapid series of thrusts and parries. “It’s hard as hell to maintain a proper stance and dealing with the pressure is throwing me off my game, no matter what attacks I try.”

That was when Edge realized Mel had vanished. He had just enough time to spin around and bring Opulence to bear before her axe landed and sent him staggering to stay upright. She Swapped behind me and used Stream to bolster the momentum of her strike. I’ll have to watch out for that going forward.

He excluded the guardswoman from the domain’s effect, which made her stumble when the resistance vanished, then switched it on again, causing her to fall flat on her face while uttering a colorful curse. He excluded her again but kept the pressure on Extinction, causing the weapon to slide away as Mel scrambled to recover the axe.

“That’s a clever way to disarm someone,” Trapper commented from the sidelines.

“The gravitational effect can’t be dodged,” Sakura added. “It can knock projectiles off course, sap the force from strikes headed his way, and pull particles in the air away from his position, which will let him counter smokescreens and poison gas.”

“The biggest limitation is he can’t change the flow’s direction.” Sasha watched the match with interest. “It isn’t shifting as he turns, which means the flow is constant rather than relative to his position.”

While they analyzed his domain, Jumo and Mel came back for another pass. They had adapted to the pressure by now, although swapping them in and out of the effect was still throwing them off-balance. Even still, Edge barely managed to hold back the attacks of two skilled warriors at the same time, one of whom had just experienced two massive upgrades to his physical prowess.

“I’ve never earned this many cycles at once before, and the bonus attributes from Brawny are amazing,” Jumo mirrored his train of through. “My body feels light as a feather, and I hit like a battering ram.”

Over the next sweat-soaked minutes, Edge dueled his friends, learning the ins and outs of Tidal Stream. He got a better feel for the best times to remove or apply the pressure, forcing the duo to divide their attention by anticipating an abrupt change in resistance that might not actually happen.

He experimented with using the flow to enhance his movement too. It was similar to Maelstrom, although it wasn’t nearly as flexible, since Stream had a fixed direction. He managed to knock both warriors down several times when he added the dirt beneath their boots to the domain’s effect, quite literally sweeping them off their feet.

The experience was eye-opening. Tidal Stream’s benefits came in two broad categories—moves that were inherently useful, and those that only worked when he caught his foes by surprise, which became less effective as the sparring session wore on. The longer he fought, the more Edge had to rely on Beelo’s techniques to remain standing, brute-forcing Jumo’s spear aside with his heavy glaive.

He scored several points in the process, although he lost twice as many, which wasn’t bad at all considering it was two on one. “Do you mind if I dance with Edge alone for a while?” Mel asked.

“Be my guest.” Jumo pulled back to the sidelines while wiping sweat and dirt off his tattooed skin. “He’s all yours.”
