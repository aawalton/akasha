Chapter II.VIII (2.8)

“Damn.” Mitsuko threw herself forward, pushing herself from wall to wall to keep her balance as she scrambled onto the deck. Her first instinct was to create a sword in her hand. But in her current situation where she didn’t have eyes on an enemy, she was more likely to hurt herself as she chaotically bounced down the hall.

The beast below slammed into the hull again. This time, The Selcouth Sable lifted out of the water and then slammed back into the sea. A bit of the wall burst from the impact, the splintered wood allowing liters of water to rush in every second. She cast Mend and repaired the damage. Wooden planks popped back into place and sea water exited the ship’s interior.

It wasn’t like entering a storm, where a ship was knocked about by wind and waves. This was more sudden and dramatic. Like they were inside a ball getting kicked around by school children.

As she stumbled onto the deck, Mitsuko slipped and fell. Instinctually, before she even hit the wood, she cast Mend on her clothing, dragging her back onto her feet. Then reached out and grabbed a hold of the closest mast.

Or…what was left of it.

The mast was snapped halfway up. A glance to the side showed the other half floating in the sea nearby.

As she looked in that direction, she saw a massive body breaching the sea’s surface. It blew water up from a spout like a whale. But, unlike a normal whale, the monster’s entire body was covered completely in eyes the size of her fist.

Sailors screamed and yelled.

Mitsuko flicked her wrist and created a blade of ice. Then reached back and hurled the sword with all her might.

It whooshed as it sliced through the air. Then the point embedded itself into one of the beast’s thousand eyes.

“UUUURRRRRR!” the beast wailed. It shifted in the water and sent up a wave of sea water in their direction.

The water slammed into The Selcouth Sable. Mitsuko grabbed a hold of the mast while sailors tumbled across the deck. More than one person fell overboard, only to be dragged out of the water by Mitsuko’s Mend spell.

She refused to allow any of them to die for her and concentrated to make certain all the sailors were recovered.

The sea monster lifted a fin the size of their sails and slammed it down on the ship, once again nearly tipping The Selcouth Sable over while simultaneously ravaging the port side. This time two sailors were unfortunate enough to be under the attack and disappeared into the sea. Mitsuko cast Mend again, pulling back the two lost men and pulling the ship back together.

While the ship repaired itself and the two men returned to the deck, once released from her spell, they slumped and fell, now lacking the spark of life.

Mitsuko gripped a newly formed sword in her hand and glared at the corpses. Useless. She could accomplish so much with her magic but it remained useless when set against the powers of life and death.

“Fuck it,” Mitsuko muttered to herself. She charged forward and stepped up on the wooden gunwales. Then she leaped through the air. As the air whooshed around her, she flipped her blade into reverse grip. Her descent into the sea was interrupted by the massive, whale-like behemoth. Her blade plunged into one of its thousand glossy white eyes. Mitsuko ripped her blade through the eye while she jabbed her free fist into another eye. Her hand punctured the eye’s membrane easily.

The gooey insides were warm. Unfortunately it offered a very lackluster handhold. She felt herself slipping. The sword likewise sliced through the eye without ever getting stuck or finding purchase.

Rather than let herself fall into the sea, Mitsuko cast Mend on her clothing. They jerked her back up.

As the spell pulled her back to the ship, Mitsuko chopped at another eye. It popped and sprayed a gush of white goo.

And then she was back on the ship. She took a second to catch her breath and then prepared herself to repeat the process. As many times as it would take to convince the monster to fuck off.

“Stop her!” a clicking voice shouted.

Mitsuko spun and ducked as a harpoon blasted over her head. A crab-man stood on the deck, his skin covered in a thick red carapace. His beady eyes bulged out and he snapped his teeth together. In place of a normal cartilage nose, his face concaved and, at the center were two holes. Two of his hands held harpoons while another wielded a crossbow device Mitsuko assumed shot the harpoons with extra force.

She activated her Necklace of Mirrors which created two replica illusions of Mitsuko to either side of her. When Mitsuko dashed forward, her blade ready, so too did they. The crab man blocked her sword with one of his harpoons. The ice of the blade cracked and bits of the sea monster’s eye goo splattered on her enemy’s carapace. He attempted to jab her with his other harpoon. But Mitsuko danced to the side. The weapon passed her harmlessly, instead swiping through one of her illusionary selves. As Mitsuko side stepped, she dropped her damaged sword.

With the crab man now assuming he’d disarmed her, he grinned and jabbed again with his harpoon. Only for the attack to lose momentum as a newly formed sword stuck through his neck. Blue blood oozed out of the wound. He attempted to say something but the words came out only as a gurgle.

Mitsuko watched with some satisfaction as her enemy slumped to the deck. Dead to an old, reliable trick.

Someone screamed nearby and Mitsuko whirled around to see another crustacean man chopping at the air in her direction with a scimitar. Mitsuko licked her salty lips and narrowed her eyes at her new enemy. The last crust hadn’t posed much of a threat and this one seemed equally skilled to his friend.

But before Mitsuko had a chance to meet blades with her foe, a blast of wind punched into the crustation, punting him off the ship and sending him spinning through the air. He skipped across the sea’s surface before disappearing below.

“Clear the crusts from the ship!” the captain hollered.

The ship’s elementalist stood beside him. Air rippled around her, sending her braided hair up in tendril.

Mitsuko relaxed. The sailors now had their weapons out and clashed with the crusts on the deck. They outnumbered the invaders nearly ten to one. The crusts were being cleared from the deck.

BOOM!

A blinding flash from the port side.

Again, the ship shuddered and rocked. Then it began to sink.

Mitsuko swore and cast Mend on the ship again, repairing the damage. She felt the hull of the ship piecing itself back together. Something had torn a hole the side of a barrel all the way through The Selcouth Sable.

Once Mended, Mitsuko dashed back over to the port side of the ship. This time she didn’t leap off into the sea on the offensive. Instead, she examined the sea monster.

Dozens of the monster’s eyes had conjoined into one massive eyeball that protruded from the creature’s flesh like a massive boil. Unlike the smaller eyes, this one glowed yellow with a black pupil. It spun in its socket until it found and locked onto her. The two flickering illusions beside her were useless under this monster’s scrutiny. Then the eye glowed brighter and brighter.

Not wanting to give the monster a chance to complete whatever magical ability it had, Mitsuko threw herself at it again.

Her blade pieced the glowing eye and she felt a jolt of electricity zap up from her sword with enough power to numb her entire arm.

She instantly cast Mend on her arm, but she lacked the ability to grip the now electrical sword.

As she fell, she noticed a seagull flying overhead.

Then the world lit up with a bright yellow light. It consumed her head. Her entire body went rigid.

A brief moment of pain. Then it ended.
