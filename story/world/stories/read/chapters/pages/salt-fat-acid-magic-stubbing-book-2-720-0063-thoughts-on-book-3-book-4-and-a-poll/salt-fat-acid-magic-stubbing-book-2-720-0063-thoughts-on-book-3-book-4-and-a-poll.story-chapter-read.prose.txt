And that’s book 3! Thank you to everyone for reading and commenting, it really keeps me going. As I mentioned before, book 4 is going to be a while. I make no promises for its release date, but it almost certainly will not come out in the first half of 2026. I hope you all come back and don’t hold it against me. When chapters resume, I intend to post uninterrupted until the end of the series—I’d rather make you wait a year and then give you three books back-to-back than go 6 months on / 6 months off forever.

A few reasons for the break:

Books 1-3 need to be edited and produced! My publisher has connected me with a great narrator (who has a perfect Waldorf) and an unbelievable artist. This production schedule also coincides with the absolute busiest time of my day job, so my goal is to come out of Q2 not being too far behind on my word count.

While this has been happening, I’ve also written a whodunit that I’m hoping to get trad published. I’ve completed the second draft, but I still have probably two more to go and the whole querying process.

And I’ve been working on other projects, one of which I might bring to Royal Road.

Financials also play a role. If book 1 is published and bombs, I’ll probably reconfigure some things so that I can tell the rest of the story in 2 books rather than the expected 3—each book has taken me nearly 6 months to write thus far. So I’m still writing SFAM (I have about 15 chapters written), but I’m only writing the parts that won’t get squeezed, so it’s all out of order. I could explore unconventional posting patterns in the future, but we’ll see.

A heavier reason is that when I outlined this series in 2024, the real world wasn’t as dark as it is now. This ending of book 3 is meant to be the start of “the bad times,” but now I’m reconsidering how dark I want the rest of the series to be. For example, I’ve had a Nori storyline planned since day 1 that involves racism and refugee camps. Today, I don’t want to spend a month writing such things. The core moral message in SFAM has always been anti-billionaires, and I will keep that, but I’m not interested in paralleling every political issue happening today. I’ve already done my best to keep Waldorf from being too Trumpian and more focused on cronyism and greed, but that’s becoming more familiar all the time. I doubt the series will ever return to the coziness of book 1, but I think I would like to find more happiness in the remaining pages. Something I’ve discovered in writing this trilogy is that I really struggle mentally with making characters sad.

That being said, let’s talk about book 4!

Book 4 - The Taste of Ash

(The title referencing both Julienne’s lack of taste as well as the aftermath of the fading of all of the flames in book 3).

Julienne, as the main character of this book, struggles with his inability to taste as he constructs a new team at Cafe Julienne in preparation for a months-long culinary tournament honoring Grand King Flambé’s memory.

And no one has a better memory of Flambé than the young Chef that has been infected with those memories. Nori must deal with rising anti-Urokan sentiment while figuring out what to do with the knowledge of Flambé’s secret Urokan child. She must also settle in with her new sponsor, Blanche’s sister, Raclette, whose new restaurant aims to dethrone Cafe Julienne at a time when it is most vulnerable.

Archie, in a more minor role, grieves his father and takes care of his mother in Sain. He is joined by Sutton, who is conducting research, and Rowan and Chandler, who have fled Ambrosia City for good. While his friends try to comfort him, Archie can only focus on his legacy as he trains for the years ahead.

So, if you consider where Archie, Nori, and Julienne all started—legacy-obsessed, burdened by guilt from her family’s actions, worried that he doesn’t deserve the Julienne name—you’ll notice all three are right back where they started but in an even darker way. Of course, they’ve all grown, so we’ll see how they tackle these challenges now.

Back when I thought I would have a new small POV each book (5 chapters to Barley in book 2, 5 to Sutton in book 3, etc), I actually planned on making Blanche the 2nd largest POV in book 4 (Archie would be the “guest spot”). I doubt I’ll do a Blanche POV, but I’ll still mull it over.

I originally had doubts about making Book 4 the "Julienne book," but now I'm pretty confident that it's the way to go. While the book will have its fair share of grieving and mental anguish, I think of it as "the cooking book" of the series, and Julienne is the most interesting character in such a storyline.
