So this is what a favorite of Rising Stars looks like.

A few things struck Alex instantly.

The first was that this Caide’s features felt strangely familiar—an instantaneous feeling Alex got, at first glance.

The second was, there was this kind of recognition in the looks lilly_xd and Karnation were giving Caide—like they knew Caide outside of all this? Alex’s eyes narrowed slightly. Caide didn’t reciprocate the looks; Alex’s impression was, they knew him by reputation.

Which felt kind of weird, since surely Caide, and everyone else here, hadn’t yet had time to make any kind of serious name for themselves. It’d been at maximum 6 weeks since everyone’s Awakenings, likely less for a lot of these talents.

Caide’s username was also distinct from the rest; it put a slight smile on Alex’s face. Who puts official after their name? Really?

If you don’t become a big deal, it’ll look pretty freaking pretentious.

Even if you are eventually, it still feels a little pretentious.

I mean, he is the second seed in all of Rising Stars, he’s backed it up so far, in fairness.

Caide’s eyes met Alex’s, for a second.

It was in this second, Alex realized; a few final pieces fell into place.

caide_sc_official—why his face was so familiar—and why was Adrian St. Clair here, of all places?

I’ve run into another St. Clair.

“We’re all here, then,” Caide said, addressing the official. He glanced around, lips pursed. “Are we getting started?”

“Yes! Just one sec, please,” said the official, eyes slightly unfocused, one hand to her ear. “Let me get final confirmations—”

Seriously?

Alex’s lips quirked.

He was only kind of shocked.

The St. Clair’s were one of the most prominent families in Awakened circles, especially in the region; he supposed it wasn’t that surprising he’d run into one in a Cup that explicitly selected for talents.

It was certainly a coincidence, but it wasn’t even the biggest coincidence he’d faced in the past few days. With how these groups were decided, every group was always bound to have at least one top seed; that it was a St. Clair felt like, sure.

It was kind of funny Alex’d just been getting chewed out by his sister—or possibly cousin?—a few days before.

Also explained the looks the other two were giving Caide. As well as, actually, the looks Alex noticed the guy was getting from nearby stations, and from a few nearby spectators as well in the walkways. Clearly the guy was a mini celebrity, at least in these circles—and the curiosity did make sense; Alex imagined folks were likely wondering if he’d be the next Adrian. And Clarissa was also quite a successful talent, it seemed like, if relatively early in her career; Alex had researched her a bit after their encounter, and realized she’d distinguished herself in Interleagues as one of the premier talents in her class.

Caide let the glances bounce right off him. The guy started tapping fingers along his saber’s hilt.

Even the official was talking to him kind of awkwardly; she seemed a little nervous—“Well, okay. I’ve gotten the go-ahead, so we’ll start with deciding the first fight. That is, what the matchups are.”

The official also seemed about their age, and Alex’s assumption was she was a volunteer.

She produced two coins, one silver, one gold.

“You’ll be heads on gold,” she said to Caide. “If that’s okay with you?”

“Yes, please, let’s continue with it,” said Caide lightly.

“Of course. And, um—arcstyr—arcs—heads on silver?”

“Sure,” said Alex.

“lilly, Karnation, tails on silver and gold. Okay! Since that’s decided, let’s—”

She flipped the coins.

“—See.”

Alex watched them glitter as they flipped, before landing.

What are the odds, like 50%?

One coin, gold. Heads.

The second spun, twice, in a circle, before it landed. Silver—and, heads.

Alex’s lips quirked.

Yeah, I figured.

Alex’s first match was against the second seed in all of Rising Stars. Caide St. Clair.

It didn’t really matter when he faced the guy, but—if I was going to face him anyway, might as well get it out of the way.

How is he only 2 levels above me, and already 50% higher rated? He's hasn’t even maxed out F-rank, he’s Level 7, and there’s dozens of Level 9’s here.

Alex had dueled elite talent precisely once before, against the Devereaux siblings, and it’d been a shock at first.

He’d also very much enjoyed the duel. It’d been incredibly inspiring in helping him think about his magic, sure, but it also showed him what was possible at his level—before he’d kind of been dicking around, having fun, and it’d showed him a glimpse of a new world, far larger and more complex than anything he’d imagined in his little Training Hall.

Caide was already walking to one end of the station; he hadn’t given Alex a second glance.

Is that how it is?

Okay.

Alex was amused.

Let’s see you earn that arrogance.

Because unlike everyone else here, even the official, apparently—dude, I’m not in awe of you.

It was likely entirely delusional, but just glancing at him, Alex genuinely didn’t think—with his new gear, and his new magic gains, and his specific set of talents, he was inferior.

If you want me to respect you, show me.

I WANT you to beat me.

Alex walked to his end of the station, shaking out his body a bit; he felt quite loose.

That other official earlier had said ‘5 minutes until action begins.’ Their current official tapped the steel pillar at their station, and a timer appeared ten feet above its center—2 minutes, 27 seconds.

As the seconds trickled by, Alex also noticed the walkways around their station getting a little more crowded. A few Aegis, ArchKnights, and O2 logos, a few independents, as well as various Guild logos, which also kind of amused Alex—these seemed to be relatives of the talents, or assistants, or such. You don’t have your own talents to look after?

Except, I guess it makes sense you’d want to scout a potential rival. Especially if your talent’s like Caide, seemingly far and away the best in their group, so your presence isn’t as necessary.

Because Alex didn’t have any delusions, obviously, any of these people were here for Alex.

And even as Alex glanced at the folks who were merely observing, it felt pretty obvious.

They all think I’m going to fail. It’s just a question of how badly.

If Alex were in a Training Hall, he’d dislike an audience.

But he kind of liked this kind of audience. He liked how it was making him feel.

1 minute, 30 seconds.

“Fighters, gear up!” called the official, and Alex did; pendant, helm, twin-gem chestplate—he felt instantaneous warm rushes as the gear’s stats filled his body and soul. Alex also drew his sword. You weren’t allowed to prefill your sword with mana, at least until the duel officially began; rifts of course didn’t have such rules.

Caide geared up as well, and he was in all gold and white plate, like a custom armorset, all enchanted, it seemed like; one piece was likely as expensive as Alex’s sword. Likely the same armorer as Clarissa’s mage armor; Caide’s set was slightly heavier than Clarissa’s, but still relatively light. His helm featured a beautiful golden crest at the temple.

Except it was Caide’s necklace that actually caught Alex’s eye.

It featured a cloudy white gemstone that shone brilliantly to Alex’s soul senses, but its appearance didn’t give any hints as to what it did. An instance item.

“Ten seconds!” said the official.

Caide made eye contact with him across the dueling station.

“Good luck,” Caide said; his voice carried quite clearly across the space.

“Yeah, you too,” Alex called, blinking. It seemed a courtesy—Caide seemed a little detached from the world around him right now—but Alex was still kind of surprised.

And two banners appeared in the middle of the arena; purplish translucent walls came up.

Match assigned!

1arcstyrion (771?) vs. caide_sc_official (1158)

Alex expected the station to shift, except it didn’t; the only things that filled in were bright mana-lines along the white ground, like lines marking out a basketball court.

This arena was called, simply, ‘Standard Arena;’—zero obstacles. The most exposed kind of fighting.

3…

2…

1…

FIGHT!

Caide breathed out, once.

His body was edged in gold—Light mana, like Clarissa.

Caide’s pendant glowed a little. A few seconds passed as Light mana surged through his body; at the same time, Mana Spears materialized behind Alex.

Caide began walking toward him, and Alex braced, keeping a very close eye on him; he began filling his sword with Force. He had a feeling, just seeing his armor, and his saber, he knew what Caide’s fighting style was like.

And then, Caide ceased walking.

The guy vanished in an explosion of gold.

All Alex saw were golden afterimages. Alex’s eyes widened; a Mana Shield flew up—

And Caide St. Clair was right in Alex’s face, shining gold.

CLANG!

For the first time, their swords clashed, and Alex gasped.

If Alex hadn’t been braced for exactly this, he was certain the sword would’ve sliced into his throat.

The force was far stronger than he’d expected, but the mana in his sword was able to take the sting off.

Caide’s sword danced back like a viper; five slashes, all a blur of sheer speed, before a series of Mana Spears crashed into the space in front of Alex. At the same time, Alex unleashed the remainder of the mana in his sword, directly in front of him.

It all forced Caide to dance back twenty-ish feet.

Alex touched his ribs, and saw blood on his hands.

Holy shit he’s fast. I kind of figured he’d be, but fuck.

Alex wasn’t even sure how he’d deflected that slash at his throat; it felt like pure instinct, and it was also by a fair margin the heaviest strike of that little flurry.

He was kind of surprised—he really just tried to finish me on the very first slash, Christ. Zero fucking chill whatsoever.

Alex saw a Mana Shield standing a dozen feet in front of him, a Shield he’d tried to place between the two of them; Caide had skirted right around it.

I can’t let him get close, at all. Already new Mana Spears were forming behind him.

All around them, Alex heard a few gasps from the walkways—“When did that shield get put up?”

“What the hell was that!?”

Caide was inspecting the space before Alex; his lips were pursed.

He seems a little surprised?

Caide wasn’t at all deterred, however.

He seemed to genuinely recognize Alex for the first time that fight; he glanced at Alex’s sword and his spears, and the Mana Shield.

He’s taking me seriously.

He wasn’t just taking Alex seriously, Alex realized, because in the next instant, Caide’s eyes were filled with what Alex could only describe as murderous intent.

Mana began surging within his saber, and in his body—or rather, surging into his saber and body.

And in the next exchange, it was very clear that what Caide had shown so far wasn’t as serious an attempt as Alex had imagined. Caide had barely scratched the surface.

Starting from that next exchange, it was also very clear.

For whatever reason, it seemed like Caide was bent on crushing Alex from the very start.
