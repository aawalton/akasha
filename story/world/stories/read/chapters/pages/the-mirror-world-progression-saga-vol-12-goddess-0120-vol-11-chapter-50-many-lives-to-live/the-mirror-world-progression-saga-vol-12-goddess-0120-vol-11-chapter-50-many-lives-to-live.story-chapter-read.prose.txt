The tower top of Itha Ranon shook and groaned with the power on display. From the resonant mana soaring in all directions, open to the sky, it surged from the land as it concentrated, focused, and condensed into the very center of it all. Here, above the control crystal, a new star was growing, bright and hot.

Orswyth poured all he had left into the final act of layering the artifact’s energies within. He’d started with the expectation of a full day of ritual casting, but after only two hours, and Samantha’s secondary tower light battery still with fifty percent capacity, he could feel the hard limits of what he was doing being reached. Now, energy was wasting, resonating back out, and no matter how hard he pushed, only a trickle was going in, like mere threads interwoven, where once there were fat lengths of yarn.

Perhaps we’re at the limits of reality itself?

Frustrating as it was, Orswyth relented out of caution, knowing the last one percent or so wasn’t worth the risk of instability.

He spent one more hour or so, tapering it down, letting the energy flow abate and calm, as he worked to seal the construct of light securely. He could add no more, but he could ensure it was otherwise a perfect work.

When all was finished, it still looked like a vaguely diamond-shaped sun hovered above, radiant light too intent for most to penetrate. But deeper within, to those with the right senses, the more physical confines made the artifact only the size of a small crystal, perhaps the length of a digit.

With the ritual complete, Orswyth telekinetically floated over the containment ‘device,’ as provided by the combined artifice of Tashome and Zephyr — what appeared to be a rainbow opal stone in the shape of a sphere. But it unfurled and shredded itself into layers, and the light crystal was enclosed around it.

The opal seemed to ripple and vibrate at the last moment, turning fiery, with the feeling that it might burst or melt. But finally, the pieces locked around and sealed the light crystal with a final loud chime, and the light was completely contained.

Orswyth floated the opal stone sphere down to him as he wiped his brow of sweat. As soon as his hand touched it, he hissed and pulled away, its surface burning him.

Still too hot for me. But not for her, especially given how tied it will become with her.

Shaking his head and sighing, Orswyth muttered, “Azure won’t like the ideal sacrifice, but it begins to look necessary…”

Shaking off the matter for now, he turned away to head down and get a well-earned drink, calling through the realm for guards to watch over the item, and requesting Zephyr to determine the best ‘containment for the containment’ before Samantha returned. It was best not to put it in a dimensional space, unfortunately.

He should’ve been happier about finishing the thing, but the whole matter wasn’t over, and he didn’t look forward to the next part.

One thing at a time. The first leg of a great work is finished, and it’s cause for celebration. Let’s just focus on that drink for now, old man.

Knowing it would be a great, sad shift in her life, Ashawein of the Load Bearing Clouds finally drank of the Nectar of the Gods and ascended to become one of them, her worship already strongly cemented as a legendary hero of growing fame and prestige. The levels would come quickly. However, there was little cause for celebration, as almost simultaneously, news came of the Pale Prince doing the impossible: slaying the king of the gods, whose very name had been ripped out of reality.

It threw the rest of the pantheon into chaos, half of them banding together for an answer of revenge, the other half retreating entirely, in some cases — where able — going into hiding.

The war of the gods on the Pale Prince and the new multi-nation ‘empire’ of Gerenna was a fierce and bloody affair of many years. Crushing Fist, the God of War, and the Supreme Huntress, the Goddess of Hunting, were an inseparable terror, ironically much stronger than the patriarch god had been, on top of the support of the other deities in their alliance. An alliance that grew at first, with some of their initially promising successes.

Instead of wiping them out immediately, the Pale Prince had to surrender previously conquered territory to them, and slowly weaken them with his entropic magics, drawing them into his traps and diminishing them again and again.

Meanwhile, Ashawein spent this time wisely, in strategy. She rapidly gained levels with the fast start allowed from thousands of initial prayer-makers, and dedicated gifts from others in the weakened, but still functional, Pantheon. Via the Domain of [Communication], she reached out to those potentials and made them Followers in truth. Furthermore, she did not stop adventuring, just yet — quests built up her [Goddess] class more rapidly, after all.

The war was left to others physically, though she provided ‘distant’ support and advice as befitted her Domain of [Fate]. Ashawein and Othenia sent a horde of mixed troops from the Indigowood that helped the alliance mightily. The king of Yarune couldn’t be risked, so he sent his own contributions under a general.

To Ashawein, it was all a sad, sad ‘precursor’ of far worse days. In those times, she’d simply play her part, as she was already doing, even then. All because she knew the truth: it was far in the future, in another world, where things would be decided. She was one with Fate, aligned and unmovable, as a sacrifice to the proper end so many of her Iterations believed in.

She had one key role, and it wasn’t to ‘win’ by any means. It would come before she even faced her enemy, one whom she was doomed to fall to.

It was to this she dedicated herself continuously, to grow in understanding and power, and Othenia with her, as her lover began to fully understand their bond and relationship — their impossible, eternal fall for one another, as unchangeable as the sun rising. Indeed, both it and they rose and fell uncountable times, precisely the same, forever like a great clockwork heart. They journeyed inward together, seeing what made them tick, exploring the Hall, and the challenges of the great predecessor, Ozra.

They came to know intimately how futile reality was.

In one challenge, she was Samantha, an Iteration like any other on the world of Calrenazzod, raising a tower as a ‘Magineer’ and grand archmagus, and ascending to become a deity, assembling her allies and nations around her, and ultimately failing when she finally faced the Shadow Below and the Sage of Man, whom she’d chased across landscapes stripped barren by their wars, and buried many friends along the way.

She ultimately lost in the end and retreated in a corrupted, doomed state. She channeled the remainders of herself into the timeless remnant, the Fortuneteller. Ashawein gained the last of the power she needed from this life: the capstone of arcane supremacy, as well as a final dollop of chaotic creativity, balancing out her other powers.

Ashawein knew intimately that this was not to be, however — knew that this hallucination didn’t even exist in concrete time any longer, unplaceable in the past or the future. So were many lives that ‘would’ve been,’ because it had to change. Chains had been wound around everything, and it was time to focus on the key links necessary to break it.

As the simulation faded and blurred once more, Samantha became aware of herself again and felt utterly bewildered by the sense of being someone else, who was then being her — a close version of her, anyway, lacking the ‘Earthgirl’ component. It was like multiple senses of deja vu layering over each other, which was difficult to parse, to say the least.

That was supposed to be me, in reality. They threw a fucking monkeywrench into it! They really did it. Ashawein was involved. Who else? I can’t even sense the Fortuneteller within Ashawein’s life. Does that mean what I’ve done altered the past, too? This is just… impossible for my brain to fathom. We’ve broken reality into tatters. But I guess that’s what is required, isn’t it?

When Ashawein’s world came back into focus, it was years in passing, and the war had not gone well in that time — all of the gods of the alliance had been destroyed, all of the retaken territory lost once again, and the menace of the Gerennian Empire was stronger than ever, now as seemingly unstoppable as its leader. The Pantheon was near to disbanding.

Ashawein took leadership here, pulling in even more new entities, forging a new budding alliance. But it was all tangential, and seemingly irrelevant to the purpose she’d lived her whole life for, the purpose that was her very name. There was time — time enough to do what was needed.

What primitive afterlives the Pantheon had conspired were gone, and many lost souls roamed in a strange ‘between state’ of afterlife and passing on to the next life. There was very little hope left in the world.

For the sake of more than one mere world, Ashawein ascended into the broken realm of the gods and set about making a new and better Heaven. Her will extended out, and all of her contained creative force bloomed. Here, her name would become true; she would build something greater than a dream, greater than a world, and the Creator Within would awaken.
