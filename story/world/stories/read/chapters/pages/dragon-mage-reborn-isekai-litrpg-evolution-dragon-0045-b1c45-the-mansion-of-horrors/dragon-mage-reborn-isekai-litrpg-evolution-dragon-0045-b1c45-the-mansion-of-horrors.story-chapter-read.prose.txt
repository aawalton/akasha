I ran like an angry mob was chasing me down the street. Nolan, Nolan! I had to find Nolan! When I reached the festival, my eyes searched long and wide for him. Ah, there he was, in the middle of a huddle of mustached and long-bearded drunk men by the fire.

“And then I says to ‘em with a bloody smile and a broken nose, I says—I ain’t goin’ down without a fight!”

Nolan chuckled as he drank his beer. “Sounds like he left you with more than a broken nose, chief.”

“You bum, you outright soiled your pants!” the red-headed lizard beastkin cackled, sending the group into a chorus of laughter.

I tugged at Nolan’s trousers, trying to catch my breath.

‘Aye, short fry,’ he said through our private link with a wide smirk on his face. ‘Where the hell did you run off to?’

‘We need to talk,’ I insisted, dragging him away from the crowd. He stumbled at first, but when he realized how grave I was, he sobered up quickly.

‘All right then, spill it,’ he said with urgency as soon as we were out of earshot.

‘I saw her again—Klepto girl! The thief who stole my evolution stone!’

‘You mean the one who stole my gun,’ he reminded me. ‘Well, where is she now?’

‘I lost sight of her. A man in a white suit took her.’

‘A man in a white suit? Was he fae?’

‘Yeah, yeah! That bastard! Klepto tried to use a Kundarta Crystal on him but these two orcs came out of nowhere and stopped her! They kidnapped her, Nolan!’

Nolan paused as soon as I confirmed he was in fact fae. For a second, I swear it looked like he knew who I was referring to, that concerned look in his eye turning murderous. ‘And where the hell were you when all of this was happening?’

‘Relax,’ I said, trying to keep myself from shaking too much. ‘They didn’t notice me spying.’

Nolan ran his fingers through his hair and let out a long, exhausted sigh. ‘You shouldn’t have even been there. The last thing you need to be doing is running off on your own. You still don’t understand that you’re a dragon, Freya. Sought by dragon hunters, and a prize to beastkin dragon riders. Not to mention, you’re a baby dragon. If I lose you out there, we are fucked.’

‘I know, but—’

‘No, you don’t. If you can’t compete in the tournament, this world, as well as yours, will be traded to some scumbag galactic overlord and stripped of its resources, or worse. Everything you know will be gone, Freya.’

He turned away from me, seething with frustration. I knew he was right. ‘I’m sorry, Nolan.’ Those were the only words I could muster without sounding too desperate.

‘Look,’ he said after collecting himself. ‘If we’re going to do something about this, we need to know what we’re getting into first.’

‘The Ulmah,’ I said. ‘They mentioned it when I was spying on them. That fae sent the girl to get it, but what she returned was a fake. He wasn’t very happy about that and that’s when things went south. She stole all of those things for him, Nolan. He is keeping her brother hostage.’

‘Hostage, huh?’ He paused for a second, as if processing something. ‘I’d bet all my money that the guy you saw with her was Zev.’

‘Who?’

‘Zeverus. He’s one of Khunum’s men. His specialty—the organ trade market. He’s been working in Khunum’s operation since it began, his little side hobby growing exponentially once that leech started spreading his parasites.’ He paused, then looked down at me seriously. ‘We’ll go back to the festival and give our goodbyes. Then we’ll go to the site where you saw Zev. We’ll start there.’

My favorite beastfolk bunch were not very happy that we had to leave so soon. We had a very good reason, one that Nolan decided not to disclose to them. I was sure he had a good reason for it, but I thought that rallying up their help would have been best. After we’d bid our farewells, and caught a teleporter crystal and a neat stiletto dagger from the shaman as a parting gift, I asked my dear kill-for-hire why he didn’t tell them what had happened, and he said that more hands in the kitchen led to a bigger mess. And right now, he didn’t want anyone getting caught in said mess.

It felt more like a kitchen fire, but I understood the analogy.

What I thought was a petty little pro thief incident turned out to be much more serious than that. King Leech was involved, the same asshole Nolan didn’t want to get in the ring with. Well, now we really didn’t have much of a choice. We had to get our things back, and more importantly, save this girl’s brother.

‘We are saving her brother, right?’ I asked Nolan as we walked back to the bridge. I had to make sure, because something told me that Nolan might not have that bit on his agenda.

‘You mean the brother of the rat girl who stole our stuff?’

Oh well, that confirmed it. We weren’t on the same page.

‘So you’re telling me that you’re willing to let a girl and her brother get packed up by the Leech King?’

‘I’m not saying that… since I’m not exactly sure what the term packed-up means. What I’m saying is that it isn’t our concern.’

‘And why isn’t it?’

‘Freya, it’s dangerous enough bumping heads with the most notorious drug lord in all of Etheryn.’

‘Well, we’re already bumping heads with him now,’ I argued. ‘That guy, Zev, has my evolution stone. If I don’t get it back, I can’t evolve properly, and we’re screwed for the tournament.’

Nolan’s expression narrowed, and he didn’t say anything more for the rest of our walk. We continued in silence until we reached the bridge where I’d witnessed the exchange. The area was empty now. Nolan crouched, examining the ground where the Kundarta Crystal had shattered.

‘Residual energy signature,’ he muttered, brushing his fingers over a faint shimmer in the dirt. ‘Powerful stuff. This girl was either desperate or stupid to use this against Zev.’

‘Or both,’ I added quietly. ‘She seemed terrified for her brother.’

Nolan straightened up, scanning the area methodically, and then I asked, ‘A teleportation spell was cast here. Is there any way we could track where he went?’

‘I doubt it,’ Nolan said as he reached in his inventory for the Ulmah. “So, he wanted to get his hands on this,” he whispered to himself. “Could only imagine what that ass-kisser wanted it for….”

‘Nolan?’

He scoffed. “Grants one’s deepest desire…” The gunslinger paused and looked up into the night’s sky and said, “Nekutare vos mayum.” All of a sudden, the Ulmah began to vibrate in Nolan’s palm. Then zip—the ladybug wings sprung from the Ulmah, flapping rapidly. It gradually began to elevate off of his hand, hovering there while moving in a circular motion, as if trying to capture a direction.

‘Whoa, what’s happening?!’ I asked, watching as the Ulmah moved on its own.

‘It’s responding to my request,’ he said, his eye tracking the Ulmah as it darted forward, almost like a compass needle finding north. ‘It sent a ping to my codex map. We just need to follow it.’ He pulled out his teleporter crystal, a shimmering blue beauty that he stroked twice to activate. ‘Stand close.’

I did as instructed as he activated the crystal. The world blurred around us, colors stretching and warping as we teleported through space. My stomach lurched, and I squeezed my eyes shut against the disorienting sensation.

When I opened them again, we were standing at the edge of a dense forest. The air here felt different—heavier, charged with something that made my scales tingle. The Ulmah zipped ahead, its wings beating frantically as it led us deeper into the woods. The trees grew thicker, their branches intertwining overhead to block out the night sky. Strange luminescent fungi clung to the bark, the blue light they were giving off shining the wet ground underneath our feet.

‘Stay close,’ Nolan instructed. ‘We’re in Shadegrove territory now.’

‘What’s Shadegrove?’

‘A neutral zone between the fae and beastkin kingdoms. No laws, no protection. Perfect place for someone like Zev to make people disappear.’

We followed the Ulmah as it darted between trees, its trail getting deeper the more we traveled into this lawless woodland. The forest seemed to breathe around us. Occasionally, I’d hear rustling in the underbrush as creatures scurried away from our path.

‘Why is it called Shadegrove?’ I asked curiously.

‘Because the sun never fully penetrates here,’ he replied, ducking under a low-hanging branch. ‘These trees absorb magic from the soil and have been for thousands of years. The magic makes them grow so densely that daylight becomes non-exsistent once you're deep enough inside.’

As if responding to his words, a nearby branch creaked and shifted slightly. I shuddered, pressing closer to Nolan’s side.

The Ulmah suddenly changed direction, diving sharply to the left. We followed, pushing through a thick curtain of hanging moss that parted to reveal a clearing. Beyond that clearing was a hill, and on top of that hill, a manor. Its stone facade almost blended with the night’s sky, with only a few dim lights in the tall windows giving away its presence. The Ulmah hovered in place, wings beating steadily as it pointed toward the structure.

'“Well, if it isn’t Zev’s pleasure palace,” Nolan whispered, his voice tight with disgust. “Interesting location for a hideout.”

‘It looks….’ I struggled to find the right word. ‘Menacing.’

‘There’s probably no fortitude or warding sigils around this campus.’

‘And how do you know that?’

‘Because Zev’s too fucking arrogant to cover his ass.’

‘You say that like you know him.’ I pressed. ‘Do you know him, Nolan?’

Nolan answered me with silence. Something was clearly bothering him about this Zev guy, and he didn’t want to tell me.

I guess it’s a good thing that spiffy bastard was cocky. He believed that no one would dare cross him, or that he was untouchable because of his association with Khunum. Whatever the reason, it left us with an opening to get into his lair.

As we approached the manor, I noticed figures patrolling the grounds—orcs similar to the ones who had grabbed the klepto girl. No, bigger. They looked like they could snap my neck between their index fingers and thumbs.

‘How are we going to get past them?’ I whispered through our private link, as if those guards could hear my thoughts.

Nolan’s eye narrowed as he surveyed the manor’s layout. ‘We’re not going through the front door,’ he replied. ‘Places like this always have a servant’s entrance. Or better yet...’

He pointed to a section of the manor where the stonework looked slightly newer than the rest. ‘Underground passage. Probably leads to a cellar or dungeon.’

We circled the perimeter, keeping to the shadows cast by the enormous trees. The manor looked even more imposing up close, with grotesque gargoyles perched on the corners of the roof.

‘You’re drifting too far behind,’ Nolan insisted. I was barely a yard behind him but I quickened my pace to catch up. Something about this place made me want to hang back, to escape the growing dread in my gut. The air here felt wrong, like it was tainted with something beyond the physical—something that made my dragon senses scream danger.

Nolan led us to where the newer stonework met the old. Up close, I could see a narrow seam running along the ground—the outline of a trapdoor or entrance. He knelt, running his fingers along the edge until he pressed his fingers against the surface, and then, I heard a click. ‘Got it.’

The door opened to a darkness that made my stomach cave in. Pitch black, I couldn’t see a thing beyond the entrance. Not even my dragon vision could penetrate this void. ‘Feels steep. Maybe stairs up ahead,’ I warned him.

‘Keep the flames off unless you absolutely have to turn them on,’ he warned me.

‘I can’t see in the dark, can you?’ I sassed. ‘Ugh, if only I had my spider eye! I could sense heat signatures. It wouldn’t be good enough to light our way, but at least it’d help us map out our route by avoiding bodies.’

‘The living ones, at least….’ Nolan said just as a whiff of something vile went under our noses. My nostrils flared as I took a step back. The smell was so putrid that I thought I’d black out.

Even Nolan fell ill to the horrible stench, buckling against the wall as it looked like he was two seconds from fainting. He cupped his forehead and leaned his elbow against the stone, taking in deep breaths as he struggled to compose himself. I’d never seen Nolan so affected by anything before.

‘We should turn back,’ I suggested. ‘Your knees are weak.’

‘I’m good.’

‘Nolan, just a small break. You can barely stand.’

‘I said I'm fine,’ he snapped, straightening himself. His face had gone pale. ‘Just... be quiet and give me a second, would ya?’

Through our link, I could sense Nolan battling something more than just the stench—he had history here, something personal that made his thoughts churn like muddy water.

I gave him a second, and then he pulled a bandana from his pocket and tied it over his nose and mouth. ‘Here,’ he said, tearing a strip from his undershirt and handing it to me. ‘Wrap this around your face. The smell will get worse the deeper we go.’

‘Um, I can’t exactly tie this behind my big head! Would you kindly do the honors?’

‘Yeah, come here.’

Nolan wrapped the cloth around my snout, tying it tight enough that I could breathe through the fabric but still filter some of the stench. It didn’t eliminate the smell completely, but it made it bearable enough that my eyes stopped watering.

He insisted I stay close again, which at this point forced me to suggest hopping on his shoulder. As much as he hated my claws on him, he agreed. Down the steps he went, the both of us looking like a couple of bandits. Fitting. With his hand along the wall, Nolan felt his way down to another door, the Ulmah insisting we go through it. The only problem was that it was locked.

‘Is it something you can pick?’ I asked Nolan, who then proceeded to pull out a small kit from behind his cloak and along his belt. He selected a thin metal tool and began working the lock… in the dark….

‘Been a while since I had to do this,’ he muttered.

‘Bet it’s been even longer since you had to do it in the dark.’

‘Not really.’

I squinted, then cocked my eyes down at him as if I could see his face. ‘You’re full of surprises, aren’t you?’

In no time, the lock gave way with a satisfying click, and Nolan slowly pushed the door open. Unfortunately what lay beyond the door made me wish it had been locked forever….

Dim blue light from more of the same fungi we’d seen earlier was growing along the ceiling, illuminating a hallway of nightmares. We were in a long, narrow hallway lined with cells. Most were empty, but some were occupied….

I tried not to look too closely at the shapes huddled in the corners, covered in maggots, filth and heartache. Some corpses were barely recognizable as humanoid. Their faces gaunt, skin pulled tight across bone, body parts mangled or missing, and eyes gouged out entirely. Even through my makeshift mask, the rancid smell of brutalized death was overwhelming, threatening my last meal to rush back up.

I froze on Nolan’s shoulder, my claws digging into his cloak. The hallways stretched long and narrow, until we reached a fork in our path. We took one and abandoned the other, taking the Ulmah’s lead. More cages. These dead bodies were rotting at slower rates than the ones before. They deserved better. I didn’t know these victims, but my heart bled out for them. No one deserved this kind of fate….

As I thought we were walking through the remains of the dead, a woman on the right of us proved me wrong. She jump-scared me, my feet nearly stumbling off of Nolan’s shoulder. Instead, I dashed across his chest and ran over to the other side of his head, my heart racing to the sound of a fae woman who had banged her cell bars with her forehead.

“Please,” she begged in a frail, raspy voice. “End my suffering….”
