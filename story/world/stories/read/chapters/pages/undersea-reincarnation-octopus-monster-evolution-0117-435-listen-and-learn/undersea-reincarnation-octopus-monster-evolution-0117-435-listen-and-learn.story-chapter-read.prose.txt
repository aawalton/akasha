As the two queens stood side by side, looming over his Avatar, Henry refrained from letting any of his predatory aura leak. He’d had plenty of practice over the last week, and getting the random outburst under control wasn’t too difficult when there was someone with experience to coach him. He just had to keep his thoughts and emotions in check, while continuing to try and fail to wriggle out of his fleshy, disgusting bindings.

It turned out that aura was very much tied to emotions. In this scenario, his greed for the thousands of potential cores surrounding him was like looking at a buffet and forcing himself to not feel hungry. He just had to keep himself busy, and well, the disgust was helping. Above him, two pale, diminutive space-mantises had wrapped their fleshy tentacle-like arms around his head and limbs, which were currently both immobilizing him and suppressing his skills. It made it easier to lean into the helplessness of that specific Avatar, which should be decent enough as mental camouflage, though Henry wasn’t too confident there. These creatures were telepathic, and the architect queen knew that this was only a copy and not the real body, so there was no reason why his defiance or cravings for payback should be out of the ordinary.

He just had to make sure they didn’t take all of the spotlight. So he should not be fantasizing about how many levels and power each of the queens’ cores must contain. He should not be thinking of the fifteen hundred Avatars hovering less than a mile away from his position, ready to turn into a swarm of his own and tear into the thousands surrounding him.

Should be easy enough, Henry thought to himself as he directed his attention for a brief moment to his army, making sure nothing was sneaking up on them or was in a collision path with them. With Unfettered Echoes now at level 3, Henry had upwards of three thousand Avatars at his disposal, and he knew he hadn’t hit his limit just yet. He just had run out of mana to summon more. A week of slaughtering the space mantises had been just enough to get up to three thousand Avatars, though he was still strictly enforcing the buddy system. As for the range, it was a little harder to measure up here, but he’d say every Arcane Avatar could handle around three and a half miles before the connection began to weaken, which was plenty for Henry’s purposes. For now. So he just continued playing the victim, while a dozen miles away, his real body, along with Velistraine and Maurice watched the live broadcast which he was projecting across from their seats.

In the week they’d spent hunting and training, Henry had some time to liven up the place. A few bone seats here and there hadn’t been too much trouble to arrange with the help of his Octominds.

“Great. There’s more of them,” muttered Velistraine as he relayed to them the output of his Identify on the war queen. Naturally, the projection didn’t do the two queens justice, as Henry’s illusion couldn’t really recreate the sense of power and authority his Avatars were feeling in their presence, but… it wasn’t godly, either. They could blast him to chunks, but their power was attainable. Of that he was sure.

Though maybe not today, he thought, stilling as the architect’s humanoid hand detached from its side and reached forward. Seeing its smooth movement, Henry didn’t need to fake his apprehension, nor did he need to exaggerate his nervousness.

“[It looks like you didn’t need my help after all,]” said the whisper-like voice intruding on his mind. No—not just his mind. This wasn’t anything precise, but a broadcast to anything or everything that can hear it. Including the architect, whose arm froze in the middle of reaching for Henry’s Avatar for a moment before her antennas twitched. Henry blinked, then stretched his Telepathic Sense a little further, trying to get a better understanding of his opponents. Just like he expected, he sensed a little bit of annoyance out of the architect, while the second queen seemed… content? Its emotions felt muted. Sheathed, like a sword, and it was hard to differentiate the nuances, but there was no doubt the comment—which was sent his way as well, for some reason—had needled the architect a little.

“[Now is not the time for half measures,]” the familiar papery voice responded, closing its humanoid hand in a fist, head tilting to the side to put the other queen in her line of sight. “[It has the Graft’s touches on its soul. It stood with the ones that pushed me back. We can use it to locate the Graft again, extract it and everything worthwhile it had tucked away, then open the gateway. The mission is worth everything. Plus… its abilities are intriguing. We might be able to learn from it and improve our soldiers.]”

With every word it spoke, Henry felt its confidence and assuredness firm. That only lasted until the war queen gave her answer back.

“[If you had waited for my presence, we would have harvested the Graft and the world it’s protecting already. You failed once already. How’s this going to be any different?]”

Henry’s eyes were flicking between the two queens at this point, very much paying attention to the conversation and already considering bailing on his plan as the air suddenly twisted around him, while a great weight bore down on his soul. For a moment, Henry thought one of them had activated a skill, only to realize a moment later that this was merely the architect’s displeasure, which warped the air surrounding it as gibbering hues of purple and pink threatened to stab into his eyes.

Its aura was like nothing he’d ever seen. But it did nothing to the war queen, who just stood there, unruffled, and while the purple and pink displeasure of the architect gnashed at her, it never really managed to get near. As if there had been a glass separating them, nothing intruded on the cone of limpid, fluid-like air surrounding the war queen.

Still, he couldn’t help but consider their words. The bit about tracing back the origin of the Graft—which Henry was strongly assuming to be the System—was ringing some alarm bells in the back of his mind, though considering this was just an Avatar, what it detected should be minimal. The Avatar was connected to him, and only he had the barest connection to his Avatars in the Reach. As for sniffing out the System by using him… Henry had some doubts there. Not that he was underestimating the architect’s capabilities, but what would the “Graft’s touches” do for it, exactly? A divination anchor? There’s no way that by itself would be enough to breach the System’s protection. As for using him as bridge, he didn’t have a connection to the System at this moment, at least, none that he could detect on his soul. So how would that even work?

Then, as suddenly as it had come out, the aura retreated. The architect stood still for a moment, then without a word, it grasped for the link. Henry felt the touch itself, and he struggled to put to words what it felt like. The closest would be his experience with Arisia, where she would poke at his still forming skills with her mana and destabilize them before they could take form. In this scenario, it was less of a poking sensation and more of a tug, but… it led nowhere. The architect’s antennas danced above its head while it reached out with its second hand, attempting to grasp the link, but Henry’s connection to his Avatar only trembled. He felt his perception and control lag a little, for lack of a better term, but that was the extent of it. He wasn’t sure if it was the new Aspect levels that were helping, or because there were four extra hidden Avatars within the captured one, his connection to them was much stronger, but the architect wasn’t getting anywhere, and after another flare of frustration, it straightened up, unfurling its wings as she retracted her humanoid hands.

“What’s happening?” Maurice whispered next to him, and a part of Henry realized that neither Velistraine nor Maurice could have heard the queen’s broadcast.

“I’ll tell you in a second, just wait,” Henry managed. He might have been able to use an Octomind to relay everything that had been taking place, but he needed all of his focus on the scene right now, as with a gesture of her claw, more lumpy, skill-suppressing creatures ambled out from behind the lines of soldiers.

“[Take it to the Breeder Queen. We’ll study it in her ship.]”

“[You could not take the shell over?]”

The architect seemed to mull her words for a moment, then she stepped after Henry and his escort. “[No. But this is better. We’ll learn more.]”

And that’s my cue, Henry thought as he continued observing his surroundings for a moment. Naturally, he had zero intent on allowing himself to be taken into their ship. Who knows what else they were capable of. But it was good to know that at least these two could not take over his Avatars. At least, not easily. He now just had to detonate himself before the pale lumpy ones managed to get their mitts on him. Preferably, as soon as they made their presence known.

The pale, tentacle-using mantises lifted him off the road, carrying him to one of the larger ships, which likely would give them more room than the pill-bug ship. The architect was already following after them, likely to make sure her target was safe and tucked. The architect was ordering her soldiers about, while the war queen was staring at him.

Their eyes met, and for a brief moment, Henry’s control lapsed as a few miles away, he grinned. Some of his predatory aura must have leaked a little, because the war queen suddenly disappeared and for a brief moment, Henry believed he saw a line of darkness flash into existence. But before he could see where it’d go, this particular Avatar was already being torn from within as the four hidden Avatars he’d tucked within bone shells had already detonated, shredding both the captured Avatars and the skill-suppressing invaders.

Now, considering that his destruction of his bait had been accomplished, the question was this. Did he pull his army of Avatars back, or did he do some harvesting of his own?
