Clive pressed himself against the cave wall, barely breathing. He heard voices drifting down from outside, carried on the wind.

"Are you certain they're here?" Sion's voice cut through the howl of the storm.

"This is the Humbert range," Miranda's voice answered. "He told you himself. This was his destination. Where else would he go?"

"Assuming he was telling the truth." That was Guma, his tone carrying clear irritation. "This place is gods-damned freezing. How long are we supposed to search these mountains?"

A pause. Clive could picture Sion scanning the ridgeline, weighing his options.

"We sweep the southern face," Sion said. "Every cave, every outcropping. Miranda, take the upper elevations. Guma, work the lower slopes."

"I still can't believe he deserted." Guma's voice was bitter. "After everything we—"

"Enough." Sion's command silenced him. "Focus on the mission. We need to retrieve him."

Footsteps crunched through snow above. Heavy. Multiple sets. They were spreading out.

"And if he refuses to come?" Miranda asked. Her voice carried the tone of someone who'd already made her peace with the answer.

"Then we bring him back by force," Sion said. "Restrain him if necessary. The Pictomancer's abilities are too valuable to—"

"Prince." Miranda's voice dropped lower, harder to hear. "Be realistic. If he resists, if there's combat... we may not have the luxury of restraint."

"Are you suggesting—"

"I'm saying we need clear orders. Lethal force—is it authorized?"

There was a long pause. Clive’s chest tightened as he waited for the answer. Despite their differences, he had still considered Sion an ally. They fought the Dragon King together. Surely that had to count for something.

"If restraint proves impossible..." Sion's exhale carried across the wind. "Then yes. Lethal force is authorized. We can’t allow him to fall into Vandiel's hands."

Lethal force.

So it came to this after all. Clive half expected it, but it was still a difficult pill to swallow. Friendship had a limit, apparently, and Clive had just exceeded it.

"Understood," Miranda said. "And the dragon?"

"Azura is bonded to him. If we take Clive, she'll follow."

"And if she doesn't?"

"Then she's a hostile combatant." Sion's voice hardened. "Treat her accordingly."

The footsteps moved away, fading into the howl of wind and snow.

They'll kill us both. Azura's mental voice trembled. You and me. They'll—

"No." Clive's hand found her scales in the darkness. "I won't let them. I won’t let them hurt us."

His eyes cut across the cave to where Jill sat against the wall. Her head was turned away, but he could see the tension in her shoulders. She was listening.

"Any of us," he said quietly.

Clive... Azura shifted closer, pressing her snout against his shoulder. I've never fought other dragons before. Not like this. Not for real.

He could feel her shaking.

And Ignis—he's the Dragon King. To raise claws against him would be... She struggled with the word. Blasphemy. It would be blasphemy.

"I know."

Then what are we—

"I don't know yet." His hand moved along her neck. "But we'll figure it out. Together."

Across the cave, Jill's head turned slightly. Not enough to look at him. Just enough that he knew she'd heard. "There's a cave system. North face, maybe fifty meters down. Tunnel runs deep into the mountain with multiple exits. If you can reach it before they finish their sweep..."

Clive stared at her. "Thank you, Jill."

"I just don't want to be here when they find this cave." Her tone was dismissive. "Don't read into it."

She was lying. Clive could tell. It was the same tone she had when she’d told him she was fine after sixteen-hour shifts at work. When she’d said it’s nothing about things that were very much something.

Typical Jill, always too proud to show any weakness. But he didn't call her on it. That had never worked with Jill. Push too hard and she'd just build the walls higher, dig in deeper. Better to let her keep her pride, let her pretend this was pure self-preservation.

"Where?" Clive asked. "Exactly where is this entrance?"

Jill finally looked at him then. "I'll show you," she said. "But we move now. Before they circle back."

Clive nodded and grabbed his gear. He was ready to move out but a look at Azura slowed him down.

She was massive. Even crouched low, even trying to make herself small, she was a dragon. Her Azure scales blended well enough with snow, but not her shape. Not her movement. From the air, she'd stand out like a ink blot on fresh canvas.

They'd spot her in seconds.

What? Azura caught his hesitation.

"We need to hide you," he said, already reaching for his sketchbook.

Three separate camouflage cloaks would take too long. Miranda could circle back any moment. But one large cloak, dragon-sized...

He flipped to a blank page and started sketching.

His pencil flew across the paper. There was no time for refinement, just the essential details. Heavy wool fabric, thick enough to hold shape. The mottled pattern of white and gray and shadow that would match the snowfield. He made it enormous, draping it in his mind over Azura's back and wings, pooling on the ground with fabric to spare for Jill and himself.

His Artist's Eyes tracked the details. The way snow clung to fabric. The texture of woven wool. The weight of it, heavy enough to fall naturally but light enough to move in.

The cloak materialized and immediately tried to collapse him under its weight. He staggered, catching the bulk of it before it hit the ground.

"Help me," he said to Jill.

She hesitated, then moved forward. Together they draped the massive cloak over Azura's back. The dragon crouched low, making herself as small as possible—which still wasn't very small.

This is ridiculous, Azura muttered. I look like a moving hill.

"That's the idea." Clive pulled the fabric forward, arranging it so it covered Azura's head and neck. Then he ducked underneath, pressing close to the dragon's warm scales. "Jill, come on."

Jill stared at the narrow space between him and Azura.

"We don't have time," he said.

She ducked under the cloak.

The space was cramped. Maybe even intimate. Clive found himself pressed against Azura's side with Jill inches away, close enough that he could feel the heat of her body, smell the blood on her bandages mixed with sweat and that faint lavender scent she'd always worn.

Her breath hitched. For a moment she looked like she might bolt back into the cave.

"Stay close," he said quietly. "We move together."

Azura began walking, slow and careful. The cloak shifted and swayed with her movements, but the mottled pattern should blend with the snowfield from above. Should being the operative word.

They emerged from the cave into the full force of the storm.

Wind immediately tore at the cloak's edges. Snow pelted the fabric, accumulating on the surface. Clive gripped the material with one hand, helping to steady it, while his other hand held Azura's scales.

Jill moved beside him, limping but keeping pace. Their shoulders brushed with every step. Neither of them acknowledged it.

Dragon ahead, Azura's warning cut sharp. Don't move.

They froze.

Above them, a massive shadow passed—Guma's dragon, wings spread wide, riding the wind currents. Firelight bloomed in its throat, casting sweeping beams across the mountainside.

Clive held his breath. Beside him, Jill had gone still.

The light swept past them. Once. Twice.

Then the dragon banked away, heading toward the upper ridges.

Clear, Azura said. Keep moving.

They continued their slow traverse across the exposed snowfield. Every step felt like tempting fate. Every gust of wind that caught the cloak's edge sent Clive's heart hammering.

Jill stumbled. Her injured leg buckled and she started to fall—

Clive caught her arm, steadying her. For a moment they stood there, her weight against him, close enough that he could see the exhaustion etched in every line of her face.

"I'm fine," she said, pulling away. But she didn't let go of his arm entirely. Just shifted her grip, using him for balance as they walked.

There, Azura's mental voice directed them. The outcropping ahead.

Clive saw it—a jagged formation of rock jutting from the mountainside, dark against white. And at its base, barely visible in the storm, a darker shadow that could have been just another trick of the light.

But as they drew closer, it resolved into a narrow opening almost hidden by the angle of the rocks.

The cave entrance Jill had promised. They were twenty meters away when dragon fire lit up the sky behind them.

"Contact!" Guma's voice rang across the mountain, sharp with battle-fury. "We've got Vandiel rats! Multiple hostiles!"

Screams followed the sound of battle. Magic detonating against scales. Someone was dying badly up there.

Clive's hand tightened on Azura's scales. Keep moving, he thought. Don't look back.

But Jill had stopped.

She went rigid beside him, causing him to bump into her. When he turned, her face was pale.

Another high-pitched scream filled the air.

"My people," Jill’s voice trembled. "They're killing my people."

Your people? Azura's mental voice dripped skepticism.

Clive thought for a moment and realized that she had to be referring to the cultist he encountered on his journey up the hill.

"We have to help them." Jill grabbed Clive's sleeve. Her desperate eyes met his. "Please. They don't stand a chance against dragons. They'll be slaughtered."

Clive stared at her.

The cave entrance was right there. Escape. Everything he'd been working toward for the last ten minutes of creeping through snow and praying they wouldn't be spotted.

And Jill wanted him to turn around and fight his former allies to save the cultists who'd tried to kill them.

What now?
