Vector stiffened and tried to speak again. His throat, mouth, tongue—every organ meant to form sound—refused his command. Not a single word escaped him.

"...Unc?" the attacker muttered, the incomplete word hanging in the air like a question carved from suspicion itself.

Vector's eyes flicked desperately to the side, searching for anything that might serve as salvation.

A small table stood to his right, its surface worn smooth by years of use, and atop it rested a flower pot from which a single crimson bloom opened its petals to the morning like a drop of blood frozen in time.

If he could knock it down—just one sound, one crash of ceramic against stone—anything to shatter the oppressive silence that pressed against his eardrums like physical weight, perhaps it would be enough to alert his protector.

He tried to move, commanding his body with every fragment of will he possessed.

Nothing responded, as though the connection between mind and flesh had been severed by an invisible blade. Not his fingers, which hung uselessly at his sides. Not his arms, which might as well have been carved from granite. Not even his neck, which refused to turn even a fraction of an inch despite his screaming mental commands. His body stood frozen, rigid as a statue carved by an artist obsessed with capturing the moment before death, every muscle locked in place by forces he couldn't comprehend.

Yet the force showed strange leniency to his lungs and eyes, allowing him to breathe and see even as it denied him all other agency. His pupils still obeyed, tracking the assassin’s approach with the frantic darting of a caged animal watching its predator close in.

That was the only mercy he had been given, a cruel gift that let him witness his own approaching death with perfect clarity.

He watched the assassin. Steady. Measured steps. Moving toward him slowly.

Vector's heartbeat increased with each step.

His first thought was that the restraint came from the attacker. The attacker? No. It's not him. He was certain—he had seen the man’s shock, the confusion in his eyes, the cautious way he approached. A First-Stage Warrior didn’t possess this kind of power.

That certainty deepened when Vector felt an unknown energy begin to fuse with him, merging with his flesh and blood in a way that felt both alien and strangely welcoming. Warm and gentle, it seeped into his body like warm liquid poured into a vessel, spreading through his veins with the slow inevitability of ink dispersing through water. With each fusion, each moment of integration, he felt his strength increase in ways that defied his understanding of how power worked, and his mind cleared, thoughts sharpening to crystal precision even as terror threatened to shatter his composure.

But now wasn't the time to enjoy the unexpected gift that had fallen into his lap, to marvel at the transformation happening in his very cells. Otherwise, he would be killed before he could appreciate what he was becoming.

He tried again—to scream, to cry out, to force even a single syllable past his frozen lips—but the sound never left his mind, trapped behind walls of silence that felt insurmountable.

Ahhhh... Aaaaaaa.

It didn't manifest physically, remaining locked inside his skull like a prisoner beating against cell walls. From the outside, he must have looked insane—body frozen in an unnatural stillness, eyes flickering wildly like a cornered animal, mouth half-open in a silent grimace that spoke of horrors only he could see.

Inside, terror clawed at him with razor-sharp talons as death approached with the slow inevitability of sunset, drawing closer with each breath he took.

Uncle...

Why aren't you coming?

Your young master is being attacked.

The attacker paused mid-stride, his advance halting as his eyes swept the room one final time.

Finding no resistance, no disturbance, no hidden threat waiting to spring from the shadows, his shoulders relaxed with the ease of a man who had confirmed his safety. Almost lazily, he resumed stroking the blade with absent affection, fingers running along steel like a lover's caress, and the grin returned—broader now, stretching his lips into something that barely resembled a human expression—filled with confidence that came from knowing victory was inevitable. He must have sensed no other presence in the building, his First Stage senses detecting only two heartbeats in this dilapidated house.

With unhurried steps that spoke of a man savoring every moment, he closed the remaining distance between predator and prey.

Vector’s face remained unreadable, betraying his panicked ridden internal emotions. Terror. Horror. These were all the things Vector was experiencing. He only prayed that his protector could somehow arrive.

But seeing the attacker almost upon him now, close enough that he could see the individual pores on the man's skin, his mind dissolved into pure chaos.

I shouldn't have pushed for information.

I should've called him immediately.

Idiot.

He even began calling himself an idiot in desperation, the word repeating like a mantra of self-condemnation.

He begged silently for anything—a miracle, an intervention, even the smallest accident that might save him. A broken cup falling from an unseen shelf. Even the tiniest disturbance that might alert Sebastian to danger.

But the room remained silent, offering no comfort and no salvation.

The assassin's steps were barely audible, reduced to whispers of leather on stone, and only breathing sounds echoed in the room—his own, rapid and shallow, and the attacker's, slow and controlled.

The attacker drew closer with the patience of someone who had done this many times before, his smile stretching unnaturally wide across his face like a mask threatening to tear, eyes gleaming with the dark enjoyment of someone who derived pleasure from watching others suffer.

No... No... there has to be something.

Is someone controlling this energy? Vector pleaded inwardly, directing his thoughts to whatever force held him captive. If you are—please. Stop it. We can make a deal. Kill him first. Then do whatever you want with me.

When the attacker closed to only two steps away, close enough that Vector could smell the man's sweat and see the anticipation glittering in his eyes, his feared thoughts accelerated into a frantic spiral of desperation.

I don't want to die.

Mother... Nova...

A desperate call for rescue echoed through his mind like a scream in an empty cathedral, but not a single syllable escaped his lips to disturb the terrible silence.

Ancestors! Your lovely child is going to die.

Anyone save me.

His thoughts were fractured like shattered glass, lacking unity or coherence, calling for help from everyone he had ever known—family, friends, divine beings he barely believed in—but all beings were unavailable, unreachable, unable to hear his silent screams.

Then a change appeared in the energy that had been flowing through him with such gentle warmth.

The mysterious energy, that was till felt like warm current inside, bringing relief to him had suddenly changed its nature entirely. It brought more pain as it poured into him. The energy still continued to flow and change the very essence of his body, the physique, muscles and organs.

But this energy didn't only act tangibly but had now begun to also act intangibly—his very origin, the soul. An unknown disturbance had begun in his brain. The reason for his agony became clear as pain bloomed like fire across his consciousness. The energy was even trying to help him bear this pain.

Information he didn't know began etching into his mind like words being carved into stone, and images that he didn't remember flickered across his vision like fragments of someone else's life.

Him being hit by a massive metal box, which had metal wheels and moved with terrible speed, appeared in his mind's eye with shocking clarity.

Huh... this again?

No—this isn't a dream.

As this image revealed more detail than before, more motion, more structure, more reality than the fragmented dreams or... nightmares that had plagued him. In his dreams, he had only seen the impact—the moment of collision, the flash of pain—but now he saw the whole thing: the box moving with mechanical precision, rolling metal wheels on parallel metal rods that stretched into the distance, a vehicle of a type that shouldn't exist in this world.

A suspicion arose in his mind like dawn breaking over a dark horizon.

As more memories filled his vision—memories of strange buildings that touched the sky, of lights that needed no flame, of knowledge and technology beyond anything Ethereum had ever conceived—he reached a conclusion that should have been impossible.

This mysterious energy somehow seemed to be related to those dreams, those fragmentary visions he had experienced before but always fractured, always incomplete, never as clear as they appeared today with devastating clarity.

The memories awakening intensified, each one crashing into him like waves against cliffs—alien yet familiar, familiar because he had dreamed of them in fragments, incomplete pieces that had never made sense until now when they awakened whole, complete, demanding to be acknowledged as truth rather than fantasy.

Pain was almost unbearable, a white-hot agony that threatened to shatter his consciousness into a thousand scattered pieces.

Time stretched—thick, sluggish, reluctant to move—as though the universe itself had slowed to witness this transformation.

The attacker stopped once again, halting his slow and savoring approach.

Vector's pale face had become reddish, as if vitality were being gathered into him from the mysterious energy that coursed through every cell of his being, and muscles beneath his skin rippled visibly—fibers tightening, loosening, reshaping themselves according to some blueprint written into the energy itself. His body trembled, the vibration slow and uneven at first, then growing faster, stronger, until his entire frame shook with the force of transformation.

Rough, damaged flesh smoothed over like clay being worked by an invisible sculptor, leaving pristine skin behind that gleamed faintly in the morning light.

The attacker's eyes widened further as his smile vanished completely, replaced by naked fear. Seeing the transformation of Vector's muscles, watching flesh reshape itself according to laws that shouldn't exist, his eyes darted around the room as he reflexively tightened his fingers around the knife as if he had witnessed something that violated every natural law he understood.

Vector's unknown transformation must have startled him badly enough to overcome his professional composure.

With a burst of inhuman speed born of panic rather than confidence, he appeared beside Vector in an instant, closing the distance that had separated them.

He swung the knife down without even taking time to aim properly, desperation overriding technique.

The blade struck Vector's left shin, slicing cleanly through fabric with effortless sharpness that spoke to the weapon's quality.

The attacker paused, leaning in with anticipation creeping back into his expression as a grin slowly formed after not finding anything immediately wrong with his target.

The blade had struck true, cutting through cloth as expected.

But there was no injury, no torn flesh, no blood welling up from the wound that should have been there.

The grin faltered, receding back into neutrality as confusion replaced confidence, and he stared first at Vector's leg where the blade had struck, then at Vector's face, searching for answers to questions he couldn't articulate.

Vector felt only the faintest sensation where steel had met skin—a tickle, nothing more, no pain beyond that gentle touch. After the knife struck, he felt that mysterious energy rush instantly to the point of impact, gathering there like water filling a depression, shielding him completely from harm that should have laid his flesh open to the bone.

That was why he remained unharmed despite the blade's sharpness and the force behind the blow.

Now he was certain beyond any doubt that the energy was protecting him, forming an invisible armor beneath his skin even as it continued to make him stronger with each passing moment.

Only the timing could have been better, as it had come at the worst possible time when death stood two steps away with a blade in hand.

"How could it be..." he heard the attacker murmur in disbelief, the words barely audible.

The man struck again with more force this time—same location, same left shin, as if repetition might yield different results.

Nothing changed; the blade met the same invisible resistance that refused to yield.

Again he struck with growing desperation, and again the same result greeted his efforts.

"No!" the attacker snarled, frustration and fear bleeding into his voice. "What power are you using? Why can't I even scratch your skin?!"

The mysterious energy protected him again and again, forming an impenetrable barrier that turned aside the knife's sharp edges as though they were blunt wood rather than honed steel.

The attacker changed his aim from Vector's leg to his neck, targeting a vital area where even supernatural protection might fail. "I don't believe you can stop this either," he declared with the desperate confidence of a gambler betting everything on one final throw.

Clank!

The knife never reached its target, stopped cold before it could come even within arm's reach of Vector's throat.

A shield flared into existence—brilliant yellow, translucent, humming with power that made the air vibrate—appearing so briefly that if Vector hadn't been focused intently on the knife's trajectory, he barely would have discovered its manifestation, and he even doubted that the attacker had seen it at all through his haze of desperation.

The knife slipped from the attacker's hand, torn free by the rebounding impact of striking something far harder than flesh.

At the same instant, a violent gale exploded through the window as though summoned by the shield's appearance, and the wind—having a life and purpose of its own—slammed into the attacker's outstretched arm with the force of a charging bull.

Crack!

The sound of breaking bones rang through the room with sickening clarity, and his arm dropped uselessly, hanging limp and motionless after the impact, gravity alone dictating its motion as all strength fled from shattered bones.

The shield had defended Vector from the blade's kiss.

The gale had struck back with vengeful force, punishing the one who dared attack.

Chairs, the tea table, the flower pot with its crimson bloom—all were swept up in the violent gale that filled the room like a living thing, attaining brief flight as they were hurled upward toward the ceiling, brushing against it before crashing down in a cacophony of destruction.

K-RACK!

Burst!

Crank!

Psst—!

RACKKK!

Dust filled the room, hanging in the air like fog as debris settled.

The attacker staggered backward, nearly tripping over his own feet, eyes gone wild with terror that had replaced all traces of confidence, and his gaze locked onto Vector—not as prey any longer, but as something monstrous, something that should not exist. His gaze seemed to question whose scion he had dared to touch, whether this was some madman who enjoyed pain and had deliberately allowed the attack just to savor the look of terror that would follow.

But Vector couldn't really respond to that questioning stare even if he wanted to, being restrained still by the mysterious energy that held him motionless, and the pain that wracked his body rendered him unable to object to the unspoken accusations in that horrified gaze—he wasn't like the attacker, wasn't someone who derived pleasure from others' suffering.

The attacker's foot landed on shattered pottery that littered the floor.

K-rack.

Fragments splintered further beneath his weight, grinding into smaller pieces.

Vector understood the man's fear perfectly well, could read it in every line of his face, but he himself was no less confused by what had just transpired.

He tried moving his head to look around properly, but he remained immobile due to the mysterious energy that still gripped him, yet seeing that yellow light and feeling the wind's intervention, he was certain that someone else was responsible for his protection.

This time it wasn't the mysterious energy that had protected him from the blade, he was sure of it because the mysterious energy was being produced within him and then fusing with his flesh, not coming from outside, not emerging from the surroundings like the shield and wind had. So for the mysterious energy to act defensively, it needed to work through his body, creating that invisible armor beneath his skin.

But the shield that had deflected the knife... the wind that had shattered the attacker's bone... Those weren’t from this mysterious force. He didn't feel the same sensation of the energy coursing through his body.

So, he was certain beyond any shadow of doubt.

There was someone else.

Unable to move, frozen in place by forces beyond his control, he could only observe the aftermath of violence, the gale's destruction etched across every surface of the room.

But he didn't have the mental space to divert his thinking toward solving these mysteries because a new agony had begun to consume him—a soul-rending pain that felt like his very essence was being torn apart and reassembled. He could feel the pain with overwhelming clarity but couldn't identify its origin, couldn't point to any physical wound or injury that might explain this torture.

Vector had begun struggling wildly as the mysterious energy finally released him from its paralytic grip, his whole body quivering and trembling with movements that weren't the transformation he had been going through before.

The intensity of the pain had increased so dramatically that it became impossible to keep his eyelids open, the simple act of seeing requiring more strength than he could muster, and mental exhaustion dragged at him like weights tied to his consciousness.

The mysterious energy had released him from its restraints at last, returning control of his body to him.

This shaking, this trembling that wracked his frame, was from pain rather than transformation, and this wasn't the reshaping of flesh that had been taking place moments before—that process was already complete, leaving him changed in ways he didn't yet understand.

His pain was manifesting physically now rather than remaining trapped in his sea of consciousness as it had before, and he could no longer suppress the sounds that wanted to escape.

"Ahhhh..." The groan tore from his throat, raw and agonized.

Darkness slowly crept into his vision like ink spreading across paper, claiming more territory with each heartbeat.

Then—

BOOM!

The door exploded inward with a sound like thunder, wood splintering into a thousand fragments.

Not even a breath's time had passed after the heavy shattering sound from the wind's devastating effect, and already the door was destroyed, torn from its hinges by a force that made the earlier gale seem gentle by comparison.

Vector forced his eyes open with the last reserves of his willpower, clinging to consciousness with desperate determination because he needed to see, needed to confirm what his heart already knew.

To see the figure he had been praying so desperately to arrive before his eyes closed forever.

In safe hands at last.

The strong hands lifted him gently despite their obvious power, cradling him with the care one might show a wounded child, and relief flooded through Vector's battered consciousness like water through a desert.

"Uncle Se—" Vector managed, his voice barely a whisper, each syllable requiring tremendous effort to form, and a warm hand rested against his head with familiar comfort.

"...bastian." The name left his lips as his consciousness slipped away into welcoming darkness, and he went limp in his protector's arms, finally allowing himself to surrender to the darkness engulfing his eyes.

Sebastian.
