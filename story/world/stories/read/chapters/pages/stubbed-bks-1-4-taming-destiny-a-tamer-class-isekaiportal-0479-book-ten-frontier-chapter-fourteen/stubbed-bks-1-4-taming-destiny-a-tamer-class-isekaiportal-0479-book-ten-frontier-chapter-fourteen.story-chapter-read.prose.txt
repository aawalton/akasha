There’s a tense moment when the nunda is released from his cage of earth and glares at me, his fur ruffled. Then he starts grooming himself and I relax. It looks like he’s going to stick to his word. Good.

I catch Kalanthia’s eye, then tilt my head towards the rest of our companions waiting tensely on the sidelines.

“I think there are a few people who would like to say hello,” I tell her quietly.

She follows my gaze, then, after glancing back briefly at the male nunda, steps quickly towards the disparate bunch.

Her greeting with Bastet is dignified, her great head rubbing gently against Bastet’s. Lathani is far less reserved – leaping at her and practically burying herself in Kalanthia’s fur. After those two have said hello, the rest all pile in, ranging from a polite lift of the chin from the samurans, to a close-quarters rub from Trouble.

I don’t realise I’m smiling until the male nunda speaks to me and wipes the expression off my face.

Hurt her, and I’ll kill you, he tells me matter-of-factly, a distinct sense of menace directed my way as he pauses his grooming to glare at me.

I look at him with more frustration than fear, though the latter is warranted, certainly.

“That is not my intention. As I’m sure I’ve made clear by this point.”

Intentions are not the same as actions, Tilathis replies mysteriously.

I have to stop myself from rolling my eyes.

“However, until actions happen, intentions are all we have.”

Perhaps. However, it is actions that count. My warning stands.

“Noted,” I reply, withholding a sigh. “Though, for the record, I reckon Kalanthia is pretty good at taking care of herself.”

She has a soft heart, the nunda murmurs with a slightly contemplative air, and I can tell she’s already given part of her soul to you.

That makes me blink.

“You mean through the Bond…”

The nunda gives me an impatient look.

Not at all. Do you truly not know of what I speak?

“No,” I confirm cautiously.

I feel it in you – expanding and strengthening your own soul. You must have carried it for a while – it is accepted by your own almost seamlessly. It is one reason I was so concerned about your Bond – I imagined that you had stolen it from her, leaving only a ragged hole behind.

That raises far more questions than it answers. I’ve never noticed the ‘seams’ he’s talking about while in any level of Meditation. Kalanthia herself has never hinted at it. Could the nunda be lying? Perhaps trying to sow discord between us?

I decide it’s something I need to speak to Kalanthia about. Alone.

“Thanks for the information,” I tell him noncommittally. “Now, regarding moving forwards, do you intend to return to your mountain home?”

The nunda stretches, his claws scything out of his paws as he pushes them forward and arches his back, his sword-like teeth on full display as he opens his mouth wide in a yawn. Those canines are long enough to impale me from head to crotch – and still have a handwidth left over. Forget elephants, this nunda looks like he’d eat a T-Rex for breakfast.

But since I know that this display is intended to scare me, I keep my expression unimpressed, crossing my arms and sending a sense of ‘still waiting’.

I shall not return unless I have Kalanthia with me, he tells me with a slightly disgruntled air as he pulls himself to sit upright, lifting one paw to his mouth to lick it with a tongue long enough to wrap around me.

“It’s up to her, but I suspect she won’t want to leave without Lathani. And Lathani won’t want to leave us. Not for long, at any rate.” I’m completely confident on both of those counts. Lathani might be willing to go away for a ‘holiday’, but her home is with us – by her own will.

Then I shall remain here. He eyes the cavern. Your cave looks big enough for me, at least.

I stare at him incredulously.

“You can’t think that you’ll be permitted to stay inside the cavern.”

He pauses his grooming, glaring at me.

Why not?

I let out a short, sharp burst of laughter.

“Tell me, would you allow another Tier four beast who’s already threatened you to stay in your cavern with your mate and cub?”

Of course not, he replies immediately. But you have no mate or cubs.

I blink. How does he know that? Then I remind myself that that is unimportant.

“Irrelevant,” I tell him crisply. “I am the leader and protector of everyone who lives in there. They all count as my…mate and cubs. And I’m not letting you inside when I don’t know whether you’ll turn around and start killing them at any moment.”

The possibility of a Bond flashes through my mind. A moment of consideration later, I dismiss it – even if he agreed to accepting one, it wouldn’t offer any sort of guarantee. Tame never allows me to control another’s actions, and I’m not strong enough yet to forcibly keep a Dominate in place if this Tier four wanted to be free. And a Bond that is only there as long as he wants it would be no use if he decided to turn against us.

Alliance is another question, and something I decide might be worth pursuing. It doesn’t offer any guarantees, but it would notify each of us if the other broke their side of the bargain. A warning, at least.

I look up at the nunda who is grooming his ears with a particularly sulky air.

I do not wish to stay in the open forest. Neither do I wish to run between my home and here all the time. I am fast, but there are limits.

I shrug.

“Perhaps Kalanthia would help you make a cave to stay in, then.”

The nunda pauses for a moment. Then, resuming his grooming, he offers a thought. Perhaps. I sense that he’s thinking, so stay quiet. It seems inappropriate for the territory king to have a den so much less magnificent than a mere territory lord, he comments after a moment.

I stifle the smile that fights to emerge on my face, carefully muffling my anticipation so as not to tip him off. Hook.

“I could probably convince other Earth-Mages to help create a cavern to your specification and worthy of a…king,” I say off-handedly.

He stares at me suspiciously – he can probably tell from my thoughts that my nonchalance isn’t completely real. But he’s intrigued nonetheless.

That is how you created this cavern? he asks, curious despite himself.

“Yes,” I confirm. “A ritual allows us to do far more than a single Earth Mage could do working alone, even one as good as Kalanthia.”

He narrows his eyes at me..

And what price would I have to pay?

Gotcha.

“Nothing serious. We’d have to agree to an Alliance, that’s it.”

Of course, the nunda is suspicious, and he almost slips free of my hook when he hears that it’s a type of Bond. But the temptation of having a magnificent den not far from his mate and cub is enough to reel him back in.

Casting Alliance, it then takes a while for us to settle on the actual terms. Tilathis, obviously, wants to make it that I can’t hurt Kalanthia or send her into danger. I negotiate it down to not intentionally hurting Kalanthia or sending her unknowingly into danger – because even Tilathis has to admit that battle will inevitably happen. On his side, I insist that he can’t attack anyone affiliated with me – human or beast. He counters that with a stipulation of unprovoked attack which I agree to. I’ll need to make sure everyone knows that the massive nunda is not to be targeted on pain of death – at a nunda’s teeth and claws.

By the time we’ve finished arguing and finalised the Alliance, the sun has moved halfway into the sky. Kalanthia finished her greetings ages ago and my companions have dispersed – some back to the settlement, others into the forest in a hunting party. Bastet is leading that one – she did check with me whether I wanted her to stay nearby, but I sent her off. I’m pretty sure I have the measure of Tilathis by this point.

In the end, only Kalanthia remains, lying in the sun with her eyes closed. However, she’s very clearly not asleep, and the moment Tilathis pushes to his feet, her eyes blink open even if the rest of her remains still.

I’m hungry, he announced, his eyes on her. Will you come and join me, my mate?

Kalanthia hesitates.

I would enjoy hunting with you again, my mate. However I owe Markus an explanation for my absence.

Tilathis bristles slightly.

You owe him nothing, he asserts firmly.

Kalanthia pushes herself fluidly to her feet, then steps lightly over to the bigger nunda, rubbing her head affectionately against his chin.

I owe him much, she contradicts him, more gently than I’m used to hearing her. It reminds me of the time she comforted me after a nightmare back in the samuran’s world. Perhaps, with her clearly protective mate around, this is the first time she’s felt safe to reveal it in a while. I feel slightly sad at that thought, but tune back into the action as Kalanthia steps away from Tilathis. I will join you later. Go hunt.

Tilathis hesitates for a moment, then huffs and turns, disappearing quickly into the forest as only a big cat can, white and black pelt notwithstanding.

“Is this where you tell me where you were when I summoned you? Why you were in such a bad state?” Who you were with, I finish silently to myself.

Yes, she sighs. And more. She eyes me slightly uncertainly. I have kept my distance from you, Markus Wolfe, even when I began to trust you. Her ears droop sadly.

“It’s understandable. I know you had bad experiences with humans in the past. And you had a cub to protect.”

Yes. That’s true. Yet it was cowardice disguised as prudence. You have proven time and time again that you will not take advantage of opportunities to entrap me. And yet, I kept my greatest secrets hidden from you.

I hesitate.

“I know what you said to your mate, but you don’t really owe me anything.”

She gives me a long stare.

You do not think I can sense the cost you paid to summon me across such a distance – and through the magical net that entrapped me?

I wave that away.

“Kalanthia, you’re family. I don’t count favours between family members.” I hesitate again.

The muzzle, those ropes around her legs that I still need to figure out how to remove…. They make it clear that I absolutely do need to know the information that she has for me. But at the same time, I don’t want her to give it just because she feels she owes me. “There is no debt between us. But I would very much appreciate anything you’re willing to tell me. Especially if it will help me protect the pack – and everyone else,” I say finally. And deal out some well-earned retribution for the bastards who would do something like that to a thinking, feeling being like Kalanthia!

The female nunda closes her eyes for a moment as if suddenly pained. Regret passes across the Bond between us.

I owe you more than you think, she murmurs. For my foolishness has brought danger to your doorstep. Even if I leave, they will most likely follow the traces of my signature back here. I will tell you what I can – it may not do any good, but it will allow you to prepare for when they come.

I swallow, my muscles automatically tensing.

“What do you mean? Who are they?”

The Blood Fang clan.

“And who are the Blood Fang clan when they’re at home?” I ask, slightly exasperated. It’s one thing Kalanthia keeping secrets; it’s another when she drip-feeds information for no apparent reason. “Humans, I take it?”

No. Elves.
