Breathing a dust that stretched down the hall, a 16-year-old boy hungrily searched for a specific book. One that defied his perception of reality. Or one that was longer than five hundred pages. Yes, he was that desperate.

In the long, high corridor, books shared space with tools and long forgotten artifacts on the shelves. Maybe the pride of the bookstore was lost to time. Was one of Harry's many ruminations about the place he worked. He wasn't necessarily wrong, but it was arrogant of him to assume the shop was only about books. That's why he never told his thoughts to the owner.

The surrounding area was dense; tall but not very wide. Among dozens of shelves, a second floor, with a few tables and chairs, overlooked the books below. With his eyes gleaming, the young man devoured the book solely because of its cool cover art. The title was "Hundred Years War" from Preston Aligarti. Harry preferred fictions, but made an exception for this one.

"Kid?" A hoarse voice came from below, the distance between it and the boy only enhancing the echo that traveled between the shelves, "Did you find it? We have customers downstairs, if you don't mind..."

Giving a slight gasp, the young man marked the place where he found Aligarti's book and grabbed one next to it.

"Um... yes!" He shouted, scratching the back of his head. "I'm coming."

He braced himself for the scolding as he descended the stairs with a nonchalant smile. In his arms was a random, thick book whose title the young man hadn't even bothered to read.

On the wooden stairs, he worried about bumping into the prestigious items surrounding him, the narrow corridors filled with armchairs and tables on the sides, taking up space. Someday I'll burn this crap and say it was stolen... he muttered, grumbling as he bumped his knee against a piece of furniture.

"There you go!" An older, bald man declared, sitting at a counter, "What took you so long..."

"I, um, got lost..." With an embarrassed expression, and with difficulty, he placed the heavy book on the counter.

"It's alright." The gentleman's voice soothed the young man's anxieties, who had expected to be scolded for his delay, "So... that will be 320 Chimes, sir."

"Perfect, I..." The customer, the son of a nobleman, frowned as he picked up the book and flipped through the pages, "Wait, this isn't the book I asked for..."

"How can it not be?" the man asked as the young man shuddered, "You're not mistaken, sir?"

He then took the book from the nobleman's hand and opened the first page; "832 Ways to Satisfy a Woman" was the title. He closed his eyes and put his hand to his forehead. When he opened them again and looked at the young man, his embarrassed smile was still there. He shook his head in disapproval and without delay turned to the customer.

"I-I'm so sorry, my lord." Despite the humiliation, he carried the book effortlessly, "I'll bring you the book you asked for right away. This was a mistake."

The last phrase was said while he pointed to the book, but his eyes were fixed on the boy.

"A-actually," the nobleman scratched his head, "I'd like to rent this one too, if it's alright..."

"Well, in that case..." His salesman years proving their worth, the librarian quickly came to a conclusion, "For your trouble, I can give you a discount of 2 books for 500 Chimes, what do you think?"

"Of course..." Still embarrassed, he replied, looking down.

The old man then looked at the lad while muttering, "Never leave to others what you can do yourself..."

What? It's my fault now that a pornographic book was literally next to a history one? But he kept quiet.

After a few agonizing minutes of waiting for him to return, Harry, who was already taller than the librarian, leaned on the counter.

"Are you going to introduce a girlfriend to your family?" He asked as the nobleman blushed. "Tomorrow is the festival, isn't it?"

"Ah, yes," he replied, fidgeting with his blond hair. "My cousin is really looking forward to tomorrow. Although she already knows most of them, haha..."

Slowly, the young man's smile turned into a worried expression. Frowning, he said, "...Wait a minute, your cousin?—"

"Here it is, sir!" The librarian descended the stairs. "That will be 500 chimes."

Almost pushing the young man aside, he took control of the counter while the young man was still dumbfounded by what he had just heard. Meanwhile, the payment was made and the coins were placed in a box under the counter. As soon as the nobleman left, the owner changed his demeanor.

"Damn it, Harry!" His furious gaze lingered. "You know these days are important, with the festival tomorrow and the fact that he was a nobleman! You had a job, lad; to fetch a mediocre book for a mediocre intellectual..."

"He..." His gaze blank, Harry didn't even pay attention to the man's words, "Love is very strange, isn't it, Mr. Devon? Even with one's own cousin..."

"What are you saying—" He raised his eyebrows as soon as it dawned on him, "Ah, well. Don't trust noblemen, lad. They're false moralists and true barbarians dressed in white. Even their hair is fake. Well, only an imbecile would read Meronos, let alone his early works..."

"Forgive me," he said, defeated, "I got carried away with the books upstairs and lost track of time."

"...No problem, boy." In a friendlier tone, he was happy to see Harry taking the blame, "Just let me know when you want to know something beforehand, so I can separate whatever I need. I promise I'll find it."

"I want to know about Emblems!" He replied succinctly, much to the old man's displeasure, "Apparently, they're important for history, and maybe it's time for me to learn about them, right? I heard about these Emblems and how they give people special powers."

I'll never promise anything again in this life. The old man thought to himself as he remained silent for a few moments. A winding path, the so-called Emblems dominated the government with their promises of control. Harry, however, was curious. Anxious, perhaps even insecure in front of his clients, but curious. What he didn't find today he would look for tomorrow, so it was better to learn from Devon than from a grimoire of false promises.

"Are you sure?" he asked, his stern voice lending even more weight to his words. "In my view, they are replicas of the devil."

"Devils don't exist," the boy retorted, "You said so yourself."

"That's true. But that doesn't stop them from trying to find him. Breaking the laws of man, that damned Astronomer unleashed upon the world a unified idea that one can control even the most specific objects and concepts of this world. To tamper with nature in such a way is utterly evil. This, in turn, gives man a justification for using his sin as a weapon. And you know the worst part? We are the ones who have to pay."

"I..." Guilty, Harry sighed deeply and said, "I didn't know that—"

"No, no need." The man turned to him with an apologetic smile, "I got carried away. I threw pieces of myself at you, just like my father did to me. These are all just opinions, Harry. I'm sure you'll have yours, but something I said remains the same; the path is winding. Are you sure?"

After a few thoughtful seconds, Harry replied with a light laugh, "Of course, old man! I'm not as weak as you..."

"Alright, I see. So now I'm weak, but yesterday when you almost broke your arm over a simple—"

"Okay, okay..." He rolled his eyes as he pushed him towards the stairs, "Just show me what I asked you to."

“You’re growing up to become a real brat, huh.” Devon let out a genuine laugh as they climbed the stairs.

Reaching one of the shelves in the back, the man climbed one of the library ladders and took a red leather book. Adorned as if it belonged to royalty, its presence was almost decorative. He blew on the hardcover and reluctantly handed it to the boy.

“Don’t open it yet!” He warned him, the boy jumping at his shout, “I’m giving it to you because you asked, but that doesn’t mean you can read it now. We’re in business hours, young man.”

“The shop stays open until dawn!” Harry protested while holding the book, “A-and tomorrow we have the festival! You know very well how crowded this place gets with nobles giving gifts to their loved ones and families!”

“Wow, that’s right… I forgot about that.” He looked around casually, “I guess you’ll have to wait until all that is over then…”

“Even when you lose, somehow you still win…” Visibly irritated, he held himself back and made a proposal, “What if I say I can cover for you at night if you let me read now?”

“I would certainly ignore your proposal but would respect your courage for saying something so untrue.”

“You really think—”

“How about this,” the librarian cut him off, “If, and I say if, you want, I’ll tell you a bit of the fundamentals now, and then we go back to—”

“Yes, please yes!” Harry said, almost dropping the book with the movement of his arms, “Should I get something to write with?”

“No need; I’ll be brief. Besides, you’ll see all this in the first chapters.”

“Yes, sir.” He replied obediently while hiding a smile.

Before starting, Devon picked up 3 objects of different shapes; a circle, a square, and a triangle. He placed them on one of the shelves and then turned to Harry.

"The development of the Emblem Technology is still highly debated between scholars, but in one thing they can agree: the Astronomer was the first to find it. Before he disappeared, obviously..."

What's so obvious about it? Harry stared, confused, but didn't interrupt Devon.

"...An Emblem can be created by using a material that can hold its power, at least for a few minutes. Today, we use obsidian. Then, you draw your Symbol on the Emblem using powdered Moon Rock. Or for that matter, any composition containing Silicon, Aluminum, Calcium, up to Magnesium. Although it works better with actual Moon Rocks—"

“Spill it already!”

"Alright, I got carried away... Once you have the blueprint for your Emblem, all you need to do is crash the object strong enough that it will shatter. Don't worry, the Moon Rock will weaken it. So even your noodle arms could do the trick."

Harry simply rolled his eyes as the old man continued.

“Emblems, by the way, are divided into three shapes: Circular, Quadrangular, and Triangular." Devon held the three tiny objects in the palm of his hand. "The Circular gives you control over space based on the defined symbol. The Triangular gives you power over the symbol, and the Quadrangular gives you the ability to change your biology, your body, to fit the symbol. These are the three fundamental forms that give structure to your abilities. Now, for the—”

Interrupting him, the sound of a door opening was heard, followed by a doorbell chime. Devon promptly put the objects away and patted the boy on the back as he went down.

“We’ll see this another day, Harry.” He said with a commercial smile on his face, “Now let’s get back to work.”

"...Why are you doing this?" The young man asked, not yet used to the selflessness of Devon. "I mean, it seems a big deal for you, with the devil's talk and all... So why are you willing to teach it to me?"

Devon went silent for a few seconds, carefully choosing his wording. "I got introduced to the Emblems around your age, and it wasn't a pleasant experience. Let's just say that I saw a lot of harm before any good came into my sight. They were all because of Emblems. As I've said, they make you feel important not just to people around you, but to the universe. Emblems make you feel seen. I just don't want you to forget how awesome it is to feel human. Took me a long time to figure that one out. But don't fret over it; Moon Rocks are hard to come by, and with the government hiding anything to do with Emblems... Thankfully, the materials are used only as collars and decorations."

Not sure what to respond, Harry opened his mouth but quickly closed it, afraid of saying the wrong thing. What Harry didn't know, however, was that Devon didn't expect a wrong or correct answer, but a sincere one. As a reply, the young man gave him an apologetic smile as Devon nodded and went down the stairs to greet the customer.

Looking around, before going down to assist the owner, Harry spotted a tiny, almost imperceptible book near the one about Emblems. As he read the title, Harry almost forgot to go down.

It read "The Tale of the Logos Tower".
