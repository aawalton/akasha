Out of nowhere from the darkness, the elf came into view. Her movements were fast—there was nothing, and then she was there, standing beside the fountain.

Her dark eyes scanned the devastated surroundings as her long braided black hair swirled sideways.

The lady of my dreams was an elf, a strong one at that… she could even cast a massive Inferno fused with dark magic! It doesn’t exist, though… a figment of my dream, perhaps.

As I walked closer to the fountain, the elf’s long ear twitched. Maybe the sound of my footsteps reached her ears. And I was right—her eyes turned to me. She had red eyes.

Her red lips parted, and the expression of something between shock and relief plastered on her face.

I stopped walking toward her. I felt tired. Can one even feel tired in a waking (lucid) dream? I wondered.

"Hey…" I said, my voice low even though I intended to shout it.

Red eyes, red eyes, red eyes… I repeated in my mind so I wouldn’t forget the details when I woke up.

The elf’s right hand rose slightly as she took a step, reaching for me.

I got a better look at her eyes; it was strange. Her pupils were red, a color that, while rare, could manifest in some. Though what was stranger was the design of her pupils. There was a black circular spiral coil of sorts in both her eyes.

That wasn’t normal—well, I mean, dreams aren’t meant to be normal. What’s wrong with her eyes?

"Who—" I tried to speak but couldn’t. My throat felt heavy, likely because of the dust and smoke, and I choked. My vision blurred. I couldn’t look away from the elf.

I noticed her sharp red eyes, long ears, emerald earring. I blinked. My vision grew blurrier. My feet buckled, and I crouched, still able to see her face. Her lips were red, her skin pale… even for an elf. Black hair. And then everything around me turned black.

I gasped hard. Air rushed into my lungs as I jolted upright. Both of my hands clutched the blanket as if letting go would pull me back into the darkness… it wouldn’t though, I wasn‘t dreaming anymore.

Letting out a long exhale, I calmed myself. It was morning already—a trickle of sunlight seeped through the curtains. My eyes turned to the clock, 6:45. I still had a lot of time before school.

"Huh… man, the dream was tiring." I yawned, then kicked the blanket aside and jumped onto the floor in an instant as I realized I had just dreamed.

A shiver ran down my spine—the floor was cold. But I had no time to waste, so I rushed to the study table and began searching for my observation register, which I found in no time. Since I’d previously left a pen in the register, I opened a fresh page easily.

"Red eyes, spiral design, expression… ahhh," I groaned, rubbing my hair in frustration, trying to recall the elf’s expression, though nothing rang a bell.

Not wanting to forget the other information about her as well, I leaned forward on the table and noted down the details of my dream in a short cryptic message, just like all the others. With that done, I capped the fountain pen and placed it between the two pages, using it as my bookmark yet again. I closed the register and walked to my bed.

A long yawn escaped my mouth. I still felt sleepy. The time was barely 6:50—I still had at least an hour to sleep, and that’s exactly what I did. I mean, recovery is a part of training, right?

The next time I woke up, an hour had already passed, but I still had sufficient time. I didn’t have to rush all the tasks as the day before yesterday and could take a long, pleasant, hot bath, after which I got dressed for school and left the dormitory.

I left for class half an hour early, no specific reasons, I just had nothing to do. Training could’ve been something I could’ve used my time for, though my mana was still not completely recovered, and depleting it before training with Instructor Mirayel wasn’t ideal.

I walked out through the main gate this time, counting my steps as I walked out of sheer curiosity. "1, 2, 5, 156, 300…" I kept counting in my mind as I moved to the stone pathway between Cellera and Cryz dorms’ main gate.

The weather today was surprisingly not sunny anymore; the sunlight that trickled through my curtains might’ve been the last of its kind before black clouds covered the sky. Maybe it would rain today?

I certainly hoped it does.

"Darn it!" I clicked my tongue. Getting distracted by the clouds was pathetic. Just how bad is my concentration!? I grumbled to myself.

What was the number again… "767… ahh, let’s take 750 instead," I murmured to myself when a voice interrupted my walk.

"You seem to be doing fine," a girl murmured.

Is it a ghost? I wondered. Why would anyone talk to me like they knew me—unless it was Mabel or Noelle? "Haah." I let out a soft chuckle and kept walking.

The voice didn’t belong to Mabel, and it couldn’t be Noelle. There’s no way that girl would ever come to class this early.

"Oi, Arnos!"

The ghost knows my name! I mentally exclaimed, turning around. In front of me stood Noelle, with her black eyes squinted and both of her arms on her waist, as if I had frustrated her. I totally didn’t.

"Oh, hey Noell—" I paused abruptly. Wait! Am I late? I looked back. The gate was certainly open, but then again, it was open that day too before closing suddenly.

"We gotta hurry, Noelle!" I exclaimed.

"For what?"

"Classes wouldn’t want to be even later, right!?" I pointed at the main gate as I quoted her words from the previous day.

Noelle didn’t even faze, as expected from an experienced latecomer.

"We aren’t late; there’s still thirty— no, twenty-five minutes before class starts."

"Wait, we aren’t? So my clock hasn’t gone bad, huh?" I sighed with relief.

"Clock? Bad?" Noelle raised a brow. "Don’t tell me you thought you were late cause you spotted me!"

"Wha? I could never presume such a thing." I shook my head, clearly lying.

Noelle blinked; her expression softened a bit. "Liar," she murmured, walking past me.

I followed her, walking a step behind her at a constant speed. With the moment of silence, my attention shifted to the sky. I looked toward the school building; the whole thing was painted black. There was no way it wouldn’t rain today with the way the sky is.

Why hasn’t it started raining yet!? I grumbled internally, my hand tightening.

"Did you rest properly yesterday?" Noelle asked, breaking the moment of silence.

I increased my pace, covering the distance between us as I started walking beside her. Hmm, I nodded. I think I slept pretty well yesterday; I murmured. Raising my left arm and bringing it in front of my face, I bowed a bit, "Sorry for causing the inconvenience." I said with a mock-praying gesture.

"It’s fine. You certainly said a lot of rude stuff to me yesterday."

"Wait, I did!?"

"Yeah, you did." She nodded, not missing a beat.

"Like what?" I asked.

Noelle looked at me, her lips parted as she began, "You… ahhh… I forgot."

"It couldn’t have been that hurtful if you forgot it so fast!" I retorted. Perhaps she didn’t want to repeat what I said because it was too hurtful, I thought, biting my lip.

"Sorry, though."
