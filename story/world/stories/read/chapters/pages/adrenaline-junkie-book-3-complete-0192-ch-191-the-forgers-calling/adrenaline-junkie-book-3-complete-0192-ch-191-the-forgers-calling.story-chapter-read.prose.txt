Archie’s eyes snapped open once again as he found himself in the vast, empty white area. There were no walls, nor tables in sight, just white extending on forever in all directions. It looked to be the same place where he’d waited for Trial One to begin for his Class.

And just as the recognition settled within him, the white space around him began to shift, turning bright blue before reverting back into white for a brief moment.

[TRIAL 1: BONDING RITE]To begin your path as a Spiritforging Engineer, you must form a temporary contract with a spirit of your choosing. Use the knowledge temporarily granted to initiate the Rite of Communion. The spirit must accept you of its own will.Time Limit: 16:00:00.Location Restrictions: None.

[Spirit Contract Prior to Trial Located]

[TRIAL 1: BONDING RITE]To begin your path as a Spiritforging Engineer, you must form a temporary contract with a spirit of your choosing. Use the knowledge temporarily granted to initiate the Rite of Communion. The spirit must accept you of its own will.Status: Complete

Oh, that was fast, Archie chuckled to himself as he saw another System Notification pop up before disappearing.

A ripple passed beneath his feet, this time not feeling soft like grass, but more like wood. The brilliant white light dimmed and took on the color of wood as warm brown seeped into the floor, before roughening slightly into aged timber planks, which gave off the scent of burnt pine smoke, that for some reason eased the tension that managed to creep its way into him.

Not long after the creation of the floor, large cobblestone beams and wooden walls grew from the edges of the floorboards around him. Once the walls and roof fully formed around him, windows then began to cut themselves into the walls by the door at the far end of the room, letting him see that he was currently atop a mountain, surrounded by other even taller mountains.

Hearing a low crackle from his right, he turned and saw a low-burning hearth crackling in the corner, framed by a stone mantle. Tools hung neatly along the wall left of it, holding up tools such as tongs, pliers, hammers, and files, all hanging up just beneath a wide wooden workbench.

“What the hell is this?” Archie muttered to himself as he saw the hearth and no forge nor anvil in sight. “What, am I supposed to use my legs as the anvil?”

A second later, after he’d voiced his complaints, in place of the low-burning heart was a forge, and not just any forge, it looked exactly like his Inchoate Titansteel Forge, and the anvil, just a bit off to the side of it, looked exactly like his Obsidiansteel Anvil.

Damn, if I couldn’t still feel the soulbond connection with my own forge and anvil, I would have sworn that these two were mine, Archie mused to himself.

As Archie finished his thought, another System Notification popped up in front of him.

[TRIAL 2: FLAMECRAFT - THE FORGER'S CALLING]To further your path as a Spirit Spiritforging Engineer, you must forge an item that contains the essence of your bonded spirit.Constraints:• Spirit Fusion must be active during forging.• No Cores are to be used within the forging of the item.• No tools or materials are to be used other than those physically present within the room you are currently standing within.Completion Requirement:Item must be Uncommon quality or higher.Time Limit: 47:59:53

His boots tapped against the wooden floor with each step as he made his way toward the workbench. Upon the appearance of the System Notification, the top of it began to shimmer for a moment before the materials that were being provided to him were neatly laid out.

The materials provided to him were: twelve Steel ingots, twelve strips of leather rolled and bound: one wide, thick, and dark as pitch, another narrow and tan, and the third soft and flexible. And to the right of the workbench sat several blocks of wood, each a different color and grain.

“Steel, leather, wood,” Archie muttered, glancing over the array of materials provided to him. “Oh, and a runic scriber, nice,” Archie said as he pulled open a drawer beneath the workbench and saw yet another copy of a tool he regularly used, a runic scriber, looking almost like a one-to-one copy.

He rubbed his chin for a second, then glanced at the cold forge for a few seconds before activating Forgesmith’s Flame inside it.

“You got any ideas on what you want us to forge?” he asked the only other living being around him.

The air around him shimmered faintly just before Vul appeared, hovering beside him.

“I’m thinking,” Vul responded, the magma spirit’s voice low. “I’ve never had a contractor who was a smith; I’ve only ever had contractors who’ve used my Magma Affinity for combat. So, I’m not really sure what my Magma Affinity would be good for in regards to forging.”

Archie crossed his arms. “Well, I mean, the first item we make isn’t going to be the one I plan on turning in and etching runes onto. I want to put into practice what I’ve learned from Bralmir, so I want to make use of the 48 hours we were given to its fullest. Besides, who knows how long it’ll take for me to figure out how to use Spirit Fusion to its fullest as I forge, right?”

“Yes,” Vul replied, before floating toward the forge and staring into Archie’s Forgesmith’s Flame. “Your meeting with the Forgefather… I daren’t appear in front of him when I had found myself with your soulspace, but I heard the lessons he’d imparted for you, and putting them into practice would be wise.”

“Okay…” Archie nodded slowly at Vul’s choice of words, but shrugged it off, thinking that it was just like that one time when Syllia felt Bralmir’s presence and became all weird.

Reaching out and picking up a steel ingot, he tested its weight as he rolled it in his hands. “Alright, then. We start small by forging up something simple.”

“A dagger?” He suggested to Vul, who nodded in acceptance, just as another steel ingot appeared in the same place where he had picked up the one currently in his hand. Huh, I guess I have infinite materials, that’s good, he thought to himself as he moved aside the twelve steel ingots, there were now twenty-five of them in total.

“Right,” Archie muttered, more to himself now as the plan started to form in his head. “Dagger it is.”

“Alright,” he muttered. “Let’s do this.”

He reached through the bond connected to both his soul and Vul’s, Archie activated Spirit Bond.

His body tensed for a couple of seconds as he attempted to access the bond, but after firmly grabbing onto it, the shift was almost immediate.

Heat rushed out from the bond around his soul, starting around the center of his chest, before spreading through his arms and legs like liquid under his skin.

His skin flushed darker, glowing at the seams beneath the sleeves of his sweatshirt and pants as the fusion took hold. Smoke rolled off his shoulders, and for a second, he assumed that his clothes were on fire, but they were unharmed, it was just his body producing the smoke normally.

The moment the bond took hold, Archie felt his Forgesmith’s Flame within the forge gain a redder tinge that contrasted quite well with the green.

Archie let out a deep exhale, calming himself down just before he went to the tool rack and grabbed a pair of tongs. Grabbing the wolf jaw tongs, he placed the steel ingot in between its jaws and set it into the Forgesmith’s Flame within the forge.

His sense of temperature had grown sharper, that was one of the first things he’d noticed upon standing beside the forge, and he could feel when the bar was reaching optimal forging heat. And while he could do this just fine by eyeing the metal, he was able to instinctually feel that it was ready seconds before his eyes did, how? Most definitely an ability of Spirit Fusion.

He pulled the bar free once it hit bright orange and moved to the anvil.

The steel hissed as it met the face of the anvil, though the hiss came more from the air reacting to the heat than anything else. Archie wasted no time, grabbing a hammer from the tool rack and bringing it down with a sharp bang that rang out through the forge.

He started tapering one end of the bar, gradually drawing it out with even, measured strikes. His strokes were consistent and imbued with both his Unattuned Mana as well as Vul’s magma mana.

As he worked, he felt Vul shift slightly within his soulspace. The spirit wasn’t saying anything, instead, he was watching how he was forging with incredible fascination. It honestly reminded him of a movie he’d watched where a kid was so interested in the animals in the zoo and how they acted the way they did that he constantly had his face pressed against the glass between them.

Vul currently gave him the same exact vibe as that kid.

“You interested in forging?” Archie said without looking up.

Vul didn’t answer right away.

“All my old contractors were either elementalists who wanted to utilize my Magma Affinity for its destruction,” he eventually said, voice like cracking coals. “Or magical researchers wanting to learn how and why magma mana acted the way that it did.”

“Uh, that–” Archie said before he was interrupted by Vul.

“I didn’t mind it,” Vul said plainly. “It was a simple matter of equivalent and exchange in agreement with the spirit contract I signed. I was used by them, and them by me. And just as you are using me for your forging and fighting, I am using you for advancing my Records, leveling, mana, and entertainment.”

Another set of steady blows, then he adjusted his grip and started forming the tang.

“I am just interested in the concept of forging as this is the first time I’ve ever experienced it in a … ‘hands-on’ fashion.”

After a few more reheatings and hammering out the finer details of the metal, a dull one-sided dagger blade, cross guard, and hilt was made. He quenched it in the oil basin by the forge that appeared after thinking about it for a moment.

He turned toward the workbench and eyed the woods again. Nah, wood on blades sounds stupid.

Archie reached for the leather straps, the ones no thicker than his pinky finger and began to wrap it around the metal grip of the dagger and made sure that it fit his hand’s grip before he activated Forgesmith’s Flame atop the dagger’s grip and kept clenching it to make it fit his grip while fusing the leather onto the metal.

Once that was done, Archie grabbed the whetstone from off the tool rack and sharpened the blade’s one-sided edge until it was able to easily tear into the cloth of his sweatshirt sleeves. From there, he lathered it in oil once more as a finish before he tossed it onto the workbench and grabbed a couple more steel ingots and placed them beside the forge while taking one of them into the wolf jaws of the tongs and holding it above the flames within the forge.

“Why did you toss that dagger away?” Vul asked. “It looked just fine to me.”

“That’s precisely the issue,” Archie muttered as he activated Gaze of the Forgefather. “It was a Common rarity dagger, just like how I wanted it to be.”

“What do you mean?” Vul asked. “You know how to write runes, hence why you have that Runic Scriber on the workbench, wouldn’t you be able to bring it to Uncommon rarity if you etch runes onto it?”

“I would, and it would be an Uncommon rarity. Easily,” Archie muttered as he rotated the steel ingot in the forge. “I just didn’t want it to be one.”

“I want to use all the System can give me to hone my forging skills while in this trial. I want to not leave until, with just my forging alone, I can bring the metal to Uncommon rarity, and to do that, I need to fix as many of my shortcomings as possible within these 48 hours I’ve been given.”
