Alex, above the forest and grasslands…

The hunger crept up on me slowly, as I taught myself how to fly across the skies. By the time my stomach grumbled, creatures had arrived back at the burned grass lands. The black soot gathered near the rocks from the wind blowing in one direction. Massive areas were filled with the dark stuff.

The sky darkened faster than it had lightened. It took the sun several days to move overhead, because of the mountains, but now the mountains worked against us, as the valley was moving into shadow. The sun sank beneath the far mountains quicker, since they were apparently taller.

Herds of animals started crossing the dusty areas before dipping into the river. I studied one as it rolled in the soot after getting wet, before heading into the forest. It ripped out several ferns, eating them as it traveled through the spires.

More and more followed suit.

I snagged out some meat from my inventory to settle my stomach, as I started lowering myself unknowingly toward the beasts. Then I hovered in the air, just watching. The scene repeated, again and again. Get wet, then coat yourself in the soot before eating some plants.

I flew closer to the massive tree in the distance, to get a better look at what it actually was.

All the leaves were gone, and a cocoon like substance covered it.

[Cocooned Tree, Level 280, Unknown, Unknown.]

I backed away slowly, not wanting to get too close.

With nothing else to do, I flew back to the waterfall. My shoulders ached, and while eating the meat had reduced my hunger, it hadn’t satisfied me. My energy levels had fallen enough that what I’d eaten hadn’t been nearly enough.

My feet touched near the lake, and water sprayed everywhere as something lunged at me.

I spun, my spear appearing in my hand as the wings vanished. The tip of my spear glowed a deep black several inches beyond the actual crystal tip. It slammed into the side of the massive croc, halting the leap.

It wasn’t enough to take it down, unfortunately, as a tail snapped out at me.

I rolled away, and it barely missed my head.

[Deep Purusaurus, Level 215, Predator, Cold Spice.]

My mouth watered thinking about eating it.

The Purusaurus growled at me, spittle flying everywhere, but I didn’t care as I lunged in its direction. It retreated deeper into the water, almost mocking me.

I focused on the little energy I did have, and creating small platforms under my feet. Just enough to give me something to stay on top of.

Pitch black dots formed under my feet, giving me a surface to stand on as I continued after the creature. Its eyes widened, then it tried to lunge again with its mouth open.

Smiling, I held for a moment, letting it get airborne before lunging even farther forward, into the croc’s mouth. My spear punched into the back of the roof of the wide-open jaw, but didn’t pierce through the other side.

It strained to close its mouth, but my spear shaft held.

Then, I tore into the back of its throat with my claws. Instead of golden lines appearing a few feet out, pitch black lines sliced through the air with each of my swipes. Those black swipes tore through the creature, sending blood everywhere.

Suddenly, the Purusaurus’ mouth lifted, then slammed down.

I braced myself against the roof of its mouth, and my spear held again. Seconds later, I sent another three glorious swipes into the back of the beast’s mouth. Then it twisted. I hung onto my spear shaft as water flew and the croc rolled.

Spinning, trying not to lose my grip, I triggered my shield all around me.

The top of the giant croc’s mouth slammed into my shield as my spear finally came loose.

I snagged the butt of the spear and shrunk it into a smaller form, ready to brace it again. Yet, water rushed in all around me as the creature dove beneath the surface. The lake turned a bloody color from the wounds in the back of the croc’s mouth.

The pressure on my shield increased, and I stabbed up yet again, aiming for the area where I’d injured it. Right before the tip hit, the massive black triangle of hardened mana formed, and punched up.

The croc shuddered, then it turned 90 degrees, leaving nothing under my feet.

Gravity yanked me down, and I scrambled to find purchase for a moment, but then intentionally fell as my shield trembled again.

I escaped the croc’s mouth and found myself closer to shore than I’d expected. The croc rushed off, but circled back in my direction. I backed up slowly to the shallower water.

Somehow, I didn’t need to breathe yet, but I didn’t let that freak me out. Instead, I tracked the creature as it swam back in my direction.

My spear lengthed, and I smiled, waiting.

The massive jaw opened, and again I lunged for the back. This time, I triggered Sovereign's Call just as my spear sank into its throat.

The Purusaurus’ mouth remained open as it suddenly stopped moving. Momentum carried it forward onto my spear tip.

The notification made me smile as I yanked my spear back.

Giddiness filled me at this take down. Though these croc creatures really needed a different attack style than just trying to chomp on something and drown it, the predictability had helped me.

[You have survived combat against a level 215 Deep Purussaurus and gained bonus experience.]

Immediately, I started cutting through the back of its throat where I thought the heart should be, based on prior experience. Each chunk of meat I cut away, I added to my inventory, making a large stack.

Red dots surrounded the carcass, so I dug deeper as fast as I could, rather than dealing with the competition.

Eventually, I found the round hard ball and tossed it into my mouth. It took a few bites to crack it, but then energy flowed into me like a coffee on a chilly day.

First, cool power flooded my mouth, then it turned warm and spicy. Like someone had increased the heat level without me noticing. I’d be sweating if I wasn’t under water.

It kept building.

Then I flashed red hot all over.

[You have devoured a Deep Purussasurus and gained insight into Hardened Armor.]

I blinked a few times, then shook my head as I swam away from the dead croc’s throat and out its jaws. The mass of red dots worried me as I exited.

Several creatures swam away, while others didn’t seem to care as they tried to tear through the dead beast’s armor.

I went on my way, heading for shore. The water cleared of the red tinge of blood by the time I made it to the shore. Surprisingly, when my head broke the surface, I didn’t need a gasping breath. I still took one, but it wasn’t needed.

Did I need to breathe anymore?

Water dripped off me as I stepped onto the rocky shore.

Calls from various creatures filled the air. Most were coated in the black soot, though a few who were already coated were dipping in the water again. This time, the black coat stayed, and they rolled around again in the soot to thicken it.

Cooler breezy wind sprayed over the area, making me shiver unnecessarily. I wasn’t cold, not really, but my body had reacted before I’d thought about it. I shook a good amount of water off me, but my clothes remained wet.

Not my boots. Somehow, they dried much faster. That, to me, was a miracle.

I needed a water-repealing rune to add to my clothes. Dirt and mud fell off nicely, but not water. The soot didn’t stick to my boots, either, which was nice.

The heat in my stomach lingered nicely, and the boost in energy made me pick up my pace. I hiked toward the trail leading behind the waterfall. None of the creatures on my radar paid me any attention, though the water rippled with a ton of movement under its surface.

The climb took no time at all before the water hid me from sight, and I ducked into the tunnel.

Shy rested in the middle of the cavern floor near where I remembered the dungeon entrance had been.

[Shy Spinosaurous, Cold Claws, Level 74, Tasty Orange.]

Her level had increased a ton.

“You must have had a good wave in the dungeon,” I said, with a smile.

She shivered and jerked a little, but didn’t move from her spot. “It went well, though that was the only try I had left. Now I wait for Grizzle.”

“I checked out the area above the cliff side. We shouldn’t have a problem getting to the portal.” That sounded better than what had actually happened. Though I hadn’t given up or left Strange behind, he’d headed out without me. “Though, if I tell you guys to go ahead, just do it.”

“I will listen,” she said, nodding her head. “Anything to leave this place. Grizzle mentioned that he can teach me how to shift to the other form.”

“It can be useful if you need to go inside any of our structures, though we have a massive cavern that fits bigger beings.” I sat down near her, thinking of Lakeside Landing. Yet, images from my rank up trial flashed through my mind. All those people rushing to the cavern, while only a few had tried to hold the attackers off.

It didn’t sit right.

Even though I’d argued for humanity to have the freedom of choice, to decide how they would grow, and progress, so many choosing not to protect felt wrong. Fighting. crafting, exploration, and settlements were all necessary, but still. I clearly leaned toward one extreme.

People like Benny who grew food needed to be able to progress, too. Though, knowing him, he also was a decent shot.

“Everyone helps everyone?” asked Shy, in a tiny voice.

“People do their best to help one another, from growing food to hiding together when the bugs show up. The strong protect the weak.” That kind of happened in the grasslands, but not nearly as much from what I could see.

“The strong here protect us until we start to get too strong, then…” She let out a sigh. “Sometimes they vanish, or aren’t helped when others attack.”

“If you want to become stronger, we have allies in other places with higher level dungeons. We also have access to a school that helps those who want to learn about classes and professions.” I nodded in her direction. “You’ll fit right in.”

She didn’t respond, which was fine by me.

I leaned back on my hands and took a few deep breaths, concentrating on the bond between Strange and I.

As far as I could tell, he still was as happy as could be. Satisfied even.

I didn’t understand, and I wanted to. Either way, the next stop was Lakeside Landing, then I’d need to track down Strange. Maybe I could cut across the Sisters to get to the Megacity. That’s what Dengu had done.

Noseen shouldn’t have a problem with that.

Energy gathered in the room, and I leaped to my feet, triggering my second sight. Mana gathered all along one area, right where I knew the door to the dungeon needed to be.

Grizzle stumbled out, all in one piece. He almost tripped over Shy, pivoting out of the way at the last minute.

[Grizzle, Invisibility, Spinosaurus, Level 115, Prey.]

That run had netted him more levels than I thought he would get. Yet, a wild look hung around his eyes. He shook his head frantically, then paused, taking a deep breath.

“You okay, my friend?” I asked, quietly.

He froze, nostrils flaring. It took a few seconds for him to respond.

“Alpha?” he asked.

“Yeah, it's me.” I didn’t move from my spot.

“I don’t sense you at all,” he said, still searching the room. He turned looking in a few different directions.

That’s right. This cavern was dark. “Sorry about that, not sure how to turn it off yet.”

He shook his head. “It’s okay, it will be helpful in fights.”

“Are you ready to head to the portal?” I asked, ready to get moving.

“We should hurry,” said Shy, scrambling closer to Grizzle. “The cold will be here soon.”

“Cold…” I said, thinking of how Grizzle could barely move in the pre-dawn hours.

“Yeah, the ice from the sky hurts,” she added, still quietly.

What the heck was that? Freezing rain?

A flash of the giant ice hunks littering the ground when we first arrived on the planet came to mind. I still had a few in my inventory. If those fell from the sky, then we needed to freaking hurry.
