I killed the monster two more times as I made my way systematically through the cavern, searching for anything that might be animating the creature. Each death was identical to the first: brutal, efficient, temporary. The construct would fall apart into its component bones, lie still for precious seconds that I used to extend my search, then begin reassembling itself with the same mechanical precision that had created it originally.

The pattern was becoming familiar, almost routine. Strike at the joints where energy flowed strongest, sever whatever passed for vital connections, watch the massive frame collapse into scattered fragments. Then the desperate hunt through the shadows for whatever force kept breathing purpose into dead bone, knowing that my time was measured in heartbeats before the next assault began.

I couldn't find anything that looked like a controlling mechanism. No glowing crystals hidden in alcoves, no ritual circles carved into the stone, no obvious weak points that would explain how thousands of bone fragments maintained perfect coordination. The cavern walls were smooth dark stone, unmarked by symbols or technological devices. The floor was nothing but layers of accumulated remains, generations of failed challengers ground down to their essential calcium.

I did not allow frustration to mount, recognizing it as a useless emotion that would only cloud my judgment. Instead, I began to systematically deny the creature its resources. If I couldn't find the source of its animation, perhaps I could starve it of the materials it needed to maintain physical form. Simple logic, the kind of practical problem-solving that had served me well in construction work back when such things mattered.

I dodged and flowed around the monster's increasingly aggressive attacks as I methodically crushed the bone piles scattered throughout the cavern. Each pile required sustained attention, my Flux Crush ability working overtime to reduce femurs and skulls and ribcages to powder that couldn't be repurposed into weapons. It was slow, methodical work that demanded patience and precision.

But then again, so had been building houses. Laying foundation, framing walls, installing electrical systems, none of it glamorous or exciting, but all of it necessary for the final product. I was no stranger to that type of labor, to the steady application of effort over time until complex problems yielded to simple persistence. The work suited me just fine, even under these circumstances.

The cavern became a veritable sea of crushed bone powder as I continued my systematic destruction while avoiding the creature's strikes. White dust hung in the air like snow, coating everything in a fine layer that made breathing difficult even through my respirator's filters. The monster adapted to my strategy, becoming more aggressive in its attempts to stop my demolition work.

It had started leaping at me when simple swipes failed to connect, massive claws extended like some prehistoric predator pouncing on prey. The first leap nearly succeeded in killing me because I hadn't been expecting such tactics from something that massive. The creature moved through the air with impossible grace, covering distances that should have been beyond its capabilities in a single bound.

Little lessons like that were ones I committed to memory immediately. In a place where mistakes meant death, you learned to catalog every surprise, every unexpected capability, every assumption that proved false. It was the small oversights that often led to your destruction, the tiny details that experienced fighters took for granted until they became fatal blind spots.

I would learn that truth on a deeper level in the future as well, but for now it was enough to remember that large didn't mean slow, and predictable could become deadly if you let yourself get comfortable with patterns.

The scent of death hung thick in the air, growing stronger as my demolition work progressed. Void and vital aspects continued their impossible waltz around me, death and life energy swirling together in ways that defied natural law. I could feel the tension building as I systematically denied the monster the resources it needed to reform properly, its available materials diminishing with each pile I reduced to powder.

I could not find what was animating the construct, but if there was nothing substantial left to animate, then surely that would accomplish the same goal. Starvation had always been an effective siege tactic, whether applied to cities or monsters or any other target that required resources to maintain itself. Cut off the supply lines and eventually even the strongest fortress would fall.

That was the only viable option left to me, since killing the monster repeatedly wasn't producing any permanent results. I would tire far faster than something that felt no pain and required no rest, wearing myself down through constant combat while it simply reassembled itself from an seemingly endless supply of components. Eventually my reflexes would slow, my attention would waver, and those massive claws would find their mark.

When I had finally crushed the last accessible pile of bones, reducing every fragment I could reach to fine powder that coated the cavern floor like macabre snow, I turned my attention back to the monster. It had been growing noticeably smaller during my demolition work, forced to cannibalize its own structure as the available raw materials dwindled. What had once been a towering horror was now merely human-sized, though no less dangerous for the reduction in scale.

The creature launched itself at me with desperate fury, mouth agape, rows of yellowed teeth gleaming in the white and crimson light of my resonance blade. Its movements carried a frantic quality now, the desperation of something that understood its resources were finite and diminishing. This might be its last chance to stop me before complete material exhaustion rendered it powerless.

I ran directly toward the leaping monster instead of dodging aside, timing my approach to slide baseball-style under its airborne form at the last possible moment. The maneuver was risky, requiring precise timing and leaving me vulnerable if I miscalculated, but it positioned me perfectly for a devastating counterattack.

My blade carved upward as I slid across the rough bone-powder carpet, white energy passing through the creature's torso like it was made of paper. The construct split cleanly in half, upper and lower sections separating with a sound like breaking pottery. I didn't slide far because of the uneven surface beneath me, bone fragments and stone irregularities bringing my momentum to a jarring halt, but the attack succeeded completely.

The monster crashed to the cavern floor in two distinct pieces, its animating energy disrupted once again. I rolled clear of the falling debris and came to my feet, breathing heavily through the respirator's filters, watching for the telltale signs of regeneration that had marked its previous defeats.

This should have been the final time. I had eliminated virtually all available materials, reduced the creature's resource base to nothing more than the powder coating the floor and the fragments of its own destroyed body. There simply wasn't enough substantial matter left to reconstruct the massive horror I had been fighting.

Instead, something new began to happen. The bone powder scattered across the cavern floor started to gather itself, drawn together by forces I could feel but not see. Rather than reforming into the familiar skeletal construct, the material began organizing itself into something else entirely. A new configuration, adapted to the changed circumstances.

I observed with growing unease as some kind of bone elemental took shape before me, a creature of living powder and suspended fragments that swirled through the air like a bleached tornado. It moved with fluid grace that bore no resemblance to the lumbering giant I had been fighting, its form shifting constantly as individual particles flowed through patterns that suggested intelligence without structure.

The thing was beautiful in its own horrible way, thousands of bone fragments suspended in perfect formation, creating shapes that dissolved and reformed with hypnotic rhythm. But beauty didn't make it less dangerous, and I could feel the same void and vital aspects flowing through its diffuse form, seeking new ways to bring death to the living.

I had no more piles of bone to crush, no way to further reduce its available materials. I could not find the source animating it, despite having searched every accessible inch of the cavern. My strategy of resource denial had forced it to adapt rather than surrender, creating a new form of threat that my previous tactics couldn't address.

I was effectively out of options and staring down death itself, now wearing a form that seemed immune to the crushing techniques that had served me so well. The bone elemental swirled closer, its movements creating sounds like wind through a graveyard, and I realized that this fight was about to enter an entirely new phase.

One way or another, it would be decided soon. Either I would find a way to permanently destroy this manifestation of accumulated death, or I would join the countless initiates whose remains had provided its raw materials. The creature seemed to sense my realization, its swirling accelerating as it prepared for what might be our final exchange.

Time to discover if the man who had once built houses for the living could find a way to tear down monuments to the dead.
