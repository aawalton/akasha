The next morning, as my squad and I left the mess hall to begin our training, a horn sounded and the castle gates opened. We moved off the side of the road, so we wouldn’t get trampled by whatever entourage was incoming.

“What else can happen now?” Anchor complained.

I figured Sage might have had a response for him, if she’d been here, but she already split off to continue her training with the druid groups.

So, I opened my mouth, about to make an equally exasperated comment—as if things hadn’t gotten bad enough—but before I could say anything, a group of nearly thirty riders trotted through the gate, wearing orange cloaks and shawls, and carrying Gate flags. All were Dupes, and all of them were heavily armed.

I let out a sigh of relief when I picked out Romance and Elf riding at the front of the column.

“Ah, it is your old battalion, brother,” Brawl said.

The riders that Lord Ceonwin had requested from the 294th were here. Only about thirty of them, but if worst came to worst, at least we had some backup.

I stepped up to the edge of the roadway, approaching Romance, and said, “You guys got more horses.”

“A donation from a local farmer,” Romance said. He leaned down. “It’s good to see you, Levi.”

“You as well,” I said. “I assume you’ve been training?”

“Every waking hour,” he said.

“Romance advanced to Steel along the way,” Elf provided. “Some of us are getting closer, too.”

“You should see how far Levi has come,” Anchor said.

“No need to brag,” I interjected.

“Yes, I’m sure they’ll all see soon enough,” Owl told us. “Now, you lot still have your daily conditioning. You can catch up with old friends later.”

“Sorry, lieutenant,” I said, then followed him off across the castle grounds.

~ ~ ~

Today was my first chance to test out my enhanced body. I needed to check my limits and figure out what I could do, not just rush blindly into combat and hope for the best. And I only had a few days to get used to fighting after almost doubling all my attributes.

I breezed through all the regular riftrock exercises, forcing Owl to double my riftrock load within a half-hour. It wasn’t a challenge, though, it was only a baseline, and I still needed to push my attributes a little more if I wanted to hit Silver.

So I had him add more stones until I was exhausted. My kit bag was as full of riftrocks as it could be.

How the hell did people reach Gold, then, if their physical attributes were so hard to improve? There had to be a plateau and a point where riftrocks lost their effectiveness, where you couldn’t just hold any more.

But that was a problem for future me.

At the end of our morning training session, I practiced flipping the riftrock disks around the outer green at the edge of the castle wall. Although all fifteen Dupes in our training group started at the same time, I pulled ahead, nearly doubling their pace. Yesman and Brawl kept up for a little bit, at the start, but the longer it went, the more they fell behind.

When Brawl reached the same stage as me, he’d certainly be stronger and more durable, but for the time being, I was stronger than either of them.

I felt their eyes boring into my back as I worked, but soon, I rounded the side of the wall, leaving them behind. Owl kept pace, following me and staying close.

“What did you do, Levi?” he asked.

“Master Thunric didn’t say?” I panted.

“No.”

“I…completed a regular body enhancement, like a normal Path-walker would.”

"How?" Owl asked.

I gave a quick explanation. There was no reason to hide it. Owl shook his head, then muttered, "You're advancing like the world is ending."

"And it isn't?"

"Well...I suppose you're not wrong.”

After that, I worked in silence. Lifting the stone wasn't as much of a strain as before. I didn't have much to compare it to, but even with the speed I was moving, my lungs didn't ache. My arms were probably going to feel it tomorrow, though.

Before I knew it, I'd circled halfway around the wall, then three quarters of the way.

"A few minutes left..." Owl warned. "Keep pushing. And pick up the pace if you want to finish a full lap."

I nodded. Though I was able to keep pushing, I was out of breath. Speaking was impossible.

With a shout, I gave the rock a few more flips. The starting line was right ahead, and this would be the first time I'd made a full lap.

Clenching my teeth, I forced myself to move faster and faster, abandoning technique and just forcing it. The edge of the disk wedged into the grass, but I kept pushing. Something twinged in my back, and a pain shot through my knee, but that's why we had Sage.

I gave the rock one last flip, then let it settle down---one full lap completed.

"Good," Owl said. "I think you've earned yourself some lunch."

~ ~ ~

Not even all the infused food I was eating could stave off all the exhaustion, but I attended sparring practice, anyway. As if I had much of a choice.

My first sparring partner was Brawl. He said, "I'll go easy on you, brother. I know you are tired."

"Don't," I said. "I need the training."

"If you say so. We will see how you hold up. And no slimy tricks."

" ‘Course not," I said.

Brawl lunged forward, swinging his flail in a wide arc, but I leaned back, and the wooden training weapon breezed past my nose.

I darted to the side, but I pushed off too far. Instead of putting my effort into speed and strength, I launched myself too far. I still had plenty to get used to.

Brawl struck again, sweeping his flail my way with far more control than he normally did. I felt the rush of air playing on my skin before the weapon hit, and I sprang backward, reacting far faster than normal. I tried to control myself, and I didn’t launch as far. When I planted my feet, I stuck them firmly into the ground, steadying myself, then tilted my training shield to deflect a follow-up swipe. It barely even jarred my shoulder when it hit, and I stayed steady. My feet shifted in the sand, but otherwise, I didn’t move.

The rest of the sparring match took longer than expected, with me forcing myself to control my movements. I needed speed, certainty, and power behind everything I did. I could flail randomly, launching myself across the sand pit with every motion, but that wouldn’t get me anywhere in the long run—even if I might have been able to use the raw force of my impacts to defeat Brawl.

Eventually, near the end of the match, I circled around his back and wrapped my spear around his neck, forcing him to surrender. He still hesitated, but he was under orders not to fight until he was unconscious, because of how much time that wasted in our training sessions. Brawl, with a show of reluctance, let go of his weapons.

“You are faster, Brother,” he told me. “Much faster than before.”

“I’d hope so.” I patted him on the shoulder. “You’ll catch up soon. Besides, with your Art active, you’ll be an effective tank still.”

“Not if you’re also more durable than me.”

“But I’m not the only one in the squad,” I said. “We still need you to protect Sage and Anchor. I can’t heal myself, and Anchor will always be a better sniper than me.”

We switched up sparring partners, and all throughout the afternoon, we continued our sparring matches. I steadied myself, doing my best to learn control and get used to how my old fighting techniques worked with my new body.

Finally, at the end of our sparring session, I was paired with Yesman for our final fight. Though our rankings had declined over the past few weeks, even our personal rankings (and not our squads as a whole), I hadn’t seen anyone who could match him in sparring yet.

He carried an axe and a shield, as usual, and I faced him with my training spear and shield.

We circled, both panting, our backpacks still full of riftrocks. Mine had to be much heavier, and I could probably win by crushing him with the weight of it, but I didn’t want to even try that. I was here to practice, not win by overwhelming him.

I lunged in, trading jabs with slashed, deflecting what I could, dodging what I couldn’t. My movements were sharper, now, and faster, but it would take a few weeks to perfect them. And I didn’t exactly have that kind of time—not when the next tournament joust was in three days.

I’d have to make do with what I had. I directed my Focus and my memory, controlling each muscle independently, but with lightning-fast speed and precision.

I relied on my defensive Skills to defend myself, then on my jousting thrusts when I had an opportunity to attack. Each time I struck his shield, a wooden crack echoed through the castle, and Yesman stumbled.

After using a heavy thrust, I switched to my standard offensive medley. It didn’t seem any faster, or like I was deploying any special techniques, but Yesman lagged behind, moving slower, rushing to block every hit, his counter-attacks coming too slow.

Within a matter of seconds, I’d pierced through his defenses. I didn’t trust my control yet, so I didn’t put my spear up to his chin, but he knew he was beaten, and he surrendered.
