Joselia rose, dusted off her hands, and gave the elderly couple a deep bow. “Jareth, bring the good captain back in. I’ll share my thoughts with him, and then I’m sure these fine young folks could use an escort home.” She winked at Resa, whose eyes grew watery again.

Brother Ilim’s thin voice murmured a response, but I was already out the door. Two heartbeats later found me with my back pressed to the sun-warmed stone wall.

Why me, indeed? Why does it come with me? Is it because I’m a runekeeper? Because I somehow have magic in a world where magic is dwindling?

My brows knotted. Almost as tightly as my fists. One thought rose above the others. Fierce, flooding my insides with heat.

I’m valuable. I may not understand almost anything about all this, but Velidan isn’t going to kill me. I have what he needs, and I’m the only reliable way he can get it. And after the rune this morning…

My most recent bit of magic was a thrilling, terrifying puzzle, but thinking of it- and of the now-larger shard tucked away in my pack- only fanned my inner flame.

I don’t need to be mortally afraid every time I’m face to face with him… and it’s time I start acting like it.

I’d scarcely taken two steps when the Watch door creaked open. A moment later Renner blocked my path. “Going somewhere?”

“Yes.” I lifted my chin, squared my shoulders, looked him in the eye, and declared, “Shopping.”

He blinked. Then cocked his head, clearly not having expected that answer. “For?”

“Peppermint leaves, gallea petals, cloves, and shill root.” I ticked each off on my fingers. One of his eyebrows inched higher with every word. “I’ll need a pestle, too, and a small pot. I can summon the water myself. Steaming it all will be tricky, but veth should work. I’ll just need a space to…” I took in his obvious confusion. “Those are ingredients for a sleeping tonic.”

“Huh.” The brow did not lower. “Didn’t expect charging off to take a nap to be your first instinct.”

“Well… look, I don’t know if instinct is the right word, but I’ve got to do something! Either I take some salted initiative and try to figure out what in the realms is going on and how to stop it, or… or I just keep on feeling helpless!” I folded my arms and huffed, “So either I’m going shopping, or I’m going to sit against this wall and cry.”

He snorted. “Shopping sounds better.”

“-you make of it? Related to the breathless?” Jareth was saying. Several pairs of feet thumped onto the paved stones.

The elder paladin seemed lost in thought. “Impossible to say. Certainly worth looking into, and we’ll brief the others.”

“Why would a monster want part of a divine sword?” Royce asked, tone carefully neutral. His gaze was fastened on a drifting cloud. “Could it… he… hurt people with it?”

Jareth snorted. “The same as with a rock or a butter knife. Which is to say, yes.”

My shoulders stiffened. I looked away, jaw tight.

“It’s not so simple,” Joselia sighed, giving him a stern glance. Then, in the manner of a parent giving a lecture, “Our understanding of divine magic is… a ripple. A drop of water in a lake. What use that relic may have is beyond my knowledge. It may be no different than a butter knife,” she arched a brow at Jareth, who ducked his head, “Or it may hold fierce power within. Something dormant, something to be awakened, something to be called upon… quite often, Fae magic relies on sacrifice. Perhaps using the relic in a ritual of some kind could… well. I could guess all day, but the simple answer is that I don’t know.”

“‘The most dangerous place in the mortal realm’.” Jareth massaged his neck, looking at me. “That was the phrase, right, your lady- ah, Brin?”

I nodded.

“Maybe he’s worried about what that relic could do to him, then. If Fae are about to descend on a place, they’d want to strip away its defenses.”

“An excellent thought,” Joselia agreed.

A twinge of pride made me flush. That had been my first thought, too, back when I’d first seen Velidan with the shard. And it could very well still be true.

“I wonder if this creature’s goal is single-minded. He matches your description, the two of you.” She glanced between me and Royce. “Was there a piece of this sword in Fellbrook?”

I hesitated. Royce did not. “Yes, in a statue. He… I mean, it was all chaos, but yeah, he definitely took it.” He resumed staring upwards, studiously avoiding my gaze.

“Curious. The hilt itself, and a fair bit of the blade’s base, lie in Pinnacle. I’m not sure about the others. Commemorated in the cities who devoted their resources, I imagine.”

“Part of it’s in Pinnacle,” I repeated slowly. I wasn’t sure what to do with that information at the moment, but I was certain of its importance. Pinnacle, Respite… where else? Saints, if he finds out, will he run off and grab them? Torture people in those places, too?

‘I don’t kill things needlessly, Brin.’

I shivered. He hadn’t killed them, for all that was worth. Just left an old woman bleeding and unconscious at her husband’s feet. Ashes, what a monster.

“Well,” Joselia’s crisp voice pulled me from my thoughts, “I say we set speculation aside and make a plan. I’m due to report soon, so I’ll be heading back to… oh, stars, what was the name of the inn?”

“The Deeproot,” her compatriot supplied with a chuckle. “I swear, half the signs I’ve seen in this city are farming puns.”

“Whole troops’ staying there?” Renner asked. His wince was practically audible.

Joselia shook her head. “Only Jareth and I. There’s a Watch barracks right up the road, and most of the Order opted to stay there. I’m old, therefore I need an actual bed.”

The younger Thalessian’s laughter boomed off the stones. “Hah! She’s being modest. Dame figured we’d hear better gossip at an inn. Might pick up a tip or two.”

“Well, yes. I’m still old, though.” She cracked a smile in Renner’s direction. “Can I convince you to stay with us? At least for the night? It sounds like your mission has been fulfilled, and the stars know I’d like to catch up.”

Copper eyes strayed to her. Then, hesitantly, back to me. “Sounds like I’m going shopping, at the moment.”

She blinked at him, then muttered something that sounded quite a bit like ‘my ears must be going’ and gave her head a little shake.

“Shopping? We’re going shopping?” Teela practically bounced on her heels. “Ooh, really? What are we shopping for?”

“A new nightgown, first off. And… um, just some things to help me sleep. Herbs, mostly.” I took in her obvious enthusiasm. “Er, and whatever else you want to shop for, I suppose.”

She squealed.

“An important mission, indeed.” Jareth chuckled, glancing between Renner and Joselia. “Perhaps I’ll escort the ladies while you two catch up? Then we’ll come to the inn after?” He arched an eyebrow between me and Teela. She beamed at him.

I nodded, noting the way Renner’s shoulders relaxed again. “I think that’s a lovely idea. Royce, do you-”

“Want to watch you fawn over herbs for the next hour?”

My face grew warm. “I won’t-”

“You absolutely will.” He snickered. “And yeah, sure. I’d actually love to see as much of this place as possible.”

That settled things. A short while later the charming Thalessian had found his way back to the busy market square with Teela, Royce, and myself in tow. What followed was a truly harrowing experience.

When I’d told Teela I’d wanted to go shopping, I hadn’t really understood what that meant. In Fellbrook, shopping meant that I would bring a basket of goods and a cheerful smile to someone I’d known my entire life. We’d chat and laugh and trade furs for soap, seeds for bread, meat for cloth, and so on.

This was not what happened in the Sedgewick Vale marketplace.

I found the peppermint leaves first. A rotund middle-aged woman stood before a line of wooden bins filled with herbs and spices. I gathered what I needed, complimenting her on the selection and asking about her garden. She chatted back, occasionally trying to convince me to buy a few things I really didn’t need. Eventually we came to the subject of payment.

The bundle of leaves was worth one copper coin. I did not have one copper coin, so I asked if she had the funds to make change if I gave her gold. She looked at me very strangely. I pulled out a coin, and her eyes grew very large. Then, as if a beacon had been lit within my palm, I found myself swarmed by vendors with greedy grins, booming voices, and armfuls of goods I certainly did not want.

Teela bravely fended them off while I stammered apologies and asked if anyone could make change. Royce and Jareth watched from the side, laughing, until a very tall man carrying bundles of dyed wool tripped in all the chaos and crashed headlong into Teela. She was knocked off-balance and, more problematically, her hat went flying.

I helped the ashen-faced young woman scramble to her feet. Both hands had snapped up to cover her horns, but the crowd around us had already seen. Most didn’t seem to care and continued waving things at me, but a few backs stiffened. More than one glare was aimed her way.

Jareth scooped the green felt and, in one smooth motion, held it out with a bow. “Your features become you, my lady.” His voice was clear and loud. Not to the point of boisterous, attention-drawing acclaim, but enough to be heard by the unfriendly faces nearby.

Teela stared at him. Eyes rounded, fingers splayed, knees locked. She looked ready to bolt.

The paladin cleared his throat, stepped closer, and murmured much more quietly, “Wear it only if you want. If you’d rather, I’m happy to carry it and stand nearby.” The sheer, steady calm in his deep blue eyes and the way her chin quivered in response made my heart ache. “With a hand on my sword, if need be.”

She said nothing. For a moment I thought she would blush again, but only her nose turned pink. Then she turned my way, reaffixed the hat with trembling fingers and notably misty eyes, and without a word I slipped an arm around her waist.

Royce then pointed farther down the square, where rather than wooden stalls and craftsmen peddling wares on the street, there were several stone shops with brightly painted shutters and signs written in curling script. I was able to find a white nightgown quickly enough. It was made from silk that shimmered in the sunlight and draped past my knees, with a high bodice and nearly-sheer fabric across the shoulders. It was a much finer garment than anything I’d normally wear, but it was modest and expensive, and I was able to make change for a handful of silvers during my purchase.

After some cajoling, Teela also tried on several nightdresses. Her exuberance returned as we twirled in front of our audience, and it was with bright eyes and curved lips that she purchased a lovely green gown.

Next we revisited the stalls, where I found the rest of my quarry. Herbs, a small clay pot, a mortar, and a pestle. Royce found a small woodworked rooster that he bought for his mother, and I bought a carving knife for Durst. Teela commented on a few things her mother might’ve liked, but made no more purchases.

Jareth waited with the patience of a saint. He seemed to have no interest in shopping for anything himself, but he made cheerful conversation and offered no complaints throughout our lengthy browsing. Eventually I asked him about Renner; how they’d met, how long they’d been with the Order together, and whether they were very good friends or they actually did want to kill each other. I was pretty sure it was one of those two, I told him, I just wasn’t quite sure which one.

“Hah! He and I go way back. We joined around the same time. About… ten years ago? I was seventeen, him sixteen.” He thought for a moment, rubbing one mailed hand through thick black hair. “Yeah, that sounds right. Ten years. Feels like longer.”

I trailed alongside him as Teela and Royce scurried ahead, examining glass ornaments in a shop window. We’d escaped from the chaotic crowds of the market square and were now strolling along semi-crowded streets lined by quaint stone shops. “Are you from Pinnacle?”

He gave a hearty laugh. “Oh no, my lady. Not at all. I grew up in Tithe; city boy, born and raised.”

“What made you want to join the Order?”

“Heh. Nothing as exciting as you might hope for.” He flashed a wide, white smile my way. “What boy doesn’t dream of growing up to wear shining armor, wave a sword around, and save damsels in distress?”

I bit down on a grin. “Royce did.”

He chuckled. “I think quite a few young men do. It was always what I wanted, and my family… well, I don’t think we’re quite wealthy enough that someone from Fellbrook would recognize the name, but ‘Paltier’ carries a lot of weight back home.”

“You’re from a noble house?” I looked the man up and down, thinking once again that he was the very picture of gallantry. “I… believe you.”

The grin turned wry. “Heh. I didn’t always look like this, but thanks. I was given an excellent education and free to oversee merchant dealings in a very luxurious estate. That’s what my family wanted.”

“But not you?”

Another chuckle. “No. I wanted to do some good with my life. I also found that I look exceptional in a white cloak. And,” He winked and, noting that I was beginning to lag, took an armful of my goods. “Rescuing damsels is quite fun.”

“Excuse me!” A bell-like voice called out alongside the scuff of boots on stone.

I turned to see a flaxen-haired young woman trotting up. She wore a long blue tunic and a delighted smile.

She looked Jareth up and down. Her eyes shone like fresh, clear water in the midday sun. “You’re a paladin, aren’t you?”

“At your service, your ladyship.” He dipped a polite bow, adjusting his armful. “How can I help you?”

She beamed. “Actually, I was hoping to help you! I have a message for you.”

Jareth inclined his head politely. “Oh? From whom?”

The woman clasped both hands behind her back and, in a sweet tone that turned my blood cold, sang out, “A very good friend of mine. His name is Embriel.”
