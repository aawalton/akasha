Henry was still floating above the arena, lost in his own thoughts while food stands and tables continued to be brought and set up when he felt something creep closer from his right. He didn’t need to look in its direction per se, as he already had a couple of Avatars scanning the patch of air. But whether they used their eyes or Mana Sight, nothing seemed amiss. There was no odd scent, no sound of a heartbeat. It was almost as if there was actually nothing near him.

But Unfettered Echoes told him that most certainly, there was something there. He wasn’t really extending the aura or pushing it out, but even in its neutral state, it reached out by itself. Henry couldn’t exactly describe how or why, but he just knew there was someone there.

Glancing toward the empty patch of air, Henry raised a brow, having already scanned the crowd and deduced who this might be and effectively, a beat later, the invisibility fell off to reveal a sheepish Zerathstra, holding two wooden cups in his hands.

“Why did I think I could sneak up on the Thousand-Eyed Sovereign?”

Henry smiled, a little embarrassed at the grandiose title while he accepted the cup extended to him. “You gave me time to sense you. You might have gotten closer otherwise.”

To that, Zerathstra just shrugged. “Maybe. Maybe not. If I had been getting closer with a sharper intent, you might have sensed me faster.”

Henry hummed, and he realized, the ape might have an explanation for what just happened. “I’m not even sure how exactly I sensed you. It almost seemed like my aura did it?”

“Probably. Auras don’t need to be pushed out to affect their environment,” the ape said, waving toward the giant trees around them. “I take care of this forest actively, of course, but they are still benefiting from my presence, even when I’m not paying attention. Aspects, when powerful enough, affect their environments that way. Mine helps living beings heal and grow, while yours seems to be good at perceiving the smallest details. There’s surely more to it than just that, but that’s on you to discover and figure out.”

The conversation lapsed for a few seconds where the two watched the celebrations below.

“Thanks for bringing her back home.”

Henry wasn’t sure exactly how to respond at first, but before he came to a decision, he found himself shaking his head. “I’m just glad we got there fast enough. Did she tell you about the time thing?”

“That time’s dilated here? Yeah. That’s… interesting. And worrying.”

Worrying hadn’t been the aspect Henry had thought about the most, so he was a bit surprised.

“Worrying how?”

Zerathstra was quiet for a moment, gently swirling his cup as he stared past the sloshing cocktail within. “It’s likely wildly expensive to maintain, and it reinforces what we spoke of before. That the System’s giving us more time.”

“We’ll know for sure soon,” Henry said. “My Avatars are closing in on the last two orbs.”

“Good. But in the meantime, you should take a break. And you should… look into ways of keeping yourself grounded.”

Henry blinked, then slowly turned to find Zerathstra staring at him, his gaze not exactly warning, but intense nonetheless. What surprised Henry wasn’t the worry he could sense in the ape’s words, but the voice he heard in the back of his mind that told him the senior A-rank was right.

“I knew of your capabilities before, how you could act through your clones with ease and whatnot. But their number has grown, hasn’t it? The last time I’ve seen you, you could barely handle a hundred or two of them. Now Velistraine tells me you had thousands? What’s the upper limit now? If you don’t mind me asking?”

“A little higher than eight thousand.”

The ape shuddered and grimaced, baring his teeth for a brief moment. “Hells. Even a hundredth of those would have my brain melting out of my ears. I know you’re well equipped to handle all of them, but… definitely make sure to just be you, once in a while. I haven’t had to deal with this myself, but some Aspects can sometimes eat up their user, as it were, and yours, while not the most dangerous I’ve seen, seems to be just doing that. And it feels… particularly potent, for some reason.”

Henry listened and digested what he was being told, and while he felt in control, he couldn’t really refute the possibility. And while the warning might be overblown, he knew deep inside that the bit about needing to ground himself was really needed. It wasn’t the solution to everything, but it would likely help. As for the potent remark, when Henry told Zerathstra that it was a twice-fused Aspect which took him a few weeks of non-stop meditation, the ape had sighed, then shook his head ruefully.

I guess I really should take that break, he thought, then a slap to the back of the shoulder jolted him out of his funk.

“Come on! Let’s get down there and have some fun. A couple of hours of rest won’t change much about the end of the world.”

Henry couldn’t argue with that, so pulling his attention away, he let his Octominds take over what he’d been doing, then floated down along with the ape.

A break shouldn’t hurt that much. Especially considering he couldn’t even remember the last time he had cut himself out of his network.

***

Taking a break turned out to be the right move. Of course, there was no such thing as completely cutting off his network, but Henry had plenty of practice steering his own conscious thoughts of what his Octominds were up to. They continued training his skills and navigating their way toward the last two remaining orbs while he snacked and occasionally joined in on the stories and tidbits that Velistraine and Maurice were recounting. Minutes turned to hours, and the sun dipped away below the horizon while the party still went on. Groups formed and broke down, drinking games were played, and a second round of meals was brought up.

It was fun. And for a brief moment, Henry felt the companionship push away at the—not loneliness, exactly, but at the feeling of being a watching outsider, disconnected from most.

As for Maurice, the crab was hopping from one group to the next, mostly hanging around Ash and Fabian. The girl had leveled quite a bit, now in the high eighties of B-rank, while Fabian seemed to be taking it easy. Then again, that might be more of an average pace. Henry didn’t have that many references aside from himself and Maurice, and clearly, the two of them had quite an intense work ethic.

The break continued for a few more hours. Henry socialized, ate good food, then, when a call to rest sounded out, promising more celebrations in the morning, Henry retreated to the cabin he’d used before, which was in the same state he’d left it. In fact, Henry found a little sign right before the entrance with his name on it. Maybe he wasn’t as homeless as he thought he was. Or maybe he just needed to have small homes everywhere he went. Whatever the case, Henry continued to ignore his Octominds and their activities, as nothing they were doing was urgent, then, once he made sure one of his Octominds was keeping an eye on Maurice—who was still hanging out with his friends—Henry slipped below the water of his pool and took a nap.

Sleep found him pretty quickly.
