Chapter 381

Eik returned to the laboratory directly after his meeting with Mn’Toakh.

General Pölse never did manage to turn up before he left, but that was fair enough. Mn’Toakh alone was more than enough to receive his orders, and the powerful swordmaster was surely needed elsewhere after an attack like that.

The man had gotten used to Eik’s eccentricities and found a quiet tolerance for his methods. There was no use denying his strength, and even a spicy son of a gun like Pölse would swallow even the most annoying antics if it meant keeping the people of the Alliance safe.

As the thoughts had swirled around in Eik’s head, he had eventually come to the decision to relocate his family and friends to Gimleh for the time being. There was no real way to predict what the next target of the Lord of the Moon would be, and Gimleh’s defenses were far greater than Earth, even with Eik’s massive investments.

Not to mention the Profound Toxic beasts deployed throughout the city numbering more than a million in total. They were present in Forest as well, but Gimleh still felt safer.

As a matter of fact, his family had already been installed in Andihar’s mansion thirty minutes after he had sent a Profound Toxic beast to make the arrangements. Ihasu had had no issues with that. She was just as anxious for the kids’ safety as Eik was.

And from that moment on, Eik didn’t voice a single complaint about the harvests or the long hours at the cauldron. He just settled in and worked.

Again and again, he suffered through the mutilation to acquire the active ingredients for the potions, and brew for hour after hour without end.

As much as he wanted to see his girl and his kids, that was the exact reason he kept going. He worked nonstop for a little over six and a half days before he finally decided to give himself just a short little break.

***

“Daddy’s… well, not home, exactly. But I’m here!” Eik called as he stepped through the large glass double doors and inside from Andihar’s enormous backyard.

“Mr. Magnasen, welcome,” the head housekeeper of the Dayarunar estate greeted, offering a polite bow. “Will you be staying the night?”

“Hey, Eme, thanks. No, not this time, unfortunately. I’m just here to say hi to Ihasu and the kids. Are they here right now? I know I’m arriving kind of out of the blue, but now was the time I needed a break, so…”

She smiled warmly. “They’re all here. It’s almost bed time after all.”

Eik looked at his bare wrist. “Oh, wow, would you look at the time! Sitting room?” he asked, already making his way there.

“Indeed. I’ll bring you something cold to drink.”

“I love you, Eme. You know that?” he said and thumb launched a Legendary Mystery Medicine straight into her hands.

She gasped, nearly fumbling the catch, and gasped again when she saw what it actually was that she had caught. “M-Mr. Magnasen! I can’t possibly accept—”

“Yeah, you can. It’s easy. Just give it a try,” Eik told her as he headed deeper into the house.

The kids were both in their pyjamas when Eik entered the room, barely able to keep their eyes open as they lay slung across their mother’s lap while the adults talked.

“Eik!” Ihasu breathed. At the sound of his name, both kids jumped up and barreled into his chest. “What’re you doing here?”

“I just really need a break. Just for an hour,” he said tiredly, sinking into the soft cushions of the couch. “Then I’m heading straight back.”

“No!” Bin shouted. “That’s too short! I want to sleep in the bed with you!” Goo simply gripped his even tighter.

Eik’s jaw tensed as his face took on a deep frown, desperately pinching tears. “I can’t do that this time, kiddos.”

Molanda smiled softly at the scene, Andihar sitting at her side. Rasmus sat next to Ihasu on the couch, Eik’s cat, Mis, curled up in his lap. Andihar and Molanda’s daughter, Thalandi and her girlfriend, Uja were there as well.

“Anyway,” Eik redirected. “How’s school?”

“We’ve been doing journals! I wrote about you and mom! Wanna see?” Bin asked.

“Oh, man, there’s nothing I would rather see,” Eik said with a laugh, his daughter dashing off to their room somewhere in the house, the hollow thuds of her feet on the wood floor echoing reverberating throughout the house as she moved.

Eik watched her go, and when she was gone, he turned to the adults. “Sorry for the sudden intrusion. I know it’s a little late, but—”

“That’s a bunch of nonsense,” Molanda broke in, waving off his apology. “There isn’t a time of day where you’re not allowed to show up here. We’re family, after all, Eik.” Unexpectedly, Thalandi was actually the one to nod her agreement. She had always been so irritated by him, but her demeanor had changed quite a bit since she got together with Uja.

“Thanks for letting so many of us bunk up in your spare rooms. I know it’s a bit of an inconvenience.”

“Would you stop that?” Molanda admonished. “It is quite literally the least we can do. What you’re doing for us—all of us—is unimaginably bigger. You have our gratitude.”

“Oh, that’s… Nah, that’s okay,” Eik chuckled. “I’m just doing what I can.”

“No, you’re the only one who can do what you’re doing, my son,” Rasmus chimed in, leaning across to put his hand on Eik’s. “And I’m very proud of you for that. But you know that.”

“Because you say it all the time.”

“Because I tell you all the time, yes,” his father laughed. “And I will continue to tell you. So stop being so timid and just accept it when people express their gratitude for it.”

“Where do you think I got this awkwardness toward praise from?” Eik asked with an arched eyebrow. “It sure as hell wasn’t from Mom. You have to practice what you preach, Pops.”

Rasmus groaned. “You know that it’s really hard for me.”

“Yes, I do! That’s what I’m saying! I got it all from you!” Eik snorted with a gesture at himself. “Neither of us are capable of taking a compliment.”

“Okay, okay, I’ll leave it.” His father was grinning and Eik returned it brightly.

“Where’s Olivia anyway? And Michael? Heath’s obviously at the workshop, so I’m not even going to ask about him.”

“Well, you’re exactly right about Heath, first of all,” Rasmus said. “Oli and Michael are on Earth. He’s refused to leave his patients. Oli has agreed to come and sleep here at night. Or at least that’s the case for those nights where she actually does sleep,” he said with a roll of his eyes. “I’ve tried to talk some sense into that girl, but it’s like she’s not hearing me.”

Olivia, the girlfriend of Eik’s deceased brother, Torbjørn, had stepped into the role of Forest’s de facto leader until a proper council was established. That council was now in place and active, but Olivia had, by far, been the most popular candidate for the head of said council, and she had found it difficult to turn down.

That meant her work load had lightened somewhat, but with everything going on on Earth right now, she had eventually ended up with just as much work as before. Eik somewhat regretted pushing for her appointment, but the fact was that she had run the city better than anybody else could. She had kept them stable through incredibly turbulent times.

All he had provided were money and firepower. She was the true backbone of it all.

“Olivia has one of the strongest senses of duty I have ever seen in a person,” Andihar added. “It’s extremely admirable, if a little inconvenient when she should be taking a break for her own sake.”

“She’s an absolutely amazing woman,” Rasmus agreed. “But she’s even more of a workaholic than my son.”

Eik snorted. “I’m not a workaholic. I hate working. The moment this whole thing is over with, I’m never lifting a finger again. And I mean that literally. I have like five million super servants.”

Laughs chimed all around, soon accompanied by the percussion of Bin’s footsteps.

From the threshold she leapt into his lap and opened a little book she had bound along the spine with rough cord. It was the best thing he had ever seen.

She read aloud to him from every page, describing her relationships to him, Ihasu, Goo, Rasmus, Mis, Uncle Heath and Uncle Michael, Aunt Olivia, Grandpa Gul, Uncle Andi and Aunt Molanda.

She had written about tending the garden, and cooking dinner with Mom and Dad. She had written about going to Dad’s work and drinking chocolate milk, and going to Mom’s work where she learned about fracture magic. She thought she might like to become a fracture specialist when she grew up.

“That’s an amazing dream,” Eik said, ruffling her hair. “I’m sure you’re going to be almost as good as Mom.”

Taken aback, Bin gasped. “I’m going to be even better!”

“Well, if that’s what you’re aiming for, then you know you have to study really well, right?”

Bin looked at him with puzzlement. “But you’re the strongest in the whole universe, and Grandpa said you never studied a day in your life.”

“Oh, did he now?” Eik asked, lightning flying from his eyes as he glared at his father.

Rasmus could do nothing but chuckle nervously. “I’m… I’m sure I wouldn’t say something like that, would I?”

Bin wiggled her finger at him. “No, no, Grandpa, I remember it. You said, ‘when your father was your age, he would use his math homework to wipe his shoes when he came home after playing outside in the mud. Frankly, it’s a stroke of luck that he even knows his a b c’s.’”

Unable to control herself, Ihasu burst into hysterical laughter, her fingers digging into Eik’s shoulder to keep herself from tumbling off the couch. “Whenever I try to explain fracture math to him, he always looks like he forgot his own name,” she sputtered, crying with mirth.

Eik couldn’t hold back a laugh either. “Okay, okay, that’s very funny, but can I get this sweet moment with my daughter?”

“Sorry, sorry. I’ll shut up now,” Ihasu breathed, barely able to get out the words between snorts.

The last page of Bin’s journal was filled out by a nice, colorful drawing. It was a bunch of human kids with one little, green girl in the center. They were all smiling widely. “That’s me in the middle, there!” she told him, pointing to the green girl. “and the boy next to me is Philip,” she continued, throwing a hesitant glance up at him.

He squeezed her and smiled warmly, with not a hint of his usual antics. “That’s really nice, Bin. Seeing this makes me so very happy.”

Goo, who had been awake when he arrived, had fallen into a deep, calm sleep the moment his head hit his father’s lap.
