I draw my flames into a ball in the middle of the deck, choosing not to extinguish them completely in case I need them. I do my best not to burn any more of the deck than I already have, but there’s still plenty of debris around that I can use as an interim fuel to avoid spending too much of my dwindling mana.

Drawing my mind mostly back into my body, I feel the nausea and throbbing headache of over-extension of my mana. Ignoring it as best I can, I call for Sirocco to come out and watch the captain and other man specifically. She soon reports that, though the captain quickly takes advantage of the withdrawal of the flames to move and speak to those who haven’t yet fled the ship, the other man stays where he is, cradling Kyrian. He does drop the shield, though. I take it as a good sign.

House Siren’s ship is still battling with Valence and Sor’az’s ship, but it’s starting to look a bit worse for wear. The battle between House Bladestrike’s, Elarion’s, Josiah’s and Layton’s ships has come to an end – recently, I’d guess. Bladestrike is flying the blood-red flag of surrender – no white in this world – but the other ships are still grouped around it.

Hunter, Happy, I have a message for each of the enemy ships. Ask for help from your mages to project your voice if you need it. Tell me when you’re ready.

I feel a sense of acknowledgement and then a couple of minutes pass. Down below, the evacuation is in full swing. I see sailors and fighters stripping off jewellery and weapons with varying degrees of reluctance. I’d feel bad but they were attacking us just now, and I’m not willing to let them take storage bracelets with them. Classer Inventories are bad enough, but I don’t have anyone who can check those. Hopefully the lack of time will limit what they can grab. Though I don’t know what’s happening below the deck….

Since the main deck has cleared a bit and no one has tried to attack the alcaorises – not even the three they pulled back onto the deck because they’d dived in without stripping away a bracelet or a ring – I swoop down warily.

Fearful glances are sent my way – and not a few resentful ones too – but no one is stupid enough to take a pot shot. I keep my tattoo shield up anyway – it hasn’t fully recharged yet, but there’s enough charge in it for now.

Kalanthia? Lathani? Would you be willing to help me here? I explain what I need them to do and they both agree.

Those still on deck recoil as a massive Tier three nunda suddenly appears in their midst, but she keeps her domain in check – we don’t want people becoming thoughtless in their fear. When she doesn’t attack, the onlookers relax a little, but still keep a wary eye on her. She’ll tune into the thoughts those around are projecting – someone who has something to hide will generally be rather loud about it, she has told me before.

Meanwhile, Lathani, less noticeable when compared to the bulk of her mother, slips down below decks. With her control over shadows, she can go unseen, and then run away if attacked by someone she can’t defeat quickly. She’ll find anyone who’s hiding – or who’s taking the opportunity to loot.

When Trouble complains irritably about not having anything to do, and Bastet actually agrees with him, I decide that it’s probably safe enough for them to come out. Plus, they’ll help make sure no one does anything they shouldn’t.

If the crew’s eyes were fearful when Kalanthia came out, they become even rounder when an army of beasts suddenly appear one by one out of nowhere. But it becomes clear that this was a good idea when Bastet and Ninja working together catch the captain trying to sneak off the ship. I send him a hard look and he cringes a little before regaining his composure. Fenrir is deputised to guard him and make sure he doesn’t go anywhere while Artio uses his vines to begin to collect all the dropped items.

I’m ready, Hunter reports. She’s the one on Elarion’s ship.

Tell House Bladestrike these words: Heir Titanbend has defeated Heir Torrent and taken him captive. You are to pull all ballistas away from firing ports, lay down arms, and sail your ship to within three ship-lengths of the ship that previously belonged to Heir Torrent. There, you are to drop anchor and wait for further instructions. If your shield recharges, you are not to raise it once more. Heir Torrent’s former crew have been ordered off the ship he has lost. If you wish to offer them shelter, you may do so. We will respond to any act of aggression on your part with lethal force. You will not have a second chance to surrender.

I sense Hunter concentrating hard, then she lets me know that she has delivered the message.

As if waiting for Hunter to finish, Happy announces that she, too, is ready now. She’s the one on Valence’s ship.

Tell House Siren these words, I start, and then repeat a similar message to Happy as to Hunter. The difference is that I start it with a demand to surrender, signaled by raising a blood-red flag. As far as I know, the reason they use that colour here is to signal that enough blood has already been spilled and no desire to see more of it. Possibly the original flags actually were stained in blood.

By the time all of those communications have happened, my ship has returned. The crew on board throw grappling hooks across to pull the two ships together. I notice that they’ve dropped fenders on that side of the ship – no doubt to stop the ships damaging each other as they shift with the movement of the waves.

I didn’t order them to do that, but Loran must have picked up my intentions from the conversation going on. I feel a bit bad for going over his head to speak to Hunter and Happy, but I didn’t feel like having a middleman would improve things there.

Already, the other two ships are sailing towards us, with an escort of our ships. I’ll need to speak to the lords or ladies on board to find out how much blame should be assigned to them for this mess before I can consider what consequences to impose on them.

With everything here under control, I extinguish the ball of flames still burning. It disappoints Aingeal who was enjoying playing with them, but it saves me that slow mana drain, allowing my reserves to start to increase again.

Alyna, are you near Master Mirena? I ask.

I am, Alyna agrees, a touch warily.

Can you ask her to come up on deck, please. I have a question for her.

I feel Alyna’s acknowledgement.

She’s coming, she tells me less than a minute later.

While I wait, I check in with Sirocco. Her report has me frowning. Not because they have moved, but because they haven’t. To all appearances Kyrian remains unconscious. That’s rather odd for a Classer. What happened to him?

“Please! It was my mother’s!” A distraught voice catches my attention and I see Kalanthia glaring down at a cowering sailor. He’s clutching at a feminine-looking bracelet with shaking fingers.

Is it? I ask Kalanthia. She doesn’t relent from her predatory position, but responds easily.

His thoughts are true.

Then allow him that. I won’t take keepsakes from people. Then I add a qualifier. Though feel free to scare anyone half to death if they try to claim something is a keepsake when it isn’t.

As you wish, Kalanthia agrees, though I have the distinct sense that she will take pleasure out of instilling the fear of a Tier three nunda in anyone who tries it.

Markus, Lathani breaks in, her tone unusually serious. It isn’t the first time she’s spoken since she went below decks – there were three crew members who were attempting to loot and who she encouraged to return everything they’d taken; one of them tried to attack her and learned the error of his ways – but she was a lot more off-hand about those encounters.

What is it? I ask, alert and prepared to redirect my smaller companions to go and help her.

There are a load of humans here. In two different rooms – males, and females with cubs. They all have that metal thing around their necks like Alyna and Maxen.

I clench my teeth as a surge of disgust goes through me. I wonder why they’ve been left locked up – because they didn’t care to release the slaves while saving their own skins, or because they included slaves as the ‘property’ I ordered to be left on board?

Loran, can you organise for that to be dealt with, please? I ask him. You know what I would do.

Yes, sir, Loran agrees immediately. Technically, I suppose this is more of a matter for the Expedition Steward, but I trust Loran’s judgement more for something like this. And he’s my manservant, so he has more than enough authority to manage it.

A gangway is laid over the railings of both ships and lashed into place. Soon, Maxen joins me with members of his squad prepared to fight. They quickly spread out over the deck, looking for any threats. They’ll probably comb every square inch of the ship – a good idea since we have no idea what traps might be waiting for us. Loran comes with them too, bowing quickly to me and then disappearing below deck. I’m glad to see one of the cooks – Rory’s sweetheart – the healer, and the seamstress accompany him.

Sarran is next, quickly followed by Lizil who looks rather nervous as she crosses the gangway and needs a bit of support from my Steward.

“You called?” she asks me a little archly, clearly not enjoying having to change ships.

“Yes, I have a question for you.” I pull out the suppression cuffs that a group of bandits once used to try to capture me. “If I take out the chain, will these still work? And is it possible to reinforce them with enchantments so that the only way they come off is if I remove them myself?”

She takes the cuffs and bends over them, studying them closely. While she does that, I turn to Sarran who’s watching me warily.

“Loran indicated that you have Heir Torrent captive.”

I point at the quarterdeck and shrug.

“He’s up there with someone who’s clearly responsible for him. Sirocco’s watching them.”

“You haven’t bound him in any way?” Sarran asks sharply.

“He’s unconscious right now.”

“That could change any moment and then we’ll have another fight on our hands unless he chooses to surrender.” Sarran’s voice is tight.

I indicate the cuffs I’ve just given to Lizil.

“That’s why I’m considering these. I figured that just killing him in cold-blood wouldn’t be a good idea.”

“No,” Sarran agrees, his gaze calculating. Then it snaps back to me. “You’re taking the ship?”

I shrug.

“There have to be consequences for attacking me,” I point out.

“But you’re allowing the crew to escape?”

“I doubt it was their choice to attack. If I’m not executing Torrent for ordering the attack, and almost killing several of us personally, I’m not going to just kill people who were just following orders.”

Sarran hums.

“Where do you wish me to be – organising the processing of your new property, or guarding Heir Torrent?” Good question. “I have a Skill that could trap him for a few minutes even if he wakes up,” he adds. That decides it for me.

“Guard Torrent, please. I’ll be up there as soon as I’ve decided how to proceed. Sirocco will let me know if there are any problems.”

“Yes, my lord.”

“Actually,” I call him back just as he starts turning away. He gazes at me with lifted eyebrows, briefly reminding me strongly of Nicholas. I hand him a knife that I quickly coat with anti-magic potion. “Here – cut him with this. It will limit the amount of magic he can do for a while.”

Sarran’s eyebrows rise a fraction further, but he bows his head briefly in acknowledgement and then strides towards the quarterdeck. I have to admit that he has a point – and one that I should have considered before now. At least I know it’s all in good hands now.

My attention is drawn back to Lizl when she shifts and lifts her head.

“So?” I ask, a little impatiently.

“Removing the chain will not cause any issues if I ensure that each cuff has a power rune.”

“And can you do that?” She gives me a very cutting look. “Sorry. I mean, how long will that take you?”

Master Mirena pulls out a rune carver from one of her pockets. She makes a few marks, moving fast enough that she almost blurs, and then, after a brief pause for thought, she returns the carver to her pocket and pulls out an orange-coloured vial and something that looks like an eye-dropper out of another pocket. After a few more movements, a rune flashes orange on each cuff, and then fades and disappears entirely.

“Done. And I ensured that the power rune is a stronger version than the original one there. You can remove the chain now.”

She sounds very smug. Deservedly so, I suppose. Taking the cuffs back, I use my Metal-Shaping to weaken the chain at the points where it joins each cuff. Not actually being trapped in it myself means that I don’t have any difficulty in using my magic and I can soon pull the chain away leaving two separate bands of metal.

“And what about my other question?”

Lizil gains a more thoughtful look on her face.

“That kind of thing is less the domain of general enchantments and is more in the realm of curses.”

“Curses?” I repeat, my eyebrows twitching upwards a little. They’re not just bad words? “Tell me more, please.”
