Jade turned a page in her book, moving carefully so as to not get it wet. The hot springs lived up to their name; large pools of shallow, steaming water shaded by the woodland foliage. The smell of earth permeated the area, reminding Jade of camping trips she'd taken with her family as a child. Warmth flowed into her, easing tension and leaving her languidly reclining against the rim of the pool.

"Luis, I admit it." She called through the reed screen that separated the men's bath from the women's. "You were right, this was a great idea."

She couldn't make out his response, but it sounded characteristically cheerful. Smiling, she marked her spot and closed the book, setting it down on the dry rim of the spring.

"You still haven't finished that?" Naomi asked, glancing her way. "Didn't you buy it the first time we were here?"

"We've had a lot going on." Jade shrugged. "Besides, I like to savor books. If you go through it too fast, you miss things."

"I'm the same way." Tara said. The tek'kalla was sitting on the lip of the pool, dangling her legs into the water. "There's nothing better than a good book. I always reread them as soon as I finish, too."

"I can't reread books." Now it was Naomi's turn to shrug. She smiled at the surprised looks Tara and Jade turned on her, holding up her hands in apology. "I prefer the excitement that comes with something new."

"What about you, Siora?" Tara asked, seeking additional backup. "You reread books, right?"

"I don't read much fiction." The elf said, looking over her shoulder at them from across the pool. The tan-skinned elf had her head propped up on her elbows, scrutinizing the collection of leafy plants and flowers that surrounded the pool. "It's nature and alchemy books, for me."

Siora blinked at the surprised looks she received from each of them.

"Why not?" Tara asked. "Fiction is the best! Novels aren't constrained to the stupid rules of our Universe. When you open a book, you can be anywhere, anyone, experiencing anything."

"The rules of our Universe are stupid?" Jade asked, amused.

"Of course.  Magic helps, but it can't do everything. Yet."

"I just like learning." Siora said, sounding wistful. She turned her attention back to the flowers, running a finger along their stalks. "I'm always short on potion ingredients, you've seen how quickly I go through them. The more I learn about what I can use, the more I'll be able to help everyone."

Naomi scooted over to sit next to the elf and gave her a hug.

"We appreciate that about you. Especially now that you're one of us. I mean, you've always been one of us, but, you know…"

Naomi gestured to Siora's hand, which now bore the same shifting patterns as the rest of them. The team had escorted Siora to the closest Sanctuary the previous evening, offering their support as she accepted the trial from one of the Labyrinth attendants. Jade had been hoping they'd encounter Damos again, but this Labyrinth spirit had been different. She had granted Siora her class and departed, leaving Jade's burning questions about the Labyrinth's mechanisms and history unanswered.

"I know." Siora smiled again. "And now that I have the alchemist class, I can do so much more with these ingredients! It's like taking the first step into a whole new world."

"Frightening?" Jade asked.

"Terrifying?" Naomi added.

"What?" Siora looked confused.

"We've been there." Jade smirked, waving her hand in a vague, all-encompassing gesture. "Taking our first steps in a new world?"

"Oh!" Siora's face reddened a little. "Sorry, I wasn't thinking."

"It's fine." Naomi laughed. "Just a little joke. But really, you didn't have to do that. We would've been happy to keep travelling with you even if you weren't a Wayfarer.

"I know, but…" Siora sighed, finally turning away from her plants. "This is my home too. I want to do my part to keep it safe."

"We're all in this together." Jade nodded. "We'll do some training on the monsters in this quadrant before we head out, that should help get your level up."

"Alright, enough Labyrinth talk." Naomi clapped her hands, sliding lower in the bath. "Back to relaxing. Tara! You should get in."

"It's too hot." The tek'kalla huffed, still sitting on the edge of the pool. She kicked her legs a few times. "I'm still adjusting to it.""Really?" Jade asked, surprised. The water was about hotel hot tub temperature and felt much the same, minus all the bubbles and churning water. It was perfect.

"The world my people come from is cold." Tara shrugged, looking a little embarassed. "Our bodies are good at holding heat. Not so good at getting rid of it."

"Well, I'll join you then. I'd like to get a little further in my book."Jade slid herself up and out of the pool, finding a perch near the tek'kalla. She dried her hands then retrieved her book, flipping it open.

"When you're done with your porn book, can I check it out?"

Jade gave Naomi a flat look and Siora giggled. Tara, on the other hand, looked confused. She cocked her head.

"I don't think that translated right…"

"No, it did. And this is not a porn book. It's a fantasy romance!"

"Can you really call it fantasy?" Naomi asked. "We live in a world of magic and mystery now. Wouldn't that make it grounded? And besides." She gave Jade a knowing look. "Everyone knows that romantasy books are basically porn."

"This one has an excellent story!" Jade said, clutching the book to her chest. "I don't know what you're talking about."

"I've seen some of the drawings in it." Naomi arched an eyebrow. "How many pages was the first sex scene?"

"…Shut up."

Luis glanced over his shoulder as the sound of laughter drifted through the partition between the men's and women's baths.

"What do you think they're talking about?" Fenrin asked from the other side of the steaming pool. He was sitting up with a straight back and proper posture, which Luis felt was a little out of place in the bath.

"Girl stuff." Luis shrugged. "Probably best we don't know."

"Another truth that stretches across worlds." Fenrin laughed.

"More importantly…" Luis pointed a finger at his friend. "You should let yourself relax some, man."

"I am relaxing."

"You're sitting like you're at a formal dinner. Gotta lie back and enjoy the bath." Luis gestured at himself, sprawled across the submerged ledge that ringed the inside of the pool.

With obvious effort, Fenrin allowed himself to lie back, reclining against his corner of the bath.

"It's all that etiquette training my mother put me through." He said, smiling wryly. "She wanted to get me ready for all those balls and ceremonies. Not that I ever ended up attending many of them."

"Why's that?"

"After my father went missing on one of his expeditions, I put everything into training to become a Wayfarer. But with everything that's been happening lately, I wonder if leaving home was the right decision. Rimehold isn't a large city like Illysport, we don't have much of a guild presence. Just a small local militia." He sighed. "I hope they're okay."

"You've already done a lot for your people out here. There'd be a lot more demons running around if it weren't for you. And…" Luis bowed his head. "Mirella."

They shared a moment of silence for their departed companion. Luis held it, not wanting to interrupt his friend's thoughts. Eventually, Fenrin spoke up.

"You're probably right, but it's easy to doubt."

"I know what you mean. But if you want, we can take time to visit your home and check in. I'm sure Jade and the others would be okay with it."

"Thanks, but it's okay. It's not an easy place to get to without node access. I am looking forward to seeing your home, though. That's our next stop, right?"

"Yeah, as soon as Siora is done with that potion of hers." Luis grinned. "It's very different than the Labyrinth. You're in for a surprise.""You've spent the past month telling me all about it." Fenrin laughed. "What more is there to be surprised about? Oh!" He snapped his fingers, and Luis was struck again by how similar mannerisms were across worlds. "I almost forgot. I need to get a good translation item before we go. Otherwise, we'd step through the portal and suddenly not be able to understand each other."

"That wouldn't be good." Luis agreed, then grinned. "But it would be funny.""For the first minute, maybe."

"Tara could translate. I think her suit has that function."

"I'm sure she'd love to be stuck as a translator while there's a whole new world to take in."

"Good point."

More laughter drifted through the partition, and Luis spared another glance in that direction. Was Tara doing alright? She'd had some trouble meshing well with the group early on. The tek'kalla shared Jade's penchant for blunt candidness. That wasn't a bad thing, but it did lead to them butting heads from time to time.

"She'll be fine."

"What?"

Luis turned back to Fenrin, who was smiling at him.

"Tara. She's been fitting in better, lately. Thanks to you. And, if you care about my opinion, I think she feels the same way you do."

"…Is it that obvious?"

Fenrin chuckled.

"If I can notice it? Probably." He nodded to him. "You should talk to her."

"Maybe…" Luis sighed. "It's just… complicated."

The conversation lapsed. The two of them relaxed in the bubbling spring, letting their worries drift away with the curling wisps of steam. Well, most of them. Now, Luis found himself wondering what the others saw between him and Tara.  He'd only just realized that his feelings for the tek'kalla went beyond friendship. But even if she felt the same, that path led to murky waters. They were from entirely different worlds and cultures, not to mention different species. But there was something about the woman's drive that resonated with him. She gave the impression that nothing could stand between her and her goals. But there was a tenderness beneath her resolve. She, too, was apart from her people, navigating a foreign land where most people seemed to dislike her just because of how she looked. Luis knew what that was like, in more ways than one.

He sighed, leaving the bath and toweling off. It was a pleasant day, so instead of heading back into the lodge Luis decided to enjoy the weather for a while longer. He sat with his back to the partition and leaned against it, drawing in a deep, relaxing breath. One that was suddenly interrupted by a surprised squeak from right behind him.

"Tara?" Luis started, glancing back at the reed screen. He could make out a vague silhouette on the other side.

"Luis?"

"Sorry." He chuckled, feeling his face redden. "Didn't know you were there. I can move."

"No." She said quickly. "It's alright."

"If you're sure."

He shifted a little, not sure what to say. Or if he should say anything at all. Fenrin's comment had him overthinking everything. Unfortunately, simply recognizing that didn't do a whole lot to fix it. A people person by nature, Luis was used to knowing how to fill awkward gaps in conversations. For once, he found himself drawing a blank.

"Um… Your magic is really cool."

Luis winced before he even finished the sentence.

Smooth, Espanoza… Smooth.

"You really think so?" Tara asked, perking up. Reeds rustled against his back as she shifted, and he was suddenly reminded of how close the two of them were. The mental image was not helping.

"Really." He said, wrenching his focus back to the conversation. "You haven't used many Labyrinth spells lately. Most of it has been your own work, and you've been getting better. At least, that's what it looks like to me."

"I've always studied traditional spellcasting techniques, ones that don't rely on the Labyrinth's soul-imprinted powers. But it turns out what worked in a classroom isn't always practical in the real world. I've had to make a lot of changes."

"I wouldn't even know where to start." Luis laughed softly. "A few months ago, I didn't know any of this existed."

There was a beat of silence, and then, in a tentative voice:

"Would you like to learn?"

"Magic?"

"Magical theory. I could help you with some fundamentals. Even a little knowledge could help make your Labyrinth spells more efficient. Maybe you could even come up with one of your own."

"I'd like that." Luis said. "I'd like that a lot.""I would too. I was…"

Her voice trailed off, and he felt her shift again.

"You were what?"

"Worried you'd think it was boring." Tara finished quietly.

"Of course it isn't! Who wouldn't want to learn more about magic? Especially from someone so passionate about it."

"You mean that?"

"I do. You can have Jade check, if you want."

Tara giggled. It was the first time Luis had heard her laugh like that. The sound spread a warmth though his body in a way the springs hadn't quite managed.

"I'll take your word for it."

The reeds rustled again, and he sensed Tara getting up.

"Where are you off to?"

"To get my books!"

"Maybe we should wait until we're out of the bath."

He felt more than heard her slowly sit back down.

"That… has some merit, yes."

From Tara's tone, Luis could perfectly picture her somewhat abashed, yet still so unashamedly enthusiastic, expression. Then, he remembered she was naked and the image changed. He blushed, burying his face in his hands.

On the other side of the partition, Jade hid a slight grin behind the cover of the book she was back to reading. The conversation taking place through the reed screen was too far away for her to make out, but the emotions drifting from the pair told her everything she needed to know.

Good for them.
