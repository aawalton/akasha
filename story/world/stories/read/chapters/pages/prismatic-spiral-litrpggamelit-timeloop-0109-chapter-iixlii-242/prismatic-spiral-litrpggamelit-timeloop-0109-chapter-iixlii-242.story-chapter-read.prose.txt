Chapter II.XLII (2.42)

“Welcome, to my abode.” Zena leaned into the bannister and leered down at them from her perch on the balcony over the entryway. She was a mature woman, maybe in her mid 60’s, and wore all black with a veil of spiderwebs partially concealing her face. “You must be Fuku. I am ever so grateful you had the wisdom to heed my call. Who is your companion? A servant? A member of your coven?”

Rather than answering, Mitsuko tossed the doll up at the woman.

Despite her age, she caught the fabric doll in a blur of nimble movement. Clearly some sort of enhancement spell or enchantment aided her reflexes. She held it up, letting the chandelier’s flickering light illuminate it.

“Interesting. Why—”

Mitsuko cast Retrospection on the doll and the woman. As the spell took her, Zena went slack-jawed and the doll nearly tumbled from her limp hands. After the moment passed, she recovered and clutched the thing to her bosom, as if it were a suckling infant.

“Oh, how you’ve blessed me,” Zena crooned, a manic smile behind her veil. “You will be treasured, trust me in that. To think you brought me a message from her.” She shuddered and bit her bottom lip.

“I take it that witch is a known factor in the community?” Mitsuko assumed.

“A known… are you seriously ignorant of whom you’ve placed me in contact with?” Zena stared at her like she was an idiot. “This isn’t a witch. It’s the witch. Bah! How can someone so unschooled in the ways of witches have her blessing? Then again, how can I question her wisdom?”

Zena glided down her staircase like a phantom. When she joined them on the first floor, she dramatically waved a hand and ushered them into her sitting room.

A fire crackled in the hearth and an animal rug stared up at Mitsuko, its face half melded into the wooden floorboards. She didn’t even recognize the dead creature. Some sort of large, green-furred mammal with three eyes. The eye in its forehead seemed to follow her.

“Sit and relax,” Zena ordered them. Mitsuko and Fuku glanced at one another and then did as requested. “A assume your familiar is still outside? Prudent of you to not bring him in. He acts as a scout in case of ambush or treachery. Though, fear not, I am only here to offer you exactly as my letter requested. You will have full reign over the libraries of Amber Island so long as I can discuss the nuances of witchcraft with you.”

“And me?” Mitsuko asked. “Can I access the libraries freely?”

“If you choose to. I was commanded to assist you in all your endeavors."

Mitsuko considered that. Whoever that witch was in her vision through the doll, she was clearly using her influence and infamy to help Mitsuko succeed in her task.

“Even the restricted library?” Mitsuko asked. “For example, I want to learn more about Azure Island.”

“That failure?” Zena made a face. “I don’t know why you’d waste your time on it. However, I might be able to pull some strings to gain you access into those files. It will take a day or two. For whatever reason, a past governor put that library in the category of high restriction. Most people don’t realize the island even exists outside of myths.”

“Failure?” Mitsuko raised an eyebrow. “Care to elaborate?”

“The island sank and was swiftly infested by crusts after a war. I believe I’ve read that Amber Island assisted in the creation of the weapon that sank it. I don’t know much more on the topic.” The old woman waved a hand dismissively. “It’s old news and not of any relevance to the modern world.”

It certainly was relevant to Mitsuko’s current objectives. Which meant that information was a necessity to not having the entire archipelago being frozen in time for eternity. So, to her, the island seemed very relevant to the modern world. But, Mitsuko kept those thoughts to herself.

She let Zena’s conversation turn from her over to Fuku. The old woman peppered the witch with questions. Everything from brewing recipes to familiar bonding rituals. Mitsuko listened with a moderate amount of interest, though she only understood half of the terms being tossed around.

After some coaching from master brewers, Mitsuko knew it wasn’t a talent she held. She could cook up a lackluster healing potion with enough time and proper ingredients, but it was usually just more effective to purchase what she needed.

The potential dangers of attaching her soul to a familiar while in the Prismatic Spiral also made that section of the conversation less important to her. Still, it was something she might someday do if she ever escaped the archipelago, so she kept half an ear open. Plus, it might still be relevant with her connection to the sages and the Prismatic Spiral.

Her mind wandered to what animal she’d choose if she had to pick a familiar. Maybe a cat like Sterling? Would a different animal synergize better with her temporal spells though?

Realizing her thoughts were meandering, Mitsuko stood up. “As interesting as all this is, I am on a time crunch. Do you have a robe with stripes I can use to access the library?”

“Yes, yes. In the foyer closet,” Zena replied, sounding irritated about the interruption. “However, it will only be three striped. You’ll need to wait a few days before I can gain you access to the Azure Island Vault.”

“Three is enough,” Mitsuko replied. In her previous loop on Amber it had been what got her into the main library. She’d have plenty of other books to read while she waited to dive into Azure specific books. On her way out of the house, Mitsuko grabbed one of the robes and threw it over her current clothing. It was enchanted to keep her cool, so she wasn’t concerned about overheating under it. Plus, she could always just Mend herself if she got uncomfortably warm. She’d done as much for Verna nearly a dozen times already since their departure, to keep the reptile’s shell from melting.

Walking out of the home, Mitsuko took a moment to appreciate the grounds. She wondered if the headstones strewn across the dead grass actually marked graves or if they were just for show.

“Too bad I can’t bring back the dead,” Mitsuko said as she stepped up to the gravestone. “Maybe that’ll be my last Mending level.”

“Don’t hold your breath,” Verna said from within her bag. “Any spells that involve souls are extremely complex. Even the highest levels of necromancy only return a fraction of a soul to a body. And you’re not a soul mage specialist like they are.”

That was basically her spelling out the fact that Mitsuko wouldn’t be achieving resurrection through that spell. A bummer, but Mitsuko had achieved so much power already that she struggled to feel too let down about it. Maybe someday she could team up with a necromancer and combine their specializations.

“You tied your souls to the Prismatic Spiral, so you must have either enlisted a soul mage to help you, or have one among your number,” Mitsuko commented.

“Is that a question?”

“Simply an observation.” If Mitsuko had to guess, she’d say the most likely culprit of soul magic would be on this island, amongst the thousands of scholarly wizards. But she figured she’d learn soon enough.

She left the depressing house behind and strolled into the city. Unlike her first visit, Mitsuko didn’t go out of her way not to create ripples, though she did keep her hood up. She’d met one of the guardian’s acolytes already and he knew her face. There were plenty of people to hide among in the streets, so as long as she wasn’t too obvious and remained on the look-out, she thought she’d be fine.

She silently observed a plethora of interesting characters as she strolled through the streets of Amber Island. An upside down woman walked on her hands in front of her. Thankfully, the wizard’s robes had been enchanted to remain covering her, despite gravity’s pull. A dog-sized duck wore yellow wizard robes with five stripes and hissed at anyone who bumped into him. A man with a yellow octopus for a hat tipped his head at her as they made eye contact. The octopus, likewise, saluted her. A woman who kept flickering with transparency nearly walked right into Mitsuko, clearly unaware of her surroundings.

“It reminds me of one of the more unorthodox mage academies,” Mitsuko reflected. “Part of me thinks I’ll blend in better if I cover myself in flour and dance naked.”

“Do so, and I’ll give you a bonus question!” Verna chimed in.

“You can do that?”

“Nope! But it would be worth any punishment dished out by the others.”

As Misuko moved through the crowd, she periodically cast Dilation. The entire street dipped in activity for those moments, slowing and allowing Mitsuko to dance around them with ease. For any onlookers, it would seem Mitsuko was just enhancing her body with a spell. To be honest, that was exactly what she was doing. Just not with the traditional muscle enhancements most people knew.

The election was easily the biggest talk of the town, trumping even the new forest that had emerged in the middle of the island. Mitsuko passed hundreds of posters for Deelina Zuinn, one of the women in the lead for taking governorship of the island. She also overheard snippets of conversations about the other candidate. After half an hour of getting only bits of information, Mitsuko got frustrated and deviated from her path in an attempt to gather information.

Normally, Mitsuko would go to an inn to pick up gossip; however, Amber Island had another useful means of accomplishing the same thing. She approached a middle-aged man, leaning back in his wooden chair and smoking a pipe. On the table in front of him were stacks of papers, folded and bound up.

“What can I getcha?” he asked, speaking around his pipe. “Newspaper? Sweet drink? Something ta gnaw on?”

“Newspaper.” Mitsuko pulled the doubloons out of her purse and paid.

In return, the man passed her one of the bound up papers and then blew a puff of white smoke from his pipe. Mitsuko watched as it formed into two knights jousting in a circle. The smoke drifted skyward then faded away.

“Knights?” Mitsuko commented. With a tug, she untied the sting that bound the paper and unfurled it. “Bit archaic.”

“We like archaic here,” the newspaper salesman said. “It’s why Fribbolt’s gonna win.”

Mitsuko grunted in acknowledgment and stepped away. She found a bench outside a saloon and unfurled the newspaper. According to what she read, the polls agreed with the man. Out of the ten most popular candidates for the position, Fribbolt was leading by eight percent. His image didn’t match the man who’d attacked her last time on Amber Island, but that didn’t necessarily mean he wasn’t also a puppet of the guardian.

“Sterling mentioned that strong willed individuals were harder to make acolytes,” Mitsuko said, expecting Verna to hear her.

“That’s accurate,” the sage said from inside the bag.

“Wizards are probably stronger willed than children or animals, the usual target of these guardians.”

“Assuming they’re still mentally stable.”

“Mentally unstable people have the strongest wills of any I’ve ever met,” Mitsuko countered.

“An… interesting observation. But I caution you from believing all instability is the same.”

“Fair enough. My question is, can guardians change who they’re targeting as an acolyte?”

“You know that answer is going to cost—”

“Yes,” Mitsuko replied. “Just tell me. It’s important.”

“No. Once their souls are tied to the guardian, they’re locked in for the rest of the Prismatic Spiral. Perhaps if you knew a powerful soul mage you could disconnect them from the cycle, but there would still be nothing stopping the guardian from simply latching onto them again in the next loop. Or worse, it would give the guardian the chance to switch targets. Nothing much to be done about it. My suggestion is to keep the status quo. The only time you’ll ever likely encounter an acolyte not under their guardian’s influence is in the same loop you vanquished them in.” Verna paused. “The exception is someone like the man on your ship. He started outside the island’s influence and Ashen Island’s guardian can only latch onto him once he steps foot on the island.”

Mitsuko nodded, she agreed with that assessment. It matched her experience so far with the acolytes she’d encountered on Ashen Island and Verdant Island. “So, based off that information, I think the one acolyte I met so far is likely the only one on Amber Island.”

“There could be one more. Perhaps two or three if they’re extraordinarily weak willed.”

“The kid who escorted me here was the youngest person I’ve ever seen on the island. And, unless the guardian plans to attack me with sea gulls, animals don’t seem like a viable option.”

“Careful with assumptions,” Verna warned.

Mitsuko’s eyes fell on the bottom left corner of the newspaper, the request section. The wizards were requesting all sorts of odd items, like seaweed and bottled wind. Of course, there was also a wizard currently obsessed with centipedes. It was as if fate was attempting to prove Verna’s point. Mitsuko decided to avoid that section of the island if possible. She hadn’t forgotten her experience with the fiery pests back in her very first loop.

“It says here there’s a political rally at the city center,” Mitsuko noted.

“Risky location if you want to keep a low profile.”

“Hm.” Mitsuko supposed that Verna was right. She folded up the newspaper and they continued on their way to the library. The rally could wait for a future loop.
