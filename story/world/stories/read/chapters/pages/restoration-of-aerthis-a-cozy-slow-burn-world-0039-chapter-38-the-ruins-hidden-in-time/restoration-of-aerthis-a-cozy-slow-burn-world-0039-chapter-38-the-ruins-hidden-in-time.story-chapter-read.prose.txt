The next few days passed without pause.

Whatever comfort the villagers could give, they needed tenfold in return. Elynara woke with the first light, the sound of distant chopping already echoing through the clearing, and worked until the sky bruised purple.

Originally she hoped to go to the ruins straight away.

But the enormity of the tasks at the village had her grounded for longer.

There were no pulleys here, no convenient tools, and only a single cart that could lift things for them. Every task demanded hands and patience.

So Elynara gave both!

With her own little touch of magic.

She shaped flame to thaw frozen buckets before drawing water from the stream, sealing the warmth around each one to keep it from freezing solid on the walk back.

Her warming spell still hummed faintly beneath her cloak, steady and low, just strong enough to keep her fingers from stiffening.

The forest folk spoke little. They worked with a quiet rhythm, gestures replacing words. When they did speak, it was plain and necessary – short nods of approval, murmured thanks, a quick warning when a board slipped or a nail bent.

The stillness wasn’t cold, it was simply efficient.

At dusk they shared bread and thin soup by the communal fire. The broth always smelled faintly of herbs, and the heat of it grounded her after long hours in the wind. It was during these evenings that the talk would shift, always circling back to the same place.

The ruins.

“What did the hunter find there?” she asked one night.

The nearest woman shook her head.

“Not much. Just shapes in the ground.”

Another, older man added,

“The trees grow wrong there. Crooked. Too close together. Their roots drink from things they shouldn’t.”

Elynara frowned.

“Things?”

He didn’t answer right away. The silence filled with the crackle of fire.

“Metal, maybe. Or stone. Rusted through. The ground splits open around them.”

The words sank deep into her thoughts. Rusted shapes beneath the roots. It felt more like their own fears speaking than anything else. And the hunter told her he didn’t get close either.

Though that didn’t mean they were necessarily wrong.

Elynara just didn’t know what to expect of ‘magitech’ ruins to make sure.

Later, as the fire thinned to coals, the elder gave the last warning that stayed with her the most.

“The land there looks ripped apart,” she said. “And the forest moves with it.”

That phrasing clung to Elynara long after the fire went out.

When the hunter returned two days later, his arrival was quiet. He dropped a bundle of snares beside her and crouched near the fire pit. His coat was dusted with frost.

“I can take you halfway there. Beyond that, you’ll need to find your own path. The ground isn’t right. I don’t go past it anymore.”

Elynara nodded, already rolling open her map.

“Halfway is enough.”

He glanced at her sketching, the faint scratch of her pencil against the paper.

“You plan to come back?”

Her smile was small but certain.

“Always do.”

He didn’t argue.

They left at dawn.

Frost rimed the edges of the grass, crunching beneath their boots. The forest rose thin around them, its trunks gray where the bark had split from old weathering. Mist clung low to the ground, sliding between roots.

Starpuff darted ahead, back to its more energetic self. Its faint glow painting soft trails through the cold air.

The hunter led without words, his steps sure and quiet even over uneven ground.

Elynara followed with more trouble, her staff tapping lightly against the earth with each careful stride.

As they climbed a shallow ridge, the land changed. The air grew heavier, the smell sharper, carrying the scent of metal and dust instead of loam. When the wind came through the trees, it carried no warmth.

The first rupture appeared soon after.

A gash through the earth, several paces wide, cutting clean through the forest floor. It ran so deep that even the light failed to reach its bottom. Roots dangled from its edges like frozen veins, still clutching handfuls of dirt. Elynara peered over the rim and caught the faint glimmer of rust far below.

“This used to be solid ground?” she asked quietly.

The hunter nodded.

“Before the quake. Or whatever it was. There are dozens more ahead.”

They crossed on a fallen trunk slick with frost. Starpuff zipped across first, humming impatiently from the far side until she followed. Her boots slipped once, and the puff let out a scandalized trill that made her laugh despite the chill.

“Alright, alright,” she said. “I’m fine.”

Beyond the first cut, the forest turned stranger.

The trees leaned at sharp angles, their roots half exposed, their trunks twisted from strain. Whole patches of earth had sunk lower than the rest, creating shallow hollows where water froze in still pools. Every few steps, the ground creaked softly underfoot, the sound of strain deep within the soil.

By midmorning they had reached a cluster of trenches intersecting at odd angles. Some were narrow enough to jump, others gaping and jagged. The land looked torn, pulled apart by hands too large to imagine.

Elynara traced a line of faint mana through the air, but it frayed and disappeared almost at once. The pull of the Deadzone was already reaching this far, despite not being anywhere in sight.

The hunter stopped at the edge of a wider chasm. His expression didn’t change, but his shoulders eased – as though ending a long obligation.

“This is where I turn back,” he said.

Elynara looked across the broken terrain. The horizon glowed faintly pale beyond the trees, the color of dust.

“You’re sure this path reaches the ruins?”

“They lie straight ahead,” he said. “If you keep heading ahead, the forest will open to them. You’ll know the place when you see it.”

She nodded.

“Thank you.”

He gave a brief nod in return. His eyes drifted once toward the pale distance.

“The air changes from here. You’ll feel it.”

“I’ll manage.”

He seemed to accept that. With one last glance, he turned and retraced his steps, soon fading between the leaning trees.

Elynara watched until his figure disappeared entirely. The silence he left behind pressed close, broken only by the faint hum of Starpuff circling near her shoulder.

“Well,” she said, drawing her cloak tighter, “looks like it’s just us again.”

The puff chirped, spinning once in acknowledgment before floating ahead, toward the pale light filtering through the crooked trunks.

Elynara followed, her boots crunching over frost and brittle leaves. Each step carried her deeper into the quiet, into the strange rhythm of a world that had not yet finished breaking.

And ahead, somewhere beyond the next ridge, the ruins waited.

The forest swallowed the trail within minutes. Trees leaned in from both sides, their roots tearing through the soil like old bones breaking the surface.

Each step sank into uneven ground that slanted when it shouldn’t, rose where it hadn’t before. Elynara pressed forward, breath steady but slow. The air was colder here, heavy with the damp scent of moss and distant rot.

“Would’ve been nice if flying was an option,” she muttered, planting her staff against a slick ridge and hauling herself up.

Starpuff chirped overhead, spinning a smug little circle before darting ahead through the branches.

“Oh, hush,” she said, brushing mud from her glove. “Not all of us are puffs of glowing air.”

The puff bobbed once in victory and zipped onward.

The slope dropped again two steps later, forcing her to brace against a fallen trunk before sliding down the loose soil. Her boots dug furrows as she landed at the base, breath fogging. She considered using a burst of flame to clear the worst of the debris, just a quick strike to burn away the tangle of roots and mud in her path.

Tempting, but wasteful. She could do it once or thrice. But there was a lot of roots and room to cover. Her capacity wasn’t quite enough for that.

Besides, it would make a mess.

“Fine,” she sighed. “Manual labor it is.”

She climbed instead. The trunk groaned under her weight but held, bark slick and soft from years of rot. At the top she paused, scanning ahead. The forest stretched endlessly, the light dim and filtered through a ceiling of tangled branches.

It should have felt enclosed, but there was too much space between the trees – unnatural gaps where nothing grew.

The silence pressed.

And just when she finally stopped to drink, the ambush came.

Movement snapped through her peripheral vision. A shape unfolded from beneath an upturned root, thorned limbs scraping the dirt. Reflex overtook thought. Her staff came up, and flame erupted in a short, harsh burst.

The creature reeled back, shrieking, its edges hissing into smoke.

Another lunged from her flank.

She pivoted, swept her staff around, and sent her trusted explosion spell forward in a tight ball that hit it mid-air. One moment it was mid-lunge, and the next it was just a spray of burnt shards of bark.

Silence returned just as fast.

The smell of burnt bark filled her lungs. She exhaled slowly, pulse settling.

“Lovely welcome.”

Starpuff hovered above her, tiny light flickering in disapproval.

“Don’t start,” she said, crouching to examine the scorch marks. The ground was torn and blackened, but beneath it she found what the creatures had hidden – roots that bled faint sap, faint traces of red veins winding through the soil.

She leaned closer.

“Mana-rich,” she murmured. “That explains the pests.”

Though not how the plant had survived so close to the Deadzone.

But she supposed while it was close, it was no Deadzone yet.

Still, Elynara made sure to take a small sample, and draw the plant out in her notebook.

Maybe somebody back in Oltenar would be able to help her identify what it is.

Starpuff smacked her right in the cheek then.

Of course it did, that rascal!

“Drawing bores you, is that it?”

It trilled, unrepentant.

“Hmph, no appreciation for fine art!” – not that her ‘art’ counted as ‘fine’.

She stared her puff down.

It stared back, bobbing up and down.

“Fine, you win this round, let’s go.”

It flew ahead the instant she moved. Elynara just shook her head. Really, her puff companion wanted excitement and attention in equal measures!

She followed after it anyway.

Further along the ridge, small clusters of herbs had pushed through the cracks. Red-veined leaves, pale blossoms streaked with gray.

Elynara plucked a few and laid them flat across her palm. She didn’t need her notes this time, her time studying in the library and asking Alicia for information coming in handy.

“Moonveil, froststem, and that new one…” She paused, smiling faintly. “Progress. Maybe I’ll even figure out how to use them before next century.”

Because yes, she could recognize them, but knew not their uses yet.

Starpuff bobbed in cheerful agreement.

She tucked the herbs carefully into a cloth wrap and stood. The forest around her had grown darker the deeper she went, yet something in the air felt stronger.

The dull, hollow pressure of the Deadzone no longer clung to her skin. Mana here was thicker, faint but steady, threading through the air in quiet resistance.

She could feel it – life pushing back.

It surprised her enough to make her stop and focus. She reached outward with her senses. The flow was uneven but present, moving beneath the roots in slow pulses.

The forest certainly wasn’t thriving, but it endured. Its rhythm was different, and defiant, refusing to surrender even when the rest of the world thinned.

Her heart lifted at the thought.

“So you’re still fighting,” she whispered.

The puff hummed softly, mirroring her tone.

She followed the current through the trees, letting it guide her forward. The light dimmed further, replaced by faint glimmers in the shadows – tiny motes caught between branches, drifting like dust. At first she thought they were insects, but no.

They were like little clumps of mana…?

But of the type Starpuff didn’t seem eager to nibble at.

She brushed a few with her hand. They broke apart on contact, scattering harmlessly across her sleeve.

Whatever they were, one thing was certain,

The forest was trying to heal itself.

A sense of quiet pride stirred in her chest. If even this place, pressed against the edge of nothing, could still hold remnants of life, then there was hope.

Her boots caught on another incline, dragging her back to focus. The ground trembled faintly beneath her heel. She stilled, staff rising. No ambush followed this time, only the slow creak of roots settling deeper into the earth.

The motion continued for a heartbeat and stopped.

“Unstable,” she murmured, glancing back. The landscape shifted subtly under her gaze. Fallen trees leaned in directions that didn’t match gravity, hollows deepened where her footprints hadn’t been moments before.

It wasn’t just the ground that had moved. The forest itself shifted in tiny, continuous adjustments, refusing stillness.

She understood then what the villagers meant when they said the land looked ripped apart.

Whatever set the ground in motion wasn’t quite done yet.

Elynara took another careful breath and pressed forward, her staff finding the rhythm of her steps again. The hum of mana beneath her feet steadied with it. Starpuff trailed close, quiet now, watching the strange motion of branches above them.

They passed through another clearing where the light pooled faintly on the ground. She knelt and pressed her hand against the soil. Warmth met her touch, faint but unmistakable.

“Still alive,” she said softly. “You’re just tired.”

The air stirred in answer, brushing her cheek, carrying a single falling leaf across her hand. She smiled at the simple gesture and rose.

“Come on,” she said, glancing toward the puff. “Let’s see what else you’re hiding.”

It trilled once and darted ahead through the narrow path.

Elynara followed, the soft hum of her magic echoing faintly in the distance, the promise of the ruins waiting somewhere beyond the next fold of shifting ground.

The forest broke without warning. One last curtain of tangled brush gave way, and Elynara stepped through – then stopped cold.

The world ahead didn’t belong to any map or book she had studied.

Metal rose where stone should have been. The ground dipped into a shallow valley filled with shapes that weren’t natural and weren’t ruins in any sense she understood.

Towers, or what remained of them, tilted at broken angles, ribs of rust and steel cutting through soil and moss. Cables as thick as her wrist draped between them like vines, coiling into shadows.

She took one slow breath. The air smelled faintly of dust and oil, sharp even after all these years.

Her boots slid over the edge of a collapsed ridge, gravel crunching. She steadied herself and let her gaze travel. The nearest structure was a block of fused metal and cracked glass, half-swallowed by earth. Beyond it, a tangle of beams formed something like a bridge suspended in midair, leading nowhere.

“Not old stonework, that’s for sure.” she murmured. Her memories suggesting a word for what she was seeing here.

Machinery.

Starpuff floated forward, its faint glow reflected in the broken glass. It turned slow circles, curious but wary, the sound of its hum small against the heavy stillness.

Elynara followed the faint trail of an ancient road that wound through the wreckage. The pattern of it still showed through the dirt – lines too even to be natural, edges that caught light. She traced one with her boot. The material felt strange, not stone, not metal, but something between.

And despite feeling ancient, it still screamed to her of higher tech than whatever Oltenar could possibly offer.

A part of her even wanted to rush forward, to see everything at once!

Another part – the one that had learned patience from long nights of failure – forced her to slow down. She raised her staff and focused on her Mana Sense. The mana flared, thin and shallow, then faded again. The current was weak here but not gone.

Nothing dangerous she could detect, at least.

But that didn’t make her drop her vigilance. Just about anything could be hiding out there. And she would be ready.

The puff bobbed close to her cheek in quiet agreement.

Elynara started walking. Each step echoed faintly, dull where the soil had softened, sharp when she crossed patches of metal.

The structures grew larger the deeper she went. Some were intact enough to hint at their design: long corridors bent into the earth, round chambers lined with frames that once held glass or crystal. Her fingers brushed the surface of one, and the texture startled her—it was smooth and cool beneath the dust, even after years of exposure.

She smiled faintly.

“So you were built to last.”

A part of her memory stirred then, small and unhelpful. Fragments of the old world, from long before she was born again, tried to arrange themselves into meaning. Sparks of familiarity without shape. The way light once fell on tall windows. The hum of machines that listened to their users.

But the images faded before they could become anything solid.

Her hand dropped to her side.

“You would think having lived through one age of wonders would make this easier,” she said, half to herself. The half-remembered glimpses often just added to confusion instead.

The forest wind didn’t care to answer her complaints.

She tried to make sense of the memories still. Yet every detail she recognized brought two more that refused to fit.

The towers were built with logic she couldn’t follow. The lines between function and ornament blurred into something more deliberate.

Even decay had order here.

She stopped beside a collapsed archway, crouching to examine a panel half-buried in the soil. The metal was thinner, the pattern across it etched in looping designs that shimmered faintly when the light hit it just right.

She brushed more dirt away. And, in the spur of the moment, inserted a tiny bit of mana into it. The lines pulsed once under her touch – faint but alive.

Oh!

Her breath caught.

The network of tiny patterns shimmered before stopping. Was that magitech?

Or just the material they used at the time?

She didn’t know, but at least it confirmed what Alicia had guessed.

Starpuff trilled, wings fluttering fast in excitement.

“Yes, I see it too,” she said, laughing softly. “Don’t start glowing too bright, you’ll make them jealous.”

The puff spun once and dimmed obediently.

Elynara rose and stepped carefully over a cracked beam. The valley opened wider now, the far side filled with shapes more complex – curving pillars, a half-buried dome with shattered windows that still caught light in their edges. For a moment, the reflection looked almost intentional, as if the ruin wanted to remind her what it had once been.

She could almost see it whole. The towers upright and humming, the roads alive with color, the air thick with energy that had once bent to will and purpose.

Her fingers tightened around the staff.

“You must have been beautiful,” she said softly.

A gust of wind cut across the valley, stirring dust into thin spirals. The sound that followed was faint but sharp – a metallic groan as one of the structures shifted slightly under its own weight. It faded as quickly as it came.

Elynara stayed still, watching until the last echo vanished. Then she smiled again, smaller this time.

“You’re holding on,” she said. “Even after everything else let go.”

The puff settled on her shoulder, its warmth a small, steady comfort.

She pulled her notebook from the satchel and began sketching the ridge line, marking the layout of towers and paths. Her handwriting stayed quick but even, each detail measured and clear.

The work steadied her.

When she closed the book, twilight had begun to thicken between the towers. The last light stretched long across the metal, catching in broken glass until it looked almost whole again.

Elynara lowered herself to a piece of fallen beam and sat. Her body ached pleasantly from the climb, her hands dirty, her mind alive.

She tilted her head back, studying the faint glow that clung to the edges of the valley. Even this far from the Deadzone, the air carried its strange stillness. The thought struck her that whatever force had consumed the world might have started here – or ended here.

Or at least had been part of the reason. Or an accidental casualty, a consequence of other events.

But that mystery would wait until morning. For now, discovery was enough.

She drew the warmth spell across her cloak and watched it bloom faintly through the fabric, a soft pulse against the cooling air.

Below her, the ruins glimmered faintly in the dark, half-buried but found anew.

Elynara smiled into the quiet.

“Let’s see what you’re still hiding.”

The puff chirped once, sharp and eager.

The valley seemed to listen.

The deeper Elynara moved into the ruins, the less the air felt like air. It pressed close, damp and heavy, tasting faintly of copper and dust. The faint lines of what she assumed was part of some old magitech underfoot pulsed faintly now and then with dying light, threads of energy barely able to cause a flicker in something that likely used to be far more impressive.

Starpuff hovered low near her shoulder, its glow muted to a dim green that flickered with each pulse from the floor.

The corridors here had shape but no logic Elynara could discern. Angled halls that ended in walls, doorframes that opened into nothing.

Either the builders did things without rhyme or reason, or whatever used to be inside was gone now.

Whatever the case, Elynara could only move forward, hoping to find something that would make some semblance of sense.

Every few steps she caught herself whispering small observations, half for focus and half for company.

“The same alloy as before,” she murmured, brushing a finger along a corroded wall. “Still holds mana. If barely.”

The puff hummed faint acknowledgment.

The sound that came after had her stiffening up.

At first it was faint, too low for words to form from it. A dry scratch, the rhythm too deliberate to be wind. She stopped, tilting her head toward the noise.

“Did you hear–”

The next sound cut her off. A wet chitter. Then another.

Elynara spun toward the corridor behind her, staff raising instinctively. The faint flicker of her flame spell painted long shadows across the walls. One of them twitched – and moved.

The creature that crawled into view didn’t belong to the forest above. Its carapace gleamed translucent under the light, veins of pale blue blood running beneath its shell. Two eyes blinked wetly from the wrong side of its head.

“Oh, perfect,” she breathed.

The thing hissed, and more answered from the dark. Claws scraped, echoing along the metal floor. A second creature emerged, then a third, their bodies sliding over one another in twitching rhythm. The glow under their skin pulsed faster, feeding on the light.

A monster nest.

Of course there had to be one, this was all prime real estate!

Pushing her idle thoughts aside, her staff flared before thought even caught up. The first firebolt struck center mass, the burst throwing heat through the hall. The lead creature shattered, ichor hissing as it hit the ground. Smoke rose in thick coils.

The next wave came through it, undeterred.

Elynara pivoted, spinning the staff in one smooth motion. In a burst of inspiration, she made water gather at its tip, spiraling down to coat the floor. Her focus sharp just as it hit. Frost bloomed instantly, spreading thin ice beneath their feet. The nearest creatures slipped, claws scraping for purchase.

She didn’t give them time to recover. A sharp twist of her wrist sent a powered up explosion spell right into the heap of bodies. The resulting boom was followed by pitter-patter of falling pieces, and the air filled with the shriek of boiling moisture, the mist thick enough to sting her throat.

Still, while that cleared the passage, more still pressed forward, coming out of their hidey holes.

“Persistent things,” she muttered.

She stepped back, eyes scanning for escape. But there were none she deemed ‘good enough’.

The corridor curved sharply to her right, ending in what might have been a collapsed support column. The ceiling above her sagged, the metal straining with every vibration of the creatures’ movement.

An idea sparked. Risky. Effective.

“Alright,” she said under her breath. “You asked for it.”

She slammed the staff into the ground, channeling heat upward in a concentrated surge. Fire rushed out in a wide arc, catching the nearest beam. The metal glowed red, groaned, and cracked.

The ceiling gave way.

The sound that followed was more felt than heard – a heavy, shuddering collapse that rippled through her chest. The shock knocked her backward, the force throwing dust and heat into the air. For a heartbeat, the world disappeared into smoke and falling grit.

When the echo faded, silence followed.

Elynara coughed, brushing dust from her hair. Her shoulders ached, and something sharp poked at her ribs through the cloak, but nothing felt broken.

She blinked until her vision steadied.

Starpuff hovered anxiously above the rubble, its glow darting back and forth in tight circles.

“I’m fine,” she rasped. The words came rough but certain. “Mostly.”

The puff trilled sharply, not convinced.

“Next time, warn me before the monsters start surging in.”

The tone was scolding but light. The puff spun a sulky loop and chirped again, softer this time.

“No, I’m not mad, you’re still a lovable puff!”

It flew right into her hands at that, nuzzling up to her, before zooming away to find whatever other fun things to explore.

Elynara laughed once, the sound catching in her throat but easing the air.

She leaned her staff against her shoulder and surveyed the wreckage. The passage ahead was completely sealed by the fallen beam, the nest buried under a heap of twisted metal and stone. Steam curled between the cracks, trailing up to the fractured ceiling.

Her fire had left dark streaks along the walls, mingling with older burns that time had carved there.

Whatever this place had been, it had known destruction long before her arrival.

She straightened slowly, stretching her back.

“They were right about the air changing,” she said to no one in particular, remembering the hunter’s words. “Didn’t say it would start hissing at me, though.”

The puff buzzed in mild agreement, drifting toward a narrow gap between two columns.

The corridor beyond sloped down into silence.

Every step she took stirred old dust that glimmered faintly in the pale light of her staff. The air was warmer here, thick with the weight of years.

The walls were not uniform. They curved and bent in ways no mason would choose, though whether by choice or warped by years, she could not tell.

In places they were covered in faint lines that caught the glow of her light. Symbols, maybe – half-melted and warped by heat or time. She brushed her glove across one, tracing the shallow grooves. The material beneath her hand wasn’t stone or metal, but something that felt in between, too smooth to crumble, too soft to ring.

It hummed faintly when she pressed harder. The sound made her pause.

“Still functional down here,” she murmured.

Starpuff floated close, its glow rippling green across the marks. The faint hum of its wings blended with the quiet vibration in the walls, and for a strange moment, it felt like the place was answering. Then the hum faded again, leaving only still air.

“Right. Noted. Moving on!”

She adjusted her cloak and walked deeper. The corridor opened gradually, the floor widening until her light began to scatter ahead instead of rebound. She felt the shift before she saw it – the space pulling wider, the air loosening in her lungs.

The next chamber was vast.

Half-collapsed towers of machinery rose through the center, girders and broken platforms leaning at strange angles. Cables hung from the ceiling like roots, their lengths twisted together in sagging clusters.

The soft blue pulse she had seen earlier flickered here and there among them, faint as breath.

Her footsteps echoed, carrying farther than she expected.

And yet, it was…

“Empty,” she said under her breath, but even saying it felt foolish. No place this big stayed empty forever.

The first sound came from the far end – small, quick scratches that multiplied faster than thought. She froze, listening. The noise spread, layers of movement building over each other until it filled the space.

A single wet chitter rolled through the dark. Then dozens answered.

Elynara spun, staff raised, mana already gathering at her fingertips.

The first shape lunged from behind a collapsed beam, its shell gleaming with faint blue veins that pulsed under the light.

It was larger than the ones before, its limbs thicker, claws serrated. Then more followed, crawling through the wreckage in a coordinated surge.

“Of course,” she breathed. “Another nest of those weird bugs.”

Her first explosion of fire hit the front line hard enough to blow up quite a few and stagger the rest, leaving them reeling. She shifted from explosion to fire, the twin surges of flame striking the stragglers precisely, one by one. The sounds of shells cracking echoed through the chamber.

Heat rolled across her face, forcing her to squint through the haze.

And yet they didn’t stop coming.

Bodies clambered over their fallen brethren, claws striking sparks from the floor. She struck again, another explosion hitting the gathering surge, wiping out another group. The air filled with shrieks of the survivors and the smell of burning shell.

A pair broke through, too fast. She dropped back behind a fallen girder, heart pounding, forcing her breathing steady. Her hands moved automatically, drawing another surge of flame into her palm. She cast it forward, ending the approaching creatures before they had any chance of doing damage.

Still they pressed closer.

“Persistent,” she muttered through clenched teeth.

Starpuff zoomed over just as she began shaping another spell, insistedly poking at the mana construct. A split second was all it took Elynara to trust it.

And as soon as the mana was released from her control, it shot past her shoulder in a sudden flare of light, darting through the chaos. It zipped between the creatures, flashing bright bursts that left them hissing and disoriented. The momentary confusion was enough to give her space.

Her eyes sparkled at the display, even as she readied another spell.

“Amazing.” she called out.

Her little friend was growing up!

The puff chirped, its glow flickering brighter in proud defiance.

Elynara took one glance around the girder. The floor beyond the nest was fractured, a wide crack running under the support arch at the far end of the hall. The beam above it sagged under strain, already weakened by age and fire.

Her focus narrowed.

“Alright,” she whispered. “You asked for it.”

She drew in mana from her core with every thread she could reach, forcing the channels in her arms open until heat burned under her skin. The air itself began to shake from the build-up.

“Now!” she shouted.

The puff darted higher as she slammed her staff into the cracked arch. Fire surged through the metal, filling the fractures with red light. For a breathless moment, the structure held.

Then it gave.

The sound tore through the hall – metal screaming, stone snapping, the collapse crashing down in a roar that drowned everything else. Elynara ducked behind the girder, arm over her head.

When the shock faded, the world slowly went still.

She stayed there, waiting for movement. None came. The air hissed with settling steam, a low crackle of cooling stone.

Elynara coughed, each breath tasting of smoke. Dust drifted down like ash, catching the edge of her staff. The ache in her chest pulsed with her heartbeat.

Starpuff floated down through the haze, its glow dim but steady, tracing small circles above her.

“I’m fine,” she said, though her voice came rough. She pushed herself upright, wincing as her knees protested. “Mostly.”

The puff trilled, unimpressed.

“Yes, you’re allowed to worry,” she said, smiling weakly. “But that was your cue to warn me, remember?”

It tilted, indignant, then settled on her shoulder, poking her for the reminder.

Her laugh came quiet but genuine.

“Alright, alright. I’ll give you half credit.”

She took a slow breath and surveyed the damage. The hall was unrecognizable – rubble buried the far side, molten streaks cooling across the floor. The faint blue veins in the walls flickered uncertainly before fading out.

But at least the nest was gone.

She lowered her staff, letting the magic ease from her fingers. Fatigue rolled through her in a warm, heavy wave, but satisfaction cut through it just as quickly.

“Good work,” she whispered, reaching up to tap the puff’s side. “Really good work.”

It chirped again, softer now, leaning against her neck.

The silence that followed was clean this time.

Elynara leaned back against the broken girder, the faint hum of her warmth spell keeping the chill at bay. Around her, the ruins settled, their slow breathing returning. Somewhere deeper inside, another light blinked to life, faint and waiting.

“Rest first,” she murmured. “Then we see what else survived.”

The puff’s glow dimmed in answer.
