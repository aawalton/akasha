Phillus, the Great Stag, stood mighty across the Lightless Spring. Every part of his body still glowed, pulsing with that polychromatic light. At any moment, it seemed as though he could unleash another blast of power.

Perri flew closer to the edge of the spring but stopped before crossing over.

“Phillus. We aren’t here to fight.”

The stag lowered his enormous head slightly. His eyes were a deep green, reflecting the faint light of the spring as his gaze moved past her and settled on Veronica and the others.

It wasn’t a polite stare.

“And yet you bring outsiders into my domain.”

“They’re with me.”

“I don’t recall whether that matters. My domain, my rules. They are not welcome here.”

Perri frowned. “They are here to help. They want to break the seal on the castle and free the others.”

At that, Phillus’ expression changed. The light running across his body brightened.

“I’ve told you, Perripippa. We will not open the castle’s seal. The Last Gold is too dangerous to let out.”

Her brows furrowed. “It’s been nearly four hundred years! The Last Gold might already be dead! The others could still be in there, waiting for us to help them!”

Everyone around Veronica still had their weapons drawn. She kept her mana primed as well, though she lowered her barrier for the moment. “You’re the one who sealed the Last Gold inside the castle, correct?”

Phillus turned toward her. “I am.”

“Then you must know quite a bit about him, right?”

The stag’s gaze remained fixed on her, but he didn’t respond.

“Perri told us that some of your friends are still trapped inside the castle. That none of you know whether they’re safe, and that the Last Gold may still be alive. Is that right?”

Again, no answer.

“We can break the seal on the castle and check inside for you. We just need to know what we’ll be up against. Perri told us that you have the body of one of the dead Asvarians with you and—”

At that, Perri whipped around and frantically waved both arms at her.

Phillus glanced toward the fairy. “You were spying on us? How many times have I told you not to enter my territory? You should have been hiding in Faewood at that time.”

Perri turned back to face him with a gulp. She chuckled nervously. “Haha… um…” A few beats of silence passed. “Alright, I don’t have an excuse. I did sneak out that day and saw you take one of the bodies.”

Phillus huffed. “I’ll deal with you later.” His attention returned to Veronica. “You will not be getting that body, nor will you be breaking the castle’s seal. Regardless of what the fairy says, everyone knew what they were getting involved in. We all understood the sacrifices. We made our choice.”

“Are you planning on never opening the castle again, then?”

The light across his body flickered. “I sealed that place under Morgan’s instructions. The gold ones are too dangerous. We cannot risk the Last Gold escaping. The decision to open the castle once more is mine and mine alone. The warriors who sacrificed their lives inside paid the price of necessity. Without them, the entire island might have been destroyed, razed to dirt.”

“Don’t say that like my father is already dead!” Perri shouted. “It was your decision for everyone to act as bait. You could have been the one to lure him inside and seal yourself in with him. But no, you were too much of a coward to go by yourself, so you forced everyone else to act for you!”

The atmosphere around the Lightless Spring changed. Every path of light across the stag’s body glowed brighter, nearly overflowing.

The sound of shifting metal and weapons reached Veronica’s ears. Her own mana coiled tighter as well, ready to unleash an attack.

Phillus’ expression darkened. His voice no longer carried that low calm, replaced by restrained anger. He took a single step forward, and the earth trembled beneath his hoof.

“Your father knew exactly what would happen if the Last Gold escaped. Every one of them did. Had I entered with them, there was a chance I would die, yes. In order to win, someone had to remain outside. That role fell upon me. If it hadn’t, then someone else would have carried that burden.”

Perri opened her mouth, but nothing came out. Instead, her fingers curled as she clenched her teeth.

The Great Stag stood taller, looking down at her from across the water. “You may hate me for surviving, Perripippa. But you hold no more hatred than I do remorse and regret. I despise myself for what happened more than you ever could.”

His gaze hardened. “I will not dishonor the sacrifice we have upheld for the past four centuries simply because you are impatient.” He turned toward Veronica and the others. “You may enter the castle. However, only after five thousand more years have passed. That should be enough to confirm the Last Gold has died.”

Veronica glared at him. “Sorry. That won’t work for us. None of us will live that long. We need to get in whether you permit it or not. And we’ll be needing that body you’re keeping.”

The demons would invade in twelve years. Everything would be destroyed.

Avalon was a treasure trove of opportunity, spoken and gossiped about even among eighth- and ninth-tier mages and martial masters. She couldn’t simply walk away because some ancient guardian believed waiting was safer.

The Great Stag faced them fully. He took another step forward, and the earth trembled again, sending ripples across the surface of the Lightless Spring. “You certainly have confidence.”

Nasha pulled an arrow from her quiver and rested it against her bowstring. “Get ready, everyone. He’s coming.”

“Hide behind the girl with the staff,” Orban told Theo. He drew his hammer and gripped the handle tightly. His expression had turned serious; even he could tell the stag in front of them was no pushover.

“M-master, are we sure we need to do this? We aren’t really involved, right?” Theo asked.

The dwarf leaned forward and tightened his grip on his metal shield. “Probably not,” he said. Then he grinned. “But a legendary forge from another dimension? I don’t know what else could convince a dwarf more.”

Theo frowned, but quickly made his way behind Gwen. He wasn’t a fighter.

“W-wait! You all don’t need to fight!” Perri exclaimed. She looked between the two sides, but it seemed no one was listening to her.

Phillus tilted his head upward. Light shimmered across his antlers, intensifying until it gathered into a ball in front of him. “If you won’t take no for an answer, I suppose I’ll have to make you leave.”

Veronica didn’t waste any time. She threw her hand forward and projected a full wall of mana in front of the group just as another polychromatic beam erupted from Phillus.

The impact struck like she was holding back a massive boulder.

Veronica’s feet dug into the dirt as the barrier shook, pushing both it and her backward. Tiny cracks appeared across its surface, but it held. After reaching the fourth tier, her power had increased immensely. A third-tier barrier wouldn’t have been enough.

That meant the Great Stag was a considerable opponent.

“Everyone, spread out!” Veronica shouted, glancing back without focusing on anyone in particular. “I’ll draw his attention. You guys try to take him down!”

She didn’t need to repeat the command. Everyone moved at once. Nasha veered left with her bow, dashing past the wall of light and into the trees. Orban ran right, keeping his shield close in case he was struck by a stray beam. Gwen and Theo both retreated several steps.

Gwen began casting her healing spells, applying their effects first to Veronica, then to the others. She only needed to cast each spell once, as their effects would linger for several minutes. Once they were set, she could focus on offense.

As the remaining energy from Phillus’ beam scattered, Veronica twisted her hands, lowered the barrier, and formed another spell. With her palms facing one another, she created a large, perfectly spherical ball of fire. It was a second-tier spell, but she poured far more mana into it than normal, using her new Overload perk.

Just as Sage had explained, Veronica supplied the mana while Sage expanded the equation to contain the excess.

The spell fully formed in only a few seconds.

Phillus’ eyes narrowed as he saw the flame, and the light across his body flickered once more.

Veronica thrust her hands forward, sending the compressed ball of fire shooting across the clearing.

Before the attack could reach him, Phillus gathered more of that colorful light and released it outward. This time, however, he didn’t form it into a beam. Instead, the light spread across his body like a thin membrane.

Veronica’s fireball crashed against him, erupting into a massive explosion. The trees behind Phillus caught fire, sending smoke and ash into the sky.

However, just as quickly as the flames erupted, they died away. A gust of wind burst from Phillus’ body as his strange power dispersed the fire.

Well… a second-tier spell is still a second-tier spell, no matter how much mana I pour into it, she thought.

Two arrows tore through the clearing, aimed straight at the Great Stag’s front legs. He noticed them just in time and reared back, causing both shots to miss.

At the same time, Orban had run around the spring and closed the distance with his shield raised. Phillus swung his head, trying to catch the dwarf with his antlers, but Orban blocked the strike. He immediately countered, swinging his hammer into the stag’s leg.

A loud clang reverberated across the spring, like metal striking solid wood.

Orban smirked as the harsh resistance vibrated up his arm. “Damn body’s hard as steel, huh?”

Phillus applied more pressure and hurled him several meters backward before he could follow up.

As Orban landed back on his feet, he glanced down at his arm, feeling a dull heat spreading through it. Then he looked over his shoulder and spotted Gwen pointing her staff toward him. The ache was already fading beneath her healing spell.

He gave her a quick nod before turning back toward the Great Stag. That was when he noticed the fairy hovering above Phillus’ antlers.

“Phillus! Stop this already!” Perri shouted, having finally crossed the spring.

The stag glanced at her but quickly ignored her. He gave Orban no time to recover. One of his front hooves slammed into the ground, and a pulse of energy cracked across the earth, sending polychromatic light racing toward the dwarf in a web-like pattern.

“Two can play that game!” Orban shouted.

He raised his hammer and slammed it into the ground. Instead of answering with another wave of energy, the impact shattered the earth completely, sending a shockwave of loosened stone toward Phillus’ attack.

The broken ground swallowed the incoming light, exploding into chunks of dirt and rock when the two forces collided.

Orban jumped aside and thrust his shield forward. The earth beside him rumbled before a massive stone fist broke through the surface, rising high into the air and arcing toward Phillus.

The Great Stag stared up at it. “So you use the power of spirits, outsider.”

His eyes narrowed, though he didn’t look concerned.

Massive roots exploded upward from the ground beside him, reaching toward the stone fist. Each one wrapped around its knuckles before trailing down the wrist and arm, suspending the earthen construct in midair and stopping it cold.

The remaining roots snaked toward Orban, wrapping around his legs and chest and pinning him in place. He punched and tore at them, but they were too thick. He was slowly breaking free, but it would take time.

“Damn plants,” he grunted.

Just as Phillus turned toward Veronica, who now had a spell circle formed in front of her, a sharp pain struck his front leg. He looked down. An arrow had pierced straight through his front-right knee. Red aura clung to the shaft, slowly dissipating into the air.

Phillus grunted and looked toward the trees. The archer had already vanished from sight. The shot had been so quiet and precise that he hadn’t even noticed it until he placed weight on the injured leg.

That was when he realized Perri had disappeared as well. His gaze swept across the clearing, but neither of them was anywhere to be seen. His attention was quickly pulled away when Veronica shouted at him from across the water.

“Hey! This might kill you, so I suggest you surrender now!”
