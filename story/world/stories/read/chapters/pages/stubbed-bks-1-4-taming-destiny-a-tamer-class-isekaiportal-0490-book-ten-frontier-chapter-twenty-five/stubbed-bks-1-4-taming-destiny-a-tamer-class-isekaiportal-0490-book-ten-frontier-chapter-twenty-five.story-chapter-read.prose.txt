Gulnis’ offer hangs in the air. I stay silent for now. Elarion was right to be wary – keeping the merchants guessing at this point in the game is probably a good idea.

If Gulnis can give us an idea of who has landed where, that is indeed useful information. However, it’s also nothing that we can’t find out for ourselves in time, so I’m not willing to trade anything I consider truly valuable.

Gold, perhaps. We have enough of it for now after taking so much from Kyrian and his two allies. On the other hand, we may need it to pay our settlers.

Or perhaps…

Elarion, I know that you’ve only just discovered a method for growing edible food, but do you think we could grow enough to resupply the merchant with grains and root vegetables?

Elarion considers it for a long moment.

Depending on how long you intend to provide them rations for, and how many people are on board the ship, I estimate it would take at least two or three days to grow sufficient food to resupply them with our current methods. However, that relies on the alchemists producing sufficient quantities of the necessary potions.

Alright, thanks, I tell him. I can work with this, because surely food is something they need – or will need.

The merchant leans forward again before I can speak to make the offer. The silence seems to have got to him. Good – perhaps he’ll reveal something we can use.

“My lord, I know that you are a talented Tamer,” Gulnis starts, his admiring and appreciative tone immediately putting me on edge. “I know multiple groups who would appreciate a beast or dozen to aid in their defences.”

Clearly he hasn’t heard that much about me, I conclude grimly, forcing my instinctive reaction under control, to think that I would be willing to sell my companions like objects. And to start the negotiations with this!

Despite my attempt to control myself, the way the three merchants flinch subtly indicates that they have read my displeasure somehow.

“Or perhaps you have found some natural resource to exploit,” Gulnis hurries on. “This continent is clearly rich in many such treasures. You could establish yourself as a trading post for your specific resource and ensure that no one supplants you for a while yet.”

He cuts himself off, a single bead of sweat trickling down his forehead. Not nearly as calm and collected as he’s trying to appear, then.

I wonder what I should do next – this is the first time I’ve negotiated with a merchant like this. I consider passing the discussion over to Layton – a lord who I’ve personally seen is practised in dealing with merchants.

Then I steel myself and decide against it. Sending someone else to negotiate, especially in the first meeting, has connotations I’m not keen on giving. That I feel myself above such an interaction, or that I’m only nominally the head of the settlement.

But there’s nothing saying I can’t get advice, and our Bond is a perfect solution to that.

Should I let him stew or make an offer? I ask everyone in the Alliance Bond. It’s a pity that Sarran isn’t connected to my network in any way – his viewpoint would be very useful too. We could provide food, or I could offer gold.

Your approach has been good so far, Layton reassures me. Better to remain silent than offer too much.

We need to find out more about what is valuable to them before we make an offer, Valence agrees, echoing my own thoughts earlier. Otherwise, we will find ourselves disadvantaged in the trade. We don’t know whether food or gold is more important for those he’s trading with at this point.

Thanks, I tell them gratefully. Alright, gather more information it is.

I lean back in my chair, allowing my finger to tap casually on its carven wooden arm and regarding him with as much coolness as I can muster.

“How many other settlements have you traded with so far?”

Gulnis shoots a look at Rithan when it looks like he might speak, then returns to me with a polite smile on his face. I see his refusal before he verbally gives it.

“Such information is certainly something we could trade, however, simply giving it…” He trails off with an expressive wave of his hand and saddened shake of his head.

“Call it a friendly gesture towards trading with us,” I suggest, leaning forward with a smile that shows the hint of teeth. “After all, how else can we make an offer that will be valuable to your other apparent trading partners?”

Gulnis gazes at me with a hint of respect, but stays silent. Then Oisella, the female junior partner who has been mostly silently watching up until now, leans over to whisper in his ear, quietly enough that I can’t hear the words she’s saying. Given that they’re all Classers, I suppose I shouldn’t be surprised that their senses are probably as good as my own. Possibly better if they have Skills for that – I wouldn’t be surprised if Merchants do since knowing secrets can often be…profitable.

Gulnis’ expression becomes thoughtful at whatever Oisella tells him and he nods very slightly. His polite smile reappears on his face.

“Very well, you make a good point, my lord. I can assure you that we can find a market for a wide range of valuable goods. I am proud to boast that Mithril Trading Caravans already have trade agreements with three different settlements, and are in negotiations with a fourth party – separate to yourselves, of course.”

“Of course,” I echo thoughtfully. Four settlements – five including us. Not bad at all, especially for this early in colonisation. We must be placed relatively close together, I muse. Otherwise it would have taken longer for the merchant to get to us.

An important network, I comment in the Alliance Bond, thinking about the threats that are likely to menace us at some point in the future.

Assuming he’s not dealing meanly with the truth, Elarion comments derisively.

It wouldn’t be in his best interests to do so, Josiah puts in. We would quickly discover the truth and it would damage any burgeoning relationship.

Elarion’s faintly audible snort indicates that he wouldn’t put that past the merchants he despises so deeply.

I see Oisella’s eyes narrow and suspicion gathers in the corners of Gulnis’ eyes. No doubt they’re savvy enough to pick up that we’re communicating.

Thinking about it, maybe we should be a little more discreet about that. The merchant’s first thought is hardly likely to be that we have an Alliance Bond, after all. Rumours that I’ve Dominated all of my allies aren’t likely to paint me in a good light, and could lead to serious consequences back in Moriax either for me or Nicholas.

Better be more subtle about our silent communication or communicate in whispers after this, I suggest tensely even as I lean backward again and prop my head on one fist. “Four settlements is an impressive number to have already encountered,” I tell them, allowing a hint of admiration through. “You must have travelled far.”

Gulnis gives me a conspiratorial smile.

“Now that, my lord, is a trade secret – meaning one available for trade.” He smiles at his own joke. I smile politely in return.

“Oh, I promise I wasn’t trying to get more information out of you,” though I certainly wouldn’t have complained if he gave it. “I was just curious whether you have enough food for the journey. Perhaps we could resupply you.”

Gulnis’ eyes sharpen.

“You have food to spare?”

I grin inwardly, but force my expression to remain politely interested. Gotcha.

“Not to spare, exactly, but to trade? Perhaps.” I pause a moment, regarding him thoughtfully, then continue. “Why? Are your trading partners in need?”

It’s a little heavy-handed, I know, but the faint reaction I see in his face makes my ploy worth it. I strongly suspect that we’re not the only ones to be struggling to produce sufficient food.

“Not of meat,” Gulnis replies slowly, “but grains, vegetation…. The Lost Continent has proven more hostile than we were expecting.”

I nod slowly, then give him a sunny smile.

“It’s just as well that we’ve found a method to render the land more hospitable, then, isn’t it?”

I hear a rustle of movement behind me, as if someone has just jerked. I don’t turn to look, but see how the merchants’ eyes are all drawn briefly to my right.

“Markus, you can’t mean to trade away my discovery!” Elarion hisses, leaning forward so he’s speaking directly in my ear.

Because I’m looking at the merchants so intently, I see the interest that flickers across the faces of Gulnis and Oisella. Narrowing my eyes very slightly, I turn my head to hiss in Elarion’s ear in return.

“We need information.”

“But this is far too valuable!” he objects. “With this, any settlement with the right people will be able to produce food for themselves.”

“Isn’t that a good thing?” I reply pointedly. “Would you be able to live with yourself if people starve because we didn’t share it?”

“Can you live with yourself if people here suffer because we didn’t sell the information dearly enough?” Elarion returns.

I quickly glance around, spotting the thoughtful – and slightly suspicious – looks of our allies, paying attention while not interfering, and the intrigued looks of the merchants. A suspicion I held earlier coalesces. This isn’t how Elarion usually argues.

Elarion…are you acting? I ask him in the Bond while outwardly seeming to consider his words thoughtfully.

To an extent, he confirms. I’m still not all that keen on having the method traded so soon after its discovery, but it is unrefined at present. Inefficient. We will give up little advantage by trading it – and perhaps gain a great deal.

Especially if we make it seem incredibly valuable – and ourselves reluctant to give it up, I agree. Any thoughts? I ask the others.

I didn’t know you had it in you, Elarion, Layton replies, sounding impressed.

I sense Elarion barely holding back a sniff.

Such underhanded tactics are usually beneath me. But when dealing with creatures like these….

I decide to tune out – clearly the others don’t have any helpful comments at this point. Refocusing on the merchants, I see that Elarion’s machinations have had the desired effect. Whether or not they realise that they were being played and their heightened senses exploited, their interest is plain.

I allow a faint hint of regret to enter my expression.

“It seems like my allies are not keen on sharing the method behind making the land hospitable. However, we are willing to set up lines of trade for any surplus we have. And, of course, the offer of resupplying for your ship stands – for the information you carry, of course.”

Gulnis leans forward.

“Are you sure there is no price we can offer you for the information on the method?”

“It is valuable,” I reply slowly. “I doubt even the information you have on the other settlements would come close to matching it.”

I see Gulnis’ eyes gleam avariciously as I trail off with a nonchalant wave of my hand.

“But perhaps it is too early to talk,” I say decisively, cutting off what Gulnis was about to say. “It is late, you only arrived recently and must be tired. Perhaps we should resume this conversation tomorrow. I would welcome you to join us for breakfast.”

Markus? What are you doing? Josiah asks warily.

Getting a better feel for him – and letting him get a better feel for us, I reply. It’s a bit of a gamble – if the other settlements are far more impressive than my own, then it could backfire. But it will also allow us to continue prodding them for any titbits of information they let drop – and they’ll walk past the newly-created fields burgeoning with this world’s equivalent of wheat. Though on that note….

Elarion, can you make sure that as much of the land as possible is growing tomorrow morning when they come past? And that the kitchens have had enough grain to make some impressive breakfast dishes?

Clever, Layton compliments.

It might work, Valence contributes, the wary one for a change, however we must be careful not to be too obvious. Merchants are well-practised in spotting manipulations.

I value any advice you have to give, I say, expecting a short night as I speak with everyone who has experience of negotiating trade agreements with merchants – actually, wasn’t Sarran’s father one too?

Gulnis, who had turned to converse with his two junior partners, turns back to me with a smile.

“We would greatly appreciate the gracious hospitality you have offered us, my lord. What time would you like us to come tomorrow?”

“Let’s say the eighth mark?” I suggest. It’s relatively late for breakfast, allowing Elarion to get the farmers and Plant Mages mobilised. “A unit of Warriors will wait for you here and guide you to the com– to my manor.”

Gulnis bows his head deeply.

“Then we shall see you tomorrow, my lords. We look forward to it and appreciate the honour you show us.”
