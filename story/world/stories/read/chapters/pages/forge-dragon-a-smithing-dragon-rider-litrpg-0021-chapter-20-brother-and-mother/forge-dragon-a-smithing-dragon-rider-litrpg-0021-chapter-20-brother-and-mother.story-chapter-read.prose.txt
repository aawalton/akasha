Immediately after voiding his stomach contents all over the floor, Bog perked up. He still looked ill and moved lethargically, but it was a vast improvement.

“Feeling better?” Caleb asked.

Before Bog could answer, both their heads shot to the entrance, hearing a distant crash.

They ran over to investigate, extinguishing the light before peeking out the door to the wider caverns. With the door cracked, the sounds became more clear. Someone was out there—a lot of someones—and their footfalls could be heard echoing up the caverns.

“Time to go!” Caleb said, turning to leave.

He was halfway to the crack in the wall when he heard a loud squeak. His heart leaped into his chest at the thought that the enemy was already upon them, but he turned to see Bog dragging a bunk bed across the room to block the door.

“Good idea,” Caleb said, moving to grab more items and block the passage. One by one they piled the spare timbers onto the bed frame, weighing it down even more.

Then Caleb went to the anvil, and using his equipment-enhanced strength of 14 along with his willpower fueled Imperium, he lifted the massive steel block with relative ease. He almost dropped it, so surprised was he by his ability to lift it. The two stats combined equalled 34, and from his previous testing, he was fairly certain the stats were all equivalent. That meant he would have the same power lifting metal with a willpower of 10 as he would have using his hands and a strength of 10. Combining both, he had the power of three uninitiated men.

He threw the anvil onto the bed of timbers, certain that the troglodytes wouldn’t be able to immediately follow them, and then turned to the crack.

“Now we have to go.”

Bog sent reluctance, sending through the bond that he still didn’t feel up for a fight—let alone one with a dragon.

“Well, it’s a fight if we stay for certain. And who knows, maybe the dragon won’t be there.”

Caleb gathered his bag of tools, the magical ore—but not that which Bog had vomited—and the remnants of the bat meat and mushrooms. The tools were heavy, and without his gear he’d have struggled greatly to lift them, but now with 14 strength, he could do so with ease, not even needing to use his Imperium. He did, however, still use it to lighten his burden. Holding the bag over his shoulder grew tiresome, but he found if he supported it with his Imperium, the weight of the bag was distributed through his entire body, vastly easing the strain.

This was a thing he’d noticed when pulling himself toward his spear while it was lodged in the basilisk. Imperium Metallorum used himself as the anchor for whatever force he exerted, while Imperium Ventorum anchored itself on wherever he stood. That meant he could cause a gust of wind that sent something much heavier than himself flying, but if he tried the same with a giant block of iron, he’d go flying instead.

So, bag stowed, he gestured for Bog to get moving. The little dragon worked up a heat in his mouth and started walking down the cramped crag. It took him longer than before to do so, more evidence he wasn’t completely recovered.

By the dim light of the glow, Caleb’s boosted perception could make out the cavern perfectly. He’d originally wanted to cap his perception at 18, but more and more he was seeing the benefits of the stat. He would need to experiment to know for sure, but he thought the stat also helped him to perceive nuances in the mana strands that made up enchantments and wind. If that was the case, and it wasn’t just acuity that helped here, he would want to keep investing in the stat long-term.

So, guided by the glow of a dragon made of iron, Caleb made his way up through the crag to face the dragon that waited beyond—all the while marvelling at what his life had become.

As he walked, the journey felt far less claustrophobic than before. While he hated being so deep underground away from the sky, his wind sense allowed him to tell he was making measurable progress towards the surface. On the way down it had been a blind descent, but now he was moving with eyes wide open.

Faintly the pair heard the rhythmic booming as the troglodytes tried to break down the door to their room. When this happened, they picked up their pace, and Caleb was pleasantly surprised by the increased endurance that came along with the extra 2 points of constitution. Just like his strength, his endurance had always been lacking. One of the reasons he’d made his water-driven hammer was, he’d grown tired far too quickly to keep up all-day forge sessions. His endurance had improved as he’d grown and practiced, but he was always a step behind the others.

Eventually they heard the crash of the door to their room breaking and shouts of outrage, but the sounds of pursuit ended there. The troglodytes, it seemed, had no wish to follow them to the surface—or more likely, had no wish to tangle with the dragon they knew guarded it.

Bog and Caleb took a break after that to finish off the last of their rations before heading up the rest of the way. Caleb’s wind sense told him the surface was close, and while he was eager to get out of the darkness, he wasn’t confident in his ability to slay a dragon alone.

“You ready for this?” Caleb asked Bog, receiving a resounding no.

“Well, it’s now or never. We aren’t being followed, but we are out of food and water. So it’s either do it now or do it later after we are drained.”

Bog reluctantly agreed, and they continued on, taking great pains to move slowly and silently. This was difficult for both of them, and Caleb had little hope they wouldn’t be walking out into an ambush.

Finally a familiar bend appeared and Caleb told Bog to hold back as he went ahead to explore. Questing out through his magical senses, he tried to detect signs of an ambush. He felt the wind stronger than he’d ever sensed it since unlocking the ability deep below, and the feel of it gave him the courage to venture out. He rounded the last bend as quietly as he could with his spear held in both hands.

The cavern was empty. The bodies of the dead young dragons were gone and no sign of the mother could be seen.

Caleb called for Bog through their bond and the loud scraping shuffle of the dragon resumed.

“No one’s here,” he called back to Bog. “We better get out of here before she returns.”

The pair made their way to the cave mouth, which opened up, revealing the wide expanse of Caleb’s mountain home. Distantly he saw the trails of smoke from his village leading into the sky. He paused to take in the majesty of it all while reveling in the wind that buffeted his body.

Bog was not impressed.

The young dragon didn’t pause for an instant. Instead he continued on, retracing the steps he’d taken on his arrival. As the dragon crawled out, his iron talons pierced deeply into the stone of the mountain, leaving jagged handholds in his wake for Caleb to traverse. Even with Bog’s aid the path was treacherous, but with the help of his magics, Caleb was able to avoid a fatal fall. Twice he slipped, needing to call upon a gust of wind to push him back into the mountain to catch a handhold.

Soon they were free of the rock face, and once more on solid, level ground.

***

“What am I going to do with you?” Caleb asked Bog when they were further down the mountain.

He’d briefly put thought into how he’d hide Bog’s draconic nature lest his villagers want to kill him for extra initiations, but those had been distant hopes he’d not analyzed too closely. Now he knew the idea would be tricky. Bog was clearly what his village would recognize as an iron salamander, but he was much better fed. The forged steel Caleb had fed him gave the dragon a well-defined shape and scales that made his dragon heritage much more apparent next to the lumpy rusted messes that raided their village.

Before Bog could answer the question—not that he had any idea himself what ought to be done outside of feeding him copious amounts of iron—they spotted the dragon.

At first, Caleb thought it was a bird, but as it swooped down, its scales reflected the sunlight in a way that feathers never would. His enhanced perception kicked in as he looked at the diving creature, and the details of it came to focus. It was certainly the frost dragon they’d been fearing and it was currently somebody else’s problem.

Caleb was stunned by how well his eye’s functioned. He could make out details nearly a mile away that would have been a blur the prior week. In fact, he could even recognize the target of the dragon as his brother.

“Grisim!” Caleb shouted, his brain catching up to his eyes.

He broke out into a run and shouted louder, “Grisim! Look up!”

Caleb’s voice echoed off the rocks. His brother looked toward him, at the source of the noise, and not up as he had suggested.

Seeing Caleb, Grisim broke out into a run, thinking that he was coming to great him. Then Grisim’s eyes caught sight of Bog loping after Caleb and he sped up, drawing his spear.

“No, you idiot!” Caleb shouted, pointing up. “Wrong dragon!”

The words registered just in time, and Grisim followed Caleb’s pointing finger up to the swooping beast. He dove to the ground just in time for the frost dragon to miss him by a meter.

“You’re alive!” Grisim said, getting to his feet.

“We’ll see for how long,” Caleb answered. “What are you doing here?”

“Looking for you! What happened to you?!”

“I’ll tell you later, we need to deal with this. Take this.”

The first “this” in question was swooping back around. Catching sight of Caleb, it let out a roar of rage. The second “this” was Caleb’s smithing hammer that granted 1 point of strength.

He brother took the hammer reverently and then slipped it into his belt, drawing his spear.

“Looks like it recognizes us,” Caleb said to Bog.

“Salamander!” Grisim said, holding his spear in a ready position, his aim alternating between Bog and the other dragon.

“This is Bog,” Caleb explained quickly, “he’s a friend, focus on the other dragon!”

Caleb winced internally at his use of the word “other,” but consoled himself, knowing his brother was likely too stupid to pick up on the slip.

The dragon came down for them, and the two brothers raised their spears to ward it off. It banked wide around, just out of their reach, before coming back at them. This time it inhaled deeply on its approach and let out a long exhale of frost breath that froze the very water out of the air.

Caleb had been expecting this, and drew upon his mana to push the wind. A gust manifested out of nowhere, blowing the frost breath back at its originator. Ice particles gathered on the dragon, and its course was altered, but the cold did nothing to it. As it flew past, Caleb sent a spray of metal shrapnel after it, but it bounced off the scales harmlessly.

“How in the First Emperor’s name did you do all that?” Grisim asked.

“Later!”

The dragon was back, flying in low and fast directly at the brothers.

Caleb drew on his wind magic once more and, when the dragon was twenty feet away, pulled the air out of the sky, drawing it down into the ground. A huge downdraft appeared, and the dragon crashed easily into the rocks. Bog had been working up a shot of melted slag, and he spat it at the downed dragon, only for it to impact and bounce off harmlessly. His heat was still hampered by the metal flakes, and the chunk he spat had only a dim red glow.

Together Grisim and Caleb charged the downed dragon. It rose to its feet and let out a wide spray of ice. Caleb activated Vortex, using as much power as he could without needing to mentally control it, and the ice whipped around him and Grisim, flying out to the sides and away.

Grisim shot Caleb a quick look of alarm before bringing his spear down. Together they ran at the grounded dragon. The mother bellowed once more in impudent rage and whipped a tail around at Caleb. He tried to dodge, but the tip of the tail struck him right in the chest, knocking the wind from his lungs and sending him backwards.

Grisim slashed his spear at the tail, hitting it with his system-enhanced strength and leaving a deep laceration. The dragon buffeted its wings and jumped back, readying yet another ice attack.

From the ground, Caleb called on his wind once more, and drew a cross breeze between them. The wind sucked the breath away. Grisim and Bog closed in on the dragon, and Caleb clambered to his feet following. The long, serpentine dragon attacked Grisim with its head while dodging Bog’s slower attacks.

By the time Caleb reached the fight, Grisim had already taken deep cuts to his arms, as he’d used them to block bites. With the battle now three-on-one, the dragon began to rethink its decision to start fighting, but the anger at Caleb for killing its young won out. It threw caution to the wind and opened its wings wide, throwing Grisim and Bog aside and opening itself up to an attack from Caleb.

Caleb had seen those scales deflect all his brother’s spear thrusts, how they gave the dragon a false sense of security against their weapons, and he took advantage of that. Running at its head to meet its charge, he brought his spear up in a thrust to its chest. He drew on his Arcane Power Attack, dumping all his stamina into it along with the remainder of his mana. The force of his attack was so great that he lost his footing. Still, the tip reached its target, digging deep into the scale before passing through it and into the dragon’s heart.

It let out on last roar of protest before collapsing to the ground.

Ding!

Caleb collapsed next to it, his whole body burning with pain.

It occurred to him then that using all of your stamina on a single attack was not a wise decision.
