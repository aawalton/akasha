The Watch interior was mostly unchanged from yesterday. Small stacks of leather-bound books, glass inkpots, and crinkled parchment lay scattered across wooden desks and a tall, solitary bookshelf. Barrels topped by whetstones and oil cloths sat against a far wall. Flickering orange candlelight and the scents of leather and oak shrouded everything in hazy warmth.

One thing was markedly different, though; a wooden door in the back was open. Dancing light and muffled voices came from beyond.

“S’not worth tending,” the slurred, weary rumble made my knees lock into place.

Josiah. I swallowed at the sensation of a huge hand closing around my arm, tugging me forwards through crackling leaves and brittle plants. Pushing me into a cage of warped wood and decay, where a dozen dead little eyes stared up-

“Stop fussing,” came the terse reply. That voice was familiar, too, though instilled none of the same fear. Sister Embra continued, “I’m not worth my station if I leave you in such a state, and all your jostling isn’t helping.”

“Do as she says, Josiah,” Captain Rell sighed.

I crept forwards, heart hammering and breath trapped in my throat, until I reached the open doorway. I peered in, steadied only by the feel of Royce’s enormous frame at my back.

What lay before us was a rectangular room with its far half cordoned off by thin steel beams stretching from floor to ceiling. A dark, grainy wooden bench wound around the rest of the space.

Sister Embra sat on the bench, back ramrod straight and ivory hair pulled into a severe bun. Beside her lay a black-iron lantern, several long strips of white linen, and a familiar brown satchel doubtlessly filled with herbs, tinctures, ointments, and other apothecary tools. She was a large woman, but was dwarfed by the man before her.

Josiah towered even when sitting, all dark hair and corded limbs and deep-set, weary eyes. His skin was dappled with sweat. He wore a long cloth tunic and trousers, and the fabric around one shoulder had been pulled down.

The sight beneath made my gorge rise; fair skin and veiny muscles were stretched taut around a gash in his shoulder. Black fuzz peeked out from the cracked flesh and the skin around was an angry shade of red. Though the wound had been inflicted mere hours ago, it looked like it had partially healed, then fallen to infection.

We met each other’s eyes, and each of us froze. Me with limbs numbed by a wash of choking fear, and him with what appeared to be shock.

“Miss Brin!” Davith Rell stood opposite the pair with an armed wooden crossbow pointed downwards. “May I ask what- stand down!” The sudden, deep bark made me jump, but his ire was clearly directed at Josiah.

The huge man had started to rise. He flinched at the captain’s order and lifted both hands, palms-up, easing himself back down with a groan. He was breathing hard, gaze fixed on my face. Tracing the features, doubtlessly looking for the injuries that no longer existed.

“You,” Agnes Green hissed from beyond the bars. She sat cross-legged on the floor, narrow features drawn and bony hands clenched. Her ash-gray hair hung in a loose, disheveled mass. She bared her teeth at me. “You witch. You treacherous little snake.”

I shrank back, straight into a broad chest that was practically vibrating. One of Royce’s arms wrapped around my shoulders. An angry sound rumbled from the back of his throat.

“Mind your tongue, Agnes,” Captain Rell growled. “I’ve avoided irons out of courtesy, but that can change.”

She spat onto the floorboards.

The cell’s other occupant dragged himself upright and hobbled towards the bars. Gil’s narrow shoulders sagged beneath a thin brown tunic. His matted black hair was a mess and his skin was sallow in the flickering lantern-light, but his blue eyes were bright. “My lady.” The words were breathy and hitched as his mangled, three-fingered hand pressed against his side. To the knife-wound beneath. “You’re well?”

Agnes made an ugly sound. Josiah’s heavy head swung back and forth. Looking unreadably between me and his compatriot.

I swallowed. “I’m… I’m well.” It was a lie, but I certainly wasn’t going to throw myself to the floor and weep in front of two people who’d tried to kill me. I eyed the hunched man worriedly. “Are you alright?”

His cracked lips parted in a wheezy, harsh laugh. It wasn’t a malicious sound, but rather a very weary one, and it choked away into a sob at the end. He spent a moment collecting himself, one shaking hand pressed to his mouth, then said gravely, “Aye. Aye. Better than I’ve been in eight years.”

My chest tightened. I believed him.

“He’s all sewn up,” sister Embra interjected curtly, pulling a thin pair of shears from her satchel, “and the wound will heal. I’d like to say the same for you, but you’ll have to actually hold still.” She tutted at Josiah and lifted a damp cloth to his shoulder.

The huge man was still staring at me. Lips parted, eyes glazed, chest rising too fast.

I clenched both hands around my hem, returning the look nervously. Will he say anything? Mention my wounds- or the lack of? I can lie, of course, and support Renner’s story, but I certainly don’t want to. And I’m a terrible liar. My gaze cut towards Agnes, who was still leering at me. Did they give their own account of what happened? Did they tell the captain I have magic?

I cleared my throat, very aware of all the eyes on me. Captain Rell seemed to be waiting for an explanation as to my presence. “I’m sorry to… intrude.” I looked away as Embra pressed the shears against Josiah’s black-stained wound, and the big man gave a hiss of pain. “I just wanted to ask some questions.” I met the captain’s eyes and offered, “We’re going to tell the Order what’s happened, of course, and I just… I thought perhaps… that is, I wanted to make sure everything’s thorough.”

That’s believable, right? It makes sense. It’s the kind of thing Renner would say. He’d probably say it a bit better, though.

Captain Rell gave a slow nod. “Ah. I suppose I can understand, and there’s nothing wrong with being thorough. Was there something in particular you wanted to ask?”

I smoothed my hem and stepped forwards, drawing in a deep breath and looking to Gil. Of the pacted present, I felt certain that he would provide honest answers. “Just one thing. About the hag. Baltha. Did you… did you ever… dream about her?”

The dark-haired man’s brows drew together. “Of course, my lady. All the time.”

My heart stuttered. “You did?”

He bowed his head. “How could I not? A creature like that, the things she did… how could a person not have nightmares about it all?”

I swallowed tightly, wringing the coarse fabric of my borrowed cloak. “R-right. Nightmares. But… but did you ever have dreams that were… m-more than that? That were real, I mean.”

Beside me, the shears gave a quiet snick. Josiah moaned.

Gil’s forehead wrinkled. “Real?”

“Just… I’ve heard,” I said slowly, chewing my lower lip, “that Fae can… I d-don’t know, exactly. Trap people in their dreams with them, or bring you to another realm, or…”

He seemed to grow more puzzled with every word.

A knot wound through my belly. Perhaps it’s just me, then. Or just him. Velidan. Perhaps the bond, or whatever it is, works differently for different Fae. Any further questions died on my tongue. If these three had never had dreams like mine, then they could offer no advice on how to avoid or escape them. That’s it, then. No hope. No running. It’s not as though I can avoid sleeping, and if there’s no advice to be found here…

“Nightmares are very normal, love.” Sister Embra’s voice was as smooth and sweet as honey. She spared me a sad smile before refocusing on her gruesome task. “I can’t imagine the ordeal you went through. I expect you’ll dream of that wretched creature for a while, and there’s no shame to be found there.”

My throat grew tight. Her attempt at comfort was misdirected, of course, but the sentiment was not lost on me.

Captain Rell was watching me closely, but it seemed to be with pity rather than suspicion. “The Sister’s right, Miss. I don’t envy the nightmares that may come your way, but the hag is dead. You’re safe.”

I looked down in the ensuing silence, unsure if I wanted to laugh or cry. I suppose I should just be glad they aren’t reading too much into the question. They just think I’m frightened.

“Baltha’s alive,” Agnes hissed, cracking the quiet. “A creature like that, power like that… I don’t know what you did, witch, but she’s not dead. She’ll come back. She’ll free us.” The elderly woman prowled to the bars as I stepped back. Her gnarled hands wrapped around the steel. “You hear me? She’ll save us. She’s not dead. You can’t kill something like her!”

I shuddered, but not from the threat. From the image of a huge, mangled frame lying limp on fetid earth. Skin slick with blood, limbs twisted and snapped, head torn away. And standing over her…

“Maybe… maybe you can’t,” I whispered, ice crawling through my insides. I slammed my eyes shut. No escape. No way out. Baltha’s hulking body lay motionless, just a shadow in the haze of smoke, as starlit eyes and a cruel smile drew closer. Can’t get away. Not tonight. I’ll dream and he’ll be there and there’s no escaping him ever, I’ll be trapped forever for every night for the rest of my-

“The head would say otherwise, Miss Agnes,” Davith said gruffly.

She sneered at him.

“She’s dead,” Gil murmured. He was searching my face intently, a deep crease between his brows. “I felt it. Like… like a balm on the inside. Something sliding loose. We all felt it. Baltha’s dead, thank the stars.”

My chest swelled with a steadying breath. “Y-yes. She’s dead. She can’t… can’t hurt anyone. Not anymore.”

Gil’s thin, cracked lips pressed together. Rheumy blue eyes shone, just for a moment, with the light of triumph. “Thank you, my lady. Thank you. And… please,” he blinked rapidly, voice thick, and whispered, “forgive me.”

It took me a moment, but I managed to offer the man a solemn nod and rasped, “There’s nothing to forgive.”

He gave me a wilting smile in return, one that clearly indicated he did not agree.

I cut my attention briefly to Josiah. The huge man’s eyes were closed and his pale skin shone with a sheen of sweat. His jaw was clenched and the muscles of his neck and shoulder bulged, but he made no noise as Embra continued her grim work. I turned to Agnes next. She was staring straight at me, sheer hatred written across her wrinkled features.

I have nothing to say. Not to the two of them. Not after everything they’ve done.

I tangled my fingers into Royce’s and turned, leading him away. We paused at the door and, clutching one final shred of steel deep within my chest, I turned to meet Agnes’ spiteful gaze. My voice was soft and solemn as I murmured, “You’re wrong.”

She curled her lip at me. “Oh? About what, witch?”

I looked at her, proud and unrepentant in her dingy cell, but saw a very different sight. An ivory balcony gleaming beneath a rosy, swirling haze. Cruel, dark eyes lit by flecks of fierce, sunlit gold.

My voice dropped low, so soft that Agnes probably couldn’t even hear me, but that was fine. The words weren’t for her. Not really. “Everything can die.”
