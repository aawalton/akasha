I glanced over my shoulder again, unable to stop myself. I knew it was unlikely that my mom was following me; she’d been fast asleep on the sofa, but paranoia still pulled at me. It had been too easy. Sneaking out of the house had gone too smoothly, and part of me was starting to worry that Mom had been faking it the whole time. I was almost convinced that I’d see her stalking down the street behind me, thrilled to have finally caught me in the act of lying to her.

Another car raced past, travelling far too fast on the uneven road, and my head snapped around. My eyes found it immediately, and relief washed over me. It wasn’t Mom’s car. The colour was wrong, and the licence plate was different. I was safe.

I let out a slow exhale, looking behind me again as I attempted to bring my breathing under control. It felt impossible, though. No matter how many times I tried to focus on my breath and inhale deeply, it stayed shallow. My heart was pounding too fast; that was the problem. And it wasn’t just because I was terrified about Mom finding me. Another worry was consuming me.

It had been blocked out briefly. The triumph I’d felt as I left the house without waking Mom up had pushed it from my mind, and before that, I’d been so distracted by what I’d say to her to convince her I wasn’t doing anything I should, so I didn’t have a chance to think about it. But as I got closer to Duncan’s house, the anxiety returned.

Would the date make things weird between us? I asked myself that again and again, but I wasn’t able to come up with an answer. I hoped it wouldn’t, but I just wasn’t sure. I liked hanging out with Duncan too much. He was one of my best friends, one of my only friends, and I was so scared that if the date went badly, things could become awkward.

Or maybe he didn’t mean it. Perhaps he never meant to make me think he could be into me. He might have been joking when he called it a date, and he was just a flirty person. Maybe he didn’t realise I thought anything more of how he’d been acting, and he’d be horrified to realise I thought there was something more going on between us.

That would ruin everything. I knew he’d feel so guilty for having misled me, and I’d feel awful for making him feel that way, and then what? How could things possibly go back to normal between us? I’d never be able to text him again without the hollow ache in my stomach, that plagued me as I walked along the street, gnawing at me again. We’d never be able to hang out or even look at each other without cringing.

Our friendship would be over.

The sound of tyres speeding down the road made me jump, and I couldn’t help but look around again, but it wasn’t Mom. It was just Sarah’s dad, who waved as he passed. Automatically, I lifted my hand, returning the gesture.

It had been years since I’d spoken to her. I was pretty sure we hadn’t talked since primary school. Or maybe the first week of secondary school. We used to be pretty close, but we kind of just… drifted apart. She found other friends and stopped speaking to Phoebe and me.

And that could happen with Duncan if the date went badly.

My hands squeezed into fists at my sides as the urge to turn around and go home thrashed within me. I’d made it so far, though. I’d gotten out of the house without my mom realising. I didn’t want to have to sneak back in. That could wake her, and then I’d have to explain where I’d been, and that might be difficult.

Maybe I could just go to the gym, I considered. After all, I did have all of my stuff with me, and I’d already had a look at the classes. I knew the self-defence one would be pretty good, and perhaps I’d actually enjoy the gong bath thing, whatever that was.

But then I’d have to tell Duncan that something came up, and I couldn’t make it to his. I’d need to think of an excuse, and I wasn’t great at that. And what if he decided to go to the gym and saw me there? That could happen. He’d left training early, so what if he decided to make up for the workout that he missed? That would be awful.

He’d be so disappointed. Even just the thought of that made my stomach clench painfully. I could almost picture his face as he spotted me across the room, the confusion, the heartbreak. He’d definitely come over to me and ask what was going on. He’d be concerned, more than anything. I was pretty sure of that, and it would be so hard to explain why I’d lied to him. I couldn’t do that, I realised. And that meant I had to keep going.

I sucked in another deep breath and forced myself to start moving again. I hadn’t even noticed, but at some point, I’d come to a stop. My anxiety, my racing thoughts about Duncan, had distracted me so much that I wasn’t even paying attention to what was happening around me. Mom could have snuck up on me at any point, and I would have had no clue.

That realisation sent a fresh wave of fear through me, the sensation sharper than the rest. I looked around quickly, making sure she wasn’t there, before glancing down at the phone clutched in my hand as something occurred to me.

I had to text Phoebe. I was almost halfway there, and I’d told her I’d do it on the walk, but I’d completely forgotten. That would help, though. Talking to her normally stopped me from spiralling quite as much.

Okay, I’m on my way, I wrote, hesitating before adding, I’m kind of nervous though.

Phoebe must have been waiting for me to text her because my phone buzzed almost immediately.

YAY, her first text read. Wait, why are you nervous? It’s just Duncan!

I paused again, my thumbs hovering over the screen. I knew exactly why I was nervous, but part of me didn’t want to tell Phoebe the truth. Even though I’d told her almost everything else, it felt strangely embarrassing to say that.

I don’t know. I’m just worried this will be weird, you know?

Why would it be weird? Phoebe asked.

A soft groan slipped from my lips before I could stop it, and blood rushed into my cheeks as I looked around, making sure no one had seen. Luckily, there was no one else about, and my gaze returned to my phone as I tried to work out how to reply to Phoebe.

Surely, she must have understood why I felt that way. She’d known Duncan for ages too, and they weren’t as close as Duncan and I were, but they were still pretty close. It would have been weird for Phoebe to suddenly go on a date with him too.

Because it’s Duncan, was the best explanation I could think of.

Yeah, exactly! Phoebe wrote back. It’s just Duncan, and you’ve been to his so many times. It’ll be completely fine and not weird at all.

I chewed on my lip as I read her message, unable to truly believe it. I had been to his house a bunch of times, but never on a date. That would make things different. We weren’t just hanging out and playing video games. We were…

My brain wasn’t sure how to finish that sentence. Instead, my thoughts just ground to a halt as I turned off the main path and began walking down a much narrower one. Branches formed a canopy above my head, shielding me from the sun, and overground brambles reached out towards me on either side, but I barely looked away from my phone as I skirted around them.

Maybe, I typed, sending that to Phoebe before writing another message. Do you think this is a mistake? I mean what if he’s not into me?

I hesitated before hitting send, my stomach churning as I waited for Phoebe to reply. Straight away, three dots appeared at the bottom of the screen. She’d immediately started to write a response, and luckily, I didn’t have to wait long before my phone buzzed again.

He is.

That was all she’d written, and I stared at it blankly for a second before replying.

How do you know?

She seemed so sure, and I wanted to believe it too, but I couldn’t. Something was holding me back, and I just couldn’t convince myself that Duncan felt anything for me other than friendship.

Are you kidding? Phoebe’s text read. I’ve been around you two too much. It’s so obvious.

My eyebrows drew together in confusion. I didn’t think there was anything weird about the way we acted together. We were just friends; that was it. We acted like friends.

What do you mean? I started to write, but another text from Phoebe came through before I could hit send.

WAIT!! it read.

Immediately, the three dots appeared on the screen, and I waited impatiently for Phoebe’s next message, but she seemed to be taking a while to write whatever it was she was about to say.

Anxiety pulled at me, and I looked around at the path again before my eyes returned to my screen.

Yes? I prompted, unable to stop myself.

Okay, okay, I just remembered something he said ages ago, Phoebe had written. I was super drunk at the time, and so was he, so I didn’t think anything of it, but I think he almost told me he was into you like… a year ago??? Maybe longer??? I can’t remember if it was at the party he had at the beginning of summer last year or at the end, but it was definitely one of them!!

My heart thumped in my ears as I stared at the message. A million questions flooded my mind, and I quickly began typing again.

Wait, what did he say??

I glanced up at the trail in front of me, eyeing the light at the end of it. My steps slowed as I looked at my phone again. I was moving too fast, and Phoebe was taking too long to reply to me. Once I reached the end, I’d be right by Duncan’s house, but I needed to know what he’d said to her before then.

Restlessness bubbled within me as I waited for her message to come through, but she must have been typing so slowly. Either that or her text was going to be really long. I wasn’t sure, but I couldn’t take it any longer. Before I could stop myself, I tapped on her name at the top of the screen and called her.

“Oh my god,” Phoebe cried as she answered the phone.

It was so good to hear her voice after not seeing her for almost the entire summer, and part of me wanted to revel in that, but the rest of me demanded to know more.

“So? What happened?”

“Okay, so, keep in mind that it was ages ago, and I was really drunk,” she told me.

“I know! You already said! What did he say?” I asked, trying to keep my voice quiet.

There was no chance that Duncan would be able to hear me from his house, but that worry still pulled at me. I hadn’t said his name or anything that would make it obvious that I was talking about him, but part of me was convinced he’d be able to guess.

“So, we were on the roof, and I think you must have gone to the toilet, or maybe you’d gone to get another drink or something because you definitely weren’t there, and I don’t remember what we were talking about before, but then he just randomly asked if you were seeing someone,” Phoebe rambled.

“What?”

“Yeah! I think you’d been on your phone a bunch or something?” she explained. “Oh, wait. Maybe it was the end of summer because that was when we were both into that game where you, like, build a city and create all the people in it. Remember?”

“Yeah?”

I hadn’t thought about that game in ages, but I’d gotten into it when I’d been in Scotland the year before, and Phoebe quickly got addicted to it too. It was stupid. The game wasn’t even that fun, but we’d both gotten fully hooked, and I vaguely remembered checking it a few times when we were on the roof at the party. Phoebe had no issue telling Duncan she was playing it, but I didn’t want to. It embarrassed me for some reason.

“Okay, so I think he must have assumed you were texting a guy, but at the time, I thought he was just asking because he was curious, you know?” Phoebe asked, her voice high-pitched as she hurried to tell me. “Like, I probably would have asked if I were him too.”

“Okay…” I said. “Is that all?”

It didn’t seem like that weird a question to ask. I mean, I might have done the same if I’d noticed he’d been on his phone a lot too. It didn’t necessarily mean he was into me, and I was pretty sure he’d been dating someone at the time too, so he definitely wasn’t.

“No!” Phoebe cried. “So obviously, I said that you weren’t. Like you had told me about that cute guy you’d seen in Scotland, but nothing had happened between you two, right?”

“Right…”

“And then he just said ‘oh’ and was kind of quiet for a little bit. But then,” Phoebe said, drawing the word out, “He said ‘hey, do you think’, and then you got back, and he just stopped speaking. He’d sounded so nervous, though, so I’m sure he was going to say something about you!”

My mouth was open. I wasn’t sure when that had happened, but I shut it quickly, my head reeling. What had he been about to ask? Perhaps it had been something completely normal. He could have just been asking if Phoebe wanted another drink or something, but I needed to know.

“Wait, so that’s it?” I asked. “He didn’t, like, mention it again?”

“No! I’m almost certain he didn’t ask again, but I know it was about you! It must have been! He was probably going to ask if you were into him or if I thought you’d say yes if he asked you out!” Phoebe speculated.

A flare of hope burst in my heart, but I shoved it down violently. As much as I wanted to believe it, I just couldn’t. There was no way Duncan had been into me for a whole year without having said anything to me about it before then. He was terrible at keeping secrets. He would have caved and confessed ages ago.

Plus, the way he’d been acting towards me hadn’t changed at all. He’d been more flirty since the beginning of summer, but that was it. Surely, if he’d realised he had a crush on me that long ago, I would have noticed it.

“I doubt it,” I told her. “Wasn’t he dating someone back then?”

There was a slight pause before Phoebe spoke again.

“Was he?” she asked. “Wait, was that when he was with that girl? Hannah? Or… Harriet? I don’t know. That one from the other school?”

“Yeah, I think so,” I said, trying to sound casual.

It was Harriet. For some reason, I could remember her name. I’d only met her once. At that party, actually. Duncan had introduced us right at the beginning, but I’d not seen her since then, and he’d spent most of the night out on the roof with us.

“Huh, maybe,” Phoebe replied. “I’m pretty sure they broke up, like, right after that, so maybe he just hadn’t realised he was into you before then?”

“Mmm, maybe.”

“I mean… it is kind of suspicious, right? Like if he was about to ask something normal, why did he stop when you came out?”

That question had occurred to me too, but I’d already come up with an answer to it.

“I don’t know. Maybe I brought a bottle of mystery drink with me,” I suggested.

“You didn’t,” Phoebe was quick to say. “Because I remember he was super weird and, like, jumped up to go get another drink. Remember? He almost fell off the roof?”

“Oh yeah.”

I did remember that. It had been hilarious at the time, but looking back, it was kind of scary. He probably would have been fine. It wasn’t that high, and there was grass beneath it, but it could have been bad.

“Right?” Phoebe cried, her excitement audible. “I mean, I think he was just rushing to get away because he’d almost blurted out that he was in love with you—”

“I doubt he’s in love with me,” I said, interrupting her.

Embarrassment rose within me, and my eyes darted towards the path ahead of me. My voice had been much louder than I meant for it to be, and if Duncan was standing outside for some reason, he might have heard it.

He wouldn’t be, though, I tried to tell myself, but it was too late. I could already feel my chest turning blotchy as blood rushed across it before reaching up my neck and into my cheeks.

“I think he is,” Phoebe said with such certainty that it made me feel a little unsteady.

Could he really be in love with me?

“I don’t know…”

A loud sigh came from the other end of the phone, making my lips twitch despite my nerves.

“Well, I guess we’ll find out soon.”

“I guess,” I repeated.

There was a slight pause before Phoebe spoke again.

“It’ll be fine, Grace,” she promised. “You’re going to have a great time. I know it.”

“Yeah, I’m sure it will be fine,” I said without any conviction.

“And if you change your mind and need to get out of there, just text me, and I’ll come up with some kind of emergency that means you need to leave immediately to deal with it. I’m thinking… either a fire or maybe I was bitten by a squirrel or something.” Phoebe paused. “Wait… do they have squirrels here? I don’t think I’ve seen any since I arrived. I’m not exactly in the countryside, but…”

A laugh slipped from my lips before I could stop it.

“I have no clue,” I said. “I assume they do? I mean, France isn’t that far from England, and we have them, so… probably?”

Phoebe joined in my laughter.

“Yeah, there must be some somewhere around here,” she agreed. “I’ll Google it so I’m prepared. I mean, I could always just say it’s a rat. I know they have them here.”

“Ew, really?” I asked, unable to stop myself. “Have you seen one?”

There was an audible shudder from the other end of the phone.

“Yes. One literally ran across the floor of the restaurant we were in the other day.”

“What?” I cried. “How big was it?”

“Huge! And no one said anything! Like everyone saw it, but no one complained or anything!” Phoebe told me, her tone disgusted. “Some lady just lifted her bag when it came close so it wouldn’t climb inside, but then it just ran out the door, and that was it!”

“Ew!”

“And then everyone just continued eating! I asked my cousin about it, and she said it’s not even the first time she’s seen a rat there!”

I was just as horrified as Phoebe sounded.

“What?” I asked, the question tumbling from my lips. “Why did she go back if she knows the place has rats?”

“That’s what I said!” Phoebe exclaimed. “She literally just shrugged at me and said the food is good. Like… what? Surely if there’s rats, the food can’t be that good!”

“Surely not?”

“Like, rats are carriers of disease. That’s how the plague started, right?” she demanded.

“Yes!

There was another loud shudder from Phoebe.

“I swear, if I get the plague now, I know exactly where it came from.”

“You didn’t keep eating after that, did you?” I couldn’t help but ask.

“No! I literally could not bring myself to eat another bite!”

I pressed my lips together, trying to hold back the laugh that threatened to escape.

“Okay, good. That’s probably for the best,” I told her.

“I know, but what if it’s too late, and I already have the plague?”

She sounded so worried, and that only made it harder to stop myself from giggling.

“You don’t have the plague,” I reassured her.

“God, I hope not,” Phoebe muttered darkly. “So, how far away are you?”

My eyes flicked towards the end of the covered path again.

“Literally like a minute away.”

A squeal came from the other end of the phone. It was so loud that I had to move it away from my ear to stop the noise from deafening me. Phoebe’s enthusiasm made me smile, though. Despite my anxiety, I could feel some excitement starting to build within me.

Even if nothing happened, I told myself, it would still be nice to hang out with Duncan.

“Oh my god! You have to tell me everything, okay?” Phoebe demanded.

“I will, I promise.”

“Okay, you better! I mean, it will be a little weird to hear because, you know, it’s Duncan, but I can’t wait!”

I bit my lip, unsure how to reply.

“Yeah… I mean, I doubt anything will happen,” I said for what felt like the hundredth time.

“We’ll see!” Phoebe replied in a singsong voice. “But I’ll leave you to it! Have fun, and text me!”

“I will. Bye.”

“Bye!”
