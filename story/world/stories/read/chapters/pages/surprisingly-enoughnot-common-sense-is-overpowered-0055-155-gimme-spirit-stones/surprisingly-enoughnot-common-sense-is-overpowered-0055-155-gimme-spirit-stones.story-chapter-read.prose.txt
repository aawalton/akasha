"That went much better than I expected," Ning thought as he made his way back.

Not only had he won, but he'd come out completely uninjured. That alone was a significant advantage, since there would be no need to waste time or resources on treatment before the later rounds.

As he was mentally patting himself on the back for saving yet another spirit stone, he was surrounded.

"Ji Ning!"

"That was fast, way too fast! I only saw some fog, and before I knew it, the battle was over."

"Did you even break a sweat?!"

Zhang Feng was right at the center of it, practically vibrating with excitement. "That was amazing! Hahaha! That's how it's done!" he declared loudly, as if the entire arena needed to hear it.

Ning paused, then smiled helplessly. "You're exaggerating. And besides, you helped too."

"Huh?" Zhang Feng blinked. "I did?"

"You did. Training together, sparring, exchanging techniques... it all helped sharpen my combat experience."

Zhang Feng froze for a moment before laughing loudly. "So that's how it is! Then I'll take some credit too. Looks like all that training was worth it!"

Ning smiled. He wasn't exaggerating either.

In his previous life, he'd watched his fair share of fighting tutorials thanks to the shorts boom, but he'd never actually had the chance to use any of them.

When it came to real fighting experience, his knowledge had existed mostly in his watch history rather than his muscle memory.

Without all the preparation, repetition, and sparring he'd gone through since arriving in this world, he would never have reached this point.

"Next time, it's my turn," Zhang Feng said enthusiastically, clenching his fist. "I'll make sure I don't embarrass myself after watching that!"

A short distance away, Wei Zhusang and Lang Rulang were also watching Ning.

"That was... unexpected," Wei Zhusang admitted.

Lang Rulang crossed her arms. "We only recruited him on a whim back then."

"And now?"

"Now he's a proper competitor."

Wei Zhusang couldn't disagree.

Both of them had grown up with the backing of their clans, with access to better resources, techniques, and inherited knowledge from an early age. It wasn't arrogance to acknowledge that ordinary disciples usually struggled to compete with that kind of advantage.

When they first approached Ning, it had mainly been because of his five-element attribute. Beyond that, neither of them had thought much of him. His talent with spells had changed that impression, and now he had already reached the fifth stage of Qi Condensation, putting him among the strongest newcomers in terms of cultivation alone.

"The fog concealed most of what he was doing," Wei Zhusang said, "but his control was good. I still don't understand how he tracked everyone inside it."

Lang Rulang glanced at him. "Worried?"

"A little."

"Would you lose?"

Wei Zhusang considered the question for all of half a second before a small smile appeared on his face.

"Nah. I'd win."

Ning hadn't even fully recovered from the barrage of congratulations when someone suddenly grabbed his sleeve.

"Junior Brother Ning!"

He turned to find Xin Fu standing there, slightly out of breath and visibly excited.

"Huh? What's wrong?"

"Everything's sold out."

Ning blinked. "...Sold out?"

"Yes! Completely. We don't have anything left."

That genuinely caught Ning off guard.

As someone who was constantly lacking spirit stones, he'd long since developed a habit of spotting opportunities to make money. An Outer Sect Competition packed with disciples and spectators who would be sitting around watching matches for hours was about as obvious an opportunity as one could ask for.

So he'd done what came naturally.

He opened a stall.

Of course, setting up a stall during the Outer Sect Competition required a permit, but with Elder Shen backing him, that problem had been resolved almost immediately. The bigger issue was that Ning himself still had to participate in the competition, so he couldn't exactly stand behind the stall all day.

That was where Xin Fu came in.

After the Spiritual Farmer assessment, the two had stayed in touch. Most of the time, it was Xin Fu asking Ning questions while preparing for another attempt at the assessment. He might not have been particularly sharp, but he had experience, and more importantly, Ning trusted him.

So Ning had recruited him to manage the stall.

As for what they sold?

Food. Just food.

Ning had brought out a few inspired creations from his transmigrated junk-food memories, including marshmallows, cotton candy, and several other oddities that offered more novelty than actual nourishment. He hadn't expected much from them.

Apparently, that had been a mistake.

"So soon?" Ning muttered as they reached the stall and he stared at the empty counter. "I thought it would at least last until the afternoon."

Xin Fu scratched his head. "I thought so too. At first, everything was selling normally. Then one person showed up and ate almost everything. He even asked for more." He hesitated before adding, "And... he's still here."

Ning raised an eyebrow. "Oh?"

Xin Fu pointed discreetly toward the edge of the plaza.

A round-faced, heavily built man stood nearby, his robes stretched tightly over his stomach. In one hand, he held an empty paper cone and stared into it with unmistakable disappointment, apparently hoping more cotton candy might somehow appear.

As if sensing Ning's gaze, the man looked up.

Their eyes met, and his face immediately lit up.

"There you are!" he boomed, hurrying over at a speed Ning hadn't expected from someone his size. "Brother! Are you selling any more of that fluffy stuff? It melts in your mouth! I've never tasted anything like it!"

Ning stared at him.

Xin Fu leaned closer. "That's him."

The fat man leaned in as well, his eyes shining. "I'll pay double. No, triple! Just spin me another one."

"We're... completely sold out," Xin Fu reminded him.

The man's expression fell immediately. "...Completely?"

Seeing how devastated he looked, Ning narrowed his eyes slightly.

"Well, I prepared another batch for tomorrow—"

"Can you bring it out now?" the man asked immediately.

"Sure," Ning replied. "If someone likes my food that much, it wouldn't be right to deny them."

He handed a jade token to Xin Fu. "Senior Brother, go find Elder Shen. I stored most of the supplies with him since he has a space pouch."

"Alright!"

Xin Fu took the token and hurried off without wasting another second.

Ning turned back to the man. "I'm Ji Ning. And you are?"

"Ji Ning..." The man thought for a moment before recognition seemed to flicker across his face. Then he patted his chest. "I'm Li Langtian. Everyone just calls me Fatty Li."

He looked Ning over with renewed interest. "Are you a Spiritual Chef? Anyone who can make food like that should be famous, but I've never seen you around the Spiritual Chef Hall."

"Spiritual Chef? No. I'm just a farmer," Ning replied. "The food is only novel. It doesn't have any enhancement effects like actual spiritual cuisine."

"I see." Fatty Li nodded seriously. "Still, don't sell yourself short. Making something nobody else has thought of is impressive too."

"I think the praise belongs to you," Ning said. "If I remember correctly, you're one of the newcomers who passed the Spiritual Chef certification."

Spiritual Chef was one of the hundred secondary professions, and getting the spiritual certificate as a newcomer was no small feat.

"Haha, you've heard of me?" Fatty Li rubbed his head, barely suppressing his grin.

"Of course. Are you participating in the competition?"

Ning didn't remember seeing Fatty Li anywhere on the list of notable competitors.

"No. I'm just here to cheer for my friend."

"Oh?" Ning tilted his head. "Who?"

"My good brother, Xiao Fan!" Fatty Li said proudly. "You might've heard of him. He's incredible."

He immediately launched into an enthusiastic retelling of Xiao Fan's exploits, ranging from stumbling upon rare spiritual bees to narrowly surviving dangers that would have killed an ordinary disciple several times over.

Ning simply nodded and listened.

As expected.

Fatty Li was acquainted with the "protagonist" Xiao Fan, and from the way he spoke, the two were close enough that Fatty Li was probably one of Xiao Fan's only real friends in the outer sect.

Ning immediately recognized the role.

The classic protagonist's fatty friend.

To be honest, after discovering that Xiao Fan was a xianxia protagonist, Ning had briefly considered becoming that character himself. According to common tropes, the protagonist's fatty friend had a surprisingly good survival rate and usually received a few mysterious power-ups along the way just to keep pace.

Unfortunately, fate had already filled the position.

Since Xiao Fan already had a Fatty Li, Ning wasn't going to force it.

Instead, he continued chatting with him. Once Fatty Li realized Ning also knew Xiao Fan, he somehow became even more animated.

"I remember now! You're Ning! Xiao Fan mentioned you."

"Oh?" Ning asked. "What did he say?"

"He complained that you trusted his luck too much and gave him a ridiculously troublesome task," Fatty Li said, scratching his head. "Something about finding a beast?"

"I see." Ning nodded.

So Xiao Fan was still working on that.

They waited another twenty minutes, but Xin Fu still hadn't returned. With nothing else to do, Ning ended up listening to Fatty Li talk, and the longer he listened, the more enthusiastic the man became. Fatty Li seemed capable of speaking indefinitely, pausing only occasionally to drink some water before immediately continuing.

Eventually, Ning noticed a crowd gathering nearby and pointed toward it.

"Those people seem to be betting on the competition."

Fatty Li's eyes immediately lit up.

Yes. Focus on something else. Please.

"I want to check it out!" Fatty Li declared, already heading toward the crowd.

Ning sighed and followed.

...

In this world, gambling was nothing unusual.

Most of the time, it revolved around spirit ores, but a very close second was gambling on fights.

After all, fights were everywhere. People fought because someone looked at them a moment too long, to settle old grudges, or sometimes simply because there was nothing better to do. It wasn't without reason that Ning was cautious around others.

And where there was conflict in such abundance, naturally, someone would find a way to profit from it.

Unlike the shady gambling dens Ning had imagined, however, this place was openly sanctioned by the sect. Formations glowed faintly along the walls, while several floating jade boards displayed names, odds, and brackets that constantly shifted as results came in. Behind the long counters, attendants calmly recorded wagers while spirit stones changed hands nonstop.

Fatty Li's eyes shone as he scanned the boards.

"Whoa... they really have everything. Single-match bets, advancement bets, top ten, even champion wagers."

Ning followed his gaze and quickly understood the layout. The odds shifted in real time as information from the arenas streamed in, and everything seemed surprisingly organized.

"It seems this place is quite professional," Ning remarked with satisfaction.

He wasn't entirely unfamiliar with betting houses. There was one operating near the training grounds, but Ning had never visited it for one simple reason: it was unofficial.

Those places were usually run by small factions backed by inner sect disciples. Profitable, certainly, but also risky. If something went wrong, there was no one to appeal to, and the possibility of being cheated—or worse—was more than enough to keep Ning away.

This place was different. It was operated directly by the sect, which meant that as long as he didn't attract unnecessary attention or do anything stupid, it was safe enough.

"Let's go place our bets!" Fatty Li said hurriedly.

Without another word, he strode up to the counter and slapped down a heavy pouch.

"I'm betting on Xiao Fan. Champion bet. He's taking the whole tournament."

The attendant paused for a moment before nodding and recording the wager.

Several nearby disciples turned at the declaration, and a sharp laugh soon came from behind them.

"Did I hear that correctly?"

A tall youth wearing Xiao Clan robes stepped forward, his arms crossed as he looked at Fatty Li.

"Someone actually bet on Xiao Fan?" Xiao Hong sneered. "What a joke."

Fatty Li's face flushed. "What's wrong with that?! I can bet on whoever I want!"

"On trash?" Xiao Hong scoffed. "You might as well throw your spirit stones into the gutter."

Fatty Li opened his mouth to fire back, but another voice interrupted him.

"Say that again."

Xiao Fan had appeared beside them. His expression was calm as he looked directly at Xiao Hong.

Xiao Hong's smile widened. "Oh? The trash himself decided to show up."

"Brother Xiao, don't." Fatty Li quickly tried to stop him. "This guy..."

Xiao Fan ignored him.

"Watch your words."

Xiao Hong stepped closer. "Or what?"

Neither moved.

Then, before the argument could go any further, both of their jade tokens lit up at the same time.

A clear announcement echoed from the arena.

"Those whose tokens have been selected, please enter your assigned arenas."

The surrounding disciples went quiet.

Xiao Hong stared at the glowing token in his hand for a moment before laughing.

"Perfect."

"Exactly," Xiao Fan replied.

A slight smile appeared on his face, which only seemed to irritate Xiao Hong further.

"I won't show you any mercy in the arena."

"We'll see."

Xiao Hong snorted and strode toward the arena.

Xiao Fan turned back and placed a hand on Fatty Li's shoulder. "Don't worry. Your spirit stones won't go to waste."

Then he noticed Ning and gave him a nod in greeting.

"Do your best," Ning said with a smile. "I'm betting on you to win as well."

Xiao Fan paused for a moment before turning away. He raised one hand in a lazy wave as he headed toward the arena.

As expected of a protagonist. Even now, he still remembered to act cool at moments like this.

Well... that escalated fast.

"Brother Fan will be fine," Fatty Li said, though from his expression, he seemed to be reassuring himself more than anyone else.

"I agree."

Ning calmly stepped forward and placed his own pouch of spirit stones on the counter.

"All of it. Xiao Fan, next match."

The attendant looked at the pouch, then back at Ning.

"Are you sure?"

Ning nodded without hesitation.

Sometimes, going all in was also a type of wisdom.

Especially in a case like this.
