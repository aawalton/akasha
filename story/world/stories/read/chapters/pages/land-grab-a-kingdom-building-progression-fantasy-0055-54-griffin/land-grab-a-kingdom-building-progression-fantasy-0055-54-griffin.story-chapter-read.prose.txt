Soldiers filed into the warehouse behind Tavian, looking around nervously as they awaited orders. Whatever was screeching in the darkness had a strange, animalistic quality to it.

Inside the structure gloom hung over rows of shelves packed between thick stone pillars. Many of them were bare, but others bore crates, likely of valuables shipped in and stored here. Narrow corridors ran through the warehouse, providing cover for anyone that might be inside. Tavian confirmed from the purple banners hanging overhead that this had once been one of Brask’s warehouses. Apparently Brask had abandoned it when he set sail with his fleet to avoid the civil war.

“Take these crates,” Tavian ordered. “If they’re empty, burn them. If they’ve valuables inside, carefully remove those and bring crates and straw to the fire. It needs to be convincing.”

The soldiers started working, unpacking crates and hauling wood and straw to the fire that Braun managed outside. If they could make Anarvia think that they were burning down his warehouse, he’d surely send his forces here to try and stop him.

Tavian took the black dagger from the scabbard at his hip and laid it on an empty shelf. His fingers seemed unwilling to release it for a moment, before finally letting go.

He and Lea had discussed it, and decided that the dagger was trying to connect his mind with the dungeon mother. Since she was dead, no voice spoke to him when he connected with it. But the dagger still blocked his connection to Lea. Others who wielded the daggers could benefit from their traits, but only Tavian could use the ability, thanks to his miasmic injury.

He hadn’t had any more visions of Cormath.

Tavian stepped back, feeling the pull as Lord Anarvia’s influence now weighed on him. Removed from the dagger, his connection to Lea was restored.

“Has Donath gotten our message?” Tavian asked.

“Yes,” Lea said. “He’s moving out now.”

Outside, the sun disappeared below the horizon. Tavian had about a half an hour now, to do his best to attract as many ghouls as possible to the warehouse. He retrieved the dagger, slipping it back into his scabbard.

Something rattled and scratched in the darkness. It sounded like something large, moving around.

Tavian lifted his lantern.

Another forlorn cry rose up from the bowels of the warehouse. The shouts of soldiers, moving around the perimeter and securing the warehouse reached him.

Tavian walked into the heart of the warehouse.

He rounded a corner, and discovered a huge cage. Inside the cage sat a griffin.

The creature stared at him, lanternlight reflecting off of large, dark eyes. It snapped its wickedly pointed beak, and crooned again. The sound was no longer frightening, but filled with melancholy.

Griffin feathers were a rare and valuable resource. Had Brask brought this creature here, keeping it caged as he sold off the feathers?

Tavian approached the cage, whispering soothingly to the wild creature, as he would a horse.

He’d seen griffins before, when he was a boy. They’d been far off, and he’d thought they were birds at first, wheeling in the sky. Only when they landed on the plains, and loped along rapidly did he realize what he was seeing.

The griffin lifted its wings, but in the confines of the cage it was hardly able to move them.

It was a tragedy, keeping such a magnificent creature in a cage.

Tavian found the latch. It was locked.

The griffin chirped at him, hopefully.

The griffin was the size of a horse. It sat back on its powerful lion’s haunches, eagle head cocked to one side as it examined Tavian through its bird’s eye.

A creature like this could kill a squad of men on its own.

Locked up like this… it would die.

Tavian couldn’t just leave it here.

It took a couple of minutes to clear a path and gather a dozen men. They lifted the cage together, and carried out of the warehouse. The griffin’s sharp beak flashed in the torch light, and it turned its head around, this way and that, but it didn’t try to attack anyone lifting the cage.

They set it down just outside the warehouse.

“Are you sure about this?” a Squad Captain asked Tavian. “That thing could rip us all to shreds.”

They all stared at the griffin for a long moment. It stared back at them.

“Everyone back up,” Tavian said. “I’m letting it out.”

He brought the shield around, the emblem of the griffin glittering in the darkness, and lifted it over his head.

With one mighty blow, he smashed the lock from the latch, and pulled the gate open.

The griffin exploded out of the cage, sharp talons clattering across the Aegis, in an explosion of wind and feathers.

It would have torn Tavian to pieces but for his shield.

Tavian scrambled to his feet and watched as the griffin leaped into the air. It unfurled massive wings, longer than three carts end to end, and sprang up onto the upturned cart blocking the hole in the damaged gate.

Everyone stopped what they were doing. They really didn’t have time for this, but they all stood, watching the griffin. Outside the walls surrounding the warehouses, ghouls were gathering. They pounded on the stones, shook the gates, and screamed. The griffin screeched, and began plunging its beak down into the clustered ghouls, snapping them up and tossing them into the air.

A cry went up of “Hail Veros!” and Tavian realized that his men took this as a good omen. The griffin was the symbol of his house, after all.

Then, with a final cry, the griffin flapped mighty wings and soared into the air. The griffin wing wash fanned the flames of the bonfire, sending burning crates and timbers across the ground and into the entrance of the warehouse, smashing into soldiers and catching loose packing straw alight.

Another mighty beat of wings toppled the upended cart, and ghouls began spilling into the courtyard.

Men shouted and ran about in the chaos, fighting fires and ghouls. They quickly formed a line, soon plugging the hole and driving back the ghouls.

Tendrils of smoke wafted up from the warehouse, and more soldiers ran to put them out. Soldiers beat at burning embers that had landed on their clothes.

If they could save the warehouse, that would be good. But Tavian would trade it all for that one moment, of seeing the griffin stretch its wings and leap into the air.

Braun found Tavian. “What is Horel’s bloody halls was that?”

“A griffin,” Tavian said, still watching the dark shape of the creature against the night sky.

“Well, we have another problem,” Braun said. “Prisoners.” He waved for Tavian to follow.

Braun spoke as Tavian followed. “We lost too many men on that cart ride,” Braun said. “There are more ghouls out there than we expected.”

“We need to get going,” Tavian snarled. “I don’t have time to deal with prisoners.”

He followed the man around to where about five dozen men in Calenor livery knelt, guarded by about two dozen of Tavian’s own men. Weapons were strewn on the ground, as though the men had thrown them aside and dropped to their knees.

When he walked up, the Calenor captain raised his eyes. “House Veros lives!” he declared, with unmasked enthusiasm. “Forgive us, my lord! If our Lady knew, she would tell us to serve you!”

“What should we do with them?” a captain asked Tavian. “When they saw the griffin, and noted our livery, they threw down their weapons.”

Tavian fought to keep his mouth from dropping open. Did they think that Tavian had rode the griffin here himself? Surely they knew that the bird was a captive in the warehouse.

“Incredible,” Braun breathed.

“Forgive us, lord,” the Calenorian said again. “You burst in here, slaughtering my men, and—”

Tavian held up a hand, cutting him off. This was a problem.

They had no way to hold these men as prisoners, especially not when they were subjects of Anarvia’s kingdom, benefiting from his influence.

“You would betray your lord?” Tavian asked. “Anarvia?”

“We serve Lady Calenor, not Lord Anarvia,” the man replied.

“And your orders?”

“To protect this warehouse.”

“You’d abandon your post? What kind of soldiers are you?”

“Please, my lord, I am only concerned about my men. With the damaged done to the gate…” he gestured helplessly as ghoul screams drowned his voice out. “We will certainly be overrun.”

“Will your Lord Anarvia not call off his hounds?”

“I don’t know how much control he has over the ghouls,” the man said, his voice trembling. “I doubt it is so significant.”

Loud strikes and blows rang out against the nearby wall. A tree hanging over the wall shook as something climbed into its branches.

Tavian sensed the ghouls. They knew where he was.

“Don’t trust them,” Braun whispered in his ear. “Send them away, we have to get moving.”

“They would double our force,” Tavian replied.

“They could stab us in the back.”

Tavian eyed the men. He couldn’t administer a binding oath unless they swore fealty to him, abandoning Anarvia’s kingdom. That would make them less valuable to him, since they’d suffer the ill effects of Anarvia’s influence. Even then, the oath wouldn’t mean much outside of Tavian’s own claimed territory. And he couldn’t spare the men to guard them.

Something dropped over the wall, a snarling woman in tattered rags. She ran in a rage toward Tavian. Braun drew his sword and severed the ghoul’s head. It dropped to the ground.

The wall trembled.

His own men still fought ghouls as they held the damaged gate. If these Calenorians were really Anarvia’s men, they would have stalled him instead of throwing down their weapons.

“It could be some kind of trap,” Braun urged. “Are you sure?”

“These men knelt for the griffin,” Tavian said. “I believe them.”

He turned back to the captives.

“If you join us you might still die,” Tavian said. “We’re attacking your Lord’s compound.”

The man’s eyes widened. “With a hundred men?” he asked.

“With ninety,” Tavian shrugged, glancing at his men. “Or with a hundred fifty.”

The captain shifted uncomfortably. Men screamed and shouted at the gate as they clashed with ghouls.

“Are you loyal to Calenor? Then obey your Lady’s wishes. Stay here, and die. Protect the warehouse from your ally’s ghouls. We will not threaten it further.”

“Our Lady seeks peace, before war,” the captain said.

Tavian pointed to where his men were fighting. “Then take up your weapons. Prove your worth.”

The captain looked back at his men, seeming to draw strength from them.

“We follow the sign of the griffin,” he said, stooping to pick up his spear. “We can’t serve a man turning our city into a graveyard.”

The other Calenorians followed suit.

Tavian saluted them. “Choose your kingdom, men.”

They charged into the fray, linking shields together and driving into the ghouls with the force of a hammer.

Braun pursed his lips, nodding in satisfaction. “I’ll put them on the left flank,” he said.

Tavian smiled.

Ghouls began pouring over the wall beside him. It was time to go.

“We’re going out the side gate,” Tavian said, and Braun looked confused.

Tavian hefted the shield, and aimed it toward the ghouls. Braun understood, and ran off, shouting orders.

Shockwave.

With a surge of energy, the shield hurled ghouls into the air, smashing broken bodies into the wall.

Tavian ran forward and blasted the wall. It exploded outwards, bricks and mortar crumbling to dust.

His body vibrated with power. He felt the will of the shield; how it longed to be used.
