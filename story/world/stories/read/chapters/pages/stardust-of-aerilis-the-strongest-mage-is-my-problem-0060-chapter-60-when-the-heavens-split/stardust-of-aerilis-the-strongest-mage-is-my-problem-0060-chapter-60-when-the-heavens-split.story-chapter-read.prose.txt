Sylphaine didn't fly; she drifted through the stone like a phantom. She moved with a manic glee, her white twin-tails whipping behind her as she phased through the shifting walls of the Sunken Fane. The sentient dungeon was still trying to reconfigure its hallways to trap her, but to a vampire who could treat solid matter as a suggestion, the labyrinth was merely a playground.

"So easy," she giggled, her voice echoing in the hollow spaces between the masonry. "The Fox thinks she’s a genius and the human thinks he’s a negotiator. But in the end, it is I who hold the leash."

She reached into the pocket of her dark coat and pulled out the Loom-Breaker. The artifact had shrunk in her possession, now barely the size of a jewelry pendant, pulsing with a faint, rhythmic crimson light.

The Loom Breaker was something her Master had been searching for.

Once she was clear of this rock, she would send word to the Master. She would reveal Cyria’s plan to double-cross him, hand over the Loom-Breaker, and position herself as the most dependable instrument. She would be the favorite.

The main hallway finally came into view. The massive white marble gates loomed at the far end.

Sylphaine slowed her flight and held the tiny Loom-Breaker toward the gate. The artifact began to float, suspended in a localized gravity well of its own making. It expanded rapidly, returning to its original forearm-length size, the crimson crystals screaming with an intense, unbinding frequency.

The blue sigils on the dungeon exit responded instantly. They flared with a blinding, desperate light before turning a dull, deactivated grey. The stone began to groan as the ancient seals were unraveled at the molecular level.

"Finally," Sylphaine whispered, her red eyes wide with anticipation. "The door is—"

BOOM.

A violent, hyper-condensed blast of violet mana lanced through the air from the darkness behind her. It didn't just strike Sylphaine; it vaporized her. The vampire didn't even have time to hiss before her upper torso was disintegrated into a cloud of red mist and charcoal dust.

The heavy stone gates of the dungeon swung wide, yielding to the Loom-Breaker’s power.

Virelle materialized out of the shadows, her hair fluttering in the sudden influx of fresh sea air. She reached out a slender hand, catching the Loom-Breaker as it fell. The moment her fingers touched the crystal, it shrunk back down into its portable form, nestling into her palm.

"Master! Faster! I find the humidity of this tomb increasingly offensive!" Virelle called out over her shoulder.

Seconds later, Aiven, Vane, and Pelka burst into the hallway. They were running with a speed that defied human or beastfolk limits, their bodies surrounded by a soft, pulsing violet glow. Virelle had cast a high-tier enhancement on them, artificially boosting their stamina and leg strength to keep them from being swallowed by the shifting floors.

Aiven skidded toward the exit, his eyes widening as he saw the red mist on the floor. "Virelle! Did you—"

"I performed a necessary cleaning," she replied airily, tucked the Loom-Breaker into her bodice.

On the floor, the red mist began to churn and solidify. Crimson sinew spun into bone, and within heartbeats, Sylphaine’s face emerged from the carnage. Her clothes knit themselves back together in a flicker of dark mana, and she stood up, wiping a smear of imaginary blood from her lip.

"You really are a violent one, aren't you?" Sylphaine hissed, though her playful smirk was beginning to look strained.

"Move! The seals are trying to fight back!" Pelka shrieked, her sheep ears vibrating as the marble floor beneath them began to tilt toward a bottomless pit.

The group dashed through the opening, crossing the threshold just as the massive marble gates slammed shut behind them with a tectonic thud. The blue seals flared back to life, locking the Sunken Fane away from the world once more.

They stood on the jagged obsidian rocks of the islet, the morning sun of the Azure Sea blinding after the bioluminescence of the dungeon.

Sylphaine floated a few feet away, her expression shifting into a look of genuine, sharp-toothed offense. "You foiled my plans, little human," she said, narrowing her red eyes at Aiven. "We had a deal.”

Virelle let out a sharp, mocking laugh that cut through the sound of the waves. She drifted between Aiven and the vampire, her prismatic orb humming with a predatory sass.

"A deal?" Virelle purred, her eyes dancing with a lethal amusement. "Master told me all about your little 'proposal' while we were navigating those dreary hallways. A fake escape? Handing me over to some Master like a common parcel?"

She loomed over Sylphaine, her hair fanning out like a storm. "I would never participate in such a pathetic, low-effort scheme just to help a spoiled brat gain the favor of her 'Daddy.' It is beneath my dignity, and it is an insult to my Master’s taste in companions. Besides, the deal was that you gave the Loom Breaker to my Master, and you actually stole it. You are the one who backed down from the deal."

Sylphaine’s face turned a dangerous shade of pale. "The Master is not a 'Daddy-like' figure, you arrogant doll!" she snarled, her hands clawing the air. "He is a visionary! He is someone who is trying to save this rotting world from—"

SCHWING.

A blade of pure mana lanced through the air with a sound like tearing silk. Sylphaine’s head was severed mid-sentence, spinning through the air before landing with a wet thud on the obsidian rocks.

Pelka let out a shriek of pure horror, burying her face in her briefcase. Vane simply closed his eyes and let out a long, weary sigh.

"She was talking too much," Virelle remarked, idly inspecting her fingernails. "I find that I truly detest exposition from leeches."

Seconds later, the head on the ground dissolved into mist, and Sylphaine stood whole again, her face twisting into a mask of unbridled fury. "I am going to enjoy watching the Master break you, Starweave," she whispered, her voice a jagged line of ice.

"How brave," Virelle smiled sweetly, her eyes glowing. "You may argue with my magic as many times as your regeneration allows. I have plenty of mana, and I find the repetition quite... therapeutic."

The sea breeze should have been a relief, but the air on the jagged islet remained thick with a different kind of pressure. Sylphaine hovered a few feet away, her red eyes burning with a humiliated fury as she straightened her coat.

"You speak of dignity, doll," Sylphaine spat, her twin-tails twitching like the tails of a cornered cat. "You seem to have a very convenient memory. Do you already miss the time when your knees were buried in the dirt? When you were spasming from my blood magic and begging for the white light to stop? I can easily remind you of how it feels to have your mind pulled apart."

Virelle didn't flinch. She idly adjusted a translucent sleeve, her prismatic orb spinning with a low, dangerous hum. "That was a fluke, you tedious leech. I was momentarily distracted by your stupid brother. This time, there are no distractions... and it shall be you who learns the proper posture for a servant."

As the two prepared to launch their respective storms of mana and gravity, the world suddenly went silent.

The sky above didn't darken; it cracked. A jagged, vertical line of golden light tore through the atmosphere, splitting the blue heavens in two. Then a white light fired upon the ground, and a hooded figure was standing between them.

Sylphaine’s arrogance vanished in a heartbeat. The vampire, who had just been threatening to unmake Virelle’s mind, dropped to the obsidian rocks instantly. She knelt, her head bowed so low her forehead nearly touched the stone, her entire body trembling with a mixture of reverence and primal terror.

Virelle, never one to miss a theatrical beat, let out a sharp, mocking laugh. "Oh? Look at that, Master! It seems the little leech finally found her calling. She is actually kneeling this time! Such a vast improvement in her aesthetic."

Aiven, however, didn't laugh. He felt a cold sweat break out across his back.

He looked up at the figure. "That’s probably him," Aiven breathed, his right hand gripping the hilt of his sword. "That’s the Master."

The hooded figure stood tall, his posture relaxed and unburdened. Slowly, he reached up and pulled back the heavy fabric of his hood.

Aiven blinked in surprise. He had expected a monster—a creature of fangs or glowing runes. Instead, he saw a human. The man looked remarkably normal, perhaps in his late thirties, with short, neat dark hair and features that would have been handsome in a quiet, academic sort of way.

But then, the man looked at him. His eyes weren't brown or blue; they were twin pools of molten, brilliant gold.

The Master began to walk toward Aiven, ignoring Sylphaine as if she were a piece of the landscape.

"STAY BACK!" Virelle shrieked.

Her protective instincts overrode her caution. She thrust her palm forward, unleashing a hyper-condensed beam of violet mana—the same attack that had deleted the first sentinel. The beam shrieked through the air, aimed directly for the man’s chest.

THWIP.

An invisible shield, perfectly transparent save for a faint ripple like water, manifested inches from the Master’s face. The mana beam didn't explode; it was simply absorbed, the violet energy dissipating into the shield like ink in an ocean.

The Master didn't even break his stride. He looked at Virelle, his expression one of mild, patient curiosity. "I mean no harm, Starweave," he said. His voice was resonant and calm.

"I simply wish to speak with Aiven Roan."

"Why would I believe a word from your mouth?" Virelle hissed as she prepared a dozen mana spears. "You sent these leeches to torment us! you tried to break my Master’s!"

The Master sighed, a sound of genuine, weary disappointment. He didn't raise his voice, but he raised his right arm.

CRACK.

A crushing force of absolute gravity slammed into Virelle. The mage was driven downward instantly. Her knees hit the obsidian rock with a sickening crack, and she gasped for air, her prismatic orb falling to the ground and rolling away. She struggled to lift her head, her mana sparking and fizzing, but the weight was absolute.

"Virelle!" Aiven cried out, stepping forward to reach for her.

"She will be fine," the Master said softly, stopping just three feet from Aiven.

The man offered a small, disarming smile that didn't reach his gilded eyes. He looked at Aiven’s obsidian arm, then back at his face, as if he were an artist admiring a particularly interesting sketch.

"Now then, Aiven Roan," the Master purred, his golden eyes shimmering with a dark, prophetic light. "Shall we talk?"
