When Edge stepped out of the bedroom, Sasha switched on the floor’s privacy device, letting the crew speak freely as the final platter of grilled meat finished cooking.

“Enough stalling,” Trapper said when he appeared. “The suspense is killing me.”

The tattooed warrior threw his arms around her, drawing her into a bear hug as he blinked away tears. “Wow. I didn’t see that one coming. Thank you. I’ve been worried that I will fall behind the rest of you. Now I should be able to keep pulling my weight. I know this was a gift, but I promise to find some way to return the favor. I can’t believe how lucky I am to have a friend like you.”

“Don’t worry about it, although I appreciate the sentiment. I don’t have enough mana for another Epic skill anyway, and I can’t wait to see you in action once you slip that bad boy into an empty slot.”

Jumo was so touched that he didn’t even poke fun at her questionable choice of words. He asked Edge to store the gem in his vault for safekeeping, and the crew moved on to discussing the implications of Mirrored Retribution.

He waited until his friends had said their piece before sharing his thoughts, but everyone agreed it would be best for Sakura to equip the Epic aura. She had a larger mana pool than Trapper, and Lore-Weaver’s powers were a perfect fit for Retribution, since it would be far more effective if she used it at opportune moments rather than mirroring every attack until her reserves ran dry.

“We’ll have to stock up on mana seeds and berries,” Trapper said. “We need to anyway, but I want to make sure that you always have enough magicytes to use the aura in a pinch.”

Sakura still had some free skill slots after evolving, so she went ahead and used the gem then and there. Edge watched on with interest as the jewel crumbled to dust in her hand—reactor pulsing as the power it contained was bound to her core. When the process was complete, she walked over and placed her hand on his shoulder, staring at him with her striking amber eyes.

“Thanks, Edge. I’ve been hoping to pick up an aura for some time now, since I’m the only one who doesn’t have one yet, and this is significantly better than anything I’ve come across. I promise to use it well.”

“Of that, I have no doubts,” he replied. “You’ve already saved our lives more times than I can count, and there isn’t anyone I trust more to make the most out of that gem.” He bumped fists with his fellow scion, then burned the slips of paper. Before he forgot, he shared the information Skill-Eater had revealed, then the crew grabbed all the food and carried it through the front door.

When they descended the stairs, the ground floor was packed with people filling the inn’s common area and dining room. The tables were laden with snacks and drinks, and the magitech speaker was playing a selection of highly danceable music. A paper banner hung from the ceiling, with “Congratulations, Edge and Mel,” painted in bright colors.

Everyone burst into applause when they made their appearance, at which point one hell of a party ensued. He’d had no idea that his friends had made such extensive preparations, and he felt a warm glow when he realized how confident they must have been in his victory to set this into motion before the big fight.

Astil and Stellia were there, along with Helena and both her apprentices. Gerg was in the middle of an animated discussion with Pip, and Beelo was dancing with the warrior he was dating. Several hunters Trapper was friendly with had joined them, including Vela the shadowkiller, and Mel had invited a pair of warriors from the city watch.

Sasha had invited two acquaintances who worked with the Magisters. They were siblings and covered in tattoos that were even more extensive than Jumo’s—hailing from a planet Edge wasn’t familiar with. He was pretty sure Sasha was dating one of them, but he respected her privacy and didn’t intend to pry unless she wanted to share.

Astil had one of her guide friends with her, and it was nice to see the serious young woman relaxing and enjoying herself for a change. Lanna was swapping stories with Nel, who owned the café Edge had stopped at during his first day in the free city, along with one of the men who guarded the inn.

All in all, it was a great group, and he thoroughly enjoyed himself as the night wore on. At one point, Astil walked over and initiated a Credit transfer. Gladiators weren’t allowed to bet on themselves, so Edge and Mel had asked Lanna and Astil to place wagers for them. He wound up with another 100,000 creds to his name, minus a tip for the guide to show his appreciation.

The only thing missing was his friends from back home, including Riller, Violet, Blue, Lilly, Ann, Rita, and Sam, not to mention Earl, Able, Ander, and Melchior… He broke into a broad grin when he realized how many individuals he had grown close to since arriving on Ord. That list didn’t even begin to cover all the wonderful people from Puppet Town, emphasizing the fact that his social life had changed just as much as his body and the core burning behind his ribs.

With any luck, we’ll get some updates when the delegation arrives with the Wave-Bound Warrior. Edge enjoyed the delicious food and potent drinks. He wound up dancing with most of the women before the night was done. He spent at least a few minutes chatting with everyone, and Rue got a month’s worth of scritches and treats packed into a single evening.

His friends had been too distracted by the Epic gems to tease him about his Sentinels’ appearances earlier, but they made up for it now. He heard a dozen jokes at his expense, mostly centered around the theme of “harem gone wrong.” He had known it was coming and surrendered to the enviable, laughing along with each witty comment. He didn’t mind a bit of humor at his expense and was too thrilled by the skill’s performance to care what form it took.

By the time the party wound down and people began making their way home, morning sunlight was shining through the skyscreen above. Edge thanked Lanna for putting everything together and fell asleep with a smile on his face, feeling fortunate beyond measure to be surrounded by good people while living a life that exceeded the wildest dreams of his former existence.

Edge strolled down the gold ring with Sakura on one arm and Trapper on the other.

Both women looked stunning in the formal attire they’d purchased for tonight’s auction, whose doors would open in just a few minutes. It was his first time perusing the exclusive venue, which was only accessible to people stage three and above. He chatted with his friends while checking out the gorgeous artwork and high-end establishments they passed along the way, although the lion’s share of his attention was devoted to the commercial contest that would momentarily ensue.

While this was a vital opportunity to get their hands on top-grade gear that couldn’t be acquired any other way, attending the event also provided an opportunity to rub elbows with the free city’s movers and shakers. Making a good impression was just as important as placing a winning bid.

With two lovely ladies at my side and a suit that cost a small fortune, I won’t look like I wandered in by mistake. Before, I would have been worried about saying the wrong thing, but preparing for our meeting with King has taught me to guard my tongue.

As they crossed the final few blocks, Edge reviewed his plans, including the strategy he would employ once the bidding began. The auction would last for three or four hours, starting with a reception featuring live music and dancing, along with an exotic buffet and an open bar. After everyone had time to mingle and sample the tasty treats, the mercantile melee would begin.

The first half would feature the auction’s less-exclusive items, which were still quite pricey by the standards of a stage-two hunter. There wasn’t anything he planned to purchase during that portion, though he would keep an eye out for good deals on bulk resources that would benefit his people back home.

After a brief intermission, it would be time for the main event, where a carefully curated selection would be contested by the city’s richest residents. While most of the wares had been revealed in advance, there were a few mystery items intended to attract collectors and shopkeepers, as they were invariably hard to source and incredibly powerful. In the past, they had included Epic skill gems, Rare cores, and top-grade implants, along with mastercrafted weapons, luxury resources, and one-of-a-kind magitech devices.

The proceeds from the first half would be distributed during the intermission, making those funds available for the bidding wars that followed. Edge had a fat pile of Credits sitting in his account, and that didn’t include his share of the monster parts the crew was selling. Even so, he doubted that he could purchase more than one or two big-ticket items, which meant he needed to play this smart.

He was hoping to pick up some potent spellshots to replace the ones the System had awarded for killing the kaiju, each of which had been invaluable in their own way. That reminded him that he still had the Sensory Deprivation curse and the Skeleton Key round resting in his revolver’s chamber, which he was saving for a rainy day.

His musings came to an end when they arrived at the auction hall. Edge took in the sprawling structure as they climbed the marble staircase leading to the main entrance. The crown jewel of the gold terrace was a grand affair. In addition to countless rooms used by the staff, there were two main areas—an expansive lounge in the front and the auction floor hosting the main event.

The lounge had room for a thousand people. Several hundred were already in attendance, and more were arriving by the minute. It included an open area where attendees could mingle, a section with tables where people could enjoy their food and drinks, and a spacious dance floor filled with cored warriors capable of performing superhuman feats of athleticism.

When the trio arrived at the reception, they split up until the first round of bidding began. Trapper went off to mingle on her own, while Edge and Sakura stuck together. They would try to network as much as possible while searching for any sign of the Ancient.

Although he hadn’t had a chance to test it yet, his sensitivity to the taint should have sharpened considerably after equipping Monster Slayer. They would also keep their eyes open for any scions in attendance, as identifying other Unique core-bearers and tracking their movement was vitally important.

Edge was glad that his partner was with him. Sakura knew almost everyone on sight, including the free city’s elites. “The heads of the hunting societies are standing by the bar.” She gestured. “The man wearing the red hat is Deadeye Abon. He’s the chairman of the entire Hunters’ Association—not just this branch.”

As if summoned by her words, Abon met Sakura’s gaze, and his group started walking her way. Even without their weapons and armor, their equipment was impressive. Edge nearly blinded himself when he switched on Dragon’s Greed, revealing a fortune in runic equipment, magitech devices, and top-grade accessories.

He broke into a cold sweat when he focused on his skill senses. Abon and the hunters accompanying him were well past stage three, but his powers couldn’t discern the details of cores that were so much stronger than his own.

“I hope you’re enjoying the reception.” The chairman of the Association came to a stop and stuck out his hand. “I thought I spotted our newest arena champion. That was one hell of a fight. I haven’t seen someone evolve mid-match for ages, and that finale is the talk of the town. Give my regards to your partner—her axe magic was a sight to behold.”

“Thank you. Melenia will be thrilled to know you were watching.”

Edge and Sakura shook hands with Abon, then he introduced them to the rest of his entourage. Trove oversaw this branch of the lootfinders’ society. She had the thickest puppet body Edge had ever seen. Not fat, but squat, muscular, and almost twice as wide at the shoulders as anyone else in the room. She was friendly and gregarious, smiling in a way that put him at ease, despite the potent weaves of mana swirling within her core.

“I don’t believe you’ve registered with the treasure hunters.” Trove’s bright-blue eyes bored into his own. “You should join us when the Death-Race Dungeon unseals. It shouldn’t be more than a month or two, and I’d love to see what you’re made of when the stakes are life and death.”

“Prowler.” A sun-bronzed man shook Edge’s hand. “I oversee the free city’s beast hunters’ society. I’ve been hoping to meet you, as your crew has made quite a splash since arriving in town.”

“Likewise,” he replied. “Trapper is here too. She would love to meet you if you happen to cross her path.”

Prowler had no problem picking the crew’s leader out of the crowd. Edge had the sense that the man knew who everyone in the room was and where they were standing, effortlessly tracking every variable in his environment while scanning his surroundings for danger. He was exactly what you would expect for someone who made a career out of hunting high-stage beasts—alert, agile, and so stealthy that he faded into the background the moment you took your eyes off him.

Next came Bounty, the head of the harvesters’ society. She was short, with sea-green eyes, dark hair, and a perennial look of distraction, as if she were deep in thought and only conversing with a fraction of her attention. Despite her bookish demeanor, Edge had no doubt that Bounty could have wiped the floor with everyone in the Duo’s Cup at the same time. He did his best to make a good impression before introducing himself to the final hunter in their group.

“Talon.” A shiver ran down Edge’s spine when he gripped the man’s hand. The leader of the shadowkillers’ society moved with inhuman grace. Every inch of exposed skin was covered in a multilayered latticework of scars—evidence of countless battles fought and survived. Although it was contained, killing intent radiated from his posture like heat from a stovetop, and Edge’s instincts were screaming that if the man were so inclined, Talon could end his life in a single strike.

His amber eyes had vertical pupils like a viper, and motes of corrupted magic formed a mantle around his body. Edge almost ignited his core when he sensed the taint, but then he noticed that Talon was dressed from head to toe in equipment made from high-stage monsters, and the filth was emanating from the materials rather than the man himself. Even so, he found the hunter’s presence deeply disturbing, and he shuddered with relief when Abon’s party made their farewells and mingled with other attendees.

“I thought I spotted one of our most promising up-and-comers.” A redheaded woman of indeterminable years stuck out her hand as she approached and met Edge’s gaze. “I was hoping I’d get a chance to meet you.”

“Edge Vasher.” He shook while meeting her probing gaze.

If the dense mana emanating from her core wasn’t sufficient proof that he was standing in front of one of the strongest people in the free city, she was wearing a hunter’s badge with a platinum-rank emblem for the monster hunters’ society.

“Sky Renona,” she replied. “I’m Talon’s second in command.”

He didn’t recognize her from the feed, which meant Sky was one of the rare individuals who had chosen to remain anonymous until her stay on this world ended with the death of her puppet.

“I’ve never heard of someone stage two reaching platinum with the beast and monster hunters’ societies. Not to mention kicking off a promising career as a gladiator. I would love to speak at length later and get to know each other more… intimately.” Her flirtatious banter caught him off-guard, which Sky found deeply amusing.

After Edge introduced Sakura, they made small talk for a few minutes before the hunter bid him farewell with a wink. “That was intense,” he whispered. “I’m pretty sure all of them were stage four.”

“There are rumors that Abon is stage five,” she replied. “I’m glad we had a chance to meet him.”

The preeminent hunters weren’t the only powerhouses in attendance. Sakura pointed out three of the top ten gladiators, any of whom would be a formidable foe for the entire crew combined. The head of the city watch was almost as imposing, although Edge had the impression that she was only peak-stage three.

He spotted dozens of heavy hitters who were at least a half-stage above him, along with several people he couldn’t get a read on at all. The man who was speaking with the First Among Equals fell into that category—a grizzled fighter whose killing intent filled the room, although it wasn’t directed at anyone in particular.

“That’s Gill,” Sakura murmured. “He’s the First’s private enforcer. Rumor has it that his power is on par with the Wardens’ captains. Try to stay off his radar. He has a short fuse, and people who rouse his ire have been known to disappear.”

It was eye-opening and humbling to realize how many powerhouses called the free city home. Edge had grown an incredible amount in a short span of time and would continue bridging the gap at an unprecedented rate, but many of these dynamos had been thriving on Ord for years, if not decades.

It was a timely reminder that he still had a long way to go before he scaled the peaks of power and arrived at the summit. On the other hand, it was a weight off his mind to meet so many mighty individuals—people with sufficient strength to defend this planet from the monsters’ invasion who weren’t beholden to the Wardens or the Coalition.

Edge wound up attracting a small crowd as the reception wound down. His performance during the Duos’ Clash had made him an overnight celebrity, at least for the time being.

He spotted Helena and Beelo while making small talk, along with several gladiators he’d fought beside in the arena. The Best of the Worst was here too, and he spoke to Frederick briefly before grabbing a bite to eat. That was when Edge spotted the man with the wolf mask on the far side of the dance floor. He started to walk over when an announcement set the room into motion, and he lost the man in the crowd.

“The auction hall is open for business,” a woman’s voice rang out. “Please move to your assigned balcony or find an open booth. The catalogue will be synced with your Guides, and bidding will begin in thirty minutes.”

That marked the beginning of a mass migration. Most of the people walked through the row of doorways on the ground floor, although quite a few were heading for stairwells leading to the balconies reserved for VIPs.

Side by side, Edge and Sakura crossed the threshold and stepped into the enclosure beyond. Trapper had covered the deposit required to reserve a booth, though the fee would be refunded if they spent even a fraction of the Credits he was planning to blow.

He sat beside his friends, running his gaze across the room while everyone found their booths. The auction hall was shaped much like World’s End itself. A dais rose from the center of the floor, where the auctioneer and the assistant showcasing the goods would work their magic. The booths were arranged in concentric circles, and terraces lined with balconies rose five stories into the air.

Edge looked down when his Guide appeared of its own accord. The mote of golden light formed a screen, then opened to a menu he’d never seen before, which interfaced with the magitech device that facilitated the event.

The setup ensured no one could read over his shoulder when he was ready to make his play. While the participants could see each other, the bids themselves were anonymous—largely to prevent people harassing each other afterward if he was reading the situation properly.

A catalogue listing the offerings was synced with his display, minus the special selection that wouldn’t be revealed until the last minute. He browsed the extensive lists, sharing a grin with his friends while waiting for the fun to begin.

There were hundreds of amazing items on display, but Edge was only interested in a handful. After evolving to stage three, he had four more implant slots to work with. There were some decent options available in the Mortium exchange, but none of them were ideal for his purposes. Given the unique interactions between his Spatial Storage Rune and his avatars, he was hoping to pick up another core rune if possible. Unfortunately, he didn’t see any listed. It wasn’t too surprising, as runes were incredibly rare. His best chance of finding one was coming across a sealed chest while exploring an uncharted biome or conquering a dungeon.

There were, however, several powerful implants—custom devices made by master crafters that couldn’t be obtained anywhere else. He decided to acquire one during the auction, even if it cost him every Credit he had. The top-grade implants would be difficult to upgrade, but the power they offered was worth the effort.

Shortly after, the auctioneer and his assistant climbed the dais. The chatter died down when a spotlight sprang to life, illuminating the center of the room. The man was a natural showman. He welcomed the attendees and told a few jokes before the first round of bidding began. A pile of beast parts rose from an elevator leading to a vault below, and the assistant held samples up for the crowd to inspect as the opening offers came in.

Since he already knew what he wanted and it wouldn’t appear until the second half of the event, Edge sat back and enjoyed the spectacle, watching expressions shift in the blink of an eye as the people reveled in their victory or seethed in defeat.

Trapper wound up purchasing several items for Puppet Town, which he assumed Dialla would reimburse her for later. The trio squirmed with excitement when the parts they’d gathered from the Haunted Vale were featured. They wound up selling quite well from his perspective, and the crew’s leader was certainly pleased.

An hour and a half of intense bidding later, the auctioneer brought the first round to a close. “We will be taking a half-hour intermission to prepare for the second round. Complimentary refreshments are available in the main hall. Please return to your seats before the bidding begins.”

The three of them left to network with their peers, downing a few drinks while introducing themselves to as many people as possible. Edge returned to his booth twenty minutes later, looking forward to the fierce battle of acquisition that would begin any second.
