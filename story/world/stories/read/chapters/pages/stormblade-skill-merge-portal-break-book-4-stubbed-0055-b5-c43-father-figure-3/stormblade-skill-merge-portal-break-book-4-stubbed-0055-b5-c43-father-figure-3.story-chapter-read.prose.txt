The Stormkin Avatar awakens. Prepare yourself.

Ellen appeared in a flash of lightning, her back pressed against Kade’s. Her head cracked against his a second time, and stars filled her vision. Then the lightning disappeared, casting both of them into darkness.

Around her, a massive storm wall was mid-collapse. Grey-white clouds poured downward, forming a massive dome just like the one that had sat over Phoenix. Unlike that dome, though, this one darkened by the second until it blotted out even the faint sunlight that had thrown twilight and shadow across the eye of the storm a few seconds before. Before Ellen could even get her bearings, the only light came from rapid-fire flashes of blue-white, yellow, or red-orange lightning that came in waves and threw ephemeral shadows across them, with long periods of darkness between each set of flashes.

“What’s going on?” Kade asked as his breathing subconsciously synchronized with hers and his shoulders relaxed against the back of her neck. “I was just finishing up the Stormbreak rank-up.”

“You did finish it, then? That’s great,” Ellen mumbled. She was only paying a small amount of attention to Kade’s words, though. The rest of her thoughts were on the trial in front of them. “You sure took your time.”

“I did? How long? That must’ve been the time before the rank-up. I spent time with Dad.”

“You saw your dad?” The trial slipped to second in her mind instantly. That shouldn’t have been possible.

“Yes.” Kade ran through his explanation—the meeting with the Stormkin Avatar, the chessboard, and the questions about all the promises Kade had made and kept. As he talked, she nodded slowly. The back of her head rubbed against the nape of his neck, and he shifted a few times to avoid her ponytails’ hair-ties. “Then, after all that, I ranked up Stormbreak. It was a kind of…synthesis? A culmination, maybe? All the suffering and fear the ability caused coming together into a weapon I can use to create change—not just against the God of Thunder, but against…well, it sounds stupid, but the process that creates beings like him.”

“That’s great,” Ellen repeated.

“What’s going on with you?” Kade asked.

“I have no idea. We shouldn’t be here, though. We have nothing to rank up—especially not with Dual Skill Progression. So, I’m trying to figure out what’s happening, and why we’re here. Did you get a message just before I got here?”

“Yes.” Kade’s hand reached back and rested on Ellen’s knee. “The Tides of Darkness awaken. Pre—“

“—pare yourself. Yeah, I got a similar message, but it was about the Stormkin Avatar.”

Kade tensed. Ellen reached down and put her hand on top of his, pinning it in place. She understood, intellectually, what he was feeling. He’d just had a meaningful conversation with the man who’d raised him, for the first time in a year and a half. And now, here they were. If they went through with this, there was every chance that both Tides of Darkness and the Stormkin Avatar vanished, replaced by whatever this process created.

“We don’t have to go through with this, Kade,” she said, trying not to look over her shoulder at him as his shoulders shuddered against hers. “We have what we need to win.”

“Do we?” Kade asked.

“I believe we can win without this, yes.”

A long silence hung in the air. Lightning coursed around Ellen and Kade, and darkness pressed down on them in between the flashes. The smell of fresh rain filled her nose as she waited for Kade to make a decision.

Then he nodded. “I think…It’s not about winning or losing. Dad deserves to rest now. He’s done everything he set out to do, and he knows I’m the person he wanted me to be. Let’s keep pushing forward.”

The world shifted as I spoke.

Around us, the dome of dark cloud stayed. But where we’d sat suspended in the center of the storm’s eye before, the grit of the desert outside Phoenix crunched under our legs now. The flashes of red-orange, blue-white, and yellow lightning illuminated Palo Verde trees and Saguaro cacti, prickly pears, and tall, dark ocotillo.

The storm wall formed a barrier of black all around us, with one exception. Directly in front of me, looming in the darkness, stood a spire of stone. My mountain.

I thought I’d left this behind.

But then again, until ten minutes ago, I thought I’d left behind Dad, too. The Stormkin Avatar was supposed to have been nothing but an echo, a memory. Instead, it had turned out to be the real thing.

Ellen’s hand was still on top of mine. She lifted it and stood behind me, and I joined her. Lightning cracked around us, and shadow poured into the moment of brightness as soon as the arcing, brilliant blue faded.

So, this was about our auras. I closed my eyes and reviewed what I knew about mine.

The original form of my aura had been Negative Space. It had been about the void in my core. About the missing people who should have been there. At its center, it was about Dad. I’d realized that while I’d been learning it, of course. Without that understanding—that the broad-shouldered shape of my father had been essential to my being, and therefore, my aura—I wouldn’t have been able to properly push to B-Rank.

Then there’d been the Stormkin Avatar, the aura I’d come out of the Fallen Delvers Portal with. If anything, that one had been even more blatant. It wasn’t just the shape of Dad. It was everything he’d been as a warrior, in lightning form. At the time, I’d assumed it was only a memory of him.

But no. It hadn’t been. The conversation we’d just had proved that much.

And I’d told Ellen the truth. Dad deserved to rest now. This process was about giving him that, even more than it was about gaining a new weapon against the God of Thunder.

Lightning flashed. Shadows burst into existence, then faded to nothing.

“Where are we going?”

Ellen’s voice sounded both whispered in my ear and shouted from miles away at the same time. It was the only sound in the desert; the wind and rain and thunder were still silent.

“I’m not sure. We’re looking for the key to this puzzle. I want to say it has something to do with the Stormkin Avatar or the Tides of Darkness, but I’m not sure. It probably shouldn’t. Not if it’s trying to be something new.”

But whatever it was supposed to be, neither of us could see it.

We wandered the empty desert for a long, long time. For what felt like hours. Time had no meaning in the shadowy dome. Lightning pulsed around us intermittently, but aside from that, there was no indication that dawn was coming, or that the storm would ever falter.

Then, after so much time had passed that I was beginning to lose focus, I turned to Ellen. “I think we—“

A massive bolt of blue-white lightning hammered into the desert behind her. At the same time, a green-black flash illuminated the world behind me, and a yellow one pulsed repeatedly over almost two seconds to one side.

Ellen’s eyes narrowed against the light, then widened as she saw it. So did I.

Our shadows had meshed. They moved as one, neither mine nor hers.

As the lightning faded and darkness pressed in around us, she cleared her throat. “I think that’s the key. Let’s sit down and talk through this.”

“Yeah. Let’s do that.”

We sat facing each other, legs crossed and hands at our knees. Ellen spoke first. “My original aura was about fear. Specifically, it was about Bob, and my relationship with him. For a long time, I was afraid of him—not in a physical way, but because he was so…everywhere. So, when the time came for an aura, I got one of fear and anxiety.”

“And mine was about someone who was missing from my life, and the empty space he left behind.”

“No, it wasn’t.”

I stiffened. “What do you mean?”

“It was, but it wasn’t. You made that promise, to take care of your sister and make him proud. But that wasn’t about the space your dad could have—should have—filled. At least not directly. It was about trying to be the person he wanted you to be.”

Another peel of green-black lightning threw shadows across the world, including our enmeshed, intertwined one. I stared at it for a long few seconds as the lightning flickered and pulsed. The shadow moved as one, neither my shape nor Ellen’s breaking from it. When the pulsing green light finally vanished, I nodded slowly. “I think I understand.”

Ellen’s eyebrow raised. She didn’t say anything, though—whatever revelation she’d had, neither of us could finish this process until both had it, and she’d done all she could to guide me to it.

She’d done enough, though.

It was fear. For a long time, I’d been terrified. Not of my stepfather, like Ellen was of Bob. But of letting him down. Roger Gerald had been the man I wanted to be when I was growing up, and my promises to him had felt impossible. How could I possibly make him proud? How could I take care of Jessie as well as he had? The standard Dad had set had felt impossible to meet. The promise would always be there, but I’d never be able to fill it. That was why, even after the Fallen Delvers Tournament, I’d kept feeling the pressure of those promises.

But now?

Now, they were gone.

Ellen nodded. “You got it.”

“I did.”

It was simple. Ellen, even up until the moment she’d confronted her dad herself outside the wreckage of the Desert Wind Guild’s building, had feared her dad. She’d feared him from her hospital bed when I’d thrown him out. Feared him when Jessie, Jeff, Yasmin, and I had forced him to sign the papers freeing her from him. Feared him from the moment she’d realized what he was as a teenager. That fear had gotten deep inside her, and just being rid of Bob wasn’t enough to change that. She’d needed to force the issue herself.

She nodded. “Jessie’s filing the paperwork on my behalf. Together, we’re going to bring the fight to the Traynor Corporation. All the things I was afraid of…well, they don’t apply anymore. Not to us, not to Jessie, and not to our allies. He can’t hurt us anymore. I’m not scared of him anymore.”

Fear.

It was about fear. About mastering fear.

Just like Ellen had been afraid of Bob, and that fear had driven her decisions, I was afraid of Dad. Of not being the man he wanted me to be. I’d been afraid of that for…for a lot longer than he’d been gone. But my conversation with him had given me the reassurance I’d needed my whole life.

I was exactly who he wanted me to be. Myself.

And with that revelation, I wasn’t afraid of my promises anymore. I’d made him proud. Jessie was as healthy as she could be, as safe as I could get her, and living the life she wanted to live. Jeff—assuming he survived the next hour—would get a second chance. Ellen and I were together at the end of the road. We’d survived the long haul, and we’d stand as one.

The promises were all fulfilled.

The moment Kade truly understood, the discomfort that had plagued Ellen returned with a vengeance. She squeezed her eyes shut even as bile built in her throat. The shaking, vibrating sensation in her core redoubled, and she bent over like a kid who’d eaten too much candy, trying to stop the sensation. Her core felt like it was trying to vibrate itself apart.

No.

Not quite like that. As she sat there, doubled over from the sheer, stomach-churning shaking, she focused in on her core itself.

It was perfectly still.

How could it be perfectly still when the sensation of vibration was so overwhelming? That didn’t make sense…unless…the issue wasn’t with her core, but with something related to it. She checked her spells. Shadow Puppeteer. Black Hole, Abyssal Storm? All intact. All functional. Voidwalker? She’d used it so many times she’d know instantly if something was wrong. Echoing Black? No damage. No changes.

So, if it wasn’t a spell, it had to be her aura. Tides of Darkness was a powerful aura to unleash. It had changed the flow of more than one hopeless battle, ripping weaker enemies apart and giving Ellen’s allies the edge they needed against stronger ones. It felt too strong to change, too strong to lose. But at the same time, the problem wasn’t truly with her core, and it wasn’t with the spells she’d inscribed on its surface.

Ellen focused her inner eye on the Tides of Darkness. Or, more accurately, on what had been the Tides of Darkness. Her stomach settled, the cramping, half-pain/half-discomfort feeling vanishing as she read her new aura.

Unique Aura Learned: The Stormshade Hammer

The stormhead-shaded core within you has been strengthened. Your fear and terror have been mastered. Now, the Stormshade becomes your weapon. Consume large amounts of Mana to infuse all shadows within range with the Stormshade itself. The shadows inflict terror on and rip at any enemies caught within.

The moment I truly understood, Ellen doubled over. Her face was a rictus of nausea and pain, and I tried to reach for her to help her.

But our shadows interfered.

They rose as a single, twisted shape, looming over both the writhing form of Ellen and me. Four eyes locked onto me, their irises blazing green-black.

I stared back, eyes narrowing. The pillar of shadow seemed…familiar. I recognized an element of its core.

“Dad?” I asked.

The shadow didn’t move. Didn’t acknowledge my question at all. But I knew I was right. And if that shadow was—or had been—the Stormkin Avatar, then I was face to face with a representation of my own aura. And so was Ellen. The bile leaking from her lips wasn’t a problem. It was part of this trial, and we’d both come out of it stronger.

I pushed Ellen from my mind. She’d be totally fine. She was safe here. Right now, the only thing that mattered was the shadowy shape in front of me. I tightened my vision on it. And as I did, something shifted.

The battle trance hit me.

But instead of rage and anger, or even fury, I felt…nothing. No tightened throat, no pulsing in my ears. All of that was still there, ready to surge forth the moment I needed them. But the battle trance had changed.

When I’d hit S-Rank, I’d marveled at how fast my reaction time had gotten. I was fast. I could keep up with anyone short of SS-Rank. And that had served me well against Quelat and the Craftsman. It had kept me alive against the Placid King and Retan. But this? This was different. It wasn’t just that I was fast. It was that the shadow looming over me felt…slow.

I recognized this feeling. I’d felt it minutes earlier, when I’d seen that one opening in Dad’s defense and moved that one pawn, the only move I could make to turn the game in my favor. But now, instead of moving at the speed of chess, it moved at S-Rank.

The shadow vanished, surging into my core, and I read my new aura.

Unique Aura Learned: The Stormshade Anvil

In centering yourself, Kade Noelstra, you have transcended your fear. That fear has driven you your entire life. It has pushed you toward this moment, whether by your fallen family or threats to those you love. But it no longer binds you. A zone of charged energy covers the area around you, increasing the damage your lightning-based spells do to nearby enemies. The Stormshade Avatar can form a separate, independent fighter, sacrificing the aura’s effect to summon an ally at the cost of Mana. When neither effect is active, your reflexes and reaction time accelerate.

It wasn’t Dad. Not anymore. And that was okay. It was all the power I could have asked for. And I didn’t need him anymore. The time had come to let him rest.
