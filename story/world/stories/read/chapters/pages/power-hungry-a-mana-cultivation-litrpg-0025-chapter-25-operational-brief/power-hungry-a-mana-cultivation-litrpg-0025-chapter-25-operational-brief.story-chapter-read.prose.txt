Cale woke up to Fiara shaking him gently. The smell of fresh coffee was very welcome. He took the offered mug gratefully and enjoyed the warmth of it in his hands.

“It’s not the same if I don’t get to steal it off you,” Cale said. “But thanks.”

“I swear I should have just poured it on your head,” Fiara said, but she was suppressing a smile as she sat down.

“I didn’t get to ask,” Cale said and got up. He wasn’t even sore from sleeping on a cold stone floor. Perks of cultivation. “Why did you participate?”

Fiara was about to say something, but stopped mid-breath and sucked in her lips. It was pretty cute. She turned her sharp eyes on Cale. “Stop staring at me.”

“Then start answering me.”

“I don’t have an answer.”

“Huh,” Cale said and smiled. “So you just entered the competition on a whim? Didn’t take you for an impulsive girl.”

“You don’t know me that well,” Fiara said.

“Well I’m trying to,” Cale said and glared at her. “So loosen up.”

“Hmph, fine,” Fiara said. “I mean, I did know what the prize was, so that played a factor… But most importantly… I wanted to see if I could do it.”

“How far did you get?”

“I was fifth…” Fiara said and shrugged while holding her coffee mug in both hands. She was silent for a beat. “Better than I expected.”

“The competition was tough,” Cale said. “And Darius wasn’t holding back punches.”

“He actually wasn’t,” Fiara said. “Which is nuts. He could have killed us, you foremost.”

That made Cale think. Was he pissed? Alarmed? No. He knew a little bit about Darius now. Darius was pushing him. That meant his aloof bastard of a mentor was getting invested.

Imagine that… Pills aren’t the only things I’m winning here.

“There are almost two hundred and fifty young cultivators working for the Gray Lotus Syndicate,” Fiara said and looked down at her coffee mug. “Of those, the firm will sponsor twenty for the Corussi entrance exam. These recruits are all in the top twenty.”

“So you held your own,” Cale said, watching her closely. “Guess that means you’re better than your common sense told you.”

“Don’t push your luck, or I might steal that coffee back,” Fiara said and narrowed her eyes.

“I’ve killed for less,” Cale said and made her snort.

They sat for a while in a surprisingly comfortable silence. Then Fiara spoke.

They sat for a while in a surprisingly comfortable silence. Then Fiara spoke.

“How was that pill?”

The answer was obvious. Awesome. Even though Cale wasn’t working with any Mana, he could still feel the improved effects. For lack of a better word his whole body felt more... ready. Like he was ready to spring into action. He knew his reflexes were better, and his strength and speed greater. Cale was about to launch into a grin, but he suppressed it. It didn’t do well to gloat. Fiara must have wanted that pill more badly than him.

“It improved my physique,” Cale said “Darius didn’t mention that.”

“A Sulflower pill will improve all aspects of cultivation. Since you’re Body Composition, and don’t have access to spiritual organs like Mana Circuitry or Mana Core, you’re only privy to the basic improvements.”

“I thought I was only going to be able to manipulate mana more easily and efficiently,” Cale said.

Fiara looked at him confused, her mouth falling slightly ajar. “What do you think happened when you jumped ten feet into the air off your hoverboard to slam in a blur against Zavio?”

“I was... using mana?”

“It’s infuriating how much dumb, raw talent you have,” Fiara said and shook her head.

“You keep using that word,” Cale noted.

“What else do you account your success for?” Fiara asked, her tone sullen.

Cale thought of it for a moment. He didn’t really have an answer. He just tried really hard. Refused to quit, always sought to find an answer. Why? That was easy. He needed power. He needed power on some strange primal level that he couldn’t explain, but he also needed it for agency. Without agency he could never find out who or what he was, and that was not a pain Cale wanted to live with. Cale thought about Darius. His every move was based on his want to win. His want to succeed at the game. That is how he saw the world. Cale’s every move was based on needing to get stronger. It was not a want. It was a need. And with every win, it was starting to grow into an obsession...

“Mindset,” Cale said.

Fiara snorted in disbelief. “That’s ridiculous. This is just the kind of crap Darius goes on about. He’s just like you. Raw talent that he is blind to, and then he makes up stories about how he learned to see the world as a game, and that’s how he keeps winning.”

“Why is that so ridiculous?”

“You’re tellng me I can just decide to think in a certain way, and suddenly the world opens up to me? The world doesn’t work like that!” Fiara said. She got up and glared at Cale, her delicate hands curled into fists.

“I’m not saying it’s that simple,” Cale said, and also got up. “I’m just saying Darius might have a point.”

“And what point is that? I should be just like him? Just shift my mind into looking at everything like a game to be won? Is that what you do?”

Cale’s mouth slacked open and his eyes glazed. Fiara was repeating her question, but Cale barely listened. He remembered what Darius had said about Drive. About him being an unapologetic bastard. Darius saw everything like a game to be won. That made him an asshole. Cale needed to grow stronger, so he could be the master of his own fate. He needed to be stronger so he would not be threatened by the operatives of Big Six. So he could keep on growing. So he could eventually go his own way.

Cale felt a knot loosen inside him. It had nothing to do with mana. This sensation was higher in his torso, around his chest. He exhaled and the knot seemed to loosen further. Suddenly it was easier to be. Just easier to be in his own skin. Cale laughed. He felt amazing. He was a stranger in this completely crazy world that was downright cutthroat and murderous. But he would be okay. He would just have to do his own thing and do it as well as he could. He would just have to...

Follow my Drive.

There was no great sensation beyond that. Nothing changed in Cale’s energetic nature. His mana did not stir. But Aura did.

“What did you do?” she asked suddenly.

That snapped Cale back to reality.

“Hey?” Fiara said. “Are you even listening?”

“No,” Cale said distractedly and turned away from Fiara. “What did you sense Aura?”

“I don’t know yet...” Aura said. “You should cultivate.”

Cale immediately plucked a crystal from his pouch.

“Oh no you don’t,” Fiara said. “We are going for breakfast. After that briefing and then we’re off.”

“But—”

“No buts,” Fiara said and grabbed Cale’s hand as she started stomping out of the hall.

*

The breakfast was miserable after the lavish buffet of the Gray Lotus syndicate’s cafeteria. Cale looked down on his plate as a Sullied slid two brown bricks on his plate and poured a ladle of brown slop on it.

“Any vegetables?” Cale asked and attempted a smile.

The smile was not reciprocated and Fiara pushed him towards the table the rest of the recruits were sitting in.

“The boy wonder deigned to join us,” Galvon said with a sneer.

“You sound jealous,” Artan said with his eyes closed as he steadily forked the sloppy brick in his mouth.

“Shut up,” Galvon said.

“How are you feeling?” Sintra asked from across the ten seater table.

“Good,” Cale said as he sat down next to Ragnak at the edge of the table. “Thanks for asking.”

Cale stabbed one of the bricks with a fork and bit down. It tasted like slightly burnt cardboard. It was chewy and sticky and his saliva contracted a bitter aftertaste. the bar of whatever horrid hell was tough to swallow and Cale grabbed Ragnak’s water to force it down.

“What is this?” Cale asked with a suffering face.

There were some chuckles about the table. Cassidy smiled at him. “First time in the Citadel?”

“Uhhuh,” Cale said and poked at the brown brick. Poking probably didn’t make it tastier, but it wouldn’t hurt to try.

“It’s spagon,” Cassidy said.  “A tuber that grows near the edge of the Citadel. Mortals grow it there. Eat two of these bars in the morning, and you’re all set for the day.”

“I’m not sure I can get through two of these...” Cale muttered.

“Eat!” Ragnak said and clapped a hand on Cale’s shoulder. “If you don’t, you won’t keep up with me.”

“How do you not even know what spagon is?” Madia said and glared at Cale. Fiara shot a sharp glance at Cale from the other side of the table.

“I was... raised elsewhere,” Cale said.

“Where?” Madia asked sharply.

“In a place they taught me to not speak with my mouth full,” Cale said and stuffed half a spagon brick in his mouth. He started chewing, looking at Madia with a deadpan stare. She bristled, her face turning slightly pink, which was a fun contrast to her blonde hair, tied in a tail.

“There’s also bread,” Risto said and threw a piece at Cale, flicking it with his wrist. Hard. Hard enough to kill a mortal. Cale caught it, and his chair rocked back. There was a new texture to his movements and his reflexes. Everything felt just a slight bit smoother. Maybe Risto noticed that too, because he lifted a subtle eyebrow.

“Thanks,” Cale said between his teeth and Risto smiled lazily at him. His hand hurt, but he would not show it. Cale looked at the bread. It was dark and hard. Cale tapped the table with it and there was no give. He managed to rip a piece of it with his teeth, and he wondered if a mortal could do the same.

“Goddamn prison food,” he muttered to himself.

After the breakfast Fiara led them to a room upstairs in the four story fortress. In there Darius was waiting with the ridge-faced Sullied, sitting on a simple wooden table.

“Sit down,” Darius said.

There were only six chairs. Cale watched the recruits rush to them and scramble. Madia and Ragnak escalated it to the point that the chair cracked and broke. Cale chuckled to himself and leaned against the wall next to Fiara.

Darius watched them with an amused look on his face. “You know, you’re going to hear me just as well if you stand.”

None of them had anything to say to that. Darius produced a stack of papers.

“"Despite some of you showing disappointing levels of will to win, you all scraped past my standards. Lucky you—I’m not sending anyone back. You’re qualified for the mission and will be eligible for a full compensation of contribution credits based on your performance.”

“Credits?” Cale whispered to Fiara. “What he mentioned on the jet?”

“Yes,” Fiara whispered. “Good performance with the syndicate is rewarded with points you can use to buy elixirs, gear upgrades and—”

Darius stopped and looked at them under his brows as he leaned with both hands against the table. “You two are really cute, but you do know that we are all cultivators and can clearly hear you whispering five feet away from us?”

“Ahem, yeah,” Cale said and rubbed his neck. “Sorry about that.”

Fiara shot a glare at Cale but said nothing.

Darius continued. “The mission objective is simple. Secure my ascension. I am at the peak of Core Formation, and I am ready to attempt a breakthrough into Core Crystallization. This means we are going to raid a spire. Is anyone familiar with the protocol?”

Madia’s hand shot up. Darius nodded and she stood up. “A spire raid consists of three phases. Escorting a payload, cleansing the area of corruption and delving the spire.”

“Good answer,” Darius said and Madia blushed a slight pink and smiled to herself.

“I’ll elaborate on that,” Darius said. “The first mission is to deliver the payload, which is a shitload of mana crystals I have attuned to myself, as well as my Integra and items related to my Drive. And of course the equipment required to burn away the corruption.”

Darius started sliding pieces of paper around the table. Cale went and collected two copies for himself and Fiara.

“Here’s a treasure map. If you don’t know how to read a map, it's high time you learn. I assume one of you knuckleheads has some navigation skill. I know Fiara does, and she will be the leader of the mission while I am away clearing the way. Your job is to get the payload to the spire and defeat or escape any threats along the way. You are going through an area that has already been cleared by the Sullied and I will go past it before you, but you know how things go. I assume you’ll face some resistance from the Citadel.”

Darius stopped leaning over the table and started pacing back and forth as he spoke. “The second phase will require you to remove the corruption of the Citadel from the surrounding area around the Spire and inside it. This, as some of you might know, will allow me to power up the Spire for its original purpose, advancement.”

“Original purpose?” Cale asked. “The Omnic Manifest designed the Spires?”

Darius smiled and was about to say something, but clearly stopped himself. He adjusted his tie and said: “This used to be the Nevani capital.”

That sentence ran through Cale like a jolt of lightning. This place? This hellhole had been the Nevani’s seat of power? The thought felt off, like trying to imagine a sunken palace beneath a polluted swamp. But if it was true…

A whole history was buried here. Treasures. Artifacts. Knowledge.

Cale was starting to understand why the corporations were collaborating in this place through the Sullied. The Big Six weren’t just here to hold back the Citadel. They were here for power. Cale thought back to the Warseed. The ancient monster was pushing into the inner sanctums of the Citadel and probably finding unimaginable treasures, True Integra and the like.

Cale found himself clenching his fists. He was jealous. He wanted to take a shot at the Citadel. He wanted the treasures. But more than anything he wanted the knowledge. They had to be here. If not here, then where?

In a sense this was the origin place of cultivation.

That meant a lot of things, but one thing rose to Cale’s mind foremost. He could find answers here.

Cale imagined vast and majestic libraries with rows of tomes and scrolls of ancient times in high shelves. And databases and monitors of mana glass from their advanced technology, now seemingly half lost, as only imitations of their lost power remained.

Darius’s voice cut through Cale’s daydreams and he focused back. He needed to be alert and in the now to get what he wanted.

“The third phase will be the most difficult. Which is why we will have three Mana Circuitry tier Sullied with us. My personal capture, DR-12 will be responsible of them. I will take the two with me ahead, and DR-12 will travel with you. He will not participate in any fighting unless Fiara calls on him. You need the experience. Immediately after the Spire is cleansed, I will enter the Ascension chamber of the upper sanctum and your objective is simple. Anyone care to tell me what it is?”

Madia’s hand shot up again. Darius nodded at her.

“Activating the spire requires the power room ritual to be completed. We will clear it of Praetori and potential mutated mana fiends. Then we will ready the ritual and wait for you to begin.”

“Touche,” Darius said. “You almost make up for your bad performance in my test with this.”

There were some sniggers and Cale couldn’t help but to snort into his fist. Madia turned sharply to glare at Cale, her face flushed pink.

“The better job you do at clearing the Spire and its surroundings, the easier time you will have defending it. The Omnic Manifest will sometimes react to spire activations and attack it. That is why we have the Sullied with us. DR-12 is Mana Circuitry, stage two. He and the other stage one Mana Circuitry Sullied will defend the perimeter.”

Darius gestured at the ridge-faced sullied who bowed. Cale watched everyone, but no one seemed to ask the obvious question. What the hell kind of a name was DR-12?

“Why is the threat level so low?” Ragnak asked. “Core Crystallization is high rank. Why doesn’t the Citadel try to prevent you from advancing.”

“It doesn’t think actively like that,” Darius said. “It just follows its programming. The Spires suitable for Core Crystallization advancement are here on the outer edges of the Citadel. But any advancement beyond that...”

Darius grinned to himself. “The stories are wild. Soul Core requires you to go deep to find the higher spires. The operation to ascend takes months to prepare and launch.”

Even Risto’s lazy smile faded. He sat up straighter and listened. Cale leaned closer.

“I was in the crew when director Wren ascended. We faced a horde of tier four Praetorians. That’s also where I found my current mana attuner, which is tier five. This can be a chance for some of you to score some loot that will define your cultivation careers. Big risk, big reward. That’s how I like to play.”

There was a reverent silence reserved for war veterans telling their tales. Darius broke it by snapping his fingers so loud the room rang.

“But don’t get it twisted. You are here for me. If you get some experience along the way for yourselves. Great. But if you screw this up and lower my chances of advancement, I’ll make sure you’ll wish I had made you a Sullied.”

Cale glanced at the ridge-browed Sullied, who kept his head down. DR-12...

“That’s the general idea,” Darius said, clapping his hands together and rubbing them. “I’ll go ahead and get myself set up. You will get the payload to the Spire. You shouldn’t need to worry about sabotage, since the Gray Lotus has scouting around the perimeter, and the next fortress is seventy five miles away. Unlikely, but not impossible. If you get even a whiff of the other factions, you report it immediately to my secretary Fiara, and she will message me. Understood?”

There was general nodding and voice of assent.

“Good,” Darius said. “I’ll see you there. Make sure to bring supplies for three days. DR-12 will help with the supplies and get you guys going. Remember. Don’t screw this up, or you’re done.”
