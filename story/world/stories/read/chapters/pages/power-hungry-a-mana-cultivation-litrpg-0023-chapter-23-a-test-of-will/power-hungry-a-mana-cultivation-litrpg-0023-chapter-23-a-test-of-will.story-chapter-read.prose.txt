“What kind of a test, sir?” Madia asked in a much higher pitch than before.

“One that lets me know you’ve got what it takes,” Darius said as he made his way towards the dais. He removed the small table and sat down on the chair like it was his personal throne and crossed his legs.

Cale and the other seven recruits formed a loose line facing towards Darius.

“You eight are Gray Lotus Syndicate’s best candidates for the Corussi entrance exam. So we expect a great deal from you. You are also going to help me advance to Core Crystallization. Now, personally I don’t give a shit about your entrance exam. But I need to know you are up to snuff in assisting me with my advancement. And if you can’t do that, I don’t see why you should attend the entrance exam.”

Some of the recruits shared nervous glances and muttered to each other. Few of them like Risto and Cassidy only stood still, relaxed. Cale glanced at Fiara who was standing away from the line. Her expression was unreadable.

“The test is simple. I will apply spiritual pressure and you will try to approach me. The one who gets closest gets a prize. The ones who don’t meet my arbitrary standards can find their own ride home. And Fiara, you might want to step towards the doors. I don’t plan on going easy on these guys.”

“Actually,” Fiara said. “I would like to participate.”

Darius tilted his head and watched Fiara. Then he nodded to himself. “Suit yourself.”

Cale flashed a smile at Fiara, but she was determined to not look at him, even though she came up next to Cale in the line, pushing Ragnak away.

“What’s the prize?” Madia asked in that high voice.

“A divine Sulflower pill.”

A wave of excitement passed through the recruits. Even Risto perked up.

“How refined?” he asked.

Darius smiled and plucked the pill out of his pocket. It was large and heavy, like a golden pearl. He smiled fondly at it. “It was made by Ravia Wren.”

Cale had no idea what were the implications of a Soul Core cultivator crafting a pill, but the other recruits were beyond impressed. Even Cassidy straightened up and was no longer idly smirking. Her face was flat and focused. Cale looked at Fiara for more clues. Her jaw had dropped.

“Let’s go!” Ragnak shouted. “I’ll swear on my name, Ragnak Terr, that I will be the one swallowing the pill!”

“Come and get it,” Darius said and put the pill in his pocket and got comfortable on the chair.

Immediately a crushing pressure landed on Cale. Like someone had tossed an invisible lead blanket on him. A really thick one. Cale took a step towards the dais. It got worse. Another step. This one was uncomfortable. The next one was painful. The blankets kept piling up.

“You need to respond to this!” Aura said urgently. “Call mana from your body and circulate it. Expand it to brace yourself. This is not a physical attack. It is a mana attack, and it needs a proper response.”

Cale stopped moving and breathed. He watched the others take pained steps. The pigtail girl was already on her hands and knees, crawling forward. Ragnak was too. Fiara was still standing among the others, but her knees wobbled with every tiny step she managed to take.

Cale breathed and called upon the mana. It came out of his muscles, bones and tendons and even blood in trickles, and gathered around his solar plexus. He started cycling it, breathing with it. Once he had collected enough and attuned it properly with his breath, he imagined a thin matrix, like a fishing net expand from his navel into his extremities, from toes to top of the head. He kept the matrix tight and found it was easier to advance.

He looked at the competition racing towards the dais.

Cassidy's smirk was long gone, but she still moved forward with slow, careful precision. Risto, his usual laziness erased, was silent and relentless. Ragnak was on all fours, growling hard with effort.

Cale passed Ragnak and the pigtail girl. Then he passed Fiara. Every step was still a struggle, but he managed to move forward. Galvon looked like he was dragging himself forward as Cale passed him. Cale kept pushing and soon he was catching up to the front, which was a race between Cassidy, Risto and Madia.

Cale kept working with his mana but even that did not help with the pain. It was starting to get from noticeable to alarming, and Cale knew it would be excruciating soon. He kept on advancing. One step at a time.

And each step he took closer to the lounging and smiling Darius, the worse the pain and pressure got. Now it felt like Cale was carrying a tow-truck on his back. The pressure was pressing him down and backwards.

It didn’t matter. It was just pain. What mattered was surviving, improving, advancing. He passed Madia who had fallen on her knees.

She immediately grabbed his leg and toppled him. He was slammed on the ground knees first with the power of the goddamn roof crashing down on him.

Cale hissed with pain. He tried to get up, but his shaking shoulders could not hold him up. Cale gritted his teeth and kicked backwards. There was a crunch and a yelp. Extremely satisfying and well worth the effort.

Cale crawled forward. The world was pain. His vision was swimming, blackening and narrowing with each passing second of agony.

“Cale, you are starting to take damage. I would not advise you to continue.”

Cale felt it. His bones felt like they had fractures on it. His skin felt like it was a flame. His lungs burned with every breath. And Darius, that smug bastard lounged and smiled. Cale would not give up. He felt a warm trickle of blood run from his nose to his mouth.

Cale turned his neck slightly to the side, to see. Everyone had been reduced to a crawl. Some had completely stopped moving. Risto was still coming, but his movements were slow and pained. Cale had passed the guy with the medallion, but he was on a second wind, clearly advancing. Cassidy was dragging herself on the floor with a dagger.

Cale dragged himself a bit further, but then hit a complete wall. The agony had mounted to levels that had been previously unimaginable to Cale. He could not go on. There was only pain. It had to stop. Please make it stop.

No.

There was still some defiant, willful part of Cale that was in the fight. It was a small voice among the internal screams of pain, but Cale’s mind lunged for it and held on to it. The fighting spirit growed in his mind. It gave him hope. Maybe, maybe he had a chance.

“H—heal,” Cale managed to rasp.

“What?” Aura asked. “Cale stop talking. Stop moving. Just wait for Darius to stop.”

“Heal me.” Cale croaked, his voice barely audible.

“Oh. OH! Cale you dumbheaded idiot, why didn’t you say this earlier!”

A surge of warmth passed through Cale’s body. The pain eased. It was still pressing on him, but his vision unblurred. His world was no longer a singular point of complete agony. He could think.

[A.U.R.A skill advanced.]

[Molecular Tissue Regeneration: 4]

Cale crawled. The pain was stupendous and almost overwhelming, but he could function. He could push forward. And Aura kept healing his body. Another drag forward. Then another. One by one, Cale inched closer towards the dais. Every second was as long as eternity, every breath was a sharp reminder of the cost of being alive right now. But Cale would not give. He clung to that fighting spirit that had raised its head in his mind. He clung to it and he struggled forward.

The blood eventually started bursting from his eyes and ears, but the healing kept him going. Cale tilted his head, a big mistake because his neck lanced with pain. But he had to see. He had to see Darius.

Darius was not smiling anymore. He was leaning forward and staring at Cale dead serious.

Cale reached out with a hand, but the effort was too much. The dais was just beyond his reach. His arm fell on the stone floor and no amount of struggle could get it to move. He was done. Cale felt the last dregs of his spent strength leave him. Darkness took him.

“Cale!” Aura shouted, and that was the last thing Cale registered.
