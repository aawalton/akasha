“Swarm.”

The word rang out, and the world listened. Eric felt reality constrict around him, felt mana charge the air; and then there was nothing. The beast half-turned, confused.

Did it not work? Eric wondered, swallowing dryly. He braced himself all over again. He’d have to stake it all on this jump—

A cockroach skittered out of the forest. It saw the beast and leaped at it, jaws clamping shut around its toe. The beast jumped, kicking its leg, but the cockroach clung on. Another cockroach scuttled out of the woods, then another and another and another, until cockroaches poured out of the woods and threw themselves at the beast. Slick brown bodies clambered up its limbs and chewed under its fur. The beast yelped and screeched, biting the cockroaches off its limbs, but it only had one mouth, and the cockroaches had many. The roaches tore into it, shredding the beast, and in a few seconds, it was nothing but a pile of rapidly-diminishing bones on the floor.

“Cy!” Giving the cockroaches a wide radius, Eric hurried over to his familiar’s side and scooped up the little rabbit. Cy laid unconscious in his hands. He checked the rabbit’s vitals as best he could, but he was breathing steadily and his heart was beating, so as far as Eric was concerned, all was in order. Maybe he just exhausted himself and passed out. Putting the rabbit in a pocket for safer safekeeping than his shoulder, Eric crossed to his abandoned saber and picked it back up, fat lot of good it had done him. Still, it looked cool, and there was probably something to that channeling-magic thing, so he kept it.

The cockroaches skittered off, and Eric wandered over to the bones. Using the saber in case there were some straggler roaches, he checked under the bones for some kind of drop or reward. At the very bottom of the pile, he found a glass vial of yellow mana potion and a few crinkled foil packages that the cockroaches had already chewed through.

Eric shrugged. It wasn’t much, but it was something. He snatched the potion up and downed it, then grimaced. It tasted like artificial banana-flavored cough syrup, but double the medicinal flavor and half the banana. And he didn’t even like artificial banana flavoring. Yuck.

Nothing happened for a few seconds, and then he felt warmth stir in the pit of his stomach. His exhaustion receded, and color returned to the world around him as mana flowed into him. He activated Regeneration and set it to work fixing the damage that remained on his leg, then turned to the open gate. The mist swirled gently, wafting back and forth on its own power. Go deeper, or turn back?

He pulled up his map. There was a dungeon just on the other side of the gate, just off to the right a little ways. In comparison, the safe zone he’d come from was an hour’s walk back, if not more, and there were lots of snakes and cockroaches along the way.

Fuck it. I’m not doing that walk again. And who knew? Maybe the gate would respawn its guardian if he walked away. He wasn’t that injured and he’d just topped up his mana. He could do it. Whatever awaited him in the mist: he could deal with it.

Limping just a little, he stepped through the gate and into the fog. It was a warm late-summer day outside, but a chill fell over him as he stepped into the gloom. The fog blocked out the sun and wreathed the world around him in a soft gray. Mana swirled in the fog, but Eric could still see past it to catch glimpses of monsters in the distance. He took a deep breath, and mana flowed into him, collecting in his body. The thick mana in the fog was probably for some nefarious purpose, if he knew the Game (and by now, he was starting to get the hang of it), but it also helped a Mage like him.

He walked softly and kept his head on a swivel. The poisoned dagger hung loosely in one hand, and he kept the saber at the ready on his hip. A few gold blobs lingered close by the path. He couldn’t see them with his ordinary vision, and Mana Sight left out the details, but he could feel their eyes on him nonetheless. They were watching him. Waiting. Looking for weakness.

Not sure how much weakness they’re waiting for if a bum leg isn’t enough, he thought to himself with a wry smile, not that he was mad about it. The longer they sat by and let him limp along, the further he got to the safe room where he could sit and heal his leg with no interference. That, and check on Cy. Get the guy a carrot and some water, or whatever it was rabbits actually ate. He vaguely recalled that carrots were like rabbit donuts or something, and that they mostly ate lettuce or the like. Damn, imagine a world where carrots are like donuts to you. That would suck. Oh no, I can’t have a carrot, I’m trying to lose weight. Forget the ranch dressing; even the carrots themselves were too heavy for a dieting rabbit.

He raised his brows. It was a good thing he wasn’t a rabbit in a lot of ways, but especially that one. He’d be a billion and a half pounds from all the gas station junk food he’d been ingesting lately.

I’m so tired I can’t keep my mind from wandering. Casting magic really took it out of him in ways he hadn’t expected. Then again, he was essentially flexing muscles that hadn’t existed days ago. Of course it would wear him out.

The path had become white stone at some point, and his boots clicked over the hard surface. The fog pushed close, so close it obscured the edges of the path. Eric strode steadily forward, eyes ahead, where he knew his prize laid. Just a little further, there should be a safe zone. Just a little further.

Soft footsteps echoed his, tap-tapping on the path behind him. Eric stopped, and the footsteps stopped. He started, and the footsteps began again. Am I really hearing anything, or is it just the echo of my feet? With his tired brain, he couldn’t be sure, but he knew he didn’t like it. He sped up; not taking more steps, but stretching his legs out to eat up more path with each step. At first, the footsteps began to grow softer and softer, and then, abruptly, they broke pace, tap-tap-tapping at a rapid staccato as whatever it was closed the distance.

Don’t like that! Eric sprinted too. He could see the safe zone now, the construction breaking out of the forest. It looked like an ordinary house, though an older one with a wraparound porch fully equipped with a bench swing that looked absolutely ominous in this weather. He sprinted up the stairs, shoved the screen door open, and all but fell through the door behind it, then whirled.

“Welcome to the safe zone,” a familiar automated voice intoned.

Something stood in the fog, just out of sight. It was human-sized and upright, walking on two legs, but not quite human shape. He couldn’t make out any details, but he was sure of that: not human-shaped. It was twisted. Misshapen, somehow.

Wait—my Mana Sight isn’t picking it up. He squinted, trying to swap to the alternative of viewing the voids, but in the fog, it was harder to see the spaces where something wasn’t. As if it was aware of what he was doing, the second he tried, the thing vanished into the fog. He didn’t see it leaving. It didn’t turn and walk away. One second it was there, and the next it wasn’t. It was as if it despawned, or simply vanished.

Eric rubbed his hands over his arms. Creepy.

He looked around him, finally taking it all in. He stood in what appeared to be an old-timey country store, with the ye olde font on the price tags and all. The place had a small section with the essentials that seemed built for the desperate or for locals, with everything from cold medicine to laundry detergent on the shelves, along with a few dusty cans of beans and a very small refrigerator section that mostly sold ice cream. There was also a gift shop section—the part with the ye olde font on the tags—that sold old-fashioned candies, cheap mockups of old timey dolls and wooden toys, and so on. There was even a clothes section, which he was both frightened and excited to see. Maybe he could ditch the tire-track t-shirt! Though… only for a tourist t-shirt, which he wasn’t sure was any better.

There was also, enhearteningly, a small café. He wandered over and checked the menu. It wasn’t extensive, but it sold sandwiches (breakfast and otherwise) with sides, coffee, and ice cream. Wonder if I can order myself something somehow? If the gas station coffee refilled itself, why shouldn’t he be able to order from the unmanned café? Worst case, he clearly had unmitigated access to the flat top and the café’s walk-in freezer. He wasn’t exactly a short-order cook, but he could turn on a grill and slap some eggs on it with the best of them.

Everything had that lived-in, caked-on filth that small-town stores and cafes had without fail, the brown smudge on the wall from a thousand hands accidentally touching the same spot at a pinch point so baked into the drywall that it had just become part of the wall, the paint worn off the railing where every tourist had grabbed it to catch themselves from the dip worn in the wooden floor. It was dirt, but it was familiar and comforting, somehow. If a general store didn’t have that kind of grime and wear, it just wouldn’t feel right.

Eric nodded to himself, already happy with his upgrade. He could cook real food, and he had new wardrobe options. The only thing that could make this room better was a bed.

And we’re not out of hope yet! He poked his head around the corner in the kitchen. There’d been a second story. Surely there was a way up… and there it was! A commercial outdoor metal door swung open to reveal a slightly-rickety wooden staircase. Eric ascended it to the second story to find that he’d hit the jackpot. Beds. Toilets. Showers.

I’m in heaven, he thought, dropping backward onto the first bed he encountered.

All the beds and rooms were clearly someone’s bed. A family had lived up here, then worked downstairs in the local general store-cum-tourist trap. There was a child’s room, with a racecar bed and fire engine wallpaper, a teen’s room, all dark grunge and rock posters from bands that hadn’t been relevant in decades in a true mom, it’s not a phase kind of way, and the master bedroom, with its generic adult beige paint and beige comforters. There was a guest room too, with generically pleasant flowers printed on its sheets, but the mattress wasn’t nearly as nice as the master bedroom’s.

Standing in the hallway, Eric looked around him, able to catch a glimpse of all four rooms from where he stood. I wonder which one was the short order cook. For a split second, his brain generated an image of a toddler rising from his racecar bed, giving a big stretch, then strapping on a stained apron and waddling downstairs to handle the breakfast rush. Eric chuckled at the idea. No, I don’t think it was him.

It was far more likely that the family ran the store, while they hired someone to be their short order cook. He poked around a little longer and found that most of the family’s accoutrements were still here. The master bedroom featured women’s clothes in size XXL or larger, the toddler’s room was full of worn and clearly second-hand toddler clothes, but the grunge teenager’s room had potential. He held up a pair of ripped skinny jeans that were more hole than cloth and raised his brows. There’s no way this stuff is still in. This kid was definitely one of those “born in the wrong era” types.

Skinny jeans and his still slightly expanded waistline aside, there were clothes in here that he could fit into that didn’t make him want to experience the tire track on his current shirt personally. Some of the vintage band tees weren’t in horrific shape, and the teen also had a few pairs of mesh shorts (probably for gym) that were a slightly more muted color than his current bright yellow. The kid’s unfortunate taste in jeans meant he couldn’t squeeze into a single pair, but if there were whole homes out there being used as safe zones, there was still hope for him yet. Eventually, he’d find a pair of jeans out in the wild that fit him. Eventually.

Having replaced the roadkill shirt with a black t-shirt from a band he hadn’t heard from in years and the bright yellow sweatshirt shorts with mesh shorts in a relatively muted maroon, Eric wandered back downstairs to the café. Shrugging at no one, he wandered up to the counter. A scrap of paper had been scrawled on in ballpoint pen, each line repeated over and over again until it was dark and thick: RING BELL 4 SERVICE. The sign was helpfully cellophane taped to a worn brass bell, with a helpful arrow pointing at the bell in case you couldn’t figure it out.

He looked over the menu, then with a might as well attitude, dinged the bell and said aloud: “Hot turkey sandwich combo with fries and a Sprite please.”

There was a pause, and then the flattop snapped on. The squeeze bottle full of oil lifted itself up and squirted oil over the rapidly-heating plate, which began to hiss and pop moments later.

Eric raised his brows, a little surprised despite himself. He sat down at the counter to watch and rest his leg. Dinner and a show.

Something shifted in his pocket. A muffled voice asked, “What’s that? Smells delicious.”

“Good morning, Cy.” Eric fished the little rabbit out of his pocket and set him beside him on the counter. “I ordered a sandwich. You want something?”

He yawned. “Leafy greens.”

Eric dinged the bell again. “And a plate of lettuce and spinach, thank you.”

With a leg to heal and no pressing desire to head back into the mist, Eric spent the rest of the day in the safe zone or on the porch, practicing Firebolt. He paused between bolts, looking at his hand. The whole time he’d been trying to learn Firebolt, he’d held back, using only the absolute minimum of mana, but that was exactly what had been keeping him from success. If he’d only committed all his mana to the task from the start, he would’ve been able to cast Firebolt as soon as he’d learned it. He’d just been afraid. Afraid of wasting his resources… and as a result, he’d wasted so much mana trying to figure it out, when if he just committed it all in one big chunk, he would’ve known his problem in a heartbeat.

I learned an important lesson. Commit. Don’t be afraid to use resources. In this new world he found himself in, not using a resource could be as deadly as depleting it.

A chime played, and a television he was completely sure hadn’t been there moments ago flickered to life in the store part of the room. Eric turned back inside. It was time to watch today’s episode.
