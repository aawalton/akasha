Flynn couldn’t shake the feeling that they were descending into a tomb. Dark stone surrounded them on all sides. There was just enough light to keep them from missing a step and tumbling to the bottom of the steps.

After several dozen steps, they came to a landing that was lit by a dozen tall candelabras. The flickering light seemed to illuminate their surroundings and accentuate the dreariness of the place more than provide adequate lighting. The main feature illuminated was a large door. It was the only feature on the landing other than the candelabras.

The door was made of a gray wood and stood out from the black stone that surrounded it. A stone arch with stairs descending into darkness was painted onto the door. It reminded Flynn of what it looked like from above, before they went down the stairs.

If Nyx had any reservations about the place, she didn’t show it. Neither did Throm, though Flynn thought the mercenary was likely portraying more confidence than he truly felt. Or maybe not. The man had been pretty cavalier about everything they had come across so far, no matter how dangerous.

She stepped forward and pushed the door open.

What they found on the other side of the door was completely different than the dark landing and staircase they had just descended to reach it. The room looked almost exactly like the Mercenary and Adventurer Guild offices. A clerk sat behind a counter much like the other offices.

That was where the similarities ended. The woman behind the counter had dark hair and wore a black hooded robe. Her skin was incredibly pale, as if it was rarely exposed to the sun. A battle scythe with a wicked-looking curved blade leaned against the wall behind her.

“Are you here to explore the Deeper Realm?” the hooded woman asked in a monotone voice.

“Yes we are,” Nyx asked in an overly cheerful voice. “My friends have never experienced this little dungeon party you put on.”

The Church of the Deeper Realm clerk gave the blue haired woman a look that conveyed both contempt and apathy.

The woman pulled out a book and opened it to a blank page. “Name and affiliation.”

“They don’t have any affiliation,” Nyx said.

The woman closed the book. “This is a Transcendence Council monitored dungeon. You must be associated with one of the temples or guilds to run the dungeon.”

Nyx forced out a breath. “I am affiliated with the Temple of the Storm.”

The clerk raised an eyebrow but didn’t say anything. She did open the book back up and repeated her question. “Name and affiliation.”

“Nyx Wilder.” She grimaced as she said her last name. “As I said, I am affiliated with the Temple of the Storm.”

“And them?” the clerk asked, not looking up from the book.

“Flynn Finnigan and Throm Hartgrove.” Nyx answered.

“Since they are unaffiliated, you will have to vouch for them.”

“That’s not a problem,” Nyx said.

“Are you familiar with the requirements of this dungeon?” the pale woman asked.

“Yes I am.”

“Again, you will be responsible for making sure these two,” the clerk waved at Throm and Flynn, “follow all the rules and aren’t involved in any mishaps.”

“Of course.”

The woman silently wrote in her book for several moments, turning pages back and forth as she wrote on different pages. She then closed the book and looked up at Nyx, barely glancing at the two men. “The dungeon has not been entered today, but it was cleared yesterday. That means all monsters have respawned and all rewards have been regenerated. The monsters will not have had time to level up, though, so this will be a pretty basic run for you.”

Nyx clapped her hands and cheerily declared, “That will be perfect.”

The clerk stared at her with a blank expression, blinked twice, then said, “You may enter the dungeon antechamber.”

A pale hand extended toward an arched portal on the side wall that had not been there an instant before.

Nyx nodded at the clerk then turned to Flynn and Throm. “Let’s go. I will tell you everything you need to know once we’re inside.”

The group promptly went through the portal and found themselves in a chamber that looked almost exactly like the landing at the bottom of the stairs before they entered the room they’d just left.

When Flynn turned back to the portal, he could see the room they had just left. The dark-robed clerk was frowning in their direction. A moment later the portal disappeared and he was staring at a stone wall.

“That wasn’t a real portal,” Nyx explained. “It was more of a gate. All dungeons controlled by the Temple of the Deeper Realm have them. It allows them to control access. There are also rules that are coded into them. For example, most dungeons only allow one party inside at a time. Others have limits on the number of people allowed to enter at one time. This one has both. Only one party is allowed in the dungeon at a time, and no more than five people can enter at a time.”

Throm wondered around the room, looking at the blank stone walls as if there were some secrets to be found. His hands were clasped behind his back, and he nodded occasionally as if he’d discovered something interesting. The mercenary ignored the one feature in the room that wasn’t a stone wall.

The only feature in the room was an arched doorway. An unnatural darkness filled the opening, and Flynn couldn’t see anything on the other side. “So the monsters are on the other side of that doorway?”

Nyx nodded. “Yeah. We are currently in the antechamber of the dungeon. Once we pass through that archway, the only way to get out of the dungeon is to defeat the boss at the end.”

Flynn swallowed and blew out a breath.

“There is nothing to worry about,” Nyx assured him. “This is a low-level dungeon that hasn’t had any time for monsters to level up. We have faced much worse before.”

Throm stopped his inspection and turned toward Nyx. “If the monsters aren’t very strong, that also means the rewards aren’t going to be very great, doesn’t it?”

“That depends on who you are,” she replied. “I have done this dungeon before so the rewards for me will be small. For you two, though, this is your first time. The rewards for completing your first dungeon are substantial, not the least of which is unlocking access to more. That is very important because most dungeons don’t allow dungeon virgins.”

Throm was suddenly at Flynn’s side, draping an arm around the young man’s shoulders. The older man leaned close and said, “I bet you never thought your first time would be with a guy like me.”

Nyx chuckled.

Flynn shot her a dirty look then turned to the mercenary. “I’m just a kid,” he said softly, then grinned. “The fact that this is your first time too says more about you than me I think.”

The blue-haired woman burst out laughing.

The smile immediately dropped from the mercenary’s face and he took his arm off Flynn’s shoulders but didn’t say anything.

“Good one,” Nyx said as she wiped tears from her eyes. “Well, before we get started with this threesome, there’s a couple things you two need to know.”

“What, like your safe word?” Flynn flashed her a self-satisfied smile.

The mercenary snorted in amusement, and the normally boisterous woman went uncharacteristically quiet. Flynn thought he saw a brief look of respect cross her face before she continued. “Unfortunately, there are no safe words in a dungeon. There are rules, however. In this dungeon, the main rules are no fire spells or abilities, no water spells or abilities, and no summoned familiars. Beyond that, the Temple of the Deeper Realm strongly encourages dungeon divers to use the minimum possible level of power so as to not damage the dungeon.”

“We’re supposed to be nice to the dungeon?”

“Yes. This dungeon is very beneficial for first-timers and making sure it’s in good shape for the next set of newcomers is a small courtesy to pay.”

“Okay, okay,” Flynn said. “Let’s get on with this little adventure.”
