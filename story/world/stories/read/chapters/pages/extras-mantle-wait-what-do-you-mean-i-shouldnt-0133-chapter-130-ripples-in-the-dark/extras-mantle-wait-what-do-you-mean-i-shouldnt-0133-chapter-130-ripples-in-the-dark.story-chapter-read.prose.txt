High priest of the Darkened One and his blessed Vakan stood at the top of the Cathedral of the Primes, his fingers digging into the stone railing hard enough that the ancient marble cracked and crumbled beneath his grip.

His gaze fixed on the distant horizon, roughly toward where the bastion sat like a wound in the earth's flesh, and his jaw clenched tight enough to make his teeth ache.

He'd felt it.

Just moments ago—a presence that shouldn't exist, couldn't exist, not now… especially not now—had bloomed from where he was certain the ancient bastion lay.

The presence was faint, distant, but to him it was unmistakably a signature of an outer being.

Vakan knew that feeling the way a man knew the touch of his loved one. The presence was full of chaos and disgusting darkness that was not from his Lord, not from his sacred shadow, but from something fouler that often crawled through the spaces between worlds looking for cracks to exploit.

Do not shame the Darkened One. Do not shame yourself. You are His instrument, His vessel. You do not fear.

The chant ran through his mind like a prayer, each repetition trying to smooth away the jagged edges of the terror clawing at his chest.

But Vakan did fear; he feared the outer ones, and he feared the Hierach because, no matter how stronger he got, he could never hurt them.

He'd spent decades studying the outer entities. Learning and understanding the threat they posed to the Great Work. And now one’s presence had manifested—however briefly—within the very city they were trying to claim for the Darkened One's glory.

How? Why? Who summoned it? Could it be Hierach’s plan?

No, he lost his play for the bastion… then who?

Footsteps echoed up the cathedral's spiral staircase, measured and deliberate, announcing approach without the need for words.

Vakan didn't turn. He knew those footsteps the way he knew his own heartbeat.

"Icas," he said, his voice flat and cold as winter stone.

"My lord." Icas Vasht emerged onto the observation platform and dropped to one knee, head bowed, right fist pressed against his chest in salute. His dark robes pooled around him like spilled ink. "I came as soon as I felt your summons."

Vakan finally tore his gaze away from the horizon and looked down at his most loyal companion. Icas had served at his side for decades—through three failed crusades, two purges, and the chaos of the Great Work's initiation—and in all that time, the man had never once questioned an order.

Loyalty like that was worth more than gold in an organization built on betrayal and ambition.

"How is the progress?" Vakan asked.

Icas hesitated… barely a heartbeat's worth of pause, but Vakan caught it like a knife between the ribs.

"It is going as planned, my lord, but..."

"We have lost too many of our brethren," Vakan finished for him. The words tasted like ash. "Haven't we."

"Yes, my lord." Icas's voice dropped lower, heavy with grief that matched Vakan's own. "The outpost losses. Our underlord’s death. Orodain. Mareth. And now..." He swallowed. "Now Priest Kiyon—regardless of how detestable he was—has fallen as well."

Vakan's hands clenched harder on the railing. Stone crumbled between his fingers, dust falling away into the wind that howled across Vienna's rooftops.

"I feel the same, Icas." His voice came out rougher than he'd intended. "We are the servants of the Darkened One. The only reason I walk among them—" he spat the word like poison "—the only reason I accepted the position of Hand was because the Lord Incarnate himself expressed approval. Because I thought I could serve the Great Work better from within another ancient order, but…"

He turned, finally meeting Icas's gaze directly.

"What of Starkiller?"

Icas's expression darkened. "He still lives, my lord. The Revenant himself confronted him three nights past, but..." A pause, choosing words carefully. "The engagement ended inconclusively. Starkiller escaped, though wounded. Crippled, if the reports are accurate."

"Wounded. Crippled… he is crippled!" Vakan laughed, the sound bitter and sharp as broken glass. "Icas, tell me something. Why, on this holy mission—why are there only five of them? No..." He corrected himself with vicious precision. "Now four of them. Not including myself."

Icas kept his silence. Smart man.

"Just four presence from the anicent and most infamous order that even the high lords and nobles fear to name. Why SANGVATH ANOR has such a weak presence!" Vakan continued, his voice rising with each word. "And they still demand our obedience like we are their dogs! They still blame every setback on us, on our faithful, while they sit in their holes and do nothing about the real threats!"

His aura flared—dark red tinged with black corruption—and the air around him thickened with pressure that made Icas's breath catch.

"The Hierarch did his work, no doubt. Eliminated all those who might have caused trouble for his precious Convergence. But why are there no more moves from them? We know Starkiller is out there. Wounded and crippled, he may be, but did that old bastard forget who Starkiller was? What he's capable of? He of all people should know the best!"

"My lord," Icas started carefully, "we should—"

"No!" Vakan's voice cracked like a whip. "I'll not stop! So many faithful souls of our Lord had to return into His darkness before they could serve Him properly! Before they could witness the Great Work's completion!"

He pushed against the railing with enough force that the entire structure—fifty feet of ornate marble and iron—tore free from its moorings and tumbled into the streets below.

The crash echoed through Vienna like thunder, but the only ones to hear it were abominations roaming the streets and the faithful hard at work.

Vakan stood at the edge, chest heaving, fists clenched so tight his nails drew blood from his palms.

"No," he said again, quieter now but no less intense. "We must do something, Icas. Pull more of our people together. Increase security on all remaining ritual sites—triple it if necessary—nothing can go wrong." His voice dropped to something almost like desperation. "I won't allow anything else to go wrong. If I must step out, I’ll. They be damned!"

He turned to face Icas fully, and the younger cultist flinched at whatever he saw in Vakan's eyes.

"We have confirmed sightings of a red-eyed doorman. We have Starkiller wounded but still hunting. And we have someone—" he gestured violently toward where the bastion sat "—causing such severe fate variance readings that made even the Hierarch pause, yet they do nothing."

"And now an outer being." The words came out barely above a whisper. "An outer being manifested in the bastion, Icas. For three heartbeats, I felt it clearly as day. That's not a coincidence. That's not random chance."

Icas rose slowly, his expression grave. "What would you have me do, my lord?"

Vakan took a breath. Steadied himself. Let the Darkened One's blessing flow through him and cool the rage burning in his chest.

"Send word to all remaining brothers and sisters of our faith… all our faithful." His voice gained certainty as he spoke, each word a command carved from conviction. "I want perimeter security doubled at every ritual site. I want watchers stationed at every entrance, every corridor, every shadow large enough to hide a rat."

"I want reports on everything. Every anomaly. Every unexplained essence fluctuation. Every new survivor, even our own, who looks at our facilities sideways." He met Icas's gaze directly. "And I want a full accounting of our remaining Blessed Ones. How many can we mobilize without compromising core defense?"

"Seventeen, my lord," Icas answered immediately. "Twenty-three if we pull from the reserve rotation."

"Pull them all." Vakan's tone left no room for debate. "Station them at the critical sites. And make it clear—crystal clear—that if anyone so much as breathes wrong near one of our facilities, I want them taken alive and brought to me."

"Captured… if they can, I want to look into their eyes and understand what kind of fool thinks they can disrupt the Great Work."

Icas saluted, fist to chest. "It will be done, my lord. What of Hand Vella?"

Vakan's expression darkened further. "What of her?"

"She..." Icas chose his words with visible care. "She has gone… She’s grieving most terribly for her creation’s loss; she and my lord have been... unstable. Violent, even by her standards. Also, it seems she is making a new creation."

"Let her grieve," Vakan said coldly. "But make it clear that grief does not mean she can sacrifice our people. If she cannot channel her rage productively, if she continues to lash out at our own people instead of our enemies...

Icas paled slightly but nodded. "I will convey your words with appropriate delicacy, my lord."

"No." Vakan smiled, and there was nothing kind in it. "Convey them exactly as I spoke them. Let her know that I said it. Let her come find me if she takes issue. No matter how insane she is here in the nexus, she’s hardly my match."

He turned back to the broken edge where the railing had been, looking out over Vienna's sprawl.

"Eighteen days, Icas. Eighteen days until the Convergence reaches critical saturation. Until the Great Work fulfills its purpose and brings the Darkened One's shadow across this entire cursed city."

"Eighteen days," he repeated, softer. "And I'll be damned to the outer hells before I let anything—anyone—stop it now."

Deep beneath Vienna, in the Inner Sanctum where shadows grew thick enough to drown in, Hierarch Malachar sat upon his throne of bone and blackened stone.

His fingers drummed against the armrest—slow, measured, patient as the grave—and the essence coiling around him shifted in response to his contemplation.

Before him, a sphere of crystallized darkness floated at eye level, its surface reflecting nothing but showing everything. Within it, images flickered and changed: the bastion's exterior, the cathedral's broken railing, Hand Vakan's furious pacing captured from a dozen different angles.

"Most interesting," Malachar murmured to the empty cathedral. His voice echoed and multiplied, creating a chorus from a single source. "Hand Vakan grows restless. Questioning. Showing initiative beyond his station."

One pale hand extended, fingers splayed, and the sphere rotated at his unspoken command.

The image shifted.

Now it showed the bastion itself, and Malachar's eyes—ancient and terrible and wholly inhuman—narrowed with something approaching interest.

"An outer being…" he said quietly. "However briefly. However faint."

His fingers twitched, and the sphere zoomed deeper, tracking essence residue invisible to lesser perceptions. Layers of reality peeled back like onion skin, revealing patterns beneath patterns, connections threaded through Vienna's spiritual landscape like veins carrying poison through flesh.

"No, it’s an outer heart… a source of great, terrible power," Malachar observed.

The implications rolled through his mind like thunder through distant mountains.

“Either a foolish one formed a pact or… it is as I feared. The hiers are rising.” His expression slowly turned cold.

"Starkiller’s wounded but remains alive," he murmured, ticking off pieces on an invisible board. "The door had accepted his bloodline duty. And the one prime fattened up for harvest is causing fate variance strong enough to ripple through the weave. Multiple underlords have fallen to unknown forces."

His fingers stopped drumming.

"The board grows crowded," Malachar said. "The pieces multiply. The game becomes... complex."

He closed his fist, and the sphere collapsed into smoke that curled upward and vanished into shadow.

"Let Hand Vakan scurry about with his increased security. Let him station his Blessed Ones and double his watchers." A smile curved beneath his hood—not warmth, not pleasure, just acknowledgment of something interesting after centuries of tedium. "Let him believe his actions matter."

Malachar rose from his throne, robes whispering against stone as funeral shrouds dragged across marble.

"The Convergence proceeds regardless..." He turned toward the altar where sacrifices were made, not to the darkened one but to him.

To the one known as The Revenant.

"When it does, all these complications will resolve themselves. Starkiller. The door. The Fate disrupter. All of them will serve the emergence of a new celestial in the end, whether they realize it or not."

He raised both hands, and essence condensed around him in spiraling torrents that made the cathedral's shadows writhe and dance.

"For the emergence is inevitable." His voice filled the space, echoing off stone and multiplying into a profane chorus. "It proceeds. Always, it proceeds."

"And nothing—not heroes, not resistance, not fate itself, not even the meddling of outer entities—will prevent its completion."

The shadows bowed in response, eager and hungry, awaiting command.

Hierarch Malachar, who had walked the earth when dragons still darkened the skies, settled back onto his throne and closed his eyes.

He would watch.

He would wait.

And when the pieces finished their dance, he would harvest what remained.

Jin woke up feeling the most rested he had been in years.

His body didn't hurt. His essence channels felt smooth and stable. Even his head—usually pounding with Angel's constant commentary or the weight of too many responsibilities—felt blissfully clear.

Finally.

He rolled over, fumbling for his spatial ring to pull out a water bottle, only to remember—with growing annoyance—that he'd given the ring to Joe for the repairs and upgrades.

Ah, fuck.

Jin groaned and sat up in bed, running both hands through his hair.

« There's a refrigerator here. Should be stocked with the necessities. »

"There is?" Jin blinked, looking around the quarters with newfound appreciation. "That's really nice of them."

He pulled himself upright and moved toward where he vaguely remembered seeing a kitchen area.

« Not there… that’s the bathroom. The kitchen is on your left. »

Jin paused mid-step. "I know that, duh. I'm going to splash some water on my freaking face first."

« Sure. Whatever you say. »

Jin made his way to the bathroom, splashed cold water across his face until his skin tingled and his hair dripped onto the floor, then finally shuffled toward the actual kitchen.

The fridge was small and, as Angel said, well-stocked. Water bottles, apple juice, wrapped sandwiches… someone, probably on Mathew's orders, had made sure he wouldn't starve while recovering.

"Man, I really wanted something strong for a bit." Jin grabbed a water bottle and cracked it open, drinking half in one long gulp.

« You are seventeen years old, Jin. »

"Yeah, but that's my physical age, Angel. I'm actually—"

« Twenty years old in your last life, and if I remember right, the age limit is twenty-one… or more correctly, twenty-four.»

"Shut up!" Jin grabbed the apple juice, scowling at nothing. "Never heard of getting others to get you stuff?"

« Ah, smart. Very clever. » Angel's tone dripped with sarcasm. « Except you're high Order III now, Jin. Normal alcohol won't even get you tipsy. Your body processes toxins too efficiently. »

"Great." Jin unscrewed the juice and took a drink. "Just great."

« I know, right? Sad to be you. » A pause. « Now drink your apple juice and imagine it's whiskey, and have those sandwiches. Because we're almost at the second milestone. »

"Sure I'll imagine this is my bottle of Jameson," Jin chuckled and then paused mid-sip. "Milestone already?"

« Harvest absorption is accelerating. You have maybe an hour before the next consolidation hits. So rest and recover before that. »

"Okay, mom." Jin grabbed two sandwiches and settled into a chair, trying to enjoy the brief moment of peace before reality came crashing back down.

Rudy stood in the empty training room on the bastion's fortieth level, his hands clenched into fists at his sides, sweat dripping down his face despite the cool air.

Something was happening inside him.

He could feel it—a pressure building behind his sternum, like a balloon inflating slowly against the walls of his ribs—and every breath he took seemed to feed it, make it stronger, push it closer to... something.

But he couldn't quite reach it.

"Frustrating, isn't it?"

Rudy spun toward the voice and found his father leaning against the doorframe, arms crossed, watching with an expression Rudy couldn't quite parse.

"Dad." Rudy straightened, wiping sweat from his forehead with the back of his hand. "I didn't hear you come in."

"That's because you were focused. Too focused." Mathew pushed off the doorframe and walked closer, his single eye studying Rudy with the kind of attention usually reserved for tactical planning. "Which is good. Focus is half the battle, but I'm still surprised to see my Rudy, who hated meditation, doing it finally."

"Hah… the past few weeks have been very enlightening, Dad. And Jin said to learn sorceries and spells, I need to get that into a habit.” He said.

“You learning sorceries?” Mathew asked incredulously.

“I want to, magic has proven to be a useful way, not just for attacks but for utility and support, rather than just relying on my swords.” He shrugged.

“Good to see you all growing up, Rudy.” Mathew sighed. “Just wished the circumstances were…”

“It’s okay, Dad. I trust Jin, he’ll make sure we pull through this.”

“Haha,” Mathew laughed and then, with a serious look, continued. “Besides, what are you feeling?”

It feels like..." Rudy struggled for words, gesturing vaguely at his chest. "Like there's something right there, just within reach, and I can't quite grab it. Every time I think I've got it, it slips away."

Mathew smiled—genuinely smiled, not the commander's mask he wore in briefings—and reached out to ruffle Rudy's hair the way he used to when Rudy was eight years old and just learning to throw his first punch.

"That's the feeling of aura, son."

Rudy froze. "Wait. Really?"

"Really." Mathew's hand settled on Rudy's shoulder, warm and solid and reassuring. "You're standing at the threshold. The pressure you're feeling? That's your essence trying to manifest externally, trying to break through the barrier between internal power and projected will."

"How do I..." Rudy swallowed. "How do I actually do it?"

"You don't force it," Mathew said. "That's the mistake most people make. They try to grab it, wrestle it, dominate it through sheer willpower." He shook his head. "But aura isn't something you conquer. It's something you become. It's the representation of your soul, will, and purpose."

He stepped back slightly, his own aura—a grey ashen sheen—flickering to life around him, steady, controlled, radiating authority and protection in equal measure.

"Feel that?" Mathew asked quietly.

Rudy nodded, unable to speak. His father's aura pressed against him, but unlike the once warm sunlight, it was still warm, but burn the world kind of warm.

“What happened?” Rudy whispered, tears welling up in his eyes.

“Later… after all this is done. I’ll tell you.” He said. "But note on the feeling… that's what you're reaching for. Not power for power's sake, but an expression of who you are. Your essence made manifest. You will projected into reality."

Mathew's aura faded back to dormancy. "It'll come when you're ready. When you stop chasing it and simply... allow it."

They stood in silence for a moment, comfortable and familiar, and Rudy felt something in his chest ease slightly.

Then Mathew's expression shifted—became more serious, more uncertain—and Rudy recognized that look. His father wore it whenever he was about to ask something important.

"Rudy," Mathew started, then paused. Tried again. "I wanted to talk to you about something."

"Okay." Rudy waited, patient despite the curiosity burning in his gut.

"We came up with an operation based on Jin’s earlier information, which he left us with. It’s named Operation Vienna's Wrath." The words came out carefully measured. "It's what we're calling the assault. Although we still need more details for him, but—"

Rudy's Asura heart beat harder, responding to the shift in conversation.

"I know you've been training," Mathew continued. "I know you've been pushing yourself. And I know Jin's planning to have you as part of the assault force."

A pause.

"But I need to know, as your father and as your commander, do you want to be part of this? Because if you say no, if you want to stay back and handle support operations instead, I'll back that decision. No shame. No judgment."

Rudy looked at his father—really looked at him—and saw the concern there, barely hidden beneath the commander's facade. Saw the father worried for his son going into combat. Saw the man who'd already lost so much and didn't want to risk losing more.

But Rudy also saw something else: trust. Respect. The acknowledgment that Rudy was old enough to make this choice himself.

The Asura heart in his chest beat steadily and strongly, responding to the decision crystallizing in his mind.

"Tell me about it," Rudy said, and his voice came out calmer than he expected. Steadier. "Tell me everything."

Mathew's expression softened—pride and worry mixing in equal measure—and he nodded once.

"Alright, son. Let's talk."
