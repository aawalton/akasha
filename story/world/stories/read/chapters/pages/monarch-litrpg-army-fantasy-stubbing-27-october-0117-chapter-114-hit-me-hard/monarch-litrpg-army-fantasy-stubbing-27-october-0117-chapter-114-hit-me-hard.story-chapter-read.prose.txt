Rayne twisted to the side as the wooden axe came swinging toward the back of his head, his body moving before the attack had even fully formed. The strike passed through empty air as he shifted, and in the same motion, he drove his foot forward, kicking Jason square in the chest.

The impact pushed the larger man back a step, but Rayne didn’t focus on him longer than a few seconds.

The moment Jason stumbled, Rayne became aware of the others closing in, their presence pressing in from all directions while a sharp ripple passed through his senses. Arrows were already in the air behind him.

He only had seconds.

Rayne turned immediately, raising his shield as the arrows came streaking in. The wooden tips struck against it in quick succession, dull thuds echoing as he angled it just enough to deflect them away from his body.

At the same time, he lifted his arm to brace. Nate and Kesh reached him together, their wooden swords striking almost in unison.

The impact landed against his guard, a faint sting running through his arm, but he barely felt it. He pushed forward, forcing both of them back with a burst of strength before they could follow up.

But before he could land an attack, he sensed Heins rushing in from the side, spear thrusting toward his ribs. Rayne stepped aside, the wooden tip brushing past him as he brought his sword down sharply, knocking the spear off its path before snapping his leg forward and kicking it aside.

The weapon shifted out of line, and Rayne moved in, shield already coming up to smack Heins in the face—

Just then, he felt another attack coming from his blind side.

Rayne turned instantly, the motion smooth, bringing his shield up just in time to block Jason’s incoming axe. The wooden weapon struck against it with a heavy thud.

He held his ground and looked up, a faint grin forming on his face as he met the burly man’s gaze.

Jason looked anything but amused. There was frustration written all over his face, his grip tightening on the axe as if he wanted to swing again, but he didn’t. Instead, he pulled back slightly, letting out a low growl.

“This is bullshit,” Jason said, shaking his head. “I can’t even put a scratch on you. What the hell is that skill?”

Rayne said nothing, his smile only widening.

“It’s not even fair,” Jason continued, his tone edged with irritation. “It’s just the gods handing you a blessing.”

Behind him, Nate had also stopped mid-charge, lowering his weapon as he frowned.

“Yeah,” he said, looking at Rayne with narrowed eyes. “What even is that? You’re dodging things you shouldn’t even see.”

Kesh shifted slightly beside him, his gaze just as sharp. “Does it give you eyes on the back of your head or something?” Kesh added. “Or did you just turn into a demon overnight?”

Rayne didn’t reply right away.

For a moment, he simply stood there, shield still raised slightly, his gaze moving across the group as he considered what to say. It wasn’t that he didn’t have an answer, but rather that he didn’t want to give the full one.

The truth was that he was holding back.

Compared to his party, his stats were far higher, and if he actually let himself go all out, the spar wouldn’t have lasted this long. They would have been on the ground within moments, and that wasn’t the point of this.

He wasn’t here to win. He was here to test out his skills.

More specifically, to test [War Pulse], and the result had been better than he had expected.

The skill had settled into him more naturally than he thought it would. Right now, its range stretched to around thirty meters, and within that space, he could sense everything.

He could sense everyone around him, their movements, their positions, even the arrows as they cut through the air toward him. There was no delay, no need to turn and look. The information simply reached him.

It made reacting easier and faster.

Rayne exhaled quietly before finally lowering his shield a little and looking at his party.

“The skill just lets me sense everyone around me,” he said. “But it costs a lot of mana, so there's downsides to it too.”

That part wasn’t a lie.

He had been using it for nearly twenty minutes now, and he could already feel the drain. Around sixty percent of his mana was gone, and if this had been a real fight, he wouldn’t have been able to keep it active this long, especially with using his other skills.

“It’s still very impressive,” Bran said, walking towards them from the edge of the training ground, his tone thoughtful. “Against larger groups or stronger enemies, that kind of awareness would be a blessing.”

His gaze shifted briefly toward Nate and Kesh. “Will probably keep you two alive for longer.”

Nate let out a grunt at that, rolling his shoulders slightly. “At least if you’re turning into a demon, it’s one that benefits me,” he said.

That drew a short laugh from Jason, who finally lowered his wooden axe, resting it against his shoulder.

“Should we start calling him the all-seeing demon now?” he said with a grin. “Might get you another title.”

Rayne shook his head lightly at that.

“You can try,” he said. “But I doubt the gods will be that generous every time.”

He had already told them about getting the gravewalker title, though he had left out what it actually did. That hadn't stopped Nate, Kesh and Heins from wanting to know about it before the spar, but he planned to keep the information to himself.

Jason snorted at his words, still grinning. “Who knows,” he said. “The gods have been pretty generous with you so far.”

His expression shifted slightly, turning more thoughtful as he looked at Rayne.

“Makes me wonder if I should just join your party,” he added. “With all the news of shifting, new squad leaders and all that.”

Everyone in the warband had already heard rumours about the changes that were coming. Some of the squad leaders had died during the dungeon campaign, even under Captain Edran. Jason’s old squad leader, Rancour, had been one of them. Axel was also stepping down, leaving more gaps to be filled.

There were going to be new squad leaders.

Not just that, but there was also a chance that soldiers from other warbands would be reassigned into theirs. It wasn’t uncommon after losses like this, and the army always reshuffled its forces when needed.

Still, none of that was certain yet. Everything would only be finalized once they reached the capital and reported properly. Their new orders would come from there, and until then, things would remain as they were.

All the other warbands were actually expected to stay in Haven City to wait for their new orders while only his warband would move toward Valers, thanks to Rayne. At least for the next month, none of them were likely to be sent out on any new quests.

A part of Rayne did wonder if all this shifting might work in his favor. His quest had been sitting at the back of his mind for a while now, and if new squads were being formed, he might be able to make one of his own. But that was something for later.

For now, his focus was the capital.

Rayne shook his head lightly before looking back at Jason. “If I’m still a leader in the next month or so,” he said, “then sure. I’d welcome you.”

Jason grinned at that, clearly amused. “You might be more than that,” he said. “Either way, we should keep sparring. I haven’t gotten any proper practice in a while. Got any other skills you want to show?”

Rayne smiled faintly at that, his eyes moving from Jason to the rest of his party. They were all watching him now, curiosity clear on their faces.

Without saying anything, he lowered his shield and raised his hand.

For a brief second, nothing happened. Then, thin strands of mana began to spill out from his fingers.

They were faint at first, almost invisible, but as more of them formed, they became clearer, fine threads of pale energy, stretching outward and wrapping around his hand, then the rest of his body.

His party reacted as he had expected with widened eyes and shocked faces as they watched the threads intently.

Rayne let them gawk at them for a few seconds, then he asked, “How hard can you all hit me?”

***

As it turned out, the answer was not hard enough.

No matter how much force they put into their strikes, their normal attacks weren’t able to break through [Weaver’s Protection]. The mana threads might have looked thin and fragile, but once they were in place, they held far more strength than expected.

Rayne had tested it thoroughly.

Jason had swung his axe at full force, aiming straight for his shoulder, and the threads wrapped around it had held without breaking. The impact had been there, a dull force pressing through, but it hadn’t been enough to actually injure him.

Bran had followed it with arrows from the side, targeting the same area from different angles, and even then, the threads had absorbed the strikes without letting them pass.

Rayne had walked out of the spar without a single scratch.

It wasn’t perfect. He could still feel the force behind the hits, and stronger mana attacks would likely break through, but for normal strikes, it was more than enough.

Though, mana was still a big problem. Just like [War Pulse], the skill drained him quickly. In barely five minutes of use, nearly forty percent of his remaining mana had been consumed.

Part of it was likely his own fault. He had created too many threads, layering them across his body without much control, trying to test the limits rather than conserve energy.

With better usage, the cost might go down. Even so, the drain was noticeable.

In the end, it was still good practice.

Rayne kept at it over the next few days while his party and Jason made it their personal goal to land even a single clean hit on him. The first spar had clearly bruised more than just their bodies, and none of them were willing to let it go so easily.

It didn’t take long before others started to gather as well.

At first, there were only a few soldiers passing by, slowing down to watch for a moment before moving on, but as the rumors spread about him breaking past the first wall, more began to come intentionally. Soon enough, there were always a handful of people watching from the edges of the field, some curious about his new powers while most of them simply needed some entertainment.

The spars weren’t always one-sided either. Even with [War Pulse], he didn’t win every time. Knowing where an attack was coming from didn’t always mean he could deal with it, especially when multiple opponents pressed in at once.

There were times when he got overwhelmed. Too many angles, too many movements, and even if he sensed everything, his body still had to keep up. That alone was a limitation he couldn’t ignore.

Using [War Pulse] alongside [Weaver’s Protection] only made it harder.

The two skills demanded different kinds of focus, and trying to maintain both at once felt like forcing his mind to split in two. There were moments when he lost track of one while focusing on the other, and more than once, he had instinctively dropped one skill just to keep control of the other.

It was a serious problem.

Rayne knew he needed guidance to deal with it, but Casper was still busy most of the time, and Captain Edran’s paperwork never ended. He might have asked Varrick, but the man and the rest of the Crown’s Hand had already left by the time he had properly woken up.

A part of him also wanted to thank him. Varrick had been the one to find him alongside Fredrick’s body, and yet he hadn’t twisted the story or claimed the merit of killing the necromancer for himself.

Still, he guessed it was better that they were gone. Crown's Hand still made him a bit uneasy with them having killed most of the Fraysers.

The days passed quickly like that, filled with sparring, and him getting to know more about his skills, and then, just as suddenly, it all came to an end.

One evening, Commander Evans gathered the entire warband and gave the order to pack up. They would be marching back to Fort Algar, and from there, they would continue on toward Bricksall.

Cheers broke out across the camp when the soldiers heard it, all of them raring to go back to civilization.

Within a day, they were on the move through the dirt roads that led towards Fort Algar.

It was during that time that Rayne finally found the chance to speak with Casper, and like he had expected, she was shocked by seeing his new skills.

But he didn't realise that doing so was going to put him through hell throughout their journey.
