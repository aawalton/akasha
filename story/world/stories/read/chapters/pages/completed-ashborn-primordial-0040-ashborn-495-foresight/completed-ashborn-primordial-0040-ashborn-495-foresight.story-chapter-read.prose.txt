Vir said, “We’ll back you up,” as dust from the kicked-down door filled the chamber. Cirayus turned back and scoffed.

“If you claim to know me as you say, you should know how I would respond. Just who do you think I am?” he said before stomping away.

Vir chuckled. “I should’ve known better.”

True to his word, Cirayus incapacitated every demon who attacked or got in his way, all the way to the entrance of the heavily fortified compound.

Vir noticed that not a single one was dead. Prana still flowed in all of them, and though this Cirayus wasn’t comparable to the one from Vir’s own universe, accustomed as that one was to the prana of the Mahādi Realm, he was still an absolute force of nature. He’d be more than enough to end Andros.

In no time at all, and despite facing down dozens of demons, they reached the entrance to the prison compound. Half of it was on account of Cirayus’ sheer, overwhelming strength.

The other half was due to the trail of bodies left in his wake. Bodies that, to outsiders, looked dead. This, plus his reputation as a demon of legend, made more than a few demons turn tail and flee upon seeing him unleashed.

It wasn’t long before they were bounding away, with Tia held once again in Vir’s arms.

“Balancer of Scales, too,” Cirayus said. “And you use it well! Too well… I sense my own signature touches in your usage, but I do not know you. Had I not known better, I’d think you were a certain individual. An impossible reincarnation of an ancient being who could not possibly exist.”

“Then it’s a good thing you know better,” Vir said coyly. “From here, we head north to Andros’ garrison. We can only pray he hasn’t already left.”

After linking up with Maiya and Ashani, they set out at maximum speed until they finally arrived at Andros’ mountain keep.

The compound, though hastily erected, was nothing short of astonishing. In both the scale of its size and the visual impact.

For above this base hovered yet another Vimana.

“Even without that floating citadel, this would be a heavily defended base,” Vir commented. “As much as I wish to minimize casualties, I don’t see a way to win this fight without some bloodshed.”

“I reckon so,” Cirayus replied, giving Vir a probing look. “Truly, you did not strike me as the type to be so ruthless. You are so young.”

Vir pressed his lips together. “I once was. Naive and gullible. But time and experience have shown me that sometimes, to do what’s best for the realm, one must get their hands dirty.”

“Well said,” Tia replied, nodding. “At this point, no cost is too high to rid the world of Andros and his ilk. As much as I hate killing innocents, I fear it is the lesser of the evils.”

“Then I suggest we hold Cirayus in reserve,” Vir said. “He’s our secret weapon to take down Andros. What we need now is a distraction.”

“That will be difficult,” Tia said, grimacing. “Even with my rebels, I don’t see how we’d pull off such a thing without putting ourselves in great danger.”

That Tia, of all people, was agonizing over the health of demons brought Vir more joy than he thought possible.

“Relax,” he said with a smile. “That’s where we come in.”

He pointed to Maiya, Ashani, and the Ash Wolves that suddenly swooped in on them, earning themselves a surprised shriek from Tia.

Even Cirayus braced himself, looking like he was about to engage.

“They’re friends,” Vir said, petting Shan’s fur. “And worthy Warriors. I put their Balar Rank somewhere in the low thousands, each. And together, they are much more than just their sum.”

Shan puffed out his chest, looking very much like he was basking in the attention, and there was plenty of that to go around. Vir had never seen Cirayus so impressed, let alone Tia, whose jaw hung comically open.

“They’ll do most of the work, but we’ll assist where we can,” Vir continued. “In the meantime…”

“Yes, I will prepare to deal with this human conqueror,” Cirayus said, barely masking the fury that simmered beneath his skin.

“We have to do this without being seen,” Vir reminded the group. “Once the chaos starts, we bring Cirayus in against Andros at the last minute.”

“As despicable as that man is,” Tia said, “he tends to take to the field. Not for glory or honor, of course. He simply likes to show off his strength.”

“Which means if we cause a big enough disturbance, he’ll come out,” Vir said. “Then that’s our plan.”

“And what about them?” Maiya asked, pointing to the wolves.

“They’ll be the stars of the show,” Vir said. “While unwanted eyes might recognize me, they’ll have trouble differentiating these wolves from any other Ash Beasts. It’s a pity a nasty horde of Ash Wolves happened to the camp, resulting in an opening for an assassination led by the rebel princess, yes?”

Vir, Maiya, and Tia all grinned.

“Mai, Ashani? Find a good vantage and stay with Tia,” Vir said. “She needs to be seen to sell this lie, but you cannot be. Engage only if needed.”

“Understood,” came their response, and then Vir sank into the shadows, leaving Cirayus to his own devices.

He needed to make sure whatever he did kept attention off Cirayus and the others until the last minute.

Luckily, Vir was uniquely suited to infiltration and assassination. Which mattered now more than ever. Not only for their own safety but for the entire realm. Who knew where the Fateweavers might be watching? They likely had several eyes within Andros’s garrison, and the Imperator himself could be an unwitting agent.

It did not take long at all once Vir unleashed the Ash Wolves. Chaos reigned supreme, and the camp's Balarian guards were cut down mercilessly and without warning.

Had they been normal Ash Wolves, they might’ve succumbed to the combined might of the Balarian Guard and their demonic slaves, but these were no ordinary beasts. They were Ashfire Wolves forged in the pranic fires of Mahādi itself.

Andros’ garrison never stood a chance.

Vir found he only had to intervene in a handful of places to keep the wolves safe, striking from the shadows, killing and disappearing long before anyone could even suspect he was there.

As Tia predicted, Andros charged onto the field, dropping from the Vimana like some god. With as many Artifacts as he bore, he might as well be. Vir didn’t wish to risk letting him attack them.

It was a good thing he didn’t have to.

The moment Andros closed within striking range, Cirayus appeared behind him, dropping out of the sky.

The Imperator barely had the time to widen his eyes before the half-giant unleashed his Crown Chakra and slammed a Warrior Chakra-laden attack into Andros’s back.

The Imperator stumbled, shocked and barely keeping his balance... but not out of the fight. Not looking like someone who'd just had his soul shattered.

But the Balarian Guard nearby did, wailing as he clutched his chest and crumpled to the ground.

What in all the Realms?

Cirayus struck again. And again, and Vir watched as one after another, Balarian Guards fell as though it had been they who had been hit.

Vir's eyes widened in a sudden, horrific realization. “Cirayus!” Vir roared from the giant's shadow. “He bears an Artifact that can absorb and redirect Chakra attacks!”

It should have been impossible. The Imperium had no defense against Chakras. So how?

The answer was as obvious as the eternal sunset. Saunak. The Mad Thaumaturge had somehow devised a way to, if not nullify, then at least redirect Warrior Chakras. Even those empowered by a centuries-perfect Crown, apparently.

“Aye, I see it,” Cirayus said, continuing to pummel the Imperator with both physical and Chakra attacks. With the force behind each of his attacks, Andros had no hope of fighting back. Still, his barrage could not last forever. “Have you a plan?”

Vir's expression darkened. “Maybe.”

No Art had unlimited range, and considering this was a Saunak invention rather than a true Artifact, its range could not be all that great. It was likely why only the closest Balarian Guard seemed affected, and not a single regular Balarian Warrior.

The answer was revealed with Vir opened his Third Eye Chakra, surging prana through Clarity to glimpse the near future. The two abilities synergized beautifully, and Vir saw through them the lines connecting Andros to his guard.

A dozen lines, and no more.

“Keep hitting him,” Vir said, sinking into the shadows. “I'll be right back.”

As with the army, the Balarian Guard stood no chance against Vir's might. They would not have before his recent upgrades, and they certainly did not now.

Vir's Chakrams did more than half the work, whirring like a swarm of angry hornets, separating heads from necks with such deadly sharpness that their victims died before they knew what hit them.

The rest fell to simple surges of prana, exploding the Warriors' bodies from within.

The final of the Guard fell. Cirayus attacked again. And Andros’s soul cracked.

The four armed half-giant moved in for a finishing strike, delivering a punch to Andros' head—but all it did was knock him back, sending him tumbling. The Artifacts Andros possessed might have made him a demigod, but they also doomed him to the eternal suffering of a shattered soul, and stripping them off one by one to kill him would take time Vir didn’t plan to offer them.

The whole army froze, shocked as Andros collapsed unconscious. As Tia, who’d ridden on Cirayus’ back, proudly proclaimed his death to the world, giving a fiery speech that, while improvised and rough around the edges, came straight from the heart.

Vir only heard the first bit of it, for he sank into the shadows not long after.

His work wasn’t over, and his next target was just as foul.

It didn’t take long to find Saunak seated in a nearby tent, utterly oblivious to the chaos outside and poring over sheets of notes. No doubt caught up in another experiment.

Vir didn’t announce his presence. He didn’t give Saunak a chance to react or the Fateweavers to guess what had killed their pawn.

He simply located all the lines connecting Saunak's own device to those of his sacrificial guardians and ended them before unleashing his Warrior Chakra on the Thaumaturge, slicing through Saunak’s bare soul. Though the Thaumaturge was outfitted with an array of Artifacts—gifts from Andros, no doubt—they couldn’t save him now.

The demon collapsed into a frothing, broken mess. For his part, Vir did launch a barrage of prana darts at the demon, and even swiped at him with Artifact katar, but as he’d surmised, the demon was immune.

Not wishing to linger, Vir returned to Maiya and Ashani.

“Do you know how long Ira and I worked to kill that man?” Maiya asked, looking conflicted. “Do you know how much we suffered? And you just went and offed him.”

“Cirayus did,” Vir corrected, only for Maiya to roll her eyes.

“You know what I mean. It’s just hard to believe, is all.”

Vir concurred, despite being the mastermind. It felt so easy. If he had this knowledge the first time…

Foresight was a terrible thing. He thanked the gods that only Seers bore that power, and that their visions were so unreliably vague.

“Shall we wait for them?” Ashani asked.

“No,” Vir said, looking at Cirayus and Tia be swarmed with thankful demons. With a new army that swiftly cleaned up any who remained loyal to Andros.

“No, I think we’ve done enough here.”

In less than half an hour, Vir had eliminated the two greatest threats to their universe and changed the fate of the realm forever.

A staggering amount of effort to access a single Vimana, to be sure, but now, he could go through without regret.

And that? That made it all worth it.
