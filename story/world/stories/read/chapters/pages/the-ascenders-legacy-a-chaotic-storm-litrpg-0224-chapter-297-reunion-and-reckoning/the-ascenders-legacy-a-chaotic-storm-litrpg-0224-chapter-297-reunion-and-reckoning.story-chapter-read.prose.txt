“Carry on with your war, Lutians. I truly didn’t mean to intrude.”

The words of the Calamity echoed in Aldric's ears like a boom of thunder, and though it sounded very much like an insult, Aldric couldn't help but think the man was genuine. He really meant what he was saying.

After killing thousands of soldiers and eliminating three Calamities in mere minutes, the man truly hadn't meant to intrude on their war. He didn't care less what they did or who was fighting whom.

He didn't even seem to care that the shockwaves of his battle against the Watchers had stolen the lives of thousands. To him, all of this was a mere intrusion, and now that it was over, they could all go back to killing each other while he went about his own business.

It was a bizarre mindset that put into perspective just how Calamities viewed the rest of the world—like ants. Insignificant in the grand scheme of things.

Aldric had heard about the legendary nonchalance and apathy of Calamities. No one could advance as high as they had and still retain a concern for mortal trivialities, after all. Their mindsets were always altered. Even the mindsets of advanced individuals were different.

Still, the Calamity's words simply showed how trivial all of this was—how futile and meaningless he considered it. Though not immortal yet, Calamities operated on a level far above that of mortals—like lions watching ants battle for food and territory.

They could end it all with a simple move but didn't care enough to do so.

That was the real meaning behind the Calamity's words, however genuine they had been, and almost impossibly, the entire battlefield grew even quieter as everyone reevaluated their purpose in the war.

For many members of the Alliance, it was loyalty and patriotism. For some it was fanaticism and devotion. Others, revenge. And for Mythics like Matharantha, it was simply a means to alleviate their boredom or seek inspiration.

But for a select few, it was salvation and deliverance.

The telepaths were here for Mirith. The Runes for Sebastian. The Valerions for Cyrus. The Sunstonians for Seojun, Yuna, and Mira Sōsei.

And Aldric for Aodhán and Daruk.

His purpose in this war was to save his brothers, and as of now, they were still trapped. His gaze darted to the shattered remains of the black tower, feeling a weak pull of emotion from within, and without hesitation, he began moving toward it—new tendrils of boundless empathy blooming out of him to seek new targets.

His previous victims had been flattened to dust during the Calamity battle—their bodies forming a wall that had saved him and Tyrus at the cost of their own existence. It had been a gruesome sight, and Aldric had yet to move past it, but if he was to come out of this war alive, he needed protection, and this was the fastest way to get one.

Tyrus tensed beside him as a new set of cultists gathered around them, blank-eyed like mindless drones, but Aldric yanked him forward before he could protest and began racing toward the floating box.

His sudden motion, however, seemed to trigger everyone else, and in an instant, chaos erupted once more—shouts and screams echoing as the cultists cried out in righteous vengeance, fury at the loss of the Watchers burning in their veins.

The war resumed, and this time it was far more brutal.

The cultists weren't just fighting for survival now. They were fighting for revenge. For honor. For anything other than surrender.

With the Watchers gone, there was little hope they could win this war. But to surrender would be to besmirch Sárán's legacy and name, and so, without care for their own safety, they threw themselves back into the battle, their attacks fast, sharp, fueled by pain and rage—painfully aware they most likely wouldn't come out of it alive.

Explosions rang out as skills clashed. Cries echoed. Blood rained, and all around Aldric, desperate emotions swelled, washing against his senses like waves against sand, seeking traction but ultimately futile.

With Tyrant of Boundless Empathy, Aldric's mind was like a wall of reinforced stone. Impenetrable. Unalterable. And with each emotional assault that came, his mind only grew stronger.

Screams erupted and died around him as his thralls cut down their colleagues, many of whom were too shocked to react in time. Essence and bodies exploded around him, but for every thrall that died, Aldric's tendrils simply sought out a new target.

With each thrall he found, the task became easier, and slowly, fifty turned to seventy and seventy to eighty, making Tyrus even more uncomfortable.

Grumbling incoherently to himself, he followed alongside Aldric, surrounded by a protective wall of cultists. But their solo journey toward the floating box soon came to an end when a group of telepaths joined them—mental energy surging out of them in waves as they put entire battalions of cultists to sleep in an instant.

Their leader—Kira Valeh—glanced at Aldric, and though she didn't say anything, Aldric understood her urgency just fine. His pace quickened, and before long, they reached a less chaotic stretch of land leading straight toward the floating box.

Aldric felt his heartbeat pound louder as the pulse from Soul Imprint intensified.

He glanced at Tyrus. "They are in there. Are you ready? I can feel them."

Tyrus nodded somberly, his discomfort evaporating in the face of immediate action. "I am ready."

"We too," Kira nodded, her gaze hardening slightly. "Let's go save our friends."

She created a platform of azure mental energy for the telepaths, but just as Tyrus moved to create a platform of fire for them, a platform of solid air appeared beneath them, shimmering with blue-green highlights.

Aldric climbed onto it without hesitation, and Tyrus followed along with an annoyed mutter, glaring daggers at the thrall who had constructed it.

Unlike the first set of thralls, this group was different—faster, smarter, more organized, seemingly anticipating Aldric's needs before he even voiced them. But unknown to Tyrus, Aldric's skill was even more complex than that.

It wasn't mind-control or any kind of control as he assumed. It was resonance. Simple empathic resonance, currently linking a hundred emotional pools to one man.

The thralls were no longer individuals. They were a group. A functional unit with one head. A system with emotions as nerves. An organism with Aldric as its brain.

The telepaths noticed this, and they exchanged amazed glances, but Aldric didn't notice—his gaze was locked on the floating box as their platform streaked toward it in a blur of colors.

Despite their speed, three people still beat them to the box.

Artemis, Warp, and Damien Rune, their gazes hard as they climbed into the shattered remains of the box. Aldric, Tyrus, and the telepaths entered an instant later, only to come to a sudden halt at the devastation around them.

The amphitheater was a ruin.

Everything within the space was completely destroyed—not just broken, but crushed nearly to powder from the shockwaves of the Calamities' battle.

The only thing that survived—and survive was stretching it—was a large console spanning several meters in length and width. Electricity sparked, and runes flickered across its surface like dying embers.

A red scar ran across its surface like a searing wound, and standing over it, looking almost mundane now that he wasn't killing Calamities, was the Calamity killer.

The man barely spared them a glance as they all filed in, electricity zapping and fizzing around him as he put the console back together and somehow repaired it with energy itself.

Aldric couldn't even understand how that was possible, but the man was a Calamity. Perhaps the real question he should be asking was what exactly wasn't possible for him to do?

Silence reigned within the space as they watched the Calamity work, both terrified and in awe at the same time. Nobody spoke, lest they anger the man who had just killed three Calamities like he'd been battling children.

And so, they all waited in silence, watching as the console slowly came back together, sparking and humming like a machine on the edge of an explosion. Or perhaps another explosion.

The Calamity grimaced and glanced up at them. "I take it you're here for friends of your own."

They all exchanged glances, hesitant to speak until Damien Rune did. "Yes, Calamity. Some of us are here for family."

The Calamity's expression sobered at that, and he nodded. "Well, let's see if we can get them all out, both family and friends. You can call me Zarxan."

Another beat of silence. Another thrum of fear. And then someone else spoke.

"And what about you, Elder Zarxan? Who are you here for?"

Zarxan glanced back at them, his expression a tinge frustrated. "A curiosity."

He didn't bother to explain, neither did he give them a chance to ask any more questions as he placed a finger on the center button.

Lightning didn't crackle. Didn't flash. It simply flowed into the runic circuits of the console, rewriting and breaking down whatever security measures the cultists had installed.

For three heartbeats, as far as the console was concerned, Zarxan Al'ruhn was the Handler. Was the authority. Was the architect of Abyssos itself.

The runes on the console flared gold, then violet, then settled into steady white. A panel slid open, and within it were three large buttons.

Aldric couldn't see what was written on the buttons from the distance, but he saw a red button.

The Calamity pressed it.

For a moment, nothing happened, and then the entire theater exploded—not with light, but with pressure. Reality around it contracted. Space folded inward, drawn into the console like water spiraling down a drain.

And then—

The console split—a seam appearing along its center to divide it cleanly in half. Both halves began to separate, and as they did, an inky darkness began to bleed out. The dimension that had existed inside the console—compressed, folded, and contained—began to decompress.

The space that had been locked outside causality suddenly snapped back into normal reality, and the transition was violent.

Aldric shielded his eyes as a beam of pure white suddenly shot out of the console, drenching the entire theater in blinding brilliance.

And from that light, a black hand emerged—grasping the edge of the console, knuckles tight and sparking with electricity.

"Aodhán," Aldric breathed in shuddering relief, eyes wide as he took a step forward, then another, and then he was running.

Behind him, steps echoed as they all rushed toward the console, panic and joy tangling into a single desperate motion. But in their relief, none of them noticed the Calamity slip away, vanishing into the shifting shadows to watch the mortal reunion from afar.

Az'marthon's Legacy would be coming out soon, and Zarxan intended to examine him greatly before making his approach.

If nothing else, perhaps he might uncover why this child had earned the favor Az'marthon had denied him.

Why Az'marthon had chosen this child over him.

But even as Aldric raced toward Aodhán’s grasping hand, even as hope surged in his chest, he couldn’t have imagined how close he’d come to never seeing him again.

Fifteen minutes earlier, back in Abyssos, the earth had just thundered with the fall of the Death Wolf, a tsunami of inky darkness billowing toward the sky like the souls of dying men clawing their way to freedom.

The death of the second creature brought a wave of shuddering relief to the group, but Aodhán didn't let himself join in it this time. They weren't out of the woods yet. The sky still thundered from the battle between Serythion and the Blood Phoenix.

And if for one reason or another the Blood Phoenix won, then they were all dead.

And he couldn't let that happen—not when he could stop it, or at the very least help Serythion out.

They had roughly ten thousand abyssal points left, which was approximately eight tiers. It wasn't much, but it would be enough. Enough to tip the scale in Serythion's favor and help him destroy the phoenix.

Once the Phoenix was dead—truly dead—then he could rest. But not now.

Breath hitching, Aodhán raised the abyssal spike and pointed it at the Blood Phoenix. However, just as he prepared to allocate the points to reducing the Phoenix's tier, the sky cracked—a single jagged line splitting the firmament from horizon to horizon like glass.

That fracture cut through the group's relief like a blade, and every head snapped upward as a thousand smaller cracks spiderwebbed outward, liquid nothingness bleeding into reality like viscous ink.

Almost at once, the Blood Spawns fled, their shrieks blending with the howls of dozens of Death Wolves into a discordant cacophony that filled every heart with dread—and the promise of annihilation.

Whatever was happening, it wasn't good—it couldn't be good—and they were directly beneath it.

The cracks widened, more darkness spilling through, and Aodhán just knew that if they stayed here even a moment longer, they would all die. The group came to the same conclusion almost immediately, and without hesitation, they all ran, skills and abilities pushing them forward.

Aodhán's body erupted with violet lightning as he prepared to teleport. But before he could, space itself curdled—glitching into existence and collapsing again, heaving around them like a turbulent sea.

"No teleportation!" Baxter shouted as he ran, eyes wide in panic. "Space is too unstable. We have to run!"

Nothingness burst into reality immediately—not shadows, not darkness, but a flood of pure oblivion pouring through the broken sky like a dam cracked wide open. It slammed down to the earth an instant later, a cascading wall of erasure so absolute that everything it touched simply ceased to exist.

Aodhán exchanged a glance with Daruk and Baxter, and though he didn't say a word, they both knew this was goodbye. There was no escaping this flood of oblivion. Not unless they teleported, and with space so unstable, the probability of making it out alive was next to zero.

His entire chest cavity, gouged out.

Aldric. Artemis. Tyrus.

Jethro.
