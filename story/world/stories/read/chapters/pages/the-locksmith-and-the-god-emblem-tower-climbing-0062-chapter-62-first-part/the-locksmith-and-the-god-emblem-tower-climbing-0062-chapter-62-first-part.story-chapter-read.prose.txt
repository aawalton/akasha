What a delay... Harry slid through the darkness, wondering why the descent was so long compared to the other floors. Besides, he still hadn't gotten used to the fact that they had to go down to go up a floor.

Even after a few seconds, it felt like he would fall forever until he finally collided with a small door. Opening it and crawling to the next floor always made him uncomfortable as gravity shifted. Compared to the sand surrounding the coliseum, this time he could feel cold planks covering the floor.

Surrounded by tall shelves of esoteric books, Harry felt like he was back in Devon's bookstore. A well-lit room where candles seemed to ponder the manuscripts dragged on the floor, placed on the wall, or scattered across the tables.

Behind him and the hatch, a staircase beckoned him to continue his journey and discover what this place was all about. The Locksmith, however, had enough self-control to wait for his colleagues. As each one knocked on the hatch, Harry opened it to greet them, since he had been the first to jump and the first to arrive. First came Madeline, then Goldman, then Manes, and then Brun; all of them seasick from the journey that had passed so quickly.

"Did you see my—" Harry's sentence was cut off by a bang coming from the hatch, which forced open. From inside, an arm emerged, its fist smoking.

"...I don't remember it taking this long," Matthews said, his voice hoarse. "At least the journey was uneventful."

Uneventful? Harry added inwardly as he simply smiled at Matthews, who refused his help to leave the hatch.

"So, what place is this?" He asked, looking around. "A bookstore?"

"...What a difference..." Madeline said to herself as she checked the books and artwork that Harry hadn't noticed. "That... That's a Beinhaw! This painting alone must be worth thousands of Chimes. I thought they'd burned them all. What's this doing here?"

"I recognize these statues," Brun said in a honeyed tone, approaching a golden statuette of what appeared to be a mythological figure. "Even in my time, this religion had already been abandoned..."

Harry, listening attentively to what his colleagues were saying, soon found himself exploring the books and manuscripts that seemed to rest beneath the immaculate floor. He picked up one that was covered in scribbles, with only a few curious symbols on the cover. Some were like a circle and a square together, and others were a combination of a triangle and a square. What's the purpose of this?

Goldman, not understanding much about either literature or religion, stood near the stairs, alert to any dangers.

"The evil that plagues..." Matthews said softly, but Harry heard and noticed his attempt to read the piece of literature. "A..."

"The Evil That Plagues the Dangers of Living." Harry took the book from Matthews' hand, who seemed irritated. He then turned the cover over. "A biography of Grute Malbourne. Seriously, you can't read? Didn't they teach you that in the barracks?"

"I didn't like studying..." Matthews said softly, looking down, then took the book back from Harry's hands. "Now give it to me! It's mine."

"It's mine?" Harry chuckled slightly. "How old are we, 8? Man, not one of these books is yours. That makes me wonder about this floor, actually. So far, they've all been at least open or wide. This one looks like a confined space, like a fisherman's house."

"I don't think a fisherman would write this much, Harry," Madeline said, holding up a piece of paper with its lower half burned. "This certainly looks like the work of an academic."

"One who's lost his mind." Goldman said, leaning against the stairs, still alert.

"His mental state may have been compromised, yes," Brun replied. "Did you find anything useful? For some reason, my Emblem isn't giving me any information whatsoever. And this place doesn't even look big... Either it's bigger than it seems, or there's some kind of interference."

Raven... Harry pondered the mysterious figure.

"What could possibly be useful?" Matthews asked, rattling the books. "A key?"

Moving away from the group, Harry found what appeared to be an adjacent section of the bookstore; a study. Stepping over piles of books and shelves stretching to the ceiling, the Locksmith cautiously entered the section unnoticed by the others. The lighting seemed the same, coming both from above and from some candles precariously placed on the shelves. If someone accidentally pushed, this place would be engulfed in flames in seconds...

The questions of the group behind him seemed more distant as he ventured deeper into the room. Harry then reached a more open area with a table in the center. On the right side, a blackboard was covered in equations Harry didn't understand. Squinting, searching for linguistic clarification among the numbers, the young thief found only more calculations.

With his foot, he tripped over a book, almost the same shade as the one Matthews had found. When he picked it up from the floor, he saw that the title was the same, "The Evil That Plagues the Dangers of Living." He flipped the cover, unsurprised to find the same synopsis.

The books are repeated...? Harry added inwardly. As much as it seemed like just another mannerism of the Logos Tower, Harry felt this was far from trivial. Perhaps it was his anxiety talking, but he could swear he had a connection to this place. One that transcended his memory of the Devon bookstore. One that had gone beyond that.

Harry then looked at the picture again. Thump! Thump! His heart skipped a beat, startling him. He turned around in a jump, only to find himself alone in the room again. His foot then tripped on one of the books left behind, causing Harry to fall hard onto the cold floor of the study.

"Ouch..." He contorted his face while scratching his head, checking for any cuts or bumps from the impact. Thump! Thump! His heart seemed to want to tell him something as it continued to pound in bursts. He realized this only happened when he looked at the blackboard.

Wait a minute... Still lying on the floor, he could make sense of one of the symbols that were centered on the board. It was the joining of three elements. A square shape, a triangular shape, and a circular shape. No. There were dozens of joinings. Dozens of symbols that combined and separated these three shapes in many different ways, as if they were chained together.

He stood up, brushing dust off his knee, and approached the board. What does this mean? Noticing some imperfections on the edges of the drawings, Harry noticed that the white seemed somewhat faded, as if the chalk itself were disheartened by something.

He then grabbed a piece of chalk lying at the bottom of the board and examined it. He coughed violently as he inadvertently sniffed a smoke that had been created when a piece of it shattered. But wait. Harry recognized that smell. It was moon dust.

"This..." Harry murmured as he dropped the chalk, which cracked into even more pieces. Noticing a bulky part on the left of the board, the young thief then pulled it forward.

It's a secret compartment... The Locksmith conjectured as he removed this part of the board with difficulty. The tip came loose, causing Harry to fall hard again, this time onto the table.

As soon as the board collapsed, he heard agitated footsteps behind him, followed by a new presence in the room; Madeline.

"Harry! Are you alright?" She said, helping him to his feet. "We heard something falling here earlier, but we weren't sure if it was you, so we came cautiously."

"It's alright..." He gladly accepted her help and gave her an apologetic smile. "I just..."

As if his words had choked his throat, Harry made a strange expression as he looked at what lay behind the blackboard, covered in symbols. Several engravings, some pasted and others falling to the floor, shed light on the possible reason for all this; the moon.

It was known throughout the continent, at least on the black market, that moonstone was the catalyst for the Emblems to function. All of this, meticulously described in the book "The Emblem Theory" by an unknown author — presumably identified as the Astronomer — was common knowledge among Emblem users. The author, however, didn't give many plausible reasons why this would be the case. Too much conjecture involved the extraterrestrial composition of the natural satellite or even that the moon was a construct of another civilization, hence its mystical properties. The photos, however, indicated something else.

Scribbled around the various photographs still pinned to the wall were question marks and nonsensical phrases, involving the joining and separation of objects as if representing a machine. No. Upon closer inspection, Harry could see that it referred to an object... a living being.

"The moon..." Harry said, holding up a image. "Is it alive?"

Like a coin whose half had been exposed to acid, the photo showed the smooth, immaculate contrast of the satellite, its scars giving texture to what seemed like a volumeless object. The dark part merged with the rest of the universe as it remained still, observing.

"I didn't know it had so many dots..." Goldman said, narrowing his eyes. "And they're big!"

"...They're craters." Harry said, paying little attention to the gentle giant's words. He could only focus on one in particular.

Next to the chosen photo was a scribble that read "Logos." Harry then noticed a tiny dot in its center, something inside a crack. Like a color from another world, the more Harry looked, the more it shone. It was a moss green combined with the yellow of the sun. No. It was a cosmic purple that illuminated the darkness of the night. It was a word. It was a meaning. It represented the joining of everything and, at the same time, the distance between all things in the universe. It was...

"Harry!" Madeline pushed the drawing away, snapping the young thief out of his trance. "What happened? You've been staring at that picture for about five minutes. Your nose...!"

The Locksmith could smell the fresh blood as he wiped it with the hem of his sleeve, not caring about the mess he'd made. His erratic eyes now seemed to rest under Madeline's embrace, who was visibly worried.

He saw Brun and Goldman, their faces confused about what had happened and the meaning of those photos.

Harry then pulled away from his beloved's embrace and said frantically, "There's... There's something out there."

"What are you talking about—" Madeline began, but was quickly cut off.

"It's Logos!" Harry exclaimed, gripping her shoulders. "It's always been Logos! It's the reason for everything. Infinite wisdom. It's all about the Emblems, Maddy. The God Emblem comes from Logos; the source."

His words seemed so enigmatic, and yet so clear.

"...You look like a drunken sailor, Harry," Madeline said, taking a step back. "I'm scared."

"...I'm sorry." He scratched his head and adjusted his tie. "I'm sorry, but I had a moment of clarity, and I feel the answer is near. We just need—"

"'Day one.'" Brun said, examining a document on the floor, spread out near the engravings. "Guys, I think this is a diary."
