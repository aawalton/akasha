"Right then," Gleeward said, settling into his stance as the last of Jack's footsteps faded into the corridor behind him. "Jana Husk, aye? That's yer name?"

"Hask!" Jana snarled, her remaining hand already dripping green with toxins.

"Husk, Hask, who gives a shite," Gleeward adjusted his grip—left hand forward, right hand back, point aimed at her center mass—and smiled at her. "Let's see what ye've got, ye miserable one-armed sack o' piss and spite."

The cultist didn't give him the courtesy of letting him finish.

She came in screaming—not words, just a torn, animal sound that scraped his eardrums like a blade on stone—and her remaining hand's nails extended into three whip-tendrils of green-black venom that lashed outward in a fan of corrosive death.

"Why won't you people let me kill her!" Jana's voice was so high-pitched it grated on his ears. "I'll kill you all! The priest and plan be damned!"

"Shut yer trap! You can speak more about the plan, though—" Gleeward ducked the first whip, caught the second and third on the flat of his spear, but the first one arced back with a serpentine intelligence and caught him across the ribs and shoulder.

Piss on tha'—FUCK!

The venom bit through his flimsy leather armor and rapidly sank into the flesh beneath. It felt like a hundred wasps stinging in unison, the entire area across his side blooming into a fire that raced along his nerves.

His grip on the spear weakened. The point dipped toward the floor.

Shite. Shite shite shite—

Jana pressed in—three rapid strides, nails reforming into fresh whips.

Jana saw the opening and pressed in—closing the distance in three rapid strides, her nails already reforming into fresh whips for another strike—and Gleeward threw himself sideways, pressing his back against the corridor wall just as the whips cracked through the air where his torso had been.

He planted the spear's butt and swung the blade upward in a rising cut that sheared one tendril apart. The severed nail dissolved into green mist.

Gleeward had been hoping—praying, if he was honest, and he wasn't a man who prayed for much besides a decent pint—that severing the whips would hurt her.

They were a part of her body for fuck’s sake—extensions of her mantle probably—and cutting them should have dealt a blow to the woman's core the same way losing a finger would hurt anyone else.

But Jana didn't flinch.

She glared death at him and rotated her wrist. And the remaining two whips swept low at his legs. The remaining two whips swept low, aimed at his legs, and Gleeward had to vault the spear like a pole to clear them before landing on the other side.

His instincts were screaming with the venom rapidly spreading in his body. He could feel a cold numbness in an expanding ring, turning muscle sluggish and mind dull.

"Stand still!" Jana snarled, and she lashed out again.

Three whips became five, splitting at the tips like a hydra's heads.

She's stronger than I am.

Not in skill—no, the woman fought like a berserker, all forward momentum and no footwork, no techniques—but in power.

The tyranny of rank.

She was an Overmortal, and Gleeward was not, and her aura—that faint green-tinged sheen wrapped around her body—reduced his attacks to shallow cuts that closed almost as fast as he opened them.

His parries turned her strikes aside, but didn't stop them. Wherever her venom touched his armor, the enchantments failed and the leather dissolved.

He was fighting a battle of attrition he couldn't win, and every second that passed, the venom in his blood was tilting the odds further.

"Hold still, you cockroach!" Jana snarled.

And Gleeward had no choice but to move right into the whip's range—because her attack style relied on range, and at close quarters those tendrils should lose their advantage—and if he could get inside their reach—

He ducked the first. Dodged left around the second. The third caught his shoulder guard and sheared a strip of leather away.

Gleeward found the opening, and his spear reached Jana's throat.

And stopped.

The tip pressed against the skin of her neck and simply… didn't go through. Gleeward felt the resistance—a wall of force, invisible and holding him back—and with a roar poured every ounce of strength into the thrust.

Her aura. That bloody aura. I’ll not be stopped!

The tip pushed and managed to pierce the surface, drawing a thin line of green-tinged blood.

"DIE!" Jana screamed, talons swiping for his throat.

Gleeward dropped—slammed his back flat against the stone floor—as the claw sliced the air above him. From the ground, he could see it: the faint green sheen wrapped around her body, thin but chaotic; he could see faint ripples in the surface that opened up—like chinks in her armor—every time she moved.

She's got aura, and I don’t. That's the whole problem in one word. But her control over it isn’t crazy… I can do it!

He kicked, and his boot connected with her ankle, catching Jana by surprise. She stumbled, and in the half-second her balance was gone, her aura flickered, just like he wanted.

Gleeward grabbed his spear and drove the point up through the gap, under her ribs, through the chink, and piercing clean through.

He poured a massive amount of his essence into the weapon and wrenched it free, opening a hole in her side that sprayed blood and mush of her insides across the floor.

"AHHH!" Jana screamed, pressing her hand to the wound. The venom cauterized it shut in a bubbling seal that smelled like burning copper.

Then she hiss-smiled at him.

It was the worst expression Gleeward had ever seen on a human face. Not because of the fury—that was there, aye, burning behind her eyes like a house fire—but because layered beneath it was pity.

Nae. Can't have tha'.

He'd take a raging berserker over a rational one any day of the week. Rage made people stupid. Pity meant she was thinking again, and a thinking Overmortal was a dead man's opponent.

So Gleeward did what he did best. He opened his gob.

"Listen, love—" He spun the shaft to deflect a whip that arced for his head. "—och, cannae even let a man finish his bloody sentence, can ye? Rude. Anyway, I've been kneed in the bollocks by meaner women than you, and they were sober."

"Shut up and die!"

"Can't, sweetheart. Mouth runs when I'm havin' fun." He set his feet, brought the spear back into guard, and smiled at her. "An' I'm havin' the time o' my miserable life."

The pity in her expression cracked. Fury flooded back in.

Good.

Jana launched herself at him, all five whips lashing from every angle. Gleeward surged his essence through his body—pushing muscles past their safe limits, forcing speed out of legs the venom was steadily claiming—and his spear became a blur.

Quick successive thrusts—neck, eyes, neck, eyes—forced her onto her back foot. She was ready for the spear's reach but not for the speed of his combinations, each attack flowing into the next without pause, driving her backward step by step.

His blade grazed her cheekbone, and he followed up low—a butt-strike with the spear shaft against her knee with full force of the momentum—and Jana buckled for the second time.

Her aura flickered in a desperate, uneven pulse, and Gleeward—waiting for that to happen—drove the spear through the gap and into the meat of her left thigh, pinning the blade into the stone beneath.

"AHHH!" Her shriek nearly burst his eardrums—he felt blood trickle from his ears and nose—but he didn't let go.

He held the spear steady, keeping her pinned, and when her hand clawed at the shaft to wrench it free, he cocked his right fist back and cracked her across the jaw.

Not elegant, but he had learned how to punch purely from the countless times he had been in a pub brawl.

He used the embedded spear as a fulcrum, rotated around it, and drove his boot into the side of her head in a roundhouse that snapped her neck sideways.

He was moving for a follow-up when her nails found his legs. Venom sank into him—cold, spreading, hungry—and Gleeward stomped on her hand, wrenched free, and staggered backward.

Jana ripped the spear out of her own thigh with a grunt and hurled it at his face.

"Ye goddamn, shite-lickin'—" Gleeward caught the shaft mid-air, the edge slicing into his palm. "—did ye just throw my own bloody spear at me?!"

"I'll throw your HEAD next!"

"Lady, human heads don’t just detach—that's no way anatomy works, ye deranged—"

"DIE!"

What followed was the worst two minutes of Gleeward's life.

Jana stopped fighting like an assassin entirely and fought like a wounded animal, and every attack was a trade.

She'd take a spear thrust through the ribs if it meant landing a whip across his arm, eat a slash to the face if her nails found his leg.

Every trade favored her because Jana Hask didn't care about dying, and Gleeward very much wanted to keep breathing.

He landed seven clean hits in those two minutes. His spear work was better than it had been in the year. But her aura, her damn aura, ate half the damage from each strike.

She just bled, and bled, and kept coming.

How bloody unfair having the fucking aura was.

Gleeward had taken four hits in the gaps—right shoulder, left forearm, back of his thigh, and a shallow rake across his stomach—and his right arm was half-dead.

Both legs were starting to stiffen. The wound across his gut burned with every breath.

I'm losin'. I can not for fuck’s sake hurt her fast enough, and she can hurt me plenty.

He parried a whip—barely, the spear wobbling in failing hands—and countered with a thrust, her aura reduced to a shallow puncture.

"Tha' all ye've got?" Gleeward slurred through the blood filling his mouth. "My dead gran hit harder, an' she was blind in both eyes an' missin' a soddin' leg."

A whip caught his right shin. His knee buckled. Jana's follow-up raked four gouges across his chest.

Blood—his blood—dripped between his boots.

I’m gonna die here—

But… but

—an' I'm lovin' every second of it.

The thought stopped him dead. Because it was true.

The pounding wasn't slowing. The tingling wasn't fading. With every hit he took and every hit he landed, the feeling was getting stronger. The thing he'd drowned in ale was screaming at him now with a clarity that cut through pain and venom and exhaustion.

CALL UPON ME!

And for the first time in years, he just might…

"What're ye grinnin' at?" Jana hissed. "Has approachin' death made ye senile?"

"Aye," Gleeward admitted, his grin widening. "Ain't it grand? Besides, calling me senile isn’t that like pot calling kettle black?"

She screamed in frustration and launched herself at him, and the whole area around him trembled.

It started on the floor. A tremor rolled upward through the stone like the bastion had been struck by a fist of god.

Then the sound hit.

It came from below—from floors beneath them, deep in the bastion's guts—and it wasn't a boom.

It was a roar. The kind of sound a volcano makes when nature shows its wrath.

BOOM!

The second tremor was worse, and it carried heat with it… rolling through the corridors like a flash flood, unstoppable and indifferent, and he had to pour all of his essence through his channels just to resist the heat that washed over his skin and kept going, carrying the scent of burned stone and ozone and a power so vast it made his soul shudder.

In the sudden dark—lit dimly by only a couple of lights and by his spear's green glow and Jana's toxic luminescence—Gleeward watched the woman freeze.

The fury drained from her face. All of it—every ounce of rage, every shred of howling grief—just emptied. What was left was blank, staring horror that aged her ten years in a single breath.

"That's not…" Her voice was barely a whisper. The whips had retracted. Her remaining hand hung at her side, trembling so badly the venom dripped in an uncontrolled stream. "That's not possible. Mareth—he's—that can't—"

Gleeward didn't know who Mareth was. Didn't care. What he cared about was the fact that the mad bitch had stopped attacking for the first time in their entire fight, and the thing that had scared her witless had come from the direction where Jack was running from.

And that meant that scary bastard did this.

Gleeward surged his essence and called upon his mantle for the first time in years.

[The Mantle of Martial Might].

The change was immediate. The trembling subsided. His breathing evened. His grip on the spear steadied.

The venom was still spreading, still eating at him, but the mantle's influence pushed the pain into a register where it could be acknowledged without being obeyed.

And the pounding in his chest didn't quiet.

It got louder.

Jana threw her head back and howled.

Her venom surged from her wounds, her pores, the stump of her arm, and cocooned her body in a shell of writhing toxic fluid. Her shoulder widened. Her nails extended into foot-long talons.

Her eyes went fully black, and Images flooded Gleeward’s mind—Vienna burning, garrison walls crumbling, Veric dead on the floor, Jack with his throat torn open—and beneath them, a whisper cold and patient as his own voice after the third bottle: Ye were never enough. Just a drunk with a spear. Ye'll die here, and nobody will remember.

Not real. This isnae—It’s just a mental attack.

But it felt like real, whatever transformation the woman had undergone, amped her aura and powers. All of them holding Gleeward in an iron grip.

"Find somethin' to hold onto. A person. A promise. A grudge, if that's all you've got. When the fear comes, you hold that thing, and you don't let go."

Then suddenly, he found himself recalling Veric's advice. The old man leaning against the wall, cigarette in hand, scarred eyes steady.

A grudge works.

Not against Jana. Not against the cult.

Against himself.

Against twenty-five years of drinking away the best thing about him.

Against his parents throwing him out for being a disgrace to thier prestigious magus lineage. Against them, mages.

Against every fight, he'd sandbagged. Every drill he'd half-arsed.

No more. No more running.

His hand closed around the spear, and his mantle responded. Light green essence erupted from his core throughout his body, and where it passed, the venom stopped spreading. The numbness retreated. His hands steadied with a certainty that went beyond muscle.

A fragile green sheen like frost on glass manifested around him, and the moment it formed, he could feel everything.

The enemy before was a wall—an unbreakable wall—now it was a door he could push.

Gleeward stood up.

"Alright, ye miserable, screamin', venom-pissin', cockroach-shaggin' sack o' rotten shite." His voice was steady. His hands were steady. The spear burned green. "Let’s have a round two."

Jana—or what the monstrosity she had become—charged, and she was faster, a lot faster than before. The venom cocoon acting as both armor and weapon made it very difficult to deal with.

Foot-long talons came in high—a double overhead slash—and Gleeward met the strike horizontal above his head.

The impact crashed through his arms, but for the first time, the blow didn't fold him. His own aura held.

I can feel where she's goin' tae move—

Right talon swept low. He read it through his aura and sidestepped. Claws gouged the wall, and his counter came on instinct.

This time, the cut went deep.

He pressed. Thrust-thrust-sweep and Blade found her thigh, her hip, and her Shoulder as each hit finally dealt damage.

Jana wasn’t idle; she, like a raging animal, lunged at him. Talons raked his chest. A venom whip caught his spear arm.

But Gleeward didn't stop taking on the attacks head-on, taking more risks because he knew his newly awakened aura was surging because of the rush of battle, and it was slowly guttering; if it failed, he was dead.

And so he pushed himself to the limits as his spear became a blur.

Jana buckled. The cocoon was fracturing—patches sloughing off in smoking clumps.

She swiped one last time. Desperate. Off-balance.

Gleeward batted the attack aside, stepped inside her guard, and drove the spear point up under her sternum.

Through the cocoon. Through leather. Through skin and muscle and rib.

Into the heart.

Jana's black eyes went wide. Her talon hand came up—slow, trembling, the venom gone—and her fingers brushed the shaft protruding from her chest.

The cocoon dissolved. The transformation peeled away. Just a woman—burned, bleeding, impaled on the spear of a man she'd never bothered to learn the name of.

Her hand fell, and soon all life faded away.

Gleeward held the spear until the weight on the blade was just dead meat and gravity.

He pulled it free, and the mutated corpse crumpled to the stone.

He stood over the corpse—shaking, bleeding from his wounds—but he was standing. And she wasn't.

He pulled a bent cigarette from his belt pouch with numb fingers. His hands shook too badly for the flint striker, so he touched the spear's tip to the end—a tiny pulse of essence—viola fire.

He inhaled. Coughed. Inhaled again.

"Veric, ye lyin' bastard," he muttered through the smoke. “This still tastes like shite!”

That was when his newfound aura senses trembled.

The wave of power that rolled through the bastion. Not like an explosion, but in the sheer quantity of essence and power.

"Holy screamin' shite, what are you even doing?" Gleeward croaked, staring at the ceiling. The image of a young man formed in his mind. "Wha' an absolute bloody monster."
