Chapter V.XXV (5.25) - Witches Return

Kizu barely brought up his barrier in time to block as the massive insect slammed its glowing backside into it. But, before he had time to even let out a sigh of relief, the glowing end of it increased in its intensity.

With a curse, Kizu jumped to safety and watched from a different angle as, a moment later, a beam of light erupted from the creature and pierced the barriers he’d left behind.

Cheers erupted from the crowd, but Kizu ignored them. Instead, he scanned the arena floor for his fellow participant in today’s combat challenge. It was a fifth-year student scribbling in the dirt with a stick, muttering to himself.

Kizu jumped to his side. “What’s your plan?”

The boy flinched. “I’m going to summon a dome shield,” he muttered, not looking up from his sketch.

“How is that supposed to help us?” Kizu asked, annoyed.

The insect buzzed around the outskirts of the arena until it found its opponents once again. Its wings buzzed and its black eyes focused on the two humans trapped in the arena with it.

“I’m sorry,” the boy wailed. “I don’t like bugs!” Then he slammed his hand down and Kizu was nearly split in two as a dome of steel emerged from the summoning circle. The casing surrounded his companion, sealing him off from the battle.

Kizu jumped once again. This time he watched as the insect’s beam of light slammed into the summoned metal dome. It ricocheted off the magical steel and was dissipated by a professor before it could touch the audience. While it didn’t puncture the dome, the impact still left a massive dent in the student’s defenses.

Originally, Kizu had wanted to try working as a team to take down their foe to practice developing plans on the fly and adapting to the resources on hand; however, he was confident he could handle this bug solo.

This time when the insect flew at him, Kizu readied his stance, hands apart and open palms facing his foe. His monster leg tore the soles of his boots and gripped the dirt ground. He focused entirely on his buzzing opponent as it rocketed in his direction.

It glowed as it flew closer. Every second it cleared meters of distance.

The moment before impact, Kizu jerked one arm skyward and bent space around himself. Instead of careening into him, the giant insect curved over his head and slammed into the arena floor.

Kizu pivoted on his monster leg. A flick of his wrist and he held a blade of ice in his hand. He chopped in a downward arc.

While he intended to slice it open entirely, the insect recovered quickly and shifted its position. His blade lopped off half a wing but missed the main body.

It glowed brighter than ever before as it aimed its backside in Kizu’s direction. It would be a point-blank shot into his ribs. And his sword was basically useless. Even should he skewer the monster now, the attack would likely still go off and blast him.

Rather than dodge, Kizu brought forth more magic. His monster leg heated up as he flexed its innate affinity. With a shove of channeled magic, the insect burst into blue flames in front of him.

The beam of yellow light shot from within the flames.

Kizu was expecting this though. He shifted and felt the heat of the beam as its trajectory bent around him in altered space.

The beam’s collision caused an explosion behind him and he heard his teammate yelp in terror. But Kizu had no time to help the boy.

He snuffed out the blue flames with a wave of his hand and lunged forward.

The burned and battered monster attempted to crawl away, its one good wing fluttering madly. Kizu’s monster leg slammed it back into the ground. His claws tore into the creature’s exoskeleton. And this time, when Kizu lit his leg aflame, the fire scorched the monster’s insides.

It squirmed and he saw its light flickering as it attempted to strike once again. He pressed down harder. With a crunch, the enemy was defeated. Black flakes leaked out of the vanquished summoned monster.

Over to the side, Arclight was announcing his victory to the assembled crowd. Kizu turned and stared at his teammate’s battered metal shield. Dented and with a hole seared through the dome. It slowly dissipated, revealing a shaking boy inside it. He got to his feet and stumbled off to the prep room, head down and ignoring the jeers from the audience over him. Kizu followed after him.

“Sorry,” the boy muttered, not meeting his eyes as Kizu kept pace with him. “I just really can’t do bugs. Just the look of them….” He gulped. “The way they move is… uncanny. Did you see the striped antennae on that horrible thing?” He shuddered.

“It’s fine,” Kizu lied. He was still annoyed he didn’t get a chance to practice spontaneous teamwork with an unknown ally.

Kizu wiped off his leg with a magical rag that helped remove dirt and grime. One nice thing about fighting summoned creatures was that their insides always dissolved after being defeated, so no blood and guts on his outfit. He’d worked on an enchantment for his boot so the sole slowly mended itself over time. His academy uniform was fireproof and even managed to withstand some brutal flames from a Dragon; however, his monster leg continued to destroy his footwear. While his mending enchantment wasn’t quick, he found this to be a better solution than purchasing the same boot five times a week.

Not bothering to stick around for the new ranking placements, Kizu departed the arena. With this out of the way for the day, he had other studies to go catch up on.

His mind drifted from his normal subjects over to spatial magic. Wakino had mentioned meeting with him about some new spatial techniques. Their meeting was still a little ways off and she hadn’t clarified what exactly she intended to teach him. Even still, his mind went wild as it considered all the possibilities.

“Well fought, Kaga Kizu!”

The voice snagged shattered his thoughts. He pivoted to face the speaker.

Standing on the grass off to the side of the walkway, stood a tall girl with a black, wide-brimmed, pointed hat. Her academy uniform was modified to a stark black.

In her shadow, huddled a young red-haired girl with a similar hat and black robes.

“Mae!” Kizu hurried over to the young Kemon girl. She stared at him with wide eyes as he crouched down next to her. “You’re already back! You’re safe?”

“I am,” Mae said quietly. “The witches… they weren’t as bad as in the stories.”

“Of course we’ve returned!” Stina declared. “Have you forgotten the movement of the stars and universe above your head? I promised our fateful reunion!”

Kizu’s head spun. The stars and universe? Then it clicked. “Yesterday was the summer solstice!”

“Indeed.”

“You said that you’d officially form a pact with Mae as your apprentice with your coven as witness. Everything went smoothly?”

“Nothing to concern yourself with.” Stina set a hand on the Kemon girl’s shoulder. “Mae is my apprentice. However, fear not for her mistreatment. She is my sister now. Not my slave.”

Kizu nodded. That was a distinct difference between the covens of Hon and Tross. While the witches of Tross were widely considered weaker than Hon, they were considerably more united. And, most importantly, significantly less apprentices were eaten by displeased masters.

“How are you feeling?” Kizu asked Mae.

“Fine. I want to see Anata and Kon.”

“Permitted,” Stina said. “Your previous living arrangements, while less ideal, are satisfactory for now. After we are more settled into our roles, I will assemble quarters for us.”

Kizu let out a sigh of relief. At least Mae could go back to Anata and Kon. Things could return to normal for a while.

“Kaga Kizu.” Stina turned her attention back to him. “I require a sanctuary in which to teach at.”

“I suppose you can use my ship,” Kizu said, reluctantly. “I have the supplies you’ll need for any brewing experiments.”

“Regretfully, I am lacking in my education in that field. My master, before her death, specialized in hexes, astronomy, and the forming of pacts. She passed her talents onto me. I fear the mediocrity of my brews and soothsaying brings shame to my coven.”

“What’s your rank?” Kizu asked, curious. Now that he thought about it, Stina was indeed not in the S class last semester.

“21.” The witch bowed her head shamefully at the admission.

A far cry from the worst in the academy. But Kizu still considered himself only passable in the subject when compared to the crone’s acquaintances. Being a witch ranked lower than his S class must have meant Stina really struggled in the subject. And the crone had told him on numerous occasions that he had no talent for divination, so for Stina to be in his Divination B class also meant she was in the same boat as him.

Kizu glanced up at the sun overhead. He still had plenty of free time on hand. While he’d rather use it to get ahead in one of his other studies, Mae’s wellbeing took priority right now.

“Follow me. I’ll show you Owl’s Respite.”
