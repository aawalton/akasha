Kage stood on the Noble's Promenade for a moment, letting the dying light do nothing useful for his mood. Aldric had escorted him out in silence, the old steward's face arranged into something professionally blank, which on Aldric read as loudly as a shout. The man knew things. Servants always did. That was a thread for later, assuming later ever got around to the Vane estate again.

One day left.

He pulled the quest log up as he walked, more from habit than need.

[Quest: A Private Commission (House Vane)]

The bottom line of the commission had turned out to be a joke dressed as a job. Write a formal introductory verse for the Guild debut. Simple. Fifty gold. Except the woman paying him didn't want flattery, didn't want a sales pitch, didn't even seem to care whether the Gilded Brush accepted Lyra at all. Honesty. That was the deliverable.

She wants the truth read out loud in a room full of people who've never heard one. That's what I still don't get. Why? Accepted, rejected, doesn't matter. She's not paying for the verdict.

Which was, in a way, the easiest brief he'd ever been handed and the most annoying. Fifty gold for honesty. He could write flattery in his sleep. Flattery had rules. Honesty had a girl in it who'd painted a bird tearing off its own wings to fit through the bars of a cage, and who had a habit of wiping her thumb exactly like the mother she'd never known.

He'd figure that out tonight. Words were cheap to draft and expensive to finalize, and he worked better with a deadline breathing on his neck anyway.

The bigger item on the list had been sitting in his inventory since the raid, quietly radiating importance like an unread message from someone dangerous.

Crafting.

The walk from the estate district down to the Golden Plaza took twenty minutes and passed through three separate arguments about him.

He noticed because he'd started noticing. A woman in a spellthread robe stopped mid-sentence when his boots left grey dust on the tile in front of her. Two players hanging off the railing of a second-floor terrace tracked him past a whole city block, the taller one lifting a hand to point and the shorter one immediately slapping it down. Somewhere behind him a voice said charcoal longcoat, beret, that's got to be and then got swallowed by the crowd.

Congratulations, he thought. You're a rumor with a silhouette.

Nobody was walking up to him. That was the metric that mattered.

He put it out of his head and opened his inventory instead, walking on muscle memory, letting the crowd part around the dust.

Inventory review.

He didn't need to open the panels to see it. He'd been turning the pieces over in his head since the white.

[Concept: The Stifled Toll] - Legendary. Sound, Silence, Grief. A sorrow with mass.

[Concept: The Maker's Abandoned Breath] - Mythic. Genesis, Draft, Art, Potential. The leftover exhale of the being who had apparently drafted reality itself and then, for reasons unknown, stopped.

Two souls. That was the terminology he had settled on, and he'd stopped arguing with it. Conceptual Forging needed three components. The Soul, the conceptual material, the story you were feeding into the item. The Vessel, the physical item that would carry the legend. And the Anchor, the stabilizer, the medium that kept a concept from tearing the whole attempt apart and taking his Awen pool with it.

The Anchor had been the wall he'd been staring at until now. Legendary? Mythic? Trying to bind the Stifled Toll with anything he or the market had would be like mooring a warship with dental floss.

Except the wall had fallen over sometime yesterday, and he'd been too busy being nearly erased by a page monster to notice.

Ink of Origin. Three uses.

He turned off the Mosaic Path onto the Busker's Walk, threading between the frozen statue-poses of AFK sellers. A player in full plate stood mid-flex with a shop tag reading CHEAP ENCHANTS!!! hovering over his helmet. Kage sidestepped.

[Ink of Origin]

[Effect: A statement written in this ink is woven into reality's original text rather than imposed upon it. The world does not resist the edit, because the world remembers it as having always been true.]

If that wasn't an Anchor, nothing in the game was. The stuff… it convinced reality the concept had been there all along. Anchor was almost an insulting word for it. It was a cheat code wearing an Anchor's coat.

Three uses, though. Three.

Which brought him to the idea he'd been circling for a while, the one he kept almost-thinking and then backing away from because it smelled like greed.

A set.

Three uses of the Ink of Origin. Three separate pieces of gear. If he forged three items with the same conceptual lineage, the same soul-source, the same ink, would the system recognize them as related? Tier sets existed in every MMO ever made. Set bonuses were practically a law of physics. If Morpheus's reasoning system had any respect for novel ideas, and everything he'd seen suggested it had too much respect for that, then three pieces forged from one conceptual family might click together into something greater than the sum.

He'd had the same thought staring at the three Titan's Tears in his inventory. Three uses, three pieces. But the Tears were Rare catalysts, and feeding a Mythic concept through a Rare catalyst was a waste bordering on vandalism. The math had never justified it.

The math had changed.

One problem. A set implied one soul split three ways, or three souls with a shared theme. He had two souls, and they had nothing in common. Grief and Genesis. Those were different stories. Forcing them into one set would produce exactly the kind of incoherent mess the system loved to punish.

So. He'd have to gamble stretching The Maker's Abandoned Breath into three pieces.

He stepped into the shadow of the Grand Theater and the noise of the Golden Plaza folded over him. The open-air amphitheater was packed, auctioneers performing on the central stage with the dramatic flair of men announcing executions, holographic sheet-music tickers scrolling gold prices across the air above the crowd. His synesthesia translated the whole scene into pressure and smell, thousands of item values pressing on his gaze like coins of different weights, the auctioneers' voices arriving as bright brassy percussion.

He filtered it down to background static with a practiced irritation.

Focus. Souls and anchors.

The Stifled Toll, he understood. Grief, silence, sound. He'd watched where it came from. He could already feel the shape of the story it wanted to be, something heavy and patient, and he had a suspicion about what vessel it would want, but that forge could wait until he had a vessel worth the soul.

The Maker's Abandoned Breath was the strange one. Mythic, and the description field sat there empty, offering nothing. No narrative. Just the four keywords glowing in his mind like signs over shop doors. Genesis. Draft. Art. Potential.

Normally that would bother him. A soul without a description was a story he couldn't verify, and forging blind was how you wasted Mythic materials.

Except.

He stopped walking. A duelist's practice swing nearly took his ear off; he leaned out of it without looking, and the duelist apologized to the back of his head.

Except the Ink of Origin came from the same place.

The ink was the medium the First Maker had used to write drafts of the world. The Breath was the First Maker's leftover concept, the exhale of a work never finished. They weren't two materials that happened to synergize. They were two halves of one act. The thought and the breath. The pen and the hand holding it.

The world does not resist the edit, because the world remembers it as having always been true.

And the Breath was, what, Genesis? Potential? The raw could-be of an unfinished draft?

His heart rate ticked up. He caught himself cataloguing it, pulse elevated, palms warm, diagnosis pending, and then the label machinery jammed, because the obvious clinical word was excitement and the Operator didn't file excitement under crafting logistics. Excitement was a resource leak. Except the feeling kept sitting there anyway, warm and insistent, and the only honest name for it was the one his younger self would have used without embarrassment.

Anticipation.

"Annoying," he muttered to the tiled street. A passing roleplayer in wizard robes gave him a sympathetic nod, as if he'd agreed with something.

Because the combination had a shape, and the shape was familiar. Painfully familiar. Ink that turned statements into things that had always been true. A concept that was pure unwritten potential. He'd done this before, once. That was how Mumyo had been born. A story given a body.

He was standing in front of a jewelry-box building that gleamed at him.

Deja vu, he thought. The whole recipe is deja vu.

The Curator's voice surfaced, that vast breaking sound from the moment the doorway had recognized him. Master. And then, later, colder, the question that had been sitting in his chest like a swallowed stone ever since. Has my Master finished writing the world?

Kage exhaled slowly through his nose.

He didn't know. He didn't know what the First Maker had done, where he had gone, if the world ran on drafts and annotations and a god-AI with inscrutable goals. He didn't know how Valerius and the shattered crown and the war beneath existence connected to the class sitting in his character sheet like a loaded weapon. The Songstone might have answers.

The Amphitheater was his, in some bureaucratic Patron-of sense, and if there was behind-the-scenes lore about Valerius anywhere, that was the door to knock on. Realistically, though, the most he'd find there was another legacy breadcrumb for the Architect of Verse. Another long thread tied around his finger.

The threads were getting long enough to trip over. He shelved it. Deliberately. One commission, one craft, one day at a time.

So. The Breath.

He resumed walking, into the Grand Theater proper, and found a quieter tier of seating away from the stage theatrics. He pulled up the auction interface and let the categories bloom in front of him.

The constraint was the theme. That was the part he kept arriving at. The narratives he wrote into vessels weren't free-form. The system allowed interpretation, yes, generous interpretation, but a forged item was still a story, and a story had a spine. He couldn't take Genesis-Draft-Art-Potential and write it into a shield that meant endless endurance or a ring that meant sharp revenge. The soul would reject the lie, or worse, accept it and produce something warped. He had to move inside the theme. The item he made from the Maker's Abandoned Breath had to be about beginning. About the first stroke. About the thing that exists before the thing. Maybe. Just one of the many ideas.

An item about potential, he thought, drumming two fingers on the stone bench. What's the most broken possible expression of "potential" in a game?

His brain, the Operator part, the part that had spent years finding the seam in every system it touched, started generating candidates and discarding them. Experience gain. Skill growth. A weapon that grew. He already had one of those. An item that...

He shook his head. Vessel first. You didn't write a poem and then go looking for the paper; you found the paper and let it argue with you, instead.
