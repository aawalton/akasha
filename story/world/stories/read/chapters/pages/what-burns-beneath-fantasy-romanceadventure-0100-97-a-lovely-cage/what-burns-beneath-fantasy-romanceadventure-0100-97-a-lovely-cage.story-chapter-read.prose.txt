I felt very alone.

Darkness swept out in the direction Velidan had gone and cold marble loomed high on either side. And ahead… an ocean of flowers swayed beneath a sea of regal black and twinkling silver.

It all made me feel very small.

I sat for a little while, staring at the stars and replaying every fragment of the past few minutes. My gaze trailed to the broken pedestal and Velidan’s ruined meal.

Apparently I hadn’t shattered ‘pleasant,’ after all.

I shouldn’t be surprised, though, should I? This is how he was before; when he wanted to ‘sway me with charm,’ in his own words. He was… saints, he was nice. He healed me and made jokes and flirted.

And then as soon as I became a threat, he turned vicious. As soon as he realized asking me to do things wasn’t working, he promised to hurt me instead.

I stood. Paced. Eyed the mass of moonlit glass.

He said this whole situation will be much easier for him if I’m there willingly, if I’m listening to him and cooperating. He doesn’t want me to be frightened or defiant, he wants me to trust him. So he’ll keep playing nice, he’ll keep being polite and charming, as if I’ve forgotten all the horrible things he’s done.

The logic was sound. Velidan wasn’t going to drop ‘pleasant’ and ‘cordial’ anytime soon. I was… pretty sure.

Half of me felt almost giddy with the realization. I had some small fragment of value, of power, in our current dynamic. I was taking action, I was doing something- even if I didn’t fully understand what- to keep people safe.

And half of me screamed that I was only stepping further into a gilded cage. That the Fae’s facade would shatter again, sooner or later, and whatever happened would be much worse than his treatment in Baltha’s cave or on the balcony.

I sank back into the cushions with a sigh. Saints, and I don’t get a break from him, do I? I thought if I agreed to go with him I’d wake up and have a chance to collect myself, tell everyone the plan, then fall back asleep, or… I don’t know. I guess I didn’t really know what to expect.

A lump formed in my throat and was not easily swallowed. What would my friends think? What would they do? When morning came and went and I remained unconscious, would they assume it was intentional? Would they panic? Would they think I was in danger? Would they try to wake me up?

The questions crashed one after another as I searched the gleaming floor, gnawing my lower lip and twisting at my hem.

Well, there’s no point in sitting around thinking of all the ways this could go wrong. Salt, and I felt so good when I fell asleep! So brave and happy and… and there’s no reason a little uncertainty should make me so nervous again! For all I know, my mom saw this, too! Salt, maybe she wanted me to panic and break his fancy table! Maybe being trapped here for a while is exactly what I’m supposed to be!

I just need to distract myself. That’s all.

My gaze drifted to the pile of food and crystal once more.

I wonder if the goblins come in and tidy everything up. I certainly can’t imagine Velidan doing anything so menial. He probably glares at his messes and expects them to clean themselves out of fright.

The thought brought a little grin, and with it I hopped to my feet.

Well, I don’t care what he said. I’m definitely not going to leave a pile of broken glass for someone else to deal with. Hm. There must be a broom somewhere.

I paced a little ways down the hall and opened a door at random. Sconces of pale candlelight illuminated a small parlor set with gold-wreathed furniture and an assortment of wine bottles. The next door revealed a washroom larger than my entire home, and the next showed a small library with cozy-looking armchairs and a fireplace lit by smokeless, powder-blue flames. Welcoming heat emanated from the blaze. I found myself drawing near with my fingers outstretched and my eyes wide.

I suppose nothing should surprise me about this place, but… still. A magical fireplace! It’s so beautiful and warm, and it smells like… like…

It took me a moment to place each comforting scent, and a moment longer to break through the stunned realization that I was smelling my very favorite things. The fireplace gave off aromas of rich earth, woodsy cinnamon, and crisp pine nettles. And beneath that… the woodsmoke that often clung to Durst’s clothes and the familiar musk of a leather-bound runebook.

My eyes grew misty. I stood trembling, thinking with something between fear and awe that I could’ve stood before that fireplace for days and barely noticed the passage of time.

I turned quickly, knees knocking together, and hurried back to the door. On the way I bumped a shelf, sending several books thumping onto a soft gray carpet. One fell open and as I stooped to retrieve the tome my brows knit together.

Its pages were made of what looked like very thin sheepskin and covered in cramped, uneven script. I recognized many of the words, but… their arrangement didn’t make sense. The page I peered at seemed to be a nonsensical rambling about the color green, about its different shades and a long list of green things- some familiar, some not- that continued on for several more pages. I flipped along, growing more confused as I went.

Eventually the author switched to a list about their favorite kinds of weather. Many of the sentences were rambling and disjointed, hardly sensible, and some ended mid-thought before jumping to an entirely new topic. A quick glance at the other fallen books showed similar nonsense.

I wondered over the mystery for a few breaths, then set it aside with the thousand other questions I already had and slid the books back into place.

The next door revealed an oddly barren space. The room was not particularly large, cast mostly in shadow, and had no furniture or decorations besides a thick, dark rug. I crept in, hoping it was perhaps a large closet of some kind, then stopped as a familiar smell filled my nostrils.

My feet locked into place as I tried to place the odor. It was faint but unpleasant, reminiscent of spoiled fruit, metal, and something vaguely sour.

Ugh. It’s like… like… hm. Like that time farmer Jeth cut his leg on a wood axe and the wound didn’t heal right. It smells like when Clem showed me how to treat infected flesh.

The thought, and the vivid memory it instilled, made a shiver sweep down my spine. Another glance around showed no source to the odor and nothing else noteworthy in the room. I left with a tightness in my stomach and an uneasy prick at the base of my neck.

Perhaps some mysteries are better left unsolved.

The next door smelled faintly like roses blooming in damp soil. An excited shiver ran through me as I turned the knob.

Just as I’d thought, I’d found the bath. The expanse of water was glassy and deep, with curls of pearly mist and crisp white rose petals drifting along the surface. They stood in stark contrast to the deep sapphire hue and the whole room smelled divine.

I eyed it all longingly and, before I could stop myself, had knelt beside the edge. The motion brought an angry throb from my hip, and a quick peek downwards showed a sizable, blue-black bruise along the bone.

I waited a moment, ensuring that there were no footsteps echoing down the hall, then tugged my boots off and rolled my trousers up to the knee.

I’ll be quick. Just dip my toes in, just for a moment. Just a tiny, harmless indulgence before I’m trapped here all day.

Silky heat enveloped my feet and I had to stifle a little moan. It felt like bliss.

If I survive all this mess, the first thing I should do is get a room like this. Maybe I can hire someone to build a giant, heated bathhouse in Fellbrook.

The groundbreaking- and silly, of course, because a fancy bathhouse in the middle of a tiny farming town was quite ridiculous- idea had me giggling as I swished my feet through the pool. Rose petals swirled lazy trails around my calves.

“You seem to struggle with simple commands, mortal.”

I whipped my feet out, sending a spray of water splashing to the marble floor.

Velidan stood leaning against the doorframe. His stained shirt had been replaced by one of embroidered forest-green. “This is not ‘waiting here.’”

My face grew warm. “I was looking for a broom.”

One brow rose as fathomless eyes trailed from me to the water, then back. His silence spoke volumes.

“Well, I was.” Hard marble dug into my backside as I quickly laced my boots. The witchwood dagger felt solid and reassuring against my calf. “I just got distracted.”

“So I see.” He tilted his head to one side. “Can you swim?”

I squinted at that, rising and dusting off my knees. My hip throbbed with the motion and I hid my wince behind a tart, “If I say yes, are you going to push me in?”

Soft laughter curled like smoke between us. “Not at present. You’ll bathe later.”

My heart stuttered at the thought. It was an enticing one, certainly, but… not with him anywhere nearby. “I’d… rather not.”

“It’s not a request.”

The knot in my belly reformed. I leveled a wary look at him, and the look he returned was nothing short of smug.

“You’re going to play the part of a cowed plaything tomorrow, Brin. You can hardly do that smelling like… mm.” He took a step towards me, nostrils flaring and voice dripping with disdain. “Graveyard soil, some kind of honeyed wine, winter trees, human blood, and leather.” His eyes narrowed. “And several other men.”

“You…” My jaw dropped. I spent a moment floundering in stunned silence, then shook my head. “You can smell all that? Wait, can you always-”

“Yes.”

That brought a fresh rush of embarrassed heat, but this was no time to think about what a sensory assault I must have been on more than one occasion. I slowly folded my arms, shoulders back and voice even. “And will I have privacy while I bathe?”

“Well,” the answer came low and edged by a predatory little smile, “that depends on if you can swim.”

My nails dug into my palms. “I can.”

“Unfortunate.”

My glare just earned a chuckle. Then Velidan pushed off the doorway and waved a hand, beckoning me after him. “Come. It’s time to ensure you stay sleeping.”

I followed on unsteady feet, watching him warily. The feeling pressed in again, with an almost palpable weight, that I was walking deeper into a cage.

Breathe. Breathe. Everything’s alright. He’s a monster and a prick and definitely enjoys making me uncomfortable, but he hasn’t hurt me. He’s playing nice.

I trailed behind the Fae, fists clenched and heart beating just a bit too fast.

My mom saw what the runes did, and she still chose to give them to me. Velidan and Faer and all of them. She wanted me to be here. It’s a good thing that I’m here.

…Hopefully.

Velidan’s frame disappeared into a vaguely familiar doorway. I followed and had scarcely entered his workshop when a green, child-sized figure crashed into my legs.

“Pet!” Slip threw her knobby arms around my knees. “I was so worried about you!”

“You were?” A surprised smile puzzled its way across my face. I knelt, stifling a grunt as my hip gave a fresh ache, and tentatively returned the goblin’s embrace. “Really?”

“Of course I was! Dawn, lord Vel warned me it would look strange when you left, but I wasn’t expecting you to get so fuzzy! I thought for sure that big monster was… I dunno. Unmaking you or something.”

My mind wanted to latch onto quite a few parts of that statement. I settled for, “Big?”

She pulled back, face scrunching into a grimace. “Well, he smelled big.”

I searched her expressive features. “I’m glad you’re okay, too. I was worried about you after I left. I’m so sorry if I scared you.”

She flashed a wide, spike-toothed grin. “Worried about me? Aw. I stepped straight back to the Dreamweaver den once you slipped out, told them the whole story. The younglings couldn’t believe I’d talked to a human. Heh, half of them couldn’t believe you could talk at all!”

My smile wilted at that. Right. Because however much I might like Slip, the reality is that she doesn’t see me as a person. The goblins think I’m a pet and a snack and… stars’ know what else.

Slip’s head tilted to a sharp angle. Her coarse, choppy black hair swept down, brushing the scaly patches on her cheeks. “Uh-oh. Did I make you sad again?”

The sheer concern in her scratchy voice made my heart throb. Not knowing quite what else to do, I just offered a weak shrug and stood back up.

Velidan had paced farther into the workroom and now stood examining the contents of a cabinet. A little crease puckered his brows and his mouth was turned down at the corners, as if he was having trouble deciding something.

My gaze trailed from him to the stone table nearby. It was clean, now, absent all traces of baliker blood and flesh. The only evidence of the creature’s existence- and evisceration, I thought with a shiver- was a glass jar on the table. Several sharp black teeth sat inside, looking quite malicious beneath the hanging lavender lights.

Velidan trailed along, reaching for a high shelf. Bottles clinked and cloth rasped as he rummaged around, seemingly lost in thought.

Slip’s attention turned towards him, too. Her fingers spasmed and she began hopping from foot to foot. Her jagged claws clicked against the marble and her little shift- one made of sunset-orange cloth instead of leaves, this time- hissed with the movements.

“Are you alright, Slip?”

“Huh?” She tore her gaze from the larger Fae. Her sharp teeth flashed in the light. “Oh, yeah! Right as a dream! Dawn, pet, aren’t you excited?!”

“Excited for what?”

The goblin’s enormous, wet eyes blinked several times. Then her grin turned sly as she turned back towards Velidan. “Is it a surprise, master? Should I not say?”

“You may.” His thoughtful expression smoothed as he gathered a few bottles- some of them gleaming with vibrantly shaded powders or crushed petals and some of them definitely filled with what looked like bone fragments and shriveled organs- and began transferring their contents into small leather pouches. The scents of dried herbs, mildew, and spoiled meat briefly wafted through the air.

Slip’s smile widened, nearly splitting her expressive face from ear to ear. “Oh, pet, you’re in for a treat! The Moonlight Market’s real special! Well, this one is. All the other ones are, uh, dead. Everybody ate each other, right? Bloodbaths.” She cackled. “But this one? Best place in the realm, other than here. You’re going to love it!”

“The ‘Moonlight Market?’” I turned a puzzled glance between the two Fae. He did say we were going to procure an item. Did he actually mean… “Wait, we’re going shopping?”

Slip hopped up and down. “Isn’t it exciting?!”

Velidan turned, seemingly satisfied by whatever strange assortment of things he’d collected. The leather pouches all hung from his belt, save for one, which he handed to the much smaller Fae.

She took it with a delighted squeal. Her claws sank into the leather as her laughter rang off the walls.

I found myself unable to hide a little grin. Rather than two otherworldly monsters, the pair resembled a child who’d been gifted something very special by an adult and couldn’t contain their joy.

My gaze trailed behind them, catching sight of something else noteworthy, and my heart gave another little throb. Velidan stood a short distance from the table he’d so casually trapped me against earlier. His bloody handprints were gone from the stone, and on it now…

A wooden bowl full of seeds and half-crushed herbs sat perfectly intact.

I was so startled by the sight, I hardly realized Velidan had moved until he was practically on top of me. I skittered back, but he just walked past with a sidelong glance and headed back into the hallway.

My gaze trailed back to the bowl. My heart thrummed unsteadily, and I found myself wondering if perhaps my impulsive, hopeful little gift hadn’t been for nothing, after all.

But I didn’t have time to dwell on that thought. Slip’s bone-thin, knobby fingers clutched at mine, and I found myself being tugged after Velidan as the goblin squealed and danced.

He led us down the main hallway, almost towards the great double doors, then into a side chamber lit by hanging silver globes. There was no carpet, just a polished floor of segmented stone slabs. A section in the room’s center was stained in blood and bore a hatchwork pattern of familiar, carved lines. The room smelled of iron.

“It’s the same as the one in the barrow!” I burst out, looking between Velidan and the markings with wide eyes. “Almost exactly the same! What does it do?”

Slip scampered straight into the rune, grinning at me, as Velidan removed one pouch. I watched curiously, then took a step back when his other hand produced a wicked-looking black knife. The blade was water-smooth and blacker than sin, almost seeming to swallow the pale light around it, and was curved into the shape of a half-moon.

He turned to me, looking as calm as if he’d pulled out a waterskin. “You were correct, Brin. It’s a rune, and it’s nearly identical to the other.”

I couldn’t seem to tear my eyes off the knife. “And what does it mean?”

“Felinor.” He raised the blade. “Open.”
