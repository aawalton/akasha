Are they cheering? Daniel thought to himself as he looked around the crowd that chanted his name; Bodkin. He thought about using regular Daniel, but it wouldn't have that much impact. Maybe I should not have listened to Manes and just used my real name...

"You're still troubled by the name?" Manes said to him. "Come on, it doesn't mean anything! It's just a war name."

"...War name?" The viking replied, uncertain of Manes perspective. "I go to war an honest man. My name should represent that."

"Daniel isn't a good name. Trust me, I know it."

"You're not even old enough to hunt a deer. How could you—"

"And now, ladies and gentleman, we introduce you to Bodkin, a viking from old ages, and Manes, a... starving child!" The crowd went crazy.

"Starving child!?" Manes looked to Daniel in search of solace.

"It is true." He agreed. "That is a good thing, though."

"How!?"

"If they see you as weak, they perceive you as a weakling, and therefore, your value decreases. The owl strikes in the dark, you see."

"...I am the owl?" Manes looked to the other side, now watching as two competitors appeared. "In this case, what animal are you?"

"...Bear."

Sneaking through the shadows, Harry watched the cooks preparing the new batch of food for the foreigners. It consisted of the same lumpy, and certainly unhealthy, ingredients. Who would care about eating something spoiled, though? If prepared carefully, certain foods were even more delicious when spoiled. Not that the young thief would love to taste them, of course... Anyway, the loop took care of the rest.

Crouching, he realized he had entered a room that didn't remind him of the tavern he was looking for. He had been wandering the corridors beneath the coliseum for a while now. How do they manage to find their way around this place? Harry added inwardly while avoiding the guards' gaze. That room, however, was too quiet. He soon understood the reason for the silence.

"Boo!" Harry whispered in a lady's ear after emerging from one of his doors. "Did you miss me?"

The woman, in a reflexive attack, threw a slap with all her might aimed at the Locksmith's face. She missed him by mere centimeters.

The place looked like a storeroom; tarpaulins thrown to one side, a few candles for light but far from the pieces of wooden chests and reams of paper in the opposite corner. Stuck to one of the tables, in the dark, Brun had several papers scattered across the table, all blank.

"Locksmith!" She raised her voice, but quickly lowered it and approached the young thief with a frown. "What are you doing here!? They don't like snoops, much less one in this place."

"Ah, so I imagine you're the protector of this room... Are you that important?"

"Don't joke about this, Harry. Just say what you want and get out. I have bigger problems to deal with..."

Pragmatic as always... Harry thought.

"Do you know where the exit is? It's crazy up there..."

"No." He replied curtly.

Expecting a more...developed answer, Harry quickly realized that was all he would get. He cleared his throat and scratched his head.

"Look, I need to talk to my brother before I do what I want to do. I think—"

"What are you going to do?" Brun looked at him with fear disguised as curiosity.

"I'm going to get us off this floor. How does that sound?"

"How do you intend to do that?"

"Let me worry about that... You really don't know where he is? And how much time before the loop ends?"

"I..." The woman then sighed deeply. "Damn it, Harry! He's at the end of the fifth corridor on the right. Follow it and you'll find him. Besides, we have another half hour until the end of the loop. Enough time for the current battle to be over..."

"Thank you so much, really."

"Yeah, yeah... Now go!"

And Harry almost followed Brun's orders. What caught his attention was how scared the woman looked. He recognized the hardness in her gaze and imagined she had been through a lot to get here.

"...These papers aren't blank to you, are they?" He couldn't see the information, but because of her Emblem, he knew she could see. "Why are you exercising your Emblem so much? Didn't you see what happened to me?"

"Unlike you," she began. "I'm not a complete suicide! Besides, they need me; they would never let me get to this level..."

"They? The nobles?"

"Yes. No. I mean, I don't know what to call them... I knew things in the West were complicated, but this difference is absurd! Whoever they were in their past lives, here they're just scared little bunnies. They're looping dozens of times trying to find the exit to the next floor. The Sand Chasers, the fastest ones, are sent out there in an attempt to find the exit if it's crammed under layers and layers of sand. Luckily, I managed to convince them that I belonged here..."

"What does that mean? You could be out there, like, right now?"

"Yes... Remember I told you about how the exits are usually in the center of each floor? They think something changed that pattern for some reason, and that's why it's out there. With my Emblem, however, I can map these corridors down here. The problem is that there are many, Harry. A lot. That's why I'm telling you to leave soon; I need to finish this and get our group to the end."

"It's frightening that they're making you work so hard..." Harry said, shaking his head.

"Hey!" She slapped him across the face. "Don't talk to me like that! I may look like a lady, but I'm far from feeling old! Besides, they're not as bad as you think..."

"Believe me when I say this: they can be quite cruel." Harry frowned now.

"...They're human too, Harry." She said in a more maternal and fragile tone. "Do you know how many people each of them has lost to get here? Even for us, who have Emblems and are in a qualified group, it was difficult, now imagine them. Something has to happen, Harry, but it doesn't have to be this way."

What's going on with everyone today? Have they been bitten by the wisdom beetle? Harry thought of retorting but found himself at a loss for words again. I need to get to the end... "Alright..." He said and left the room unceremoniously.

Following the lady's orders, Harry soon found the place. Walking through the tavern, Harry looked around, worried if he had interrupted anything. Creak! The Locksmith turned like an owl on a full moon night. It was nothing... He sighed. He saw the candles on the counter and drinks on the shelves. Where is he?

"Here." As if he could hear his thoughts, Matthews emerged from the shadows and, after looking around, removed his hood, revealing a skeletal face that still rattled Harry's mind. "What are you doing downstairs? Shouldn't you be watching the other competitors?"

"...That's the first thing you ask?" Harry said, his voice tired. "Why do you care so much about this? I thought you worked for the police..."

"I do." He declared with a tone of uncertainty. "I like to think I'm applying justice to Logos Tower, however..." And then he stopped when he heard a chuckle coming from Harry. "What?"

"Nothing, it's just that..." Harry looked back and saw only darkness engulfing the corridors. "Is that your concept of justice?"

"Do you know how things were here before I determined them?" Matthews said. "Damnable. Repulsive, at the very least. I'm the one who left it like this, and be grateful for that. These... nobles do nothing but consume and jump to the next loop and repeat it all over again. They know they won't get any higher than this, and so they define this floor as their new dwellings. These games give them something to look at in a future loop. 'Look, maybe someone new will arrive.' They say, and that keeps them active."

"Is that the lie you tell yourself?" Harry confronted, taking a step forward. "Were you like this since childhood, or did the nobles make you this way?"

"You know, you've always been a fucking brat, haven't you?" Matthews said, his voice as full as a graveyard. "You thieves and ragamuffins think it's easy, don't you? We're the ones who have to bear the sins of your mistakes. It's the police who are to blame for the dozens of nobles killed every year. I'm the one who had to look mothers and fathers in the eye and explain why we couldn't catch the thief who stabbed their daughter in broad daylight."

"Oh, spare me!" he said. "You expect me to have compassion for the fucking nobles? They're the ones who ruined my life, you know? One moment I had Devon, and the next I was wanted for murder. My whole life I've been hunted, you know?"

"Oh, by the gods. You want me to feel sorry for you now!? Harry, I...!" Matthews was speechless. He simply turned and grabbed the first bottle of alcohol he found. Before Harry could answer, he put it in his mouth and downed it all. The liquid continued to flow down until it completely disappeared around what had once been his trachea.

The flickering of the candles was the only audible sound in the room, which now seemed to contain two dead people. Harry refused to continue arguing, visibly exhausted. I didn't come here for this. He closed his eyes and sighed heavily. Was this how all their interactions would go? Forever a pointless argument about the past? Brun was right: something had to happen.

"I just want to end this and go home, Matt," Harry said. "Isn't that what you want? Then you can come back and shove me into the first dirty cell you find. In the end, we all win."

"You know that's not what I want," Matthews replied as he put the bottle back on the shelf. "Why are you here in the first place? Don't tell me you were caught by the commander along with everyone else. Tell me the real reason you're here."

"...I want to save Mom." Harry said, his eyes closed. After a few seconds without hearing a response from Matthews, he opened them only to see the man clenching and clenching his fists. Is he upset...?

"I thought you'd be happy. I—"

"You think this will solve everything? It won't!" The skeleton threw the bottle to the ground, shattering it into a million pieces. "I can't believe I sacrificed myself for... for this! How selfish!"

"Selfish!?" Harry took a step forward, ignoring his instincts. "How dare you say that? She was your mother too, damn it!"

"Exactly! Have you ever thought that maybe this was our mother's fate? Have you ever thought that maybe there never even was a God Emblem in the first place? This whole Tower is a fucking maniac's experiment who wanted to play king, and look what happened. Are you seriously trying to use our mother to justify your delusions of grandeur?"

And it was at that moment that Harry realized his brother didn't understand. How could he? He'd lived under the wing of people whose biggest problem was whether to choose red or green for the carpet in the drawing room. Yet, that was the general. The Locksmith believed his brother knew him. Not the general, but Matthews. His brother.

"...Is that what you think this is about?" Harry said in a voice so tired that Matthews found himself speechless. "I thought you knew me, man."

"I..." Matthews said unpreparedly. I know. He thought, but couldn't bring himself to speak. Did he really know? The truth hurt more than the lie.

"That Meta, out there," Harry said, a non sequitur. "Why doesn't he attack the coliseum?"

What? Meta? Matthews looked confused, but soon straightened up and paused for a moment to answer his brother.

"The boat? It doesn't attack the Colosseum..." Matthews sighed. "Just follow the script, Harry. I've done too much for you already. Don't destroy yourself over an idea."

Said the skeleton with two Emblems. Harry didn't say anything, seemingly ignoring him, and fled the way he came. He crept through the shadows again and then left the place, which was almost empty of guards.

Harry climbed all the way up, vaguely remembering where the window he hung from was, and left his doors aside. Seeing that the battle of Daniel and Manes was still going on, Harry gave a slight smile. I still have time.

He returned to the ground using his passages, making sure no one had seen him, and looked at the dunes beyond the coliseum.

Something has to happen. He stepped onto the sand.
