Trevor was woken up by Clay running onto his chest. He yawned and started to stretch before becoming aware of Adabelle draped over him, still sleeping. His muscles tensed in place instead, since he had already started moving, and she was none the wiser after he relaxed. Not that she would have woken for long.

“G’morning, little buddy,” the courier greeted sleepily as he checked the window. A little bit of light was coming through, though it hadn’t reached the bed yet. “What’s up?”

Clay’s form shifted from a stoat to a replica of Croal before he pointed towards the beach.

“We have a visitor?” Trevor asked.

His familiar nodded.

“Looks like it’s time to get up then,” he said before yawning again. “Would you be willing to entertain him for a few minutes while we wake up?”

Rearing up onto their hind legs, Clay returned to their usual form and performed a salute. They jumped off of the bed, landing heavily onto the rug next to it, before heading towards the stairs. Trevor could hear every thump of their stone paws against the wooden floor as they rushed to see the dragon.

“Adabelle,” the man said gently as he turned and stroked his wife’s cheek. “Croal’s here.”

Her orange eyes fluttered open. They threatened to close as she tried to focused on him, and they did when she shifted to hide her face in his chest. “Five more minutes,” she mumbled, the words almost indecipherable since they were both slurred from sleepiness and muffled against him.

“It’s time to wake up,” he replied patiently as he retrieved a Full Night’s Rest from his inventory. “Come on, drink up.”

Adabelle groaned, but pulled away enough that she allowed Trevor to put the vial to her lips. He poured it into her mouth, and she swallowed. Energy pulsed through her like lightning the moment it hit her stomach, jolting her awake. Her eyes opened, losing any sign of her usual morning fatigue.

With a smile, she leaned up and kissed her husband. “I’m sorry, Trevor, I didn’t quite catch what you said.”

“I said Croal’s here,” he repeated.

The demonkin blinked and looked towards the window. “Already?”

“We did tell him he’d need to be early,” Trevor said, chuckling. “Do you want me to go ahead? Or wait?”

“Wait, definitely,” she answered with a playful tap against his chest. “You think you get to talk to a dragon without me? Absolutely not. I’ll be jealous.”

“Then I’ll wait for you,” he replied before kissing her forehead.

Adabelle pushed away from him, pausing only long enough to untangle her tail from his leg, and started getting ready. Since she would need to get dressed the mundane way, unlike him, he gave her the extra time.

They didn’t take long. Neither of them wanted to make the dragon wait no matter how nice he was. In fact, his polite temperament made them move a little faster.

The newlyweds emerged from their villa to see Clay standing on top of a large sandcastle in front of Croal, whose head was halfway on the beach. They were showing the dragon the newest additions to their own collection, which appeared to consist of obsidian, peridot, and a few other colorful stones that Trevor didn’t recognize.

Croal’s eyes shifted to focus on the newcomers, and a smile spread across his lips. “Good morning!” he said, his voice easily reaching across the distance. “Trevor, your familiar here is very fascinating. He’s collected some very good colors in such a short time.”

“They are excellent at finding new things, yes,” he praised as they approached. The courier placed his hand on Clay’s head, rubbing it as he looked over the new items they had found. “Yeah, this is a pretty good haul. Precious metals and diamonds in the last one, and quite a few gemstones and obsidian here. Good stuff.”

Clay preened under the attention before taking the biggest green peridot, which had been smoothed into a sphere, and offering it to Adabelle. She took the gift with a smile.

“Another present from Trevor, I assume?” she asked, amused.

The familiar nodded before showing off their paws. With a flex, claws of obsidian came out of the stone. They were polished enough that they glinted in the sunlight.

“That's very handsome. Obsidian is a good choice, Clay,” Adabelle praised, reaching out to stroke their back. The earth elemental was practically vibrating from excitement.

“It does look good on you, little buddy,” Trevor said before turning back to Croal. “How did the rest of your ceremony go?”

“Really well, thanks,” the dragon replied. “The priests do most of the work after I provide the blessing. I’m just there to help facilitate the magic.”

“That must be a fascinating process,” Adabelle remarked. “Do they use your power to bind the blessing over the city?”

“Close, but, unfortunately I’m not allowed to say for sure,” Croal answered apologetically. “Paladonan secrets, you know. I don’t think they’d like it if I told outsiders what they got up to. No offense.”

“None taken, of course,” she quickly responded as she shook her head. “It was merely academic curiosity. I’m no stranger to trade secrets myself as a summoner.”

“That sounds pretty exciting,” the dragon replied, shifting to better look at her. “Lord Mital is the only one we have here. Though, he hasn’t summoned anyone in a long time. Not sure why. I’m actually kind of curious myself now.”

Adabelle’s smile thinned at the new topic. “Perhaps ask High Priest Worth what he knows first,” she offered. “While I’m sure Lord Mital would share with you, I have a feeling that it might dredge up old memories.”

Croal tilted his head. “You think so?”

“Call it an educated guess.”

“Huh, alright. I’ll take your advice, then,” the dragon said before shifting excitedly. His long body moved behind him like a snake, though they couldn’t see much of him thanks to the water. “Anyway, are you still up for taking a message to Glyrphizith for me?”

“Yep, that’s no problem,” Trevor answered easily. “So long as your letter isn’t dragon sized, then we’re good.”

Croal shook his head, leaving a large pit in the sand that water started to fill. “No, I had one of the priests write it for me. Not Worth, because I think that would have been pretty embarrassing, but one of the others I can trust not to spread rumors. They’re nice.”

The courier smiled. “Sweet. Then all I’ll need is the letter.”

“That’s great! It’s right inside here.” Raising one of his massive hands, Croal brought it out of the water and set it down on the beach next to Adabelle. A waterproof bag had been tied to one of his claws. He kept it raised up, wiggling it back and forth just in case they hadn’t seen it.

Trevor moved to the other side of his wife and opened the bag. He had seen this kind before. Rashie had one just like it for when she went swimming. Inside was an envelope with a yellow wax seal that matched Croal’s eyes and a turquoise blue picture of him on the front.

“Hey, they even added a little drawing of you on the outside!” Trevor exclaimed in delight. “They’re a pretty good artist.”

“Yeah, they were excited to draw it on there when they heard I was sending a girl a letter,” Croal admitted sheepishly. “They teased me even though I told them that it’s not romantic. I just want to meet another dragon. I don’t think that’s too much to ask. They did take it seriously, though, so that was nice.”

“It sounds like they’re a very good friend for you, Croal,” Adabelle said as she examined the envelope. “Not many people, even acolytes, would dare to tease someone in your position. So long as they’re not abusing your good nature, I would keep them close.”

“You don’t need to tell me twice,” the dragon replied. “Worth doesn’t like it much, but he puts up with it because I like them. I think they might be my best friend? Yeah, that sounds right. I can’t give it as good as I get, but I’m working on it.”

“It’s good that you’re learning, and you definitely gotta give as good as you get,” Trevor laughed before putting an arm around Adabelle. “Having friends you can rib on is a pretty good feeling.”

“You’re right. I should get going, though,” Croal said. “Thanks for taking that to Glyrphizith for me. Oh! And feel free to convert that blessing if you want. I know the increase to your overall health isn’t as meaningful to you two as it is to normal people, but I can help in other ways.”

The courier tilted his head. “What do you—”

“That is very kind of you, Croal. Thank you,” Adabelle said with a bow. Trevor was still reading that last part, making sure he understood it right.

“You’re very welcome,” the dragon replied with a toothy grin. “Alright, I’m going to head back to my mountain now. Have a safe trip, and I hope you enjoy the rest of your honeymoon, Adabelle, Trevor, Clay. And the lady who’s taking them. Bye!”

Croal lifted his head and the water started to rush into the pit he made when the rest of his body followed. He flew over their heads, clearing them quite easily, before turning back towards his island. Trevor had thought that they would at least get wet from the water droplets, but they didn’t start falling until the dragon was well clear of them. He appreciated that little touch.

Once he had left the beach, Adabelle turned to see Lora at the door to the building she had been staying at. The woman seemed awestruck at seeing Croal so close, though she had remained a respectable distance away.

“You could have come and said hello,” the noblewoman called as she waved for the teleporter to join them.

Lora disappeared for a second before showing up in front of them. She walked to the edge of the new pit and watched Croal fly away. It seemed she was speechless for a moment before she turned around and grinned.

“Nah, I’m more of an admirer from afar when it comes to dragons. Plus, I got a pretty good look at him up close and personal when he flew over the Hello Sea, so that was great,” she replied. “Plus plus, I don’t want to intrude on your newlywed honeymoon adventures.”

“Lora, I think I speak for both of us when I say that if something really cool is happening, and it’s not something traditionally intimate, then you can join in,” Trevor said, smiling. “You’re doing a great job and you deserve a reward, too, you know?”

“Okay, maybe,” she allowed before shrugging. The Summoned didn’t seem upset at missing her chance to talk to a dragon at all, so they left it at that. “There’s a lot of cool stuff out there that you’ll be seeing, so you’re offering me plenty of chances to butt into the victory lap of your engagement.”

“I’m sure you’ll recognize when the timing is right, Lora,” Adabelle said before taking her eyes off of the retreating dragon to look at the woman. “Is it almost time to go?”

“Only if you want to,” she replied with a smile. “We can wait another hour or so, but I figured you might want to get on board the airship early so you can settle in before takeoff. You know, get acquainted with your room, figure out what music is being played when, see what the schedule is like. You’ve got four days aboard the ship, after all. Not whole days, but two full ones in the air.”

“Sounds like a trip,” Trevor said. “We’ve still got a few things in the house, but I can get most of that in a few minutes. Are you ready to go, Adabelle?”

“Absolutely,” she answered without hesitation, her gaze traveling to the blue skies above. “This will be my first airship ride. I’m looking forward to it.”
