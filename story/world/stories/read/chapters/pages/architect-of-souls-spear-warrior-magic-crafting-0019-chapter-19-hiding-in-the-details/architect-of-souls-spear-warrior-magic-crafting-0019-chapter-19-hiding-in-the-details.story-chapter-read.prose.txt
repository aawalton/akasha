After saying goodbye to Serena, Ferris and Jade sought out Hemon. He had a desk outside Elder Harper’s office and was expecting them.

“Adept Ferris, Elder Harper has instructed me to assist you with finding a suitable work assignment.”

Ferris sat down at Hemon’s desk, across from the man. He kept Jade in his lap, as the desk was covered with things the slime would probably enjoy eating.

“Now, I have a list of open positions, but I am afraid the standard entry-level options are not available due to your circumstances.” Hemon gestured to Ferris’ leg, or the lack thereof. “In my experience, you would want to quickly specialize beyond the entry-level assignments anyway.”

“Unfortunately, that only leaves one position you currently qualify for,” Hemon hesitated for a moment, and then said, “the assignment for those that have no other options: spirit gem refinement.”

Ferris was unfamiliar with the refinement of spirit gems, but from Hemon’s expression it couldn’t be good. He knew spirit gems were commonly used as currency, but their true value was in aiding cultivation.

“What is involved in spirit gem refinement?” Ferris hesitantly asked.

“Spirit gems, when found in the rifts, can have various imperfections,” Hemon explained, “refinement involves removing the imperfections, although the process has been said to be tedious.”

“You mentioned there are more specialized assignments available?” Ferris asked. If refinement was tedious enough that Hemon had heard about it, then Ferris would likely not want to do it for long.

Hemon pulled out a folder and started flipping through various pages.

“These are the current openings,” Hemon started to shuffle the pages around, spreading them out over his desk, “which we can cross reference with the job summaries over here.”

Ferris found himself once again surprised by the amount of paper involved. It wasn’t as shocking as before, but it still felt scandalous to use so much. What did they do when a position was added, write out a new list?

“Standard rate of pay is one merit point per shift worked,” Hemon said, oblivious to Ferris’ feelings, “artisan jobs typically pay per item produced instead, and some specifically noted positions may pay more. Spirit gem refinement is paid based on a quota set by your manager.”

Ferris began looking through the lists. Each job had a set of requirements, such as a guard position listing ‘tier one detection ability,’ or a ‘heat tolerance ability’ for a position at a forge. There were several crafting jobs, listing requirements like ‘basic alchemy training, potion making ability,’ ‘basic enchanting training, enchanting ability,’ or ‘metal forming ability.’ Some required specific knowledge and an ability, while others only needed the latter. This probably meant the ability was rare or valuable enough to provide on the job training, or simple enough not to need it. There were also jobs that supported the clan’s farming, construction, and other fields, requiring things like ‘plant growth ability’ or ‘material reinforcement ability.’ Some appeared to be support for processing materials brought out of rifts, like one that needed a ‘corpse dismemberment ability.’

Hemon explained that each position would have an evaluation interview, administered by the department that posted the listing. The summary requirements were intended to give a general idea, but cultivation was variable enough that an interview determined if a candidate could do the work. They would usually also accept cultivators with non-standard abilities that accomplished the same goal.

After reading through the options, Ferris turned back to Hemon and asked, “I don’t see any delving positions, are they listed somewhere else?”

“No,” he shook his head, “I am afraid that clan delving positions are only available starting at the second tier, and to be frank: they are never advertised, but recruited for. Delving on personal time is encouraged, but it has been shown to be most beneficial to properly develop in the first tier before focusing on it full time.”

Ferris was surprised; that was much different than back home. Was it better for the clan this way, but not the individual? Or maybe vice versa… people did die delving.

Bluereef didn’t have many different focuses. All new cultivators delved. The clan relied on trading for support and crafted items, beyond specific critical things.

Reading through the list again, Ferris was a bit lost. After losing his leg, he had focused on finding a way to regrow it. Now he had a plan in progress, but even afterwards Clan Hollowfield expected him to spend most of his time on one of these jobs… for the next decade.

The summaries described the job requirements, but they lacked any details on the professions themselves. For example, several entries required alchemy training, but there was nothing explaining what minimum standards would be accepted.

Ferris was out of his depths, but at least he knew enough to recognize it. He shifted a bit, glancing down at Jade sitting contentedly in his arms before looking back up.

“Hemon, my clan’s education focused on our martial tradition and delving,” he began, watching the man’s face. “As an Elder’s assistant, you must be well informed. Are there any mistakes you can help me avoid?” Ferris decided to ask about failures the man had seen, rather than successes. He expected he would face the same problems others had, but his path to success would likely be more unique. Hemon may be mundane without direct experience, but the man likely knew more than him about being an Adept… at least when related to Clan Hollowfield. Leading with a bit of honest flattery couldn’t hurt.

The man sat a bit straighter, although his eyes got a shrewd glint to them.

“Yes,” Hemon replied evenly, “I would be happy to help.” The man looked back at the listings for a moment, before elaborating. “The jobs that specify ‘basic training’ are highly sought after, yet many find even ‘basic’ requirements to be beyond them. It is not a level of competency you can achieve by reading a few books in the library, although that can be a good starting point. I typically see candidates with less than a year of experience in a craft fail the interview.”

Hemon looked him directly in the eyes. “I would, however, recommend finding a true profession that appeals to you, begin studying, and then look for chances to assist various research projects. It won’t help you gain a job in the short term, but this is an excellent place to learn. Outside of the clan, you may be able to purchase similar knowledge to what the library provides, but the people you meet in our fourth branch can provide unique opportunities.”

Ferris considered his words. As a retainer with a limited contract, he knew he would never be granted access to the true secrets in the library. On the other hand, a conversation with Foreman Carter at the Slime Ranch had already taught him secrets the clan considered worth restricting. What more could he learn by working with others like Elder Harper?

Of course, Hemon could simply be attempting to acquire a willing minion for the clan. Ferris remembered Cyrus had been given a contract based on his capability to assist a researcher. If Hemon could gain more assistants for the fourth branch for free, he probably would.

As if he hadn’t sent Ferris’ thoughts through a loop, Hemon continued. “For the jobs that don’t require knowledge… I would, personally, classify them into two categories: ones that help you advance, and ones that don’t.” The man shrugged. “Frankly, they will likely all be boring or repetitive, as they don’t require anything other than access to an ability. The key is to find one that supports your growth. Initially, some may prefer the ease of working as a guard over hard labor. Yet a guard sitting around all day has not grown, but a laborer hauling fuel in a forge may have learned from watching the blacksmiths.”

Ferris nodded easily at this advice, as it was along the lines he had started thinking when presented with the list. Likely every job would provide a combination of opportunity and waste, but he could seek choices that tipped the balance in his favor.

“Thank you, Hemon, I will take this advice to heart. I will begin with the spirit gem refinement position and seek to become qualified for a better role.”

Hemon handed him the details for his initial job, after which Ferris and Jade left. He would be required to show up for three shifts every week at a building in the third branch’s district, which focused on enchantment. Luckily, they did not care which three days he worked, and he was free to change his schedule at will.

Free from his obligations for the day, Ferris decided to take a walk. Jade was happy with anything, especially when he promised to look for a snack along the way. Worryingly, the slime had forgotten the word ‘snack’ from last night, but they rippled excitedly when he explained. They did still remember the next name from that morning.

Wandering a bit aimlessly, Ferris decided to return to the sixth district. Yesterday, he had been intent on getting to the slime ranch. He hadn’t taken any time to appreciate the flowers.

The fifth district smelled somehow worse than the day before. Ferris would prefer to avoid job assignments for alchemists, if this was what they smelled like—not that he was able to be picky. He quickly passed through.

Once he reached the district boundary, Ferris slowed down and took some time to look around. The sixth district was wonderfully overgrown with trees, vines, bushes, and all other kinds of plants. Everywhere were bright flowers, fully in bloom. The acrid stink of the fifth district was replaced with a sweet perfume.

He wasn’t the only person enjoying the afternoon, as many people lingered around small parks or shops. Ferris was able to find a small café with outdoor seating and ordered for Jade and himself. Mother Lily would have happily provided the food for free, but there was something special about sitting at the table, watching the people go by, and enjoying the weather.

He could’ve probably gone back to the library for more research or returned to his room to cultivate… but he was mentally exhausted. He wanted some time to process everything. The day had been exciting, but each new thing added pressure. He felt overwhelmed.

The order, a large platter of various finger foods for Jade plus a sweet drink for himself, arrived. He fed the slime, who was casually sitting in his lap, and sipped the drink.

“Cucumber sandwich, five out of ten!” Jade sent happily, rippling as the food dissolved. The slime had continued the game without fail, unprompted. If Ferris forgot to send the name of a piece of food over the bond, Jade would ask him for it.

Looking down at the slime, Ferris considered their behavior. Jade had always expressed happiness for food; understandable, as all slimes were unending voracious eaters. Jade had also been happy to follow instructions, although Ferris believed the bond was responsible for that. Which means it is making me equally compliant, he thought with a shudder.

Jade was now also genuinely enjoying the food rating game. Ferris could feel over the bond that the slime liked giving ratings, beyond the pleasure of eating.

“What do you want us to do?” Ferris asked. He didn’t think the slime would have any specific plans, but Jade was growing smarter. Someday he would have to come to terms with his familiar, once the slime was intelligent enough. That was the price of the familiar bond. Ferris knew it going in.

“Want next food!”

“Not what you want right now, but long term,” Ferris again had to slowly form images to convey meaning where words failed.

Jade immediately responded with a series of images and emotions. First, he saw the slime pits, tainted with negative feelings of confinement. Then appeared images of Lily House, the slimespace, Mother Lily, the library, this café, and others. Each had a feeling of freshness to it. If he had to put Jade’s feelings to words, he would say it was ‘freedom to explore.’

Jade’s bombardment of desire rolled over Ferris, who was a bit stunned. All the images were mental pictures he had originally sent Jade over the bond, but now they returned to him with a new layer of emotion.

Strangely, the experience cheered Ferris up. Jade may not know his problems, but the slime’s desires resonated with Ferris. His missing leg, and each roadblock along the way, felt like chains holding him back. Just like the chain that he leashed his familiar with.

Jade’s wish for freedom spoke to the part of Ferris that was feeling adrift, trapped, and confined by circumstance. The slime’s oddly relatable emotions made Ferris feel better.

As he sat there, experiencing the mental images and associated feelings, Ferris felt… connected in a way he had been missing since leaving home. It was nice, even when the image had negative emotions like the slime pits.

Wait…

Jade remembered the slime pits. Very clearly.

That happened over a day ago. The slime had forgotten things since then, like the word ‘snack’ from last night.

There was definitely something missing in his understanding of the memory problem. Ferris asked several more questions about the day before, but he couldn’t find any pattern. Almost every question was met with confusion, although the memories Jade had just shared were still vivid in their mind.

Despite the frustration of another mystery, he’d had a good time. All too quickly the platter of food was gone, and Ferris carried Jade back to Lily House. Even the fifth district couldn’t take the pep out of his step.

Back at Lily House, Ferris and Jade settled into the common room, near the clock.

The couch was comfortable, with soft pillows and a large fur draped over it. Looking around, the room was dominated by a monster head mounted on the wall, but there were other interesting decorations. A couple of weapons ranging from old broken swords to chipped axes hung up. It appeared to be a requirement to have some sort of damage. A large map of what looked like an extensive cave system was framed on the wall, full of handwritten notes marking interesting locations.

Mother Lily found him staring at the map, trying to decipher the location it showed. In general, you would enter a slightly different place each time you entered a rift, but some special rifts would be static. That usually meant treasure, and multiple groups would fight over it. The Wild was also an option, although a map of the Wild would probably be too valuable to display casually.

“Ah, Ferris, welcome home dear,” Mother Lily said, carrying a load of food towards the kitchen. After dropping the bags off, she walked over to join him.

“My husband spent three years making this map,” she walked up to it, running a hand along the frame. “It was a stable rift that his group found by accident. In the end, someone else found the cornerstone and the rift became inaccessible, but he was close.”

“He’s a delver?” Ferris asked, a little excited.

“Yes, he was a delver, dear,” Mother Lily patted Ferris on shoulder, “he died going on nine years ago now.”

Ferris lost his excitement. A moment of silence hung in the air as Mother Lily looked at the map.

“The rifts are dangerous,” she said, sitting down on the couch next to him. “I have this talk with all of the children that come through my house, so listen close.” She looked around the room, eyes landing on the monstrous head. “Most deaths in rifts are due to negligence. Someone takes a risk they were told not to, or they ignore the rules out of laziness or greed. They get away with it once or twice, and then decide the rules don’t matter… until their luck runs out. Delving can be fairly safe if you and your comrades are diligent.”

The woman sighed.

“But never forget,” she looked directly at him, “the rules can change in an instant. You can encounter things that trap you, preventing any lifesaving treasures from working. Rifts with known difficulty can abhorrently change, giving challenges far above you. You followed all the rules, but everything suddenly and violently goes wrong.”

Ferris had heard some of this before, but only in stories to heighten the tension. Actual delvers back home hadn’t talked about personal experiences like that, although maybe they just didn’t tell them to children.

“In my husband’s case, his group was assisting an Elder investigating the nature of a particular monster in a rift. Neither the rift nor the monster was anything special, just another data point for the Elder’s research.”

Mother Lily closed her eyes. She said she had this talk with many people, but it still looked hard for her.

“They encountered a devil. My husband died before he truly knew what had happened, collateral damage in the fight between the Elder and the devil. If his group had been alone, they would all have just disappeared. As it was, a few survived to bring word back.”

Ferris’ eyes widened slightly. Devils were the nightmarish, evil villains of stories. Not to be confused with heart demons in the soul, devils were real creatures that would attack delvers in the rifts and the Wild. Unlike rift monsters, devils were sentient beings that could also travel the Wild. They could even open their own portals to travel the rifts, running into delvers… like what apparently happened to Mother Lily’s husband.

In the stories, devils came from somewhere beyond the Wild. What that place was, no one appeared to know, but it was suicide to travel through the Wild at anything less than third tier. Accordingly, the devils were all strong enough to fight at that level. Despite their horrific strength, apparently devils didn’t have a core. Besides humans, Ferris had never heard of any other non-mundane creature in the rifts or Wild without one.

He looked around the room. “I’m sorry, are all of these…”

“Mementos of him,” Mother Lily smiled, “they keep him close to me. Plus, most of the tenants the clan gives me want to be delvers, so they appreciate them.”

She picked Jade up from Ferris’ lap. Today the slime let her, rather than sliding out of her hands. Ferris sent a couple reminders not to eat her, but other than that did not intervene.

Jade rippled in pleasure as Mother Lily produced a small apple from one of her pockets and fed them.

“My husband followed one of the clan’s martial traditions, but an old friend from his group has a familiar bond,” she said, patting Jade like a pet. “If you would like, I will see if she could stop by and give you some advice.”

Ferris sat up straight. “That would be amazing. Thank you, Mother Lily.” He was excited, although the serious mood from earlier held him back from showing it. He hadn’t yet found anyone with firsthand experience of a familiar bond.

She set Jade back down in his lap, somehow slipping the slime a second small apple at the same time, and then pulled him into one of her hugs. “Of course, dear. The children of Lily House are my family, after all.”

Ferris tried not to bristle at the word ‘children,’ and accepted the hug.

“Now, dinner will be ready in a couple hours, but let me know if you need anything.” Mother Lily stood and returned to the kitchen.

Ferris turned back to Jade, and decided it was time to more regularly test the slime’s memory. It had only been nine hours since he gave Jade the names, and he knew the slime’s memory lasted for at least twelve hours. This came from recalling Mother Lily in the morning, having met her the previous night for dinner. It was a little early, but he wanted to start testing every hour.

Unfortunately, the slime had no memory of the names. Jade did recall the one he had checked for after meeting Elder Harper, but none of the others.

He was worried what that could mean. Was Jade losing memory faster than before? Was it related to how the slime remembered the slime ranch long past anything else? Did different memories last longer?

Would Jade thinking about a memory reset the clock on how long it took to forget? That felt likely, as the slime remembered the last name he had checked, but then why not the one before it?

Try as he might, he wasn’t able to figure out what was going on. In the end he just resolved to do the test again. Maybe repetition would show him something new. Next time he would start testing more regularly earlier than nine hours.

Returning to his room, he had some extra time alone before dinner. He had expected to spend the afternoon watching the clock, but now he could get some more cultivation in.

Jade’s mental body would need until at least tomorrow to reform, but Ferris could still work in his soulspace. He secured Jade’s chain to the dresser and laid down to cultivate, well out of reach of the slime. At least he didn’t need to be on the floor this time.

The day had given Ferris a lot to unpack, but he had already started to work through his feelings on his walk. There was a lot to do, a lot to decide, and a lot to be wary of. The book of acid abilities dangled the power to delve, just out of reach, while the job assignment consigned him to a different role. The entire possibility of a peaceful profession for a cultivator felt like a new concept.

I mean, he knew some didn’t focus entirely on delving. But everyone back home had.

Thoughts came and went like usual, but the most persistent were of self-doubt. Doubt in his knowledge, as everyone in Hollowfield seemed to know more than him. Doubt in his ability, as he only qualified for the worst job assignment. Most of all, doubt in his own feelings. Were they really his, or were they influenced by his familiar bond and his oath contract?

Despite the turmoil, Ferris continued to practice his meditative breathing and let the thoughts go. Eventually, he opened his eyes to his soulspace, but it was not as he expected.

The oath statue was missing.
