A transit chime sounded at the entrance to the Infinite Archive from the direction of Closed collection.Bart looked up and saw the Curator step into the foyer.He wore the same immaculate grey suit, colourless enough to make the room around him seem brighter by comparison. The damaged silver Keeper’s key hung from its chain at his collar.

In Bart’s experience, the Curator appearing in person rarely preceded anything pleasant.

Bart stood immediately.

“What happened?”

The Curator stopped.

“Nothing as of yet”

Bart still waited for a but to follow...

The Curator continued to look at him

“Nothing happened?” Bart asked finally running out of patience

“That is generally considered desirable.”

Gregor, halfway through sorting a trolley of returns, visibly relaxed.

“I like this visit already.”

The Curator did not acknowledge him.

Instead, he reached inside his coat and produced a black folder.

“Now that the Infinite Archive has resumed recognised institutional operation, and my office has undergone its overdue legitimacy review, a restoration reconciliation is required.”

Bart looked at the folder.

“That sounds ominous.”

“It is administrative prerogative.”

“That has occasionally been worse.”

The Curator placed the folder on the counter.

A blue System panel appeared above it.

EXTERNAL CLOSURE CUSTODY

RESTORATION RECONCILIATION REQUIRED

OFFICE STATUS ATTESTATION: DUE

KEEPER ACKNOWLEDGEMENT: REQUIRED

Bart blinked and sighed more paperwork to occupy him, well at least he had something to do

The Curator took two steps towards Bart’s side of the desk when a voice rang from the side.

“Name or registered designation?” Nell  asked.

The Curator stopped and turned his head towards her.

“External Closure Consultant.”

Nell wrote it down.

“Purpose of visit?”

“I just told you.”

“You told me who you are.”

The Curator looked slowly towards Bart.

Bart deliberately picked up a pencil and examined its point while trying not to smile.

A small pause stretched across the desk. The Curator was accustomed to sealed records, formal authority and considerably more deference than this.

“…Restoration custody reconciliation.”

Nell checked a box.

“Expected duration?”

“I don't know.”

“I'll put in one hour.”

She reached beneath the desk, pulled out a paper credential and fed it through a stamping device.

CLACK.

Nell handed it across.

VISITOR - EXTERNAL PROFESSIONAL

The Curator stared at it.

Gregor leaned sideways to read.

“They made you a visitor.”

“I can see that.”

“Do you have to wear it?”

“Yes, he isn't a patron” Nell said.

The Curator looked at her while Nell stared back.

Finally , he pinned the credential precisely to his lapel, just below the damaged Keeper key.

Mabel, positioned near admissions, glanced at the credential and then at the Curator.

“Visitor registered,” the Sphinx said.

The Curator's expression however did not change Though Bart suspected this was costing him a lot internally to maintain his composure.

He moved to Bart’s station and opened the black folder.Bart braced himself for lists of sealed holdings, emergency prohibitions or declarations that some ancient conceptual structure that had become dangerously unstable.

Instead, the first page was headed:

EXTERNAL CUSTODY STATUS

CUSTODIAL AUTHORITY: ACTIVE

AUTHORITY TOKEN: VALID / DAMAGED

CLOSURE CONTROLS: MAINTAINED

CURRENT CUSTODY STATUS: ATTESTED

OUTSTANDING REVIEW ITEMS: SEE RESTRICTED SCHEDULE

CONTINUING CLOSURE REVIEW: REQUIRED

KEEPER ACKNOWLEDGEMENT: PENDING

Bart read it once and another time just to make sure,

“That's it?”

“What  else were you expecting?”

Gregor raised a hand.

“Something about the end of existence?”

The Curator looked at him with a slight disdain.

“That would be a remarkably poor thing to include in routine correspondence.”

“Fair.”

Bart turned to the second page.

Most of it was equally non world shattering.

PRIMARY INSTITUTIONAL CONTACT: REQUIRED

ROUTINE CORRESPONDENCE ROUTE: REQUIRED

Bart looked at the Curator.

“Primary contact?”

“The Keeper.”

Nell looked over from her ledger.

“For routine administrative correspondence?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

The Curator turned towards her.

Nell continued writing.

“The head of institution is not the default contact for routine external correspondence.”

Bart very carefully decided not to interfere.

“Then who is?” the Curator asked.

Nell finally looked  and gave him a nice smile.

“Assistant Librarian Mercer.”

The Curator then looked at Bart.

Bart nodded. “That does seem reasonable.”

The Curator's expression suggested that Bart had betrayed something fundamental.

Nell filled in the field instead.

PRIMARY INSTITUTIONAL CONTACT: NELL MERCER

“Preferred correspondence route?”

“Closed.”

Nell stopped.

“That is not a route.”

“It is how I conduct correspondence.”

“It is a status.”

The Curator stared at her.

Nell turned the form around.

REGISTERED TRANSIT

INSTITUTIONAL COURIER

ARCHIVE LIAISON NETWORK

OTHER - SPECIFY

After several seconds, the Curator selected OTHER.

Nell handed him a pencil.

He wrote:

SEALED DIRECT DELIVERY

Nell examined it.

"At least acceptable.”

Gregor made a suspicious choking noise behind the returns trolley.Bart pulled the form closer to apply his Keeper's seal.

Previously Bart would have used this opportunity to ask about Keeper #4,091.Instead, he looked at the custody form, then at the six pending retrievals on the service queue.

He stamped the acknowledgement.

The Curator watched him hesitate.

“You have some questions.”

“Yes but its not the time”

“You normally  just blurt them out.”

Bart signed beneath the seal.

“I'm working right now.”

A small silence followed.

The Curator studied him for a moment, then closed the first section of the folder offering no comment.

While Bart entered the reconciliation into the active administrative register, an elderly patron in a heavy tweed coat approached the desk.

He stopped directly in front of the Curator.

“Excuse me. Do you work here?”

“No.” replied the Curator

“Do you know where returns go?”

The Curator looked towards the clearly marked return trolley approximately 10 feet to his left.

“Apparently to the left.”

“Thank you very much young man and if you will be so kind”

The patron pressed a brightly illustrated copy of Introduction to Subterranean Botany into the Curator’s hands and walked away.

The Curator stood perfectly still, holding the book against his chest.

Gregor clamped a hand over his mouth while his shoulders began to shake.

“Why am I holding this?” the Curator asked the room at large.

“Returns are to the left,” Nell instructed in a normal voice.

The Curator walked over to the return trolley and placed the volume on the top shelf with precision.

Later, while Nell verified the last filing references, the Curator stepped away from Patron Services and stood at the edge of the central reading room.

The room was not quiet. Gregor pushed a loaded trolley between the tables. A scholar near Cross Reference whispered a question to another researcher. Someone turned a page too aggressively. A chair scraped across the stone floor.From farther down the hall came the faint sound of Mabel informing someone that three separate academic titles still constituted one patron.

Albert arrived beside the Curator carrying a polishing cloth.The Curator watched the room for a moment.

“It is louder than I remember.”

Albert adjusted a reading lamp on an empty table.

“Yes it does seem like it doesn’t it”

Another chair scraped.

“Better, though,” the Curator observed.

Albert looked across the occupied tables.

“Yes.”

The Curator looked at him.

“You remained.”

Albert polished a small mark from a lamp base.

“Someone had to take care of what was left  and I had nowhere else to go”

The Curator was silent for a long moment then his gaze shifted back towards the Patron Services desk and returned to the counter.

Bart had just finished processing the final reconciliation entry when the main slate chimed.

EXTERNAL CLOSURE OFFICE

RESTORATION RECONCILIATION: COMPLETE

CUSTODIAL RELATIONSHIP: CURRENT

NEXT REVIEW: SUBJECT TO ACTIVE INSTITUTIONAL POLICY

REPORTING CHANNEL: RESTORED

The Curator stared at the final line.

“Reporting channel?”

Bart checked the administrative interface.

“Apparently your office can communicate normally with us again.”

“I could already communicate with you.”

“You sent the black letters.”

“They were adequate.”

Nell looked over from her ledger.

“We have standard correspondence forms now.”

Gregor leaned over the desk, grinning.

“You're going to have forms.”

Before leaving, the Curator looked around the entrance hall one final time.

“You have made it more inconvenient.”

“The Archive?”

“My job I meant, there was considerably less administration when there was no institution left to report to.”

“Sorry.”

The Curator looked at him.

“No, you're not actually though”

The Curator gave the smallest possible nod and turned towards the exit.

“Your visitor credential,please ” Nell called out.

He stopped and looked down,

VISITOR - EXTERNAL PROFESSIONAL

The Curator unpinned it and returned to the counter.

He placed the credential precisely in front of Nell.

“Thank you,” she said.

“Is that all?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

He turned and walked towards the exit towards the way of the closed collection a moment later, the transit closed behind him.

Gregor stared at the empty space after the Curator had left,

“I think Nell scares him.” he murmured slightly out loud

Nell however did not look up from her ledger.

“Next.”

Another waiting patron stepped forward.
