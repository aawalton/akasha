Of course Cryos’s family is in Fairwood. That’s where I met him, afterall. That’s where he’d been training with his master. I wonder if they know Cyros’s motivation for joining the Blackcloaks? I’d have to assume so—it wouldn’t be very hard for someone with their resources to figure out.

The trip will take about a week and place us nearly on the other side of the continent. We’ll be teleported across most of that distance; some cities in Dunmora are fitted with public transportation spell circles called telepads. I guess they’re more common on the other continent, Valenia, but Dunmora is a lot more fractured, and warring kingdoms tend to see the pads as a potential threat and destroy them at every given opportunity.

The longest part of the trip is getting to a city with a telepad that will take us close to Fairwood, and for the first leg of that we lease some stardrakes—giant ridable lizards. I know we only just got back from our last trip, but it’s kind of fun to be on the road again. I like traveling; a brilliant array of stars overhead, cool air blowing through my hair. It feels freeing in a way that the crowded cities don’t. Maybe I’d be better off traveling the world than settling in one city or another.

Once I’m stronger. Once I’ve used the Blackcloaks to grow and get my revenge.

I wonder how far away that day might be? I let out a wistful sigh.

“Getting tired?” Cyros asks me. He’s riding along my left side, with Álvaro to his left after that. “We could take a break if you want.”

The bouncing of the lizard’s gate is starting to make me sore, but I’ve dealt with worse. “No. Just thinking.”

Cyros has enough tact not to ask me what I’m thinking about. Álvaro, however, has no such inhibitions.

“Spill!” he demands with a mischievous grin. “It’s not like we’ve got anything else to do.”

Yeah, no. We might be friends now, but that doesn’t mean I’m ready to pour my heart out.

“Just thinking about the remnant,” I say, lifting my wrist. It’s a more interesting topic of conversation, anyway. “Wondering if I should try to talk to it. I could try feeding it a bit of a mana potion to wake it up.”

By now, Cyros has also been filled in on how the remnant helped me in the lindwyrm fight.

“Maybe once we set up camp for the night,” he suggests. “And at a reasonable distance away from the stardrakes. And us.”

Yeah, that’s probably a good idea. Even so, I run my thumb over the marble, feeling for the hairline crack. I focus on the remnant, and a few seconds later I’m rewarded with the impression of something stirring.

The remnant’s attention lethargically turns toward me, like a resting beast expectantly cracking one eye open. It doesn’t do anything else. It just waits.

“Are you tired?” I think, trying to understand what I’m sensing.

And to my surprise, I feel a slight impression of affirmation.

“You can understand me?” I think. I grin, unable to help myself.

“Uh oh,” Álvaro says. “That’s a menacing smile.”

“She does that a lot,” Cyros replies.

I ignore the boys; monsters are way more interesting, anyway.

The remnant doesn’t really reply to my last question. It just continues to regard me—faintly suspicious, faintly bored, but mostly too drowsy to bother with a response.

“Would some mana help with that?” I think. “Is that why you’re so tired?”

The remnant perks up. Well that answers that question.

I don’t have very much mana to offer it, but I give what I can, pressing my magic into the refiner. The remnant heartily snaps it up, and its presence in my mind becomes a little more concrete and in-focus. I go ahead and pour out the rest of my meager supply of mana—I have a potion I can use to recover it if I need, after all. The remnant eats this up too, steadily growing more awake and alert.

I chuckle. “Mana is your caffeine, huh?”

“Is it, uh, capable of understanding us?” Cyros wonders.

“Oh yeah,” Álvaro says. “Nye talks to their shield all the time. I don’t think they understand language, though. They’re more like mind-readers, as I understand it.”

Cyros doesn’t sound impressed. “Mind magic is dangerous.”

Álvaro laughs. “Tell me about it!”

The remnant nudges me for more mana, but I’m all out. “Sorry,” I tell it. “That’s all I’ve got for now.”

The remnant’s friendliness sours, and its mind sinks back down into the refiner, pouting. I snort at the reaction.

“It’s kind of cute,” I say aloud. “Think I should name it?”

Álvaro shrugs. “Sure. Why not?”

“Just be careful about it,” Cyros says. “Names can have power where magic is concerned. They can be a self fulfilling prophecy.”

Álvaro hums thoughtfully. “Interesting. Sort of fits what I saw of the other remnants. The shield was stubborn and proud, believing it was indomitable. The sword was bloodthirsty and passionate. Though I wonder if it was the objects they were sealed in that affected their personalities, rather than the names they were given?”

“Could be both,” Cyros suggests. “Regardless, just make sure to give it some thought.”

“What do you guys think about Reaper?” I say.

Cyros winces. “I just said to give it some thought!”

“Hey, if it’s a self fulfilling prophecy, then maybe you can turn it into what you need,” Álvaro suggests.

Cyros turns to him with a wince. “Don’t give her any ideas. You’re playing with fire.”

“You mean like God Killer or something?” I tease. Cyros looks horrified. Álvaro laughs, at least.

Cyros is probably right, though. I feel like if I try to give it some name that’s too on-the-nose, it might backfire like some kind of genie wish. I should pick something more flexible—open to interpretation.

I think back on how it behaved when it fought the lindwyrm. It took on the quality of my mana and used it to infect the beast—and then it curled up and went right back to sleep.

For some reason, that puts the image of a sleeping dragon in my head, sprawled out across its hoard of gold. And the way it reacts to magic like a snake reacting to sunlight—only for it to settle down when the magic dries up—does feel like some sort of coiled, scaly beast.

“Hey Álvaro,” I say. “Remember that symbol from Earth that was always used around medicine? Like, a rod and a snake, or something.”

“Oh yeah,” he says. “I remember those.”

“What was that symbol called?”

He pauses. “I have absolutely no idea.”

Dang. There goes that idea.

“Interesting,” Cyros muses. “I think I see where you’re going with this. Maybe something related to potion-making? Or alchemy? That would have a wide range of applications.”

Alchemy’s not a bad idea. I mentally go through the list of potions, spells, and even tools I’ve used over the past few months.

“How about Calx,” I suggest.

“What’s that mean?” Álvaro asks.

Cyros frowns in thought. “A powdery substance that results from heating mana-infused gold. It’s used for a variety of applications in potionmaking and alchemy, typically to stabilize the effects of the desired spell. Yeah, that could work.”

I mentally nudge the remnant, and it responds with tired irritation.

“Can I name you Calx?” I ask it. “Is that okay?”

The remnant doesn’t particularly seem to care. It doesn’t entirely understand the concept of names, for that matter.

“I’m Sal,” I tell it. “That’s my name.”

The remnant acknowledges this, then settles back to sleep.

Well, I didn’t exactly get a “Yes” on the name suggestion. But I didn’t get a “No,” either. Based on its apparent indifference, I’m going to go with “Yes.”

“Calx,” I think. Then I jump as Echo abruptly responds.

[Remnant designation accepted,] she says.

I guess that makes it official. I smile, removing my finger from the marble to finally let Calx drift back off without bothering it anymore.

As much as I hate to admit it, I’m starting to understand Lisari’s excitement about taking me on as a champion; while I have no idea what this creature will evolve into, I can’t wait to find out.

When we finally make it to Fairwood, we come from the opposite direction as the Starlight Inn, which is just fine by me. It would take hours to get there by foot, anyway.

Instead, Cyros takes us to a house built into the hollowed trunk of one of the city’s massive trees. It’s near the center of town, halfway up the trunk; more houses are stacked one on top of the other, all the way up and down the tree. It’s a bit cramped—far from Talia’s estate full of suspended rooms like fruits on a branch.

Talia. I quickly shove that thought to the back of my head.

Cyros knocks on the tree’s circular door, then swings it open. “Mapa? You awake?”

“Cyros?” a quiet voice calls from inside. “Is that you?”

“It’s me,” he confirms, stepping inside. “I’ve brought some friends too, if that’s alright.”

“Friends?” The voice lilts upward. “Yes. Of course! Come in, come in.”

Álvaro and I awkwardly duck inside as well.

The house is just as cramped as it appeared from the outside. Every inch of every wall has been consumed by something practical: shelves, or a hanging bed, or an arcana stove squeezed between a cabinet and a washing basin. On the bed is a dryad, their wood-like skin far more craggly than Cyros’s. Their hair is full of leaves and flower buds, but the stems are withered, and the leaves are turning yellow. Cyros has developed some yellow and orange leaves in his hair as well, but he told me they just change with the season. I have a feeling that’s not the case with his parent. They look at us with green eyes and a tired smile.

“This is my mapa,” Cyros tells us. “Mapa, these are my friends, Sal and Álvaro. They wanted to accompany me on my trip home.”

“Good,” his mapa says, grinning up at us. “You need more friends. Too young to be working so much. Make the most of life while you’re still in your Spring, I say.”

Cyros gestures us toward some seats that fold down out of the wall.

“Nera is out?” he asks, taking a seat as well.

His mapa hasn’t tried to get up since we arrived. In fact, they haven’t really moved at all. I Check them.

[Teltora: Level 38 dryad. Status: Paralyzed.]

Oh.

“Yes, she left a while ago to get some food from the market,” Teltora replies to Cyros. “Should be back before long.”

Cyros nods mechanically, fidgeting with his fingers.

A notification pops up in my Interface. It’s the Contact menu—something Shirasil unlocked for me, but I’ve never had a reason to use. Tipping my head, I access it, and something like a chat window pops into my vision.

[Álvaro: man is this awkward or what?]

I have to actively force myself to remain still to keep from turning to him with a horrified look. Focusing on the chat window instead, the words I think appear on the screen.

[Sal: dude, shut up]

His reply comes only a second later.

[Álvaro: hey, i’m staying perfectly quiet and respectful! i could be whispering, you know. you’re welcome]

My mouth twitches, but I don’t dignify his message with a response.

“I have something for you,” Cyros says, finally looking up at his mapa. “A new treatment.”

Instantly, Teltora’s face falls. “Cyros, we’ve been over this. There’s no healing magic that can help at this stage. Is that why you came back to visit?”

“No conventional healing magic,” Cyros says. “But I found an adderstone.”

Teltora’s eyebrows raise. “How in the world were you able to afford that?”

“It’s not important,” Cyros says. “But—”

“Did you steal it?” Teltora asks sharply.

Cyros shakes his head. “No, of course not.”

“Then how did you afford it?” they demand. “Don’t lie to me, now.”

“He earned it,” I say, jumping in. “Well, we helped, too.”

“It was sort of a group effort,” Álvaro adds.

Cyros gives us a grateful look. “It was a reward for a job. I couldn’t have done it alone.”

Teltora lets out a heavy sigh. “You shouldn’t have pulled others into this. You need to let this go.”

Cyros swallows hard, staring down at his hands. “I can’t. Not yet.”

Silences stretches.

[Álvaro: see? super awkward]

I shoot him a glare.

[Sal: and super not the time for jokes. read the room, man]

[Álvaro: i’m not joking! what are we supposed to do, just sit here?]

Honestly, good question. It is getting pretty uncomfortable. And I hate how much this room is starting to remind me of a hospital.

I stand up. “Anyone hungry? I’m going to make something.”

Álvaro snorts. “Not even going to ask?”

[Álvaro: whos being rude now]

[Sal: hey i’ve got a role requirement I need to fullfill]

[Álvaro: oh. sorry]

Not technically true, as I already fulfilled the daily requirement when I woke up this morning and made some breakfast, but I don’t feel bad about lying if it shuts him up. And whenever I go too long without prepping something, I sort of start to get paranoid I might have forgotten. Better safe than sorry.

Teltora lights up at my suggestion. “That would be wonderful! Please help yourself to anything in the pantry, though there’s not much to work with. Cyros, go help her, won’t you? Show her where we keep the spices.”

Cyros hesitates, like he wants to say something, but eventually sighs and gets up to help. “Sure.”

I’d been planning to grab some food from my Apothecary, though on second thought, there’s not much room in here to remove it from my Inventory. Working with whatever we have in here sounds like it could be fun, anyway.

Cyros opens some compartments in the wall—hollowed out spaces of the tree trunk—and starts showing me what I’ve got to work with. A handful of dried herbs and spices. Some fresh mushrooms and vegetables. A couple jugs of liquid, from honey to vinegar. Not exactly my usual ingredients, but it presents an interesting challenge. Should be fun.

“Cyros, get the fireplace going,” I tell him as I begin to dice some veggies. It has a spell circle rather than a natural flame—probably wise when your house is a tree. “Then put a pot of water on.”

Álvaro laughs as Cyros heads over to the hearth to activate the spell. “You going to let her boss you around like that?”

It feels like Álvaro is trying to get a rise out of Cyros—or any sort of reaction at all—but he just shrugs. “She’s the expert.”

I snort. Expert? I’ve barely been cooking for half a year, and even then only because I’m forced to. My Knifework skill is about to max out at level 20, and my Brewing and Cooking skills are all in the mid teens as well, but to be fair, that’s due to potion making more than meal prep.

Álvaro doesn’t seem to have given up on trying to pull Cyros out of his stupor. “Expert? Please. I bet she doesn’t even know how to make churros.”

“And you do?” I ask over my shoulder.

“That’s beside the point!” Álvaro cries. “What I’m saying is, inflating her ego is the last thing we need. Gotta nip that in the butt.”

“Bud,” Cyros automatically corrects.

“What?” Álvaro shakes his head. “That doesn’t make any sense. It’s definitely butt.”

“It’s definitely not,” Cyros says, fetching some herbs for me.

From his cot, Teltora chuckles. “Some things never change.”

Cyros pauses, glancing toward his mapa. When he turns back to help me, he’s smiling.

And that’s all it takes. The tension eases out of the room as Cyros puts Álvaro to work, too, and the boys start bantering over some other misheard phrase. The smell of roasting vegetables gradually fills the air, and the home is filled with laughter.
