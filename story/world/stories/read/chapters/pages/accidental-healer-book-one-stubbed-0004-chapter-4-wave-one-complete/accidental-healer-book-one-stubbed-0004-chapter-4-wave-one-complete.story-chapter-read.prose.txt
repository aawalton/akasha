BOOOOONNNGGG.

Wave 1 complete.

A deep, resonant gong shook the clearing.

I collapsed onto my knees. Holy crap. I actually survived.

Next wave will commence in 2 hours. HP and MP restored. Participants will receive rewards based on contribution. Contribution calculated. Reward: Crude Wooden Club.

New Ability Unlocked: Loot

ALRIGHHHT!

I got a reward! And I gotta say, I was a big fan of two of them. Having my health and mana fully restored? Huge.

The second reward? Also huge.

If I was right about what it did…

I opened my status screen and scrolled to my new ability.

New Ability Unlocked: Loot

By touching fallen enemies, you will transform them into usable parts. Possible chance of finding a random item upon looting. Upgradeable.

Oh mama.

What a gift. Who doesn’t love to loot? My face split into a stupid grin.

I wondered what Chaos Spawn would give me. Probably nothing too great, but still—

I couldn’t wait to find out.

Finally, I got to the third and final reward. A wooden club. Exactly what every warrior dreamed of wielding.

I sighed as the notification blinked in my status screen.

Would you like to receive your quest reward: Crude Wooden Club?

I confirmed. Pop. The club dropped at my feet. I picked it up.

Heavy, rough, primitive, like something a caveman would use.

Whatever. It’ll do.

Now that I was healed and armed, I turned to looting. A thought smacked me.

Did I ever get a kill notification for that mountain lion?

Nope.

Uneasy, I retraced my steps. The body was still there, right where I left it.

Through some miracle, it moved. Just barely. A shallow rise and fall of its ribs. Was it still alive?

My grip tightened around the club. That didn’t make sense. It had been bleeding out for fifteen minutes.

How? More importantly, why didn’t the Chaos Spawn finish it off? They focused everything on me. Did they see me as the bigger threat? Could those little monsters even think like that?

I stepped closer. One hit. That was all it would take.

Its sides rose and fell in weak, ragged gasps. Blood matted its fur. It seemed like it couldn’t move. It was just dying slowly.

When it was chasing me, it looked so… powerful.

Now? I shook my head.

I should finish it. It would be the smart thing to do. I raised my club high above my head.

And then I looked into its eyes.

It didn’t snarl or bare its fangs. Just stared, eyes wide. First at me, then at the heavy club.

It was just an animal, but looking into those eyes, it was clear as day. He was afraid.

My stomach twisted and I looked away.

A Chaos Spawn was one thing. They were monsters. Mindless, probably.

But this? This was different. There was intelligence behind those eyes. It fought to survive, same as me.

And I… I couldn’t do it. I made my decision. It was a stupid one.

I almost died once. Now I was about to heal the thing that nearly got me killed. Brilliant.

First, I needed to see what was actually killing it. I stepped around its body. It was lying on top of something, its body concealing the wound. Whatever that thing was, it was the reason it was still bleeding.

There was only one option. I had to move it.

The cat was too weak to fight, but I hesitated. What if shifting it opened the wound and it bled out? What if I tried to save it and just ended up killing it?

Before I moved it, I cast Weak Regeneration. If I was doing this, I was not letting it die on me.

With a grunt, I shoved both arms under the cat’s limp body and lifted. It hissed softly but didn’t fight.

Beneath it, buried in blood and dirt, was a jagged shard of some unrecognizable metal.

That would do it. That simple metal shard had saved my life. What were the odds? Was me saving this cat just throwing away incredibly good fortune?

Maybe. I shook my head. My mind was made up.

Time for the risky part.

I took a breath. “Alright, you. Listen up.” I brandished my new club. “I really don’t want to use this, but let’s be clear—I will.”

My eyes narrowed. “Hopefully, you grasp the situation you’ve put us in.”

The cat just stared at me, unblinking.

Screw it. I used Weak Heal. Mana exited through my fingertips and down into the injured mountain lion.

Cautious, I watched for a reaction.

Between Weak Heal and Weak Regeneration, the cat should have at least 7 HP—assuming it had gone down to 1 HP in the first place. No way to check its stats. No way to tell if I needed to heal it again.

So, I waited.

His ears twitched. His breathing steadied. But he stayed put.

For some reason, the big cat didn’t seem as dangerous as it had before. Maybe it was the way he was sprawled out, too weak to move. Maybe it was just that he wasn’t trying to maul me anymore.

Either way, I kept my distance.

“Alright, big guy. I could probably get you back to full health, but let’s be real—I don’t trust you at full health.” I rested a hand on my hip. “So, I’ll leave you like this for now. Hope you understand.”

Just more staring.

I smirked. “You know, you really are a walking disaster. First, you nearly get me killed, then you make me risk my neck healing you. Guess that makes you a big, furry ball of mischief.”

The cat didn’t react.

I scratched my chin. “No complaints?”

Just a blank stare. I shrugged. “Mischief it is, then.”

With a watchful eye, slowly I stepped backwards. It felt like déjà vu. This time, though, he didn’t charge. For now, it felt like a win.

Regen would do its job—eventually. In the meantime? I had spoils of war to collect.

With Mischief still in the back of my mind, I moved to the first Chaos Spawn I stomped. Its back was bent at an unnatural angle, eyes bulging, tongue lolling. My own vomit coated its corpse.

It took everything in me not to hurl again.

With a deep breath, I steeled myself. The system said I just had to touch it to loot it.

Hopeful, I jabbed the disfigured, puke-covered body with my Converse.

The body shifted and its eyeball fell loose.

“Hhhuuurrrkkk!” I felt the burn of bile in the back of my throat.

Seriously? That doesn’t count as touching?

With effort, I tried to swallow away the burning in my throat. Fine. I closed my eyes, took another breath, and gingerly pressed two fingers to the least disgusting part.

Pop.

The body vanished in a cloud of smoke, replaced by a small pile of items.

I frowned. I have no idea what I’m looking at.

I grabbed a small vial filled with thick, bright-yellow liquid. The bile I just swallowed crawled back up the back of my throat. I held it up and gave it a good look.

Maybe I’ll get a pop-up menu? Some kind of explanation?

Nothing.

Annoyed, I opened my status screen.

Crushed Chaos Spawn Eyes—Used for alchemy. (Effects: ????)

Delightful. As if their buggy little eyeballs weren’t gross enough, now they’d been smashed into paste.

I grimaced and set it aside, moving on.

Chaos Spawn Tooth—Used for alchemy. (Effects: ????)

Minor Chaos Shard Fragment—Collect 10 fragments to complete a Minor Chaos Shard.

Chaos Spawn Meat—Safe for human consumption, though not generally considered appetizing.

I rubbed my temples.

Okay. Mixed bag. Loot is loot, but most of this was just… gross. The Shard Fragment stood out, though.

The description was vague, and that bothered me. I needed more information.

I proceeded to loot fifteen more creatures. Same routine. Same results.

Teeth. Eyes. Meat. Every. Single. Time.

The fragments, at least, were rarer—I’d collected nine so far. Just one more, and I could finally combine them. Hopefully, that’d tell me what they were actually for.

The seventeenth corpse gave me nothing new. Just another pile of disgusting parts. But when I looted the eighteenth?

Something different.

On top of the pile, half-buried in the dirt, was a crude-looking dagger. I picked it up. It was rough, unbalanced, and barely a weapon. I checked it out on my status screen.

Crude Serpent Dagger

No damage bonus. No hidden perks. Just a basic, squiggly-looking knife.

Still, it was something. I tossed it near my loot pile and moved on.

Number nineteen, I finally hit the jackpot—my tenth fragment.

I’d been stacking my loot in neat little piles. Not sure how it worked, I placed the last shard with the others.

The moment it touched the stack, they began to glow. The light was building, bright enough that I had to look away.

Then—

Ching.

A soft, windchime-like sound. I pulled up my status screen.

Combination successful. 10 Minor Chaos Shard Fragments → 1 Minor Chaos Shard. Minor Chaos Shard. New Effect: This shard can be combined with items to produce unknown effects.

AWESOME!

But also—SCREW YOU, SYSTEM.

No prompt. No confirmation. Just “Hope you don’t screw yourself over, buddy!” I was seeing a pattern here. No safety nets. No “Are you sure?” pop-ups. It never even asked if I wanted to enter the dungeon in the first place.

No more sarcasm. No more screwing around. I would need to be more careful.

…

…

But not right now.

Right now, I wanted to see what would happen when I combined this thing with an item.

The obvious choice was my club. It was a system reward, and if anything counted, it’d be that. Plus, if this shard could boost damage, I’d need that badly.

Decision made, I placed the club on the ground and kneeled beside it. The shard felt oddly warm in my palm.

I tried setting it next to the club like I had done with the fragments. No dice.

I picked it up and pressed it against the club, holding it there. Five seconds passed. Hmm…

I stared at the shard. Then at the club. Then back at the shard.

Oh, come on. I’m missing something. Think, idiot. Video game logic.

Is this skill-based? Do I need to be an enchanter or something? Realistically that could be it. Still, I wasn’t ready to give up just yet. There was one more thing I could try.

Earlier, when I cast my spells, I could feel mana surging from my chest, down my arms, and out through my hands. Even now, if I focused, I could still feel it—a slow, faint current in my core.

What if the shard needed mana to activate?

I shifted into a cross-legged position and focused. First, I tried to disrupt the flow—just to see if I could.

It took a minute. A struggle. But then—a shift. It was subtle, but it was there. I let out a slow breath. I was trying too hard.

When I cast spells, mana just flowed. I didn’t force it—I just let go.

Closing my eyes, I relaxed. Just let the mana move.

It was slow, but I could feel the mana begin to stir. Originating from my chest, it moved into my shoulders, down my arms, into my hands and then finally, I could feel it enter the shard—igniting it.

A circuit formed. Power looped between me and the shard, swirling in a steady cycle. With a grin, I knew this was the missing piece. I pressed the charged shard to my club.

Upgrade successful. Crude Wooden Club → Spiked Crude Wooden Club. New Effect: Increased chance to cause Bleeding.

I did it! Triumphantly I raised my new club above me. I frowned and cocked my head.

Huh… I was expecting more. It looks almost the same. Just… uglier.

Now, four jagged, two-inch-wide blades pierced through the wood. It was like one of those bats with nails in it—except shorter, heavier, and designed to ruin someone’s day.

Not flashy, but damage is damage.

I’ll take it.
