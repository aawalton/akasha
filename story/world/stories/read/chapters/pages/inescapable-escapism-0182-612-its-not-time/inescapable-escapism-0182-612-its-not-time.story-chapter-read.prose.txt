“Text me when you get home, yeah?” Duncan asked as we reached the end of the path that led away from his house.

Reluctantly, I let my fingers slip away from his. I had to fight the urge to tighten my grip at the last second and cling to him. It was hard; I wasn’t ready to let him go, but I managed to stop myself from doing it. My hand felt cold without his warming it, despite the warm air that hung heavily around us.

Duncan had insisted on walking me home, just like he always did whenever I’d gone to his house over the last couple of weeks. He couldn’t accompany me for the whole journey, though. It would be too risky. Mom might see us, but he’d walk me that far and then wait at the end of the path until he saw me turn onto my drive.

That felt like the safest option. I still got to spend a little more time with Duncan, even if it was only a couple of minutes, but the chances of being spotted by my mom were still low enough that it didn’t make me too anxious.

We did risk holding hands as we walked that time, though. We’d done it a few times whilst walking Rex earlier, and I’d enjoyed that, even if it also worried me, but I didn’t care anymore. Duncan was walking behind me, anyway. Our conjoined hands were clasped behind my body; they were hidden from view, and we hadn’t properly emerged out onto the main road, so I knew it would probably be fine.

Still, I found myself scanning the street before I turned to face Duncan.

“Of course,” I told him. “Thanks for walking me home.”

“Any time,” he replied immediately, his lips pulling up into a smile that caused my heart to clench.

My eyes flicked towards his lips. I couldn’t help myself, but it felt impossible to look away, and I longed to kiss him. I always wanted to whenever he smiled at me or when we said goodbye, and I knew that I couldn’t, but that didn’t stop the desire from gripping me.

We’d spent most of the afternoon making out, and we’d already said goodbye properly before leaving his house. Rex had sat beside us, waiting impatiently, as Duncan and I had exchanged kiss after kiss, but it still didn’t feel like enough. I wanted more.

“Text me once you’ve spoken to your parents?” I asked hesitantly, sending another glance over my shoulder at the street behind me to make sure my mom hadn’t appeared.

“Yeah, I will. I think they’re back fairly early, so I should know when we have plans over the next few days then,” he said.

“Okay, cool.”

There was another pause, and I saw Duncan’s gaze drop to my lips. My stomach tightened as I realised he was fighting with the urge to kiss me as well. I almost hoped he’d give in, despite how dangerous it felt. I was torn between not wanting my mom to know about us and also just wanting to feel my lips pressed against his.

“Have fun with Phoebe tomorrow,” he told me after a few seconds, his eyes returning to my own.

“Thanks. Have fun with your parents,” I said before adding, “and at training tomorrow.”

Duncan’s face scrunched up in mock disgust.

“Eh, maybe,” he replied, but there was a light in his eyes that made it clear he didn’t truly mean it.

I started to step away from Duncan before hesitating. My free hand twitched as the need to reach out towards him slammed into me, and I opened my mouth, unsure of what I was going to say, but I quickly shut it again as a car sped down the road behind me.

My head snapped towards it, and my eyes dropped to Rex. Fear crashed into me, but he was unbothered by the car that drove far too quickly for the thin, windy road. Rex looked up from the bush he was sniffing for only a couple of seconds, just long enough for him to glance at the car, before his attention returned to the leaves once more.

The car wasn’t my mom’s, I realised as I peered at the car once more, and relief washed over me. It was the wrong colour, but my eyes still remained on it until the car disappeared from sight before I looked back at Duncan, and pain tightened around my heart.

“So… I’ll see you soon?” I said.

It was meant to be a statement, but it sounded more like a question.

“Yeah! We’ll do something next week for sure,” Duncan promised, pausing before bending down to stroke Rex. “And hopefully I’ll see you next week too, okay, boy?”

A smile pulled at my lips as I watched them for a couple more seconds before forcing myself to take another step backwards.

“Bye,” I said.

“Bye, Grace.”

Sadness started to pull at me as I turned away from Duncan and began to walk away. The urge to spin around again and rush back towards him thrashed within my chest with every step, but I couldn’t give in. I knew that if I did, it would be even harder to walk away from him again, and I wasn’t sure that I could do it. Plus, our goodbye had already been drawn out enough. I was worried about him getting bored or impatient.

I shouldn’t have been sad, though. It felt like I was being silly. I’d had a great time with Duncan, and I was pretty sure that Rex had too. After wandering around the entire house and sniffing just about everything, he’d spent most of the time we’d been at Duncan’s house sleeping, but it seemed like he’d liked being there. It was hard to tell.

That wasn’t why I was sad, though. It was because his parents were getting home tomorrow, and I wasn’t sure whether that would change things. He hadn’t said it would be a problem or anything. All he’d told me was that he’d find out when they were going out for a meal and shopping for school stuff, and then let me know when he would be free next, but I was still worried.

Would I still be able to go to his house with them there? They were pretty relaxed parents, but what if they didn’t like him having girls over? I’d been to his house before, obviously, but that was mostly when he threw a party, and his parents were never there for those. Other than that, I hadn’t been to his house alone since we were kids. We were so much younger, and we just spent the whole time playing rather than…

Things might be different now that we were older. Maybe they’d insist we keep the bedroom door open the entire time or something. I’d heard of parents saying things like that, but I hoped Duncan’s wouldn’t. If the door were open, we wouldn’t be able to make out or anything, and that would suck. I mean, technically, we still could, but we’d have to keep an eye on the door to make sure they didn’t see us, and I’d feel weird doing it.

I felt weird about going to his house at all whilst his parents were there, actually. It had been years since I’d seen them, and I didn’t know how they’d react to me. Would they even remember me? I wasn’t sure. I must have changed a lot since we’d spoken last, and Duncan had a lot of friends. They couldn’t be expected to remember them all.

The desire to look back at Duncan broke through my thoughts, and I tried to resist, but I was only able to for a few steps before the urge became too strong. He was still there, though. I knew that he would be. He always waited until I got to my drive before going home, but seeing him brought a smile to my face.

As if feeling my gaze, Duncan looked up from his phone. His lips stretched into a grin, and I had to look away as my heart began to thump. I loved that smile of his so much. It wasn’t his usual one, the one he gave everyone. It was different, and I wasn’t sure how exactly, but it always made me feel like I was the only person in the world. It made me feel special, like he thought I was special.

I bit my lip. I was blushing. I knew that I was. My cheeks felt hot, and I was certain they were bright red, but I needed them to stop soon. I had almost reached my drive, and I had to look as if everything was completely normal before I got to the house so Mom didn’t get suspicious.

My steps slowed as I neared the drive. I was walking at a snail’s pace, unwilling to move any faster because I knew that once I did step off the main road, I’d have to face my mom, and I’d no longer be with Duncan. We’d already said goodbye. He was at the other end of the road, but somehow, it didn’t feel quite so final until he was out of sight.

Luckily, Rex didn’t seem bothered by my slow pace. He had slowed too, and I smiled down at him before looking back at Duncan one last time. Pain reignited in my chest again as I lifted a hand to wave back at him before turning onto my drive.

The car was still there. That was the first thing I noticed. Mom’s car had not left the drive. In fact, it didn’t look as though it had been moved at all. It was hard to tell, but I hoped it hadn’t because that meant she hadn’t come looking for me. Unless she’d walked, of course. That was still possible.

But the curtains were closed. That also felt like a good sign. I was pretty sure they’d been open when I’d left the house earlier, so either she’d closed them so she could watch a movie or something, which she didn’t do often, or she had taken a nap. Both options meant it was likely she’d spent all day at home, so I was hopeful.

My mind was racing, I realised as I neared the front door. I could feel my thoughts chasing each other around my head, and I let out a slow exhale in an attempt to slow them down, but it didn’t work. Instead, a new thought popped into my mind. What should I say to my mom? If she asked me about my day, what should I tell her?

Originally, I’d told her I was going to take Rex to Phoebe’s house after our walk, but that was a lie. I’d never really planned on doing that, but I couldn’t work out whether it would be best to tell her I had or not. If I did, she’d probably ask how Rex had acted around Phoebe, if there were any problems there.

That would be fine, though. I could deal with Mom’s questions if I had to, but I’d also need to let Phoebe know if I did that. She needed to be aware that I’d said it in case Mom asked her anything about it. And there was a chance that she’d say something to Phoebe’s mom at some point too. They never tended to talk too much, but she could still bring Rex up, and that could be a problem.

But if I said I hadn’t seen Phoebe, I thought, Mom would want to know what I’d been doing all afternoon. Hours had passed since I’d left the house, and I’d need to come up with another lie. I could potentially just say that I just took Rex on a really long walk, but then she’d probably want to know where I went. I knew the village pretty well. It wouldn’t be too hard to come up with a route that would have taken us a few hours, but if Mom had left the house at all, that was an added risk. She might have gone to one of the places I mentioned, and then she’d know I was lying.

I had to say I’d gone to Phoebe’s. That was the least dangerous option. I’d just tell Mom that I took Rex to her house, and everything was fine, that Rex behaved perfectly. He was a good dog. We hadn’t had any problems with him since we took him home, so it felt believable enough. Then, I’d just text Phoebe later and let her know before deleting the messages. It would be fine.

Taking another deep breath to steel myself, I came to a stop at the front door and pulled my keys out of my pocket. My heart was beating too fast, and my body was tensed as I slipped them into the lock and twisted. It felt as though I was waiting for Mom to start shouting at me the moment I opened the door, but instead, I was met with an eerie silence.

Laughter came from somewhere within the house, the sound far too loud, and I jumped, my hands tightening around the keys as if preparing to use them as a weapon, but no one appeared in the dim hallway before me. The light wasn’t on, but the door to the kitchen was open, and there was enough sunlight streaming in to illuminate the corridor.

It was empty. There was no one waiting for me there, but that didn’t mean I was safe. Someone could have been hiding in one of the other rooms, a weapon clasped in their hand as they waited for me to get closer. Or Mom could be there. I wasn’t sure which would be worse.

Rex looked up at me, clearly wondering why we were still standing in the doorway, and I forced myself to walk forward, even as my heart pounded in my ears. It was so loud that it almost drowned out the noise of whatever Mom was watching in the lounge, but I could just about hear the voices.

It was probably fine. I knew that it was unlikely anyone had broken into the house and was about to attack me, but I kept my back to the front door as I slipped my feet out of my shoes, watching the corridor ahead of me closely from the corner of my eyes. There was no movement, but that didn’t make me feel better.

“Rex,” I said softly. “Come here so I can take your harness off.”

Rex didn’t respond straight away. He stood a few paces in front of me, his posture tight and his nose twitching as he sniffed the house before finally turning and trotting back to me. I bent slowly, continuing to scan our surroundings carefully as I unbuckled the straps and let him step out of it.

The moment he was free, Rex wandered away, and I had to press my lips together to prevent myself from calling out to him. I wanted to stop him from going any deeper into the house, and I could feel the words fighting to escape my throat, but I bit the inside of my cheeks to keep them at bay.

It was fine, I told myself as I quickly placed Rex’s harness and lead back on the hook by the door. There was probably no one else in the house apart from my mom, and he’d just gone into her lounge, so it was okay. Plus, I needed to go in there anyway. There was no way I could get away with sneaking past the door without talking to Mom. She’d be furious.

Moving quickly and listening out for any hint of a sound coming from further into the house, I moved towards the doorway and peered inside. Mom was still sitting on the sofa, a mostly empty glass of wine on the table in front of her and her phone in her hand. Her other hand was stroking Rex’s head, but she barely seemed to be paying attention to him. Most of her focus was fixed on the mobile game she was playing.

A loud laugh came from the speakers, and I jumped again. The sound was so unexpected, so jarring, but luckily, Mom didn’t even look up. I watched her for a moment before angling my body slightly, making it possible to see more of the hallway from the corner of my eye whilst still staring into the room.

“Hey,” I said. “I’m home.”

That was obvious. Mom must have guessed I was home the moment she saw Rex, but she still blinked before glancing up at me, a bored expression already plastered on her face.

“Mmm,” was her only reply before she looked down at her phone again.

I hesitated. Part of me longed to turn and go upstairs without saying anything else to Mom. She seemed to have no interest in talking to me, but I knew it wasn’t worth it. I had to at least try to maintain a conversation with her. I’d learnt from experience that if I didn’t, she could get annoyed.

It had happened before. A few times. I’d heard her voice float through my door as she complained to Dad that she’d tried to talk to me, but I just ignored her and clearly had no interest in having a relationship with her, and that always made me feel guilty. I didn’t want her to think that.

“How was your day?” I asked, subtly scanning the corridor around me.

I knew it was unnecessary. I probably didn’t need to check, and Mom definitely would have gotten suspicious if she’d noticed I was so on edge, but I couldn’t help it. I was in my reality; I was almost certain I was. The chances of someone or a monster hiding in the shadows around me were so low, but anxiety gnawed at her, and it was impossible to ignore it.

Mom let out an almost silent sigh as she sat back on the sofa, her other hand returning to her phone. Rex stood there, watching her, for a moment longer before seemingly realising that Mom wasn’t going to give him any more attention and wandering away.

“Fine,” Mom sighed. There was a pause before she asked, “How was yours?”

The question was reluctant. It sounded as though Mom didn’t really want to ask it or care about my response, but I replied nonetheless.

“Yeah, it was fine,” I said before quickly correcting myself, “good, actually. Rex was really well-behaved.”

I smiled down at him before reaching down to stroke his head. That much wasn’t a lie. He really had been well-behaved, and he wagged his tail at me, as if he understood what I was saying. Maybe he did.

“Good,” Mom muttered.

It was clear the small amount of attention she’d given to Rex and me had returned to her phone, and I hesitated before speaking again.

“Well, I’m going to get Rex a drink before going upstairs and reading something, I think.”

I waited for Mom to reply, but she didn’t. She gave no indicator that she’d even heard me, and I hesitated for a couple more seconds before straightening up and turning away. I almost expected her to call me back, but she said nothing as I walked towards the kitchen.

My anxiety did not subside as I switched the lights on and quickly scanned the room. Obviously, it was empty, but I was still on edge as I walked towards Rex’s bowl and emptied it into the sink before refilling it.

“There you go, boy,” I told him softly, but Rex made no move to have a drink. He just continued to stare at me. “That’s it. I’m not getting you your dinner just yet.”

That must have been what he was waiting for because his tail began to wag, and he sat. Despite my anxiety, a giggle slipped from my lips, and I leaned down to stroke his head.

“It’s not that time yet, Rex,” I said. “You’ve got another… two hours until then!”

But Rex just continued to stare at me, his expression hopeful. Guilt pulled at me. He was probably hungry, I reasoned with myself. He had gone for a pretty long walk, and he’d run around in Duncan’s garden a little bit when we’d let him out earlier. I wasn’t sure if he was used to doing that much exercise, so it made sense that he was hungrier than usual, and I didn’t want him to suffer.

Indecision coursed through me as I chewed my lip before moving towards the cupboard where we kept his food and opening it.

“It’s not time to feed him,” my mom snapped from the lounge.

I had no idea how she knew I’d opened the cupboard. She must have heard the gentle sound of the child lock being opened, and I was impressed. I assumed it would have been drowned out by the television.

“I know,” I called back. “I’m just getting him a chew!”

Mom didn’t reply, and I glanced at the doorway before looking down at Rex again. There was no way I could say no to him, though. Carefully, I reached into the bag of his dry food and grabbed a handful. My eyes flicked towards his bowl as I withdrew my hand, but I realised I couldn’t put it there. If Mom had heard the lock, she would have definitely noticed the clink of his food hitting his bowl.

I locked eyes with Rex, debating what to do before silently holding my hand out to him. The moment I did, fear leapt within me. I’d never fed Rex like that, and I wasn’t sure how it would go. I didn’t think he’d bite me intentionally, but I was worried he might do it accidentally.

Rex stared at the outstretched fist full of food for a couple of seconds before padding towards me and starting to eat, and my fears were immediately soothed. He was so gentle, and he barely touched my hand. I could feel his fur tickling my palm, and I had to bite my lip to stop myself from giggling.

“Good boy,” I whispered as he finished before reaching into the cupboard again and pulling out a chew. “There you go. Take it up to my room, okay?”
