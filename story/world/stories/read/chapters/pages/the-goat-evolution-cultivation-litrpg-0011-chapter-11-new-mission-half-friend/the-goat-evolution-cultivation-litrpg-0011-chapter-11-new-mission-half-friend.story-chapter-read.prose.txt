Lin jolted awake, blinking at the system notification floating in front of his eyes. For a second, he thought he was dreaming. Oh my God, what the hell is this? A whole list of rewards. Real, actual choices. But the joy faded quickly when he realized he could only pick one. The rest would vanish forever.

I told you you shouldn’t be broken, but who would have guessed you’d be this broken? Don’t tell me you’re trolling me. No, you’re not just trolling; you actually enjoy seeing me suffer, don’t you? Fuck you, system.

The choice was clear, even though the first two were good but had a time limit; the third one is consumable, and the fourth one is rare, which can be used repeatedly. Without hesitation, Lin chose the Pilpil Seed. Not just because it was rare, but because it was a type of grass that could regrow every day. A renewable resource. That’s real power.

──────────

[You have selected Pilpil Seed. Please confirm: Y/N]

He didn’t hesitate. Yes. A second later, another notification popped up.

[Pilpil Seed has been delivered to your inventory.]

──────────

Lin opened his inventory and double-checked. Sure enough, four black seeds sat next to the cursed bandage, waiting. He exhaled, closed the menu, and finally looked around.

That’s when he realized something's off. Where’s Bojo? Lin stood, looking left and right. No matter where he searched, Bojo was nowhere to be found. Don’t tell me he ran off.

With a sigh, Lin glanced around. The world was nearly dark, but hey, he was a goat now. Night vision came with the package. It wasn’t daylight, but it was good enough to get by.

We were friends. How could he leave me like that? Lin couldn’t help but feel a pang of grief. If he’d just waited one more day, maybe things would be different. Now, what would happen to his “make a friend” mission? The thought made him clench his jaw in frustration. He trudged back to the tree grove and took a bite of grass.

Damn it. So much time wasted. Sigh, let’s not waste time on useless stuff. Since he left, I’ll have to find another goat. Not sure if that’ll even work, though. The progress is still there. Maybe I won’t have to start from scratch. But that’s for tomorrow. For now, focus on what’s in front of you.

Lin sat down and started eating, but after a few bites, he stopped. He was already full; there was no way he could force down any more. Only one thing left to do: take out the grass from his belly and chew it again. As he considered this, something clicked in his mind. His daily mission.

Right. I’ve got another mission to finish, and it's my daily quest. Annoying, but it’s got to be done every day. The reward depends on how much I complete. He opened his mission panel and checked his current progress. Only one option tracked his growth: chewing grass. As for the others, they are still at zero.

────────────────────

────────────────────

He stared at the numbers, feeling a wave of disbelief wash over him. After all that walking, his step count was still at zero. Chewing? Only 276! If things keep going like this, there’s no way I’ll ever finish this. He slowly stood up and decided to try some exercise. He jumped toward the sky, then checked the panel. Still zero. What’s happening? Why isn’t it increasing? Lin stared at the panel for a few more seconds, then jumped again. Nothing. Come on, system. Why aren’t you registering my jumps?

Frustrated, he jumped with all his strength, and finally, when he checked the panel, the jump count had gone up by one. You're kidding me, system? You seriously want to give me a hernia? All those thoughts he’d been having were a waste of time. He could’ve been thinking about something else—anything else. Maybe even about that time with his ex-menegger's wife. (Not that he’d ever admit it out loud.

With a sigh, Lin leapt again, summoning every ounce of strength. He kept going, jumping after jump, until he finally collapsed to the ground, panting and drained. Oh, goat god, please, if you’re up there, don’t let anyone find me now. I can’t even move, let alone run. If another goat shows up, I’m finished.

He looked up at the sky and prayed, hoping the goat god would grant this one small wish. After a few seconds, Lin finally regained enough stamina to move. Damn, I should do something else.

Thinking this, Lin started eating grass again, the only thing that seemed to work without issue. All he had to do was sit and chew. But as he chewed, the number didn’t increase at all. Frustrated, Lin shouted at the system, “You fucking broken system, why isn’t this thing going up?”

Perhaps because of Lin's outburst, a notification finally appeared before him.

────────────────────

[System Notice] Chewing grass won’t be counted if you’re not actually eating. The host’s belly is full; no further eating detected. Unless you swallowed, it doesn’t count. ────────────────────

“Fine, I get it. But what about the step counter? I walked more than a kilometre, how come none of it registered?”

────────────────────

[System Notice] For steps to be counted, the host must run at full capacity. Only vigorous movement will be registered as progress.

────────────────────

“So many restrictions,” Lin muttered. “System, do you even want me to finish these missions? Or are you just angry at me? Why don’t you want me to succeed?” Closing the status window, Lin focused on the missions. So, to finish the chewing quest, I have to empty my stomach first? That’s out for now. I’ve already missed my chance at the 100% reward. Whatever. Let’s get a good nap. I’ll think about all this tomorrow.

He dragged himself toward the grove of trees, searching for the best place to sleep. He wasn’t too worried about other goats—no, it was the foxes, wolves, or coyotes that haunted his thoughts. Only God knows what’s on the other side of this farm. Is there even a fence? Hope there is. Just let me live in peace. Nothing else matters.

At this moment, with a ding, a new notification appeared before Lin.
