“Fine,” Gabriel said after Fiona spoke, his voice shaken but not undone. “Let’s say we believe you both. Let’s say we take your words for it. Eternus is real. People are, unbeknownst to them, playing your fuckin’ game and working toward some sort of crazy world-meld. What happens when it works? Earth gets teleported?”

Fiona clicked her tongue at the question, and a new hologram appeared, showing two spherical planets—one slightly larger than Earth beside it—and then the mock-up of immense gateways radiating with light. Jon stared at the hologram with furrowed brows, and felt his heartbeat tick up in pace within his chest. There was a strange beauty to what he was seeing, combined with a steadily growing sense of worry that, just maybe, Adrian and Fiona weren’t as batshit crazy as he initially thought. It was not a comforting feeling.

“It does not get teleported,” Fiona said curtly afterward while turning to look at them all. “The conjunction has happened before, in other worlds. Once the connection stabilizes and Revelation is completed, gateways will appear on every major continent leading to specific areas of Eternus selected by the System. The System itself will take over Earth by fully assimilating D.E.U.S. instead of using it as a remote node, and every person on this planet will be integrated into it seamlessly and painlessly. They will simply wake up one day and have System access and the ability to walk between worlds. Earth technology will cease to function, and magic will replace it.”

“Magic replaces it?” Gabriel asked quickly. “What about hospitals? What about planes in the air, helicopters, ships at sea, and everything else that runs on power?”

“There is no such thing as evolution without cost, Lieutenant,” Adrian answered with ruthless certainty. “I will issue a warning when D.E.U.S. warns the conjunction is approaching criticality. Whether or not that warning is heeded is up to those that hear it.”

“So you’d just let thousands, perhaps hundreds of thousands of people just die?”

“I cannot control the actions of others, Lieutenant,” Adrian replied once more. “I will warn them. What they choose to do with that warning is their prerogative.”

“That’s fuckin’ cold, Maxwell,” DeShaun wheezed.

“I never claimed to be charitable, Lieutenant.”

“And what about the players currently involved with Eternus?” Michael asked carefully after Adrian answered. “What happens when the conjunction occurs?”

“That, Chief Rosen, is where this becomes especially applicable to you,” Adrian said after the question while looking at Jon’s youngest squadmate. “The Eidos of every Eternus player will be merged with their Earth bodies by the System the moment Revelation begins. That means that their levels, Skills, Attributes, and more will be fused with them in the same moment as their Eidos is fused with their existing corporeal shell. The System, based on our understanding, will offer a choice: acceptance of the Eidos, or a one-time recreation of the Eidos instead.”

“Why the allowance?” DeShaun asked stiffly, still audibly displeased by the CEO’s earlier response.

“Because some people like to experience a different gender when playing games, Lieutenant. We allowed that because of the System’s laws around conjunction, or else we would have restricted Eidos creation to the sex of the player as dictated by their biology.”

“So that means that if someone wanted to change their biological sex—”

“Yes, Lieutenant Commander Yamato,” Adrian said steadily. “The System will respect that choice; their Eidos—as they chose to create it—will become their new biological norm, and they will become whatsoever they desired to be. Every human on Earth, player or not, will receive the chance to rebuild their Eidos within the permissible parameters when Revelation comes.”

“That’s suspiciously generous…” Gabriel muttered.

“That is cause and effect,” Adrian corrected without anger. “The same way that your current circumstances are. The relevance of what I am informing you of should be of great interest to you, however, because of those circumstances.”

Adrian’s eyes swept over them, and Jon thought he actually saw something approaching regret in the CEO’s steady gaze.

“You are cripples, gentlemen—by my hand, yes, and regrettably so. That does not need to be the case permanently, however.”

“What, you’re offering us prosthetics?” Michael asked suspiciously.

“No, I am offering you something far better, Chief Rosen,” Adrian replied without blinking. “If you agree to my terms, I will settle you in full-immersion pods and connect you to Eternus, and you may build an Eidos that perfectly suits your nature, desires, and preferred appearance. From there, you may work toward the conjunction and—even before it occurs—repair your damaged bodies if and when you achieve level one hundred and fifty within Eternus. That, based on D.E.U.S.’s data, is when the synchronization process becomes highly potent. Your Earth bodies will begin regenerating and changing to match your Eternian Eidos.”

Jon went silent at his words, as did the rest of Trident, and he felt his heart hammering behind his ribs as he considered what Adrian was saying. Play Eternus, and his brothers could get their bodies back? It sounded insane. It sounded completely and utterly ridiculous. Yet, there was no lie on Maxwell’s face—and worse, there was no doubt in his gaze, either.

“I thought you said that only happens during the Revelation, or conjunction, or whatever?” Aleksei asked in the same hoarse voice. “You’re contradicting yourself, Maxwell. We’re SEALs, not jarheads.”

“One truth does not overturn another, Chief Ivanov,” Adrian said instead of looking caught or like he’d slipped up. “Conjunction will affect everyone on Earth, but if you synchronize deeply enough with your Eidos through power-growth before that, your bodies here on Earth will begin changing to reflect your bodies on Eternus.”

Fiona swiped her tablet again, and a hologram of two bodies appeared to replace the planetary models, showing a whole silhouette and one lacking an arm and a leg. Levels began appearing above the whole body, and as they climbed toward two hundred, the bodies started drifting together, merging until the healthier body subsumed the damaged one entirely.

“As more people reach higher tiers of power, more latent mana will be generated through D.E.U.S. as the System becomes more closely linked to their souls. As more souls create mana generation, Earth will develop its own manasphere. As that occurs, the System will begin passively correcting the disconnect between Earth bodies and Eternian bodies, so that the soul is no longer split between two different containers. By the time someone reaches level two hundred, the functional difference between their Earth self and Eternian self will cease to exist.”

“So if we play your game,” Jon said before anyone else could speak, “my brothers will be healed?”

“Yes, Commander,” Adrian said steadily. “That is precisely the case.”

“What about people who made opposite-gender characters as an experiment? Are they forcibly changed, too?” Michael asked immediately.

“Change of that level requires consent, Chief Rosen,” Adrian answered steadily. “The System will give them the benefits of their Eidos, but when it comes time for that change, they will be prompted once. If they choose to ignore the warning, that burden is on them.”

None of his SEALs liked that answer, Jon could tell, but neither was it entirely unfair.

“What’s the catch?” Michael asked in immediate follow-up afterward. “There’s always a catch, Maxwell.”

“Service,” Adrian replied immediately and without obfuscation. “My Faith is one of cause and effect, as I stated earlier. The cause of your current circumstances was your breach of my facility. The effect was your current state of being at my mercy. You will perform a service for me, which will be the new cause—the new effect will be the healing of your bodies.”

“And this service,” Zack said quickly, “it will occur solely in Eternus?”

“It will,” Adrian said firmly. “I have neither need nor desire for your aid in this world. What I need from you, Trident, is your capability and adaptive competence. There is someone I will be sending you to protect and serve. Your task will be ensuring that they are not destroyed, and they grow in strength, and that their cause succeeds.”

He wants bodyguards for a Principal. Not himself, though, or he’d have said that—he’s too direct to make it so vague if he meant himself. So who’s the Principal?

At that moment, Adrian’s words from their earlier confrontation abruptly seared their way into Jonathan’s mind: “Now then, you and I are going to have a discussion. I happen to have an excellent use for the six of you. You see, it will be Christmas soon—and what father doesn’t wish to dote on their children?”

“Your child,” Jon said before he could stop himself. “Revenant-King means it’s a boy. You want us to protect your son.”

“What son?” DeShaun asked immediately. “The briefing packet had nothing on a kid.”

“Because nobody knows he exists,” Jon answered while Adrian watched him silently. “You’ve got a secret son somewhere, Maxwell, and you want my team to keep him safe in Eternus—to help him grow, I’d wager, and probably teach him, in preparation for the conjunction. Am I close?”

“Very much so, Commander,” Adrian said with a mirthless smile. “I see my earlier comment remained in your mind despite the hit you took to the skull.”

“Your words jogged the memory,” Jon replied neutrally. “So you want us to protect a Principal who also happens to be your kid. I take it you have terms?”

“Of course,” Adrian said while glancing at Fiona, who stepped forward immediately.

“The terms are simple, gentlemen,” she began coldly while looking down at her tablet. “You will undertake the following: First, you will enter Eternus Online and create an Eidos. Second, you will align yourself with the Dark Faith—details to follow—and immediately set to work on raising yourselves to level twenty-five, Initiate tier, before three weeks pass on Eternus. Third, you will make your way to the city of Blackstone and place yourselves in the service of the individual entitled the Revenant-King.”

Her eyes swept across them after the third condition, settling on each of them individually, and then returned to the tablet to continue.

“Fourth, you will commit every iota of your collective skill, experience, and expertise into keeping him from being destroyed. Death is not permanent for him, but he can be destroyed, and that is what you must not permit to happen. Fifth, you will not allow your own Eidos to be killed except in absolute necessity for the defense of the Principal’s existence. Sixth, you will serve as loyal soldiers, bodyguards, and advisors. The emphasis here is on service, not puppet rulership. You will obey, heed, and give absolute loyalty to the Revenant-King within Eternus. Seventh, you will not reveal Eternus’s true nature, the conjunction, or the Revenant-King’s relation to His Grace, directly or indirectly. Breach of this condition will be deemed an immediate violation of the terms in their totality.”

“And if we fail or violate any of these terms?” Jon asked carefully.

“I kill your true bodies in their pods, which would mean true death prior to synchronization,” Adrian said in the same utterly implacable manner. “Cause and effect, Commander.”

“And if we refuse?”

“I kill you now without needless suffering,” Adrian answered again calmly.

“That doesn’t seem like much of a choice,” Jon said as his heart hammered.

“You chose to enter my facility, you chose to breach my security, and you chose to set yourself against my designs,” Adrian said in return. “Your current choice is to help my son or die. I did not force any of the actions that led to your confrontation with me, you chose that path. I am now giving you a new choice. Whether or not you believe it is fair is of no concern to me.”

Jon felt his jaw tighten at the words and closed his eyes, breathing against the anger welling in his chest. If even half of what Adrian said was true, then Halsey was dead, Neptune was destroyed, the world was ending, and beyond it all, there was a chance for his brothers to not be butchered cripples for the rest of their lives. It was insane. It was nuts. It was completely outside the scope of reality.

They were also, beyond any shred of doubt, bereft of any other option.

“If we accept,” Jon said while his team immediately objected, until he spoke over them. “If we accept, where do we begin?”

“Education first,” Fiona said crisply. “You will learn about Eternus, its Divinarchs—gods, as you might call them—as well as its Powers, which are akin to demi-gods or lesser gods. You will learn about the different mana types, how Attributes function, how to most efficiently pursue a ‘build’ to use the slang term, and how to best adapt to the world in which you will find yourselves. This will take place over a full twelve-hour period, and we will equip you with special starting equipment and written guides within Eternus itself, courtesy of several vanguard agents we have already inserted into Eternus.”

Jon mulled over that in silence, glancing down at the bed, and then looked back up toward Adrian and Fiona.

“Why us?” he asked finally, carefully modulating the question. “You have thousands of loyal citizens by your own admission, and Sterling is evidently highly capable. Why choose six operators with negligible experience on Eternus?”

“Because my son is one of you in culture, even if not by blood,” Adrian replied without obfuscation. “You will understand his perspective on Eternus better than my own people would, would treat him without the senseless worship they would give to a being of his position, and will be objective where my own people would be compromised by sentiment or emotion. You are the best soldiers Earth has produced—you are also conveniently untainted by the compromising affections my own people would feel for their ancestral home.”

Jon let that answer sit for a moment as he considered it and found no immediate fault in the statement. It was, from a strictly rational standpoint, an operationally solvent qualification. It would be far easier for his SEALs to approach Eternus from a more professional standpoint than alleged aliens who traced their hypothetical origins back to it, and it would permit a level of detached objectivity that Maxwell’s own people would—if everything he said was true—most likely lack.

It didn’t mean that Trident would approach the task with the viewpoint Adrian might prefer, but he didn’t ask for that, either.

“One request, then,” Jon said warily. “I want time to talk to my team about it privately, first.”

“You will have twenty minutes, though I will maintain external monitoring,” Adrian said.

“And if we say yes?” Jon asked finally. “When would we be expected to begin?”

Adrian’s eyes brightened faintly at the question, and he smiled wryly.

“Immediately, Commander Ames. You would begin immediately.”
