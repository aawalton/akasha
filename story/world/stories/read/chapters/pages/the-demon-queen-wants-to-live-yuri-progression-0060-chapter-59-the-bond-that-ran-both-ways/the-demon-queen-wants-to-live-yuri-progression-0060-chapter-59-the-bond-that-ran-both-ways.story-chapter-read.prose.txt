"You are sure you can walk?" Ash asked. It was her third time since we had left the room, by my counting.

"I can walk reasonably well," I said. I had said the same thing the last two times. I suspected I would say the same thing the next three as well.

"Hmm…." Ash said. She walked beside me, closer than the walking required. She moved close every time I took an uncertain step, before hurriedly moving back when it became clear that I would never do something as undignified as falling.

Sol chirped from between my horns. Students eyed us as we passed. Some of them had the same fear on their faces they'd had before. Most did not. The ratio had changed again. Was that because of the news the Archon had said he would spread? That was possible. The masses were quick to go where they were pointed, especially when they thought the pointing was theirs all along.

"I didn't think you'd be excited to take a class," Ash said.

"I am not excited. And it is reasonable to not waste time." In my right hand I held one of those strange wooden devices, its face alight with engraved words. Edda had been right. There had been two of these devices inside our new room's desk. There had likely been two more in the room we had abandoned.

The tool had more uses than the girl had initially suggested. A trickle of Essence showed the classes running, or about to start, where they were held, who taught them. A thought paired with the Essence, and the result was different.

For instance, if I thought of the Body Conditioning class, then the device would show me when the next was to be held. If I thought of a class that had to do with 'weapons training', it would show me those too, even if I only vaguely knew what I sought.

In that, this tool was not very different from how the Athenaeum itself worked. How curious. Magic that could both read and interpret thoughts. It wasn't an impossible power in my time, but it was a rare one. To see it used here, to run something as meager as this Academy was odd indeed.

"It's a left around here." Ash pointed. I nodded and turned. A passing girl hurriedly stepped out of the way before I barreled into her.

The hallway opened up ahead. And then, we were in another one of these yards. It seemed we were just in time for the class I meant to inspect. Beast Taming was, by any measure, the class most beneath my notice. I did not need to know of every other class at this Academy to know that much.

Combat classes anyone could understand the value of. The conditioning, Ash had assured me, would have its uses. Even the forging, humiliating as my current failures were, at least demanded some craft. This, however, was nothing but the business of binding a beast to one's will.

I had done it before. I had bound the [Great Serpent] to my will when she had just been a hatchling. I had seen Zera bind more creatures than I could recall. The method was simple. You found the beast. You established dominance, either through strength or imprinting. The beast would either have to submit, or it would have to accept death instead.

Sol's bond had been formed through imprinting rather than subjugation, which simplified matters further. My presence had been the first she had known. And so, she became one of my subjects, and a subject would serve. This was how bonds worked. It was all rather simple.

I did not see what any instructor could teach me about a thing I had already mastered so long ago. But perhaps there was some meager instruction I might learn from. If only to learn what not to do. That was why I was here.

Sol chirped from my head. I reached up and adjusted her position, tilting her slightly so that the morning wind would not find her face. She settled. "You will be on your best behaviour," I said. "I expect you to demonstrate the superior bearing of your station. If any of the lesser creatures attempt to approach you, you may hiss. Only once. We are above displays." Sol chirped in what was clearly agreement.

Ash let out a small breath next to me. It sounded like a laugh caught too late. I regarded her, but she was pointedly looking elsewhere.

The yard for this class was the largest I had seen yet. It stretched in all directions. At its center, someone had constructed what appeared to be a course of some kind. At least, that was the only word I had for it.

It was made of small obstacles and low tunnels that ended at a narrow plank, raised perhaps a foot off the ground, and finally a ribbon tied to a post at the far end. It looked like something built for children. The Inker stood in the middle of it. Almost a dozen students had gathered, each with their own strange beast.

The Inker was a broad man with a beard that had clearly not seen scissors in some time. The man was only at Sigil, judging by the branching yellow lines trailing up his left arm. Even through the Sigil, I could see that his forearm was far more scarred than marked.

On his shoulder perched a small brown creature. It looked, at first glance, like a hellrat of some kind. It had whiskers. It was grooming one of its paws. There was nothing to suggest it was anything more than a rodent, and I judged the man accordingly.

The man looked away from the course and regarded us. Then his eyes moved to Sol. He motioned to the small line in front of him. Ash and I looked at each other, before taking our place at its end. "You are the two Consecrated." He met my gaze, then Ash's. His gaze never went to my horns, though his eyes had widened, just slightly, when we first came in. Then he looked up at Sol. "And she is the bird. Hesta told me about her."

We said nothing. The man nodded, made one more sweep over all of the students. "'Lright. Don't look like there will be anyone else. My name is Inker Tarn, though I bet most of you know that already," he said, by way of introduction. The wooden device had said as much. "We got some new faces today, so I'll go over the basics. Just once."

He took us all in. "It's all very simple. A bond is a partnership. You feed each other. You learn each other. You fail together and you succeed together, and in time you don't know where one of you ends and the other begins. Not many people are lucky enough to have a bond, and boy do a lot of people try. If you have one, it's more important than your mark." The man paused."...don't tell any of the others I said that, you hear?"

Partnerships. That was the word he had used. I filed it away as the particular delusion of a man whose familiar was a rodent. He raised one finger toward me, and the mouse was suddenly no longer on his shoulder. It was on my arm.

I did not see it cross. I did not feel it land. The small creature was simply there, and its black eyes regarded me with interest. Sol hissed from atop my head.

"Minne," Tarn said mildly. The mouse returned to him. I did not see that either. This creature moved fast. "She shifts through spaces," he said. "Don't ask me how, I don't know either. She's a good girl." The mouse groomed her paw.

It was a good thing the rodent had left, or Sol would have turned it into her next meal.

Tarn gestured to the course. "This class is real easy. Each of you is gonna guide your familiar through the course and retrieve the ribbon at the end. Simple, right? I know some of you might think this is beneath you. Well, let's see you all prove it then. No carrying or nothing like that. If you do something, use the bond."

Every student around me seemed to accept this with varying degrees of fear, or something close to it. I accepted it as the lowest possible bar. Sol would retrieve the ribbon, or she would incinerate it. Either outcome would suffice.

"Well now, let's take turns. More 'an enough time for everyone. Look at the course first. I'll pick who goes when." At this, the line of students split. One of them -a boy so pale he looked a ghost- gave me a wary look. None of the others had so much as looked away from their beasts. None of these beasts reacted to Sol the way the ones in the Beast Hall had. Was that because of their proximity to their masters? It had to be.

The Inker stepped forward, and his gaze was only on Ash. "Well, lass? Why are you here?"

"Me?"

"Who else? Don't see nothing with you. The bird is hers, but you don't have nothin'. Why are you here?"

Ash hesitated. "I want to be here. Can I not watch?"

The man's fingers trailed over his beard. "She is my retainer," I cut in, my voice sharp. "If she wishes to watch, that is no concern of yours."

The Inker looked at me, then back at Ash. "Ah…it's like that, huh? These young ones these days," he muttered under his breath. We almost didn't hear him. "No skin off my back. Watch if ya want." Then, he turned away.

"I do not entirely disagree," I said in a low voice. "You do not have to be here. I am perfectly capable of attending this myself." My legs did not ache at all, so long as I stood still. Even the Ember Core seemed more agreeable today.

"I want to be here," Ash said.

I looked at her. Her jaw was set, and I knew by now what that meant. She would not budge, no matter how I prodded. "Very well," I said. "In exchange for your service…I will observe a class of your choosing, next."

Ash blinked. "You will what?"

"There must be something in this Academy that is for you, but not for me. I will watch, and I will pay attention."

"Lys, you don't have t-"

"It is an honor. Would you spurn it?" My red eyes bored into her blue ones. "Besides…I am curious about what you'll pick," I said, this time in a much lower voice.

I almost thought Ash would disagree. That I would have to press her more firmly. "Okay, I'll think-"

"Will ya two lovebirds join us?!" The Inker's voice was very loud when he tried.

A couple of the students did snicker this time.

All of the students took this trial one at a time. The first to take it was a girl who had a creature the Inker referred to as a beetle. If this was a beetle, then it was unlike any of the other beetles I had seen in this world. This one was far larger, almost as large as some of the small dogs I had seen in Hamel. And then there was the strange coloring of its shell. A mix of green and bronze. Strange that two things could carry the same name and yet be so far apart.

The girl knelt and spoke to it in a soft voice I could not quite hear. The beetle clicked its mandibles twice. Then it began to move.

The strange creature had little speed. All it could manage was to slowly scrape itself over the ground with what appeared to be great effort. The girl walked beside it, and she walked slowly so as to match its pace. Eventually, they reached the tunnel. The beetle stopped at its entrance. The girl kneeled down and whispered something to the creature. The beetle's mandibles clicked again, and then it crawled through. It retrieved the ribbon some time later, and the girl cradled it in her hands. The entire endeavor had taken four minutes.

I approved of their effort. Here was someone who understood the proper treatment of a bonded creature.

Master Tarn nodded once. "Nice work, the both of you."

The girl smiled at him, moved to the back of the waiting line. The next boy had a turtle-like creature.

The beast was barely larger than Solara herself. Green vines covered the ridges of its shell, blooming faintly as the creature moved. They somewhat resembled the fountain I had once seen in Koralis itself. The boy walked beside the tortoise at the tortoise's pace, which was very slow indeed.

The tortoise eventually reached the first obstacle. I saw no way for the beast to make it past. Not unless it grew legs twice as long, or perhaps wings. I watched. The tortoise just lay there. The boy stepped forward and extended his hand. The tortoise climbed on. The boy walked over to the far side and set it down. Through all of this, the Inker said nothing. Their trial continued.

"He's carrying the thing himself," someone grumbled.

"That shouldn't count!" another voice said.

They were rather petty grievances. I found myself agreeing with them anyway. If you could do that, then this would be rather simple indeed.

"He's giving assistance through the bond," Tarn called. "The boy knows the difference. Eyes on your own familiars and wait your turns." That made some of the murmurs stop. At least those loud enough for the Inker to overhear.

Eventually the creature reached the tunnel. It stopped outside. Was it refusing to enter? The boy waited and after perhaps thirty seconds, the tortoise seemingly changed its mind on its own terms and entered the tunnel. Start to finish, the retrieval of the ribbon took ten minutes.

The boy walked back beside it. When they reached the starting mark, the tortoise stopped, and the boy set a hand briefly on its shell. The green shoots pulsed faintly.

"Well done, Pell. And well done, Bertt." I believed Pell was the name of the boy, while Bertt was the name of the turtle.

The other students cycled through after that. A nervous girl whose small ape mimicked her trembling hands. A boy with some manner of shimmering crystalline cat. A girl with a floating thing I did not understand. It looked like something made out of light, and yet it was no construct, I was sure. A few others whose familiars I did not bother to examine.

Then my turn came. "Good luck," Ash said. I did not need luck. I did not say it, but I made sure my expression did.

I stepped forward, lifted Sol from my brow, and set her down. Sol did not move. She remained where I had set her. "Come on, this task is well beneath you. You should do it anyway."

Sol looked at me, then at the course, then at me again. She did not move. She let out a small chirp. Even the chirp sounded bored.

"Acquire the ribbon," I said, and pointed. Perhaps she could simply not see the prize from where I had set her. Sol groomed a wing. "Sol."

She chirped. It was a dismissive sound. She continued grooming. Someone snickered. It came from somewhere behind me. I did not turn.

I tried again, cupping her and raising her so she might focus on the ribbon. "That is your target. Look at it."

Sol did not look at it. She yawned as she met my gaze. The faint glow of flame at the back of her throat made the girl nearest me take half a step back, but Sol did not move otherwise. She settled more firmly on my cupped hands, tucking all four wings against her sides.

"Having some trouble?" the Inker asked. He had stepped in next to me.

"This prize of yours is meaningless. It is no prize at all," I said. "It is beneath her to-"

Tarn reached into a small pouch at his belt, and produced a strip of something that might have been meat, if it wasn't so pale. He held it up to her. Was the fool really trying to feed her something that wasn't bathed in the Cr-

Solara looked at the meat, and then at the man. Her wings flared once, and she hopped from my cupped hands and ate the offering in two large bites. She stayed there. In his hands. I stood in the middle of the yard with my hands still raised, holding nothing.

"She's a friendly one. Beautiful too. Never seen anything quite like her. Would you like her back?" Tarn said. There was no mockery in his voice. At least, none that I could find.

What manner of trick was this? I did not know. I only knew that I wanted her back. Slowly, I nodded my head. Even that felt stiff.

The Inker regarded me and held his arm out. "Go on," he murmured. "She wants you to come back."

Solara turned and looked at me. She let out a small chirp, flared her wings, and crossed the distance in one hop. She landed in my hands and sat herself down easily. The phoenix began to groom one of her black wings, as if she had no awareness whatsoever of what had just been done to my dignity.

The Inker looked at me, and then glanced around. At some of the other students, perhaps. Many of whom were looking in our direction. He stepped forward and kept his voice low. "Lass, that bird of yours ain't a soldier. She's a friend. You ask a friend. You don't point them at things."

"She is my familiar," I said. There was no heat in my voice. There should have been.

The Inker slowly nodded. "Aye she is. And familiars are friends. They choose to help. They're not yours to swing at things. You have to ask 'em to do things."

I stared down at Solara, still grooming her wing. The baby phoenix was small, small enough that she weighed nothing at all. I had not swung her at anything. I had not demanded she do what I wished. I had asked her…hadn't I? The baby phoenix paused in her grooming and looked up at me with her big golden eyes. Perhaps I was wrong. I did not know how to ask Sol for anything. I had only ever known how to demand.

Then I heard a whisper, aimed at me. "Maybe she bonded to the wrong one," a voice said, deliberately loud enough to carry. "Came as an egg, right? Sometimes they hatch wrong. Seen all kinds of weird rejects. Guess we have another one now." Sol did not even turn her head. She was busy examining a speck on her wing.

I turned. It was that pale boy who had spoken, the one who had eyed me when I had first come in. He looked around, as if to see if someone else would say anything. Nobody did. Then, loudly, he said, "Lena did say the beast was supposed to be special. Now I think I know what she meant!"

Lena. That same name again. The girl who had dared to touch Solara's satchel in the mess hall, and whose two sycophants had found me in that accursed desert. Now I was hearing her name from this boy. Just how many servants did that girl have in this place?

I saw Ash's face flush. She reached for a hilt that was not there. My right hand closed at my side. Small wisps of black fire trailed from my right hand and I let them. The boy was not far. One step, and he would be in range for my Ruin. I knew exactly how much of him would remain after. Nothing. I would take even his boots.

Sol chirped softly, now grooming a third wing. She had not heard the boy's insult. Or she had heard it and judged it beneath her. The chirp was simply a chirp. A comment on her wing, perhaps.

I let go of the Requiem. I unclenched my hand. Ash met my gaze. I nodded, and she settled. The Inker stood at the side, watching this all. He had not said a thing, though surely he must have heard. Nobody else had.

I did not think it was this boy's power that silenced words. It was his master, this 'Lena'. She had not seemed so impressive when I had seen her with my own eyes.

Sol did not care. Therefore, I did not care.

I leaned down next to her, ignoring the phantom pain from my legs. I had not tried this before, not properly. There was a bond between us. A tenuous thing so faint I might have dismissed it if I hadn't been looking for it. I had thought of it as a tether between Sol and me, as all bonds had been, almost a thousand years ago. What if they weren't? What if I was simply wrong? No…I had felt something from Sol before, hadn't I? I was wrong.

I reached for the bond. It had always been there, from the moment Sol had hatched and first met my eyes. A faint tugging, somewhere in the corner of my mind. I reached for it now, properly, for the first time. It was deeper and richer than I had expected. With it came feelings. They all came through the bond at once, and they nearly knocked me over. A current running both ways.

The first thing I felt was boredom. Solara was bored. She was bored with the course in front of her. With this entire morning's proceedings in general. She had taken one look at this class and decided that all of it was thoroughly beneath her.

The next thing to come was her disdain, directed at each of the beasts we had seen. The beetle's size was nothing, because the creature itself was so ponderously slow. The tortoise was small, and while it and Sol were almost the same size, the phoenix was sure the thing could not breathe fire like she could. Then the strange crystalline cat, which was, in Sol's assessment, a fraud. None of the creatures she had seen were worth her attention.

And past the boredom and the disdain, there was a third thing. Far stronger than either. I stopped the moment I felt it. Solara looked at me with her big golden eyes, and what she felt whenever she looked at me was warmth. The same warmth I had felt from her shell on many a night. The same warmth I felt whenever she sat upon my head and looked out at the world. It was Sol, recognizing me, and finding me good. And I understood well what this feeling was. And recognizing it made my hands shake.

I am a fool. I'm the Eleventh. She was not my subject and had never been. She was a small creature who…loved me.

This bond I now shared with her was little like the bonds of the old world. Those bonds had only ever gone in one direction. From the master to the familiar. They were chains, of a kind. That was how it had been with Echidna -not by my wanting, but it had been so. This thing I reached for now was not that. It had never occurred to me that things could be so different.

I took a deep breath and opened my eyes. The Inker was watching me. "Now ask her right this time," he said quietly. "Go on."

I looked from him to Ash. The Hero had a small smile. Her eyes were warm. She gave me the faintest of nods. I turned my attention back to Solara, who was staring up at me. She tilted her head to the right in one jerky motion. I knew what she felt. She was curious. I had touched the bond, and she had felt my touch. I did just as the Inker had said and I asked rather than demanded. Would you try the course? Do you want to?

I was not entirely sure how this was supposed to work. So, I simply offered the image of the course, and the ribbon. If she did not want to do this course, then I would accept her answer. Sol considered it. Through the bond, I felt her weigh the matter. The course was still beneath her. The ribbon was still a ribbon. But I was asking.

Solara let out a loud chirp and hopped off my arm. She turned around and faced the first obstacle. It was a jump, perhaps a foot and a half high. Solara chirped again and flared her four wings. Then she launched herself into the air.

She flew. Her wings beat in rhythm and she crossed half the distance. Then she fell, striking the low wooden beam face-first. She landed on the ground far less gracefully than she had flown from it.

There was laughter. It was from the pale boy again. He laughed, and this time, others joined him. I heard at least three voices. And more comments, buried under all of the laughter. Solara was already rising and turning toward the obstacle again.

I took a step towards Solara. Ash reached her first. She gently gathered the phoenix up in her arms, turned, and offered her to me. I took Sol from her. "Thank you," I said.

"You can do this," Ash whispered, half to me, and half to Sol. Sol let out a small chirp. "You still want me to just ignore him? And the others?"

I wondered what she would do if I said no. "Yes," I said instead. "They are beneath Sol's notice." I searched the bond, and knew it to be true. If it had been anything else….

I paused. Solara was trembling in my hands. She was trembling with frustration. Frustration at the accursed wooden beam that had dared to strike at her. Frustrated with this foolish course. Most of all, she was frustrated with herself. She let out an indignant chirp of tremendous volume, aimed at the whole of the course. I knelt and brushed dirt from her feathers with my thumb.

"Do you wish to continue?" I asked her. Sol chirped. Through the bond, the answer was clear. She would try, and try again. "Very well. It seems we will be here for some time," I said. I set her back down on the ground.

Sol's four wings beat in rhythm, and then she launched herself upwards again.
