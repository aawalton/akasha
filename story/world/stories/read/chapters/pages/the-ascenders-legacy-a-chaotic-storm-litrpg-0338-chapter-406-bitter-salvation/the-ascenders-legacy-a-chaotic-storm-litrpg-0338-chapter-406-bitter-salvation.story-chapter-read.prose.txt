A whistling wind teased Yelena awake, its caress gentle against her skin, its song soft. She opened her eyes slowly, blinking as an endless expanse of blue filled her vision.

The sky was clear. Almost too clear.

Yet that struck her as far less strange than how close it was. How terrifying close.

Brows furrowing, she turned her head, obsidian-black eyes searching for the horizon, only to find it gleaming above the clouds.

Above the—

Her eyes flew wide. Her hands shot out instinctively, grasping for something—anything—to hold on to.

There was nothing.

Her fingers swept through cotton-like vapor, and the cloud that had supported her for heavens knew how long suddenly dissolved beneath her. The world lurched. Cool droplets splashed across her face. And before she could so much as gasp, she was falling to her death.

"Helpppp!"

The scream tore its way free from her throat, agonizingly raw against—

"You're not falling."

The words cut cleanly through her scream, rolling across the heavens like distant thunder. And for a brief, infinitesimal heartbeat, the world stilled. The rush of wind vanished as suddenly as it had begun, and she found herself suspended in empty air, her body no longer plummeting but drifting as lightly as a feather.

Yelena had barely managed to reorient herself before reality suddenly shifted, the sky itself tilting until she was no longer hanging upside down, but standing atop the air itself.

The same way cultivators did.

She grimaced.

There was no doubt about it. She had been taken hostage by some deranged cultivator.

Cursing under her breath, she swept her gaze across the endless sky and the distant earth below, searching for whoever had done this to her. When she found no one, her grimace deepened into a scowl.

"Whoever you are, let go of me now."

"If I do, you'll fall to your death." The voice answered again, this time carrying a note of amusement.

Yelena didn't flinch.

"Better to die than to be held aloft by the will of a monster."

"I am not a monster."

"Yet you keep me suspended in the sky against my will, dangling me hundreds of feet above the earth, so the moment you let go, I'll burst upon the ground in a smear of black and crimson." Her lips pulled into a sneer. "I assure you, cultivator, I do not fear death. If you expect me to beg for mercy, I'm afraid you'll be waiting a very long time."

There was a pause before the voice responded this time. When it did, it didn't boom across the heavens like it had done earlier. Instead, it came from directly behind her—calm, warm, yet unmistakably authoritative.

"That's quite the imagination you have there, young lady."

Yelena turned around slowly, a cold defiance gleaming in her obsidian eyes as she came face to face with her captor. She spared his incredible looks no moment of appreciation, nor did she care for the aura of immovability radiating from him. It didn't matter how otherworldly he looked. How beautiful.

Every time she saw a cultivator, her mother's murderer was who she truly saw, and he was the ugliest thing that had ever existed.

"Cultivator," she said, spitting out the word as though it were poison. "What is the meaning of this? Why am I here?"

The cultivator regarded her for a moment before smiling gently, his silver mane drifting in an invisible breeze. "Whatever preconceived notions you have of cultivators, Yelena, I promise I'm not a monster."

"You are all monsters," Yelena replied without hesitation, her voice growing even colder. "Without exception. Every time I try to think you're not, one of you comes to prove me wrong. Just like you are now, dangling me in the air so I'm at your mercy."

"Or perhaps, it is so I can show you something." The cultivator countered with a sad smile. "You really do hate us, do you not?"

Yelena held the man's gaze a moment, a fiery anger rising to pull her lips into a sneer. She lifted her gaze to him defiantly.

"You know nothing about me, cultivator."

"On the contrary, it is you who doesn't know anything about me." The man smiled. "I have learned more about you in the last few minutes than you perhaps know of yourself. For instance, I know that you're currently on the verge of death, having had your soul torn in half by a particular soul cultivator."

Yelena frowned. "Wh… what?"

And then her expression faltered as the man's words triggered a series of memories, each one flashing across her mind like images from a bright screen.

A calamity holding two other cultivators hostage. Violet runic irises turning towards her with cold malice, and then—

She shuddered, feeling an agonizing stab of pain before the memory went black. Her hands rushed up to hug her chest, expecting to find it ripped apart. A relieved breath escaped her when she found it whole, and she immediately looked up at the strange cultivator.

"Did you heal me?"

"I'm currently healing you," the man said, smiling. "You're not in perfect condition yet."

"Then why am I dangling so dangerously in the air?" she frowned, glaring at the man. "Is this part of the healing process? I doubt my father will approve of this."

The man's smile widened. But rather than explain like any normal person should have, he gestured to the earth below, and reality shifted once again.

The lush earth bled away from existence like silk ripped off a wooden table, and in its place rose a vast mountainous region. Above her, the clouds gathered and darkened, hiding the sun from view. Thunder boomed loudly across the heavens. Lightning struck the mountains in a blinding lattice of white. And an instant later, the sky broke with a torrent of rain, none of which touched her.

Yelena took in the display with a scowl. So he was also a storm cultivator? How could her father have handed her over to them after what they'd done to Alcatraz. Had her injury truly been that severe?

She glanced at the man, unable to hide the unease on her face.

"So what are we doing up here? What have you come to show me?"

The man pointed down, and Yelena followed his gaze to the earth below, only to find the formerly barren mountain range now populated with thousands of people, each dressed in a familiar style of robes.

Storm cultivator robes.

The sight of so many cultivators filled her with disgust, and she very nearly recoiled from it. The memory of her mother's murderer flashed across her mind, and she took a second to steady herself.

Then another. Before finally turning to glare at the man beside her, hate etched onto her face.

"Take me home now," she whispered, but her voice was ice cold. "I think I'm healed enough. I want to be away from this place."

The man said nothing, his expression growing even more sympathetic.

It made Yelena even more uneasy, and she snapped. "Take me home. Now! I'm healed enough."

The man shook his head. "You seem to have forgotten, child, that you are on the verge of death. Not were. You're not healed yet."

"If I can stand dangling above the clouds, then I'm healed enough to return to my home and the devastation you and your people left in it." Uneasiness turned into anger, and she scowled. "Do you know how many of my people they killed? How many properties they destroyed just to get some worthless treasure?"

She shook her head. "I cannot fathom what my father was thinking to give me to you to heal. Take me back now. I need to help him."

The man regarded her for an infuriatingly long moment before slowly shaking his head.

"What is it?" Yelena snapped, growing impatient. "Why do you keep shaking your head? I'm fine! Am I not fine?"

Rather than respond, the man pointed again, this time to an area in the upper realms of the mountainous region. After a moment of hesitation, Yelena let herself look, anger churning within her.

For several heartbeats, she saw nothing. Then slowly, small movements appeared as figures emerged from the clouds, descending with impossible grace. They were cultivators. Eight of them. Seven above and—

She frowned in confusion, glancing at the man beside her before looking back at the eighth figure below the other seven.

They were the same. The same face. The same silver mane. The same radiant eyes. The same impossible sense of weight.

Her brows furrowed further. "Is this some kind of test? That… that is you, isn't it? How are you in two places at once?"

The man said nothing, and with a growl born from a thin thread of patience, Yelena turned her attention back to the eighth figure, as he and the others descended into a vast clearing filled with thousands upon thousands of cultivators.

It was a sickening sight. But if this was the test she had to pass to return home to her father, then she would do it. Not gladly, though.

Scowling, she tried to make sense of what she was seeing. But she knew nothing of the ways of cultivators, or why they were gathered, or why none of them were noticing her standing right here, floating in midair.

If she didn't hate them all so much, she would have shouted to one of them for help.

And so she watched cluelessly as one of the eight figures began speaking, addressing the thousands of cultivators below. It wasn't until the eighth figure cast off his helm and began levitating upward that Yelena finally began to suspect what she was watching.

When an ocean of blinding rune light bound the earth and heaven in a single colossal circuit, and a staircase made from nothing her mind could identify descended, she finally confirmed it, her eyes widening in shock as she realized what she was witnessing.

An ascension.

Her pupils contracted.

No... Not just any ascension. The Ascension of the seventh-step storm ascendant. Hadn't it taken place about a month ago? Was this another?

But if it was, how was he standing…

She glanced at the cultivator beside her, a sinking feeling curling in her gut. The man said nothing, seemingly expecting her to come to a realization without his help. But the realization she was coming to wasn't one she liked at all.

Swallowing back her fear, she took a leap of defiance and asked.

"You are him, aren't you? Edrick Ghaltris. The man we're watching right now."

The Ascendant nodded once. "I am."

Yelena felt her heart stutter, fear tightening in her chest. "How are you here then? How am I standing beside you—why am I standing—"

The answer to her questions struck her like a bolt of lightning, connecting every piece together in an instant.

His words about her still being on the verge of death. His ascension. The mysterious treasure the storm sect had come to find, and lastly… the fact that she was here, standing in an event that had happened nearly a month ago.

Her eyes widened, despair flashing across her face.

"No…"

"Yes, Yelena." Edrick finally spoke. "I am not here. You've not gone back in time." He gestured to the world around them. "This is not reality."

"No…" Yelena shook her head violently, refusing to believe it. Believing meant her worst fears had finally come true. After she'd scaled the age of sixteen without awakening, she had thought herself free.

But if this was true…

"No," she shook her head again, glaring up at the ascendant. "This is a nightmare. I'm having a lucid nightmare." Grabbing hold of her cheeks, she pinched them hard. Then harder. "Wake up, Yelena. Wake up."

All while she did this, Edrick Ghaltris said nothing—simply watching and waiting for her to accept her new reality.

It didn't take long.

And when it did, Yelena's expression contorted from fear to horror—an emotion that hardened almost instantly into rage.

"No!" she screamed, ripping her hands wildly through her hair. "I don't want it. I don't accept it." She turned to him immediately, a blazing fury in her eyes as she spat. "Return me to my dying body, now. I don't need your legacy. I don't want to be a cultivator. I refuse it."

Edrick smiled sympathetically. "I'm sorry, child. But fate is—"

"I don't give a bloody rat's ass about fate." Yelena nearly screeched. "Where was it when my mother died in front of me by the hand of cultivators? Where was it when thousands of others experienced the same fate? And now," she sneered, "to choose me to become the very thing I hate—"

"It is cruel," Edrick said.

The words gave Yelena a brief pause before she surged towards him once more, hope lighting up her gaze.

"Yes, it is." She said, her gaze pleading. "You have to save me from it. Reject me, please."

Edrick gave her another sad smile—and something about that smile, about the sheer certainty of it—set Yelena's entire world aflame. She grabbed him by his robes immediately, defiance and rage returning in equal measure.

"Why won't you do it?" she spat. "I hate you. I hate your kind. All of you disgust me. Reject me if you know what's good for you."

But even with her face in his, the Ascendant remained unfazed. His sympathetic smile never left his face.

"I'm sorry," he replied quietly. "But the awakening is too far gone to stop now. The only people who can affect it are those in reality. You are stuck with this fate."

"You're lying, you fucking monster." She snarled, her tone venomous. She gripped his robes tighter. "You're an Ascendant. I don't care what you have to do or how you have to do it. Just get me out of here now." Her voice broke halfway through, and tears pooled in her eyes.

"Please. I'll get on my knees and beg if that's what I need to. I swear any oath. I'll even change. I'll stop hating you and your people if you just save me from this fate. If you just…"

A sob tore itself free from her throat as she clutched the Ascendant's robes tighter, knowing from his expression that there was no escaping this fate.

Yelena cried, and raged. She slammed her fists into his chest and tried to rip his flesh with her nails. She spat at him, and called him every bad word she could think of.

But none of it changed her fate. None of it changed what she was becoming.

And in the end she simply sobbed, her shoulders shuddering violently as pain and disgust churned within her. Disgust for what she had become. For the future that now lay before her.

And before long, a third emotion rose. Hatred—now sharpened to a knife edge.

Those bloody cultivators had not been contented with taking her mother's life and destroying her city. They had taken her future too. Taken her sense of worth.

And when she closed her eyes, she saw the face of the one who had done it. Frost white hair gleaming in the sunlight. Golden lines arcing across dark skin.

He had been the one to save her from the soul calamity's attack. And most likely the one to implant her with the blasted seed.

"If he was the one, then he did it to save you," Edrick whispered, but Yelena didn't care. Not now, not with so much hatred churning within her.

She glared up at him. "This power you have refused to take away is a mistake. I will use it against you. I will forge it into a weapon and use it against you if you do not do something about it now."

Edrick Ghaltris stared at her for a long moment, then slowly whispered.

"Come. I have something to show you."

"Nothing you show me will change my mind," Yelena spat. "Nothing you tell me will absolve my hatred."

"Maybe not," Edrick grimaced. "But it might give you perspective."

He raised a hand, and reality shifted again. The Storm Sect vanished in a blur of light, and in its place, a new scene unfolded. A scene so familiar it barely took her a second to recognize it.

Alcatraz.

Only this time, it wasn't being leveled by storm cultivators. It burned, every single building engulfed in crimson flames.

It was the night her mother died.
