Jane made her way home slowly, her mind churning with everything that had happened. The letter from Felicity Cast sat in her pocket like a small weight, pulling at her attention even as she tried to focus on the familiar streets passing by.

Her bakery was quiet when she arrived. She locked the door behind her and stood for a moment in the empty shop, listening to the silence.

What do I do?

The question felt bigger than it should have been. She needed advice on it, somehow, and she knew just who to ask.

She climbed the stairs to her living quarters and went to the corner of her room where a circle had been scratched into the wooden floor. It was a permanent fixture now, etched deep enough that no amount of sweeping or scrubbing would erase it. She had drawn it months ago, back when she first moved to Glenfall and was still figuring out how to stay in touch with her aunt as she traipsed here, there and everywhere around the continent.

She hadn't used it in a while. Her aunt traveled constantly, moving from crisis to crisis across the kingdom and beyond, and catching her at a convenient moment was always a challenge. But if there was ever a time when Jane needed her perspective, it was now.

She knelt at the edge of the circle and pressed her palm flat against the wood. Magic flowed out of her, filling the etched lines with soft light. The circle hummed to life, reaching out across the miles to find its partner, wherever her aunt might be.

Then she waited.

The waiting was always the hardest part. The circle would stay active as long as Jane kept feeding it power, but there was no guarantee her aunt would be able to answer. She might be in the middle of something dangerous, or asleep, or simply too far away for the connection to reach properly. All Jane could do was sit and hope.

She settled into a more comfortable position, crossing her legs beneath her and letting her eyes drift closed. Time passed. Jane let her mind wander, thinking about the letter in her pocket.

Why did you write it? Why did you stop writing? Do you still bake? Did you ever imagine someone like me would use your book?

The circle flickered, and Jane's eyes snapped open.

"Jane? Are you there?"

Her aunt's voice came through clearly, though it sounded slightly strained. Jane leaned forward, focusing her magic to strengthen the connection.

"I'm here. Are you all right?"

"Fine, fine. I heard you calling right away, but I was up to my ears in snow gear and it took me forever to get it all off." There was a rustling sound, like heavy fabric being tossed across a room. "I'm in the cold country. The far north. There's a situation with some frozen something-or-other that I won't bore you with the details of."

"The cold country?" Jane tried to imagine her aunt tumbling through snowdrifts. The image didn’t fit, somehow. "That sounds miserable."

"It's not my favorite." A pause. "But never mind that. You called for a reason. What's going on?"

Jane took a breath, trying to organize her thoughts. There was a lot to cover.

"I heard you cursed out the king," she said finally.

The silence that followed was almost offended.

"I did not curse." Her aunt's voice was filled with mock offense. "I used very stern language. There's a difference. Cursing would have been undignified."

"Whatever you want to call it, it worked." Jane smiled. "He apologized to me this morning. Said he's not going to push the thing with Brand anymore."

"Good. He needed to hear it from someone who wasn't afraid of him." Her aunt's tone softened slightly. "I'm glad it helped, dear. I was worried I might have made things worse."

"You didn't. He also said he owes me a favor now."

"Did he?" Her aunt sounded genuinely interested. "That's nice of him. And it's a very real thing, Jane. A favor from a king isn't just words. If he said it, he meant it, and you should use it carefully. Not never, either. It’s a useful resource. It shouldn’t sit forever."

Jane filed that away for later consideration. She wasn't sure what she would ever need from a king, but it was good to know the offer was genuine.

"That's not why I called, though."

"I didn't think so. What is it?"

Jane pulled the letter from her pocket, even though her aunt couldn't see it. The paper was already starting to crease from being folded and refolded.

"Do you remember when I first started learning to bake? The was a cookbook I used. It was the one that actually taught me how to do things properly. It was scientific, methodical. Everything was explained in terms of ratios and reactions. I don’t think I could have done it without it. It was like it was made exactly for me.”

"I don’t recall, actually. But I know just what you mean. I’ve had spellbooks that were that way. Little treatises that saved me weeks of effort.”

"That's why I'm calling." Jane smoothed out the letter on her knee. "I never knew much about the author. The book was old, and there wasn't much information about her. But Emily, the librarian here, did some research. She found the woman who wrote it."

"Found her?"

"She's alive. She's been living in a small town a few days from here." Jane heard her own voice shake slightly. "She wants to meet me."

The silence that followed was different from before. Jane could almost feel her aunt thinking, processing the information and considering its implications.

"Well," her aunt said finally. "That's wonderful news."

"I know. I just don't know what to do about it."

"What do you mean, what to do? You go, Jane."

"Just like that?"

"Just like that. Tomorrow, if you can manage it."

Jane blinked at the circle, surprised by her aunt's certainty. "So soon? I have the shop to think about, and the showcases just ended."

"Jane." Her aunt's voice cut through the excuses. "Listen to me. I've been doing this job for a very long time, and if there's one thing I've learned, it's that you can't wait. Waiting means wasted opportunities.”

"But…"

"This woman wrote a book that changed your life. She's alive, she's willing to meet you, and she's close enough that you could be there in a few days. Do you know how rare that is? It’s a gift."

Jane didn't answer. She was thinking about all the authors she had read over the years, all the people whose words had shaped her understanding of magic when she was at the academy or whose stories had taught her about normal life when she still wasn’t living it. Most of them were long dead. She had never even considered the possibility of meeting any of them.

"Sometimes meeting your heroes goes differently than you expect," her aunt continued. "I won't lie to you about that. But that's not a reason to avoid it."

"And if it's disappointing?"

"Then it’s disappointing. But it’s better than regret at not knowing. Believe that."

Jane looked down at the letter in her hands.

"There's something else," her aunt said.

"What?"

"People don't live forever." The words hung in the air. "This woman doesn’t sound young. If you keep putting it off until the timing is better, you might miss your chance entirely."

Jane let that sink in. She hadn't considered that the woman might not have unlimited time left.

"I'm not saying this to frighten you," her aunt added. "I'm saying it because I've made that mistake before. I've waited for the right moment and missed it before.."

"What happened?"

"It doesn't matter now. What matters is that you don't make the same mistake. Go tomorrow, Jane. Build a habit of taking these opportunities when they come. It avoids regrets."

Jane knew she was right. It was what she had needed to hear, and her aunt had told her. Her uncertainty resolved in an instant.

"All right," she said finally. "I'll go."

"Good." Her aunt sounded satisfied. "Do you need help arranging things? There’s precious little I can do from here, but I can at least send word.”

"No, I can manage. I have people here who can help." Jane thought about Bella, about Allen, about all the friends she had made since moving to Glenfall. "I won't be alone in this."

"Even better." There was warmth in her aunt's voice now, pride mixed with something softer. "You've built a good life there, Jane. I'm proud of you."

"Thank you for always being there when I need advice."

"That's what aunts are for. Now, if you'll excuse me, I need to get back to my frozen situation before it thaws in an inconvenient direction. Call me when you get back from your trip. I want to hear how it goes."

"I will."

The circle dimmed as her aunt ended the connection, and then Jane was alone.

Go tomorrow.

—

Jane stayed up late making arrangements, writing notes to customers who might come by while she was gone, and preparing a sign for her door. By the time she finally fell asleep, she had everything planned out as well as she could manage on short notice and with all shops closed.

The breakfast rush was already in full swing when she arrived at Bella’s shop the next morning. The stall was packed with customers, the line stretching out the door and halfway down the street. Jane spotted her friend behind the counter. The first-place certificate from the showcase was already hanging on the wall behind her, and Jane noticed several customers pointing it out. It was clear where most of the new business had come from.

Jane waited until there was a brief lull in the crowd before catching Bella's eye.

"Jane!" Bella wiped her hands on her apron and stepped to the side of the counter where they could talk without blocking the flow of customers. "I’m sorry, but if you want breakfast, you have to get in line with everyone else. I’ll have a riot otherwise.”

"It’s not that. I stopped by to tell you I'm going on a trip," Jane said. "The author of that cookbook I told you about. I'm going to meet her."

Bella's eyebrows rose. "Already? Emily was just trying to find you yesterday to tell about that. That was a fast decision."

"My aunt convinced me not to wait." Jane glanced at the line of customers still waiting to be served. She needed to make this quick, or that riot just might happen. "I wanted to let you know before I left. I'll be gone for a few days at least."

"Do you need me to come with you?" Bella asked as if it wouldn’t bother her, Jane could see the conflict in her expression. The shop was busier than Jane had ever seen it, and pulling Bella away now would be cruel to everyone involved.

"No," Jane said firmly. "I wouldn't feel good about taking you away from this. Look at your line. You'd lose half your new customers if you disappeared right after winning. I’m fine, anyway. I think I have the hang of travel, now, and there’s no jobs on the other side I need help with. Just a visit."

Bella's relief was visible. "I'm glad to hear it. I was going to offer anyway, but honestly, I have no idea how I'd manage. It's been like this since I opened. I think I'm going to need to hire help."

"That's a good problem to have."

"It is. Are you going to walk? It's a few days' travel, right?"

"No." Jane shook her head emphatically. "I've learned my lesson about that. I'll find a carriage. It’s faster, and my feet will thank me."

"Smart. Who are you going to hire? Dave and Brand will be gone by now."

"I'll ask around." Jane stepped back as customers pressed toward the counter. "I should let you get back to work. I just wanted to say goodbye before I left."

"Be careful out there." Bella reached out and squeezed her hand briefly. "And come back with good stories."

"I will."

Jane made her way back out into the street, leaving Bella to her newly founded breakfast empire. The morning was still young, and she had one more thing to do before she could leave.
