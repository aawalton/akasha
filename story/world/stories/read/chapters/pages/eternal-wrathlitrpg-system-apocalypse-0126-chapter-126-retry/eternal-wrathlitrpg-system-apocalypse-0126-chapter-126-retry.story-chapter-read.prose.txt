James ordered that his cape—the one he got from clearing the first trial dungeon—be sent to the dungeon posthaste. Despite the unreasonable schedule, Ryan agreed and sent the Cloak of Dark Night over by express. It had proved crucial before, when James was about to invade a castle under similar moonlight.

“You guys ready?” Andrew stood at the entrance of the dungeon the very next dawn.

James yawned. It took only several days of living inside a dungeon for his circadian rhythm to disobey things like schedules.

Luckily, none of the group bailed out. Three ex-soldiers even offered to join, padding the number back up to thirty-five.

James and the group were then transported inside the dungeon. However, instead of grasslands, they appeared in a desert. The arid sand filled their lungs as forty-five civilians bowed before James and Andrew.

“Savior! Please save us from the tide! We shall do anything!” After the pleading civilians came the notice from the system.

[Survive for 5 days with 10 casualties maximum.]

[Time until the first wave: 4 hours. A standard supply of 500 logs, 500 nails, 50 meters of rope, and 5 types of tools—including 5 hammers, 5 saws, 5 hoes, 5 pickaxes, and 5 shovels—will be provided. Shops are currently unavailable.]

The notice was exactly the same as the one James had seen before. However, the area he was in now was different. Instead of a grass plain, he was in a desert.

“Shit! We’re in a different place!” the soldiers began to panic.

“Don’t worry, everyone. Some changes are normal. Let’s work together as planned. Civilians, go and make walls. We’re aiming for thirty meters wide this time. Once the forest opens, James will grab some logs, and we can make a roof,” Andrew said. His natural charisma had won over the team, including James.

“Make more space here. This will be the north gate. That will be the south gate, east gate, and west gate,” Andrew explained. With the lack of logs, the team could only make impromptu corner walls for their base. With over twenty meters of gap where the gate was placed, James wondered whether Andrew was being too greedy with the land grab.

“The wall’s not doing anything. Why make them?” James frowned.

“Yes, they’re just wooden walls. A single weapon skill is enough to smash them. That’s why I decided to make a funnel later, once we have enough logs. We’ll also need more logs for the roof, just in case the moon reappears,” Andrew said as he explained his strategy. Dungeon monsters were not very smart. They could be funneled, packed into one side with clever tactics.

But before he could finish, the earth trembled. A stone portal rose from the depths of the ground. From within the portal, monsters began to emerge. Black claws protruded from their fingers. They stood tall on their hind legs, ready to pounce, barking like rabid dogs. Their razor-sharp teeth gleamed, ready to tear flesh.

“Shit! The monsters are different this time!” The men from Xiaoming’s and Bernard’s divisions faltered. James frowned at the humanoid dogs. His instincts were already analyzing their movements.

They bore no weapons, which meant they shouldn’t have weapon skills. But that didn’t mean they were weak. From James’s experience, monsters without skills often had either higher stats or special abilities compared to those that used weapon skills. He hoped it was the latter.

James was not the type to sit idly by while enemies approached. He dashed forward, activating his Parallel Thought, and a flurry of spells gushed forth at the horde. Once again, his mind magic proved invaluable against living enemies. The monsters broke their charge and clawed at their allies when his spells struck them.

“Ready your skills! Charge!” Andrew led the attack.

[Congratulations. You have cleared the first wave. Casualties: 0. The next wave will begin in 6 hours. You and the rest of the survivors have obtained 500 coins. The forest has been unlocked.]

The battle went on without a hitch. A translucent pink barrier in the distance shattered, revealing a patch of verdant green amid the scorching desert.

“James, I’ll leave it to you. Ah, bring Sanders with you—he can carry the logs back with your raptors while you continue logging. Ah, wait a minute. Please leave about fifteen meters of this area inside the forest intact. We might need it later,” Andrew said.

“We should’ve built the settlement there in the first place,” James muttered. Andrew stared at him for a moment before nodding.

“Good idea. Let’s move.” Andrew rallied the citizens toward the forest. But as he stepped forward, a purple barrier blocked his path.

[The maximum number of participants allowed within the forest has been reached.]

[Current Count: 10 / 10]

“Should’ve known it wouldn’t be that easy,” Andrew said.

“Shit, we wasted two hours getting here,” Xiaoming cursed.

“All right, on the double, people! Let’s run back. James will stay. Sanders, you bring the logs to camp with James’s raptor.”

“Yes, sir.” Sanders saluted.

“Hm… hey, Sanders. You can go back on your own and take the raptor with you,” James said. An idea struck him as he gazed at the lone portal standing in the distance.

“Eh? What are you going to do, James?”

“I’m thinking of camping near the portal. Instead of letting the monsters come to the unfinished base, I want to hold them off at the portal,” James said. He knew he was powerful, but he also knew he had limits. He could clear an entire horde alone, but how many citizens would die in the process? If too many died, he’d be kicked out of the dungeon. Andrew understood this and had assigned him to handle one portal from the start.

“Understood.” Sanders simply nodded and returned on his own.

James prodded the portal, and a purple barrier glowed around it. He chuckled, thinking how easy it would be if he could just destroy the portal outright. Taking a deep breath, he prepared himself as the portal groaned and began spawning new monsters. Just like before, they were low-level creatures.

“Is that the boss?” James muttered as he cleaved another rabid jackal with his axe. One dog-headed creature stood out—it wielded a shield and a curved sword, a khopesh, that glowed gold. It dashed toward him, its blade slashing through the air.

Before James could react, a purple bolt shot toward it. The beast lunged past him, striking its own allies instead. His mind magic—Illusion—was a godsend. But there was no time to admire the chaos it caused; another naked jackal lunged from behind, its teeth sinking deep into James’s shoulder. Thanks to his stats and his firefur, the creature’s fangs only barely pierced his flesh.

“Fuck! It hurts! These dogs are more troublesome than I thought.” James winced as another dog bit his calf. He stepped on the rabid creature, crushing its head with a stomp.

Thirty minutes later, the horde on James’ side was cleared. The boss proved to be a small fry thanks to James’ mind magic. It had both shield and sword skills—a deadly combo—but with its eyes blocked by James’s illusion, he had no trouble putting it down.

[Congratulations, you have cleared the second wave. 2 survivors lightly injured, 0 deaths. The next wave is in 12 hours. You and the rest of the survivors have obtained 500 coins. You may use the shop function for 1 hour.]

By the time James returned to the camp, they had just killed the last of the jackals from another portal that had spawned south of the encampment.

“James! Anything you’d like to buy? If not, we need to go to the forest and collect more logs,” Andrew said.

“Yes. Let me cut my steel wire first,” James replied. The wire was 20 meters in length, and to restore it to its original size, he needed to pay 200 coins for every 10 meters. That meant 10 meters of wire cost 200 coins.

“Well… this is necessary.” James frowned as he spent 1000 coins to buy 50 meters of steel wire—half of which he gave to Andrew while carrying the rest himself. He would use them to tie logs to his raptors.

“Thanks, James. This is a godsend. We can make a roof easily with this,” Andrew smiled.

“Don’t waste it. I’m not buying any more. That thing costs 500 coins.”

“...Understood.” Andrew’s gaze hardened when he heard the price.

“Ah, I’ll be heading back at the four-hour mark, alright? I suggest you all take a break as well,” James said.

“Yes.”

And so, the team worked around the clock, taking shifts. Those who were not on log collection duty worked on the base camp. Using James’s wire, they made abatis—wooden stakes lined up and facing outward. After crafting about a hundred of them and spreading them across the open gates, the timer reached zero, and monsters began spawning from the gate.

Just like before, the doghead army charged out. In addition to them, there were flyers this time. Not eagles like before, but five-foot-long crows spawning from both portals. The overall black theme of these monsters unnerved James. It was as if a deluge of black tar came rushing down the sandy desert, ready to swallow everything whole.

“Here come the flyers!” Sanders prepared his magic.

The enemy proved little trouble for the group. It ran just like the previous dungeons. James’s group handled one side of the gate—being helped, or more precisely smooched out of EXP—by Xiaoming’s group, while Andrew and Bernard handled the other side.

In less than two hours, they cleared the wave with zero accidents this time.

[Congratulations, you have cleared the third wave. You have received 500 coins. 0 casualties. A new supply of 500 logs, 5 planes, 5 chisels, and 50 meters of rope has been provided. The next wave will arrive in 6 hours. Please prepare for the next wave.]

“Alright!”

“Yeah! Zero deaths and injuries! Take that!” A soldier cheered. His shouts of joy rang through the group.

“Good work, everyone. If anyone wants to rest, you may. Civilians, please get ready to make roofs. We don’t know when those moons will come out, so let’s get to work.”

James sighed. The battle had barely begun—why were they so happy? This was just a minor victory. The real deal would come in the next two waves.
