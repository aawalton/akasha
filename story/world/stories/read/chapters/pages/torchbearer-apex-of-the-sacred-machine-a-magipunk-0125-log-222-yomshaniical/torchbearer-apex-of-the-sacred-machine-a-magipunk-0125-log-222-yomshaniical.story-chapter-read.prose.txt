I had expected her to call me a liar, or to make me prove it somehow, which I obviously couldn’t, but to my surprise, she just nodded and got lost in her thoughts again.

Finally, the girl shook her head.

“You’re an unshackled, crazed Machine that summons Ferals. There is no guarantee you won’t kill us all if you get even stronger.”

And yet, I heard the undertone in Pina’s voice, the silent struggle against herself. Even when she reached for the laptop lid with a sudden, almost comical reverence, I stayed quiet for a moment, weighing my words.

In the end, it all came down to something that had been playing in my mind during this entire conversation. Why had she opened my laptop today? Just to gloat about how she was going to sell me into slavery or death? Maybe. But my gut told me the only reason we were talking was because I had promised her the one thing she wanted more than anything else.

She ran her hand through her dirty hair, bit her lip, and didn’t say anything for a long while.

The wind rustled through the trees behind her and played with her hair. The sun began to set and shone straight into her face. It must have been blinding, but apart from turning her head to the side, she paid it no mind.

“What do you want?” she asked, still staring off into the distance.

I didn’t know if another shackle would take, but I wasn’t going to risk it. Besides, if she objected, I could always pull that demand back and in my counteroffer, I could—

No, I wasn’t at the negotiation table.

“You will keep us safe?” She had been glancing at the screen to read my replies, but now her head snapped back forward.

She took a deep breath, but it didn’t hide the slight tremble in her jaw. Not angry this time. Just… scared.

“I can agree with that, I think. But you have to get it done before we reach the Battalion.”

“Two weeks… maybe a bit more. We’ll have to make a detour to avoid Cult territory, I think. Pina made the plan last time.”

As much as I wanted to know what “the cult” was, now was not the time. Before I could reply, however, Pina suddenly straightened, a bit of her earlier passion returning to her features.

“But if… if I so much suspect that you are trying to betray us, I will jam whatever is left in the Hexbreaker into your carcass and drown you in the deepest lake I can find.”

And it was. I wasn’t going to betray her, and as long as I kept warning her in advance whenever I used Logic, she had very little to going by thinking I was going behind her back, and everything to lose.

Pina’s eyes scanned the text I was putting out, and she nodded after every line.

“Open the what?”

For a second, I didn’t know what she was talking about, but then it hit me.

Then, realizing how weird that sounded, I wrote,

As a former project manager, hell, as a former regent, I had plenty of reasons to ask for regular contact. Still, I almost felt ashamed, begging a teenager to call me every day. Even so, this was the first real human interaction I had in weeks, and now that it was drawing to a close, I knew I needed more of it.

To my relief, the girl didn’t hesitate to nod. Obviously, she was also interested in keeping tabs on me.

“So we have a deal?” she asked, voice breaking toward the end. The sun finally set fully, and only the faint blue glow of the laptop illuminated the girl’s face. Her hand hovered over the keyboard, trembling like the leaves slowly fading into shadow behind her.

She nodded and pressed her hand onto the keyboard. It took me a second to understand why.

She was… making a pact. I didn’t grin or chuckle, even though usually, the situation would have seemed funny. The show of trust, from her, was oddly heartwarming. It also told me just how far she was willing to go.

“You got two weeks.”

Instead of responding, she closed the laptop, but I had no doubts she would call me again tomorrow evening.

As reality’s silver light flickered and faded, I got to my feet.
