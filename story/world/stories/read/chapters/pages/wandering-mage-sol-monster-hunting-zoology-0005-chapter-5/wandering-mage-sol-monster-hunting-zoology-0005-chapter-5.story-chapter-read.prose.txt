Whatever teasing patience Aldmern usually carried was gone. His jaw set tight with a hardness in his eyes she did not often see directed at her.

She held the notebook, hesitating a moment.

“It’s just notes,” she said.

Aldmern said nothing.

Tressa’s ears lowered slightly. With a small huff, she flipped back several pages, revealing line after line of notes, rune sketches, and lines of old elven script copied from the library.

Aldmern stared down at them, his expression tightening further.

Tressa frowned, having expected to get an earful from him, but not for him to look so upset.

“I’ve made two spells,” she said, a faint note of pride slipping into her voice. "You altered that preservation spell for your trial, right?"

She watched his face for a moment, then smiled down at her notebook. "Well, I've made a light spell and a flying spell.”

He blinked. “What?”

“Here, I’ll show you.”

She rose to her feet as if it were nothing at all.

Holding out one hand, she reached with her mana toward the staff leaning beside the wall all the way back in her bedroom, miles away. A breath later, it appeared in her grasp.

Aldmern stumbled back.

The staff had simply appeared, pulled from nowhere into her hand. “How…?” he whispered. “That type of spell is…”

“It’s actually quite simple. I figured that one out a little while ago. It’s little more than a parlor trick, truly.” She pulled back her sleeve to show a bracelet with a small stone embedded in its fixture. “The trick is connecting your desired item to an anchoring stone. I’ve yet to test the distance, but I imagine there must be a limit.”

Tressa looked over her staff fondly, ignoring his attitude.

It was a long staff of pale, ashen oak, similar to that of the library doors, with a twisted top and a purple gem resting inside it. The twist on the top almost looked like claws holding the gem.

“Levant,” she said.

At once, she rose from the ground, lifting slowly into the air until she hovered ten feet above the earth. Wind stirred around her, teasing strands of her silvery-blonde hair and rustling her clothes. She looked down at Aldmern with an easy smile, as though floating in open air were the most natural thing in the world.

He stared up at her in shock. “Impossible.”

Tressa descended again just as calmly, her boots touching softly on the ground.

“Not really,” she said. “I added wind magic and other manipulations to the levitation spell so I could control my movement. I’m calling it Levant.”

“You… you’re saying you created that spell?”

She smiled and tilted her head, feeling excited as it was something she’d never shared with anyone before. Aldmern was the first.

No elf her age should have been capable of magic like this. Let alone the fact she created it herself, which technically was illegal.

He’d know she had practiced a few spells; basic levitation is a popular first spell many aspiring or hobbyist mages would start with, but that spell was only meant to hover one's feet a few inches above the ground, or a small item. Those who became skilled could even move around at a walking pace, as Tressa had shown off doing a few times before. Even the staff calling spell wasn’t so unheard of. But what she just did went far beyond any of that.

“How?” he asked again, his voice strained.

Tressa tapped a finger to her chin. “Let’s see… how do I explain it…”

He watched her think while his own mind reeled.

“Well, you know that spell they use in the library to move books around?”

“Yes.”

“That’s a form of wind magic. So I took that as a base and expanded it. I adjusted the mana flow so it surrounded my whole body. Then I mixed it with a gravity rune—well, more like a reversed gravity effect—and wove the two together.” She shrugged. “That’s how I made Levant.”

Aldmern stared at her.

She spoke of combining runes, manipulating mana, and reversing gravity as lightly as someone describing how to season stew. As if it were mundane and not at all unsafe.

“What other spells have you created?” he asked, more cautiously now.

“Well, there’s the light spell.”

His eyes narrowed. “A light spell?”

Tressa nodded eagerly. “Mm-hm. Here.”

She adjusted her grip on the staff and frowned in concentration. “It should work fine through the gem… I just need to change the mana output a little…”

She closed her eyes, focused, then opened them again.

“Lumas.”

Little lights blossomed into existence around her.

Some were the size of marbles, others as big as apples, and each varied in their brightness. They drifted upward while the violet gem in her staff pulsed faintly as she guided them into the air.

Then, one of the lights suddenly flared far too bright.

“Shit,” Tressa muttered.

She cut the mana flow at once, and several of the smaller lights winked out. The larger one dimmed back to a soft glow. Then, with a small movement of her free hand, she whispered a wind spell. The remaining lights stirred, drifting through the air around them. Floating between trees, weaving gently through shafts of sunlight and shadow. It was as though she were arranging lanterns in a ballroom rather than standing in the middle of the woods.

Then she pulled back on her mana flow, and the spell faded. One by one, the lights dimmed and vanished, the final glowing orb blinking weakly before going dark.

Tressa smiled, pleased. “What do you think? I think it went quite well that time.” She crouched and began scribbling notes into the margin of her book. “I’ve learned that if I anchor more mana in one core light, the others can stay active longer without constant adjustment. Though I still have to feed a little more into it over time. The longest I’ve kept one glowing so far was… seven hours? I have it written down somewhere.”

Aldmern remained motionless. Tressa had not only shown him one new spell but two. The staff calling spell was one thing, but an enhanced levitation spell, a controlled light spell, and pulling it off by manipulating runes. She had cast them one after another with no visible strain, no trembling hands, no exhaustion. If anything, she looked more energized than before. He knew Tressa was a prodigy, but this was beyond even what the highest-level elven mages could do at her age.

And now she was kneeling in the grass, scribbling notes, as though she had done nothing remarkable.

“Tressa,” he said finally.

She looked up from the page. “Yes, Aldmern?”

“How did you do that?” he asked. “Not just the light spell. All of it. Summoning your staff. The flying. The mana control—how did you do any of that?”

She sat back on her heels and chewed thoughtfully on the end of her metal quill.

“Hm. How to explain…” she murmured. “Maybe if I—”

He shook his head sharply.

“No. Never mind.” His voice hardened. “I don’t want to know.”

Tressa blinked. “What?”

He took a step toward her. “Do you have any idea how dangerous what you’ve been doing is?”

She stared at him, confused. “Dangerous?”

“Yes, dangerous.” His hands curled into fists. “Tressa, this isn’t harmless experimenting. You’re tampering with runes! Forcing them into new forms and to do things they were never meant to. Do you understand what could happen if you got it wrong?” He started rustling his own hair. “This isn’t just dangerous, it’s illegal!”

She slowly rose to her feet, notebook still in hand. “It’s not dangerous if you’re careful! I mean, I've had a few mess-ups, but it hasn't brought me any harm.”

“No harm?” he repeated. “What if you mixed them wrong and you melted your flesh? You could tear your mana channels apart. Start a fire. Blown off a limb!”

She chuckled. “You’re being so dramatic—HEY!”

He reached for the notebook.

Tressa jerked back and clutched it to her chest before he could touch it. “What are you doing?”

“Tressa, listen to me,” he snapped. “Even temple mages and court sages do not meddle with runes this way. They study them, yes. They cast established spells and only manipulate them in highly controlled experimental settings and after careful research. But creating new ones on your own? Altering old ones? With no guidance or partner? At your age? That has maimed people. Killed them. What you are doing is dangerous.”

“Well, it hasn’t killed me yet,” she shot back.

“Give me the book.”

She took another step away from him. “No.”

His face darkened. “Tressa.”

Tressa stepped back again, her voice beginning to shake. “N-No! You have no right to demand my book! You are my guard. S-So you can't! You’re out of line—y-you’re acting above your station, Aldmern!”

“I can if it threatens your safety. And in any case, it is illegal. You know the laws!” He held out his hand. “I was charged with protecting you. And right now, I am telling you that this is dangerous. Hand me the book.”

Tressa glared at him. Hurt, anger, and betrayal on her face. Tears stung her eyes.

A moment ago, she had been excited—nervous, yes, but excited. She had trusted him enough to show him. Trusted him enough to let him see what she had to keep hidden from everyone else. Now, he stood before her with his hand out, looking at her not like she had shown him something brilliant, but like she’d just confessed to a crime, which, technically, she just did. She just didn’t think it should be seen that way, and thought Aldmern would at least try to understand her.

“Aldmern…” Her voice turned small. “I thought you were on my side.”

His expression flickered.

“My lady,” he said more quietly. “I am. But I am also your father’s retainer. And I will do what I must if I think you’re endangering yourself.”

She stepped back again.

“I showed you because I trusted you,” she said, her throat tightening. “I would never have shown you if I thought you’d react like this.”

That made him pause.

He had watched over her since she was young, and he was very fond of her. He had tracked her through forests, covered for her more times than he ought. Now seeing that look on her face—betrayal, hurt, disbelief—struck him harder than he expected. Not just from his master's orders but because of his own feelings.

“Tressa,” he said, forcing the words out, “I am doing what I think is necessary. Magic like this is not something to toy with.”

Tears welled in her eyes.

“No,” she said, voice trembling. “You’re just like Father. You don’t trust me. You don’t believe I know what I’m doing. You don’t care that this is the one thing I’m actually good at.”

“That’s not what this is—”

“I thought you had faith in me!” she burst out. “I thought you understood me!”

“And I thought you were filling these books with monster sketches,” he said, his own voice rising now. “Not breaking the law and experimenting with runes, Tressa!”

“I’m not just blindly experimenting!” she yelled. “I’m creating. I can see what I’m doing.”

“Well, you can explain it later, but I have to report this.”

He stepped forward again, reaching for the book, but Tressa was faster than he expected.

She darted around him, snatching up her satchel and quill in one quick motion. Her staff was already in her other hand.

“Levant!”

The gem at the top of her staff flared and Tressa shot upward, lifting off the ground and rising high into the air—well beyond his reach.

“Tressa!” Aldmern shouted.

She hovered above him, staring down with watering eyes.

He sighed, tapping a foot and frowning. “Tressa, please come down here. It is not that I want to get you into trouble, but it’s not something I can ignore either.”

“You’re going to report me?”

“I don’t have a choice. What you’re doing is illegal and dangerous. The council needs to—”

“So that’s it?” she snapped. “You’re just going to hand me over! They’ll search my belongings and take all of my research! How can you do that to me!”

“It won’t be so bad. I’m sure you’ll get all of it back, but under more careful…”

“I can’t believe this! I trusted you!” Tressa screamed, and without another word, she turned and launched herself away, flying fast over the forest canopy.

“Tressa!” he shouted after her, but his voice disappeared in seconds.

Wind whipped past her as tears streamed down her face.

He had tried to take it. Her book. Her research. Everything she had worked so hard on. Grinding her teeth, she flew faster.

Only after several minutes did she slow, hovering above the trees as the anger in her chest slowly faded into something heavier.

She wiped at her eyes and took a shaky breath.

“He was just worried…” she muttered.

Deep down, she knew that. Aldmern had always looked out for her. But still… that wasn’t how she had hoped he would react.

Her shoulders sagged.

“I should go back.”

A piercing screech tore through the air.

Tressa looked up just in time to see a massive bird diving straight at her.

“Gah!”

Her concentration shattered, and the spell collapsed as she plummeted toward the forest below.
