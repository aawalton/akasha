It took Asher a solid ten minutes to explain Smiles’ origin, and then about twice that to convince Draken to keep the severed head around and occasionally bombard it with his Death Aura to keep him from ‘drying out’ in terms of death energy. If Smiles still had his body attached, Draken would have essentially been forced to hit him with his concentrated aura every few minutes. But as just a head, Smiles required far less upkeep.

Asher had to promise to look into a more long-term solution for Smiles in order for Draken to accept in the first place, and the gang leader made his stance on accepting Smiles into his gang quite clear.

“This is a temporary measure, Asher,” Draken stressed for the third time as Asher prepared to head out again. “And one I’m only willing to accept because you helped me get revenge on the Assassin’s Guild here in Whikoga. I’m expecting you to keep me informed on your progress. If I don’t hear from you in a couple of days, I’m ditching the head.”

“His name is Smiles,” Asher frowned, still a bit surprised that Draken hadn’t been nearly as interested in having Smiles around as Asher had expected. “I’m telling you, he’s probably the deadliest opponent I’ve ever gone up against. If I can figure out a way to get his head reattached to his body and solve the death energy issue, he’d be an absolute monster in your gang.”

“My people get enough trouble just working under a man with a forbidden Death element,” Draken scowled. “The last thing I need is for word to get around that we have an actual zombie in our ranks as well.”

“I promise you I’ll do what I can,” Asher reassured him. “There has to be a wizard or two who knows what to do. The wizard in the capital owes me a favor, I can check with him.”

“Of course the High Prince’s personal wizard owes you a favor,” Draken sighed, shaking his head as he leaned back against his desk. After he wouldn’t stop trying to convince Draken to pour some of his expensive liquor down his throat to see if he could get drunk, Smiles had been relocated to one of the private bedrooms. “Dare I even ask why?”

“He accidentally threw me into the wild realm for a couple of hours,” Asher grinned, laughing at the shock on Draken’s face. “I’ll be back!”

With Smiles taken care of for the moment, Asher Recalled straight back to the lich’s workshop, finding himself standing before the very same door Smiles had directed him to less than an hour ago. Daggers at the ready, Asher slipped into the astral and stepped through, peering around the dark room.

Something that looked like a horrific combination of a wolf, a lion, and a snake poorly stitched together was chained in the center of the room, snarling and roaring at the door as it fought against the heavy chain on its neck. Curious, Asher simply stood there in the astral for a minute, watching the monster do the same thing over and over again without error.

It’s like it’s programmed to follow the same ten-second loop, he realized, understanding what Smiles had meant by the experiment being fake. Returning to the material, Asher ignored the fake monster and took a few steps forward, slowly moving around the tiny room until he found what he was looking for.

Secret Seeker - Lvl 29

Jackpot, he thought, grinning as he stared down at the section of the floor that looked identical to the rest of the room. He was sure there was some incredibly convoluted magical method of opening the hidden trapdoor and accessing the lich sealed within, but Asher really couldn’t be bothered with any of that.

Instead, he fell straight through the floor.

Landing in some form of underground tunnel leading down, Asher looked around. Unlike the smooth stone of the above-ground workshop, this sloped tunnel looked like it had been quickly carved out of the earth without too much worry for aesthetics. There were no torches, no gemstones, not a single source of light leading down. Returning to the material realm, Asher kept his daggers at the ready as he slowly followed the tunnel deeper into the earth.

Down and down the tunnel went, the temperature dropping lower and lower with every step he took. It wasn’t until he’d been walking for almost a minute that Asher thought to experiment.

Complete Resilience… Cold?

He felt his very first third-tier skill activate, but to his dismay, the freezing chill persisted all around him, continuing to bite into his flesh as he followed the tunnel deeper into the earth. While he supposed that made sense, as this was a natural cold and his skill specifically mentioned it making him immune to energy that came from elements, he was still mildly put out that he wouldn’t be able to just declare himself immune to fire and then dance around in a burning building without worry. That said, seeing as his skill had made the King of Death’s Death element all but worthless during their fight, he certainly couldn’t complain.

Asher didn’t know if all third-tier skills were quite so powerful, or if it was an added benefit of his unique Eternity element, but either way, he was beyond excited to keep leveling his skills and see what effects the others would gain upon their second evolution at level 40. Eternal Regeneration was already sitting at the precipice at level 39, and a few others weren’t too far behind. Part of him was a little surprised he’d still yet to receive even his first blended skill, but the more he thought about it, the more it made sense. From what he’d heard, blended skills were based less so on the levels of a person’s other skills and more so the time one actually spent within the Stage of Bleed. While Asher did enough insane feats to level up his skills faster than most, he really hadn’t been in the Stage of Bleed for very long.

Damn, I really do get hurt a lot, huh? He thought as he continued down the freezing tunnel. I wonder what that says about me as a fighter that my healing and resistance skills are my highest leveled ones? Moxy would probably tell me I needed to do better.

That line of thinking came to a quick stop, however, as the tunnel slowly leveled out after it had descended at least a solid hundred feet below the surface. Asher found himself standing before a shining door that appeared to be enchanted to the nine hells and back.

[Steel Door] - Masterwork. Heavily Enchanted.

“No kidding,” he muttered, pulling out the bone key he’d taken from the King of Death’s body. The key slid into the lock without issue, and after a tiny ‘click’, the door cracked open. Carefully, Asher pushed it the rest of the way open, ensuring he was ready for anything as he dropped into the stance Mornamol had been drilling into him. Though while he was ready for something to leap out at him or some unknown magic to assault his very soul…

He still wasn’t ready for what he found. The door opened to reveal a small room no larger than a broom closet, filled with one thing and one thing only.

A large, metallic mannequin standing in the dead center of the tiny room.

It took Asher a few seconds to understand what he was seeing, before his jaw dropped in horror and he nearly lost his grip on his daggers. The mannequin wasn’t a mannequin at all.

Someone had bound the lich from head to toe in a thick layer of metal, preventing it from so much as twitching a finger.

My God… Asher thought, staring at what had to be the most horrifying thing he’d ever witnessed. For six hundred and thirty-two years, this lich had lay buried beneath the earth, encased from head to toe in metal. Asher had expected to find the decrepit undead heavily bound in enchanted chains, or with some other magical restraint placed upon it to keep it from accessing its magic. But fully encased in metal?

And not just any metal either.

Identify informed him that this was soulsteel, and a hell of a lot of it based on how large the mannequin looked. When Asher had been stabbed by Smiles’ longsword, he’d thought it was strange how the metal only prevented him from accessing his first-tier skills. But perhaps the blocking effect wasn’t just limited to the first tier. If the strength of the blocking effect was based on the quantity of soulsteel utilized…

At the very least, that would explain how the lich hadn’t managed to escape in all this time.

One of the liches on the Council must know the secret to working the metal, Asher realized, trying to remember what he’d manually read from Zygrul’s notes. Strangely, Zygrul hadn’t made any mention of soulsteel anywhere in the information he’d left them, though there were a few liches who he had next to no information on. Either way, at least now Asher knew they’d have to be even more cautious if they ended up facing one of those unknown liches.

Though now, he had a bit of a problem on his hands.

How did he kill something protected under what looked like a three-inch-thick layer of soulsteel?

It was ironic that the very coffin binding the lich in the first place had now become a nearly impenetrable suit of armor. Asher walked around the metallic mannequin, looking for any sort of opening he could exploit, or a magic keyhole used to release the lich. Secret Seeker didn’t pick out anything in the smooth metal, however, and he soon came to an even more horrifying realization. He’d first assumed this was some sort of punishment for the lich daring to go against the council, and that they would be released once the experiment was done. But as far as he could tell…

There wasn’t any way to get them out from the metallic bindings.

Asher did another two laps around the lich, frowning as he tried to come up with an idea. Crescent was sharp, but not even mithral was capable of cutting through a few inches of solid soulsteel. Distortion didn’t work on items currently being ‘worn’ by others, and he quickly confirmed the metal prison counted in this case. That really only left one option, but it hinged entirely on the lich hiding within. He half considered simply leaving the lich here as it was, or even turning it over to that paladin he’d run into earlier, but he couldn’t risk it. For all he knew once the Council of Death realized they were running low on members they would retrieve this lich and give them a second chance to bolster their power once more.

“I don’t know if you can hear me,” Asher began, raising his voice almost to the point he was shouting. “But I imagine you’re probably tired of being trapped in that soulsteel prison. I can get it off you, but only if you’re willing! My skill isn’t going to work otherwise!”

Asher didn’t see any reason to add that the moment the lich was free, he was going to cut off their head and end their life for good. Honestly, after being trapped down here for six hundred years, even if he admitted he was going to kill the lich, he figured they would probably still go through with the plan and thank him for his efforts.

From his attempt at using Distortion, Asher had already discovered that soulsteel’s unique property of severing a person’s connection to their skills and energy wasn’t actually based on touch. If laying a hand on the metal did nothing, whereas getting impaled by it sealed his first-tier skills, Asher could only assume the terrifying effect was reliant on the metal being embedded into one’s flesh somehow. That meant he was free to place both hands on the metallic prison without worry of losing access to his skills.

“I’m going to set you free now! Remember, you have to be willing!” Asher shouted, feeling the cold bite of soulsteel against his hands.

Thanks to some… experimentation with Samantha in the bedroom, he was well aware that he could move articles of clothing that were currently being worn from another person's body directly into his rift, so long as they were willing. He’d tried the same move on Rulfar during one of their many spars to try and throw the bloodhound off his game and get an easy win, and had nearly had his arm chopped off for his failed efforts. He also knew that he could only pull things into his rift if they were light enough that he could personally carry them, which meant his only idea for accessing the lich relied on two factors.

The lich had to want to be free, and Asher needed to be able to lift the human-sized chunk of soulsteel. He was pretty confident in that first one, but the second…

Thank God he’d purchased that Belt of Strength when he did.

“It’s only a shell of metal,” he muttered to himself, taking a deep breath as he prepared himself and shouted one last time. “Here I go!”

Pressing his hands firmly on the sides of the cool metal coffin, Asher heaved with all his might, triggering his skill at the same time. In an instant, the entire metal coffin popped out of existence, revealing an emaciated corpse that looked like little more than bones and skin. Even for a regular lich, which already didn’t exactly look like the picture of health, this one was in pretty bad shape. His body was covered from head to toe in hundreds of deep puncture wounds, revealing exactly how the soulsteel coffin had been designed to sever his connection to his skills.

The moment the coffin vanished, the lich’s body crumbled to the floor, unable to support its own weight after being freed from its centuries-long imprisonment. At first, Asher thought the lich had already been killed somehow, perhaps unable to survive being severed from his tritessence for so many hundreds of years. But as he watched, he witnessed the faintest flickering of dark green flame reignite in his empty eye sockets, the fires gasping at life as the weakened lich slowly tilted his head to look up at him.

“Human,” he rasped, the fires in his eye sockets flickering and nearly going out before blazing with an unknown strength, seeming to convey nothing but rage and hatred as they danced. Shaking as if the effort was practically too much for him, the crumpled, weakened lich raised a trembling hand, slowly clenching it into a fist.

“Let us see the Council of Death burn beneath our feet.”
