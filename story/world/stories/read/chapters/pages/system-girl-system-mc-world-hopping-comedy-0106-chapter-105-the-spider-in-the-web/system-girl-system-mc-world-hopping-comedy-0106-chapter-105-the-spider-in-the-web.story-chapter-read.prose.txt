Laurana was about to be moved by her sister. She was now able to walk a bit, a slow, shuffling limp that sent flares of dull agony up her thigh. She leaned heavily on Larena's small shoulder. The younger girl, still trembling from the terror of the past few days, bore the weight bravely.

They took two steps before Larena was stopped by Kiren.

The Head Maid of the Greenwood estate simply stepped into their path. Her signature, perfectly polite smile was plastered across her face, making it blindingly obvious that Larena should move out of the way.

Larena swallowed hard. She practically shrank back into herself, sitting back down silently on the edge of the cot with her head bowed.

Kiren was the one who originally insisted the two sisters join her in the lead carriage for the transport from the holding cells. Laurana understood exactly why. She was meant to be a prop. A broken, bleeding prop for Kiren to use in her bid to show the new masters how much she 'cares' about the people under her charge.

Laurana’s skin crawled as Kiren grabbed her gently by the upper arm. Laurana's body betrayed her. She was involuntarily shaking and jerking, her muscles spasming in pure terror at the physical contact.

Kiren noticed it. She leaned in close, maintaining her pleasant, serene expression for the benefit of anyone watching. She discreetly whispered directly into Laurana's ear.

"Stop twitching if you know what's good for you, cow."

Laurana tensed her entire body. She locked her joints, doing everything in her power to stop her involuntary repulsion at being touched by Kiren.

Kiren, the master of manipulation, needed this to work perfectly. She needed this tableau to cement her position. From the corner of her eye, she noticed a Knight of the Royal Order—under the command of the terrifying battlemage—standing near the doorway, observing the transfer of the elves.

Kiren instantly saturated her performance with a sweet, gentle, overbearingly motherly tone. "It's okay, dear. Don't be afraid. I am with you."

She gently caressed the injured girl's head, stroking her hair. It was a picture-perfect display of maternal affection.

Anyone from the outside would be fooled by the Oscar-worthy performance. The Knight escorting the elves was none the wiser, leaning casually against the doorframe, not rushing them out of the holding area.

Kiren looked directly at the armored man. She pitched her voice to a gentle, pleading cadence. "Sir Knight. There was a wheelchair in the second wagon, unloaded earlier. May I fetch it for dear Laurana here? She is struggling so."

The Knight, puffing out his chest in a sudden display of chivalry, replied instantly. "Do not fret. I shall fetch it for you."

He turned and marched away heavily.

The moment the Knight was gone from earshot, Kiren ended her performance. The sweet smile vanished, replaced by a cold, calculating glare. She looked down at the two girls.

"Listen up," Kiren hissed, her voice a sharp whip crack. "I will push the wheelchair. Don't mess up again." She turned her sharp gaze to the little girl. "You. Stop hanging on your sister so much. Instead, grab on to me. Hold my arm, or the hem of my dress. Make it look like you need me."

Laurana nodded numbly, fully understanding the assignment. If they didn't play along, Kiren would ensure they suffered for it later, assuming she managed to claw her way to the top of this new household's hierarchy.

The Knight returned with the heavy wooden wheelchair. Laurana limped forward, forced to use Kiren's shoulder for support. The physical contact made her want to vomit. She finally sat heavily in the wheelchair, her broken leg throbbing.

Kiren pushed her out into the bright afternoon sun.

Laurana finally got a glimpse of the estate they had been brought to. It was quite magnificent. They were in the back courtyard. On one side, a sprawling, beautifully constructed timber-and-stone mansion could be seen. On the other side was an entire elven village, attached directly to the estate grounds, bleeding seamlessly into the ancient forest.

Involuntary tears welled up in Laurana's eyes at the sight of the village. The place was so open. So free. There was a profound sense of serenity and peace here, a feeling totally alien to the dreadful, suffocating gloom of the Greenwood estate.

She could spot a few elven huts built directly under the massive oak trees, and others resting on the thick branches, connected by swaying wooden bridges and carved stairways tying everything together. There were elven children running around, actually playing in the shade of the trees. Other elves were up on the higher floors of the treehouses, laughing while hanging laundry on lines strung between the branches.

Most shocking of all was the twinkling lights of nature spirits darting through the canopy of the trees, weaving through the leaves and playing hide-and-seek among the branches.

The sight made even Kiren stand completely still. She looked gobsmacked. This was how elven villages were described in old, forbidden fairytales. The modern ones she knew, the ones Lord Greenwood maintained, were essentially concentration camps used only to breed elves for labor. This, on the other hand, was a genuine habitat. A sanctuary.

The large group of newly arrived elves from the Greenwood estate kept looking longingly at the village, hope fighting a desperate battle against their ingrained cynicism.

Behind them, the Fenris staff were officially handed custody of the people. Ren and Parisa were standing near the main house, formally confirming the transfer of the contracts in the main manor, processing the paperwork. Parisa had been given temporary oversight of the refugees by the Crown until the transfer was complete.

Three estate guards came along with Sylas, stepping out to take physical custody of the arrivals in the courtyard.

Sylas addressed the Royal Knights. "The Lord has prepared food and lodging so that you may all rest. Our people will handle the rest of the processing here. Please, follow me."

Sylas led the heavily armored knights toward the main doors of the Seaside Inn. Some of the newly arrived elves looked longingly at the departing knights. They had last eaten a meal twelve hours ago, sharing rations with the soldiers last night.

The Knights had shared some of their travel rations with them—a thing they called 'hardtack'. It was extremely dried, rock-hard bread, served with a cup of watered-down soup. The bread was so incredibly hard that even dipping it in the thin soup wouldn't soften it. To the starving Greenwood elves, the thick, hearty gruel usually made from the Greenwood estate's rotting leftovers now seemed like a luxury compared to the hardtack. At least with the gruel, there would be chances of finding chunks of questionable meat or random, bruised spices in them.

Kiren, on the other hand, wasn't thinking about food. Her attention was solely on the guards.

She was alarmed by the three elves standing near the courtyard entrance with steel swords strapped to their belts. All her indoctrination, all the mind-washing she had endured and enforced at the Greenwood estate, kicked in hard. She firmly believed that elves possessing weapons was a cardinal sin. It would be promptly punished by the Crown. She saw a chance to earn her place, to prove her worth to her new Lord by bringing this dangerous infraction to light before common sense could catch up to her train of thought.

She acted immediately, pointing an accusatory finger directly at the man at the center of the guard detail.

"You there!" Kiren shouted, adopting the harsh, commanding tone she used on the lower maids. "Who handed you that blade? Don't you know elves are not allowed to be in possession of weapons?"

Kaelthos looked at the woman. He saw her pushing a wheelchair containing a severely injured girl, with another little girl grasping onto the hem of her pristine dress, looking terrified.

Kaelthos’s heart softened immediately. He assumed she was terrified for his safety. He was about to explain the situation calmly.

One of his subordinates, a young scout named Zairen, spoke up first, his tone flat and unamused. "You are talking to the Chief of Security for House Fenris. How is he supposed to protect the Lord without weapons? Ask would-be assassins nicely to go away?"

Before Zairen could say anything more sarcastic, Kaelthos put a heavy hand on the younger scout's shoulder, silencing him. Kaelthos felt bad for these people. He knew what they had survived. He knew how their minds had been warped by Greenwood's cruelty. This lady must not have any ill intentions. She was just regurgitating the only rules she knew to keep her 'family' safe. His subordinates were not fully aware of what these people had been taught to fear.

"The Lord has entrusted me with this blade," Kaelthos said, his voice deep and rumbling with a gentle, reassuring authority. "So that I may guard what he holds dear to him."

Kaelthos shifted the conversation to something helpful, the main reason he had come out here to meet them.

"You all will first be provided breakfast," Kaelthos announced to the shivering crowd. "Line up in a single file. Each person will have to take one plate, cutlery, and one cup from that table there."

He pointed to a long wooden table set up near the edge of the lawn, where several maids in crisp white uniforms were bringing out stacks of wooden plates and placing them in neat rows.

Kaelthos continued. "Then you all will move to the second table, where you will be provided with food. Feel free to sit on the lawn and eat. Are there any questions?"

No one questioned him. The crowd of elves slowly, hesitantly lined up in a single file, shuffling forward with their heads down.

At the head of the line was Kiren, pushing the wheelchair containing Laurana, with Larena clinging tightly to her skirt. Kiren was still processing the baffling revelation that the head of the guard detail at this estate was an elf. She deeply regretted not paying attention to Lord Greenwood's butler last month when he kept rambling on about House Fenris in the servant's quarters. She had thought his panicked babbling was irrelevant to her survival.

They took the heavy ceramic plates and cutlery from the first station. Kiren put on a deliberate, exaggerated show for everyone to see, fussing over the two sisters, helping them hold their cups, ensuring she looked like a dedicated, selfless caretaker.

They shuffled toward the second table. It was massive, completely covered with a thick, white cloth, keeping the food hidden and warm. They were entirely uncertain as to what they would be served.

Soon, a man stepped up behind the serving table, accompanied by a female elf. Both of them were wearing the exact same thing: a white chef's uniform with a heavy, stained apron wrapped around their waists.

The man was clearly human. He had black eyes and messy black hair tied back with a bandana. Both of them were clearly cooks. They must be members of the kitchen staff sent out to feed the refugees.

Kiren noticed the renowned, terrifying battlemage, Lady Parisa, standing near the serving table, talking animatedly to the human cook.

The human man lifted the heavy cloth covering the food to reveal the feast.

There were several large, ceramic jugs filled with some kind of cold, orange liquid.

Next to that sat a massive, steaming metal vat containing a bizarre dish. It looked like light, thick, rope-like food, maybe made of wheat, swimming in a thick, rich red sauce. A few odd, perfectly round red balls were floating in the steaming liquid.

Kiren watched the interaction between the battlemage and the cook with keen interest. They seemed to be having an argument. The battlemage seemed to insist on the food being served to her first. She kept complaining, pacing back and forth, eventually just grabbing a plate from the stack and shoving it aggressively toward the chef.

The chef looked completely exasperated. He gave in, shaking his head. He used a pair of tongs to pull a massive serving of the long, rope-like wheat onto her plate, then used a ladle to pour a generous amount of the thick red sauce over it.

The battlemage pointed demandingly at the floating red balls in the vat, clearly wanting extra. The mad, scary battlemage, a woman who said to have slaughtered entire battalions, was utilizing puppy-dog eyes on a kitchen worker.

The chef, visibly losing his patience again, sighed heavily and gave her three extra red balls, dropping them onto the pasta with a wet splat.

Interesting, Kiren realized, her calculating mind whirring. This human chef must have a lot of influence in this house if a Royal Inspector is begging him for scraps. I need to get close to him to get information on the household's inner workings. He is a weak point I can exploit.

When Parisa received her portion and sat down cross-legged on the lawn to enjoy the food, everyone in the refugee line was shocked at her exaggerated reaction. She seemed to enjoy this bizarre, messy food a lot. She was practically inhaling the long ropes, getting red sauce all over her chin and not caring in the slightest.

Kiren and the two sisters were the first refugees to reach the serving station. The chef gave them each a generous serving of the red rope-food.

The two girls received extra meat balls. Kiren noticed that the two sisters, Laurana and Larena, seemed to be trying to recognize who this man was. They stared at his face, almost as if they had seen him before. It was an odd reaction to a simple kitchen hand.

The chef handed Kiren and Laurana cups of fresh, cold orange juice. He handed Larena a cup of warm milk.

Kiren stared at the plate of steaming, complex food, utterly baffled. The aroma of garlic, tomatoes, and herbs was overwhelming. She couldn't help but ask, slipping into her manipulative, inquiring persona.

"Is it not better to keep this high-quality food for banquets, or to serve it to the nobles?" Kiren asked, offering the chef a sweet, servile smile. "Why give such rich fare to lowly servants like us?"

The chef looked up from the vat of sauce. He wiped his hands on a towel tucked into his apron string. He looked her dead in the eye.

"Everyone in the estate eats whatever I cook for them," the chef declared, his voice carrying a strange, unquestionable authority for a kitchen worker. "The only one allowed to say otherwise is the Lady of the House."

Kiren looked at the chef, taking in his stained apron, the bandana tied tightly over his messy hair, and his aggressive, unapologetic stance.

The elf standing beside him at the serving station bristled. She looked furious. She stepped forward, preparing to berate the refugee for questioning the quality of the meal.

"What, my Lo—" Cynthia began, her voice rising in defense of the food.

The chef interrupted her smoothly, cutting her off before she could finish the title. "Cynthia. How many times do I need to tell you? Call me Head Chef. Or just Chef."

Cynthia clamped her mouth shut, her cheeks flushing. She bowed her head slightly. "Yes, Chef."

She then looked back at Kiren, her eyes flashing with territorial annoyance. "What the Head Chef means," Cynthia hissed quietly, "is that only the young Lady Lily, the sole heir to House Fenris, is allowed to criticize or choose between the Chef's food. That is the absolute law of the house."

Kiren blinked, thoroughly baffled by the exchange. She moved along the line, pushing Laurana's wheelchair onto the grass. She made a very careful mental note.

This house has a female heir, Kiren calculated, feeding the information into her mental rolodex. And this 'Lady Lily' holds a lot of power over the staff, even dictating the menu to the arrogant Head Chef. I can use this to my advantage. I need to get closer to this Lady of the House.

Kiren imagined an older, snooty, high-maintenance noblewoman. A spoiled aristocrat who threw tantrums if her tea was too hot and demanded absolute perfection from her servants. A woman like that would be easy to manipulate. Kiren just had to figure out what the Lady Lily liked, flatter her ego, and use her to secure a permanent, untouchable position within the estate hierarchy.

She continued her performance, her bid to 'care' for the two sisters. She put on a show for everyone on the lawn, making absolutely certain to push the wheelchair near where Lady Parisa was sitting so she could observe the battlemage's actions.

Even though the terrifying lady was entirely too distracted by aggressively slurping her pasta to notice the scheming maid sitting three feet away, Kiren smiled softly. The pieces were already falling into place.
