Acrypha didn’t fully understand what was going on when the Living Mana surged around her and Viren. What was it trying to do now? How had Viren freeing her unleashed it into such a monstrously powerful state?

It was roaring past them now. Waterfalls of livid Living Mana surged around this way and that, reconstructing the entire moon they were both trapped within. A part of her was wondering why Ryland hadn’t come to rescue the two of them yet. But there were many possible reasons as to why.

She had learned from Viren that he was fighting against the Demiurge himself. The thought was insane. Another part of Acrypha was ruing the fact that she was missing one of the most titanic clashes in the entire history of the Accorded Realms.

Then there was supposedly a gigantic barrier around the moon making rifting difficult. Ryland had barely managed to get Viren in. Injecting himself inside while being harried by Mavar the Eternal might just prove too difficult a task even for a great mage of his calibre. She didn’t blame him at all for taking time.

But that didn’t mean she could stop worrying. The moon was literally tearing itself apart around them. That they hadn’t been crushed into fleshy and bloody pulp was a miracle.

“What are we going to do?” Viren shouted over the din of the rushing Living Mana. “Where are we supposed to go, Acrypha?”

He was so panicked, he had kind of forgotten that Acrypha probably knew no more than he did. With how everything was a chaotic mess, every bit of information Viren had gathered from their surroundings was now unreliable. The Glyphtech had broken and shattered. There were no schematics or anything of the sort she could glean any more understanding from.

Acrypha sighed, and headed over to one of the streams of Living Mana nearby. The only thing she was certain of was that [Afterlife Guardians] spell had come about on its own, summoning the spirits of her parents empowered by her Soul Glyph of Legacy.

This was proven by the gigantic figure taking shape. It was quite obviously a mixture of her father and mother, a strange conglomerate of both guardians joined together like a painting.

A painting… Why did that sound familiar?

Acrypha frowned when she spotted something intriguing. The nearby Living Mana was forming a strange barrier around Acrypha and Viren, recreating the chamber they were trapped in. The chaos of the strange formation of the gargantuan figure was occurring outside the weird cocoon they were now enclosed within.

Her frown deepened at a realization. Was the Living Mana actively protecting her and Viren?

Or was it cocooning something else, and Acrypha’s safety was merely a coincidence?

For some reason, she really wished to believe it was the former. That would at least give her the impression there was a method to the madness she was witness to.

“How is this even happening?” Viren asked when Acrypha had pointed it out. He didn’t need any real evidence. Viren had known they were being protected for a while now.

“I don’t know why the Living Mana would protect us…”

Actually, she had her suspicions. It was just difficult to fathom. That gigantic colossus was the combined figure of her parents, after all. Even when they were people-sized Pure Manifestations, they had always acted in accordance with their wish to safeguard her.

“No, I mean, how did the Living Mana grow so powerful?” Viren scowled. “What exactly has that bastard Demiurge been planning all this while? This thing is… impossible.”

He swallowed as he tried to wrap the full scale of the behemoth they were facing in his head and practically failed to do so.

“He wanted to destroy all Living Mana,” Acrypha said. “Maybe it doesn’t want to be destroyed…”

She stopped thinking about the mechanics behind Mavar’s intentions. The magic used here was obviously beyond her ability to discover and comprehend on her own. Right now, that barrier of Living Mana intrigued her far too much.

Acrypha approached and reached out her hand to touch it despite Viren warning her against the idea.

The moment she made contact, the instant she felt it lighting up with blistering energy, she sort of understood what was happening. For Acrypha was no longer just present inside the moon of Rhel that was being destroyed to reform as a gigantic version of her Guardians.

Now, she was in the past. Memories arrived. Memories that weren’t her own but felt intimately familiar all the same.

Two figures, a burly man and a lithe woman, cradling and cooing over a tiny infant with wispy blonde hair and glimmering silver eyes. The same two figures now running through a burning forest, the child desperately attempting to keep up with her stubby little toddler legs. The same figures who secreted away the sleeping child before leaving, as they often did.

The same figures who entered a burning city with their manasouls ablaze, facing off against a monster oozing out virulent liquid magic.

Figures who would never again return to their lone daughter.

Acrypha pulled back with a sobbing gasp.

“Hey!” Viren said in rising alarm as he hurried over to her. “Acrypha! Are you alright?”

She didn’t answer him, just furiously rubbed at her leaking eyes and tried to gather her breath and thoughts again. Why had she felt like she had been drowning in those memories? They hadn’t exactly been her own for she had seen herself directly.

Viren was still looking at her in concern, rubbing her back to help her get a hold of herself again. She appreciated that he was biting his tongue against any more questions.

Acrypha could barely wrap her mind around what she had just seen. The Living Mana had grown to a point where it was pulling out memories of her own parents somehow? Was Mavar right? Had they left a bit of their integral essences? Did her Soul Glyph of Legacy literally contain a bit of their spirits? Their manasouls?

Or was the Living Mana fabricating it all as a means of protecting itself?

Acrypha shook her head, her expression solidifying into one of resolve. It had no reason to trick Acrypha. With the power it was displaying this instant, it could have simply eradicated her if it considered her truly dangerous.

“This thing…” She took a deep, shuddering breath, waiting a moment till her voice stabilized. “It’s evolving, Viren. It’s becoming more alive, somehow.”

“What did you see?” he asked, correctly guessing she had somehow been visited by visions.

She explained everything about her parents that she had just witnessed. “I think it wants to protect us. Or me, at least.”

Viren scowled. “Right. I’ll just pop myself out, then.”

Acrypha was almost tempted to laugh, though it probably would have come out a little too hysterical. Whatever she had been about to say was interrupted by a lot of violent shaking.

“Now what?” Viren asked, his words reverberating as his teeth chattered together.

The shaking lasted for a long time. Acrypha was positive something was going on, likely something Ryland was causing, though she and Viren remained safe and protected. Nothing truly harmed them.

But the Living Mana around them was certainly harmed. She could see it easily. Could almost feel it herself.

Chunks of it were falling off. Strands that were thicker than buildings were fading and breaking apart. There were now literal large holes in the figure, wounds that Acrypha could see the wider cosmos through. Or she would have, had there not been some sort of pure black barricade around them all. What even was that?

But beyond all the physical effects she witnessed, there was a part of Acrypha that seemed to throb at seeing all those wounds. Like there were phantom scars of her own, causing her phantom pain.

And then, before long, Ryland himself appeared.

He looked determined. And concerned. She had never seen his brows crowded with this much worry before. He also looked like he was about to tear through the Living Mana barrier, but she got to him before he could do anything.

“Don’t do it!” she shouted. She made sure not to touch the barrier, though. If she got sucked into memories right now, Ryland’s worry would only deepen. “Don’t attack, sir!”

Ryland’s frown deepened. “What is the meaning of this, Acrypha?”

“Sir, the Living Mana is just trying to protect us. It doesn’t want to cause anyone any harm.”

“Really?” The scepticism in his voice cut into Acrypha like a knife. “I just had a small scuffle with it, and I wouldn’t really call that not wanting to cause harm.”

A small scuffle for Ryland was probably annihilation that would have wiped out a small planet. So, Acrypha gulped, realizing it would be nigh on impossible to convince him of anything at the moment. How was she going to explain that they didn’t need to be at each other’s throats any longer? It would be so much easier if she could just communicate with the Living Mana.

“She’s right, sir,” Viren said. Her heart filled up with warmth. He was backing her up! “We weren’t hurt or anything.”

She took a deep breath. Acrypha had to make an attempt towards the Living Mana to—

Ryland suddenly jerked forward in alarm before freezing. At the same instant, Acrypha felt a sudden presence emerge behind her, one whose appearance sent a shiver of malicious familiarity coursing down her spine. She cursed as she turned around.

Mavar was there, his implacable hand on Viren’s surprised and frightened shoulder. He had appeared in the exact same manner as he had in the Crypts when he had abducted Acrypha.

“Release him this instant, Mavar,” Ryland said.

The words echoed in the chamber of collapsing Living Mana. There was so much threat in those few words, so much unbridled promise of pure eradication, Acrypha found her heart skipping beats and her lungs momentarily refused to keep supplying her body with lifegiving air.

“Everything will be perfectly fine, Ryland,” Mavar said, not missing a beat. If he was affected by the sheer sinister promise radiating off Ryland in his blistering and burning Incarnation form, then he didn’t show it at all. “All you and these children need to do is follow my lead.”

“Not a chance, Mavar. You’ve violated everyone’s trust. You’ve caused this entire mess. None of this would have happened had you not let your madness take a hold of you.”

The Demiurge’s grip tightened on Viren’s shoulder, who winced. “Madness? You think it’s mad to want to rid the Realms of a hated enemy? You think it isn’t mad to go around collecting little trinkets turning into Calamities?”

“Deflecting isn’t going to work. Release Viren and Acrypha now, or I will be forced to step in.”

Mavar’s form started glowing. “You don’t know what you’re so desperate to stop just to save two children. Why are you three so selfish? Can you not think of all the other children whose parents won’t be murdered by Living Mana? Can you not think of other families who won’t be torn apart at our success?”

Just like his words at the Crypts, they were once again poisoned honey. Acrypha really, truly wanted to believe him. If her sacrifice really did mean the end of Living Mana everywhere, then she was prepared to lay down her life for it.

But what was happening to the Living Mana was not at all something worthy of being eradicated any longer. They were literally inside its bowels, and it was doing absolutely nothing to them.

“What are you truly enacting, Mavar?” Ryland asked. “You’ve kept us in the dark for long enough.”

“I’ve already told you—”

“Enough of your half-truths. Enough. I’ve seen the cracks that began spreading the moment you broke into my barrier. I noted how you froze when I called upon the Overrealm. Enough with the poorly hidden lies, Mavar. Answer me, now!”

Mavar remained mostly silent, content to just let his power grow and simmer, content to hold onto Viren. The impasse they were stuck on left no room for doubt. There was no more hiding for the Demiurge. The inescapable truth was that he really did have some ulterior motive.

Something, according to Ryland, to do with the Great Gods themselves.

Acrypha’s eyes widened. No, he couldn’t be attempting that right? That was insane to even think about.

But as the expression on Mavar’s face shifted, as the realization dawned that this man was more unhinged than he had appeared, a sinking feeling took hold at the pit of Acrypha’s stomach. They were dealing with a madman.

“Fine, then,” Mavar spat. “If you really must know, I intend to become a Great God. I’m tired of being shackled to a single plane by this Grand Frame that you all worship and slave after.”

“I had an inkling it would be something like this,” Ryland said, while both Acrypha and Viren gasped.

She had been suspecting something this megalomaniacal for a little while now, but it was one thing to suspect. It was wholly another to have it be so brazenly admitted.

“Don’t give me that look,” Mavar said. His grip on Viren’s shoulder was on the verge of crushing. “I of all people deserve ascension. I have led a whole world, created an entire civilization, maintained, protected, and ruled it for centuries. I am about to achieve the greatest accomplishment in all the Realms. If not me, then who? Who else could deserve to be a Great God?”

Acrypha just shook her head. There was no point in listening to his ramblings any further. That a man of such insanity had been allowed to do all this broke her suspension of disbelief.

She looked to Viren. The only thing she had to do here was get Viren out from Mavar’s clutches. She couldn’t let anything happen to him. But how? Mavar’s death grip was even preventing Ryland from acting.

But he was stepping forward finally, standing right before that barrier of Living Mana. “Enough, Mavar. You and I both know that I have more than enough power to stop you before you get started on foolish notions of ascension.”

Mavar shook his head. “Ha, I know you wouldn’t dare do anything while I have this brat with me!”

“Wouldn’t I? Think for a moment. I attack you, you harm Viren, and then I close in on you and end you once and for all. Is that how you wish for things to proceed?”

The tension was getting so strong, Acrypha was starting to feel like she was being choked. Ryland’s logic wasn’t wrong. There was a reason the Demiurge was resorting to using a hostage to keep Ryland at bay, and it could only be because he knew that he couldn’t match up to his opponent when all was said and done.

But Mavar continued to be belligerent. Where was he getting this sort of confidence from?

“You’re working under the foolish assumption that I can be stopped,” the Demiurge said. “But that’s incorrect. Behold, the might of a rising Great God!”

Acrypha’s heart started galloping in her chest as the Living Mana surged. Ryland rushed forward to try and secure Viren, moving faster than her eyes could note him. But it didn’t work. Not even when he clearly attempted to use his rift as well. The barrier of Living Mana turned fully opaque, occluding him from view. Beyond him, the titanic creature roared.

Right. It was up to Acrypha.

But she was only able to whirl to face Viren before the Living Mana was swarming their chamber. It washed her away, creating a pocket of free space that allowed her to continue breathing, but carrying her away like flotsam caught in a flood all the same.

She cried out Viren’s name, but it was pointless. Everything was awash in the Living Mana now. Light and power sparked and blitzed through it all, dissolving any spells she might have tried to cast. Acrypha could only rely on the fact that the Living Mana didn’t want her dead, regardless of what Mavar intended. She only hoped that grace extended to Viren as well.

Then she was out. For the first time in a while, she had been ejected out into the cosmos. She was actually free.

If only she could have revelled in that freedom instead of worrying about Viren.

There was a lot going on in the wider cosmos that had her trying to gawk. All those golden cracks, all the ways that enormous black barrier blotting out the stars was failing. But she forced herself to focus on the present.

Acrypha was starting to wonder how exactly she’d go back to where the Demiurge had been. The collapsing enormity of that combined Pure Manifestation had ejected her quite far away. Most of the spells she had to aid her motion relied on an atmosphere. It was actually surprising she was still managing to breathe and not freeze to death.

Ryland appeared next to her all of a sudden. “I’m glad you at least are safe, Acrypha.”

She felt the same way. But there were more important matters afoot. “Sir, Viren—”

“I’ve confirmed he’s still alive. We just need to rescue him.” He looked around briefly. “But first, we need to act. Mavar has started his ascension. And if we don’t take the right precautions, we’ll get caught up in the Overrealm.”
