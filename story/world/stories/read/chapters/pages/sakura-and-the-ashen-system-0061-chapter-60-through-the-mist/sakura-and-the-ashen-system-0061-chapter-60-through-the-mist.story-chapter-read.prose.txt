"Close the gates!" the senior guard on duty barked. "Stop her!"

Now he knew, and the whole prison knew, that Sakura was an impostor. That something extremely bad was happening. And the Castle instantly threw its forces into stopping it.

Sakura snapped the reins.

"Go! Faster!"

The horses surged forward, and the wagon lurched so hard the girl barely stayed on the driver’s bench. Orders and shouts rang out. One of the workers rushed toward the front of the wagon, but immediately jumped back, nearly ending up under the hooves.

The gates that had been opened so Sakura could pass quickly began closing again, cutting off her path of retreat. The iron gate leaves moved toward each other, and the gap between them grew narrower with every second. Sakura ducked as if that could help and snapped the reins aggressively again.

"Come on, girls! More!"

The horses raced straight toward the closing gap. The right side of the wagon slammed into one gate leaf, and the impact ran through the whole wagon, tilting it slightly. Something cracked behind her, the wagon shuddered painfully, but the wheels did not stop.

The wagon burst outside at the last second. The gate leaves slammed shut behind her with a dull iron blow.

There was no time to think about who had been cut off from the pursuit, but the fact that she had slipped out at the last moment was a huge advantage. After that, the green mist swallowed everything at once. The yard disappeared, the gates disappeared, and the Castle became a dark shadow behind her, almost impossible to see. All that remained before her eyes was murky green, the horses’ backs, and the barely visible road beneath the wheels.

At that moment, Sakura really hoped the tarp on the wagon was secured well enough, and if it was not, that Gilbert and Lir had managed to change into protective suits in time. Before the wagon was swallowed by this green hell.

Sakura drove on, barely seeing where she was going. Panic pressed in and tangled her thoughts, because she could already hear horns and the sounds of pursuit behind her. Sakura kept to the road from memory, then a wheel jumped on a stone, the wagon lurched to the side, and she realized she had already slid off onto the roadside, and getting back was not working. In the mist, everything mixed together: road, bumps, wet grass, low bushes.

Behind her, she could hear the pounding of horses. Sakura still had not fully understood how this was possible, but the poisonous mist did not affect horses, and maybe other animals too. They calmly entered and left the Castle grounds as if the mist itself had been adjusted to kill only people. There was probably some local scientific or even magical explanation for it, but now was definitely not the time to look for it. The important thing was that her horses kept dragging the wagon where Lir and Gilbert were hidden. The important thing was that they were moving away from the Castle. But it was no less important to shake off the pursuit, which was catching up fast.

Inside the wagon, something heavy slammed against the side.

"Hold on!" Sakura shouted, not even knowing if they heard her.

Something scraped across the roof above her. Sakura nearly struck with her knife before she realized it was not a guard. A hand in a green glove appeared from under the tarp, then a second. Gilbert pulled himself up to the front frame and, with difficulty, climbed onto the driver’s bench.

He was already wearing a protective suit. The mask hid his face, but he was moving badly, far too slowly for Gilbert. Still, it was Gilbert.

"Are you an idiot?!" burst out of Sakura.

"Not the best time for tenderness," the mage answered hoarsely and collapsed beside her, grabbing the edge of the driver’s bench.

Sakura exhaled heavily through the filter.

"And Lir?"

"Inside. In a suit. Alive."

Sakura felt an incredible wave of relief after learning that he was alive after all. Lir was alive. And Gilbert too, even if he was clearly not whole, but alive. And both of them had broken out. And now all three of them were outside the Castle grounds. How could all that not be good news? They had done it. Their insane, unbelievably risky plan had worked. They had gone through so much, but now they were together, and almost free. Gilbert had not lied. He had really managed to tear Lir out of the prison.

Sakura breathed out from that relief, and then the rear wheel hit too hard. At first, Sakura thought they had run over a stone, but soon the impact repeated. Then the wagon began to shake as if something below was hitting the axle from inside. Sakura understood at once: that wheel. The one she had loosened, the one that had flown off in the prison yard, and the one that had been hurriedly put back on. It could not withstand this insane race through grass, swamp, and bumps.

"No," Sakura said nervously.

Gilbert sharply turned his head. Another impact. The wagon pulled to the side. Sakura tugged the reins, but the horses could no longer straighten the course. The rear end sank, jumped, and then a dry crack came from below.

Gilbert managed to grab Sakura by the shoulder when the wagon spun sideways. Bundles rolled dully inside. Something struck the side with force. The horses neighed, the harness pulled tight to the limit, and the whole wagon nearly tipped onto its side.

The lead mare was the first to panic. She jerked to the side, but the harness yanked her back sharply. There was a short, ugly crunch that made Sakura go cold inside. The mare collapsed onto her side.

"Damn it!"

Sakura jumped down first. Her feet hit wet ground, pain immediately shot through her knee, but she held herself up and rushed to the horse. The mare thrashed, wheezed, tried to rise, but her front leg had bent at an unnatural angle. Gilbert climbed down after her, almost falling to one knee.

"The other one!" Sakura shouted. "Hold the other one!"

She drew her knife and rushed to the wounded mare’s straps. If they did not free her now, she could cripple herself completely in the harness or drag the whole wagon with her. Sakura’s fingers slipped over wet hide and muddy straps. The mare thrashed, tossing her head, and Sakura had to press her shoulder against the horse’s neck so she would not take a hoof to the face.

Gilbert swore through his teeth as he hurried toward the second horse. That one already smelled blood and panic. The animal was tearing away, tossing her head and striking the ground with her hooves. Gilbert grabbed her by the bridle, but at that moment his body failed him. The mage bent sharply, and his fingers loosened for a heartbeat. Sakura saw the rein slip free.

"Gilbert!"

The second mare bolted. A strap tore loose, and the horse vanished into the green mist, dissolving almost at once in the murky sludge of air. Gilbert tried to rush after her, but it looked like he was failing again. The mage grabbed the side of the wagon and froze, head lowered.

Sakura could not run after the mare. First, she simply would not make it in time, and second, she was still standing by the wounded horse, cutting the last strap while the mare wheezed and thrashed on the ground. When the harness finally loosened, Sakura stumbled back and drew a heavy breath through the filter.

They had no usable horses left. One had escaped, and the second lay on the ground and could not even get up. The broken wagon stood crookedly, its rear sunk down, missing a wheel.

Its rear door swung open with a dull thud. Lir climbed out. His suit was crumpled and a little twisted, his mask sat crookedly too, and his hands were trembling. The fisherman’s son jumped down onto the ground, swayed, and nearly fell.

"I... I’m alive," he said, as if he was not sure himself. "I think."

"Good," Sakura breathed. "Good. Just stay close."

She needed to think quickly, but for a moment her head went blank. Without horses, the wagon became a useless box in the mud. With one broken mare, they were not going anywhere either. But abandoning everything and walking on foot through the green mist with wounded Gilbert and Lir fresh out of a cell seemed almost as insane as trying to swim across a river. And behind them, the noise of voices and hooves could still be heard. It was far away, but gradually and relentlessly drawing closer.

Gilbert pushed himself heavily away from the side and straightened.

"We go on foot," he said.

Sakura looked at him sharply.

"What?"

"We take only what we can carry and leave."

"Can you even walk?"

"Sakura," the mage said hoarsely. "If we stay here, they’ll take us in a minute. If we go, we have a chance to break away. The mist zone is not endless."

It was a terrible plan, but the only thing worse was having no plan at all.

Gilbert pulled a small bundle of spare filters and fabric from the wagon, Sakura grabbed a water flask and her knife, and Lir simply adjusted his suit and mask. Everything unnecessary stayed in the wagon with the wet bundles, the broken flask, and the ruined wheel. They left the road almost immediately, moving into wet grass and low bushes, but the mist did not fully muffle the sounds of pursuit. Behind them, shouts, horns, and the wet slap of hooves through mud grew louder.

They had not gone far from the wagon when figures of riders and guards on foot began to emerge from the green murk. They were Castle guards, all in protective suits and armed. They had found them. The guards advanced in a half-ring, careful but confident. The mist hindered everyone, but there were too many of them.

Sakura understood that even with her magic, with her knife, and with Gilbert beside her, they were unlikely to win. There were too many guards. Gilbert was too weak. Lir probably could not protect himself at all.

"Stop!" one of the guards shouted.

He stepped closer. His gaze moved over all three of them, but froze on Sakura. Maybe the Gray Veil had smeared, or the Castle guards had turned out smarter than she would have liked.

"Sakura Arai!" he shouted. "It’s Sakura Arai!"

That was it. The end.

Several crossbows rose at once. Sakura heard Lir gasp in fear. Gilbert shifted slightly, covering her with his shoulder, though if a fan of bolts flew at them, there would be no point in that anymore. Fire appeared on Gilbert’s hand, unstable and strangely pulsing.

"Put it out," the guard barked, aiming at Gilbert. "Now."

Gilbert did not. The flame on his hand flared brighter, then jerked as if struck by an invisible wind and went out for a moment. Sakura saw his fingers trembling. Gilbert was trying to hold the spell, but his body was failing him again, and it seemed the System still had not forgiven the core that had been under his skin.

One of the crossbowmen took a step to the side, choosing an angle. Another was no longer aiming at Sakura, but at Gilbert. That was reasonable: Gilbert was not as valuable alive as Sakura. Besides, no matter what, right now Gilbert, even weakened, was more dangerous than Sakura and Lir put together.

"Gilbert," Sakura said anxiously.

The mage did not answer and did not put out his fire. He was ready to fight and defend them to the end. Lir was breathing short and ragged. It was obvious he was terrified, that more than anything in the world, he did not want to return to the cage he had just been torn out of. But he did not try to run. Lir stood with them, ready to accept any outcome, even the worst one.

The guards tightened the ring. Sakura gripped the knife harder. Drop it? Try to hit the first one? Rush to the side? Use a skill? Everything seemed equally bad. At best, she would manage to wound one or two. Or she would be shot first.

"Last time," the guard said. "Weapons on the ground."

Sakura did not lower the knife, and Gilbert did not put out the flame. And for one long, vile moment, everything went quiet. The silence before a storm.

And in that silence, Sakura suddenly heard the wind. At first, it seemed to her that it was just the mist stirring the wet grass at her feet, but then thin voices emerged in the rustle. They whispered something incomprehensible, overlapping each other, and Sakura caught only separate words:

"Not the end."

"Not here."

Sakura froze, not understanding whether the voices were trying to warn her, or whether panic was already breaking her mind from inside. The crossbows were still pointed straight at them, and Gilbert was barely holding the trembling flame on his hand.

The children’s voices kept whispering:

"Coming."

"Big."

"Black."

Then they suddenly fell silent, as if they too were holding their breath.

And then something growled low and threateningly in the mist behind the guards’ backs. Even the ground vibrated under their feet. Sakura flinched.

The nearest horse went mad with fear. It neighed, reared, and threw its rider into the wet grass. Then one of the guards screamed, and Pampli stepped out of the green murk.

Mist ran down his back and left no burns or marks on him. He looked as if the green death around them had nothing to do with him at all. Like the horses, he did not fear the poison in the mist.

Pampli struck with his paw, and the nearest guard flew aside, crashing into another. One of the enemies tried to strike with a spear, but the shaft cracked against Pampli’s side like a dry branch.

"Pampli!" Sakura breathed joyfully.

The beast turned his head toward her and gave a short greeting growl.

Gilbert was the first to come to his senses.

"On him," the mage suddenly said.

Sakura did not have time to understand. Gilbert had already pulled her by the arm.

"Quickly!"

Pampli crouched on his own. Sakura helped Lir climb first. He barely understood what he was doing and simply clung to the obsidian back with both hands. Then Sakura climbed up, and Gilbert settled behind her.

Everything happened very quickly. The guards were shouting, and the first bolts were already flying. Pampli knocked a couple of bolts away almost literally with his paw, and another bolt struck Gilbert’s magic and bounced aside.

Pampli burst forward. Sakura pressed herself to his cold back, holding Lir with one hand and feeling Gilbert’s hands on her waist. The green mist closed around them so tightly that it was as if the world behind their backs had disappeared.

Pampli carried them through the green haze, and Sakura no longer tried to understand where the road was, where the forest was, or how far behind the Castle had fallen. The shouts behind them gradually died down. The mist hid them from other people’s eyes, Pampli moved farther and farther away with confidence, and only then did Sakura allow herself, for the first time in a long while, to believe that they had escaped. That they were finally free.
