Darkmoor’s Crucible.

Ace and his team had finally made it.

With a deafening boom, the massive doorway slammed open to reveal a seemingly endless darkness. The darkness pouring through the opening still curled around their legs like a midnight fog, cascading across the stone platform and leaving ribbons of frost in its wake.

For a moment, there was nothing but the void to greet them.

Slowly, Ace’s eyes adjusted to the pitch-black nothing beyond the doors. He caught snippets, at first, mainly flashes of light that didn’t quite make sense. Over time, he found that before him stood not a room or corridor, but a yawning abyss that seemed to devour everything it touched. Veins of a phosphorescent purple glow pulsed through the darkness in blinding rushes of light. The stone floor where the shadow essence touched transformed, developing crystalline fractal patterns that grew and receded as if the cavern itself could breathe.

“Still. Do. Not. Like,” Marcus said under his breath.

The temperature plummeted as the darkness flowed around their ankles—not the passive cold of winter air, but an aggressive chill that drove needles of frost into Ace’s skin, only for the ice to evaporate with wisps of black steam. The shadow essence carried weight and texture—thicker than fog, heavier than water, clinging to him with the persistent grip of something that recognized prey.

“Welcome to the Shadow Realm,” Ace said dryly.

He recognized the distinctive feel of it—the same power he tapped into during Shadow Realm Immersion, but infinitely more concentrated and wild. It carried the scent of lightning-struck stone and tasted like copper pennies dissolved in winter air. The cold that emanated from beyond the doors wasn’t the absence of heat but an active force that siphoned warmth from their bodies.

With the doors fully open, they stood at the precipice between worlds. Behind them stretched the familiar brutality of Floor 1—a place of monsters and murder but with rules they understood. Ahead lay the unpredictable dangers of the Shadow Realm, where even the most basic assumptions might prove fatal.

“I’ll go first,” Ace said, mustering every ounce of confidence he could. “Wait until I’m through before following. One at a time.”

He crossed the threshold, and a ripple of light shivered through the air with his body at the center. The sensation defied description—reality folded around him like paper being crumpled. His body momentarily separated into countless shadow particles, each retaining some fragment of consciousness before violently reassembling. The surreal experience lasted both an eternity and an instant, and he was glad when it finally faded.

When Ace reformed, his breath caught in his throat.

He stood in a colossal throne room suspended in an absolute void. At the far end of the impossible chamber, a massive platform made of gold rose from the floor like a shimmering island in a sea of shadow. Gilded stairs ascended in three distinct sets that converged at the top.

On the platform stood three thrones, arranged in a perfect arc that faced the entrance. Two of them flanked a central seat that rose a full head higher than its companions, its positioning making it clear that only one soul truly ruled this place. Each throne was crafted from the same living gold as the stairs, their surfaces etched with runes that shifted and changed moment by moment. Curved spines extended from the center throne’s high back like frozen rays of sunlight, each tipped with blood.

The seats themselves remained empty, yet they carried an unmistakable sense of recent occupation—indentations in the cushions, lingering warmth that Ace could sense even from a distance, and most disturbing of all, what looked like fresh droplets of blood that occasionally slid down the armrests and disappeared into the floor.

Beyond the thrones, the cavern had no walls, no ceiling, nothing at all but an endless stretch of emptiness that flowed out in all directions. The only light came from what appeared to be a crimson sun behind the thrones, its light perpetually eclipsed by a black disk. The surreal star cast blood-red light across the gilded floor. Massive stone columns rose from an abyss beyond the edges of the ground, stretching endlessly into the empty nothing above.

One by one, his teammates materialized beside him with varying degrees of discomfort. Marcus stumbled to his knees, dry-heaving from the transition. Tara steadied herself against Ace’s back, her slender fingers pressing into his shoulder blades as her face drained of color. Olivia alone seemed unfazed, her eyes wide with wonder as she turned slowly in place, drinking in the impossible scene.

“Three guesses who those thrones belong to,” Ace said under his breath.

“It feels like…” Tara trailed off as she took a cautious step forward. “It feels like they were just here.”

“You noticed that, too?” Marcus let out a sigh of relief. “Good. I thought I was going crazy.”

Ace laughed. “We’re in a death maze, running an errand for a sadistic child-goddess, about to face down three immortal sisters and whatever traps await in another realm entirely.” He grinned and tilted his head toward the telepath beside him. “I think we passed the point of crazy a long time ago.”

“Alright,” Marcus said with a shrug. “That’s fair.”

A vibration hummed through the ground—subtle at first, little more than a mere tremble beneath their feet—and it quickly grew into a tremor that rattled loose stones across the lakebed path behind them. The sound shook Ace’s bones with a deep, rhythmic pulse that shook the walls of water beyond the still-open doors.

Ace spun around. Beyond the entryway, the walls of water that had stood impossibly suspended on either side of their path began to ripple. The corpses suspended within the lake shifted, their preserved faces turning toward Ace and his team as the current through the water grew more violent.

“Something’s wrong,” Tara said.

Just as she spoke, at the far end of the lake, the barrier failed. Water crashed down onto the exposed lakebed with the force of a collapsing building. The roar echoed through the air, sending a cold gust billowing through the chamber and drowning all other sound as the deluge rushed toward them.

“Well, that’s not good,” Marcus muttered.

The wave gathered momentum, swallowing the path they had walked only moments before. What had been an orderly collapse at the far end transformed into chaos as it approached the open doors—a churning wall of water that carried broken branches, stones, and the disturbed dead within its surge.

And it was headed right for them, toward doors they couldn’t close.

Light refracted through the advancing deluge, casting prismatic patterns across the chamber threshold. The sound grew from a roar to a physical presence, pressing against their ears and chests as the pressure front raced ahead of the water. Ace stared transfixed as a body tumbled within the approaching wave, its desiccated arms outstretched as if reaching for them.

“The doors!” Olivia’s voice cut through the thunder of the approaching water.

Sure enough, the massive stone slabs were swinging inward, moving with ponderous inevitability. Whatever ancient mechanism controlled them had triggered, responding to the collapsing barrier.

Water surged to within twenty yards of the entrance, and yet the doors still slowly closed.

Ten yards—and the doors continued their slow arc, as if they had all the time in the world to shut.

“They won’t close in time!” Marcus shouted as he backed deeper into the chamber.

Ace stood his ground, calculating the doors’ movement against the water’s speed. He refused to budge, and he wouldn’t run.

This had to be just another ploy to test them. To gauge their courage in the face of what they couldn’t control.

The gap between the closing doors narrowed to six feet as the first spray hit Ace’s face – cold and carrying the metallic tang of blood. The wave loomed before him, its crest rising far above the height of the doorway itself.

Five feet left.

Four.

The pressure front hit him full force, driving the air from his lungs as the first rush of water slapped against the threshold.

Three feet.

Two.

The doors slammed together with a sound like thunder, cutting off the flood in a clean line. A puddle of water that had breached the threshold pooled around their feet, but the deluge itself remained sealed outside. The sealed doors groaned and creaked as unimaginable pressure built on the other side.

But they held, and Ace’s team was unharmed.

Dry, perhaps, but not safe.

Not in this place.

In the sudden silence, Ace became aware of his own ragged breathing. Water dripped from somewhere deep in the cavern in a slow trickle, the steady plop echoing through the empty throne room like a countdown to something terrible.

“Guess there’s no going back that way,” Tara said.

Behind them, the chamber’s darkness seemed to deepen, beckoning them forward into the Crucible’s depths.

Time to face the music.

Ace drew Moonfall from the sheath at his side and led the way toward the thrones—the only way he could go in the otherwise endless void around them. Instantly, his team fell into formation at his side without so much as a word being spoken. He didn’t even have to use the hivemind—they just moved as a single unit now, their synchronization born not from training but from blood spilled together, from battles survived side by side.

Three weeks ago, they’d been strangers thrust together by circumstance and fear. Now, they moved as one organism, each compensating for the others’ weaknesses and amplifying their collective strength.

The System had wanted them to turn on each other, to break apart under pressure, but she’d underestimated what shared suffering could create amongst survivors.

“Whatever happens next,” Ace said, his voice carrying across the impossible space. “We face it together.”

Tara moved closer, her shoulder brushing against his in silent agreement. Marcus shifted to guard their rear, his usual nervous chatter replaced by watchful silence. Olivia completed their circle on Ace’s other side.

When they reached the bottom of the stairs leading up to the thrones, they all paused.

They stood at the threshold of something greater and more terrible than they could comprehend. They were four vampires bound not by blood or loyalty to the System, but by choice—the choice to protect one another when the world had offered them nothing but violence and betrayal.

Somewhere in the darkness, the sisters were watching. Ace could feel eyes on the back of his head, and though he peered over his shoulder, he found nothing.

In the crimson half-light, facing unknown horrors, they stood together. This wasn’t the afterlife any of them had chosen. It wasn’t the future any of them had wanted. But at this moment, as the ancient darkness beckoned them forward, Ace finally understood what had been happening since their first kill.

Every drop of blood spilled, every battle survived, every death witnessed had been transforming them—not just into predators with sharper claws and keener senses, but into something far more dangerous.

Something superior.

Something the System could never destroy.

Separate, they were merely lost souls in a nightmare. Together, standing shoulder to shoulder in the heart of writhing darkness, they had become something the System never intended—a rebellion.

Ace grinned, his fangs flashing in the room’s crimson glow. Whatever came next, he and his team were ready.

Darkmoor’s Crucible had begun.
