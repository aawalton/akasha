“So you really can’t heal?”

“He’d harvested from me over twenty-two times….” Her voice shook. “My vitality is spent, my soul, tired. These will not work on me.”

What a sick bastard that Leech King was!

Nolan made sure she finished the food and the water before he left. She hid in the same tuck of darkness we’d been in a few minutes ago, just in case someone wanted to pay her a visit. We made for the exit, but as we were about to pass the dead bodies of the scumbags, I eyed a ring of keys, the same one the orc had. Yoink. These were certainly going to be useful! Even though they… didn’t have any shanks? Yeah, just a ring of crescent copper bows. Peculiar.

Nolan and I followed Elara’s instructions, but the Ulmah wanted us to go another way—up. Which meant that there was a possibility that Klepto Girl and our items were on two different levels of this hell mansion.

‘I hear voices, Nolan,’ I said as we stopped halfway down the steps. Whispers, cries, pleas. Then someone shouted, a rough burly voice that had me thinking that one of those prison guard orcs were down there.

‘Yeah, I hear them too,’ he said, and then he turned around and asked, ‘Let me get the lasso.’

‘It’s a whip!’ I corrected.

‘Trade you for a healing potion and a mana restoration vial.’

I gave him my weapon, and he gave me some of his looted vials. When the sound of a snap followed by an ear-piercing cry echoed up the stairs, Nolan turned around again. At the end of the flight of steps was a heavy wooden door hanging ajar, the torchlights from within the dungeon peering through. The gap allowed us a glimpse of what was in store for us inside—two masked orcs this time, and a row of filled cages.

At least these weren’t filled with corpses.

Men, women, even children—this Zev guy didn’t discriminate. Fae were packed in cages, some five per cage, others double that. These captives didn’t look as bad as Elara did, which confirmed that we were in the right place.

‘Zev had recently kidnapped these people, Nolan.’ I looked up to him as he tucked the Ulmah away. ‘So, what’s the plan? They are too far for your last trick to work.’

‘So we improvise.’ Suddenly, Nolan pulled out his teleporter crystal, you know, the valuable one that the shaman gave him, and chucked it across the dungeon! His trajectory was precise, the crystal landing just a yard in front of the lash-happy orc who was beating on a man suspended in chains within the holding cell. His friend who was rolling a cart of knives shouted and pointed a finger at Nolan, but by the time they registered we were here, the teleporter crystal did its thing. In an instant, both orcs disappeared—to where? I hadn’t the slightest clue….

‘I… didn’t even know teleporter items could do that….’

Nolan smirked. ‘This one does.’

We proceeded to walk down the rows of captives, their faces lighting up once they saw us. Whispers filtered the dungeon, talking about us being heroes here to save them in their native tongue. Others knew the same language Nolan did, and when he approached the cell in the middle, the one with the suspended man in chains, he addressed them and said, “You’re all safe now.”

They all collectively took a breath of relief. The sound was definitely uplifting to hear!

“I’d just teleported those two bastards to nowhere land, and judging by the rotation of these guards, we should have enough time to unlock all of these cages.”

“Excuse me, sir,” a small fae boy said, approaching Nolan from behind the bars. “You sent them both away, and one of them had the keys.”

“That’s fine, my dragon friend here has a set of keys,” he said, looking down to me. I didn’t even know he noticed I’d snatched them up!

Well, I guess if I hadn’t he would have anyway.

I pulled them out of my inventory and handed the ring to him between my teeth. ‘Surely, one of these has to open these cages, right?’

‘That’s the plan. These cells though, they aren’t your typical rods of metal. There’s something more powerful keeping these fae caged up. Remember, fae are naturally gifted like dragons. If they were able to, they would have already broken through those metal bars. You don’t see it, but these cells are laced with a protective barrier limiting fae powers.’

“Is he… talking to that baby dragon?” a woman said when we grew silent and exchanged looks. “Remarkable.”

‘Is that a Zev exclusive power?’

‘Not sure. Let’s hope it’s not. I’ve heard of a legendary item with that capability, but I’ve never seen it in person.’

Nolan proceeded to try the different keys to unlock the prison cells. The shanks were in fact invisible, each of them materializing once close to the lock of the cell. I wasn’t sure about the methodology behind that strange phenomenon, but Nolan said that it had something to do with the shanks matching the same aura of the laced inhibitor on the bars. He went on to say that the bars themselves were protected from being destroyed, so my bright idea of using [Acid Bath] wouldn’t work. After a minute of fishing for the right key, he opened the first of many cages.

The fae from inside poured out like water breaking through a dam. Some fell to their knees and wept, while others rushed to help those still locked away. I scurried between legs, dodging the crowd as Nolan worked through the remaining cells, the click of each lock releasing another wave of gratitude.

“Thank you, thank you,” a mid-aged fae woman sobbed, clutching Nolan’s arm. Her face was gaunt but not yet hollow like Elara’s—she hadn’t been here long enough for the worst to happen.

“Save your thanks until we’re out of here,” Nolan muttered, the woman loosening her grip on him as he moved to the next cell. “They’ll notice their missing guards soon enough.”

The suspended man groaned as two others carefully lowered him from his chains. Blood trickled from welts across his back, his skin an angry patchwork of fresh wounds.

“How many more of you are there?” Nolan asked, his voice low but urgent as he worked another key into a stubborn lock.

An elderly fae with silver hair stepped forward. “We were brought in with thirty-three. They’ve already taken three for...” His voice cracked. “It is uncommon for a human to assist us fae. You must have friends or family like us.”

“Or, I just might hate seeing innocent people suffer,” Nolan replied tersely, freeing the last cage. “Now, there’s another survivor upstairs—a fae woman named Elara. She’s in bad shape, but she’s hiding. You need to collect her on your way out.”

“Elara lives?” A young fae man with copper-colored hair pushed through the crowd, his breath chasing him. “Long blond hair, big eyes? And a star marking on her wrist?”

“Yeah, that’s her.”

“She’s my mother—she was taken days before us!”

The young man’s eyes filled with tears. “I thought...” He swallowed hard. “We must hurry then!”

“Now, hold on. We need a game plan, here. We can’t just rush an escape.”

“Then, what’s the plan?” someone from the far end of the aisle asked. Nolan surveyed the freed captives, most of whom were helping others to stand or comforting those too traumatized to move. “Listen to me,” he announced, his voice carrying the authority of someone used to commanding in crisis. “There’s a way out through the upper corridor. Two guards are dead, but there might be more in the area. Since your powers are shot, you need to navigate with stealth. Stay quiet, stay low, and follow the path until you reach a split hallway. The one on the left takes you to a door that leads you to a service entrance that should put you outside the mansion grounds. Before you take that path, check the other road outside the fork for survivors.”

“And what about you?” another one asked.

“I have business to deal with, and will stay behind. Speaking of which, have any of you seen a short blond human girl?”

“We haven’t seen any human girls,” someone from the back of the crowd answered. When I turned around, I saw an old man with a bitter look spread across his thin face. “We haven’t seen anyone but orcs.” He scoffed. “I can’t wait until the beastkin empire annihilates all of those rotten filthy green monsters!”

“Funny you say that, because the man these orcs are working for is fae.”

The fae slowly craned his head to Nolan upset.

“Pure-blooded, too. I have no reason to lie to you. Zeverus is the one who captured all of you and brought you here for organ harvesting.”

That H word really rattled the crowd. I guess their captives didn’t clarify why they were being kidnapped.

“He had your items forfeited, and stripped each and every one of you powerless so he could fuel his black market business. He works for King Leech, and together, they have been targeting fae communities for years, taking what they need and discarding the rest.”

“We are… victims of the Leech King?” a woman said in a quivered breath. “By the gods!”

“We are going to die!” another cried, and just like that, everyone’s panic levels skyrocketed.

“Hey!” Nolan shouted. “All of you, relax. No one else is going to die here tonight. You do as I say, take the path, and head to the forest. Keep each other safe.”

They nodded, the fae pairing off into groups just as Nolan had instructed. They disappeared one by one, Nolan leaving the keys with Elara’s son. I wondered why Nolan allowed them to leave and not help us. Then I thought about the type of person Nolan was. He wouldn’t want those fae to get hurt. The gunslinger without his gun hardly had the tools he needed to protect himself, let alone a group of powerless fae.

Man, I really hoped whatever hex Zev casted on them was temporary.

Even with the description we had of the thief, they didn’t recall seeing anyone like her. And it was hard asking about her brother, since we had no idea what he looked like.

I then turned to Nolan and asked, ‘Why question them about Klepto Girl’s whereabouts?’

‘That’s a silly question. We need to find her so we can move on with your quest.’

‘If that’s the case, then why don’t you just follow the Ulmah?’ I insisted. ‘Doesn’t it bring you to what your heart desires? It’s bringing us to Scarlet, isn’t it? I’m sure wherever your gun is, the rest of our stuff would be there, too.’

‘I… can’t be too sure. Like Kauli said, it tracks what your heart desires, and what your heart desires doesn’t always correlate to what you want.’

‘True, but we really don’t have much of a choice but to follow it. Even if they had seen her, who’s to say they know where she is now? And who’s to say they know where our stolen stuff is? I say we continue to follow the ladybug.’

‘You say that like it’s that simple.’

‘Look, either way you slice it, we are heading toward the path of uncertainty here. We just slaughtered two pricks working for the Leech King, and banished two more to the shadow realm, each pair on different levels of this hellhole! Now, unless we are going to scale this entire building top to bottom, then I suggest we use the Ulmah to save us time we literally don’t have!’

Nolan paused for a moment and considered my request. The Ulmah had gotten us this far—I trusted it to get us to the finish line.

‘We don’t have an escape route,’ Nolan reminded me. ‘The teleporter crystal is gone. If shit goes south….’

‘Hey, you’re Sol Badguy. The shogun, the 14th Hokage! The main character! Erm, well, eheh, I’m the main character.’ I giggled.

He deadpanned me. ‘I seriously don’t know what the hell you’re talking about.’

‘What I am trying to say is, Nolan Black has found himself in worse scenarios, and walked out victorious! For Christ sake, he raided a tier five dungeon with no party! So what if he doesn’t have his magical scrolls? And so what if he’s tight on battle items? All I know is giving up isn’t in his vocabulary. A wise man once told me when things seem impossible, there is always another way. So let’s find it, together.’

‘… Your motivational speech was cheesy, at best.’

‘But did it work?!’

He sighed exasperatingly. ‘Yeah….’ He scratched the back of his head and pulled out the Ulmah again, then off we went.
