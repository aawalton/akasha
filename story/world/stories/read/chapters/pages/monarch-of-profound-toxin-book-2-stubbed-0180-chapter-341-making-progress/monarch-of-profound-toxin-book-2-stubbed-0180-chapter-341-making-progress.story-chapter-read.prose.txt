“What’s up with you guys anyway?” Eik asked as he bit into a homemade pastel de nata. He had just returned from his trek into the miasma. “What are you doing all the way over there?”

Almost from the moment he had opened a doorway in the solid crystal dome and walked back into the protected zone, people had reacted to his presence with panic and fear and now huddled up by the largest tent, basically as far away from Eik as they could possibly get.

Between Eik and the rest of the people stood Andihar and Gul as well as Mn’Toakh, who had arrived to help oversee the proceedings with the analysis of the effigy. Andihar had his hands held out toward Eik with his back to the others like a zoo keeper trying to discourage an aggressive polar bear from mauling a child who has fallen into the enclosure.

Chop still sat exactly where he had been sitting hours before, digging around inside his own ear with his pinky finger in search of treasure. Apparently, he didn’t care enough about the situation to look up.

“Eik, listen,” the elf said cautiously as Eik took another bite. Mn’Toakh held her massive bow in what looked like a casual stance at first glance, but the whites of her knuckles as she squeezed the arrow nocked on the string between her fingers betrayed her mask of calm. “Please, just… for once try to act like a normal, reasonable person.”

“What?”

“What you’re holding in your hand… How about you put that down?” Mn’Toakh added.

For a moment, he regarded the half-eaten pastry crumbling between his fingers with a raised eyebrow. “You want my pastel de nata? You know, if you’d like one, all you really have to do is ask.”

“Your other hand…” she muttered.

Eik held up his other hand. They really were so damned ugly. Like, did their teeth have to be like that? Well, at least this spawn of the Lord of the Moon wasn’t laughing. At this point, once Eik got his hands on them, they seldom did.

It had taken a little while, but he had managed to catch one of the stronger spawn. Despite the clear power difference between the two of them, the disgusting fiend never quite seemed to give up on the idea of escaping.

In spite of its greater strength compared to most of the other spawn running around these parts, this one was actually quite small. Only just rivaling a large dog in size, its humanoid head and facial features looked horribly mismatched with the almost reptilian body.

With a fat, powerful tail that easily scraped along the ground as Eik carried it, the only thing that really took away from the crocodilian look was the fact that, similarly to all the other spawn, it sported human hands, legs, fingers, and toes.

And they wiggled separately as they creepily searched for anything to grab. It was absolutely disgusting. Eik was actually quite surprised by the fact that he hadn’t cut off the lot yet at this point.

“You want me to let this guy go?” he asked with a frown.

Without warning, it suddenly lunged for him, toothy maw snapping wildly and senselessly, but before it could make contact, the Supreme Divinity of Toxin rammed a knee into its chin like a sledgehammer and shattered the entirety of its lower facial structure. “See, now, why did you have to go and make me do that? So?” he pushed, wiggling the spawn that now hung slack in his grip from side to side.

“How about you hold onto that for a little while longer?” Gul suggested.

“Oh, he’s harmless, I tell ya,” Eik insisted and raised the spawn above his head for all to see clearly. “However, everybody, this is what we are up against. This is what desires to slaughter your children and spouses, parents and siblings. Take a good look and burn this sight onto the back of your eye. Remember this horror and bring that memory home.”

People stared at the spectacle fixated while Eik continued. “This is reality. We must all work together. All of you are doing your duties in this very moment. You’re doing noble work. Thank you,” he shouted at full lung. “Thank you for stepping up for the good of all.”

Hesitatingly it may have been, but cheers rose up from the crowd and much of the tension seemed to evaporate.

“You didn’t just bring that thing here to be a prop in your motivational speech, did you?” Andihar asked although his face said he might not want to hear the answer.

“Of course not, Andi. Is that really the kind of guy you think I am?”

“Yes,” Gul broke in without a moment of pause.

“Well, I didn’t!” Eik insisted. “I had a good idea, okay? Is that so difficult to believe?”

“Yes,” Gul said, again in an instant.

Eik’s lower lips quivered. “This is workplace harassment!”

“What was your idea, please?” Mn’Toakh interjected.

“Right,” he said and beckoned for everybody to follow him to the semi intact effigy. “I figured, since there is obviously some kind of purpose for the placement of this thing here, albeit a purpose we haven’t been able to ascertain properly yet, there is one thing at our disposal, if anything, that could possibly trigger that purpose,” he said with a grin, shaking the spawn that had now begun to wake up again, the bones in its jaw crackling audibly as they melted back together.

“Good thinking,” one of the ritualists agreed with a nod. “That line of thought makes sense.”

“Behold!” Eik shouted and held the spawn aloft while it struggled frantically in his grip, his fingers digging into its thin, pale skin as if it was a wad of malleable dough. With a grunt, he slammed it into the ground only to drag it through the dirt toward the effigy.

With a grasp like that of steel reinforced a hundred thousand times over, he forced the top of its head to just barely touch the effigy, careful not to let the spawn thrash and destroy the relatively fragile, construction. He held it there for five seconds.

Nothing.

“Hold on a second, everyone,” he announced, frowning as he rubbed the monster’s scalp lightly against the blackened surface of the occult object. “I think there’s some problem with the connection here. Bad reception or something.”

Another ten seconds went by, the spawn complaining more and more as he continued to rub its face harder and harder against the effigy. Why the hell wasn’t this working? It was so obvious that the effigy and the spawn should have something to do with each other, so why were there no reaction?

There should have been… something.

There was always something. Between a bound owner and their bags of holding there was a constant, albeit faint, flow of connecting Ak’ki. The same was true for a swordsman and his personal weapon. An archer and their bow. Even Eik and any of his Profound Toxin that had left his body.

Should he move closer to the large dome that surrounded and protected them from the miasma, monsters, and oppressively heavy atmosphere outside, there would also be an almost imperceptible flow between it and him.

And as an X-ranker, he should have been more than capable of seeing this connection establish between this ugly bastard of a spawn of the Lord of the Moon and the effigy. Especially with the variety of analytic skills and effects currently deployed around the black construct.

“Sorry, Mr. Magnasen,” Mn’Toakh piped up, her grip on her bow slightly more relaxed than it had been when Eik had first arrived with the monster under his arm. “It was a good idea, but it doesn’t always work out the way we hope.”

“It’s only been like twenty seconds!” he protested with frustration. “It’s way too early to say that it’s no good! Maybe it needs blood or something.”

Without another word, he ripped off one of the beast’s forelegs in one, smooth motion and rubbed the torn stump against the effigy while the thing barked and snapped fruitlessly.

The different investigative groups had come closer to observe but still nothing happened.

“You guys try to think of other ways to get this crap to work and I’ll go and get more samples,” Eik said as solid crystal flowed over the spawn’s entire body and encased all but its face and the tip of its tail completely in Profound Toxin.

Four powerful Living Manifestations spawned around it. “Chop,” Eik called and started back toward the edge of the dome. “if anybody here gets hurt while I’m away, it’s bread and water for you for the next five years.”

Reluctantly and with a sigh, the old man got up and wandered over to the spawn and sat down on the chunk of crystal with his scabbard resting across his thighs. Eik nodded to himself and disappeared through a doorway briefly opened in the crystalline dome.

Chop unsheathed his blade as the first of the experts began to approach him and the spawn. He had spent the past few hours studying it—getting to know his new partner. It was a worthy companion no matter how he looked at it.

Ever so slowly, they moved, afraid that the beast would suddenly escape. Despite the ridiculous ease with which Eik had been able to handle it, none of them harbored any delusions about their own ability to do the same.

This monster was stronger than any common S-ranker.

“Let’s see if we can’t figure out something while he’s gone then,” the lead analyst suggested. “M-Mr. Chop…?” he stuttered, used to Eik but still afraid of Chop the Blade. “You don’t mind if we… try a few things, right?”

The Pristine Blade Saint shrugged. “Whatever. Be my guest.”

“Thank you,” the man said, taking the lead with a few hesitating steps.

***

Starts jolted through the gathered researchers when a small section of the dome suddenly rocketed in toward the center, Eik’s foot sticking in through the hole left behind.

With four more spawn of the Lord of the Moon—two in each hand—he wandered into the safe zone, the dome closing back up behind him. All four were snarling loudly, attacking Eik’s back relentlessly. Reflexive shells of blue snapped into existence and easily blocked every single attempt to injure.

One of the spawn was the size of an elephant from old Earth.

“I found some more stuff!” Eik announced happily. “Surely one of these will work, no?”

“We can only hope,” Mn’Toakh said as she came to meet him. “We’ve tried myriad different things while you were away. Nothing’s worked…”

“Well, let’s not lose hope yet, all right?” Eik told her with a smile.

The others stepped back to allow Eik through, the four new arrivals quickly encased near completely in crystal. Wasting no time, Eik shoved the first of the four against the effigy.

After a minute, he opened an enormous gash in its exposed neck and pushed that against the black construct as well. It thrashed against his grip, trying desperately to wiggle out of the inescapable prison. The occasional guttural curse was mixed into the snarling.

It barely seemed to register the black object at all.

Nothing.

Clicking his tongue Eik tossed the monster aside. They would all be recycled as research subjects back in Gimleh.

The second one was the weakest of the five but when Eik shoved its flat, frowning, human face into the rough surface of the effigy, something finally happened.

Once it got within five meters of the effigy, the eyes of the otherwise belligerent beast suddenly glazed over, and a moment later its head lolled forward flaccidly. Quietly and slowly at first, black miasma began to flow from a limp jaw.
