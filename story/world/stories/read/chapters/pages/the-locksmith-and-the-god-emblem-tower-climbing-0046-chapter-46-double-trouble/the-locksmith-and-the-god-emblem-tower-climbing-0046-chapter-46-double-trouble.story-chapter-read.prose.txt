The smell of beer and damp wood filled the air. It could be described as a bar, although the word didn't do justice to the dust visible in the air. Tavern? Too casual. Spoiled beer shed. Yes, that was it. But how could there be a place like this under the Colosseum? The Locksmith thought to himself.

Harry and the group, after a few minutes walking through semi-dark and winding corridors, arrived at what seemed to be a strange kind of bar underground. Although not entirely like a tavern, the resemblance was palpable; tables in every corner; a smooth but dusty counter; candles that seemed to burn endlessly and even more than they should (although that was probably just Vela).

"I didn't know Emblems could make permanent changes," Goldman said, giving an apologetic smile to Matthews, who looked away with his arms crossed.

"All Emblems bring permanent changes," Brun said, sitting alone at one of the tables, far from Matthews. "

"Yes, I know, but they're not uncontrollable. You understand what I meant..." The pirate said melancholically as he carried three mugs of beer.

"...That's the curse of using a second Emblem," Matthews finally said. His voice was deep and solemn. "We lost some things along the way."

Shit... Should I say something? After the skeleton's words, the atmosphere once again descended into a melancholy plane. Harry, trying his best to pretend it didn't affect him, was betrayed by his trembling hands. After so long, how could he re-establish the relationship he had with Matthews? Being brothers, that should count for something, shouldn't it? Even so, the Locksmith constantly found himself avoiding Matthews' cold stares, who now leaned against a red curtain in the corner of the spacious room.

"Should you be drinking that, Harry?" Manes asked, afraid of reliving past events. "You were feeling quite unwell until about half an hour ago."

"Of course I can..." He gently ran his hand over his still-aching stomach. "My body is strong, you see."

A laugh could be heard coming from one of the corners of the tavern. Everyone then turned to Matthews, who was simply staring at Harry. "Really?"

"What?" And before he could understand his brother's ironic laugh, Madeline took a step forward.

"I apologize for interrupting this dick-measuring contest," The young redhead said, "But what do you want to do with us? That woman over there said—"

"Judge Jules," Matthews clarified.

"The judge…" Madeline chuckled slightly as she looked to the side. "Yes, Judge Jules. She said you're in charge of Harry and any members of his group…?"

"…I can't help but notice you're still confused. That's exactly what she said."

"Stop being a jerk, Matt," Harry said, setting aside his slightly watered-down beer. "You understood what she meant. You don't need to do anything to them. It's me you want, isn't it? So let's go. What am I going to have to do to earn your forgiveness? Get on my knees? Lick a wall?"

With each sarcastic suggestion Harry made, Matthews seemed to clench his bones tighter. How could he ignore everything he'd done all these years? And it wasn't just about the robberies, but how he left Matthews alone, especially after her death. ...That's just how he is. No need to get angry. The skeleton added inwardly, a weak attempt to calm things down.

"You'll participate in the games," Matthews said bluntly. "I already asked for the pairs to be separated, but if you want I can change them. This season promises a lot of entertainment for them, so we're all having to work hard."

"...Entertainment?" The young thief had difficulty choosing a topic to address. "Games? Them? Don't tell me you're still working for the nobles."

"We're protecting them," Harry's brother said in a slightly muffled voice due to the mask on his mouth. "It's different."

"How can you be so naive? They're using you, man. It doesn't even make sense for them to be here."

"The nobles are the future, Harry. Whether you like it or not."

"Future..." The Locksmith chuckled slightly.

"The world is a mess out there. With more and more pieces of the moon falling, it would only be a few decades before everything turned to dust. We had to secure a piece of civilization so we could one day return. I'm here to guarantee that."

"Oh yeah, and participating in violent death games is certainly necessary for civilization, right?"

Matthews wanted to retort but found himself at a loss for arguments.

"They've changed, Matthews. We both know that." Harry looked around with his arms outstretched. "We all know that. That's what the Tower does, it changes people. They've become elegant savages. Although even elegance doesn't last entirely..."

"Anyway, the Tower is their home now, and I'll follow their lead. And they hate you, Harry. Putting you in the games is a blessing I'm giving you." Matthews was going to stay silent, but then decided to add after a moment. "None of this would have happened if you hadn't killed that librarian..."

Kill...? Harry closed his eyes, promising himself he wouldn't let it consume him. That's what they always threw at the Locksmith. If he were a simple thief, he wouldn't have all this stigma or hatred, but murder? That wasn't just a minor crime, but a great sin. Even more so the murder of a poor, humble librarian. Merchant killed by his own apprentice. The newspapers said. What could Harry do? Disprove an entire continent? It was better to just run away and make a name for himself, that way maybe someday he could be special.

"What's all this?" Goldman asked, pointing to a picture of the Colosseum. "What's the purpose of this circular structure? Maybe a war arena?"

"...Circular structure?" Matthews said, his tone less severe. "You mean the Colosseum?"

He met the group's stares. While Harry, Madeline, and Brun knew what the word meant, the rest seemed to look away, embarrassed.

"You don't know what a coliseum is?" Matthews asked. Manes then raised his chin as he walked towards the counter.

"Y-Yes, of course we know... I mean, I know," he said smugly. "It's had various meanings over the, um, years, you know? Hey! What do you think you're doing?"

At that moment, Goldman took a mug from Manes' hands.

"It's an arena. You know that, don't you?" Matthews said, taking another sip of his beer.

How can he drink that? Madeline thought, slightly horrified by the man's curious build.

"Now we're speaking the same language!" Daniel's eyes gleamed. "What are the rules? Is there team ranking or are the battles individual? Are Emblems allowed?"

"The organizers decide everything, okay? I just put them in check and try to keep the nobles entertained. It's been a while since I've seriously gotten involved in this stuff..."

"You look like someone who escaped from a cult, Matt," Harry said with a smirk.

"It's almost as if you're saying I'm the most faithful and loyal out of the two of us, Harry."

"Faith and loyalty are two distinct concepts, little man."

"The same way leaving and escaping are different, right? Because I'm sure you know a lot about those two. "

As if the tension had become palpable, the atmosphere in the place had just grown thicker. Before things could get worse, however, Daniel put down his beer and looked at Matthews.

"Wait a minute, you're his younger brother?" he asked. "That doesn't make any sense."

"What do you mean it doesn't make sense?" Harry said, indignant. "And how did you only notice it now!?—"

"The games will be good for you. They always are." Matthews cut him off. "If you're good enough, you'll earn your way out. That's what we value here; good work. Maybe you'll learn a thing or two about that, alright?"

He had hoped Harry would arrive, and when he did, he had a whole speech planned for his brother. He had practiced so many times before a fight, even after. He had spent his entire life seeking confrontation, whether emotional or physical. All of that converging at the Logos Tower. Despite this, he realized, belatedly, that their relationship wasn't broken; it never existed to begin with.

Thump! Thump! Knocks on the door made the entire group, except Matthews, turn around.

"Done with them, boss?" The one-eyed man's voice could be heard through the door. "We'll start the fight in the next loop. We have several rookies we still have to deal with..."

"Yes, all right." Matthews said as soon as he reached the door. Then, the guards entered the bar, leaving the group perplexed.

"But..." Harry thought of protesting, but quickly came to the fateful conclusion; there was no escaping this. This was what Matthews wanted, for some reason. I understand him wanting to see me suffer... I mean, I don't understand, but I do at the same time. But the others? How long has he been waiting for this?

Daniel, suddenly recovering from the effects of the cold beer, thought about what would happen to Brun. Clearly, the oldest woman in the group was in no condition to fight properly. If placed in an arena as violent as that one, she wouldn't last the first five seconds of the first round. He decided to wait and see what would happen, but he was ready to defend her with his life.

"I hope to fight you, Mr. Matthews," Vela said with a smile as she passed the skeleton man, leaving him confused. "If you share the same courage as Harry, then I'm sure our fight will be interesting. See you later."

Why is he so relaxed? Matthews thought as he spotted Harry in the background, coming last, reluctantly agreeing to the situation.

"If we ever do fight," Harry whispered. "Please go easy on me. I'm an old man, must you know."

"No fucking way I'm letting this opportunity go." Matthews replied, as calm as Harry.

"I know you like to think me as the weak link," Harry added confidently, no longer whispering. "But you haven't seen what I'm capable of, little guy." And just like that, Harry left the tavern.

Fuckin' hell. We're all dead. He added inwardly.
