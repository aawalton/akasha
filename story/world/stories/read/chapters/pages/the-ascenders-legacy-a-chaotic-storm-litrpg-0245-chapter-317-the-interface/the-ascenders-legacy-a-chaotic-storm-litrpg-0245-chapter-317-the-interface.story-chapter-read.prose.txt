While Specials lived in mansions, and Elders—Mythics and Calamities alike—lived in estates, the High-Elders lived in palaces: massive structures crafted from the purest storm treasures, anchored atop six of the tallest mountains.

Each palace was majestic and beautiful, inspiring awe in the hearts of all who so much as gazed upon them—except, apparently, for their owners, who couldn't have cared less if they'd been shoved into an old, musty room instead.

At such high tiers of advancement, only those with ideals tied to sect development or the empowerment of others could bring themselves to care about anything beyond their own advancement and personal interests. Such ideals were therefore one of the major criteria required to become a High Elder.

As a result, the High Elders weren't merely the most powerful people in the sect; they were also the most powerful people who still cared—at least to a certain degree—about the mundanities of mortal life.

And yet, even for them, caring for too long took its toll. They became irritable and distant. Thus, their constant need for seclusion and meditation.

High-Elder Zarxan, however, had cut short his seclusion to save Aodhán—and now he was paying the price.

Thunder boomed in response to his irritation as he returned to his palace after yet another session discussing the imminent war—a very important matter, of course, but one Zarxan had to keep reminding himself of, each reminder only souring his mood further.

Thunder boomed again, clouds darkening, but he dispelled the gathering storm with a thought, a heavy sigh escaping his lips as he turned his gaze to his meditation chamber. He only needed a few hours—no, days—a few days of seclusion to get himself back. And the earlier he began, the better.

His eyes narrowed with resolve, and an instant later, he appeared within the chamber, a shudder of relief passing through him as the energy and silence wrapped densely around him. Here, the unending tasks of the sect were distant, and the talk of war was nonexistent.

Here, surrounded by the purest expression of storm conceivable, Zarxan could finally be himself. He could finally shut the world out and pursue his advancement.

A smile touched his lips as he settled into a meditative pose, closed his eyes, and inhaled deeply. He didn't need a week. He didn't even need days. He just needed one day. One day of silence and meditation, but apparently, even that was too much to ask.

A knock sounded on the door just as his breath began to slow, eerily loud within the silence of the chamber, and Zarxan nearly blew the entire place apart in irritation. Whoever was on the other side must have sensed the swell of energy and pressure. They must have sensed his irritation and anger.

And yet, the knock came again. This time with a voice. "Zarxan, I know you're in there, and I know how much you wouldn't mind murdering me right now, but duty calls—and it's very important."

The voice belonged to his aide, Luseph—a Mythic-class cultivator at the 164th tier, and the only underling he had retained since his time in the core sect. The man had been with him for over a century, and he loved him dearly, but in that moment, Zarxan could barely bring himself to care about that history.

Barely.

With far more physical and mental effort than should have been required, Zarxan sighed.

"Come in, Luseph."

Luseph stepped into the chamber and smiled. "I very much appreciate the effort you're putting into sparing my life at this moment, but a particular someone has requested an audience with you—someone you tagged as very important. Aodhán Brystion."

The mention of Aodhán's name sparked a sliver of interest in Zarxan's eyes, and he perked up. Slightly. "Did he mention what he wished to speak about?"

Luseph shrugged. "I'm certain we'll find out once we meet him. I should also mention he's not alone. He's accompanied by four other specials, as well as the administrative head of the special sect."

Zarxan sighed heavily, as if weighed down by the burden of the world. "Fine. Take them to the elder hall. I'll meet them there."

Luseph stared at him for a moment before nodding. "One of these days, you'll explain to me why this Aodhán Brystion is so important."

Zarxan glanced at him from the corner of his eyes. "Did you miss his trial at the Mountain of Tempest this morning? I should think he's proved why he's so important."

"To the sect, yes." Elder Luseph nodded. "But you can't hide the affection you bear toward him. It's unusual, and we both know it's much more than him being Az'marthon's Legacy. There's something more you're not saying."

Zarxan looked away, his thoughts on Aodhán's heritage flashing across his mind. He stayed silent for a long moment before shaking his head. "Get them to the elder hall, Luseph. I'll await them there."

Luseph closed his eyes and sighed. "As you wish."

He departed immediately after that, leaving Zarxan to stare at his chamber walls in a silence that was no longer comfortable.

Thunder rumbled once more in response to his emotions, and Zarxan let out another heavy sigh. With an irritated mutter, he pushed himself to his feet, bent reality, and simply appeared within the elder hall, crossing a distance of several miles in an instant.

Unlike the palace he called home, the elder hall was crafted with less refined materials. Its link to the Origin Plane was thus more muted than in his chamber, its essence density thinner.

Still, Zarxan enforced his dominion over the hall, leashing his aura tightly and bringing the atmosphere down to what he considered manageable for those below the Mythic class. And when he was done, he closed his eyes and sighed again, leaning into his throne as if it were a balm to soothe his constant irritation.

Fortunately—or perhaps intentionally—Luseph didn't arrive until nearly half an hour had passed, giving him some time to meditate. It wasn't nearly enough to satisfy his thirst for solitude, but it certainly took the edge off, and when the massive doors swung open a moment later, Zarxan felt less likely to murder someone on a whim.

"High Elder," Luseph bowed as he stepped into the hall, accompanied by an array of individuals, all of whom bowed the moment their gazes fell upon him.

"This is Elder Vasmos, administrative head of the Special Sect," Luseph introduced a familiar woman with marble-white skin, and Zarxan smiled. "It has been a while, Vasmos. It is my joy to see you doing so well."

Elder Vasmos smiled. "And you too, High Elder Al'ruhn. I was remarkably teary-eyed at your coronation as High Elder. The High Elders chose rightly."

"Ah," Zarxan smiled. "Thank you, Vasmos. I was told you wanted to see me."

Vasmos nodded. "Not me, High Elder, but our newest special." She gestured to Aodhán, who was still bowing. "He insisted on speaking to you on an urgent matter. Shall he state his case?"

Zarxan glanced down at Aodhán and nodded. "He shall. And you may all rise."

The specials rose, their bodies shuddering under the weight of the atmosphere in the room despite his actions to lessen it. When they finally all raised their gazes, Zarxan fixed his attention on Aodhán, runic irises gleaming. "I assume this is important, Aodhán?"

Aodhán stepped forward. "It is, High Elder Zarxan. I believe I have something that can help the sect greatly."

Zarxan frowned, silent for a moment before asking, "What is it?"

Aodhán raised a hand to his neck and pulled out a lightning pendant. "I gained my familiar under special circumstances. Circumstances, I believe, can be replicated for the benefit of everyone within the sect."

Zarxan stilled, every iota of irritation left in his spirit vanishing as the implication of Aodhán's words settled on him. "You mean...?"

"Yes, High Elder Zarxan," Aodhán smiled. "For the whole sect: a step-by-step process on how to gain a familiar, and perhaps a way to replicate it."

Behind him, Elder Vasmos, Sophran, and the other specials swallowed nervously, shock and amazement radiating off them. Luseph himself was shocked, his eyes wide in disbelief as he stared at the boy whose significance Zarxan had refused to disclose.

There were hundreds of thousands of cultivators without familiars within the sect. To somehow grant them all familiars was a monumental undertaking. No—a colossal one. And if this boy could somehow do it...

Luseph glanced at Zarxan, expecting to see shock in his expression. But Zarxan didn't seem shocked. He was surprised, yes, but not shocked. Instead, his gaze was serene, his mind already racing as he calculated the wider implications of Aodhán's words and how greatly they would help the sect.

It seemed this meeting was very important after all.

Aodhán stood before High Elder Zarxan with his head high, doing his best to ignore the strain of the Calamity's aura as it pressed against his core and spirit.

He knew the implications of his idea and just how greatly it would help the sect if things turned out right. What he didn't understand was why he hadn't thought of it before.

To be fair, Aodhán wasn't sure he would have shared this idea in Lutia even if he had thought of it, but it was still strange that it had never occurred to him. He could still remember clearly the first appraisal he'd gotten on the pendant back in sector eight.

At that time, he hadn't quite understood the functions of the pendant. And even now, he couldn't particularly say he fully understood it yet.

Perhaps it was time for another appraisal.

The pendant glinted in his palms now, bright against the darkness of his skin as he raised it for everyone within the hall to see. Silence stretched for a moment longer before Zarxan finally spoke.

"That is a huge claim, Aodhán. Are you sure you understand the gravity of the words you're speaking?"

Aodhán nodded. "Yes, High Elder."

Zarxan exchanged a glance with Elder Luseph, then nodded. "Go ahead then. I believe we will all benefit from an explanation."

Aodhán launched into his explanation, recounting how he'd bought the pendant simply because he'd thought it would fit him nicely. "I didn't even think it was magical, let alone an item. But I soon discovered it was anything but ordinary when I entered the Origin Plane the first time."

He took a breath, the memory replaying in his mind as clear as day. "Chaos rushed into my mind overwhelmingly, promising creation and destruction at the same time. I couldn't think. I couldn't do anything. But just as the whispers became overwhelming, threatening to make me a part of itself if I so much as listened, I felt the pendant grow warm on my chest and then searing—"

"Wait a minute," Elder Luseph cut him off with an expression of disbelief. "Are you trying to tell us that the pendant followed your consciousness into the Origin Plane?"

Aodhán frowned as he mulled the question over. "I've never thought of it that way, but you're right. Every time I've entered the origin plane, the pendant has always been with me, even when I'm not wearing it." He glanced at the pendant with a frown. "I imagine it's a result of our bond."

"Bond?" Zarxan asked with a curious expression. "Are you bonded to the pendant?"

"Yes, High Elder." Aodhán nodded. "I am bonded to it by blood."

Gasps echoed from all three elders, and Zarxan leaned back on his throne. "By blood, you say."

He exchanged a glance with Elder Luseph once more, then nodded. "Continue, Aodhán. What happened after that?"

Aodhán hesitated before proceeding, not missing the glances Zarxan and Elder Luseph were exchanging. "The whispers of chaos grew muffled after that, as if they were being filtered. It never lasts long, but it lasts long enough to help me think straight for a few seconds."

"And the day you gained your familiar?" Zarxan asked, his gaze intent.

"I was climbing the tower of ascension," Aodhán smiled at the memory. "I had just reached my limit. I was injured and bleeding. But I wanted to continue. I needed help. The world fell away in that moment. Chaos rushed in once more, overwhelmingly, until the pendant glowed, granting me a burst of clarity. And with that clarity came my most imminent desire. I needed help to proceed. I needed a partner. The moment I had the thought, I could suddenly see it—offers of familiars from chaos itself. I picked Varéc, and when I exited the origin plane, there he was."

Silence settled thickly over the hall as he trailed off, until Seamus whispered, "It granted you a moment of clarity. A moment to think."

"It did more than that." Elder Vasmos shook her head, her amazement palpable. "It bridged an interaction between him and the chaos of the Origin Plane. Chaos is the force of all creation and destruction. To create a familiar requires an interaction with that force."

"Which means the pendant gave you the ability to understand the whispers of chaos," Elder Luseph finished. He glanced sharply at Aodhán. "Tell me, what do you hear whenever you enter the origin plane?"

Aodhán frowned as he considered the question, trying to remember all the instances he'd entered the origin plane. But the truth was that, even with the pendant, the number of times he'd truly interacted with the chaos was surprisingly few.

One couldn't simply enter the origin plane at will. They had to be invited, either by deep meditation or inspiration, both of which consumed the mind for the duration of their stay. Very rarely was a person's mind free when they entered the plane, and most times, they were ejected the moment that inspiration was complete.

Aodhán realized in that moment that he had never entered the Origin Plane with an open mind before—an inspiration, ideal, or seal had always occupied his thoughts.

Except in that moment when he'd gained Varéc.

And he hadn't gotten to the origin plane on his own that day.

Az'marthon had opened his mind to it.

Eyes widening in realization, Aodhán looked up at Elder Luseph and shook his head. "I can't say for sure, but I think I've just realized another requirement for gaining a familiar."

"What?" Elder Vasmos exclaimed beside him. "We're still trying to understand the first."

"And we will." Aodhán glanced at Zarxan and raised the pendant once more. "But first, I think this item needs a proper appraisal."

Zarxan regarded Aodhán with an unreadable expression, a hint of excitement bubbling in his veins. Excitement? Zarxan couldn't remember when he'd felt the emotion last, but there was no doubt that whatever conclusion Aodhán's mind was leading to, it was significant.

The boy seemed to be connecting the dots in real time, slotting pieces into place even as they spoke, as if he'd come into the hall with a half-formed idea and was only now perfecting it. Working through it.

A trait that had frustrated Az'marthon's allies to no end.

It was amazing how similar, yet different, they both were. And even now, after all he'd seen the boy do, Zarxan couldn't help but think he hadn't seen the half of it.

Glancing at Elder Luseph, he commanded, "Get me the chief architect. There's no one better than her when it comes to appraising awakened items."

Luseph bowed and exited the hall. While they waited for his return, Zarxan asked, "Have you shared this information with anyone else back in Lutia?"

Aodhán shook his head. "Not exactly."

"Why is that?"

Aodhán frowned, thinking before he responded. "I don't know, High Elder. I don't have any particular reason. It just never occurred to me, I guess. Many people tried to steal the pendant from me. It didn't exactly foster trust."

"And you trust us?" Zarxan probed.

Aodhán met his gaze steadily. "I trust you, High Elder Zarxan. You crossed a continent to save me. This can be considered me repaying the favor."

Something shifted in Zarxan's expression—gratitude, perhaps, or satisfaction—but before he could respond, the door swung open.

Elder Luseph walked in, followed by an elderly woman with hair the color of liquid gold and eyes like a galaxy of stars. She moved with the quiet confidence of someone who had long since transcended the need to prove herself, her obsidian cloak flowing behind her like water.

But it was the intensity of her core that gave Aodhán pause.

Tier 190. Mythic.

Aodhán had never seen a Mythic so advanced—so powerful that a single glance at her core blinded his core sense completely. It was a known fact that tier 180 was when most cultivators began pushing for the calamity class. Some even attempted it at the 170th tier and still succeeded. But to hold off until 190?

Aodhán couldn't understand the logic behind it, but the woman's power was undeniable.

When she bowed in greeting, Zarxan's response and tone grew noticeably less authoritative. "Please rise, Elder Gumi. Thank you for heeding my call. There's an item I would love for you to appraise."

He glanced at Aodhán then and nodded. "Whenever you are ready, Aodhán."

Aodhán inhaled deeply and placed the pendant in the Elder's hands with a bow.

She closed her palms around it and closed her eyes.

A minute passed. Another. And then she froze. Her fingers tightened imperceptibly around the pendant—a motion so slight Aodhán almost missed it. “By the Storm!”

Zarxan leaned forward a fraction. "What is it?"

"This..." Elder Gumi looked up, her eyes wide with amazement. "I've never seen an item like this before. How extraordinary."

Elder Luseph frowned. "What can you tell us about it, Elder Gumi? Is it an artifact?"

"No," Elder Gumi shook her head slowly. "I doubt it's even an item, though it does present itself as such. The word that would best describe it is... an interface."

"Interface?" Aodhán asked in confusion. "What is it interfacing with?"

"Chaos," Elder Gumi replied quietly, her voice tinged with awe. She raised the pendant to eye level and shook her head in wonder. "The craftwork is masterful. A dense array of runework, chaos weaving, and storm intents layered with precision I've rarely seen. Whoever crafted this must have been at the Calamity class at minimum. Their understanding of the Origin Plane is profound."

Zarxan's runic irises brightened, and he leaned forward. "What is its function?"

“It interfaces with chaos like a filter—partitioning it into layers a mind can endure.” She shook her head once again, her gaze still glued to the pendant on her palms. “It’s just like those chaos suppression items we handed over to the specials a few years ago, but much more precise.”

“It doesn’t suppress the whispers.” Elder Luseph deduced, glancing up at Elder Vasmos, who had said as much earlier. “It’s filtering the information from the noise. That’s why Aodhán could interact with it and pick a familiar because he could understand it.”

Aodhán’s eyes widened as the memory flashed across his mind with perfect clarity, and he whispered, "It created a framework my mind could stand on. Translating the whispers of chaos in tune with my desires."

"Exactly." Elder Gumi looked up at him in surprise, a hint of respect gleaming in her eyes. "You've put it very concisely, young man. The pendant is a translator of the whispers of chaos, filtering out all the noise to isolate that which you desire."

"Hence the bonding by blood," Zarxan finished, a small smile on his face. "This explains how it follows you into the origin plane. Not physically—but existentially. It's anchored to your identity."

"Hmm," Elder Gumi smiled, clearly pleased with their deductions. She pointed to Aodhán and continued, "The pendant is bonded to this young man. His blood defines it—keys it to his desires—"

"—and thus brings it to life through the power of creation," Elder Vasmos finished. She glanced at Elder Gumi, eyes almost pleading. "Can it be replicated?"

"Whether it would be safe to replicate is perhaps the better question, Elder Vasmos," Zarxan's voice rumbled, and Vasmos nodded quickly. "Apologies, High Elder Zarxan. I got too excited."

Zarxan smiled at her and glanced back at Elder Gumi. "What do you say, Elder Gumi? Can it be replicated? Is it safe?"

Elder Gumi nodded. “It should be safe, and replicating it will be a challenge, but when have I ever shied away from one? I fear, however, that it cannot be mass-produced. The materials required to craft it will be extremely expensive. We’re talking chaos-attuned treasures, high-grade orichalcum ores, and a host of other materials we probably can’t even begin to imagine…”

“But it can be done?” Zarxan pressed.

"I believe so, High Elder," Elder Gumi replied. "Though we're looking at perhaps a dozen, maybe two dozen replicas at most with the sect's current resources."

Behind Aodhán, he heard Sophran's sharp intake of breath. Even two dozen familiars would shift the balance of power significantly.

"And it's safe?" Zarxan asked again.

"I believe it is, High Elder." Elder Gumi met his gaze steadily. Then in a quiet voice she added. “I will make sure that it is.”

Zarxan nodded slowly. "Then I shall discuss the matter with the other High Elders and get back to you, Elder Gumi. Thank you. You may go."

Elder Gumi hesitated, the pendant clasped tightly in her hands for a moment before she reluctantly handed it back to Aodhán. Her fingers lingered on it just a fraction too long, and Aodhán understood—this was the kind of item that called to someone like her. A masterwork that demanded her attention.

Her gaze strayed to Aodhán a moment, then she nodded. “You’ve caught my interest, young man. The harder task is keeping it.”

Aodhán smiled. “I’ll try my best, Elder Gumi.”

She nodded once more, then turned her gaze to Zarxan. "I shall await your call, High Elder." Her gaze narrowed. “I shall await it very impatiently.”

Zarxan smiled. “I understand.”

She bowed once more, and after a glance at Aodhán, she departed the hall, a subtle pressure leaving with her.

When the doors shut behind her, Zarxan fixed his gaze on Aodhán. "This is a great gift you've given us, Aodhán. Mass replication or not, whoever we grant these pendants to will gain familiars in very little time, empowering the sect greatly." His expression softened. "How can we repay you?"

Aodhán shook his head. "I didn't do this for a reward."

"Still," Zarxan smiled, and there was genuine warmth in it now. "There must be something you want. Something the sect can provide you to right the scales, if only a little bit.”

Aodhán opened his mouth to speak, then hesitated, a wide smile blooming on his face. Behind him, excited murmurs erupted from Sophran and Seamus, eyes wide as they speculated what Aodhán might ask for.

“Actually,” Aodhán smiled widely. “I have a list of things…
