“BUAHAHA!” Master Colorful erupted with laughter.

“It isn’t funny, Master!” Gary said.

“It’s hilarious! Did you really burn their hair?”

“I didn’t burn them, they fell! No one told me this would happen!”

The old man was doubled over in his chair, rocking with laughter. Gary waited until his master wiped his tears.

“Sorry, disciple,” he finally said. “I guess this is a disaster for you.”

“I don’t want to humiliate random people,” Gary said.

“I do,” Goldie said.

“Goldie!”

“What?”

“Please don’t be like that!”

“It’s their fault for being weak,” she replied, crossing her tail in front of herself like it was arms.

“Hair is ephemeral. It means little to a cultivator,” Master Colorful said.

“See?” Goldie said.

“Prestige, however, means a lot,” the old man continued. “I won’t lie to you. Making every opponent lose their hair is a terrible condition. You will struggle to find sparring partners, could make arbitrary enemies, and overall, you will be blamed for things you didn’t intend to do. This is something we must get in check immediately. At the very least, you must learn to control when your vibration burns or doesn’t burn the opponent’s hair.”

“Can I do that, Master?”

Master Colorful pursed his lips. “I’ll see if I can figure out a way. Most probably, you cannot. This weakening of hair follicles is an inherent property of your vibration. Unless you change vibrations in the future, it will always be there. Stronger opponents might be able to resist it though.”

“Seriously?” Gary said. “There’s nothing I can do? This is turning me into a joke!”

“I’m sorry, disciple. Some vibrations have side-effects. It could be worse.”

“How could it be worse?”

“You could be exuding lethal levels of radiation,” Colorful said, then flicked a hand. His chair and teapot disappeared. “Enough of this. How is your training?”

“I can use Fan the Spear now.”

“Show me.”

Gary grasped his spear and span it in his hands. It quickly accelerated until it resembled a black-and-golden wheel. His aura dove into the spear, using the spinning to affect his aura field until it, too, was spinning.

Master Colorful watched for a few seconds. This was longer than he’d ever need to use it in combat. Without warning, a firebolt flew for Gary’s chest, but was pulled off course by the spinning aura field. It barely missed his ribs.

“Decent,” Master Colorful said, nodding slowly. “This is better than I expected after only nine days, disciple. You must have practiced hard. Can you also spin it the other way?”

“Yes, Master. I practiced both ways.”

“Which comes easier?”

“Clockwise.”

“As for most. Many cultivators rotate their aura in some way. Spinning your spear in the appropriate direction is a crucial part of this technique, as well as one of its strongest points. You can choose to negate or exaggerate the enemy’s techniques. With time, you will learn by instinct which option works best in any given scenario, and you will be able to adapt on the fly.”

“Yes, Master.”

“What about your aura control?”

Gary emitted his aura field at maximum power. There were less clashing ripples than before. After ten days of hard work, he’d managed to grow his aura field from four feet to five.

Observing his disciple’s results, Master Colorful squinted.

“What you have shown me, disciple, is a month of work. How did you compress it into only nine days?”

“I just worked hard,” Gary replied.

A hint of recognition entered the old man’s eyes. It was swiftly gone. “You have been diligent and mastered the basics of Fan the Spear as I instructed,” he said. “I suppose, then, it’s time for me to hold up my end of the promise. I will now teach you the Nine Songs of the Spear.”

A long stick from the ground flew to Master Colorful’s hands. Gary watched with his eyes peeled. It wasn’t just that he’d learn a new technique—from the very first time he saw this Nine Songs of the Spear, it had called out to him. It felt special.

“I will do it as slowly as I can. Watch closely,” Master Colorful said.

His aura activate like a sleeping beast rousing from its sleep. One moment, the air around Master Colorful was peaceful. The next, it was awash with energy so potent it almost painted the wind—and Gary knew this was only a tiny part of his master’s power.

Colorful’s aura felt neutral. Balanced. As it gathered around the stick, however, it changed. From neutral, it became slow and dense, almost heavy. It would be hard to move and hard to stop. The stick stabbed forth in Master Colorful’s aged hands, and following it was the aura, which flew out alongside the path of the stick. It crossed the air slowly but with power. A straw dummy was in its way—Gary wasn’t sure when it appeared.

As the brown blow struck the dummy, it did not immediately pierce through. The straw, enchanted as it surely was, offered resistance. This was nowhere near the oppressive strike Gary had expected.

Yet, though the strike hadn’t immediately pierced the dummy, it hadn’t been stopped. It carried on like a ship striking the harbor, slow but unbelievably destructive. Straw bent and snapped in its wake. The dummy caved in. Its back bulged out and exploded, the brown bolt emerging to continue its path with a destructiveness belying its speed.

Gary watched the aura strike until it dissipated. “How did you do that?” he asked.

“The Nine Songs of the Spear is a sequence of nine strikes, each based on a different vibration. The first strike is the Song of Mud, relying on the properties of mud to compress its power. Did your planet’s elders teach you about the conservation of energy?”

“Yeah,” Gary said.

“That’s the first strike’s foundation. Reducing the speed of your aura while expending the same energy makes it heavier. Do you understand this?”

“Yes, Master. We called this conservation of momentum in my planet. However, if the strike becomes slower but heavier, isn’t its overall power the same?”

“Momentum is energy. Fake distinctions only muddy the path,” Colorful said with a shrug. “Your conclusion is incorrect. You’re thinking about your body and your energy as if they were purely physical. Reducing the speed of your strike doesn’t increase the efficacy of your aura, but it lets you pack more aura into the strike, making it stronger overall. It’s the blueprint of powerful techniques.”

“I see!” Gary exclaimed.

“However, the real objective of this Song is not power, but variety. An inexperienced opponent could easily underestimate this slower strike, giving you an opening. Most importantly, it’s easy to use. By varying the speed and power of your attacks, you can keep any opponent on their toes and distract them from your true intentions.”

“Then, this Song of Mud is a variation of aura use for any strike.”

“For the current you, yes. The Nine Songs of the Spear is a comprehensive technique created by the Ancestral Founder. It contains many core properties and strikes of the spear, like the connection between speed and weight you just experienced. In fact, the Nine Songs of the Spear are an entire path of cultivation. Some spend their whole lives mastering it. Once you learn more strikes, the reason will become clear.”

“I see,” Gary said. “I have a question, Master. In the full-powered thrust I showed you before, the Thrust of Mercy, I did not decrease the speed of my strike at all. I just held it back while I added more aura to it. Is that the same principle as in the Song of Mud?”

“Precisely. That strike was an extreme variation of the Song of Mud. Standing still to channel a strike is seldom useful in combat.”

“Then, don’t I already know the Song of Mud?”

“Varying the speed and weight mid-strike is harder than doing it while standing still. It requires a completely different technique. However, the first song is the easiest to learn. Practice it in place of Fan the Spear for a few days and you’ll get it. You’ll know you’ve succeeded when you see your aura taking on a brown-ish hue.”

“But my aura is black.”

“You’ll know when you see it, disciple.”

“Okay!”

“Give it a shot.”

Gary fell into a thrusting stance. He performed the movement slowly, letting more aura build up around his spear, then shot it out.

“You’re putting the cart in front of the glyan,” Master Colorful said. “Your spear mustn’t be slower because you’re slowing yourself down, but because the density of aura it’s pushing forward is greater. Focus your aura on its tip. Make it hard to push.”

Gary adjusted and tried again. His spear wasn’t particularly slower than usual.

“Wrong. If you’re pushing aura from your hands into the spear, of course it won’t be hard to push. It’s already moving. The key lies in your aura field—before you thrust your spear, enrich the area of your aura field in the trajectory of your attack. You must condense your aura before the spear, preparing it ahead of time, then using your spear to force it forward. When it’s outside your aura field, the aura will stick together, giving it the slow and heavy feeling of mud.”

Gary paused, focused, and retried. He got the general idea, but implementing it was harder. He tried imbuing more aura into his aura field, which did result in his spear pushing through some of it, but most of it was wasted in other parts of his aura field.

“That tickled!” Goldie exclaimed from inside the spear.

“Practice it slower at first,” Master Colorful suggested. “Do it like your Thrust of Mercy. Pull the spear back and hold it, but focus your aura in the proper area of your aura field instead of the spear itself. Then let it rip.”

Gary did as instructed. He pulled back his spear. In his mind, he saw the trajectory of his attack—a thrust starting from the spear’s current position and extending in a straight line. He tried to pour his aura into the part of the aura field that belonged to that trajectory. It was tricky.

Pouring aura into the air itself just made it spread in all directions. The aura field itself consisted of overlapping ripples from the aura vibrating inside his body, so to enhance certain parts of it, he needed to vibrate specific parts of his body harder than others. The aura control and awareness that required was no joke.

“You’re getting it now,” Master Colorful said with an encouraging smile. “You don’t need to master this move. The number of spear strikes you use is limited—only the attacking foundational moves—and every strike follows a set trajectory around your body. You only need to experiment and find the right way to vibrate your body for every strike, then practice it until it becomes muscle memory. As for the aura control required, you should be able to handle it with a little bit of practice.”

“Thank you, Master. I do want to accelerate my training as much as possible.”

“Of course. You have a Duel of Honor coming up. To accelerate further, learn how to use the Song of Mud only for your thrust. Once you can do that, we’ll move onto the second song.”

Gary hesitated. “In my training so far, I’ve always focused on a steady foundation…”

“We can fix your foundation later. For now, our utmost priority is to make you as strong as possible for the duel against Sui Duang. Do you understand?”

“Yes, Master!”

“Mm. You have enough work until we meet again. You may go now.”

“Can I ask you one last thing, Master?”

“You may.”

Gary resheathed the Golden Bolt. “During the first day of the tournament, I watched Feng Shen’s duel. It ended in a single thrust, but I couldn’t see it all. Something happened, like the world turned black and white, and then the strike had already ended. I don’t think it was pure speed. What exactly did he do?”

Master Colorful caressed his beard with a proud smile. “Feng Shen is an exceptional cultivator, disciple… Quite exceptional indeed. I know you have a fire in your belly, but you don’t need to worry about him yet. You cannot surpass him during the tournament. Focus on your training, and if you increase your aura control enough, maybe you’ll notice what he did next time…”

Gary left Master Colorful’s manor with the fire in his belly burning even hotter than before.

I cannot surpass him… Is that so?

He clenched a fist.

Sure, I don’t need to surpass him during this tournament. Maybe I really can’t. But if I don’t aim for it, how can I call myself a cultivator? How will I have the courage to reach the top of the tower and force people way more powerful than Feng Shen to pay for their crimes?

Gary paused and looked at the afternoon sky. The sun wasn't even down yet. The day was long. So was the night.

He had a match scheduled for the next day, as he would for the seven days after that, but he didn’t need to rest. He needed to train. No—he wanted it.

You’re just a stepping stone, Sui Duang. Even Feng Shen is just a larger stepping stone. My true goal is revenge, and to achieve that, I must reach…the top!
