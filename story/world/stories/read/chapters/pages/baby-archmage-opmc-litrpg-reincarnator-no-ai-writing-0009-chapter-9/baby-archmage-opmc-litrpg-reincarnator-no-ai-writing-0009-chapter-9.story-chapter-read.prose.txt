Description: Your control of mana is significantly increased. You can weave multiple mana particles into coherent, stable formations. Mana Manipulation and Magic Power determine the precision and complexity of the structures, while Focus and Mana Flow influence how many can be maintained and controlled simultaneously.

I immediately understood this new Perk was pivotal. Weave mana into structures? That sounded a lot like casting magic.

There was also a substantial change in my relationship towards the floating strange lights. The connection doubtlessly deepened. I tried stopping one. It stopped. And another one. It resisted, but stopped. I held them still for several seconds. Then I stopped a third one.

Then I did something I couldn’t do before. I made them all jump orbit to another sun. It was beautiful.

Okay! I was getting somewhere. Now I wanted to pull the particles out of orbit entirely.

I plucked five free and pushed them together. They resisted like magnets with the same poles facing each other, but eventually they fused into a tiny sun. A superparticle! I had made one myself.

Naturally, I wanted to make it bigger.

I added more particles. Six, eight, then ten. Now it looked like one of the little suns floating around me. I even managed to make six loose particles orbit it.

Beautiful. But how much could the sun itself hold?

I started pushing those orbiting particles into its center. Eleven. Twelve. I was completely lost in play. I still didn’t know shit about how magic worked, but clearly I was creating a MEGAPARTICLE.

The sixteenth particle went in.

Pop!

The whole thing burst apart and scattered some sand. Exactly like the little explosion during my breakthrough.

“Oh shit,” I muttered to myself. “This is real magic.”

I did it again and made some sand scatter.

I was filled with true blissful joy. I had done something real with magic. This was a proto-spell. This was assurance that I was on the right path. I sighed and plopped on my back on the blanket. A deep sense of relaxation came over me, and I lost tension I didn’t know I had been carrying. I could do this. I could beat Vorok using magic.

I wanted to keep going. I wanted to revel in my achievement, but my body betrayed me.

“One small step for baby,” I muttered to myself as I started drifting. “One giant leap for—zzzzzz”

*

I woke up in my bed and stretched and yawned. Father had already left for work, but mother smiled at me from the kitchen area.

“Morning little gem,” she said. “Breakfast?”

“Not yet, mom.”

I looked around the room, hunting for a victim to unleash the full power of my arcane might on. Mother often used candles to light the cabin, because she didn’t want to open the shutters for the bugs. One was standing at the dining table ten feet away from me. Perfect.

I concentrated and started applying my newly learned ability by amassing a cluster of mana particles right at the root of the candle’s wick.

“Look mama!”

Pop!

The candle snuffed out and a clump of wax flew on my mother’s dress. She narrowed her eyes at me.

“Sorry!”

Then we burst into laughter. She picked up the candle and used another to relight it and asked me to do the trick again.

“It’s not a trick,” I said and sulked. “It’s magic.”

“Sorry, of course, little gem,” she said and gave me a patient smile.

Pop.

Mother squealed as the wax flew at her again, but she shielded herself with a wooden plate.

I cheered and my mother turned to smile at me.

“That was amazing, little gem! How did you do it?”

“Watching the starry sky really helped, mama.”

She celebrated with me, but I don’t think she fully realized what I had just done. This was nothing but a cute party trick, but the implications…

The essence of magic was imposing your will on reality. And I could now do that. My poor baby heart fluttered with pure joy.

Mom convinced me to have some breakfast, but after that I immediately got to playing with Mana Weaving.

For the next few days I experimented with creating and disassembling mana. What I had found at 16 particles was not some cantrip, but simply the point at which the structure became unstable and I lost control of it. Whether that was the limit of my abilities, or some kind of structural limit of mana, I wasn’t sure. I would need to level to find out. But in the meantime, I figured out a neat way to bypass the limit to create an even bigger boom.

Ten mana particles could form a superparticle. And if I could add six particles into that, it destabilized and created a little explosion that scattered the mana.

So what if I exploded an overloaded superparticle right next to another superparticle.

Naturally I did this on a lit candle to observe the results of my experiment.

POP!

SPLAT!

“Eep! LUKA!” mother yelled and looked at the destruction I had caused. The candle had burst and sprayed wax everywhere. Not molten wax, but the candle was ruined. Mother picked a chunk of wax from her hair and glared at me.

She forbade me from exploding any further candles, so I needed to figure some other kind of fun mischief with my magic.

I spent some time experimenting on creating other kinds of structures with mana. Lattices and strands, like silk strung from tiny gemstones. I was so engrossed with this, one time when I ignored my mother’s call to join dinner, she just sighed, picked me up and carried me to the table.

Father liked the candle explosion trick. One time he went to pick up one of the green tubers from a barrel and asked me to try to explode it. I applied the full strength of my mind and abilities to impress my father, and managed to blast a chunk out of the tuber. I high-fived with father, but mother told me to practice something else to expand my repertoire and increase the rate of leveling. I had the distinct feeling that she did not share our boyish excitement over making things explode.

She had a point, though, as she so often did.

I continued my experimentation as time passed. That’s when I learned something interesting.

See, I could hold a grain of sand on the tip of my finger and envelop it with mana particles, creating a superstructure around the grain of sand. Once I did that with 12 particles, they could hold the weight of the grain. I could remove my finger from its place, and the grain of sand would float in the air. My parents were amazed by this, and my father asked me to do it multiple times. I indulged him and preened with pride. I kept showing off until I got such a monstrous headache, that mother forcibly put me in my crib for a nap. I dreamed of telekinetically throwing Vorok around. It was a good dream.

But as I continued to pursue my mastery of mana particles, I found that my floating sand grain was not a spell. It was not true telekinesis. At least not yet. I couldn’t move the mana structure I created around the grain of sand. Once it solidified, it remained there until I broke it or lost concentration. And if I tried to move the particles, it would break the structure. I couldn’t move twelve of them in tandem while keeping the grain of sand in the suspension.

Even so, I was excited by this progress. Just being able to levitate a grain of sand was a massive victory!

I was finally doing magic. Real magic. Not just observing it as a phenomenon, but applying it to create something. Did creating explosions count? If you used your powers of creation for destruction was that a chicken or an egg? I didn’t know, since I was a magician, not a philosopher. And I certainly wouldn’t ask my explosion hating mother.

During this training I gained two levels in Mana Manipulation and one in Mana Sight. Satisfied with my progress, I decided to reward myself by admiring my Status.

Over the weeks I improved my control to the point I could topple a mug of water from across the room. Without exploding the mug, mind you! I was using a contained burst of unstable mana to only knock it down. Now that was something.

When it came to magic, distance mattered, but not as much as I initially thought. Controlling the particles was not really dependent on distance. It was simply a case of intent, precision, mastery and… faith, for lack of a better word. I wasn’t sure what to call that sense of imposing your will on the particles, but the better I got, the more confident I got, which created a virtuous cycle.

One evening, shortly after father came home, there was a sharp knock on the door. Father opened it, and I saw the largest man I had seen in the doorway. Broad shouldered, muscular and tall. He had a wide jaw and long blonde hair. There was no mistaking those dull, unfriendly eyes and that protruding brow. This was Marek, father of Vorok. I scowled and wondered what the hell he was doing here, for he was certainly no friend of our family.

“Jonaka,” the enormous man said.

“Marek.” My father crossed his arms. “What do you want?”

“There's a shoal of Bluefin Lancers past the northern reef. The younger boys spotted them this afternoon. Big ones.”

Father immediately looked interested.

“How big?”

“Big enough that you'll need my help catching one.”

Father snorted. “This your way of saying you’ll need help tomorrow?”

“The shoals roam. This is luck. Use it you fool and come help yourself and the village. I heard you’ve been spending quotas to teach your runt to read.”

“I think I have enough spare quotas.”

Marek grunted, but the corner of his mouth twitched. Mother sighed and invited him in. My father and Marek shared a long look as Marek brushed past him inside.

Mother brought Marek a mug of water and they talked about the hunt for a while. I mostly ignored them. Marek wore a constant scowl and demanded that my father needed to do this and that, and bring a list of items in the morning. Father countered and they wrangled about who gets how many fish. Then Marek’s gaze wandered to fix onto me.

“So this is the little runt.”

“My son,” father said proudly. “Luka.”

“Verana saw them scuffle. She says your boy fights dirty.”

“Vorok started it.”

“Hmph. The boy has been training his Wrestling. He's eager for the festival. He likely just got excited.”

“That’s certainly a word for it…” mother muttered.

Marek scoffed.. “It doesn’t matter what happened. My boy has been training hard for the Moon Festival. That is what matters.”

“So is Luka.”

Marek looked down at me. “Is that right? He'll have to learn more than throwing sand if he wants to compete.”

He was chuckling at his own joke and looking at me. Was this guy really taunting a two year old? Yeesh, what a character. I decided to ignore the conversation, and instead turned my attention to finding mischief to cause with my new magic. Next to Marek's elbow sat a clay mug filled with water.

What an inviting target.

I gathered a small formation of mana beside its base and held it steady. Compressed it just enough…

Pop!

The mug tipped over and poured its contents directly into Marek's lap. The enormous man shot to his feet. “Gah!”

Father made a strangled noise. I bit the inside of my cheek to suppress laughter. Unfortunately mother wasn’t on the same frequency and she immediately turned toward me.

“Luka.”

Uh-oh.

“Sorry, mama.”

Marek was staring at the mug. Then he looked at our exchange. “What is this?”

“My boy has been practicing,” father said airily.

I had never heard so much pride packed into so few words.

“Practicing what?”

“Magic.”

Marek narrowed his eyes. “Magic? What do you know about magic, Jonaka?”

“Nothing,” father said. “But Luka is a natural.”

“Is that so…?” Marek said and thought for a moment. His face was unreadable as he studied me. Then he turned to sneer at father. “So that is the plan, huh? You figure the runt is too weak to become a Fisher so you’re turning him into a Shaman.”

Mother smiled politely at Marek as she offered a towel. “Are you saying being a Shaman is not a respectable vocation, Marek?”

Marek grunted and took the towel and wiped his pants before sitting back down. I was extremely happy that it looked like he had pissed himself. He noticed my glee and his face twitched.

“What Skill allows you to do that?” Marek asked me.

“A good one,” I said and smiled.

Then I pointed at a candle on the windowsill. Marek turned his head towards it. I snuffed the candle. The big man stared at the smoke trailing upwards for a good while.

“Tricks is what you seem to be good for,” Marek muttered. Then he turned to my father. “So you have Piraka teaching him?”

“Not yet,” father said. “We were going to go see him tomorrow.”

Nice. I was going to ask about that. It seemed like this encounter with father’s rival had spurred him on. I couldn’t wait for tomorrow!

Marek didn’t give me another look. He took his leave, but stopped at the door. “That will run your quota dry. Tomorrow at the northern reef at dawn, Jonaka.”

Marek was confused. He walked through the muddy trail towards his hut as he had done his whole life. He had always felt assured walking through the village. But something was bothering him. Why was there doubt?

Jonaka had always been soft. So it was no wonder that he had produced a soft son. But the runt was only two years of age and already with an awakened System? How was that even possible? Not even the noble families of Aligapa had methods to awaken so young. Idly he brushed at the wet spot by his pants. It really looked like he had soiled himself. He growled.

That tricksy little runt… He thought himself so clever. But what in the name of the spirits had the boy done to topple the mug? Maybe Jonaka was lying about Piraka not teaching the boy yet. But he had never seen a Shaman do what the boy had done.

Marek tried to push these annoying thoughts away. It didn’t matter. The boy was small and frail. Marek could see it from the way he carried himself and the slump of his shoulders that he was not training in the Fisher arts. How could the runt beat Vorok with such a physique? It was not possible.

And yet… Marek had a creeping sense of doubt.

The Moon Festival was still months away. If Jonaka’s son could do this now, what happened when he leveled up and got more Skills. Surely he had awakened the System only recently and had one, at most two Skills.

This tricksy business with magic and early awakenings made Marek uneasy. Why couldn’t Jonaka just respect him and train his son like a proper Fisher. If the runt was weaker than his Vorok, that was just the way of things, and Jonaka should accept it.

Marek gritted his teeth and stomped towards his hut. He still had time to make sure his son wouldn’t be caught off guard by tricks. He didn’t like this nervous feeling.

“Vorok,” he said when he came home. “You are skipping dinner. We are going to work on your Wrestling.”
