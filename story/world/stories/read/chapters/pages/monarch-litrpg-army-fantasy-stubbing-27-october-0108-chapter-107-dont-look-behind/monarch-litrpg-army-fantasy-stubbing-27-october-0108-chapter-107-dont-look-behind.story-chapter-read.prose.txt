The moment Commander Evans saw the two flares ignite above the battlefield, a surge of relief burst through his chest.

Even as he watched them, the master necromancer attacked.

Death mana twisted through the air, gathering above the lich’s broken body before forming into enormous claws of black energy. They ripped forward with monstrous force and tore straight into the massive form of the crimson bull standing above him.

The manifestation shattered within seconds.

The construct had already endured far too much punishment during the battle. The moment the claws struck, cracks spread across its massive body before the entire thing exploded into fragments of fading mana.

The bull vanished.

Despite that, Evans smiled. For hours he had fought under the quiet assumption that this dungeon might very well become his grave.

The necromancer had proven far more dangerous than anyone had expected. But in the end, Edran had been a good bet.

Evans had felt the chamber shake earlier. But the entire battlefield had already been trembling constantly due to the force of spells colliding around them. At the time, he hadn’t paid it much attention.

Now he knew that the dungeon core was gone. Edran's warband had done its job which meant they could retreat.

Of course, retreating would not be easy.

As he thought that, the necromancer pointed his staff at him. A storm of death mana surged forward like a tidal wave the very next second.

Evans responded without hesitation. Several projectiles of condensed mana burst from his blade, colliding with the spell mid-air and tearing it apart before it could reach the soldiers behind him.

Even while doing that, his mind was already thinking the way out.

Earlier, Evans had expected to hold the necromancer here himself while the rest of the army retreated. They would withdraw outside, and he will follow before they blocked out the portal till it disappeared.

But that plan had assumed the necromancer was merely a mage. A strong one, but still a mage. Not a lich.

Now that the creature had revealed its true nature, that plan was suicide.

Fortunately, Evans had already prepared for something like this.

Long before the battle had reached this point, he and Verella had discussed ways to control the necromancer’s movements if things went wrong.

He turned his head slightly and saw Verella fighting nearby.

Even with one arm gone, she moved across the battlefield with frightening precision, her blade flashing as she cut down undead lords that attempted to approach their line.

Blood soaked her armor, but she hadn’t slowed down once.

Evans smiled, and raised his voice. “Trapped monkey!” he shouted across the battlefield. “Get everything in position!”

Verella glanced toward him briefly, eyes blinking before she grinned and nodded.

The next second she twisted sideways as jagged bone projectiles burst out of the ground where she had been standing. The spikes erupted upward like spears, shattering the stone beneath them. She rolled past them and immediately moved back across the battlefield, already shouting orders to the captains nearby as she began setting the plan in motion.

Evans huffed, knowing that they needed at least ten minutes to get everything into motion, and for those minutes, he would need to keep the lich occupied.

Unfortunately, that also meant he would have to squeeze every drop of mana out of his body.

Evans exhaled before he surged forward.

Crimson mana flared around him like burning armor as he charged straight toward the necromancer. The energy wrapped around his body, enhancing every movement as he slid sideways to dodge the first wave of attacks coming for him.

Until now he had maintained distance. The crimson bull had been doing most of the heavy work, shielding him while he struck from afar.

But the construct was gone, and Evans didn’t have enough mana left to summon it again.

That meant he would have to fight the rest of the fight more with his reflexes than his mana.

He closed the distance rapidly.

The necromancer watched him approach, and a thin smile spread across the lich’s cracked face.

“So,” the creature rasped, voice echoing unnaturally across the battlefield. “You come for your death.”

Evans didn’t answer. The ground beneath his feet suddenly exploded.

Dozens of bone spikes erupted upward exactly where he had stood a heartbeat earlier. Evans jerked sideways at the last moment, the jagged spears missing him by inches.

Then he jumped. Mana surged through his legs as he propelled himself forward in a final burst of speed.

The necromancer raised his hand instantly. Claws of condensed death mana manifested in the air between them.

Evans’ sword met them head-on.

Steel and death mana collided with explosive force. The moment the blade struck the claws, a shockwave erupted outward, blasting dust and shattered stone across the battlefield.

Evans gritted his teeth and pushed forward, trying to break through the spell and drive the lich back.

But there was a reason liches were feared, and were called the pinnacle of magical existence.

The necromancer didn’t move an inch. Mana flooded the creature’s body, anchoring his feet to the ground.

Then, the claws twisted violently.

The next second they exploded outward.

Evans was thrown backward, his body skidding across the shattered battlefield as the death mana hurled him away like a rag doll.

He slid across the shattered stone, his armor grinding against the ground as the force carried him several meters back. The impact tore through his flesh in places where the armor had already been damaged, and warm blood ran down his side.

But he didn’t stay down.

With a grunt, he pushed himself back onto his feet.

The lich didn’t waste the moment. More spells burst toward him immediately—jagged bone shards, spears of condensed death mana, and whips of dark energy tearing through the air.

Evans didn’t dodge, and stepped forward to meet them head-on.

His sword moved in brutal arcs, crimson mana flaring along the blade as it smashed through the spells one after another. The impacts rattled his arms, but he forced his way through them.

Then he charged again, knowing that the necromancer's attention was locking onto him exactly as he wanted.

But before he could unleash another barrage of spells, Selene appeared beside him.

Her sword flashed forward like lightning.

The necromancer reacted instantly, a burst of death mana erupting outward in a violent pulse meant to throw her back. But Selene didn’t retreat.

Her blade met it head-on. Light mana flared around her sword, clashing against the darkness in a blinding flash. The collision sent ripples of energy tearing through the air, but instead of being pushed away, Selene stepped into it.

Then she jumped and twisted mid-air before landing along the lich’s side, blade already driving forward toward its torso.

But a thin shield of condensed death mana formed at the exact point of impact. It wrapped around her sword the moment it touched—

And then it exploded.

The blast threw Selene backward, her body spinning through the air before she landed several meters away, boots skidding across the stone.

Evans didn’t give her more than a brief glance. The moment Selene was pushed back, he surged forward, closing the distance between him and the necromancer again. He knew she would be fine.

For the next few minutes, only one thing mattered—Survive, and keep the lich’s attention locked on him.

That was exactly what he did even as the necromancer’s attacks came harder with every passing second.

A wave of blackened fire erupted across the ground, forcing Evans to leap over it as it devoured the stone beneath him. A spiral of bone shards followed, twisting through the air like a storm. Evans cut through the first few, letting the rest scrape against his armor as he forced his way forward.

Then came the worst of them.

A sphere of condensed death mana formed above the lich’s hand before splitting into dozens of jagged projectiles that shot toward him like falling stars.

Evans raised his sword and met them. Each impact rattled through his body.

His bones themselves seemed to tremble under the force of the strikes, but he held his ground and didn't fall.

Each time he survived, the necromancer’s irritation became more visible.

His spells grew faster, and more destructive. It was clear that the lich was trying to overwhelm him, trying to wear him down.

Liches didn’t tire. They could fight for hours without pause, and the creature was clearly relying on that advantage, waiting for the moment Evans would falter.

But he didn’t realize what was happening around him, and just as Evans tried to stab the necromancer's heart, Verella’s voice cut across the chamber.

“Retreat!”

Evans didn’t hesitate, even as his sword was deflected again.

The moment the word reached him, he jumped back, creating distance between himself and the necromancer in a single motion.

His eyes flicked across the battlefield. The center of the chamber was already clear. The captains, mages, and soldiers had all pulled back, their positions abandoned in perfect coordination.

Only Selene and two members of the Crown’s Hand remained nearby, holding off a group of undead orcs that were still around.

For a brief moment, his gaze met Selene’s, and the next second, he turned immediately, and ran.

Behind him, the necromancer’s voice echoed through the chamber, filled with cold fury. “You can’t escape me.”

Evans didn’t even look back. He pushed forward at full speed, crimson mana flaring faintly around his soles as he sprinted toward the exit path which was chaos in one word.

A swarm of undead pressed forward in waves, clawing and tearing at the retreating army as they tried to break through the formation. The entire corridor had turned into a choking mass of bodies.

Despite that, Evans didn’t slow down.

He raised his sword and forced the last reserves of mana through his body. Crimson energy flared weakly along the blade as he swung.

The strike carved a path straight through the swarm. Undead were split apart, crushed, and thrown aside as he pushed forward, cutting his way toward the army. Each step forward felt heavier than the last, his body tiring under the mana drain.

Then something whistled through the air.

Evans’ eyes flicked up, and he glanced back just for a moment. More spells surged toward him from behind.

But none of them hit him.

Instead, they tore through the swarm around him, obliterating dozens of undead in a single instant. Bodies exploded, limbs scattered, and the path ahead became a little easier.

For the final stretch, Evans didn’t even bother cutting. He simply jumped.

His body surged forward as he stepped over an undead lord just as it turned toward him, too slow to react. The next second, he was back behind the shield line.

Landing there, he exhaled sharply and allowed himself to breathe. Then he looked up beyond the swarm, and saw the necromancer coming at him.

The lich rushed forward through the collapsing swarm, death mana already gathering along his staff. Just in front of him, Selene and the remaining members of the Crown’s Hand were running, firing back projectiles and exploding potions at the remaining undead orcs chasing after them.

The entire battlefield had turned into a mess of movement and destruction.

Evans didn’t waste a second now that everything was finally at place. “Do it now!” he roared.

The moment the words left his mouth, a group of mages to the right raised their hands.

Mana surged around their fingers.

Even as the shield line trembled under the pressure of the undead, a massive joint spell matrix formed in the air above the mages. Threads of golden mana shot outward from it, spreading across the chamber like a web.

They latched onto the walls, and then, the runes they had secretly carved into the stone began to glow and the threads became thicker, and stronger.

The entire chamber lit up at once. The necromancer froze for a fraction of a second as realization dawned.

Then he raised his staff, preparing to destroy the walls and break the formation—

The threads immediately moved towards him and snapped tight.

They wrapped around his limbs, his torso, his staff, binding him in place. More strands burst from every direction, anchoring him to the very structure of the chamber.

The lich struggled. Death mana surged violently around him, trying to tear the bindings apart.

But the array held.

Evans allowed himself a brief smile. [Holy Dominion Array] was one formation designed specifically to restrain necromancers and liches, and as he watched, he could see the light aspected mana around the threads fighting back the death mana.

It wouldn't hold forever, but it should be enough, and he soon turned his gaze away from the necromancer to Selene, and shouted, “Selene, now! We don't have any time left!”

She immediately grunted, sheathing her sword. Then, she grabbed the two members of the Crown’s Hand running besides her and surged forward. Light mana flared around her in sharp blades as she cut through the remaining undead in a single motion before leaping back behind the shield line.

The moment she landed and let go of the two men, she turned, took out her sword and raised it high.

It trembled with light mana, and then struck. Arcs of blinding light shot upward, slamming into the ceiling across the chamber.

At once, the stone above them cracked.

Massive slabs of rock broke loose and crashed down into the swarm below. The rest of the captains followed immediately, unleashing their own attacks. Explosive potions were thrown into the air, detonating against the ceiling and weakening it further.

The entire chamber began to collapse.

Undead were crushed beneath falling debris. Even the orcs had their heads cracked. The swarm broke apart immediately.

Evans stood still for a moment, watching as the array held the necromancer in place, even as chunks of stone began crashing down around him. Every time he snapped a thread, more held him in place, and he screamed.

But he wasn't able to watch for long.

“We need to move now!” Verella’s voice cut through the chaos.

Evans immediately turned back and ordered. “Fall back! Now!”

The shield line began pulling back, step by step, maintaining formation as the rest of the army retreated ahead of them.

Behind them, the chamber continued to collapse. Stone fell, and more undead were buried. And at the center of it all, the necromancer remained bound, trapped within the crumbling ruin.

Evans didn’t look back again.

With the last of the soldiers falling into formation, the entire force turned and moved as one towards the exit.

Leaving the lich behind to die beneath the dungeon.
