The massive yellow teeth loomed closer as the entire landscape shifted. What I thought were rocks lifted, as a creature bigger than I could comprehend stood. The enormous eyes leaned closer, and a rush of air pushed back my hair.

The red dot covered up a whole chunk of my radar. My hand itched to grab my spear, but it didn’t appear from my inventory.

“What do we have here?” The voice literally made my ears ring, and I clapped my hands over either side of my head. Thankfully, I didn’t impale myself on my claws.

“So small.” This time, the voice had lessened a bit. But the eyes moved closer, and its head leaned down near me.

The purple light made it hard to see the body, but everything inside me screamed this was a dragon. Nothing popped up, either. No system notification, nothing.

“Hello,” I said, trying to not let my fear of the creature overwhelm me. When in doubt, be polite. Right?

“It speaks!” The dragon jerked back a little, then leaned closer. “How are you here? I don’t think you should be here…”

As it spoke, something pulsed along my back, and the purple light darkened in the far reaches of the sky.

The dragon's head snapped up, away from me, staring at the darkness.

A shimmery light sprang up all around me, and everything vanished, transforming back into the white sterile room as before. This time I wasn’t sitting, and instead I stood.

The reflection of me stood across the room, with a smile.

“You resisted your instincts, and helped those who needed help. But, you trusted your fellow humans to handle what they could handle, too.” The reflection paused as she eyed me. “You flung yourself into danger and confronted the source of the disturbance, ignoring any danger it might place you in.”

“Who was the dragon?” I asked, my voice coming back to me. That hadn’t felt the same as the rest of the trial.

She paused for a second, like she was suddenly processing information.

“Did I at least pass?” I asked, changing the subject.

Fluidity came back to the reflection.

“You gave a clear example of who you are, and what you value.” She smiled again, and then they dissolved into silver white light that rushed in my direction, before flowing into my chest.

I staggered back as energy surged into my limbs.

Pain slammed into my head, and light shimmered in front of me as three streams from my chest formed into images of myself. Full-length mirrors stood, side-by-side.

For the first, the reflection of my face changed. My jaw became more pronounced as my dark armor took over. Then, my teeth lengthened and glowed with a silver tinge. The dark armor gained a metallic shimmer.

[Raw Power & Gluttony: Void-Core Devourer]

Nothing else popped up, but the more I studied the reflection, the more the bestial areas highlighted. Less mana usage, more eating cores.

It made me frown, though I couldn’t really pinpoint why.

The next reflection went the opposite direction, reducing the devourer aspect. My eyes changed to multifaceted orbs, and my claws thinned to something closer to needles. Mana spun all around the reflected being, and as my claws tapped, the mana changed.

[Adaptability and Aetheric Power: Aetheric Ruin-Sovereign]

The last reflection focused more on my Shadowstalker form, but faint silver light flickered around the edges of my carapace. My claws still glowed with silver tips, but dark shadowy lines stretched from each hand.

[Stealth and Dominion: Umbral Aether-Stalker]

I leaned toward the last one the most, but even that felt lacking in the adaptability that I’d come to rely on. What had I said about needing freedom?

Turning back to all three reflections, I struck out, shattering the mirrors. The reflections grew static on the shards of glass, which thankfully floated in the air.

I snagged bits of pieces from each. The metallic shimmer, the strange eyes, along with the claws, and the shadows stretching out from each. This time when I reached into my inventory, it worked.

The mana spike appeared, and I added that to the new image I was creating.

Yet, this wasn’t all. The whispers from Apochas, and the wind rushing over my body as I flew, formed additional shards. Other memories from different meals I’d had rushed through me, as I picked and sorted various traits.

Finally, I stepped back from the weird, cracked, and layered mirror in front of me.

[Do you wish to proceed?]

Did I ever?

Always. I bounced on my toes as light flashed over the mirror, filling in the missing pieces. The remains from the three choices vanished.

[Trial Complete. You have overcome your own reflection, and established your Divine Archetype.]

Wait, Divine Archetype?

For once, I took a deep breath and just waited. My prior choices during rank up had fit me. The system did a good job at what I threw at it. This time it wouldn’t be any different.

Hopefully.

For sure.

The mirror in front of me glowed a bright white, then a deep purple, almost a black, before funneling into my chest. My lungs froze and I couldn’t breathe as energy surged through my entire body.

My muscles turned to liquid, but I didn’t slump to the floor. Instead, I hovered in the air as something held me up. A slow, creeping sensation started in my chest and seeped outward, bit-by-bit. It didn’t hurt, yet something had definitely happened.

It was almost like I was healing, without an injury. It made my head go fuzzy, and I wished I could blink. Or sleep. Sleep sounded amazing.

The ding of notifications stopped my brain from turning into goo.

The light suddenly cut off, and all that remained was the mirror. It floated in front of me and reflected my image back.

[Alex, the First, Hungry Shadow, Level 200]

The strange sensation creeping along my body had completed.

Suddenly, gravity resumed, and I landed on the floor. As soon as my feet made contact, the mirror vanished.

“Good luck,” whispered the soft voice, and then the white room dissolved.

I appeared back on the boulder from before, but no longer sitting. I was now standing, somehow.

Sensation rippled into me. The musty smell of damp rock, along with an acidic stink of fear from the far reaches of the cavern. Water lapped against the stone from the pool.

“Alpha?” asked Grizzle, in the smallest voice ever.

“I’m here…” I said, though my voice barely worked.

“You weren’t… and now…” His voice trailed off.

From the weird plants growing near the bottom of the tunnel to the shells literally growing out of the rock on the sides, I could see just as well as out of the water.

More pleasure rippled down the bond from Strange, and he moved again, this time faster. I didn’t care what he wanted, getting taken by Clan Vielether was not on the to-do list! I needed to hurry.
