[Time]: 1981

The steel screamed before it shattered.

It was a sound Irene Habsburg had grown intimately acquainted with over the course of the afternoon: a resonant, agonizing shriek of enchanted metal pushed leagues beyond its structural tolerance, followed by the silver rain of debris against scorched marble. Irene stared at the ruined hilt and exhaled once.

Across the training grounds, Ovelia floated with one tail still extended, its tip spinning in a lazy, contemptuous arc. The civilized world referred to this as her [Tail Sword Art]. It was a convenient fiction. After weeks of dying against it, Irene had decoded the underlying structure: it was, preposterously and insultingly, a Sorcerer's spell. Ovelia had fused specialized runes directly into the fur and bone, converting tails saturated with raw ley-line energy into a conceptual Mage's Blade. The formula stacked her Intelligence and Charisma modifiers with the inherent Strength and Dexterity of a Greater Lantern Cat. A four-attribute singularity swinging at supersonic velocity. A standard Tier-9 abjuration ward contacted by that tail simply detonated.

Understanding the mechanism provided zero alteration to the physical reality of a broken sword.

Ovelia raised a single finger. The temporal field around Irene's hand inverted. The scattered debris arrested in midair, then streaked backward with violent clarity, fusing seamlessly into the hilt until the blade hummed pristine in her grip once more.

If Ovelia lacked the basic courtesy to reverse the damage herself, the entire Habsburg treasury would have been liquidated by lunch.

Irene had been a Legendary Sword Saint before the Inner Sea had any knowledge of her existence: a mortal emperor who rode at the front of her own armies. Of Ovelia's eight secret sword arts, she had since mastered seven; only the sixth, the Tail Sword Art, remained structurally closed to her, requiring nine ley-line conduits she did not possess.

Seven mastered forms, however, did not translate to applying them against their own inventor. Technique was leverage against a body of comparable architecture. Against this body, the physiological gap was a geological event, and the forms she had learned from Ovelia were too intelligent, too precisely calibrated to their original chassis, to survive unchanged through a frame two orders of magnitude less durable. What emerged in the fighting was something that had no predecessor: a blade language with Ovelia's seven forms as its skeleton and Irene's own instinct, pressed through this specific, lethal crucible, as everything else.

Irene lunged, and Ovelia moved the way natural disasters do: without particular attention, and with comprehensive consequences. Nine tails lashed outward simultaneously, a synchronized hurricane of compressed ley-line energy that deleted the ambient air and replaced it with pure annihilation.

Irene danced into it.

All her power originated from three anchor points: her waist, her knees, and the balls of her feet. Her wrists worked with liquid flexibility, converting inertia into redirection at sharp, unpredictable angles. Where Ovelia was the storm, Irene was the leaf that had learned exactly where the currents moved. Her footwork was weightless, a drifting, ephemeral waltz that left afterimages fading in the scorched air, but every step carried her to the precise blind spot of the next strike.

Her blade ignited. Like a striking falcon, like a pouncing leopard: enchanted steel met rune-reinforced tails.

Clang. Clang. Crack.

The impacts rang out like detonations, but Irene was never there to absorb the full kinetic transfer. She used the blade as a flowing ribbon, her defensive projections sweeping like a dancer's skirt, gliding through the onslaught with breathtaking, lethal grace.

It was purely, uncorruptedly beautiful. But the truth of what was happening was only visible in the aftermath: wherever her blade had kissed a tail, jagged fragments of weaponized mana hung suspended in the air, slowly detonating in crackling, volatile cascades.

Her golden eyes bled luminescence. She shattered the local temporal axis. Fast, slow, halting, blurred: she wove a dizzying, unpredictable rhythm of time distortion through her own body and the airspace around Ovelia's strikes, transforming an act of swordsmanship into something closer to navigating an avalanche in reverse.

She survived exactly seven seconds of the full onslaught.

She read the closing window in the mana pressures, planted her back foot, severed the spatial tether, and blinked three hundred meters backward in a single frame. The instant her boots struck the marble, the sky ignited. An ocean of Tier-8 spell matrices erupted simultaneously across the airspace, threaded through with Legendary-tier spell, a localized apocalypse raining directly upon Ovelia's coordinates.

Across the ruined courtyard, Ovelia did not even shift her weight. Beside her, a heavy grimoire materialized in the air: black hardcover, embossed with nine cat paw prints, its pages flipping in an idle, frictionless blur. The book simply opened its jaws and swallowed the tide, dissolving the barrage into harmless, glittering ash with insulting ease.

As the residual glare faded, Ovelia politely covered her mouth and stifled a yawn. "Your output," she said, without urgency, "is lacking. Can't you give Cat a little pressure?"

Without reply, Irene raised her sword and brought it down in a single, vertical slash. The ambient temperature spiked to the threshold of combustion. Six rifts tore open in the air behind her. Six Legendary-tier Sunfire Elementals poured from the fractures, their molten forms cooling and solidifying in the same instant, compressing into six perfect, blazing replicas of Irene Habsburg, golden eyes converging on the Grand Witch.

As you wish.

Ovelia's eyes widened, the boredom evaporating into fierce, unguarded delight. "Now that's interesting. Declaration, face—"

Irene's eyes flared with blinding, petrifying gold. An empowered, amplified Petrifying Gaze slammed into Ovelia's coordinates before the second syllable cleared. A morphing shield materialized violently in front of the Grand Witch's face, crystallizing into a flawless mirror that caught the beam and scattered it into the stratosphere.

Irene blinked to the flank. Above her, the six Sunfire replicas raised their hands in perfect synchronization, unleashing a simultaneous cascade of Greater Dispel and fundamental source magic that rained down upon Ovelia's defensive constellation, attempting to strip it one thread at a time.

The sky above the training ground fractured; the earth liquefied.

Irene stood at the edge of the cleared arena, her breathing measured and controlled. She had been killed over a hundred times during the day. Her nervous system still carried the phantom memory of being crushed, incinerated, severed. She was by now thoroughly accustomed to it. The immortality of a Witch was a tolerance built through repetition: through dying until the process became tedious.

The fatigue sat beneath everything. What rose above it was the tactile memory of the final exchange.

I almost had her. The certainty settled with the cold, precise weight of an established fact. A fraction of an inch.

Every Witch, regardless of magnitude, harbored psychological rhythms. A baseline in their combat reasoning. Over the final ten rounds, Irene had spent those exchanges constructing a framework.

Three times consecutively, she had refused every frontal approach. She had skirted Ovelia's guard, prioritizing evasion. In the penultimate round, she had exaggerated a pivot, leaving a deliberate gap wide enough that Ovelia had punished it effortlessly, exploiting the opening within a single frame.

Ovelia was fighting seriously, yes, but within the context of training. She was not engaged in the paranoid, meticulous process of scanning her own responses afterward, asking: Am I operating on a conditioned assumption? Did my opponent manufacture this blind spot in my defense?

So in the final round, Irene acted on the position she had spent three rounds manufacturing: she stepped directly into the path of the unmitigated strike.

The blunt force of a tail contacting her ribs had shattered the structural integrity of her entire left side, a suffocating agony that replicated the precise physiological signature of imminent death.

She suppressed it. She forcibly diverted every faculty of thought away from the screaming report of her nervous system and into the single, blazing tip of the blade. She channeled the entirety of her mana into the razor edge of one clean strike. The spine. Just sever the spine.

She remembered the shock in Ovelia's eyes. Genuine, unscripted. Irene had broken through the perimeter, bleeding and broken, the blade driving with total, concentrated intent.

The sword pierced flesh. It drove through Ovelia's abdomen, the tip bursting clean from her back.

Missed.

The angle had drifted a millimeter from the spine.

Irene looked up. Her vision was already graying at the periphery. She saw Ovelia's face drawn tight against the sudden, searing intrusion of the blade, bearing the pain with precise, terrible grace.

"Well done," Ovelia said, her lips curving.

The tail came down.

Irene stood in the quiet aftermath, her hands fallen to her sides, fingers curling and releasing in some rhythm the session had left behind, her eyes bright and strange.

Across the courtyard, Ovelia reclined against a low chaise, a crystal goblet resting between her fingers, the catastrophic wound reversed and perfectly erased before Irene's consciousness had finished reconstituting: not a blemish on her skin, not a trace of blood on her garments.

Ovelia paused mid-sip, tracked the line of Irene's gaze, and set the goblet down. Her palm drifted across her own stomach, flat and perfectly knitted, and her thumb came to rest on the exact point where the steel had come through.

"Want to take another bite?" she asked, slow and thoroughly wicked.

Irene's right hand had closed on a hilt that was no longer in it, and the whole of the thing was still in her palm: the terrible give of living muscle parting around her edge, the wet heat of an immortal's core sliding along it, the jagged hitch in that breath.

She closed her eyes and breathed out, long and slow, until her hands were still. She shook her head.

But when her eyes opened, the gold had gone wide and dark, and it went to the coordinate under Ovelia's hand and stayed there, measuring the millimetre it had missed. Behind her lip, without instruction, her fangs had come down.
