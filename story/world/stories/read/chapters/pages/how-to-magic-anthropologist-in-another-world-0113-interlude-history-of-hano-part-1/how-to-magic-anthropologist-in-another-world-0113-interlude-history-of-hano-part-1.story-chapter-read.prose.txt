Commander of Hano's Freelancer Guild

Compiled for the Guild Archive

The place that was Hano was not an important place. The telepaths built their cities in clusters, and most of the northeastern continent was one continuous urban stretch. Hano was on the opposite side of the world from that. The only notable feature marking the region was its nearness to the Kurco Range, which fed the Lorial River year-round, making the ground suitable for agriculture. What is currently the largest city in the Seven Realms was once nothing more than a footnote to the ancient telepaths.

The dwarves currently live on that same northeastern continent now, and their whole society is built around trying to restore the old telepaths' glory. Using archaeology, or rather scavenging, they dig up artifacts, try to repair them, study them, and reverse-engineer whatever they can. I guess it makes sense for them to occupy the continent with the most telepath ruins.

When the Silent One began making Rift Gates to other realms, it was the requirement that the gates be spaced evenly across the realms that eventually earned Hano its own. High Rock was first. Tree Gate was second. Sky Island was third. The Sealed Realm Rift was fourth. That left only a gap in the south of the western continent. Hano.

So Hano exists because of geometry. I wonder if someone like Ni'a Outspring would subscribe to such a claim, especially because he takes pride in everything the telepaths have done.

Five gates. High Rock, Tree Gate, Sky Island, Sealed Realm, Hano. Five Realms, evenly spaced. Does anyone else notice which one isn’t on that list? I noticed.

The gate that would later stand inside Hano opened onto the Elemental Bloodline Realm. At the time, the realm was nothing more than an unstable ecosystem, thick with monsters far outpacing anything the telepaths had prepared for.

Among them was a single fire-aligned monster that had already reached its pinnacle. Its name was Agame.

Agame crossed first. It found, on the other side of the gate, several hundred thousand minds joined into a single undefended lattice. So it started feeding. Devouring a telepath sharpened its intellect, and though its own soul strength could not grow any further, it learned that cunning was a power of its own. It carried that surplus back through the gate and used it, deliberately, to uplift other monsters toward sentience the same way.

Most newly uplifted Dravak were either already pinnacles or grew to claim that role in no time. And this spelled the doom of the Telepaths.

"Pinnacle" is the actual term for a being that's maxed out its soul strength within its own Path, a ceiling nobody could theoretically cross. Lady Laurel the Fair is the pinnacle of the Holy. Kurt the Forgotten Knight, after the Third Holy-Unholy War, was able to match her strength by becoming the pinnacle of Unholy. I am not sure if every Mythic god could be called a pinnacle, or if the classification is reserved for their strongest champions, since not all gods are equal, and some of them are waxing and waning in strength.

I don't know whether Raik's family took that name to honor that monster, or if it was a political move to claim its fearsome reputation. Either way, I think it has worked out well for them.

Three thousand years ago, both the Contested Realm and the Bloodline Realm were under Dravak rule.

The Dravak dominated both worlds through consumption. Sentient life was their most important resource, an important delicacy as well as a way to create smart underlings. And with the extinction of the telepaths, they learned an important lesson: restraint.

People living within their territory started to serve as livestock, allowed to grow and reproduce so they could keep feeding from them. They learned that there were different flavors of people, each giving them different characteristics. Eating cyan-haired ones helped weaker monsters grow faster, while others tasted better depending on their affinity. Most importantly, they found that eating a hundred of the physically strongest would allow the Dravak to make their own bodies more malleable, and this is how a monster that was nothing more than bones and flame was able to sire a hybrid child with a woman. Because why lose a thousand humans to create smart monsters through feeding, when you can do it with just one woman through breeding?

I know the Dravak have been eating people, but breeding hybrids was just yikes.

The need to feed on Kindred people to gain that ability makes sense, though. Just like Nada needs to keep eating squid for her transformation, it makes sense that they needed to keep eating Kindred people for the Dravak to sustain their own transformation.

The hybrid children were born weak, like any human child. However, being sired by a pinnacle made their talent cap impossibly high, allowing for exponential growth. Their talent, however, was still capped, making them a manageable threat.

Many hybrids were sired, tortured to grow stronger, and then put in charge of protecting the sentient livestock from rival Dravak. Hundreds of years of this new equilibrium followed, in which the Contested Realm was driven back to the Stone Age, while the Kindred, Soulit, and Dreamer Realms resisted the Dravak invasion and prevented them from taking a foothold in other realms.

That's when a hybrid child broke this arrangement. His name was Kitchi Agame, First of His Name.

I can see how the Kindred and Soulit Realm were able to resist the Dravak, but I can't help but wonder how the Dreamers managed it. Was it because the sky Island was more defensible? Was it through illusion and mind alteration, or did the Sleepless Father step in and defend the rift personally?

Some hybrids saw themselves as humans protecting their kin the best they could, driving off enemy raiders out of care, raiding other villages so that strangers were sacrificed rather than family, and when all else failed, allowing a few to be sacrificed to protect the many when it was time for the harvest.

Other hybrids didn’t care much for the cost of life; they just saw themselves stuck, with no chance for upward mobility while their progenitors still lived.

Kitchi Agame, Lia Lore, Ira, and Zanka belonged to the former, while Lawshak Kahal, Yama, Son of the Colossus, Todor the Failed Dravak, and Kiri the Necromancer belonged to the latter. Each one had their own reason to rebel, even if their vision of the world after the death of the Dravak did not match.

They devised a three-step plan for their rebellion.

The first step was to take over the Rift Gate and fortify it, so that the Dravak would be split between both realms.

That's how Hano was founded.

This is why the Agame clan holds so much sway in Hano and owns most of the lands in the Bloodline Realm immediately next to the Rift Gate; their progenitor, Kitchi, founded the city.

Lia Lore was the progenitrix of the Lore Clan, Nada's clan. I remember seeing a depiction of her on the ancient Hano gold coins I found with random teleportation, Kitchi engulfed in flame on one side, while she, naked inside a bubble, was on the other.

To think that Todor, the one who attacked my friend in the Pikar Steppe, was among those hybrids.

Victory, once secured, revealed a fracture in the war and the alliance.

Kitchi Agame and those who shared his vision, Lia Lore, Ira, and Zanka among them, wanted what they had promised the Bloodline population from the beginning: freedom. The cattle slavery the Dravak had built, the breeding pens, and the sacrificial harvests, all of it was to end. In its place, they proposed to breed new offspring into the Bloodline masses to provide a population strong enough to withstand the harsh world that was the Bloodline Realm, with its mana storms, monster surges, and rough environments.

Lawshak Kahal wanted something else entirely.

He and those who had rebelled beside him, chief among them Yama, Son of the Colossus, had not fought to free anyone. They had fought because they were tired of living beneath their own fathers' shadows while the ceiling above them remained closed. What Lawshak wanted was to succeed to the Dravak throne. He meant to gain enough strength to claim pinnaclehood for himself and remake the world in the same shape the Dravak had left it, only with him at the top.

So the rebellion had more or less two factions. The Kitchi side and the Lawshak side.

While Kitchi technically created the unfair system that catered exclusively to Bloodline nobility, if this accounting was accurate, Lawshak would have been worse.

The same coalition that had brought down pinnacle-strength Dravak could bring down their weaker hybrid descendant.

Which meant that when Lawshak moved to seize the world that was just finished being set free, he discovered that every outer-realm ally who had fought beside him now had every reason to side against him instead. The Soulshapers, the Dreamers, the Mythic Priests, and the Kindred fighters, even the Satori Clan, who alone among the Bloodline Realm's population held the secret of independent portal creation, all of them looked at Lawshak's ambition and recognized that it promised them nothing they hadn't already bled to escape.

Almost to the last soul, they sided with Kitchi.

I wonder if Lawshak believed himself betrayed. People who believe that might makes right are always the first to cry foul when considerable might is mastered against them.

Did he just miscalculate why his allies had been fighting for the entire time? Or did he just believe them soft and weak?

Lawshak was not an idiot, he knew his former allies' strategy and thought he had prepared something to counter it.

Agro, the Nullifier, whose Bloodline gift stripped away the soul-strength advantage of anyone standing within his reach. Under Agro's influence, anyone weaker than him had their soul strength stripped bare to be nothing more than the strength of a peasant soul, completely countering the Soulshaper aura-stacking strategy that had toppled the Dravak.

The miscalculation, however, was that Agro did not side with him. The man's apathy toward sentient life didn't mean he would bother subjugating them. So when the Kitchi-Lawshak conflict started, Agro chose neutrality instead, withdrawing to claim a stretch of territory for himself alone. And he held it the only way a man with his gift ever needed to: by killing, without exception, anyone who trespassed. That territory still carries his name, the Agro Dead Zone, situated west of Tree Gate, forcing people to hug the coast when traveling the southeastern continent.

I need to know more about this. A man whose entire power is "you don't get to be special near me," carving out a kingdom of one inhabitant by enforcing a zero-tolerance trespassing policy for two thousand years.

If Agro had sided with Lawshak, I might have appeared in a completely different world.

I remember that when joining Raik's team, Calr was asked how he would defeat Agro the Nullifier. His solution was twenty Katar-like individuals that only relied on weapon skill, rather than magic or Soul Strength.

Without Agro, Lawshak's bid for pinnaclehood collapsed before it truly began.

What remained of his following did not get to burn in a single glorious war between both sides. Instead, from his ambition rose something more dangerous for being smaller and insidious: the Old Realm Cult.

To them, the world, as Kitchi and his allies had built it, was wrong. Weakness had been given walls to hide behind. The gates, the guilds, the cities, all of it existed to let the many shelter under the strength of the few, and in doing so, robbed the world of the Competition that had once forged pinnacles in the first place.

Lawshak's cult held that Hano, Tree Gate, and High Rock should be opposed... no, unmade, so that the old order, the true order, where the survivor of the fittest ruled, could rise in its place.

Alice's Margin Notes

And there it is. To think the Old Realm Cult isn't some recent fringe movement that popped up just to make my life difficult. It is two thousand years in the making, born from the exact same rebellion that founded Hano.

Lawshak lost the world to Kitchi, so he decided the solution was to burn it down and start over.

I don't know what happened to Lawshak himself after this. Hopefully, the next volume will contain that information.
