The morning meeting was pure drudgery as always. It was so expected that there was something almost peaceful to the way Senior QuestWright Mirelle chewed out every guildmember one by one.

Cass soaked it up, even going so far as to ask a few questions to delay having to return to his Annex. But all too soon, a grumpier-than-normal Mirelle sharply glided away, and Cass had nowhere else to go. He opened the door to loud exclaiming.

“Knock next time, ya fuddy budger, oh, it’s Flappy.”

“Please tell me that name isn’t going to stick,” Cass said with great remorse. “I don’t think I can take it.”

“I bet there’s a lot of women who say the same about the many Flappydangles in the world.”

“Enough, Noah. God! When does Ren get here?” Maggie demanded.

“About two hours, then it’s pick-”

“Don’t you dare.”

By some miracle, Noah knew when he’d pushed her to the edge. Biting his lip, he held back from speaking further just as a peppy Quirrel walked in with the first stack of the day.

“Hey, Cass. Oh, we have visitors? Why wasn’t I told?”

“Residents, for the time being,” Maggie corrected. Standing up, she introduced herself and Noah, then warned the man not to talk too much to the Crass Cretin who guarded her. Noah looked proud of it, rather than put out.

Following his new procedure, Cass began documenting every new Quest into his notepad. As it was his fifth day, the rotation for Liberty Loaders had landed on G. He made a note of it, as well as what deliveries were going out for his daily count. Then, he felt a bit of hot air touch the back of his neck.

“Whatcha doing Flappy?”

Cass encircled and leaned over the notepad protectively, “Noah! Don’t sneak up on people like that!”

“Let’s see watcha working on here,” Noah said as he reached an arm beside Cass and lifted a folder high into the air. Thumbing through it, his tongue stuck out of the side of his mouth as he read. “They’ve got him just doing deliveries. Don’t they know this kid was a Guildmaster?”

“Pro Tempore,” Cass automatically replied.

“Pro what-”

“An affectation by Guildmaster Hollis, Noah. God, you’re such an uneducated fool.” Again, the man looked proud of himself as she stood up and came closer. Taking the folder from him, Maggie placed it neatly back on the pile. “So, Cass, deliveries only? That seems far below your station from what I know of QuestWrights.”

Cass leaned back in his chair, “It’s what’s been given to me, Margaret.”

“Maggie.”

“Whatever.”

“Cass, you don’t have to accept everything they’re throwing at you. I know what’s been done to you is an injustice-”

“An injustice?” Cass spluttered, “You stole me away from my home. You kidnapped me!”

“Yah kidnarped m-”

“Not now, Noah!” Maggie yelled before he could make fun of him. The man put his hands in the air and took a few steps back to lean against the wall as she looked back at Cass.

“Look, we’re not just here to look at the finances, but also how they treat their people. Membership in the Guild, especially the Central Guild, comes with a lot of perks. Some can be upgraded, or downgraded, depending on what we see of how they treat their people, and I don’t just mean those who work in or for the Guild.”

“Like?”

“Like they import almost all of their Vellums,” Maggie said with a sly smile, “There’s a specific process to creating them, and the Central Guild doesn’t share it willingly. Some Alchemist came up with it, and the way it’s made is more than a little complicated.”

Then a thought arrived, ‘How were Quests assigned before Vellums were invented?”

“No clue,” She said with an apologetic smile, “But I’m sure it was hard to do. Everything with the System in the early days was, according to what I’ve heard. It doesn’t make changes too often, and when it does, there’s always a price that has to be paid.”

Cass scratched his chin, the feel of it rough on his hand. “You’re the first person I’ve heard talk about the System that way. Like it’s a living thing.”

“It is, in a way. That’s what makes all of these weak Callings so frustrating. It’s only people who push the edge of what their Calling can do that get real power.”

She adjusted her glasses, then looked down at his desk and the small tower waiting for him. “I’m going to try very hard to not only get you a pass to leave the tower, but also to reduce your daily Quota. What they’re doing is a sham, and I’ll make sure they and the Capitol know about it. You have my word on that, Cass.”

“I-”

That’s actually nice of her, but it still doesn’t erase her kidnapping me, Questor or not, word or not. I don’t need more words; I need the actions that fit them.

He said, “Thank you, Maggie,” got a nod back as she returned to her work, then focused on his Quests again. An hour and another visit from Quirrel quickly sped by before Ren opened the door and walked in. Standing behind the toweringly armored man was Ana, holding a black bag.

“Cassio, I believe this woman is here to see you.”

“Yes, I know her, Ren, thank you.” Cass frowned at the man, then smiled at Ana past his shoulder. “Heya Anna, how’s the day going?”

Ren cleared his throat as Noah said a few uncouth things to him, then joined Maggie as Ana placed down the bag at Cass’s Annex.

“The day is the same as always, Cass, even if my mornings are better since your arrival.” She looked over at the armored man with a light blush before her eyes went back to him, “How do you know tall, dark, and proper over there?”

“That’s Ren, and that group is the one I told you about. You know, the kidnappers.”

“Realllly?” She said, stretching out the word with an expressionless face. “He doesn’t seem the type. Acts a bit like my Bob, only with a little more charm to ‘em.”

“You just met him, Ana?”

“That was enough for me, Cass. Meet enough people in your life, and you’ll get the sense of things. Looks a bit like Bob, too.”

Cass’s mind connected the dots, “Please tell me you’re not interested in him that way. I don’t think they’re going to be here that long.”

“That’s the idea. I’m getting up there in years, Cass.” She subconsciously flattened her robe across her stomach, “A little bit of fun can carry a long way.”

“Isn’t he a bit young for you? He’s gotta be not even thirty.”

She fanned herself with a hand, “That’s why his brief stay is a benefit, not a negative. I’ll get all of the rush without any of the downside that comes with really knowing somebody. If you’ll excuse me, I think I’ll go get better acquainted.”

Cass snorted as she glided forward, her walk much more graceful as she went on the attack and easily pulled the man away from a confused Maggie. Noah was heading to the door, but not before he noticed what was happening. Looking at first Ren, then Cass, the weirdo gave them both a double thumbs up and a lascivious grin before leaving.

Cass cleared his throat, “gross.”

The day sped by as he did his best to ignore the new guests in the room. Eventually, he managed to finish his full tower before six, setting a new personal record and smiling at a relieved Quirrel after dropping off the stack at his desk. After explaining that he still wouldn’t have a chance to give him his gift, Cass left the Clerk behind and headed to his room.

Ana was already waiting; dinner started as she hummed softly to herself while cooking. Cass sat down at the table and tried to ignore the idea of why she was in such a good mood. Sitting across from him, they ate quietly before she excused herself for the night, veritably flying from the room once her duties were done for the day.

Cass spent the rest of the evening going over his experience screens and studying Command Center. At his late-night Bella ping, he could swear they were almost in range of the map. With that happy thought in mind, he lay down on his bed and slept dreamlessly, even though it took him longer than normal to zonk out and catch some rest.

A quick ping on waking told him Bella was the same distance away, but it was still early morning, and he had hope in his heart that today was the day.

The Stalwarts came and went quickly, but the Exponential daily was a chore and a half as he was forced to find enough weight to see how much he could curl. Without many options, he eventually settled on a pot full of water for the test, and that’s when Ana walked in. The scene was thus:

A half-naked Cass sat upon the couch.

A pot full of water rested on the edge of his fingers as he strained to curl it.

The couch, most of Cass himself, and several locations around the area were soaked in water.

He smiled at her as he lost focus and spilled a great deal of cold water across his lap.

She paused at the entryway, then closed the door behind her with a thoughtful expression.

“Exponential Daily?”

“Yep.”

“Right…right. I’ll get your porridge started.”

Breakfast and Tempered came and went, and just to challenge himself and complete something he didn’t want to wait on, Cass copied it and performed things a second time. Ana didn’t ask why she had to throw pillows at him again, but she did have a look on her face that implied she was thinking about it.

After they finished, she started cleaning up the room without being asked, and Cass stepped away to shower, saying thanks. With warm water drifting across his body, he opened up the Tempered Way and selected only the second node he’d ever unlocked.

[SURVIVOR PATH: WAY OF THE TEMPERED- STEP TAKEN]

Second node unlocked: Targeted Dexterity.

New Way function unlocked: You can now pick one of several dexterity-based routines each day.

Your daily Quest has been reset

You’ve gained a slight increase to the Way of the Tempered’s passive ability.

[WAY OF THE TEMPERED INTEGRATING]

Immediately, the feeling of energy rushing through his body kicked off as Cass leaned his full weight against the tiled wall beside him. His breathing continually stuttered as each pulse flushed his cheeks and woke him from the relaxed mood the warm water had put him in.

“Shit,” He panted, scrubbing away the soap stinging his eyes. “I forgot that happened on the second step.”

Shaking his head at his own callousness, Cass caught his breath and tested out the changes. When Stalwart had upgraded, he’d found a small but noticeable increase to his self-healing. Just by the history of the path, that should be the same here.

Lifting a small bottle of shampoo, he held it up in his right hand as high as he could, then dropped it. That same hand zipped unerringly into its path and caught the bottle just below his chest. In another test of his new reflexes, Cass did the same, but used his left hand to catch the bottle after keeping it resting at his side.

This time, he caught it just a little higher.

Closing his eyes, he tried the trick one more time. Holding the bottle up, Cass dropped it and tried to sense where it would be. His left hand moved from its resting spot on a course he somehow knew to be wrong. A twitch sent it just a little higher, where the bottle fell into place as his fingers closed on it quickly.

“That’s crazy,” He mumbled to himself. “But there’s one more test still to try.” Eyes still closed, Cass flipped the bottle, chucking it randomly at the shower wall.

Where it would bounce, he had no idea. Except, as the sound of it plinking against the tile, parts of his mind and body connected. Between the motion of flipping the bottle, the time it took to reach the wall, and the sound it made, parts of him recognized exactly where it would be. And as it fell, gently colliding with the skin of his palm as he reached out, the QuestWright was no longer surprised by how heightened his reflexes had become.

“Haha!” Cass held the bottle up victoriously, “I may not be a warrior like I wanted when I was younger, but it can’t be denied that I have the reflexes of one.”

Shaking his head, he finished up in the shower, then hopped out and toweled off. Looking in the large mirror over his golden sink, Cass really took a look at his body for the first time in a long time.

A strange man looked back at him. Where he had once been smooth-shaven and rather doughy, what remained after months of Stalwart workouts, time, and stress-filled days was something else entirely.

Gone was the young boy who hadn’t wanted to be a QuestWright. In that child’s place was a young man, face littered with unshaven stubble, scars across his body, with one brown eye and another pale. Of his physique, noone would be calling him pudgy now as clear muscle stared back from the mirror.

He was completely changed.

“Cassio Vale, QuestWright,” He said with a smile, his one good eye taking in all of the work he’d put in as the pale one dully glimmered. “Cassio Vale, he who fights with horses. Or maybe that’s Adya, who knows.”

The thought of Bella made him ping his equine bonded again. It was easier to tell this time; she was definitely moving toward him, and based on how far it felt, would likely arrive that day.

“Let’s hope Margaret wasn’t lying.”
