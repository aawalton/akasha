“Hi Fenrir,” I greet him, trying not to let my sudden surge of guilt colour my voice or Bond.

Why you make fake me? Fenrir asks immediately, direct and straightforward as always. He doesn’t make any attempt to conceal his hurt and the fact that he’s verbalised his feelings instead of just sending them through the Bond makes it clear just how serious this is for him.

I sigh and drop the facade, allowing my guilt and sincere regret for unintentionally hurting his feelings seep through the Bond.

“I didn’t want to bother you,” I tell him honestly. “You were sleeping peacefully when I reached out.”

My job, to guard, he tells me firmly. Sleep, wake, no matter. You need? I guard. Or what here for?

The last question comes out plaintively and I can’t resist the urge to turn and throw my arms around his neck, cuddling him tightly. I feel like the scum of the earth. Fenrir has been reliable and loyal since the day our Bond was created. He’s never caused me trouble, nor has he sought the spotlight. Instead, he’s just been content to fade into the background, protecting us, guarding us, and tipping the odds in our favour.

He deserves far more than to fear being replaced. And, though I never intended to do so, it’s clear that that’s exactly how he feels about coming across a golem of rock, sculpted in his own form.

He’s stiff under my embrace, the gesture not one lizogs use, so, instead of being selfish any longer, I pull away and shift around him so I’m staring him in the eyes. He avoids my gaze, looking sideways. I don’t try to stop him, sensing how overwhelmed he’s feeling.

“Fenrir, I would never replace you. I could never. You are one of the core members of the pack, our quiet guardian. Integral to the pack. I swear it.”

I sense that my words help a little, but less than I was hoping.

Maybe should.

“What?” I wonder if I’ve maybe heard him wrong, his communication was so quiet, so muffled.

Maybe should replace me, Fenrir repeats, a sense of resigned sorrow filling the Bond between us.

“Why would you suggest that?”

No good. Parasite infected pack, pack cubs died, too weak to protect from Enlightened beasts, watched Packleader face King alone because too weak…maybe Packleader right to prefer magical constructs.

“No Fenrir! None of that was your fault!” I immediately reject. “How would you have known about the parasites? You have no ability to heal others – or even to detect what the problem is in their bodies. As for the samurans dying, you weren’t even involved in that!

“And facing Enlightened beasts, let alone Beast Kings? You can do that when you’re a higher Tier. I know you’ve been putting a lot of effort into shoring up your internal matrix, and no one expects you to punch above your weight.” After barely managing to Evolve the first time, Fenrir has clearly learned his lesson and his internal matrix is far stronger now than it was before. Though, since he has a good deal of ground to make up, I suspect it will still be a while before he manages to reach Tier three.

But guardian no good if cannot manage threats, Fenrir points out relentlessly – I’ve never come across anyone so determined to be fired.

However, I have to admit that he has a point. Fenrir gives his all to every fight, but it’s true that the ‘all’ he has to give is a lot less impactful against Tier threes – and fours – than it was against those of lower tiers. However, maybe there’s something I could do to help him. After all, lizogs don’t normally act alone….

I nod at Rock Fenrir.

“I know you see those as taking your place, but what if you took charge of a pair?”

Fenrir sends me a sense of confusion.

“I mean, what if you were able to order a fire and a rock version of yourself around to help? That would boost your fire power.” I can’t help smiling at my own intentional pun. “You’d also have a wider range of strategies available to you, and be able to order them while you sleep.” I find myself warming to the idea. “I’d have to figure out how to recharge them on a regular basis, and we’d need to check that they will follow your instructions, but it should be possible.”

Fenrir reacts with a sense of thoughtfulness.

Be like pack leader? Of small pack?

“Yes, exactly.”

Fenrir gives the idea a few moments of thought, but then I feel a sense of interest coming from him.

Be able to protect more. Good.

I smile, happy to see he’s feeling better as he shifts his head forwards and almost meets my eyes even as his mouth relaxes open. I try not to screw my face up at the rotten meat scent of his breath. Still, he obviously picks something up from my body language or the Bond as he closes his mouth quickly.

I rub the scales on Fenrir’s head, scratching at a spot on his neck that I know he loves. The relaxation in his body only increases and his heavy tail starts bashing into the ground as he tries to wave it, only to have it stopped by the hard earth. I glance at it with some concern – we don’t want him accidentally embedding his tail spikes into the earth. Again.

It’s been too long, I realise. Too long since I just spent time with Fenrir, with no battles to worry about. And he’s not the only one. I’ve been neglecting pretty much everyone but the female raptorcats and nundas. I want to check on the male raptorcats, particularly Trouble, and also see how the deris are doing after their progress during the manastorm.

As for the others, I’ve said barely two words to Pride since we arrived here – he’s been spending most of his time outside the settlement, only coming back here to sleep with the baby samurans as a nighttime guard. I also want to touch base with Noir – see how his Class is coming along. Now that Loran has Evolved and shown what a non-Classer human can achieve, I’m curious to see where a beast with a Class is going.

Finally, he pulls away, his need to be useful overriding his need for comfort.

Test? he asks.

I feel a pang of loss as I lose contact with him, but respect his boundaries.

“Alright.”

Standing up, I walk over to Rock Fenrir. It doesn’t shift its head, but I know it’s paying attention to me.

I touch its back, feeling for the store of mana in its body. To my surprise, there’s a good deal left – far more than I was expecting given how many hours have passed. When I made Fire Fenrir, it ran out of magic far more quickly.

But then, perhaps that’s to be expected – the nature of Earth is that of stability and lack of change except under influence from other forces. Perhaps rock constructs are most effective when used as guards and are expected to sit or stand still in a single place and guard it.

At the same time, fire’s nature is that of change and transformation. Perhaps that’s why it ran out of mana far more quickly – to be still and guard someone unmoving is completely different. It is worth experimentation, but my theory is that Fire Fenrir will be more effective in battle than Rock Fenrir. Though I do wonder how much Fire Fenrir might be a glass cannon.

I pull my thoughts away from that for now – I’ll experiment later. Do what Fenrir tells you to, I instruct the construct, then step away.

“Try ordering it to do something.”

Fenrir doesn’t say anything, but his tail slows and then stops as his eyes fix intently on the construct.

A moment later, Rock Fenrir turns its head to face the real Fenrir and then it rolls over, exposing its underbelly. Its gaze looks indifferent, but satisfaction pulses through Fenrir’s Bond.

He looks up to me and I smile back at him.

“A success, I take it.”

He replies with a wave of agreement.

I consider summoning Fire Fenrir but decide not to for now – let Rock Fenrir and real Fenrir get used to each other before adding a third party to the mix.The smile on my face fades as I consider something else I’d like to test – but how, when I don’t want to risk hurting any of my companions?

What? Fenrir asks me, stepping closer. I realise that I must have let more through the Bond than I thought.

“I…never mind. It’s fine,” I dismiss.

Not fine, Fenrir argues. I half-glare at him. Since when did he get to be so sensitive?

I hesitate for a moment, then as he just waits patiently, I decide to go for it.

“You know I have Bond Teleport, right?”

Fenrir sends acknowledgement – he’s never been teleported personally, but no doubt those who have been have shared the experience with those who haven’t.

“OK, so, it was damaged when I pulled Kalanthia back. I’ve been trying to fix it and think I have…but I’m not certain I’ve succeeded.” For all I know, activating the Skill will cause a whole load of damage to me, my companion, or both of us. I’m pretty sure that it won’t hurt me too much – I have enough of an instinct for things in my Core space to be confident that I would sense that particular consequence. But I’m far less certain about the state of my companions post activation. “I need to test it but I don’t want to hurt any of you.”

Use me, Fenrir tells me with confidence – no, with enthusiasm. His posture perks up, becoming excited even at the thought. Lots of health. Not die easily. Good choice.

“Lots of health might not be enough if your body is spread in multiple directions at once,” I warn.

Likely?

I consider it.

“No,” I admit. Again, after the amount of time I spent immersed in the Skill, I’m pretty sure I’d have picked up on that. But I’m not a hundred percent certain, so that makes me reluctant to use Fenrir. At the same time, I can see the argument for why he’s the best choice. A Tier three will take more mana to move than a Tier two and he does have the biggest health pool of my Tier two Bound. Hopefully enough to keep him alive long enough for me to heal him – if such is necessary.

And, ultimately, I need to know what my edited Skill does – before it’s necessary to use it in a sticky situation. And Fenrir is both here and volunteering – my alternative is finding a beast to Bind just as a test subject, but my inner being recoils at the idea.

Finally, after several more rounds of going back and forth with myself and even diving into my Core space to check through my Skill another time for any danger signs, I decide that this is the best path forward, however little I like it.

“Alright, thank you for volunteering,” I agree, with reluctance clear in my voice. “But drink this first.”

I hold out a vial and Fenrir obediently drinks the contents as I tip it into his mouth. It’s one of the most powerful health regeneration potions I have available to me and will last for a few minutes – just to stack the deck further in Fenrir’s favour.

Fighting back the fear that attempts to fill my heart, I take several steps away from Fenrir. Then, concentrating on my Skill, I concentrate on pulling Fenrir to me.

With the amount of attention I’m paying to it and my new multi-tasking upgrade to Meditation, I can actually see the mana pour into my Skill and through it, connecting with Fenrir through the Bond that connects to Companion Bond. I see more and more mana flow into him, enough to make a faint glow around his body – to my eyes at least.

And then the mana in Bond Teleport pulls tight, like an elastic band that has been stretched to its limits.

There’s a flash of bright light in my Core space as Bond Teleport fully activates, drawing Fenrir through a portal it has created through and with the Bond.

One moment Fenrir is standing several paces away, the next he’s standing right next to me.

I feel the nagging of a notification, but I ignore it for now, instead immediately placing my hand on Fenrir’s neck and sending magic through his body, checking for anything even the slightest bit out of place.

I close my eyes and take in a deep, relieved breath when I find not even a scale in the wrong place.

A growing smile at the realisation that I’ve fixed my Skill – and made it more efficient, if the reduced amount of mana I had to pour into it was anything to go by – tugs at the corners of my mouth.

And then I give into the nagging sensation of notification and access my messages. My smile fades and I blink in astonishment as Energy swells within me.

What the hell?
