It took Peter a couple of minutes to gather a trio of thick-armed Integrated. All of them stood there with their hands on the severed arm, squirming as they did their best not to look at the ruin that was Jeff's face and head.

Not harboring much hope that the patient would live past the next couple of minutes, Luke readied himself by extending six threads of mana around the edges of the claw. If they just pulled, the whole head would come off, he figured. Perhaps Jeff's chances of survival would improve a little if at least part of his head remained. Either way, it was a small chance, but for what Peter offered, Luke was willing to try.

"Ready?" he asked the muscle-boys. They nodded. Looking over at Dot, she nodded as well, and the group of healers stood there, pouring their mana into the healing bed.

"Well, here we go, I guess," Luke said, some of his apprehension leaking into his voice. "Pull on three."

"One..."

"Two..."

"Three!"

A wet, sloughing sound made Luke shudder, despite knowing it was coming. The immense trauma made Jeff's heart stop less than a second after the claw came away, but Dot performed her task as Luke used Weaver's Renewal, pumping Jeff's meta-heart full to the point of bursting. Siphoning mana from the strange enchantments on the bed, Luke directed to the head even as it reformed from nothing.

Someone puked. Then another. Gross. One of the healers passed out, and Luke opened his eyes to see Jeff's head piecing itself back together. New matter formed to fill the gaps, taking the shape of what he guessed was Jeff's face.

"Keep going with the compressions," he told Dot. There was no skill to this healing, nothing he could do other than keep up with the healing mana. Since nothing was pushing back on the skill, it would all come down to whether Weaver's Renewal was powerful enough to get it done.

Turned out, it was. A head and face took shape from nothing more than strips of flesh and bits of skull and brain, but something wasn't quite right. The miraculous healing wasn't complete, because the brain was nothing more than a black void of inactivity.

All the physical necessities were present, but that wasn't enough. The neural web was dark. No impulses passed between the lobes, and not a single current ran through the brain's pathways.

While Luke pondered the problem, he began helping Jeff to breathe. While Dot kept the heart going, she'd either forgotten or was too stunned to realize the lungs had fallen dormant. If you wanted to tickle a brain back into activity, you needed the rest of the body to act the part.

With no activity, there was no real way of bringing the brain back. Brain dead was brain dead. You didn't come back from that, not with modern medicine. Good thing Luke didn't work within the bounds of that old system.

People seemed to be holding their breath while Luke worked, pushing mana through the inactive mana channels in Jeff's neck. Building new ones branching up into the patient's brain took more than a little effort, and it was like Luke was creating a weave within the man. The parallels to his healing of Alan weren't lost on him, and the consequences screamed at him to stop from the back of Luke's mind, but he ignored that scared voice. He'd decided to help Jeff and would see it through to its natural conclusion. Either this worked, or it didn't. What came after was not relevant to this particular moment.

Still, this time Luke didn't go crazy with adding pathways and mana channels before setting off sparks with Weaver's Rupture. Again and again, he attempted to get a synapse response, but the individual blasts were like a lone piece of firework in a dark sky. Brilliant for a moment, but once it passed, nothing of its light remained.

Refusing to give up, Luke upped the intensity, adding more and more blasts, with several going simultaneously, sparking short bursts of activity between them. That was it!

"More," Luke muttered as he waved a hand toward the medical staff. "More circulation, yeah? Give him something to get the blood pumping!"

"Right!" he heard a voice call out. "A vasopressor IV!"

Someone else added. "We should put him on a ventilator!"

"Cold! We should induce therapeutic hypothermia!" a third voice called out.

"We have an ice mage in the building," Peter suggested.

"Call them in! Now!"

Dot: "I should have thought about those things before we got started."

Luke, focusing on keeping up momentum in Jeff's brain, didn't have much attention to spare, but sent a quick message.

Luke: "You're not the only doctor here. Won't matter much unless the guy's brain starts firing on its own soon."

For once, mana wasn't an issue. Siphoning off the other healers through the bed, Luke had plenty to spare, but trying to get Jeff's brain going was like attempting to start an old lawnmower. It would sputter and die a second later.

There was nothing else to it. Luke needed to overpower the damn thing's wish to remain dead. The problem was building on that initial spark. Even if he forced the matter and powered through, adding to the first burst of synapses, it wouldn't go over the threshold necessary to stabilize itself, if there even was one.

Reading through the skill list, ignoring the bustle of activity in the room as more people entered and some left, Luke found something that might be the answer. Something he hadn't used a whole lot.

Weaver's Mercy (Rank 2) [Active - Mana Cost: Moderate]: Allow your target another few moments in their current condition.

It would work. Luke could feel it in his gut. Building up to the maximum of sparks he'd managed before, he readied the skill, triggering it at the apex.

Building on that static glow in Jeff's brain, Luke pushed himself, adding more and more threads of mana in pairs to fire and spark synapses, forcing life back out of its slumber.

Once Weaver's Mercy ran out, it was time for the ace up Luke's sleeve. The Healer's Moment froze time. This was it. The make-or-break moment. Straining to keep the blasts smaller than small and firing at an incredible rate, Luke pushed, then pushed a little more, and in the final moment, he let out a low growl as he used Weaver's Rupture through every single thread of mana he had available.

Head spinning, Luke staggered back, dropping his threads as time resumed. He didn't need a thread connecting himself and the patient to know the outcome.

It had worked.

Luke made a fist, muttering. "Fuck yeah."

The room was hushed once Luke notified everyone of Jeff's rebooted brain activity. The ice mage, a short Korean woman with purple-dyed hair and one of those spiked collars you see on some dogs in comics, had made the place way too cold for comfort, but one of the doctors explained how it made sense to cool everything down.

"So it worked?" Dot asked after a drawing in a deep breath, as if she'd forgotten to breathe while they all stared down at Jeff on the bed.

"His body isn't pulling in more healing," one of the healers said, and the group of them stepped away, giving the still unconscious patient some room.

"He's waking up," Luke said, stepping to the side and over the now dislodged claw, surrounded by blood, on the floor. The Integrated who'd helped pull it out had left, but hadn't brought the thing with them. Lazy bastards.

"We're seeing increased eye movement," another doctor said. She was leaning over Jeff's face, obscuring him from view. "Oh," she continued, bobbing her head. "Welcome back."

Peter stepped up to the bed. "He's awake?"

Everyone crowded in, wanting a peek, except for Luke. Weaver's Perception was long since sensitive enough to pick up on a person's state of wakefulness. Jeff had woken up. The procedure had been a success.

The skill told him something else, though. It wasn't all sunshine and rainbows. Luke wasn't quite sure how it worked, but if he concentrated and sort of immersed himself in the sea of information Weaver's Perception provided him with, almost nothing about a person's state of physical or mental well-being was beyond his ability to read.

And read it he did. Something was wrong. Jeff was awake and his brain 'worked' for lack of a better word, but his cortisol level was through the roof, meaning incredible anxiety. As if that wasn't bad enough, adrenaline surged through Jeff's system, meaning he was in a full-on state of fight or flight. This wasn't right.

"Step away from the bed," Luke said, raising his voice.

"What's wrong?" Lemira asked, picking up on Luke's own anxiousness.

He shook his head. "I'm not sure."

"Hi?" Luke heard a new voice ask, and when the medical staff, Peter, and Dot moved out of the way, he saw it was Jeff who'd spoken. His eyes were wide open, looking from face to face as he pulled the blood-soaked covers up to his cheek. "Where am I?"

"You are in the headquarters of Integrated Solutions Group, in the medical wing," Peter explained. "You sustained an injury, and we brought you back for healing."

Jeff brought a hand to his forehead, touching it with some apprehension. "I-I don't remember."

A doctor who hadn't spoken before pushed past the ice mage and leaned over the bed. "It will come back to you soon. Some memory loss of recent events is to be expected after a head... injury.

"Oh," Jeff said.

"What is the last thing you remember?" that same doctor asked.

Jeff frowned, looked down, and blinked a few times. Luke sensed the man's anxiety spike. Serotonin and dopamine dropped at the same time, indicating sadness.

"I," Jeff began, licking his dry lips. "I don't remember anything. Nothing but darkness."
