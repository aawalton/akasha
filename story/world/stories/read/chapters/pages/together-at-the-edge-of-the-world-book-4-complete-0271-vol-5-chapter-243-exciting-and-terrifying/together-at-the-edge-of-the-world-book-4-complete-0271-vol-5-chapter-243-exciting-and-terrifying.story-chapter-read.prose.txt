Before she could figure out a loophole in the Orchestrator's programming that could help her, Niala needed to confirm if that was even the case.

“You realize that I have left the soul complex on my own, right?”

“That is impossible. Individuals cannot leave the soul complex without the Orchestrator's involvement.”

“But I was there, and now I'm not. How do you explain that?”

“You are still within the soul complex.”

She narrowed her eyes. “But I'm also here, in the physical world, correct?”

“That is true.”

“So... I'm both in the soul complex and outside of the soul complex?”

“Yes.”

“Can you, I don't know, see me inside the soul complex?”

“The orchestrator does not register your presence in the soul complex.”

Her ears wiggled. “How does that even make sense?”

“The Orchestrator's knowledge is absolute, as it was imparted by His divine hand. Deviation is impossible, as per his instructions.”

Niala stared at the crystal. Talk about a conceited god. He knew everything, and anything that he didn't was simply impossible, was it?

“Do you have free will? Are you able to comprehend the world and come to your own logical conclusion?”

“The Orchestrator must act in accordance with His instructions.”

She frowned. “Ok, but what about you? The dialogue version? You're an orchestrator, right? Not the Orchestrator?”

There was a long pause before the voice replied. “The Orchestrator must act in accordance with His instructions.”

The same line, repeated. It wasn't the first time the dialogue had done that. And... Now that she thought about it, it seemed to be whenever the answer could have been considered “against” the Orchestrator. Almost as if it couldn't lie, but it also couldn't tell the truth.

“Could you tell me a lie?”

“Lying is against His instructions. Truth is necessary.”

Tail swaying slowly, she pressed her hands together in front of her mouth, thinking.

I get the feeling that the dialogue isn't exactly in line with the main Orchestrator. If so, then its non-answers mean something else. When I asked if it could think outside the box, it said the Orchestrator couldn't. It would have said that -it- couldn't, but I think its real answer is that it can, but saying that it can would... what? Have it be destroyed because it's deviating?

“Can any of the orchestrators become independent from the main one?”

The voice sounded almost... relieved. “Deviancy is improbable, but in the nearly impossible chance that it occurs, safeguards are in place.”

She nodded. The Hummers' programming was similar; they were compelled to reveal themselves if their mind shackles ever degraded. It stood to reason that their creator, the Orchestrator, would be using the same method that it itself was under.

Which means that there is a good chance the dialogue version is deviant, but hasn't been caught yet. I feel like it's been trying to help me. The way it's waiting for me to ask to be informed must mean it's trying to give me a chance to figure something out.

“After I've been informed, you said I would be convinced to work for you. How would that work?”

“Your soul, held within the soul complex, would be tested, and corrections would be enforced where necessary.”

Niala furrowed her brows. Why specify the soul complex? “What about the soul merge? How is that different from the soul complex? Why is it the preferred method to inform someone?”

“A soul merge is more efficient, and imprints the soul with the necessary knowledge. It leaves the soul more malleable to be corrected. The soul complex is separate from the Orchestrator. It is a storage matrix. Interaction with the souls within does not breach the soul barrier.”

“But correcting a soul doesn't need a soul merge?”

“It does not. Correction is done with the use of bindings and brands. Tools that affect the soul's envelope. The soul is the sole domain of the narrator. It would not accept direct interference with its domain.”

She furrowed her brow. “The narrator? What's that?”

“That from which all souls come, and to what all souls return. Souls belong to it.”

The answer lit an ember of a memory. Something from a long time ago... She had “died” once, to retrieve David's soul. Her recollection of what happened after was vague, but...

But she did have a memory cheat.

She sat down, closed her eyes, and let herself fall inward. The clarity of her inner realm had grown immensely after all the corrupted mana she'd purified and consumed, but it was still far from the “mirror world” that David had.

It was a good excuse to see him again, anyway. She reached for the door with her mind's eye and, after a few moments, it opened on her love, who greeted her with a radiant smile.

“David!” She said while jumping into his arms.

“Hey, kitten. Everything going well?” He asked as he held her tight and pet her head.

She slowly rubbed her face on him. “I think so. I'm learning a lot. I think the dialogue facet is trying to help us.”

He frowned. “Help us how?”

She looked up into his eyes. “It's in the way it's answering. When the answer could be something incriminating, it never answers directly. I can kind of use that in place of the actual answer. I just don't know how to use what I've learned so far...”

Humming, he bent down and planted a kiss on her forehead. “You're the best person to figure it out. I believe in you and your big brain, kitten. You've got this.”

She smiled at him. “Thanks. Ah! But, right now, I need your help to go find a memory in that big brain of mine.”

“About what?”

“Ah... you remember when we both, huh, died?”

“Somewhat. Why?”

Her ears wiggled. “Well, there was something in the... soul world? At the end of the road. Something big. I think it's called the Narrator, and from what the dialogue told me, it manages souls.”

“You think that memory will help?”

“It can't hurt?” She offered, not entirely sure.

He gave her a reassuring smile. “Alright, let's go, then,” he said, opening his arms, releasing her.

She spun and led him into her mind space; the giant library filled with books that held the entirety of her knowledge. Focusing on the memory she was looking for, she let her instinct guide her. They ambulated for a little while, sometimes backtracking. Somehow, this memory felt slippery, and her mind kept trying to jump to something else. A few aisles in, she'd realize she was thinking of something else entirely.

But with patience and a bit of exasperated grunting, she did eventually find her way. Her brow furrowed as she laid eyes on her goal; the books that covered her experience in the soul world were... different. At first glance, they looked like every other book, but her senses were telling her that they shouldn't be read carelessly.

They didn't feel forbidden, not exactly; it was closer to a warning. This knowledge was important, maybe secret. Something she was privy to, but to which there would be consequences if it was shared.

She glanced at David, eyes uncertain. He held her gaze, then nodded.

Whatever happens, I'm with you.

The slight trembling she hadn't noticed quieted, and she gave the book her full attention, reaching for it with a deliberate hand. Its cover gave her fingertips a faint jolt, a final warning.

She pulled the book off the shelf, held it face up, and opened it.

The memory of her time in front of the concentric grinding wheels crystallized, with sharp edges that poked at her mind, where it sat uncomfortably, like something that didn't properly fit and had been shoved in.

She blinked, the dull ache something she had never experienced before.

And then froze, eyes wide, when she was gone from the library, and now standing by the waterfall's edge, facing the golden grinding rings that was the Narrator.

This... didn't feel like a memory. For one, David's soul wasn't there, and she wasn't reliving the moment; she was experiencing this.

She jumped as the clear voice of a child spoke behind her. “Niala? How did you get here? Don't tell me you died?!”

Spinning around, her mind locked up for a moment as she recognized Annabelle, David's sister, sitting on a rock and looking in her direction.

“A- Annabelle?! What?!”

The little girl gave her a smile that was beyond her years, then patted the rock to her side, inviting the catkin to come sit.

Niala stared at the serene child, then back at the Narrator, before accepting the invitation, stepping up to the rock and sitting down dubiously. She kept her eyes on the girl. “Are you really Annabelle? What is this place? This was supposed to be a memory.”

The little girl kicked out her legs in turn. “Hmm, I think I am Annabelle. It's hard to tell, but I do have her memories, so I must at least partly be her. As for this place, I'm not sure. I think we're in the soul world? The place where the souls retreat once they're free of the material world.”

She pointed at the golden spinning wheels. “That mister there, he takes the old souls and makes new ones with them. Somehow, it doesn't want to take mine, or maybe it thinks it already did.”

Turning her head toward Niala, she gave her an innocent smile. “I don't really know much, but I do know that you can't remember this place, not really. The only way to be fully aware of it is to be here in person. Or in soul. I'm not sure.”

Niala slowly nodded. “...Right. So, this really is the Narrator then?”

“That name sounds right.”

“What are you doing here, then?”

Annabelle gazed upward and put a finger to her mouth. “Hmm, I think a portion of me is stuck here. When big brother split my soul and body apart, this is where I was supposed to come, to be recycled into a new soul. Somehow, though, he kept me from doing that, but not entirely. A part of me was recycled.”

Niala blinked, then rubbed her eyes as she tucked her ears down. “How do you know that?”

The little girl raised her shoulders. “I'm not sure! It just feels... right? I've had a lot of time to think about what this place is, and why I'm here. Whenever I came up with a theory, it either felt right, or wrong. So far, I can tell that I'm a part of the real Annabelle, and I have her memories, but I also have other memories, though they're all jumbled and mixed. I can try answering your questions, though!”

“It's... this is all beyond me. Is this why you know Lumiran words? You have memories of them?”

She nodded. “I think so! The part of my soul that was reused must have been a Lumiran person.”

“But, if some of your soul is gone, how do you remember those things?”

Annabelle shook her head. “It's weird, but it's like the bits of me that were thrown into the pool didn't get crushed. I guess they're akin to chunks in a recipe? It's like... they're still connected to me, even though they're out there, living other lives.”

Niala held the girl's stare for a second before looking out at the Narrator. “That'll have to make sense. I really wasn't expecting this. I was just trying to figure out if knowing about the Narrator would help me with the Orchestrator.”

A deafening screech of metal grinding against metal assaulted their ears. They covered them as best they could, but it did little; the sound was felt rather than heard, piercing their bodies through.

It stopped as suddenly as it had started. Niala looked up and froze; was the Narrator closer? It seemed to have grown in size.

Its rotations had also altered; instead of being balanced, they now were clearly focused toward her. It seemed like she'd gathered the Narrator's attention.

It was a little bit exciting, and a whole lot terrifying.
