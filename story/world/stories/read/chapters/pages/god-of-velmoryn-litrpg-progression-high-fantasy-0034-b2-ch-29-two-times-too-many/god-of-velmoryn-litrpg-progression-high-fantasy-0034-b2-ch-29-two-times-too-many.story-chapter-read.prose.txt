Reest was buckled on one knee, paralyzed by the backlash of his own exertion. He could only watch as the condensed sphere of white-hot annihilation he had just fired whistled back toward him. He was drained, unable to move a muscle, let alone cast a spell to intercept the incoming inferno.

“This is outrageous!” The Marquess roared from the hovering platform, swinging his hands. His refined, humanoid form couldn't contain his fury any longer; it cracked as a suffocating, violent black fire enveloped him.

[Due to high risk of death, the duel has been stopped. The winner is Aure…]

The blue notification flashed before my eyes, but before I could even finish reading it, it distorted, collapsing into a cloud of glitching, colorful pixels.

What the hell?

Down in the arena, the orb crashed into Reest.

There was no sound at first, just a blinding flash that erased everything. Then came the roar. The explosion surged upward, transforming into a spiraling tornado of devastating fire. The inferno surged in all directions, licking at the edges of the arena.

Yet, despite its overwhelming power, the inferno didn’t last. The flames died out abruptly, revealing the center of the impact.

Reest stood there.

He wasn't ash. He wasn't even burned. He stood amidst the darkened earth completely unharmed, as if the attack had simply passed through him.

“How…” Aurelion muttered the word through gritted teeth. That single word took the last of his remaining powers as his legs gave out. He stumbled forward, barely managing to catch himself before his face hit the packed earth.

He had given everything, survived the impossible, and reflected a god-tier attack... for nothing.

“ATTACK IMMEDIATELY!” the Marquess yelled, voice far too deep and hoarse for anything humanoid.

Down in the arena, Reest stood with his head lowered. But at the command, the demon snapped to attention.

He flapped his wings, the air cracking around him, and surged toward the defenseless Aurelion. A fresh sphere of blazing orb began to form above his palm.

“The Pantheon will stop the duel,” V exclaimed, his voice rising in panic as he felt the air around me grow heavy. “Please, Your Lordship! Don’t break the rules or there will be punishment for both of us!”

I ignored him. I looked toward the arena, my focus narrowing to a single point. Reest was only a step away from Aurelion, his hand extended, death hovering inches from my unconscious Angel.

“What are they waiting for…” I hissed, already trying to channel more of my divine power into my spectral body.

But before I could, the arena froze as though time itself had stopped.

A pillar of blinding white divine energy slammed down, enveloping Reest. It was brighter than anything I had seen and immensely more domineering. It plucked the demon from the ground as if he were a toy and instantly moved him back onto the Falvan platform.

Aurelion, however, didn't get transported. The moment Reest’s charge was halted, my Angel simply vanished.

[Duel Verdict]

The deity who challenged you has broken the laws and intervened directly in the duel. They have paid the appropriate price for this transgression; thus, the result stands and the winner is Reest.

However, due to the forceful alteration of the laws, you shall be compensated. 10,000 Divinity Points (50% of the divinity spent to intervene). 1,000 Divinity Points.

Additionally, your Angel will be healed completely.

If you feel this judgement was unjust, you may seek The Five.

I was left speechless. It turned out that even in the realm of gods, the golden rule applied: he who has the coin, makes the rules. The Marquess hadn't just broken the law, he had purchased an exemption.

Well, at least I was compensated.

11,000 Divinity. It was a huge number - 11% of what I needed to ascend to the next Rank. But the compensation came short compared to the lesson I had just learned. The laws of the Pantheon weren't absolute commandments; they were merely a mechanism to extract tribute for whoever sat on the throne of this realm.

I glanced at the system notification offering me the right to seek judgment from "The Five." I scoffed internally. I couldn’t trust that. If those deities were the true architects of this place, then the corruption I had just witnessed was woven into the very fabric of their creation. Expecting justice from them would be like asking a wolf to punish the pack for hunting.

I should buy the glaive for Aurelion, see if I can acquire the blueprints the Velmoryns need, and leave. This place is a dangerous…

“My King.” I heard Aurelion’s trembling voice before his form even fully manifested.

A sphere of pure white light ignited a step away from me, swirling rapidly until it solidified into my Angel. He appeared already on his knee, his head bowed low.

“I let you down. I brought shame to your name,” he rasped, his voice sounding hollow, as if his spirit had been crushed alongside his body.

“What?” For a moment, I failed to understand why he was blaming himself. But looking into his thoughts, I found a resolve that surprised me in its nature. Aurelion knew the rules were broken. He knew the odds were stacked against him from the very beginning. He simply didn't care.

In his eyes, the unfairness of the fight did not excuse the outcome. He was born to be victorious. More than that, he was born to never let me see his defeat. To him, the circumstances were irrelevant; the failure was his alone.

“I do not blame you,” I said, trying to soften my voice, but it came dull through the spectral body.

“I do not deserve to serve such a magnanimous Liege,” Aurelion mumbled, refusing to lift his eyes. He stared intensely into the floor as if wishing the stone would open up and swallow him whole.

“Aurelion,” I started, trying to comfort him, though I struggled to find words that would reach a creature whose entire existence was defined by martial perfection. “I’m glad you surviv…”

I instinctively stopped speaking as two blue windows flashed before me.

[Divine Vow]

Aurelion has willingly sworn an oath. He pledges never to fail his liege or suffer defeat again. Should he break this vow, he accepts death as a worthy punishment.

[Last Chance]

A passive skill generated by your Angel’s absolute resolve. Effect: +40% increase to all Physical Attributes.

“What have you done…” My mind went numb as I read the text.

I was furious, but at the same time, I struggled to bury it deep, hiding it from Aurelion. Through the connection between us, I could feel his despair as clearly as my own emotions.

This link…

Showing my anger to Aurelion right now was dangerous. He was clearly prone to reckless decisions. If he sensed my rage, he might mistake it for rejection. But I still needed to make it clear that I didn’t agree with the price he had just paid.

A 40% boost to physical attributes was massive. If it stacked with Aurelion’s other bonuses, he would become significantly stronger.

But the penalty attached to it was catastrophic. With the fight against the God of Night and Moons approaching, the likelihood of losing my only Angel had just increased immensely.

Even one defeat… if he loses a fight even once, he will die just because of this stupid vow.

A fresh wave of anger surged through me, and it only intensified when a voice I had no desire to hear broke the silence.

“And here I thought for a moment you were a worthy replacement for me.” The Marquess landed heavily on my platform, a cruel laugh rattling in his throat. He had regained his humanoid form, his lips curled in a sly, arrogant grin.

I chose silence. Creatures like him hated it the most when they were ignored.

But Aurelion couldn't let it slide. He was already drowning in shame, desperate for any chance to prove his worth. He shot to his feet, placing himself between me and the Netherworld deity.

“Take another step and I will finish what Reest started,” the Marquess sneered, his voice dropping low.

“Oh,” I snorted, trying to act calm. “I would love to accept yet another 11 000 Divinity from you, but sadly, I am in a hurry. I will gladly accept your tribute some other time.”

I turned my back on him as his face twisted in rage, and I floated toward the portal. I needed to get away from this lunatic before the situation spiraled further.

“You!” he roared. “Stop immediately!”

I kept moving.

Then, a surge of Divinity erupted behind me. Even through my astral body, the sensation was nauseating - like the air had turned to rot. Evil itself was blooming at my back.

“I warned you,” he bellowed, the voice no longer human. It was guttural and hoarse.

I turned around.

The Marquess had fully transformed. A nightmare of four arms and a head crowned in black fire. His wings were devoid of feathers, composed instead of wet, bloated chunks of flesh.

He leapt.

He moved with the speed of a flash. One moment he was standing there; the next, he was closing the distance.

His clawed hand was inches from my astral body. Then he froze as though time itself had stopped,

White chains materialized from the thin air, shimmering with a faint, cold silver light. They coiled around his limbs, his throat, his wings. They tightened instantly until the bloated flesh bulged against the links, threatening to burst.

“Your Lordship, they serve directly under The Five,” V whispered.

I hadn’t even noticed the merchant get so close to me. I was too focused on the monster frozen mid-strike in front of me.

Then they came.

Two figures descended from above, radiating light like stars. They wore silver plate armor and heavy white cloaks, both engraved with the symbol of five interlocking crowns.

“You have violated the laws of the Pantheon twice this day,” a woman said the moment her foot touched the floor.

Her voice was calm, terrifyingly so. She walked toward the immobilized Marquess, her pace slow, soundless, and utterly unconcerned by his struggles.

“You were pardoned once because of your patron, but…”

She raised her hand, bringing a gloved finger toward the Marquess’s forehead. His eyes widened, but that was all the freedom of motion he had.

“I, Tenth of the Order, sentence you to death,” she declared, voice emotionless.

The Marquess’s figure suddenly blazed with white light. Cracks spread across his dark skin. Then, he began to peel away - flaking off layer by layer into the air, dissolving into nothingness until only a bright white light remained.

The woman snapped her fingers and the white orb vanished, taking the chains with it.

The Marquess was dead.
