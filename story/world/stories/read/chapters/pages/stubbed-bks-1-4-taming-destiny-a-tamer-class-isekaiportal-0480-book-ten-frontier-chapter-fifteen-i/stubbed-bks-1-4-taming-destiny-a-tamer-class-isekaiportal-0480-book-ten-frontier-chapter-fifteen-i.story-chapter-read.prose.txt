I blink.

Elves. Why didn’t I think of that? I ask myself sarcastically. Then to Kalanthia, “I’m going to need a bit more than that. Are there humans on this continent – apart from the ones who have come here for the expedition? Or just elves?” Though, as I ask that, I consider how Kalanthia would have become so wary of humans if there weren’t any here – unless she met them somewhere else?

There are both, Kalanthia responds. There are some places where they mix; others where they are separate. Many elven clans seek to oppress the human guilds while some work together with them. It is how I first encountered humans – they sought to Tame my mother as a weapon against a local clan.

I hold up my hand to stop her.

“Hang on a moment. I feel like I need so much more context than I currently have.”

Growing a boulder behind my back, I lean back and steeple my fingers. Then, as my stomach rumbles, I realise that Tilathis wasn’t the only one hungry and thirsty.

Figuring that Kalanthia probably is too, I pull out the biggest carcass I have from the battle with the Tier four’s forces, laying it in front of her. She sends me a grateful look and uncharacteristically doesn’t complain about the lack of Energy in the meat, tucking in straight away.

Then I take some time to refresh myself from rations in my Inventory – fortunately I had replenished those with leftovers from the feast. A few minutes of silent munching is enough to allow me to gather my thoughts sufficiently – and to put some pieces together.

“Moriaxar history indicates that the original settlers were refugees from this continent, though, from what Nicholas said, the original documents were all burned in an unfortunate fire and the details became lost to time. Do I take it that the refugees were running from the elves?”

I know little of human history, Kalanthia replies, predictably if I’d thought about it. And I was not alive at the time. However, it seems likely from what little I know of their interactions.

“And what you know comes from when you were held in captivity?”

Yes. As a cub. I learned little that was new in my recent captivity.

I nod thoughtfully. While recognising that Kalanthia’s information is limited, anything is better than my current state of knowing nothing. Because if there’s a whole civilisation somewhere on this continent, we need to know about it. Especially if it’s run by elves who are inclined to oppress our species.

“Alright, where are these elves and humans? And do you have any idea of how many there are? Approximately, if nothing else.”

They are on the other side of the mountain range that divides the continent almost in half. I cannot estimate their numbers, however, they cover a large part of the other side of the continent. They number many, the humans more than the elves.

I frown, pausing before putting the next piece of stewed meat into my mouth.

“If there are more humans than elves, why are the humans the ones being oppressed? And why only on the other side of the mountain range? Why haven’t they come to this side?” Every question Kalanthia answers only leaves me with more.

I cannot be certain as to why the humans are not dominant, however, from what I heard as a cub – as little sense as it made to me at the time – humans cannot advance as beasts do; elves can. As for why they have not crossed the mountain range, there are too many powerful beasts on this side, and even two Ascendants who rule the mountain range and benefit from the Pure Energy stream that comes close to the surface within it.

“I thought your mate’s territory extended to the mountains,” I question, even as my mind turns over the new information. Do humans here have Classes? They must do – the refugees brought Classes with them to Iseria. That’s how they pushed back the Nethyar to establish Moriax in the first place. Yet elves can advance ‘as beasts’? Why the difference?

Paying no attention to my internal questions, Kalanthia answers the one I asked aloud.

The mountains we can see from here are mere foothills in comparison with the range that splits apart the continent like the jagged teeth of some massive beast.

I sit up a little and gaze at Kalanthia in astonishment. I can feel that she’s being utterly sincere, but when I consider the size of the mountains we observed from the sea, a range that dwarfs those seems simply impossible.

“Right,” I say slightly faintly, trying to rejig my world view. Literally. I always knew that the scouts had only seen a fraction of the Lost Continent’s coast, but I’d still somehow created an image in my head of a place that was a whole lot smaller than Kalanthia’s words imply it to be.

In comparison to the Lost Continent, Iseria, of which Moriax covers only a third, seems like an island.

Maybe it’s not so surprising that the elves and humans and whatever haven’t come this side of the mountain range – they must have enough land to be getting on with on the other side, and if there are Tier sixes who claim the mountains as their territory, I can understand that would be an incredibly risky expedition.

However, Kalanthia seems to think that they will pursue her, despite that. There must be more to it than her being a Tier three.

“Why do the elven clan – this ‘Blood Fang’ was it?” She sends a wave of confirmation. Otherwise known as ‘The Bastards’. “Yeah, them. What makes you think they’ll risk provoking the Tier sixes? Or do you think they’ll come via ship?”

Though, thinking about it, if they have ships, why wouldn’t they have populated this area? Yes, they do surely have plenty of land, but there are always enterprising people who are willing to put their life on the line to seek the next opportunity. I highly doubt that a different world has changed that about humans, at least.

They seek a device I stole, Kalanthia sighs. And they will not rest until it has been retrieved.

This just gets increasingly convoluted the more I learn. And worrying in its implications.

“What device, and why did you steal it?”

That story is one that begins in many places. She is silent for a long moment. I sense her gathering her thoughts so just wait quietly for her to continue. I believe that I have told you that it is difficult for my kind to conceive?

“You indicated something along those lines. And also that it was even more difficult to…keep your cubs alive even after they were born.”

Yes. Tilathis and I mated when we were both mere Evolved beasts. We had several cubs, but all were killed or kidnapped before reaching adulthood – the other side of the mountains is not an easy place for beasts to survive.

“The humans and elves?” I ask, with some compassion.

If not them, then other beasts who saw our cubs as easy and nutritious prey. She hesitates for a moment, then I feel her resolve. I did not tell you, but several of our body parts are prized among the elves and humans. Our brains for mind-sharpening tinctures; our livers for powerful healing potions; our claws for strengthening blades, and our skin for magic-resistant armour. The humans who captured my mother and I harvested what they could – and waited for me to grow sufficiently to harvest the rest. Fortunately, I was able to escape before that time.

Lifting her right forepaw, she turns it over, then shifts so her claws scythe out. The elves did not take my claws only for their own safety. Then she pins me with a look of such vulnerability that my heart aches inside me. Without a Flesh-Shaper of your proficiency, that was the only ingredient they could take without risking killing me as my skin must be removed in its entirety to gain its magic-resistant properties, and neither my liver nor brain would regenerate quickly enough to save me either.

I hear her unspoken message – that, if I chose, I could violate her far worse than those who had held her captive before I summoned her from their grasp. Inside me, the ember of fury at these Blood Bastards grows in heat.

In that moment in time, I’m glad that they will be chasing her – that means I don’t have to go to them to teach them the mistake they made. Instead, I can make sure that we’re ready to…greet them when they come looking to take her back.

“Thank you for telling me, Kalanthia. I’m sorry for your experiences with humans – and elves. I swear, I will never use this knowledge against you or Lathani.”

Kalanthia feels my utter sincerity and I feel something tight inside her relax for what feels like the first time since she accepted my Bond.

I know you won’t, she replies with the certainty of trust rewarded.

I lean forward and scratch her under the chin for a bit, the gentle contact settling both of us after such a shocking revelation.

Finally, I lean back again.

“So, you were saying that having cubs was difficult – and keeping them alive to adulthood was worse?”

Yes, Kalanthia replies, her eyes shuttering slightly, the vulnerability hidden behind her usual mask of coolness. Yet now I’ve seen behind it, I will never be completely fooled that it is all that exists. Tilathis and I decided to make the trek over the mountains – seeking out land that was not inhabited by either elves or humans. Through an…unfortunate series of events, we accidentally came into contact with the Ascendant of the territory.

Her ears pin themselves back, and I feel the fear that ripples through her just at the memory. He was in a good mood, and allowed us to escape his presence with only a minor price. I wonder for a moment what that price was, but stay silent for now. As we left his territory, he sent a message to me – only to me. It did not need a response at that moment, yet haunted me long after.

“What was the offer?” I ask curiously, as Kalanthia trails off with a faraway look in her eyes. My question pulls her back to the present – she blinks and is back with me.

In good time, she chides, sounding more like her normal self. We found an appropriate territory – all the fighting gave us what we needed to Evolve and we did so almost on the same day. She looks briefly sad, but then shakes herself and continues. Yet even once we had fought our way to become territory lords, there were still those who saw our cubs as prizes to snatch. To be born more powerful than most for our Tier is a curse as much as it is a blessing. No matter what we did, no matter what we tried…we couldn’t raise a single cub to adulthood.

She sighs and looks out into the forest in the direction where her mate had gone.

And then I fell pregnant once more. Despite Tilathis’s reassurances, I knew this cub would be killed and eaten as the others had been – unless I did something. It was then that I decided to accept the Ascendant’s offer. He had told me that he knew of a way I could achieve my heart’s desire. If I did something for him.

“What did he want you to do? Is that where the Blood Bastards come in?” I ask speculatively.

I am trying to tell my story, Markus Wolfe, Kalanthia tells me reprovingly. I hold up my hands in apology and do my best to appear attentive. She stares at me for a long moment as if just checking that I’m not planning on interrupting again. I wait her out and eventually the look in her eyes turns contemplative once more.

This Ascendant was very interested in forms of travel that did not involve the physical.

“What, like teleporting?” I blurt out. She glares at me, genuine irritation coming through the Bond. “Sorry.”

She huffs.

Teleporting, yes. He wished for me to steal an object from, yes, the Blood Fang clan. It was one of their most prized artefacts – an untraceable emergency teleport that, upon activation, would take whoever touched it to a safe place for between sixty and seventy cycles. And then it would bring them back to the same place from which they had left – or as close as could be, assuming that the exact place was no longer accessible. It was assumed that, in that time, the danger should be done.

That seems a little too much of an assumption to me, though I note that elves apparently naturally have longer lives than humans – otherwise the teleport time would surely have been far shorter. Or something else was the deciding factor, always possible.

“Given that I doubt a Tier six needed an emergency teleport, and that you needed a safe place to raise a cub, I take it that he meant for you to use it?” I ask, putting the pieces together. Kalanthia sent confirmation. “Then what did he gain out of it?”

Observations of the magic as it activated. She hesitated. Which is where I failed.

“You didn’t get the device?” I frown. But no – that doesn’t make sense at all.

No. I managed to use my magic to get into the treasure room without being caught – and to get away. I returned to the Ascendant and activated it in front of him. I did not expect it to take me to another world, but since I have managed to raise a cub to adulthood – more or less – I count it as a success. However, I was supposed to be brought back by the emergency teleport in front of the Ascendant – so he could observe how the magic worked in reverse. And since I came with you through the portal, I cannot be sure that it will function as it should. I will not know for another twenty cycles – the token should have activated by that time.

She falls silent to give me a moment to process. I take full advantage of that, the uncertainty of what might happen to Kalanthia within the next twenty cycles disturbing me.

Most of the pieces are slotting together – how Kalanthia ended up in the other world even though it’s now very clear she was from this one. Her knowledge of humans – but not of Moriax. How hard she argued with Lathani to break the Bond. Yet, despite all of that, she came with me.

“I had no idea,” I tell her quietly.

You were not meant to, she replies, just as subdued.

“You didn’t have to come through the portal – you could have waited and then met up with us on this side.” But even as I say it, I know why she couldn’t have done that.

It was a surprise to learn that your portal brought you to the same world I had originated in. However, even if I had known, I would have done no differently – there would have been no guarantee that I would find you again. No, if Lathani had come here by herself, I strongly suspect that she would have been lost to me. And after so much…I could not let that happen.

A few moments of silence lapse between us as we both contemplate what might have been.

“So, if you haven’t come back the way you were meant to, does that mean you have an angry Ascendant on your trail as well as these Blood Bastards?” I try to keep my tone light, though the topic is far from that. Because if Kalanthia is being pursued by a Tier six, what do I do? So many lives depend on me making the right decisions.

But I do know one thing. Tier six or not, there has to be a solution to get it off Kalanthia’s back.

It doesn’t get Kalanthia; it doesn’t get Lathani. Not even if they end up teleported to its very den in a double handful of years.
