The brothers worked through the day helping to repair buildings destroyed in an elemental attack. Caleb was able to use his full 22 willpower to lift the nail ridden boards, and when none were available.

As they worked, the collared unutilized came to work beside them.

“Hi, I’m Grisim,” Grisim introduced himself. “This is my brother Caleb.”

The other man looked them up and down and shook his head.

“Two more eager fools,” he muttered to himself, outside the range of a normal human’s hearing but both brothers heard.

“Excuse me?” Grisim ask.

“You heard me,” the man said and continued his work of shifting fallen timbers.

From how he moved, his points were mostly in agility though he still had far more strength than an uninitialized. Strength of 24 made you twice as strong as the strongest mortal man, and going much beyond that had limited applications for low level combat.

As Caleb was starting to learn, without special enhanced gear, weapons and armor couldn’t hold up to blows from full strength initialized. In that sense this high agility medium strength build made sense to Caleb, though he was unsure why he didn’t wear armor.

“What’s with the attitude,” Grisim asked. “We saved your life.”

“My life?” The man asked, gesturing to his collar. “This is not a life. Welcome to the empire. Make sure you stay valuable or you may end up like me.”

“And what exactly is that?” Caleb asked, forestalling his brother from saying more.

“A Bestiarius, a gladiator, call me what you will. It all means the same thing. A slave.”

“How would an initialized like you be a slave?” Grisim asked.

This took the man a back. And he turned to the brothers and reassessed them.

“We really must be far from the empire. Do you two not know of the games?”

He said, speaking “games” dripping with derision.

“No…” Grisim said.

The gladiator laughed.

“You fools rushed to join up with the empire before the cleanup even finished and you know nothing about it.”

“We didn’t exactly sign up willingly,” Caleb threw in to try to get this man on their side so they could at least get some answers. “If was join up or be conscripted anyway. If I joined up willingly, at least it saved half my village.”

The man’s eyebrows rose once more at the claim of saving half the village.

“So, you have more than just the one trick—as gruesome as it was. Let’s start over, I’m Flavius Bestiarius.”

“Grisim,” Grisim said, then pointed to Caleb, “Caleb. Now what was with the attitude?”

“I’m sorry,” he said, less hostile but not kindly. “I misinterpreted the speed at which you to enlisted. There’s always a few that sign up the moment the Legion’s dust can be seen over the horizon.”

“Yeah, I got that,” Grisim said, though Caleb could tell he hadn’t fully understood that. “Why do you hate the empire?”

Flavius laughed, “alright, at least one of you is a fool then.”

“We have no love for the empire,” Caleb said. “They took out mother from us. My brother wants to know why you hate it.”

“I’m a slave. I fell behind on a debt and my life was suddenly no longer mine. I was collared, thrown into an arena and force to kill others like myself all so I could gain levels and entertain. Now that I am sufficiently leveled and no longer entertaining, I was sent out to the battlefronts to fight and die.”

“Oh,” Grisim said dumbly, as Caleb bowed his head solemnly.

“Oh indeed,” Flavius said.

“Can you tell us anything about Canus?” Caleb asked. “Is he good to his word?”

Flavius nodded.

“He’s an honorable one—and that’s rare. They all aspire to nobility, but few actually espouse it. If he made you a promise he’ll keep it to the best of his ability. Bear in mind his ability isn’t limitless.”

Caleb explained the debt the village owed.

“You may be alright. Some more fools will join up, and Flavius may inflate their credentials slightly to reduce the need to conscript. No one wants to fight alongside a conscript.”

He noticed the blade on Grisim’s hip and asked.

“Why aren’t you dead already? You had a class before right?”

“Canus said it would be up to the Legate to decide my fate,” Grisim said. “How’s he?”

Flavius sucked in air between his teeth and then exhaled slowly.

“You are going to want to prove yourself really useful in the weeks to come. There are two ways to get out of a soulbound class for the empire to reclaim that sword. Death, and a collar. These collars will override any class a person might have had before donning it.”

“Craters,” Grisim said, looking around for Canus.

He spotted him a way away overseeing some work and started working himself.

“Good idea,” Flavius said, and returned to work himself.

They worked long into the night, and getting moved over to grave digging duty when all the rubble had been cleared. When finally they were free of their obligations, they were allowed to return home. As rare initialized—even new recruits—they were offered liberties not available to the bulk of the army. Once the army left their home village, they would be able to “requisition” quarters in any town they found themselves in. Caleb didn’t like the sound of that however, and both brothers simply returned home to check on their father when their shift was over.

“Any new of Kelia?” Caleb asked his father as he had asked everyone he’d spoken to that might know something.

Caleb had informed Canus of his wife’s absence, and he’d agreed to instruct the scouts to search for her, but there had been no reports. Kelia’s father similarly had been absent since the battle though he wasn’t dead as many reported seeing him.

In response, Caleb’s father handed him a note on the small slips of paper Kelia used for delivering messages with Avis.

Caleb,

I’m okay, I’ll be back tomorrow morning.

Love,

Kelia

“Tomorrow morning? What?” Caleb asked himself.

When none of them had any idea why she might be delaying, or refrained from providing any details, his father cleared his throat.

“I think you owe me an explanation,” Kavil said to his sons. “Both of you.”
