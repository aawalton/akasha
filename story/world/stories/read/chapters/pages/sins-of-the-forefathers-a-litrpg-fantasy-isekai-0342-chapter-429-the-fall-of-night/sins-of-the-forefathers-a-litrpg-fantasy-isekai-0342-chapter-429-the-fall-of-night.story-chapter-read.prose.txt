I had…mixed feelings about the Ashen Bride.

The strangely convertible sea vessel had been my home for several months now. Across the oceans of Vereden, and then over the land itself, it had been a pillar of the expedition. It hadn’t always been a comfortable ride, that was for sure. The few times I’d been aboard it during the sea voyage, I had been very thankful for the much smoother motions of my own Astray. The odd proportions and construction meant that it was a tad rocky upon the waves. That wasn’t even counting the equally rough overland journey, once we had transitioned into Velancian terra firma.

But we would have been in a rough spot without the strange, boxy, barge-like vessel. In its own way, I had grown even a little fond of it.

Which made it all the more distressing to see what had happened to the backbone of the expedition, while the battle for Rhoscara had been underway.

The Ashen Bride had been reduced to nothing more than a burnt-out shell of splintered timbers, listing onto its side. The wheels on the right side had been outright shattered, and as I watched, the entire scorched skeleton of the vessel groaned ominously and tried to fall over. Instead, it just split down the center and crumbled even further, an enormous puff of sparks bursting into the air above the burning tanners district of the city.

As much as it pained me to admit it…this ship was dead.

Because there had very obviously been a battle here.

While the Savoy and their Divinely mind-washed Rhoscaran soldiers had been going door to door, slaughtering the innocents of the city as they went, it seems the bulk had come here. Which…made sense, I suppose. My forces were the only ones in the city that hadn’t been comprised mostly of Dwarves, and Anguis had known that. We were the only ones that Anguis couldn’t have just wandered through, forcefully enthralling their hearts, minds, and souls as he did. It…followed that he would have simply ordered all of them put to the sword, if he wanted to minimize risk.

I was startled to find that I was…almost hurt that he’d done so. These days, I didn’t think I was particularly naïve. I had been around the block more than once at this point, and I was well accustomed to both experiencing deception and being the deceiver myself.

I had…really, actually thought Anguis had been being honest with me, there in that cell under the Scarlet Palace. Some instinct born from experience, or some sense within my soul, had told me that the Dwarf commonly known as the Serpent had really been trying to extend a hand to me. That he had actually been attempting to neutralize me and the expedition in a way that resulted in the least bloodshed, and placed Herztal in his debt at the same time, ripe for negotiation after his ascension into Divinity.

But…I suppose it had all been a ruse.

Because the entire square that the expedition had set up our camp in was covered in bodies, all the while the flames in the district raged around us.

Whatever happened here looked like it had only been settled moments before we arrived. Even with the heat, there still existed pools of blood upon the cobblestones, wet and tacky beneath our boots. I kept my eyes out as my squad and I warily crept through the death and destruction all around us. To my relief…I don’t think the Savoy had managed to slay all of the expedition. There were too few bodies of my people strewn through the wreckage. That wasn’t to say we didn’t appear to have taken any losses…but that for every expedition member slain, they had taken out at least three mind-controlled Dwarven thralls.

I was quietly, fiercely proud of them, even as I passed their butchered bodies.

I would avenge this, I swore I would. But I had to focus on other matters, right now. I could mourn my brave men and women later. Even though they might have chosen to follow Marcel’s insurrection, they were still my soldiers, and I wouldn’t forsake them.

Death wiped away all dishonor in my eyes.

And I would keep my promise to them by finding everyone else. I had a good idea where they had gone, from what Sylvia and the other had told me. My people seemed to have put up a strong fighting retreat, decimating squads of Savoy as they had fled the district. Carefully, with one final mourning glance at the blazing remains of the Ashen Bride, we followed after their trail…

Only to come upon a far more distressing scene.

Next to me, Sylvia emitted a strangled gasp, freezing in place at what lay before us. She wasn’t the only one; the entire group followed her lead. I couldn’t help but curse violently under my breath at what I saw, my heart sinking in my chest.

Lying slumped against a shattered water fountain in the middle of the street, its waters tainted with the ash, soot, and blood of battle, was…

“Nyx!” Sylvia sprinted past me, fumblingly sheathing her short sword as she did. She didn’t even bother to correct herself when she tripped over a shattered hunk of stone from the fountain, instead turning it into a slide that carried her forward until she reached the side…

Of Nyx. Or…

What had been Nyx.

The third in command of the Order of the Eclipsed Dawn was…very obviously deceased. She lay slumped halfway over the lip of the fountain, one arm hooked around it like she had been trying to brace herself to stand with it, the other hanging by her side. That arm still clenched the handle of a short-sword, hewn from a pitch-black Mystical metal I wasn’t familiar with. Her shield, a large, round example of the same metal, was lying discarded upon the cobblestones before, just below her other arm. Great rents were visible throughout her Eclipsed Dawn armor, where blades or even abilities had torn it apart. And yet, for all of that…

It wasn’t hard to just what had finally felled her.

The Sculpted woman’s head had been caved in.

Just the sight of it threatened to give me vertigo. Nyx had liked to fight in a manner that was the polar opposite of Sylvia, in an almost amusing mirror of their respective sheens. She…had been a woman who fought like a fortress, preferring a thicker armor of interlocking plates and chain. Her choice to fight with sword and shield had reflected that. But as part of that paradigm, I’d known her to always wade into battle with a face-concealing helmet upon her head.

It hadn’t been enough.

From what I could tell, consulting with my Lattices, it looked like a blow of enormous strength might have struck her on the right side of her head. It had been great enough to not only completely rip the faceplate of the helmet from the greater bulk of it, but also…destroy half of the obsidian woman’s head in the process. Her skull had been shattered so completely that nothing on the right half of it even possessed a face anymore.

Not only that, but I was not appreciating this sudden lesson in higher-level Cultivator Sculpted anatomy. Through the shards of her metallic skull, I could see something that almost appeared to be a brain, wet, oddly fibrous, and yet immediately recognizable.

I shuddered and looked away from the corpse of a woman who had not only been one of the primary commanders of my forces…but someone who had become a friend. Someone whose judgment I trusted implicitly.

Who had done this to her? Nyx was no slouch in the combat department. You didn’t rise as high as she had in a Martial Order like the Eclipsed Dawn without being able to carry your weight in battle. Just as Leopold was being groomed to be the new Grand Marshal of Grey’s Order, she had been groomed to be his second in command. I’m pretty certain that Nyx had actually been one of, if not the highest-level person in the entire expedition other than Alveron. Certainly higher Level than me.

And yet, she looked to have been slain in a single blow.

Anguis wasn’t capable of this, I was sure of that. I could tell right off the bat, this wasn’t his style. He was all deception, mind games, and sliding a discrete dagger into your ribs when you couldn’t pin him down. Possibly even an affinity for Affliction based Spells, considering the public reputation for ‘Sir’ Stonebreaker.

Couldn’t have been Leonard, either. I had seen the former Warden fight. In the same way that Meia was a Sorceress, the Ashran was a Sorcerer. Someone who fought purely with Spells, mostly his own personal kind of Evocation. Whoever had killed Nyx had done so with pure, physical might.

The fact that I had no idea who could have done this…

It sent a thrill of dread running up and down my spine.

While I had been contemplating all of that, Sylvia had been kneeling before the corpse of Nyx. She was still as she studied the oddly peaceful, remaining half of Nyx’s ebon features, almost as if she were searching for something. When I took a step forward to do…something, I don’t know what.

Comfort her? Offer my condolences?

Sylvia instead broke out of her trance and looked down at the blackened short sword still clutched in the dead woman’s hand. She stared at it for a moment as I came to stand at her side. Carefully, she reached down and pried the stiff fingers away from its hilt. When Sylvia had finished, and had grasped the sword herself…

She stood up from her crouch and abruptly saluted the deceased form of Lieutenant Nyx, of the Order of the Eclipsed Dawn. Almost involuntarily, I straightened up to a near parade rest. Out of the corner of my eye, I could see that I wasn’t the only one. None of the people here in our squad were Eclipsed Dawn members like Sylvia and Nyx had been.

This was her show.

“Rest well, Lieutenant,” Sylvia said strongly, her eyes clear and bright. If there was a slight shine to them, nobody said anything about it. “May you find further worth in what comes after. Now…” She shook her head, turning to shoot me a flinty look. “Let’s go. She wouldn’t want us to dally around like this. Not when there’s work to be done.”

It may have been morbid to just leave Nyx’s body right here in the street…

But we had no other options. It’s not like we could take her with us, as we searched for the remaining members of the expedition

I let my eyes drift down to the sword now clutched tightly in Sylvia’s left hand, the ebon sheen of it a stark contrast to the azure Lunar Basalt blade sheathed at her hip, before I shook it off. I nodded at her wordlessly, which, from the glint in Sylvia’s eyes, she appreciated.

We both knew this wasn’t the time to mourn.

“Move out,” I said roughly, loud enough for the rest of the squad to hear me. My words broke them from their mournful contemplation, and everyone straightened up. “Follow the trail of dead Dwarves, and we’ll find our comrades. Let’s go.”

Without another word, my people did as I commanded. Sylvia was the first to go, her head held high, hands gripped tightly around the hilts of both of her swords. More than one person hurriedly paid their respects to the eternally still form of Nyx as they left the scene of her murder, and I didn’t blame them.

Finally, when I was the only person left, I moved to leave as well…

Before pausing, and shooting her one last look.

That was the second Sculpted comrade I had lost, at this point.

I needed to make sure there would never be a third.

And then I left her, sitting half supported by a shattered fountain. Something about that…

It felt…fine. Not right. Not good.

But…acceptable.
