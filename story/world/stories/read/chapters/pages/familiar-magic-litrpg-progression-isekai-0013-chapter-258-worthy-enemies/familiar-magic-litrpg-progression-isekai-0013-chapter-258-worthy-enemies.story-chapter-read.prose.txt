Valanor had lost track of the days, long ago. It might have been weeks since he watched his brave group of warriors fall to the relentless attacks of Potentia. He mourned for each of them, the nobility and worthiness of their cause providing little comfort. Cara Fletcher had helped save the world…

But he couldn’t let himself think of that right now. There was no room in his mind for the kind of terrible regret that came with those memories. Not when another battle was always moments away.

The rock he’d collapsed on dug painfully into his back, which only meant that he hadn’t yet re-conjured his obsidian armor. Arguably that skill might be the only reason he still lived, as it had been torn from him piece by piece a hundred times. Each time he dug deep, finding the mana to bring it back, so he could keep fighting.

He looked across the battlefield, which told the story of endless fighting and destruction. His opponent crouched among the broken bodies of the most recent demons foolish enough to attack them, feasting. Valanor could never be sure if the powerful creature healed faster than him or not. All he knew was that when he stood back up, the battle would start again.

He could scarcely believe it was the same demon, the one who’d decapitated the black dragon, yet he knew it to be true. He hadn’t slept since before the ambush, and the world had turned into a hazy, dream-like existence. He didn’t need sleep anymore, not since reaching Twilight, but his mind could still grow weary, and Valanor didn’t trust his own grip on reality any longer.

Was this a nightmare? Or perhaps the terrible “hell” that Ethan had spoken of?

Perhaps Potentia was both. Either way, he only knew battle, fighting desperately for his life. His opponent stood half-again Valanor’s height, and was a frustrating combination of great power and greater speed. Its red eyes watched him warily from under thick horns, and it was already growing back the white spikes that jutted out from its rust-colored body.

How many times had they fought? How many times had it resulted in a draw, with both combatants stumbling backward to heal, and fight again. Their Twilight bodies could regenerate from terrible wounds, and neither one of them seemed capable of finishing the other. Valanor’s powers were meant for defense, and while the demon’s vicious claws had cut into his flesh countless times, they weren’t enough to end him, not so long as he had his shield.

And so they fought, over and over, perhaps pointlessly. Valanor wished the demon would give up, and seek out easier prey, but it never happened. Each time they separated, it would hunker down, staring daggers at him as they put themselves back together.

Yet, there was undeniable intelligence to the creature, more than just the normal cunning of beasts. Gazing at those curved horns, Valanor’s mind kept going back to Selina, and the terrible truths he’d discovered at her side.

Some demons had been Nator once, long ago. They’d been corrupted by Potentia–the same corruption that gnawed at Valanor’s insides even now. They’d been killed and reborn, over and over. Each time a little worse than the last, a little more demon, a little less Nator. But could something remain?

He narrowed his eyes at his enemy. Was Valanor imagining things? Was this all in his head?

Their battle had taken them across many miles, crashing into one another with all their power, and they’d been attacked numerous times by lesser demons. Something always watched and waited, looking for any chance to devour weaker prey. Many had made the mistake of believing Valanor weaker, and just as many thought the same of his demon counterpart.

They’d all been proven wrong, and the pair had left a path of slaughter in their wake. And yet, Valanor killed slowly. It was his way–nigh unkillable, but little in the way of offensive abilities. The demon had a more balanced way to it, easily tearing through the lesser creatures that ambushed them.

Several times, Valanor could swear it had waited for him to finish dispatching his own foes, before returning to their fight. Demons didn’t possess honor, the thought was laughable. Most were the tormented de-evolutions of animals and monsters, not so unlike those on Nexum. Beasts didn’t fight with honor…but Nator?

Valanor had yet to find himself in a similar situation, had yet to be given an opportunity to take advantage, and he found himself wondering if he truly would.

A bond formed when you fought someone. Some severed easily, some not. A skilled opponent who truly pushed you could earn your respect, whether you meant for it or not. Valanor had never fought anything for as long as he’d faced this creature, and he couldn’t pretend it hadn’t long-since earned his esteem.

He’d even stopped using his Familiars. Yes, they were risky in battle, as they could be temporarily killed, depriving him of his Bonds, and they recovered more slowly than he did. But he had to acknowledge the truth to himself: he didn’t want to summon them…because it didn’t seem fair.

Had he already lost his mind? Had the corruption of Potentia long-since broken him, and left a fool in his place?

Perhaps. But what would he do if he did somehow manage to kill this unusual demon? He’d still be alone, lost on this terrible world. He had the church relic in his inventory, but he wasn’t meant to use it. If he did so, it wouldn’t save his life, merely doom others. He’d stipulated the conditions himself, and they were simple: only if victory was possible.

Looking around at the bleak, red landscape, Valanor couldn’t pretend that there was any real hope of victory. At least…not alone.

His wounds had closed. His mana had recovered enough, so Valanor regained his feet. Across the battlefield, the demon did the same, tossing aside the legbone it was chewing on as it did so. It stared back at him with those red, uncaring eyes.

“Were you a Nator once?” he whispered. “Were you like Selina, and all the rest? Just a person, ravaged by this dark world?”

His obsidian armor covered him, and he hefted his warhammer and shield. The demon charged at him once again.

Valanor chuckled from inside his helm, then strode forward. “Or perhaps I am just lonely, and you are just persistent.”

Claws met shield.

Emperor Gallow watched the recording again. The male who’d caused them so many problems joined hands with a red-skinned female, then walked through a rift. The recording cut off. He watched it again.

Why now? Why did that frustrating champion of the supposed-gods have to wait until now to finally leave this world?

Gallow struggled to accept how much that one male had been responsible for. The traitor has insisted that Bishop had freed one of the scourges, starting the Union. This “Ethan Bishop” had also been the one to discover The Devorare buried beneath the ice, which should have been a service to The People. Yet then he’d been one of those attacking their installations, somehow appearing to be in two places at once, and even bringing some The People to his own side.

Then he’d stolen Twilight from their grasp, and unleashed the blood rain. Now he went to Potentia, seeking to steal The Throne from Gallow. The Emperor had no doubt as to what would happen to the remaining members of his race if this male was allowed control of the Union.

At last he acknowledged Bek, waiting for him patiently. “Tell me it’s ready,” he commanded.

Bek’s fingers tapped wildly in excitement. “It is ready, my Emperor. The recording you’ve watched was the final key in targeting our own rift. We’ve managed to use the last of the power stolen from the fourth scourge to activate the Rune Circles.”

“And our weapon?” he asked.

“Still in stasis,” Bek said. “Capturing him was more difficult than expected, it took several of the generals working together, and he eluded us in the tunnels for several days. He even managed to free most of the captured humans before being subdued. Unfortunately, the breeding pits will no longer be sustainable.”

“No matter, that plan wouldn’t have lasted long after our departure,” Gallow said. “When will he be ready?”

“Difficult to say, Emperor. He has been inoculated against traditional methods of assimilation, as we’d expected. I’ve managed to augment the method we employed on Planet 57, but it has cost us three of the remaining dark-borne. The process appears to be a success, but he must be kept close to you or I to suppress his will.”

“I’d have rathered a full conversion,” Gallow said.

“We explored the possibility, but such a process would have eliminated his Familiars, and we confirmed the Anima would be lost as well.”

“As you say, Bek. How soon can we leave?”

“I would recommend that we wait several more days for the process to complete. In the meantime, we’ve identified a suitable location to hide The Devorare under one of the oceans. It should be safe until–”

Sirens blared across the ship, and Gallow’s head whipped about furiously. Green warning lights shone brightly, something he hadn’t seen since they’d faced the fourth scourge.

“What’s happening?” he demanded.

Bek rushed to the nearest monitors, swapping views until it displayed a feed for some deep part of the ship Gallow didn’t immediately recognize. A rift had opened. That shouldn’t be possible, to overcome the ship’s defenses they’d need…

A male emerged from the rift, hovering, and draped in robes. He only moved one arm, which pointed at the black metal floor beneath him. A Rune Circle burst into existence in a flare of light, and the male sat in the center of it, eyes closed. Gallow looked at the symbol emblazoned on his robes.

“The Vitus Domini are here,” he said. “The fourth scourge has sent one of his pets to attack us.”

“Emperor, there’s more,” Bek said, then began flipping rapidly through security feeds. It was always the same. A single being in the robes of their enemy, moving into the center of Rune Circle. Some were attacked by The People, but these were Masters. Crippled Masters by the look of it, but untouchable by the Dusks and Dawns bravely throwing themselves at the intruders.

“I don’t recognize those runes,” Gallow said. He was sure he could defeat all of these broken fools, given time. But they were spread across the ship, and they wouldn’t die easily.

Bek was already adjusting the feeds, using multiple rooms to get the right angles, and building a model of the magic’s purpose. Noon sooner had a composite image appeared before the scientist that he took a quick step backward, dark eyes widening.

“We need to leave,” he said. “Get to the transport room, I’ll gather those I can, and have our true kin bring the weapon.”

Gallow looked at his scientist with utter disbelief. In a hundred thousand years Bek had never forgotten his title, and certainly never deigned to give order to his Emperor.

“This is the end, isn’t it?” he asked.

Bek turned to look at him, hands still rapidly tapping against the monitor’s interface. “Hurry, my Emperor,” he whispered.

Gallow ran. Only the relative slowness of doors opening caused him any delay. Although, he saw two of the Vitus Domini as he descended into the belly of his ship, and it took all his willpower not to pause to destroy them. Judging by how brightly the Rune Circles around them were glowing, Bek was right that they lacked the time.

When he reached the cargo bay they’d repurposed for the rift, Gallow finally stopped. He saw that Bek’s machines and magics were still in place, and none of the enemy had chosen this place for their final moments.

Gallow took a moment to collect himself, then moved through the wide doors. The rest of their kin should be coming down in the elevators at any moment. They would escape this trap, and complete their mission on Potentia, then Gallow would become the instrument of his peoples’ wrath! He would–

A force crashed into him, and he felt a thin blade pinning him to a wall. He looked down at his own blood. Today was truly a day for surprises, he genuinely couldn’t remember the last time he’d experienced physical pain.

“Where are you going, old monster?” a male’s voice demanded. He wore a deep blue outfit, one with the cut and precision that typically meant military. His left arm was secured behind his back, but he seemed to be in better shape than the rest of those invading the ship.

Gallow swiped a hand down at the blade, which snapped apart. His backhanded strike sent the Master sailing backward, though he recovered quickly enough to keep his feet. The Emperor disdainfully plucked the broken blade from his chest, tossing it aside as the wound sealed.

“I don’t have time for you,” Gallow said, eyes flickering to the elevator in the corner of the room. He could see five of the kin surrounding a stasis tank.

“You don’t have time for anything, monster, not anymore,” the man said. “The Vitus Domini act! How long I’ve dreamed of this.”

“You dreamt of your own deaths?” Gallow chided. “How strange that we share such a connection.”

A second elevator, Bek moving down with a portable console, no-doubt finalizing the commands for the rift. Gallow had wished to bring all of his people through–Bek promised the rift would be strong enough for only a thousand.

Now there would be only eight.

The enemy Master glanced at the approaching elevators, then to the rift platform in the center of the room. “Leaving? Running?” he said, then laughed. “I don’t blame you for your cowardice, but I’m afraid I can’t abide.”

He shot toward the platform, the easiest target to destroy. Gallow caught him halfway there, hand clamped around the male’s throat. They locked gazes, and the Emperor felt spiritual attacks crash into him, while his own power snuffed out the man’s attempts to use his abilities.

Gallow turned to Bek as the scientist arrived. He could feel his opponent trying desperately to lash out with his respectable power, but this was The Devorare, an extension of the Emperor’s own body, and–for now–the Master’s efforts were wasted.

“Is the rift ready?” Gallow asked.

Bek leapt from the elevator long before it arrived, not even glancing up as he landed. “We’re prepared, my Emperor.” Blue-white light bathed the room, punctuating his words.

The rest of the kin arrived, rushing toward the portal with the stasis pod. Each took a fraction of a second to salute before diving through, and Gallow allowed himself a moment of relief when the pod–and their weapon–passed through. Bek paused just before leaving.

“We’re nearly out of time, my Emperor,” he said urgently.

“Go, Bek, I’m coming.”

The scientist nodded, though his fingers tapped out his reluctance, then he obeyed. Gallow looked down at the struggling Master he held. “I leave,” he told his enemy. “I lack the time to kill you, and together our power would overwhelm the rift. Your cowardice has served you well, and you may even have time to escape while your brethren sacrifice themselves.”

The Master laughed.

“I am Arthur Penson, Master of the Vitus Domini. I have seen more friends die than I ever expected to know, and I have longed for death.” His one working hand reached up and ripped open his jacket, revealing a Rune Circle carved directly into his flesh. Then he grinned. “Die, monster!”

Gallow ran as the first explosion went off directly behind him. He raced toward the rift, which on any other day would be so close as to not even register to one with his speed. But today he tried to outpace the erupting power of a Master, who’d turned every scrap of energy in his ancient body into a single, suicidal attack.

The worst seemed to slow around him as Gallow moved. Each footstep crashed into the floor beneath him with such force that he left a trail of craters in his wake. The portal grew closer as two-dozen more explosions echoed behind him. He felt the death of The Devorare as if it were his own, and the pain that lanced through his chest seemed appropriate for such a loss.

But he’d made it. The blue-white embraced him, then just as suddenly he was surrounded by shades of red.
