Striker slashed twenty times in thin air and held the sword lights there—vertical lines hanging in the air. With an effort of will they shattered into thousands of splinters.

An instant later they were all slicing through the mists, leaving thin trails in their wakes. He closed his eyes, mapped the sword light splinters in his mind, and quickly had a lay of the land as well as an estimate as to his location.

The sword lights also functioned to set off any traps, if they existed. Striker didn't encounter any.

He considered the mists curiously. This place was emptier than he'd expected.

He continued onward, sure of his location, and encountered the Carved Saint soon after—he'd messaged the man via their charms as soon as he'd felt his signature.

They'd been deposited fairly close to a staircase, which was lucky. Dragonspire’s assumption was that Malzareth's army had left the inter-realm staircases intact, since they wouldn't have expected an attack on this floor for days—and would therefore still be utilizing the staircases for their own army.

And removing those staircases was no trivial task. By the time they realized the extent of the infiltration, it'd be far too late.

Striker was interested to see if he'd encounter any resistance on his way. It was an idle curiosity. Truthfully he was far more interested in the events taking place on the floors below. Once the Scryer's Guild managed a foothold in the middle floors, they'd likely be able to glimpse all the way until the final floor.

He wasn't at all concerned about his own role in all this.

He simply wasn't the focus—which was rather an amusing position for him. Striker was hardly a modest man, and he knew what he was capable of. To use a man like him as a distraction... he'd grinned when he'd heard it, and he grinned now. Such a luxury.

Not long after, the Carved Saint halted him with an outstretched hand. Striker considered the mists, saw what the man had sensed, and slashed.

Two hundred miles of mist were split wide open like a parting sea, revealing a landscape scarred with fissures of Destruction and Corruption.

It also revealed the Prime standing there, thirty feet in the air, who'd caught the sword-light between two fingers.

Below the Prime's feet lay a series of pale bodies—well-muscled, eight or nine feet tall. They didn't feel like corpses to Striker's cursory inspections, but treasures.

"Gentlemen." The Ghoul Doctor nodded.

A slight smile on Striker’s face. They sent someone interesting after all.

They didn't exchange any more words; it wasn't necessary.

Striker drew his sword languidly. The duel started.

Even as he fought, he was still thinking, rather idly, about the situation in which he found himself.

A few of the other top-ranked elites had more difficulty accepting their new positions. It wasn’t always a matter of ego—not purely, at least. Each of these top-ranked men and women believed reflexively that they should have a hand in the Fate of the Galaxy. It was the role they were used to playing.

Few would deny Zane Walker his credit, but it was still difficult to accept an auxiliary role in all this—whether distraction or support—to place so much of the responsibility on that man when he hadn't yet faced Malzareth directly.

Up until very recently, it’d been easy for Dragonspire’s Top-20 rankers to imagine that this man would hit a wall like every other genius-level talent, and settle just beneath that true Galaxy elite range.

If not that, then at least that his strength was still a concern for the future—that they'd only need to consider him a true equal once he hit Empyrean.

A few egos were certainly checked these past few weeks, in amusing fashion.

It was difficult not to see the humor in it.

Striker dodged six man-sized cursed needles in a row by the slightest fractions—swaying just slightly or tilting his head to get out of the way—and resumed his train of thought.

The more he saw of his junior apprentice-brother, the less he seemed to understand. There were secrets to that man's body and soul that simply couldn't be found in Dragonspire. And the man himself was a paradox—a simple man, and Striker meant that as a compliment, and yet quite unique.

He watched a potentially fight-ending needle sail past his nose and caught a glimpse of his reflection in the silver before it passed.

I do hope the Guild run a feed by the time the two breach the Lair floors.

Until then there was nothing more to do than to trust, he supposed—and let his Master's and the Mistress's plan play out.

Then the Doctor placed nine giant needles into the bodies at his feet, and Striker was forced to get a little more serious.

***

The man and woman currently occupying Striker's idle thoughts had long since touched down.

They'd found themselves in a vast space full of broken pillars—pillars of pure Corruption reaching halfway to the sky, each hundreds of miles tall. The realm was fairly bare otherwise.

Two months ago this place was likely overrun, but now Malzareth’s army, especially at the top-end, was decimated, which Zane supposed explained its current rather empty state.

"I think they made this a holding pen of sorts for Greater Endbringers—once upon a time, at least," mused Reina. She checked the spatial landmarks and nodded. “This is the right place.”

The floor seemed to go on and on—and they'd based on their understanding of the floor design it'd take the best part of six hours, even when his current speeds, before they reached a set of stairs.

Possible, but still longer than they'd like, ideally.

"Let's try your idea," he told her. They found a promising location, and she gave him a lesser Blessing and stepped back—putting a few miles' distance between them.

The System powers that'd once ensured the integrity of these floors were gone. The bedrock levels of these floors—the structuring that made them—were still some of the highest-grade dreamsteel in this Galaxy, and thought to be unbreakable, even for those nearing a complete 1024 shards.

Her thesis was quite simple.

"We know the Superdungeon can break," she'd told him—this was right after he’d finished up his 1000th Creation Shard. "It has limits. I think it was built to specifications, specifications based on the prisoners it's meant to contain."

“And after all this—” he nodded to his Atlas Cuffs as he unclasped them. “You think I might just exceed them.”

"It's worth a try," she'd said.

He grabbed hold of his axes and faced the place he'd chosen—a fissure striking quite deep into this realm of a floor.

And then he slashed.

And slashed again, and again—making sure to put the full heft of his new physique into the strikes, as well as his Destruction shards.

He completed the full set of Star-Crushing Slashes and had carved that fissure straight into a sheet of System-Blue bedrock steel.

Eight more chained slashes, and he received clear visual confirmation that he could, in fact, slice into Superdungeon System Steel.

Seven more after that, and he'd opened up a river of void. She landed next to him a few moments after, seeming amused. "You liked that."

He didn't deny it—he did quite enjoy that.

She shook her head, smiling slightly.

"What is it?"

"It just fits you."

She took a look at his handiwork. He was a bit amused at her amusement.

"That's the next floor, then?" he said. He saw a dim blue plane across that hundred-mile stretch of void, and she confirmed it.

"The Superdungeon is a series of stacked realms, placed quite close in space—that isn't the true void, like you'd see in void-walking. It isn't chaotic," she explained. "It's more like a buffer zone in space. So—yes." She seemed certain.

After that he attacked again and slashed open an entrance into the floor below.

At which point he scooped her up and crossed the void in three shining steps, his new Concept and its space-Law fragments swirling at his feet.

The two of them kept moving.

***

In the background, they received reports that teams were finding each other on the higher floors—and a few encountered Primes. Daressa was engaged elsewhere, as was the Ghoul Doctor, in short order.

"That leaves three Primes unaccounted for," said Reina. They gave each other a look and decided to push on.

They found the next floor almost as empty as the one before; there was a cluster of ten Class-II Endbringers which Zane dispatched in a single blow.

The floor after that held what he'd count as their first true encounter.
