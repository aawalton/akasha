Something soft and dense cushioned my back. Not the hard, rancid wood of the house, nor the cold stones outside its stoop. Grass? It felt wet, too. I inhaled… and smelled flowers.

“Renner?” I croaked. My throat felt like dry parchment.

No answer.

If I open my eyes, will he be standing over me? Holding another knife? Is this how I die? After everything else, after all I’ve been through… is Renner going to murder me for accidentally setting him on fire?

I… I could have killed him. Ashes, I could have killed all of us. Summoning fire in a room full of spilled ale? What was I thinking?!

Cool, clean air filled my lungs. Renner must have brought me outside.

Maybe he prefers to murder people outdoors. Hasn’t stabbed me yet, though. That’s got to count for something.

“I hope you aren’t drowning.”

I bolted upright with a shriek. The movement brought a sharp ache to my temples and a wave of nausea to my belly.

I was in a garden. No, the garden. His. Fragrant plants waved and swayed in a pleasantly warm breeze. The sky was steel-gray, sunless but not dark, frothed with clouds shaded like ripe plums and lush cherries.

He was nearby, kneeling before a rosebush. Its blooms created a cascade of lush green and vivid scarlet. Most of them, at least; there were a few scattered buds with shriveled petals and blackened tips. Something thick and red dripped from them.

“You seem able to breathe well enough, so I can assume you’re out of immediate danger. The state of you implies… some kind of water-based disaster. Did you fall into a river, Brin?”

I panted, jaw slack and fingers curling into the lush, dark soil. How can I be here? I’m not asleep. I’m not dreaming. I just… I fainted. That’s all. Why am I here? My mind spun. Then, with bladelike clarity, I remembered his warning.

I need to answer the question.

“No,” I gasped, fighting to steady myself. “No river. I just… over-compensated.” I was still soaked, and there was a puddle of muddy soil forming beneath me. Cold water sloshed through my boots, and my cloak was so heavy it was practically choking me. I undid the clasp, letting it fall to the ground.

He rose, head tilting to one side. Immaculate black hair graced one shoulder, which was wrapped in green fabric. The cloth shimmered like water under sunlight, and cast his features in an almost ethereal glow.

“Ah. My next guess was that you’d fallen asleep beside a washbasin.” He chuckled, stepping closer.

I took a deep breath. The garden air was… intoxicating. Sweet and spicy and calming. Everything around me seemed decadent and alluring and… just so achingly beautiful. Especially after what a nightmare the entire morning had been.

And what it still was. This may have been the first time I wanted to be here, rather than in the waking world, but reality couldn’t be escaped. Renner was livid, and perhaps hurt, and Gil was certainly in need of help.

“I shouldn’t be here.” I pressed a hand to my throbbing head, stifling a groan. Did I hit it when I fell? I don’t remember. Maybe I didn’t just faint, maybe I’m actually lying unconscious in that awful house. And Renner… salt, what is he going to do?

“I can’t be here.” The words rode out on a swell of panic. “I need to wake up.” I pressed both palms into the soft soil and heaved myself upright on shaking legs. The ache in my temples became abruptly sharper, almost knifelike, and the world turned sideways.

He caught me.

Strong hands gripped my waist, righting me… and did not pull away.

I was immediately aware of how cold I was and, in startling contrast, of the sudden spark in my belly. And of how my soaked clothes clung much too tightly to every curve. I tilted my chin up. Met those endless eyes for a fleeting moment.

Run, my mind whispered.

Stay, my body purred.

“I n-need to wake up.” I tore myself away, cheeks burning.

That perfect mouth twitched into the shadow of a smile.

I swallowed and looked away, swaying. Fighting to stay upright. Don’t fall, Brin. Don’t. He might catch you again. “Gil… Gil’s been stabbed. I need to wake up and help him. Salt, and stop Renner from finishing the job. And find… f-find…” My chin quivered. I clenched my fists, forcing the panic down, and looked back up at him. “Please, please, let me go back. We can speak tonight, whatever you want, but I need to wake up now or people are going to die!”

One eyebrow arched. “How dire. I had no idea your life was so exciting.”

I gasped, shivering despite the warm air whispering at my skin. “This isn’t funny! You… you keep saying you don’t mean me any harm. If that’s true, then send me back!”

The little smile faded. “Would that I could, mortal. Our bond draws you here in sleep, but I can’t hurl you back.” He knelt back down, one hand cupping a wilted rose. “You’ll wake in your own time.”

I glanced around, teeth gnashing and fingers tangling into my sopping hem. “No, no, that can’t… Isn’t there anything you can do?”

He spared me a glance that, though voiceless, spoke volumes.

I started pacing. Icy water sloshed through my boots. Drops glistened, falling from my hair to the grass around me. “Salt and ashes, what do I do?!”

“You could make yourself useful.”

I stopped midstep, glowering as he cast one hand out with enviable, careless grace. Gesturing towards… Is that a watering pail?  My freshly kindled fury sputtered. “Is that… are you… you can’t be gardening.”

“Can’t I?”

“No.” I folded my arms. “Evil doesn’t tend plants.”

Broad shoulders trembled with laughter. “I hadn’t realized. But perhaps the plants are evil, too.”

I snorted, despite myself.

“You’re intelligent, Brin. In a realm with no sun, did you truly assume this place tends itself?”

Now that was an interesting question. I closed my eyes briefly, inhaling the warm, fragrant air. I just have to wait. The stablemaster, Renner, Teela… there’s nothing I can do. I fainted, so I’ll probably wake up in a minute or two, and then I can… I don’t even know. But right now, perhaps it’s alright to be distracted. Perhaps it’s even good. Just… just for a moment.

I tilted my face up, examining the sky. It was beautiful, though nothing compared to the vivid, breathtaking majesty of my previous visits. Which had all been at night… it stood to reason, then, that this was what daytime- or some part of it, at least- looked like in this place. No sun, no stars, but a vibrantly clouded sky still bright with… something. The light was faint, a distant glow from somewhere behind the clouds. I squinted and turned, but couldn’t tell where it was coming from. It was just… there. Dim light as far as I could see, in every direction.

“Honestly? It’s not something I’ve given much thought to. Is there truly no sun here? Ever?”

He glanced up, face twisting into a grimace. “The presence of a sun would be death, here. This sky has its shades and tempers, but never a glimpse of anything from the Day Court.”

My jaw went slack. Once again, the fact that I was being told things that were perhaps unheard of to mortal ears hit me. I felt cowed by the knowledge… and wildly curious. “Day Court? So there’s… two? Is it always daytime there? What do their nights look like? Wait, and why are there two?”

The Fae chuckled. “More than two.”

“How… how many?”

He didn’t answer, just indicated the silver pail again.

He means it. He wants me to water his plants. He’s a powerful, evil monster and he’s… trying to get his rosebush to bloom.

I’d scarcely wrapped my fingers around the metal before glancing his way and dropping it with a gasp. The blackened bud beneath his fingertips was stretching, unfolding out towards him, and there were little crimson veins threading through the ebony.

It’s alive. It’s trying to touch him. It’s just like… My chest tightened, vision blurring with sudden tears. I turned quickly, covering my mouth and praying he hadn’t noticed my reaction. I’m being silly. Ridiculous. It’s the stress of everything. It’s getting to me.

“Not all of my peers,” every word dripped venom, “Share my appreciation for the more charming aspects of life. One heedless misstep, and half my garden withered. My own fault for allowing a raugnot on the grounds.”

“Raugnot?” My hands wrapped around the cool metal, grounding me amidst the tide of emotion. I tilted the pail slowly, sprinkling out clear water that smelled like honey.

“Mm. Detestable things.” His hand slid deeper into the vivid cascade, caressing another withered rose. This one shared its state with some nearby leaves. They, too, began to unfurl.

“Worse than humans?” I offered a watery smile with my half-hearted attempt at humor.

He paused. Smiled slowly, starlit eyes gleaming in a way that made me shiver.

I whirled away. “W-we make messes too, you know. And we, er, steal things and try to set your carpet on fire and-”

“Was that a joke, Brin?”

My face burned as I stepped to another plant. This one had a charred orange stem and sharp-tipped leaves hued like freshly churned butter. It smelled of ginger and burning wood.

“I’ve been known to make them.” I stifled a little grin, glancing over. “Sometimes we stab you with witchwood, too. Very troublesome.”

He rose, laughing. It was a deep, sonorous sound that made my pulse throb.

Warm air ruffled my hair, inviting me to continue. To relax, to let my guard down, to just… be. Here, in a garden, cushioned by soil and whispering grass, tending to plants that reached…

I wanted this. This beauty, this fragrant peace. For just a moment, I wanted it more than almost anything.

But this was not my reality.

“This doesn’t… nothing’s changed,” I whispered, lowering my lashes.

He cocked his head to the side, drawing closer. Still was that unhurried grace, as if he had all the time in the world. “Oh? I don’t recall you being this exposed, before.” His gaze dragged very deliberately downwards, along the length of my dripping tunic. “And I’m not just referring to your attire.”

I set the pail down, face burning. Wrapped both arms over my chest. “I just m-mean… this is nice. R-really. This place, being here like… like this. Gardening. Saints, I miss gardening! That was my whole life, you know? Almost every day, growing tomatoes and squash and tending to the… I’d give anything for… I just… This was… thank you.”

The amusement on his face vanished, replaced by something I couldn’t name.

My vision began to swim. “It was nice to have a moment of peace. Things are bad. Really bad. My friend’s been taken by one of you, and Renner…” My eyelids slammed shut. “He says she’s dead, but I’m going to find her. And I know that’s probably very foolish and I’ll probably die and Renner might kill me first because I set him on fire but-”

“If you are near a Fae, you need to flee.” He was in front of me so quickly that I stumbled back. All the velvet and humor of his voice had disappeared, replaced by cold steel. “Abandon your friend. If not for the sake of your own life, for the sake of the weapon you carry.”

“Or maybe I should just bring it to you tonight, right? Then it’s fine if I run off and get myself killed.” I sniffled, looking away. “Heh. Nice try.”

The air around us grew still. Even the plants stopped rustling. I dared to peer back up and was met with bared, flashing teeth.

“Or perhaps you can ask a different Fae for help.” Every word oozed malice.

“Ask… what? Who?”

He moved closer. Too close. Eyes narrowed to slits, endless silver glittering with cold light.

I backed up. One wet boot hit the pail, knocking it to the grass. Sharp, butter-hued leaves poked against my trousers.

“That’s exactly what I want to know.”

Everything tilted. Steely gray melted into hazy gold, and the snarling face above me vanished.
