Time seemed to slow as the spearhead closed in on Luke. His body was sluggish, still reeling from the unexpected blow—his reflexes dulled and delayed.

Just when it seemed his life would end, a blur streaked past his face. A warm liquid splashed across his skin. Blinking through the mess, he wiped his eyes—and saw Victoria standing over him.

In the dim light, blood dripped from the dagger buried deep in his attacker’s throat. The man's eyes were wide with shock, lips trembling as he gurgled out his final breath.

''Is this how you fight on a battlefield too?'' Victoria asked, yanking her dagger free. She offered him a hand with her other, lifting him to his feet.

''I prefer to fight from a safe distance, if I’m honest,'' he admitted, forcing out a pained chuckle.

''Well, you’re doing a great job,'' she said dryly.

Luke wasn’t sure if she was mocking him or not, but there was no time to dwell on it. As they stood, another arrow sliced through the air—this time from the east, along their path.

Victoria reacted instantly. With a swift motion, she slashed her bloodied dagger through the air, intercepting the arrow and knocking it harmlessly to the ground.

''Keep heading to the Academy. I’ll run ahead,'' she said, vanishing into the shadows.

Luke could only stare, awestruck by her speed. He had thought Sebastian’s movement bordered on inhuman—but Victoria, even without the system’s enhancements, was something else entirely.

He couldn’t help but wonder what would happen if she became his subordinate.

Pushing the thought aside, Luke snatched the fallen spear from his attacker’s lifeless hands and broke into a jog. Earlier, he’d been running without paying attention—nearly skewered through the heart for it.

That would not happen again.

As he scanned the road ahead, distant screams echoed through the night. There was no doubt in his mind—Victoria was wreaking havoc.

''I hope she’s only killing the assassins…''

He continued warily through the chaos-strewn street. From time to time, he saw citizens fleeing the area in panic, but he didn’t encounter another assassin—not until he reached the Royal Academy gates.

''Were there really so few of them?'' Luke wondered, scratching his head.

A figure emerged from the street. Her black-and-white robe was now soaked red with blood.

''Are you injured?'' Luke asked, his voice laced with concern.

''How sweet of you to worry about me,'' Victoria giggled, wiping sticky blood from her forehead. Her radiant smile was a chilling contrast to the gore clinging to her.

Luke did his best to keep his composure and changed the subject. ''Let’s head inside. I’d rather not fight in front of the Academy gates and cause even more trouble.''

''As you wish,'' she said, a playful tone in her voice.

As they approached the gates, two guards stepped forward to block them. One of them was the same guard who had lent Luke a sword earlier that morning. Both men paled as Victoria’s blood-soaked face drew near.

“W-what happened?” one of the guards stammered.

Luke cleared his throat. “We ran into a little trouble on the way back from the palace. Nothing we couldn’t handle,” he said, trying to sound casual. “Can we head inside?”

“Okay…” The taller guard quickly turned and rushed to open the gate, clearly eager to distance himself from Victoria’s blood-soaked presence.

“Viscount, could you return my sword? It was brand new,” the other guard asked, eyes gleaming with hope.

“Ah… about that.” Luke’s expression faltered. “How about I give you this spear instead, and we call it even?” He pressed the weapon into the man’s hands.

“But I don’t use a spea—”

“Thank you, gentlemen. Enjoy the rest of your night,” Luke cut him off, striding toward the opening gate with purpose. The guard swallowed his complaints, his shoulders slumping as a defeated look spread across his face.

Luke slipped through the narrow gap and didn’t look back. He felt a twinge of guilt—but truth be told, despite holding a noble title, he didn’t have a single coin to his name.

He was probably the poorest Viscount in the history of this world.

They moved swiftly through the Academy grounds, heading toward the dormitory. It was late, and most students had already retreated to their lodgings—thankfully sparing him the attention he didn’t want.

Luke tried to ease the door open quietly, but the rusty hinges groaned in protest, announcing their arrival with a screech. The main lobby and dining hall were connected, so every head turned toward them at once.

The room fell silent.

All eyes locked onto Victoria, who stood bathed in blood. She didn’t seem the least bit embarrassed—in fact, her radiant smile returned as she blew a playful kiss toward the stunned noble scions.

In an instant, nearly everyone looked away.

Everyone except for two.

Kayson and Sebastian shot to their feet and rushed over, concern etched into every movement.

“What the hell happened!?” Kayson exclaimed.

“I’ll explain later,” Luke replied, motioning for them to calm down. His gaze shifted to Sebastian, whose expression hovered somewhere between fury and relief.

“Go eat while we clean up—and maybe order some food for me. I haven’t eaten since this morning.”

Without waiting for a reply, Luke turned and made his way to his room to grab a fresh robe. Once acquired, he made his way to the bath house.

Fortunately, the dorm servants kept the wooden basins filled with warm water, so he didn’t have to wait for it to be heated.

He quickly stripped down and sank into the steaming bath, letting the heat ease the tension in his muscles. His body ached, his mind felt heavy—after nearly dying twice in a single day, the exhaustion hit him all at once.

If it weren’t for Victoria, he might not have made it back at all.

He leaned back, resting his head against the rim of the basin and closing his eyes.

‘I should probably thank her later.’

Just as his thoughts began to drift, he felt the water shift—just slightly, as if stirred by a gentle ripple. His eyes snapped open.

His jaw slowly dropped.

Through the rippling surface, he saw a sight both dangerous and captivating.

A woman’s silhouette—milky white skin, twin peaks barely veiled beneath the water. Distorted, but unmistakable.

His heart thundered like a warhorse in his chest.

For a moment, his thoughts scattered—evaporating as if they owed him money.

"Young Master, you're staring," Victoria said, her velvety giggle echoing through the steamy room.

"Victoria… why are you in my bath?" Luke finally managed to form a coherent sentence, his brain struggling to catch up.

She fluttered her eyelashes, feigning innocence. "I thought we could wash each other’s backs. Why? Are you embarrassed?"

Was he embarrassed? What kind of question was that to ask a man who wasn’t even eighteen?

Of course he was.

His hormones were raging, but Luke clenched his fists beneath the water, trying to wrestle his thoughts back under control.

Truth be told, after everything that had happened, he couldn’t fully trust Victoria. Yes, she was powerful—and terrifying—and if she could be kept on his side, she’d be an incredible asset.

But could she be controlled?

Though she called him “Young Master” now, what did that truly mean? Could he be certain of her loyalty?

Victoria had successfully infiltrated both Clayton City and Valand City, masquerading as a servant to serve her own ends. Who was to say she wasn’t doing the same now?

As if sensing the shift in the air, her playful smile began to fade. Though she remained alluring, the tension in the room thickened, and Luke instinctively raised his guard.

Victoria let out a slow sigh and draped her arms lazily along the edge of the wooden tub. The motion caused her chest to rise above the water’s surface, floating just at the edge of decency—but Luke didn’t dare let his gaze wander. Not now. Not when her mood had shifted.

"It seems I’ve been too forward," she said, tapping her fingers gently against the rim of the tub. "Even though I warned you about Lhair, and even saved your life today, I can tell you don’t trust me."

Her voice was no longer teasing. The deference was gone—but strangely, it didn’t put Luke on edge. In fact, the honesty in her tone made him feel more at ease.

"Can you blame me?" Luke replied. "While I do appreciate what you've done, your behavior has been... unpredictable, to say the least."

Victoria laughed—lower this time, with a sultry edge that replaced her usual playful giggle. "That’s how I know you’re not very familiar with women, Luke. We all enjoy a little chaos."

Luke wasn’t sure if he should be offended by the comment—but she wasn’t wrong. The only women he’d met in this world were Hilda and Victoria… and both of them had been walking calamities.

"Even if that’s true," Luke said carefully, "I don’t know enough about you to trust you completely. I don’t know your goals, your dreams, or what drives you. And let’s be honest—we’ve only known each other for a few short months. Do you really expect me to trust you just like that?"

His gaze stayed locked on her deep brown eyes.

Victoria tilted her head, her expression unreadable. "Well, since you put it like that..." she began, her tone cool but composed, "you’ve only known Kayson a little longer than me. Yet you call him your brother. Are you saying he’s more trustworthy than I am?"

Luke frowned, preparing to respond—but Victoria cut him off, her chest rising and falling with emotion.

"And don’t say it’s because you fought on the battlefield together," she snapped. "You know as well as I do—men will sacrifice their so-called brothers the moment it becomes convenient!"

Her voice sharpened with venom.

"Men lie. Thieve. Rape. Pillage. All they do is take, take, TAKE! I have done nothing to warrant your suspicion, yet you call me untrustworthy?"

Luke’s eyes widened, stunned by the sudden outburst and the raw fury behind her words. He hadn’t expected this. Not from her.

"What do you need from me to put your doubts to rest, huh? Should I prostrate myself for three days and three nights? Should I surrender my body to you?" she shouted, voice trembling with rage and pain.

Her face was flushed, eyes glistening, her composure cracking completely. Victoria looked like she was standing at the edge of something—something she hadn’t dared show anyone in years.

Luke swallowed. He needed to choose his words carefully. One wrong move, and whatever fragile thread between them might snap for good.

"I would never ask you to do anything you’re not comfortable with, Victoria…" he said softly, his voice low and sincere. His eyes, no longer guarded, met hers without flinching. "If I’m to trust you… I need to know you. The real you. Not the persona you wear like armor."

He took a slow breath. "Let me see the truth. Show me who you really are."

Victoria laughed—harsh and broken. Her shoulders quivered.

"You want to know the real me?" she asked, voice hollow. "You want to meet the slave? The girl who was beaten, raped, starved… discarded like garbage once she lost her worth?"

Her hands clenched against the tub’s edge.

"You want to meet the pathetic girl who could only cry and wallow in her own filth, drowning in self-pity?"

"THAT GIRL IS DEAD!" she screamed, slamming her hand against the wooden rim.

The sound echoed through the room like a thunderclap. But it wasn’t the noise that struck Luke—it was her words. They stabbed through him, deeper than any blade. Tears glistened at the corners of Victoria’s eyes, her pain laid bare at last.

Without thinking, Luke leaned forward and wrapped his arms around her, pulling her trembling body into his chest.

He felt her freeze—just for a second—before a searing pain ripped across his back.

"Let me go!" she cried out, thrashing in his arms.

Her nails raked down his skin, drawing blood—but Luke held firm, gritting his teeth through the pain.

Slowly, her resistance faded. Her sobs replaced her fury, and she clung to him as her body shook with the weight of buried anguish.

She laid her head on his shoulder, crying until her voice turned raw and hoarse.

Luke said nothing.

He simply held her, absorbing her sorrow in silence. The sting on his back dulled, replaced by a deeper ache in his chest. He realized then—he’d pushed too hard. In seeking the truth, he’d forced her to reopen wounds that had never fully healed.

They stayed like that for a while, suspended in fragile stillness… until the door creaked open.

Kayson stood in the doorway, blond hair tousled, eyes going wide as saucers.

Luke glared daggers at him.

Wordlessly, Kayson understood, backing out of the room with exaggerated care before slowly closing the door behind him.

When Victoria’s sobs had quieted and her breathing steadied, Luke gently pulled away and lowered his head.

"I’m sorry I made you relive that nightmare," he said. "From this moment forward, I won’t treat you as a servant… but as a trusted friend."

For a few seconds, she said nothing.

Then, slowly, a smile broke across her face—not a teasing grin, not her usual playful mask, but something honest. Warm. Beautiful.

It was the first time he’d seen her truly smile.

And it was the most breathtaking thing he’d ever seen.

"Mmm. Then let’s be friends," she whispered.
