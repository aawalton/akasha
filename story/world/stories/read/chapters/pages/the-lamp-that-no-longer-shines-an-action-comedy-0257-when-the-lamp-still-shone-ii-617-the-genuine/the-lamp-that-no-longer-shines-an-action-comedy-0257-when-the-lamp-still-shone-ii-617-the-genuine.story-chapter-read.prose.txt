[Time]: 1980

The cold war was into its third week. When the Lord of the Umbral Cathedral declared one, she did not cut corners.

Ovelia had bolted the doors of her inner sanctum and said nothing to anybody since the sentence she left in the hall on her way out:

"If she requires a master, one of you may go and instruct her."

Somewhere in the silence it had stopped being a tantrum and started being an administrative problem, and the Aspects convened on the Nine-Tailed Cat Stone.

Ovelia had slept on it for years in her Lantern Cat form, sometimes with all nine of them piled on at once, and it gave under a cat's weight like warm water: the definitive luxury cat nest. Nobody on it was relaxing.

The agenda had one item, and the discussion went round the circle twice. Vila heard the question, considered it with evident seriousness, and went back to sleep. Of the two who had not come, one would have done the job properly, if anyone had known which continent she was currently on, and nobody had cared to put the question to the other. Everyone else at the table had refused or been refused in the hall on the first night, and none of them had improved since, which returned them, with the inevitability of water finding the floor, to the one they had banned.

"If nobody goes..." Daena's whisper came from the far edge of the stone, where she had wedged herself in against the sleeping Vila, her eyes fixed on nothing at all. "She thinks she was thrown away. And she goes back." A long pause. "And I have to bury it again."

"Ovelia has already had the fight with her about it," Allison said. "The line has been drawn. Whatever happens in that room, she will know better than to..." She got no further than that.

"You will play Ovelia," she said, turning to Kara. "If she sees your own face, she will know she has been fobbed off with a sister."

Kara had barely finished unfolding into her human shape before the council swarmed her. They put her in a vanity chair, contoured her jaw, pinned her soft curls back into severe lines, and loaded her with frantic, overlapping instructions.

"Yawn before you say anything! She has to wonder whether you will stay awake!""Never explain. If she asks why, it is because you felt like it!""And if you run out of ideas, throw her into a paradox and go back to sleep!""Under no circumstances let her realize she has been handed a substitute!""No head-pats. No biscuits. And you are not to tell her she is your favorite!"

Kara held very still and let them line her eyes.

Ever since that night, Irene had not set foot in the red-light district. Her cane tapped a steady, irritable rhythm against the cobblestones as she walked, ignoring a cluster of aristocratic Witches waving from a lounge across the street. The undisputed Queen of the Night's sudden, absolute abstinence had sent her vast network of acquaintances, informants, and casual lovers into a mobilized frenzy, flooding her with invitations and desperately probing inquiries.

It was exhausting. She could give nobody the real reason. The real reason was soft, smelled of green grapes and shochu, and set her alight all over again every time she thought about it.

Worse still, since that night Ovelia had vanished without so much as a whisper. I hate cats, Irene thought, and stepped off the curb, and her boot found no cobblestone. The shadows beneath the streetlamps rose around her ankles with the hesitant, fluffy warmth of a heavy winter blanket, and led her gently down.

Irene narrowed her golden eyes as the scenery resolved into a study: bookshelves rising to the ceiling, clusters of orange luminescent minerals casting a warm, fragrant glow across the space, and uncut gemstones piled carelessly in the corners like discarded gravel. A white fur carpet swallowed her boots to the ankles.

Standing behind a heavy desk was "Ovelia." She wore the right coat, the right silhouette, the towering frame, and an expression that could only be described as... intensely goofy.

This Aspect shares perhaps sixty percent of Ovelia's facial architecture. The rest is heavy contouring and a desperately maintained poker face.

Kara cleared her throat, squaring her shoulders. She reached into her coat and produced a meticulously bound stack of parchment, sliding it across the desk with both hands. "Today's lecture will cover Esoteric Arts and Ritual Mechanics."

Irene looked at the parchment and left it where it was. Since when have we needed a syllabus? "Ovelia," she said. "Why did you prepare a syllabus today?"

Kara froze. A fatal breach of character inside the first sixty seconds; she dredged up the frantic warnings. Never explain. Everything is a whim.

Her spine snapped ramrod straight. She slammed a hand on the desk. "Cat had a sudden whim!" she announced, her voice cracking on the final syllable. "Cat felt like preparing one, so Cat prepared one! What, do you have some dissatisfaction with Cat's methods?!"

Irene blinked, smoothing the amusement off her face before it could arrive there. A capricious, unreasonable whim, violently defended the instant anyone inquires about it. Full marks for the script. She inclined her head, drew the parchment toward her with both hands, and settled in to see how far the earnest impostor was willing to commit to the bit.

The answer turned out to be that Kara could play the tyrant right up until it involved actual pedagogy. Where Ovelia would have thrown her into a lethal paradox to see what she built out of the wreckage, this one offered structured, step-by-step guidance, paced the material like a standard academy lecture, and answered every question thoroughly. Most damning of all, before the practical demonstration she actually stopped to outline the safety hazards and the side effects of improper execution.

Irene rested her chin on her hand and listened with serene, polite indulgence, though a quiet impatience was already settling behind her eyes. If Ovelia were here, she reflected, accepting a neatly drawn safety diagram she had no intention of reading, we would be two stages past this by now. Only last week the real one had driven an experimental potion straight into her arm, watched her roll across the flagstones, and observed that the agony meant it was taking hold, because a great potion pushes its subject to the edge of madness. She had been right. That single injection had given Irene two new spell feats, and the two of them had spent the rest of the afternoon taking the formula apart and refining it on the spot. This one would have waited three weeks for clinical trials to clear before letting me near the syringe.

Irene crossed one leg over the other. Enough. If she pulled the mask off, perhaps the real one would have to come down and correct the mistake in person. "Which Aspect are you?" she asked.

Kara's tail, which had been swaying in a cheerful, rhythmic arc, stilled, and the clumsy, theatrical rigidity went out of her all at once. She leaned forward across the heavy desk, and the air in the study took on a sudden, localized gravity that Irene recognized in her bones.

A smile curved Kara's lips, serene and devastatingly focused. "Bad child," she murmured, her voice dropping into a teasing, honeyed warmth. "Do you not even recognize your own master?"

For the past hour Irene had been watching Kara try very hard to play Ovelia. Now it was as though Ovelia sat across the desk wearing a doting sister's face just to tease her, and the certainty of the gaze locking onto her was Ovelia's entirely.

Warmth rushed to the tips of Irene's ears, the interrogative edge melted from her posture, and the undisputed Queen of the Night lowered her gaze and did not press the issue.

Back in the council chamber, sparks were literally flying off the assembled fur.

"Useless! Catastrophic! She surrendered her identity in the first engagement. What is the meaning of this instantaneous capitulation?!"

"We cannot send anybody who enjoys her company," Allison said, two gloved fingers at her temple. "We are past wanting a better performance. We need overcorrection."

They needed Daena, who had never held a conversation that survived to a second exchange.

"If she cannot talk to people," Avenka said, tapping a claw against the stone, "she cannot surrender to Irene. It is foolproof."

They put her in one of Ovelia's coats, which swallowed her, and painted her mouth a severe funeral matte, and neither of those things helped in any respect. Daena stood in the center of the chamber, eyes brimming with unshed tears, shoulders hauled up to her ears. She hated socializing. She hated eye contact. She hated the concept of an exterior world.

The council herded her toward the portal without mercy, with the united energy of a household closing a bunker door. Allison sighed, shifted into her Lantern Cat form, and drifted onto Daena's trembling shoulder to provide emotional infrastructure.

[Location]: The Umbral Cathedral · The Lightless Sea

The classroom was a sensory deprivation chamber: a single wooden skiff at the center of an infinite, pitch-black ocean, the water as dead as a mirror in a sealed tomb, without wind, current, or a single ripple. The only illumination in the suffocating void came from the faint starlight drifting from Allison's fur as she floated near the stern.

Daena sat on the bench opposite Irene, and she looked profoundly, incurably depressed. The black on her mouth left the rest of her the color of alabaster cut for a monument. Her hands, sheathed in black nylon gloves, gripped the edge of her skirt with white-knuckled intensity. She had not looked up since they arrived.

Allison drifted down and poked Daena gently in the stomach with her glowing tail.

"Meow-awoo."

Daena flinched. She drew a shaky breath, pressing her painted lips together, and whispered, dragging each syllable out as if it carried physical weight:

"Class... begins."

Irene studied the woman across from her. Daena's eyes stared into the void with the unfocused, deadened quality of someone whose eyelids had been held open by force. Even when Irene leaned forward to catch her eye, nothing came back. Yet the feeling leaking off her was immense, rich and fragile as a vascular system spun from ice, so delicate that a single sharp word might shatter it and seal her away forever.

Daena began to teach. Or rather, she began to recite the script she had memorized before being shoved through the portal. She delivered the lecture in a single, unbroken, monotone exhalation, addressing the floorboards of the skiff.

"Excuse me," Irene said, keeping her tone warm and gentle. "If the mana phase-shift in that last sequence were inverted at the third node, wouldn't the whole matrix run in reverse? You could use it as a—"

Daena stopped. She rewound her internal tape and mechanically repeated the exact paragraph she had just finished, word for word.

"Yes, I followed that part," Irene said. "I meant, if one took it further, the practical application at the—"

Daena's eyes widened by a fraction. Panic spiked in her aura. She engaged the fast-forward protocol, accelerating to a frantic, blurring auctioneer's cadence, skipping over Irene's question to reach the relative safety of the next chapter.

Irene watched the retreat with a sharp, sudden appetite. Lady Berenice had coaxed a great many timid creatures out from behind their fans; she had simply never met one who treated a question like an assassination attempt. And this one, black-painted mouth and strangled velvet and all, was Ovelia too. Utterly delicious.

A panicked cat only bolted if you chased it on foot. Irene leaned back, opened her leather-bound notebook, uncapped her fountain pen, and began to write.

Daena's rapid-fire recitation faltered. In the silence, she sat perfectly still, her black-nylon-gloved hands resting on her velvet-clad knees, and waited.

As Irene traced the geometry of a complex rune onto the parchment, the math balanced, and smiling quietly to herself, she drew a clean, decisive line through her first question.

Across the skiff, Daena's gaze fixed on the notebook, and the corners of her black-painted lips moved. It was a two-degree alteration, barely registering on any scale, but in the gloom of the Lightless Sea, lit only by Allison's soft pulse, that faint curve landed like moonlight breaking through storm clouds.

There you are. Irene's breath caught, the game momentarily stalling in her chest. You look devastating when you smile.

Irene exhaled a soft, self-deprecating breath and tapped her pen against her chin. "It would be wonderful to receive a few more hints," she murmured, to no one in particular.

Silence stretched. Then a black-nylon glove crept across the gap, pressed its fingertips to the cover of the notebook, and with a deliberate, curling motion, exactly like a careful cat dragging a stolen treat toward its chest, pulled the book across the wood.

Scritch. Scritch.

Daena's hollow eyes went down to the remaining questions.

A whisper drifted across the skiff, soft enough that it felt like a kitten's whiskers trembling against the earlobe, articulating the secrets of the runes with slow, deliberate care. "How a rune is applied within a mature spell matrix... can be viewed as an amplifier. Placing the rune at the point of mana export or import... or installing it like an external pendant..."

For the remainder of the session, neither of them spoke above a whisper. Whenever Irene encountered a bottleneck, she took the notebook, drafted her question, and waited. Daena would sit in absolute, patient silence until the pen stopped, then scritch, scritch, claw the notebook back across the wood to whisper the solution into the dark. It was a wildly inefficient method of high-tier magical education, but the atmosphere felt entirely like two schoolgirls illicitly passing notes in the back of a silent classroom.

Two Aspects in a row had made the arrangement plain: Irene had been downgraded to a recurring administrative task on the Umbral Cathedral's chore wheel. Better still, every turn of the wheel delivered a brand-new edition of Ovelia to her doorstep. So when the third materialized in front of her, Irene did not even blink; she leaned back and waited to see what flavor she had drawn today.

This one stood an imposing two meters tall, with a thick mane of naturally curly hair that defied structural logic. Noticing Irene's appraising gaze, the Aspect offered a chilling smile that immediately decayed into a smug, W-shaped feline grin, chin tilted up, eyes curving into haughty crescents.

Irene's fingers twitched against her knee. I am going to scratch that chin, she decided, and I am going to pinch those ears.

"Cat's name is Avenka!" the Aspect announced, puffing out her chest, her voice booming with theatrical entitlement. "Today, Cat shall be your master, and the subject of today's curriculum is Lantern Cat Studies!"

Irene sat forward, and Avenka produced a tome large enough to function as a murder weapon, slamming it onto the table with a heavy, reverberating thud. The title, embossed in peeling gold foil, read:

One Thousand Forms and Processes of How Long-Term Ingestion of Their Beloved Foods Alters Lantern Cats.

Irene opened the cover and leafed through the dense nutritional data. Within five minutes she closed the book and looked up. "Who authored this text?"

Avenka placed both hands on her hips, striking a pose of unassailable pride. "Cat did, of course!"

Irene exhaled a long, steady breath. Thousands of words of critique, confusion, and despair compressed themselves into a single, diplomatically armored sentence. "Writing this book... must have been quite an arduous journey."

Avenka's ears twitched violently. Her smug expression collapsed into profound, dramatic emotion, and she leaned across the table. "Exactly! Cat labored over this masterpiece for ten agonizing years! But for some inexplicable reason, commercial bookstores refused to stock it. Only a single visionary establishment accepted a batch of twenty-five copies, and to this day, only sixteen have sold. A complete mystery to Cat. This reception has severely damaged Cat's creative enthusiasm!"

Sixteen people actually bought this?!

Avenka's theatrical melancholy evaporated and she clapped her hands. "Since you have absorbed the theoretical foundation, it is time for practical application. You shall now assist Cat with fieldwork!"

That was how Irene, future Fifth Seat of the High Council, found herself conscripted into manual labor under a cat's paw. For five days she worked three-hour shifts as Avenka's field researcher, interviewer, and data-entry clerk, gathering raw material for the first volume of the next magnum opus: she logged the dietary preferences, sleep schedules, and ley-line outputs of Greater Lantern Cats of every breed and affinity, while Avenka questioned each of them down to its favorite brand of dried fish. The stated goal was to catalog every variety of Lantern Cat in the universe. The universe produced new varieties every second. In five days of fieldwork, Avenka had cataloged exactly ten.

Naturally, Irene Habsburg did not work for free. If this internship had no exit condition and was destined to outlast the heat death of the universe, she intended to extract her wages in full.

She opened negotiations on the first afternoon with a stray brush of knuckles against Avenka's temple while passing a clipboard, disguised as an accident and paired with breathless praise for the rigor of the methodology. On the second day she smoothed a rebel curl away from the base of an ear whenever Avenka leaned in to inspect a chart, and whenever those ears flicked back in suspicion, Irene asked a question about the socio-dietary hierarchy of third-tier Lantern Cats, and scholarly vanity overrode the guard every single time. By the fourth shift Avenka no longer paused her dictation when Irene stepped up behind her chair; she simply tilted her two-meter frame back into the touch with a small, contented hum.

By the end of the fifth shift the clipboard lay forgotten on the table. Avenka was slumped back in her chair, trailing off mid-sentence on dried-anchovy metrics into soft, wheedling little noises whenever the hand paused, while Irene stood behind her with one hand resting beneath the Aspect's tilted chin and the other unhurriedly kneading the warm velvet of her right ear between thumb and forefinger. Exquisite, Irene thought, as the tip of the ear flicked against her palm. Worth every hour of cat food.

The studio door opened, the shadows in the corners leaned toward it, and Ovelia stepped through: the genuine article. Where the others had worn goofy arrogance, paralyzing anxiety, and theatrical pride, the Witch of Eternal Slumber wore the crushing weight of her own sovereignty and nothing else.

Ovelia's gaze swept the scattered interview notes, the measuring tapes, and the half-eaten cat treats littering the floor, and came to rest on Irene's fingers, still buried in Avenka's curls.

Irene gave the ear one last, unhurried stroke before letting her fingers fall away.

Ovelia's eyes narrowed and moved to Avenka. "What," she asked, her voice a soft, vibrating hum that rattled the window glass, "is your concept of teaching? You have completely derailed her developmental timeline."

Avenka crossed her arms, completely unbothered. "Big Cat said one of us could teach her. Big Cat never said what, and Big Cat never said for how long."

Ovelia dismissed the insubordination with a slow blink. She turned, her gaze moving across the room with arrogant possessiveness until it found Irene and settled there. "Make your choice. Do you wish to remain here in her servitude, or are you leaving with Cat?"

Irene met those starry eyes. Three weeks of silence, and the master had come down off her throne to ask that question out loud, in a room with witnesses in it, and what came up through Irene's ribs was bright and electric and very close to hysterical. So you could not stay away after all.

A slow, provocative smile spread across her face, the full lure of Lady Berenice aimed squarely at the First Seat. "I confess," Irene purred, letting her gaze travel up the line of Ovelia's throat, "I could never presume to know the thoughts of the best cats in the world."

The hook set clean. Ovelia closed the distance before Irene saw her move, caught her wrist, lifted the hand, and brushed her lips across the pulse point, fleeting and almost careless. "Try making that expression at anyone else again," she whispered against the skin.

Irene looked up. Ovelia was smiling, peaceful and flawlessly gentle, and the eyes above the smile had no bottom at all. Irene's pulse went wild and sweet under that mouth. Yes, she thought. Just like that. Look at me exactly like that.
