Crow watched Samantha set the sack down on the table in front of him. He looked up to her and nodded, noting she was looking very relaxed. That was good. Less chance of getting nagged. He took a swig of the foul-tasting root tea a herbalist had supplied him, supposedly great for wakefulness. He wasn’t impressed, but it served to some degree, at least.

Samantha frowned at him for some reason and glanced around briefly at the ‘common room.’ There were a few armed and nervous men about, standing and pretending to be vigilant in front of the supreme ultimate boss lady. One was looking out of a window. The house had been a small inn in Oxentine before being abandoned. Samanthian agents had purchased the ‘rights’ on the cheap from the city, and with the startling powers of Her Majesty’s cleromages, made it good as new on the inside within days. They didn’t want to draw attention, so the outside was still in-process.

In all honesty, Crow was grateful for the gesture. It was mostly for him, being the main one constantly trafficking out this way to get out from the mountain caves where his realm lay each time he conspired himself there. It was frequent enough that they had men at every hot button spot to prevent monster ambushes and such. And it was thus frequent enough that he was often in Oxentine, the first place of civilization outside the Wildlands and the mountains. Having a place he could just pop right into — and that was discreet — was preferable to constant inn stays.

Ironically, for a guy with a bardic type class, he was bored with inns. If he went as his typical disguise — which was more ‘him’ than most — they’d ask him to pay with entertainment instead of coin. Or even offer to pay him, instead. As if he had time for that shit.

“Nice place,” Samantha muttered, and — surprisingly in the extreme — she sat down at the table in front of him. “How are things?” An awkward tone.

Crow had the awful tea at his lips, staring. He’d assumed she’d just go immediately, having surely a thousand better things to do after dropping off her little prize. It wasn’t like he wanted or needed pleasantries. She knew better; knew his preferences. He lowered the drink and gave the goddess a level look. “Here’s the part where you express you’re worried about me.”

Samantha put her hands on the table and leaned over them to meet his eyes seriously. “I’m worried about you.”

Crow snickered. “Refreshingly direct.” I knew I was gonna get nagged.

“You look drained. Unhealthy. You aren’t sleeping much, or well. You’re not eating much.” In the pause, Crow simply shrugged. Samantha took a breath, searching his face unblinkingly. “Is it nightmares?"

“I wouldn’t call them that. Visions, more like. But that’s not really the issue. I’m just committed to my task, have been constantly traveling, and end up getting less sleep. Daily sleep, that is. My habit is to nap when the days are easier.”

Samantha’s lips curled up one way in a grin. “You mean catnap?”

Crow scowled at her and repeated insistently, “Nap.”

Samantha snickered but quickly smoothed her face as she sat up and looked away. “Teasing you is only consistent, you know.”

Crow didn’t reply. He grunted and lifted the tea to his lips, trying desperately not to cringe as it went down. He didn’t bother explaining that the tea, which helped him stay awake and dodge naps, also suppressed his appetite. All ‘natural’ complications. Not like Tashome.

[Death] is onboard, Samantha. You don’t need to worry about us. I know it in my bones.

Samantha seemed lost in her own thoughts, eyes vacant.

Perhaps hearing that other voice. We all do, at times.

“To think,” Samantha mused, “you were the one to first tell me about becoming a [Wizard].” Her voice suddenly became a disturbingly perfect and accurate pantomime of Crow, back when he was Merril. “ ‘Take [Wizard] or you’re a fool!’ ” Samantha dropped the cringeworthy voice and giggled. “And you told me about those Runebooks.”

Crow eyed her flatly. “So you sat down to reminisce, did you? About a shell I grew out of? Not much different than you shedding yours, or I would be calling you by the cutesy, shortened version of your name right now.” Crow didn’t say it directly, much as he wouldn’t want anyone saying ‘Merril’ outloud for any reason.

Samantha turned back to him, face going sober again. “A shell grown out of? Perhaps that’s what happened. I feel as if ‘Samantha’ took on new and greater meaning here, in stages, and I could do nothing but grow into it. Helplessly transformed.”

“Maybe that’s just what you want to believe.”

Samantha nodded slowly, once again looking away, but as if from a sudden new thought, she turned back quickly with a look of whimsy. “Crow, do you ever wonder if things could’ve been different with all of us? When it all began, I… I imagined being an adventurer alongside you all. As soon as I dropped into the world, that was the first persona I cultivated.”

“You’re a goddess — that was a doomed fantasy. Aside from that, my life never cooperated for me, either. Perhaps I could’ve had a quaint, satisfying adventure back then, perhaps not. I didn’t. What followed is something else. It varies, doesn’t it? Bast always seems to enjoy himself.”

“Mmn. This life, at least. I suppose I had my fun, too, for a time. But you and I — and most of us — our dreams were ultimately broken; we were. Which has happened a million times, perhaps. Now, we race to an uncertain completion, fighting, fighting, fighting, always fighting the war. I think our souls just can’t do anything else, forged and contoured to struggle…”

Crow had nothing to say to that. He let that broody thing between them hang and sipped his awful, awful tea.
