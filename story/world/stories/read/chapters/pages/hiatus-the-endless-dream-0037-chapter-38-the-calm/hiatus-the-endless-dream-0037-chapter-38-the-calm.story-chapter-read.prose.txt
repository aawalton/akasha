We didn't linger long after that. We had to get going, and both of us knew that, but before we descended the stairs, I decided on a small mercy. "Have you decided on who? You don't have to give the order in front of them. If you tell me now, I'll just make sure it's done."

Maerin hesitated, indecision written plain on her face, before she shook her head. "Alder. I'll say it myself. But if I freeze up again. I want it to be Alder."

To my surprise, Krevith and the Huntress weren't by the cells and instead had claimed a spot a ways away on the same level. They waved us over before we could re-enter the holding area. They didn't ask where we were, and neither of us had a chance to get a word in before Paltra started talking.

"I looked at the body more." The Huntress began, her voice low and soft. She wasn't subtle about gauging my reaction through small, stolen glances, "and got more information from Jeren. There's two people we can rule out as suspects."

"Two people you can rule out as being Visitors." I corrected dully.

That got a brow raise from Paltra, who motioned for me to explain.

"If I was a Visitor and trying to get us to fail this Instance the first thing I'd do is recruit a Survivor." I started counting off reasons on my fingers, "All the Survivors here are largely scared, many of them" I shot a side long glance towards Krevith, "still believe in your God, and most importantly no one has a way to confirm if they're lying if they offer, say, absolution and a chance to leave the trials alive without being ejected into the dream."

After a beat, Krevith answered, "That doesn't make sense. Why kill Talin in that case? She was a Priestess."

I didn't actually have an answer to that, but I had a suspicion, "Well has anyone been pardoned from a trial before?"

The Survivors looked to each other for confirmation, but found nothing. Maerin spoke for the group, "Not that any of us know of."

I nodded, "Then it could be why she was targeted. If she was a Priestess, someone who knew a bit more about all of this then she could spread that knowledge."

"It's still just a guess," Paltra said. She crossed her arms and glanced towards the back of the ship, where the cells were. "I'm all for traps and planning, but there's a lot of assumptions here."

Refutation burned on my tongue, but it was a petty thing. I wanted to squash it entirely, but only managed about half. "It's a good theory. Either way. It doesn't matter. We won't be staying here long enough for it to matter. I'm not keen on playing murder mystery. We just need to pick one of the Survivors out—Maerin's choice—tell Jeren about the moon wanting to kill us, and then leave."

Everyone froze at that, their faces twisted and pale. "What?" I asked.

"The moon is trying to kill us?" Krevith asked.

What? Had I really not told anyone? I sighed, "It's complicated, but yeah. I think so. You felt the burning sensation from outside, right?"

Krevith nodded after a second to think.

"Yeah. That was the moon trying to kill you." I pointed to one of the broken wood planks, whose damage allowed a sliver of pale crimson moonlight to peek its way in. "The redder it gets the more it hurts."

I looked over my group's faces and saw fear and exhaustion in equal measure. I checked the time.

3 hours 07 minutes until we won.

1 hour 07 minutes until my Stalker arrived.

The latter filled me with dread I couldn't afford to focus on.

"There should be water ways underground. We'll go there and hide out from the moon." I opted not to tell them that the monster in the forest had likely gone down there.

Maerin paled, "That's where it went. Right? The fog monster?"

Well, there went that plan.

It got a snort out of me, which turned into a chuckle.

"Something funny?" Paltra asked, looking more than a little aggravated by my laughter.

I pulled myself together, "Sorry. Sorry. It's just. I hate when people figure things out. Yeah, that monster is probably down there."

Paltra leaned back and closed her eyes with pursed lips while Maerin started drumming her fingers against a plank before speaking. "The ship has enough dark spaces we could hide out in. If the moon is killing things then nothing else should make it on the surface either."

Krevith gave us a sad smile. "The Spirit of Judgment. Excise. We can not. It may eat the ship."

An oppressive weight settled over the Survivors, one that didn't affect me, so I asked the question on my mind. "There's four of these Spirits. That Frog thing we saw earlier, the worms, what about the other two?"

Krevith straightened and cleared his throat.

I held up a finger signaling the man to pause and pulled out the [Communication Stone]. "Jeren. We're talking about plans. Figured I'd loop you in. Krevith is telling us about four of the Spirits. We uh. We have six big enemies to deal with, probably."

The stone crackled to life, "Hey. Thanks for looping me in. All good over here. The rest of the Survivors freaked out after you left, but they're calm now. I'm good to talk about this, but we need to figure out what to do about the other Visitor."

I nodded, "Thanks for the update. We'll figure it out after this. Krevith. If you would."

The Gladiator cleared his throat.

"Four Spirits of Judgment.

One to separate body and soul: Rend

One to judge each part: Appraise

One to cut along judged lines: Excise

One to draw the pieces in: Sheppard

Each works in tandem to create us anew, to separate sin from virtue so each generation is molded from purer souls.

I glanced at Maerin, concerned about how she'd react, but to my surprise, she had her hands clasped with closed eyes—irreverent. I frowned.

The stone crackled again, "I've heard similar things. Important question. The fuck does this have to do with the moon turning red. This didn't happen befo— er. Uh Dorian. How much do they know?"

"Everything. Keep going."

"Right. Yeah what does this have to do with the moon? It didn't turn red like this last time. You also seemed worried about the whispering in the forest."

I grimaced, wondering if I should lie, but ultimately there was no reason to, "[No Free Kill] is an awful Restriction Jeren how were you using it all this time?"

A sharp breath from the other side as he put two and two together. "Shit." After a moment, he returned with a worried, yet slightly joking tone, "Hey, don't blame me. I live on the lower layers, man. You practically ever run into Instances with this many monsters. Or monsters at all sometimes. It was basically just a free Restriction!" His tone wasn't exactly defensive, just exasperated, as if this entire situation was utterly ludicrous.

For my part, I was equally as confused, "What? How the hell do Instance Holders make you lose then?"

"It's like puzzles, or traps, or weird requirements. You know, the one that I got my [Title] in was just walking right? Never mind, now's not really the time, Dorian."

I shook my head. Yeah. Just walking my ass. 60 years of walking wasn't 'Just walking'. Still, it was good to know these Instances were out of the norm, or at the very least, for the lower layers. He was right that now wasn't the time for that, though. I quickly caught him up to speed about how the Instance Holder was using [No Free Kill] to make the moon and that Voice in the Forest creature stronger—and how it was likely waiting for us underground.

Paltra cleared her throat when there was a lull in my explanation and gestured at Krevith. "The other spirits. We still need to account for them."

Shit. Right. I gestured at Krevith to continue.

"Rend takes a form similar to a wolf, Appraise to a hawk, Excise as a worm, and Sheppard to a … frog." He said the last one while glancing at me. "They have their specific names, but I do not believe that to be relevant."

My mind went to the flying creature I had seen ferrying monsters to the moon. I'd bet anything that it was Appraisal. Collectively, we had seen three out of the four Spirits. The only missing one was Rend and … "How much do we know about Rend?" I asked. Everyone looked at me.

"We have stories. Tales and lessons. Many of them. Why?" Krevith asked.

Paltra's eyes widened, and I could tell she had a similar train of thought to me.

"Could it-" Talin's face flashed before my eyes, and my throat suddenly went dry. I pushed the words out anyway, "Could it cut someone's head clean off and dispose of the body?"

Maerin shut her eyes once more, Jeren cursed, and Krevith put his head in his hands, "Yes. Rend could do that."
