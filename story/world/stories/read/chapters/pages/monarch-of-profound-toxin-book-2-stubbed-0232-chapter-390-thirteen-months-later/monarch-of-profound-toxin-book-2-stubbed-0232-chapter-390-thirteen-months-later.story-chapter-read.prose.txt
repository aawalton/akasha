Chapter 390

Thirteen months later, Forest

“Are you coming or what?” Bin shouted into the house.

Philip turned the corner as he wrestled to get his jacket on and walked through the living room to the front door. “I had to get my clothes if we’re going out.”

“It’s not even cold out.”

He shrugged. “My mom says I have to. Says I’ll miss too much school if I catch a cold. Or worse—the flu.”

The green girl snorted, the tiny tusks protruding from her lower jaw giving the noise an airy whistle. She was quickly growing taller than all of the other girls her age, as well as most of the boys. “Yeah, that sounds absolutely terrible. Just go to the shop and get some medicine.”

“I can’t just go and get free stuff any time I want like you!”

“Oh, come on, Philip!” she groaned. “We both know what my dad would have said.”

Her friend smiled as she pushed the door closed. “To make sure I didn’t forget my free chocolate milk on the way home?”

She grinned. “Something like that, yeah.”

Bin didn’t bother to lock the door. She never did. What was going to happen, really? Burglary? Theft? Vandalism? Assault? Not likely. They were the most powerful family on Earth. By far. And among the most powerful in the Alliance. Wealth tended to do that.

Grandpa Ganyak hir Gawak had kept his promise to her father. Every day, without fail, he would dutifully come to the laboratory beneath the offices and create Legendary Mystery Medicine and give it all to Bin’s mom to sell through the company. It wasn’t the strong stuff her dad had been able to make, but it was more than enough to rake in credits like nothing else.

Her maternal great grandfather, Clan Leader Gul of the Ougi Clan, elevated their position further.

Then there was her uncle, Andihar Dayarunar, patriarch of House Dayarunar—one of the most influential factions in the Alliance—eternally loyal.

Auntie Mn’Toakh, High General of the Nidafjeld Alliance, still came around to have lunch with them once a week, so long as she wasn’t too busy.

Guild Master of the Alchemist Guild of Gimleh, Ganyak hir Gawak, now the only person in the Unified Mass capable of creating Legendary Mystery Medicine, came around pretty much every day and Bin hung around the laboratory and the offices just as often.

Grandpa Chop, legendary X-ranker and the current most powerful entity in the Alliance—perhaps even in the entire Unified Mass—lived in an annex in their garden.

The list of allies felt endless, but while Bin felt genuinely loved and supported by all of these people around her, there was an emptiness deeper inside. Those closest to her were either familial relationships, like her grandpas and those she called uncles and aunts, or people she had known since Eik had first taken her in as his daughter.

But the rest of them, although they would doubtlessly be on her family’s side always, were allies because of promises made to her father for the eventuality of his death. Promises made to ensure hers, her brother’s, and the rest of the family’s well-being.

Not to mention that it was incredibly lucrative to be connected with the family who owned the rights to Legendary Mystery Medicine. It was a gold mine.

Her father had done all he could to make sure they would be safe, and he had succeeded.

“Where are we going anyway, Bin?” Philip asked.

She arched an eyebrow at him. “Are you serious?”

He grew slightly nervous at that. “… What?”

“What’s happening today? Come on, Philip, you know this. I thought that’s why you came over!”

He went through eight grueling, lip-biting seconds of desperately trying to remember what was so special about this day in particular. On the ninth second, he inhaled sharply. “Uncle Heath’s exam!”

She grinned. “Damn straight. We’re going to watch him crush it. My Mom’s at work today, so we’ll get through the fracture in two seconds.”

Yes, they were as safe as they could have been.

But the hole in her heart just wouldn’t heal. Honestly, even after this long, she couldn’t tell you what it was. Guilt? Anger? Hopelessness? Loneliness?

Here she was, living the life her father had made sure she could continue, and they hadn’t even been able to find his body. They hadn’t been able to find anything at all.

Suddenly, while the war against the Lord of the Moon had been going at full swing, with spawn swarming the territories of the Alliance, all Profound Toxic beasts had just suddenly… dissipated into the air and floated off.

All in the same moment.

That was also the moment that had pierced her heart. She may have been young—and she still was—but she wasn’t an idiot. And even an idiot could have figured out what it meant, anyway.

If the Profound Toxic beasts were gone, then her father was also gone.

She hadn’t been the same since.

The others—Goo, Mom, Grandpa, and the rest—seemed to have gotten over it so quickly. Like they had been desperate to get back to some kind of routine. She felt alone in her grief.

But intellectually, she knew that that wasn’t fair. She, too, had quickly put up a facade of good spirit, and that was probably the case for all of them. Nobody wanted to be an emotional burden, and that sucked.

Fortunately, when the Profound Toxic beasts had dissipated, something had happened to the spawn of the Lord of the Moon as well. Unlike the azure guardians, they had not disappeared outright, but they seemed to grow sluggish and mindless, starting at that same moment.

They began to lose their balance and their strength was sapped. With every second that passed after the dissipation of the Profound Toxic beasts, the spawn seemed to become less and less formidable opponents.

Eventually, it reached a point where even the most powerful of them—ones that had been terrorizing entire armies on their own—had become bumbling messes, incapable of reacting to the attacks of even the weakest among the Alliance troops.

From then on, it had been a bloody, one-sided massacre of the lord’s forces. It had been too easy. As they tore into the creepy monsters, even the most optimistic among the members of the Alliance had been just waiting for the Lord himself to make another entry and put an end to the world.

But it had never happened.

For thirteen months it had been quiet, everything had gone back to normal, and by now the Lord of the Moon, as terrifying and unforgettable as his short return had been, had been retired to a chapter in the history books.

The damage he had caused was far from healed, but they had made it to a point where they could focus on healing rather than desperate survival.

“Shouldn’t we, like, get Uncle Heath a gift or something?” Philip asked as they elbowed their way through the busy market district. “You know, to celebrate his graduation from apprentice smith.”

Bin’s jaw hung slightly ajar for a moment as she stared back at him. “Damn… I totally forgot about that. Do you think we have time to make a detour?”

The boy shrugged. “I mean, not really. We’re already late as it is. If we make a stop on the way, we might not get to see much more than Uncle Heath wiping his beard clean of the crumbs of the cake Uncle Mike will surely have bought for him.”

Bin groaned and kicked a pebble. People around them weren’t happy to get dust kicked on their pants. “We have to get him something at least, don’t we?”

“We can just tell him that it’s not ready yet and that we’ll go and get it for him after.”

She frowned. “That’s just not the same.”

“What do you want to give him?”

Bin chewed on that for a little while. “You know… Something meaningful.”

“You’re saying you don’t have a clue, right?”

“Yeah…”

He chuckled. “The guy will be happy with whatever you give him. That’s just who he is. A real ‘it’s the thought that counts’ kind of guy.”

“Still.”

Even though her father’s direct summons had vanished all together, the more permanent things her father had done didn’t go anywhere. Her brother, Goo, didn’t change at all, except becoming even quieter than he had already been. Bin felt like she had lost a part of her being when Eik was lost, but perhaps, for her brother, that was the case in a very real way.

When she thought about that possibility, it was difficult to keep the tears at bay.

Those who had been granted power by her father’s Toxic Initiation also didn’t lose them. That was why Grandpa Ganyak hir Gawak was able to continue producing the Legendary Mystery Medicine.

Profound Toxin still flowed through him.

So where was her dad?

Grandpa Chop had eventually gotten to a point where he got an ability that her father had wielded as well. Admission to the Realm.

He had been the last person to see Eik alive. Eik had sent him to protect them all while he dealt with the Lord of the Moon himself. It had been an idiotic thing to do. His odds of winning had been abysmal. She understood that now.

It had been Admission to the Realm that had allowed him to do it. It had forcefully brought the Lord to Toxin, the world where her father reigned supreme.

Grandpa Chop had been training tirelessly with the aim of getting that specific ability for a little over eight months and had finally gotten it. They had all been gathered when he used it. Hundreds of people, all hoping to see Eik Magnasen’s return from wherever he must be trapped.

Chop had activated the ability.

And it had done nothing.

Absolutely nothing.

It had been a dud.

He had been convinced that it was real and that it should work. Said he could feel it. Feel the response when he activated it. It sent out a signal. An attempt to connect to something far, far away.

But a response to that signal was never returned. No matter how many times he tried, it was the same. A grappling hook shot into the darkness with nothing to grab onto.

There should have been something, however. Many of them had been there themselves. There should have been a whole world, so how did it make sense that it wasn’t there now?

“I know what I’ll get him.”

“What?” Philip asked.

“A bun stuffed with chocolate and whipped cream.”
