The Lost Sanctum of the Sage was the only reason the Godless Ruins were known throughout the continent in the first place.

There were countless books describing what had happened there, each contradicting the others in one way or another, but every single one agreed on one thing. Whatever had destroyed the city had begun inside the Sanctum itself.

Centuries ago, the Sage, a woman whose name had spread across kingdoms and whose discoveries had changed magic itself, had chosen that city as the place where she would spend the final years of her life. She built her last sanctum there and lived in seclusion for several years. Then, one day, something went horribly wrong.

No record ever explained exactly what.

Some claimed one of her experiments had failed. Others believed she had attempted to challenge the gods themselves and had been cursed. A few even suggested she had deliberately ruined the city.

Whatever the truth had been, the result remained the same.

A freezing fog had spread outward from the Sanctum and swallowed the entire city within minutes. Every resident caught inside had died before they could escape.

The cold itself became infamous., and eventually, everyone abandoned the place altogether.

No priests even entered to perform funeral rites for the countless dead left behind. No gods ever reclaimed the city.

Over time, people simply began calling it the Godless Ruins for those reasons.

That had happened centuries ago.

Since then, countless expeditions had entered the outer regions of the ruins searching for forgotten treasures. Many returned carrying ancient artifacts, enchanted weapons, old spell books and relics worth fortunes. Others never returned at all.

Yet despite generations of searching, nobody had ever found the Lost Sanctum.

Some believed it no longer existed. Others argued that the path leading to it had simply been lost with time.

Either way, every expedition had failed.

Which was exactly why Prince Caelan's words sounded so unbelievable.

Had anyone else been sitting across the table making such claims, Rayne would have dismissed them immediately as another dreamer chasing fairy tales, but this wasn't just anyone.

This was a prince.

As Rayne silently tried to process what he had just heard, Prince Caelan calmly cut another piece of steak before speaking again.

“I know this is probably a lot to take in, especially since you already know about the Lost Sanctum. Most people who have read anything about it assume it can never be found.” He paused for a brief moment before meeting Rayne's eyes. “But I've found a way to reach it.”

The words hung in the room for a few seconds.

“Whether the method actually works remains to be tested,” Caelan continued, “but I believe there's a very good chance I'll be able to step inside the Sanctum itself. And you know what waits inside.”

“The Sage's lifework,” Rayne answered quietly.

“That,” Viscount Aldric said, finally joining the conversation again, “and so much more.”

The old noble leaned back in his chair, his expression growing thoughtful.

“The Sage wasn't merely brilliant. She was wealthy beyond imagination. Even the great ducal houses of her time struggled to compare with the fortune she accumulated, and today that wealth would be worth even more.” His eyes shifted toward Rayne. “Gold loses value. Kingdoms rise and fall. But magical creations endure, and she had a lot of them.”

Prince Caelan nodded. “The artifacts hidden inside that Sanctum—The enchanted tomes, experimental spellcraft, alchemical formulas, enchanted materials... centuries have passed without anyone touching them. And finding them would change not only my future, but also the future of the kingdom itself.”

It didn't take a genius to understand what Prince Caelan truly meant.

The wealth hidden inside the Lost Sanctum wasn't simply about becoming rich. A prince was already rich. It was about gaining enough power, influence and resources to stand shoulder to shoulder with his brothers and perhaps even surpass them.

Until now, Rayne had never been entirely certain whether the Third Prince even wanted the throne, but looking into Caelan's eyes now, he no longer had any doubts.

There was ambition there.

Not the loud, burning ambition, but something quieter. More patient. More calculating.

Even so, Rayne still couldn't understand where exactly he fit into all of this.

The entire conversation felt like a game of chess where everyone already knew their roles except him. He didn't even know which piece he was supposed to represent.

Taking another bite of the steak, he finally decided to ask.

“And where do I come into all of this, Your Highness?” he asked. “You probably know everything there is to know about me by now, and I'm certainly no wayfinder capable of leading you to the Sanctum.”

Prince Caelan smiled. “No, you aren't.” He folded his hands together before continuing. “But you are something far more difficult to find. You're capable. You're a spellsword. You've repeatedly shown that you can lead others, if your files are correct. You're courageous, even if from the outside it occasionally looks more like recklessness. More importantly, you learn quickly.”

The prince paused for a moment before a faint smile appeared on his face.

“And perhaps the most valuable thing about you is that nobody would ever place a bet on you.”

Rayne blinked. For a second, he genuinely couldn't decide whether that was meant to be a compliment or an insult.

“So,” he said slowly, “I tick all the boxes, and despite the damage it could do to your reputation, you still want someone like me beside you?”

Before Prince Caelan could answer, Viscount Aldric spoke first.

“Reputation changes,” the old noble said calmly. “It comes and goes with time. People rarely remember the past forever.”

His gaze settled on Rayne.

“You're living proof of that. Only a short while ago, people looked at you and saw nothing more than the traitorous bastard of House Frayser. Then you dragged Atticus into the dirt in front of half the capital.” A faint smile tugged at the old man's lips. “Now that's the story people remember.”

Prince Caelan nodded in agreement.

“Exactly,” he said. “Which is why you're perfect, Rayne.” The prince held his gaze for several seconds before adding with an amused smile, “I'd even go as far as saying we're perfect for each other.”

Rayne slowly nodded, though he still couldn't fully understand why Prince Caelan was betting on him so much. Everything the prince had said made sense. He understood why someone underestimated and isolated by everyone else could become useful, yet even then it still felt like something was missing.

There was another reason hidden beneath everything Caelan had told him, something the prince hadn't revealed outright.

As Rayne silently went over every sentence spoken since he entered the room, fitting each piece together one by one, the answer finally clicked inside his head.

His expression shifted almost immediately as he looked back at the prince. “You want me and my squad to be the ones to actually find the Sanctum,” he said. “After all, you still need to search the way leading to it, and Captain Edran doesn't know anything about it. I doubt anyone else in the army does either.”

To his surprise, Prince Caelan simply nodded.

“You're right, Rayne. I respect Captain Edran, but he isn't an ally. Not yet. More importantly, making the Lost Sanctum an official objective would guarantee that others learn about it, and that is the last thing I want. If word spreads that I know how to reach the Sanctum, every one of my brothers, dukes and noble families would immediately begin making their own preparations.” He paused for a moment before adding, “The fewer people who know, the greater my advantage.”

“So...” Rayne said after a brief silence, “this is a secret quest.”

“Yes,” Viscount Aldric answered before the prince could. “Officially, His Highness is travelling to the Godless Ruins to recover treasures and prove himself capable of leading men in dangerous territory. You are going there because it is your punishment, while your warband is accompanying him because arrangements have already been made with House Sinclair and House Evans.” The old noble calmly folded his hands together before continuing. “Rest assured, the warband itself won't be moving deep into the ruins. Their official deployment will remain around the outer regions.”

Rayne frowned slightly. “But the Lost Sanctum is believed to be somewhere near the central parts of the ruins.”

Prince Caelan nodded without denying it. “It is, which is why none of this will be easy.”

Rayne quietly cut another piece of steak and took a bite, finally feeling as though he understood the entire plan.

After swallowing, he looked back toward the prince and said, “So you also need me because... no one will care if I die there.”

The room became completely silent.

Rayne kept his eyes fixed on Prince Caelan and, for the first time since the meeting had begun, saw the confidence on the prince's face falter ever so slightly.

It lasted no more than a heartbeat before the expression disappeared again.

“If I believed there wasn't a good chance your squad could come back alive,” Caelan said calmly after a few seconds, “I wouldn't be having this conversation with you. This isn't a death quest.”

Rayne lowered his gaze toward the table for a brief moment before shaking his head. “I'm sorry, Your Highness, but from where I'm sitting, that's exactly what it looks like. You may believe the chances are good, but you're still asking me to lead my men toward something that is known as one of the most dangerous places in the world.”

“I know how it sounds,” Prince Caelan admitted. “But once you discover the entrance, I won't be sitting somewhere safe while you risk your lives. I'll be there with you. It won't only be you walking into that Sanctum.”

“It's not just about my life,” Rayne replied quietly. “Or even yours, Your Highness. It's also about the lives of the men under my command. They're already marching towards the ruins because they have no choice. I can't ask them to take an even greater risk unless I'm absolutely certain it's worth it.”

Hearing those words, Prince Caelan and Viscount Aldric exchanged a brief look.

For a moment, Rayne wondered if he had spoken more than he should have. The silence stretched for only a few seconds before the prince finally looked back at him.

“But it's going to be worth it,” Caelan said calmly. “Not only for you, but for your entire squad.”

Rayne raised an eyebrow. “How so?”

A small smile appeared on the prince's face, almost as though he had been waiting for that exact question.

“I know you're far more than what others see, Rayne. There's no way you could have moved the way you did and defeated Atticus otherwise. A duel in the middle of the capital attracts many eyes, and you've done exactly that.” He paused before continuing. “Don't misunderstand me. I'm not interested in uncovering your secrets. I'm only offering you a chance to keep them.”

Rayne's heartbeat quickened slightly. “By following you?”

“Precisely.” Prince Caelan nodded without hesitation. “There's no better backer than a prince. Help me reach the Lost Sanctum, and I promise I'll stand behind you. Whatever secrets you're carrying, whatever enemies you make in the future, you'll no longer have to face them alone.”

Viscount Aldric gave a slow grunt beside him. “A prince's word is worth more than gold.”

Rayne quietly looked between the two of them.

He knew that wasn't a small offer. If anything, it was exactly the sort of backing he had hoped to obtain one day. With a prince supporting him, many doors that were currently closed would suddenly open. More importantly, if he proved himself valuable enough during this expedition, perhaps he could even ask Prince Caelan to knight him afterward.

That alone would complete the next requirement of his lineage quest. Still, this decision wasn't only about him anymore.

He had a squad now.

“What about the others?” Rayne asked.

Prince Caelan smiled, clearly expecting the question.

“The forsaken soldiers who accompany you will be given an opportunity to earn their freedom from military service once everything is over. As for the common soldiers, I will personally see to it that they're rewarded with promotions, one artifact recovered from the Sanctum each and my support.” He paused briefly before adding, “It may not be to the same extent as the support I'll offer you, but they won't leave empty-handed.”

Viscount Aldric nodded. “My family will also offer those who distinguish themselves an opportunity to serve under House Aldric. Opportunities like that rarely come to commoners.”

Rayne fell silent after hearing that. The offer was far greater than he had expected.

Simply giving the forsaken a chance to leave the army would already be enough for many of them to risk everything. Nate alone would probably accept without a second thought if he heard it. The promises made toward the common soldiers were equally tempting.

Even so, Rayne couldn't bring himself to make such a decision on behalf of everyone.

Hence, he quietly finished the last piece of steak before placing the fork and knife down.

“I'm sorry, Your Highness,” he said after a moment. “But I'll need some time before giving you an answer. At the very least, I need to consult a few members of my squad.”

Prince Caelan didn't appear disappointed. Instead, he simply nodded as though he had expected that answer all along.

“You may do so. Just make sure nobody else learns about what we've discussed today. Even to your squad, you can't give them all the details.”

“They won't know,” Rayne replied immediately. “You have my word.”

“Good.” The prince leaned back slightly in his chair. “Then give me your answer in a week. I believe that's more than enough time.”

Rayne nodded before standing from his chair. He bowed respectfully to Prince Caelan and Viscount Aldric before turning toward the door.

But his hand had barely reached the handle when the prince spoke again.

“Rayne.”

He stopped and glanced back over his shoulder.

“Don't disappoint me,” Prince Caelan said quietly. “I don't place my trust in people easily.”

Rayne held his gaze for a brief moment before taking a slow breath.

“I'll remember that, Your Highness.”

With those final words, he opened the door and stepped out of the room, already knowing that the coming week was going to give him far more than a few headaches.
