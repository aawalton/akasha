Day 373, 5:00 AM

Floors down to seventy-fifth remained as empty as the fifty-first, but the greenery had started to recover, growing lusher with each floor we descend. Most trees are already saplings while the budding bushes and grasses make for a fine, green carpet. As we descend the stairway leading to the seventy-sixth floor we are greeted with a change. The light glows at the end of the tunnel, meaning something is down there. Much to our relief and disgust, it’s another hatchery.

Unlike before, there are three lobster larvae, their carapaces in various stages of hardening, and the hatchery is in the process of spawning yet another egg. Edna destroys the abominable flesh contraption while I execute the shrimps. The most advanced one moves, trying to resist, but it’s as helpless as a newborn. I end its suffering, but notice the slime trails on the ground all around.

“Edna, some of these have matured enough, and whoever is the architect behind the dungeon pulled them down to the lower floors.”

“Makes sense.” She heads for the tunnel leading to the seventy-seventh floor. “These floors can spawn abominations in two weeks. That should amount to one or two minor abominations a day. If the eldritch creatures you spoke of herd them down to the ninetieth floor, they could have an army of minor abominations ready for us by the time we reach it.”

“Why stop there? They could drag them all down to floor one hundred and form an army of abominations. They would have at least a hundred by the time we’re down there.” The number would be no more than a speed bump, hardly even that—“The eldritch creatures are making a play to drain our mana. We have no way of dealing with a hundred or more abominations without spending large amounts of mana.”

I run the numbers. Undoing the guardians on floors ninety-one through one hundred would take me a good chunk of my mana, if not all of it. I don’t know what they had warped the wormlords into, or whether they had left them intact as their final bastion of defense.

Am I really trusting a BSD-provided vision and gambling my life on it? It would seem foolish if this was the only life I have, but with Redo, I’m willing to take the chance.

“What are you thinking about?” Edna noticed my vacant look while waiting by the entrance towards the next floor.

“Wouldn’t it be nice if this was a wild goose chase?” I smile at her. “Imagine the problem being as simple as visiting all the dungeons, clearing the hatcheries, and then the world fixes itself as time passes and we can just relax and enjoy life.”

I have spent a year on Everrain, but it’s been closer to a century from my perspective. It was a jumbled mess of running around, trying to figure out how to survive, how the BSD and Guide work, and how to free the people of a bunch of zealots trying to exterminate humanity to deliver them to the holy one, who is in fact an eldritch horror or a group of them trying to devour the world.

Sure, there were several leisure redo loops, but all in all, this has been a life of learning, misery, and running around to get things done. I really think I deserve better than to die here as a martyr. If I survive, I promise to take better care of myself and to enjoy life more. I owe it to Manny. Should have done it earlier, but I expected I was playing the long game instead of a blitz to save the world.

Some of my emotions must have leaked to my face, and Edna tilts her head, examining my features.

“I guess it would be.” She draws a deep breath, obviously hesitating about something before she speaks. “Is there anything you would like to tell me?”

My thoughts are private, but she knows a part of my secret, namely that I hop worlds, dying all the time.

“I think I would like to lead a long and enjoyable life here on Everrain. Maybe make a mansion like Hadriuse, get a restraining order against Lucy.”

That last bit stumps her.

“I don’t want romantic involvement. My loss seems too recent, and I think I’ve made a mistake with Lucy…” I pour my heart out. My life is insane, the second track of my parallel thoughts realizes.

Here I am, walking down a disgusting aberration dungeon, potentially heading towards my doom to save the world. Can’t say I’m all that enthusiastic about it, but either I die and save the world, or the eldritch things devour the world and I’m dead again. Given my options, I guess not sending an entire world down the drain is better, including the people I met along the way, my unborn child, and the psychotic witch I saved along the way.

We reach the next floor, and I’ll be damned if this dungeon doesn’t feel like a giant redo loop. Identical floors, going through the same motions over and over. I coup de grace the shrimps while Edna torches yet another hatchery and we’re down another flight of stairs.

Is there anything you would like to tell me? Edna’s words kind of haunt me. Redo is ready, and what the hell, one last confession. If it doesn’t work out, I just won’t tell her in the next loop. God, just the thought of doing the dungeon all over again sickens me.

“Edna, I have another confession to make.” That line gets her undivided attention. “I have read every book in Hadriuse’s library. I have explored a good chunk of his house, played most of his board games, found his stash of booze, and man are those things vile.”

She stares at me, and there’s a bit of Manny when she’s angry about those eyes. Manny would have folded her arms right about—Edna does it too, but her glare lacks the hint of amusement my wife always hid somewhere deep down where she thought I couldn’t find it.

“I have the ability to travel back in time. So, I had all the time in the world to experiment with skills, classes, and do things I wanted to do. The ability comes with a price, but I think I’m managing it better now.”

Much to my surprise, Edna sighs in relief.

“The day you decided to go down here to kill the otherworldly eldritch monsters, the Guide told me you can time travel, and that it would grant me power beyond my wildest dreams if I killed you.”

Well, there’s a shocking revelation if I’ve ever heard one.

“And will you kill me?”

She cocks her brow and spears me with a gaze I guess all women are born with. “Yes, right now. I thought I should give you a heads-up, since catching you by surprise would be too unsporting.”

I smile at the bit of snark, and she shakes her head, exasperated. “Of course I won’t. Assuming you’re right, these creatures are behind the deaths of my friends and family, and they plan to devour the world. Even if we ignore the threat to the world, I am a vengeful person.”

With all the cards down on the table, what’s one final confession?

“I had a vision of Gila giving a speech to a class of young mage-aspirants. She talked about our heroic sacrifice, about how we died to destroy the monsters trying to devour the world. It was a nice and touching speech, and she wore a really smart uniform; I think she made it herself, but what I’m trying to say is that if you follow me down to the bottom, you may die down here.”

She looks at me without a hint of fear.

“I think that leaving a legacy of saving the world, and clearing up the names of my ancestors, and restoring and passing their traditions to new generations is something I’m willing to die for. I think I will die smiling.”

Damn! My stomach falls. I will have to redo this conversation. The genuine emotions both of us are feeling will wear out, become plastic. Maybe they won’t if I only redo once, and then all of this is over.

I’m happy and proud of her, and of my choice to cure the condition she was suffering from.

We head down, floor after floor. Floors ninety-one through one hundred are also hatcheries, and the floor one hundred teems with a swarm of lobster-like monsters.

Abominations of all shapes and sizes rush towards us. Edna would have stood no chance against them, but I sing. I sing of the release one experiences after becoming healthy after a long illness; I sing of the piled up snow melting and receding to give way to new life.

An invisible wave surges out of me, consuming everything before me, and the army of abominations melts into flakes and crumbles along with the hatchery as they devolve into life mana. The victory cost me seventy percent of my reserve, but the way to the final floor is clear and we’re ready to face the monsters of the one hundred and first floor, whatever they are.
