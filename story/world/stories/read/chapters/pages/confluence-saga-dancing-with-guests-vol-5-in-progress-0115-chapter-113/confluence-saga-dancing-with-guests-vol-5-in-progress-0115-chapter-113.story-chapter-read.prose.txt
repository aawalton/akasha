Why do Diving teams dissolve?

Many teams will Dive until they choose to retire. The eventual desire to relax, to enjoy their gains, to no longer put themselves at risk, is the end of the line for many.

Debilitating injuries are rarely the cause, Husks are repairable, or barring that can be rebuilt from scratch. Injuries that leave permanent lasting damage to the True Self are rare.

Disagreements within a team can lead to dissolution. If you cannot trust your team you cannot fight safely. If you cannot fight safely, you should not be Diving at all.

The final reason is perhaps the most common, and certainly the most tragic. The loss of one or more team members. It breaks the spirit, of the individual and of the team as a whole.

Fay couldn't help herself, she giggled into her coffee. Luckily her mug was down to the last sip so it wasn't a disaster, but the following silence made her realize she hadn't been quiet.

"Fay?" Anya asked, a twinkle in her eye hinting that she suspected what had happened.

"Sorry," she said. "Just. George."

"Yes?" George asked. "Did I say something wrong?"

"You said you were surprised Seer Phelann wasn't at breakfast."

"I was hoping to meet her. I'm sure I'll have to work with her at some point, or have questions for her."

Fay glanced at Anya, then at Seb as they sipped at their coffee rather than answer. Turning her head to look into the kitchen she saw Briana making an effort to avoid the conversation.

"Really?" she said. "None of you?"

She didn't bother looking at Alex. He'd kept his face buried in his breakfast from the moment George made a comment about how much calmer he seemed than their first discussion. Shaking her head she straightened and looked at George, forcing herself to assume the more formal persona she'd had to use for decades.

"Healer Georgette. Seer Fay-lahn. I look forward to assisting you within the scope of my skills."

She saw George blink, then blink again. She didn't seem to be upset, despite the frown that flickered across her face for a moment. Finally she smiled and held out her hand.

"It's George. But you knew that."

"Fay," she answered.

"So when Briana said she wasn't a retainer but was with Fay, she meant she was part of the inner circle because of you."

"She- Bri? You!"

"I'm not a retainer Fay," Briana answered. "I'm here because you are. You're my reason."

I'm yours.

Fay blushed. The extra words over the band had not been needed. She could tell that despite the teasing tone the others had heard Briana was worried about how she'd react to the layered meaning. It wasn't as though she could answer right now either, not and remain focused on the conversation at hand. Despite that she knew she had to say something, anything.

Yes. Me too.

She heard a mug clatter as Briana went to refill it. She could see Anya biting her lip to not say anything. She had to get away, this wasn't the time or place for this conversation, this wasn't something she could talk about around everyone.

"Briana," Seb said. "I know it's your day off, but do you think we could have some of those pastries George liked so much? Maybe Fay could help you get some?"

"It's fine," George said. "I had so many yesterday, I couldn't-"

"We'll get them!" Fay said as she all but dragged Briana out of the kitchen and into the Holding proper.

"Bacon is almost ready," Briana called back to them before the door shut. "Don't let it burn."

Anya glanced at Seb as the Holding entrance swung shut. She wasn't sure what exactly had been said by the two but clearly they needed a chance to talk privately. The band might not be overheard, but that didn't help with facial expressions.

Pastries?

They're good, and gives them an excuse if they need longer.

"Did I say something wrong?" George asked.

"No," Anya said. "I think one of them, maybe both, sent something over band that wasn't expected."

"Oh… Oh!"

"Fay is family," she said. "She's very competent as a Seer."

"They've made grand gestures once or twice," Alex said, finally joining in. "And keep being surprised when the other does."

"They're cute together," Sandra said as she came in. "They didn't even say hello, just avoided me. Oh! Alex finally invite someone?"

"I did n-"

"What?" George asked. "Who?"

"George. Meet my actual little sister, even if she's taller than me, Sandra. Training to be an Engineer and Princess Lysandra, Heir Lys."

"She's joining the Holding as our Healer," Alex said with a glare. "She's not-"

"I'm not what?" George asked sweetly. "Not willing to talk to an Arbiter about contracts before I talk to the Holder and her Partner who's life I saved? Not willing to conform to your expectations of a Healer to be hired?"

"Yes. I mean no. I mean."

Anya saw her sister laugh and try to circle into the kitchen.

"Bacon isn't ready. You can wait."

"But Anya..!"

"Or you can head to work now and impress Yshe by being on time."

"I'll wait," Sandra said before flopping onto the couch. "I'm not late yet."

~ * ~

"Bri?" Fay said once she'd managed to all but drag her into her office.

She would have headed to their chambers but then they might- No. They had to focus, they had to talk, to clarify, to understand each other. They had to go get pastries and they didn't have time to- No.

Briana wouldn't meet her eye. Not that she actually could even if she wanted to, Fay had been too busy glancing at her out of the corner of her own and trying to hide behind her hair to actually look at her.

"Fay?"

I- you- we- Fay sent.

We- If you-

Fay's thoughts stuttered. How did they manage all the time? How did they deal with what they heard, what they understood, what they felt? How was she supposed to talk to Bri about how she felt if every thought was being shared? She knew it wasn't every thought, that they were both filtering, but it felt so open.

"Words?" she suggested.

"If you want. If you think that's better," Briana answered.

This wasn't fair. Briana was the confident one, the one who had answers, who knew what she wanted. Briana saw her, listened to her, let her be herself. She ran her own business, she organized things. Why was she not just saying what she meant, what she thought. Why did it have to be so hard to-

I love you, Fay sent before she could reconsider.

There. She'd said the words, she'd let Briana know how she felt in no uncertain terms.

I love you too, Briana sent back a heartbeat later.

"Why was that so hard," Fay asked. "You know how I feel about you, you know what you mean to me. But the words were-"

"Because we're both silly? Because… I don't know why."

Fay nodded, blinking back happy tears as she pulled Briana close. The words didn't matter, except that they did. But they'd said it, they'd said-

"I'm yours," she said.

"I know," Briana answered.

She could feel Briana's smile, she'd already said those words and Fay had panicked because of where they were. Now they'd both said them, both been able to express how they felt.

"We should go get the pastries," Fay said.

"Yes, we should before they think we're busy-"

"Bri!"

She gave her a light, playful shove before letting them out of her office. If they hurried to get the pastries they might actually have time to dawdle before they headed back upstairs.

"We need to hurry," she said. "Sandra will probably try to eat our bacon."

"I can always cook more," Briana said.

"Do you want Sandra to say she got both our bacon?"

She giggled as her girlfriend sped up, rushing past her and tugging her along.

"We can't have that!"
