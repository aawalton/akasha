Chapter V.LIII (5.53) - Evie’s Combat

After the battle, Kizu’s father had departed the stands to go speak with Finn. Kizu, not wanting to get in the way of one of the few occasions where their father actually seemed proud of Finn, elected to stay behind and ‘scout’ for their family.

Honestly, it wasn’t that far from the truth. But it wasn’t his family he was thinking of as he examined each of the next battles. Kizu leaned back in his seat and studied his potential foes. He still didn’t know who he’d be up against in his fight after Ulric, so it was important to take each fight into consideration. Plus, he learned a lot from studying his peers about fighting in general. More students than not were terrible fighters. They didn’t know how to properly use their spells in practical combat and often folded under pressure. Not to mention, most lacked a tactical eye for adjusting their strategies for their battlefields, which resulted in more than one contestant getting eliminated from simply the biome. The most embarrassing of the fights was when a boy fell into a pond and didn’t know how to swim. It resulted in his opponent just standing off to the side, glancing over at Arclight until the boy drowned. However, even those competitions taught Kizu. Environment, positioning, adapting. It all gave Kizu several ideas. And since several of the biomes appeared multiple times throughout the day, he assumed there must be a finite amount of options available for the tournament to choose from. He actively formulated plots for several of the more interesting terrains as he watched his classmates flounder.

Still, he’d be lying if he said the duels of competent fighters like Finn weren’t far more interesting to dissect. Whoever organized the bracket did a marginally decent job of matching students against peers of their same level, so it was rare to see anyone utterly stomp their competition.

Kizu was considering the last fight’s biome, a frozen lake with ice fishing holes carved throughout it, when Arclight announced a familiar name.

Evie Alito.

His Kemon friend stepped up onto the stadium platform and faced her opponent, a bored looking human woman. It wasn’t someone Kizu recognized, but he knew Evie well enough to recognize her irritation as her quills bristled, adding several centimeters to her size. A rare expression that she usually reserved for herself when she failed at something in Combat F. Kizu had never seen it directed at another person before.

Clad only in the tournament uniform, Evie looked naked and exposed as she faced down her opponent. She lacked her usual heavy cloak she wore to conceal her quills. Strangely, she held something round in her hand. Kizu recognized it as a scrying orb, of all things. They were all permitted enchanted gear, so long there was proof that they’d made it themselves, or heavily assisted in its creation. That meant it wasn’t the powerful academy-issued orb they all had access to, but rather a basic one of her own making.

The biome shifted under their feet but both contestants remained unflappable, never even acknowledging the change.

Once Arclight announced the start of their fight, no one moved.

They stood on a castle wall, a drop to either side of them. Kizu could tell from how their uniforms rippled and Evie’s opponent’s hair whipped around that the wind was stronger than for those spectating the match.

Rather than approach, Evie lifted the scrying orb up and then closed her eyes. She simply stood there, unmoving on rampart.

The other girl checked over her shoulders, as if expecting something to strike from behind. Then she scoffed and strolled forward.

While she moved, Kizu reflected on what Arclight had said about their opponents being hand-picked as a means in which to motivate them all. Judging from Evie’s immediate reaction to her opponent, he assumed that meant the two girls had a shared history. He wondered how anyone could possibly not get along with Evie of all people. She was one of the most nonthreatening people he knew.

Then again, perhaps her quiet nature might attract undesirable people. Kizu reflected on his recent experience with Emilia and Ione. His ex had foolishly seen her as an easy target due to her lackadaisical attitude. A timid nature like Evie’s might very well paint a target on her back.

The enemy reached the porcupine girl and struck out with an enhanced open palm. Without even opening her eyes, Evie side-stepped the attack. Her opponent stumbled, over extended by her momentum. The Kemon girl’s other arm darted out, in a chop down on her opponent’s exposed neck. But, centimeters from contact, her hand froze. It quivered there for over a second, as if struggling against invisible strings.

Evie took an abrupt step back, barely dodging out of the way of an uppercut aimed for her chin.

Again, Evie’s fist struck out. And again, she hit an invisible barrier nearly at contact with her enemy’s gut.

This continued on a dozen more times. Evie, never opening her eyes, held her orb in one hand while she flawlessly dodged attacks then had her counters buffeted by some unseen force.

Kizu was starting to believe her opponent must be some sort of spatial mage. Normal force barriers weren’t completely invisible, but the girl might have extremely good control of space immediately in front of her. Like a constant layer of telekinesis actively preventing attacks from touching her. The other option was a barrier of condensed air. That seemed extremely difficult to pull off though with their constant movement across the castle rampart. Especially with external wind battering at them.

After Kizu tried to investigate with his spellsense and turned up with nothing special around Evie’s enemy, he came to another conclusion. Evie held herself back. In contrast to the lack of special residue around her opponent, Evie practically radiated unique magic Kizu had only ever seen during their spars in Combat F. He knew from experience that she was channeling her ancestors. From what he could tell, one of her ancestors had been a powerful diviner, hence the use of the scrying orb. That ancestor kept her safe through what could only be an extremely potent spell that let Evie predict moments into the future. A rare use of a temporal spell. A second ancestor pushed her to strike back, guiding her hands in flawless hand-to-hand combat maneuvers.

On several occasions, Kizu had listened to Evie voice her discomfort with violence. She struggled to even spar with him in class. Not because of lack of ability, but lack of will. Or rather, perhaps she had too much will, just in the opposite direction.

Kizu watched the fight with a heavy heart as the seconds ticked by. Evie only had so much blood to use on spells. If she continued to resist like this, she would collapse from blood loss long before her opponent. Unfortunately, there was nothing he could do for her. He simply watched.

Finally, one of the girl’s strikes passed through Evie’s seemingly impenetrable defense. A fist to her skull sent Evie staggering back. The scrying orb fell from her hands and landed on the stone floor with a crack. Blood trickled from her temple where the girl had struck her. Time seemed to slow to Kizu as he watched Evie open her eyes.

Rather than the Kemon girl’s chestnut eyes, black orbs were revealed from under her eyelids. Her opponent yelped and stepped back in fright.

“You dare strike my granddaughter,” a deep masculine hiss came from Evie’s lips. “She now rests. Unfortunate for you. I lack her kind heart. You will die now.”

A blur of movement followed the short proclamation. Kizu’s enhanced vision barely kept up with the fight. Evie’s opponent raised an arm to block, but Evie grabbed a hold of the arm with both hands. Bones snapped as she twisted the limb. Then, before the girl could even scream in pain, a hand clamped down on her throat. Blood dribbled down the girl’s neck from where the Kemon quills pierced her skin. Evie lifted the girl, fingers pressed hard into the skin. Then slammed her into the stone palisade beside her. Repeatedly.

The crowd went silent as the beating continued. Again and again, Evie smashed her opponent into the stone. It should have ended far sooner than it did, Kizu suspected that the ancestor in control held back from finishing her off as a means to revel in the violence. By the time Evie stepped back, her opponent’s face had taken the shape of a pile of cheese curds, several teeth puncturing her purple cheek. Kizu watched as she attempted to open an eye, only to loudly moan and flop to the side of the stone floor, her broken bones unable to support her weight. Quills were scattered throughout the girl’s body, adding another layer of damage and means of producing additional bloodstains across the stone rampart.

Evie stepped forward once again, lifted a foot, and brought down her heel on the girl’s skull. The crunch echoed through the silent stadium.

Even in the minute following the collapse of the biome and sudden rejuvenation of Evie’s opponent, only the quietest murmurs left the lips of those in the audience. Kizu couldn’t help but reflect that the whispered quiet that took hold of the stands in the battle’s wake reflected Evie’s natural state.
