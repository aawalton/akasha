‘Micky? Did your mind also get duplicated?’ Percy asked through the connection.

‘I don’t think so, though I did find myself suddenly connected to a couple of “you”s,’ the Huehuan replied. He didn’t ask for a full explanation, indicating that he had been paying at least some attention to Percy’s experiments. ‘Hang on. The other Percy is also talking to me.’

Percy nodded in understanding. His fusion with Micky had been caused entirely by the soul version of his bloodline. Despite their ability to synchronize their minds, their combined cognitive prowess was still technically spread between two bodies and brains, so it wasn’t too strange that the new ability hadn’t directly affected Micky.

‘At least he can act as a mediator between us, allowing us to communicate without having to flood our head with useless thoughts. I suppose that we could have achieved something similar by passing each other written notes, but this is less of a hassle.’

Well… they could also just take turns speaking out loud, though that would feel a bit weird. Things would also get quite chaotic if there were more than two of them. Percy hoped that he would be able to generate several mind clones, and that his capacity would keep growing with each promotion, meaning that he’d have to figure out a more reliable way to handle them all.

Nephthys might have some ideas, though Micky’s assistance would do in the meantime.

After some back and forth, they got the Huehuan to randomly pick a “leader” between them, who would be in control of their body. Meanwhile, the “clone” would resume working on their mind channels, since they still had plenty left to clear and temper.

Percy spent the next couple of hours working on unrelated tasks, mostly to make sure that his two minds were stable and hadn’t come with any unforeseen side effects. Having a second person moving mind mana inside his body was a little distracting, but not much crazier than everything else he’d done with his bloodline over the years.

Flying leisurely through the outermost tunnels of the hive, he examined the colossal mana-gathering formation being constructed. Its diameter wasn’t much larger than the one that he had personally drawn on the surface, though this one was three-dimensional and surrounded the underground kingdom from all sides. Each unit cell was relatively simple, but the runes were larger than houses and constructed out of whatever magical minerals existed in the nearby tunnels, so that the wasps helping the humans wouldn’t have to carry the heavy materials very far.

The array took the shape of a multicoloured, hemispherical shell that slightly resembled Percy’s Cradles. At least, it would look like that if one could somehow see the whole structure from afar, though that was impossible due to the layers of dirt and crystal obscuring it. Even with his Internal Sight allowing his sensory domain to penetrate physical obstacles more easily, Percy struggled to examine more than a neighbourhood-sized section of the formation from any given vantage point.

The array was about thirty percent wider than the hive. The royal wasps had asked Nesha and her subordinates to give them some leeway for further expansion, since they would hopefully have access to more ambient mana after the project was completed.

There were around five hundred trained enchanters from House Etna working on the array, along with seven hundred labourers without any specialized knowledge in the field. That was less manpower than Percy had made use of when creating the original formation on the surface, but he’d had to do all the actual runecrafting by himself. The fact that nearly half of Nesha’s subordinates knew what they were doing made all the difference.

‘Your clone says that you should stop lazing around, since your minds seem stable enough,’ Micky informed him at some point. ‘He thinks that you should spend some time testing the limits of your new ability.’

‘Fine, but we need to ensure that we can merge back into a single mind before doing anything else. I don’t want to end up with a dozen clones in my head and no way to get rid of them,’ Percy replied with a sigh as he flew back to his cavern. ‘Tell him to prepare himself for some discomfort. I’ll connect us again, and I’ll have to push further than before.’

Wasting no time, he flooded his brain with another wave of mind mana to serve as glue. The irritating chorus of stray thoughts returned with a vengeance, though it was easier to ignore when he knew it was coming.

He pulled the two layers of his mind closer as he kept adding mana, until they finally snapped back into one with a satisfying click, silencing the whispers once and for all. His counterpart’s memories from the past couple of hours merged into his own, though that wasn’t any different from how his soul clones worked.

‘Okay… despite their flaws, creating them, putting them to work, and reabsorbing them seems simple enough. Let’s see how many mind clones I can have at once.’

Repeating his previous actions, Percy created an extra mind with each activation of his bloodline. Part of him had expected the number of minds to double each time, though he was both relieved and disappointed that each attempt only yielded a single clone.

With every new Percy swirling within his skull, they took a short break to ensure that everyone understood their role and that their condition remained stable. As they had deduced earlier, those roles were completely arbitrary, because each mind was fully capable of acting as the original. A few quick tests proved that each of them could cast any of Percy’s spells, communicate with the soul clones through the cords, and even fuse their minds with Micky’s.

Unfortunately, when Percy tried to merge multiple clones with Micky, he inadvertently caused them to fuse with one another, essentially undoing his bloodline entirely and robbing him of the extra processing power. This was admittedly a faster and more efficient way to reabsorb his mind clones, but not a solution for cooperating with them properly.

When he tried creating his tenth concurrent clone – or eleventh mind overall if he included the “original” – he was finally met with some resistance. The magical claw inside his third core seemingly refused to jump out of the organ unless pushed.

Percy felt that he could force the bloodline to activate if he wanted, yet he could also tell that he wouldn’t like the consequences. It was an instinct that he had slowly developed after using the soul version of his bloodline countless times.

He wasn’t sure how his body would punish him for exceeding his capacity – it might be the same disorienting state he experienced after making too many soul clones, or something even more debilitating. Either way, he didn’t feel brave enough to try. Not after all the craziness that he had put himself through recently.

‘Nine it is, then,’ he concluded with a frown. ‘Just like my soul clones.’

Both versions of his bloodline appeared to scale similarly, though they didn’t necessarily have the same starting point. Percy would have only had four and a half soul cords if not for the Blessing of Permanence, and he didn’t think that the Blessing of Connection had the same effect on his mind clones. His new blessing might help him interact with his mind clones more easily when paired with the right mindset, but he hadn’t figured out how to create one yet.

‘This must be one of the main ways your strain of the bloodline differs from your grandfather’s,’ Micky pointed out.

Archibald’s life and soul clones tripled with each promotion or under the influence of his boosting art, though his capacity didn’t seem to be directly affected by the number of mana cores that he had. Combining the two types of clones had indirectly allowed him to grow his army, but that had more to do with the synergy between the two affinities.

Percy’s capacity grew linearly with each promotion. The growth was obviously much slower, though he couldn’t complain about the quality of his clones. Unlike his relatives, his boosting art hadn’t affected his limits until he had partially converted the spell into a proper promotion, yet each core appeared to contribute to all his capacities regardless of affinity.

‘I suppose that my relatives’ bloodline depends solely on their mana reserves. That’s why the Dance helps them while having an unrelated mana core doesn’t. For me, it seems to depend on something deeper – like the very quality of my existence… or something.’

Percy felt reasonably certain about that guess, though it didn’t really change much. As things stood, both capacities would grow by one whenever any of his cores advanced, which was all that mattered.

‘Let’s bring your findings to Nephthys,’ Micky suggested. ‘She’ll know what to do next.’

Percy nodded.

Before flying to his familiar’s cave, he made sure that each mind clone was busy clearing and tempering channels in a different body part. Doing anything more complicated would be difficult until he found a better way to interact with his counterparts, but he could still save himself several weeks of work like this.
