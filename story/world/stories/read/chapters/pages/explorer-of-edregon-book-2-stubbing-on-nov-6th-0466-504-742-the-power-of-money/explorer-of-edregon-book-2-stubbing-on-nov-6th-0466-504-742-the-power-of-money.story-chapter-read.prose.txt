Brian’s world’s method of trading spells between established mages was clinical, and an interesting change of pace from what Vin was used to. After the two of them finished showing off what they had available to trade, which turned into a small magical showcase that even drew a few of the locals over to watch, it was time to barter. In exchange for Vin giving up the runic formations for his tier-2 Aqua Sphere and Tremorsight, Brian gave him the formations for a tier-2 water spell called Current, and a tier-2 vibration spell called Tremor. Brian proved to be just as eager of a magic user as Vin was, because immediately upon trading formations, he invited Vin to join him in a small basement room within the large building to begin studying their new spells.

For the sake of establishing good relations with this new fragment and ensuring Terra looked good, Vin was more than happy to oblige. The two of them each took one side of the room, slipping into their respective Introspections and getting to work.

Current was a spell Vin was quite excited to get his hands on, both because it sounded like a lot of fun, and because he knew it would be incredibly helpful in the event he did have to chase after Lumel back into her home fragment. The spell was simple enough, but that didn’t make it any less useful. It created a powerful current surrounding the user so long as they were submerged in water, allowing them to swim much quicker than they’d be able to on their own. Knowing just how fast pulmons shot through the water, Vin was hopeful the spell would allow him to keep up with their incredible speeds. As he was now, even with his Swimming skill up at level 17, Lumel could easily swim circles around him and leave him in the watery dust if she so chose.

Tremor was a bit more interesting. True to his word, Brian didn’t have any sort of combat spells available to trade. Instead, he had a spell he’d designed to function in self defense. Tremor built off of Vibrate, but was much more focused and capable of being cast at range. The mage used it to cause weapons to start violently shaking within a warrior’s hand, making it far more difficult for someone to aim a ranged weapon or swing a sword properly. Of course, a high-enough strength attribute would make the spell little more of an annoyance than anything, but for the less impressive fighters, it was a fantastic tool.

Not to mention Vin had a few ideas around how it could potentially be upgraded down the line.

Seeing as he’d had the runic formations literally handed to him and wasn’t designing his own spells this time around, Vin blazed through the two spells, finishing them up one after another. He lost track of time a little bit while doing so, but the results were more than worth it.

New spell learned! Tier-2 Water spell (Current). 10,000 exp gained.

Level up! Adventurer of the Arcane lvl 56!

+3 Attribute points to spend

+1 Skill point to spend

New spell learned! Tier-2 Vibration spell (Tremor). 10,000 exp gained.

Not only did he manage to master two new tier-2 spells, to Vin’s shock, he even received experience for each one as well. After hitting level 40 and becoming an Adventurer of the Arcane, it had seemed as though gaining experience for spells was only something he achieved if he created his own spells, as he’d stopped receiving any for just learning spells that were handed to him. Though based on this latest discovery, he could only assume so long as he bartered for his spells with appropriate magic of his own, or rightfully earned them somehow, the System chose to award him with appropriate experience. He had a feeling this wasn’t some sort of system he could cheat, but either way, he happily took the unexpected boost of experience.

Rather than split his attribute points from his most recent level up like he often did, Vin tossed all three of them into focus, eager to get at least that one to 100 in order to see just what new passive the System would end up offering him. That shiny passive point had been sitting in his interface for a while now, and while he agreed with Lumel’s suggestion, it was pretty hard not to go and spend it on something.

The fact that 10,000 exp isn’t even a fifth of what I need in order to go up a level now is crazy… I miss the days of leveling after only a few thousand!

When Vin emerged from his Introspection, Brian was nowhere to be found, but a note had been left for him on a nearby table with directions to a tavern that would probably let him stay for free for the night, provided he did a little manual labor in exchange. Realizing that was their best bet in the event Alka failed to finish off the monster she’d set out to hunt, Vin took the note with him as he made his way upstairs.

Only to find the large common room in the midst of what looked like the party of the century.

Vin stared at the dense crowd in shock, his eyes sweeping over the gathering of warriors and common folk who looked like they were trying their damndest to drink themselves into a blind stupor as they danced and downed tankard after tankard of ale. Bards were playing music off on a small stage and people were dancing in the center of the room, and a number of too-drunk warriors were attempting to display their skills for all to see. Vin watched as two muscular women tried to arm wrestle, each one so evenly matched that the wooden table splintered and gave out beneath them, sending them both careening into a man attempting to balance on top of a barrel of ale, who somehow managed to leap over them, before rolling the barrel away as others chased after him with empty tankards.

I guess the room downstairs must have been soundproof… Which makes sense if it’s where mages go to work on learning new spells. Can’t have any annoying explosions worrying everyone.

“Vin!” a familiar voice called out, and Vin turned to find Alka waving at him. The Slayer was sitting in a chair large enough to be considered a throne at the front of the room, watching over the rambunctious crowd with her eyes glowing bright. He could easily read the amusement in the way her eyes glowed and shifted, and he carefully made his way across the merriment until he was standing before her.

“Alka, what is going on?” he called out, having to raise his voice a bit to be heard over the party. “And why do you look like you’re sitting in a chair of honor?”

“Remember that monster I set out to kill?” she asked, laughing as he nodded his head. “So, turns out that thing has been killing people for months! It’s a hidden elite monster that roamed down here from a different fragment a while back. Some sort of giant spider with the power to attack minds directly and paralyze people with little more than a glance from one of its many eyes. Everyone who went out to try to kill it ended up dead, so the town decided to just cordon off those few miles of land. I waltzed right into the forest and stabbed the thing, and after bringing back its corpse… well, this happened.”

Alka raised her arms, gesturing to the dancing and merriment filling the room. Vin could only laugh at the unexpected turn of events, grinning down at his companion.

“Sounds like you had a productive afternoon! How was the spider?”

“Surprisingly challenging, even with me being immune to its mental attack,” Alka admitted. “It was big, fast, and keeping track of so many legs wasn’t easy. I’m actually thinking about heading into the fragment it came from at some point for a little extra practice. Oh, here. You should hold onto this.”

Alka tossed him a small pouch, and Vin’s eyes widened when he glanced inside and spotted what looked like a few hundred small gemstone chips. He didn’t have the faintest idea what any of them were worth or how currency on this fragment worked, but he had a feeling Alka may have just made a hefty sum for herself. The moment he realized that, he was struck by an idea.

“Do you have any plans for this? Or would you mind if I spent it?” Vin asked, feeling a little bad about just taking Alka’s hard-earned coin like that.

“I didn’t throw it to you just to carry it around for me,” Alka snorted, waving a hand dismissively. “I don’t sleep, I don’t eat, and my gear will never really need to be replaced. What good does money do me? I was originally just going to see if I couldn’t pour enough booze down these people’s throats to make them all pass out in one giant pile, but I figured you’ve probably got a better idea of how to spend it.”

“Thanks, Alka, I appreciate it!” Vin said, smiling as he shut the pouch and tucked it safely into his pocket. “I’ve got an idea, but there’s no telling if it will work.”

“In that case just bring the pouch back to me and I’ll go back to trying to make that drunken pile!” she said, cheering as two warriors stripped off their shirts and began wrestling one another in the center of the dance floor.

Making his way along the edge of the party, Vin headed straight for a woman in a red robe he’d spotted the moment he’d come upstairs. Unlike the barely controlled partygoers drinking and dancing the night away, she had remained off to the side of the room, leaning against the wall as she nursed a single drink. Her eyes were sharp and seemed to take in everything, and more importantly, she had a gemstone-tipped staff leaning against the wall beside her similar to Brian’s. When Vin made his way over to her, she grunted, not even looking at him as she spoke.

“I won’t be teaching you any of my magic, wanderer, so don’t bother asking.”

“You spoke with Brian?” Vin asked, surprised she already knew about him somehow.

“News of the little magical display the two of you put together earlier today got around,” she explained, sipping from her drink as she continued watching the crowd. Following her eyes, only now did Vin spot the younger woman wearing a similar red robe, happily drinking and dancing with the others. “My apprentice told me all about the wandering mage giving out spells. Even wanted to try trading with you.”

“And that’s a bad thing?”

“It’s a dangerous thing,” the woman said, putting down her drink as she turned her hard gaze onto him. “Unlike Brian, our spells aren’t intended to be helpful or solve problems. They are designed to kill. When we put together a runic formation, something dies. It is the reason we refuse to teach anyone beyond our own apprentices, and them only once we are certain they are prepared for the responsibilities of being a Battle Mage. So I say again. I don’t care what it is you’re offering me, you won’t be getting any of my spells. Not unless you want to spend a year apprenticing under me, and that position is already full at the moment.”

As disappointing as that was to hear, Vin couldn’t really fault the woman for her caution. If anything, he was happy to hear that the Battle Mages of this fragment were so wary when it came to who they were willing to teach their magic to. He was briefly tempted to try and explain to the woman that he understood that better than anyone, what with his divine boon literally preventing him from launching the first attack against someone. But one look at her hard face was all it took for him to understand nothing he said would matter.

Nothing he could say, maybe… but he knew there were other methods of talking that were more impactful than others.

“I understand,” Vin said, getting a curt nod of appreciation from the woman before she went back to sipping at her drink. “In that case, would you happen to have any ideas as to how I could use a pouch filled with these gemstone chip things to learn new magic? Otherwise I guess I’ll just give it to Brian and ask him to show me another one of his runic formations.”

The Battle Mage’s eyes widened slightly as Vin revealed the pouch Alka had given him, but she controlled herself quickly enough. Clearing her throat, she glanced around the room, making sure nobody was watching them before she looked at him.

“Even for a hefty sum such as that, I will not compromise my principles and teach you battle magic. However, I may have a spell or two I can show you after all. Ones I picked up over the years that I have only recently managed to relearn after restarting from level 1. They are focused on defense, so I’d feel comfortable enough teaching them to you. Would that be acceptable?”

“I think I can live with that,” Vin grinned, tossing the woman the pouch as he motioned for her to follow him. “There’s an empty room downstairs we can use. I doubt your apprentice can get up to too much mischief before you get back!”
