Chapter 199 / B3-44: Having a Ball

After leaving Mojo with Mahq and his grandparents, Joe once again found himself ascending Park Road to the High Park district, though this time he was surrounded by many more Forters with him on the roadway.  Carriages trundled up the middle of the lane while those on foot climbed sidewalks.  Something Joe hadn’t noticed the first time was that the outer walkway was for those taking their time.  This way, they could gaze out over the city or stop and relax on one of the benches.  Those with the ability to march straight up the taxing slope used the inner sidewalk.  Which is what Joe, Kenda, and Wen did.

Joe learned that Wen’s much-quoted father was someone of importance in the elven court of Hart’s Hold, and so she had received an invite to the Jarlscarp ball.  It turned out she was invited to most of the noble galas.  Many of which she attended for political reasons.  With a twinkle in her stunning emerald eyes, the elven archer made it clear she was going to a dwarven ball she normally wouldn’t because she was dying to see how Joe would fare in polite society.

Thanks to Wen’s acceleration aura, Joe’s stamina boosts, and the general fitness of the three adventurers, the trio easily outpaced most of those walking the inner path.  The only exceptions were a few messengers or porters bearing stylish packages.  These speed-based runners slipped past everyone with a grace and speed that would have challenged Hah’roo.

When they finally reached their destination, House Jarlscarp was not at all what Joe expected.  It was located at the far north-eastern edge of High Park, situated right up against the steepest part of the cliff that elevated High Park from the rest of Fort Coral.  The dwarven house on the surface did not look like much from the road, just a low single floor that resembled more of a bunker than it did the surrounding mansions.  That illusion was broken as one approached the noble hall.    Instead of lawns and gardens, the land around the residency sloped downward, exposing a majestic manor carved from the natural rock.  Only that low-cap of a top floor one saw from a distance was fabricated; everything else was etched out of the stone foundation on which the city sat.  Heavy columns and awe-inspiring statues had been hewn around the structure.  Joe could count three floors at the lowest portion of the walls, but there were sections of worked stone that ran from the middle walls of the manor into the side-slopes, suggesting the dwarves had a network of underground rooms surrounding the main hall.

The road down to the massive front doors switched back and forth three times.  The pathway was lit by large bonfires in massive metal braziers.  They joined the line of those waiting to be admitted and announced.  Through the huge open doorway, Joe could hear the pulse of drums and laughter. By the time the three of them reached the great doors, the air itself seemed alive, thick with the scent of roasted meat, rich ale, and the ringing echo of dwarven celebration.

At the threshold, a jovial dwarf with a braided beard and a vest adorned with polished copper buttons took their invitations.  Joe identified him as Bomeam Jarlscarp. The cheerful greeter sorted them, placing Wen first, followed by Kenda and Joe paired together. In a booming voice, Bomeam announced, “Her ladyship, Tywysoges Earcellwen Rhuival of Hart’s Holt.” While his thunderous voice must have been able to be heard on the far side of the massive hall, somehow, it was not overwhelming to those standing beside the living bearded megaphone.

Joe had no idea what the ‘Tywysoges’ title denoted, but it must be something important.  Every elf in the room turned to watch Wen enter the massive hall.  While Illuminaria did not have the Tolkien-esque friction between dwarves and elves, it seemed as though the percentage of the graceful folk was lower than typically seen around the city.  Joe wondered if it was just a difference of taste.  The hall was built with a heavy architecture, far different from the elegant, fey-like style of most elven homes.  Elven structures seemed to reach upwards, like trees lifting toward the sky.  The hall of House Jarlscarp hunkered down, feeling more akin to a chef or smith, working over the flames.

Then it was his turn.  “Liberator of the Lost General, Margen the Mighty.  Joe Morris, Healer of the Guild, and his guest, Kendell Bracey, lieutenant of the Adventurers Guild,” boomed Bomeam.  Joe focused on his [Undaunted] trait, squashing any chance of his nerves getting the best of him.  Cool as a cucumber, he and Kendell proceeded into the amazing room.

The inside of the hall stretched ahead, long and wide like a Viking longhouse.  It was even more elaborately carved inside than it was outside.  The way that this chamber had been hewed both from within and without, etched out of the stony bones of the cliffs of Fort Coral, boggled Joe’s mind.  Amidst the polished stonework, massive beams of honey-hued timber accented the chamber, giving it a warmth that the stone alone would have failed to do; at least for the non-dwarves in the room.

Looking with [Witchsight], Joe found countless formations of runes etched everywhere, shimmering with metallic-hued auras.

Long tables lined the space, already crowded with dwarves pounding mugs and shouting to one another. A large open area was filled with dancers who spun in tight, rhythmic circles; their boots hammering the floor in time with drums and pipes.

“Wow,” Joe breathed.  The great hall throbbed with voices raised in cheer, stamping dancers, and enough savory scents to drive the wildness crazy.

Kendell grinned beside him, her arm looped through his.  “I told you House Jarlscarp balls are amazing.  This is going to be fun,” she beamed.

The gown she was wearing was a silky wonder of flowing green lines.  Unlike the dress she wore on stage, this one made no pretense of martial prowess.  The upper half of the emerald gown looped around her neck with tightly pleated panels, leaving her back bare.  After hugging her slim waist, the train flared outward; small folds gave the sheer cloth texture and elegance.

To top it all off, Kenda's hair was woven into elaborate French braids. Two bands of her auburn hair circled her head like a crown, folding together into a woven bun that highlighted her shapely neck.  While the gown was stunning, Joe found his eye drawn to her braids and neck every time he looked in her direction.

The three adventurers were immediately thronged by a pack of stout folk demanding a telling of Glen Cuhma.   Kenda had warned Joe to expect this, and so together, with Wen, they had worked out how the telling would go.  Earcellwen started with the first quest, where she and Joe discovered the restless dead and the dark valley.  Kenda took over describing the guild’s preparation and entering the ziggurat.  As a group, the three together spoke of being overrun by the wights; the roundelay method gave the telling a frantic sense.

Finally, Joe finished the tale of his trek alone through the dark until he found the great man trapped by his connection to the trapped blade and the dire curse eating away at him. Joe hated having to exclude Yuk from the story; without his friend, the tale felt hollow and false.  It had been Yuk who kept Joe sane in the dreadful labyrinth under the Erlkings pyramid. Yet the raggedy man was very clear that their name needed to be left out of all the retellings.  Only Joe and two others knew Yuk’s secret, and they were adamant that the fewer people aware of it meant that their connection to the primordial demon, Khimbar, was less likely to be uncovered.

Describing the final battle was heavily abridged.  Joe had no intention of mentioning how he had fragmented his pattern and ceded control over to his wild side.  Instead, he kept the focus on Taylyn's bravery and unparalleled power.  He tried to downplay his part as just the messenger, but his bearded and braided audience was having none of his modesty.

This tale was not just iterated once, but several times throughout the night.  After the first pass, Joe gave up trying to soften his part in the story.  These rambunctious revelers wanted heroics, not humble restraint.  The more Joe downplayed his part, the more clarifications they asked for.  It was actually easier to rev up the saga’s telling. If Joe barreled ahead with full heroics, the party-goers hung on his words, shushing anyone attempting to interrupt.

Kendell had been right about House Jarlscarp’s conduct.  There was not a single person there who turned up their nose at the common man in their midst.  Though Joe was a bit surprised at how physical the noble dwarves were in their acceptance of him.  The crowd of Jarlscarps welcomed him with bruising claps on the back and jostling shoulder squeezes.

And ale.  Oh, so much ale.

Joe never saw the bottom of a mug.  By some miraculous beer-sense, his hosts seemed to know every time his tankard dipped below a third full, before it was swapped out by a fresh pour and another toast.  If it hadn’t been for [Dispel Noxa], Joe would have succumbed to alcohol poisoning halfway through the gala.  It turned out the bigger danger was preventing his bladder from exploding.  Thankfully, his passive constant healing managed to stay ahead of the massive influx of frothing brews.

Much to the wildness’s chagrin, this meant that Joe dined very sparingly, even though there were numerous tables laden with vast feasts distributed around the great hall.   The smell of grilled meats filled the room: spiced lamb, honey-glazed boar, drumsticks of some huge avian creature, and many more smoky and enticing aromas.

After recounting the saga of the Vale of Lament for the fifth time, the music shifted. The drums grew louder, faster, joined by sharp, bright notes from something trumpet-like and the deep, lower, humming of large horns. The dancers in the center changed formation, forming lines and circles.

Kendell’s eyes lit up. “Oh, we’re doing this.”

Joe looked up. “Doing what?”

She stood, grabbing his hand. “Dancing.”

“I don’t … Kendell, I don’t know any dwarven dances …”

“You’ve got feet, don’t you? That’s enough.”

He had no time to protest before she pulled him into the crowd.

Joe didn’t dance, but it was not for the reason most non-dancers avoided the dancefloor.  He was not at all self-conscious about looking ridiculous.  During rock concerts, he was on his feet, jumping around like everyone else.  That was easy and fun.

His problem was that he had an absolutely abysmal sense of rhythm.  In any formal form of dancing, Joe was always a beat off.  No matter how hard he tried to follow the same timing as everyone around him, for some reason, he was always a second too fast or too slow.  He went through three piano teachers until his parents gave up, thinking Joe was being difficult on purpose.  One time, he agreed to join a dragonboat charity event, and thanks to his constantly mistimed strokes, the only reason his boat didn’t come in last was that one boat of frat boys was so drunk they capsized.

So when Kenda hauled him towards the dwarves lining up into what was obviously going to be a communal dance, Joe broke into a sweat.  This was going to be a disaster.  He tried to follow Kendell’s lead as the dancers formed a circle. The steps were quick and precise, heavy stomps, quarter turns, sharp claps. She picked them up effortlessly, her athletic instincts taking over.

Joe did not.

At least not until he was smart enough to employ one of his favorite skills.  For the millionth time, Joe sent a mental thank you to the young cleric of Onhur while activating the focusing skill Mazsy had shared with him.

There it was.  The beat.  As clear as day.

[Crystal Mind] sharpened everything, and Joe felt himself lock into the rhythm.  He no longer felt like a trailer swishing around behind the car that was pulling it.  He was now part of the car. He went from constantly missing steps to another component of the well-oiled pattern of wheeling dancers.

Together they spun, clapped, stepped, and stamped.  The lines swooshed away from each other to the edges of the dancefloor and then rushed inward, slotting through each other in boisterous patterns.  Kenda whirled away and then back into his arms. Even when his hand rested on her warm, bare back, he didn’t lose focus … much.

His eyes met hers, and something changed.  Even though the rhythm surged again, drawing them apart, he felt a tether inside himself, connecting the two of them together.  When she spun back to him, the space between them felt tighter, more intimate.  In the midst of the rowdy, shouting dwarven chain dance, somehow Joe knew he was dancing just with her.

When the procession finally broke apart, they stepped out of the circle together, both laughing and flushed.

“Okay,” Joe said, catching his breath. “That was actually fun.”

“Told you.” Kendell bumped his shoulder with hers. “You did quite well there, Mister Morris.”

Joe kissed her.

Around them, the hall roared on: dwarves singing, mugs slamming, the celebration in full swing.

“Hey,” he said, a little quieter. “After this… maybe we could …”

“Yes,” Kenda affirmed, her eyes locked with his.

“I didn’t even finish the question.”

“You didn’t have to.” Her smile was a thing of wonder to Joe. “I was just waiting for you to ask,” she uttered.

They stayed a while longer as the drums and horns called them back out onto the floor, each one savoring the growing warmth building up between them.  Even though the pair felt like this inner glow was a secret just between the two of them, it was glaringly obvious to the rest of the members of the merry gala as well.  Unseen by Kenda and Joe, the dwarves around them all exchanged knowing, happy grins, and the demand for the tale of Glen Cumha was shelved away to let the young lovers have this moment together.
