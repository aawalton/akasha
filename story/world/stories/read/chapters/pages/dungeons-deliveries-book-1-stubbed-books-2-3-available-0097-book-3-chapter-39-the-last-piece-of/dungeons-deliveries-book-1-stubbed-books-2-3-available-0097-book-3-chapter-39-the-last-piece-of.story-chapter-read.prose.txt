Zippy partitioned the discussion at the Safety Spires to his background processes. He couldn’t sever it completely, connected to his brother with [Better Together] as he was. Nor did he want to. It was an important meeting. But he had a critical mission of his own that required his full attention, and not every process in his mechanical heart could be relegated.

He attempted to control his sadness as he coasted high in the atmosphere and scanned around the Blightwood Dungeon. With day reaching its peak, the Blightwood monsters should be less active. That was what the Paladrones had gathered in their data collection. Conditions had changed, however, and there seemed to be no rest waiting for the monsters below.

Hundreds of the things relentlessly hacked and tore around the Dungeon entrance. Cold satisfaction filled Zippy, watching them struggle against the green thicket. The monsters slowly beat it back, sure to overwhelm it in the end, but Petal had not gone quietly. The Paladrones had borne witness to that.

The three of them had struggled to not aide his solo charge, watching from the sky instead. Petal had made the Paladrones swear multiple times prior, and they had reluctantly kept the promise. Their part in the breach, planting the Soil Mind behind the Dungeon, had been simple. Aria leapt off, planted it, and leapt back for a getaway. The monsters had been distracted, forced to coordinate into a massive horde in the end as Petal felled his way closer and closer to the entrance, unrelenting and seemingly unstoppable.

Emotions, the trio of drones had long ago realized, were mechanically different than their rational minds. Beepy and Aria frequently debated what they were. Were the Paladrones little more than extremely advanced code with access to the System and essentially mimicking life? Where exactly was the intersect of emotions they couldn’t splice out? If their minds could calculate like machines, how come they felt internal turmoil, pleasure, happiness, anxiety, self-consciousness, and every other thing related to chemicals their fabricated forms did not possess?

Zippy refused to participate in the debates with his siblings. He existed and he felt. Wasn’t that enough? Yes, it came with the painful cost of emotions. An example of that was how horribly nervous he was about his current mission, and Petal’s sacrifice was like a hole in his metaphorical heart, but distraction was non optimal in the face of precision calculations.

He also surprisingly felt profound pride and giddiness over his gnomish friend’s departure. The gnome had shown neither fear nor respite, leaving devastation and growth in his wake. When Petal finally fell, he’d erupted a last bellow, exploded his infused body in all directions, including a soaring bright glowing core back towards the gnomes that followed, and once the melodic wind had stilled, the ground stirred in front of the entrance of the Blightwood Dungeon for a parting gift. A brilliantly green patch of growth that refused to be weeded out.

The Beast of Blightwood Dungeon monstrosities stamped and ripped the plants. They’d been at it for two days, and it just kept growing back. Petal’s death garden pricked their bodies and tore limbs free, but bit by bit, the monsters made progress. They paid for every inch, slowly exhausting from the effort required.

There were so many new conditions to consider. When information was predictable, planning paths and surmising outcomes came naturally to the drones. That was just raw statistics and possible decision paths. When things went awry, however, game theory got infinitely more complex. What should happen didn’t, or there were too many factors to measure. During those long, long debates between Aria and Beepy, this was where Zippy would chime in to remind them of something they could all agree on.

Prepared rational outcomes be damned in the face of what felt right.

With cameras for eyes, all three had seen the same thing when Petal exploded out, and an opportunity had arisen the Paladrones could not pass up. The plan had to be loose, which added risk. There were also other current events where their collective participation was required, leaving few options. They had to split up and act separately this time, and the skills required deemed Zippy the man for the job.

He was a bundle of nerves as the rational part of his mind noted the opportunity approaching. The monsters were tired, moving slower, and the garden Petal left behind did not have much fight left. If they spotted what the Paladrones had, his mission would be unsuccessful. With the sun blaring directly above, he hid his small frame amongst the light to descend upon the forest below.

It was still there, held stubbornly by a root. Zippy curled both hands tightly around a pair of [Ancient Paring Knives] and prepared his strike.

Through [Better Together], he felt Beepy carefully poke their connection. Just enough to notify without being distracting. The message was packeted with raw emotion and words.

We believe in you, brother, it communicated, coated in reassurance and confidence in his ability.

And his siblings debated whether they mimicked life. Zippy found that ridiculous. Their friend was dead, and while the gnomes would receive his transformed core, the Paladrones would do something simply because they felt it was right. Zippy would collect the last piece of porcelain from Petal Gravewhistle.

Even with the sun hiding him, he’d descended enough to put him in possible sight of the monsters. The speck of Petal the drones spotted was there. This was the chance where calculations didn’t work. He had to take faith in himself.

Zippy shut off his hovering and dove headfirst into the center of Petal’s death garden. He fell fast through the air, and the monsters realized he was upon them too late. None had been watching the skies.

He fired his thrusters at the last possible moment, whipping his body upright and landing hard on one knee. Before they could react, Zippy was already rushing to get what he’d come for.

The tiny fragment of porcelain came free easily. Barely the size of a seed, it was still pale white and stood out amongst the green and mud. The monsters finally reacted, rushing in to attack him. It didn’t take a genius to figure out that would happen once he landed, and Zippy was well prepared for his escape.

He activated [Sacrificial Strike], controlling the skill with his will. One of the [Ancient Paring Knives] in his hands disappeared from existence as a horizontal portal split reality.

Two monsters were spliced in their chests as the portal ripped open. Their legs faltered and fell to the ground and gravity pulled their top halves into the portal’s surface forever. Clawed hands reached for Zippy’s legs, but he pushed his thrusters hard and headed straight for his escape.

Before they knew it, Zippy was gone, shooting into the unspace with porcelain in hand. He snapped off the portal behind him. Closing it decapitated another three Blightwood monsters. Their heads would bob along the unspace currents for the rest of time.

Beepy cheered through the connection. Even there, in the unspace, Zippy was never truly alone, He quite liked it that way. The mission wasn’t over, however, and his discipline almost lapsed, but he prevented himself from reaching back to embrace his brother through their link.

His stomach hatch popped open as he twirled through the place that made no sense. Once the last piece of Petal’s porcelain was secured and his hatch was closed, he coasted along a wave of fuzz, then gave a thumbs up to a pair of strange looking things that appeared to be scooping vibrations.

Zippy was proud of himself. Elated beyond belief. He wanted to scream out and cheer his accomplishment. Discipline be damned in the face of triumph!

He twirled another [Ancient Paring Knife], preparing a second cast of [Sacrificial Slice]. The go-to location was Paladrone HQ. The last piece of Petal was secure in his belly, but homebase was safer.

Beepy pounded on their link, cheering and dancing mentally like a maniac. Zippy activated [Sacrificial Strike] to open the portal back home, and due to how quick the drone’s minds worked, in the next moment he reached through the connection to celebrate with his brother.

The System destroyed the [Ancient Paring Knife], chugged along to the next moment, and opened the portal in the belly of itself to that Zippy’s home reality. In the same moment, two brothers reached to celebrate with each other through a thin tendril that crossed through time and space. It sent a small shockwave through the System’s machinations, and in the next part of its existence, as the acting pair of drones was some of the System’s Favoured, the force of their mental high-five was multiplied, shaking against every plane they had touched until then.

In that space between moments, the System assessed many things across many threads. Some of those things was the effect of a particular being who coincidentally was a drone. He went by Zippy and had the unique ability to sacrifice very powerful weapons in exchange for portals. By pure circumstance, another drone in that Zippy’s reality, Aria, had the fortunate skillset to copy things three times. She had only gained that by accident, following a series of outcomes that no other reality had followed thus far.

The Paladrones had wondered where the [Ancient Paring Knives] came from. They’d even debated it fiercely. Their minds could not rationally comprehend how something could be created from nothing. Did the System inject matter and purpose into things from the outside? That would mean they were in a bubble of sorts. Were there limits to what it could inject in, if that was the case?

The drones were close. Many like them, who were similar but not quite copies of Zippy, Aria, and Beepy, had also tried to understand why their own [Ancient Paring Knife] disappeared suddenly one day. The weapon was powerful across planes, passed along in each for centuries until one day, a very motivated man acquired it. That man had fed it the lives of thousands all in the name of love every time, his goal succeeding or not.

The [Ancient Paring Knives] had to come from somewhere, and when that particular Aria copied that particular knife, like all things in the System, a tendril was left behind for it to reference in another future moment. The System would move it to another plane for use. It did not create often, though it certainly could. New matter was expensive, and very few being across its planes had sacrificial skills.

The magic it provided was based entirely on this transferring of things. It could be wind, fire, gravity, emotions, ice cream, underwear, tuna, hair, air, ice, speed, thought, life, and even death. And sometimes, things happen, like a trio of drones gaining the ability to pull extremely powerful weapons from other planes. Other times, like in this moment, a powerful high-five rippled across the reference markers at the same time as a portal appeared by their will.

That Zippy tore through the portal back to his plane to secure the last piece of his friend. Meanwhile, across the unspace, from every plane where the Paladrones had taken an [Ancient Paring Knife], a portal opened in front of those plane’s Paladrones HQ. Many such drones were too curious, and hundreds of trios came to agreement, often after intense debates. Those other Zippys, Arias, and Beepys took a leap of faith and jumped into the unspace.
