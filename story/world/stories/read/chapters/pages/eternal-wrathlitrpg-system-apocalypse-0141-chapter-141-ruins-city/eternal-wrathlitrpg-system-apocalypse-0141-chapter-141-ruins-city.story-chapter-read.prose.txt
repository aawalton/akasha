James raced toward the moon for hours—through a forest, through a plain, then over a mountain. After almost 30 hours of galloping with his horse, he found a small shrine with a treasure chest inside.

[Congratulations! You have found a Sorcerous Blade.]

[Sorcerous Blade (Wind Edition):Crafted by a mage of eternal frost for mages. The blade shimmered with wind spirit, augmenting its cutting power. By sacrificing mana, users can use the Windblade skill, attacking an enemy up to 30 meters away.]

“Oh! This is a nice sword. Moreover, it does not convert attacks into elemental attacks unless you pour mana into it,” James muttered.

The joy lasted for only a moment. After turning the shrine upside down, James found nothing of value anymore. He frowned and continued on his journey.

“This area was too large,” James muttered. Just as he was about to give up, he found a ruin.

Its wall stood high, about the same height as Goliath and Maruk’s town. The most prominent feature was the huge tree in its center that stood even taller, as if it reached the sky above. The tree reminded James of the Star Tower in the previous dungeon. It was even larger than the tower. James bet his gaming knowledge that he could go inside the tree and climb it from within.

The city was teeming with life. Not sapient lifeforms, of course, but monsters. A hungry bipedal raptor lunged at James, only to be cut down by his black sword, awarding him 300 biomass. A dozen pairs of eyes glimmered from inside the houses. All eyes were on the new intruder in the city. James felt bloodlust pricking at his nape.

“You’ve come to play, huh?” James snickered. He dismounted, putting all but his new Sorcerous Blade onto his raptor. “Time to test my new sword on you all.”

The enemies crawled out of the houses, eyeing James as if they were looking at dinner. These monsters were varied—raptors, orcs, zombies, ogres, and even bulls and beetles were thrown into the mix.

The tension was about to snap.

Until it wasn’t. A loud buzzing noise took all the monsters’ attention away from James. As if on cue, they ran as if their lives depended on it, returning to the holes where they came from. Some took their chances barricading their houses; other animals cried out, ramming the doors open. James looked at the bizarre situation, confused.

Then a black cloud puffed out from the giant tree, buzzing toward every lifeform it encountered. It shed its black bark, revealing the solid golden fresh color inside, forming millions of horrific creatures that James was unfortunately familiar with.

“Ice Wall!” James felt a shudder when the encroaching cockroaches engulfed one of the monsters. The creature looked like it disintegrated—its blood, meat, and even bones eaten before it even dropped to the ground. Nothing was left.

“God! Why does it have to be cockroaches?” James felt goosebumps when the swarm engulfed his ice dome. He could feel them chipping the ice bit by bit, trying to reach the meat inside.

Fortunately, these cockroaches were not immune to ice magic. James poured more mana into his ice dome, freezing any critter that dared touch it. An hour later, James peeled off the outer layer of his ice dome, shedding the frozen cockroaches. Surprisingly, killing those cockroaches netted him a level-up and over 2000 biomass.

[Congratulations! You have leveled up to Level 64. Your Attacker class has leveled up to Level 47. You have obtained 2000 biomass.]

“That’s enough food to last me a month,” James muttered. He tried not to think about where the biomass came from. He didn’t eat it—the Chimera skill did and offered him a chunk of beef meat. That’s how James tried to fool himself. Far away, he saw the black cloud devouring every lifeform. The verdant city had now become a wasteland.

James then inspected the nearest house, still wary of any encroaching bugs. The houses were barren, without any furniture. At first, James wondered whether the system had rendered it wrong, but he then realized any organic material such as wood or clothes would have been eaten by those insects. So only stone or iron materials were left.

“A chest!” James skipped toward the prize that lay before him. He might have been left out of quests, but chests were fair game. His heart brimmed with joy when he realized this was his raison d’être. He never wanted to work for other people anyway. He was always alone, working only for himself. Why did he even try to integrate himself into society to begin with? James felt so stupid for obeying Andrew and the others.

Then, the chest exploded right into his face. James was thrown out of the house, suffering a serious burn on his head. Luckily, his armor mitigated the damage. If not for his high stats, he would have suffered a lot more than just his pride.

[Poison resisted. Would you like to spend 1000 biomass to recover your face and eyes?]

“No… let’s use a Health Potion instead,” James groaned. It took him a few moments to register what had happened to him.

[You have bought a health potion. 100 coins have been subtracted.]

A few minutes after he poured the pink liquid onto his face, the skin regenerated. James gasped for breath as he scanned his surroundings. No monster in sight. Either they had been consumed by those cockroaches, or they were hiding inside.

“That was so stupid. How could I forget that there might be traps inside a treasure chest!” James hadn’t encountered a chest in the last dungeon, so he had forgotten that there might be a trap. Thankfully, no one was around to see his pathetic look. James shuddered when he remembered this was a Hard-difficulty dungeon. The dungeon probably didn’t think James would get caught by a trap this simple, so it didn’t put any effort into its damage. James would have died if the blast had been stronger. It even had poison as well.

James then re-entered the house and found a burnt scroll and 100 coins—the exact amount he had spent to purchase a Health Potion. James groaned at the apparent insult.

The scroll was useless now. He had lost several treasure chest items because the traps had destroyed them as well. This realization made him wonder if there were someone who could disarm traps fromother survivors. His gamer instinct told him those treasures were precious—strong swords or scrolls.

With a lot more caution, James explored the other houses. One house’s door was blocked by a stone table. This would have worked against the cockroaches, but unfortunately, James was stronger than mere stone tables. He kicked it aside only to find a three-headed chimera lunging at him.

“Wind Blade!” James swung his sword, augmented with a sword skill, Hurricane, the slash bisected the approaching chimera and leaving a gaping slash on the wall behind it.

“Hm… I need to test this skill on more monsters. Preferably not inside houses.” James inspected his new sword.

This house also had a treasure chest. James, ever cautious, poked the chest with an ice spear from afar. Then, as the chest could not be moved, he opened it with the tip of his sword.

“Fuck! Why do no traps spring up when I am cautious!” James howled. Of course, here in the middle of nowhere, days away from his friends, no one heard him. Not even animals cared. The iron spear that was left inside the chest seemed to giggle at his ritual.

James spent another five days inspecting the houses one by one. This city was a treasure trove. There was at least one chest in every house he searched. Monsters were rarer, but he got a nice amount of biomass on top of that.

[Congratulations. You have obtained:Rusted ShieldRusted SwordRusted SpearRusted AxeRusted HelmetWithered Bow1000 coinsSkill Scroll (Spear)Magic Scroll (Blizzard)10 Gold Bars.]

“Yes! I finally found a shield!” James pumped his fist. But he tilted his head. “But what’s with these rusted weapons?”

James tried swinging the sword and axe he got; he could activate his skills just fine. He wondered whether these weapons needed some special attention, but the system went silent on him. Even after inspecting the goods, they just showed a basic description.

[Rusted Spear:A rusted spear found in a ruin. Repair it to gain its full strength.]

[Rusted Shield:A rusted shield found in a ruin. Repair it to gain its full strength.]

[Rusted Sword:A rusted sword found in a ruin. Repair it to gain its full strength.]

[Rusted Axe:A rusted axe found in a ruin. Repair it to gain its full strength.]

[Rusted Helmet:A rusted helmet found in a ruin. Repair it to gain its full strength.]

[Withered Bow:A worn bow found in a ruin. Repair it to gain its full strength.]

“How do I repair it?” James asked. But the system went silent. Not even a customary “Information classified” was spoken to him.

James tried the shop, thinking it was the same as repairing his armor or chipped weapons. But of course, the system only offered to buy them for 10 coins a piece.

“Hm…” James looked at the dark sky. He had been here for over a week, so he should have had enough time to explore, but his raptor was getting full.

“Another week and I need to go back. I also need to talk to Kouki or Andrew about fixing these gears. There are blacksmiths in town. That must be the place where I can fix these rusted weapons,” James muttered. His eyes fell on the last place he hadn’t explored—the great tree.

James’ raptor galloped through the street, toward the last tree in the region. However, when he approached, four wooden knights stood guard around the tree. They didn’t look like knights; they looked like trees with two legs and two arms.

The only reason James called them knights was because of the steel shields and spears they carried. A shield—James’ least favourite weapon. Despite their lack of armor, James knew not to underestimate them. He looked back toward the street and then to the treemen.

The knights seemed slow; James could surely flee. But of course James didn’t. It had been a long time since James had found a unique monster. These Medium and Hard dungeons had been giving him ordinary meat; James needed some unique monster for his Chimera, and these wood knights fit the bill.

James stopped and dismounted from his raptor. Time for a real battle.
