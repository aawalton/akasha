I had slept poorly and woken before dawn, which put me in exactly the mood I wanted for a second meeting with the Cardinals. Which was also probably not the mood they wanted to meet me in.

The night had not been kind. I'd spent the better part of it turning over what little Snow and Tundra had told me the morning before, examining it from every angle I could find, and each time I came back to the same unsatisfying conclusion. I didn't have enough information to make a decision, which meant I needed more of it, which meant I had to walk back into that room and ask better questions than I had yesterday. Asking questions of other people was not a position I particularly enjoyed. I was accustomed to walking into conversations already knowing the outcome I wanted. This one still had too many unknowns.

Dralos, or at least I assumed it was Dralos, had arranged breakfast in the same receiving hall without being asked, which told me he'd been thinking along similar lines. A carafe of something dark and bitter sat at the center of the table alongside bread, soft cheese, and sliced fruit arranged with more care than our Holy Kingdom guests necessitated. Still, I supposed it was his way of signaling that the conversation would be conducted on my terms rather than theirs. That our wealth made us able to make decisions without care of others. Small gestures like that were really one of the reasons I kept him around.

I arrived before Snow and Tundra, took the chair at the head of the table as I had the day before, and was halfway through my first cup of a delectable drink when the two ambassadors were shown in.

They looked rested. Composed. I found that faintly irritating.

Snow moved with the same unhurried manner from the day before, coupled with the particular stillness of someone who had long since stopped needing to fill silence with motion. Her silver hair caught the pale dawn light coming through the narrow windows, and her white robes were clean and uncreased despite the fact that she had, presumably, slept in a guest room in a house that was not hers. Tundra followed half a step behind and to her left, sharp-featured and watchful, his eyes making the same brief inventory of the room they had made yesterday. They settled on Deathbringer, which I'd left propped against the side of my chair rather than wearing, but his gaze quickly moved on from the beautiful blade.

"Sit," I said, beckoning them to the opposing side of the reception table.

"Your Grace," Snow said, settling into the same chair she'd occupied the day before. Tundra sat to her left. Neither of them reached for the food, though Tundra's gaze lingered on the carafe for a moment before he folded his hands on the table and left it alone.

I didn't offer to pour for him. If he wanted it badly enough, he could ask. Or simply pour some for himself.

"Sealrite," I said, before either of them could offer a pleasantry. "You said yesterday that it was something you'd like to discuss. So discuss it."

Snow accepted the directness without visible reaction. "What do you know about what happened there?"

"I was present for part of it," I said. "A Catalyst of Destruction leveled the Duke's compound in the upper residential quarter, in the middle of quite an ugly little war. It nearly killed me. Many people died. A large portion of the city burned." I set my cup down. I chose not to add that it had allowed the Red Cardinal to escape. "You told me yesterday the Church of Light built it. What I want to know is who lit the match. I know for a fact it was not Benedict's idea. His advisors pushed him into it, every one of them at once, and I would wager each of them had been bought or bent long before he ever laid eyes on the device."

Tundra's brows rose a fraction. It was the most surprise I'd seen from him. "We had come to the same suspicion," he said. "Two of the Marquess's advisors received regular gifts from a Church of Light charity in Darh'am. A third had a nephew placed in a Church seminary the year before. None of them were ever Church members. None of them needed to be."

Another of those brief, seemingly pre-decided silences passed between them, the kind that told me they had discussed in advance exactly how much to say and in what order. Tundra answered first, which I was beginning to understand was their pattern. He carried the straight facts, while Snow carried the weight.

"The device itself, we are certain of," he said carefully. "It was made in a Church of Light workshop and moved to Cael through channels the Church has used before for similar purposes. We have evidence that agents operating under the Church's direction were active in Sealrite in the weeks before it was used." He paused. "What we cannot yet prove is who gave the order to use it. We cannot place that at any member of the Church's feet, though we know someone from the Church did."

"If the Church made a war move, would that not have had to be a decision made by its false pope?" I asked, taking another sip of the liquid. I'd have to ask Dralos what it was.

Snow gave a tight smile. "You would think so, but just like the politics in secular Kingdoms, the Holy Kingdom and the Church of Light specifically, have their own politics as well. We're trying not to assume anything."

"Hmmm," I hummed, sitting back to look at them both. "Why? Why did the Church of Light choose to get involved?"

Tundra shrugged. "Our network indicates that it was because Pandoria was involved. We believe it was because Sealrite was a prime location for a military base to establish a foothold for the empire."

"Tell me about the Church of Light," I said, changing the subject away from the why and from the Pandorian Empire. "From the beginning. Assume I am ignorant of it completely."

Snow folded her hands on the table. When she spoke, her voice settled into the measured cadence of someone who had practiced this explanation many times and had refined it down to what mattered. "The Church of Light is the oldest of the great churches in the Holy Kingdom. It predates the current papal structure by several centuries. Its founding texts name it the keeper of the divine flame, the guardian of what it calls the Eternal Radiance. They hold a belief that holy energy in its purest form belongs only to those chosen by the Light to wield it." Her face scrunched in a way that told just how deeply distasteful she found it but had been forced to repeat the words so many times the disgust had worn smooth. "In practice, that has always meant a belief that the Church of Light should hold primacy over all others. That its interpretation of the divine is the correct one, its methods the righteous ones, and its authority the only authority that truly matters."

"And the Pope stands in the way of that."

"The High Seat exists specifically to prevent any single church from holding primacy, not necessarily to prevent the Church of Light from dominating the Holy Kingdom, but yes," Tundra said. "The Pope manages the balance between all the churches. He holds authority over each of them equally, answers to none of them individually, and is chosen through a process designed to produce someone with no existing allegiance to any faction." A brief pause. "In theory."

"In theory," I said. "What happens in practice?"

Snow's expression didn't change, but something behind her eyes shifted. "Thirty-two years ago, the current Pope accepted an invitation from Emperor Niro to visit the Pandorian Empire. Before his elevation, he had been an independent scholar of divine mana with no church affiliation, which is what made him an ideal candidate. The visit was framed as a diplomatic gesture. A sign of the Holy Kingdom's openness to relationships beyond its borders." She stopped. "He was gone for four months, two months longer than planned. He returned in good health, with warm words about the Empire's hospitality and its people, and resumed his work without incident."

"But," I said.

"But," Snow agreed. "Those of us who had known him before noticed changes. Small ones, at first. A shift in which voices he chose to listen to in council. A growing warmth toward the Church of Light's positions on matters that had always been contentious. A habit of revisiting decisions that had already been settled, particularly those that had gone against the Church of Light." She let that sit for a moment. "Over the years, those small changes accumulated into something that could no longer be dismissed as the natural drift of age or the pressures of a long office. He is not the same man who left for Pandoria. He has not been for a very long time. We simply spent too many years telling ourselves it was something else before we admitted what we were looking at. Now he is considering abdicating the High Seat, and he has been advocating for the Church of Light's 'Pope' to take his place."

"You think Niro corrupted him," I said.

"We think something was done to him during those four months," Tundra corrected. "We do not know how or by whom. We have theories, of course. We know the outcome, and we know that whatever was done was subtle enough that it took us the better part of a decade to accept we were seeing a pattern rather than a coincidence."

I thought of the golden-eyed soldiers. The dormant thing threaded through the men who had served under the old Alistar rulers, waiting in perfect stillness for a signal that hadn't yet come. As Benedict described it, it was not a visible chain, not a compulsion they could feel, just a conduit running from the eyes into the mind, waiting for the right hand to take hold of it.

Thirty-two years. Patient and invisible, slowly accumulating into small changes into a massive explosion.

I kept that thought entirely off my face and reached for my cup again, taking a small sip.

"And I figure the Church of Light is more than happy to claim the High Seat," I said.

"They are positioning for the succession," Snow said. "The Pope is old, and now he grows ever ill. When he dies or steps down, the selection begins, and the Church of Light has spent three decades cultivating influence in every chamber where that decision will be made, and now they have the Pope's support." The words influence and support were spoken with a particular flatness, the way she might have said poisoning. "If their candidate is elevated, the balance the High Seat was built to maintain ends. Disastrously. The Church of Balance, the Church of the Veil, the smaller orders, all of us fall under the authority of a Pope who answers, in every way that matters, to the Church of Light. And the Church of Light, by everything we have heard as of late, answers to Pandoria."

I set my cup down and looked at the table, turning the information over. I didn't particularly care about the Church of Light taking over the Holy Kingdom. I was, technically speaking, a saintess of that religion. If anything, that might actually turn out as a boon for me. Still, the Empire extending its reach into the Holy Kingdom without marching a single soldier across the border was not something that would benefit me in any way. Especially now that I knew their emperor was my father. There would be bloodshed no matter what, and when it happened, I wouldn't want Pandoria to influence every major religion on the continent.

It was, I had to admit, quite an elegant plan for someone like Niro. I didn't enjoy admitting that.

"And the convocation," I said. "What is it meant to accomplish?"

"It was called by those within the Holy Kingdom who remain genuinely unaffiliated," Tundra said. "Senior civic officials, representatives of the smaller orders, scholars of constitutional tradition who are alarmed by what they are watching unfold. And by the Cardinals who still have the courage to sign their names." He leaned forward slightly, the first time he'd moved from his careful stillness since sitting down. "It is an opportunity for powers beyond our borders with a stake in the outcome to make their positions known. To be present. To signal, clearly and publicly, that the world is watching how this is handled."

"When?" I asked.

"The opening session is set seven weeks from today," Tundra said. "The delegations from the farthest courts needed that long simply to reach the capital. Some of them are already on the roads."

"And if the Pope dies before then? Or steps down?"

Snow's hands tightened on the table, just slightly. "Then the selection begins without witnesses, and the convocation becomes a coronation." She drew a slow breath. "His healers believe he has two months. Perhaps three. They have been wrong before, in both directions. And if he abdicates, it will not matter how long his body lasts."

Seven weeks. I turned the number over. Three of them, at the very least, I would need to set the duchy in order before I could leave it. That left four to cross half the continent, see to anything I chose to see to along the way, and arrive with enough time to learn the room before anyone sat down in it. It was not impossible. It was also not comfortable.

Which, I suspected, was exactly how they had planned it. A guest with time to spare asks questions. A guest in a hurry signs things.

"Seems like a dangerous move," I said, frowning. "If the fear is outside influence, this seems extremely likely to result in quite the opposite, even if it isn't from the Pandorian Empire in the end." I paused. "A very dangerous move."

"But a necessary one," Snow said. "Pandoria will send a representative. Their presence alone will be read, by everyone in that room, as a statement of imperial backing for the Church of Light's ambitions. Without a counterweight, without powers who have reason to resist the Empire's reach making themselves visible and counted, the convocation becomes a formality. It becomes nothing but a stage for the Church of Light to demonstrate that opposition is theoretical rather than real. However, if that political counterweight exists, we hope it all cancels out."

The word counterweight sat heavily in the air between us, like the desperate hopes of the elderly. Which, I suppose, it probably was.

"You want me to be the counterweight," I finished.

"We want you to attend," Snow said. "What you choose to do there is your own affair. But we hope you will be. You, and others."

That was carefully phrased. I respected the craft of it, but I didn't believe the sentiment for a moment. Nobody traveled this far, waited since before dawn in a cold receiving hall, and disclosed as much as they had simply to extend a polite invitation with no actual offer.

"And in exchange," I asked, raising an eyebrow in expectation.

"The Church of Balance has resources," Tundra answered without missing a beat. "Intelligence networks that extend throughout the Holy Kingdom and into several neighboring states. Supply routes that appear on no official register. And, perhaps most immediately useful to you, a great deal of documented evidence regarding the Church of Light's activities over the past three decades and its association with Pandoria. Including in Sealrite." He said that last part without emphasis, but it landed as if he had.

I studied him for a moment, tapping my fingers against the wooden table between us.

"And Mirabelle?" I asked.

Tundra's stillness sharpened almost imperceptibly, though Snow's did not change at all.

"You said she was conducting sensitive work when she disappeared," I continued. "I want to know exactly what that work was. Not the broad shape of it. The specifics."

Snow was quiet for a moment, the lines of her jaw working as she no doubt considered how much she would tell me.

"She was investigating the Cult of Power," Snow finally answered after nearly a minute. "Specifically, its origins and the way it spreads. Who founded it, how it recruits, how it worms its influence into other institutions." She paused. "She had been working on it for nearly six months. In the final months before she disappeared, her reports grew less frequent and more guarded. We believe she had found something that alarmed her enough to make her wary of committing it to writing." Her jaw worked again. "For years the cult was almost nothing. A few fringe believers at most. A handful of drained bodies a year, scattered enough that no one could fully connect the corposes. Then, within weeks of Mirabelle's disappearance, the cult seemed to catch fire. New cells in Cael, in Lysoria, and in Pandoria most of all." She met my eyes. "And the handful of people we have been able to have named as the reasons for that growth are all individuals who came out of the arena in Sealrite."

I kept my face very, very still.

The arena in Sealrite. I had bled in that sand. I had walked out of it with a revolt at my back and close to a hundred slaves bearing my crescent on their palms, and a great many of them had scattered to follow their own Paths of Desire the moment the city burned.

I wondered if any those at the center of the Cult of Power truly were some of my Paragons. It wouldn't be unsurprising if it were true, though perhaps a little unexpected that their influence would have grown that fast.

The Desire System let me feel my Paragons like candles in a dark room, but it didn't tell me where each flame had wandered, or what it had set alight. And even if I found them, it prohibited my interference with their Paths. So long as their paths didn't interfere with mine, I wouldn't bother myself with it.

"And then she never appeared again."

"And then she never appeared again," Snow confirmed. "With no warning, no final report, and no trace of where she had gone or what had been done to her. We found her trail in Sealrite, briefly, and then nothing again. Until word reached us that a woman matching her description had appeared in Elyndor under unusual circumstances, with no memory of how she had come to be there."

I thought of Mirabelle as I had last seen her, seated in one of the Spire's smaller rooms, hands wrapped around a cup of something warm, talking to Ethan with an ease I had told him repeatedly he should not encourage or entertain. Whatever she had been before, whatever had been done to her, what remained did not seem dangerous. Not to me, at least.

That did not mean I wouldn't hand her over. It also certainly did not mean that I would.

"She stays here," I said flatly. "Whoever she had been, she is now in my household and under my protection. Whatever she was to your church before is not something I have any obligation to honor, and knowing her history doesn't change my position."

"We will withdraw our request for you to return her to us," Snow agreed, lifting her hands up in the air in a way that made me think she had anticipated this exact conversation and decided, somewhere along the way, that pressing the point was not worth the cost. "We ask only that if her memories do surface, if she recovers something of what was taken from her, you share whatever is relevant to the investigation she was conducting."

"If anything relevant surfaces," I said slowly, my eyes narrowing to slits, "I will decide at that time what to do with it."

Snow looked at me for a moment, her jaw working again before she inclined her head. "Of course."

I stood, ending the meeting whether they were finished or not.

"I will attend your convocation," I decided, absently twirling a strand of hair. "I make no promises about what I will or will not do once I'm there. I also may have conditions, which Dralos will deliver to you in writing before midday. And I will have more questions before we leave Elyndor. About the route, about who else will be attending, and about what exactly the Church of Light has been doing that you haven't told me yet." I let that last part settle. "Because I am sure there is more you haven't told me."

Neither of them even tried to deny it.

"Thank you, Your Grace," Snow said, rising into a bow. Tundra followed half a second behind. Precedence observed, even here, even between two members of the same church in a room with no audience to perform for. It was seemingly a habit deep enough to be instinct.

"Don't thank me yet," I responded with a small, not so nice smile. "I haven't done anything."

I left them to the breakfast neither of them had touched and looked around for Dralos, who had no doubt been waiting nearby.
