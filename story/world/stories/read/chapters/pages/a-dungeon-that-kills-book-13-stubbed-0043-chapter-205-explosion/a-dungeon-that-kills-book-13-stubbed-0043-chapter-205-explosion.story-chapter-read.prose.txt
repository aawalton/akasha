As Viktor activated the Thauma, he converted the air around him into marsh vapor and gathered it into a sphere no eye could see, before letting it spill from his palm like water pouring from a jug. Then, with nothing more than a thought, he commanded it to creep across the dungeon floor, low and quiet. Toward the fire trap. Toward the spot where the Iskora team was heading.

The downside of the fire trap when used by itself was not only its low damage output, but also the fact that it couldn’t do a thing to anyone who wasn’t literally standing right next to it when it was triggered. And, considering that there were only just over fifty such traps scattered across a dungeon floor this vast, the odds of someone wandering close enough to matter were embarrassingly slim.

The marsh vapor solved that particular problem rather elegantly.

With it, physical proximity was no longer necessary. Now, Viktor only needed to spread the gas, making sure that what was curling around his enemies’ ankles and what was pooling around the fire trap stayed connected by one long unbroken thread. After that, all it took was a single spark, and the volatile vapor would take care of the rest.

That, in the end, was exactly why he had gone to the trouble of teleporting here in person, placing himself near the trap that sat closest to the predicted path of the Iskora team.

The vapor had, by now, reached the fire trap. He left a small portion of it there, while the remainder continued to creep steadily forward. Soon, it would reach the adventurers. Then, it would wait there, invisible and inert, for the moment it would finally be detonated.

Xander was probably going to emerge from the explosion largely unscathed. The bastard must have already assumed his spectral state, which allowed him to slip out of harm’s way as easily as smoke through a closed fist. As for the Apostle, while Viktor still knew nothing about the nature of his abilities, he doubted something like this would trouble him either. Nevertheless, the remaining four were ordinary humans made of bone and flesh. If none of them had an answer for this attack, he might just get to clear all of the fodder with one fell swoop.

Though it seemed, to his mild irritation, that they did have an answer.

As he tried to push the vapor toward the adventurers, he found himself abruptly unable to make it move any closer to them. In fact, as the party advanced, the vapor was actively pushed back.

An invisible barrier, huh? Of course.

This must be Cynthia’s doing. She was an aeromancer, after all, so it stood to reason she would know the same wall tricks her friends had once employed against him during the battle above the outpost.

If he had to guess, she had probably conjured several walls of compressed air surrounding the party, a defensive measure meant to protect them from surprise attacks like sneaky arrows or poisonous gas. And, as it turned out, they blocked marsh vapor as well.

Very prudent of her, indeed. Viktor chuckled to himself.

It was fine. The walls of air were strong, no doubt—a fact he had confirmed for himself, both back when he was on the receiving end of such barriers and when he created similar ones of his own in the fight against the shark-faced men—but they were not indestructible. And he was going to find out how durable they actually were. Right here, right now.

“Celeste, has anyone reached the detonator?”

[Yes, Master. A goblin is already there, awaiting your command.]

“Good. Teleport me back to the Core Room. Then have it trigger the trap.”

[Understood.]

The world around him shifted again. Naturally, with him no longer there to sustain his focus, the vapor would begin to disperse. But that hardly mattered, as the goblin in question would ignite it in less than a second. So the explosion came, right up against the outside of the Iskora team’s shield, though of course he couldn’t hear anything, since he was already back in the Core Room when it happened.

Viktor immediately sent his vision back to the first floor to survey the aftermath, and once he got there, all he could make out was a churning cloud of pulverized rock and scorched air, thick enough that nothing within it was visible.

“Hot!” came a panicked scream. “Cynthia, do something!”

A powerful blast of wind swept through, tearing the haze apart and driving it away.

And there they were.

Xander was still hovering above the ground exactly as before, his ugly, sideburned face registering a flicker of mild annoyance, as though the explosion that had just gone off was no more troublesome than a passing breeze. The perks of staying in the spectral state throughout the whole ordeal. Viktor couldn’t help but snort at the sight of it.

Behind the bastard, Valeska had planted her axe into the ground and was using it as an anchor. Rowan had crouched down as well, ducking low behind the broad expanse of his tower shield. Drake, on the other hand, had been sent flying by the shockwave and now lay sprawled across the ground, scrambling awkwardly to get back to his feet. Cynthia was standing, gripping her staff tightly with both hands, though blood now trickled from her nose, as if she had just introduced it to a hard surface. And, at the very end of the formation, the Apostle remained the same, unmoving and unreadable, making it hard to say whether the blast had affected him at all.

On the one hand, the wall the aeromancer conjured had ultimately been destroyed by the explosion. On the other hand, that same wall had soaked up enough of the blast, meaning the damage the adventurers had actually taken was disappointingly light.

“What... what the hell is that?” Valeska asked, her face twitching with visible discomfort as sweat streaked down her temples and jaw. Looked like despite the powerful gust of wind Cynthia had summoned, the air in this corridor was still unbearably hot.

“An attack from the dungeon,” Rowan said with a grunt. “What else?”

“But... I’ve never heard of anything like this happening in the dungeon before. In fact, this reminds me of...”

“That explosion at our outpost,” Drake finished for her, one hand pressed against the darkening bruise on his forehead. “It’s the dark spirit.”

Needless to say, every one of the Iskora adventurers flinched at the mere mention of the name, save for Xander, whose jaw only set harder.

“The bastard is here, huh? Good. Let him come. He won’t get away this time.”

“Anyway,” Cynthia said, wiping her bloody nose with the back of one trembling hand. “Someone gives me a healing herb. Damn it!”

Valeska glanced over toward Rowan. “Give her the—” But her brow furrowed again almost instantly. “What is that sound?”

“Footsteps,” Drake said. “Incoming.”

Well, with the enemies still disoriented in the wake of the first attack, there was hardly any reason not to follow it up with another. Thus, Celeste had dispatched the forces waiting nearby. However, since this corridor, along with several of the nearby passages, remained too hot in the aftermath of the explosion, she had made the sensible decision not to send in any of the living minions, who would have suffered greatly for it. Instead, the ones now advancing toward the Iskora team were—

“Skeletons!” Valeska bellowed as she raised her battle axe.

Yes, those were the skeleton warriors raised by Khenemhotep, now pouring in from both ends of the corridor in a coordinated pincer attack.

Xander looked at them with open contempt. “The dungeon thinks it could kill me with this rabble? I’m not going to dirty my hands with this. You lot deal with them.”

Valeska stepped forward, axe in hand, positioning herself to intercept the skeletons from one side, while Drake took the other with his spear, quick on his feet despite the bruise blooming purple on his face. Rowan raised his tower shield and settled into a watchful stance, his eyes tracking both flanks with the alertness of a veteran. Beside him stood Cynthia, murmuring the opening words of a spell under her breath, staff held ready to aid her companions the instant they needed it, all the while continuing to use a cloth to dab at her bleeding nose.

Letting out a fierce roar, Valeska lunged at the nearest target. Her axe came around in a horizontal arc that would have cleaved a living man clean in half, and did much the same courtesy for the skeleton. Its torso exploded into a spray of bone fragments, its skull spinning off to crack hard against the wall. The warrior woman didn’t pause to admire the destruction. She continued with a reverse swing that caught the next skeleton squarely in the hip joint, sending femurs and fragments of pelvis clattering noisily across the stone in every direction.

At the opposite end of the fray, Drake moved with a different kind of grace. His spear was a silver blur, darting in and out like something driven by clockwork rather than muscle. A thrust to the eye socket of one skeleton, a quick withdrawal, then a sweeping low strike that took out the legs of two more. His lean, athletic frame coiled and uncoiled with each strike like a spring wound tight and released again and again without ever seeming to exhaust.

“My turn then,” Viktor said.

He opened his eyes, only to close them almost immediately, though this time not to send his vision forward as he had done before, but instead to take control of one of his prepared corpses.

Just as he had done with the fire traps, he had also gone to the trouble of preparing a considerable number of dead bodies in advance, each embedded with a ringed cross and scattered strategically across the first floor, ready to be called upon whenever the need arose. One of them was already moving on its own, shambling toward the Iskora adventurers, and now he was going to take over and manually control it.

He found himself, in the next instant, standing once more within a dimly lit corridor of the maze, the sound of fighting ringing out clearly from somewhere nearby. Peeking around the corner, he caught sight of them.

Drake was about to finish off his batch of skeletons. A dozen or more lay in broken pieces at his feet, while the very last one was being steadily pushed back, barely managing to defend itself against the relentless storm of spear thrusts coming its way.

Viktor broke into a run.

The spearman brought down the final skeleton with a decisive smash to its spine before turning his attention toward the new opponent, one eyebrow lifting briefly at the sight of an undead that looked completely different from those he had just dispatched. He showed no further reaction beyond that, though. Clearly, he didn’t view this newcomer as any kind of serious threat. He simply readjusted the grip on his spear, preparing himself to skewer the walking corpse the moment it came within range.

And, when Viktor was a few steps away from being hit by that long, deadly weapon—

—he raised a borrowed hand and shot a fireball forward.

Well, yes. He had strapped the fire gems and wind gems onto these corpses, which greatly improved their combat prowess well beyond what mere punching and kicking could ever achieve on their own.

“What the hell!?”

Drake was caught off guard, but his reflexes proved to be every bit as sharp as his reputation suggested. He twisted his body at the last possible second, dodging the incoming projectile with a narrow margin to spare. The orange orb flew past him, and Rowan intercepted it moments later with his shield before it could hit anyone else.

Viktor was not done yet, however. He pointed the other hand at the spearman’s feet and loosed a blast of wind.

Drake stumbled forward, but he turned his fall into a controlled roll, coming back up on his feet as though the whole thing had been intentional. But that recovery put him right next to the corpse Viktor was controlling. So Viktor spread the dead man’s arms wide and wrapped around him in a tight embrace.

“What the fuck are you doing? Get off me!” Drake yelled.

Of course, Viktor had no intention of letting him go. The two arms remained locked in place, refusing every attempt to shake them loose.

And then.

He detonated the bomb he had planted inside the corpse.
