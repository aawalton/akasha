Callie stirred in her sleep as she felt something brush against her arm. Opening her eyes, she noticed Callen standing by her bed holding her drawings in his hand. At first, she closed her eyes again, too sleepy to realize what had happened.

But a moment later, realization dawned on her sleepy mind, and she jumped up from the bed in shock.

"Callen! What are you doing?" She asked, reaching for the pages in his hands. Callen held them up, out of her reach. His expression was filled with worry.

"Callie… Did you actually… Did you actually see this?" He pointed to the drawing of the dragon.

Callie felt ashamed, her cheeks burning hot. To have been caught in a lie by her brother felt awful. But the only thing in Callie's mind was the safety of the dragon.

"You can't tell Mom and Dad! They'll kill it!" She latched onto her brother desperately.

"Is that why you didn't tell me? You were afraid I'd tell our parents?" he asked, his expression softening a little.

"You can't!" She repeated.

"I won't, I won't!" He shook his head and returned the drawings to Callie. "Just please, tell me everything that happened."

"You really won't tell? It didn't hurt me! It doesn't need to be hunted," Callie insisted.

"I promise I won't tell," he assured. "I'm just a little sad you wouldn't trust me with this. Have I ever snitched on you before?"

"No…" Callie admitted, looking down at the floorboard, her cheeks still burning hot with shame.

"Then please trust me not to do it this time either, and tell me what really happened last night."

Callie nodded and then told her brother everything. She didn't withhold her awe of the event or the fact that she had drunk the water from the dragon's flower. She didn't hide the fact that the glowing mark on her hand had appeared right after she had drunk the magic water.

Lying to her brother was the worst thing she had done, and Callie didn't want to do it again. Especially if he promised not to tell their parents about the dragon.

"So it is magical…" Callen muttered.

"Yeah! Isn't that amazing?" Callie said excitedly.

However, her smile quickly waned when she saw that Callen wasn't excited in the slightest. "Brother, what's wrong? Is magic bad? I've seen Dad and Mom use magic. Dad could use fire magic, and Mom could use water magic, right?"

"No, it's not that magic is bad…" Callen rubbed the back of his neck, in what Callie recognized as a clear sign of discomfort. "It's just that magic like this doesn't just appear out of nowhere. What do you think Mom and Dad will do once they see a glowing mark on your hand?"

"I'll hide it!" She said firmly. "I never intended to show it to them. Besides, it's not glowing as much anymore. I think the magic is… settling down."

"Settling down?"

"Yes. From the water. It felt like I drank the magic with the water, and then it spread through my whole body. It was glowing much more brightly in the morning, and my insides were still a bit tingly. But it's not as bright anymore, and the tingling is gone."

"Okay, then let's hope the glowing stops. But is that all there is to it? Can you… Can you do magic now?" Callen asked curiously.

"Maybe? I don't know how to," Callie admitted. "How do Mom and Dad do magic?"

"Through lots and lots of training from what I've heard…"

"Brother!" Callie grabbed one of her brother's hands. "Could you ask them about magic, please? It would be suspicious if I did it."

He looked hesitant at first, but Callie kept staring at him with her best pleading face. Eventually, Callen conceded. "I'll ask them, but don't try doing any magic on your own, okay? It could be dangerous."

Callie nodded. "I promise!"

"Good girl! We're going to keep it a secret for now. If it hurts you or attracts danger in some way, we'll tell our parents okay?" Callen patted her head gently.

Callie nodded. That seemed reasonable. "Wh-What about Granny Ethel? Can't I tell her? She might know something."

Granny Ethel always understood her and explained everything to her kindly.

"I suppose… I'm not too familiar with her aside from those few times she'd healed me," Callen said. "Do you trust her more than our parents?"

"Yes!" Callie confirmed without hesitation.

Callen frowned, but didn't reprimand her. "If you trust her so much, I suppose it's fine to tell her. It's best if at least one adult knows what's going on."

"Okay! But that will have to wait until I'm not confined anymore…" Callie sighed.

"Our parents were really worried, you know. I think they just want to make sure nothing bad will happen to you," he ruffled her hair gently. "Now, why don't you rest? Maybe I'll manage to get answers from Mom and Dad by the time you wake up."

"Thank you, Brother," Callie said, lying down in her bed. She felt like a heavy burden had been lifted off her chest. It felt nice to tell everything to her brother.

However, as her eyelids drooped shut, she noticed Callen furrow his eyebrows. Something was still bothering him. But by the time Callie had fallen asleep, she had already forgotten about that.

The next few days were the most boring of Calluna's life. She was confined for the first time ever, and the thought hadn't occurred to her how horrible it actually was. She wasn't allowed to go outside unless it was to do chores, and she was accompanied by somebody else, usually Dad.

Dad didn't dare to take a single eye off her, watching her every move. As if she would try to run away the moment he looked away. Callie was tempted to, just a little, but that was only because everyone was watching her so intently. Even Callen would stay close to her whenever he was home.

"Dad, I'm not going to go anywhere. You don't have to stare at me all the time," Callie protested as she pulled the weeds out of the cabbage patch.

"I'll keep staring until your punishment is over!" He growled and, as promised, kept a tight watch on her while chopping the wood, not too far from the cabbage patch.

Things were worse while she was inside. There was nothing to do, besides cleaning. After two days, she had already scrubbed their small home completely spotless, including her bedroom, her brother's bedroom, and their parents' room. Even the storage room got a thorough scrubbing.

They didn't have any books at home. They were too expensive for anyone in the village to afford. Only Granny Ethel and the village elder had a few precious books in their possession, but it wasn't something they let out of their hands. The few precious sheets of paper Mom had bought Callie were already considered a luxury.

Callie had hoped that Callen would be able to bring her some information about magic. But it appeared that Mom and Dad didn't know enough about much to actually teach it. She was tempted to pester them herself for the little scraps that they did know. But caution was paramount, especially since her parents were already on high alert.

Thankfully, after a week, when both Mom and Dad thought that Callie had learned her lesson, they allowed the girl to return to her normal daily activities. The fact that the floorboards were so clean that they were squeaking with every step also contributed to the decision.

The moment she was allowed her freedom, Callie ran to Granny Ethel's hut, clutching two wooden boards in her hands. Between them, she had carefully placed the pages she had drawn. She was excited to show them, although Callie was unsure if she would share her dragon drawing just yet. She would see the elderly woman's reaction first. If it were anything like her parents, Callie would keep the encounter a secret.

"Good morning, Granny Ethel!" Callie greeted cheerily as she swung open the healer's hut door.

Much to her displeasure, it wasn't the elderly woman that she saw but rather her other apprentice, Tristan. The boy had been changing the bedsheets on one of the sick beds, but the moment Callie came in, a wicked grin grew on his face.

"Look who has finally been released! It's the monster-obsessed carrothead," the black-haired boy jeered.

"Where's Granny Ethel?" Callie asked, doing her best to ignore the remark, although a slight bit of heat did rush to her cheeks.

"Why should I tell you?" He countered.

"Because… Uh… Nevermind. She's probably in the back," Callie muttered uncomfortably.

She really didn't like being around Tristan. He was two years older, but acted like he owned the place and constantly ordered Callie around. When Granny Ethel wasn't watching, he would command her on how to do things and poke fun at her whenever the chance presented itself. His favorite activity was pulling on her hair.

Callie tried to get to the back room that Granny Ethel used to make her medicine and dry herbs, but Tristan sidestepped to block her way. "Shouldn't you at least greet me since you've come back?"

In her mind, Callie wanted to call Tristan that bad word Callen once used on him. Mom instantly told him it was a bad word and not to use it in front of Callie. But she didn't dare. Who knew what this bully would do if provoked?

"Hello, Tristan…" she muttered instead, cursing her inability to stand up properly for herself. It was always like this whenever she was in front of other people.

"That's better! Should have started with that," Tristan grinned with satisfaction.

He came closer to her and roughly tousled her hair, making her whimper from the slightly painful gesture. "Hey, what's that you got in your hands?"

Callie clutched the wooden boards tightly to herself. "Granny Ethel asked for it!"

"Did she?" Tristan asked suspiciously, reaching out to grab the boards.

Callie jumped back. "No! You can't take them!"

"I'm only going to take a look. Show me!" He commanded, stepping closer.

"NO!" Callie shrieked, backing away into the wall. If Tristan saw the drawings, then he would undoubtedly tell everyone about the dragon. Tears formed in Callie's eyes as she imagined the hunters rushing to the dragon with their weapons raised.

"What's all the ruckus?" Granny Ethel came out of the backroom, much to Callie's relief. "Are you bullying Callie again?"

"I…I only wanted to look!" Tristan said defensively.

Granny Ethel was having none of it, hobbling up to the boy and grabbing him roughly by the ear. "I told you to stop doing that! No girl would ever want to marry such a bully. You're supposed to be taking care of Calluna, not bullying her."

Tristan's cheeks flushed deep red. "I never said I want to marry!"

"But you'll have to one day. Unless you want to live and die alone with nobody to share your burdens."

Granny Ethel shook her head and released the boy's ear. She turned to Callie, her expression softening. "Welcome back, Callie. I'm happy to see that you've returned safe and sound."

"I'm happy to see you again!" Callie said, throwing her hands around the elderly woman for a hug while keeping a tight hold on the wooden board with one hand.

"There, isn't Callie's smile so much more beautiful than her frown?" Granny Ethel said while gently stroking Callie's back.

"No!" Tristan said stubbornly, his cheeks still flushed red from the reprimand.

"Bah! Then you can stay here and sweep the floor. Callie and I will catch up. I'm ever so eager to hear what happened to you in the forest," Granny Ethel said, urging Callie to follow.

Once Callie and Granny Ethel were alone in the medicine room, Callie threw her arms around the elder woman again. She was still too small to fully reach her arms around Granny Ethel, as the woman was rather round. But Granny Ethel, on the other hand, could easily bury Callie in her embrace.

"I missed you, Granny Ethel! It was really boring at home," she said, enjoying her soft warmth and the familiar scent of herbs that lingered on her clothes.

"I missed you too. Tell me what happened to you to have gotten lost in the woods at night. I thought you knew better than to stay outside after sunset," the woman reprimanded gently.

"Of course I know that. I remained outside to help a glimmerhare." Callie pulled out her drawing of the creature from between the wooden boards and showed it proudly to the elderly woman.

Granny Ethel's eyes widened, and she took the page. "A glimmerhare? This is a very accurate drawing. You really saw one?"

"No, I saved one. It was stuck in a horrible trap. It just took me a long time to get the thing to open. That's why I stayed outside after dark."

"What about the wound on its leg?"

"I rubbed millefolia leaves on it and tied a part of my tunic around it. It was bleeding a lot but didn't look broken," Callie informed.

"Good job. I'm proud that you went out of your way to help that poor creature," Granny Ethel said kindly and gently patted Callie's hair.

All of a sudden, Callie felt tears well in her eyes. This was the first time somebody had actually been thankful that she saved the glimmerhare. Everyone else had only chastised her for endangering herself.

Granny Ethel must have noticed her distress as she sat down in her lounging chair in the corner of the room and motioned Callie to sit down next to her. Callie wiped the tears out of her eyes and squeezed herself into the chair next to the woman.

"Let me guess—everyone was angry that you helped the glimmerhare?" Granny Ethel asked.

"Yes…" Callie sniffed. "But it would have died in that trap."

"You did the right thing, Callie. Helping another creature in need is never a mistake," Granny Ethel soothed, gently rubbing Callie's back.

Feeling the warmth from the hand gave Callie courage. Granny Ethel deserved to know the whole truth. She would understand.

"There's another thing… That happened after I met the glimmerhare," Callie said, and after hesitating for just a moment, withdrew the page with the dragon drawing on it.

Granny Ethel sat up straight in her chair as she looked at the page with wide eyes. Callie had never seen the old woman so shocked before.

"A dra…" Granny Ethel stops midword, as if not daring to say it aloud. "Are you certain this is what you saw?"

Callie nodded and explained how it had grown a magic flower that had healed her and given her the unusual mark on her forearm.

Granny Ethel listened intently to her every word, and her eyes opened wide when Callie rolled up her sleeve to show the gently glowing pink mark on her hand.

"Magic…" Granny Ethel said, shaking her head as her face scrunched with worry. "Oh, dear child… If somebody were to discover that…"
