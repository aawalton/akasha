Ethan searched for the Followers for three weeks, finding nothing. He’d begun to feel like there was nothing to find, until he realized that was exactly the problem. At first, he simply headed west, mostly riding on Deevee. He’d stop at times, investigating one oddity or another. From high in the air he managed to find three different Nator ruins, and stopped at each. He’d found several more samples of Nator writing for Selina, and thankfully no more skeletal remains.

As he grew closer to the coast, he felt an odd wrongness as they traveled across a vast desert and past a forest of Twilight rank carnivorous trees. The feeling increased as two days passed, and Ethan didn’t recognize it until they reached the Potentian equivalent of the Pacific Ocean. It was less than a minute before he spotted something like a two-headed shark devouring a school of smaller aquatic creatures.

That harsh bit of nature made him consider how few demons he’d actually seen the last few days. Most spent the majority of their time hiding, anyway, so it was easy to forget that they were all around you–and easy to miss it when they weren’t.

“This is not normal, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo said as they flew back inward.

“I agree,” Ethan said. Even accounting for the demons that would flee if they sensed us, we should have seen something by now. Either we’re missing something entirely unrelated…”

“Or someone else is rounding up as many demons as possible, and is doing a remarkably thorough job.”

“I wish I could think of another explanation,” Ethan said.

“How do we track them?” Tomo asked.

“The only thing that occurs to me is a bit unusual,” Ethan admitted. “I was thinking of having Deevee split up, and fly in a few directions at once. If any hydra encounters demons, they turn around, and report how far off they were. We try to stay in the middle.”

“Is that possible? I haven’t tested my own ability to distance myself from you in some time.”

“An unexpected benefit of my bloated monster of a spirit,” Ethan said. “I can feel the Bonds between us more strongly than ever. It’s like all that power my spirit has gathered transformed the strings that connect us into steel cord. I think you should be able to get a few miles away before you start to feel a tug.”

They tested that limitation, and it was proven relatively true. Ethan’s Familiars could definitely put more space between them, but it shorted considerably when Deevee split up. In the end only two hydra could do as asked, but it still proved effective–albeit the result was Ethan zigzagging north and south between demon sightings for two more days.

It was all more than worth it when they found what they were looking for.

Ethan had assumed–if he was proven right–that they’d find an army similar to their own, stretching out in all directions. Instead, the first hint of what was to come came in a deep trench carved into the dusty red ground. It wasn’t particularly wide, and he followed it for many hours, grateful for the setting sun to disguise his flight. When he finally spotted something in the distance, he dismounted, and continued on foot.

It was difficult to see everything in the dark, even with his nightvision rune, but soon he could follow based on the heavy, plodding footsteps alone. Ethan managed to get his first view from a rocky hill, confirming his worst fears.

What he saw could only be a Follower army. It was eerily quiet, save for the rhythmic footsteps of nearly two-hundred Twilight demons, each one covered entirely in the inky black alien flesh of one of their Familiars. It was all Ethan could do not to curse aloud when he saw their force, easily five times as powerful as what Apollo had amassed after the battle with the undead.

They had two advantages that Ethan could see after stalking the army for most of the night. The first was that they clearly had no qualms about the larger demons eating the smaller. Three times he saw a small horde of Dusks herded into the army’s path, only to be devoured in minutes.

The second advantage came from numbers. It wasn’t easy to tell Followers apart, but Ethan thought he saw four or five of their generals doing the same job he had been doing for his own force. They would disappear into the night, and return with demons in tow after a few hours. Not only were several of them, but they used their own Familiars to coax the demons back. No difficult kiting or surprises, they brought in Twilight demons who appeared completely placid.

One thing Ethan failed to discover was who ran the army. The Followers had constructed some kind of wooden structure, which looked a bit like an angular cottage, and it was being carried on the backs of four Twilight gigas. They marched in perfect lockstep in the center of the army, and Ethan could see strands of black extending from within.

“It is too dangerous to get closer,” Tomo said.

“Sadly, I’m forced to agree,” Ethan said. “But I have to do something. I’m not just going back empty-handed to tell the others how screwed we are.”

“What did you have in mind?” Tomo asked.

Ethan pointed. “Every three or four hours, one of their number shows up with new demons, then goes right back out. They seem to have pre-determined directions they search in.”

“You intend to follow one,” Tomo said.

“At least,” Ethan confirmed.

“Are you sure you’re willing to risk exposing yourself?”

“We’re exposed, Tomo. One of them already found us. Even if it didn’t report back, it’s only a matter of time before another does the same. I’d rather weaken their recruiting power now, as much as possible. The longer we wait, the further behind we’ll fall.”

“I cannot disagree, Ethan Bishop. But…the power of this army unnerves me. I sense fluctuations in the Astral I cannot make sense of.”

“I’m with you,” Ethan agreed. “But right now, it’s time for the assassin, not the scientist.”

Valanor had been having a terrible month. First he’d encountered a cluster of Twilight demons that actually hunted as a group. He’d never seen that before, as those he’d encountered seemed increasingly inclined toward independence as they grew more powerful. Yet these ones–relatively small, golem-like creatures with stone bodies–moved almost like a single being.

They’d been so well camouflaged among the red stone, that Valanor didn’t realize he was surrounded until he practically walked into one. Nearly twice his size, they weren’t large for their power, and while they moved slowly, there were nine of them, and they had him surrounded before he realized he was under attack.

Sylvie, his steel type crab Familiar, made a good showing of herself. At Twilight rank, her claws were enormous, and she caught one of the golems in each. Steel was a strong match for stone, and Sylvie was shrugging off their attacks while delivering powerful blows of her own, but Valanor proved the weak link.

He was struck from behind in the initial ambush, and simply couldn’t recapture any momentum. The golems announced their presence by knocking him in the back of the head, leaving him dazed, and more than a little dizzy. Worse, they showed clear signs of intelligence, focusing all their strikes on his injured skull while Valanor struggled to keep his shield up.

The battle was blessedly short, but quite punishing. Valanor ended up throwing nearly all of his mana away in bursts of power. He sent waves of steel spikes at an unsuspecting golem, blasting it relentlessly as he held off its brethren, until its stone body was in ruins. Valanor burst through the gap he’d created, but couldn’t outrun the hurled boulders the remaining demons tossed after him.

His left leg was struck badly, then twice more after he stumbled to the ground. Only Sylvie putting her heavy body between Valanor and golems bought him enough time to hurl his injured body over a nearby hill. He felt his steel Bond snap as the golems turned their full attention on his brave Familiar, and he barely had the strength to call Sif.

His frost wolf dragged him to safety, but Valanor worried he might have sealed his own fate that day.

The white demon found him in the night as it always did. Except this time, he wasn’t a powerful opponent it had come to respect, one it recognized as an equal, and had built a strange rapport with. Instead, it found him broken and bleeding, having just reset a leg broken in several places, and with eyes that couldn’t focus.

At Twilight rank, the injuries were effectively superficial, and would heal in days. But he was completely at the white demon’s mercy until then.

When it arrived in the small cave Sif had found, it had crawled to its usual place across the small fire, then stared at him. Not into Valanor’s eyes, as it usually did, but at his obvious wounds. Not only was he badly injured, but Valanor had been hunting demons and leaving them for his unlikely companion. He hadn’t managed that this time, meaning that his company for this evening was a Twilight demon that could undoubtedly kill him, and was likely hungry.

When Valanor felt himself losing consciousness–his skull was badly cracked–he didn’t expect to wake. But instead of dying in his sleep, he awoke to a pile of food, much like the piles he’d normally leave for the demon, except this one was right next to where he’d passed out, and had already been burned in the fire. The demon didn’t eat cooked food, but obviously understood that he did.

Valanor was forced to eat the corrupt meat, despite knowing what it was doing to him. He needed the fuel to heal, and he ate the foul-tasting and tough meal without complaint.

“Thank you,” he told the demon, when it appeared once more that evening. Again, it dropped a pile of meat next to him, then moved to watch from across the fire. Valanor did the cooking this time, hoping that it might taste better with less of a char.

Three nights passed like that, with the demon arriving to deliver him food, then watch him. By the third night, Valanor was nearly healed, though his leg and head still pained him greatly. He had started chatting with the demon by then, idly speaking, enjoying the parody of conversation, as he’d been starved of it for so long now.

On that third night, the demon surprised him. Valanor was telling the creature a story from his youth. Some child’s tale, possibly based on an old legend. He’d heard it numerous times back in his days in the church’s orphanage, and credited it as one of his first tastes of heroism.

“And so the knight returned to the kingdom, though it was not his own, and brought the two royal children with him. The king saw that a warrior from an enemy nation had put the lives of the prince and princess ahead of personal politics. He promised peace, in recognition of the knight’s valor.”

“Val-lorrrr,” a deep, gravelly voice echoed, and Valanor shot to his feet. The white demon was crouched in its usual spot, staring at him curiously. Its thin lips moved again, tongue struggling over sharp teeth. “Val-lorrrr,” it repeated.

Valanor laughed aloud. “Yes!” he cried. “You’ve got it. Val-or. Do you like that word?”

“Val-lorrr,” it said again, tilting its head.

“Remarkable. Perhaps you really were a Nator once.” He brightened. “You know, my name isn’t too far off from that word. Val-a-nor,” he said.

The demon watched him, then scrunched up its face slightly. “Val-larr,” it growled.

Valanor chuckled. “I think you’re getting further away from it. But don’t worry, this is still amazing. Perhaps we could really communicate if you’re willing to keep trying.”

The demon suddenly got to its feet, and Valanor stepped back, by instinct. But his companion didn’t come any closer, instead it moved to some discarded bones, from where it had stripped meat over the last few nights. There was quite the collection, and it surprised Valanor by pushing them around for a time, before, lifting up two thick thigh bones, each ending in shattered hips

It hefted one in each hand, then moved to stand in its usual spot, across the small fire. It didn’t sit, but rather stood there, looking down at the makeshift clubs. Valanor just watched, wondering what it might be trying to communicate. Then he realized it was making sounds beneath its breath.

“Zaaa…Saaa…Saaa.”

“What are you trying to say?” Valanor asked.

The demon looked back at him, and spoke once more. “Saa-val-lar…” it whispered, and Valanor’s heart seemed to stop in his chest. He looked again at the white demon, standing there with two approximations of clubs–no, maces–held in a stance he’d seen countless times. It all came crashing down at once.

He looked back at the horned demon, barely able to find his voice. “Savilar?”
