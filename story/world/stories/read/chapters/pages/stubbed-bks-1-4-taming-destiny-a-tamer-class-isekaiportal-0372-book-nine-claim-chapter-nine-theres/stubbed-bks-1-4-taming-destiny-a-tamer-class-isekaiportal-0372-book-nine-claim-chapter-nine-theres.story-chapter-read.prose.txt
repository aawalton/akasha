I briefly sense the exhaustion among my Water Mages as I pull out of the magical network. They won’t be able to do this again, though they’re still needed to help deflect the probing attempts from the other Water Mages.

But Kyrian is also tired – I felt that before the serpent completely collapsed and broke our brief connection. I don’t doubt he needs to recover before doing something like this again. I do too, though I suspect I’ll need less time than he will – he hasn’t gone through the kind of conditioning I have where the life of my companions or myself depended on me pushing through my limits.

When I settle back into my physical body, I find that Ivor is circling around the water, low on the starboard side of the ship, where the serpent had been before. Now, there’s nothing but the usual ocean waves.

Clearly aware that I’m ‘awake’ again, he sends me pictures of attacks striking at us and then his decision to put the ship between us and those attempting to attack.

Good job, I tell him, then urge him to rise again so I can see what’s happening on the ship itself.

There, I see a welcome sight – Tolly has managed to create space between us and Kyrian’s ship and we’re rapidly increasing the gap. It won’t be long before we’re out of range of the ballistas.

But it’s come at a cost. Our emergency shield is completely down and the consequences of that are obvious. More than one sail is ripped or tattered and I see burn-marks on the main mast and two of the sails. As for the deck, that’s even worse – many once-whole planks and railings are now splintered and bloodstained.

The damage to the wood seems fairly superficial, though, and that’s why we’ve brought a carpenter. I can only hope that the staining doesn’t mean we’ve lost many people – they are far more precious. Everyone had at least two healing potions with them, and several of the Classers have healing Skills of one form or another, even if we’ve only got one trained Healer with us. Hopefully the combined efforts have been able to bring sufferers back from the brink.

The stern of the ship shows more signs of damage than any of the other sections – unsurprising since it’s now the only part of the ship that the enemies can reach – but at least it only looks like surface damage. There are a couple of holes worryingly close to the water-line, but I can see that they’ve had something shoved into them from the inside so it should be fine for now.

Casting my glance wider, I survey what’s happening with the rest of the ships, I feel mixed emotions.

What’s happening with Valence’s ship? I ask Loran in concern. It’s far more battered than it really should be – his shields must have gone too.

Someone on House Siren’s ship has vocal Skills – Lord Fell struggled to keep his crew under control. Few were able to resist the effects and those who could were too busy shoving ear plugs into the others to really fight. One of those under the influence also managed to damage the shield enchantments controls, bringing down the shield.

Do they need support? I ask, already trying to work out what we can do when we’re also in a sorry state.

Captain Sor’az has been helping. Sure enough, I see the smaller ship nimbly sweep out from behind the enemy’s vessel, peppering it with attacks as it goes. And, as I see them land, I notice that the enemy’s ship no longer has a shield either. Loran pauses for a moment, then continues. Happy and Iandee report that it’s under control. House Siren’s ship has few active combatants. Now the vocal Skills are limited in effect, they hope to force a surrender in the near future. The shield enchantment will take more time to fix, of course.

It could be worse, I sigh. I look over at the other three ships. Trapped between Elarion’s, Layton’s, and Josiah’s ships, House Bladestrike’s vessel is looking even worse than Valence’s. Their shield obviously fell a while ago based on the amount of damage to the ship’s deck. And clearly they didn’t have a secondary shield – not that that is surprising.

It’s more surprising that Kyrian had one: from what the enchanters told me when the subject came up, every shield nestled inside an existing one has an exponential cost in both enchantment materials and ongoing mana maintenance. An emergency shield is another matter as that is only activated when the initial shield drops, sacrificing strength for speed of activation.

The reason for Bladestrike’s difficulties is clear: chunks of ice are thick in the water, and, even at this distance, I can see they’ve done plenty of damage at the water-line of the ship. I wouldn’t be surprised if there are leaks that the Water Mages on board are having to deal with. That’s a good way of distracting them from attacking the ships.

But I want to know why Elarion went to support that battle instead of helping me – we were supposed to sail against Kyrian together. If he’d been there, hammering at Kyrian’s shield and offering another distraction, the trap might not have closed on us the way it did!

I save my questions for later, though – Elarion is too far away now to be able to help with this battle, and he’s heavily involved in the current one. It’s possible he has a good explanation – for both of our sakes, I hope he does.

Possibly, he made a decision based on how vulnerable Josiah’s ship is – it is the weakest of all of our six ships and evidently lost its shield fairly early on. Elarion’s support has prevented Bladestrike from taking too much advantage of it and Josiah’s ship is staying far enough away that the House’s eponymous Skill is unable to bite through its masts – or anything else. I’m still irritated that that has come at a cost to my battle with the enemy leader.

Returning my attention to Kyrian’s ship, I feel unease stir within me. It hasn’t moved since Ivor rose into the air. The distance between it and my ship has increased drastically, yet Kyrian isn’t even trying to give chase. And I don’t imagine it’s because he can’t. After the last trap Kyrian, or his strategist, lured us into, I don’t want to underestimate them.

Loran, ask Captain Tolly to increase speed as much as possible.

Yes, Markus, Loran quickly acknowledges and I soon see the ship speed up more. This is the speed we would have used to outrun Kyrian’s ships if we’d decided to go down that route. Yet, as I gaze into the water below with my magical sight engaged, fear goes through me. Glancing up at the ship, I realise that there’s no way we can outrun this.

Back to the ship, Ivor, I urge, hoping that we won’t be too late. Loran, Alyna, we need any Classer with any sort of ability that can deflect or divert water to go now to the stern!

Both send me a quick acknowledgement.

What is it? Kalanthia asks, and I see her peering over the side of the ship, looking towards the stern.

A tidal wave, I inform her grimly and feel fear come from my Bonded humans on the ship too.

For the first time since we’ve been Bound, I feel fear go through the giant cat and I suddenly realise something: Kalanthia doesn’t only dislike the ocean – she’s also afraid of it. Understandably so given that the sea-bed is too far away even for her to control.

Don’t worry, I reassure her privately. I’m coming. You can enter the tattoo and avoid this whole thing.

She doesn’t reply in words, but with a sense of ashamed gratitude – like she’s grateful for the offer, but wishes she weren’t. I quickly send her a sense of understanding reassurance, but that is as much as I can dedicate to her right now – I need to make sure that we still have a ship after this.

Because with as much magic as I can see in the ocean, and the way the water is already gathering and growing, that isn’t guaranteed.

Perhaps I can pick apart the construct the way I did the sea serpent, I think to myself. I direct Ivor to fly closer – and to watch out for sea serpents that might suddenly explode out of the water.

Within less than a minute, I have concluded that it’s not possible. Not in the time before the wave grows high enough to have its own mass. Kyrian is clearly determined to take us out with this and has poured more mana that I would have thought possible into the construct. And a tsunami is a good target for such a tactic – no delicate touch necessary, no need to maintain control. When the wave is as big as this one is, we’re sitting ducks before the might of the ocean.

The only good thing is that, because of our positioning, it’s not affecting anyone else’s battle except to, perhaps, make the sea a bit more choppy.

I flick through a few other options for how to deal with this. It’s possible I could use Air-Shaping to help the other Classers cut through the wave and divide it onto either side of us. But I find that that doesn’t appeal. I’ve had enough of Kyrian, frankly, and I want to make a statement – to him, and to everyone else watching.

So, instead, I quickly swoop to the deck of the ship, pause for a few moments just to collect Kalanthia, and then take off into the air on my own wings. Ivor and Noir fly near me, knowing that they need to stay clear of the ship for now – just in case.

Just in the time it took me to do that, the wave has doubled in size. Now the crest of it reaches almost halfway up the masts – and it’s still growing. If this hits, it will wipe the deck clear of anything and anyone who’s not tied down. And probably a good number of things – and people – that are. That’s the best-case scenario. With magic involved, a wave as big as this one looks to become could sink us in one fell swoop.

Captain Tolly is no fool, nor are the rest of the people I have on board. Without enough time to avoid the ship or even to turn it so it faces the wave prow on, he’s instead made sure that it’s butt-end to the wave and has kept the ship moving at its fastest speed. At the same time, everyone has made sure they’re tied to something solid and are bracing for impact.

Aingeal, I need you, I call and the ball of fire zooms towards me. Of all my Bonded, he’s unable to enter my spatial tattoo so he had to find other places to hide himself away. This tidal wave poses a threat to him, almost as much as the Water Mages, but I can’t do this by myself.

Reducing my height until I’m almost level with the quarterdeck, I fix my eyes on the wave that has taken on a life of its own. It blocks out all view of everything beyond, extending high above my head and far out to either side.

Aingeal sends me a sense of curiosity, wondering what I’m doing. He’s not the only one – the humans on the ship are anxious to know too.

We need fire, I tell him grimly, beginning to gather it between my two palms. Lots, and lots of fire.

Aingeal, of course, is completely on board with that and soon comes to join the ball of fire that I’m forming, adding so much of his own that the ball doubles in size within seconds.

But it’s still not enough. Pouring in mana, I force the ball to increase in temperature. This needs to be white hot. Good thing I have plenty of burning anger at Kyrian’s unprovoked attack to pour in.

When my mana runs out, I draw from my companions. With them in a spatial tattoo held at the same time within me and somehow in another space, it’s never been easier. I quickly ask for permission first, but none of them, not even Kalanthia, deny me.

With access to more mana than I’ve ever had before, I create a blade of fire. And, as the wave crashes down upon us. I slice through it at the level of the quarterdeck.
