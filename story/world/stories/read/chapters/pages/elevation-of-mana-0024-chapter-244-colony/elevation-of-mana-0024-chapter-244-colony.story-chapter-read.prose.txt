Weeks we traveled, and weeks we suffered. Food was scarce, everyone was tired, even the dogs were getting snippy. This was the second time we broke to make a small camp for a few days, men going to hunt while everyone else recovered and gathered what easy to find forage we could. In our last camp we'd stopped to forage some large bushes, giving the women a sure way to get food while the men tried their luck with the local river and game.

I noticed that while this was happening there were no 'mysterious women sexy times' happening. That seemed to irk the men, but made them want to finish this trip, so there was clearly a reason. Bad moods were a thing all around these days, and I had the distinct feeling that they weren't going away anytime soon.

“This is better than we could have hoped,” mother said, going over to a group of trees, each with massive fruits hanging from them.

“It is indeed,” Ro agreed, reaching up and grabbing down one of the fruit.

“Good?” I asked as people began to move over, collecting the large things, each about the size of a mango, but very different in color and shape. They almost reminded me of the plant that had brought me to this world, though the plants themselves were very different.

“Very good little Op, you should try some,” he said, using a small sharp stone to slice one a little and handing me a piece, making my mother look at him as she'd been preparing to do much the same.

I took the piece and snacked on it. Strange flavors filled my mouth, mango, banana, and a number of other tropical fruits I couldn't quite place, it made me blink. Nothing I'd had here was quite like this, this tasted like something from near the equator, though we were well into a temperate zone. This fruit though, it was massive, and sweet, which was incredibly welcome.

“Why not eat more?” I asked, as we'd never had this fruit before. “Hard find?”“They don't stay ripe for long little Op,” he laughed. “Only a rare treat, and we don't see many.”“The older boys eat them all,” mother said.

“Haha, truth,” Ro replied with a smile. “Or give them to girls they like. Ah, I remember my younger days we would always...”

He got cut off by a few things being lightly chucked at him.

That short break was a good one, one where we had full bellies and good sleep, as the local climate was pleasant and calm.

Big Op and Ro talked as we walked, abandoning him or not the people of our group still respected the elder.

“Co told us that they odd shamans came up here somewhere to make a camp,” Big Op said.

“He seemed a good type, shame we couldn't get him to join,” the older man replied, lightly biting his tongue as he thought.

“Co didn't like any of our women, a shame, we could use more people overall,” Big Op nodded. “After those three left a few years back.”

“Regardless, this way somewhere? How sure was he?” Ro asked.

“Pretty sure, going to see if the shamans will fix your leg?”“Doubt they will, but if they will I'll ask. Think we'll have trouble finding them?”It was the next morning, when one of the men, Plu, went out to check our path for the day. He came back incredibly fast. Heading right for the gathering.

“I found something,” Plu.

“What?” Big Op asked.

“I... don't know.”“Well what's it look like?” the man who shared my name asked.

“Nothing I've seen, many shelters maybe, but strange?”Big Op went off to find it, eyebrows furrowed as he considered what we might find. He too came back thrown off, and seemed to want us all to move, and fast.

“The odd shamans are here, and we're not going to contest their territory. We're going to go around,” Big Op told the group. “Some of the other men and I have discussed it, we need to do this now.”“Why didn't you discuss this with us?” Pa asked, angry. Clearly she thought that the men should have consulted with the women before any decision was made.

“We cannot offend them, we need to move around politely and not walk on the ground they've claimed for their shelter. These shamans are more powerful than any I've seen before. If you wish to fight them, you may.” His piece said he began to gather up his things. Pa was not amused by his antics, though Ro didn't question them and began his packing up.

“Should have at least gone to see them. Shamans are useful, useful to know useful to...”

As she spoke the early morning leaves parted to one side, our group making it to a small break the little downward slope to our left side opening up so that we could see down into the nearby valley. That view alone silenced her without anything else.

She saw them, and I saw them too, elves, dozens of them at least, maybe a hundred or more, gathered under three dirigibles, massive flying things. Most of our group was looking at the larger than life balloons, while I paid attention to the valley much more.

Teams were moving back and forth, hauling stones, moving logs, cutting clear parts of the forest. This wasn't some small structure for shelter they were building. It was a city, a small one, sure, but still a city, and not a small one at that. There would be room for hundreds, maybe thousands of elves or men, whatever they intended to bring here. The wall going up wasn't a short term indicator anyway, whoever had done this, had decided that they were quite happy with the spot they'd chosen. I got the feeling sooner or later we were going to go in there and ask them, of that I had no doubt.

I could only hope they took the questions well.
