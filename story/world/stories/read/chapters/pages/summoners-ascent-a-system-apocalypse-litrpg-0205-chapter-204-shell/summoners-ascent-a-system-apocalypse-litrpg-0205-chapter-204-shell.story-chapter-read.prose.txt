The metal bar crushed the head of a goblin before it could even hear Ashton approach. He didn't have the silent step shoelaces anymore, but he still approached without making a single sound.

How? Neither Sam nor Clementine could believe what they were seeing, but upon having the monsters pointed out to him, Ashton ran forward and vaulted all the way over there. He had pushed his staff into the ground and catapulted himself in their direction with an agility and strength that neither of them could even believe.

Before the first goblin's body even properly slumped to the ground, Ashton swung the staff at the next one's neck and cleanly cracked its spine. The orc that was with the two goblins was taken out with a strike to the forehead that came with so much power, blood flowed from every orifice of its face as it fell.

"What the fuck, mate?" Sam finally reached Ashton. "How are you this strong right now?"

"Did you bring us here to show off?" Clementine asked, staring at the monsters on the ground. She knew that Ashton was strong, but this was still just utterly ridiculous.

With a slight sigh, Ashton squatted down and started harvesting the monsters' magic stones. "Of course I'm not here to show off. There are a few reasons why I asked you to come with me. One of them being that I want to explain this power to you."

Sam and Clementine both stared at Ashton. "Did you make some kind of deal with a demon?" Clementine wondered, and Ashton scoffed.

"Not in a hot second, no," he responded, handing the orc's magic stone to the Backpacker. "The way I handle power is different from you. If I wanted to, I could rely on the system completely to manipulate my mana for me for the summoning processes, or to have it channel my summons' powers for me. But I don't. I do everything manually. For the most part, at least. I still get some support from my skills."

"And that really makes so much of a difference?" Sam asked, still puzzled at the strength that Ashton was showing off. "You explained it before, and I've been trying to control the energy from my skills more, but..."

"Right, that's totally different to... this..." Clementine added.

Ashton smiled, nodded, and turned away, continuing to walk now that he collected the magic stones. "It is. What I've done is form an 'Internal Core', which I can use to refine my mana into another sort of energy. That same energy is what you guys use in your skills. The system is basically doing the step of refining mana for you. It can only be used in pre-defined ways. But, if you two form an internal core as well..."

"Then we can do way more. We can move like you do?" Sam glanced backward at the monsters' corpses, but was just met with some awkward silence. "Wait, no?"

Ashton shook his head. "No. At some point, maybe. But I've got like a century of practice on you. The techniques I've practiced are powerful, but it took me a long time to master them. But I also have to admit; the system is fucking insane. It uses a fraction of the energy to perform 'techniques' in the form of skills, and it doesn't require nearly as much control as it does for me."

"Uh..." Clementine started. "If that's the case, why exactly do you want us to form that internal core thingy?"

"Because the only thing that the system is inefficient at is the actual conversion from mana to internal energy. The system itself runs on mana, and all the complex functions and whatever drain some of it. The more complex the function, the more mana it uses. Converting mana to internal energy without a core is an incredibly complex process," Ashton explained, smiling broadly. "So what do you think would happen if you did have an internal core? One that the system can access directly?"

Sam and Clementine finally understood.

"All of our skills would cost less mana to use?" Sam scratched his beard, thinking slightly about the way he fought so far. "My strike skill is something I don't use as often as I'd like. It gives me those ridiculous headaches after a while."

Clementine agreed, quickly catching up to Ashton. "Same with my running skill... I use it mostly in emergencies or to help me avoid monsters. It makes me insanely fast, but it also exhausts me a ton. So that would change with an internal core?"

"Mm-hmm. I think with the low first stage, it would probably improve your efficiency at least two or three times over. But most importantly, if you can refine energy yourselves, you can feed it into your skills, and improve the function. Plus, because it's your own energy, you will be able to manipulate the expression of your skills far more, too." Ashton stopped, holding up a hand. He was hearing some monsters nearby, so he lowered his voice. "Now, the system will probably create a core for you sooner or later anyway. Probably around level 30, I guess? It depends on the class. But the earlier you have a core, the faster you can grow it, and the more advanced the benefits you can get from the system will be over time."

"Right, everything stacks up." Clementine nodded. Both her and Sam were clearly thinking about how much stronger they could get. Clementine wasn't a combatant for now, but even she was thinking about getting a combat class as her second. And if she could be stronger—faster—then running away from monsters like earlier wouldn't be her only choice anymore.

"So? What do we have to do? To form these cores?" Sam immediately cut to the chase. "If you only did this now, I doubt it's easy."

Ashton shrugged. He used the gnome to drag up some asphalt from the ground before turning it into a number of spikes. "It's not actually that hard. You just need to have enough mana. Once you do, I'll be able to help you form your cores. But for that, we need you guys to level up."

The trio waited around the corner, waiting for the monsters to pass by and get into his channelling range. The moment they did, Ashton channelled the gnome through his staff, spinning it around to shoot the asphalt spikes into the monsters' heads.

Sam stared at the corpses of the monsters on the ground. "And how are we supposed to do that if you kill everything before we can do anything at all?"

"Hm? Oh, no, I didn't bring you out to help you guys level up. We'll do that some other time. Though, to be honest, you've got that all covered pretty well," Ashton pointed out. There was nothing wrong with their levelling speed. It was actually pretty fast from what he could tell, not that he actually knew all too much about normal speeds. "I just thought there were a few things we had to talk about."

Clementine groaned loudly. "And we couldn't do that at Calibur?"

"No." Ashton turned toward Clementine clearly. "We couldn't. It's been... clear to me that you guys still see me as the same person that I was before all of this. And I'm not."

Sam scoffed. "Well, you're a lot more blunt, but you're the same weird-ass guy that you were just a few weeks ago from my point of view."

Without hesitation, Clementine agreed. "Right, I know that your time in Lumia was hard for you, but that doesn't mean you're not the same. You're different, but—"

"I couldn't remember you," Ashton interrupted her. "Neither of you. My memory isn't normal, and I'm pretty sure it's been like this from birth. And at some point, I suppressed it all. Things have been unlocking more and more. But Sam, Clem... when I came back, I didn't remember you."

"... What? But you..." Clementine laughed out confused, not quite sure if she should believe Ashton.

"When I met you again, and spoke to you, it came back to me. But I didn't remember you until then. Not you, nor this town, not even my own parents' names. For a century, I had cut myself off from this world, because I didn't think I would ever make it back here," Ashton replied. "For a while, I was just 'Human'. That was my name. Then I got closer to some of the other summons. A couple could speak, but it took decades for me to hear someone say my name again. And you know what happened to them?"

Sam already knew the answer. He glanced at Clementine, who just kept staring at Ashton confused.

"The Summoner killed Oka. Ripped them apart, to punish me for falling in love. For having any part of me belong to someone else, even if it was just my heart," Ashton smiled, slightly tilting his head. "Do you know what that does to a person? Do you know what it feels like to have your soul literally ripped from your body? What it's like to kill people, no matter who they were, because I would feel nothing but unimaginable pain if I didn't follow that creature's command?"

Poppy sat on Ashton's shoulder, leaning her head against him. The elementals came closer, pushing themselves against his body as well. Even the Backpacker had come out, and was holding onto Ashton's cloak. Just as he could feel what they did, they felt what he did.

"I'm not the person you once knew. I'm a shell, filled with the memories of the person named 'Ashton Garling'. The love I feel for you two is... almost suffocating. But you two love someone that doesn't exist anymore." Ashton smiled lightly, turning his head away. "And I need you to realise that. The choices I make, the things I say and do and want... those do not belong to the awkward kid you grew up with."

Clementine took a nervous step forward. She was breathing fast, and her hand was shaking, stopping herself from reaching out to Ashton. "So what, you just... you want us to just treat you like a stranger now? You want us to act like the old you is dead?"

"How are we supposed to do that? Ash, you're our best friend, we can't do this without you. And I don't fucking care what you say, but the old you is still in there," Sam said through grinding teeth.

"Maybe. But would you say that you are the same as your six year old self?" Ashton forced out a laugh. "Maybe in a few ways. Some important, fundamental ways. But the way you interact with the world, and the way you see it, those aren't the same."

Sam groaned loudly. "Mate, what are you saying, then? That you're going to help us get stronger, but that's it? That you'll treat us just like you do everyone else? All three of us know that you can't do that."

Ashton took off his blindfold. He wanted Sam and Clementine to see his entire face, without anything blocking him. They both grimaced slightly. Ashton felt his throat tighten.

"Yeah, I can't. Which is why I'm going to tell you about myself," Ashton said. The smile on his face wasn't fake this time. "About who I became in Lumia. About the powers I learned about, like the internal core, and the relationships I formed. I can't tell you every single story, or every single detail, of course... but I can tell you the things that changed me the most."

Ashton pushed his staff into the ground, as the summons continued trying to comfort him. Almost creating a shield around him. "And that's going to start with us going to see the Ancient Constructs, and whatever it is they're protecting. And in the process..." Ashton slowly focused on Clementine. He knew that it was cruel to say this, but he couldn't lie to these two, "I'll tell you about Oka. The love of my life, and for a brief while... my spouse."
