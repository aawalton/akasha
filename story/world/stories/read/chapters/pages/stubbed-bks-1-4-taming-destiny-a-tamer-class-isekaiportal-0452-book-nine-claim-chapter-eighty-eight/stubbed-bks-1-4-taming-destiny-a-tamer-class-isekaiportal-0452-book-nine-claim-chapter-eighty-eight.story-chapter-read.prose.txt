Walking towards the lines of men and women, Valence notices the nervousness practically emanating off them. He’d asked his captain to direct the ironshields to identify those in their ranks they felt were deserving of Class stones, even if they hadn’t quite earned them yet, but not to tell those they nominated exactly what they had been selected for. As a result, he’s sure that those present can’t help but wonder if they have been selected for a special – and dangerous – task.

He comes to a stop, level with the centre, a few paces away from the closest fighters, and just in front of the group of ironshields who are here to see their armsmen advance.

Pulling out a basket of tell-tale stones from his Inventory, he almost feels the nervousness turning to anticipation as those before him put the pieces together. If they were stupid, they wouldn’t have been nominated, after all. He fights back a smile of anticipation himself. He’s learned that rewards are far more pleasant to dole out than punishments.

After placing the basket down on the ground in front of him, he straightens and tucks his arms behind him. He lets the pause draw out, allowing anticipation to build even as the lines of soldiers remain dead silent.

The pause might be for the soldiers, but Valence can’t help but use it to reflect on himself as well. When he first set off from his father’s house, he’d travelled with a retinue of grizzled soldiers. He’d felt shamed by the weight of their experience, like a boy playing soldiers while surrounded by professionals.

Now, he’s a veteran himself and there are those in the ranks far less experienced than he is. His eyes linger on those with silver collars around their necks – slaves who see distinguishing themselves in battle as a way of earning a better life for themselves. He finds himself oddly proud to see them here before him now, selected by their ironshield for a reward some would kill for.

Valence hadn’t been all that convinced about Markus’ approach to slaves in general, but he’d done his best to follow his liege-lord’s direction. Though frustrating in many ways, he’s been rather surprised at how many benefits he’s seen from adjusting the way he views slaves – as people rather than the objects they legally are.

From some slaves volunteering to accomplish tasks he never thought they had the skills for, to others volunteering to become a fighter and proving themselves to be among the most hard-working of all the soldiers, it seems paying the slaves according to how much benefit they bring the settlement is far more effective in motivating them than the threat of punishment if they do not perform.

Valence has even discovered that, as long as he offers sufficient recompense, women actually compete for his amorous attentions – and when they come to his bed, it’s far more satisfying when the woman wants to be there than it had been when she’d been ordered there and feared punishment if she resisted him. It’s got to the point where some of them have actually approached him. And not only women….

Valence can’t help thinking of a certain pair of blonds who have been actively pursuing him – the server he’d apparently proposed to while drunk and a woman he’s already spent multiple nights with.

A cough from someone in the ranks ahead of him draws his attention back to the affair at hand. He beats back the heat that begins to rise in his cheeks. He has people to award with stones and then they have a battle to fight.

Still, when they emerge victorious from the conflict, perhaps he can arrange a little private celebration afterwards. Maybe he’ll even conjure up the courage to invite both blonds….

“You may have guessed why you are here today,” Valence starts abruptly, as much to centre his own thoughts where they should be as to grip the attention of the soldiers watching him. “You have been nominated by your ironshields as soldiers who have distinguished yourselves either in the field of battle, the training field, or both.”

As he speaks, his eyes wander up and down the neat lines standing before him. Ten soldiers wide; three deep. It’s a little more than he was expecting – just as well he brought extra Class stones.

It’s odd – once, all he would have seen would have been commoners. Now, he sees men and women who he has fought beside, bled beside. He sees no one here he doesn’t believe deserves a Class stone, even if it is a little premature for some of them. Still, needs must, and he agrees with Markus that it is better to hand out Class stones now – they need all the help they can get and a battle like this is likely to be worth a few levels.

"I am aware that we would normally hold a ceremony at this point, celebrating the hard work and distinction that has earned you a Class stone. However, given the crisis currently impacting our settlement and how short time is right now, we will save the ceremony for after we return triumphant."

The people assembled before him don't seem at all put out by the absence of pomp. They simply look eager to finally be given the opportunity to grow beyond mortal limits.

Valence waits a moment, then nods. "I shall call your names one by one. Come forward to receive your stone." He consults the piece of parchment he had been given earlier that has the list of names.

"Kera Mitts."

The woman on the furthest left of the front row steps forward immediately, eagerness clear on her face.

From behind him, an ironshield steps forward. Valence recognises him as Mitts’ commanding officer, Ironshield Hillian.

Valence turns slightly towards him, raising an eyebrow.

“Mitts is an archer, my lord,” Ironshield Hillian informs him. In this case, Valence didn’t need the information – he joined her shieldwall a tenday ago while they were scouting the forest so he knows exactly what she’s capable of. Still, there are faces in the crowd that are only vaguely familiar, and seeming to know more about some than others might risk seeming like favouritism.

So, Valence gives the process its due, nodding solemnly at Ironshield Hillian and then turning back to Mitts.

"For your skill in archery and your constant dedication to improving yourself through practice and experience, I award you an Archer Class stone." He leans forward and grabs one of the stones – fortunately, they’re divided into sections that make them easy to identify.

Mitts takes the stone with reverence in her expression and in her touch.

"Don't activate it until you're back in line," Valence instructs her. "We have many others to get through."

Normally, of course, it would be Markus handing out the stones, and the recipient would receive their Class then and there in front of everyone. When fewer stones were being awarded, they'd even had the chance to demonstrate a couple of their newest Skills – particularly those that built on what they already had. However, with as little time as they have available before they intend to set off, such demonstrations are out of the question.

Still, Mitts doesn't seem to mind. She's staring at the stone with something close to awe, apparently barely having heard him.

"Sera Mitts, please return to your place," Valence instructs, his tone sharpening slightly.

She looks up, startled – perhaps at both the reminder and the change in address. A little premature, perhaps, since she has not absorbed the Class stone yet, but Valence feels it’s deserved.

“Of course, my lord," Mitts replies, bowing a fraction deeper than usual in apology, then hurries back into line.

Valence calls the next name.

By the time the last armsman has been awarded his stone, Valence is tired of repeating name after name. To the credit of the last ones to receive their reward, they didn’t stir from their positions until called, showing appropriate discipline.

While the last few are absorbing their class stones, Valence turns to his second-in-command, who approached him only a few moments ago.

"Is everything else prepared, Captain Mul?"

The captain nods sharply.

"Aye, sir. A squad of Butchers are ready to travel with us and the rest of our fighters are assembling near the gate as we speak. Our scouts have sent back warnings that another beast wave is preparing to depart.”

“How long until it sets off?” Valence asks, concerned.

“Any flicker now, from their reports,” the captain relates efficiently.

Valence taps his thigh thoughtfully. A beast wave might actually be beneficial for them – their biggest challenge is not getting overwhelmed with all the parasite-ridden beasts ahead of them. However, they’d need to make sure they hit it in an advantageous area.

“Does Lord Markus know?” he checks with Mul.

“Captain Maxen has apparently passed on the message. Lord Markus is still with the healers, however Captain Maxen indicates that he wishes to leave as soon as possible and meet the beast wave while it’s in the forest. He anticipates departing in a quarter candlemark.”

A quartermark…. That should give Valence just enough time for his own preparations.

“What of the other lords?” Valence checks.

“Lords Josiah and Layton are with their unit already. Lord Elarion is still giving final orders to the non-combatants but his captain has indicated he will be ready soon.”

"Good,” he tells Mul with a sharp nod. “See that these newest Classers are equipped with weapons and armour appropriate to their new stations, then send them to assemble with the other shieldwalls. I'll meet you at the main gates – I need to equip myself first."

"Yes, sir."

Valence strides away, the sound of the captain barking orders at the new Classers ringing in his ears even after he's put some distance between them. Ideally, they'd have time to experiment with their new Skills before going into the field. However, this situation is far from ideal. At least Valence knows they'll each be paired with a more experienced Classer of a similar type – they’ll have to learn as they go. With his scouts reporting another beast wave on the way, there is no time to spare.

He makes a brief stop at his accommodation. His enchanted sword, Indomitable, and his carefully-crafted armour would lose their imbuements if placed in his Inventory, so he had left them in his room for safekeeping.

If he'd been back home, he would have considered the small apartment in the main command building meagre and unbefitting of his station. But given that Markus himself has no better, Valence recognises he can hardly complain. He is, however, rather looking forward to establishing his own residence and returning to something closer to the comfort he is accustomed to. That will only be possible once things have stabilised – and dealing with this Tier four that dares to challenge them is the first step toward it.

He presses his palm to the enchanted storage closet and it opens with a click, revealing the armour and sword in pride of place. Nerves are beginning to coil in his belly. It’s ridiculous – they’ve already fought and won against a Tier four. The prospect of fighting another shouldn’t induce any fear in him.

Yet it does. Valence swallows, remembering advice his father has repeated many times about entering a battle: be calm and still; let chaos rage without you and not within. The enemy can only hurt you if you allow them to.

Though he knows that it’s not quite as simple as that, he feels slightly more centred. Brushing his fingers across his armour and sword in turn, he checks that the Skills he imbued into them the previous night are still present and strong. Respecting his limits, he has only three skills imbued in the sword and four in the armour. Feeling that the armour has lost a little mana, he presses more into it.

Then he kneels before the armour stand, facing the effigy of Warrior carven into it. He closes his eyes.

"Warrior," he murmurs quietly but fervently. "Grant me shelter for my allies, fear in the hearts of my enemies, and conduct becoming of your blessing. Please, bless me in my battles today and, if I am to die today, let it be with honour."

As always, there is no response, yet Valence feels warmed nonetheless. Whether that sense comes from Warrior himself, or simply from the completion of a ritual that has been part of his family for as long as anyone can remember, he has never been entirely certain. Few in their family have had the honour of being spoken to by the god directly, but Warrior remains their patron, having blessed Valence's ancestor with the insight that allowed the development of their family Class.

His prayer complete, Valence stands, retrieves a candle, and lights the lamp set before Warrior's carved face. He leaves the cupboard open – Warrior will protect it from any pilfering hands, unless he withdraws his blessing. And if he does so, Valence will need to worry about more than thieves. His father’s expression had been answer enough when he’d asked about what happened in that case.

He was glad he hadn't performed this ritual before his duel with Markus. He fears he might have lost Warrior's favour for that affair given how dishonourable it had turned out to be. If ever Valence regretted anything, it was falling for Lord Torrent's deceptions.

Despite himself, Valence smiles with satisfaction as he recalls the humiliation that Torrent’s son suffered for pursuing his father’s vendetta against the Titanbends. A shame that Markus decided against a Bond in the end, though he cannot fault his liege lord for obeying the king’s law.

Still, if Kyrian had experienced a Bond the way Valence did, he was sure the younger heir would have quickly changed his tune.

Pushing thoughts of Kyrian out of his mind, Valence draws upon that inner sense of peace, dons his armour, slides Indomitable into the scabbard on his back, and strides out of his accommodation, moving as fast through the streets as he dares. He took longer than he’d intended, and has no desire to be the one holding up the expedition.

As the gates and the forces arrayed before it come into sight, Valence feels nerves curdle in his belly. However powerful they might be, this is a Tier four.

Then he draws on that sense of peace and increases his pace even more. He is a warrior – he will not shy away from battle. He will bring honour to his House, and fight until he and his allies find victory.

He refuses to consider anything else.
