Iru and I were enough to stop the worst of the injuries. Not a full healing, that would take us time, but stabilization, and making sure infection was no longer a serious concern for those who were hurt. Most of it was pretty straightforward at least, holes from spears or arrows, a few breaks from clubs and the like. These were the common injuries of our people when we fought.

Fighting was, sadly common among the tribes. It wasn't that every time groups met they would go to combat, but rather that they too often wanted similar territories and didn't want to move. Unfortunate, but the way of the world. The strong took what they desired while the weak held all they could.

“Hold still, it will be over soon,” I said to a man as I worked.

“Ah, it itches...”“Yes, I've heard that, something about how things fit back together,” I told him with a tired smile.

“Be quiet, what if he were to decide not to heal you,” a nearby woman chided angrily, his mother perhaps, no, maybe an older sister, most people lost their mothers by his age.

“It's fine,” I told her. “But you'll all be a bit sore for awhile yet, probably, we'll have to see what everyone says.”

“Ugh, I don't want to have to do rounds tomorrow,” I complained to Iru, since we'd have to do them regardless, if for no other reason than to keep up the crops at current levels.

“You can go back to town and tell miss Adia while I do them,” he suggested.

“Alone?” I asked.

Another twenty minutes of back and forth, of arguing over who would go where and stay where and we finally came to some conclusion that we could all stand. Some of the locals with with Iru. Some stayed with the newcomers while I returned to the city with the a couple of the refugees. That may have seemed a bit dangerous, but none of them had an aura and I was pretty sure I could wreck them with magic, not that it would come to that.

“I see you cut your rounds short, and brought visitors,” Adia said as we were led into her meeting room.

“Seemed more important than standard round.”

“Trust in your judgment on this account Op, you've earned my trust in this and much more over the years,” she said to me. “What brings you?” she asked the newcomers.

“Greetings great shaman, I am Lee, song of Lee,” that was about as formal of an introduction as we ever saw, though most of my tribe didn't keep track of parentage. “I come seeking aid, we were thrown from our lands by others, who were driven from theirs by monsters.”“How polite, please explain to me what you can,” she said.

“It began nearly three moons ago, and slowly at first. A few scouts, from tribes further east than our own. These men were seen, but kept away when we tried to approach. We worried, and gathered ourselves together, but we did not expect what was coming. It happened during a hunting trip. My tribe was attacked by over twenty men, armed with spears and clubs they came as we cleaned our kill for the day and set upon us. We lost three before we drove them back but they fought like angry boars, eyes full of fear and hunger and rage.”“And you brought your injured here?”“No, we pulled back, others were friends to us, and we sought their aid first. But what we found concerned me. Three tribes we sought to aid us against those who'd attacked, one came without question. Another had suffered an attack much like the one on us and was damaged. The third...”“Tell me,” Adia insisted.

“While the hunters were away they attacked the camp, taking the women. The men of that tribe were frenzied, had gone looking but couldn't find them. What happened I still don't know, but we believe they took them back to wherever they were hiding. There are hills just to the eastern reaches of our lands, and they are full of places we couldn't easily search.”

The room went cold, not physically, but from the way Adia's face sat, angry, hard, I was quite sure the same expression was on my own countenance. There were reasons, and this world was harsh, but there were few things to do with kidnapped women.

“Why have they done this?” she asked, voice like steel. “Was there some issue with them?”“We had no issue with them previously and spoke little in the past. They lived in the mountains, we in the forest, some trade, but little passing the peaks is hard and only by doing so can you get to the valleys within, where there is food. We did manage to capture one. He told us that they had been beset upon by some form of monster, one that took the faces of others. Many of the tribes in the mountains had been lost, or had lost many of their own.”“That would be the reason they took them, but if it were multiple tribes, surely they had some weapon against their attacker. With more than one there should have been a shaman or two to fight,” I pointed out.

“From what I have heard the creature sought out their shamans itself, hunting them particularly and viciously. It seemed drawn to them, slaughtered them, and broke the rest of the group. At least that is what the one we captured reported,” he admitted.

“Where is the captive? I would speak with him,” Adia said.

The man was silent for a moment, looking away from her from the first time. That answer was enough, and honestly I wasn't sure if I could blame him.

“I was not the only one there, and the others were not merciful. Had I known he would be of further use we might have convinced them to hold off, but then again, perhaps not, they were for good reason very angry.”“Unfortunate, but understandable. We may need to send someone to deal with this group, then bring our power to bear against whatever monster is there.”

“If aid can be given even to our people that would be more than we deserve,” Lee said, hopefully.“I could go,” I offered, seeing this as a good chance to support people who did seem to need it.

“Yes, but not alone. I can contact some of the other cities, but they may not be able to spare many, and it may take time.” She sighed, rubbing her eyes. “Though they may resist as my father is making his once a decade return soon they're being prickly. Don't want to look bad in front of the boss, same reason I can't go either...”“We'll figure something out,” I offered. “It's our duty to protect the people after all.”
