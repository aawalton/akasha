Chapter 197 / B3-42: The Power of Oaths

Seated in the stately study, the four [Aspirants of the Spring Crown], overseen by the silent falconer in the corner, considered Dorian’s question about who wanted the crown. Joe was still torn about how he should answer. Thankfully, he didn’t have to go first.

“I, for one,” Myllo began, “am bowing out of the selection. I’ve got too many responsibilities here in Fort Coral and way too many kin across the Veil in Winter’s Court of Crows to have any desire to take over Spring.”

“Not for me either,” Margen announced. “I’ve had too many dealings with the Feyfolk, and the only reason I hadn’t run afoul of them was because I had Tinze at my side. That clever bugger could see through their muddle of honeyed words and kept us from getting trapped into bargains I didn't even know were on the table. Give me men and a field to fight on, and I’m fine, but I was never the founder called on for the delicate negotiations.”

“Wise,” Dorian agreed. “The deep ties both of you have with the city would embroil Fort Coral into the conflict to come. Both Joe and I are only surface members of this community. The queen’s war on either of us should only impact Fort Coral tangentially.”

The master trader leveled his gaze on Joe and waited. The look made Joe squirm not only from the force of that discerning regard but because he still was not sure about his answer. He decided to reiterate what he had told Kenda.

“I’m not sure what I should do, sir. I really don't want the crown, but I would love to borrow it for just a bit before I’d be happy to give it to you. There is something I think I need to do, and the only way I feel I’ll be able to accomplish the task is with the power of the Spring Throne.”

Both Myllo and Margen looked completely surprised by Joe’s statement, and Joe didn't blame them for it one bit. He was a low-level healer with only a faint trace of a fey heritage. By any sane logic, he should have been the first one eliminated from the race. Yet Joe was certain down to his bones that the key to removing the curses shackling the chimeras was in conjunction with the Crown of the Spring King.

“You just have to love changelings,” the mogul laughed. “They jig every time you expect them to jag. Are you free to explain what this task is, Joe?”

“I can try.” Joe decided to start from the beginning of what he knew, hoping these men might fill in some more details for him. “From what I understand, back in the early days of Anwnn and Illuminaria, the Oak Baron, who was a treant at that time, not the talking tree he is now, created the chimera for a war between the two worlds. The problem with them was that the chimera were too unruly. They wouldn’t fight alongside the other army. It sounds like they were just as happy to fight with their supposed allies as they were with the enemies of the fey.”

“That is true,” the leprechaun agreed. “The Folly War that Never Was. The royals lost a great deal of standing with that fiasco. The queens wanted someone to blame for it, and so began the animosity between the feyfolk and the chimeras.”

Dorian’s words brought to mind the encounter between the hounds and Finn. “So, there’s more than just a curse to punish the chimeras.”

“I wouldn’t say more. Rather, it is another aspect of the same curse. The Morrigu had her champion make sure that not only would the beasts suffer some affliction from the malison, but also that they would never be accepted in the Gossamer Lands. All full-blooded fey feel the hex-wrought wrath Devilbone Kava worked into her terrible curse. So the beasts were driven from Annwn into Illuminaria, never to return. Granted, here they have thrived in numbers even if they have known no peace from the hag’s hex.”

“Do you think the curse can be broken? The whole curse, or would it have to be one at a time?”

“I do not know much about hagcraft and their banes,” Dorian admitted. “Yet I can see why you are reluctant to reject the [Mantle of Spring]. It is one tool that would give you enough authority to possibly accomplish your desire.”

The short magnate drummed his fingers on his armrest, considering Joe. “Before we delve any deeper into our collective ascension, I’m curious about how much you know about Annwn, young man?”

“Almost nothing, Master Dorian. I have heard the first two tales of Annwn, but other than that, just rumors.”

“Interesting. Most folks learn of the Gossamer Lands the other way around. Tidbits first, then the tales. Alright. I think the most important thing to know is that the lands are opposites in one crucial aspect. Here in the Land of Stone, the world is bound by rules, and yet its people are not. It is the inverse in Annwn.”

Even though it was clear the master trader had planned to elaborate, Joe must have looked confused enough that Dorian held up a finger to pause any questions.

“Here in Illuminaria, the world marches along at a steady measured pace. Twenty-four hours make a day, roughly half of which is light and the other half dark. The seasons dutifully follow each other in order. Power is codified by attributes and skills. Water flows downwards, and fire burns upwards.

“Yet its people are not so bound. A person can make a pledge they have no intention of keeping. Kings can issue decrees, and yet their subjects can choose whether or not to follow those edicts. They may be punished for breaking these laws, but it is still in their power to be unlawful.

“This is not how Annwn works. The Gossamer Lands are a realm unbound by order. A fleet runner could pass through regions of winter snows, autumn russet foliage, fields of spring flowers, and bask under a summer sun all on the same day’s journey and in any order. One could spend hours supping with a friend and return home after only minutes have passed … or days. Power is determined by will, not numbers. If a fern’s yearning is sufficiently potent, it can become a stag if it desires to be. If a place wants to remain hidden, no casual wanderer will ever stumble across it.

“Yet, its denizens are bound by word and wyrds. An oath is not enforced by the gods; it is binding simply by being spoken. The feyfolk cannot choose not to follow the commands of their lords or break a law that binds them. Doing so would rupture their essence. They would no longer be the being that they were before their transgression, for a fey cannot be a lawbreaker. They would have to become something else or cease to exist.”

“So you cannot lie?” Joe asked.

“That depends,” Myllo replied. “Outright lies are not spoken by fey, especially in the Feylands, unless the occasion requires lies. If all agree to tell tall tales, then the truth of a story is free to be embellished. More often than not, when we wish to hide unwelcome facts, the truths are obscured by misleading phrases, not true falsehoods.”

“It is an art among my people. Words have power among the Children of Arawn in the same way codification defines those shaped by Robin Goodfellow,” Dorian remarked.

“But I have identified feyfolk and seen classes and levels,” Joe countered, instinctively assessing Dorian, only to hit a nearly blank wall. Much like how everyone else saw his identification before [Ideal Alias], he just saw,

Dorian Barrburrow

and nothing more.

The leprechaun smiled slightly, likely having sensed Joe’s attempt. “Here on Illuminaria, the Goodfellow … sorry, that is an old habit that springs up when speaking of my homeland. As I was saying, here on Illuminaria, The One Above applies his system to us. We don’t actually have classes; what you are seeing are titles. The numeric levels are Robin’s estimates based on his measuring rod. In the Feylands, determining who or what you face is not simply ascertained by an identification skill. A wisp in the wood may be the feeble spirit it appears to be, yet it could just as easily be a mighty elder fey. There will be no convenient assessment to tell you which is which.”

“Which is why it is difficult for either folk to spend long durations back and forth across the Veil,” Myllo stated. “Days or weeks are not an issue, but months or moons will cause the shift. Classes become titles, and levels become essence. Each time the shift is imperfect, and more often than not, power is lost. This is why people choose to live in one land or another. I was born in Sneachd Saoi, the High Drift Hall of Lore. I visit my kin a few times a year, but I have been here in Illuminaria so long that it is uncomfortable for me to stay in the Gossamer Lands for more than a week or so.”

“The same holds true for us if we spend too long across the Veil,” Margen added. “I knew a man named Gildust whose apple orchard became a feyroad. He spent so much time tending his trees across Veil that eventually he found he was no longer a man of Illuminaria, but Gildust the Apple Keeper, a being of the Feylands.”

“Do you have a bit more of a picture now, Joe?” Dorian asked.

“I think so,” he replied, knowing he had only the barest bones of a concept of the world across the Veil.

“Then is this still something you wish to pursue? You will be dealing with beings you cannot categorize and playing by rules unlike any you have ever known. I cannot stress how dangerous this course will be. The queens have held the Feylands with only each other to contend with for two hundred years. They will not be happy should a king, or more likely both kings, return. There is a balance to the rule of Annwn. I believe that should the Spring Throne be occupied once again, the Oakbaron will have no choice but to reclaim his throne or abdicate in lieu of another.”

Joe knew that Finn, his podmates, and every other chimera were constantly suffering under the curse of Devilbone Kava. Could he really stand back and not try to break it? He had not yet attempted to unravel the curse from Finn specifically. Yet, even if he could break the banes one at a time, he knew it would bother him that every other chimera was being punished from the moment it was born because of an ancient bout of spite ordered by the Queen of Winter.

The Mark of Death was telling him he had a chance to make a real change in the worlds. Chimeras might be monsters, but that didn't mean they were evil creatures. The akhluts had almost no regard for boundaries, but Joe had sensed that they were not intrinsically wicked or malevolent. Their idea of fun just happened to be rougher than any sane being wanted to endure. That and they were driven to hunt constantly to gain any respite from the ravenous nature of their curse.

Joe realized that maybe other chimera could actually become allies. In folklore, griffins were noble creatures. Some sphinxes were benevolent beings who guarded sacred places. What about a creature like a pegasus? Joe couldn’t imagine leaving something that majestic tormented by the hag’s ancient bane.

He took a deep breath and blew it out before locking eyes with the shrewd tycoon. “I’d like to stay in the running. At least until I can do this one thing. After that, it’s all yours, sir.”

“So be it. I like your style, my new friend. It is a sign of character that you would pick up a fight that was not your own because it is the right thing to do.” Dorian drummed his fingers on the armrest of his chair again; this time, he seemed more excited than contemplative. “The good news is I know exactly what our first step must be. We will present our case at the Royal Moot.  Moots are held when the seasons are turning. Since we are already into summer, that means the next moot will be on Lughnasadh, when summer and autumn meet. That gives us two and a half months to prepare. The queens will be unable to act against us until after the moot. Even then, if we play our hand well, it is likely we can get an even longer stay of war. I have no doubt they will come against us in time. Our first priority will be to gain as much time as we can.”

“Won’t the queen just send assassins, instead of giving us time to prepare?” Margen asked.

“You are thinking of Illuminaria politics,” Myllo pointed out. “Remember the fey are bound by their laws and their oaths. Once a moot is called by someone with the authority to do so, the queens will be completely unable to deny the laws of the moot. They may find small ways to harass us, but they will be prohibited from any truly overt acts of aggression.”

“Exactly. Therefore, I hereby pledge to work in accord with ye, Joe Morris,” the tattooed man spoke in earnest, his voice taking on a more accented tone, as if he were speaking in the formal tongue of his folk. “We be true and allied until such time as we unbind this contract. Such is my word as your brothered heir to the Mantel of Spring.”

“Um. I swear the same,” Joe replied, quickly realizing that that small confirmation was not enough. “I will be your ally and fellow heir to the title until such time as I relinquish my claim.”

Myllo clapped Joe on the shoulder and, in the same tone and style as Dorian, spoke the words that forfeited his claim to the Spring Throne. Margen did a better job than Joe of mirroring the archaic style of speech. He too abandoned the [Aspirant of the Spring Crown] quest.

They spoke for a few more minutes until the busy master trader announced he had other pressing matters to attend to. It was delivered politely, but was an inarguable dismissal. The leprechaun noble informed the others that he would call for them again when he had word back from the Feylands.

Joe left with Myllo and Margen feeling far better about this whole quest than he had since Hawking dropped it on him. The knowledge that a war with the queens seemed inevitable was daunting, yet he was fairly confident the canny Master Barrburrow would find a way to swing the odds in their favor before the time for hostilities arrived.
