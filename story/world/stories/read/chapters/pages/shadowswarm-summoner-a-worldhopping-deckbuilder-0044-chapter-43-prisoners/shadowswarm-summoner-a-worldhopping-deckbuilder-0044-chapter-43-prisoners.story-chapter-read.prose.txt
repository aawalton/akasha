Chapter 43: Prisoners

“Why would we give them water?” Ambrose asked, not bothering to hide his confusion. “They’re not surviving this.”

The boy let out a whimper and retreated until his back was against the fallen ship. The knife remained between him and the world, trembling.

Luthor shot Ambrose a look. “They are obviously in no condition to fight, and as we are not cold blooded murderers, we’re going to have a nice, long talk about what they need to do in order to be released. You two do want to live, right? What are your names?”

Then he understood. Luthor would be the kind, reasonable one, and Ambrose would call for their blood and make it clear what the consequences for refusal would be. He wasn’t opposed to the role, but a bit of warning would have been nice. Scoffing, he summoned his imp over to land on the ship above the teenager.

The two of them squeaked in near-panic. “I’m Dexi,” cried the bronze dragon-goat with white hair down to her mid-back. “Dexi’Lynn! I’ll do whatever you want. I’m not a fighter, I’m just a mechanic. They don’t even let me have combat cards!”

“Dexi, you traitor,” the boy gasped. “Don’t you dare.”

“Stow it, Jack. I’m not dying for you or anyone.” She bared sharp, pointed teeth at him and hissed.

Jessica stepped forward. “Jack, is it?” Her voice was soft, reassuring. As if the boy was ten years younger instead of only five. “Are you the pilot? I’m a pilot too, though I’m kind of new at it.”

Ambrose snorted and moved over to stand beside Raeva. “This is anti-climactic.”

“Shh,” she said, with a quick elbow to his ribs. “The best fights are the ones you win ahead of time. Besides, maybe they can help us.”

Jack finally lowered the knife. Not all the way, but enough to show relief and at least a little trust. “Y-yeah. Well, I’m also kind of new. But I’ve been learning fast, Ian even said so. Before you…” The initial trust turned to grief and rage.

That must’ve been their leader, who she squished, Ambrose mused. “Maybe this Ian shouldn’t have tried to kill us,” he said loudly. “Remember that, kid. He attacked us to sell us to some guy named Raynor. Who’s that?”

Jack’s jaw set, and the flicker of their campfire almost made him look like a vengeful demon. After a few silent, sullen seconds, Dexi’Lynn spoke up.

“He’s the governor of this region. He’s a bit protective of his territory, and doesn’t like it when other big shots send their people instead of asking permission. You know how those kinds of jackasses are, right? Rule over a few worlds and they start thinking of themselves as actual gods. Bleh.”

The longer she spoke, the more she relaxed, and a bit of a twang entered her voice, the light accent deepening. To their surprise, she pulled out a pipe from a bag around her waist, and lit up with a match. She puffed away like she wasn’t in any danger.

A tinge at the edge of his senses made Ambrose look behind him. His other imp had scouted out the other end of the chasm, and it saw something. Through their bond, he should’ve been able to see it clearly, but while his Signature Creatures could see shapes and shadows, it wasn’t as detailed as it could be. He mentally commanded it to return.

“What do you know about Bedrock Spire?” Raeva asked, arms crossed over her chest. She’d crafted a perfect mask of casual disdain, a quiet threat to put her in Ambrose’s camp.

“I’ll never tell you anything,” Jack muttered.

Dexi’Lynn blew smoke at the kid, and answered while he sputtered. “Oh, you know. Kind of exhausting to get around, with all those stairs. Mara’Kahn has the best odds on cards and dice, but will rip you off if you pass out drunk. The human sector can be a bit prickly about other races, but they’ll wander to the rest of our places, of course. It’s got its problems, but it’s still home, you know?”

Oh, that was perfect. A native. The boy was probably expendable, but Dexi’Lynn spoke Ambrose’s language. “How big is the human sector? How many humans are in the city in general, and what kind of muscle does the Empire have here?” They knew some of the rough numbers thanks to SNAC, but it didn’t hurt to double check.

The kid lost his mind. “You can’t tell him these things! Where’s your loyalty? Where’s your patriotism?”

Dexi’Lynn shrugged and took another pull on her pipe. “Hard to be loyal to people who conquered you. Right now, I just wanna live to see another day. I’ll deal with the consequences later.”

Anyone could’ve predicted what came next. Jack’s face hardened, and then he pointed his knife at the weird woman. “Not if I stop you.”

With a roll of his eyes, Ambrose snapped his fingers. The imp on the ship jumped the kid and tackled him to the ground. In the scuffle, Ambrose scooped the knife up and admired it while the imp and teenager wrestled on the ground. Although he directed the imp to not do any real harm, it did bite Jack’s hand hard enough to make him scream.

“Ambrose,” Raeleq said reproachfully. He’d been silent this entire time, content to let others handle it until now.

With a sigh, he called the imp off. Jack got back up and cradled his injured, bleeding hand, crying a little. Dexi’Lynn cackled, then pointed the stem of her pipe at Luthor. “You’re the leader, right? Good-looking guy like you has to be.”

Luthor blinked in surprise, like it was the first time he’d ever been called handsome to his face. “We don’t really have a leader. I’m more of a team mediator than anything.”

“Whatever. Let’s talk terms. I have info you want. What do I get out of it? Give me some guarantees if I’m going to turn traitor. Woo me.” Smoke trailed out of her mouth, curling upwards like dragon whiskers.

Ambrose liked her more and more.

Luthor answered with, “You give us information, get us to Bedrock Spire, and sign a contract stating that you will not rat us out within a certain amount of time. In return, you two will get to walk away with your lives and the understanding that we will not betray you if you don’t betray us first. We don’t want to have to hurt you. All we want is to repair our ship and continue on our way.”

Dexi’Lynn perked up. “That really was a magnificent ship. I’d drink two gutcannons in a row if it got me a chance to look at that beauty. Any chance of that?”

“Don’t get greedy,” Raeva said, but Jessica spoke at the same time.

“Maybe you could help us repair it, if you’re any good as a mechanic.”

The two looked at each other, trading looks of mutual apology and also irritation.

“What are you?” Raeleq asked suddenly. “I’ve never seen a creature like you before.”

“Creature? Suck my eggs, jerk.” She didn’t sound offended so much as bemused. “I’m a kobold, obviously. What, your world doesn’t have kobolds?”

They did, but theirs didn’t look anything like her. Though now that she named it, Ambrose could see some similarities in the height and build. When standing, Dexi’Lynn was probably no more than four feet tall.

“I don’t want this deal,” Jack said stubbornly. “You’re going to have to kill me, or I’ll kill you.”

Ambrose grinned with excitement, more to intimidate than anything, but Luthor put himself between the two. “We don’t have to do anything. We could just leave you here, and let the creatures of this world find you. I don’t want to do that. Work with me here, Jack. Don’t you have a family you want to get back to?”

Wrong question. The bitter grimace, the way the teen’s jaw set, he probably had no one. “Not anymore.”

This entire thing was taking too long. Just as Ambrose was ready to lose his patience and actually scare the shit out of the kid, maybe into compliance, his other imp flared with panic. His bond was cut off before he could register what happened, and his other imp screeched with pain and fear as its counterpart disappeared.

“What the hell?” Raeva snapped, covering her ears.

Ambrose looked behind him. “Something got the other one. I think we need to get moving. Look, kid, you either give in for now, or we will leave you to be eaten. Dexi’Lynn, you in?”

“I’m in,” she echoed, hopping to her feet. A scaly tail twitched as she found her balance. “Let’s get the fuck out of here. Bye Jack, nice knowing you. You were annoying to the end.”

Luthor motioned with his head, and they all went around the fallen ship, where they were at the bottom of yet another chasm. How deep did this place go? At least this one was brighter, with more of those weird mushrooms, and also moss growing along the stones, slow pulses almost hypnotic in their cascade.

“W-wait,” Jack yelped, and followed.

On a hunch, Ambrose left his imp there as they went.  This time at least, they could see well enough to take off at a jog. Their packs jostled with the movement, and after the arduous climb, he really felt the weight with each step. To his shame, he was slower than everyone else thanks to his damned tight, uncomfortable shoes. He could already feel blisters forming.

Which became unimportant a second later, when his imp saw what had gotten the first one.

They were humanoid, more or less, but more like a mockery of people. With their hunched backs, long arms, and clawed hands, they almost looked like deranged apes. Thick, pulsing blue and red veins glowed, visible under translucent skin. No eyes, but a huge, open maw with dagger-like teeth.

The worst part was their numbers.

In the three seconds he had before the imp died, Ambrose counted at least two dozen. The imp flared with hate for him, and then the monsters ripped it apart and the connection vanished. His Signature Creatures were now deactivated for the next four hours.

“Oh gods,” he groaned, and put on a burst of speed. “They got my other imp. There’s a lot of them, and they’re fucking ugly.”

Dexi’Lynn slowed and turned, slitted eyes widening. “Were they pale, with big teeth and claws, and running on all fours?”

“Yep.”

“Shit on a stick. Ghouls.” Dexi’Lynn trilled in fear and scampered ahead, almost on all fours herself.

Now that they were no longer in the Forlorn Pit, the cavern opened up significantly, and Ambrose could actually see the moss-covered ceiling, such as it was, and countless tunnels and caves along the far-off walls, high and low. Enormous bats flew overhead, and beetles the size of aurochs traveled in herds. Unlike the pit, this place had real life to it, not hiding just out of sight.

He had just enough time to appreciate the odd beauty of the place before he heard the scrabbling of the ghouls in pursuit. This was going to turn into a fight of some kind, and dammit was he tired and in need of a break.

A cry of surprise and pain behind him made Ambrose look. Jack tripped, and off in the distance, the dozens of ravenous ghouls moved to close the gap.

All he had to do was keep moving, and the problem would be solved. No defiant little shit to try to turn on them later. But…

He was just a kid, maybe a year older than his sister Cynthia.

With a curse, Ambrose skidded to a stop and bellowed out, “FIGHT!”

His friends had made him stupid, and weak. Ambrose grabbed Jack by the wrist and hauled him to his feet. Then, sighing, he put himself between the boy and the ghouls, and felt the world stop.
