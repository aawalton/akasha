Chapter 90

Riley sat at the table in the upper room of the tower with the three items in front of her, lit by the strong afternoon sun coming in through the high window at her shoulder. The chest was on the left, still wrapped in its canvas, still locked. The history book was on the right, mentally earmarked for another day. The scroll sat in the middle.

She had decided to start with the scroll because the Corvessa seal pressed into the wax was the only piece of any of it she thought she could read at a glance. She reached for it.

The whip twitched on her hip.

Her hand stopped halfway across the table. She felt it more than saw it, a small live shift in the leather where it hung at her belt, the same kind of motion a sleeping cat makes when something walks past too close. She pulled her hand back. She looked down.

The whip sat there, coiled, dark, harmless. She watched it. She gave it a long count of ten, then twenty, and was about to decide she had imagined the whole thing when the leather moved again, the smallest possible flex along the body of the coil, and this time she felt it on her skin, a small warm pulse that ran up under the belt and into her side.

Riley stood up. The chair jumped back from her legs and skidded with a screech that filled the room.

She stopped breathing, her body simply forgot the next breath while she stared down at the thing on her hip and waited to see what it would do. Her heart kept time in the silence, thumping in her chest. Outside, somewhere down in the courtyard, a soldier called something to another, and then the day's hum continued.

She moved her hand down toward the whip slowly, her fingers got within an inch of the handle.

The whip jumped.

The handle leapt up off the belt and into her palm and her fingers closed around it before her brain caught up, and a very small, very short scream came out of her mouth before she could stop it. She flung the whip down. It hit the floor and uncoiled partway and lay there. She jumped back two steps and put her free hand flat against her chest and stood with her other hand held out in front of her staring at it as if she had just been bitten.

Her breathing was uneven. She looked at the hand that had touched the whip. She looked at the whip on the floor. She looked at her hand again. The hand was fine. The whip was still. Nothing was burning, nothing was bleeding, nothing was wrapping itself around her throat.

She thought, very clearly, this thing killed a man. She had seen the body in the basement of the raiders' compound. The whip had been around his neck so tight that neither Thorne nor any of her soldiers had been able to pry it loose, and she had reached down and lifted it off him with one hand. She thought, it choked him out and would not come off for anyone but me.

Then she thought, slower, but it did not do that to me. It came to my hand.

She let her arms drop down at her sides. She made herself take a real breath, deep and slow, and held it for a count and released, the warm air curled over her upper lip. The whip lay on the floor between her boots and the table.

She took a step forward. She did not bend down. She put the toe of her boot against the body of the whip and gave it the lightest possible kick, the way a person tests a snake to see if it is dead. Nothing happened. The whip rolled half an inch and stopped and lay there exactly as it had lain before.

She kicked it again, a little harder.

This time the handle lifted off the floor, the dark leather of the long coil shifting around it, and the grip swung up to point at her chest.

She stared at it. Her mind worked faster now, the way it did when a real-time strategy game suddenly required her to do six things at once. It is presenting.

Her next thought was that she should call for the guards, but what could they do. Two grown men had pulled at this thing on a dead raider's neck and not been able to move it half an inch, and when she had reached down it had come free in her hand.

"Nothing ventured," she said quietly, to the empty room, and reached for it.

Her fingers got close. The whip jumped again, smooth this time, a clean small leap from the floor to her palm, and the handle fit itself into her grip, and she drew in a sharp breath and held very still.

She straightened up slowly. The whip hung from her hand. Something began to move up her arm from the place where her skin touched the leather, a thin warm running feeling that spread out across her shoulder and down her back. Goosebumps stood up on her forearms. The hair at the nape of her neck lifted.

Riley thought, I have never held one of these in my life. How do you even start? Where is the on button?

She held the handle properly, the way she had seen people in movies hold a whip, and let the length of it trail behind her on the floor. She looked at the empty space in front of her. She tried to remember every cowboy movie her father had ever made her sit through, and could only come up with a vague memory of a man on a horse and a sound like a gunshot. How many cowboys actually use a whip, really?

She raised her arm in something close to an overhand throw, the way a person throws a baseball, and snapped it forward.

The whip went with her. The body of it lifted off the floor, traveled the length of her arm's motion, extended out into the air ahead of her, and ended with a sharp clean crack like a piece of dry wood splitting in a fire. The sound rang around the stone of the upper room and came back at her from two walls at once.

Riley stood there with her arm still extended. The end of the whip drifted slowly back down.

She thought, no one who has never touched a whip should be able to crack it on the first try.

She smiled a small private smile to herself.

"Magic," she said.

She moved her arm to the side, slowly, and the whip followed. She drew a circle in the air with her wrist and the whip turned with her, the long body of it coiling around in a loose loop above the floor, tracking her motion like it knew where she wanted it to go before she did. She pulled the arm wide, brought it back behind her shoulder, and snapped forward again. Another crack. Cleaner than the first. Louder.

Her smile was as wide as she could make it now. She thought, Riley the Lion Tamer. Step right up. Come one, come all.

Then she thought, with my luck I end up as the sideshow at the Clawborn three-ring circus, billed as some kind of weirdo dominatrix in a leather corset. She winced at her own brain. Thank god there is no pleather in this world. Is there? She shuddered at the thought.

She let the whip droop back to the floor. She turned her wrist and watched the leather follow the motion, watched the coils gather themselves back toward the handle in her hand. She did not have to tell it to come. She thought of it coming, and it came.

She lifted the handle close to her face and looked at it properly for the first time since she had taken it off the dead man's neck. The leather was old and dark and unevenly worn. The grip had a wrap on it that was finer than any leather work she had seen in Rivermark. There were no markings she could see. No runes. No inlays. Nothing to indicate it was anything other than a piece of well-made tack that any horseman might carry on his saddle.

It hummed faintly under her fingers. Not a sound, just a feeling, something that lived in her hand the way a held animal does, alive and still and waiting.

She thought, whoever made you knew what they were doing.

She thought, and whoever owned you before me did not deserve you.

She walked the whip back to her hip and threaded the handle into her belt and the whip went obediently into its coil and hung there, dark and motionless, exactly as it had been. She stood with her hand resting on the grip for a count of three and felt the small warm hum of it under her palm and felt her own pulse slowing back down to something close to normal.

Then she turned around and looked at the table.

The chair she had kicked back was still out of place. The scroll with the Corvessa seal sat where she had left it, untouched. The chest was a dark shape against the canvas it was wrapped in. The history book waited at the right.

She walked back across the room and pulled the chair back with one hand and sat down in it and put both hands flat on the table on either side of the scroll.

"All right," she said to the empty room. "One thing at a time."

She reached for the wax seal. The whip stayed quiet on her hip, a small live presence at her side.
