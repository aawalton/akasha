Steam whipped around me as my wings unfurled fully, their size making me feel bigger than I actually was. My world shrunk when I grew—what was once big to me seemed regular sized now as a juvenile dragon, about the size of a cassowary bird. I looked down at my new body and stiffened my muscles to, you know, break into my new suit. It was such a rewarding transformation, and I couldn’t wait for Nolan to see it.

I bounced through the curtain of steam and smoke, my party now visible. There Nolan was, in a purple transparent bubble that began to dematerialize, with a big and proud smirk on his face.

“Looks good on you,” he complimented.

“Huh, an ether dragon,” Lun said as he nodded. “Acceptable.”

I jumped up and down like a little girl in a candy shop, asking Nolan to ask Lun to spawn in a mirror.

“What do I look like? A walking furniture boutique?!”

“She wants to see herself,” Nolan explained, clearly amused. “Can’t blame her for being excited about the upgrade.”

With a lighthearted sigh, Lun summoned my mirror. It was a tiny thing that levitated in the air as I swooshed my tail back and forth, looking at my marvelous reflection. From what I could see, Mable, the former Etheryian champion, was spot on—I was downright gorgeous.

Did I get a charisma boost? Because I was feelin’ myself! The shiny scales, the imposing wings, the sheer size of my body—everything was just perfect! Every scale on my body shimmered like pure silver. My tail was long and strong, covered in the same lustrous scales, ending with a sharp and mighty point that looked like it could pierce even the hardest of rocks.

Looking close, I noticed that each scale had its own unique design. Small swirling patterns etched into each one, making me look even more special. My horns curved majestically from my head before tapering to a point.

I’d never felt this good about the way I looked until now, and it felt amazing….

Stage 2 Transformation Complete

‘Congratulations, you’ve grown. You’re still a short fry, but a short fry with wings. No more spitting out coins for teleportation crystals and cards from now on.’

‘Hardy har har, bandit,’ I sassed. ‘I’d abandon you before you get to ride my back. I’m still no adult dragon.’

‘Heh, you’re right. Far from it. Like I said, the next stage will be a lot harder to complete. So prepare to be this size for a while.’

“Great, now that the dragon is now one step closer to becoming an absolute menace to society, can we round up our three and a half heads and figure out how we are going to raid this dungeon on an inventory handicap?” Typha complained.

I rolled my eyes at Typha’s attitude. If my draconic form intimidated her, that was her problem.

‘Let her stew,’ Nolan communicated to me privately. ‘Some people can’t let go of their prejudices.’

‘Fine, but I’m keeping an eye on her,’ I replied. ‘I don’t trust that klepto as far as I can throw her—which might be pretty far now, actually…. No worries! I’ll hit her with a mean gust attack before she even knows what hit her!’ I huffed, and then I flapped my new wings experimentally. The air whooshed around us, sending dust spiraling.

‘Easy with the wind tunnel,’ Nolan cautioned, adjusting his grip on Zaiden. ‘Save your energy for inside.’

Lun dismissed the mirror with a flick of his wrist just as I tucked my wings away, and then turned to Typha and said, “To answer your question, there really is only two ways about it. Short version: we’re screwed. Long version: we’re royally screwed. Tier five dungeons are no joke—they’re designed for experienced adventuring parties with specialized gear.”

I snorted, sending twin plumes of steam into the air. ‘Well, that’s a real confidence booster.’

“Now, that’s not to say that it is impossible,” Lun continued, tapping his scepter thoughtfully against his scaled palm. “Just highly improbable. We’d need perfect coordination, flawless execution, and a heap of luck.”

Typha paced in a tight circle, her fingers twitching toward the empty spaces along her waist where her stolen gadgets should have been. “So we’re trapped in an ancient temple with no exit except through a death maze designed to kill seasoned adventurers. Great. Just great,” Typha said, kicking at a loose stone.

I stretched my new form, feeling the strength coursing through my draconic muscles. ‘What about me? I’m basically a walking tank now, a true vanguard.’

‘Don’t get cocky,’ Nolan warned. ‘You still don’t know your new limits.’

Lun seemed to be deep in thought, his tail swishing back and forth across the stone floor. “Actually, the dragon might be our ace. If we play this right...” He paused, his eyes narrowing as he studied me. “You’re a multi-elemental dragon, right? With natural affinities for elemental magic? And this is an elemental temple. There might be some factors here we could exploit. Environmental buffs, natural elemental resistance…. yes, yes this could work….”

Nolan shifted Zaiden’s weight on his shoulder. “Lun, if you’ve got an idea, now’s the time to share it.”

“Elemental temples are tied to specific primal forces,” Lun explained, his eyes lighting up with enthusiasm. “If this is truly Salzer’s temple, then it’s one of the core elemental nexus points. Legend has it that Salzer was a master of all six basic elements: light, dark, earth, air, fire, and water, but favored none above the others. Which means that this temple is actually a stationary powerhouse to different elemental affinities.”

“And that helps us… how?” Typha demanded, crossing her arms.

“It means that Freya here is essentially a walking elemental conduit—with my help, we might be able to manipulate the temple’s own energy against it. Freya could potentially absorb or redirect elemental attacks, maybe even trigger beneficial environmental changes!”

I perked up at this revelation. ‘So I’m basically the dungeon’s kryptonite?’

“If Lun’s right, you might be the only reason we make it out alive,” Nolan said as he turned to me.

Lun nodded vigorously, his excitement growing. “Ah, what a perfect plan! However, don’t be confused. The temple won’t just roll over because you're a dragon. It'll still try to kill us at every turn. But if we’re smart about it...” He trailed off, studying the dungeon entrance. “We might just survive this, and I may finally reunite with my beautiful Brava again!” he tweeted, folding his hands together all lovey dovey like. I swear I saw heart eyes on that love-struck face of his.

“Then let’s do it,” Nolan added. “Dungeons allow only one party at a time, so we need to group up.”

“This is only temporary,” Typha said. “As soon as we get out of here, we break the party.”

Nolan wasn’t thrilled about partying up with an ungrateful brat either, but it was what it was. As soon as we partied up, Nolan accepted the invite to the dungeon of elementals, and as soon as we entered, we fell through a trapdoor!

Initiate flight! Initiate flight!

I flapped my new wings as hard and fast as I could, but the rush of air and the sudden drop caught me by surprise. My wings weren’t responding properly—too new, too unfamiliar. Instead of a graceful hover, I tumbled awkwardly through the air until I felt Nolan snatch me with his arm around my neck.

‘Gah! Too tight!’

“Catch them!” I heard Nolan shout as we plummeted, but barely. Typha’s ear-splitting scream was so damn loud. Lun swung his scepter and a brilliant web of golden light exploded outward. That web of light stretched beneath us as I made the mistake of tracking it with my eyes. Seeing the deep fall only petrified me more. Luckily, Lun had us covered, the web forming into a net. All of us dropped into it with a big bounce, the magic net stretching like elastic before settling into a gentle cradle. We lay there for a moment, hearts pounding, and breaths coming in ragged gasps.

“Everyone alive?” Nolan called, still clutching me around my neck.

‘Can’t... breathe...’ I wheezed through our bond.

“Oh, sorry,” he muttered, releasing his death grip.

I gulped in air, my new lungs expanding. I gave him a playful glare and huffed. ‘Why, I never!’ I bit at him, dusting myself with my chest puffed out. ‘Handling a lady like that!’

Nolan rolled his eyes. ‘You shot up a few inches and all of a sudden you think you’re grown.’

“Let’s… not do that again…” Typha said as her fist gripped her shirt over her chest, her other grip on her brother’s back. I could tell that she was trying to be tough about the entire situation, but she was terrified inside. The subtle shaking, the paleness on her cheeks. I’d bet she’d never been in a dungeon before, the girl being at a measly level 7….

“Well, I can’t keep bubbles around you guys the entire time,” Lun said. “I’ll fry my mana gauge. So we have to just roll with the punches.”

“That’s not… reassuring to hear.” Typha’s voice sank.

“We’ll deal with it,” Nolan said before he got up and scooped Zaiden over his shoulder again. “We’re lucky to even have a workable plan in a tier five dungeon with barely any items at our disposal. Not to mention, we have one man down.”

Lun gently descended the safety net until we reached solid ground. The chamber lit up as soon as we touched ground, the torch lights along the walls magically illuminating a narrow hallway.

“Feel like a real guest of honor,” Nolan muttered under his breath.

“After you,” Lun said to Nolan, “team leader.”

Nolan grunted and took the lead, shifting Zaiden’s weight on his shoulder. I padded after him, my new claws clicking against the stone floor. I felt like I was walking on lava the way I tip-toed, avoiding cracks along the stone, like I was two seconds away from triggering another trap.

The hallway stretched before us, seemingly endless in the flickering torchlight. Down the hallway felt cold despite the torchlights along the walls. As for the walls themselves, they weren’t empty. Symbols were etched along them, not glowing, not pulsating. Just basic unrecognizable symbols.

However basic, staring at them too long did give me a migraine. So I stopped stressing myself out and kept my eyes forward. ‘So what’s the plan exactly?’ I asked Nolan through our bond. ‘Just walk into whatever death trap is waiting and hope my shiny new scales can take it?’

‘More or less,’ he replied. ‘Stay alert. This place is going to test us at every turn.’

Tests, I wasn’t a fan of those. Unfortunately, we wouldn’t get to decide when or how we got tested. As soon as the hallway began to open up, I heard something off to my right. Quick-footed, I leaped forward, narrowly avoiding the large grating rectangular stone block that extracted itself from the wall. It nearly nipped my tail! Except, I realized that someone else fell victim to the colliding stone—Typha!
