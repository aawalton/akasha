The Artisan District of Ashenfall was a sensory overload of noise, heat, and bustling commerce. Located around the lower ring of the city, the district was a dense maze of heavy stone buildings, roaring furnaces, and brightly painted wooden storefronts. Thick plumes of grey smoke continuously choked the pale winter sky above, while the air at street level was thick with the sharp tang of burning coal, the chemical smell of alchemical reagents, and the rhythmic, deafening clang of heavy iron hammers striking steel anvils.

When Josh, Brett, and Carcan first arrived in the district, the morning was still young. The merchants and craftsmen were going about their daily routines, entirely oblivious to the absolute nightmare currently marching towards the northern walls. Apprentices swept the cobblestone streets, stall owners arranged polished weapons on wooden racks, and a few quiet whispers were circulating among the early shoppers. The gossip mostly consisted of vague rumours about the distant warning horns that had sounded before dawn, but there was no genuine panic yet. The people of Ashenfall felt safe behind their high walls.

Their first stop was the largest forge in the district, a massive, open-air smithy operated by a broad-shouldered, incredibly muscular dwarven woman named Helga.

As they approached, Helga was in the middle of quenching a massive, glowing red broadsword in a long trough of dark oil. A huge cloud of foul-smelling steam hissed violently into the freezing air. The heat radiating from the massive stone forge behind her was intense, instantly reminding Brett of his own newly acquired thermal abilities.

The blacksmith nodded as Josh stepped up to the heavy wooden counter, dropping Bhel’s ruined, mud-caked leather and iron chest piece onto the scarred surface with a heavy, metallic clatter.

"I need the iron plates hammered back into shape and the leather straps reinforced," Josh requested, his voice raised to be heard over the roaring fires. "And we need two sets of throwing axes. Put it on the Guild's emergency tab."

Helga wiped a thick layer of soot from her brow with the back of a calloused hand. She picked up the ruined chest piece, her dark eyes critically assessing the catastrophic damage. She traced a thick finger over a deep, jagged gouge in the central iron plate.

"This looks like it was chewed on by a cavern bear and then dragged through a swamp, lad," Helga noted, looking up at Josh with a raised eyebrow. "The iron is completely warped, and the leather is rotting from whatever foul magic hit it. I can fix it, but it will take a few hours of hard hammering. The Guild tab is fine. Rochelle sent runners down here an hour ago to authorise the open credit line for high-tier fighters."

"We will be back for it before the sun sets," Josh confirmed.

He turned away from the counter, his storm-grey eyes scanning the massive racks of heavy armour and weaponry lining the stone walls of the forge. His own gear had taken a beating over the last few days, and his encounter with the Bone Goliath had proven his current defensive setup was no longer sufficient.

"I need to replace my helmet," Josh stated, reaching up to tap the dented, scratched steel enclosing his head. "I borrowed this one from Garrik back at the tavern when we first arrived in the city, and I fully intend to return it to him in one piece. And my shield is practically falling apart. Plus, I think I can handle stronger enchantments at this point."

Helga wiped her soot-stained hands on a thick leather apron and beckoned him towards the back of the shop. She unlocked a heavy iron grate, leading him into a secure, dimly lit storeroom where the highest quality steel was kept under heavy lock and key.

Josh spent the next twenty minutes carefully inspecting the heavy armour. He ran his gauntlets over polished breastplates and tested the weight of heavy chainmail. He eventually settled on a beautiful, master-crafted greathelm. It was forged from dense, polished steel, featuring a narrow, reinforced T-shaped visor that offered maximum facial protection without sacrificing peripheral vision. The interior was lined with thick, magically treated leather padding that passively absorbed concussive impacts, adding a solid ten points to his vitality and dexterity stats.

Finding a replacement shield proved slightly more difficult. The massive, deeply gouged slab of steel he currently carried had saved his life countless times, but the structural integrity had been compromised by the Goliath's club.

Helga noticed his frustration as he discarded a third kite shield for feeling too flimsy. She grunted, walking to the very back corner of the room and pulling a heavy canvas tarp off a standalone weapon stand.

"Try this," the dwarven smith said, hefting a massive, heater shield with both hands. "It was forged for a Royal Guard captain who deployed to the eastern front last year. He never returned to claim it. I call it the Bulwark of the Fallen."

Josh took the shield, sliding his left arm through the thick, perfectly adjusted leather straps. The metal was dark, almost charcoal-grey, inlaid with intricate, geometric silver runes along the heavy rim. It was significantly heavier than his old shield, but the moment he locked his grip around the handle, it felt perfectly balanced.

As soon as he strapped it on, a blue system notification chimed in his mind. The shield offered a massive twenty-five-point boost to his constitution stat, and it possessed a unique passive enchantment. The silver runes would actively absorb a small percentage of kinetic energy from heavy physical blows, subtly reducing the recoil he experienced when blocking massive monsters. It was the absolute perfect complement to his Titan's Anchor skill.

"We will take it," Josh declared, feeling a renewed sense of unshakeable confidence with the heavy, pristine steel resting on his arm.

Leaving the blistering heat of the forge, the trio moved further down the cobblestone street. The atmosphere outside had noticeably shifted in the brief half-hour they had been inside. The quiet whispers had grown into anxious, loud conversations. The streets were significantly more crowded.

They passed a delicate glassblower's shop where the merchant was frantically packing his fragile, colourful wares into heavy wooden crates packed with straw, shouting nervously at his apprentices to hurry. Across the street, a vendor selling roasted spiced nuts had completely abandoned his wooden handcart, leaving the food to burn as he jogged hurriedly towards the upper district.

"The panic is starting to set in," Brett observed quietly, pulling his ruined fur cloak tighter around his shivering shoulders. "The Guild must have officially notified the city council."

They entered a high-end magical tailor and enchanter's shop next, stepping out of the freezing wind. The interior of 'The Arcane Loom' smelled of expensive incense and dried lavender. The walls were lined with shimmering silks, glowing magical focuses, and heavy velvet drapes that actively muted the noise of the street outside.

Brett immediately began browsing the heavy travel cloaks. His current coat was practically in ruins, singed black at the edges from his own plasma spells and heavily stained with freezing, necrotic mud.

The shopkeeper, a tall, slender elf wearing elaborate purple robes embroidered with silver stars, approached the shivering mage. The elf immediately noticed the faint, unnatural heat radiating from Brett’s bare hands and the distinct lack of a traditional wooden staff or wand.

"You are a raw elemental channeler," the elven enchanter noted, his eyes widening slightly with profound professional interest. He reached out, holding a hand a few inches from Brett's shoulder to feel the ambient thermal output. "Astounding. Most traditional fabrics will simply ignite if you attempt to cast spells while wearing them. You need something specialised, or you will burn yourself entirely naked during your next battle."

The enchanter disappeared into the back room, returning a moment later carrying a beautifully woven cloak draped over his arms. It was a deep, vibrant crimson colour, the fabric shimmering faintly like liquid fire in the ambient light.

"Ember-silk," the enchanter explained proudly, holding the garment out for Brett to inspect. "It is woven directly from the webs of volcanic spiders found in the southern ash-fields. It is completely fireproof, but more importantly, the fabric passively insulates and regulates thermal energy, feeding it back into the wearer's core."

Brett eagerly took the cloak, swinging it around his shoulders and fastening the heavy silver clasp at his throat. The moment the fabric settled against his back, he let out a long, highly satisfied sigh. The persistent, freezing chill that always haunted his bones vanished entirely. The system informed him that the Ember-silk Mantle provided a fifteen percent boost to his total mana regeneration, while simultaneously increasing the raw damage output of all thermal-based spells. It was an incredibly powerful, perfectly tailored piece of gear.

"It is magnificent," Brett praised, pushing his imaginary spectacles up his nose with a warm, gloved hand. "I feel like an entirely new man. Add it to the Guild's tab, please."

While Brett admired his new cloak in a tall silver mirror, Carcan was quietly inspecting a glass display case filled with enchanted jewellery. She did not need heavy armour, and her pale green travel robes were new enough, having only been commissioned a few weeks ago, but she desperately needed something to ease the sheer magical strain of the impending siege.

She pointed a slender finger at a delicate silver chain holding a teardrop-shaped, pale blue crystal that pulsed with a faint, inner light.

"The Tear of the Mother," the elven enchanter smiled, unlocking the glass case with a small silver key. "A very astute choice for a healer of your specific calling. The crystal was mined from the deep roots of the ancient forests. It actively purifies ambient mana in the air, feeding it directly into the wearer's core. It significantly reduces the physical burnout associated with casting restoration spells."

Carcan clasped the silver chain around her neck, tucking the cool crystal beneath the collar of her robes. She could instantly feel the subtle, refreshing pulse of the enchantment, a gentle hum that began to soothe her deep, spiritual exhaustion, returning a faint blush of healthy colour to her pale cheeks.

Their final stop was the busiest shop in the entire district.

The local fletcher and alchemy supply store was absolute, chaotic bedlam. The creeping panic in the city had finally boiled over into a full-scale, desperate rush for survival supplies. Dozens of lower-level adventurers, terrified local merchants, and frantic city guards were crammed into the small wooden building, desperately trying to buy every single health potion, bandage, and iron arrow they could get their hands on.

Josh had to use his massive, steel-clad bulk to physically force a path through the shouting, shoving crowd. He kept his new shield raised slightly, creating a solid wedge that allowed Brett and Carcan to slip safely in behind him.

"I need heavy, steel-tipped broadheads!" Josh yelled over the deafening din, slamming Perberos's crumpled list down onto the scarred wooden counter. "And armour-piercing bodkins! As many as you have!"

The harassed human shopkeeper looked up, his hair wild and his eyes wide with stress. He was currently trying to negotiate with three different screaming customers at once. "Normal arrows are completely sold out! The city guard requisitioned everything an hour ago!"

"I am not asking for normal arrows," Josh growled, leaning his newly acquired helm closer to the man, allowing his intimidating size to command the merchant's full attention. "I need the heavy draw stock. The arrows that are too dense for a standard bow."

The shopkeeper blinked, instantly recognising the heavy, dangerous presence of a high-tier frontline fighter. He ducked beneath the counter, hauling up three massive, tightly bound leather quivers that clunked heavily against the wood.

"These are for siege bows and specialized ranger classes," the shopkeeper explained nervously, wiping sweat from his forehead. "Solid steel heads. They will punch cleanly through heavy iron plating, but they are incredibly expensive."

"Put it on the tab," Brett interjected smoothly, stepping up beside Josh and placing a heavy hand on the counter. The ambient temperature around the mage spiked sharply, the air shimmering with a subtle, threatening heat that caused the nearby arguing customers to step back in fear. "And we need concentrated stamina draughts, greater mana elixirs, and high-grade health potions. Do not tell me you are out. We know you keep the top-tier stock in the back room for premium clients."

The shopkeeper swallowed hard, thoroughly intimidated by the imposing duo. He hurriedly locked the front till and disappeared into the back room, returning a minute later with a heavy wooden crate carefully packed with straw. Inside were dozens of glowing red, vibrant green, and deep blue glass vials.

"That is absolutely everything I have left of the high-grade stock," the man admitted, his hands shaking slightly as he recorded the massive purchase on the Guild's ledger.

With their heavy bags fully loaded and their gear upgraded, the trio finally pushed their way back out of the crowded shop and onto the cobblestone street.

The time they had spent shopping had allowed the grim reality of their situation to fully consume Ashenfall.

It was now early afternoon, but the pale winter sun offered absolutely no warmth or comfort. The oblivious, peaceful morning was completely gone, violently replaced by a city-wide panic. The streets were choked with people. Town criers were standing on every corner, frantically ringing heavy brass bells and officially announcing the state of emergency to the terrified populace.

The refugees from the outer camps were flooding through the main gates by the thousands. It was a chaotic, heartbreaking tide of humanity. Mothers carried crying infants, desperate fathers dragged heavy wooden carts filled with whatever meagre possessions they could save, and the elderly stumbled blindly through the crushing crowds.

The local merchants were no longer sweeping their steps. They were frantically hammering thick wooden planks over their glass windows and dragging heavy wooden furniture to barricade their heavy oak doors. Detachments of the Ashenfall city guard were jogging rapidly through the streets in tight formations, their spears raised, shouting hoarse orders and attempting to direct the massive influx of displaced civilians towards the already overflowing public squares and temple grounds.

"It is happening," Brett murmured, his voice completely devoid of his usual sarcastic wit. He pulled his new crimson Ember-silk cloak tightly around his shoulders, watching a terrified family huddle together against a cold stone wall as a squad of guards rushed past. "The reality has finally hit them."

"They are trapped," Carcan observed softly, her luminous silver eyes filled with profound sorrow as she watched a weeping child desperately clinging to its mother's skirts. "There are simply too many people, and the walls are a finite barrier. If the undead breach the gates, this city will become a slaughterhouse."

Josh adjusted the heavy leather strap of his new shield, feeling the cold, uncompromising weight of the dark steel. The guilt he had felt earlier in the Guildhall, the secret he carried about his unnatural presence in this world, felt suddenly insignificant in the face of the apocalyptic threat bearing down upon them.

He was not from Eldanar. He was a regular guy from a world of internet and electricity. But standing in the freezing, panicked streets of Ashenfall, watching thousands of innocent, terrified people seeking shelter from the dark, Josh finally accepted the terrifying weight of his class. He was a Guardian. And right now, these people needed a shield.

"Then we make sure the gates do not break," Josh stated firmly, his storm-grey eyes hardening into cold, unyielding resolve.

He turned away from the tragic scene, leading Brett and Carcan back through the winding, chaotic streets. They headed back towards the massive stone Guildhall, heavily burdened with their new magical gear and a fresh, terrifying sense of purpose. The shopping was done. The time for preparation was over. The siege of Ashenfall was about to begin.
