Chapter 137: Cooked

The heat inside the metal bubble quickly grew to ‘cooking’ levels, and his dad cried out from the pain. Jack briefly adjusted the metal on Freddy to the minimum needed and stayed away from any open or thinly-protected skin. Otherwise, there wasn’t much he could do, as the heat transfer wasn’t something he was innately equipped to block, and trickier ideas weren’t something he had time for. He was lucky to keep the damn fire out. Jack didn’t bother breathing, saving the oxygen for his dad.

He was also feeling significant alarm and panic from Neex. He tried to communicate that he was okay as best he could with the limitations in place.

He tried to ‘flow’ the metal in some fashion while bracing it, to pull and absorb heat upward through the metal and away from them, but he had no idea what he was doing. Maybe it helped.

Thankfully, the blast was a momentary thing, and just as quickly as it pressed and tried to crush his protective barrier inward, and it was dishing out earthquake-like vibrations, it weakened rapidly over mere moments, and Jack could ease off of the dangerous fatigue levels. Nonetheless, he got a message about falling into Fatigue Status: Serious from the high level, continuous output.

The heat was slower to abate, which felt like a too-hot sauna to Jack, and was certainly worse to his dad. Freddy’s skin was highly reddened, and he kept his eyes closed. He kept struggling and shouting, to which Jack could only hear bits and pieces, but it sounded like he was blaming ‘them’ for what happened.

Jack felt sick to his stomach. He knew exactly what had happened — all those people had been funneled to some killing room to eliminate information leaks. They thought they were going to escape. In some sense, they had.

That bastard knew they couldn’t escape Memoria if the situation of a raid came up, not in numbers. This was a prepared eventuality. What about him? There’s no way he was in there.

The heat finally felt a measurable decline, and Jack cautiously poked holes in the top of the metal for ventilation, even as he continued trying to ‘leak’ heat, fumbling about with his powers as he ‘flowed’ the metal continuously. There was a faint scent of smoke, but nothing too dangerous.

Jack’s hearing recovered a little. His dad was certainly still deaf and had blood coming out of his ears.

Freddy was sobbing and shaking his head now. “You killed them… I made sure everyone got in… I should’ve been with them, I should’ve…” He squeezed his eyes shut and cried.

Clearing his throat and swallowing, Jack stared at his dad and shook his head. He spoke as loudly as he could, “It wasn’t us, Dad. It was your people. Heavenward did this, damn it!” The last was stated less calmly than he’d wanted.

But Freddy didn’t respond. His eyes opened, but he didn’t even look at Jack. He withdrew into himself.

Finally, the heat started rapidly dissipating, and Jack widened the ventilation holes a little more.

Neex’s voice finally broke through into his head, sounding distant and muted, but discernible. “Jack! Are you really okay?!”

“Peachy,” Jack replied. “Can you update Memoria on where I am? I went below. Down the shaft. Was close to an explosion. Made a bubble to protect my dad and me.”

“You found him! Oh, thank the Deep! Okay, okay — I can’t directly track, but I’ll do my best, hold on…”

Less than a minute went by, and there was a sudden series of knocks on the outside of the bubble. Jack quickly moved particulate dust out to verify who it was, and soon felt the outline of an ANP mask. Jack made the surface of his bubble ripple as it turned to a semi-liquid, hopefully enough of a clue…

Jetset’s familiar helmet poked in through the metal. “Well, well, if it isn’t Agent Steel! Glad you made it. You, ah… You were close enough to this business that you’re kinda lucky you did.”

“Luck had little to do with it,” Jack replied.

Jetset laughed. “Heeey, nice comeback! I respect the confidence.” He looked over at Freddy, who was trying to slink further away, his eyes wide, but to little avail, considering Jack was still restraining him. “Now this is lucky, though.”

“No argument there…” Jack sighed wearily. “He was apparently helping get everyone into the killing room before it detonated, maybe as an assigned role. That’s the only reason he was last, and I managed to save him…”

“How’s that for irony? I guess he’s been rewarded for his misplaced altruism.” Jetset moved slightly more inward, holding out a high-tech rebreather-type mask. “Smoke isn’t bad. You can handle it, but put this on him and evac him up. Through the hole is the easiest. You’re not far.”

“Roger that, sir,” Jack replied as he took the mask. Before Jetset disappeared, Jack hurriedly asked, “Did we get Heavenward?”

“Nope. Just a few other VIPs not quick or savvy enough to come get blown up down here, apparently. Ooze epically failed in finding our main target, but it was apparently a deliberate tunnel maze, complete with traps. Found ourselves a comic book villain, am I right?! Ha! We’ve got no idea where he is or got off to. Anyway, gotta go — after checking for survivors around this big hole real quick, they want me to start helping patrol around the entire platform, can you believe that shit, my brother?”

There was no time to answer the question, though, as Jetset was gone in a flash.

Jack scowled at the news. Could someone even get away from Memoria under the circumstances? That was ludicrous. But in the chaos of a giant explosion…

Shit, could it have been multi-purpose? Mass murder of compromised people, massive distraction, and then scattering material and energy everywhere, making it impossible to track everything happening at once.

Jack stared at Freddy, who was looking off at nothing with a hollow expression. “Dad, why did you do all this in the first place? Why did you believe in this cultic stuff? You never bought into this kind of shit before; you taught me not to.”

His dad didn’t quite meet his eyes at first. He opened his mouth, it hung, and then he suddenly looked right at Jack, and his face became some combination of horror and spite. “I got nothing to say to you, imposter! Where is my son?! What did you do to him?! Did you kill him?! Tell me! I swear, I’ll find out the truth, whether-”

Sighing, Jack hung his head briefly and tuned out the rest of the pointless ranting. He needed to get the mask on…

In touching his dad, he hesitated to ping Q-Loth, but did anyway. Q-Loth only faintly responded. It was something of a vague impression, of someone turning their back on a doctor or healer. Jack quickly understood.

He intrinsically refuses help. He wants this, clings to it, doesn’t even internally believe something is wrong. Damn it… can a psychic like Scaper even help with this?

Grimacing at the dire situation, Jack nonetheless got the mask on his dad’s face, requiring holding him firmly in place to do so, as he was constantly struggling uncooperatively.

After taking a deep breath and holding it, Jack finally opened the metal bubble. Indeed, some smoke was still sifting around, but not much. The corridor was scorched black, and the rough stone had cracks spiderwebbed through it. He began leading his dad around the corner. Not long after that, he stopped dead, as there was a sudden giant hole in the bedrock where the room had once been, enough to see a maze of tunnel openings like a cut-out anthill. He didn’t see the sky from his perspective, as the tunnel had to be rather deep before finding it, even for a small platform like Heavenward.

The explosives were designed to mostly blow outward, hence the floor being so rigged. They wanted a huge cloud of debris, I bet.

There were vessels already floating up into the space in large numbers. Jack didn’t get far before getting corralled by ANPs and Exemplars, and quickly hustled into vessels. Jack tried to argue briefly about being separated from his dad, but he was shouted down quickly on account of needing to be checked over medically. He was ordered to remove his metal brace on Freddy, who was then put into a containment apparatus and flown away.

Pretty much simultaneously, Jack was taken into an ambulance, sat down, and checked over, with questions about ‘how he was feeling’ thrown in from a calm and collected doctor.

Jack sighed internally. Ah, this again. Psych evaluation, here I come.

🌑 🌒 🌓 🌔 🌕

He played the twenty questions game as calmly as he could manage. It thankfully wasn’t too long in the ambulance before he was released, as an injured Exemplar was brought in, and he was transferred to a simple Q-23 for departure. This took a few minutes, as they waited for a protective formation of drones before heading out.

The hole blown out of the platform was an unusual and disturbing sight, as Jack watched the vessel he was in navigate out into the sky, going straight down. He was glad that most of the force went in one direction and was successful, as otherwise, he’d probably be dead.

In any case, the framework of the platform was never in danger, and it was not really worse for wear as a whole. Levitational frames didn’t really care about sectional compromise.

His sharp eyes even caught a piece of bare, metal frame, like a ripped-up cable, perhaps partly melted before cooling. It was out in the open sky, floating where momentum had blown it out far enough. That was the magical beauty of levitation — oh, you wanted to compromise a platform, make it crash? Tough luck, because each piece of the framework floated.

Staring at the strange sight, Jack knew it could only be an oversight related to the current set of higher priorities.

Memoria is busy.

Indeed, when they passed across the threshold of Heavenward’s airspace, though Jack felt a sense of access — as the quickest way to get a report in emergencies, no doubt — Memoria didn’t contact him directly.

Your Level has improved to 6.0! Please enter the trance and Calibrate, as this changes your total Allotment. Note: Please allow time for decision-making between the three options available with a full Level change. You also have 1.0 to allocate to any statistic other than Create.

Evasion has improved to 4.7!

Augment: Absorption has improved to 0.8!

Augment: Tensile has improved to 0.6!

Control: Cord has improved to 3.7!

Control: Cloud has improved to 2.0!

Control: Strike has improved to 0.7!

Create: Lift has improved to 1.2!

Create: Grapple has improved to 1.5!

Interpret: Fieldsense has improved to 1.5!

Transmute: Quick-change has improved to 1.6!

Transmute: Fluidity has improved to 2.0!

Wall Art has improved to 1.3!

Parry has improved to 1.6(+1.1)!

New Technique gained! Create: Brace 0.1 has been added to your stat bio.

Jack marveled at the internal news. He hit 6.0 — one of the biggest to gain, and guaranteed something powerful, often transformational, as could be seen from the likes of Bo’s disorienting debuff zone, Délight’s crazy large and strong buff, and Defiant’s super-fast interruption ability. Conjecture a bit on the last, but it was probably true.

It’s probably usually a bitch of a choice, too. I almost hope one is too good to pass up. Ah, who am I kidding?! It doesn’t even matter. I still wince when I think about not getting Long Bomber, even if Anchors was a no-brainer.

He took a look at the new technique almost automatically.

Create: Brace — Locking and holding material in place against force or strain. The power quality of ‘blocking.’ Integrates Augment into such acts more efficiently.

Good stuff. ‘Jack the Protector’ buffed once again, with synergies.

It was a somewhat sardonic thought, as he’d just come away from an experience where dozens of people had been completely incinerated. At least it was basically instantaneous for them — a small mercy.

We still failed those people, damn it. Not sure what I could’ve done differently. Maybe I could’ve been more ready for that bastard, Mr. Heavenward, to pull something. His vibes were off every which way, after all. But frag, tossing me like a sack of grain through a window? Hard to predict from what seemed to be merely a creepy Cult of Personality type. A Faceman.

As his vessel approached Nimrod, amidst an absolute storm of drones everywhere in the sky, Agent Bermuda contacted him mentally. “Agent Ferro. Quick debrief. We’d do this in person, but everyone is busy right now. I’m doing this while doing multiple other things, myself.”

“I can understand,” Jack replied. “High alert?”

“To put it mildly. Memoria wants to activate wartime powers and occupy every platform, more or less temporarily breaking treaties, and instituting martial law protocols.”

Jack let out a whistling breath and shook his head. “If she wants it, why hasn’t it already happened?”

Annoyance was clear in her tone. “As I understand it, you’ve had this explained multiple times, so stop being obtuse about it. Central is more complex than ‘the Archon show,’ though this is what cadets and the public are directed to think, so they get their warm fuzzies about Mommy taking care of everything. You’re in our world, now, Agent. Grow up.”

Frowning, Jack nonetheless replied, “Yes, ma’am, Agent Nonpareil. Okay… others are disagreeing enough to give her-, er, to make Central pause.”

“Accurate enough, Agent.”

Jack’s vessel had landed on the roof of Central Processing within Nimrod. As soon as Jack stepped out, the Q-23 took off again. As he walked over to the edge to view the swarming base, he asked Bermuda, “Is it because of the strangeness of Mr. Heavenward? He really juked us out?”

“He did. Though we’re still combing through the entirety of the platform inch by bloody inch, it’s believed he somehow got away, likely during the explosion.”

“Advanced stealth tech?”

“Extremely, and obviously, it couldn’t be levitational tech. Something else. No surprise, considering the unknown tech that could make a man nearly boneless. That would be extensive and difficult even for Stitcher.”

“Yeesh. Worrisome.”

“The bigger issue, or perhaps more indicative of the source issue, rather, was that harmonic effect. The buzzing. It even interfered with your psychic link to Neex. This means the Dreamer has managed to import at least some small part of its supernatural abilities right into the center of our territory.”

Watching Nimrod’s many flyers coming and going continuously, whether drone or transport, Jack drummed his fingers on the wall top and felt the same worry Memoria must be feeling, deep in his bones. “But we countered it, didn’t we? Neex got back in touch after the explosion.”

“The harmonics came from a source we couldn’t locate, using the entire construct of the mansion and underways as a kind of sophisticated amp. The explosion interfered with it, but then it just tapered off, with a million different, tiny final notes spread all through our territory. Untrackable.”

“Holy unholy shit…”

“We’re developing a countermeasure, but the Dreamer will have us chasing one step behind its machinations forever and ever.”

“So what do we think is happening and what will we do?”

“The ongoing conundrum. It seems likely the Dreamer found someone truly special to leverage in a way… we don’t fully understand, but certainly is capable of innovative technological invention, in some sense ‘corrupting’ our own. It seems more and more likely that the end result is we have an enemy akin to a versatile Artisan, a gadgeteer, though not through Memoria. That would be truly impossible.”

“Damn,” Jack said verbally, shaking his head. This really was grave. What they’d uncovered made the whole scenario worse than previously imagined. To Bermuda, he said, “It seems likely that the Dreamer has evolved its understanding of us recently. We might’ve stirred that ourselves.”

Bermuda sighed as she replied, “Even if we did, it was inevitable. So, Agent Ferro, what do you think? Do you agree with Memoria?”

“I don’t know. I’m not exactly qualified to make that call.”

“There’s a wise conclusion. But if you had to make a call?”

Jack chewed his lip and considered it. “I would. Frag treaties. This is existential. Too many groups have been manipulating our organization through the rules. They exist for fairness and protection, not to abuse and jeopardize the survival of the species. They can’t protect themselves or the greater public good behind those treaties, so it’s our job. It’s their failure if they allowed our enemies to make dark holes to hide in, carved out of their own territory. Unless you consider the treaties themselves the issue, but that brings us to the same conclusion, doesn’t it?”

He was sure Bermuda was smiling as she declared in false cheer, “You’re a true son of the Mother, Agent Ferro.” After a brief pause, she continued more soberly, “The issue isn’t their feelings, actually. It’s that we play our hand too soon. The main argument is to wait and bait the enemy into thinking we are still dismissive of their relevance. It’s far too likely that the Dreamer has a contingency involving those harmonics. For example, a public message. The Dreamer may not even understand yet how damaging such a thing would be. Otherwise, it would’ve already happened. If we go hard on more versatile and modular countermeasure development against that, perhaps we can block it in time.”

“Oh. Oh, shit, I didn’t even think of that. I take it that’s information from eggheads and such?”

“Yes. Guesswork from the team. Pattern recognition. The Dreamer is deduced to more likely not know the right action to take against humans than not having that capability with harmonics, being such a specialist at… communication. But it's firmly lodged in a mode of clandestine operations, likely from how much it relies on its current figurehead’s perspective. Even with this staggering set of setbacks we just delivered, it will probably try to recover and pursue a similar strategy.”

“And if we go hardcore, playing our hand very publicly…”

“The Dreamer will go Scorched Earth and do as much damage as quickly as possible, instead.”

Taking a deep breath, nodding to himself, Jack found that his adrenaline from the recent events was wearing off, and supreme weariness was hitting him. Physical, mental, emotional, and maybe even spiritual stress was becoming a greater and greater weight. On top of this, it was ‘Too Early O’Clock,’ and he probably needed to sleep. As excited as he was about Level 6.0, Calibration was out of the question right then.

Jack turned and began toward the elevator. He needed to shed the stupid disguise that had been added to him and get home. To Bermuda, he said, “Thanks for making me think about all this. It’s instructive.”

“Of course, Agent,” she replied. “Learning is forever among us.”

“I have one last question: what will happen to my dad?”

“A damn good question, Ferro.”
