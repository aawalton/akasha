The first thing Ryland did was cast a Glyph of Tranquillity. While he wasn’t afraid of anyone doing something regrettable out of fear and whatnot, it would be best for everyone to stay calm. Which was why his blinding Glyph was now high in the air, bathing them in its soothing rays.

Calmness clearly helped. Sint quickly left, saying she’d bring the priest from before. Linsby stepped in front of the rest of the younger students in order to give them some sense of security.

And Ryland was of course in front of the girl afflicted with possession. He modified his next spell circle a bit before casting. “[Dimensional Lock].”

That stopped the Eviliyen girl from moving entirely, apart from the minute motions needed for breathing and heartbeats and other vital functions.

With her now restrained, Ryland deliberated on using his Glyph of Exorcism.

The answer was of course a yes. But he couldn’t be impulsive about it. Much as he hated to see the poor girl’s soul being unseated by a foreign half-spirit, he couldn’t just cast his powerful magic with no thought. The first time he had used the Glyph of Exorcism on Vyrd recently, Ryland had taken Viren high into the air. He had no easy means of doing so here.

Rift magic was more or less blocked by the way the Crypt was constructed, as evidenced by his earlier spells and the need to use summoned ants.

Ryland’s main concern was the widespread effects of the Glyph of Exorcism. It wasn’t a Soul Glyph so he couldn’t modify its parameters as he saw fit with a spell circle. As such, it being over Tier [20] meant its effect would spread far and wide.

An effect that would disrupt mana in much of the Crypts if not all of it.

Ryland still intended to use the Glyph. The only question was just how far reaching its other effects would be. Maybe the priest had an answer, now that Sint had dragged him over.

“Do you foresee any issues?” Ryland asked once he had explained both the situation and his intentions regarding it.

The priest was a lot calmer now that he was under the light of the Glyph of Tranquillity. “You’re likely correct in surmising that the Glyph could cause disruptions throughout the entire Crypts. But that is unlikely. Our walls are robust and have contained magic of all sorts. We hold many souls, including the strongest of those who have passed on in Rhel. Robustness is a defining feature of the Crypts.”

Ryland was quite sure they had never tried to contain him, but maybe he could take some solace in the fact that Glyph of Exorcism wasn’t his strongest magic. He considered using a spell like [Void Seal], but the issue was that the Glyph’s effect wasn’t restricted to mere space.

Things should be fine. Plus, the girl was hurting and Ryland had no intention of waiting for too long.

So, he cast his magic. First came the precautions. “[Ephemeral Fortress]. [Oneself World].”

He cast the combination of spells on everyone present, including the possessed girl. That should hopefully protect their integral, real souls from suffering too much.

“What in all the Realms is this magic?” Linsby asked.

Sint looked like she was torn between laughing at Linsby and just sighing exasperatedly. “First time?”

Ryland shook his head and focused on the magic he needed. Lines and curls of irrepressible mana formed the powerful Glyph of Exorcism in the air, the intensity of its magic rivalling the light from the Glyph of Tranquillity.

Its power acted immediately. The shining strands of mana emanating from the Glyph scoured through space and time, reaching through reality to connect to a level of existence occupied by souls.

Ryland felt the undeniable tug on his own spirit, threatening to break the binds of his power and unleash the manasoul he normally kept suppressed. And if he could feel it to that extent, it was no doubt several degrees worse on everyone else. In fact, through the brilliant spray of magic bursting out of the Glyph, Ryland spotted the others reeling and struggling.

It was the girl who obviously went through it the worst. Her whole body shuddered, her mouth opening in a soundless scream and her manasoul desperately fighting on two fronts.

Ryland tried to hold her steady against the shock, wishing things hadn’t deteriorated to this level. It hadn’t been this severe when he had Exorcised Fate from Viren, though the clutches of Fate were a lot different from the claws of a foreign soul seeking overt dominance.

Thankfully, the Glyph only needed to act for half a minute or so before it was done. With a surge of energy, the foreign soul departed the girl’s body, appearing to the naked eye like a violent spark of electric mana. Ryland frowned. That wasn’t quite an actual soul. Hmm. He was starting to get what exactly these “ghosts” in the Crypts were.

The girl slumped, essentially unconscious, but Ryland caught her before she could fall. He was able to gently hand her off to Sint and the priest.

Neither the priest nor Sint appeared to be in the best of conditions, but at least they were conscious. Both of them were giving Ryland the same sort of look—one of muted awe and wariness. They both almost snatched the unconscious girl away from him like he was the one who had possessed her.

Ryland checked on the other students next. They all appeared woozy and dazed.

“Can you take care of them all, please?” Ryland asked as all his Glyphs faded to nothing.

The priest managed a tight nod. “They will be fine, sub-Academ Marionys Ryland. You need not worry.”

Ryland frowned a little at the use of his full name. He certainly hadn’t gone about telling his name to anyone, and it was hard to fathom someone else doing so. The priest had already turned away, however, so he couldn’t glean anything further from the old man.

Sint was looking around in worry. She looked troubled. “Do you feel that?”

Ryland wasn’t sure what she meant at first. But then, he realized it was her keener physiological senses coming into play.

For in the very next second, the Crypts began shaking.

“Priest,” Ryland said, a little harshly. “I thought you said your Crypt walls could endure magic.”

“I—” The priest looked around madly, eyes wide in fear. “I don’t understand what’s happening!”

The trembling didn’t grow extreme. It never got to the point where cracks appeared on the walls and no dust nor any small debris ever rained down. There was never a danger that the tunnels would collapse on them. Yet, with all of them so many armspans underground, it wasn’t a fear they could easily push away.

Things did, eventually, calm down again. Nothing appeared to have gone wrong, though Ryland didn’t let himself relax just yet.

“What just happened?” Linsby asked. He had been instinctively holding his hands over his head to ward off any falling rocks and such, despite the glint of a barrier spell around himself.

Ryland tried casting [Omniscience] and the fact it worked quite well told him far more than what he actually saw with the spell. “The Crypts’ natural defences have become a bit… unstable.”

“Which natural defences? You mean the mana-conducting walls and ceiling, Ryland?”

“And the floors, yes.”

“What do you see, Ryland?” Sint asked with a note of worry.

“I see… far too much mana. Rioting and storming. Obscuring what’s really going on. And the Crypts’ walls are trying to reinforce its integral properties of conducting and guiding mana, so there’s even more interference than normal. But—”

Loud stomps and the arrival of a powerful magical presence had Ryland focusing away from [Omniscience] for the time being. They all turned to look over at the newcomer hurrying towards them.

“Who amongst you did this?” High Academ Zevo asked. His hard eyes scanned over them all with great speed. Unsurprisingly, he ended up focusing on Ryland. “Ah, it was you.”

“Do you know what’s going on, High Academ?” Ryland asked.

“Do you know—I’m supposed to be asking you that!”

“Well, I’m aware of the technical issues the Crypts are experiencing and I know why it’s occurring. But I’m asking about the overall perspective. Why are we here in the Crypts? What are we doing—”

“Enough.” He started approaching, steps so heavy that it seemed to leave impressions on the ground. Footprints on solid rock. “You’ve disrupted the conduits that carry magic within the Crypts. But there is a way to reverse that. As long as you don’t resist. You don’t want the students to become possessed, do you?”

“They’ve already been possessed,” Ryland said. “Even before the tremors started.”

That made the High Academ come to a stop. “What?”

Ryland explained what had happened leading up to his use of the Glyph of Exorcism. Sint and Linsby added their own corroborating perspectives, as did the priest. The High Academ’s eyes widened more and more.

“That makes no sense,” he said. His eyes were far away, his attention lost to thought. “How are students already being possessed?”

“What do you mean by already?” Ryland asked.

Zevo sighed. “The trace amounts of the spirits that can emerge don’t form actual ghosts or any projection of manasouls that can enter other people to take them over. Spirits need to be quite strong and well defined for that. The ones that might escape the conduits in the walls and floors are broken things, mere formless expressions of directionless energy.”

Ryland thought back to how the girl had acted before the exorcism. He recalled what he had seen emerging from the girl after his Glyph had come into play. “And yet, a possession occurred. You have multiple accounts proving it. You may use a Glyph of Truth to verify, if you wish.”

“No need.” The High Academ stomped over to where the possessed girl was lying in between her unconscious compatriots. “Is this her?”

Sint nodded. “Yes, High Academ.”

Zevo observed the girl for a moment. “She’s a Rhellian.”

They all stared down at the girl. She was wearing a black-and-white uniform, her skin the typical ashen grey of demons.

“She’s a Kuldorean,” Sint said. “From Eviliyen.”

Zevo shook his head. “This girl is originally Rhellian. Of that I’m certain. And it seems, among all the students present nearby, she’s also the one to get possessed.”

Ryland was starting to feel a little bit of foreboding, like he was standing at the precipice of a rather dark cliff. “Even though you’ve said possession should be impossible for the kind of ghosts that the Crypt contains.”

“Even then…”

Ryland considered the sheer number of Rhellian students present in the Crypts. He was trying not to think about the implications of a Rhellian girl in a foreign academy—for there were supposed to be no Rhellians anywhere but on Rhel on a permanent basis—because there was a different kind of worry at the forefront of his mind right then.

Just how many possessed children were running around throughout the Crypts? And why were the Rhellians the target of possession?

“I don’t think this is the best idea,” Acrypha said.

Viren rolled his eyes. “Sometimes, the best idea isn’t always the best idea.”

“What? That doesn’t even make any sense.” Acrypha turned to their other two teammates. “You two agree with me, right? That makes no sense whatsoever.”

Their two new friends, Lyen and Saera, both looked torn. On the one hand, Acrypha was probably right from a moral perspective.

On the other hand… well, on the other, Viren was just being smart.

“Listen Acrypha,” he said very patronizingly on purpose. “Sometimes, working hard isn’t always the best. In fact, it’s actually stupid sometimes. You want to work smart, not work hard all the time.”

“Work smart?” she hissed. “We’re literally just tailing a group of Rhellians!”

“Keep it down, will you?”

“I’m hissing!”

They continued their bickering in hushed tones. Tailing the Rhellians wasn’t proving difficult. They seemed entirely ignorant of the group from Arcoryx far back in the shadows.

Viren had to admit it was a little spooky. Everything was quiet, save for the odd noises here and there. All other sounds were too loud. Acrypha telling him off all the time, his own footsteps and breathing, they all made his heart beat faster and faster.

And it wasn’t like Viren didn’t understand Acrypha’s actual recalcitrance about the matter. The Rhellians moved with a purpose, like they knew exactly where they were going, yet they hadn’t talked with each other in ages and weren’t referring to anything at all. He had suspected that the Rhellians might know more about this Dreadlich’s location than them, but this was uncanny.

Unless, of course, they were being led around to nowhere. No, it couldn’t be. The Rhellians had given no indication they were aware of being followed. They couldn’t be up to some weird trick.

A few moments later, everything began shaking.

It was all Viren could do to keep himself from shouting out in panic while Acrypha had thrown herself backwards to keep Saera’s mouth closed by clamping her hand over the girl’s mouth. She looked terribly scared. Naturally. Viren himself was afraid all the while that the shaking went on too, and Lyen looked like he might just keel over in fright.

Thankfully, it stopped before long. And it stopped without leaving a single mark anywhere.

No cracks, no fractures, no debris. It was as if the shaking hadn’t even happened. Especially when he saw how the Rhellians had continued walking like they hadn’t felt a single thing.

“What’s wrong with them?” Lyen asked.

“What’s wrong with her?” Saera pulled herself away from Acrypha and glared. “I could have died the way you were holding me like that!”

Acrypha rolled her eyes but said nothing in argument.

“Let’s get going,” Viren said, already starting to walk a little quicker since he didn’t want to miss the Rhellians entirely.

“But what about—”

“We’ll worry about it once we get those ashes and then get out. Come on! They’re getting away.”

The rest of the Arcoryx students moved to follow Viren as fast as they could, recognizing that he was right in this case. But they had only been moving for a short while before they all froze.

It was Acrypha who paused first, hissing at Viren to stop. He had, mostly because he recognized the tone in her voice.

They were in danger.

From a group of students who were rapidly starting to look… not really themselves.

Viren and the rest of the Arcoryx students were passing by a junction where multiple tunnels connected. Just as they had entered the centre of the junction, they had stopped. Because from every other tunnel, a group of Rhellians were approaching their location. They were practically surrounded.

What was worse was the vacant, slightly glowing look in the students’ eyes. There was an eerie regard, like none of them actually saw Viren and the others. Not as fellow students, at least.

Just as another obstacle in front of an unknowable goal.

“Run?” Saera suggested.

Viren nodded quickly. There weren’t supposed to be this many of them all at once. He twisted around. “Let’s—”

Then he stopped, a curse almost springing off his tongue. There were Rhellians behind them too, even in the tunnel they had come through.

“There’s something wrong with them,” Lyen said.

Viren agreed with a hasty nod.

“Great,” Acrypha said, her magic flaring. “We really are surrounded.”
