Chapter 3

River

It’s a strange feeling, coming back to the log cabin and its rustic, rough wood front porch. We haven’t really been gone that long, all things considered, but it’s been for longer than this cabin had been standing before we left. Despite this, it still feels like coming home, and a part of my mind is already thinking about how I’m going to make another bed for Korrigan if we’re staying here for long, where I’m going to put it, and whether it’s time to expand.

On the other hand, the house isn’t empty. Someone else has been living here the entire time I’ve been gone. In fact, she’s still here.

No, this isn’t a home intruder situation, and I’m not about to Castle Doctrine a squatter. I paid her to be here, after all.

That’s right, I’m talking about the House Matron who’s been taking care of everything while I’ve been gone!

… Except she’s been living in this house now for longer than I have.

I can’t kick her out, I just can’t. I don’t know what her own living arrangements are, but I can’t make her life unmitigated chaos by flitting in and out of here willy-nilly. I’ll have to find an excuse to keep her on all the time, even if, when I’m here, it means she has mostly paid leave. Of course, that, too, will necessitate a bigger space.

Maybe it’s time I pull the trigger on the construction of the actual house. I need a secure place to work, anyway, and I might as well get that ball started rolling now, instead of waiting for a time that will never be perfect. The workshop could be finished quickly, which would allow me to work on projects while still supervising the rest of the construction.

Of course, that also means that all of us, including the Matron, will likely be staying at the inn. Well, she’ll probably go home, actually, wherever that is, but I definitely at least want to ask her for her input on what the house needs.

Elara, the House Matron, is, eh … I want to say a young woman, but that’s really from my old world’s perspective. She’s probably in the latter half of her twenties, if I had to take a guess, as she’s past the awkward “still looks like a teenager” phase. Unfortunately for her, that makes her almost twice my current age and the oldest-looking person currently seated at my dining table.

A table which, by the way, is completely maxed out on seating. If Kyuuga wasn’t here to play with Korrigan over by the beds, they’d probably both have to be on somebody’s lap. Ironically, it’s my own fault for insisting on making it a western-style table. If it were the local type you kneel at, it would have been a simple matter of dragging over an extra cushion. Instead, Ayre and Yorin are sitting in the chairs at either end, while Elara sits across from me.

Apparently, in addition to coming to greet us, Yorin had arrived in time to also send the woman in to start preparing tea. Courtesy of that, my self-heating kettle and a tray of sweet rice cakes sit between us as we each have our own cups and plates.

ESSENCE RICE CAKE WITH JAM

------------------------------

A sweet and savory snack made from Essence Crops, consisting of a ball of sweetened rice flour dough wrapped around a dollop of fruit jam and then chilled. Simple, yet a classic, flexible favorite of all ages.

Get the recipe.

Consuming this item provides the following benefits:

- Increased Stamina Recovery for 30 minutes

- Increased Mana Recovery for 30 minutes

- Increased Childhood Nostalgia until they run out

Well, gee, I’d almost thought the marketing department had taken the day off upon seeing an entirely normal item description for identifying the cakes. If I didn’t know better from the part telling me what to do, the addition of the extra, completely fluff effect at the end certainly gives it away. Maybe the main department made her tune it down? Or maybe she’s just a foodie and takes good food seriously.

“Remmi,” Yorin’s voice cuts into my focus, “pay attention to what is being said to you instead of Identifying the food.”

I pout a bit, wanting to deny that’s what I was doing since she called it out so precisely, but unable to do so since she’s right. Still, Elara was speaking to me, and I owe her the courtesy of listening.

“Right, sorry.” I place the cake back on my plate and turn my attention back to her. “You were talking about the crop yields?”

She nods, and is kind enough to backtrack a little for me. “I was saying that they’ve shown no loss of quality or quantity. Bushes, vines and trees continue budding and developing in cycles no matter how many times they’re harvested, and ground crops regrow no matter how many times they are cut. The only ones I have to replant are the root vegetables, and if they are delayed any further than any of the others as a consequence, I can’t tell it.”

“Because of this,” Yorin steps in to continue, “your stockpiles waiting to be picked up are so significant that we have increased shipments to the capital, both in variety and quantity, merely to make room for new produce. On the other hand, you are being very generously recompensed, and Imperial chefs have dedicated great effort to deciphering the best combinations and preparation methods. I have a report for you on their findings, though perhaps it is better described as a cookbook.”

I’m almost distracted by the idea of a cookbook assembled by the greatest chefs in the capital specializing in essence crops, especially given that many of them are ingredients they’d never seen before. Still, another concern quickly rises to the front of my mind.

One hand cups my chin as the other arm folds across my abdomen, and I furrow my brows as I chase the thought down. “That may be great from a production standpoint, but isn’t it terrible from a security one? Many of the crops will contain seeds, or don’t need them at all. Every potato, apple or sugar cane that leaves this property is a propagation risk.”

“One would assume as much,” Yorin confirms, “and the thought certainly occurred to us. However, though the protection methods elude us, some do, indeed, seem to be present. We experimented with planting some of the crops elsewhere to see how difficult it would be for a third party to do so, but once removed from the property, even the root vegetables cease to grow new crops. I do not mean that they merely ceased to grow essence crops, but that they refused to grow at all. Even though all of the conditions were met, potatoes rotted in the ground and seeds refused to sprout as if barren.”

That surprises me, and I’m sure it shows on my face. “Wait, they’ll grow when replanted here, but won’t even try anywhere else? Did the seed bullets permanently alter the soil, or something?”

But the priestess shakes her head. “One of the first things we did in response was send specialists out to examine the estate for just such a change. Alchemists and druids both concluded that the soil and environment are, in all relevant, detectable ways, identical to anywhere else in the region.”

She meets my eyes and raises a finger. “The answer is likely far simpler. I remind you, Remmi, that the Essence sees everything we do, constantly monitoring, recording and evaluating us. That these are essence crops implies that they retain some sort of connection to the Heavens. It would thus be a simple matter to witness that they are being used in an unintended way and merely terminate their flow.”

So the System is just unplugging anything used without permission. Huh, that’s convenient. I’m not convinced it’s quite that simple, but it’s as likely as anything else I might suggest, and the difference with the alternatives is probably largely semantic.

“Well, that’s a weight off my chest,” I confirm. “How generous a compensation are we talking about, here?”

“Your exact worth is a somewhat abstract value, and I confess that, for all of my talents, property assessment is not among them,” Yorin admits. “If you wish an exact number for your own records, I can send for a copy. As a rough estimate, you can consider yourself equivalent in income to a moderately successful provincial lord.”

That gets my thinker thunking again. Serazin Province is way too small to meet that description, so does that mean I’m wealthier than Lord Ettia now? Or did I just elevate his wealth by proxy? Though I suppose it’s still possible to out-earn him by exceeding what he collectively takes in taxes.

There is definitely one way I’m surely saving a lot of money he’s not, however. “What about my expenses? I assume I’m on the hook for shipping, guild fees and assorted taxes, and I know I’m covering the inspection fee to the temple and Elara isn’t working for nothing, but I’d guess that’s a lot less than it costs to run a province.”

“It is,” the priestess confirms with a nod. “That will change as your enterprise takes on more responsibilities, such as security and transportation, that have thus far been handled in your name for sake of your role in defense of the empire. It shall not be to a meaningful degree in the grand schema, however.”

Her eyes narrow sternly, in the way they do when I’ve done something wrong. “Normally, a portion would also go to the Temple as tithe, but since you are a foreigner and of differing faith, I have made certain that you are exempted. This will not go unnoticed indefinitely, however, and shall draw considerable ire from clergy and competitors alike when it is uncovered. Before that time comes, you should decide if you want to continue your exemption or choose to contribute formally.”

I can’t hide my nervousness with my grin. I try, but the expression is infected by it as I rub the back of my head. “Gee, Yorin, no pressure … If you think I should be paying tithe, just say so!”

She makes a conscious effort to relax her face as she closes her eyes and tilts her head slightly downward. “Of course I believe you should be paying tithe, Remmi, and not merely because I am a Sacred Priestess and know full well the good those funds would go toward. Everything you have and do is thanks to the heavenly blessing of Essence, especially as a Hero. Even your very crops that have earned you this money came from the System. The Holy Temple of Ascendence represents the mortal servants of those same Heavens, those who seek to understand and enact its mission on this mortal plane.”

Yorin opens her eyes again, though she has carefully avoided the sternness when she looks at me this time. “Tithe is not given in the name of the Temple, but as an expression of thanks for the generosity of the Heavens it represents. For a Hero, of all people, to be seen refusing to pay it … why, it could only be seen as ingratitude.”

I take a deep breath and lean back in my seat as I exhale. “Alright, I’m not blind to the value of optics. I assume you have access to my accounts. Take the appropriate amount out. But if it’s at all possible for it to work that way, I want it to go to the local temple first and foremost. If that money’s really going to improve the lives of the faithful, I want it helping the poorer people here, not the wealthy merchant princes of the capital.”

Yorin’s face immediately lights up in a warm, proud, motherly smile. “Of course, Remmi. I will see that the funds are allocated according to your wishes.”

I know I just sighed, so I resist the urge to do so again as I try to bury the suspicion that I just got taken. “So how much does that affect my actual purchasing power? Now that I have the ability to come back here regularly without interfering with my running all over, I’m thinking it’s time to start on some essential projects for the estate. This cabin is now officially too small, and I need a separate, additional building for working in, plus parts, tools and materials. It’s time to do some upgrades again.”

And then I'm rotating back to Elara. “I also want to expand the crops, potentially by a lot. I’ve got a better idea of what I want now, plus a whole lot more native stuff I’ve learned about. Not to mention, eventually, those cinnamon trees are going to be ready to harvest, if they’re not already. I’ve also got projects planned for essence spirits and essence honey. I’ve got a feeling, though, that we’re rapidly exceeding what you can handle when we’re not here.”

The matron looks apologetic at that. “Yes, I’m afraid that what you’re at now is already a nearly full-time job for me. That they mostly care for themselves is the only reason I can keep up with it. I only need to take care of harvesting, replanting, weeding, fertilizing, feeding the livestock, keeping the records and tending the grounds. The guild comes by to handle sorting the shipments, thankfully. But, yes, if you’re expanding that much, I’m going to need extra hands.”

I nod to her in heartfelt sincerity. “Thank you, Elara, for all of that hard work.” Then, once I get a grateful smile from her, it’s back to Yorin again. “So, yeah, all that, can we afford it without wrecking the Saving the World budget?”

It’s her turn to sigh, at least. “To oversimplify it, you can pretty much afford any single purchase you desire, so long as it is not a village of your own. You are not dealing with a total, absolute sum, so much as you are dipping into a river. It will lessen the amount of water for a time, but it will refill. If you, indeed, intend to delay your return to the field to improve your performance, your finances will have the necessary time to recover. I have already been in touch with amenable contractors in anticipation of such projects, and they can be here to compare plans within the week.”

I beam her a bright grin of my own. “You’re amazing, Yorin!” But then I put my hands on the table and lean forward again. “Speaking of the animals, though, how’s the experiment progressing?”

Elara and Yorin share a look between the two of them before turning back to me, and it’s the matron who answers.

“Would you like to come see?”
