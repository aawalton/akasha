A thing may speak, bargain, flee, plead, and still be no more conscious than a sufficiently advanced summoning construct. Apparent personhood is not proof of personhood.

- Dr. Adrienne Calloway, lecture transcript, San Aurelio Institute of Technology.

Kal knelt in the dirt and pressed his fingers firmly against Erin’s throat, alongside her windpipe. Her pulse was quick, but not emergency-room quick. More like right-after-a-sprint speed, and her breathing sounded shallow. Her face was covered in a sheen of sweat, but if he had to guess, gauging by what he had experienced when the Dusk Wraith had dropped in from the ceiling, she was locked in some sort of nightmare.

“Erin,” he whispered, shaking her gently. When he got no response, he tried again a little more vigorously.

She’s not waking up.

Kal rose, scanning the cavern. Every single member of the harvest crew was down, lying motionless on the ground. The only people moving were the hunters, himself, and the Lycan who sat beside Erin, sniffing the air with eyes that relentlessly scanned the dark shadows.

Healing. She needs healing.

He searched for Nirelle, but found the druidic healer huddled together with Brannic, speaking quietly. The armored fighter suddenly turned and fixed Kal with an unreadable gaze.

Kal’s heart froze.

“I’ll deal with that later,” Brannic said, loudly enough to hear as he turned back to the healer. “Wake up the harvesters.”

“Right,” Nirelle said, and she waved her staff before sending out a ripple of an almost invisible greenish glow that suffused the chamber in a soft warmth. Immediately, the sleepers, locked in their nightmares, began to stir and moan.

While the healer worked her magic, the heavily armored warrior strode across the cavern toward the first Dusk Wraith corpse. The demon lay slumped against the wall, like a mound of shadow made real. Several grievous slashes leaked dark blood onto the rock, sizzling quietly. The entire left-hand side of the corpse’s soft black fur had been scorched away by the intensity of Tess’s flames.

As Kal watched, a glow formed within the dead monster, somehow seen through the flesh and fur, intensifying as it rose from within. It was a phenomenon, rarely seen, that never failed to catch his breath. The glow deepened, and a crystalline protrusion sprouted from the corpse. It grew longer, thickening quickly as it refracted the dim light of the cavern, casting patterns of light out in all directions. A second branch appeared, and then another as the crystalline Aether Blossom emerged, at once solid, and yet simultaneously insubstantial—as if it wasn’t quite attached to reality, phasing through the corpse, rather than sprouting out of it.

When the blossom tree reached the height of about two feet, a bud appeared, thickening and strengthening as if reaching for the sky.

Slowly, almost majestically, the Aether Blossom unfurled its crystal petals, and the spherical mana seed floated up out of the center, hovering an inch above the crystal flower that had spawned it. The prize. A jewel barely the size of an egg, it rotated slowly, scattering sparkling refracted light across all the nearby rock surfaces.

Deep within the crystalline orb, something pulsed. Immediately, a malevolent light shot up out of the corpse, a red glow so dark it seemed black began to stain the crystal tree from the base to the tips of the branches, and the petals of the Aether Blossom darkened. The light burst into the center of the floating orb and began to pulse slowly, like a heartbeat pierced through with ominous flecks of darkness that swirled like animated wraiths. Just looking at it caused mad whispers to echo through Kal’s mind, and he felt the sounds of an endless scream prickling like ice down his spine.

Brannic’s fist closed about the orb, and it vanished in a flash. The delicate crystalline leaves, petals and stem shattered into chunks that spilled out across the ground in a musical tinkle. Aether Crystals.

Kal took a deep breath and swallowed. It was an E-rank mana seed of the worst compatibility grade—useful only as crucible fodder, to be broken down for mana. Still, a red crystal like that had to be worth a minimum of twenty-five thousand credits, maybe as much as fifty, enough money to pay for Mira’s tuition for the rest of the year, and Brannic had absorbed it like it was nothing.

“Who gets the other one?” Drex asked, plucking the second mana seed out of the air, holding it between his thumb and forefinger as he gazed longingly at it.

“I do,” Brannic said, snatching it out of his grasp and absorbing it immediately.

“Hey! How is that fair?” Drex exclaimed, his eyes narrowing as he glared at Brannic. A couple of his teammates seemed to go green at the sight.

“I saved all your useless asses just now, and these two measly E-rank red seeds will barely cover my mana deficit. If you want to earn your share, you’d better start doing your jobs,” Brannic snapped. “Now, prepare to move out as soon as the harvesters are all up. And you’d better not let another one of those demons into this cavern without noticing. You’re monster hunters. A seedless harvester saw the demons while you lot were busy navel-gazing.”

He spared Kal a glance and a nod of approval.

Drex spun on his heel and stalked off stiff-legged, his face red with rage, but he held his tongue. Nirelle continued pouring her healing magic into the air, and the other two silently carried out Brannic’s orders, staking out the exits carefully, but there was a fresh undercurrent of sullen anger in the glances they exchanged.

Huh, I guess that’s one approach. Despite his worries about being outed by Nirelle and potentially facing being ejected from the run for working with mana sickness, Kal couldn’t help but worry about the tension Brannic had just tossed among the hunters. Nobody liked being treated like that. Made me a target, too. Best tread carefully now.

Beside him, Erin stirred, and he bent to help her up. The hunters would just have to take care of their own issues for a bit.

“Ugh,” she managed. “Bad dream.”

“Reality was worse,” Kal admitted, reviewing his recollection of the fight again in detail. “Those things must have some kind of psychic attack.”

That fear was definitely unnatural. Kal frowned to himself. Both Dusk Wraiths had been E-rank, and both had dropped only the single mana seed each. Which meant that they had, at most, two Aether Capacity available for skills. The trouble was, he’d seen evidence of three. The psychic attack, the billowing shadows that cloaked their presence, and then there was the insane speed with which they’d tangled with Brannic and his fiery sword. Clear evidence of haste or melee attack speed enhancement. Unless the monsters had an unknown extra source of capacity, one of their skills had to be some sort of hybrid with multiple effects.

The Lycan scampered over and sniffed Erin and then licked her on the cheek. “Protect.”

“Hey, that tickles.”

“Good job,” Kal said, earning a happy grin from the Lycan, which revealed a pristine white set of small, sharp canines.

“What happened?” Erin said, clambering to her feet clumsily, apparently still feeling the aftereffects of that mental battering.

“We were attacked by two demons,” Kal answered, twisting his lips into a rueful line. “Dusk Wraiths.”

“At the same time?” Erin gasped, her eyes widening dramatically.

“Yes.”

“How did we survive?”

“Brannic took them out,” Kal said, gesturing to the corpses. While the shot he fired might have been what snapped Brannic out of the demon’s psychic attack, it was also possible that the D-rank warrior would have overcome it on his own, even without his gunshot. Still, Kal had been so rattled he’d been unable to shoot effectively. I need to do better. What was all that training for?

“Ooh!” Erin said, quickly scrambling to open several pouches. “Get your vials! We need to collect the blood before it all leaks out! And bring your tools, that pelt is worth a fortune. We need to load up both corpses!”

“Right,” Kal managed, taken aback by her sudden burst of energy. Hopefully enough of the pelts remained unburnt—Tess had been enthusiastic after her run-in with the demon’s fangs.

“Wait, what happened to the mana seeds?” Erin asked, snapping her gaze back at him.

“Brannic used them,” Kal said, fighting to keep the sour note out of his voice. Fifty thousand credits down the drain. Those seeds alone would see Mira through her studies at least five times over.

“Shit. Well, that’s the hunter’s prerogative,” Erin muttered, but then she brightened. “Come, we need to collect those crystal trees. The leftover aether crystals are worth a fortune. Arkanis will probably pay nearly as much as one of those seeds for this haul; those Barrier Pylons aren’t going to power themselves. Then, harvest the demon corpses. Let’s earn our paycheck.”

Kal suddenly found himself very busy.

***

Kal was already unreasonably tired from dragging the cart, now much heavier and filled with the harvested remains of a Dusk Wraith, when Brannic raised a hand and their party ground to an immediate halt. “Drex,” he said, his voice a clipped imperative order.

“Yo,” the big man said, causing a visible wince at the casual departure from the military-style discipline Brannic clearly favored, but the heavyset bruiser lost no time stepping up to join their leader as the two of them ventured into the wider opening in the tunnel system.

“Tess, Sarn, stay sharp, we’re going in,” Brannic ordered.

In the darkness beyond them was a fork in the path, but where the tunnels met, the flow of crimson water had eroded an opening bigger than Kal’s apartment. One of the passages leading onward was clearly a river channel allowing the flow to exit, and the other seemed to be a tunnel leading onward at a slight upward slope.

The moment they entered the wider area, several pitch-black, gleaming shapes fell from the roof. The first cracked heavily against Brannic’s armor, the second struck Drex on the back of his neck and stuck there, while the third thumped down heavily between them.

Then the legs unfurled. Far too many legs and glowing crimson eyes.

Mandibles like obsidian ice picks bit down, scraping across Brannic’s Arkanis armor with a shudder-inducing shriek, while the one clamped onto Drex’s back drew a roar of fury and a spray of crimson blood as those fangs pierced the side of his neck.

Kal had never heard of a demon like it. It looked like a cross between a dog-sized spider and a razor-wire fence. Drex spasmed, lashing out and flinging his assailant across the chamber, causing it to smash into the wall near the entrance where Nirelle and Tess stood guard.

It hissed, rolling back upright, and Kal was granted a perfect view of the wickedly sharp barbed spikes that formed a thick ruff along its thorax and head, each leaking a steady flow of fluorescent green ichor that matched the venom on its mandibles.

Drax was already howling in pain and clutching at his neck as a visible dark necrosis began to set in. “Fucking help me!” he screamed. Fortunately for everyone, the bruiser’s screaming had the monster’s full attention.

The shadows shivered, and Sarn appeared, stabbing several times in quick succession into the abdomen while avoiding the flared bouquet of dangerously sharp venomous spines. “Stalker,” he muttered. “Great. Just what we needed. Tess, finish this one off. I’ll go help Brannic.”

“I’ll keep the big oaf alive until that shit wears off,” Nirelle grumbled. “Don’t get bit, Sarn. I don’t have enough mana for two of you—don’t make me choose.”

“Right,” he said, and vanished with a parting grimace. He appeared moments later, darting around Brannic’s fiery sword arcs to land opportunistic slashes wherever he could, while Tess opened up with a stream of firebolts.

The fight was over in minutes, Brannic once again proving his competence and the effectiveness of his armor, but Drex continued screaming in pain while Nirelle fed healing magic into him.

“Can’t you cure it?” Brannic asked.

“Nope,” Nirelle said. “The toxin is still destroying him. I can only keep healing until it wears off.”

“Fuck,” he said. “Take one of the seeds; we can’t have you running out of mana.” Brannic turned away but then looked back over his shoulder. “Nirelle?”

“Yes?”

“If you get close to saturation, stop healing him.”

“Right,” she said, a grim expression on her face.

“What the fuck!” Drex screamed.

“Don’t get bitten next time,” Brannic said.

“Asshole,” he hissed through clenched teeth.

“Everyone inside,” Brannic ordered, ignoring the insult. “Let’s lock this place down.”

***

Kal dragged another of the strange, spikey Stalker demon corpses out of the way to give Erin room to collect every last shard of the Aether Blossom trees, exquisitely aware of the placement of every ichor-smeared spike. Those aether crystals were Kal’s best hope of profit, and his eyes were peeled, searching for even the slightest glint from the dirt.

Brannic had demonstrated his experience, and after he’d set a proper marching order and outlined a strategy, things had gone much more smoothly for the next several packs, and the hunters were now mostly in good spirits, given the number of seeds collected.

The obvious exception was Drex, who had been bitten by the spider demons’ Venomous Bite three times now, and nobody was even remotely interested in sharing the ten minutes of agony he’d had to endure each time while Nirelle kept him from dying.

“This is a good spot,” Brannic announced when Drex’s latest round of wailing faded to whimpers, and he finally sat up. “Let’s take our lunch break here.”

Oh, thank goodness. Kal stood there for a moment with his hands on his knees, catching his breath after he’d dragged yet another of the forty-pound monsters out to the pile against the side wall. He stripped off his heavy work gloves, making sure not to get any of the green stuff on his skin.

While not nearly as dangerous individually as the Dusk Wraiths, these hell-spider demons were technically a rare find. They hunted in packs of three to five, stalking along the roof until they could fall onto their prey, either impaling it with their spines or biting to inject their nasty blood toxin. Erin had insisted he add one to his cart, along with more than a dozen vials of that nasty glowing ichor.

It meant Kal’s cart was even heavier now, but Erin was certain they could get good coin for them. He was bushed. Grateful for the break, he pushed his cart up against a wall in the secured cave and began to stash the remaining vials of venom.

The tremors are getting worse. Surreptitiously, he studied his hand, noticing that the trembling seemed to be coming more frequently now, and it was growing harder to hide. Not as much as the Spark addict, but somehow, Tess got a free pass for being a hunter.

“May I have one of those?”

Kal turned to find Sarn standing nearby. The rogue nodded toward the vials. “Uh, sure,” Kal said, grabbing a full one and handing it to him.

Sarn unstopped it and decanted a few drops onto the blade of his dagger, where the fluid seeped into the grooves that had been added for precisely this purpose. “Thanks. Better than my old stuff.” He grinned wickedly and then turned and headed back to where the rest of the hunters were sitting.

Kal sank to the ground and dropped his shaky hands into his lap with a sigh. Glad he didn’t notice. He raised his eyes to study the cavern. It seemed unremarkable, just an irregular oval hole in an alien world with nothing but rocks and the ever-present crimson pools fed by the slow dripping of water from above. The wall to the left had collapsed outward, creating a floor-height window into an enormous chamber that dropped away sharply into a complete underworld ecosystem far below their vantage point. It was the same chamber that had been beside the portal when they’d entered this world, but now seen from the opposite side, confirming his earlier suspicion that they had been circling it.

When Kal had been searching for a place to dispose of the excess corpses, he’d glanced down, finding a dense field of sharp metallic stalagmites, shrouded in Dusk Ivy and shadows more than twenty feet below. He’d promptly decided not to ditch anything out there just in case he attracted something worse.

As soon as he’d recovered sufficiently, Drex had been assigned watch at the window for anything crawling or flying in.

“How are you doing? Hanging in there?” Erin asked, plopping herself down nearby.

“I’m ok,” he said. It was not like he could do anything about his condition. Mana sickness did not get any better by diving into a mana-rich portal world.

At the opposite end of their resting cave, Brannic and Sarn were inspecting two exit tunnels, chatting quietly together, their voices too low to carry. The larger tunnel seemed to have an upward slant, twisting away from the huge central chamber, and cloaked in shadowy darkness. The second, smaller tunnel went downward at a steep angle and glowed with a faint violet light, and whenever Kal’s gaze came anywhere near it, he felt a subtle pull, like something was calling to him, but not in any language he knew.

“Hey, does anything about that smaller tunnel feel strange to you?” he asked.

“No. Seems like a perfectly good tunnel,” Erin smiled, unpacking her lunch and shifting her back against the rocky wall to find a more comfortable position. “For a demon-infested hellhole, that is. We might want to check out that glow, though, in case it’s more of those Shadestone Crystals—that would be sweet.”

“Good point,” Kal said, nodding. Is it just me feeling weird sensations down here? Still, with the two Dusk Wraith corpses stashed and a moderate pile of aether crystals from the grisly work by the monster hunters, he was feeling a little more secure about the value of this run, but if they could find another Shadestone Crystal deposit, that would push it well into ‘lucrative.’

If someone doesn’t get trigger-happy with their fireball again.

Brannic walked back to the group, his heavy boots crunching on stone, and Sarn was nowhere to be found. In a flash of white, the Lycan bounded up to him and sniffed at his arm. “Can I see it?” she asked with her tail wagging excitedly.

When he paused and stared at her, she pointed enthusiastically at the sword sheathed at his waist.

“No.”

“Please?” She bounced up and down eagerly.

Brannic stopped and turned toward the Lycan. He drew himself up, towering over the girl, and glared at her. “I said no. Buzz off.”

Her tail drooped, and she hunched over, making herself smaller, and she scampered away to find a rocky hollow and curled up beside the wall.

Kal kept his expression carefully neutral. Head down. Stay invisible.

“What is she thinking?” Erin muttered. “I’d never bother a D-rank like that. He’s grumpy and strong enough to crush her.”

“I think that’s why,” Kal said, eyeing the big warrior as he patrolled the cavern restlessly.

“What do you mean?”

“Lycans are an evolved pack species, right?”

“Yes?”

“I don’t think she’d be working this job if she were a member of a pack,” Kal said. Finding a pack of Lycans on Valmyra had to be slim pickings at the very best. “She must be alone.”

“Oh, you think she’s looking for a new pack?” Erin asked, glancing speculatively at their white-haired companion sitting by herself.

“Seems that way. That’s why she’s interested in all the hunters. They’re the strongest.”

“So, she’s lonely?”

“Yes, I guess. If one of the hunters took her in, she’d be part of a strong pack. That probably represents safety, food, prestige, and access to eligible mates in her culture or instincts. If a D-rank like Brannic took her in, I doubt it would matter to her if she was treated poorly.”

“That’s sad,” Erin said. “Those instincts are going to get her taken advantage of. Hopefully she’s learned her lesson.”

“I don’t think so,” Kal said, nodding to where the Lycan had raised her head and was sniffing at the air. “If I’m right, she will try her luck with Drex next. Or Sarn.”

“Not Nirelle or Tess?”

“I don’t think she likes Tess, and I’d bet Nirelle isn’t enough of a leader for her,” Kal said, turning the thoughts over in his head. “Besides, I think she favors physical strength.”

Sure enough, a few minutes later, the Lycan bounced to her feet and bounded across the rocky ground to the window leading out onto the vast open cavern beyond. She came right up to Drex, sniffing his arm and then the chunk of sashimi held between his cheap bamboo chopsticks. Then she sniffed at the expensive bento box resting in his lap.

“Food?” she asked, wagging her bushy white tail as her ears twitched expectantly.

“Yes, it’s food,” Drex said, his eyes narrowing. “Smells good, right?”

“Mhm!” she nodded vigorously.

He popped the piece of fish in his mouth and chewed with exaggerated relish. “So good!”

The Lycan’s eyes tracked his chopsticks back to the box as he snagged another piece.

“Do you like fish?”

“Yes! Yes!” She wagged her tail again, her eyes following the path of the fish into his mouth.

“Well, it’s mine. Piss off, bitch. Go find your own food.” He pushed his steel-clad left fist against her stomach and shoved with enough force to send a normal human flying. The Lycan stumbled backward and stared at him with naked hurt in her eyes.

“Drex! Language!” Nirelle snapped.

“What? She’s literally a bitch,” he said. He grinned smugly and shrugged. “It’s the correct word.”

“You’re an asshole.”

Yes, and a few other words I could name, Kal thought sourly. What was the point of that?

“Yeah, but you like me, don’t you?”

“Fuck you, Drex. In your dreams.”

“Fuck me, eh? So, you’re warming up to me then?” Drex said. “Just say when.”

Nirelle coughed and spluttered, and her face flushed red. She leveled an angry glare at him with her fists on her hips, but after a moment, she just spun around and stalked off.

Drex laughed loudly and shoved another piece of fish in his mouth.

I bet it mysteriously takes longer to heal him next time. Can’t say that aggravating the healer’s a great survival strategy.

The Lycan slunk off to lick her wounded pride, curling up against a rock nearby. Erin stared forlornly at her as she began to whimper. “Girl, you have the worst taste in men,” she muttered.

“You can say that again,” Kal said. So far, Drex had proven to be exactly as mean as he’d guessed. Good to know my instincts are sharp, he thought as he unclipped his plastic sandwich container from its place on the utility belt and cracked it open. Immediately, the Lycan’s head snapped toward him, her nose sniffing the air, but her ears drooped and she began to turn away. Kal caught her gaze and then flicked his left ear with his hand, then his right. He placed the backs of both hands up against his chin and said, “Awoo.”

I don’t have a tail, I hope that’s enough.

The Lycan stared at him, her eyes opening as wide as saucers.

“Do you like chicken?” Kal asked.

Hesitantly, she nodded.

Kal pulled half his expensive sandwich out of the box, noting that, at least, the vendor hadn’t skimped on meat, and held it out. In a flash, the Lycan bounded over to him and sniffed hungrily at the sandwich and then gazed up at him with big violet eyes.

“Yes, you can have it. Come sit with us. I’ll protect you,” Kal said. With my mana-poisoned, seedless body. Nice promise, Kal.

Tentatively, she took the sandwich and then squeezed herself in between Erin and him. She peeled off the bread and dropped it.

Kal’s hand darted out and caught it. “Hey, don’t waste it.”

She froze in the middle of stripping the lettuce and tomato off the sandwich and stared at him.

“This is good food,” he said, waving the rescued slice of bread at her.

She wrinkled her nose at him and shook her head vigorously.

“Ahh, carnivore. Right,” he said, reaching out his hand. “Why don’t you give me the pieces you don’t like, then?”

Smiling, she slapped the wet slice of tomato into his outstretched palm and added the lettuce and the remaining slice of bread. She paused, glancing speculatively at the pile in his hand, snatched the bread back, licked the butter off it, and then returned it. Then she shoved most of the chicken in her mouth and made a little, happy moan as she wolfed it all down.

Kal shrugged and took a bite of his now much smaller sandwich. Damn. I was looking forward to that chicken. Now it’s just a soggy salad sandwich.

“Eww, she licked that,” Erin said.

“I’m hungry, too,” Kal offered. Living with Mira had long cured him of any squeamishness about dogs, among other animals, and the Lycan was basically an evolved wolf. It’s probably fine.

The wolfkin girl gazed at him, and then her eyes dropped to the remaining half of his sandwich, still nestled in its box. She looked up and repeated the gesture twice more.

“I wonder what you could possibly mean…” Kal said drily.

Erin chuckled.

Kal opened his remaining sandwich, stripped out the chicken, and gave it up, spawning a flurry of rhythmic thumping as the Lycan’s tail slapped Erin’s ribs and the cavern wall. Then he finished the tomato and lettuce sandwich, wishing he’d had the foresight to buy another. Not like they could afford to be fussy about food.

Please, please let this be the payday we need.
