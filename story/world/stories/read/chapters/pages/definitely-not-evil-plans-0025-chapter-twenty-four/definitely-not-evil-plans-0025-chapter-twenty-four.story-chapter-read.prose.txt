Thursday November, 3rd

The coffee shop was busy when I arrived. People on laptops, carrying various drinks from the counter to their table, probably more than one working on their ‘novel’ that was sure to be a breakout hit. Hushed conversations drowned out the light radio station playing over the shops speakers.

I spotted Kate at our usual table. A large latte sat in front of her and dark circles framed her eyes that were staring absently into space. My heart dropped and guilt clawed at my throat threatening to move to my eyes. She didn’t look good.

I put in my order and tried to steel my nerves. After I’d ordered I made my way over to her table.

“Hey, how’s it going?” I asked cautiously.

When she didn’t respond I pulled the chair out from across from her. I laid a hand on her arm, “Hey, you okay?”

Kate jumped, “Oh hey Sam. I didn’t see you come in.”

“Doing okay? You don’t look so good.”

“Honestly? I have no idea.” She wrapped her hands around her mug like she required it for warmth. “It’s been a very long twenty-four hours or so.”

“I know you only talked briefly on the phone about it. Tell me everything.”

My heart was aching. I didn’t want Kate to get hurt. Hopefully once the board got things settled things would get way better.

“The board is in full panic mode. Like I said I got called in to help with the designers and to do damage control with clients. Which has been a nightmare in of itself. The stock is dropping, there is talk of selling off branches of the company. The California office might be the first to go.

They’re talking about a complete HR and Management overhaul to appease the stock holders. No one knows what’s going to happen.” She took a long sip of her latte. “Julia has been in the bathroom almost all afternoon yesterday and then this morning. She and Dev just bought a house. The only reason they went for it was because her job was secure.”

“Oh fuck.”

“And Terrence in security was let go yesterday for ‘restructuring.’  As if security had anything to do with any of this. He’s got three kids and his wife just had surgery. I just feel terrible.”

I thought about Terrence’s kind eyes. The way he lovingly watered that probably fake succulent on his desk. He’d squeezed my shoulder and told me that I didn’t deserve to get escorted out. Now, I wondered how much he’d unwillingly known or suspected.

Kate went on, “Oh, and IT is also going through a huge culling. Something about security flaws in the system. The security analyst, Chu, has already been let go. A bunch of others have been in meetings all morning.”

The IT team had always been really nice, if not painfully awkward. Not that I had much room to talk mind you.

“What about you?” I managed to squeak out. “Is your job safe?”

Kate shrugged. “For now? Probably. I’m one of the most tenured designers in this location which means I’m fielding most of the client escalations.” She sighed, “I’ve been updating my resume anyway. Maybe I can find us a new job together somewhere. I know we’ve talked about starting our own consulting company but I didn’t think it’d be this year.”

A hollow and humorless laugh escaped her mouth.

I grabbed her hand.

“Oh. Kate.” My voice cracked. What had I done? “I’m so sorry.”

“It’s not your fault.” She paused a second, tired eyes staring deep into mine. “It’s not right?”

“Kate–”

“I know. I know. Greg confessed, so all of this is his fault.” She looked away, “You have to admit this is all such weird timing. First, Daniel and then your question about karma, and now this. It just feels like a month ago everything was fine and now everything is imploding.”

“Sometimes the universe has a sense of justice though, right?” Even I didn’t sound convinced as it left my mouth.

“The universe doesn’t usually lead a man with nothing gain to confessing crimes he’s been committing for years. And, like not even confessing to a priest and then retiring to Bermuda. To confess to journalists is just crazy.”

“Maybe, it is.” I stirred my coffee. “Maybe he wanted to tell his version before someone else did.”

“Like who?”

“I don’t know… his priest?”

She chuckled and rolled her eyes. “Touche. Although I don’t think priests can tell anyone what someone’s confessed.”

“Really?”

She shrugged.

“Well,” I continued. “At least people will be getting justice. All those women, especially Jessica.”

Kate’s brow furrowed, “Jessica? Like Jessica Hudson?”

Shit. They hadn’t released any names yet. Shit. shit. Shit. I tried to keep my voice casual, “You mentioned her on Halloween and it got me thinking, maybe that’s why she ghosted us.”

“Maybe I should reach out to her again, it’s been awhile.”

“Don’t do that. I was just saying, you know, if he has been doing this to women for years that she could’ve–” I realized I was digging myself deeper, “You know what? This is all speculation and I shouldn’t do that. Reality is already bad enough.”

Kate was quiet for a moment as she studied me. She was doing that Kate thing, reaching into my soul to pull out my secrets. She rubbed her head and closed her eyes.

When she opened them again, they looked moist, tear-filled. “I guess you’re right. When I think of all the people who left suddenly over the years.” She trailed off and shuddered. “I always thought Jessica just got a better offer somewhere. She was so talented.”

“And so kind,” I added, looking down intensely at my latte.

“Remember how she would always stay late to help with our presentations?”

“Yeah.” My throat was tight and I knew Kate would hear how close I was to losing it.

“After she stopped responding I figured she was busy, or just wanted a clean break.” Kate looked back out the window, “Now I wonder if she just couldn’t talk about it.”

The NDA and accounting pay outs burned in my brain. “At least now people will know.” I said. “The truth is coming out. Hopefully that counts for something.”

“I hope so.” Kate didn’t sound convinced. “I just keep thinking about everyone we know at H&S. How many people are going to pay for Greg’s crimes? Like if he was really sorry maybe he should’ve stepped down and confessed like.. Months from now when no one cared who he was.”

“Greg really sucks.” I said weakly.

“That’s an understatement. Like, how many board members knew about all of this? Did his dad?”

“If they did, they should’ve done something decades ago.”

“I know,” She sighed again. “I’m just tired Sam. I can’t sleep, I can't get rid of this headache. I’m just tired of everything being terrible. And, my eyes hurt!” She rubbed at them. “Working on a screen all day really sucks when your eyes burn just from looking at it.”

Kate looked up pleadingly, “Why can’t every day just be Halloween. Can we just go back and have Halloween every day? Just me and you and Witches brews?”

“Hey.” I reached across the table and squeezed her hand. “We still have our date tomorrow right? Just you and me and over priced cocktails. I can call it a witches brew if it’ll make you feel better.”

“Will you wear the dress?” She asked innocently and did her best impression of being excited.

“For you, I’d live in that dress.”

Kate smiled.

“Just you and me and overpriced cocktails and that dress. We’ll ogle men, judge strangers, it’ll be just like Halloween.”

Some of the tension left her shoulders. “You’re right. We need to find that mystery hottie.”

“That’s the spirit. In a city of hundreds of thousands we’re bound to run into that one guy again.”

“Although, if we’re going to a bar, you actually need to talk to people this time.”

“I talked to people!”

“Yeah, okay.”

“I got that card from that one lady.”

“Keeper Witch?”

“Yeah, I think so.”

“Well, the last time I talked you into going to a bar you spent like an hour on wikipedia reading about the history of gin!”

“Come on, it was interesting. How else would we know that juniper berries aren’t really berries?”

Kate laughed and for a moment I believed everything would be okay again. Maybe not like old times, but it could be better. Kate and me against the world and I’d tell her about the code and we’d figure it out together.

A commotion behind us caused me to turn. Someone swore and a metal chair scraped against the tile causing the hairs on my arms and neck to stand on edge. And then something warm splashed all over our table and down my pant leg.

“Oh no. Oh god. I’m so sorry. I’m so sorry… Here let me help.”

I blinked and sat back to see a man about my age, maybe a little older holding an empty coffee cup and looking mortified. He was looking around frantically for, I could only assume, the napkins. After spotting some he scampered away.

Kate and I looked at each other and I scooted out of the path of dripping coffee.

He was cute in a kind of nerdy way. He had plastic rimmed glasses and just slightly too long, messy hair. Gentle laugh lines around the eyes gave the impression that he smiled a lot. And his shoulders definitely made it well known that he’d been in a gym once or twice.

I recognized him. Holy shit, it was the Dead Pirate Roberts from Halloween. Did I speak him into existence?
