Gods, I wanted that Boon so badly. Especially since the Instance looked to be about protecting ten people. The only issue was [Confiscation] — last time it took [Open the Path], but I could easily imagine it taking this one if I didn't have enough Restrictions.

I finally understood how truly unfair the five Restriction Instance was, and how unfair even the offered three Restriction Instance would be. I had at least chosen to go into the fields. Sought it out even, but I had no say in this one. To add insult to injury, I was given just twenty-two hours to prepare. How was I expected to get a Boon and two Restrictions in that time? I doubted I was. I stared up at the shattered starry sky and sighed.

There was no point in complaining. The Dream just did not care. If it was possible to win, and whatever it arbitrarily decided was fair, it was acceptable.

I turned my attention back to Jeren, my voice strained.

"Do you think someone would be willing to sell a Restriction or two?"

He gave me a sad shake of his head. "Maybe if it were another time, but after what just happened, I doubt anyone would be willing to part with theirs."

I swallowed. I saw the path forward closing around me as every possible escape turned out to be just another damned wall. I tried again anyway.

"Nate- I guess just Maggie. She had some decent Boons and at least three Restrictions." My lips were dry, my throat was hoarse. "If you don't have plans for her. I'd like her kit."

Quinn's head snapped to me, her eyes narrowed, and even Jeren's breath seemed to hitch. It was Quinn who responded, not yet hostile, but definitely getting there. "The only way is for them to be given willingly, and she's not completely coherent at the moment. Even if she were, I doubt she'd hand it over willing, and I'm sorry to say, but we're not in the business of torture."

I knew that—damn it, I knew. She had told me that much herself, even. Words poured from my mouth. Responses were given, and it felt like I was spectating it.

"I could convince her.""She's in no state to talk, she's not even Lucid.""Isn't that better?""What?""She might not understand it's a bad deal.""We're not doing that.""Why do you even care? She tried to kill all of you. If I get her kit, all the better, right?"

Silence.

"Kid…" It was Jeren this time.

"What!" I snapped.

He opened his mouth, and I could see the responses forming, but he said nothing. Just turned to Quinn.

I couldn't read the expression on her face, but like Jeren, it looked like she had a lot to say. She said nothing for a long while, too.

I wish Nate hadn't been taken by the Dream. At least then I'd have someone lucid to negotiate with, but if I could trick Maggie.

Finally, Quinn spoke, "Our company doesn't deal in Boons and Restrictions on a large scale. We bought two before we got caught in the shuffle. They aren't good, but our company hadn't seen them before, so we picked them up."

I said nothing. She twisted a ring on her finger, and I saw her eyes fill with white and black pixels. Then two motes of light sprang into existence in her hands. "You did us a good turn, and you're in a shit situation. So here."

I reached out to the balls of light.

[Restriction]

[Untrustworthy: In an Instance, every word you say sounds like a lie regardless of sincerity or truth. People will have a natural inclination to distrust you regardless.]

[Tracking Wound: Wounds make you more visible. Any injury you receive inside an Instance will softly glow until you re-enter The Endless Dream. This glow penetrates clothes and covers, and most environments, making it impossible to hide.]

I had no clue if these were good or bad, but I knew they wouldn't synergize with the Instance, so I took them. Anything was better than [Bad Start].

"Thank you," I choked on the words a little. "I'm uh," I started, a little awkwardly, "probably not able to join you all on your descent, because of the Instance, but if all goes well. Where can I find you?"

Quinn paused before letting out a heavy sigh.

"Quinn," Jeren pressed, but the woman shook her head, "I'm sorry, but I can't tell you."

My heart constricted, and I wanted to ask why, but the words wouldn't come. She held up her hand and continued. "You haven't been in the Dream for long, so you won't get it. There are so many things here that it's impossible to defend against, and more often than not, those threats are other Visitors. There was a world that used to be close to ours—a trade partner. One day, a lone wanderer came, saved the Prince of one of the most influential countries from a particularly nasty Instance. The Lone Wanderer and the Prince became inseparable. Together, they grew powerful enough to bring the Princes' world under a single banner. Then the Wanderer killed the Prince and brought a terrible host to pillage everything they had built. The host infiltrated refugee groups, and they cascaded their conquest from world to world. The only reason we survived is because our world was moved somewhere else in the shuffle."

She stared at me for a long moment. "About a decade back, there were many stories like these. There is too much risk in trust. Kindness. We will always choose to offer that, but trust is something we can't afford. I'm sorry, Dorian, but we can't bring you with us or even tell you where we are."

It felt like getting a door slammed in my face, and while I wanted to argue, I understood that no amount of words or actions would sway Quinn. It made sense. It really did.

"Can I ask one question though? Why did the Wanderer kill the Prince?" I don't know why I asked. I just really wanted to know.

Quinn shook her head, my own somber mood clearly affecting her as well, "No one knows. Some think that it was planned from the start, others thought that it was the result of learning secrets about the Dream. Typically once a certain threshold of Presence is reached … people start acting strange."

Ha. If I had to bet, that threshold was about 2,000. What is it that the System could share with me that would make me go mad, I wondered? I was under no illusion that I would succeed where others failed. No one would risk a 100% failure rate surgery. So I understood Quinn's decision. Respected it even, but it still felt like shit.

I nodded and quickly turned on my heel and headed towards the edge of one of the clearings. I wanted to be alone for a little while. It was fair, in a cosmic sense at least. Saving all these people, all the ones who had given me gifts and left me with kind words and praises—it hadn't been my goal. I hadn't done it to save anyone. I did it to spite the Scientist and Instance Holder, so really it was only fair.

I didn't give them my all, so why would they do the same? It was only fair.

But damn if it didn't sting.

I kept walking. I couldn't stop. If I stopped, I wouldn't get back up. So even though I really wanted to lie down, I paced and focused on the problem at hand.

I had two options for Instances. The Mourning Circle, two boons and five restrictions; and Haunted Graves, two boons and three restrictions. Risking the original Instance wasn't on the table unless I could scrounge up two more Restrictions. I knew for a fact the fucker of an Instance Holder would force [Bad Start] on me if given the chance, and they wouldn't make the mistake of giving me [Fragile] this time.

I thought about the last time I was there. Cutting through an arm or leg might take days instead of hours without [Fragile]. Even though I knew how to deal with the circle, I wasn't going to risk that. I wouldn't do that again. I couldn't–

I found a canteen of cool water in front of me. I turned my head to see Jeren holding it out with a strained smile. "Hey kid. How you holding up?"

I didn't like being called a kid. I was almost twenty, and if I could be drafted and die—or sent to a hellish dream world, I wasn't a kid. I didn't say that. Instead, I gently took the canteen and began to drink. Mostly just so I didn't have to respond. I didn't trust my voice.

Finally, I composed myself enough to respond, "yeah. I'm fine. Just planning"

Jeren gave me a long look, and I turned away first – he clearly didn't buy it. A bubble of indignation rose within me. Why be here? Just leave me be and let me plan. I didn't need pity. I didn't need well wishes. I didn't need–

"Invite me to your party."

"What?"

He gave me a smile, "Quinn said you couldn't come with us. I won't argue with her, but I am my own man. So. Invite me to your party. I'll help you with this Instance."
