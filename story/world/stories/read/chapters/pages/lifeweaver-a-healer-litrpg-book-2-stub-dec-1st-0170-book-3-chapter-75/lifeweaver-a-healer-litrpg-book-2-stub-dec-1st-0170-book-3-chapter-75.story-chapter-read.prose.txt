Dot's eyes widened, and the words she was about to shout caught in her throat, along with her breath. Luke watched her turn toward the patient in the bed, her face going white. Relian was unconscious but alive, and Luke realized with a flood of relief washing over him, it wasn't him. Not really. Rather than having a more than impressive Meta-Heart in his chest, this one sported a monster core. A large and intricate one, to be sure, rather than the usual splintered orbs, but a monster core nonetheless. This wasn't The Fallen Shepherd. It was a copy, a replica.

Even a copy of this man was a terrible danger, though, and Luke gestured for the not-nurses to get the hell out of the room. Rather than attack, they complied, staring as they went without a sound. Those monsters gave Luke the creeps, and he wished they would make up their minds. Either attack him or don't. For now, their weirdness didn't matter. All that mattered was the man in the bed in front of him, or perhaps he should be called a monster, no matter what he was in his chest.

"Is it really him?" Dot breathed.

Luke shook his head. "No, I don't think so. You both see the monster core, don't you?"

Leom didn't answer, but Dot nodded. "Yes, I do."

"Then why were you healing him? Inspecting him tells you who he is."

Integrated Relian. Fallen Shepherd. Level ???

Her voice came out meek and afraid. "I didn't check. They just kept bringing me people to heal."

"The nurses?"

Dot nodded.

Luke sighed. "You know they are monsters too, right?"

"Yes," she got out. "I didn't want to come here."

"You didn't have a choice," Luke said.

Leom stepped up next to the bed, peering into Relian's face. "Destiny portals show you where you need to go and what you need to do. Not going inside would be an affront to the system itself." She looked up at Luke. "You know this man?"

"You could say that," Luke said.

A tear ran down Dot's cheek, and she sagged down onto a chair in the corner. "I hate it here. Why did this have to happen?"

"Let me just deal with Relian, and we'll get out of here," Luke said, annoyed at her weakness. She was Integrated, so why did she embrace weakness? Way back in the car, on their road trip to find the very man they now shared a room with, they'd sparred with Threads of Mana, cutting each other and healing. Then, she'd shown promise, had made progress. Joining Integrated Solutions Group had drained all that out of her, since they allowed her to be weak.

"Deal with?" Dot asked, giving him a suspicious look.

He glared at her. "It's a monster. Don't start now."

Relian stirred, and Luke pushed away all other thoughts and slammed several threads of mana into The Fallen Shepherd. First, he shaped Needle of Life into rings, which he wrapped around the copy's aorta, main mana channel, his windpipe, and the nerves inside the spine where it connected to the neck. If the bastard tried anything, he could squeeze and cut off all of them at the same time. Since Relian looked like himself and not Tim, Luke figured he didn't have Lifeweaver powers, which may or may not be a good thing. There was no corruption to target here, and he doubted he would go down without a fight either way.

"Are you going to kill him?" Dot asked, her voice cold.

"It's a monster," Leom said.

Cold irritation gripped Luke. Dot knew what Relian was capable of, what he'd done, and this was, like Leom said, just a monster copy. If they'd come to some sort of limbo-world where injured people end up, he very much doubted healing this bastard would let him walk the earth again. Not that Luke would allow that. Under no circumstances could that be the outcome of what happened in this room.

"Luke killed another Lifeweaver, a Singer. Why would he spare a monster?"

"Shut up and let me concentrate," Luke said.

The precautions weren't enough. Weavestepping to Dot's location had taken a lot out of him, even if he hadn't bled out this time, and healing the internal damage had taken a lot of mana. To remedy that loss, he reached straight into The Fallen Shepherd's monster core and drained it. Just like with a Meta-Heart, a monster's core could function like a battery, and Luke drained the juice from this one until it was too low for Relian to even wake back up.

Problem was, Luke couldn't help but feel curious. There were a lot of questions he wanted to ask the man lying on the bed in front of him. He'd put everything in motion with the Singers and even the void with the sole purpose of getting off Earth, as far as Luke could tell, but the convoluted way he'd gone about it still left large gaps of knowledge, and Luke wanted them filled. It was a huge risk, but he couldn't let it go.

Hoping he wasn't making another huge mistake, Luke allowed a trickle of mana back into Relian, just enough to get him above the ten percent line. That should be enough to wake him up.

"What are you doing?" Leom asked.

Luke grunted. "I've got questions."

After waiting a good ten seconds for the bastard to wake up, Luke had had enough and slapped him, and the sound of his palm against Relian's cheek filled the otherwise silent room. Relian stirred, squeezed his eyes shut tight, then opened them. A slight frown crossed his face, and he put a hand to his chest before turning his head to gaze up at Luke.

"Ah, Lifeweaver. We meet again."

"Relian," Luke said stiffly, not liking how in control The Fallen Shepherd was despite the lack of mana in his system. His breathing was under control, and his heart rate wasn't even elevated. Perhaps this had been a huge mistake.

"You're a copy," Luke said.

Relian nodded. "So it seems. Interesting. We are in a dungeon, I take it?"

"Destiny dungeon," Leom said, her voice a whimper. It was almost as if she instinctively reacted to Relian's presence, without even knowing what kind of terror he was capable of.

Relian turned his head toward Leom and smiled. "Ah, a Singer. You know, I've never heard one of your kind sing. Would you do me the honor?"

"We don't have time for singing," Luke said. "You're a copy, Relian. From when? What do you remember?"

"Judging by your stranglehold on the bleak monster representation of a Meta-Heart in my inner self, I take it you have an interrogation in mind? You should know by now that I tend not to rush things."

"Just answer the question."

His smile deepened. "I see that you have grown. That is well, because things are spiraling out of control and the powers that be are clinging on to old ways of thinking, and history shows us no way to escape the void."

"You didn't answer the question."

"Perhaps my aim is to extend my life?" Relian said, raising an eyebrow before looking down at himself. "As pitiful a life as it is. All beings struggle against death."

Luke gave him a look.

"Very well. My last memory of you is from the moment you tore me apart just before my communication with Raezeliem The Empty could lead to something productive and fruitful. Perhaps even an end, of sorts. Pity."

Luke's prepared line of questioning faltered at getting a name for the void being he'd been seeing beyond The Greater System Weave. "Raezeliem? It has a name?"

"It's pronounced Rae-zel-i-em, not Raez-el-iem, but yes, it has a name. Most living entities have names, either given to them by another or one they bestow on themselves. In this case, I believe it is the latter."

It took a lot of restraint not to punch Relian. "What did the deal entail?"

"Luke?" Dot said.

"Not now, Dot," Luke said. "Answer the question, Relian."

"A way forward for us all. With my guiding hand at the helm, of course."

"And your deal with the Singers?"

Relian gave Leom another look and an even deeper smile. "Let's call that equal parts insurance and an offering. Void beings are not prone to dialogue and bristle unless you offer them a system or two. If communication broke down, the Singers represented a nice backup plan in case of emergency. A way out, if you will."

The Fallen Shepherd's eyes flickered to something, then back to Luke.

"Luke!" Dot shouted.

"What?" he barked, taking his eyes and attention away from Relian, despite the risk inherent in such a decision.

Not-nurses were crowding into the room, pushing through the open door. That wasn't all, a bunch of doctors had joined them, all of them wearing the appearance and the self-satisfied grin of The Fallen Shepherd.

"There he is, Dr. Saints!" the nurse from way back when Luke entered the dungeon shouted.

Just as the nurse finished speaking, the entire group of monsters stiffened, their eyes getting a glazed look, and they swept into the room with a renewed fervor, now in a far more controlled fashion.

Relian, still on the bed, let out a low chuckle. "Oh, look. Reinforcements have finally arrived."
