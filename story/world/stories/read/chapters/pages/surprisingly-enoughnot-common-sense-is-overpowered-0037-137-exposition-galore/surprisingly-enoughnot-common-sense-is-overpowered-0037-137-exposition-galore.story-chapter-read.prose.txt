It had already been two years since Ning entered the outer sect. Between tending to his fields, hunting boars, cultivating herbs, and practicing his techniques, the time seemed to have slipped from his grasp in an instant.

And today, he found himself in the alchemy courtyard.

A large, dedicated space had been built specifically for outer-sect alchemists. Of course, only those officially registered with the Alchemy Pavilion were allowed inside.

It sounded prestigious and refined.

Reality?

BOOOOM!

A nearby furnace exploded, sending black smoke billowing into the sky.

"Damn it, AGAIN?!"

"Whose cauldron is leaking?! That thing costs more than my life!"

"Move! Move! Fire! No, not the herbs!"

So much for elegance.

Inside one of the allocated private rooms, Su Fan carefully adjusted his cauldron. It was a specially crafted first-tier high-rank cauldron, worth several thousand spirit stones at the very least.

Beside him, Ning picked up a sprig of ebony with his right hand and deftly removed the withered leaves. His movements were swift and precise.

Alchemists usually worked alone or with the help of disciples or servants. That was the norm.

But because Su Fan despised anything considered the "norm," he didn't mind accepting some extra help.

Especially since ingredient preparation happened to be the part he disliked most.

That was where Ning stepped in.

Ning wanted to become an alchemist.

This wasn't an impulsive desire. With the spiritual plants he cultivated himself, he could attempt simple alchemy at a much lower cost than most beginners.

There was another reason as well.

After reading about how the Eastern Region was regularly plagued by beast tides, not to mention having a protagonist like Xiao Fan right beside him, Ning had already accepted that some kind of calamity was probably only a matter of time.

After all, adversity was where protagonists shone brightest.

In most xianxia and fantasy stories, the protagonist's novice village eventually got destroyed so they could venture into the wider world. Pure Qi Sect might not exactly qualify as a "novice village," considering a Golden Core cultivator was leading it, but Ning wouldn't be surprised if something disastrous eventually befell the sect.

All for the grand purpose of character development, of course.

Because of that possibility, Ning wanted a second profession. More specifically, something portable.

Spiritual farming was tied to the land. One couldn't exactly uproot five acres of fields and run away with them. If something happened to the sect and Ning was forced to flee, everything he had built here would be left behind.

But alchemy?

It was a respected profession almost anywhere, and more importantly, it didn't require fixed land.

That was ideal.

"Still... I didn't expect it to take more than a year just to gather the funds." Ning sighed.

Among all his progress over the past year, one of the most significant achievements had been his recent purchase of an alchemy inheritance.

It only covered first-tier low-grade pills, but it still cost a pretty penny: 1,000 spirit stones upfront.

Outside the sect, such inheritances were rare and normally appeared only in high-grade auctions. Sellers often added restrictive clauses preventing buyers from passing the inheritance to a third party, while transferable heirloom versions were even more expensive.

Fortunately, Ning only needed to visit the sect's exchange center to purchase one.

He had explored cheaper options as well.

For only 250 spirit stones, he could obtain an inheritance under a different agreement. The problem was that, in exchange, the sect would take fifty percent of his alchemy profits for the next ten years.

That was far too steep.

So Ning bought the non-shareable inheritance outright and resolved to squeeze every last spirit stone of value out of it.

It helped that he'd been saving for quite a while.

For that, Ning would particularly like to thank the gooners of the sect, who had somehow earned him more than six hundred spirit stones over the past year.

A very surprising amount.

Then again, considering the number of simps in this world, perhaps it actually made some sense.

Combined with the spirit stones Ning had been hoarding like a squirrel, it had finally been enough.

And that was why he was here now.

After preparing and portioning the ingredients, Su Fan began the alchemy session.

Ning, still very much a novice, watched intently. Observing Su Fan work was his true purpose for coming here.

In theory, pill concoction could be divided into three major steps: gathering and condensing the medicinal liquid, solidifying the pill while locking in its spiritual essence, and finally opening the furnace to collect the finished product.

Simple enough when written down.

In practice, one tiny mistake could turn several hundred spirit stones' worth of ingredients into ash.

Improvement came through repetition.

Su Fan approached the furnace and placed several spirit stones into its slots, activating the engraved fire arrays. Flames immediately rose within.

He executed the Fire Control Technique, gradually stabilizing the temperature before adding the processed herbs one by one. Then, using another fire technique, he caused the flames inside the furnace to rotate, pulling the herbs into a uniform spiral.

The method distributed the heat evenly, reducing the chance of the herbs burning and losing their potency. At the same time, the rotation helped remove impurities from the medicinal essence.

Before long, several clumps of medicinal liquid floated within the furnace.

"Gather Liquid!"

Su Fan's hands formed several rapid seals, guiding the separate clumps toward one another until they slowly merged into a single mass.

"Spinning Condensation!"

Another series of seals followed.

The medicinal liquid spun faster and faster until it finally condensed into a shimmering sphere.

At that moment, Su Fan immediately cast several Solidifying the Elixir and Locking the Spirit spells.

Three incense sticks later—

"Open the furnace!"

Su Fan surged his spiritual power and cast the Collecting the Elixir technique.

With a roar, the furnace opened and released a pale yellow pill, which landed neatly in his hand.

But before he could celebrate, the pill rapidly darkened, turned black, and crumbled into ash.

Ning immediately understood the problem.

"The locking-spirit and collecting-elixir steps were incorrect. You collected it too early, before the pill's spiritual power had been completely sealed."

Though Ning wasn't deeply trained in alchemy yet, his perception was sharp. This was precisely why Su Fan didn't mind having him observe.

Whenever Su Fan became too absorbed in the process and developed tunnel vision, Ning could sometimes notice what he missed.

"Tsk!"

Su Fan clicked his tongue in frustration.

Meanwhile, Ning calmly gathered the pill residue.

It could still be mixed into the soil as fertilizer.

This was one of those cases where alchemy supplemented spiritual farming.

Balanced, as all things should be.

...

Elder Shen took a slow sip of spiritual wine, exhaling contentedly before finally beginning his lecture.

"The path of qi cultivation begins with essence and qi," he said, lazily waving the wine gourd in his hand.

Ning straightened slightly. It wasn't every day one got to hear a Foundation Building cultivator preach, especially one who had to be pestered for several days before finally getting into the mood.

Normally, insights gained through cultivation weren't passed down so casually. Fortunately, their recent collaboration had brought the two of them much closer.

Truly, spirit stones were the foundation of many beautiful friendships.

"Essence, qi, and spirit. You already know the basics, so there's no point making you listen to them again." Elder Shen tapped his chest. "What matters is understanding how they work together. A mortal already possesses essence, qi, and spirit. Cultivation strengthens them and brings them into harmony until the body can begin interacting with the spiritual energy of heaven and earth."

He took another sip before continuing.

"The body nourishes essence; essence and blood support qi, while the spirit governs and guides the whole process. But don't treat them as three completely separate things. A change in one affects the others. Injure the body and your circulation suffers. Exhaust your qi and the body weakens. Let the mind fall into disorder and even perfectly good qi can run wild."

Ning nodded. That much was straightforward.

"This is also why cultivation texts keep talking about yin and yang. Yin and yang aren't two objects you can dig out of your body and point at. They're used to describe opposing aspects of the same cycle. Stillness and motion, gathering and release, nourishment and exertion. Yin transforms into yang, yang returns to yin, and the cycle continues."

Elder Shen shook his gourd.

"Different techniques handle that cycle differently. Some emphasize the body, others the spirit, others qi. Even two techniques leading to the same realm may take very different routes. Don't become so attached to one explanation that you start thinking everything else must be wrong."

That was worth remembering.

Ning had a habit of breaking things down until they made sense to him, but cultivation had existed for far longer than he had. A model could be useful without being the only possible model.

Simply put, keeping an open mind was the key.

"The spiritual root allows you to sense and absorb the spiritual energy of heaven and earth," Elder Shen continued. "Your cultivation technique guides that energy through the body, refining it into qi you can actually use. Naturally, there's a limit to how much your body can handle at once. Take in more than you can refine and you'll only hurt yourself."

He moved on to the three phases of Qi Condensation.

"You've already experienced qi like silk and reached qi like a river, so I won't waste time explaining what you already know. What matters now is the tide."

In the earlier stages, qi was thin and moved through the meridians in strands. As cultivation progressed, those strands grew denser and joined together, eventually forming the continuous flow Ning was familiar with now.

"The river continues to grow as you cultivate. Your qi becomes denser, your meridians adapt to carrying more of it, and the flow gradually gains strength. Eventually, the momentum of one circulation begins carrying into the next, and that's when the river starts becoming a tide."

Ning considered his current circulation. "Can that process be forced?"

"If you want to spend the next few days coughing blood."

Ning immediately abandoned the idea.

Elder Shen chuckled. "There's no trick to it. Keep refining your qi and strengthening your meridians. When the time comes, you'll feel the change yourself."

That was enough for Ning. He mainly wanted to know what to expect as he approached the next phase.

"The tide also marks the final stretch toward Foundation Building," Elder Shen continued. "As your qi grows stronger, its circulation tempers the meridians and nourishes the body. By the peak of Qi Condensation, your body, qi, and spirit should all be prepared for the breakthrough."

His tone grew slightly more serious.

"Foundation Building is the culmination of Qi Refining. You draw upon everything you've accumulated and use it to establish your Dao foundation. Rush the earlier stages, and whatever flaws you leave behind will only become harder to deal with later."

Ning kept that warning in mind.

"Depending on the Dao foundation, one may also introduce Mysterious True Essence or Heaven and Earth's Various Evils. The former refers to wondrous qi of heaven and earth, such as Great Sun Purple Qi or Moonlight Essence. The latter is divided into Heavenly, Earthly, and Human Evils, such as Earthly Lung Fire Evil and Military Formation Blood Evil."

Those names Ning had encountered before, though they were still far beyond what he needed to worry about.

"The exact choice depends on your cultivation technique, spiritual root, constitution, and the foundation you intend to establish," Elder Shen said. "You're still a long way from that point. When the time comes, find a senior who knows what they're doing and seek proper guidance."

Ning certainly wasn't going to experiment with something as important as his Dao foundation. For now, knowing what lay ahead was enough.

Elder Shen waved his hand dismissively. "Once the foundation is complete, the next step is opening the Purple Mansion. But that's enough. You're getting too far ahead of yourself."

Ning waited for a moment, but Elder Shen showed no intention of continuing.

As expected of an elder-type character, they always stopped right before the important parts.

Still, Ning had gotten what he wanted. He now had a much clearer idea of the path ahead, especially what to expect as his qi progressed from river to tide.

He bowed respectfully. "Thank you for the guidance, Elder."

"Go, go." Elder Shen waved him off. "Cultivate while the insight is still fresh."

...

Returning home, Ning activated the small Spirit Gathering Formation. Spiritual energy gradually gathered within the room, growing denser until he could feel it against his skin.

Once the formation had stabilized, Ning sat cross-legged at its center, swallowed a Purple Cloud Pill, and closed his eyes.

He had to admit, Elder Shen's explanation had been useful. Ning had already known about qi like silk, qi like a river, and qi like a tide, but understanding what actually changed between them gave those names considerably more meaning.

No wonder novels always had such long exposition scenes.

Ning was currently in the Qi Like a River phase. His qi already circulated continuously through his meridians, and for now, there was no need to chase the tide. He simply needed to continue accumulating and refining his qi while allowing his meridians and body to adapt alongside it.

The barrier between the Fourth and Fifth Stages wasn't a major transformation either. As long as his accumulation was sufficient, breaking through should only be a matter of time.

Ning settled his thoughts and began circulating the Pure Qi Sutra.

Spiritual energy flowed into his body under the influence of the Spirit Gathering Formation, while the Purple Cloud Pill dissolved within his abdomen and released its medicinal power. Ning guided the incoming energy through the familiar circulation route, refining it cycle after cycle before allowing it to join the qi already flowing through his meridians.

His circulation gradually strengthened.

Each completed cycle drew in and refined more spiritual energy. The flow remained steady, but the amount of qi passing through his meridians continued to increase, placing greater pressure on the bottleneck ahead.

Ning didn't hurry. He maintained the circulation and allowed the pressure to build on its own.

One cycle passed, then another.

The medicinal power of the Purple Cloud Pill continued to feed into the process, and soon the pressure reached its limit. His dantian trembled as the accumulated qi pressed against the barrier separating the Fourth and Fifth Stages.

Ning guided another full circulation through his meridians.

The barrier finally gave way.

Boom!

Qi rushed freely through the newly opened pathways, and the circulation that had been straining against the bottleneck immediately smoothed out. The pressure faded as his dantian began accommodating the greater flow, while spiritual energy continued pouring in from the surrounding formation.

Ning continued cultivating for several more cycles, stabilizing his newly advanced cultivation before slowly opening his eyes.

Fifth Stage Qi Condensation.

He exhaled a long breath of turbid air, feeling considerably lighter afterward.

Satisfied, Ning opened his panel to check his status, only to freeze in surprise.
