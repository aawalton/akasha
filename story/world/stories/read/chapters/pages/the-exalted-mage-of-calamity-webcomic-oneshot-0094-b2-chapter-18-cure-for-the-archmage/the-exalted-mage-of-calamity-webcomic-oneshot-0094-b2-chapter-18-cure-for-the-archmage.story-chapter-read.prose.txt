“Nasha! You’re bleeding all over!” Gwen was crouched, working on healing her injuries.

“I’m… alright,” Nasha groaned. She tried to regulate her breathing, but it was far more difficult than she thought.

“You are not alright!”

Shortly after defeating Hevestra in battle, both Nasha and Veronica collapsed from exhaustion. Nasha was the first down, while Veronica was able to at least walk over to them first. Now, Gwen had moved them both side by side somewhere clear and flat that hadn’t been destroyed by the fight, so she could begin mending their injuries.

Nasha had the worst of it physically. From burn marks due to the demonic energy she touched when saving Dawn, to the countless stamina-supplementary spells Gwen had on her, forcing her body to keep going. With Selene’s Tear further fueling her adrenaline and will to fight, it was no surprise Nasha’s body was in a terrible, overexerted state.

Veronica’s injuries were mainly internal. She had been knocked around the battlefield, but a mana barrier had always protected her from the worst injuries.

While Nasha’s injuries were more dire at the pressing moment, Veronica’s would be permanent. At least until she fixed them. Damaged mana cores and ruptured mana rings meant she’d need to reform them. For the time being, she would be nearly as weak as Gwen was.

“I’m… going to sleep…” Nasha whispered, words drifting quieter and quieter.

“It’s going to take so long to heal all of these injuries. She feels like dried jerky. Her arms barely move and her muscles are so rigid,” Gwen said, eyes narrowed, watching Nasha fall asleep.

“The fights over, so we have all the time in the world,” Veronica said beside Nasha. Her eyes were closed, and she tried her best to rest as well. “We got what we came for. We can stay as long as needed until we are healed,” Veronica said.

“Right. Selene’s Tear,” Gwen said.

Her expression was complicated. While she was happy they had obtained what they came for—she wasn’t happy with the cost.

None of them were.

That was especially after promising Dusk that they’d search for Selene. It was a promise made on the spur of the moment. There was no way to search for her if they had all died, after all.

However, letting Dusk simply sacrifice his life like that just to save them… it lingered in their memories. While they could technically bring them back—it was a tough call whether Selene was still alive after a millennia. And that was paired with finding the lost, ancient Asvarians—two tasks that were nigh impossible.

For the time being, Gwen put it away in one of her pockets for safekeeping. While the power it granted was great, it was limited. Unless there was something that needed it, they’d have to conserve it as much as possible.

According to Sage, it was likely that the Tear of Selene mainly boosted aura-capabilities, along with brief control over portals and space.

In the future timeline Veronica had come from, it was also an aura-user that had obtained the tear and used it. Whenever Veronica laid her hands on it, Sage could feel the spiritual power within, but it didn’t react cleanly with her mana. It seemed that Nasha would be the primary user of it in their party if the need arose.

“What about that creepy-looking stone?” Gwen asked, looking over at Veronica.

“Sage is still trying to identify it,” Veronica said, resting uncomfortably on the stone floor.

In her clutched hand was a small gemstone. It was the reason why Sage wanted her to avoid striking Hevestra’s head. It had been embedded within the demon’s skull, shrouded by shadows. As the head began to disintegrate, the stone naturally fell onto the steps below.

Now, it was just up to Sage to finish analyzing it. However, Sage definitely had some theories as to what it was.

[This gemstone appears to have absorption capabilities. It is likely that this was what granted Hevestra the ability to absorb different energies.]

[It will take some time for me to fully analyze this gemstone. Once done, I will grant you the appropriate quest reward.]

For now, Veronica didn’t complain.

At the end of the day, their mission was complete. And she had also learned several important things. That Asvarians were actually real, and that they had captured Selene, presumably for experiments and research.

Although it all happened nearly a thousand years ago, Dusk’s memory was proof they existed. And if she were to trust the memory, they likely had a base or home somewhere north. Maybe she’d be able to send an expedition team up there to search for it sometime.

“Alright, Gwen. I’m trusting you to heal us to full health. I’m… going to take a long sleep now.”

“Eh? Just by myself?” she responded. “Your conditions are terrible. It’s probably going to take several days before Nasha can even walk.”

Veronica smiled with her eyes closed. “I trust you.”

Gwen furrowed her brow. That word of encouragement did not make her feel better.

“Um, wait, as you going to sleep as in just a nap or a coma?” Gwen asked. “Because I need to know if I need to feed you guys or something.”

Veronica didn’t answer.

“…Hello?” Gwen kept her palms above Nasha’s chest, still applying more of her magic, leaving pockets of restorative energy along her body. “Are you… already asleep?”

Silence.

Now, it was just her and two unconscious women in front of her.

In the distance, Gwen heard the howl of a carrion beast that echoed through the skies.

She frowned. “I should’ve just taken up gardening…”

The private infirmary of the upper spire was quiet. Several people moved about the chamber, but all of them took care to preserve the stillness.

The room itself was large and pristine, enclosed by walls that curved around the space in a perfect ring. Enchanted healing arrays had been set evenly across the floor, their thin bands of green light rotating in slow, measured paths. A single minute inside the chamber would leave most people feeling refreshed.

Above the central bed, several diagnostic crystals hovered in the air, turning faintly as they monitored the patient below.

A woman lay on the bed, her skin pale and sickly. Dark veins crawled up the sides of her arms and legs, while black patches had settled around her throat. She looked to be in her mid-forties, though the sickness made it difficult to tell. Her cheeks had hollowed, and each breath came shallow and strained. Every few seconds, the black lines along her skin pulsed, tightening around her body like a contracting heartbeat.

Although this was an infirmary, she had been its only patient for the last several years.

A man stood beside her bed.

He was in his late fifties, with dark gray hair threaded lighter at the temples. His face was sharp, but burdened with a grief that had worn itself deep. Despite not being a patient himself, he looked almost ghostly, pale and hollow from years of pain. His robes were simple, deep blue trimmed in silver, without the excessive ornamentation many others of his rank seemed to favor.

Archmage Ballamus.

To him, this room was everything.

And yet, there was no place he hated more.

His sister had been afflicted with the disease four years ago, forced to endure its pain while bedridden. Not once had she seen the sunlight outside since her admission. Her body had simply become too weak to move.

Ballamus had done everything he could. He had gathered the greatest healers and the most skilled alchemists. As an archmage, there were few resources in the kingdom he could not obtain. Yet for all the power he possessed, even he had failed to find a cure for nether-grasping disease.

At least, until a mysterious letter arrived for him.

It came from some no-name mage. Someone who had caused a riot within the Arcanum Spire nearly two months ago.

The door to the healing chamber clicked open behind him. Footsteps followed them.

“Archmage Ballamus.”

A man in dark green robes walked inside. He had a trimmed mustache, a clean-shaven jaw, and rough dirty-brown hair that matched the blunt edge of his voice. His eyes were sharp, his face narrow, and he carried himself with almost as much authority as an archmage. He had long since grown used to acting with that kind of prestige.

Master Alchemist Kasmeran.

He was a 6th-tier mage. Average in most regards when compared to the wider association, and low compared to those who usually stood in Ballamus’ company.

That alone meant the man was not simple.

Kasmeran was one of the current generation’s finest medicinal alchemists. He was in his mid-forties, yet he had already collected numerous achievements, earning him the title of Master Alchemist. He had created more than three dozen recognized tonics, several of which were still used in military infirmaries across the kingdom. His greatest concoction by far was a stabilizing treatment for the Writhing Toxin, a rare disease with an extremely high fatality rate.

And for the last four years, he had been the head doctor in charge of Ballamus’ sister’s condition. It was thanks to him that she was still alive.

“Kasmeran, you’re here,” Ballamus said, turning to regard him. “Are the wyvern claws processed?”

Kasmeran held up his fingers. Between them was a vial of crushed wyvern claws.

“Just finished.”

Several other blooming mages were inside the room with them, all assistants with far less standing than either man. At one station, a small group had been placed in charge of preparing the mixture.

The supposed cure would be similar to a soup, all of it prepared in a simple clay bowl.

Kasmeran walked over and passed the vial to a male assistant. The man accepted it and immediately began working.

“Have you gotten a chance to complete your final review of the ingredients?” Ballamus asked.

“I have,” Kasmeran replied.

His tone was level, but there was a rough edge beneath it.

He glanced over at the reagents laid out in small porcelain dishes atop the workstation. Some were dried roots. Others were crystallized extracts, powders, or dark mineral flakes. Every one of them had been purified, processed, and measured with exacting precision.

It had taken nearly two months to gather everything.

Two months of procurement requests, favors called in from neighboring cities, and enough coin spent to put even an archmage’s fortune under pressure. Several ingredients had required private trade agreements. One had even required direct approval from another archmage.

“Everything on the list is viable,” Kasmeran said. “Expensive beyond reason, but viable. The royal expedition provided more than enough wyvern material. Once reduced and concentrated, the claws should act as a binding catalyst.”

Ballamus stared at his sister’s sleeping face. Although she seemed serene, he knew better than to think she was resting peacefully. Sleep was the only way she could escape the pain.

“Kasmeran,” Ballamus said slowly.

Kasmeran turned to face him.

“What are your thoughts on the recipe?”

“My thoughts, huh?” Kasmeran folded his hands behind his back as he walked around the preparation table. “The recipe is coherent. Surprisingly so. That much, I’ll admit. I can understand each ingredient’s purpose. The wyvern claws as the binding agent, and the ash-root to prevent the mixture from dispersing too quickly once ingested.”

He walked a full lap around the table before coming to Ballamus’ side, stopping in front of his sister’s bed. “Whoever wrote it certainly has an understanding of medicinal alchemy.”

He paused for a moment, narrowing his eyes. “But understanding is not mastery. They are realms apart. One is required for the other, but it is not the only quality needed.”

“You are skeptical,” Ballamus stated. His gaze didn’t leave his sister’s face.

“Of course I am,” Kasmeran replied. “The recipe itself is too fishy. It’s practically the entire ocean. A recipe from some unregistered mage in Ronswick? A woman with no known medical credentials? No background? No backers? Not even a teacher or a master? From the reports, the woman is a ruin mage, not even blooming.”

Ballamus stayed silent.

“I’ve heard of her exploits here. Her spell compression notes that caused a ruckus in the Arcanum Spire. Everyone has been in an uproar about it lately.” Kasmeran’s voice sharpened, taking on a deeper edge. “But spell theory and medicinal alchemy are not the same discipline. Archmage Findel can construct an entire castle if you give him a large enough stone block. Yet I would not trust him to brew finer tea than my own grandmother.”

Kasmeran turned, watching the mage assistants begin the final mixing process for the supposed cure.

“I’ve created dozens of tonics in my life. Plenty of cures for nasty poisons that would give children nightmares at the mere mention of their names. And yet some unauthenticated woman who isn’t even in her twenties dares claim she has the cure to nether-grasping disease?”

“Is that your pride talking?” Ballamus asked.

Kasmeran crossed his arms. “Hmph. It’s pride, but it’s also experience.”

“You do not trust the recipe?”

For a moment, Kasmeran stayed silent. It was as if he already knew the answer, yet was unsure how honest he should be. “That remains to be seen.”

After a few more minutes, the mixture was finally complete.

“No toxicity. The ingredients have bonded without instability,” one of the mage assistants said. “It is reacting safely within the barriers. None of my blooming spells detect poison.”

“None of mine either.”

“Likewise.”

Once the final checks for the cure were complete, Kasmeran picked up the bowl.

He looked over at Ballamus. “Are you sure you want to attempt this? A cure from some unknown mage? Even if there is no toxicity, there is no telling how it will react to the nether-grasping disease. It could cause the disease to swell, or even kill her outright.”

Ballamus stood beside the bed, his expression serious and unchanging.

“The mage’s letter wrote that this recipe will cure the disease. Not that it may, or that it might. For that reason alone, I am willing to take the risk,” he replied. “I am no longer willing to watch my own kin suffer.”

Kasmeran frowned. “And if it kills her?”

A sudden pressure pressed down around the room.

Every mage inside felt it. Mana leaked from Ballamus in a dense, heavy field, settling over the chamber until even breathing felt more difficult.

A mage’s domain.

Even Kasmeran found it difficult to move.

A moment later, Ballamus retracted his domain. “If this is deception, or a trick,” he said calmly, “I will have the Ronswick tower stripped of its charter. The tower head will explain to me exactly how this mage passed through them. And the mage in question will be brought to me in chains.”

His eyes sharpened as he folded his hands behind his back, fingers clenching tightly.

“And if malice was the purpose of this recipe, then I will personally raze everything down until I execute that mage myself.”

He turned to Kasmeran. “Enough talk. We will administer the cure.”

Kasmeran stiffened. “Yes, Archmage Ballamus.”

He lifted the bowl carefully and carried it over to the bed, handing it over to Ballamus. An assistant moved to the other side to support the woman’s head, while another group of blooming mages adjusted the preservation arrays around the bed itself.

Her eyes slowly began to flicker open.

Ballamus stepped closer. “Frescia.”

Her lips moved, but no sound came out.

“It will most likely taste very bitter,” Kasmeran warned, watching from the side.

Ballamus glanced at him for a short moment and nodded, before looking back down at his sister.

Frescia’s lips seemed to frown, which made Ballamus smile faintly. “Don't worry. I know. You always hated medicine.”

Slowly, he brought the bowl to her lips.

She grimaced immediately, but Ballamus supported her with one hand while carefully helping her swallow. It took several attempts. Each gulp looked painful, and twice, her throat seized before the mixture could go down. With the help of the assistants and Kasmeran managing the array, she finally finished the bowl.

Several seconds passed, then turned into minutes.

One of the mage assistants noticed something irregular with the array.

“Her pulse is spiking!” an assistant yelled.

“The disease is spreading!”

The veins along Frescia’s arms darkened at once. Black lines shot up her neck and across her jaw, branching beneath her skin like roots seeking deeper soil. Her back arched from the bed, fingers tightening around Ballamus’s hand with what little strength she had left.

Ballamus stood.

Mana flooded the room.

The healing arrays along the floor flared wildly. The lights overhead flickered. Every assistant froze as the pressure of a Ninth-Tier mage filled the infirmary, cold and vast enough to make the air feel thin.

Kasmeran’s face paled. “Ballamus. Wait,” he said, forcing the words out through the pressure. “According to the formula, this is exactly what should happen.”

Ballamus knew. He had read the instructions, too. But seeing the curse spread so rapidly was different. Never before had he felt so many emotions at once, each one dragging against the others.

Frescia choked, the sound caught somewhere between breath and pain. Black vapor seeped from the dark veins along her wrists.

It was thin at first. Then it thickened.

It rose from her skin like smoke being drawn from a dying fire. More threads followed, peeling away from her throat, arms, and collarbone. Each strand left behind paler skin. The black patches around her throat began to fade, shrinking inward as though something hidden beneath her flesh had finally let go.

Kasmeran stared.

For the first time, he looked truly speechless.

“Seal it,” he said, his voice sharp and leaving no room for argument.

The assistants obeyed.

A glass vial floated from the preparation table and hovered above the bed. The containment circles compressed, drawing the black vapor into the vial. It writhed as it entered, twisting like something alive.

The final thread left Frescia’s throat.

After a few moments, Ballamus called out to her.

“Frescia.”

Her eyelids fluttered. Slowly, she opened her eyes. They were tired and dry, but clear.

She took a breath. A long, deep inhale.

Ballamus’ shoulders lowered for the first time in years.

Frescia stared back at him. Then a faint smile formed on her face.

“That tasted disgusting,” she said.

For a moment, no one moved.

Then one of the assistants made a small sound that might have been a laugh before quickly covering their mouth.

Ballamus did not reprimand them.

He bowed his head over his sister’s hand, and the room went quiet.

“Yeah,” he said, voice rough. “I’m sure it did.”
