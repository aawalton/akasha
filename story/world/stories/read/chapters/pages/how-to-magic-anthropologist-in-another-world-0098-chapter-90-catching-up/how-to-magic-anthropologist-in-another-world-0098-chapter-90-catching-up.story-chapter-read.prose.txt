Apparently, each glyph was composed of three symbols representing sounds. The first stood upright, the second was rotated 120°, and the third 240°. The sound symbols themselves could be analyzed further based on visual similarities.

The first example she showed me resembled a lowercase t. A single cross stroke represented the [a] vowel. Two strokes represented [i], while replacing the line with a small circle represented the [o] sound.

She demonstrated similar patterns using V-shaped and A-shaped symbols.

"How did you determine that one stroke represents a instead of o?" I asked.

"It's an educated guess." She shrugged. "Common seems to have borrowed a lot of its three-syllable words from this language. Once I managed to translate a few words into Holy using the Saint's journal, I'd compare them to their Common equivalents. If the Common word had three syllables, six times out of ten the sounds matched my interpretation."

"Interesting..."

"By combining the individual syllabic sounds into increasingly complex trios, you get a much richer phonetic structure."

I found myself standing in front of a large warehouse somewhere within the Guild District near the Merchant Guild.

Inside, I watched workers pushing handcarts between towering stacks of crates while others carried heavy sacks across the warehouse floor. Curiosity got the better of me, so I wandered between the rows of cargo.

The nearest stack contained steel ingots arranged into neat pyramids. The next held brass, copper, tin, and lead. Farther along, I found sacks filled with coal, river sand, limestone, and powdered clay. Another aisle contained barrels of lamp oil, bundles of hardwood, and several crates of mana lights.

Eventually, the warehouse transitioned into food storage. Flour, salt, preserved meats, cooking oil, Fire Roots, spices, and sacks of dried coffee cherries that had yet to be roasted.

It was only after walking deeper inside that the atmosphere changed.

The ordinary shelves disappeared, replaced by thick stone walls interrupted by reinforced iron doors. Every door shimmered faintly with enchantments. Whatever lay behind them was valuable enough that even Je'e didn't trust mundane locks to protect it.

Probably something related to her jewelry business.

Piece by piece, the entire harvester slid free until its tremendous weight finally rested on the warehouse floor.

Ja'a immediately stepped backward while three more workers hurried in to help guide it down.

When the machine finally settled with a heavy metallic thud, the entire room fell silent.

Nobody had ever seen anything remotely like it.

The complicated gears, the cutting blades, the steel frame, every part of it looked completely alien compared to the simple tools people normally used.
