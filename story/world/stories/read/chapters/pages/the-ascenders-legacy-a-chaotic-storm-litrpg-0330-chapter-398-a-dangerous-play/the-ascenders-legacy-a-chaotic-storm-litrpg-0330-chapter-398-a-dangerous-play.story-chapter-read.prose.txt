Malverin glanced over the far eastern district and scowled in distaste.

It was the district of industry, housing the factories and workers responsible for Alcatraz's impressive technological advancement.

In simple terms, it was a busy district. And even before activating Severian's blessing, Malverin knew she was in for a great deal of work.

Even still, she underestimated just how much.

The moment soul sight activated, the world beneath her exploded in ghostly blue light. Soul signatures filled her vision, so dense they blanketed the entire district like fog.

But this wasn't merely the souls of the humans below. Those she filtered out almost immediately, their signatures dim and weak compared to what truly clogged her vision.

No, this sea of light came from thousands upon thousands of soul-bound items and soul-infused enchantments, each one shining like a distant star.

Once again, the hypocrisy of it all was jarring. A city that prided itself on mundanity, drowning in magic. The irony was almost laughable, and as she stared at the countless signatures scattered across the district, she finally understood why Severian had struggled earlier.

How was anyone supposed to find anything in this mess, let alone a legacy seed?

Muttering a curse under her breath, Malverin swept her gaze across the district in search of the brightest signature. She found it within a massive factory only a few blocks away, and without hesitation, she flew toward it, weaving an illusion of translucence over herself as she crossed into the sprawling compound.

It was a foundry—a vast power-generation complex responsible for a significant portion of the electricity that powered Alcatraz. And even now, in the early hours of dawn, the factory buzzed with activity.

Or perhaps it never truly slept.

Workers moved between colossal runic machines while automated cranes transported enormous metal containers overhead. Conveyor belts carried materials from one section of the complex to another, and everywhere she looked, gears turned, pistons pumped, and machinery roared.

The noise alone was enough to irritate her. Humans shouted instructions over the deafening din. Steam burst from thick pipes. Sparks rained from elevated platforms where technicians worked on humming machinery.

At the center of it all, hidden within a large rectangular building, was the signature that had drawn her here. Grimacing, she flew toward the structure and descended onto the roof, soul sight fixed on the presence below.

A group of soldiers filled the roof, eyes narrowed as they gazed at the storm gathering in the distance, whispering to themselves in fear. But Malverin barely spared them a glance as she moved toward the hatch installed near one corner of the roof.

An illusion of stillness settled over it the moment she reached it, and with a firm yank, she tore it free, ignoring the high-pitched screech that tore through the air as metal ripped from concrete.

None of the soldiers heard it. The illusion of stillness had trapped the sound.

Swiftly, she descended into the building, her attention drawn instantly to the soul signature below. The source was impossible to miss.

Suspended at the center of the otherwise empty chamber was a massive sphere of layered metal, held aloft by a framework of steel supports and thick cables anchored to walls covered in reinforcement runes. It was a machine easily the size of a small mansion. Its polished surface was covered in elder runes, and beneath it, etched into the concrete itself, lay a massive runic formation several times the size of the sphere.

Energy streamed into it from the atmosphere with such force that she had to brace herself against the pull.

Yet that wasn't what drew her attention. It was where all that power was going. Her gaze shifted to the center of the formation, following the torrent of energy to find it all funneled into the blessed item above, which in turn converted it into electricity.

Malverin slowed, eyes wide in disbelief and amazement.

The complexity of the formation surpassed anything possessed by even the Illusion Sect. Why any runic architect worth their salt would create something like this and hand it over to humans, Malverin had no idea. But one thing was apparent. Whoever had created this hadn't just possessed extraordinary skill—they had poured their soul into it.

She sneered, barely resisting the urge to destroy the item right then and there as anger ignited within her.

But it wasn't the crafter's decision to gift such a marvel to humans that truly vexed her. It was the fact that the people of Alcatraz would take something so beautiful and desecrate it like this. How could they take something as profound as magic and reduce it to mere electricity?

It was preposterous. A marvel built upon the very laws cultivators revered. The laws they defied.

She raised a hand, her aspect of reality already humming beneath her skin, before reining in the impulse. Destroying this machine would only reveal their presence in the city. And that was the last thing she wanted.

Exhaling slowly, she stared at the transmutation device for a long moment before flying out of the building, mentally marking the soul signature as checked.

She moved to the next signature after that. Then another, and another, and another.

Minutes passed as she darted from factories to homes, shops, and underground caverns, searching for something less metallic or runic in nature. But she found none. Every soul signature in this damned district was an item.

Eventually, after nearly an hour of futile searching, Malverin was more than ready to explode in frustration. Floating back up to the sky, she glared down at the hundreds of signatures she had checked, and the hundreds she still had left.

This city was a mess of irony and hypocrisy, and she would be glad never to step foot in it again. For now, however, she couldn't stop searching. Lysandra would never forgive her if she gave up now, this close.

They had to find that legacy seed before the Storm Sect did, and if that meant getting her hands dirty, then that was what she would do.

Inhaling deeply, she dove back into the district to continue her search.

***

But Malverin wasn't the only one growing frustrated with the futility of their search.

Far to the south, Severian hovered over the mess of souls he had just scanned. There were thousands of them. Even the signatures too weak to be the seed, he had checked thoroughly—both with soul sight and with his own eyes.

Yet he had found nothing. Nothing that could ease the desperation in his chest or stay the hand of the system looming over him.

With every passing hour, he could feel the limit drawing closer—an invisible weight pressing down on him with terrifying inevitability.

But ever since the deaths of his parents, Severian had never been one to accept the hand fate dealt him. For the last hundred years, he had forged his own. He would do the same today, even if it meant exposing himself and his sisters to the Storm Calamities.

And the first step was seizing the hand of fate itself from those it had favored before.

Scowling, he turned his gaze north, where he could sense the bright souls of the four Storm Calamities.

Of course they were in the north. The only part of Alcatraz that hadn't been searched yet. The very part he'd dismissed as unlikely.

With a growl, he flew toward it, warping the distance into seconds. He would find Edrick Ghaltris's legacy one way or another, even if he had to fight four Storm Calamities for it.

Back in the west hall, Vaymoz Alcatraz breathed a subtle sigh of relief as the door shut behind the storm cultivators. Around him, his soldiers did the same, each glancing at the others as though to confirm no one had been abducted without their noticing.

For a brief, tense moment, no one spoke.

Then thunder rumbled outside, the sound so forceful it shook the entire hall to its foundations.

Vaymoz grimaced. His daughter let out a dry chuckle. "Any more of this infernal posturing and I fear we're going to have to commission a structural engineer before the day is over."

Vaymoz glanced at his daughter. "Mind your words, Yelena. The last thing you want to do is scare your soldiers to death."

But the soldiers were already scared, each eyeing the ceiling and walls suspiciously, as though they could come down at any moment.

Scowling, Vaymoz glanced at his right-hand man. "Take an airstrip and follow them, Rohir. I want to know what they do and what they find."

Rohir's eyes widened in a flash of panic, but the moment his gaze met Vaymoz's, the fear evaporated—not because the task had become any less dangerous. If anything, it seemed even more suicidal now that he had time to think about it.

Yet beneath Vaymoz's steady stare, panic found no purchase.

Rohir drew himself up, jaw tightening. "Yes, sir. Shall I take a few soldiers with me?"

"Take as many as you need. I want hourly reports."

Rohir saluted. "It will be done."

He left the hall immediately, and the moment the door shut behind him, Vaymoz glanced at his daughter.

"So, what do you think, Yelena? Are you impressed?"

Yelena looked up at him for a long moment before answering. "With you, or with the cultivators?"

Vaymoz raised an eyebrow. "I'm not a man who fishes for compliments, daughter."

"And I'm not a daughter impressed by the people who killed her mother," Yelena replied quietly. "Father."

Vaymoz grimaced, then nodded. "I needed to make sure." He leaned back in his throne and swept a hard gaze across the soldiers still lingering fearfully around the hall.

"We may be human, but we are not pitiful. We do not need to bow and scrape before cultivators simply because the heavens shunned us." His voice hardened. "We must be defiant."

The soldiers saluted immediately, and for the first time since entering the hall, Yelena smiled.

"Perhaps I should go with Rohir, Father. Be your eyes and ears."

But Vaymoz was already shaking his head. "No, Yelena. It's too dangerous. Defiance isn't carelessness."

A flicker of disappointment crossed Yelena's face, but she concealed it quickly and changed the subject.

"It seemed the cultivators knew the function of the glowing fragment in the basement. I was hoping you would ask them about it."

"No." Vaymoz shook his head again. "Inviting them to investigate it further would have risked drawing their interest."

Yelena snorted. "If it was truly worth noticing, Father, they would have taken an interest in it even if you'd buried it a mile underground." She glanced at him. "I don't think the fragment is important."

Vaymoz's grimace deepened. He glanced at one of the soldiers and commanded, "Go down to the basement and check it. Important or not, it belongs to me."

The soldier left immediately, and Yelena exhaled slowly. "What will you do if it's gone?"

Vaymoz didn't respond. He kept silent, his expression cold as he stared at the doorway through which the soldier had departed.

For several minutes, no one spoke, until finally the soldier returned and bowed.

"It's still there, sir."

Vaymoz breathed out, then glanced at his daughter. "You said you wanted to be my eyes and ears?"

"I did," Yelena replied quietly. Confidently. "There's little Rohir can do if they find something we don't want them taking. You're too important to go yourself. I'm just right." She smiled at him. "Let me go and be defiant in your stead."

Vaymoz stared at his daughter for a moment before slowly nodding.

"Fine," he said. "Take another airstrip and join Rohir. Be my will."

Yelena's smile widened, and she bowed. "At once, Father."
