Just as Asher feared, it took them four whole days to find the missing Void dagger.

The morning after his conversation with Donvath, he and Samantha took off through the skies, doing their best to fly in a straight line directly from the royal palace toward Zygrul’s hidden mountain base. Asher first tried to use his Eternal Mark to pinpoint the exact location, only to find that his mark was gone.

And not gone as in moved elsewhere. For the very first time, something had actually managed to destroy his Eternal Mark.

Thankfully, the skill didn’t appear to be damaged, and Asher confirmed he still had nine available marks he could use on whoever or whatever he wanted. Though that was a small consolation. Knowing that Zygrul’s Void element was powerful enough to remove his Eternal Mark was scary in a way Asher had never felt before. He’d once marked a corpse that had then been cremated, utterly destroying the body and turning it to nothing but ash. Even that hadn’t been enough to destroy the mark he’d placed, which had instead moved from the body to floating in space where the body had been destroyed.

If it could remove an Eternal Mark, would a wound from Zygrul counteract his Eternal Regeneration if the lich wanted it to?

Asher did his best not to dwell on such thoughts as he and Samantha flew back and forth across the kingdom, but it wasn’t easy. Talking was difficult at the speeds Samantha flew at, meaning Asher had plenty of time to dwell within his own thoughts. Four days into their fruitless search, while Samantha flew lower to the ground and far slower than she was capable of, he finally felt it. He didn’t know if it was because his Spatial element was influencing his physical body, but he could feel how the dagger was slowly eating away at the surrounding realm. Pointing for Samantha to set them down, he sighed as he approached the dagger. His missing arm had managed to grow back over their four days of hunting for the blade, but he still wished they'd managed to find it sooner.

The Void dagger appeared to have landed in a large tree in the middle of a forest, before gradually eating its way through the wood. Based on how it was only a few feet from the trunk, if they’d been much slower, the dagger may have very well eaten its way into the ground and become lost to time, slowly working its way down into the core of the planet.

“Well… we finally found the stupid thing,” Asher said, staring at the black dagger as he scratched his head. “The question now is what do we do with it?”

“If it eats its way out of your Personal Rift, and it will eat its way out of your bandolier, what can we do with it?” Samantha asked. “Actually… How did Moxy even find it in the first place? Wouldn’t it have eaten its way through whatever was holding it up?”

“I think its only the blade that does that,” Asher admitted, walking around the tree as he examined the dagger from different angles. “Supporting it by the hilt is fine for a display case, but not exactly all that easy to do on the move. Honestly, I just don’t see any way we can safely carry this with us.”

“So give it to the High Prince,” Samantha suggested. “He probably has a vault he can store it in.”

“I can’t, I already told him I wasn’t going to,” Asher said, doing his best to ignore Samantha’s eye roll.

“Seriously? That’s your reason?”

“There has to be something else we can do with it,” Asher said, carefully picking up the dagger by the hilt and frowning at the irritating sensation of his energy being drained through his fingers. He could practically imagine the fine, wispy strings of pure tritessence slowly being peeled from his body, drifting through the air and connecting to the black knife, spooling around the blade before being sucked in like a miniature black hole. Blinking, he shook his head, the image vanishing as quickly as it had appeared.

What the hell… Was that Identify or something?

“Asher?” Samantha asked, peering at him. “Are you okay?”

“Yeah… Actually, let me try something.”

Holding the Void dagger in one hand, Asher pulled out a simple steel dagger with his other. He stared at the Void dagger as hard as he could, trying to will those weird threads to reappear in his vision once more. Yet regardless of how hard he stared, they refused to show themselves again. Shrugging, he decided to try his idea anyway. After all, even if he couldn’t see them, the threads of tritessence were still being pulled from him into the knife.

And he had the power to cut those threads.

Lining up his steel dagger with the hilt of the Void knife, Asher focused on Sever the Thread’s second-tier effect. Then, with a flick of his wrist, he sliced through empty space. Despite it looking like he’d cut through absolutely nothing to any outside observer, Asher noticed the effect immediately.

The Void dagger had stopped draining his energy.

“Yes!” he shouted, laughing as he stored his steel dagger once more. “I cut the tritessence! I stopped the dagger from draining me! Well, that solves—”

Asher’s excitement was cut short as he felt the dagger begin draining him again, and he swore. Of course cutting the threads would only stop the dagger for a moment. He wasn’t cutting its ability to create new ones, after all, just severing the current connection.

“False alarm,” he sighed, right back to square one regarding the dagger. “I can briefly stop it from draining the energy around it, but only for a few seconds. No new Void dagger for my collection.”

“At least that means you can Recall with it now, right?” Samantha asked, looking for the silver lining. “That’s good!”

“I guess,” he said, still a bit put out that he was being bested by a dagger. “Though I still don’t want to give it to Donvath. Where to bring a possibly evil weapon…”

Asher blinked as the answer came to him out of nowhere, and he laughed as he turned the option over in his head. It might not work, but if it did, it was probably the safest place for the dagger in the entire kingdom.

“I take it by your expression you’ve got an idea?” Samantha teased, poking him in the side. Though even as she did, she made sure to stay far away from the dagger he was holding.

“Yep. Want to come with?”

“If it’s all the same to you, would you mind dropping me off at the palace first?” Samantha asked, giving him an apologetic smile. “I’ve spent almost the entire last week flying, and I’d like to take a break. Not to mention that dagger freaks me out a bit…”

“Don’t worry, it freaks me out too,” Asher laughed. “Alright, all aboard!”

Helping Samantha into his Rift, Asher severed the tritessence being pulled by the dagger before quickly recalling to their room in the royal palace. Thankfully, Samantha’s theory was correct, and he didn’t end up losing the Void dagger in transit. Wishing Samantha luck with her relaxation, Asher severed the tritessence connected to the dagger one last time, before recalling to what he could only hope would be the dagger’s new home.

Helpha’s shop in Dormaul.

As the dark, empty weapons shop appeared around him, Asher sighed at the distinct lack of an iron woman frowning at him. Though he didn’t know why he was surprised. Not once had the ferrum actually been at her shop in all the times he’d Recalled back to Dormaul. He knew she spent most of her time at the mines in the fifth level or actually forging her weapons in her workshop, wherever that was, but surely she had to sell her weapons sometime!

Deciding the least he could do would be to wait for a couple of hours, Asher pushed a barrel filled with various swords to the side and swept a couple of fallen knives under a nearby table. Once enough space had been cleared, he pulled one of the comfy beanbag chairs Donvath used in his lounge out of his rift. He’d snagged one of those chairs a few days into their time at the palace, after Donvath had functionally given them free rein of that lounge room. Flopping into the comfy seat, Asher pulled out one of the journals they’d gotten from Zygrul’s base.

Not willing to show the lich’s journals to just anyone, Asher had actually briefly met up with Richard, asking the wizard for his opinion on the gem-dust covered booklets. Despite Richard being… Richard, the wizard was still a national asset of the Noala Kingdom, and Asher knew he could be trusted when it came to things like this. Richard had taken one look at one of the journals before frowning and demanding to run some tests. Asher hadn’t gotten it back until two days later, where the irritated wizard admitted he didn’t have the faintest idea as to what had been done to the journals. Richard had informed him they were safe to touch and handle, but had recommended he not use Incorporate on them, on the off chance there was in fact magic hiding within the gemstone dust.

And thus, Asher was forced to do something he hadn’t done in a couple of months. Not since his time back on Earth before unlocking his various skills and magical abilities.

Read a book.

While waiting for Helpha to hopefully show up, Asher sat in his comfy chair, going through the journals Zygrul had left them one page at a time. He’d never been a huge reader even back on Earth, and after getting to rely on his combination of Incorporate and Comprehend here in his new life, reading felt like even more of a slow drudge than it had been before. Having the power to instantly absorb a book and then read it in his head at ten-times speed made actual reading feel like the slowest thing in the world.

The only saving grace was how interesting each of the thirteen journals actually were.

Just as Asher had hoped, each journal was filled with things Zygrul had managed to uncover about the thirteen other liches. Information like their names, which was already a massive start, as well as things like confirmed or suspected elements. Zygrul even included a list of various defenses they had in place, and, most importantly, their suspected locations.

In one fell swoop, Asher went from knowing next to nothing about the different members of The Council of Death, to having his own personal dossier on every single member.

Of course, things were never that easy.

While a few of the members had dozens of pages of information written about them, more than half of the council had journals consisting of barely even a page or two. It seemed certain members were more forthcoming than others, and a few of them went to incredibly drastic lengths to hide any information about them whatsoever. One of the members, Jormon, had a ‘journal’ that was barely three paragraphs long. According to what Zygrul had found out, the lich had destroyed his entire country just to keep any information about himself and his past hidden.

While Asher was working through Hylm’s journal out of simple curiosity, there was a bang as the shop door swung open, nearly giving him a heart attack as he was ripped from the tale of how Hylm had stolen the hearts of quite literally everyone in her entire kingdom with her Love element. He scrambled to his feet and shoved both the journal and his beanbag into his Personal Rift before Helpha walked in. The ferrum, a woman born hundreds of years ago from a blended skill combining the Iron and Life elements, stepped into her shop with an armload of shiny new hammers. Slamming the door behind her, she turned and spotted him. Blinking, she let out a deep laugh, shaking her head as she smiled.

“Well, took you long enough.”
