Chapter 67: Burn it Down

Ambrose was beginning to believe that everything on this world stank like wet dogs. The bat currently flying him away from Bedrock Spire was friendly enough, but smelled like the bad part of a circus. And though he was friendly, the bat’s barely audible screeches made his brain itchy.

“We’ve been waiting for someone like you for a long time!” the bat, Skott said to him as they flapped chaotically through a dark cavern. Several times they narrowly missed hitting a stalactite. Other times, they did hit one and Ambrose feared for his life. Flying this way, shirt grasped by the bat’s powerful feet, was terrifying.

“Someone like what?” Ambrose shouted, though he knew the bat would probably be able to hear him if he whispered.

Luthor, Raeva, and Mulch were nearby, being flown by bats of their own. It took two to bring Mulch, and he didn’t look happy. There hadn’t been time for explanations. They’d been scooped up quickly and taken away, where their escorts chatted haphazardly.

“Someone willing to fight against tyranny! Someone with balls.”

Ambrose wanted to scoff and say something about his flyer’s lack of pants and shame, but another drop made his stomach churn. He promised himself he wouldn’t puke, but he was known to be a liar sometimes.

“Balls are soft and vulnerable,” said Ambrose, “One good kick and you drop. The Empire’s got a pretty big foot, and I don’t know what you’re expecting of me.”

But he did know what was expected of him.

Take this power, Ambrose, and break things.

It wasn’t hard to figure out what it meant, the real question was how. He couldn’t remember seeing a card ever address someone individually. The closest it got was a person being bound and the card text reflecting who they were in some way. A personal address…that felt like madness. It had to have been his imagination.

If so, his imagination turned that card into exactly what he needed to survive. Or the Wildcard that had given him [Oops All Imps] was…What? Every time he tried to think it through, the words bounced around in his head uselessly, failing to find anything coherent. It was weird, but he didn’t know what to do about it, or whether he should even bring it up with the others. They’d just think him crazy.

And then there was Jessica and Raeleq, trapped at Bedrock Spire, but alive…probably. What could they do to get them out? After today, security would be tighter than ever. Slipping back in wasn’t going to be possible.

And yet, Skott and Roy and the others were incredibly happy to see them, and screeched back and forth to each other, snippets of some conversation even the translation cards couldn’t fully convey.

It took them about half an hour to leave the city behind and find safety in the numerous, labyrinthine caverns of Stonskarr. As safe as anywhere in this world could be, at least. It got pitch black for two minutes, but then they came out the other side, where a normal looking village waited below.

Unlike Crag, this had actual structures built out in the open rather than just built into the rock itself. These even looked mostly human, but…Oh no.

As Skott lowered him into the dim, humanoid village of mushroom zombies, Ambrose felt several intrusive touches against his mind, like dogs coming around to sniff at him.

“Okay, okay, knock it off,” he said, struggling to break free before they landed. Skott let him go and flapped off a few more feet, landing on top of the roof of a house. Ambrose rolled his arms in their sockets, wincing at the pain of being carried by them for so long. “Stay out of my head, or we’re going to have a problem.”

Impossible, but we’ll do our best.

He sighed.

The others were dropped off over the next half a minute, and they joined Ambrose in looking at the village, and the animated corpses that started coming out from houses and hidey-holes to surround them. With them were some molekin, and even a handful of kobolds.

“This is weird,” said Raeva, loud enough to be heard and uncaring. “They’re looking at us funny.”

“We were headed for the local rebellion,” said Luthor, also stretching out his poor, abused arms. “This is them.”

Greetings

Greetings!

Welcome!

Please, enjoy --

“Okay, okay,” Ambrose held up his hands, breathing hard. “For the love of everything good in the universe, pick one of you to speak for all of you. Preferably someone not violating my brain every time they talk.”

Raeva and Luthor shot him dirty looks, but he doubted they disagreed. Mulch stepped up, coming between the three humans and the residents of the outpost. “Welcome to Loam. This is where a bunch of the people who didn’t bow to the Empire ended up. There’s three villages standing up for what’s right, and plenty of independents too. Those bastards got our world, but not all of it.

“This is just one of our outposts. We reckon we make up a third of the world’s natural population, but there’s a lot of Hermanic boys in Bedrock Spire. As you might’a seen today. We need more resources, but more’n that, we’ve needed someone who can fight and do some real damage.”

Raeva was the first to speak up. “We are not unsympathetic, and we want to help out, but we need to get my brother and friend back. That’s my priority, and everything else comes after that.”

Jessica. They couldn’t assault Bedrock Spire. The gears in his head turned, and he chuckled.

“What’s so funny, Ambrose?” Luthor asked, not judgmental, just curious.

“We’re going to help these people out as best as we can. They want people to lead them into fights? I doubt the soft-hearted people of this world mean to kill their friends and neighbors, so we won’t. Not if we don’t have to.”

“Ambrose? Are you okay?” Raeva’s concern was genuinely touching, but the implication was irritating.

“Obviously not. Are any of us okay? I’m tired and cranky and want to get revenge and make so much noise that they just throw the two of them back at us and tell us to leave. If we can’t take them in a direct fight, we annoy them. We bleed them. We hit them where it hurts.”

A silent murmur went through the crowd, a psychic wave that would’ve been annoying if Ambrose hadn’t realized how effective it was. They were listening to him. Not just peeking inside his head because he was close, but actively paying attention to him.

And more than that, so were his friends. Luthor in particular had a small, pleased smile, like he’d expected this. Raeva looked started, but increasingly giddy.

“And where’s that?” asked Mulch, speaking for the rest of the underground.

Take this power, Ambrose, and break things.

Okay. He could do that. In fact, he very much wanted to do that. Jessica was likely protected, and could bullshit enough to maybe extend that protection to Raeleq. If not…Well, the big guy was as tough as steel and too likable to want dead. Either way, it meant they had room to break things.

“We go after their resources,” said Ambrose. “We take out the mines, the metalworking, and the cards. More than anything we take those cards and distribute them among your people and we use them to overwhelm the bastards. We harass them until we’re impossible to ignore.”

“Yes,” said Raeva, pleased to see her peoples’ methods brought up. “Burn it down.”

“Burn it down,” Ambrose agreed. “Until they give us our friends back and let us leave, or someone kills us. But I don’t feel like dying just yet. And more than that, I’ve got a secret weapon. We’re going to outnumber and overrun them. Just you wait.”

Another murmur passed through the crowd, a shift in the bodies hosting the sapient mushroom colonies. Mulch nodded as they touched his mind and gave their opinions. “If you can help us free our people and get the Empire off this world, then our resources are yours. We’ll follow you into battle, and we’ll take back our world!”

They cheered, unable to hold it back, a psychic wave of joy and relief, washing over Ambrose and the others, uncomfortably intimate. These people would know them and their secrets, but…if they did that and were still willing to listen, then they would be helpful. More than helpful, depending on their use. Intelligence and communication would be big.

Ambrose grabbed Raeva and Luthor and pulled them close in a tired but boisterous hug. “They’re going to regret this, and the reckoning with that mole is going to be sweet. We’ll get them back, liberate this world, and move on with a mountain of cards at our disposal.”

“Then let’s get started,” said Luthor, squeezing him back.

“Don’t sound too happy about it,” Raeva half protested. Despite her pain or maybe because of it, it had been easy to goad her into destruction. She knew how fun it would be.

He chuckled. In the end, he’d embraced Luthor’s stupid group and their ideology. Things could’ve been worse. Ambrose did like destruction, and whether or not some people wanted to admit it, some things deserved to be broken.

[Imp King]. More than just a card: it was a role to accept and embrace. It would be an honor to do the breaking.
