Escaping his current predicament wouldn't be easy, as just hiding in the middle of the slums or running out of town. It'd also be impossible for him to reach the next town on foot within the night, which left Levi with one option, one so bold and outrageous that he repeatedly stopped to think about it on his way there.

Unfortunately for him, he didn't have much of a choice once it came to hiding spots. If he'd stayed in the city, he'd risk being found out by Killian. All the Damned taskmaster would have to do was flick a few silvers to those in the slums, and they'd rat him out. The untamed forest outside Gantus was also a no-go for Levi; the place was too open and, not to mention, crawling with the few monsters that'd been able to slip out of the nearby dungeon.

His fear of dying to an errant monster made Levi's final location for a hiding spot look less and less reasonable the longer he stared at the place. To the unassuming, it was nothing more than the entrance to a cave, but to the residents of Gantus, this was the dungeon. The city had originally been built closer, but with multiple monster leaks from the dungeon, they'd had to settle a bit farther.

Luckily for him, he hadn't been attacked by any of the numerous monsters that undoubtedly infested the forest, and now he stood at the threshold of the dungeon, contemplating his decision. He was just a level one synced, a thief —a rogue. Fighting directly wasn't his style. Never had been, so apart from his already terrible level, his class wasn't suited to soloing a dungeon.

"But what other choice do I have?" He whispered.

Sucking in a deep breath, he said a brief prayer to Cerpital. With his whole world having turned upside down on a day that was supposed to be filled with celebration, he didn't know what to think. If anything, he struggled to piece together where exactly it had gone wrong for him. Maybe when he stepped into Killian's office, or when he'd picked the rogue class over the mage class, which Cerpital had recommended.

Ifs and buts leave a man stuck.

Levi chuckled at the thought. It'd been something Conor had told him when he'd refused to sign a contract to Sync like the priest. He'd said there was still a bit of hope that he could sync without a contract, and now he had, and that might've been the decision that led him here, in front of a dungeon, contemplating if he was in the right place. Regardless of his misgivings, he knew that it was his best option; the dungeon would hide him, and if he could survive whatever was inside, he'd come out better for it. Besides, there'd be a chance to transport himself miles away from Gantus if he could clear it. Of course, it was a big if.

"Ifs and buts leave a man stuck," Levi muttered, with a bit of hesitation. He analyzed the dungeon for the second time that night.

F-ranked dungeon, I should be able to handle this. It'd be hard to do alone, but not impossible, and if I can't handle this, then I might as well stand out here and wait for Ghost to kill me.

He sucked in a deep breath, exhaled, and walked into the dungeon. Thinking had its place, but much thinking without action was the same as not thinking at all. He wasn't going to waste his time thinking about the endless possibilities; no, it was time for action, and he prepared himself as best as he could.

Levi held both daggers in a tight grip; they were his only means of attack, as he only had one skill [analyze]. His lack of options meant he had to be sharp; his mind could be the difference between life and death. His eyes scanned the entire place, noting down what he saw.

The walls were awfully good for the inside of a dungeon, a few chips here and there, but the overall layout looked like he was in a regular hallway, with two dim red glowstones mounted against the walls illuminating his path as best as it could.

The red glow made the dungeon look eerie, which made a lot of sense to Levi. There was no point calling it a dungeon if the place was normal or welcoming. At the same time, a dungeon with a welcoming setting might even be more of a threat because it could cause him to lower his guard.

The red glow had the opposite effect; it set him on edge, and each step he took only happened after he'd sufficiently scanned the surrounding area. The dirt floor made his footsteps muffled, but he could feel each grain of loose sand his boots pressed upon just as well as he could hear his own heartbeat in his ear. The events of the day had left him fidgety and too pumped up with adrenaline, so much so that he'd begun to hate the lack of action.

His very being was wishing to be attacked, wishing to get something to hit, some thin—. Levi abruptly paused in his tracks, the rogue slowly turning around to confirm if the foes he'd been searching for were actually behind him. A wave of relief washed over him at the sight of a dead end. The entrance to the dungeon had disappeared; in its place was a blank wall, similar to the ones that surrounded him on both sides of the eerie hall.

Well, that was expected.

Turning back to face the proper direction, the red glowstones did little to illuminate anything other than his immediate surroundings. It seemed like he'd have to manage with the two glowstones, and if his foes wouldn't come to meet him, then he'd have to risk going beyond the reach of the glowstones; he'd have to risk going into darkness.

I wonder what monster this dungeon would throw at me.

He'd heard stories of the kind of things synced saw in these sorts of dungeons, often times there were little threats, the sort that lurked within the forests, goblins, gremlins, imps, Nalumps, Kiralnis, things like that, other times there were worse things. Very rarely did monsters beyond the ranks of F pop up in an F-ranked dungeon. At the end of the day, rarely did not mean never, and judging by his luck so far, Levi couldn't rule out an overpowered monster coming for him.

Still, he crept, inching closer to the darkness. His fears and worries wouldn't hold him back; he had to do this, and it wasn't going to be for the mage that had helped him escape but for himself, for Silas ... For Bree. An invisible hand tightened his heart at the thought of never seeing them again. He loosened his grip on his fingers and instantly tightened it again before pausing in his tracks.

"What is that?" he whispered.

His face scrunched up in disgust, and his eyes began to water at the whiff of odor his nose had picked up. He'd just gotten to the edge of the reach of the red glow stones when the odor had reached him, causing him to discard his former line of thought. The odor was worse than anything he'd ever smelled, and he'd grown up in the slums.

More than that, it confirmed what he'd expected: he wasn't alone and his quarry was nearby. Taking his time, he squinted at the darkness, trying to pinpoint his foe. Even a glimpse of an outline would greatly help him in identifying what he was dealing with, but no matter how hard he looked, he couldn't see anything. Just as he was about to give up and risk going in blind, an idea struck him.

He'd been going at it all wrong; it was too dark to see beyond the darkness, but he could certainly sniff his way to his foe, as proven earlier, or even better, he could listen for it. So he did, he stopped trying to see his foe and started trying to hear where it was. Crouched as he was at the edge of the glow stones' reach, he did not make any movement. He stayed still and listened.

There, I've got you.

Scuttling just a few feet in front of him, to the right. It was coming for him fast, and its footsteps were faint; he'd had to strain his ears to pick up the movement. The dirt floor muffled the sound of the creature's movement, just like it'd done for him with his boot. Luckily for Levi, he'd had an alternative way to track his foe. He stayed unmoving, his dagger angled for a quick attack. A tight smile split his lips as he waited patiently for his prey to come to him.

When the clash eventually happened it ended within a heartbeat, the already prepared rogue striking as soon as the monster launched itself at him, the short stocky creature erupting from the darkness at full speed, its claws angled for Levi's face but unfortunately for it, two daggers ripped through it's throat with frightening precision and ease, warm blood spraying onto his wrists and cloak. The monster was dead before it even knew it, a weak gurgle and twitching limbs the only sign of struggle before the monster went limp on his blades.

Levi barely had time to celebrate the kill before the sound of scuttling reached his ears, the sound growing louder with each passing moment. With a look of alarm on his face, he quickly pushed the monster's corpse off his blades. The lifeless monster dropped to the ground with a thud. Its beady black eyes stared right at Levi's, shock evident in them even though the monster was already dead.

Breaking eye contact with the dead monster, he moved backwards, eager to get away from the stinking corpse as well as prepare himself for whatever foe ... No, foes were coming. The scuttling sounded like it was multiple foes coming for him. So it made sense to move closer to the red glow stones. These monsters were made to thrive in the darkness; he wasn't.

His only advantage was that they were dumb enough to come to him instead of waiting for him on the other side. That choice of theirs was something he could exploit to his own advantage. The red light from the red glowstones was dim, but it'd be enough for him to see just what exactly he was dealing with, and even better yet, it'd help him see where his foes were.

That's an advantage to die for.

Levi chuckled at the thought, his eyes drifting to the monster he killed. It'd been the single push he needed to get back to himself, it's blood coating his wrists. For some reason, it made him feel more alive, more alert; his eyes were sharper. In short, he felt more. It was almost imperceptible, but he could tell that he didn't need to strain his ears nearly as much as before to pick up the sound of feet hitting the ground.

It might've had something to do with the fact that it was multiple creatures heading towards his direction this time, or maybe it was something else; either way, it was clear to him that something had changed, but he didn't have time to confirm what it was, as multiple monsters entered his field of vision. He counted six at a glance, and he could tell that more lurked within the darkness.
