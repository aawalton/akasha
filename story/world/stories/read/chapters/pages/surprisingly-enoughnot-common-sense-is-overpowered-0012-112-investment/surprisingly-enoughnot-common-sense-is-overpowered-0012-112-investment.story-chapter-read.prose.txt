Ning stepped into the center of the first plot and formed the hand seals.

Qi stirred within his dantian and flowed steadily through his meridians as mist gathered overhead, gradually condensing into a cloud suspended above the field.

A moment later, spiritual rain began to fall.

Ning walked slowly across the plot, guiding the cloud along with him while adjusting the density of the rain. Too much spiritual energy could overwhelm the plants, while too little would fail to nourish them properly. He made sure each section received what it needed without wasting qi.

After all, every bit of qi spent here was qi he wouldn't have available for cultivation later.

By the time he finished, the soil across the field had darkened evenly, and the cloud gradually dissipated.

"That's amazing, Brother Ning!" a voice called from behind him.

A young woman in green peripheral disciple robes stood at the boundary of the field, her eyes bright with relief.

Ning turned and offered her a polite smile. "Just doing what I was paid for."

For over a week now, Ning had been watering five neighboring plots every day.

It had started when he offered to help a newcomer who had nearly collapsed from hauling water. Word spread afterward, and several other disciples approached him with similar requests.

Most were newcomers with low-grade spiritual roots. Unlike Ning, they needed much longer to learn even basic spells, which meant they had little choice but to haul water from the river until they could cast the Small Cloud Rain Technique themselves.

Having experienced the same routine, Ning immediately recognized the opportunity. For two spirit stones every three days, he would take care of their watering needs.

"Cultivate while the soil settles," Ning advised. "You should be able to make better use of your time that way."

The woman nodded, then hesitated before handing over her share of the payment.

There was a reason these newcomers could afford spirit stones while Ning had arrived at the sect nearly penniless.

The sect only accepted disciples with low-grade spiritual roots if they came from families with at least three generations of cultivators. Such families usually had some accumulated wealth, which was essential given the resources required for someone with a low-grade root to advance.

The sect wanted disciples who had at least some hope of reaching late-stage Qi Condensation, or even Foundation Building. Without family support, achieving that with a low-grade root was extremely difficult, and even those with sufficient resources could spend years stuck at bottlenecks.

"Um... Senior Brother?"

The young woman's voice interrupted his thoughts.

"How about we have some tea together sometime?" she suggested, her voice growing softer. "Could you also lower the spirit stone fee a little?"

She looked up at him with what was clearly intended to be a charming smile.

Was she serious?

Ning had nothing against opportunistic behavior. After all, he was running a side business himself. But when that opportunism was directed at him, especially like this, he suddenly found himself feeling much less appreciative.

"Sorry, I'm afraid that's not possible." He shook his head politely.

"Ah... is it really not?" she asked, taking a small step closer.

"I'm afraid not. Please just pay the agreed amount."

Ning had read enough clichés in his previous life to know where such situations could lead. If she was willing to charm him for a discount, who knew how many others she might try the same trick on?

That sounded like an invitation for jealousy, rivalry, and all sorts of unnecessary trouble.

He had no intention of getting dragged into someone else's romantic subplot. Let the protagonists handle that.

Besides, spirit stones were simply worth more.

"Hmm, this little hustle doesn't seem to have much potential anymore," Ning muttered as he left the field. "Looks like it's time to put that plan into action."

...

Without external support, he wasn't going to secure the sect's reward.

Despite his disciplined routine, two months of cultivation had barely carried him halfway through the First Stage of Qi Condensation. At this rate, reaching the Second Stage would take another couple of months, and the Third Stage would require even longer.

The problem was that he only had a year.

If he reached the Third Stage within his first year, the sect would allow him to select a new cultivation technique, an opportunity he had absolutely no intention of missing.

A cultivation technique was like the trunk of a tree, while spells and martial arts were its branches. It determined how a cultivator absorbed, refined, and circulated spiritual energy, influencing nearly every aspect of their development.

Ning already had the Pure Qi Sutra, so he was more interested in obtaining a body cultivation technique.

Over the past two months, he'd noticed that his physical condition directly affected how long he could cultivate. Strengthening his meridians and improving his endurance might allow him to extend his cultivation sessions before fatigue forced him to stop.

He'd first noticed this while practicing the Bone Forging Fist. Although it wasn't a proper body cultivation technique, it still tempered his physique, and each improvement allowed him to meditate a few minutes longer.

A few minutes might sound insignificant, but accumulation was everything.

Proper cultivation techniques, however, were among the sect's most valuable inheritances. Some cost thousands of spirit stones, far beyond what someone in his position could afford.

That was why the reward mattered so much.

To reach the Third Stage in time, he needed more resources, which meant more spirit stones. The problem was obtaining them.

Drawing from his previous life's experience, Ning's first thought had been to borrow money.

Unfortunately, this world didn't have banks.

It did, however, have moneylenders.

In fact, Ning had already visited one of the establishments operating near the sect's market. Before going, he'd asked Old Zhou to meet him there after half an hour. The old man had warned him against borrowing from such places, but eventually agreed to show up at the appointed time.

The establishment looked respectable enough, with a neat reception area and an attendant who greeted Ning enthusiastically.

"Fifty spirit stones? Of course, Fellow Daoist! We can arrange that immediately!"

The attendant produced a contract and pointed to the empty space at the bottom.

"Just leave your qi imprint here, and the spirit stones are yours."

"Can I read it first?" Ning asked.

"Of course." The attendant handed him the contract, which was several pages long.

"That's quite a long contract," Ning remarked.

"Haha, that's perfectly normal around here. We're a reputable establishment, so you have nothing to worry about." The attendant pointed to the space for the qi imprint again. "You can go ahead."

"I'd still like to read it first."

Ning opened the contract and began going through its contents.

"...Very well," the attendant replied, his smile faltering slightly.

Surely no one in their right mind would bother reading through all those clauses?

Ning, however, continued reading.

At first glance, the terms seemed reasonable. There was a fixed repayment period, an interest rate, and the usual penalties for late payment.

Then he reached the later clauses.

Hmm?

Ning read them again.

Failure to repay the debt would allow the lender to claim compensation through his future earnings and labor. More interestingly, the agreement gave the lender considerable discretion in determining what constituted a breach of contract.

Several conditions could even be adjusted under circumstances described as special considerations.

This shit was shady as fuck.

"Everything should be in order, Fellow Daoist," the attendant said with a smile. "You only need to leave your qi imprint."

Ning returned the smile. "The terms are certainly comprehensive."

"Our establishment is the most reputable in the area. You can rest assured."

Ning continued examining the contract, occasionally asking a harmless question about the repayment schedule. The attendant answered patiently at first, though his smile gradually grew strained.

Just as Ning reached the final page, a familiar voice came from the entrance.

"Ning! There you are!"

Old Zhou stepped inside, glancing between Ning and the contract on the table.

"Ah, Old Zhou!" Ning rose with a smile. "Perfect timing."

"Didn't you say you had something to discuss with me?"

"That's right." Ning turned toward the attendant and offered an apologetic smile. "I'm afraid I'll have to leave for now. Thank you for explaining everything."

"Fellow Daoist, the agreement is already prepared. Leaving your qi imprint will only take a moment."

"I wouldn't want to make an important decision while keeping my elder waiting," Ning replied politely. "I'll return once I've had time to consider it."

He placed the contract neatly on the table and followed Old Zhou outside, leaving the attendant watching them with a stiff smile.

Only after they had walked a comfortable distance from the establishment did Old Zhou speak.

"I told you those fellows were trying to trick you into an unfair contract."

Ning chuckled. "You did."

"Then why bother going?"

"I had to see for myself. Who knows? Maybe the terms would've been reasonable."

Old Zhou snorted. "Reasonable? From those people?"

"Well, now I know." Ning shrugged. "Thanks for coming, Old Zhou."

The old man shook his head, though he seemed pleased enough with the outcome.

Ning glanced back toward the market.

Yeah, no.

He might be desperate for spirit stones, but he wasn't desperate enough to bind himself to such terms over fifty of them.

He'd arranged for Old Zhou to arrive in case the negotiation turned unpleasant. Considering what he'd found in the contract, that little precaution had paid off nicely.

Moneylenders weren't his only option, anyway. He could always look for investors.

According to Ji Ning's memories, wealthy martial artists sometimes invested in promising youths with spiritual roots, hoping to receive enormous returns once those youths rose to prominence.

Within the sect, however, everyone possessed a spiritual root. That alone wasn't enough to attract investment.

He needed something that would make him stand out, preferably a reputation for being talented in spellcraft.

After all, was it really wrong to present himself as a "genius in magic" when he had the results to support it?

His panel helped him track his progress, and his comprehension wasn't bad either. Ning felt he could make a convincing case.

Still, talent alone wouldn't persuade someone to lend him spirit stones. He needed to show that he could repay them.

His Small Cloud Rain Technique had already reached Minor Accomplishment, allowing him to maintain the conditions needed for stable harvests. That gave him a source of income, even if his cultivation didn't progress as quickly as he hoped.

He'd also been preparing another advantage.

Over the past week, Ning had encouraged several farmers he'd helped to join the Mutual Aid Association. Most were newcomers with few connections, so joining an association where they could exchange information and seek assistance made sense.

Wei Zhusang had welcomed the introductions, and the association gained several new members. It benefited everyone involved, including Ning, who had quietly strengthened his relationship with Wei in the process.

After all, why approach someone empty-handed when you could give them a reason to welcome you?

Thus, Ning decided to approach Wei Zhusang, one of the wealthiest people he knew.

...

Wei Zhusang welcomed Ning warmly and invited him to sit. After exchanging a few pleasantries, Ning decided to get straight to the point.

Well, time to see if those connections pay off.

"Brother Wei, I'd like to ask you for a loan."

Wei Zhusang blinked, surprised by the sudden request.

Ning smiled and continued, "I've recently discovered that I have something of a talent for spells. After two months of practice, I've managed to bring the Small Cloud Rain Technique to Minor Accomplishment."

"Minor Accomplishment? In just two months?" Wei Zhusang leaned forward with interest.

"That's right. Spiritual farming disciples are exempt from missions during their first year, and I intend to reach the Third Stage of Qi Condensation before that year ends. Once I succeed, I'll repay the spirit stones."

Wei Zhusang studied him for a moment before asking, "May I see the technique for myself, Brother Ning?"

Even a basic spiritual rain technique usually took years to reach Minor Accomplishment. Achieving it in two months was impressive, especially for someone who had only recently begun cultivating.

Wei Zhusang wanted to verify the claim himself.

"Of course."

They made their way to Ning's farmland, where the lush, evenly nourished crops already offered some evidence of his abilities.

"Please observe."

Ning formed the hand seals, guiding qi through his meridians as a cloud nearly thirty meters across gathered above the field. A steady drizzle descended, spreading evenly over the soil.

Wei Zhusang watched with growing interest, examining the cloud and the precision of Ning's control.

"Impressive! With comprehension like this, Brother Ning, I'm certain you'll accomplish great things in the sect."

Ning smiled modestly.

In the cultivation world, talent was generally judged through two important factors: spiritual roots and comprehension.

Spiritual roots represented a cultivator's natural affinity with the spiritual energy of heaven and earth. Comprehension, meanwhile, reflected their ability to understand techniques.

Two cultivators could spend the same amount of time practicing an identical technique and still achieve vastly different results.

Ning wasn't sure how his own comprehension compared to the geniuses of this world, but he suspected his education on Earth had given him an advantage. Twelve years of schooling had trained him to approach problems logically, recognize patterns, and examine why something worked instead of simply memorizing the process.

Of course, that didn't make him some heaven-defying genius.

Old Zhou had once mentioned true prodigies who could grasp a single principle and derive ten different applications from it. Ning doubted he belonged in that category, though he was doing quite well for himself.

"How many spirit stones do you need?" Wei Zhusang finally asked.

"How about fifty, Brother Wei?"

Ning hadn't chosen Wei Zhusang at random. From their previous interactions, he'd noticed that Wei was gradually building a network of talented disciples around himself.

Someone with his resources and ambitions would be interested in supporting a promising cultivator, especially one who had already helped expand his association.

Sure enough, Wei Zhusang chuckled.

"Brother Ning, fifty spirit stones won't be enough for your cultivation. I'll lend you a hundred."

Ning's smile widened slightly.

"Besides, you've already helped our association by introducing those new disciples," Wei added. "It would hardly be right of me to refuse a little assistance when you're the one asking."

"Brother Wei is too generous."

Wei Zhusang retrieved a pouch containing a hundred spirit stones from his storage device and placed it on the table.

Ning almost winced at the sheer richness radiating from the man.

A hundred spirit stones, just like that?

He maintained his composure as they briefly agreed on the repayment terms.

"I can also ask Brother Feng and Sister Rulang if they're willing to lend you some," Wei Zhusang continued. "Though I hope you won't mind if they decline."

Ning was pleasantly surprised. He'd planned to approach the others separately if Wei Zhusang declined, but it seemed that wouldn't be necessary.

Having connections really does make life easier.

Even if none of them agreed, he still had his farming income to fall back on. It would take longer to accumulate enough spirit stones, but at least he wouldn't be putting himself at the mercy of a suspicious contract.

"If I get them, it's luck. If not, it's fate," Ning replied with a smile. "Everyone has their own difficulties, so I wouldn't hold it against them."

Wei Zhusang nodded approvingly.

He had already found Ning's willingness to ask for a loan interesting. Most disciples would have been too embarrassed to make such a request, but Ning had approached him openly.

Wei Zhusang had been taught the value of building connections from a young age, so he appreciated Ning's approach. Besides, lending Ning a hundred spirit stones wasn't especially risky. His talent was promising, and his spiritual farming skills gave him a reasonable means of repayment.

If Ning continued progressing, a hundred spirit stones might eventually become little more than pocket change to him.

"Brother Ning, with that kind of mindset, I believe you have a real chance of reaching the Foundation Building Realm," Wei Zhusang said sincerely.

For a disciple with a mid-grade spiritual root and no powerful backing, that was no small compliment. Most never made it that far.

Ning blinked.

Huh. I didn't realize getting into debt was one of the prerequisites for Foundation Building.
