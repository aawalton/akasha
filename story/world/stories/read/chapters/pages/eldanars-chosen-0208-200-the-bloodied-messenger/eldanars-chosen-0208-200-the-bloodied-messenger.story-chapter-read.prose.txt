The evening bore on, the sky transitioning from a sickly bruised grey to a pitch black. The pale sun had struggled to pierce the thick layer of necrotic smog that was now actively leaking from the northern forest, creeping over the frost-covered plains like a physical tide of rot.

The atmosphere within the camp was a highly volatile mixture of frantic energy and simmering, absolute dread. Caistina had issued the immediate retreat order late after hearing Josh and Brett's horrific intelligence report, and the logistical nightmare of breaking down an encampment in the dark was currently in full swing.

"Move those supply crates to the heavy wagons!" Torin roared, his gravelly voice carrying easily over the clattering din of panicked work. The scarred adventurer was pointing his heavy mace towards a cluster of terrified civilian labourers. "Leave the heavy iron braziers. We do not have the draft beasts to drag dead weight, and we certainly do not have the time. Prioritise food, medical supplies, and steel!"

The lower-level adventurers, who had originally signed on expecting a safe, highly managed perimeter sweep, were now working as glorified porters. They dismantled the large canvas pavilions with frantic, clumsy haste. Their eyes constantly darted towards the dark, silent treeline, jumping at every snapped twig or gust of freezing wind.

Josh stood near the northern edge of the camp, leaning heavily on his deeply gouged steel shield. The exhaustion of the day still ached deep in his bones, a dull throbbing that ran from his bruised collarbones down to his calves. However, the system notification he had received had provided a much-needed surge of fresh stamina. Hitting level twenty-six had subtly refined his dense musculature, granting him a small but vital buffer of new energy.

He was not helping with the packing. He, along with the other high-tier adventurers, was standing in a loose defensive line, watching the rotting forest. They were the physical wall keeping the creeping panic of the labourers from devolving into an outright rout.

"They are taking far too long," Perberos murmured, stepping up beside Josh. The elven scout had his longbow unslung, his luminous silver eyes narrowed as he scanned the suffocating gloom. He held a heavy steel-tipped broadhead loosely between his fingers, ready to draw and fire in a fraction of a second.

"The Silver Griffons returned over an hour ago," Brett noted, rubbing his temples as he watched a group of labourers struggle to push a heavy wooden cart through the freezing mud. The mage looked pale and deeply unsettled by the rushing chaos. He had spent the last two hours theorising about the terrifying implications of the General of Bone, and the anxiety showed in the dark circles under his eyes. "That leaves only the Iron Gauntlet party unaccounted for in the designated grid."

"Maybe they pushed deeper than their designated boundary before the retreat horn sounded," Josh suggested, though his storm-grey eyes were hard with worry. "Or maybe they got bogged down in the mud while fighting a heavy patrol."

"Or maybe they found the same guards you did," Ronald interjected, stepping out from behind a tall stack of supply crates.

The feline beastkin had his arms crossed over his chest, his jade-green eyes fixed entirely on the treeline. He moved with a restless, coiled energy, his tail lashing behind him beneath his muted green cloak. "If they encountered a Bone Goliath without a dedicated heavy tank to hold the frontline, they are already dead. We cannot afford to wait for ghosts."

Josh tightened his grip on the edge of his shield, a low growl of frustration rumbling in his throat. Bhel was currently resting in the back of a heavily reinforced medical wagon, deep in a restorative magical sleep. The dwarf was safe, but the thought of leaving another party out there in the rotting dark felt entirely wrong. It felt like a betrayal of everything he had fought for since arriving in Ashenfall.

"We give them twenty minutes," Josh stated flatly, his voice carrying the uncompromising weight of a man who refused to abandon his own. "If they do not break the treeline by then, we form a squad and we go in after them."

"No, you absolutely do not," Ronald countered sharply, his slit pupils narrowing as he turned to face Josh. "Caistina’s orders are absolute, and Torin agrees. We break camp and we retreat to the city walls. We do not risk our high-tier assets on a blind rescue mission when an army of thousands is currently massing just a few miles away."

"We do not leave our people behind to be butchered," Josh snapped, his jaw setting stubbornly as he stepped closer to the beastkin.

"You are thinking with your heart, not your head," Ronald hissed, his ears flattening slightly against his skull. "If you march back into that forest, you risk losing the only fighters capable of holding the main gates of Ashenfall. One party is not worth sacrificing the entire town."

Before Josh could argue further, a sharp, frantic snapping of branches echoed loudly from the dark forest.

The reaction on the defensive line was instantaneous. Every weapon was drawn. Bowstrings hummed with lethal tension, and the heavy, metallic clash of steel swords being ripped from scabbards filled the freezing air. The terrified labourers behind them stopped working, staring in wide-eyed horror at the trees.

A figure burst violently through the dead, thorny briars and stumbled onto the muddy path leading to the camp.

It was not an undead creature. It was a lithe human woman, wearing the shattered and torn remnants of studded leather armour. She was missing her helmet, her blonde hair matted heavily with dark mud and thick, red blood. She was missing her sword, her right arm hanging limply and unnaturally at her side, leaving a distinct, horrifying trail of crimson droplets in the frost with every agonising step she took.

"Elara," Torin gasped from further down the line, instantly recognising the skirmisher from the missing Iron Gauntlet party.

The injured woman did not stop to catch her breath. She sprinted towards the heavily armed defensive line with the last frantic, burning reserves of her adrenaline. Her eyes were completely wide with absolute, primal terror, entirely devoid of the usual cocky confidence she carried in the Guildhall.

"Help!" Elara screamed, her voice cracking violently, echoing across the silent camp. She stumbled over a frozen rut in the road, falling hard into the mud just a few yards from where Josh was standing. She did not try to stand back up. She simply pointed a trembling, blood-stained finger back towards the dark, silent trees. "They are surrounded! The whole area is completely swarming!"

Josh did not hesitate for a second. He stepped forward, dropping his heavy shield slightly so he could grab the woman by the shoulder of her ruined leather armour, hauling her forcefully back to her feet.

"Where?" Josh demanded, his voice completely devoid of panic, offering only a solid, unbreakable wall of calm focus.

"The eastern side of the ravine," Elara gasped, coughing a splatter of dark blood onto the frost at Josh's steel boots. She gripped his arm with her one good hand, her fingernails digging desperately into the leather beneath his gauntlet. "Maybe a mile deep into the forest. We were falling back when we found a nest of Weavers. Hundreds of them. The ground just opened up beneath us. They are completely trapped at the bottom of the gorge."

Josh shoved his heavy shield back onto his left arm, settling the thick leather straps securely. He looked at Perberos and Brett, giving a single, decisive nod, and immediately began marching towards the dark treeline.

"Josh, halt!" Ronald barked, stepping directly into his path to physically block him. The beastkin had his hands resting threateningly on the hilts of his twin daggers, his posture aggressively defensive. "Did you not just hear what she said? A nest of Weavers. We do not have the time for this. The caravan is nearly packed. If we send a rescue squad into an ambush of that magnitude, we risk losing everything."

"Move out of my way, Ronald," Josh growled, stopping mere inches from his former trainer. He towered over the lithe beastkin, his storm-grey eyes burning with absolute, uncompromising resolve. The latent magical energy of his new level hummed dangerously beneath his skin.

"This is a tactical error," Ronald hissed, his tail lashing angrily, refusing to yield the ground. "If her party is surrounded by hundreds of monsters at the bottom of a gorge, they are already dead. You are marching directly into a trap designed to bleed our high tiers before the main siege even begins."

"I am marching to save our people," Josh replied coldly, his voice ringing with absolute certainty. "If I have to knock you into the mud to do it, I will."

"He is right, Ronald," Brett said, stepping up firmly beside Josh. "If we abandon our own people to die in the mud simply because the mathematical probability is unfavourable, then we have already lost this war. Morale is a tangible asset. If the city guards see us leave the Iron Gauntlet party to rot, they will break and run the moment the undead hit the walls."

Perberos flanked Josh on the other side, while Carcan stepped forward, her staff glowing with a brilliant, resolute golden light. Further down the line, several other veteran adventurers began drawing their weapons, muttering their loud agreement with the shield-bearer.

Ronald looked at the heavily armed, incredibly stubborn group of fighters arrayed against him. He let out a long, frustrated hiss, his ears flattening entirely. The beastkin was clearly itching for a fight, his predatory instincts fighting an internal war against his cautious, pragmatic tactical training. He knew they were marching into a nightmare, but he also knew he could not physically stop them without drawing civilian blood.

"Fine," Ronald snapped bitterly, pulling his hands away from his daggers and throwing them in the air. He pointed a sharp, accusing claw directly at Elara. "You. Get to the medical tent immediately. Carcan can stabilise your arm before we move out."

"No," Elara refused vehemently, her voice raspy but entirely unbroken.

She ripped a long strip of relatively clean cloth from the hem of her ruined tabard. Using her teeth and her left hand, she began tying a crude, brutally tight tourniquet around her bleeding right bicep. Her hands were shaking violently from the blood loss and the cold, but her movements were entirely deliberate.

"I am not staying here," Elara stated, spitting a mouthful of blood into the mud. "I know exactly where the gorge is. You will waste precious minutes trying to track our footprints in that necrotic sludge."

"You are a massive liability," Ronald argued, his jade eyes narrowing in disbelief. "You are bleeding out, and you cannot even hold a sword."

"I can hold a dagger," Elara hissed, reaching down and pulling a sharp, steel hunting knife from her boot with her left hand. The sheer, desperate fury burning in her eyes was startling. "They ordered me to run. They bought me the time to get clear so I could bring help. I am going back for them, with or without you."

Ronald stared at the severely injured woman for a long, calculating moment. He recognised the absolute, uncompromising fire in her gaze. It was the exact same unyielding fire that currently burned in Josh's storm-grey stare. It was the stubborn refusal to submit to the dark.

"We move in a strict formation," Ronald finally ordered, completely surrendering to the inevitable reality of the situation. He turned to the gathered adventurers, his voice ringing with sharp, unquestionable military authority. "Heavy fighters take the lead position. Skirmishers and rogues take the flanks. Rangers and casters stay entirely in the centre. Elara, you stay right beside me and point the way. If you fall behind or collapse, I will not stop this column to carry you. Carcan, heal her as we move."

"I will not fall behind," she promised grimly, gripping her dagger tightly.

Before they moved, Carcan stepped quickly up to the injured skirmisher. She did not ask for permission. The elven healer pressed her glowing hand directly over the crude tourniquet. She poured a rapid, concentrated ten-second burst of golden holy magic into the mangled limb, forcefully sealing the major arteries to prevent Elara from bleeding to death on the march.

The rescue squad formed up with terrifying, professional speed. It was a mixed group of nearly twenty high-tier fighters pulled from several different parties, united instantly by a singular, desperate goal.

Josh took his place at the very front of the column, raising his steel shield. He stepped past the treeline, leaving the bustling panic of the camp behind, and plunged directly back into the suffocating stench of the rotting forest.

The brief, exhausting respite was forgotten. They were moving incredibly fast, their boots churning the black mud, ready to slam like a solid steel hammer into whatever horrors were currently tearing the Iron Gauntlet party apart.
