Leaving the inn, Damon stepped into the twilight with his chest unarmored, heart raw but free. Copper padded at his side, head low, nose twitching at the scents of bread, smoke, and waking life. For the first time since he’d cried out quietly in that alley, Damon wasn’t carrying the weight of what he’d done, but the weight of who he was doing it for.

Love steadied his steps. Not the soft kind, but the fierce, anchoring love that tethered him to the women he’d promised to protect, and to the fragile hope they still embodied. Copper brushed against his leg as if feeling that thought, tail flicking once before resettling in an easy rhythm beside him.

The city unfolded before them in a light he hadn’t truly seen before. Where once his sharpened senses had found only suspicion and prejudice, he now caught something quieter. A tenderness still survived.

Parents guiding sleepy children through the river’s evening fog. Merchants laughing over chipped cups of morning brew. An old dryad brushing the frost from her human husband’s hair while he locked the shutters against the coming storm. Her wooden artwork bloomed in the coming night, flowers glowing to provide soul and joy to the street. Copper watched as they passed the house, ears flicking, muzzle tilting toward the warmth and laughter.

Even in a world ruled by gods and demons, where bloodlines defined destinies and the heavens played dice with souls, people still reached for something good. Damon saw it in every small act—the struggle to hold onto decency in a place that had forgotten how simple love could be.

It wasn’t hatred that had once turned eyes away from him. It was fear. Fear of truth, of change, of a world crying out to be made right. Titania had shown him that—had peeled back the veil to reveal not just the rot, but the roots still fighting to grow.

Even this so-called lawful nation, gilded in its morality, bore fractures spreading through its holy marble. But beneath it all, its heart was true. Families still loved. People still believed. It was worth saving; not for glory, not for vengeance, but for them.

So when Damon walked the streets toward the thorns beneath this good city, struggling against the dark tide rising from its edges, Copper stayed close, silent, watchful. The cub resonated and learned, sensing the ache in his chest.

He saw what his grandfather now fought viciously to protect—what his tired soul bled for, doing everything in his power to bring his ideals into full blossom. To fight for what he and the Holy Emperor embodied, not as god and follower, but as men, attempting to keep stability in a naturally chaotic world.

From inside, Damon couldn’t keep the love from bursting over the muddy banks of his heart, and a few tears fell from his eyes. In his mind, he saw two women walking beside him, and the little bear cub plodding faithfully along, entering the darkness to save a crying teenage saintess.

I will give all my heart and soul to see them happy again… Everything I am.

The descent into the underground arena hit him like a physical force; the sound, the scent, and the raw emotion crashed over them in waves that made Copper press tighter against his leg, a low whine vibrating through their bond. Yet, the light his goddess brought him parted the sea of overwhelming bloodthirst.

He bent down to rub the cub’s head as several masked figures sneered at him. It’s okay, bud. These guys don’t stand a chance… We have a mission to complete.

He rose and stepped forward, the crowd parting before him as if he had the plague.

This wasn’t the scholarly quiet of his earlier visit. The fountain entrance remained the same, but everything beyond had transformed into something primal.

Hundreds of magical crystals pulsed overhead, their rhythm matching the crowd’s roar like a massive heartbeat. The scholarly blue light had been replaced with amber and crimson, casting everything in shades of old blood and fever dreams.

The air hung thick with sweat, spilled ale, and fear-scent from the caged participants; the nobles bringing their own monsters to attempt to win the pot prize. Things inside growled feral, and scraped below, and underneath it all, the copper-iron tang of blood made his fangs ache.

The stone stairs his boots found were slick with substances he chose not to identify, and Copper’s claws clicked nervously on the ancient stonework, his fur bristling at every new sound. It seemed as if they’d already started the preliminary, showcase rounds.

Pools of monster gore and parts were being passed around by the masked throng, laughter ensuing as they painted themselves with it.

The cub’s [Predator’s Sense] overwhelmed him—too many threats, too much bloodlust, nowhere safe to rest his attention.

Easy, bud, Damon soothed, his own pulse oddly steady. This is different from the ambush. This is contained chaos—lawful evil made manifest beneath the shine of the good. We’re going to shatter it…

The amphitheater opened before them, and Damon couldn’t help but marvel at how far the scum of this city had fallen. His head was held high, refusing to shrink and let the jeers and names called at him from every race, elf to fairy to human—nightcrawler, dark ear, woman slaver, Spider lover.

That last one made his fingernails curl into his palm as the tattoo burned hot. He refused to show weakness in his path, though.

The elegant dwarven architecture remained, but it had been perverted into something Rome would have recognized but twisted through a lens of magical sadism. Recycled monster blood channels had been carved into the original stonework. It was neat, with efficient trenches that would direct the flow away from the fighting floor, where it might make footing treacherous.

Practical, even in their brutal theatrics… It’s even worse than I thought. I’m almost impressed at their imagination. But it’s only monsters, after all.

Betting boards covered every available wall, chalk calculations shifting by the second as odds rose and fell like tide charts. Numbers appeared and vanished—not just steel amounts but strange symbols, contract seals, even what looked like property deeds. The underground economy lay bare in chalk and fevered calculation.

Even with Grandfather’s banquet aboveground, some still found their way here. Maybe he can use this pit to identify who truly feeds it, since this is probably the most hyped monster fight pit they’ve had since their founding…

Noble boxes hung elevated above the common rabble, draped in silk that hid faces but not the eager lean of masked bodies, the way gloved hands gripped railings when something interesting happened below.

“The fallen prince graces us with his presence!”

The boss’ voice boomed across the space, and suddenly every eye turned toward them. The man stood on a raised platform, arms spread like a carnival barker who’d found his prize attraction.

“And with his pet bear, no less! Ladies and gentlemen—humans, elves, dwarves, gnomes, beastfolk, and everything in-between—our special event can truly begin!”

The crowd’s reaction hit in waves as some sort of projector showed his progress through the parting groups. He became the curious spectacle, disgust at his dark elf features, excitement at the prospect of non-monster flesh and blood in the ring.

Someone spat, the glob landing near his boot; it was symbolic, since spitting on a citizen was a crime. Someone else laughed. A woman’s voice, refined and drunk, called out something about “watching the Spider’s pet burn.”

Damon rubbed his wrist as the fire flared again, yet his eyes and steps never wavered.

Copper growled, confusion rippling through their bond—instinct begging to attack—but he stilled at Damon’s quiet pulse of calm. The fact he was controlling himself with the sheer wall of bloodlust pressed in from all sides was proof he’d chosen right.

It’s okay… We’ll beat them where it hurts them the most…the pocketbook. I’ve made some bad decisions, but I have no regrets, because I know our future is golden, despite the sorrow. We are not alone.

Titania’s summer warmth filled him; Aira’s playful pout sparked a ghost of a smile; Sophia’s poetic whisper—arms around his chest—held him steady.

He’d seen her. She waited for him.

“Come, come!” The boss gestured them forward, his grin too wide, too pleased. “You’ve caused quite the stir, my boy. Your little copper coin investment has grown into something…magnificent. We’ve never had a bigger pot than you on the ticket!”

Damon showed a half smirk. “Can’t say I’m surprised.”

“Oh yes.” The man’s eyes glinted. “Word spread about tonight’s special event. Titania’s dark elf prince, entering my arena? With his own bonded beast? The betting has been…”

He kissed his jeweled fingers and flexed them at him, showing the reflection of a storage space within where the pot was kept. “…Extraordinary. Your contribution has helped build a pot worthy of legend.”

He gestured to a board where the numbers were carved in chalk that seemed to glow with its own inner light:

GRAND POT: 2 SILVER, 55 COPPER

ADDITIONAL: 12 SERVITUDE CONTRACTS (PRE-PAID)

ESTIMATED VALUE: 300+ COPPER EQUIVALENT

Damon couldn’t help but laugh, casting his gaze around and seeing a box where one woman sat upon the railing—Ashcroft, snake-masked and showing a serpent’s smile.

Half of that, huh… One copper turns into one silver. A 150x profit… I’m going to have the biggest bounty on my head after this.

Showing a smirk in return, Damon snatched one of the wine cups off a serving staff’s tray in passing and lifted it. “Cheers. I couldn’t have asked for a better start.”

“Only if you win, boy!” The boss laughed and spread his arms wide. “Every monster owner in the city wants your corpse. Every shadow dealer, every merchant prince with a taste for the forbidden, every noble with debts to settle—they’ve all bought a piece of you. And, hey, if you do win, you could purchase a full mainstreet merchant’s establishment. Exciting?”

“Can’t wait…” His vision drifted to Ashcroft above as she tilted her head in an almost playful way, pointing a paper fan at an elevator box, draped with black silk. Copper didn’t miss the deadly serpent wrapped around her waist and neck, a low growl sounding. “Well, I should get ready to make you rich then, right?”

The boss followed his gaze with a short snicker. “Ah, your beast patron calls. Find me when you’re done plotting with the snake… By the way, you know she bet against you?”

The man laughed as if that was a nail in his coffin. Yet, he didn’t know the details of their contract—it only made sense to bet against him. In both cases, she got what she wanted. At least, she thought she did.

He proceeded to the elevator, Copper close to his side and preparing for a fight, though this was not a physical contest they were entering. The bulky guard by the machine pulled the silk aside, allowing them to rise to the woman’s box.

The noise cut magically as they entered some kind of noise-dampening enchantment on the box and he stepped forward to see the extravagant, well-stocked area.

Ashcroft sat like a queen among thieves—mask tilted, green eyes gleaming through the slits. Her serpent coiled lazily around her neck, tasting the air as if sampling the souls below.

“Lady Ashcroft. I didn’t expect you to dirty your shoes down here talking to me. Couldn’t this threaten your neutrality?”

Her fan fluttered once, slow and amused, vision only flicking to him for half a second before returning to the pits, where a slime fought a big, horned rabbit. “Oh, Damon. You should know better by now. Wherever profit and chaos dance, I never miss the tune… Mmm. I like that look in your eyes now. You’ve matured.”

He raised his stolen wine cup in mock salute. “All thanks to you. If I would have died, all your investment would have been wasted. You just wanted to know I had what it took before setting everything in motion. And I hear you bet against me.”

Her laughter, delicate and venomous, cut through the stillness. “I always bet with the house…” Her fan closed with a soft click, bright irises sliding back to him. “My dear prince, you mistake orchestration for neutrality. You should be thanking me for the useless rabble assassins I nipped off your long ears. Plus…I actually lost a bet on you. I don’t plan on losing another.”

Copper rumbled low beside him. The snake around her throat lifted its head, its hiss sounding more like taunting laughter than true threat.

Their gazes locked—predator to predator, investor to asset—each reading the other’s mask for weakness. Damon’s smirk deepened.

“Careful, Lady Ashcroft. When I win, our little contract might sting worse than your pet.”

Ashcroft leaned forward, lips curving behind her mask. “Then let’s both pray the bite draws blood. It keeps business interesting.”

A ripple of laughter stirred even through the silenced enchantment below as she reclined again, fanning herself lazily. “You’ve caught eyes that match your mother’s… Sometimes a woman’s heart is the poison that kills her.”

“Ominous riddles… We’ll see who comes out on top at the end of this game.”

Damon turned back toward the elevator and left the snake charmer to start this battle. He went straight for the orchestrator of this event. It was time to put his mouth where his heart was.

The roar of the pit returned all at once, reality snapping back as the elevator descended.

He glanced up at the many contracts of the boards, vision narrowing—some of those were probably worth more than the coin to her. Pre-paid means someone’s already served their time, but the ownership papers were still ‘on the surface’ willingly valid—legal slavery with a pretty bow.

I don’t know what Ashcroft plans from that, but that’s something we can discuss when this is over.

“Are we ready now?” the boss inquired as he turned to greet him. “That has to be the quickest conversation I’ve seen anyone have with Lady Ashcroft. In any case, I have something special prepared—ah, here she comes now.”

Movement from a side gate drew every eye, and Damon’s chest tightened as Cassy emerged into the torchlight. She wore a new maid outfit that looked tailor-made for her unique physiology, the perfect hole for her tail making the fabric hang elegantly, exposing more skin than modesty would typically allow due to her not being seen as human.

But it was her movement that made Damon’s gut twist—mechanical, perfect, empty.

She carried a massive weapon, one of the onlookers named as a chakram—something that immediately got boos and angry shouts.

The five-foot ring of blue-studded metal should have been impossible for a teenage girl to lift, yet she held it with the same indifference she might show an Earth hula hoop.

Behind her, Damon caught glimpses of others—servants in similarly crafted dresses, some human, some not, all wearing the same dead expression. The systematic nature of it turned his stomach, but he already knew the outcome.

He would win their freedom.

“Fishbreath!”

“Sea Monster!”

“Trash Eater!”

The crowd’s jeers rained down, but Cassy didn’t react. Didn’t flinch. Her blood-red eyes stayed fixed forward, seeing nothing, that same devastating emptiness Damon had witnessed in the corridors.

She’s not here, he realized. She’s retreated so far inside herself that this is just...meat moving through required motions.

“Observe!” The boss’ voice rang out as a gate cranked open. Two men dragged a dire wolf corpse into the arena, the beast’s dead weight leaving red smears across the stone. “The Trash Saintess will demonstrate her…unique talents.”

They dropped the carcass in the center of the floor and retreated hastily. Cassy moved forward with that same mechanical grace, setting her chakram aside—only her finger flinched when leaving it.

He saw her discomfort, not at leaving it, but for the weapon itself, before her indifference returned. She took a breath and knelt beside the wolf, and for a moment, nothing happened.

Then her jaw unhinged.

Not dislocated—transformed. The biological impossibility of it made several crowd members gasp. Her mouth opened wider than any human structure should allow, cheeks splitting.

The wolf didn’t just disappear—it liquefied, flesh and bone and fur condensing into streams of matter that flowed through the air into her waiting maw. Hundreds of pounds of dead flesh vanished in seconds, leaving only bloodstains on sand to prove it had existed at all.

Half the crowd roared in awe. The rest screamed in horror. But Cassy simply stood, wiped the corner of her lips with a faint, graceful motion—and retrieved the chakram, though her nose crinkled as if the touch of it sickened her.

No emotion. No triumph. Only compliance.

And then—there. A glimmer. A flicker of moisture she blinked away before anyone else saw. A tremor in her fingers, so small it could have been a ghost of memory.

She’s still in there.

Drowning. But not gone.

Copper whined beside him, rising on his hind legs to peer over the railing. Through their bond, Damon felt it—the cub’s distress. Not fear of the monster below, but sorrow. A need to help.

“Magnificent, isn’t she?” The boss’ voice carried over the crowd. “Our Trash Saintess has finally found her purpose! And she’s ready to serve since she requires an enormous amount of food to not be in constant pain. I’m her savior!”

The word ‘savior’ hit Damon like ice water, and before he could stop himself, the question emerged that transferred through the boss’ spelled, wooden microphone: “Is the Trash Saintess part of the Round 1 prize pool? I heard her contract was a part of the bet.”

Silence.

Complete, absolute silence as several hundred people processed what he’d just asked.

Even Cassy’s eyes flickered, the first real sign of emotion—confusion mixing with something else. Caution. Distrust.

Above, Damon felt Ashcroft’s gaze sharpen. Her fan stilled mid-motion, the serpent at her neck lifting its head to taste the tension that now coiled through the air.

He smiled—not cold, but quietly reflective, carrying the words his auburn-haired girlfriend once murmured in half-sleeped poetry.

“Simple,” he began, his voice calm against the silence. “I have a hawk who flies above my field. She’s calling out for justice. She doesn’t understand why the old crow steals… Children see their parents struggle—but what is harder still? The days when they go hungry, because I’m fighting for what is real.”

He paused, letting the weight settle.

“Mercy is the burden of the poor.” The words rang clear, cutting through the crowd like steel through frost. His gaze swept the rows of masked nobles, Cassy’s tail swipes stilling as she stared at him. “The powerful…” —his tone dropped, steady as a heartbeat— “…they keep coming back for more.”

He lifted his half-drained cup toward Ashcroft’s shadowed box, a mock toast that felt more sermon than salute. He drank, set the cup aside, and whispered the final truth that didn’t need amplification; the stillness carried it.

“Only love can give what vengeance cannot cure… So again—mercy is the burden of the poor.”

He let the silence stretch, then added softly, “Tell me…do you think the Holy Emperor would agree? Show mercy.”

For a long breath, no one moved. The words hung in the torchlight, turning the air heavy and electric as he turned their ethical structure inward. Then, like ripples across still water, the murmurs began—some unsettled, some enthralled. Even the boss hesitated, his grin faltering as the moment’s gravity outshone his spectacle.

And then—he laughed. Too loud. Too sharp.

“Well!” he said, voice booming through the spelled mic, theatrical to the point of parody. “The fallen prince has a Bard’s tongue and a Priest’s arrogance! Mercy, is it? Love? Ah, but love’s no use without a little entertainment.”

He turned in a slow circle, arms wide to the crowd. “But for the right spectacle, I could be convinced!”

The tension broke like glass. The crowd roared.

The boss fed on it. “What say you, my fine citizens? Should our dark elf be allowed to claim the Trash Saintess if he survives? Shall we see if Titania’s chosen can protect our little shark—see if the Holy Emperor truly shows mercy upon this immortal, poor seafolk shark?”

The amphitheater erupted—bloodlust and mockery blending into one. Someone shouted, “Spider-lover wants his sea-trash bride!” and the laughter that followed was almost hysterical, the crowd thrilled by its own cruelty.

Damon merely silently observed the dehumanization of people who had lost the meaning of good in their lawful hatred and greed.

“Very well!” The boss’ grin stretched wide again, confidence reborn in spectacle. “But conditions apply! Full gauntlet—four rounds, no backing out. Death means all contracts return to the house.”

He turned his glittering eyes toward Damon. “And you, my boy, get one pet per round, excluding the last, of course, against the Pit Champion—don’t think I don’t know what you’re planning with that shark.”

His tone softened into mock civility. “Oh, and since you’re a Unity Tamer, the bond requires consent. She’ll have to agree to support you… Can your bardic words melt the shark’s fanged heart?”

He gestured grandly toward the center of the ring. “Take a moment. Negotiate with your…potential prize, because, if you lose…you die, monster prince.”

Without a word, Damon descended into the nearby hallways to the toxic jeers of the crowd about a fish bride and mutant children like a crown. Copper walked beside him, head high, size already swelling beyond his usual frame, responding to the venom in their voices.

But nothing could curb the warm, joyful hearth his soul basked in from Titania’s embrace.

Before long, Damon found himself walking through the blood-soaked sands to stand before Cassy in a pocket of relative quiet at its gaping mouth. The crowd’s attention momentarily went elsewhere as odds recalculated on the boards and they hyped up the first monster he’d face.

The saintess stood perfectly still, that mechanical grace holding her in place like a puppet waiting for strings to pull. He couldn’t see it physically, yet just her eyes told him how much she’d bled and died for others, rising every time to serve from some unknown code of honor.

Copper moved forward to sniff her ankles.

“Cassy,” he said quietly, pitched for her ears alone.

Her eyes shifted to his—dead as glass. “Master.” The word came out flat, her refined accent making it somehow worse. “How may this one serve?”

“Please…don’t.” The command came soft but firm. He studied the girl—about his little sister’s age—and for a heartbeat, he almost saw her instead. He forced the image away, focusing on the young woman in front of him. “I have a divine mission from Titania to save you. What you do afterward is your choice—but you’re welcome to join me.”

Something flickered in those crimson eyes, darting to Copper before returning to him. Not hope—that would’ve been too much—but awareness. Her fingers twitched, just barely, but Damon caught it.

“My apologies, Master, but I am duty- and contract-bound to address you as such. Still, it gives me…relief to hear of such a mission.”

Her voice remained flat, yet there was an edge to it—a blade hidden in silk. Her tail coiled around her front, a polite gesture that felt more like armor. “You seek to save trash? Your goddess has poor taste, Master.”

“Maybe she does,” Damon said with a quiet laugh, rubbing the back of his neck. “I mean, she chose me, didn’t she? But I think the joy of seeing a teenage girl smile again is worth more than anything I could ever do with these hands.”

His gaze softened. “Are you okay?”

She studied him for a long moment, and he could almost see the calculations behind those dead eyes. The weighing of lies and truth, the measurement of whether this was just another game, another disappointment. Her tail shifted—not the mechanical movement of before, but something unconscious, uncertain.

“You indeed have the silver tongue of a Bard, Master. Your joy is mine. What you command, I follow. Name your desire and I will fulfill your every dream.”

Damon exhaled slowly, planting his hands on his hips as the breath whistled out between his teeth before a small, pained laugh escaped.

“This…is going to be tougher than I thought.”

He steadied himself, voice lowering to something almost prayerful. “Then I’ll let my actions speak. You don’t have to trust me yet—that’s earned, not asked for. I can’t pull you out of your pain, not after how many times your heart’s been deceived. But however tonight ends…you’ll be free. I promise that, in Titania’s name.”

He flashed a faint grin and extended his hand. “Allow me to prove it, Cassy. With me, you’re safe…and I hope, someday, I can be safe with you. I trust you.”

“You cannot stop this.” Her voice was soft and certain, the kind of statement that had outlived hope. “This is how the world breathes. The strong eat, and the law swallows those outside its grace. I am outside its grace… I am cursed, Master. You would be well to abandon that quest.”

“Well, that’s the thing about divine quests, right?” Damon said, quiet but steady, keeping his hand extended. “Cursed or not. I’ll break that curse for you…because it will help me get back to the woman I love. So, if you can’t see the light in front of you, let me be the oil in your lamp… Cassy, I need you.”

Her head tilted—the first small, genuine motion he’d seen. “And what do you want in return…Master?”

“Whatever you’re comfortable giving,” he said softly. “Whatever feels right to your heart. If that means cheering from the sidelines, fighting by my side, or even cursing my name from afar…then so be it.”

His eyes flicked toward the massive chakram beside her before returning to her face. “Just be you, Cassy. Nothing more. I only want to see you smile again…like my little sister smiles. As troublesome and an adorkable loser she is,” he said with a strained laugh. “I just want to know the real you.”

He reached forward slowly. Her tail quivered—instinct, not threat—and he froze, meeting her crimson gaze. His voice trembled, but not from fear.

“And if not now…I can wait until you’re ready. Just find the tiniest seed in your heart and let it work on you. You’re safe in my hands, Cassy. No one will touch you again. Believe in me. You have a future. You can be happy again. Just find that spark that lets you believe it.”

Silence stretched between them, broken only by the distant roar of the crowd. She stared at his outstretched hand as if it were an impossible equation—something the laws of her world said shouldn’t exist.

“You would wager your life for a stranger’s curse?” Her tone trembled between disbelief and something softer—curiosity, maybe. “Do you think words can unmake the world, Master?”

“Words are empty,” Damon said, his voice gentle but steady. “I don’t expect you to believe them. I’ll let my actions speak—because faith without action is theater.” His grin came small and self-deprecating. “You be the judge, then. I’m on trial in your court, and I hope to earn your extended hand…”

He gave a low laugh, bowing slightly. “So, until then—consider yourself my executioner, Mistress. Just…don’t be too harsh on me. I’ve got a heart of clay behind all this bravado.”

For a heartbeat, nothing moved. Then—barely perceptible—her tail loosened, the tight coil easing an inch.

“You speak like a poet condemned to his own verse,” she murmured, her voice quieter now, less rehearsed. “Clay hearts crack, Master. They don’t survive the fire.”

Damon’s smile curved, not mocking but soft. “Sometimes they do. The heat doesn’t just break them—it bakes them. Turns the cracks into lines of strength. When the ash clears, what’s left isn’t shattered—it’s glazed. Beautiful.”

Her eyes flickered, the faintest tremor crossing the perfect calm of her mask. For one fragile breath, she almost looked alive again.

She looked away first, gaze falling to the sands. “You really must be a prince, Master.”

Before he could answer, the boss’ voice thundered through the arena:

“Time’s up! Let the games begin! Get out here, Former Prince Damon. It’s time to face the monsters!”

The spell broke. The crowd’s roar came crashing back, shaking the floor and the air. Damon drew in a slow breath, feeling the heat of the torches and the cool pulse of Titania’s blessing steadying his heart.

Copper pressed forward, brushing against Cassy’s leg. The small bear tilted his head, sniffed her hand once, then nudged it—soft, insistent.

Cassy froze at the unexpected touch. Then, slowly, she lowered herself, maid’s dress fanning atop the blood-soaked sand. Her movements were no longer mechanical, but uncertain, almost human. Her pale fingers found Copper’s ears, rubbing them once, as if testing the memory of kindness.

Her gaze lifted to Damon again. Something unspoken passed between them—acknowledgment, not belief. A faint shimmer in the crimson of her eyes.

“Then burn, if you must,” she whispered, her hand lingering on Copper’s fur. “I will watch…and see what becomes of this seed you’ve planted.”

The crowd screamed for blood. Damon turned toward the gates as they rumbled open, heart blooming with warmth.

“Then watch…because, with those words, I’ve already won.”

Above, the lights blinded.

And in that moment, as Cassy stood with her hand still on the cub, the faintest curve ghosted across her lips at his confidence. Not a smile, but the memory of one.

Now, it was time to face four rounds of Hell.

And he welcomed it.
