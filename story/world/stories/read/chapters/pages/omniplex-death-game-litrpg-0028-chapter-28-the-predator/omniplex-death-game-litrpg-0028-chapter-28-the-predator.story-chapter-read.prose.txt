The note was signed by M.B. Someone other than the Arbiter was watching over him and this person was trying to keep himself hidden.

Ciro looked over the note for only a second before Luka snatched it away. “Who’s M.B?” he asked. He peered at the note, assessing every inch of the page.

“Maybe it’s the Arbiter person.” One of the twins suggested.

“No.” Luka shook his head. “His name was Palou. At least, that’s what he called himself. What do you think, Ciro?”

Ciro shrugged his shoulders. He was lying to them. About the messages, about why there was a briefcase in the building. It wasn’t a matter of trust, but more so because he didn’t want to be perceived as some unknown. Being different came at a cost. He had grown a sense of comfort being with his new group, so different to the constant tension, while being with Carina and Alya.

“So, what is it inside?” The twins asked from behind, still too scared to look.

Luka put a firm hand on Ciro’s shoulder. “Do you have any guesses what they are?”“Not a clue.”

Running his hands against the edges of the open briefcase, eight syringes were neatly arranged in a row. Inside shimmered a vibrant orange liquid that swirled with silvery particles that swam. The liquid pulsed faintly. It felt alive.

Ciro’s pulse quickened. His body felt a strange attachment to each syringe. He fought back the urge to grab one and…do what, exactly? He wasn’t quite sure. Ciro heard Luka swallow hard. His eyes seemed fixated on the contents of the case as much as him.

He did not know what these substances were, but they seemed to have some sort of effect on them. Drawing them in with the allure of something greater.

“Their steroids.” Luka said, cutting away the tension with his gruff voice. He grabbed one syringe, holding it up in the air to examine. “I don’t know why. I’m fighting every urge just to shoot one of these through my arm.”

So it’s not just me, thought Ciro. As he watched Luka swirl the liquid under his fingertips, he wondered what the purpose of those syringes was. The messages promised power. Those syringes were the gateway. According to M.B anyway.

“You know a lot about steroids, don’t you, boss?”

“Yeah, that’s how you got so big.” The other twin chimed.

Letting out a grunt, Luka put the syringe back in the briefcase before punching both twins on the head. “I don’t use steroids. Not anymore. Not since my balls—”

All four men stood incredibly still. Each one of them focused. Ciro looked towards his mini-map. It pulsated and pulsated. A steady rhythm, but it was too fast. Completely different from before. A red dot suddenly appeared.

His heart pounded as the dot moved on his mini-map. It was moving fast, too fast. It was heading straight for them.

Luka sighed, walking over to one of the conveyor belts where he saw several metal poles haphazardly placed. He picked one up and looked towards Ciro and the twins. “Only with strength will we survive.”The twins’ legs shook with fear, but they headed towards Luka. They each picked up a pole and stood by him. They looked at Ciro expectantly.

Ciro’s hand trailed into his pocket, where he kept the knife the other guy took. He took a deep breath, moving over towards the conveyor belt and grabbing a pole for himself. It was about the size of his torso. It felt heavy under his arm. He peered at the tips, which were completely blunt. A weapon fit for bludgeoning rather than precise murder.

“I want some Juja juice, if we live.” whispered Ciro.

Luka chuckled at his remark. “Aye, liquor only tastes better when you’ve escaped death.”

Ivo and Birk exchanged uneasy glances. Ciro appreciated their lean and agile builds. They would surely be useful.

“So what’s the plan—”

Before Ciro could ask, a noise rumbled from above. It sounded like a war drum. The ceiling itself shook in despair. “I think it’s on the roof.” Ciro’s breath slowed. All four men watched the ceiling tremble. Ciro looked at the mini-map for only a second. His green arrow was completely engulfed by red.

Whatever was above them, it was trying to get in. It pounded against the surface. The old factory wasn’t brittle, not so easy to surrender. It withstood the constant barrage. Ciro thought they were safe.

How wrong he was.

The ceiling let out a deep metallic groan of defeat.

A deafening crash followed.

The factory roof buckled and collapsed. Metal and debris exploded down as something massive tore through the building, landing on its feet, a cloud of dust and broken steel surrounding it.

The creature rose to its feet, shaking off the wreckage like a dog. Ciro used his arms to protect his eyes from the sudden barrage of dust and debris. Through his limited vision, he could still make out the outlines of the creature. It was just about humanoid in shape, but that all changed when it hunched down on all fours.

It wore gray skin stretched tight over its muscular frame. Ribs and overgrown flesh protruded out of its body. Its mouth seemed more like a muzzle the way it jutted forward. Its long narrow head shifted unnaturally, like its neck was already broken. A pair of glowing red eyes focused on Ciro.

Sharp fangs that shot out from its mouth twitched as it inhaled deeply, its snout flaring. It growled at them. A deep, guttural growl.

Luka rushed in. Ciro’s eyes widened. He wanted to scream, to tell him to stop. That they needed a plan. But it was too late.

“Watch my back!” he yelled, his body a juggernaut. He swung his pole, arcing towards the creature’s skull. The attack reverberated across the factory. The creature didn’t duck. It stood still, completely unphased. The pole wrapped itself in the shape of the creature’s head.

Ciro couldn’t register the creature’s movement before it retaliated. It twisted, its claws swiped the air, raking Lukasz across the chest. The big grunted but didn’t slow, swinging the bent pole once again. This time, he connected. The creature took a single step back from the blow.

It paused for a moment. Its eyes shook with disbelief. It roared once again in anger. It charged towards him. Luka threw away the broken pole, opting to use his fists. The monster drew closer. Luka pivoted, launching a hook that sent the creature skidding away. Its body hit a conveyer belt, and the equipment crumbled to dust.

The creature wasn’t done. It got back, shaking off the soot and dirt. Luka towards Ciro and the twins, who watched from behind. “You gonna help?” he yelled.

Ivo and Birk bursted forward, Luka’s word giving them all the boost they needed. The twins attacked from both sides, swinging their poles wildly. They attacked low, aiming to sweep the creature off its feet. A snarl erupted from the creature as it staggered once again.

Realizing this was the kind of moment Ciro was waiting for, he charged as well. Swinging it like a baseball bat, he aimed for its head. The creature narrowly dodged, its movements swift despite its size. The pole narrowly brushed the beast’s shoulder. It let out a snarl, as if to mock Ciro’s pitiful attack.

The creature’s eyes snapped away from Ciro, shifting instead to one of the twins. An easier target, maybe. With terrifying speed, the creature lunged at him, its claws slashed at his head. His brother noticed the danger, so he launched himself, tackling his brother away from danger.

“No Ivo!” Birk screamed. Ciro held his breath. Birk was too slow. Blood erupted from where Ivo’s face should’ve been. The twins rolled on the floor, and a trail of blood followed them. In the spot Ivo was standing, the monster clawed at the ground. Commemorating its kill.

“Ivo!” screeched Birk. He turned towards the creature, his eyes filled with an intensity that made even Ciro feel uneasy. Birk picked up his pole, gnashing his teeth. “You’re going to pay for that.”

Just as he was about to move, Luka stepped in front of him. “No, look after your brother.”

“What? I can’t just let that thing get away—”

“Get your brother out of here. Now!” Luka screamed. “I can kill it. You’ll just get in the way.”

Birk hesitated. He looked towards Ciro, then to his brother, whose face was unrecognizable. “Look after the boss,” Birk called out to him, tears streaming down his face. He picked up his brother, using his shoulder as support. He ran as quickly as he could.

“Please don’t die.” His words echoed as he tried to leave the factory.

The creature could see the twins retreating. It couldn’t let easy prey go. It went against its instincts. It tried to give chase, only for Luka’s hulking body to come between them. His rippling muscles bounced the creature back to his original spot.

Luka turned to Ciro. “Help me out from the back. If things look dangerous, you run too. I don’t need help.”

Ciro didn’t speak. He couldn’t. All he could do was nod, stepping back behind Luka. The beast shifted its attention. He couldn’t have the twins, so Ciro became the next best thing. Luka stepped towards the creature, not letting it move an inch. “You may be a predator, but even prey can bare its teeth when it’s cornered.”

The beast roared in reply.

They charged at each other. Luka’s boots pounded against the factor floor. His massive arms shot out, colliding with the creature in an impact that shook the room. The force dropped Ciro off his feet.

He watched their battle in awe.

The beast snarled, swiping at Luka desperately. It’s human-like hands grew out claws rather than nails. Blood dripped down Luka’s torso, deep scratches forming across his body, but he remained steadfast. Ignoring the pain, he locked his arms around the monster’s torso, his muscles bulged, veins straining as he wrestled it.

Luka tried to crush it, using his pure strength. But the thing was strong.

Too strong.

The beast shook Luka off. One simple jerk threw him into air, his body crashing into a workbench. He let out a pained groan. The beast lunged for Luka, its eyes gleaming, its win so clearly in sight. Its jaw snapped inches away from his face, revealing rows of jagged teeth.

Luka rolled, dodging just in time. He swung a punch aimed directly at the creature's ribs, that bulged out of its body. The impact echoed across the factory, but the creature didn’t flinch. It stuck out its tongue, drool dripping from its mouth.

Luka scrunched his face. “Shit.”

The creature opened its mouth before clamping onto Luka’s shoulder. Blood exploded. Luka staggered but didn’t fall. Clenching his fist, he grabbed the creature by the arms so it couldn’t attack.

The two behemoths caught themselves in a standstill, their feet grinding against the floor. Luka stared at the creature, daring to release its grip on his shoulder.

The monster wasn’t scared. It had him. He closed his jaw tighter, sinking its teeth deeper into Luka’s flesh.

He looked towards Ciro, not even flinching as the creature ate at his flesh. “Go!” he barked, his voice strained. “You have to go! I can’t—” The creature finally released him. A huge chunk of Luka’s shoulder completely disappeared, replaced with jagged, torn flesh and blood that flowed like a river.

Luka held his shoulder, his eyes still on Ciro. “I got him, just go!”

Ciro couldn’t move. He couldn’t accept another man losing his life to protect his own. He wouldn’t be able to live with the shame. Ciro respected Luka. He appreciated his strength and in the small time, they’d known each other. Ciro had been given hope.

Who knew being told to just do some push-ups would lead him to do the unimaginable?

He ran towards the briefcase that remained by the corner. He opened the latch once again, revealing the syringes, the orange contents called out to him. Sweat poured down his forehead as he pulled one out. He ignored Luka’s screams as he continued to battle the creature to a bitter end.

Ciro turned to see Luka mounted on top of the beast, his own teeth sunk into the creature’s flesh. He bit down only for a second before he was thrown off and sent catapulting in front of Ciro. The creature huffed, stepping towards the two of them.

“I told you to leave,” Luka wheezed, crumpled on the floor.

Ciro stepped in front of Luka’s bloody form, syringe in hand. Facing the beast face to face. “You’re an idiot, muscle head, y’know.” Ciro let out a sigh. “Sometimes you have to ask someone to spot you,” he smiled

Luka watched on, blood gushing out from every corner of his body, with scratches and deep red wounds that left his gray suit stained. He smiled at Ciro. “Show it hell.”

His head hit the floor.

Ciro took a shaky breath. The creature, recognizing there was only one left, walked leisurely towards him. It wore an ugly grin, its claws scraped against the floor as it edged closer.

Ciro’s fingers wrapped around the orange syringe.

Desperation burned in his veins.

He had no clue what it did.

But he was out of options.

He pressed the needle against his arm, plunging it in.

The liquid burned through his veins.

Ciro let out a sharp gasp as his body seized. His vision went white hot. His muscles screamed like they were being set on fire.

For a moment, he thought he was dying.

Then—

Nothing happened.

Ciro didn’t feel like he gained anything. No magical power up or some new hidden ability.

Nothing changed.

He was going to die.
