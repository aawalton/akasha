I recognized the name immediately. I’d only heard it a few times, and only seen the Fae in question once, but it had been enough to make an impression.

Too-sharp teeth and a face of cruel beauty. One that had watched with nothing save delighted hunger as I lay bleeding on an ivory balcony.

“She’s pacted.” I breathed it even as the woman’s shadow started to twist. Its arms shivered like twin, writhing snakes along the pavement.

Jareth, seemingly wary even before my murmur, let my bundle of goods fall and dropped one hand to the hilt of his sword. He shifted with the motion, stepping in front of me. “And what message would that be?”

“Lord Embriel of the Endless Pit requests your presence at a celebration.” She tittered a little laugh. “He’s hosting a party tomorrow night, when the moon is at its zenith. He has fourteen Thalessian guests already, though he regrets to say that some have gone quite cold.”

His sword flashed, drawn with a hiss, and she waggled a finger.

“Patience is a virtue, sir knight! I haven’t said where, yet.”

He strode forwards. “You will.”

Rather than back away, the woman giggled again and snapped her fingers.

A scream echoed down the street. I turned to see a man falling to his knees, hands clutched at his stomach. Blood spilled down his trousers.

Another man stood before him. Tall, with an unremarkable face and holding no weapon. His arm moved, slashing crosswise, and a stream of black upon the stones shot to his hand. A shadow in awful, razor-sharp form; a blade of writhing dark. Before I could even start to scream the kneeling figure lifted both hands to his throat, futilely holding back a gush of blood.

The street erupted. People screamed. Some ran. Some stood and pointed. A few stood still, watching the scene with obvious glee, and I watched more shadows writhe beneath them. A heartbeat later they began to lunge, hands bearing dark blades, earning fresh cries and fresh blood.

The grinning man ran to the next closest person; a youth with unkempt hair and dirty cheeks. His hand was empty, but the dark pooled again as he lashed out.

Sillar. My next rune; patterned tendrils of lacing threads. It throbbed at me, demanding use, bringing my hand up without conscious thought, but I hesitated. I didn’t know what it did! Veth would hurt innocents, faer had nearly killed me, Velidan had summoned a monster… I ran for the pair, cursing my uncertainty and reaching for my sword instead of my magic.

Behind me metal rang alongside the bell-like call of, “The barrow in the northern hills, sir knight!”

The man’s victim staggered, clutching a bloody shoulder, as a solid wall of muscle and blonde hair hurtled past. Royce’s sword was out. Both hands were white around the hilt and his face was twisted in a terrified rictus.

The pacted figure looked thrilled to have a foe. Royce swung down. It was a graceless blow, but heavy with the weight of his considerable strength behind it. Writhing dark flicked up, and my friend’s blade was cut clear in half. Metal rang as the sword’s front end crashed against stone.

My heart leapt into my throat. Royce stared for the barest breath, clearly aghast, and then a fierce resolve overtook his features. He dropped the broken sword, grabbed the smaller man’s wrist, and slammed one fist forwards. It met the man’s face with a crack. His head snapped back. He stumbled, no longer grinning. The shadow fell and with another crack he went backwards into a table lined with vegetables, herbs, and pouches of seeds. Food went flying and wood crashed to the ground as the structure, and the unconscious figure draped atop it, collapsed.

My friend stood panting, looking utterly shocked. He didn’t even seem to see me as I rushed past.

My knees slammed against stone. The impact shot straight to my hips, but my hands were steady as they turned the fallen youth over.

He was white as milk, but breathing. Brown eyes open and glassy, mouth ajar, pulse thrumming in his throat. Red poured from a wide gash across his collarbone, ending at the shoulder. I said things that didn’t make much sense- ‘you’re safe now,’ ‘I’m right here,’ ‘it’s not really so bad’- but my body knew what to do even while my mouth panicked.

I drew Renner’s knife. A few quick cuts bared the wound. Bad. Deep, bad spot, can’t bind it. Bad. Pack it. I lunged towards an abandoned stall filled with woven garments, grabbed an armful, and hurtled back. Royce was bent over the unconscious man. All sounds of combat had died, replaced only with weeping, screams, and boots crashing atop stone.

“This is going to hurt,” I gasped, bracing one hand against the youth’s chest. He didn’t seem to hear me, but moaned as I gently spread the torn skin.

Teela appeared beside me. “What can I do?”

“Feed me cloth.” She started to ask for clarification, but quickly caught on as I eased thick cotton fabric into the open wound. It was a grisly task, and halfway through the bleeding child began to shake. Teela helped keep him still as I packed the wound, stemming the red tide, then propped a forearm on top. He wailed at that and I sobbed apologies, begging him to do his best to hold still. After several agonizing seconds, he fell into merciful unconsciousness.

It felt as though a heavy fog had been lifted and, steady with the knowledge that he was breathing and would likely stay that way for a while, I lifted my head to take stock of the chaos. Watch soldiers had swarmed the streets. People were being gathered in small groups along the stones. Some of them were weeping. Several were bloody. Some were unmoving in the ground. Men and women I recognized as clerics by their garb and supplies rushed to and fro. Several gently pulled me from the youth. I showed them the wound and my work on it, but didn’t stop to hear their responses.

Jareth and Royce were stooped over the pacted man. When Jareth straightened, he had the figure draped over one shoulder. The man’s hands and feet were bound with rope from one of the covered stalls.

I hurried to them, Teela at my side, looking for injuries. Royce’s knuckles were bloody and Jareth had a small red cut above one brow, but that was all. My breath rushed out and I nearly collapsed.

Jareth’s steady calm did not waver. He carried his hostage to the nearest guard, gave a report, and told us to follow him.

I paused and, with wooden limbs, gathered my fallen things. Teela and Royce did the same. My eyes strayed to the fallen table, to the spoiled bounty around it, and after a wild moment’s thought I hurried over. Shaking fingers scrabbled against the stones, grabbing at bits of the ruined food.

When my trouser pockets were filled I left a gold coin amidst the mess, then ran to catch up with my friends.

No one said much. People along the streets gaped and asked questions. Jareth’s clear voice boomed answers, reassurances, and instructions for people to return to their homes and places of business. Royce flexed his bloody hand while he walked. Teela skipped along between the three of us, seemingly unsure of what to do with herself.

“That’s the Deeproot,” Jareth said at last. He adjusted the unconscious man and pointed to a two-story wooden building up the road. Unwittingly cheerful sunlight sliced across the green roof and shuttered windows. Several hens clucked happily in a wired pen near the door. “I’m taking this one to the barracks for holding. You all should head in, tell Dame what happened. I’ll catch up.”

The hens were skeptical of us until Teela snatched a handful of ferns from the roadside and tossed then into the pen. They rushed to investigate the fresh meal, feathers flapping and voices warbling. Several scraped at the dirt or pecked against the wire near her feet.

She observed them, then gave me a tiny, crooked smile. “It’s the little things, y’know?”

I couldn’t quite find my voice to answer, but I felt quite certain she was right.

Royce held the door and we filed in. It was well past lunch and the inn was empty save for Renner and Joselia, who sat chuckling over drinks in one corner. Their mirth died immediately.

“Are you hurt?” Her tone snapped back to that crisp, clear command.

I blinked as they hurried over. Royce’s knuckles barely held a few drops of blood along the scraped skin, and Teela was perfectly fine. Then Renner was practically toe to toe with me, unfurling my hands with his own, and I realized the question had been directed towards me.

I stretched my stained fingers out, wincing at the sight of skin smeared to the wrists with dried blood. “It’s not mine.”

“Where’s Jareth?” Joselia asked.

“He’s fine. He’s at the barracks.” Royce’s voice sounded strained, and a quick glance over showed him looking studiously away from me. His jaw was clenched.

“There was an attack in the marketplace. Pacted people all over the streets with these awful shadowy… ashes. I haven’t seen anything that bad since Respite.” Teela sank into the nearest chair. She dragged both hands upwards, snaking long fingers into choppy red locks, and relayed the tale.

While she spoke I went to the innkeeper, apologized for my mess, and requested a bucket of water and a rag. She looked me over, fussed for a moment, then brought the items alongside mugs of tea and eagerly pricked ears. By the time Teela finished I had gotten cleaned up and the five of us were sitting around a wooden table, sipping at steaming mugs.

“Everyone alright?” Jareth stretched as he came in. Joints popped alongside the groan of metal and he gave a little grimace. I did, too, imagining what his back must have felt like after toting plate armor and a full-grown man halfway through the city.

“Better than we could be,” Royce muttered. He looked at Teela. “You said things like that have happened in Respite?”

She winced. “I mean, not exactly like that. But… attacks inside the city walls? Yeah.”

“Not just Respite, lately. And now we’ve a second threat lain at our feet,” Joselia said gravely. “I fear this city is indeed the centerpoint of something foul, though I’ve no idea what.” She tapped a finger against her drink, frowning thoughtfully. “The others should be reporting back soon. I think the wisest course of action is to compare notes, alert the city officials, and send scouts. Perhaps they can find some trail or clue as to what these people have been doing. To the north, she said?”

“The barrow in the northern hills.” Jareth paced before the table. “Her exact words before she fled. I gave chase, but…” he shook his head, directing a glower towards the floorboards.

Joselia’s lips thinned. “Perhaps our captive will provide more information. Willingly, I pray.”

I shivered, wondering what exactly would happen if the pacted man proved unwilling. Will they torture him? Cut him, break his bones, all the things Renner said? My gaze drifted between the two Thalessians. They wouldn’t. Surely they wouldn’t.

“Perhaps,” the younger knight intoned. Then he paused midstep and leveled a wry smile towards the table. “Speaking of the captive, if any of you ever want to join the Order, I’ll vouch for you.”

“Hah! Imagine me in armor.” Teela wrinkled her nose over a sip of tea. “And it’s not like I even did anything.”

“You most certainly did. All of you did.” Those deep blue eyes swiveled between us. They were unusually solemn. “Because of you three, a murderer is in chains and an innocent still lives. You should be proud of yourselves.”

“Pretty sure I was supposed to use the sword to get him,” Royce muttered, flexing his skinned knuckles. “Not get it broken.”

“Swords aren’t always enough against magic, and you use whatever works.” Jareth snorted. “Clearly, it worked.”

My friend couldn’t quite seem to find an answer to that.

Joselia slapped both hands down atop the table and rose. “It does indeed sound like you acted admirably. We’re in your debt, and I’ve covered your stay as a thank-you. Four rooms are already paid for.”

I shook my head. “Please, I have plenty of coin. I can-”

“It’s a matter of principle, my lady.” She gave me a tight smile as she shouldered her pack. “Now, I believe I’d like to see this prisoner for myself. Do the three of you plan to stay here for the next few hours?”

“Yes.” I cleared my throat. “Well, I do, at least.”

Renner examined me over his cup’s rim. “Still gonna take a nap?”

“What?” Royce’s mug fell with a clatter and a splash. Liquid pooled along the table and steam curled up between us. At my hesitation, his tone hardened. “You’re going to take a nap?”

I bit my lower lip. “I… just to clear my head, Royce. It’s noth-”

“Are you insane?!” He lurched to his feet so quickly the table rattled. “After this morning?!”

“Well… yes.” My shoulders curled beneath his obvious anger. “Just… I’m tired. I want to-”

“Take a nap. Instead of, I don’t know, Brin, maybe talking to people?” He slashed a hand towards the clearly surprised Thalessians. “Helping, maybe?”

That stung, but I tried not to let it show. “Royce, let’s go talk upstairs.” I stood, wrapping a hand over his forearm.

He looked incredulous. “No. What’s… last night you said-”

“Why don’t you take a walk, farmboy?” Renner’s face was stony.

My friend rounded on him. “Why don’t you back off? Your job’s done, right? So you don’t get a say in… in anything.” He gestured towards me, clearly struggling to hold his tongue.

“And you do?” The smaller man stood. His lip curled in a sneer and his voice became knifelike. “Seems to me like only one of us was asked to be here.”

I felt the muscles of Royce’s arm grow taut and, before he could respond, shifted to stand between them. “Would you both stop? Saints, as if last night wasn’t enough!”

“You’re telling me,” Teela muttered from her seat.

Royce’s face turned red. “This isn’t about last night! Brin, people died earlier! You can’t just-”

“Take. A. Walk.” Renner shouldered me aside, glaring upwards.

“Oi!” The innkeeper’s shout made me jump. “Take this outside, you lot! I’ll have no fighting in my establishment!”

“Nor will I, good lady.” Joselia’s voice was mild. She was watching both men carefully, green eyes sharp and assessing. “The two of you have had a trying few days. Perhaps some fresh air would be wise.” Neither of them moved and she added, very calmly, “I am quite capable of disarming, incapacitating, and detaining both of you. I’d really rather not, but if you don’t stand down then that’s what’s going to happen.”

Renner stepped back, though his expression didn’t change.

Royce’s shoulders sagged. “Brin-”

“Can we please go upstairs?” I slid between them again, facing my friend fully. It wasn’t just anger written across his face, it was hurt and confusion and… my chest tightened. “Please?”

His throat bobbed. Blue eyes flicked to Renner, then to Joselia, then back to me. “I… no. No, I think I’m done walking into your bedroom for a while.”

I flinched. As surely as if he’d hit me.

He flinched, too. Then he looked at me, shaking his head sadly, before heading for the door. He left without another word, leaving the rest of us in awkward, uneasy silence.

Teela was the first to speak. “Alright, the butting horns thing is getting old pretty quick. I guess we could start placing bets. Put down coin on which one of them snaps first.”

Renner snorted.

I scrubbed my face, swallowing the fresh tide of frustration, and shot him an unhappy look. “That’s what ‘getting along’ looks like?”

“I never said I would.” His brows rose. “And he started it.”

“He’s allowed to be upset!” I threw both hands skyward. “I told you, this has been hard for-“

“Him. Sure. Guess what? Things have been hard for all of us, and no one else is throwing a tantrum every time you do something they don’t like.”

My teeth ground together. “You’re one to talk.”

I’d hoped the statement would earn me some small victory, but he just rolled his eyes. “I’m throwing tantrums when you’re about to get yourself killed. That’s different.”

Teela choked on her tea. Jareth, who’d watched the entire exchange with an arched brow and a sly grin, chuckled under his breath.

I glowered at Renner. “He’s allowed to be upset.”

“Yeah. And now he’s upset outside.” He leaned in. “You’re welcome.”

Joselia cleared her throat. “It seems there’s quite a bit I’m missing. But, if I may…” she stepped forwards, leveling a stern look between the three of us. “You all have been through several extraordinary ordeals. I’m sure today was difficult, especially when you came to the city expecting safety.” Silence crept through the room and she added, more gently, “We all say harsh things in moments of anger.”

“Not sure if ‘anger’s’ the right word,” Teela muttered, rolling her eyes skyward. Then, noting the four pairs of eyes all fixed on her, she flushed and cleared her throat. “Er, yeah, though. I get the, uh, sentiment. You guys’ll work it out. Definitely.”

Renner snorted. My shoulders sagged and he nudged me lightly. “Nap. Right?”

I squinted at him. “You’re not going to throw a tantrum?”

“Not planning on it.”

“And when Royce comes back?”

“We’ll get along,” he said flatly. At my skeptical look he arched a brow. “What? We don’t have to be in the same room to do that.”

I shook my head helplessly and, steps dragging, gathered my things and headed for the stairs. They were narrow, turning sharply at a middle landing, and as I crested there I heard quiet, masculine voices from below.

“‘Butting horns,’ huh?” Jareth laughed softly. “That’s not like you, killer.”

“Shut up.”
