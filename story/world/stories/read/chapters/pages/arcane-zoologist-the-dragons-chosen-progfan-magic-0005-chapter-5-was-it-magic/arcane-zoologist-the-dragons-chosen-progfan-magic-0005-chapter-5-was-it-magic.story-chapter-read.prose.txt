Tristan ran to Callie and grabbed her roughly by the arm, dragging her away from the mireling.

"Are you crazy? Getting close to a monster?! It can hurt you," Tristan shouted, pushing her further away. He appeared terrified.

Callie fell to the ground from his rough shove. Meanwhile, the boy frantically looked around until his eyes fell upon a large stick nearby. He grabbed it and then ran at the mireling with the clear intent of hitting it with all his strength.

"NO!" Callie sprang to her feet and ran after the boy. She felt a brief heat rise within her and then flow out of her hands.

Albeit the feeling was brief, the result was instantaneous. A root seemingly appeared out of nowhere, growing right in Tristan's path. His foot got caught in a root on the ground, making him stumble and nearly fall. This gave Callie enough time to run straight at him and tackle him to the ground. She was surprised that she managed to tackle Tristan to the ground. He was stronger than her.

"What are you doing?!" Tristan growled, trying to push Callie off himself while she clung to him with all her might, hoping that it would be enough time for the mireling to run away.

"You can't hurt it!" Callie demanded.

"Let go! It's dangerous!" Tristan said, trying to squirm free.

In their struggle, with their limbs all tangled together, Tristan pulled his hand with a bit too much force and struck Callie with his elbow straight in the face. She cried out as the pain spread through her nose and stars appeared in front of her eyes. It hurt a lot.

"Why would you—" Tristan growled, but stopped midsentence, staring at Callie in horror.

She felt warm liquid drip down her nose and onto her hands. It took a moment to register that it was her blood.

"No, no, no! I didn't mean to hit you!" Tristan panicked, grabbing handfuls of his hair and looking around as if the solution to the problem would be lying somewhere in the grass.

Callie's nose hurt, and she touched it gingerly, wondering if it was broken. What would she do with a broken nose?

"D-Don't touch it! We're heading back to Granny Ethel's," Tristan announced, grabbing the basket of herbs she had gathered. "Cover your nose!"

Callie obeyed, somewhat dazed from the situation. The feeling of blood dripping through her fingers made her feel queasy, but even so, she threw a last glance at the mireling. It was gone. She had managed to protect it from Tristan. That was all that mattered.

"Can I not leave you alone with her for five minutes, huh?" Granny Ethel bellowed, pulling roughly on Tristan's ear.

"Ow, ow, ow! I'm sorry! I'm sorry! I didn't mean to hit her," Tristan whined, his eyes brimming with tears.

Callie, meanwhile, sat in Granny Ethel's lounge chair, thoroughly enjoying the scene before her. As it turned out, the hit from Tristan's elbow wasn't quite as strong as she had initially feared. Her nose was fine, albeit it still hurt a bit. She had a clean cloth pressed to her nose, making sure the blood wouldn't drip on her clothes.

But it was quite worth it to see Tristan be punished. He had it coming for trying to hurt the mireling and for all the times in the past that he had bullied her.

"If you didn't mean to hit her, then why is her nose bleeding? Or are you trying to convince me that she hit her own nose?" Granny Ethel pulled on his ear again.

"No! No! She just went crazy over a mireling! I was just trying to protect her," he said.

"You were going to bash the mireling with a stick!" Callie exclaimed.

Granny Ethel growled, finally releasing her hold on his ear. "Is this true?"

Tristan rubbed at his ear, which was now a nice shade of red. "What else was I supposed to do? This dunce was standing right next to a monster!"

"I had been standing there for a good while, and the mireling didn't hurt me in the slightest. It was you who hurt me!" Callie shot back, feeling emboldened by her fury. She couldn't forgive that he was about to kill the mireling when it had done nothing wrong at all.

Tristan flinched back, actually looking guilty. For just a second. Then his angry scowl returned. "You really are a monster-obsessed freak!"

The boy whirled around and ran out of the hut, banging the door behind himself with such force that Callie thought it would break out of its hinges.

Granny Ethel sighed. "That boy needs to learn some restraint…"

Callie nodded in agreement, but quickly regretted it as some blood gushed out of her nose.

"Stay still. It might yet take a while for the bleeding to stop," the woman warned.

"I don't regret what I did. He would have killed that mireling, but the only thing it did was eat flowers. How is that a crime? We all want to eat," Callie said, still angry.

"Yes, killing it was certainly going too far. But don't you  think it's dangerous running at someone much stronger than you so recklessly?" Granny Ethel prompted.

Callie flinched back. "But I couldn't let him hurt the mireling…"

"The boy merely panicked. I'm sure there was a better way of solving this rather than waving sticks around and tackling each other to the ground. I think it's best you don't tell anyone about seeing mireling today," Granny Ethel said with a sigh.

"Okay… Mom would probably kill the mireling. I suppose I'm lucky that it was Tristan who saw it and not my parents. I was at least able to stop him from hurting it."

"I'm surprised you managed to stop that boy. He's rather strong for his age," Granny Ethel said.

Callie thought back on the events and recalled a strange occurrence. "When Tristan was rushing at the mireling, I felt something. Some kind of heat within me. It seemed to flow out of me, and the next moment Tristan tripped over a root."

Granny Ethel's gaze narrowed. "Are you saying that you made that root move?"

"I… I don't know. I know that something happened, but I have no idea what. Could it be that I used… magic?" Callie asked nervously.

"I'm not sure, child. Did it feel like magic?" She asked.

"I… I don't know. Maybe. It appeared for a very brief moment," Callie said.

"Then let's not rush to any conclusions. Wait for the bleeding to stop and head home for today. Despite everything, you've still managed to pick a lot of herbs for me. Thank you," Granny Ethel patted the girl gently on the head.

Callie smiled, but soon her mind wandered back to the unusual feeling she felt. It must have been magic. It couldn't be anything else. But she had done it completely by accident. Was there a way for her to trigger magic on purpose?

She pondered this question among many others the whole way home. Like what kind of magic would she be able to do? Could she grow plants just like the dragon had? That would explain how the root had appeared right in Tristan's path. But what if it could use any kind of magic? Was magic even limited to a single element?

Callie had too many questions but zero answers, and it infuriated her. Was there really no place where she could learn more about dragons and magic? Then an idea crossed Callie's mind. The library.

There wasn't one in the village, obviously, but she had heard that there was one in town where Mom sometimes went to sell the… sell the beasts she had hunted. While she hated the idea of being anywhere near a dead arcane beast, the library could contain some books about magic. Maybe even a book on learning magic.

"I'm going to ask Mom to take me to town next time she goes," Callie whispered to herself right as she opened the door to her home.

"Absolutely not!" Mom said firmly.

The family was gathered together over dinner, enjoying a fresh sorrel soup with bits of boiled egg mixed in. It was one of Callie's favorite meals because the egg tasted so good in the slightly tangy soup.

"Why not?" Callie whined.

"The way to town is dangerous and long. What would you even do there? You've never shown any interest before," Mom questioned suspiciously.

"I… I just wanted to go to the library…" Callie said meekly.

Mom softened a little bit.

"Yes, I imagine she would be interested in books," Dad noted. "You can read after all. I imagine there's a lot you could learn from them."

Callie perked up. "So could I go?"

Mom frowned. "I'm afraid it's not so simple. You have to pay to get a library card, and without a library card, you're not allowed inside the library."

"Oh…" Callie deflated.

There was no question that their family couldn't spare money for something like a library card. It certainly must be expensive, judging by how expensive and rare books were.

Mom and Dad shared a glance. After a moment of silence, Dad said, "Maybe we can ask if somebody in the village has a library card. Perhaps, they would be willing to let us borrow it for a while."

"Really?" Callie asked.

"I'm not making any promises. I don't know if anyone even has one," Dad said firmly. "And even if they do, it is without a doubt that they won't just give without a favor in return."

"And you'll have to learn a few things before you can travel with me. Basic survival skills, how to start a fire, how to make basic shelter, where to find water, and most importantly, how to use a weapon."

Callie recoiled. "No! I'm not going to kill anything!"

Mom shook her head. "I'm not asking you to kill anything. I just want you to know how to use a weapon to defend yourself. Not just from monsters, mind you. Other people can be even more dangerous."

Callie shuddered. She never considered that. Actually, thinking about what happened today, it actually made sense. There must be even bigger and meaner Tristans around in town. Perhaps Mom was right that Callie needed to learn how to use a weapon. Strictly for self-defense.

"Mom, is it not too soon? She's only nine," Callen interjected.

"You started learning when you were eight," Dad pointed out.

"That's different. I wanted to learn," Callen objected.

"I'm not demanding this for my own pleasure. You of all people should know how dangerous the road can be. We can never predict what could happen. Callie needs to be able to take care of herself even in the event she gets separated from us," Mom said.

"Why would I get separated?" Callie asked. "I wouldn't run away!"

"Not run away. But some things could force you to separate. For example, if there's a dangerous monster on the road, you would hide while Mom fights," Dad explained.

"Th-That sounds scary…" Callie said, more so out of worry for the poor arcane beast. Would Mom kill it?

"Perhaps it's best that you wait a few years before attempting to leave the village," Mom suggested.

"But I'd still have to learn all that stuff anyway, right?" Callie asked.

"Absolutely. There's no way around it," Mom confirmed.

Callie thought about it for a moment. What was the point of waiting around until she was older? She wanted to know about magic now, and since nobody could teach her about it, books were without a doubt her best bet in understanding magic. If Mom needed her to learn survival skills before going anywhere, she might as well learn them now.

"I'm going to learn!" Callie said with determination.

Everyone around the table shared uneasy looks, but Mom finally nodded. "Very well. I will arrange for you to have lessons with Blackthorn."

"What?! Mister Blackthorn? Noo…" She whined. Why out of all people did it have to be him?
