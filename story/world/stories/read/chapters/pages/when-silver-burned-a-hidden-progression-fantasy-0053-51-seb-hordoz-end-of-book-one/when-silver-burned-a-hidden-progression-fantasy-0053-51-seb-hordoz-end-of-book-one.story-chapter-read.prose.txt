“You ever notice how soup tastes worse when you’re already wet?”

Kalsin glanced sideways at Fals. “...What?”

Fals had her knees pulled up, a wooden bowl balanced neatly on them, stirring it slowly with her spoon. “Wet. Sogged through. Boots that don’t dry in the span of a single morning. Boots that you then gotta pull on your footsies because there’s no darn alternative.”

Jack let out an amused huff from where she was saddling Hollow, and Kalsin had an inkling that his bodyguard was letting him stew alone in this one on purpose.

“Nobody likes eating cold soup, Fals,” Kalsin murmured. “It has nothing to do with your boots.” His hands were occupied with cinching straps on a sodden pack that didn’t want to cooperate. The leather was damp, knots slipping.

It was early in the afternoon, and the company was busy breaking camp. After a brief discourse, they had come to the conclusion that setting up a campfire wasn’t worth the risk, so the soup was cold and their resident bard somewhat cranky.

“I’m just saying,” Fals continued, digging the spoon in. Not to eat, mind you—but to scoop that congealed layer of fat and oil to the side. “It’s criminal. We’ve got spice packs, dried herbs, even that half-block of kelp salt that Cyanfish nicked from the border depot. Still tastes like wet dog.”

“Cooking is math,” Cyanfish said in passing, adding Kalsin’s empty bowl to the collection on his arm. “Imagine an equation where temperature is the final multiplier. Too hot or too cold; and the whole shebang comes out as a big fat zero in the end.” He waved away Kalsin’s thanks and wobbled off toward the thin fracture where Shoreline was waiting with a sponge.

Fals muttered, “I hate math.”

Kalsin tugged on the leather strap again. It bit into his palm before finally settling with a squeak. The pack still bulged like it wanted to vomit its contents into the mud, but it was stable enough.

Fals sighed. The soup stubbornly remained in the bowl. The surface was starting to look like abstract art.

Kalsin said, “You could just leave it. Littleguy is always hungry for more.”

“I could also bite you, Ghost, but that doesn’t mean I’m gonna.”

Ringtail and Dweller came over from where the lads were wrestling the laundry back into their bags. Ringtail dropped into a crouch, the top of her shirt breezy enough to give Kalsin a look down south. She watched him set aside the pack with fox-bright eyes.

“Didn’t you hear, Fals? Our savior boy is called Kalsin,” Ringtail said, smirking. “A fancy-ass overworld name, if I ever heard one.”

“Oh?” Fals mused. “I don’t hate it, actually. It has a cartain… a certain prose to it. Like a heroic schmuck straight out of a ballad.”

Ringtail’s button nose wrinkled. “Ugh, what? What kinda ballads do you listen to?”

“Oh, no-no-no. I don’t listen to them, girlie. I make ‘em!” Fals nearly spilled her soup on Jack in the background as she swung her arms around. “I’ll be the first chronicler to record the events of the rebellion! How it started and how our leader was truly like, told by someone who’d been there from the very beginning. Not just rhymes and pretty words: I’ll tell his story, his personal journey, the man behind the myth! Because above all else—Ghost, Kalsin, the seb hordozó—he must be humanized in the eyes of the common man.” Pike and Crux glanced over at the racket, then promptly returned to their chores when it only turned out to be Fals. “The recountings of one Noémi ‘Fals’ Slipfall will echo across the Republic, one day. And don’t you forget it.”

Kalsin shrugged at Ringtail. Now she was stewing in the soup, too.

“I hate you,” she muttered, shuffling closer to him and farther away from the erratic bard. Her shoulder touched his.

“Likewise,” Kalsin said. He rose, stretching his back.

Ringtail jumped up, following. “Wait, really?”

“No.”

She huffed, hands on her hips as she glared up at him. She really was tiny. “Hmph. This whole legend-thing is going to your head. Don’t make me smack you.”

“Try it, and I’ll clap you right back,” Kalsin said.

He paused when he saw an aggressive blush spreading on Ringtail’s cheeks.

“Um. I don’t mean, like, uh, in an inappropriate way or something…” Kalsin muttered. He then said, “Wait, how did you mean it?”

“Uuuuh… a smack?”

“To the back of the head, right?”

“Yes, well… yes. Like, an affectionate pat or sumthin’.”

“Aha. The sort of smack you give your pals when they’re being silly.”

“Yeeeah. The good ol’ back rubber!”

“Except to the neck.”

“The neck. Exactly. All buddy-pal way and shit.”

“Sure. Nice.” Kalsin cleared his throat. “Glad that’s clear. Sometimes I have trouble discerning what goes on in your head.”

“Heh. Right? When you said uuh, that you’ll clap me, that’s exactly what I imagined in my head! And not like, you know, something crazy like, I don’t know, you clapping my cheeks over ‘n over again as you violently fuck me into the ground, typa’ deal.” Ringtail grinned nervously, eyes a bit too wide. “Heh.”

Back at her perch, Fals made finger-going-into-hole motions.

Kalsin was saved from the awkward tension by a muscular arm wrapping around his hip and pulling him into a taller, bigger, and noticeably curvier body. Kalsin glanced up to see Jack smiling down at him. When his bodyguard didn’t have armor plates donned, she was a veritable cuddle-bear.

Her arm locking him in place was soft and firm—and pretty much a public claim on him. The smile she leveled at Ringtail was pleasant, confident, and made Kalsin feel like a man worth staking a claim on. Confronted by a giant like that, most women would’ve backed off with a hasty apology.

Ringtail, to Jack’s misfortune, was not most women.

Ringtail tipped her head, appraising Jack like she was a pig in the butcher’s way. The corners of her mouth curled in a way that was almost a smile. “Well, ain’t that cozy,” she said. “You two sharing body heat now or is that just part of your employment benefits?”

Jack remained looking down at her, eyes half-lidded, a hum of amusement—or maybe warning—escaping her. She didn’t move, either. Didn’t need to. The arm across Kalsin’s midsection tightened by a fraction, drawing him imperceptibly closer, as if in defiance to Ringtail’s words.

“Nah, I get it,” Ringtail said. “For such a big idiot, he’s reeeal cuddly once you get to know him. Soft at the right places… and hard at the others.”

Kalsin felt Jack’s shoulders stiffen for just a moment. He squeezed Jack right back, reassuring. “Jack and I are together now,” he told Ringtail with enough conviction to make it sound like that alone should make her back off. “Deal with it, eh?”

Raingtail ignored him, no longer attempting to maintain that smile. “I hope you remember what I said last night when we met, Jack.” She thrust a finger at her. “Once everything is nice and rolling and tied with a bow on top, I will be the first in the pecking order.”

Jack’s lips twitched in the universal ‘doubt’ expression.

“You better believe it. I’ll be the first to charge in with him and the first to steal a kiss. The first to order the lesser bitches around. The first to his bed and the last to leave.”

“There’s no ordering around anyone, Ringtail,” Kalsin said. He tugged a bit, trying to extract himself from his bodyguard’s embrace. He failed. He opened his mouth, then closed it when he realized he was about to ask them both to calm down.

Even he knew that you never tell two competitive warrior women to calm down if you actually wish for that to happen.

Meanwhile, Jack had been giving some very pointed gestures to Ringtail about where to shove it.

Thankfully, Ringtail’s sharp retort was prevented by Dweller walking up to them with a bundle of knotted firewood in her arms.

“So how exactly are we planning to build a sea-worthy vessel without any trees around?” she asked with a nod toward their general grassy surroundings.

“There should be more wild vegetation as we draw closer to the gulf,” Fals said. “An’ I suppose we can also chop any timber we come across, then make a sled or some such to pull them along.”

Dweller chewed on her lips. “You a carpenter, Fals?”

Fals pointed over at Shoreline washing their bowls. “No, but she is.”

“The word is shipwright,” Shoreline answered without looking up. “And even then, I’ll need some serious manpower of the non-dumbassery kind to put everything together.”

Fals looked back at Dweller. “We’re fucked.”

Kalsin gave Jack’s back another rub before she deemed to free him from the chokehold. He scratched at the new scar on his jaw, thinking. “We’ve got a few hives among us, right?”

“Yah. I’m abyss now, thanks to you,” Ringtail said, perking up. “Used to be… you know, before. I used to be overworld. Spark.”

Kalsin nodded. “That may be our ticket, too. With the right manifestation, who knows what other advantages we can get. If we get a single metallist who can turn wood into some kind of durable alloy, we could cross to the other side of the Republic easily enough.”

“You make advancement sound like soft candy,” Dweller said, folding her arms. “There won’t be time to meditate during the march toward the sea. Not to mention, for that to happen you still have to… remove our scars.” She got that last one out a bit nervously, as if mentioning such a monumental event in passing was wrong on a profound level.

Hearing that, both Jack and Ringtail reacted similarly.

“Like hell he will! Not now, not yet. You don’t know what it costs him.”

Jack’s face remained impassive as she shifted her feet, resting one hand on the grip of her sword.

Kalsin looked around the breaking camp, and met the watching eyes of many of his mates. Those who had followed him on a whim, and threw their futures on the gambling table for the sake of something that resembled hope.

He didn’t have the surge, and his dreams of becoming Clenn had been scorched in the bud. He remembered the looks of his father, the expectant gaze of his mother, both whispering in their own ways how useless the silver-eyed kid they never wanted was. The wasted years he spent drifting, searching, looking to fix part of himself that was irreparably broken. Anger, grief, and denial at the injustice of it all.

He could be more broken still. But maybe he could help fix others in the process.

Kalsin rested a hand on Jack’s shoulder. He gave Ringtail a soft smile. He appreciated them both, trying to protect him from the difficult choices. It'd be so easy to nod and agree with them and let inaction take its course.

He was done letting the circumstances choose for him.

“You want that, Dweller?” he asked. “Want to be Clenn again?”

“I do. Gods, yes I do,” Dweller stepped forward, chin up and back straight.

“Line up, then. All of you,” Kalsin said. “And prepare some rope for me and the saddle. This… won’t be pretty.”

Pretty or not, he was ready to finally do what was necessary.

It was time the horns of rebellion began to spread.

Well, there we go.

That was the first time I tried my hand at more traditional type of epic fantasy, and the scale is kind of daunting. It's difficult to set up so many moving pieces early on, and let the payoff circle back around in a way that feels satisfying. This book was about Kalsin's personal journey - how his past shaped him to be the jaded guy he is, and his acceptance of walking down a path that goes against every base instinct that he has.

I have so many ideas and plans for the second book already. For how his powers will evolve, the intriguing culture of the Veiled, and the godlike destruction the Arches can bring down on the battlefield.

But - and here's the bad news - I am unfortunately a very slow writer. If I focus too much on one thing, I get burned out incredibly fast. Between juggling my cyberpunk series, work, my video game, and health complications irl (the tumor they took out was NOT cancerous, thankfully), I need to pace myself and refresh my creative palette from time to time.

When Silver Burned will continue with book two! But not immediately.

For now I'll be focusing on developing my adult RPG game further. Maybe do some creative writing; just a pen, and empty paper, and see what comes out. Eventually I'll want to build up a backlog for book 2 before I start posting the chapters. Also I'll want to do an Amazon release at some point in the future, so expect a stub, but for now I'm leaving the novel on RR for everyone to enjoy.

Thanks for reading this story. When Kalsin, Jack, and Ringtail returns, it will be a mad race through the tundras.
