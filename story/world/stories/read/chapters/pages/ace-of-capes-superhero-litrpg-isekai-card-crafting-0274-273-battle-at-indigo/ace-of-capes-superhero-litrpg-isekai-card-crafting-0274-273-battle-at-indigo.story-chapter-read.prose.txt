The Heroes immediately jumped to their feet.

“Indigo?” Stella asked. “Where the Time Space Institute is?"

“Yes. They attacked near it too, dropped a few heavy hitters, confirmed omega class, and a mana bomb. Indigo's emergency forcefield activated, but it penetrated a layer already, and they don’t look like they're stopping any time soon.”

"Shit,” Stella swore, and she and Groundsmage immediately disappeared, with Shadowbane soon following.

“Calling all hands on deck,” Jerry announced into his comm. “This is command. We’ve got a bomb, Indigo district, aerial. It’s already broken through the first emergency force field. If you’re close, get there now. Evac teams first, fighters second. Reserves, I need you movin'. This is spreading east.”

The outer Lords were discussing amongst themselves, theorizing whether there would be attacks in other cities too, whether to push some reserves there, and which armies to mobilize.

It seemed like almost everyone in the room had forgotten about Lexie until Kya Wavelost, still seated, pointed it out. “What are we going to do about her?”

Jerry glanced at Lexie while in motion and stopped, his face showing a struggle. “Just have her sit tight.”

“With pleasure,” Kaia said, and she erected manacles of ice around Lexie’s wrists and legs, attaching them to the chair and also freezing the chair to the floor.

“Those are unbreakable, by the way,” Kaia said as she got up to leave. “So don’t even bother trying.”

The ice was cold, but not torturously so. Lexie would have said something in protest, but she was too busy thinking about the attack in Indigo as the room cleared.

It was near the ISTS facility, and Lexie wondered if that was the plan all along, to destroy it. Why? What could the Emperor hope to gain from that? And did this somehow have anything to do with his previous plans with Vulcan?

Monty moved out of the shadows to sit in Jerry's vacated chair, and Lexie jerked. She’d forgotten he was even in the room.

Uncle Max was there too, beside her, but from the look on his face, he was on his system screen, probably monitoring the situation in Indigo, or texting whatever friends he had there.

“Why did you say you believed me?” Lexie asked Monty. He was staring at her consideringly, with just a bit of apprehension but none of the loathing the other heroes had for her.

“Because a lot has happened in the last few days," he said. "And, on the day Vacek died, a Villain named Mindmeister escaped from captivity.”

Lexie’s eyebrows raised. “You mean your father?”

Monty frowned. “He’s not my father anymore, but yes.”

“Is that who you’re scared is going to come for you?”

"Yeah." He glanced at Max and twisted his lips. "I’m the reason he got caught in the first place. He’s not the type to let a betrayal like that go. Especially from me.”

“I see,” Lexie said. “So you think his escape has to do with Vulcan?”

“At first, I thought it was just because Vacek had been distracted. You see, the prison Mindmeister was kept in was a lot like that of the Alchemist, but not as severe. He didn’t have any human guards, just robots. The only one who was allowed to visit him at all, and make sure that he was still in there was Vacek. That was the only person whose mind he couldn't control. If he ever tried to escape, Vacek would be alerted immediately. The second I heard Vacek was....missing, I went there myself to check on him, and he was gone, which leads me to believe that his escape coincided with when Vacek was trapped in the pocket dimension."

“I knew it,” Lexie said, turning to her Uncle Max in vindication. “Vulcan and the Alchemist probably helped him break out, and they were going to use him to mind control my father so that he would let them go, or possibly do nefarious deeds on their behalf.”

“We don’t know that, though,” Monty said. “And that’s the problem here. You didn’t give us anything concrete to convince us that Vulcan was really planning something like that. You didn’t give us a chance to decide. You simply killed him because you were scared.”

“I’m not scared of anything.”

“Yes, you are, and it’s okay for you to be scared, but what you did as a result of that fear is unacceptable.” He laced his fingers together, placing them on the table. “Listen, you went about things the wrong way. We have a hero’s code for a reason because a long time ago, most heroes would have done the same thing you just did. They would kill whoever they thought the ‘bad guy’ was, with very little evidence, and they would feel justified because of a 'hunch'. They were heroes, and they were powerful, and no one could stop them. So they used that to terrorize those who didn’t have as much power simply because they could.”

“That’s not what I did."

“Isn’t it? Did you even give Vulcan a chance to talk? To fight back? To convince you that you were wrong? He was a person, too, even if he’d done terrible things. Did you try to figure out why he did those things?”

Lexie pressed her lips together, but a single trace of discomfort skittered across the base of her belly. She didn’t like it. Didn’t like doubting herself. “What was the point of hearing him out?" Lexie argued. "He would have just lied to me anyway.”

Monty gave her a long look. “Do you think Stella didn’t have the same concerns as you did? She had to have. Vulcan almost killed both of her sons. He’s most likely the reason she's been away from her family for so long. You think she didn't hate him, that she didn't know things would be easier for her if he were dead?"

“Why are you telling me this?”

“Because I know what it’s like to toe that line, to truly feel like you need to make a drastic choice to save the people you love. I've been faced with the option to take matters into my hands plenty of times. We all have. It's our job to fight that beast and do the right thing anyway."

“And are you happy doing 'the right thing'?” Lexie could read between the lines of what he said. “Are you happy that you didn’t kill your dad when you had the chance?” After all, if his father were dead, then he wouldn't have to worry about him escaping and trying to kill him.

Monty smiled wryly. "Ask me that question a year from now if I'm still alive. I might have an answer for you then.”

Lexie blew out a breath through her lips. “So are we supposed to sit here until they’re done dealing with the crisis?”

"Pretty much,” Monty said. “By the way, Max, have you made any progress on the gun?”

“Still sourcing the materials," Max responded. "It will take a while.”

“Sure,” he muttered and wiped his palm on the front of his pants, looking more than a little anxious.

“Why don’t you stay with a friend in the meantime?” Max asked.

“And drag someone else into my mess?” He shook his head. “Nah. If he’s going to kill me, I’d rather it’s just me.”

Lexie frowned.

Monty may not have been her favorite person in the past, but she didn't want him to die.

Lexie wondered if there was something she could do to help him. Then she remembered one more thing.

“Gunther,” she murmured.

“Who?” Max asked, still beside her.

“He owned the consignment shop that dad took me to on my eleventh birthday. His shop is near the ISTS. He might still be there.” Lexie didn't know what time it was in Indigo, but they were a few hours behind, so it was entirely possible he'd still been at the shop when the bomb was dropped.

He might be in danger.

“I have to go,” Lexie said.

“That's unbreakable,” Monty gestured to her ice band. “And you can’t phase through it either. You also can’t teleport without permission unless you somehow get out of this room.”

“I don’t need to. I told you I can go anywhere I want.” She closed her eyes, connected to Yasycht, and a second later, she was in the center of Indigo in front of the consignment shop.

It was pure chaos.

The streets had dissolved into noise and hustling bodies, running and falling over debris and craters that had been formed in the ground. Lexie could feel the earth shuddering, the streetlights reflecting on iced roads. The shop next door had tall windows showcasing second-hand fur coats, tarnished samovars, and cracked gilt mirrors.

At the sound of an explosion, it suddenly burst outward in a shriek of glass.

Lexie blocked the flying shrapnel.

An onion dome of shimmering force fields hung over the city, and the outermost three had cracked. Even though that last bomb had only broken through the second and third force fields, the resulting blast pulsed through the atmosphere anyway, hitting buildings and roads.

Horns blared in the thin, cold air. Announcements to get into the bomb shelters. Capes soared high above, darting to rescue the citizens. Huge boulders with people on them were being floated to safety.

Lexie ignored all that for now and turned back to the doorway of Gunther's shop. It had been blown off its hinges thanks to the blast. She walked in and saw the mess, calling out, “Gunther?”

Her breath puffed out in the cold air, but there was no answer. She searched across the room for him, but it was a whole mess, all the shelves fallen over each other to block out the doors.

Then, she heard a groan suddenly behind a blocked doorway and blasted through to run into the hallway.

She spotted the stout, elderly man in one of the rooms where they keep inventory.

“Help me,” he groaned, underneath a heavy shelf of stuff that had fallen on his legs.

“Hang on,” Lexie said, using Windbreaker to get the stuff off of him.

When she knelt to look at his bleeding, swollen ankle, he got a look at her in the dark, and his eyes widened.

“You…” He gasped in shock. “It’s you.”

“Yeah," she explained. "I heard there was a bombing close by, so I came to get you."

That shocked him even more. "You remembered me?"

She nodded.

"Of course." Even with the pain he was in, he managed a smile. "You are the Archmage's daughter after all."

She stared at his ankle, which was twisted in an odd orientation. “Can you walk?”

He tried to get up, but the second he put even a little bit of weight on his left leg, he nearly collapsed again, having to lean on the wall.

His face was strained from the pain, yet he still nodded to answer her question. “Yes. I’m fine. I need to get to my wife and my son. They’re home alone.”

“I’ll get them. We need to get you to a bomb shelter first.”

“Not without them." He shook his head stubbornly.

Lexie said, “I wasn’t asking you.”

She encased him in a force field, weaving an air band around him to secure him in place so that the trip wouldn’t jostle him too much.

Then she carried him out of there, and they took to the skies.

He yelled during liftoff because another bomb went off, breaking more of the force fields.

Lexie ignored it, busy searching for the bomb shelter. It wasn’t too hard to find it. The river of people flowed in that direction, and she saw more than a few heroes jetting people to safety, including Stella and Theo Firerbinger. Stella hadn’t seen her yet, too busy. She dropped off a few people and was headed back to the Time Space Institute, where the domes stood untouched by the chaos.

Lexie didn’t know if they had a different protection system than the rest of Indigo, or if their walls were simply absorbing the impact of the blast.

Was it indestructible? Who knows?

Lexie landed at the crowded entrance of the shelter, calling the attention of several people, including Theo Firebringer, who landed at the same time as her.

“Lexie, what are you...” His eyes scrolled down her attire. “Are you in your pajamas?"

“Dewie got it for me." She put Gunther down gently and asked him. ‘Where do your wife and kids live?”

“They live in Yeltsin, by the border. It's the one with the green roof on the street." He answered hurriedly, giving as much detail as possible. "Please…my wife is disabled, and my son is very young.”

“I thought your son went to school on Dontiorre.”

“No, this is my other son. He’s only five.”

“I’ll get them,” Lexie said, and she left him with Theo, jetting off in that direction.

She almost thought it was a hopeless case when she saw the house he'd described. It was half destroyed, as were most other houses on that street.

But as she approached, the sound of feminine screams relieved her.

“Help!” The woman screamed. “Somebody help me.”

Lexie flew in through the windows to find the older woman in a bed who had been trapped by the fallen roof, separating her from the door. There was a little boy on the ground, unconscious, and the woman was sobbing as she reached out to him. ‘Please, Ivan. Please be okay.”

She didn’t notice Lexie until Lexie went by his side. Lexie checked his pulse. It was still racing underneath her fingers. That was good.

Lexie pulled back and carried him, encasing the woman in another bubble and merging them.

"My name is Lexie Sparrowfoot," she told them. "I’m going to get you to safety, okay?”

The woman nodded frantically, and Lexie took them out through the window.

They were quickly transported back to the bomb shelter, too, where Gunther had a tearful reunion with his wife.

Lexie thought she heard them telling her thank you. She had even heard Stella screaming her name from somewhere, but there were more things to worry about.

They were down to one last force field to be penetrated.

A shadow was being cast over the city as huge submarine-looking airships floated in the air and dozens of shuttles opened on each side.

"Shit," Theo swore as everywhere quieted like a held breath. "Everyone, get inside now!"

Lexie bolted into action even before the first bomb dropped and completely shattered the last protective forcefield.

More were incoming.

Stella flew up, and Lexie was behind her. Stella caught one of the bombs in her lava, but there was an array of other bombs, at least ten of them, raining on them.

Lexie destroyed them with a spread of VOID, stretching it as far as it would go. She also created separate balls of VOID to catch the bombs that the other airships released.

And she knew she would need to stretch it even further when the second airship floated above them.

"He's gone insane," Stella gritted her teeth as she trapped another bomb that was the size of a small boat.

Lexie didn't know about 'gone'. The Emperor had always seemed insane to her.

But yeah, this was overkill.

Lexie stretched her VOID expanse upwards, and from it, she sent VOID bullets at the submarine.

It didn't work.

The airship repelled the VOID.

What the hell? How?

As more bombs dropped from the sky, Lexie stretched her VOID as far as it would go. It seemed they were trying to destroy the entire city, and so she had to cover up what felt like the entire sky with black.

It hurt.

A lot.

The card was overheating, but so was Lexie.

It had been a while since she had strained herself this much, and even though the mana bomb should have made it better, it made it worse somehow.

Lexie didn't understand why.

It would probably be easier to capture a bomb and interact with it so that she could learn how to disintegrate it, but they were going off too fast for that. She would have to form a link with them first to hold them in place long enough, and that seemed risky considering what would happen if she missed a single one.

She couldn't miss them. She had to catch everything, or it might hit the bomb shelter, and innocent people would get hurt.

Nevertheless, she tried.

Lexie caught a bomb right before it landed in her VOID pocket and held it, but let it go when it seemed like it was about to explode. The VOID swallowed it.

And it just kept coming.

So she decided to target the airship instead. She needed to destroy it.

She breathed through her nose, forcing herself to connect to it even as her body screamed in agony. Doing it while holding separate large circles of VOID felt like she was holding the entire world in her hands, and everything ached. Her insides felt like they were rupturing. Her brain felt like it was exploding.

Yet she held her position anyway while still catching each bomb with VOID until she knew exactly how to take apart the airship.

She had a single thought for the people within the airships, then she figured Stella and co could deal with that.

She linked all the ships together with magic and ripped them apart.

She screamed when she did, blood streaming out of her ears, because it hurt her as much as it hurt the airships.

She felt her vision swim and her body slip out of the air.

She lamented about how weak she'd gotten since coming to Earth, how arrogant she'd been, and how much she'd lost.

She didn’t want to pass out here. Not in front of the heroes. They would jail her. They would give her to the Fae.

They could do anything they wanted to her while she was unconscious.

She tried to teleport, but she didn't know if she was strong enough to.

But somehow, she must have done it anyway, because she no longer heard chaos and screaming.

She heard crashing waves.

She felt a familiar scent hold her.

When she opened her eyes, she thought she looked into a familiar gaze.

“Dad,” she whispered.

He didn’t say anything as her eyes closed again.
