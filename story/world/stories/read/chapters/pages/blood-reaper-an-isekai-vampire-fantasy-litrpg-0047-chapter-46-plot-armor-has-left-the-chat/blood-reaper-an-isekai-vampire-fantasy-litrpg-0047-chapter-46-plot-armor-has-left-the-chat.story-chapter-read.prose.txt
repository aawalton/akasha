The plan was simple.

Not easy, of course, but simple.

Victor’s enchanted shadows rolled across the Highlands around Ace, drinking in the twilight and swallowing everything they passed. They licked at his feet, threatening to consume him, too. And through it all, Victor watched from afar, his hands manipulating the darkness as it tried to eat away at Ace.

Despite the flare of warning that screamed at him to run, Ace paused. He needed to conserve his energy and SM. Whatever these encroaching shadows were, Victor was burning through his Soul Meter to control them. The effect was flashy. Intimidating, even, but inefficient.

At this rate, the man wouldn’t be able to sustain them for long.

If Ace played his cards right, maybe he could drain Victor's SM while preserving his own. Like the System had said, a depleted Soul Meter would drain Victor’s HP as well as his self-control—and Ace could use that to his advantage.

Victor’s shadows expanded with each passing second, claiming more and more of the Highlands. The pressure in the air intensified, and it weighed on Ace’s shoulders. His movements slowed—just slightly, but enough to notice.

Nope.

Whatever this Skill was, he had no intention of letting it anchor him in place.

Ace summoned his fourth Duskblade, and the shadow-dagger formed instantly in his hand. With slow and deliberate steps, he inched closer to his opponent. Watching. Waiting. Paying close attention to Victor's increasingly labored breathing. The shadows grew darker where they touched the ground, but they began to thin at their edges.

Victor's control was already weakening.

Instead of trying to escape the encroaching shadows, Ace darted toward them. He raised his dagger—a decoy, of course—and Victor took the bait.

The whip snapped his way, slower this time, and Ace effortlessly rolled underneath it. The shadows coiling beneath him nipped at his skin, leaving long gouges where they slashed at him, but he pushed through the pain and rolled to his feet. When he recovered, he threw his Duskblade right at Victor’s throat. The man tilted his head to the side, ducking it by inches.

Victor’s energy was fading fast.

Good. The plan was working.

Over the next few minutes, Ace’s movements were carefully made. Focused. Minimal. Each step he took and each dagger he threw forced Victor to adjust his shadow field, burning more and more of the man’s SM.

Bit by bit, Victor was losing—and the man knew it.

Time to end this, then, before Victor did something desperate.

As Victor's shadows thickened around them, Ace slipped into the Shadow Realm. The world bled away, the colors fading until only shades of gray remained. The trees became twisted black things that sprouted from writhing darkness, their branches reaching like desperate hands toward the darkened sky. Each of Ace’s steps stirred the inky void that coated the ground, and it writhed like a living thing.

Ace's gaze flicked briefly toward the treeline. In the gaps between the trees, dozens of faceless silhouettes watched them—humanoid shapes with no features save for glowing white slits where their eyes should’ve been.

No time to worry about spectators.

In a rush of inky blackness, Victor materialized twenty feet away. He wasn’t cast in shadows, nor was his body devoid of color—just like Ace, he stood in the Shadow Realm itself. The edges of the former merc’s body blurred, and his entire form flickered. It faded in and out of the darkness writhing around him.

Apparently, Victor could also use Shadow Realm Immersion—and given how taxing the Skill was on the Soul Meter, that worked in Ace’s benefit.

Ace grinned.

With a grunt of effort, Victor's whip lashed through the air. Its black coil absorbed what little light filtered through the Shadow Realm, and the steel tip flashed briefly as it neared. It cut toward Ace's head with unnatural speed, the air around it distorting as it approached.

Ace dropped low, and the whip passed overhead with a deafening crack that echoed throughout the void. The displaced air buffeted his head, and a cold emptiness radiated from the weapon. If that had hit, his head would have been severed from his shoulders.

The thought sent a cold shiver down his spine.

The whip sailed back toward Victor and circled overhead as he braced himself for another attack. It launched again, and Ace rolled sideways as it snapped against the inky ground where he'd been standing. The blow kicked up shadows that bubbled and hissed like acid.

Another near miss.

Not good.

His breath came in short bursts as he calculated distances, angles, vulnerabilities. The faceless watchers at the treeline moved closer, drawn to the violence like moths to a flame.

Ace summoned another Duskblade, feeling the familiar pull in his soul as more SM drained from his reserves. His right hand hummed with power as the dagger took shape.

This time, however, he wouldn’t just aim and throw.

He would use Shadow Mark—and end this.

Victor’s whip cracked again, and Ace twisted his body to avoid the blow. This time, however, it grazed his shoulder. Pain exploded through his body—white-hot and all-consuming.

Damn it.

Any blow he took in the Shadow Realm dealt 200% damage. The raw carnage that left behind sent him stumbling, and it took a moment to regain his balance. He grunted, and his free hand brushed the front of his Thornhide Armor. It had shielded him from some of the attack, and he could only imagine how badly that would’ve hurt without it.

Despite the agony, he had to focus.

Ace feinted left. The whip cracked overhead again, but Ace ducked to the right at the last moment. Victor tracked the movement with a furious glare, and his whip adjusted mid-flight. Before it could hit him, Ace slashed at it with the Duskblade.

The whip and the dagger slammed against each other. His blade sliced into the whip’s lash, coiling it around the metal and missing Ace’s hand by mere inches. The weapons merged into a single mass of darkness, and the whip’s steel-capped tip briefly disappeared.

It was the perfect distraction.

In that split second of connection, Ace channeled Shadow Mark. A subtle rune flickered briefly on Victor's chest, visible only to Ace.

With a grunt of effort, Victor yanked on his whip. The writhing mass of darkness dissolved in a rush of smoke, and the weapons split apart.

Without warning, Ace plunged back into reality. He needed to lure Victor back into the real world, and he knew the man would take the bait. Even as the sensory shock of sudden color and sound disoriented Ace, he sank into his stance and raised one arm to throw his shadow-forged dagger.

A breath later, Victor reappeared in a flash of smoke. The man stumbled with the sudden transition, and though he quickly regained his composure, that moment of weakness was all Ace needed.

Ace threw the Duskblade in a single, fluid motion, and it spun end over end with perfect accuracy. The Mark guided it, the blade unerringly drawn to its target like metal to a magnet.

And try as he might, Victor couldn’t avoid it.

The Duskblade buried itself hilt-deep in Victor's chest.

Victor's eyes widened in shock. The tendrils of smoke coating the ground slowly dissipated as he looked down at the shadow blade in his torso. His whip fell from his hand and landed against the ground with a soft thud. He staggered backward, one hand clutching the wound. The former merc sank to his knees. He grimaced in pain, his breath short and ragged.

Ace closed the distance between them, and his furious glare never strayed from the man who had tried to sacrifice his team. Each footfall was deliberate. Unhurried. There was no rush now.

The hunt was over.

All that remained was the kill.

He reached again into his Abyssal Armory. It was time to summon the Crimson Sword, once and for all. An icy chill shot up his arm, burrowing past his wrist as it spread upward. Shadow-energy crawled along his skin like frost, stealing away his focus and energy as the ability leeched his Soul Meter.

But the sword was still fighting him.

Only a few feet from Victor, now, tension crawled up Ace’s arm as he reached again for the sword. The weapon’s shadowy outline flickered in and out of existence, filling his palm one moment and gone the next. Slowly, it stabilized, its edges becoming more defined with each passing second. Reality and the void fought for dominance.

He was close to summoning it.

So close.

C’mon, he thought, though his glare never left Victor’s face.

The sword's resistance peaked—a final, desperate surge that nearly broke his concentration—and then it abruptly surrendered. Solid weight materialized in his hand as the Crimson Sword fully manifested.

A shockwave of relief shot through his entire body.

Ace closed his fingers around the hilt, and the runes pulsed once against his skin in acknowledgement of his victory.

The hilt of the Crimson Sword fit Ace's hand almost as if it had been made for him. The obsidian blade caught what little light existed in this place, and the blood-red grooves that had been carved into the steel pulsed eagerly, as if the weapon itself thirsted for the kill. Victor's eyes widened at the sight of it, a flicker of recognition—and fear—crossing his features.

"The System chose me." Victor spat blood onto the ground between them, and his hand still clutched at the Duskblade buried in his chest. "Me, Blackwell. Not you."

Ace loomed over the man. Victor tried again to stand, but he grimaced in agony and fell once more to the ground. Without a word, he raised his blade to strike.

Victor laughed, and it turned into a wet, rasping cough. “She won’t let you do this.”

“I guess we’re about to find out.”

Without hesitation, Ace brought the Crimson Sword down in a diagonal arc that connected with Victor's neck.

The blade sang as it cut through flesh and bone, a high keening note that echoed through the hills. Victor's head separated instantly from his body. The cut was so precise that, for a second, nothing happened.

A thin red line of blood appeared in a perfect arc across Victor’s neck, and the man gurgled. More blood dripped from the corners of his mouth. His eyes rolled back in his head. A second or two passed before the head peeled apart from his neck, and it landed with a dull thud on the trampled grass. Yet more blood shot in thin bursts from the stump left behind.

The head’s eyes blinked once, twice—still alive for those few terrible seconds after decapitation—before the light faded from them forever.

Ace stood over the corpse. His Crimson Sword hummed with hunger as it absorbed Victor’s blood into the runes along its blade. He felt no triumph, no satisfaction—only a cold certainty that this had been necessary.

And that chilling lack of empathy left him wondering just how much of his humanity was truly left.
