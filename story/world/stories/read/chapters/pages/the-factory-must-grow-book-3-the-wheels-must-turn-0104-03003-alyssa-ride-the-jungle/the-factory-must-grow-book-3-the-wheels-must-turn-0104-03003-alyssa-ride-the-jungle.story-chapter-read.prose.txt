It was a truth universally acknowledged that magic... was absolutely awesome. And that mages were the absolute best. So even though Alyssa had already been great, now that she was a mage, she was that much better.

But as a mage, there were certain abilities which just... had to be possessed. Flight, which Alyssa had but Oliver didn't. The ability to throw fireballs, again a her-exclusive. And telekinesis, which neither she nor Oliver had. But she was a fair bit closer than he was, with her ⟨Springlaunch⟩ cannon capable of flinging pretty much anything at dangerous speeds.

Actually, shouldn't Oliver's bracket thingies count for telekinesis? He is an artificer, it seems fair to include the tools he made for telekinesis when crediting him stuff.

After a moment of further consideration, Alyssa decided that no, it shouldn't count. Because then they would both have telekinesis. And that couldn't stand.

Though, she didn't really have full telekinesis... yet. But she was working to fix that!

What she really needed was a summon ability. A hand-flick yoink, making things fly to her hand. Great in the unlikely event that one of the dragons or other dumb beasts around here managed to disarm her, or just generally when she was out picking berries or hunting.

Incidentally, it was that exact thought - how she could better utilize her magic while she was out collecting things - that was what had given her this current idea at all. Specifically, she wanted to combine [Leafstep]'s ⟨Springlaunch⟩ with [Rustlewind], using the latter's ability to create currents of magic connecting her to whatever she was looking for, and instead of just using that as directions for her, to use that as a direction for a little bit of magic to attach itself to whatever she specified, and then have that launch itself at her.

This... was definitely a good idea. That wasn't dangerous at all.

Alyssa blinked.

Launching hard, pointy things at her at mach yeet... okay. Maybe she should be a little careful even from the very start. She didn't want to brain herself with a stick because she underestimated the force of her own skill. This world wasn't Chained, and Forerunner Expedition protocol did dictate avoiding dying at all costs. ⟨Tumbling⟩ was an important first step, but that skill was mostly meant to protect her from herself. It wasn't armor.

She would start small. And soft. And maybe get protective gear, but she wasn't sure how doable that was. Her newly ⟨Retouch⟩ed inkling clothing was more form-fitting and covered most of her skin in the interest of keeping biting insects and omnipresent scratchy branches and thorns away when she was outside of the protective anti-Nature wards of First Tower. Maybe she could reinforce that with some chunky pieces of wood. There were all kinds of scraps around... she could also just grab a couple of boards - standard procedure was that spare scraps and parts weren't dedicated for anything, and it would be a first-come-first-serve basis if they ever were in a situation where they didn't have tons of materials to work with.

But for now... she would just stick with leaves. No matter how fast they went, it was exceedingly unlikely that they could hurt her, and had the added bonus of probably being easier to move with her magic.

She'd still make sure Clark was vaguely within the area, just in case.

Step one was just to identify that specific leaf with [Rustlewind]. The skill really wanted her to whisper what she wanted from it into the wind, and then have the rustle of the foliage around her provide an answer, but that would not do! She wanted to be able to just point at a thing and have it instantly fly to her hand. If she wanted to talk and request something from the world, she could just ask one of her teammates. No. That would not do!

She was a mage. She had a skill. Magic would bend to her will, and her will alone!

"The leaf on the rock," Alyssa whispered, sadly.

A tugging sensation pulled her hand up entirely on its own, pointing towards the specified leaf. She turned to the left and right, and her hand kept pointing to the same place as long as she kept the skill running in that particular configuration. She dropped it, and her hand fell to her side. She closed her eyes, kicked into the air at a semi-random direction and spun randomly, landing at some other point in the part of First Tower she'd been working in, while hopefully not also making her land like... on top of the head saw of Lumbermill.

It was a small enough chance she felt fine about it.

[Rustlewind]

"The leaf on the rock," she muttered, pushing her Skill to activate in the same way she'd just been using it. She felt her arm raise on its own and point to her back left. She turned and walked towards the sensation, until her hand landed on... the leaf, where it sat on its rock.

Alyssa sighed slightly, but carried on all the same. Even if she hadn't managed to get rid of the speaking requirement, she had figured out a lot about the magical connection using the skill forged between herself and her target. She'd even managed to translate that base information-gathering spell into one that could compel motion... from her. Now the trick was delivering that magical jolt to the leaf instead of her.

Working with magic was one part idea, nine parts practice, and so even once she had a good idea as to what she needed to do, it took a few hours before she actually got a grasp on it.

Literally.

She could wrap her fingers around the spell structure - it felt like a slightly pointy golf ball - and then manipulate it a bit. If she did it wrong, it would be crushed or otherwise dissipate. Getting a hang of what was right or wrong was tricky, because it needed to be moved quickly if at all, could only be rotated clockwise, and she needed to be exhaling it any time she wanted to move it. Humming also counted, but only a particular tone.

But if she did all that right, and she lined it up just right, she could flick her wrist in a particular manner and send it hurtling downstream of the magic. She could confirm that it was working because she leveraged the spell to help her right hand find her left hand, and thereby could feel the magic land on either side. She could even toss it back and forth to herself, juggling the magic for a few passes... until she messed up her breathing pattern, and it all fell apart.

Still, it worked.

The next thing she did was set the spell-packet on fire with [Ignite] and ⟨Conflagrate⟩. That proved easier, because she'd already done the skill-stretchy bit, and now just needed to use her other skills as intended.

The end result was really fun, and while it wasn't quite a fighting-capable spell... Alyssa was absolutely interested in figuring out how she could make target-seeking fireballs in the near future. It also wasn't what she was trying to do, because the magic was very much flowing from her to the target, and if she wanted this to be any real degree of useful, it couldn't just be a one-and-done blast. That was an excellent way to brain herself with a rock.

So she pivoted. She tried practicing moving small things around her open palm with a combination of very gentle ⟨Springlaunch⟩-⟨Tumbling⟩ action, and her own general knowledge of how to use Force magic in subtle ways. Usually, that was the kind of thing done when fighting incorporeal enemies, used to reinforce grip-strength, and that kind of stuff, but especially when she had a Skill to lens her existing skills through, it was pretty easy.

Once she had that... mostly mastered, she tried using [Rustlewind] as a means of extending her 'touch' range, so she could 'grab' and 'move' things even at a distance. That was substantially less successful. The spell simply didn't want to actually carry much of anything. It broke under the slightest amount of provocation, and thanks to the fully-overlapping elements between it and [Leafstep], she couldn't even get the two skills to properly engage to do her whole throwing the skill down the line... thing, like she could do with [Ignite]!

And she couldn't do the thing she'd done in the past, where she [Ignite]d one of her skills to increase the elemental 'bite,' because that was literally playing with fire, and she wanted the things she collected to be unburned.

Although....

She had been learning Light magic. And sure, she couldn't do the thing where Oliver could just summon pure light, rather than just using the light given off by her flames. But [Ignite] was about conjuring - or was it transmuting? That actually sounded more right based on what Ollie had been lecturing her about - but still. It was about making the element where it hadn't been before. How was that not conjuration? It totally counted. She definitely could ignore the general guidelines of magic for this, unlike with the whispering at [Rustlewind] thing!

Alyssa was almost mad about just how easy it wound up being. There was no grand fight against the concepts of magic as it sought to constrain the indomitable human spirit, or anything like that. Alyssa had just... used ⟨Blowtorch⟩ to conjure a flame at her fingertips, used some incantations to make the flame even brighter, and then kept repeating the incantations that pushed the flame in that direction, whatever that direction was, and then...

It had turned green. And was glowing, completely not a flame, just a little ball of light cradled in her palm.

⟨Illuminate⟩, her soul echoed at her.

She... she could have done that so long ago! This was an indignity! She had spent hours, days even, trying to learn how to do light magic the 'proper' way, and practically all she needed to do was to push on her skill a little harder than usual while chanting Light under her breath?

Outrageous! Unfair!

....Oliver had been the one to tell her that she needed to cast a spell to create light, rather than having her just play around with her skills.

It was clearly his fault.

It obviously wasn't Ollie's fault. But it made her feel better to pretend that he was somehow to blame, and what really mattered if not feeling better based on entirely imaginary decisions that didn't affect anyone in any way except for cheering up the person thinking the thoughts?

So maybe she could pretend it was Oliver's fault, just a little. As a treat.

Those thoughts didn't last very long in the face of the simple truth that Alyssa had a new subskill now, that could... yup. It could make things glow. Wooden things only, alas. It was still an [Ignite] subskill, and that skill just worked on elementally Wood objects and magic, so yeah.

Still awesome. If Alyssa sought to ⟨Illuminate⟩ her [Leafstep], she could actually pretty clearly see the currents of the skill whereas before she could only kind-of feel it. It was all woosh-y and aura-y - those obviously being the technical terms - enveloping mainly her feet but also the rest of her body thanks to ⟨Tumbling⟩, and whenever she moved, she left little light-trails behind, which faded after a few seconds. It felt a bit like skating, with her feet slightly gliding across the ground.

It also made her fast. Light, Air, and Force were all elements that cared about moving, and the slow-and-steady stability that Wood normally provided was substituted for raw speed.

Of course, losing that stability meant she couldn't run across the rough ground like it was nothing, or along thin branches without them bending even a bit... so this was more of a trick that would show its utility when running down roads, or once they started expanding into places that were better for running. A nice grassland, or even a desert would be the best to experiment with her new 'lightspeed' skill combo.

She teased Oliver for a little while, needling him about just how much better her mage-lights were than his now, but he was being boring and working on making a drone body for his familiar core, swinging his new staff all over the place and generally being a poor sport.

Though maybe it was for the best, because that got her back on track to her actual goal sooner.

As it turned out, giving [Rustlewind] the ⟨Conflagrate⟩-⟨Illuminate⟩ treatment... still didn't let her get away without a verbal description of what she was looking for. But it did change how she got feedback from the spell. Now, instead of generally pulling her along and sometimes giving flashes of intuition about subjects, it just... made whatever start to glow.

"The rock in my hand," she directed, and watched as the pebble became wreathed in a green aura. She tossed it into the air, and the glow stayed attached.

She cut the skill.

[Rustlewind], "Tully the petalfur."

There was a moment of pause, and then the named critter yelped as she started glowing. She scrabbled out of Clark's lap and tried to dive into a puddle of shadows, only to chase away the darkness she was trying to either hide in or melt into. That led into a bit more distress, and a frantic display of trying to dig into the hardened dirt floors of their common area, to no effect.

"Ride," Henrietta gently warned.

"Yep! Sorry, Commander." She let the skill fade, and the shadows swallowed up their little mascot. Tiny eyes looked out at the surrounding giant world, and Clark reached over to scratch the head of his pet.

[Rustlewind] "The most annoying human," she prompted, trying to push her skill just a little bit. 'Most annoying' wasn't an elementally meaningful designation, and so it was a much weaker 'search term' than just 'human.' In practice, that meant that Alyssa should be able to direct its 'search' to focus in a specific area, and then stick with the absolutely first thing it identified, rather than maybe any best match it might come up with if she gave it more time and mana to work with.

Oliver lighting up in a halo of green fire showed she had been perfectly successful.

His gaze latched onto her. "Rude."

"Hey," Alyssa flicked her hand, "Don't take it up with me. Take it up with magic. You heard me ask my question, and I think the results are plain to see."

"You do realize that elemental Arcane arcanoception means I can clearly sense exactly how you meddled with the skill, right?"

"I," Alyssa puffed up her chest, "Couldn't possibly know what you're talking about. I wanted to know, mana mana of the world, who's the most irritating of them all?"

She pulsed her skill for emphasis, to make Oliver's glow redouble and prove her point, only for him to flick his hand and speak a word of power... and her skill felt like it bounced off something, landing... squarely on her.

It tickled, and Alyssa flinched before she got the mana to disperse, "Hey!"

"Oh, I'm sorry. Did the mana change its mind?" Oliver asked with faux-concern.

"You..." she grumbled. "Hmph." [Rustlewind] "A chunk of air near my head in the shape of a fist with one finger raised."

Alas, that didn't work.

Except for making Oliver more smug.

Damnit.

Alyssa highlighted a handful of berries sitting on a fallen log.

Then, with a bit of a 'twist' to the magic, in a way that felt a bit like using a handle that [Ignite] had installed in the divination, she tightened the 'lines' of magic that flowed out from her and to the berries. Then, by pushing more mana through ⟨Illuminate⟩, she made those lines visible, and a thin thread of green light faded into visibility between her hand and the still-illuminated berries just a couple of feet away.

Then, the part that kept failing. While trying to keep everything together, Alyssa carefully pushed a bit of [Leafstep] into the lines, contorting her body and fingers to keep the magic cohesive. Unlike before, when the perfectly-matching elements between it and [Rustlewind], Alyssa found that the two skills now actually had a bit of 'bite,' pieces of their respective magical structures that couldn't freely intermingle and separate. The Light of her [Rustlewind] simply didn't correspond to the Wood of her [Leafstep], and she leveraged that little mismatch as much as she possibly could.

Right now, she was trying to utilize the wind-up part of ⟨Springlaunch⟩ to her advantage. That particular subskill worked by drawing Wood mana back like a spring, compressing it into tension as through it were a bow or a branch leveraged for a snare. Normally, Alyssa worked to minimize the amount of time and investment that stage of the skill usage required. But for this, she was trying to be... gentle. And selective. And slow. None of which were usually things she associated with ⟨Springlaunch⟩.

But here, she was trying to entwine it with her Light-⟨Conflagrate⟩d [Rustlewind] so that the Air and Force could follow the conduit created by the divination, and then have ⟨Springlaunch⟩ latch on to the Wood-Associated berries and, as it compressed itself, draw them in along the conduit like a fishing line.

Almost to her surprise, it half-worked!

Sure, the berries just... rolled off their perch, instead of gracefully moving through the air, but it was proof that her idea was working!

Some tries later, her hands were quite stained with berry juice.

A few more sets of berries later, Alyssa couldn't wipe the smile off her face as handful of berries slowly pulled itself off the log and levitated towards her extended palm, gently arcing over her outstretched fingers and setting themselves solidly in her grasp.

Then the grip she had over the strongly-charged ⟨Springlaunch⟩ coiled in her palm slipped, and she got a high-speed pelting of berries directly to her face.

Once she had the base steps down, Alyssa started experimenting with different configurations, trying to get things from further away, trying to get them to come faster, trying to get them without building up a ton of stored energy that would need to be released somehow. She kept doing things, feeling it out little by little and trying to get everything working.

Unfortunately, her mind kept wandering even as she experimented, so she wasn't sure exactly what she did differently when her soul decided it had seen enough.

⟨Gather⟩, it whispered to her. And the berries she was using for target practice flew into her hand with almost perfect precision.

She thought for a moment. How did she feel about not strictly knowing how she succeeded in her goal and got a new subskill? On the one hand, it felt like she'd somehow cheated herself of knowledge about how to better do this sort of thing in the future. On the other, it meant she was just so good at magic she didn't even need to pay attention to do awesome things.

So yeah, she was going to be happy about this.

Because Oliver was going to be so annoyed, and what was more important than that?
