I tried to summon Serp, but the blade refused to appear in my hand. That had to be another particularity when it came to awakening living weapons: I wasn’t allowed to use them during their own awakening.

It would have been nice to mention that. I drew my divine blades.

For some reason, they seemed a lot darker than usual, as if the trial itself was rejecting them.

I was no longer in the room. Rather, I was no longer in its usual form. Hundreds of paths curved around me, as if someone had cut up the space into fine lines, then curved them like ribbons. Looking closer, I had to admit that this did resemble a labyrinth of sorts. The only difference was that instead of the expected obstacles, I was surrounded by air on both sides. One wrong step and I could well fall into the endless void that surrounded us.

Creatures of various types filled the paths as far as the eye could see. All were aware of my presence and were eagerly rushing in my direction.

“Goblins?” I asked, amused at the notion.

“Hey! The Grand Arcana decides what’s keeping me down,” Serp replied, ashamed by the fact.

That was slightly unexpected. I would have thought that I would be presented with tougher entities. Then again, this was only the start of the trial. Unlike the divine blades that faced me with one opponent of increasing skill, the living weapons apparently were using numbers to drown me.

Thread strike! I slashed the space in front of me, sending a destructive thread of aether straight at the approaching creatures.

Two of the goblins were sliced in half by the attack. Three more were pushed off the path by the force of the strike. Over a dozen remained rushing at me, not to mention the many more approaching along other paths. Apparently, this wasn’t going to be a walk in the park after all.

Keeping track of all my enemies, I cast several spell circles, sending wind, fire, and water in their direction.

This time, the attack achieved a far more adequate result. Dozens of creatures were cast off into the void.

“How many do I need to kill?” I asked.

“All of them?” Serp responded in a mocking tone. “It can’t be that hard. I mean, you’re old, but I’ve seen you fight.”

Old but seen me fight, I repeated in my mind. If we were to consider the ten years of my previous run, he wasn’t off.

Gripping my blades, I took a few steps forward. The Boots of Swiftness ensured that I didn’t slip, despite the curve of the path. That was good. All that remained was to overcome the illusion that the paths would let me drop to my death.

Thanks to my eagle eye special and the Observer card, I could picture the strands coming together, forming the original shape of the ballroom. Part of me was tempted to use the Acrobat to check whether I could leap from one strand to another. I quickly dropped the idea.

Mistakes during weapon trials were no different from mistakes in actual ones. If I messed up, I wasn’t going to just end up with a faulty weapon; there was every chance I would poof out of existence like the many participants who spontaneously vanished during trials.

The more goblins I killed, the more seemed to appear. It was almost as if a den of them was somehow attached to the platinum labyrinth. I knew that couldn’t be the case—I would have seen if it were—yet there was no denying that the number of monsters progressively grew.

Where are they coming from? I wondered.

Caution gave way to speed as I ran forward, slaughtering everything in my path. For the moment, I had stamina to spare, but if things continued as they were, that was quickly going to change. Also, I couldn’t afford to end this trial wounded or exhausted; there still was a whole different battle expecting me back in Anubis’ chamber.

There had to be a reason, some twisted logic hidden within the rules of the trial. For the most part, the goblins were weak. So far, I hadn’t come across any archers or sharpshooters. The wave of initial critters had progressively been replaced by bashers and leader-rank monsters, although nothing I couldn’t handle with my current cards. As Serp had said, it was literally the numbers that were keeping me down.

Just like scrubbing grease off a pan, I thought. No matter how many of them I removed, somehow there were always more.

Power thrust! I drilled through the massive creature standing in front of me. Five times the standard goblin, it would have made for a tougher enemy if it wasn’t for the abilities of my blades to slice through flesh.

As another corpse fell into the surrounding darkness, I paused for a moment to get my bearings. I had long lost count of how many I had killed. It felt like it had to be thousands. If I were to have a weaker set of weapons or lacked the cards I currently possessed, I might very well have been overwhelmed minutes ago.

“Freeze!” I ordered, using the Emperor card.

The goblins obeyed, stopping in place as fear petrified them.

More thread slashes followed, sending dozens to their deaths.

Efficiency, I thought. The trial had to be about efficiency. Only the result mattered. In this case, the result I was aiming for was to deprive the space of goblins. They weren’t my enemies, per se, but rather an infestation I had been called to exterminate. Killing them off one by one, or even by the dozen, was possible, though troublesome.

“Walk straight into darkness,” I sang, thrusting every instance of dread and depression I could imagine into the monsters’ minds.

Strictly speaking, the Bard was meant to inspire and encourage those that heard it. Doing the opposite required a lot of practice and imagination. And it showed. Not even one of the monsters cast itself into the void.

There goes that idea. I stopped.

“Don’t you give up on me, old man!” Serp’s voice filled the air. “We’re in this together, right?”

“I’m not giving up,” I replied.

That didn’t mean I liked my options. If singing didn’t work, it didn’t seem I had a choice other than to kill them off one by one. That meant the only realistic options I had were whether to go after them or wait for them to come to me.

Brushing the sweat off my forehead, I took my standard defensive stance.

Suddenly, an idea sparked in my mind. It was so obvious that it verged on the absurd. Could it be that was what had been expected of me all along?

“We’re in this together,” I repeated.

“That’s what I said,” Serp sounded annoyed.

“Does that mean you’re still part of me?”

“Err… what does that mean?”

I wasn’t even listening. Returning my divine blades to the inventory, I bent down and touched the floor with both hands.

“Water!” I said.

Hundreds of spell circles formed all over the platinum strands. Each of them required a small amount of stamina, though not nearly as much as a strike. Water flowed out, transforming the entire tangled space into the most elaborate fountain in existence.

Dozens of screams echoed all around as hundreds of goblins were thrust off the paths by the initial burst. Hundreds more were swept away as the rivers flowed along the narrow surface, taking anything not firmly attached with them.

More spell circles formed nearby, forming wedged walls on either side of me. Even the Boost of Swiftness wouldn’t be enough to keep me from falling should the full force of the water strike me directly.

“Old man…” Serp began, his voice echoing with doubt and disbelief, “you know that’s cheating, right?”

I only laughed. Leave it to a sword to insist on a hands-on approach. If I were to follow his advice, I would probably have had to spend the next several hours killing the goblins one by one. A more skilled participant might have done just that, and in doing so won the battle only to lose the war.

“Next time I’ll try to play fair,” I assured him. The important thing was for the trial to count.

Several of the paths shook and twisted. No longer burdened by goblins, they changed shape, reattaching to the ones beside them. It was like watching a sheet of paper being glued back together, only without the actual glue.

Strand by strand, the original hall took shape. In the process, the sword monument came into being again. Rising from the center of the strands, it shone with a bright silvery-white light, indicating its new state of being. All the spheres Serp had consumed up till now glistened like stars.

That’s enough, I thought, destroying all the spell circles I had created.

“I’m not fine with this,” Serp protested in a semi-reluctant fashion. “Thanks, though. It’ll be useful.”

Judging by his tone, I could feel the conflict brewing inside him. He wanted to become strong, and a sign of strength was never to cheat. The siren princess had also mentioned that. Was this some rule of the Grand Arcana I wasn’t familiar with? When it came to survival, I always lived day by day, taking advantage of everything that would keep me from dying. Fair, literally, was for the strong, and in the past, I had never considered myself such, not even after a decade spent in the game. The most I could say was that I thought of myself well enough to survive… and even that turned out to be wrong.

“The first of many.” I tried to sound as encouraging as possible. Usually, that was Avon’s role.

Looking at the sword monument, I could see it had slightly transformed. A new branch had formed on the side, making the base of the blade extend in that direction, as if a snake was coiling around it, slithering upwards. Seeing it made me curious how much the monument would transform at each awakening. How much would Serp himself?

There was no time for that now. Avoiding the remaining cracks of void, I rushed towards the spot I wanted to emerge from back in my other trial. It was a lot more difficult having a single point of reference, but the Observer card let me instantly see the spot. If I were correct, I would appear right behind Max.

“Strike him when we get back,” I ordered.

“I knew you’d say that,” Serp laughed. “Already ahead of you, old man. He’ll know exactly what hit him. Just one question. How hard do you want me to bite?”

I knew exactly what he meant by that. Even with his awakening complete, he still needed a boost of my blood and flesh to increase his power.

“As long as I’m still standing as much as you want,” I replied.

SERPENT BLADE has awoken (Level 2)

Minor Serpent Form: take the form of a serpent at will.

The platinum room vanished, bringing me back to the dark and dirty chamber of Anubis. As always, only a moment had passed since the start of the awakening trial. Eleonor was still standing, though barely. I could see her tilt, struggling to remain on her feet. One of her eyes had lost its gold, making her look like a car with a busted headlight.

Thrust, Serp said in my mind.

Fifteen feet separated me from Max. The spot at which I had appeared was directly behind him, although not as close as I would have liked.

Even with a Sprint it was unlikely I’d reach him without getting noticed.

Eleven of Chalice. Twelve of Rings. Four of Rings. Thirteen of Swords. One of Swords!

I triggered the combined cards as I performed a power thrust.

Green flames covered the saber, but it wasn’t just that. Before my very eyes, the entire blade transformed into a platinum snake, shooting forward at its target, fangs bared.

I felt a shot of pain in my hand. Two spikes had sunk into my flesh, like a pair of teeth, sucking up whatever blood Serp thought I could do without.

Cheeky bastard, I thought.

Ten feet from its prey, the upper part of the serpent doubled in size, then again and again.

A monster almost resembling a miniature version of the Serpent Blade bit into Max’s shoulder.

BACKSTAB SUCCESSFUL

Neither the exoskeleton nor the poison acid it was coated in offered any protection. Serp bit off the entire arm to the shoulder blade, as if it were a chicken leg. If Max weren’t undead, his entire body would have been consumed by now.

That impressive enough for you, old man? Serp asked, overflowing with pride.

I couldn’t blame him. If I were capable of all that, I’d brag as well.

ANUBIS boon activated.

MAXIMILLIAN SWANSEN has been reconstructed.

“No!” I shouted. Even after all that, he was still alive.
