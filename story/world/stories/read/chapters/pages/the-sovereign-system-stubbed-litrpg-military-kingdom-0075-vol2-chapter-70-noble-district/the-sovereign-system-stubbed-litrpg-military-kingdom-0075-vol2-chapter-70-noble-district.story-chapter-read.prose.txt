In another part of the city, a man in a hooded robe strode through the streets with purpose, deftly weaving around passersby. His sturdy build and steady gait exuded quiet confidence.

As he moved northward, leaving the bustle of the main district behind, the crowds thinned. The architecture shifted subtly—modest, utilitarian buildings gave way to grander, more ornate designs. Even the cobblestones beneath his feet looked cleaner, better maintained. The streets widened, and the air grew still.

Soon, a large gate came into view, flanked by two stone pillars and a guardhouse. It resembled the gates of the Royal Academy, though even at a glance, it was clear there were far more guards stationed here than usual.

The hooded figure didn’t go unnoticed. The moment he entered their line of sight, the soldiers stirred, eyes narrowing. Spears were raised without hesitation.

“If you value your life, you’ll turn around and walk the other way,” one of them barked, his voice gruff and commanding.

The man let out a low chuckle, utterly unfazed. With deliberate calm, he reached up and pulled back his hood, revealing a bald head crisscrossed with scars. A wide, toothy grin stretched across his face.

“My life is not mine to give,” he said boldly. “It belongs to General Drakon.”

“Lieutenant Commander!?”

The soldiers instantly lowered their weapons, recognition flashing across their faces. Their demeanor shifted completely.

“I’m not your Lieutenant Commander anymore, boys,” the man replied with a wry grin. “But if it pleases you, feel free to call me ‘my lord.’”

Silence followed. No one seemed eager to adopt the new title.

From behind, a familiar voice called out, light with humor. “Put that bald head away, Christian. I could see the moon’s reflection from a mile off.”

Laughter rippled through the guards.

Christian smirked and turned. “Zion, is this how you treat your fellow disciple?”

Zion approached, placing a firm hand on Christian’s shoulder. “It’s good to see you again.”

No further words were needed. The bond between them was palpable.

Christian’s smile faded. “Any movement?”

Zion shook his head. “Nothing. I’m starting to think they’ve already slipped out of the noble district.”

“Then shouldn’t we go after them? If we’re just standing guard—”

Zion raised a hand, silencing him. “We don’t move without the General’s command. You know that. The last thing we need is a fight with the nobles before identifying the traitors. Kill the wrong ones, and it’ll be our heads rolling.”

Christian didn’t look convinced but gave a reluctant nod. “Any word on the Vice General?”

“Jason went to see him today. He should’ve gotten the message.”

Christian exhaled softly, some of the tension leaving his frame. “Then… will he be joining us?”

“I doubt it. Blake is stationed at the palace, so hopefully they’ll run into each other—”

Zion’s words were cut short by a thunderous explosion that echoed across the city. A burst of light flared in the night sky, casting brief shadows across the rooftops. Shock flickered across their faces—then came a second boom, snapping them out of their daze.

“That’s the signal!” one of the men shouted.

“Wait, what does that mean?” Christian asked, his voice tinged with urgency.

Zion’s expression hardened. “It means we move in and take prisoners.”

He spun on his heel, raising his spear high.

“Men, advance! Capture the nobles. If they resist… kill them.”

A heavy silence fell over the group, the weight of his command sinking in. No one dared voice their doubts. Zion issued a few sharp orders to a nearby soldier, who took off at a sprint.

Christian’s eyes widened. He blinked in disbelief. “Wait—since when was this part of the plan? I thought—”

“This is a contingency,” Zion interrupted grimly. “If the General couldn’t extract the names of the traitors, we were instructed to seize the entire noble district. Everyone. Neutralize and detain.”

Before Christian could react further, a spear was thrust into his arms.

“I want you watching my back, brother,” Zion said, voice low. “Don’t hesitate. Strike down anyone who resists.”

Christian hesitated, giving him one last searching look. Then, his grip tightened on the weapon. Determination flared in his eyes.

“You can count on me.”

The noble district lay in the northern quarter of Ralis, a world apart from the crowded central square. Grand estates lined the wide, pristine streets, and immaculate shrubbery gave the neighborhood the appearance of a lush oasis amidst stone and smoke.

Its single entrance had once been a point of pride. Tonight, it was a tactical advantage.

The soldiers moved in silence, boots crunching against cobblestones as they advanced through the open avenues. Street lamps flickered overhead, casting long shadows that danced with every movement.

Despite the grandeur, an eerie stillness clung to the district.

Zion gestured wordlessly, signaling the soldiers on the outskirts to begin clearing homes. One by one, small groups peeled off, entering mansions and villas with caution, while the main formation continued forward in a slow, sweeping arc.

A few minutes passed.

No screams. No struggle. Only the unsettling quiet of empty homes.

Zion slowed, his brow furrowing. Something wasn’t right.

“Lieutenant Commander!” a soldier shouted from a nearby doorway, face pale as he stepped into the lamplight.

“What is it?” Zion called back.

“They’re dead…” the soldier said, voice tight.

“What!?”

Zion’s jaw clenched. Emotions flashed across his face—disbelief, fury, calculation. Without hesitation, he motioned forward.

“Keep moving. Check every house—groups of three or more. No exceptions.”

“Yes, sir!”

Christian fell into step beside him as they approached the nearest estate. Both men wore grim expressions, their previous confidence now edged with unease.

Crossing the threshold, they were immediately struck by the iron scent of blood. The metallic tang hung heavy in the air.

The soldier led them into the dining hall—and there, they froze.

Three bodies lay slumped over the dining table, their blood soaked into the linen and dripped across the polished wood in a macabre tableau. Judging by the half-eaten dishes and their positions, the family had been in the middle of dinner when death arrived.

Zion stepped forward, eyes narrowed. He gently lifted the head of the nearest victim.

It was a teenage boy, no older than fifteen. A deep gash had been carved across his neck, clean and precise—straight through the carotid artery.

Christian swallowed hard. “Who could’ve done this?” he asked softly, disbelief lacing his voice.

“I don’t know,” Zion muttered. “But it looks recent…”

Before they could examine further, a chorus of urgent shouts rang out from the street beyond.

They exchanged a quick glance and hurried outside.

Dozens of soldiers were emerging from nearby homes, their expressions grim and unsettled. By now, not even a quarter of the houses had been searched, but the shared look in their eyes told the story well enough.

“Lieutenant Commander…” one soldier began, but his voice faltered.

“How did they die?” Zion asked flatly, not waiting for a full report.

“Most had their throats slit or were stabbed with short blades. It looks like the work of someone… highly skilled.”

Christian’s brow furrowed. “There are at least fifty houses in the noble district. There’s no way a single person could’ve done this without—”

“Fuck!” Zion cursed, eyes widening in realization.

“Everyone regroup! Center of the street—now!”

The sharp command snapped the men into motion. Soldiers near the outer edges of the formation relayed the orders with urgency, while others sprinted back from distant homes to rejoin their comrades.

They quickly formed up in the center of the boulevard, encircling Zion and Christian, weapons drawn, eyes alert.

Moments later, approaching footsteps echoed from one of the side streets. Tension surged—until familiar armor came into view. It was a relief, but brief.

“Head count,” Zion ordered, his voice tight.

His heart pounded. If the nobles had been killed silently—despite the soldiers guarding the only entrance—it could only mean one thing:

They were already inside.

Whoever had done this was already here when they arrived.

A soldier returned, saluting.

“Lieutenant Commander, everyone is accounted for.”

That news eased the immediate fear of an ambush. Still, the tension lingered. The men stood with weapons ready, scanning rooftops, alleyways, and windows for signs of movement.

“Could the assassins have already escaped?” Christian asked warily.

“Assassins?” one soldier repeated, voice cracking with fear.

“I don’t know,” Zion admitted, eyes darting across the street. “But I’m not taking any chances. We need to alert the General and await further instructions.”

“But what if there are still survivors?” Christian pressed, though his tone made it clear he wasn’t eager to search further. “Wouldn’t he want us to find them?”

“There might be,” Zion said. “But I think he values us more than some bullshit nobility.”

He raised his voice.

“Men! We’re moving out. Keep in forma—”

“Don’t worry—there are no survivors.”

The voice echoed through the ghostly streets, smooth and enchanting. It sounded distant, yet unnervingly close—like a whisper at the back of the neck.

The soldiers tensed, heads turning sharply as they searched for the source.

Zion and Christian exchanged a glance. Then Christian stepped forward, raising his voice. “Who’s there? Are you one of the assassins?”

“Assassin?” the voice repeated, followed by a soft, velvety laugh. The sound was almost playful—on its own, it might’ve been charming. But in the dead silence of the blood-soaked district, it was nothing short of chilling.

“Yes, I suppose I’ve been called that before.”

Zion’s eyes narrowed. “Show yourself! Or are you just a coward hiding in the shadows?”

More laughter rang out, but this time it carried a sharper, darker edge—like silk drawn over a blade. Zion and Christian looked around warily, unsettled by their inability to locate the speaker.

Suddenly, a rush of wind swept through the street.

Clink.

A metallic clang echoed as a dagger landed at their feet. It was simple in design, unadorned—except for the vivid streak of red smeared along its edge.

Christian’s frown deepened. He turned to Zion—then froze.

A thin cut marked the Lieutenant Commander’s cheek, a sliver of blood glistening under the lamplight.

Zion’s hand slowly rose to touch the wound. The color drained from his face as realization dawned.

Whoever they were dealing with had just delivered a warning—and they hadn’t even seen her move.

“Choose your words carefully, Lieutenant Commander,” the voice warned. The earlier playfulness was gone, replaced by an unmistakable edge of threat. “I’ve been called many things… but a coward is not one of them.”

Silence followed.

Zion stood still, his jaw clenched tight. Then, after a tense beat, he cupped his fist and bowed deeply.

“My sincerest apologies, Miss. Please forgive my words.”

Gasps broke out among the soldiers. Even Christian looked stunned.

The sight of their superior bowing in submission only served to heighten the already suffocating tension. The air felt colder now—not from the wind, but from fear.

Zion remained bowed for what felt like an eternity. No response came. Only the soft whistle of wind weaving between buildings.

Just as some began to wonder if the assassin had already vanished—

The soft tapping of footsteps echoed from deeper within the noble district.

Weapons were raised, eyes scanned wildly—everyone turned toward the sound.

Everyone but Zion.

Emerging from the flickering lamplight came a lithe figure dressed in tight black robes that clung to her form like a second skin, revealing her sultry curves. Her movements were fluid, feline—every step both graceful and lethal.

Her presence alone halted any soldier foolish enough to stare too long.

She stopped twenty feet away, giving them their first clear look.

She was stunning.

Even in the dim light, her beauty was undeniable—sharp, otherworldly, and cold. But there was no warmth in those deep brown eyes. Only calculation. Only danger.

She looked at them the way a hawk looks at mice—unmoved, unbothered, unmerciful.

Deep brown eyes stared back at them—not with warmth, but with a cold, predatory stillness. It felt less like they were being watched, and more like they were being measured.

Like prey beneath the gaze of something far above them in the food chain.

“I have a question, Lieutenant Commander,” she purred. This time, her voice didn’t echo—it was close, intimate, as if spoken directly into his ear. A playful smile danced on her lips, but it never reached her eyes.

Zion shivered. Slowly, he lifted his head, finally daring to meet her gaze.

“What is it?” he asked, his voice just barely steady.

A silence fell between them. Long. Heavy. Every soldier in the formation held their breath, unwilling to break the tension.

Then the woman spoke.

“Which master do you serve?”
