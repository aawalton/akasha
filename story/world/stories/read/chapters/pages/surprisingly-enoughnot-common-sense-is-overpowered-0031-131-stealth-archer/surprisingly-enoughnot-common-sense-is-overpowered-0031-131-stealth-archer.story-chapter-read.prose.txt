Glancing at the fallen body, Ning couldn’t help but tremble.

As a cultivator with heightened senses, it took him only a moment to realize that the “senior sister” lying on the ground had no breath, and he couldn't sense any spiritual fluctuation at all.

In simpler terms, she wasn’t alive.

He had walked into a weapon shop, talked to the saleswoman, and she promptly collapsed.

Having watched plenty of detective shows in his previous life, Ning knew exactly how this story usually went.

The one who discovered the body was always the prime suspect. Even worse, he was the only person in the shop.

He was cooked. In this unreasonable world, even a little suspicion was enough to get him in trouble.

His thoughts spiraled.

'Was this a setup? A revenge plot? Should he bury the body? Where would he even bury the body? Should he flee the sect? Could he even flee the sect?!'

Just as he imagined sixty-seven different scenarios ending with his wrongful execution by the Law Enforcement Hall, someone entered.

Ning froze.

Because the person who walked in… looked exactly like the “dead” senior sister.

His mind supplied the only logical conclusion:

Twins?!

But that theory died instantly when the newcomer walked over expressionlessly, grabbed the “corpse” by the ankle, lifted it like a sack of rice, and stuffed it into her storage bag without the slightest hesitation.

Ning stared, horrified beyond measure.

Then the newcomer spoke.

“…This. Puppet.”

Her voice was flat. Her expression did not shift even half a millimeter.

Ning blinked. “P… puppet?”

The truth struck him instantly.

The cheerful, hyperactive, dangerously enthusiastic senior sister from earlier… wasn’t real.

A puppet refiner. One of the many professions in this world. It involved building a puppet and controlling it to perform tasks.

Looking back, Ning realized he really hadn’t felt any warmth in his body when she dragged him. But then again, it was difficult to focus on details while dealing with such a whirlwind of chaos.

Now that he understood the situation, Ning let out a long sigh. He wanted to give the real senior sister a piece of his mind.

Did she know how terrifying it was to have someone drop dead mid-conversation?

But the moment he sensed the faint Foundation Establishment pressure emanating from her, all desire to complain evaporated.

While puppets were quite handy, cultivators required a spiritual sense to operate them. Moreover, operating the puppet with such ease showed that the senior sister's spiritual sense and qi control were way beyond anything Ning had encountered yet.

Alas. In this world, those with the bigger fist had the bigger right.

While Ning was still having melodramatic thoughts, the real senior sister, Ling Yao, spoke.

“Junior Brother. Your… weapon?”

“Yes.” After everything that happened, he only wanted to buy a weapon and leave as quickly as possible.

She stared at him for three long seconds. “Look.”

Unlike the puppet, who had been terrifyingly energetic, the real Ling Yao was the complete opposite.

She was stone-faced and quiet. Even her speech was so minimal it was concerning.

Truly, an unsuitable personality for a shopkeeper. No wonder she relied on puppets to run the front.

Though… the puppet was likely configured based on her own inner thoughts, so was that the personality that this real senior sister wanted to exhibit? Ning mused.

“Hm?” Senior Sister Ling Yao suddenly glanced at him.

Such sharp instincts!

Ning just tilted his head innocently. He even blinked innocently, too.

There was nothing to see here, nope!

If the senior sister had found out something, she didn't say, but led him across the store. He followed her quietly through the rows of swords, spears, sabers, and hammers.

Ling Yao stood silently beside him like a guardian statue, waiting for him to choose so she could return to whatever ascetic activity she did in the back.

Ning’s eyes scanned the racks. His gaze landed on a simple yet elegant bow hanging in the corner.

Dark, polished wood streaked with faint cerulean lines. It looked kind of plain compared to the others, but Ning rather liked the simple appearance.

Ning stepped closer and tested its flexibility, rigidity, and how it felt in his hands. “Senior Sister... this bow?”

She blinked once, slowly. “...Good.”

“Does it have any special properties?” Ning didn't underestimate its simple appearance. Who knew what kind of enchantments it might have? Maybe something like auto-aim or true damage.

“...Shoots straight and fast.”

Ning took a very deep breath to steady himself. “How much is it?”

“150 spirit stones.”

A fair price. Azureback Beast Spine Wood was flexible, resilient, and an excellent conductor of spiritual energy, making it a proper first-tier low-grade weapon.

So, while it wasn't quite the overpowered bow Ning had been hoping for, he could work with it.

“Can I get a discount?” Ning asked with his best puppy-dog eyes.

Ling Yao nodded… then shook her head. “…No. But ten arrows free.”

Ning nodded. “Alright.”

She handed him the bow without flourish, without a single word of salesmanship, simply placing it in his arms like she was giving him a broom.

Then she pointed toward the door.

“…Go.”

Ning blinked. “…Go?”

“…Puppet needs repair.”

“…Ah.”

He bowed politely, despite her lack of reaction, and stepped outside.

Only after leaving the shop did he exhale deeply.

“Truly, the people in this sect are way too diverse when they are not facing the protagonist.” Ning sighed, glancing at the abruptly closed door.

Weapons.

From the moment some prehistoric human picked up a pointy stick and thought, "Huh... hitting things is much easier with this," civilization had begun its long march toward dominance. Tools shaped history, built kingdoms, and decided wars.

Even in a world where cultivators could throw fireballs from their palms and punch boulders into gravel, the principle remained surprisingly unchanged.

A good weapon was still a good weapon.

After an entire year in the sect, Ning had finally reached the point where he could afford one of his own. After all, fighting barehanded was a privilege reserved for protagonists blessed with absurd physiques and even more absurd plot armor.

He possessed neither.

Broadly speaking, weapon cultivators fell into two categories.

Sword cultivators.

And everyone else.

Of course, "sword" was a rather flexible definition. Whether it was straight, curved, three meters long, as wide as a door, or suspiciously similar to a metal rod, as long as the wielder insisted it was a sword, then it somehow qualified.

The Sect Master was the perfect example. His Dao revolved around a single belief: one sword shatters ten thousand techniques. Every battle, every insight, and every breakthrough in his cultivation ultimately returned to that single blade. Rumor even claimed he slept with his sword within arm's reach every night. Whether that was true or merely another exaggerated story about sword cultivators, Ning had no desire to find out.

But he couldn't deny their dedication.

Nor could he deny the terrifying strength such dedication produced.

Unfortunately, that path simply wasn't for him.

Ning appreciated swords as much as any other man. They were elegant, powerful, and undeniably cool. But admiring a weapon and devoting one's entire Dao to it were two entirely different matters. He was, above all else, a pragmatist. He had no ambition to become some peerless Sword Immortal.

His only ambition was to become Ji Ning, the cultivator who kept surviving. If a weapon improved those odds, then he'd use it without hesitation.

After weighing his options, he eventually settled on the bow.

Its advantages were obvious. It allowed him to attack from a safe distance, reduced unnecessary risks, and suited his cautious temperament far better than charging headfirst into battle.

Then another realization struck him.

Looking back at everything he had learned over the past year, he realized he had accidentally built the perfect toolkit for a very specific archetype.

The inevitable final form of every Skyrim player.

The stealth archer.

His Turtle Breathing Technique concealed his presence almost completely, while Shadow Steps allowed him to move without drawing attention. Months of cultivation had sharpened his eyesight far beyond that of an ordinary person, and he had even succeeded in extracting a mild toxin from Poison Lilies-nothing lethal, but certainly unpleasant enough to make early-stage spiritual beasts regret being on the receiving end of an arrow.

The more Ning thought about it, the more ridiculous the combination became.

Hidden presence. Silent movement. Long-range attacks. Poison.

He nodded to himself in satisfaction. "...Honestly, this build is kind of broken."

No wonder every Skyrim playthrough somehow ended with a stealth archer. Even in another world, the strategy was disgustingly effective.
