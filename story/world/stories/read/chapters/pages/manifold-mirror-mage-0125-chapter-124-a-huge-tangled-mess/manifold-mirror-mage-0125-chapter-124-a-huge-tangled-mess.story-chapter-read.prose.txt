Tatters of blue thread drifted through the air. Before they could touch the floor, Saint Baphelus reached out with magic and captured every last fiber in his hand.

“I may not be in tune with this Weave of yours, Arseya… but even I felt that,” the lich responded. He watched with cautious curiosity as the destroyed threads wriggled like worms in his grasp, staining his conjured flesh blue and producing foul-smelling oil that began running up his arm.

“Ah,” Said the lich. “This will need to be destroyed thoroughly. I may need to consult with-”

Chandrika’s Aunt Mata approached, holding up a palm. “Saint Baphelus, if I may?”

Saint Baphelus cocked an eyebrow before rolling up his sleeve and holding his arm out to the side. “Go ahead.”

The mysterious woman and apparent spymaster activated some sort of hidden device on her person, causing dark metal plates to engulf her right arm, forming into a gauntlet and vambrace set that pulsed with malicious energy. Her eyes momentarily nictitated as a lizard’s would, a secondary unnatural membrane closing and then revealing black sclera with glowing purple irises that shone like amethysts under moonlight. Mata’s strange gauntlet blasted out a beam of bleak energy that engulfed the corrupting thread (as well as Saint Baphelus’s hand) in an annihilating light. As the light faded, Saint Baphelus withdrew a skeletal arm, completely devoid of flesh. He gave the hand a few perfunctory squeezes, as if to make sure that his bones were still intact.

“Now that is what I call a deep clean. Quite an interesting relic to bring to a civil get-together.” The saint lightly prodded.

A relic?!

Gio thought of the necklace that still rested at the heart of Telchine’s Garden. Gio looked at Chandrika’s aunt, trying and failing to detect any trace of the relic on her person.

Such power… and to be completely concealed when not in use… scary.

Mata simply bowed her head out of respect, wordlessly returning to the side of her sister, the Queen.

“As I said, we weren't sure what we were walking into. I wasn’t exactly expecting a tea party.” Arseya sniped back.

“Hahaha!” Baphelus chortled. “Then I hope to continue to ruin your expectations. A tea party, you say? I would love to make that a reality.”

The Saint of Magic summoned a banquet table, tableware, new flesh for his arm, and a magical doorway leading to the school kitchens, all with a snap. Twelve copies of Chef Marie Bain dutifully marched forward from the door, wheeling trays of finger sandwiches and jam tarts forward and setting them in front of the delegates.

“Well,” Began a still-recovering Arseya, “the bad news is that someone has possession of a piece of his body… perhaps several pieces. This is troubling because I should have the whole thing locked away in a deep, dark, secure vault under my spire. Eudoria, your agents will likely be looking for a braided piece of black hair.”

The Headmaster nodded, clicking her fingernails against the table.  She did not betray any spark of magic or other indication that she was in communication with anyone, but still spoke after a long pause.

“We found it,” she announced. “It should be on the way shortly.”

“Formidable as always,” Arseya noted.

“What is the good news, then?” Gio asked.

“The good news, my boy… is that the Cannibal King’s curses were black,” the old weaver answered. “When I killed him, I wrapped him in a woven coffin of his own pitch-black web of hair, fate, and hunger before driving threaded needles through his many eyes and hearts. It took some work to make his death stick… I remember it as if it were yesterday. These blue threads… I don’t know exactly what it means, but it means that no sage works against us… which was my greatest fear. If one of our number had betrayed us and had obtained a relic of that man…they could use my predecessor’s black magic just as he did. As it stands, I believe that our enemy is ham-fistedly using magic that they do not fully understand.”

“So we need not fear another with the power of the Cannibal King.” Baphelus nodded. “Small mercies.”

“This does put my mind at ease,” Eudoria answered. “The girl was good at stealing luck… I stagger to imagine the harm Amity could do with the ability to consume the fate of another.”

“Consume… fate…” Gio squinted.

“Indeed. A fearsome power.” Baphelus noted.

“Even still…” Aayana hummed. “This girl, that book, and then the puppeting of the poor Sundleah boy, are all gravely alarming matters. Someone is using [Sage] magic to spin a tangled web of their own… and they intend for my family to pay the price for it. With today’s misunderstanding cleared, I would like to begin talking about what we will do about it.”

The Queen’s sharp wit and even tone seemed to give life to her words. Even without a social skill to manipulate his feelings, Gio felt the desire to fix his posture in her presence.

“What can we do?” Chandrika asked. “We don’t actually know anything about this ‘enemy’ of ours. I’ve been assuming that it’s the same guy who tried to kill Gio last night… but what’s to say that he’s working alone? For all we know, this blue thread business could be unrelated. There’s also the matter of the Ataraxian Princess…”

“He called you dangerous, Rika. He said that you were a threat that needed to be dealt with… who knows how far he would go to accomplish that?” Gio retorted.

Chandrika bit her lip.

Before she could respond, an inquisitor approached. The masked figure presented a wooden box, inside which a sad little pigtail of black hair sat lifelessly.

“That’s… odd.” Arseya frowned. “It’s a genuine article, but it feels like someone wrung the magic out of it like an old dishcloth.”

Saint Baphelus took the braid and examined it.

“Sloppy work,” Baphelus grunted. “It has the fingerprints of a system-granted skill on it. This was used as a one-time-use artifact, similar to what Gio did with the Torrential Reservoir Key.”

“That was the name of the thing that I broke to kill the dragon, right?” Gio asked.

“Yes. Much like this lock of hair could have been used by a proper [Sage] to channel the abilities of the dead tyrant Mahishasura, that key could have been used by a trained mage to siphon mana from a secret reservoir that powered the potent enchantments that kept that particular vault hidden from my domain. You clumsily - yet ingeniously used it to sunder that enchantment open like an egg, sucking out all of its power for a single, inefficient use of your reflection spell. I would wager that our mystery man used a skill to accomplish something similar with the braid: a one-time use of Mahishasura’s binding and control magic.” Baphelus explained.

“But he did it twice,” Chandrika noted. “The boy that they blamed me for, and now this.”

“Perhaps the braid lasted through two uses. Or perhaps he has more braids.” Baphelus shrugged.

“That should be improbable. For that to be true, my father would have had to hide his hair during his sane years before his corruption. As I have stated, I know for certain that his remains have not been accessed since his death- as his remnants still infect a divine artifact in the heart of Bangla.” Arseya replied.

“Is it not possible that he could have given away some of his hair?” Headmaster Vespertine asked.

“I… I suppose he could have.” Arseya admitted. “But for it to have made it this long without resurfacing, only to be used as a consumable item… I suppose it does bring me some small amount of joy to imagine my shameful father’s last act of relevance to the world amounting to being used as he used others.”

“Grandmother… I know that this troubles you, but could we not… ask him?” Chandrika wondered.

“Under no circumstances are you, or any of our number, to engage with his ghost.” The Grand Weaver coolly commanded. “I will not yield on this matter. The risk of his corruption spreading is too dire.”

“I understand.” Chandrika readily conceded. “But then, where does that lead us? The Ataraxians?”

“Queen Vera has her own concerns. This fugitive debacle has been a national embarrassment for the Grand Coven, and they are overstepping boundaries on several fronts to try to recover the girl and her companion.” Eudoria candidly spoke, to the surprise of many present. “I cannot imagine that she would be picking fights or stepping on my toes so brazenly while she deals with that mess.”

“But… what about their Goddess?” Gio asked. “We saw her manipulating dungeon creatures in [Telchine’s Garden]... for some unknown reason.”

“I cannot begin to postulate on the whims of the divine,” Eudoria replied. “To my understanding, Cybele is a Goddess of pacts and laws- her presence in that place could have been some ancient vow, or some other such confounding thing. It is… possible, perhaps, but then I would struggle to place Ataraxia’s motives in this puzzle.”

Gio looked at the gold-masked Inquisitor standing behind the Headmaster. He quickly thought of an idea.

“Could you… Extract my memory?” Gio asked. “Maybe there’s some detail about the stranger in my memories that I’m not properly explaining.”

Headmaster Vespertine’s eyebrows raised. “Are you certain, Giorgio? I would not ask for such an invasion of privacy under normal circumstances.”

“Respectfully, Headmaster, I think that this guy tried to kill my best friend and me, or have us killed, at least twice, not even counting the dragon. These are not normal circumstances.” Gio said.

She hesitated for a moment before nodding slowly.

“Very well.” She said.

Arnault stepped forward, still wearing his featureless reflective dome. He placed his hands on Gio’s temples, and Gio was transported back to the events of last evening.

“[Share Memory]”

The man stood before them. Gio was shocked at his own memory, unaware of just how detailed the image could be. Gio walked closer to the projected image, marvelling at the rips in the man’s leather trenchcoat.

“Interesting.” Saint Baphelus said. “Blazing blue eyes, blonde hair… He’s a Firekind.”

Gio turned to his mentor.

“A Firekind? If I recall my history classes, I thought the Firekind were a type of extinct fire elemental.” Gio guffawed.

“They were.” Baphelus agreed. “Humanoid, mostly flesh and blood, but yes, fire elementals. Their unique physiology makes it difficult for them to reproduce, which is why we mourned the loss of their species when my sister transcended her physical form. Saint Serelei was a Firekind… and we thought that she was the last. She will want to know about this."

Weird.

Headmaster Vespertine clicked her tongue, which seemed to instantaneously cause Arnault to stiffen.

“I will investigate. Let us discuss that matter later.”

“Chandrika is dangerous, and Jean… would have been an unfortunate sacrifice… but you? I can’t say that I’ve grieved you.”
