I'd expected the barrier between zones in Penumbra to be large, yet this wall exceeded my wildest expectations. It stretched at least a dozen stories high, made of stone, yet seamless. No indication smaller blocks were stacked and mortared together. Sins would not be able to scale or break this monstrosity of engineering.

Though—looking closer—perhaps monstrosity was the wrong word. Marvel was more apt. For there, carved into the surface across the entire length as far as the eye could see, was a grayscale depiction of what lay beyond the wall. Tall grasses in the foreground, endless fields of flowers, patches of forest in the distance. Birds and creatures watching, statuesque in the stone.

It was beautiful, even more so than the impressive map illustrated on the Meridian's ceiling.

A rustling of leaves reminded me of the real creatures watching me now.

The monkey creatures were a dark blue in fur. More of them had arrived, and more continued to flow toward us through the trees, chattering to one another. Uncertain. Agitated.

Several leapt from bench to branch, pounding their hands on the bark or on their companions shoulders. Faint trails of smoke followed like an after image as they moved, the same as with Nuzzle and the birds and other critters I'd seen in the Meadow.

Though, now that I thought about it, Nuzzle's trail seemed to be far less distinct that it had been when I first met him. The lizard prowled on my shoulders, feeding off the anxiety of the other creatures. He spat at the monkeys, eyes wild, trying to warn them all that he'd destroy them if any came near. Of course, even after his growth, he was about a quarter the size of these beasts.

"Shh…" Trix whispered, stepping back toward the cave without taking her eyes off the gathering monkeys. They had to number nearly a hundred now. "As much as I'd love to party with these fellas, there's far too many of them. We've learned all we need for now."

I followed her lead, backing to the mouth of the cave.

A twig snapped beneath my foot.

In one horrendous chorus, the monkey's screeched. A harrowing cacophony that sent chills down my spine.

Then they charged.

We sprinted into the cave. Trix drew up a curtain of roots to cover the opening, but the monkeys swung through the gaps with agile grace, slowed but undeterred.

Animalistic hoots and hollers ricocheted off the cave walls, like the war song of an angry mob. They were faster than us. I could already feel them at my back. A tidal wave of shadow priming to strike.

Trix summoned another cluster of roots, this time, surrounding us in a sphere. More roots joined the cage, shrinking the holes, blocking even the minimal light in the cave. Clawed hands reached in. A simian face appeared beside me, just outside the protective root system. It smiled, wide jaws lined with fangs. Then it snapped at me, head squeezing to try to get through. Spittle sprayed my face and I backed away as it reached fingers through and almost grabbed my wrist.

Nuzzle chomped at it and took off a thumb, which he proudly tucked into his cheek like a hunk of chewing tobacco. The monkey howled and yanked at the roots like a hysterical prisoner in a cell. Unfortunately, we were the ones trapped.

My heart pounded as we huddled in the center of the sphere. More monkeys groped and hissed at us from all sides. Nuzzle hissed back, but I kept him out of range of their swipes. Trix appeared fatigued. Maintaining this was using a lot of Volition. I used Life Drain on a few of the more bold beasts and Trix skewered some with a projectile spade she summoned.

Most of the monkeys broke off and continued past us, realizing we weren't worth the effort. We took care of the stragglers and soon the throng of the beasts was nothing more than a distant echo traveling down the tunnel.

Toward the Meadow.

"Ugh… Not good," Trix grunted, kicking the deteriorating remains of the root cage in the dirt.

"We just released a barrel of monkeys into the wild, didn't we…?"

"They're harmless in small groups, but this horde… It could be a problem if they come across anyone out on their own."

"Shit." My mind flashed to the two girls and their father who led us to the cave.

Trix's face went pale, coming to the same realization I had. "They might have made it back to the village," she said, though I could tell she didn't believe it.

Our fears were realized when we came across a graveyard of monkey corpses a few minutes from Talbot Village. The father had killed a score or so before being phased out to Vitria in what was likely brutal fashion—if the bodies of his Penumbran daughters were any indication. Clothes were torn and bloody, limbs missing. Hair ripped out. Eye sockets empty. At the crest of a nearby hill, one of the straggling monkeys stared down at us drenched in blood. It held a severed arm in its teeth.

I vomited into the grass.

By the time I recovered, the monkey was gone.

Trix didn't try to console me. She simply waited, back turned to the carnage. Nuzzle just slurped up my vomit.

When we returned to the village, she informed the elder of what happened. The monkey's hadn't come this way, likely making for one of the nearby forests.

I mounted my undead horse. They probably wouldn't have travelled far once they found trees to call home.

"Where do you think you're going?" Trix asked.

"Hunting."

"No, you're not."

"What do you mean I'm not? We can't just leave them out there to kill like this again."

Trix grabbed the reigns of my horse, holding me from steering the animal away. "They aren't our problem anymore."

"What do you mean they aren't our problem? We caused this!"

Trix had sealed the cave as best she could when we left to prevent any more creatures from entering the Meadow that weren't supposed to be here, but that wasn't enough to ease my conscience. We had an obligation to fix this.

"Their deaths aren't on you."

"If we hadn't gone in there, they'd be alive!" I pictured the girl's faces. So young… The father would be heartbroken. The mother too, wherever she was.

"You need to look at the big picture, Zephyr." She tugged my horse's reigns tighter, pulling my attention to her face. "Whoever created that tunnel in the first place… That's the enemy here. That's who caused this. Not you. Not me. Them. We discovered where the influx of Unchained are coming from. How high level Sins are appearing in the Meadow. Now isn't the time for a side quest. It's time to attack the source."

"How the hell are we gonna do that?" I snapped. "We don't even know who the source is."

"I have an idea," Trix said. "And it's not the Shepherds like you were thinking. If anything, they might be opposed to this whole situation. Why would a cult who worships the Sins want to enslave them?"

That made sense, but it didn't explain Lancelot's presence at the lighthouse. Unless my suspicion that he was a Shepherd were unfounded and he was actually involved in this madness. Or, perhaps the Shepherds were investigating this like me.

"Care to enlighten me?" I suggested.

"You'd mentioned that the Iron Reaper you saw had an emblem on his cloak, like us."

I nodded, not entirely sure where she was going with this.

"Every god oversees their realm of Penumbra a little differently. Some have different views on how things should be done. And some… some are more aggressive about their stance than others."

"What are you saying?"

Trix sighed, annoyed at having to spell it out. "Those monkeys weren't just perched there, right outside the cave, for fun. They were waiting. These tunnels aren't random. They're coordinated. Someone is overseeing all of this."

Realization dawned on me. "The god of the jungle zone..? Does he have that kind of power? To control animals like that?"

"Not he. She. And no. Not control, but influence? Sure." Trix finally released my reigns and extracted her scythe. "You want to go hunting, Zephyr? Monkeys are small game. Think you can kill a god?"
