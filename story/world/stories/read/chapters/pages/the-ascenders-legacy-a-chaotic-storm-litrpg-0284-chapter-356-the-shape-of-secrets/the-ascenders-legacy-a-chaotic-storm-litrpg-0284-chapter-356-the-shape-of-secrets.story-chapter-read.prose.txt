A heavy silence descended over the room as Aodhán's words settled in the air.

His mother. An Ascendant.

It was an absurd thing to say out loud, and at first Nymera thought it was an awkward attempt at a joke. But the expression on Aodhán's face was too raw for a joke.

"Aodhán." She squeezed his hands gently. "Are you sure you're alright?"

“I’m fine… I,” He pulled his hands from hers and ran them through his hair. "How is this possible?"

"It is not," Elder Gyatso replied slowly, his frown deepening with worry. "Perhaps there is a resemblance—"

“No, no.” Aodhán shook his head. “I know what I’m saying. I can recognize my own mother.”

"Okay." Nymera's voice was calm but firm, grounding him before his emotions could sweep him under. "Say you're right. How is it possible?"

Aodhán paused, eyes narrowing as he thought. But after several seconds, he exhaled sharply. “I don’t know, but you have to admit it is weird that he found the legacy seed in my house. In the last place I saw my mother.”

Meredith shook her head. "Ascendants drop legacies on foreign worlds all the time. This could be a coincidence."

"No." A few months ago he might have entertained that idea. But after everything Zarxan had told him, he doubted anything in his past was a coincidence. Zarxan had said it was very likely that Az'marthon had been present the night of the fire that took his parents. His mother. But if his mother was an Ascendant—how could she have died in a house fire? How would Az'marthon not have sensed her?

He shook his head again. It didn't make sense. And yet the image before him couldn't be disputed.

That was his mother.

He knew it the way he knew his own name. He was not confused.

"Aodhán," Elder Gyatso said quietly, drawing his gaze. "When you came to the monastery, you had no recollection of your life before the fire. No memory of what your parents looked like."

"And that in itself is strange," Aodhán pushed back. “I survived three days in a dangerous forest without food or water. I conveniently get found by a group of travelers that bring me to the monastery. And then eleven years later, fate pulls me to a cave holding my patron’s legacy only miles from my home where, conveniently, another legacy seed awaited—untouched by time, preserved for my return because I would have returned.” His voice tightened. "Someone knew I would return."

"Aodhán—"

"Don't you see it?" His hands were in his hair now, eyes wide. "Either way, I would have made it to AEFLYM. My transmigration wasn't an accident. And that is my mother."

Another beat of silence passed, but Aodhán could see that despite their best efforts to believe him, Nymera and Elder Gyatso were having a hard time doing so.

Their eyes held the deepest disbelief , the look of people watching someone they cared about come apart at the seams. As though he had suddenly become unhinged.

He couldn’t blame them. Even to him, it sounded like a string of coincidences he was desperately knotting together. It made no sense on the surface. But the image before him…

Aodhán shook his head. He wasn’t confused. There was something here. He just needed someone to shed some light on it.

Someone to believe him. And that someone, clearly, wasn't in this room.

“I have to go,” he jumped to his feet immediately and teleported out before anyone could stop him.

Nymera called after him, but Aodhán didn’t respond. He was out in the open air an instant later, winds howling around him as he flew straight to the administration building, tapping his communication ring continuously.

His mind raced with ideas, with impossible scenarios that simply couldn’t all just be coincidences.

He needed answers—or at least—a semblance of order in all this confusion.

And there was only one man who could give him that.

Zarxan Al’ruhn.

High Elder Zarxan sat within his cultivator chamber, meditating.

He had just arrived from the elder hall, from another war statistic hearing, and despite the seemingly endless amount of strength flowing through his veins, he felt weary—his mind fraught.

He needed rest—not sleep, exactly, but a moment to close his eyes and exist alone with his thoughts.

The last three days had been fraught with mourning, hearings, and meetings concerning the war. But while it was mentally taxing, Zarxan was just grateful he had the chance to complain at all. Things could have gone differently very easily.

The air heaved as he let out an exhale.

He had become too invested in the war. Too emotionally attached.

He could still remember the fear he’d felt when that attack slammed into the viewing deck—the pure unadulterated terror that gripped him when he thought he’d lost Az’marthon’s legacy forever.

He, a Calamity, reduced terror. He was too attached.

But how could he not be? Aodhán Brystion was like a nephew to him, even if the boy didn't know it yet. Perhaps even more than that, if his suspicions were true. And he had not pulled Aodhán from the jaws of certain death in Lutia only to lose him two months later to the same war.

If not to Az’marthon, he owed it to himself to keep that boy alive. And the only way to do so was to keep him and the other transmigrants within the sect walls while the Mythics and Calamities fought on the battlefield.

Unfortunately, he doubted their enemies would let them do that.

Sighing again, Zarxan let himself settle deeper into the tranquility of the moment, space shifting to accommodate his conceptual weight. The peace engulfed him, and slowly, his mind began to slip into a meditative state—the noise and weight of reality fading as his consciousness shifted inward.

However, just as space settled, and his mind pierced cleanly into the Origin Plane, Zarxan felt a disruption echo through his spirit.

Knock. Knock.

The sound was like that of metal grinding against stone and it sheared through his meditation like a knife through butter. Anger surged first, then frustration as his consciousness was yanked back into reality, the delicate peace he’d only begun feeling, evaporating like smoke.

The disruption came again—audible now that his consciousness had returned to reality and with gritted teeth, he called out. “What is it now, Luseph?”

“It is the boy,” Luseph’s voice echoed from behind the heavy door. “He’s requesting to see you. He seems very… agitated.”

Every iota of frustration vanished immediately and Zarxan frowned. “Did he say what it was about?”

“No,” Luseph’s voice came again. “It did seem very personal though. Shall I take him to a private hall?”

“No,” Zarxan shook his head. “Bring him here.”

“What?” Luseph asked, shock obvious in his tone. “You want me to bring him… here? Into your private cultivation room?”

“He can handle it. Bring him in, I’ll wait for him here.”

Luseph couldn’t believe his ears. The cultivation chamber of every cultivator was a place considered sacred, only to be breached by the owner themselves. And for a cultivator as advanced as Zarxan, that sacredness was even more serious.

To invite a mere advanced cultivator into that space was unheard of.

But then again, Aodhán had never been just another transmigrant to Zarxan. Luseph didn't understand their relationship—Zarxan wouldn't tell him anything—but at this point, with this decision, it was obvious: Zarxan considered the boy family.

Back within the cultivation chamber, Zarxan had spread his aura to engulf the entire space, enforcing his authority on the essence to make it less hostile for when Aodhán arrived.

However, five minutes later, when the door was finally pushed open, he realized he’d wasted his time. Aodhán barely felt the hostile essence. He barely even seemed to notice it, his emotions too agitated to be affected by a little hostile essence.

“High Elder Zarxan,” he greeted with a bow, his voice heavy. “I need to ask you some questions about Az’marthon. About my relationship with him.”

Zarxan frowned, feeling a tension settle over him immediately. “He’s your patron is he not? What other relationship do you have with him?”

Aodhán looked up then, and Zarxan felt a slight shiver at the conviction in his eyes. “Is he my father?”

Zarxan went still. For a long moment, he didn't speak. Then very slowly, he gestured to the seat across from him.

“Tell me why you think so.”

It had taken Aodhán half an hour to get in touch with Luseph, and in that time he hadn't been able to stop thinking.

He still couldn't make sense of all of it. But he'd managed to press the pieces into some kind of shape, something he could hold up to the light and at least name.

He stared at the floor.

“My mother is an Ascendant.” he said quietly, then looked up at Zarxan, his gaze intent. “Did you know that?”

Zarxan blinked. Then shook his head. “No. I… I didn’t.”

Aodhán nodded. “Me too. I only just found out half an hour ago when I visited the space transmigrants and recognized one of them as a friend from my home world.”

He huffed a smile and shook his head. "He was the closest thing I had to a father after the fire that took my parents, and seeing him here, seeing him now,” his smile widened. “I was happy. Genuinely. Who would have thought right?”

Aodhán’s smile wobbled slightly. Half an hour ago, he would have considered his encounter with elder Gyatso a blessing. But now, he wasn’t so sure.

He took in a deep breath and continued. “We started talking, and after a while he showed me an image of his patron.”

His voice dropped.

"It was my mother, High Elder Zarxan. My mother. The same woman who had cared for me for the first six years of my life. The one who had told me to run while attackers burned our house down.” He shook his head, the words coming harder now. "That same woman is his patron. And I can't make sense of it. I can't—"

Aodhán hadn’t expected that. Even though he had come to Zarxan for this exact reason, he hadn’t expected Zarxan to believe him. Not truly.

And in that moment, his mind shifted from defensiveness to vulnerability.

His voice cracked. His vision blurred with tears, and when he next spoke, it was with sobs.

"Then why didn't he save her?" The words came out broken, wet with tears he couldn't hold back as the memory of his mother’s screams rose in his mind. “"Why did he follow me instead? Why didn't he fight them? Why didn't she fight them?"

“Aodhán—”

“No!” Aodhán cried. “She was a fucking Ascendant. I don’t care how weakened she was, how could she have died in a house fire?”

“You are assuming that she died!” Zarxan’s voice boomed, not loud, not thunderous, yet cutting straight through Aodhán’s spiral like a knife.

Aodhán stilled, his emotions roiling within him like a river of toxins.

“You are assuming that she died.” High Elder Zarxan said again, his voice gentler. He closed his eyes and let out a deep breath. “Now I didn’t know your mother, but I knew Az’marthon, and I knew he loved a woman—a space calamity who ascended decades before he did.”

It was around that time that Az’marthon grew exceedingly violent, slaughtering Sunstonians in their thousands every day. He acted on every order, moving from sector to sector and butchering thousands. This lasted for several years until he too, ascended, leaving Sunstone to sigh in relief.”
