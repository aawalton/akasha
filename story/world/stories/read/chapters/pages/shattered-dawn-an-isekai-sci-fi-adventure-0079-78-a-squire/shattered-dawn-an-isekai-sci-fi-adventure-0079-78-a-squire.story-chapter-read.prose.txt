Yaret emerged from the shadows as Elion turned off the washout shower.

“That was amazing!” he said. “Are you okay?”

Elion powered down, his wet armor clothing and breastplate swapping with his dry clothes. He coughed. “I think so. I hope I got all that acid out of my clothes.”

Yaret surveyed the scene. “What’s your plan now?”

Elion planted his hands on his hips, turning to face Yaret. “Who are you, really?” he demanded. “Why are you helping me?”

Yaret’s eyebrows raised. He took a half step back. “Me? I’m just Yaret.”

“You’re working for Prator? Or someone else?” Elion demanded.

“I’m bonded,” Yaret said. “I work for whoever has my bondhold.”

“Who is it now, then?” Elion asked. “And what are your orders? Don’t give me any kind of excuse or make up a story this time. I know what it feels like to feel compelled to obey an order.” He felt the prick at his conscience, the need to go back to Prator and obey him as he spoke. “You don’t have an order to go do a job up in the refinery. There’s no way you could have hung around here so long.”

Yaret rubbed his hands together, then pulled at his ear. “Well, I guess… If you must know…” He pulled a small metal stud from his earlobe. “I’m working for myself.”

“You have your own bondhold?” Elion exclaimed. “How did you get it? Why are you still here?”

Yaret grinned. “I’ll explain it to you another time. Where’s the girl?”

Elion eyed the man suspiciously, but then shrugged. He ran back to the empty barrel where he had stashed Keyla.

Her preserved body slumped at the bottom of it. Elion needed Yaret’s help to lift her, and position her limp limbs so that he could carry her over his shoulder. He renewed Save a Friend and they continued forward along the bottom of the pit.

When they reached the far side of the pit, a sloped path took them up to the ridge of the gorge. The point where they stood was higher than much of the surrounding land. Elion looked around, following the wall encircling New Kairn Tol.

In the distance, beyond the wall in every direction, lights flickered in the darkness.

Elion set Keyla down on a rock, breathing heavily. It felt good to be out of the pit, and the toxic fumes that settled down at the bottom of it. He wondered if he’d permanently injured his lungs. Yaret stripped off the sweaty protective suit that he wore.

Keyla, preserved by the magic ability, hadn’t been breathing. She wouldn’t be affected at all.

The vantage point allowed Elion a view of the streets of New Kairn Tol. The main road leading to the gate was lit up by lights in buildings and the occasional lamp. Dark figures hurried up and down the road, between the refinery and Prator’s mansion.

Arachnatronics scrambled around in the darkness, giving Elion the heebie-jeebies, like he’d just opened the door to a dark room and insects scrambled out of the light.

The warlock camp buzzed with activity. The man with the Elion-locator machine must have delivered his message by now. They’d be searching for him; would probably send a party down into the pit and discover what he’d done to their friends.

Elion gazed out past the wall. Somehow, the army had encircled New Kairn Tol. About a hundred yards away, the nearest portion of the wall stretched. The occasional guard moved along the wall there, focused outward.

“Now what?” Yaret asked. “What’s your plan?”

“We have two choices,” Elion said. “Try to escape over the wall, or head back to New Kairn Tol and try to reclaim our bondholds. Prator has mine, and I would guess that Venya has Keyla’s by now.”

“Ah,” Yaret said, nodding.

“What will happen to her if she wakes up? Is she going to fight to get back to the warehouse, and obey her orders?”

“Probably,” Yaret said.

“For how long?” Elion asked. “Will it ever wear off?”

“Bondholds are interesting things,” Yaret said. “I’ve watched a lot of men and women break themselves against them. There are only a few ways to truly break a bondhold, that I know of. You can abandon everything that made the object valuable to you. This generally leads to deep depression, or sometimes a kind of manic insanity.”

“Like Roban,” Elion said.

“Yes, like Roban. Or you find something more meaningful, more valuable to you, for the same reasons. And the bond will switch. This is easier to do, the longer the bondhold is out of your possession. Or you can get a Binder to remove the bond for you.”

“So eventually she’ll be able to break free of the bond,” Elion said. “If we just run. Get over that wall, and wait it out.”

“Most bonds break down with time. Bortim comes by the owned here and checks up on their bonds every couple of months, to make sure they’re not bonding more strongly to something else.”

“I don’t think we have a couple of months,” Elion said. “Besides, Venya can track me. I can’t evade her and fight Keyla every step of the way.”

“You could leave her here?” Yaret suggested.

“No!” Elion shouted, clenching his fists. “Sorry, but no,” he said. “We’re in this together.”

“Okay,” Yaret said. “Understood. What will you do then?”

“Why don’t you tell me what you’re doing here,” Elion said. “I don’t trust you.”

“You think it’s strange that I would be slaving away in the aid pit, doing dangerous work for no pay, when I’m not compelled to.”

“That about sums it up,” Elion said.

“The truth is,” Yaret said. “I’m afraid. I’m afraid of what Prator and Bortim do to people when they fall out of line, disobey, or try to escape.”

“What do you mean?”

“There are around a thousand people living in New Kairn Tol,” Yaret said. “Only about two hundred of them are owned. The rest live unbonded. But just because they haven’t been magically bonded doesn’t make them free. They’re constantly under pressure to work and conform. Anyone who tries to leave gets bonded, and any bonded who cause trouble, well, you saw what happened to Roban.”

“I can’t imagine anything worse than being a slave,” Elion said.

Yaret shrugged. “I get fed. I get to choose how I obey orders. And… I dream, planning for the day I’ll escape for once and for all.”

“So how did you get your bondhold, then?” Elion asked.

“I slipped it off of Boff’s wrist while he was sleeping,” Yaret said. “He was wearing it tied onto a bracelet.”

“How did you…”

“Well…” Yaret shrugged. “When I was a child I used to sleep on the floor. I called it my bed. When Boff told us not to leave our beds, I was lying on the floor. Since the whole floor was my bed, I didn’t leave it when I snuck into his room that night. As far as I know, he still hasn’t noticed that my bondhold is missing.”

“How long ago was that?”

“It’s been about six months,” Yaret said. “I’ve been waiting for the right time to do something about it. But with two ascendants dropping in to help me lid barrels, I think I might have found the right time.”

Elion nodded. “I see,” he said. “Well, thank you for your help. With all the chaos tonight, I can’t imagine anyone will think it odd if you disappear. The path is clear for you; go out over that wall, and then disappear into the night.”

Yaret chuckled. “The path is clear for me, but that is not it.” He looked around. “Will you answer me truthfully, if I ask you a question?”

“I will,” Elion said. “As a knight, I’m sworn to the truth.”

Yaret shifted, scuffing the ground with his boot. “I’ve merely been observing a few things,” he said. “Dorian’s right hand warlock shows up, obviously looking for something. Prator takes a special interest in you. You turn out to be an Aurelian knight, traveling in the company of an Artificer. You’re young and naive, but brave and quick thinking. So I just have to ask.”

Elion’s heart pounded. He knew what question was coming. *Maybe I should have so readily agreed to be honest,* he thought, and *Is it really that easy to figure out?*

“Are you a Starholder? The missing heir to the throne, perhaps?”

*I am a Starholder. If Liora still lives, then she is next in line to the throne, not me. Perhaps I can—*

“I am,” Elion said, opting for the honest answer.

In the darkness, Yaret smiled broadly. “You have an interesting story to tell,” he said. “I would like to hear it one day, but this satisfies me tonight. Kylios has long awaited the time when you would return and bring back our days of prosperity.”

Elion choked. *I’m just here to find Liora and bring her back to Earth.* But he didn’t share his thoughts.

Yaret continued. “I told you about the destruction of my home and my family. I did not tell you that I was saved by a knight.” He looked at Elion with earnest sincerity. “On that day, he pulled me from the flames, saving my life. But my family was dead, and the pain too great for me to remain, so I traveled with him, and became his squire.”

Yared scratched the back of his neck. “For many years I helped him help people. Those were the greatest years of my life. They meant something. But at The Breaking of The Sky we were betrayed by Dorian and my knight was killed. I fell into a terrible depression.”

Elion watched Yaret. The man told his story haltingly, memories bringing strong emotion to the surface.

“Time passed, and a deep longing to return to my home came over me. Like a voice, calling to me, telling me that I must return to my home, and make peace with my past. So I began my journey. As I traveled, my heart lightened, and my spirits lifted. I came to the place where my home used to stand, in a green field. All that remained of it was a scorched foundation.”

Tears dripped from Yaret’s eyes. “I was overcome with memories, and emotions. For many days, pain burned in my heart. But I stayed, sitting there in the ruins of my old life, and let the fire that I had once escaped burn inside me. I wondered why I, of all my family, had been spared. And then I saw Aurelia, standing in the grass before me. ‘I need you,’ she said. Then she disappeared.”

Elion found himself nodding as he listened to Yaret’s tale. Just one month ago he would have laughed at the man’s story, but now, after all he’d experienced, Yaret’s words rang true. Elion had similar experiences.

“So I’ve been searching for an Aurelian,” Yaret said. “That’s why I came here, to New Kairn Tol. I thought that Prator was the man I was meant to serve. The first day that I saw you, down in the pits, I had the strange feeling that it might be you. Now, I know.”

Yaret knelt in front of Elion, clasping his hands together and bowing his head. “Elion Starholder, heir to the Throne, will you accept my services as your squire?”

Elion flapped his mouth, uncertain of what he should say. The moment called out for kind, brave words, some emotion to share and seal the gesture. *Is this a ritual thing? Are there right things for me to say?*

All Elion felt was fear, stress, and anxiety. He wanted to help people. But this? Elion was not the world-saving, messianic figure that Yaret seemed to think he was. And yet he couldn’t say ‘no.’

“Okay, Yaret,” Elion said, sighing deeply. “I won’t hold you to anything, but if you’re willing to help me tonight, I’ll accept.”

“Thank you, sir,” Yaret said. “You won’t regret it.”

Elion hoped not.

“What are we going to do then?” Yaret asked.

Elion held up a hand. Something crunched nearby. His ears prickled. A faint rustle of sparse foliage sent his heart racing. This wasn’t wind; someone was out there.
