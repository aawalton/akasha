“Young Master… I apologize. I have failed to meet your expectations.”

Sebastian lowered his head slightly as he spoke, unable to meet Vector’s expectant gaze.

“We only managed to learn that his name is Seran. Beyond that… we found nothing.”

After the group had completed their circuit around the village perimeter, Vector had ordered them to head toward the prison and check whether any progress had been made regarding the captured spy.

Sebastian was already locked in an exhausting struggle within his own mind. He knew the man’s origins. Knew he was a member of the Ancient Ones.

But that knowledge was not his to reveal.

The secrecy surrounding the organization was absolute, even within the Astrum Forces.

President Velric Astrum—the head of the Astral Alliance and Vector’s father—had personally forbidden any discussion regarding the Ancient Ones, and that order had never been rescinded. If Sebastian revealed the truth now, his loyalty—and more importantly, his ability to safeguard the Astrum Family’s secrets—could be questioned. It was not that he doubted Vector, nor that he believed the Young Master lacked the authority to know. He was bound by a family oath, and breaking it would mean he was not a man who could be trusted. And to a man like Sebastian, that was not something he was willing to become.

Before the oppressive silence could deepen further, Tristan’s voice cut through the underground chamber, easing the tension inside Sebastian’s mind—if only slightly.

“Young Master, we haven’t relented for even a moment since his capture,” Tristan said calmly. “But he’s far more resilient than we expected.”

His cold gaze shifted toward the bloodied figure bound to the chair.

Seran’s condition was miserable. His clothes were soaked in blood, his body covered in wounds, and even his fingernails had been forcibly uprooted. Yet despite everything he had endured, not a single useful word had escaped his mouth.

“If he doesn’t break in one night, then we continue,” Tristan said flatly. “If not in a day…”

His eyes narrowed slightly.

“…then a week. Even longer if necessary.”

Then his tone grew colder.

“And if ordinary methods fail… we’ll use the Razor Viper potion. Or perhaps the Takshaka Elixir.”

“We will continue until he opens his mouth.”

“The interrogation will not stop.”

Sebastian’s expression shifted almost imperceptibly at the mention of those names.

Razor Viper.

A potent potion capable of amplifying a person’s senses more than fivefold, turning every sensation into torment. Under its effects, even the slightest pain became unbearable — every nerve sharpened to terrifying extremes.

But it was the second name that truly caught his attention.

Takshaka Elixir.

An infamous concoction created from the venom of Takshaka serpents dwelling deep within the Naga Forest. Once consumed, it caused the victim’s blood to feel as though it were burning alive — not for minutes or hours, but for months. And there were no medicines that could subsidize the effect.

If administered the elixir, it truly means seeing the doors of the hell for months. Calling it an elixir was almost laughable. It was poison disguised as medicine. Only Third-Stage Warriors desperate to break through into the Fourth Stage dared to use it to temper their bodies. But using it on a First-Stage Warrior… Even Sebastian had never heard of such a thing before.

His gaze shifted silently toward Tristan.

This kid… He even knows about Takshaka. And more than that — He’s truly determined to complete the Young Master’s task. Sebastian inwardly acknowledged Tristan’s frightening determination.

“The Razor Viper I know about,” Vector said thoughtfully, his gaze turning toward Tristan. “But what exactly is this Takshaka Elixir?”

Tristan’s lips curved faintly, as though he had anticipated the question.

“Young Master, the Takshaka Elixir is created using the venom of Takshaka serpents native to the inner regions of the Naga Forest…”

He calmly explained everything Sebastian already knew—the unbearable pain, the blood-burning sensation, and the elixir’s infamous reputation among Body Refinement practitioners.

Then, near the end of his explanation, Tristan added carefully,

“However, Young Master… we do not currently possess such an elixir.”

A brief pause followed.

“But Elder Kaya might.”

Vector’s response came instantly.

“Then send a messenger to Ironfang Hill City.”

The corner of Sebastian’s mouth twitched slightly at the quiet cunning behind the suggestion. Takshaka Elixir was the kind of treasure that rarely surfaced even when someone offered several times its market value. Its scarcity bordered on legendary.

But Vector was not an ordinary person. He was the heir of the Astrum Family. The future head of the Astral Alliance. His voice carries even more power than a crown prince of any empire, though few recognized this authority.

Even if Elder Kaya — the head of Redfield intelligence for the Astrum Family stationed in Ironfang Hill City — did not possess it personally, she would likely procure it immediately from Elara, the capital city of Avalon.

And Sebastian found, somewhat unexpectedly, that he did not oppose Tristan’s approach. If the truth about Seran’s origins eventually emerged from the man’s own broken silence… then Sebastian himself would not have violated his oath. He would not have spoken a single forbidden word. Whatever Vector learned through that process would simply be — A natural discovery.

And that, Sebastian admitted inwardly, was the cleanest solution possible.

After another brief discussion among the group—

“You all continue your session,” Vector said calmly as he turned toward the corridor. “I’m going out for a walk.”

Without another word, his figure slowly disappeared into the dim underground hallway, leaving behind only the echo of his footsteps and the suffocating atmosphere of the prison chamber.

The suffocating stench of blood, sweat, and damp stone slowly faded behind him as he moved away from the underground Prison building. Above, the clear sky stretched endlessly over Vantara, the land scorched under Aethel's rays.

His footsteps carried him eastward, toward the hill resting within the village walls. The path was quiet. Only distant traces of life lingered in the air—the murmur of conversations, the crackling of guarded fires, and the occasional rustle of leaves stirred by the cold wind descending from the forest.

As Vector walked, his thoughts drifted back toward the interrogation room, and the memory made him feel, in retrospect, rather foolish.

A faint shake escaped his head.

How naïve I was…

Back on Earth, he had encountered countless torture methods through movies, documentaries, novels, and the endless abyss of the internet. Psychological torment capable of breaking the endurance of even the strongest-willed persons. Sleep deprivation. Water torture techniques like the infamous drip method, where droplets falling endlessly upon the forehead slowly eroded a victim’s sanity.

Earlier, before entering the prison, he had even briefly considered sharing some of those methods with Sebastian and Tristan.

He was glad, now, that he had held his tongue. It was also the first time he truly understood how sophisticated torture methodologies could become. During his time in Carnatic City, he had rarely witnessed interrogations directly; he usually received only the results, never the methods themselves.

Yet what little he had learned today was enough to make one thing painfully clear:

Ethereum’s methods of interrogation had evolved far beyond anything Earth had ever conceived.

These techniques were not designed for ordinary people. They had been refined over generations specifically to break Warriors—men and women whose vitality, endurance, and willpower far surpassed that of normal humans. Given enough time and the proper methods, even a Fourth-Stage Warrior could eventually be forced to break.

Compared to that, Earth’s techniques seemed primitive. Against someone like Seran, they would have amounted to little more than an inconvenience.

Vector shook his head and let the thought go. There were more pressing things demanding his attention than a comparison of torture philosophies across worlds.

His steps continued toward the base of the eastern hill. There, he intended to establish something that might one day become even more important to the Astrum Family than the sanctuary hidden within the Agastya Mountains.

The thought drew a quiet smile to his lips.

He was going to establish the Void Library — the gift granted to him by the Will of Void — at the foot of the Hill.

As he walked, he opened a dialogue with the System, working through the practical details. He needed to know how much territory the establishment required and whether the structure itself could be shaped according to his preferences.

What he envisioned was something built directly into the mountain—a hidden sanctuary carved into stone, refined from within until it appeared as though it had always existed there.

Such an appearance would make explanations far easier if anyone ever questioned its existence, which was certainly going to happen. He could simply present it as an Ancient Ruin, a structure hidden within the mountain itself.

And with the presence of some Ancient Ruins in the world, such an explanation would hardly invite suspicion.

“Hunh?”

Vector’s steps halted abruptly.

A new piece of information had just emerged from the System. The Void Library itself did not truly exist within Ethereum — nor even within the same dimensional framework as this world. Only its entrance gate would manifest here—a mere threshold anchored to a single point in reality.

The actual Void Library rested elsewhere. Within another dimension entirely.

A slight frown formed on Vector’s face.

Then why does it require controlled territory at all? If only the entrance existed here, shouldn’t it be possible to place it anywhere and relocate it whenever necessary?

The System answered soon afterward.

And the answer was as straightforward as it was simply inconvenient.

Once the entrance was established, its location became permanently anchored — not in the strictest sense, but relocating it would only be possible if he reached a considerable level of mastery over Origin Energy — a threshold he was nowhere near.

What that considerable level was, he did not yet know. Even when the practice of Origin Energy would become possible for him, he did not know. Truthfully, he barely understood Origin Energy at all.

For a brief moment, he considered establishing the entrance somewhere more practical—a hidden safehouse, perhaps, or even within the underground prison complex.

But the idea collapsed almost as quickly as it appeared.

The issue was never concealment.

It was explanation.

No matter where he placed it, he would eventually need to justify its existence. And a structure hidden within a mountain was far easier to pass off as something ancient rather than newly created.

A forgotten ruin discovered by chance during exploration.

People would accept that story far more readily.

Besides, the System had already provided a solution to the problem.

It would alter the structure’s appearance so that, to any observer, the Void Library would seem like an ancient construct embedded within the mountain long ago—not something recently placed there, but something rediscovered.

Any changes necessary to preserve that illusion would be handled automatically.

That settled the matter.

Without another thought, Vector resumed walking.
