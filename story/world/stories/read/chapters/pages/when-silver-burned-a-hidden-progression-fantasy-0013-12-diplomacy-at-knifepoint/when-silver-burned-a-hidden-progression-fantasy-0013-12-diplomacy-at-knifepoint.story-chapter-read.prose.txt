When they finished fastening their lanterns on the designated hoops of their hunters' belts, Jack was the first to the rope, but not before Kalsin caught her eyes. He pointedly looked at the two warrior women, then back at her. For a moment Jack watched Kalsin with a barely noticeable tilt of her head, then gave a slight nod. She turned to the women and went into an animated series of hand gestures to explain something. The specifics didn't matter to Kalsin. As long as the two Hadúr worshipers were drawn into her group, Kalsin would be left alone with the azTél couple. Siblings. One or the other. Hopefully not both.

Yeah. He was probably better off referring to them by their names.

"See to your gear," one of the women spread the wisdom as she went over her belongings, no doubt born from ill experience. "Don't imagine you'd want anything to slip." She then quickly droned off a quiet prayer.

Jack's group went first. She drew two fingers at her eyes, then motioned upwards and tapped her lantern. Keep your heads up and trained on the pocket of my lantern’s darkness. Then she grabbed hold of the rope and started the climb only using the strength of her arms. The cowled priests went next with impressive agility as they braced their legs against the wall, and the warriors followed on their tails with the last woman having to hustle in order to stay in the éj-lantern's radius. In seconds, Blindlight swallowed them all.

Kalsin stretched his neck this way and that, stealing glances at the azTél pair. He'd made his intentions clear. Seeing their reserved postures still firmly locked in place, he knew time wouldn't soften them. Time that he didn't have to spare, in any case. Erazza's eyes clouded for an instant with the telltale, far-away look of a rot's surge. The age-old ache of longing hit Kalsin in the chest so hard he had to look away, blinking rapidly.

He never knew what the surge felt like. Over time, as the years of his childhood passed and swept by, he'd accepted his disability as much as he could. He'd learned to live without it. But sometimes, at odd intervals with no regard to reason, the long buried pain of otherness came to the surface before sinking below again.

If only he could've saved—

A sharp jerk of the rope pulled Kalsin from his thoughts, its corded length rising several feet in the air, then dropping back down. The signal that Jack's group had reached the top.

Kalsin nodded to Áron, then to Erazza to get ready. His grip found the rope and he swung his feet onto the wall with a muffled thud of flaking pebbles. Then he started the climb. One hand reaching up and then one leg stepping higher, bracing himself so the other limbs could follow.

He saw Áron follow below him with Erazza close behind, but as his concentration slipped, so did his feet. He swung out on the rope before regaining his footing, steady again. He was already high enough so that he couldn't make out the ground below.

His arms burned as he pulled himself upward. Though he wasn't Jack's equal in physical strength, a life of violence and routine exercise made sure that he could hold his own. They'd trained together often, though always as equals and never taking defined roles of teacher and student. Good gods, Jack as a teacher? Would be a proper nightmare. He endured enough abuse with her posing as his bodyguard already.

Soon Kalsin spotted the top of the wall, rope hooked over and disappearing behind the stone parapet. With feigned clumsiness, he made his feet slip again, kicking in the air. Áron cursed below him, drawing back in fear for his safety lest he catch a flailing boot in the head.

Should be enough.

Kalsin climbed the remaining distance as fast as he could, and in a second he was pulling himself on the parapet. As he'd expected, Jack's group was nowhere to be seen, probably on their way to the ground already, outside the city.

"Hold it," Kalsin called out, his tone colored by grim depth.

Áron jerked to a halt, still about four feet from the top. His gaze met Kalsin's, then slowly trailed down to the serrated knife he was holding against the rope.

Nobody moved. Kalsin watched from above, knife in hand, as Áron and a barely visible Erazza immediately below him hung frozen, like flies caught on an invisible web, staring back at him without daring to move a muscle.

"It's quite a drop," Kalsin said. "One that you may survive under normal circumstances. But we all know perfectly well that without an éj-lantern, you won't last a day in the midst of Blindlight. If it's not the injuries that kill you, or the radiance that burns out your sockets, it's the patrols that will surely apprehend you to the collector."

Kalsin could feel Áron gauging the distance between them. Probably thinking about surging up, which might've worked—except for the fact that they had no idea about Kalsin's disability. Just as quickly as they might've been able to climb, Kalsin could've surged, too; quickening and strengthening himself to tear through the rope. Or so they assumed.

That assumption alone was enough to keep Kalsin in control.

"I won't repeat myself. All I've said was true without exception. We've come too far to let stray words and stubborn prides fuck this up. I spent years, years in search of a vestige of hope. I'm just as much of a heretic as you are."

Kalsin did not blink, gathering all the gravity to aid his words as he burrowed his eyes into theirs. Then he slowly, almost gently lifted his knife from the rope, sheathing it. There was no need to state the obvious. They had been at his mercy. If he wanted them dead, he had every chance to make it so.

Áron's arms visibly shook as he pulled himself to the top. Kalsin jumped off the parapet and onto the wall and they followed, each wearing a troubled expression that was a far cry from the guarded blankness from before.

Kalsin knew they didn't trust him. Not yet. If anything, he'd probably ruined any and all hopes of a friendly relationship with either of them. But he had proven himself enough to get past their initial defenses, which was the only thing of real consequence. Men didn't have to like one another to form an alliance. Having a common goal born of necessity would suffice.

Crossing straight to the opposite side and knowing what to look for, Kalsin quickly noticed the rope hanging down the outer wall. He vaulted up onto the parapet and grabbed the rope, turning until nothing but empty space was at his back. That’s when Erazza spoke.

"All this, for... What do you want with us, really?" She looked at his scar with naked disgust. It satisfied Kalsin to see her true feelings come to surface, as opposed to the polite glance to the side previously.

"Well, it's not like I can visit the local library and ask about books dissecting the topic of curing scars."

"Please. No academic interest warrants this much foolishness. There's more than curiosity that drives you."

Kalsin tsked. "Truly a revelation of tits-curling proportions you two have come to. I would applaud had I possessed empty hands." He steadied his grip on the rope. "I don't want to learn about the cure. I'm looking to use it. With reasons to be taken at face value," he said with a clear meaning at the scar between his eyes.

Áron, standing with his arms folded next to Erazza, wrinkled his brow in response. "We—"

"We can talk later. Once we've caught up with Jack, we're going to part ways from the darkfinder's caravan." Kalsin interrupted. "It's what you wanted to begin with, yes? Only now we're doubling the headcount. Strength in numbers and all that. Don't fall behind, now." With that, he let himself drop below. Which lasted exactly a second as his legs found purchase against the wall, grip steady on the taut line.

The descent took them about half as long as their climb, much to the relief of Kalsin's semi-labored muscles. He'd also taken the opportunity to close his eyes and rest his vision from Blindlight's glare. When his feet touched ground and he peeled his lids open again, the throbbing in his temples resumed with double the enthusiasm.

They were in a shallow ditch with pieces of wooden rubble crunching under their steps, the remains of stakes from age-old sieges. A handful of arranged logs sat on the edge, giving a convenient purchase to pull themselves out from the dent in the ground. Drawn onto a log's surface was an arrow of white chalk pointing away at an angle. After advancing about twenty steps or so into the rocky pasture, they soon spotted another arrow, then another further out, directing them along the darkfinder's prepared route. She'd been busy during their napping time.

The outskirts of Remallon was a barren expanse of infertile soil with only the sporadic patches of green to liven things up, but Blindlight stole even that little cheer of color during the end of months. Dew glinted on the rocks halfway to frosting over, on cracked clay mounds and wilted grass. The air was devoid of smells and movement, as if the glaring whiteness had a particularly strict general looking over its shoulder, holding its breath under sharp scrutiny. An uneasy, yet familiar feeling had settled over Kalsin's shoulders. No matter how many times he'd faced the light, it always held the potential of unsavory surprises.

The hushed voices found them first, and it took Kalsin a good minute to catch sight of Jack's pocket of darkness. The warrior women were lazing around on a flat boulder, one of them deep in the middle of some retelling as the other nodded along. If Blindlight bothered them, it didn't show.

The cowled priests, who'd been standing in unison like washed out ghosts in their baggy lapels, whipped their heads around as Kalsin approached. He noticed the lower side of their face was covered by dark cloth, reaching up and disappearing beneath the cowl’s shade without letting an inch of skin breathe.

Kalsin nodded to them as he approached Jack, his bodyguard standing to the side, looking oddly incomplete without her shield and heavy armor plates. She was rummaging through her unstrapped shoulder bag on the ground with her éj-lantern propped next to her feet, jaws working double-time.

Kalsin crouched next to her and tapped her shoulder lightly. "Are you well?"

She looked up sharply. Her eyes were bloodshot.

Kalsin winced in sympathy and tightened his grip on her shoulder, her muscles hard and unyielding under his fingers. Stress was taking a toll on her. Punching through monster bones was one thing, but with intrigue taking the front stage the past few days, she was like a fish caught on shore. Having to watch her friend, the one she's supposed to protect, getting tortured with no chance of vengeance? A bad start. Add the glare of Blindlight on top and leave her without brickleaf to take off the edge.

Kalsin knew he’d break from half that much strain. And yet she endured.

"Not much longer, now. I've found them," Kalsin whispered.

Jack’s mouth quirked with relief, glancing over at Áron and Erazza, the azTéls watching from the shroud's edge. They didn't return her smile, though Erazza did give a barely noticeable nod of uneasy acknowledgement. Smart, that one. When a woman of Jack's stature extends a friendly gesture, one does not simply refuse if they value their health.

"Anything noteworthy on your end?" Kalsin asked.

Jack finished the inspection of her bags and unwrapped another candy. After a moment of deliberation, as if pondering the meaning of life, she popped it in her mouth and crunched down on it.

Kalsin said, "Not sure what to make of those priests. They make my skin want to crawl south."

Jack wiggled her hand with pinky and thumb extended, a confirmation the feeling was mutual. She then mimicked a flapping mouth with one hand, pointing at the two priests, and shook her head. She then thrust a thumb herself and made a crude gesture. She grinned, laughing silently as Kalsin digested her self-deprecating humor.

Kalsin gave her a sidelong look, thinking real hard how to answer. Ignore her? She'd just laugh harder. What about... no. Too insensitive. He sighed. "Well, if they don't talk, they don't talk. They were in good company with you then, as you’re always running your hands about something. Please tell me you taught them signs that are actually useful, and not some… inutile handcockery."

Unfortunately, Kalsin didn't learn if the silent priests acquired vulgar crumbs of wisdom from his bodyguard, because that's when Sap, followed by Hollow, materialized from the white. Then a sweating assistant came rushing inside their gray circle as he pulled another set of horses, with Treyant and the family closing the line.

The darkfinder's commanding presence pulled the group of strangers back to attention. Kalsin approached Sap, not sparing any pats on his scaled neck, murmuring reassuringly. He figured it was a... sappy reunion. Heh.

Treyant's face was drawn as she called out and gathered them in a ring to discuss how to proceed. The warriors wanted a weather forecast, or more specifically they wanted to know how much longer Blindlight would last, to which Treyant said it was deep into the night currently, so Warmlight could be back in a couple of hours. Which technically had a thirty-three percent chance of being true.

When Erazza smiled her thanks and announced they would split off and continue separately from this point forward, Treyant must've known from the start because she just simply nodded. A frown crept on her brows, however, when she heard Jack and Kalsin would accompany them.

"I could lend you my lantern, if you need it," she said slowly, watching Erazza's face like a hawk for her reaction. "I have a few more in storage. For this last stretch, Giant and Wraith over here can provide darkness just fine."

Kalsin stomached the jab, though not without a pang of melancholy. He'd been called Silly, Wraith, Moonface and Rollback before. He knew he was an ugly fucker without constantly getting reminders, thank you very much. Damned silver eyes.

Áron shook his head. "What's a darkfinder without her éj-lantern?" He raised a hand to forestall Treyant. "No, it's all right. We've had a heart-to-heart with these two, and decided we're better off traveling in company. Your offer is warmly appreciated, though, Bellé."

His words seemed to appease Treyant. "It's your bed to make," she muttered, probably as much of a blessing as she was going to give. "Fine, enough standing around. Blindlight ain't going to wait and I've a mind to cover as much distance as possible before it's gone."

She pointed to the warrior women, commands pouring from her mouth like liquid poetry. "Big gals, take your horses from my helper already before they pull him on his face and trample his scrawny ass. Might not look it, but that backside is worth good money. Old fella's dead on his feet, so one of you be a good priest and lend a shoulder. Boy, don't look at—"

They never got to find out what the boy wasn't supposed to be looking at, because the old man's head exploded into bloody chunks as a crossbow-bolt tore through his skull.
