The rising sun shone down through the shattered fragments of the celestial sphere. Shards of glass from broken skyscraper windows sparkled on the streets below.

Zev and Elion crouched, concealed behind overgrown shrubs. Across the street from them a large park sat, filling an entire city block. The growth in the park was vibrant and well-maintained, unlike the vegetation that filled the streets.

In the center of the park, an immaculate statue of Aurelia stood, raising her arms over her head and facing the rising sun. A faint shimmering in the air around the edge of the park delineated the zone of protection around the statue.

“It’s just out in the open?” Elion asked, surprised.

“What were you expecting?” Zev asked.

“Every Altar that I’ve seen was destroyed by Dorian’s warlocks. Why is this one still standing, so close to the Palace?”

“Dorian keeps this one for the special use of what he calls his ‘Morning Knights,’” Zev said. “A few Aurelians have betrayed us, and sided with him.”

"You mean there’s going to be another fight,” Elion said.

“Probably not,” Zev shook his head, scanning the park. “I don’t see anyone over there. I hoped they would all be draw off by the fighting. But with our jet crashing nearby, there’s bound to be a hoard of goons heading this way.”

Elion nodded, tapping the pouch of Ascendency stones in his pocket. “This was a dumb place to choose as an emergency meeting point,” he said. “What if there was someone here guarding this, and Tuft showed up first?”

“That man would be fine,” Zev said dismissively, rising from their hiding position. He strode across the street, and Elion followed.

“But we can’t wait here long for the others,” Zev said. “We’ll have to keep moving. They’ll be looking for us.”

A sinking feeling filled Elion’s gut. He hoped everyone was okay.

They reached the edge of the park, when a man appeared. He’d been seated on a bench, just out of view from where Zev and Elion had watched from. Walking with a stoop, the old man approached them. He wore a simple white robe, and his white beard dangled to his waist.

“Who seeks the protection of Aurelia?” the man asked.

“Amburn?” Zev said, a slight grin growing on his face.

“Zev?” the man replied. “Zev Starholder?”

The two men stepped toward each other, and then stopped, as though remembering where they were.

“What are you doing here?” Zev asked. “I haven’t seen you in decades!”

“I might ask you the same,” Amburn replied, his face darkening. “It seems the rumors are true, and you are indeed behind this aggression.”

Zev took a half step back, falling into a guarded stance.

Elion did the same, watching with growing consternation.

“My old friend,” Zev said. “We merely wish to use the Altar.”

Amburn did not move aside. “Perhaps you should introduce me to your companion?”

“Of course,” Zev said, gesturing to Elion. “This is my nephew, Elion.”

“A pleasure to meet you, Elion,” Amburn said, offering a slight nod.

“Elion, Amburn was my mentor.”

Elion looked between the two men. Neither Zev nor Amburn looked at him. They stared each other down.

“Good to meet you,” Elion said.

“And what are you doing here, Zev? In Erod? Heading toward your brother’s palace?”

Zev frowned, his brow creased. “My brother’s palace?” he said. “My brother does not sit on the Golden Throne.”

“You pursue the path of vengeance and violence, then? Did I not teach you better than this?”

“Dorian chose that path, not I,” Zev spat. “He murdered my family.”

“We have had peace for many years. Until now. I am saddened to discover that you are the one behind this violence.”

“Step aside, Amburn. We’re here to level Elion. He’s the rightful heir to the throne, and I’ll see him seated on it before the end of the day.”

“So you will kill another, then? The true ruler uses the powers of the throne as we speak. I can feel it.” Amburn raised a hand, as though detecting a subtle breeze.

Zev moved, heading around Amburn toward the altar.

“You will go no further,” Amburn said. “I cannot permit you to continue using Aurelia’s power for your war of aggression.”

Zev continued forward. Amburn stepped in front of him and placed a hand on Zev’s chest, stopping him.

An aircraft thrummed as it flew by overhead.

“We don’t have time for this,” Zev said, pushing the elderly man to the side.

Amburn entered his ascended state. Light flared. His eyes glowed. A set of golden plate armor formed, encasing the man. He held a broadsword in one hand, which he held at Zev’s throat.

Zev activated his armor as well.

“We don’t have to fight!” Zev protested, his sword glowing as he batted Amburn’s blade aside.

Elion powered up as well.

Despite his appearance, Amburn moved like lightning. He clashed with Zev, sparks flying from their blades.

Elion held his butter knife in one hand, feeling wildly underpowered in his makeshift breastplate. Should he just stay out of the fighting? He’d probably just get in Zev’s way…

Zev stumbled backward under a rain of blows.

“Amburn!” Zev shouted. “I don’t want to fight, but I’ll kill you if I must!”

“You have violated the principles of non-aggression!” Amburn said. “I have failed you, my student, and now I must put you down!”

Amburn feinted to the side, and Zev bit, tripping over his own feet as Amburn pivoted and slammed his fist into Zev’s shoulder.

Zev fell to the ground. Amburn dove on top of Zev.

Elion ran to help. He collided with Amburn, his knee crashing into the armored side of the old man. Amburn reached back and brushed Elion aside, sending him sprawling.

Amburn pressed his blade down with both hands, holding the edge against Zev’s neck. Zev pushed back against the blade, arms shaking. A line of red blood formed as the sword cut into his neck.

“Elion stop him!” Zev shouted.

Elion felt the ascendency stones Zev had given him, considering running to the altar for an upgrade. It would take too long.

Then Yaret appeared, running across the park, waving excitedly, wearing his rifle slung over his shoulder.

Elion gestured to the fight, and ran back in, lowering his shoulder this time as he tried to give Zev a chance to escape.

A laser blasted past Elion, striking Amburn in the side an instant before Elion made contact. The combined force threw Amburn back, and Zev bucked, then wriggled out from under his mentor.

Elion and Amburn rolled together, and Amburn’s thick arms wrapped around Elion’s chest. The man squeezed, and Elion’s breastplate buckled, cutting into his sides. His breath rushed out of his lungs.

Amburn glowed, brilliant white light beaming out of his skin and armor. Yaret and Zev threw up their arms, blocking out the blinding light.

“Stop him Elion!” Zev shouted. “He’s going to kill us all!”

The light continued growing, brighter and brighter. Small plants burst into flame. Elion screwed his eyes shut, but the burning light shone through his eyelids. He struggled, batting at Amburn with his free arms. He still held his knife.

Amburn squeezed tighter, crushing Elion’s ribs. Something popped in his chest. He stabbed his blade down into Amburn’s arm, pushing it through gaps in the man’s armor, but received no reaction.

“You have to kill him!” Zev’s voice reached him as though the man called from a long way off. Elion squirmed and struggled, feeling the life go out of him.

White light drenched everything.

Elion twisted his arm, stretching it over his head. He stabbed repeatedly at Amburn’s face, his knife deflected by the knight’s visor.

His head spun.

The blade slipped through a slot in the visor. Elion pushed, feeling the knife scrape against bone and slide down into an eye socket. He kept pushing.

Amburn’s grip weakened.

Elion twisted his hips and kicked. He pulled free from the Aurelian knight.

Amburn’s body stiffened, and he toppled over backward, slamming to the ground. The burning light shining from him faded.

Elion blinked away the burning afterimage of glowing Amburn. Rubbing his eyes, he slowly rotated his torso, feeling for broken ribs. Tender and bruised in places, he didn’t notice any debilitating pain.

The power of Amburn’s ascended state dissipated from around him. His armor faded back into the Aurelian void, leaving him lying on the ground wearing only his white robe. Elion’s butter knife stuck part way out of one eye, buried most of the way into the man’s head.

Zev knelt beside Amburn, placing a hand on the man’s chest. “I’m sorry, old friend,” Zev said. “I wish it hadn’t come to this.”

Then he straightened, and, looking from Elion to Yaret he said “We don’t have time to waste. I will mourn for him later.”

He took two steps toward the altar, then turned back to Elion. “Sometimes war forces us to kill. Sometimes we must kill our friends, or even our family. We have to be strong, make the hard decisions, and fight for a better future.”

Elion’s arms and legs hummed with adrenaline. He nodded at Zev’s words, not really taking in what they meant. He looked down at the powerful Aurelian knight that he’d just killed.

A pair of vehicles flew past overhead, followed by a third, moving slower and closer to the ground. They were marked with a purple sigil.

“The black wall is closing in,” Zev said.

Yaret placed an arm around Elion’s shoulder. “You’re okay?” he asked. “Injured?”

“I don’t think so,” Elion said, still staring blankly off into space. “He just knocked the wind out of me a bit.” Elion shook his head to clear it, then started toward Zev and the altar.

“It’s okay,” Yaret said. “Killing a man like that is overwhelming. It’ll be a while before you have time to grapple with it.”

“It’s this whole planet,” Elion said. “I’ve somehow stumbled into killing people left and right since I got here. And I get the feeling the killing’s not done yet.”

“Don’t worry,” Yaret said. “We’ll get through it together.”
