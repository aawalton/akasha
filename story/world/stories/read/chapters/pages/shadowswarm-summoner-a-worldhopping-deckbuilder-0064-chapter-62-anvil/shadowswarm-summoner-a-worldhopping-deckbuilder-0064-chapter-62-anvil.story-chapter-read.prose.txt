Chapter 62: Anvil

Sometimes, Raeva wondered if there was something wrong with her.

While all of them liked what they did to an extent (except, perhaps, Jessica), she loved fighting. Here they were, outnumbered and fighting for their lives, but all she could focus on was what a challenge it was, and how many targets there were to hit.

The problem was a number of them were their own creatures, roughly half of which belonged to Ambrose. Almost everything except her cantrip hit everything around her target, and killing their own imps wasn’t a great idea.

Luckily, she was one of the most skilled and accurate people on her world when it came to throwing things. Sometimes it felt like she couldn’t miss, only be intercepted, and even then…

Round 4

Cantrip: [Singe], Creature: [Dust Devil], Spell: [Hotfoot], Creature: Kindling Phoenix, Spell: [Flame Lance] (Free)

At this point, she’d lost [Copy Spell] and [Obsidian Spear], but that was fine. The latter could be thrown, once, but there was no way in hell she was getting in close combat with this many people. It was a good way to get stabbed or hacked to death. No, she was at her best from a distance, and that’s where they needed her damage.

The fight, like many brawls between wizards, devolved into multiple smaller battles. Raeleq and Luthor were their frontline as usual, and they battled the creatures summoned by the Bedrock Spire security force. [Reggie] and Luthor’s archer and swordsman worked to provide an impenetrable shield, allowing Raeva and Ambrose to send imps and hostile spells at the people controlling the creatures.

Dexi’Lynn stayed out of it, covering the Levi-Box with her body, while Jessica and her illusory double distracted and took the occasional splash of fire or darkness from a better target. She had been in the middle of casting a spell when the Pause got them.

Luck was on their side in some ways. There were way more enemies, but according to Jessica, no one was above Affinity Level 2. There were enough of them to be a threat, but it was like facing a bunch of second years. They had enough cards to throw creatures and cantrips around, but not much else. Which is why many of them still fought with traditional weapons and armor.

It wouldn’t help against Raeva’s onslaught.

Time resumed, and with it, an idea hit her. “Ambrose, you got your darkness imp ready?” she yelled to be heard over the sounds of battle.

“Just sent it over,” he shouted back, constantly on the move behind their front line. He really needed a weapon or something soon, on top of his legion of imps. “Why?”

“Bring your imps back and let them get close, then use it on my signal,” she said, weaving to avoid a shard of ice that would’ve pierced her chest.

“Why?”

“Just do it.”

He shot her a look but did as she said. The imps retreated from the enemy backline. They’d already taken out six or seven of the reinforcements, on top of the initial four. That still left…Too many to count. And with as narrow as the streets were, plenty of the guards hung back, waiting for their turn to join the fray.

With the imp’s retreat, the backline moved forward, and some of those guys waiting advanced and began casting their creatures. That’s fine. They could come too. It would make things even better.

“What’s the plan?” Luthor asked, slamming his shield into the head of a ten foot long cobra that had been about to strike. It recoiled, then tried again, running into the same precise, cautious shield.

“Let them in, then when I say now, back off,” she said as quietly as she could while still being overheard. A grunt from her brother and a nod from Luthor was as close as she got to a good confirmation.

Bedrock Security took the bait, and they formed a wall of advancing danger. Raeva counted to three, then cast [Dust Devil] underneath them. “Now!” she yelled.

One of the imps whirled around and launched itself into the front line. One of the guards raised his spear, but then the imp cackled and let out a disgusting, wall-rattling fart before a rolling fog of darkness burst from it in a ring.

Luthor and Raeleq dipped back further as the darkness closed around their foes, blanketing most of them in deep, impenetrable shadow. A muffled cry rose, and then her [Dust Devil] finished forming in the middle of them.

Raeva couldn’t see what was happening, but her connection to the elemental gave her a good sense of things. It spun, going slowly at first but picking up speed until it whirled around and through their boxed in enemies. It wasn’t strong enough to do much damage outside of sometimes flinging weapons out of their hands.

It was the creature’s special property that interested her. A wide-spread Nuisance effect was already powerful, but Gamble 3 was brutal. Every attack, every spell, it would be randomized between three targets while within the creature’s aura.

Cries of pain, fear, and frustration came from the dark cloud, just inches away from them. [Reggie] growled, but Raeleq held him back for the critical few seconds the [Umbral Imp]’s darkness lasted.

Raeva didn’t wait any longer. She strafed past a leaping, snarling dog and called up her free [Flame Lance]. As soon as the magical fog started to abate, she hurled it into the center of the group. She heard a surprised yelp before the spell exploded, and the guards were sent sprawling.

Several creatures, including the dog that snapped at her, disappeared entirely. When the smoke settled, fewer than half of that group remained…but more reinforcements came from the direction of the train station.

“Guys?” Jessica practically squeaked. “Maybe we should retreat?”

The guards advanced. There was fear in their eyes, but also anger. Hateful, murderous anger at their fallen friends, and a clear desire to do something about it. Maybe they shouldn’t have started it. Raeva took a deep breath, exhaling slowly to avoid her own anger from consuming her.

“Agreed,” said Luthor. He’d taken a few hits and blood trailed from his temples. He glowed gold with the power of his next healing spell. “Jessica, you and Dexi guard the box, Raeleq and I will cover everyone. Raeva, Ambrose -- “

“Yeah yeah,” Ambrose waved him off. “Cover fire and annoy them with imps.”

Raeva couldn’t help but laugh as the Timer ticked down.

Round 5

Cantrip: [Singe], [Creature: Kindling Phoenix], Signature Creature: [Fiery Hatchling], Spell: [Hot Foot]

The round after this, she’d have to shuffle again, and soon after every round. The upside would mean more free [Flame Lance]s, but the more cards deactivated, the worse the pain and stress would be, and the slower the recovery. Unfortunately, she feared she’d finally face that fate, and surprisingly not because of Ambrose.

The [Fiery Hatchling] was a welcome sight to see again. A creature had ripped her apart before its summoner died, but now it and the Phoenix would be perfect for providing cover. She cast the latter first, placing it behind her.

Jessica and the kobold struggled to lift the box, but managed and shuffled as fast as they could while the entire group made their way across the narrow level. Every door was closed, but people peeked out from windows. No matter where they went, people would watch, and they would be chased.

Raeva’s stomach jumped and twisted. The joy of battle faded, just for a second, with the spike of fear and inevitability.

*****

Luthor breathed deeply, keeping it as steady as possible while he hungered for oxygen. Fighting was exhausting, and most real small-scale conflicts ended after just a few minutes. The fact that they’d managed to take out about half of the imperial guards after them was impressive. It wouldn’t be enough, though.

He pivoted to block a sword stroke and returned with a thrust into a guard’s belly. Before he could appreciate another dispatched enemy, the jaws of a long-snouted reptile closed over his forearm. The pain sunk in a second later, sharp and crushing. His sword clattered out of his hand, and a second later Raeleq slammed his club into the creature’s spine.

Luthor got his hand back, but there was no retrieving his weapon. Not while they needed to keep moving and whittle them down as they went. It was their only chance of being able to regroup and maybe survive.

Raeleq took over for him, swinging wide and keeping the line of creatures at bay, but he took several spells to the chest, detonating in flashes of red, blue, and purple. He stumbled backwards with a pained grunt, and Luthor immediately cast the [Compassion] side of his new healing card. It kept his friend up and moving, but things were turning against them.

[Reggie] fell soon after, after concentrated fire from spells and creatures alike. It took everything they had, but even he would fall, and if enough time passed, deactivate. Worse, Raeleq looked as tired as Luthor felt. He backed up, creating a bit of space between them and their enemies, now free to turn on the rest of them.

Ambrose’s imps filled the gap. Some had died along the way, but he kept bringing out more and more. There were now eight of the vicious bastards out, moving faster than they should and buying them the time they needed to escape.

Luther forced a burst of speed, waving with his now free shield-arm for others to follow. Raeva and Ambrose went first, then Raeleq. Shouts and cries followed them as the remaining guards charged, emboldened by their tactical retreat.

He caught up with Jessica and grabbed part of the box to help he and Dexi move it along faster. Their only real move was to try the other stairs. They were in sight now, and if they could get the high ground…

The sound of boots thudding on metal made his blood run cold. When he looked up, a fresh batch of fighters descended from above. They were trapped between two groups.

“Oh no,” Jessica moaned, dropping the box. She had tears of frustration and fear flowing. The others weren’t in much better condition, and they found themselves with two groups of armed, pissed off foes on either side of them.

Round 8

Cantrip: Retaliate, Artifact: Trusty Arms, Spell: Healing Touch, Creature: Spirit Hound

Half his deck was dormant now, and he only had so much left to give. His creatures had been destroyed, so he had his full 4 mana for the turn, and could cast almost everything. But…What was the point?

Frozen like this, trapped between two groups of superior numbers, he wondered what his mother would do, in this situation. She’d fought long odds and survived to come home. There had to be a way to win this. What was it? His thoughts then drifted to Mulch, and the despairing part of him was grateful his new friend had slipped away. Maybe he’d be able to get away unharmed.

As soon as he was able, he cast [Spirit Hound], mostly to try to bolster their numbers. The rest of them stayed close together, panting and as out of fight as he was. Luthor stood taller, setting his jaw in a fierce scowl.

“I can’t believe we survived Vanderborn only to lose to a bunch of fucking hired goons,” Ambrose complained as he cast yet another imp. The others were mostly dead now, thinned out or driven back. They didn’t have time for him to rebuild.

“What do we do?” Jessica begged, hands held up in a fighting position, but entirely unsure of where to direct her nervous attention.

“Try to die well,” said Raeva with a bleak laugh. “And take as many of them with you as you can. It’s been nice knowing you, guys.”

Raeleq sighed and summoned a new club. “I don’t want to die, but we’ve done our ancestors proud. Grandmother is surely smiling up at us.”

Luther didn’t say anything. He was too busy wondering why the enemies had stopped advancing. It couldn’t be that they were too scared to fight. Not with them winded and weakened.

Instead, a crackling sound caught their attention. The voice this time wasn’t feminine, but deep and dark, and as malicious as a cat with its prey.

“Well, would ya take a look at that. You folks took out a decent number of my boys, but the fun’s over. Do us all a big favor and surrender, before you piss me off. Don’t make me come down there!” There was deep, shuddering belly laughter, coming out like ‘hreh hreh hreh’.

“And who do we have the pleasure of addressing?” Luthor called out, confident that he’d be heard. The Timer ticked down slowly, and would cost him another card if they stalled too long.

“We ain’t discussing me right now. We’re talkin’ ‘bout your impending surrender, or your untimely deaths. This is your last chance, kiddos.”

One didn’t have to be intelligent or psychic to know what was about to follow. Raeva and Ambrose spoke at the same time, shouting out two different but similar things.

“This is your last chance to kiss our asses,” Ambrose said.

“Come on down here and fight us, coward!” Raeva, of course, couldn’t help but crave a more honest altercation.

Silence, then again that shuddering ‘hreh hreh hreh’. “You really want that? Awright. Better make space!”

Luthor furrowed his brow, before following the guards’ gazes upwards. A dark, heavy shape came hurtling down at them. He grabbed Raeva by the back of her collar and pulled her away just in time to see the shape become a figure.

The massive molekin crashed into the ground hard enough to not only leave a thick dent in the metal, but also throw them all to their feet. He wore a bright red, silky looking vest over silver fur. He grinned, showing sharp, misshapen teeth with a few gaps. Black, beady eyes stared right into Luthor’s soul, and he couldn’t refrain from shuddering as he got back to his feet.

“Pleased to meet you,” said the mole. And at once, Luthor understood who they were dealing with. Mulch had mentioned him with fear and disgust in his voice. “I’m Boss Parl. This is my city. My world. And I don’t got much patience for rabblerousers, thieves, or murderers. On your damned feet, and prepare to die.”
