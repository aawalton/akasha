People underestimate how dangerous a bored person is.

It's like a convincing illusion of getting caught in a pit trap; everyone tries their best to escape it. It only exists in the individual's mind, but when boredom snaps its teeth around the ankle, it can be terrifying the lengths the victim is willing to go in order to shake boredom off. It's the unseen devil whispering in the miner's ears during a peaceful tavern dinner to start a bar fight. It's the perpetrator who incites bullies to beat up the weak kid after class. Dangerous. Addictive. Outright damn deadly, in fact.

Most importantly, it's just one short step away from aimlessness. Boredom is the byproduct of having no purpose, a desperate craving to sate a hollow cavern deep inside the soul with something fleeting and temporary.

And Kalsin had been both aimless and without purpose for the past week.

They had taken the nearest fracture and, carried by boat and bahten, rode the depthless rivers north toward Újtavak. The Republic's oddly shaped archipelago was like a spiderweb gouged deep in the earth, as there was always another fracture they could follow, endlessly forking off from one another. Though many people tended to traverse the waters this time of year, they only had a few encounters as thin vessels sailed by in the narrow passages. Fortunately, they hadn't encountered any trouble to slow their progress—no bandits lying in wait, no deep-fishing operations clogging the waterways.

Even nighttime couldn't put a halt to their advance. Sap and Hollow could navigate the waters while sleeping, and though it was slower going, the current was on their side while the azTéls slept on the boat. During the first night, Kalsin had accepted the invitation to rest on the vessel, but quickly returned to the saddle when he found out not only did Erazza snore—putting an erupting volcano to shame—but she also kept rocking the boat as she flailed around in her sleep. One of those would've been manageable. But both? He'd rather wake with aching hips and legs numb from soaking in the cold water all night. Which he did.

Jack was careful with her vigil over him, and the contagious feeling quickly spread and made the entire group tread with caution. When nearing settlements, they always took the long route around to avoid the possibility of people looking for them. Though it was a much needed relief not being chased for once, avoiding outside contact meant no new developments reached their ears.

Did the overworld's lawmen already issue a warrant carried by pigeon-wings across the Republic? Or were they keeping quiet, continuing to rely on the hushed operations of the secret service. They didn't know, and asking the few travelers in their path would've drawn attention that was better avoided. Instead, when people approached, they kept their hoods up and their heads down.

Aside from their suboptimal sleeping habits, Kalsin found the azTéls to be pleasant company. Áron had a tale for every topic he was familiar with, and bucketloads of patient enthusiasm to learn about things outside his expertise. Never had Kalsin been so enraptured by the support structure of a roof as Áron painted a picture made of rafters, collar beams, purlins, and how much weight you needed before the pair of assassins hunting you buried themselves in a heap of broken planks.

Erazza, by contrast, remained relatively withdrawn. At least that's how Kalsin interpreted her quiet nature at first, but soon had to reevaluate that. Watchful. That was more like it. Though her silence sometimes rivaled that of Jack, she carried a presence that whispered 'nothing escapes my sight'. She reminded Kalsin of his childhood self, back when he observed everyone, but avoided direct confrontation like the plague. But he'd grown up since then. It was a difficult road of trial and error, making friends and enemies alike, but merely deciphering a puzzle is worthless without the ability to reach out and rearrange the pieces to your liking. Perception is only half the distance to charisma.

On the fifth night, as the group separated to get their business in order and work out their stiff legs on solid ground, Áron gave Kalsin a searching look as the pair trudged through waist-high weeds of the countryside.

"I hope our presence isn't an inconvenience for you and your bodyguard."

Kalsin raised an amused brow at that.

"What I mean is," Áron started, then thought for a moment and laughed. "Hells, why not just take a page out of your book and ask it straight. Excuse my language, but are you and Jack fucking?"

"Funny guy. I'd never let anyone rest a finger against the cover of my book, let alone tear a page out." Kalsin rested a hand on the rough leather of his faded sketchbook.

Áron didn't let himself get sidetracked. "You don't seem the prude types, so I have my doubts. But, I can't lie, curiosity is getting the best of me."

"Professional curiosity, I hope?"

Áron shook his head. "Have I told you about that one time where I got stuck under a waterwheel? Call me mad, but repeating the experience doesn't seem very appealing to me."

Kalsin didn't dispute the comparison. Smart man. When Áron kept looking at him, Kalsin gave in with a shrug. Why not. His brief romance with Jack was ancient history. "We gave it a try. Turned out, we’re not compatible that way."

"As in?"

"As in she's fucking Scarrd!" Kalsin said. As quick as it came, he reined in his outburst, shutting his eyes for a second before continuing. "Sorry. That was… how should I say this?"

"Take your time. Or, eh, you know what, it's not that important anyway. I just wanted to settle the board since you asked the same thing about me and—"

"It didn’t work because of me. I'm not exactly... wired for physical relationships," Kalsin said at last.

They walked in silence for the next minute. Kalsin asked, "You think Jack is pretty?"

Áron pursed his lips, nodded, then shook his head. "Well... I mean, obviously she is. But definitely not the type of gal I would pursue, personally. Triple my muscle mass is a little too much for me to handle."

"But at least you're able to tell. To find some appeal. Me?" Kalsin laughed bitterly. "I can only see the rotting line carved into her jaw when I look at her." Her, and every other Scarrd, Kalsin added silently.

Most of all, himself.

"Somewhere amidst the years we spent together, our fling was basically a one-week experiment. It mostly consisted of leaning in close, then me splashing the contents of my stomach into her face like I was an embodiment of a sewage disposal pipe. After that, we decided eternal friendship was a more realistic outcome."

"You both settled for less… or was it just you?"

Kalsin remained silent.

When Áron spoke next, he did so with compassion that Kalsin didn't need. "I'm sorry."

"Well. I don't worry about it anymore. I know that ship has long sailed, and honestly, I wouldn't change a thing. If you want someone to have your back, you're better off with someone who does so for you, and not for the fragile shell you inhabit," Kalsin said. "Human bodies are prone to ruin, after all."

"I'm not certain if that outlook is supposed to sound inspiring, or unnecessarily grim."

Kalsin smirked. "Depends on your point of view. Pessimists would take that as a warning sign. They'd focus on the worst parts. Me, I always thought pessimism was a fledgling philosophy, an egg waiting to hatch. Young, foolish, and not at all useful. But when you take pessimism, let it mature and grow by experience, that's when you get realism."

"That so? What about an optimist seasoned by the same experience, who remains to carry a positive view in spite of hard years. What would you call that?"

"I'd call that a dreamer."

Áron didn’t ask more questions that night.

It wasn't long until they finally left behind Grenstel and crossed into Újtavak, perhaps the most militant region of the Republic. The history of the land was a violent one, perched right on the archipelago's northeastern edge and standing as the sole defender against invaders from the east, the Veiled. Due to the constantly looming threat, throughout generations the region had developed aggressive policies, and as a consequence had remained mostly untouched by the effects of political posturing. The Arches held a tight grip over the regions, but Arch Ulra—the governor of Újtavak—was, for the most part, a governor in name only.

Why? Because forcing your will upon a people who lived in constant fear of death was a hell of a lot more difficult than doing the same with civilized city-folks. It's hard to care about some arbitrary image of an Arch soaring through the air, raining down a storm of whirling fire in their rage, when you lived only a few hundred miles from the defense lines constantly under siege.

Imagine. News come, there was another attack. Casualties high. The army needs fresh blood, and parents watch as their sons and daughters take up the sword to defend the land they call their own. It made the citizens of Újtavak wary. It made them a hardy people. And they disliked the Civil House almost as much as the Veiled.

Unfortunately, that dislike alone wouldn't be enough to save Kalsin and his companions from an execution order. For that, they'd need the military to wipe their records clean.

Warmlight had just started to brighten the horizon when they entered the valley where Lostpoint Station was located. Originally built as a backup garrison for notable cities such as Magas and aVonal, Lostpoint Station now stood as a popular stepping stone for travelers seeking—or finishing—passage between Grenstel and Újtavak. Countless taverns held lucrative business offering temporary housing for exhausted folk who wanted a warm bed before whisking them on their way, no doubt spilling some army propaganda in the process, boosting the number of people enlisting in exchange for the military letting the businesses operate on their property. A large block of barracks and administrative buildings, coupled with a recruitment station completed the town's portfolio.

That was the sum of all they learned from the description Treyant had given the azTéls. Kalsin figured it was better than nothing.

His optimism lasted until they approached the gate.

Everything seemed fine at first. Having left the boat some few miles behind, they had donned their cloaks and took on the appearance of just another group of dust-beaten travelers passing through. Unlike some western regions, Újtavak did not require Scarrd to put their blackened skin on display. Kalsin felt somewhat conflicted about that, but for the moment, it served their purpose.

Idle guards tracked their steps from the shadow of the sturdy, multi-layered wooden palisade that encircled the station. Half their numbers wore a crisp golden uniform, the other half crumpled gray, marking them as Clenn and Scarrd divisions waiting to admit entrance for their respective people as they stood on either sides of the gate.

A guard with leathery skin waved a lazy hand, signaling them to stop, then launched into a long-winded monologue about laws and expectations for their stay. Not even pretending to care about their identity, he ranted the words with the energy of a half-lidded lizard. To summarize, he told them stealing and causing violence was fair game, as long as they understood the guard had a responsibility to return stolen property, and beat the shit out of instigators. Kill anyone outside the framework of a duel, and we kill you back, he stated. He extended formal apologies in advance, just in case, and made it clear the military would not pay for any sort of official or unofficial funeral ceremony.

As Kalsin patiently waited out the rehearsed speech to end—now going on about a plethora of retirement benefits one gained upon joining the army—a commotion drew his attention further ahead. A man approached with the guards stepping out of his way, wearing the same gray colors to blend in, though it clearly wasn't a military uniform. He held a board with clipped paper, squinting at it through a monocle over his eye and surveying their group even as he drew closer.

Kalsin swore under his breath and felt Jack tense next to him. Aside from her shield, her only piece of armor was a chestplate, the rest packaged up on their bahten to arouse less suspicion. He couldn't rely on her protection.

"Halt!" called the man just as Áron slowly started shifting away. "If you're law-abiding citizens, you've got nothing to fear. Bothersome as it is, I've to conduct a confirmation of your identities." He spoke with a polite and apologetic tone, albeit slightly out of breath. A guard joked about penpushers sleeping on the job, eliciting a few laughs.

Kalsin thought quickly. Letting them proceed with the inspection was out of the question. Breaking through was impossible as well. Most guardsmen only had swords, but still he could see a good dozen crossbows ready at hand, cranked. He didn't have to glance back to know there was more than fifty feet of open ground before the rocky valley gave cover, so if they tried making a run for it, even a half-competent shooter could pick them off with ease.

"We're looking to join the army," Kalsin raised his voice, looking at the guard who had greeted them. "You, the Scarrd division. You worked a long shift, and we've come a long way. Now, I don't know if you have the authority to recruit right here, right now, but hell, doing so would save us both some time."

"They do not," the man with the monocle said, less friendly now. A few men grumbled at the Civil House official speaking for them, but the guard who had spoken—after a moment of deliberation—just shrugged with a not-my-problem sort of expression.

The azTéls had been slowly trying to create some distance, but stopped as Monocle's distrust, despite their obvious animosity, quickly spread among the guards. Brows furrowed. Hands connected with sword hilts. Crossbows leveled at their group.

"Show us your face," Monocle ordered, all but confirming they were looking for outlaws with face scars. Not good.

Praying to the trinity of gods that somehow he'd be wrong, Kalsin slowly reached up and pushed back his hood. Monocle's eyes flickered down to his paper, then back at him. His lips drew back in a small smile. Very not good.

Before the man could give the order to arrest them, the azTéls turned and ran.

The sudden yells and commotion that followed spooked their bahten, and Kalsin was dragged several feet in the mud back away from the gate, one arm caught in Sap’s reins. Áron and Erazza were already halfway to cover with the efforts they had put in beforehand, but they were still too far away from safety. Kalsin half turned, standing between the fleeing siblings and trying to keep his mount in place and yelling at the soldiers to try and calm things. They had a chance, damn it, they had a chance for this to go right, if only they stood their ground and let bureaucracy sort this out—

That's when he heard the release of strings.

Something much larger than a crossbow bolt barreled into Kalsin, knocking him on his back in a cloud of dust. Jack's body pressed on him like a mountain as she protected his body with her own, some bolts pinging off the shield on her back. Her head was above his as her hood spread around them both, and he could see the expression of pain on her shadowed face.

She'd been hit.

Kalsin tried throwing her off, but Jack redoubled her efforts, hugging him stronger and going as far as to press her cheek on top of his. He felt the dry texture of the scar on her jawline smush against his face.

And the moment it happened, an explosion of pain cracked through his mind.

Losing grasp on reality, Kalsin could faintly hear the heavy breaths coming from Jack. He heard footsteps closing in around them.

And then the vision overwhelmed him.
