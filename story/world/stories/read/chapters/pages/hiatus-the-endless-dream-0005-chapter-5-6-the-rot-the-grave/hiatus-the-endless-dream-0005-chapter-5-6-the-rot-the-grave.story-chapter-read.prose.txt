Chapter 5 - The Rot

I woke up fully conscious. I know that may sound stupid, but most of the time, when I wake up, there's a struggle. A vague acknowledgment that I was awake before flickering back into whatever dream I was in. I would roll over with a foggy brain as I hit the snooze button for the third time.There was none of that this time. I woke up refreshed and aware—as if I had gotten a full eight hours the past two nights.

"I thought you'd never wake up," a voice sounded from the ground beside my hammock. Remi Illia, one of the people in my friend group with Joey, sat on a patchwork quilted blanket, a combination of random fabrics and patterns she had gathered over the years. Its borders weren't yet framed and solidified as she continued to sew the next combination of colors that caught her eye. She held a worn book in her hands, its pages frayed from years of love. Its back matched the deep chocolate of her eyes.A sudden blinding headache. Why was she here? Didn't we stop talking? I cut ties with her, right? No.

No I didn’t. Why would I have? They helped snap me out of my fugue when my brother died. I had snapped at them let them help me, despite myself.

"I needed the sleep," I said, swinging my legs over the hammock to take a seat on the quilt."No shit," she rolled her eyes and closed the book. "You were out, dude. No snoring or nothing. I even swung your hammock, and you didn't react."I was usually a really light sleeper, but I chalked it up to the other world, keeping me in place until I finished an instance."What's up?"

"Nothin' much. On my way back to the dorms, I saw you. Figured we could head to the diner together." She paused, seemingly mulling over before asking, "I know you planned this with us yesterday, but Joey texted me about what happened last night…"The headache spiked to greater heights. Something was off. I didn't plan anything yesterday. No.

No, I planned to go to the diner with my friends.

"Yeah. I'm sure. I think I actually need it." I tried my best to sound reassuring.

I had almost pushed everyone away when my brother passed. Stopped responding to texts. I was irritable when I did see them. They had reached out—expressed their worries about me [and I had been cruel to them] and that had been what finally snapped me out of it. Not the grief of losing [a brother I barely knew] the brother I was just growing close with.

“Did Jamie Maerin and Alexi ever say if they were coming to lunch or not?” I asked, standing and brushing non-existent dirt from my pants as I went about packing up the hammock.

“Yeah, everyone should be there.” She checked her phone, “If we walk, we’ll get there on time. Rather not take the bus.”

We arrived on time, but everyone else had gotten to Jim's diner early. There were five of us in total. Myself, Joey, Remi Illia, Jamie Maerin, and finally Alexi.

Jamie Maerin and Joey were a couple and had been for as long as I could remember. They were private people when it came to their relationship but loud and expressive otherwise.

Jamie Maerin was the tech lead on most of the theater shows, designing, planning, and building truly complicated sets. I had thought she would go into engineering or architecture instead of horticulture. When I had asked she just stared at me with blank hazel eyes before saying, "I make passion projects. Not much else."

Then there was Alexi. He and I did not get along. As someone who had also lost a sibling, I thought that maybe our tensions would cool, but if anything, it only made them worse, and twice now, we had almost come to blows. He had green eyes.

We sat around the table, chatting and joking with one another. We talked about nothing of importance, and I felt myself relax with each passing moment.

Then our food came out. At first, I thought someone had farted – something harmless if not a little awkward. I moved on, not wanting to draw attention to it, but the smell didn't fade. It grew foul and aggressive. It needled into my skin, punched into my nose, and slid down my throat. My stomach twisted. I pressed a napkin to my nose, but it was useless. It was all-encompassing. It radiated – sharp and sour, layered with something like salt dumped in boiling grease. The only thing I could compare it to was a bloated fish I had seen as a kid. It had washed ashore. Festered under the sun. This was somehow worse.

"You okay?" Joey shifted back, giving me some space to breathe. I shook my head.

How was he okay? How were any of them okay?

"Food's here!" Alexi called out, not yet having noticed me hunched over.

Chairs scraped and squealed as everyone adjusted their belongings to make room for their plates. One by one, they noticed me as their small conversations were cut short. Silence spread, and unspoken concerns passed through glances.

"The Colorado Omelette?" The server's voice barely cut through the building, throbbing pressure in my neck.

"That's mine," Remi Illia muttered, raising her hand.

A plate of festering sludge hit the table.

"Yo, what's up with you? Drink some water," Alexi said. I wanted to punch him.

"Hearty Combo?"

Joey raised his hand halfway through, sliding his water cup toward me. Alexi took it the rest of the way, setting it in front of me like I was incapable.

"French toast?"

Jamie Maerin took the plate.

"Drink up," Alexi again.

"Texas scramble?"

Alexi took his food and it was almost too much as I took a shuddering breath.

"Seriously, drink some water, dude. You're scaring everyone," Alexi said.

"Alexi,"  Remi Illiasnapped his name.

"Chicken and Waffles?" The waitress sounded more unsure now.

"That's his, just uh. Set it over here for now," Jamie Maerin said.

The smell was stronger now. All around me. Latched onto my clothes, my skin, the inside of my mouth. It was suffocating the back of my throat, thick as oil, as it helped bile crawl its way up from my stomach. I pinched my nose shut, trying to close myself off from the stench, but it kept coming. It seeped through my sinuses.

Joey stood from the booth. "Come on," he said, voice low and gentle. A firm hand wrapped around my arm, the other resting on my upper back. "Let's go to the bathroom."

Just getting to my feet was a challenge as the world spun. I slowly opened my eyes. Everything seemed more reflective, causing the previously mundane lighting to turn blinding. A headache rose, which almost made me keel over then and there. The static began reforming in my vision again.

Joey practically dragged me along. I tried not to look at the tables. Instead, I looked in the serving hatch, wondering if the chefs knew what they were cooking.

Standing in the window was a larger but well-kept man with a full beard. I recognized Jim easily enough; it was his diner, after all. What I didn't recognize was the hollow look in Jim's eyes as he used a knife to garnish a burger with shavings of his open skull.

The work looked messy. The movements were uncoordinated like someone was dealing with a cursor of constantly changing sensitivity.

Jim looked up and waved with a quarter of an arm.

Fighting the bile down, I waved back.

Joey continued to drag me along. As soon as he opened the door to the bathroom, I broke into a run, barely making it to the sink before spilling digested water and acid up into the sink. I hadn't eaten anything since lunch yesterday.

Joey reached over and turned the water on, letting the sludge wash itself down the sink as he waited patiently by the door.

There was a flush from the back, and a balding elderly man awkwardly shuffled past, barely taking the time to wash his hands before leaving.

I straightened my back, almost crying out as I felt another wave hit.

"I'll wait outside. Take your time," Joey said as he excused himself.

Then I was alone. I stared up into the mirror, shuddering as I did so. This place had to be an instance on the other side.

It was real, but it wasn't real here, or it shouldn't have been. I took a deep breath in. My brother had been able to eat food. Unless he was just constantly eating rot and had gotten used to it, that meant it was like everything else and wasn't actually rotting.

My throat felt torn and scratchy now, and I struggled to lean down to wash out my mouth. With shaking arms, I leaned against the sink and stared at the reflection in the mirror.

I looked away just as quickly. I was always bad with faces.

Calming down proved impossible as every time my breathing settled, I saw the Chef in my mind. I gave up on trying to stay at the diner.

I sent a text message to the group chat: a short, concise explanation.

Seconds later, as I walked out the front,  Remi Illia texted me personally, "Sorry about your stomach. Maybe call it a day and get some more sleep. You were out cold, so maybe you need some more."

I read the text message over and over again. My legs shook as the worst possibility entered my head.

With wide eyes, I raced to the nearest bus stop. I had to get to the cemetery.

What if my brother hadn't been in a coma? What if he had just been stuck in an instance?

Chapter 6 - The Grave

It felt silly, running at full sprint to a bus stop only to have to wait ten minutes, to then have the bus slowly crawl its way across the city—stopping every fourth minute to pick someone up or drop them off. It wasn't just inconvenient. It was hell.

My insides burned and tore. The pocket in my chest coiled, tensing as it constricted the air from my lungs. Thoughts raced through my mind so fast I barely had time to process them, visiting and revisiting the same ideas – sporadically completing them less or more before forgetting and repeating the cycle.

My brother was part of The Endless Dream.

He couldn't be woken forcibly.

Remi Illia couldn't wake me up. Had she tried? It sounded like she had.

The dream must have trapped my brother. Did they check his vitals? I heard the flatline.

Did they embalm him when we buried him?

Did I kill him?

Why couldn't I remember?

The thoughts kept coming, my nausea gripping me by the throat. I was grateful I hadn't eaten. But what I wouldn't do for some water.

I jumped out of the bus three stops early and sprinted the rest of the way. I didn't even bother with the front entrance. Instead, I simply grabbed the fence bars and pulled myself up and over. I stumbled as I hit the dirt, the hairs on my neck straightening as my palm embedded itself into something sharp. I let out a hiss, reflexively yanking my hand back, only to see nothing. I looked at the ground, but there was only soft grass and plants.

Of course, the cemetery was an instance. If I slept here, who knows what I'd find.

I hadn't visited my brother's grave since the day we buried him, but I remembered where it was at the bottom of the largest hill.

I raced along the grass, dodging tombstones until I reached my destination.

It was a simple marker. A little generic. With the usual words of "taken too soon," "here lies," and the years he lived.

My eyes caught on the date. Rowan's birthday. It would've been just a few months ago. He would've been…

I thought back to the movie. To the food we got before. Our happy little day out. The overwhelming smell of popcorn movie butter. The way we recounted our favorite parts from the movie and laughed at stupid jokes that hadn't even been funny back then. The way he nudged me, saying something about doing this next year.

I was just getting to know him. I hadn't known him all my life, and just when I had begun to see a little more of him, he was gone.

It was all what-ifs. What if I had gotten to know him sooner.What if I had reached out.What if he felt he could confide in me.What if he thought I cared.What if—All lead to the same question.

Would he still be here?

I tasted salt at the corner of my lips and felt my chest heaving in patchy breaths. My face contorted—forehead creasing, eyes shutting, mouth scrunching as a whining whistle escaped the back of my throat. I tried to stifle the tears, to compress the spring in my chest again, but each deep breath caught and pushed itself out in another cry.

My knees buckled, and I dropped. Grass dampened beneath me, cool against my palms, and yet still, another sensation—one of sharp stones. I hated how loud I was, how the sobs echoed through the cemetery. How snot dripped from my nose, mixing with the tears as I curled into a ball. Words sprang from my lips, mixing with the high-pitched cries.

"I miss you."
