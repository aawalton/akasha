While Sibyl was busy modifying the storage ring into a portable bomb, Veronica took the opportunity to form her fourth set of mana rings. If she were going to get into a fight with a mythical asvarian, the progenitors of magic, she would need to be at her strongest.

She sat atop a flat stone in an open field. With her eyes closed in meditation, she formed the rings into their proper shape. Sage remained with her, documenting her progress all the way until the final step. Ever since she repaired her mana cores with the Blue Dryad’s potion, she had been making steady progress at forming her next set. Once she attained the fourth tier through the emberstone and drakebone ash, forming her rings became much easier.

As she consolidated the second ring, the final of her fourth pair, her eyes snapped open.

A chime resonated in her mind, and a blue panel flashed before her.

[You have completed a quest: Form fourth set of Mana Rings][Mana Regeneration has increased from 700 to 1420.][Reward: Mana Ring Rotational Burst and Ascended Body have been upgraded and fused.][New Perk Granted: Exalted Body]

Veronica blinked a few times, then stared at the status panel.

Aside from the obvious advantage of forming her fourth set of mana rings, she was now actually looking forward to Sage’s upgrades. It highlighted just how accustomed she had become to Sage as a whole, and how important she was to Veronica’s growth.

“Exalted Body?” Veronica read aloud. “Sounds interesting. What does it do?”

Ascended Body was already an extremely useful perk. It allowed her to mimic a low-tier aura user, or more accurately, the equivalent of a full-body enhancement spell from a second-tier ascension mage.

Mana Ring Rotational Burst was far more niche. It allowed her to immediately enter her exalted form without waiting, condensing that burst of energy into a kinetic blast. However, that was mostly useful if she was suddenly ambushed or dropped into a fight without warning. The more rings she formed, the faster she could enter her exalted form anyway, gradually making the perk less useful.

[Spell: Exalted Body][Discipline: Path of Ascension][Equivalent Tier: 3][Effect: By accelerating the rotational speed of your mana rings in an instant, you can now convert all of the initial energy gained into strengthening your body. This will grant you increased physical capabilities, including speed, strength, and stamina. This enhancement will last for as long as your exalted form is maintained. Excessive damage may overtax this ability and cancel its effect.]

Veronica’s brows rose.

She certainly liked the sound of that.

Not only did she already like the Ascended Body perk, but this version allowed her to maintain that enhanced state for as long as she remained in her exalted form. Now that she had a total of eight mana rings, she could maintain that form during a high-intensity fight for nearly four hours.

Though one thing bothered her.

“Why is the equivalent tier just three?” she asked.

[Your body is still not physically fit to handle the effects of a fourth-tier ascension perk.]

“What? But I’ve had a morning jog nearly every day,” she replied. “Well… not every day, but a few times a week. Is that not enough?”

[Simple cardio is not enough to reinforce your body. I would recommend some other physical training aside from a morning jog. Perhaps you can ask Nasha to help. I know you are envious of her body. It would do you well to imitate it.]

Veronica’s lips pressed thin.

She didn’t want to admit it, but… yeah, she was a little envious of Nasha’s figure.

Not only was she beautiful, but her body was strong and toned as well. Veronica had always associated visible muscle with men. Most male aura users and knights trained their bodies relentlessly, constantly talking about how much they could lift or how strong they had become. Men like that had been everywhere in Annesheim. One couldn’t properly use aura without a strong physical foundation, after all.

She had never found that sort of build particularly appealing on herself.

Nasha was different.

She was slim, but there was obvious strength beneath it. Her arms, stomach, and legs were toned from years of training without making her look bulky. If anything, it only made her more attractive.

Veronica frowned slightly.

Maybe Sage had a point.

Before she could ask more questions about her newly received perk, she heard someone jogging toward her.

She turned and spotted Gwen.

“Hey! Sibyl finished making the bomb. We’re all ready to go now,” she said.

Veronica’s expression turned serious. She stood and nodded.

The Last Gold would make the perfect target to test the power of an exalted mage no longer hindered by her physical limitations.

After an hour of walking, Veronica arrived at the entrance of Afallon Caer. According to Phillus, that was the name Morgan had given to the castle.

Since the drawbridge wasn’t down, Phillus commanded nature around them to grow, forming a makeshift path of roots to cross the moat. Once they crossed, nearly all of them—besides Phillus, Perri, and Sibyl—were left speechless.

It was a massive white structure, much larger in person than seen from afar. The walls were partially engulfed by thick vines and roots.

She wasn’t surprised, considering there were massive trees growing along its edges, some even branching inward, smothering the castle in a blanket of green and brown. It reminded her of an expensive plant in a noble’s home she had visited in the past. A young tree that grew beneath a suspended glass crystal; eventually, as it grew, the branches wrapped over the crystal almost like a prison.

She stared at the entrance. Two enormous white doors stood before them, covered in faded carvings and filigree-like designs depicting everything from roses to animals.

Sibyl walked ahead of everyone and stopped before the doors, staring straight up.

She turned to face them. “Alright. This is as far as I’ll go.”

She held up one arm, and the limb began to shift. Wood and vines twisted as the storage ring she had hidden away was carefully pushed into her palm. Sibyl glanced at Veronica and tossed it through the air.

Thanks to Sage’s visualization perk, Veronica saw the trajectory of the throw and caught it. Her past self would have simply caught it with mana, or let it fall, out of fear of rupturing the stone within her body.

“I’ve modified it to explode. The range is about one and a half meters, like I explained before. You just need to imbue it with enough mana to shatter the safeguards. Once that happens, you’ll have about three seconds before it detonates,” Sibyl explained.

She raised her other arm, and a pair of small metal devices crawled up along it. She tossed one toward Nasha, who promptly caught it.

“What’s this?” Nasha asked.

“A communication node,” Sibyl replied. She held up the matching device between two fingers. “I’ll receive one signal when the storage ring detonates. If you confirm the Last Gold is dead, destroy that node afterward. Once I receive both signals, I’ll deactivate the castle seal. But you must destroy the node within five minutes of the ring exploding.”

“Why the time limit?” Gwen asked.

“A failsafe,” Sibyl said, turning toward her. “If the storage ring detonates and fails to kill him, there’s a chance the Last Gold kills all of you instead and finds the node himself. If he happens to destroy it, I might mistake that for your signal and accidentally release him from the castle.”

Veronica’s eyes narrowed. “So if we don’t destroy it within five minutes…”

“I’ll assume you failed,” Sibyl said. “I won’t deactivate the seal, and you’ll be stuck in there for the next five thousand years. So you’ll only have one chance to do this correctly.”

Everyone looked at one another.

Veronica, specifically, looked at Gwen. “You don’t have to go in there if you don’t want to. You’re still getting used to your phoenix spells. You aren’t as strong as me or Nasha quite yet, so this will be a big risk.”

Gwen looked at her, and for a moment, she said nothing. Her brows furrowed as she pursed her lips. Eventually, she spoke.

“No, I’ll go. I need the experience. Besides, even if I’m weak, I can still help with your injuries. Who knows? Maybe some of the guardians are still inside, hurt.”

Veronica smiled lightly. “You sure about this?”

Gwen nodded. “It’s not like I can get back home if you and Nasha die, right?”

Veronica glanced over at Nasha, who simply tilted her head. She turned back to Gwen. “I guess you’re right.”

“You stay out here with the tree, boy,” Orban said beside them, looking up at his apprentice.

“You don’t have to go with them, do you, Master? If you only want the forge, then maybe you can just stay out here and wait for them to finish things inside,” Theo said.

Orban shook his head. “No. I have a feelin’ they might need me in there. ’Sides, nothin’ is free in this world, boy. I gotta fight for my scraps. I can’t watch them ladies go in there and fight for their lives just so I can use some fancy forge. I’ll gut that Last Gold myself if needed to show mah worth. I ain’t no freeloader.” He jabbed a finger into Theo’s chest. “You ain’t either, boy. While yer out here, I expect ya to learn a thing or two from that talking root.”

Theo glanced up at Sibyl, then back down at Orban. “Sibyl? But she doesn’t really seem like the type to teach others. Or…” He pursed his lips. “Even talk to them.”

“I dun wanna hear yer excuses, boy. If she don’t like you, then you find a way. Blacksmithin’ ain’t only ’bout iron and metal. I saw some things in that root’s home that were forged nearly perfectly. If a root’s beatin’ you in metalwork, then you dun deserve to learn under me, understand?”

Theo straightened. “Yes, Master,” he said with a short nod.

“Hmph. Good.”

“I’ll stay out here, Phillus,” Perri said, flying backward toward Theo.

Phillus nodded. “I’ll be back soon, Perri.”

Once everyone looked back at Sibyl, she continued.

She stared directly at Veronica. “I’ll open the seal now. You’ll all be able to enter, but you won’t be able to leave until I receive both signals.”

“Got it,” Veronica replied.

The Great Dryad nodded. She turned and walked toward the tall castle doors. She placed one hand against the surface, and the entire gate began to rumble.

It only took a few seconds for the doors to open inward. Beyond them was a courtyard with a path leading toward the main castle entrance. Everything inside looked maintained and new, completely undisturbed. There was no sign of destruction or even evidence of a battle.

Sibyl turned around. “The Last Gold should be sealed within the castle itself, not the courtyard. Don’t worry about destroying anything. The castle is enchanted with an automatic repair spell.”

“That answers my question of why it looks so green in there,” Gwen said softly.

As everyone stepped forward, Sibyl gave one last warning. “Remember, I said the seal was one of the only reasonable methods that worked to contain him. We tried other methods, but they proved less effective.”

Veronica paused before crossing the threshold. “What do you mean?”

“Within the castle, there’s a chance you’ll run into a few traps. Some of the guardians set up their own methods of stopping the Last Gold, so you’ll likely encounter them while you’re in there. Illusions or mazes. That sort of thing.”

“And no,” Sibyl added before anyone could ask, “I don’t know how to break the traps, either. I wasn’t the one who set them up, so you’re on your own.”

“How reassuring,” Nasha said.

Veronica looked over at the Great Stag. “I’m sure Phillus might have some insight into them?”

Phillus nodded. “I should be able to recognize any traps if they were made by Avilla. Just stick by my side.”

“Now that’s reassuring,” Gwen chimed in.

With that final warning from Sibyl, they all stepped forward, passed through the barrier, and entered into the castle’s courtyard.

It felt… different inside.

The air here was… odd. Much colder and drier compared to the nature of the forest outside. Despite how great the grass looked, there was an emptiness to everything. Almost an… eerie trait to them.

Gwen turned, seeing that the castle’s doors were slowly shutting behind them. Theo and Perri both waved at them before the doors eventually fully closed.

“Anything we should be worried about?” Nasha asked Phillus while drawing the bow from her back.

The Great Stag took the lead, looking left and right. “No. The castle is generally safe. There are some measures to deter thieves and trespassers, but none that are fatal. The problem is if Avilla created anything in here.”

“You mentioned that name earlier,” Gwen said, turning back around. “Who’s Avilla?”

“Avilla is one of Avalon’s guardians. She’s obsessed with magic and arrays. She helped Morgan and Sibyl create the barrier that protects Avalon. She is also the one most prone to making traps and other strange illusions. Since you’ve all met Perri, I assume you all know about the infinite maze within Faewood.”

Veronica stepped forward. “Yeah. Perri told us we wouldn’t be able to leave unless she guided us out.”

Phillus nodded. “That is correct. That enchantment was created by Avilla. She happens to be great friends with the fairies, always doing new things for them to trick wanderers.”

He stared ahead at the inner castle’s doors. “Come on. Let’s go and see if anyone is still alive.”
