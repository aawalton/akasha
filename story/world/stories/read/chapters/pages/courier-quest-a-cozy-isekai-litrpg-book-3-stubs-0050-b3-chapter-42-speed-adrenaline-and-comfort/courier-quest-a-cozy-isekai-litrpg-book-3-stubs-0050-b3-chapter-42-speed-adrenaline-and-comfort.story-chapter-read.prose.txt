The next morning was a whirlwind of activity. Clay did end up wanting to go back to the garage. Now that they weren’t so tired, they were ready to tackle another day of playing magitech engineer. In fact, the earth elemental was so animated that they pantomimed what they had done to Trevor in a bout of acting and shapeshifting that lasted nearly ten whole minutes.

He hadn’t understood most of it, but he knew his familiar had a good time and he wanted to indulge their interest. It was nice seeing them fired up about something. They also knew that roller coasters were going to be worse than Rambunctious C when it came to vibrations and seemed to want to put their talents to good use.

Lora, always helpful, teleported them to the garage first where one of the mechanics had to wake up Matilsa, who had crashed only an hour earlier. While the head mechanic was in the process of being roused, the newlyweds were briefed on how the results of last night’s experiment went.

The initial tests of Clay’s engine in the Duck E had been promising, though they had needed a larger, and thus rarer, power source to keep it running. It was a step in the right direction even if it wasn’t economically viable, and they already had some ideas on how to get the familiar’s work closer to the original.

In her sleep-deprived state, Matilsa had started crying and attempted to hug Trevor and Clay for coming back. Lora had been quick to stop her, but the courier understood at the very least. The woman was thankful, tired, and probably hadn’t been expecting them again so soon.

Trevor warred with himself about whether or not to pass off one of their extra Full Night’s Rest. They still had more than enough for the rest of their honeymoon, his worries was more that the car-crazy Matilsa would find some way to never sleep at all after figuring out such a thing existed.

Their technology was ahead of Lucent’s, but their alchemy and magic were far behind. In the end, he had given it to one of the saner mechanics and left it up to them on whether or not to administer the potion. He didn’t tell them how it worked, simply describing it as coffee with an extra, long-lasting kick and only giving it to their boss if it seemed necessary to make progress. They nodded, taking his words seriously.

There was nothing to do after that but head to their next destination. Adabelle had put up with the mechanic’s shenanigans out of respect for Clay’s wishes, but she grew- increasingly restless. No one else had caught it except for Trevor, who had come to learn her tells well. She could hide from everyone except her husband.

When they prepared for their final teleport, she remained dignified. He could see the fierce alertness in her eyes, though. The thrill seeker she used to be back in her adventuring days had awoken last night, and she was ready for more.

With Clay situated and Matilsa possibly taken care of, Lora teleported them to the entrance of the amusement park, Lemon World.

They appeared off to the side in a small section that had been cordoned off, as was normal for the Summoned’s Power. It was still early in the day, so there wasn't very crowded yet. Adabelle’s ears twitched as the sound of the attractions reached them.

Roller coasters barreled down their tracks, people screamed in joy, fear, or both, and music filled their ears. A giant tower that reached at least a hundred and fifty feet in the air had rows of people slowly rising upwards on its four sides.

“Lemon World was mostly built by Sammie the Gamer, but she was guided by the architect Dof Lemon based on the writings of an Otherworld Dreamer who was alive four hundred years ago,” Lora explained as she gestured for the newlyweds to follow her. She ducked under the fence, and they did the same.

“It wasn’t all that difficult according to the book he wrote, The Construction of Lemon World, after it was done,” she continued. “Sammie was all about speed, adrenaline, and comfort. She was no stranger to amusement parks, so getting her to make all of this was simply a matter of keeping her on track. Difficult, but it aligned with her interests so the Summoned gave it some level of priority.”

“How long did it take to build?” Trevor asked as they approached the ticket gate.

“In total, about ten years,” Lora answered. “Some level of priority didn’t mean that it was fast, unfortunately. Sammie had a lot of other interests pulling her in different directions. When she started a ride, she finished it, though. This is the culmination of all of that.”

“It’s impressive,” the courier said as they stopped in front of the gates. A roller coaster twisted over the entrance and they were treated to the dual rider’s screams.

Adabelle watched them come and go with interest. “It’s not as fast as Rambunctious C, but it is still faster than anything we have to ride on our continent.”

“The speed is one thing, but the twists and turns are the rest of it,” Trevor replied, squeezing her hand. “There’s going to be a lot of pressure put on your body. Nothing that you can’t handle, but it’s going to be a different kind of experience than just going fast.”

“I’m looking forward to it,” she declared with an easy smile.

Lora reached into her bag and pulled out two tickets that were neon blue, the color of lemons in this world, with a picture of the fruit in the middle. She handed them to Trevor. “Those will get you in, but they’re also specially marked,” she explained as the man took them. “You’ll be able to eat for free wherever you decide to go, and there are several restaurants inside. Some of the bigger rides have an option to skip the line with these tickets, too.”

“Fast pass and unlimited meals, nice,” Trevor said, handing one to his wife. “As someone who’s probably been here at least a couple of times, is there anywhere you think we should go first?”

The teleporter thought about it for a moment. “Left, I’d say.”

Adabelle tilted her head curiously. “Left? Do you mean the entire left side? Or something else?”

“You’ll know after you see the map,” Lora answered with a wink. She moved around the newlyweds and started to push them towards the ticket booth. “There’s one right inside, can’t miss it. It’s also a school day, so the park shouldn’t get too packed. I’ll be back an hour after nightfall, so feel free to come and see me. If you’re not there, I’ll check again an hour later, but I’ll assume you went back to the hotel on your own if you’re still absent. I’m going to go watch Clay now, so, bye!”

The pressure on their backs disappeared as the woman teleported away, leaving them by themselves. They received a bunch of strange looks from the demons who had been keeping an eye on the two humans amongst them, but Adabelle gave them no mind as she pulled her husband the rest of the way to the booth.

They were quickly let in to Lemon World as the woman at the gate took their stubs, leaving them with the majority of the ticket. While Trevor was used to paper maps at his amusement parks, the one that Lora had told them about was a permanent fixture. He scoured it with a smile on his face.

The entire area was set up to look like the pattern inside of a lemon. There was a circular path going all the way around the park with rides both inside and out. The very center had a plaza that was connected to the outer ring at regular intervals. It appeared as though there was also a train called the Lime Line that traveled to some of the more popular destinations.

In fact, every ride had a fruit-based name. The Citrus Cruiser, Watermelon Waterslide, Mango Mountain, Dragon Drop, and more.

“This is definitely Lemon World,” Trevor remarked with a chuckle.

“You can say that again,” Adabelle agreed as she studied the map. Reaching out, she tapped the star indicating where they were, and then traced left. The first ride was the Tangerine Tango. “I’m ready when you are, son of Ander.”

“Oh, I’ve never been more ready for anything in my life,” he said as he started leading his wife towards their first ride.

The whole time, Trevor was looking around. There were a lot of plants, especially those that bore fruit, and there were enough leafy bushes and hedges that they couldn’t see across the park. Everything was segmented according to the architect’s design. It was very similar to what the courier was used to, but he enjoyed the wonder in his wife’s eyes as she saw it all for the first time.

Cindal had been the only Otherworld Dreamer, someone who could observe alternate universes as they slept, that Trevor had heard about. He knew that there had to be more out there because they had a title for people with that ability. Rare, but not unheard of.

Now, he was clearly in the presence of the vision of another. Even if Lora hadn’t told them about it, he would have had an inkling that there was more than just Sammie behind all of this. Trevor had seen the chaotic design philosophy behind her vehicles already, and this was clearly guided by a hand that loved both the idea of an amusement park and fruit to an almost weird degree.

The courier wouldn’t knock it, though. Lemon World was visualized by a man named Dof Lemon; he could excuse the fruit focus as long as the rides were fun.

Trevor squeezed his wife’s hand as they approached the entrance to the Tangerine Tango. The roller coaster was painted green, making it easily distinguishable from its counterparts, and carried only two riders at a time. He thought it fitting given the name.

While he had also thought it would lead to a longer line, it was moving surprisingly fast. It seemed as though the park workers had found a groove long ago with the riders when it came to loading, unloading, and getting everything ready for the next set. There was no pass to the front on this one, but it still only took a few minutes.

“I hadn’t realized that getting our thrills would mean so much waiting,” Adabelle had said.

“This isn’t even bad,” Trevor told her. “I know of some rides back home where people would wait well over an hour to get on.”

The noblewoman had given her husband a glance that told him she didn’t really believe that, but he nodded. It was the truth.

They reached the front of the line and were quickly ushered into the twin seats, which straddled the black track. Tangerines were that same color in this world, but the cars were orange with red and yellow stripes down the sides. Trevor thought that was a nice touch given the inspiration the park came from, and he looked forward to seeing if that trend continued.

Sitting with confidence, Trevor noted that the bar went over his lap and then a separate section came up to protect his chest. He was curious about that for a moment before Adabelle repeated the process after watching him.

With so many different styles of horns, there would be no way to accommodate them all with a seat that came down over the head. There was also an empty spot in the seat for tails, though it closed gently behind him with a mechanical whir. This was safer for everyone.

The attendant checked to make sure they were secure before gesturing towards another park worker, and their car lurched to a start. They cleared the loading station and emerged into the open air. Trevor reached over and took Adabelle’s hand while she looked at the track ahead. The cars turned a corner before starting their ascent, chugging along with an all too familiar sound.

“So, there’s a tradition as we get to the top,” he said over the sounds of the track.

“A tradition?” Adabelle repeated curiously.

“Yup. Now, this might be a bit advanced for you considering it’s your first time,” he explained teasingly, eliciting a snort from his wife. “But once we reach the top, we raise our hands in the air and scream on the way down. Afterwards you can grab the bar, hold on to me, continue screaming, whatever you want.”

“And what do we get if we keep our hands up the entire time?”

“Personal pride and your friends won’t tease you about it, not that I would anyway.”

Adabelle turned her head to glance at her husband, clearly not believing his words. “You wouldn’t?”

“Okay, I might. And, by might, I mean definitely,” Trevor conceded, waggling his eyebrows. She laughed at his antics. “Anyway, do what you want. This is your first time, so I won’t be too mean afterwards.”

Defiant, Adabelle raised her arms. She didn’t let go of her husband. “What’s the verdict on holding hands?”

“We can,” he answered as he raised the arm that wasn’t attached to her. “No matter what it was on Earth, I’m not going to want to let go of you. This is fine.”

The two smiled at each other as they reached the apex of the initial hill. Slowing to a stop, the car sat there, allowing them to survey the park. Their gaze panned across Lemon World, seeing the other roller coasters, rides, and attractions. The Dragon Drop was the tallest, and it was near the center.

Trevor, having been to several parks like this before, quickly picked out the places he wanted to go the most. Some of these rides looked better than others. Not all of them could be created equal, unfortunate as it was.

Adabelle looked at the track ahead. “Are we going to—”

The ride started, and they plummeted towards the ground.

Her words were lost as she screamed and Trevor whooped. Adabelle’s grip on him tightened as they picked up speed. They went into a tunnel underground before curving sharply upwards. The tracks immediately emerged into a corkscrew, sending the newlyweds into two full rolls before giving them some breathing room.

That didn’t last long. Once they received enough height, the tracks turned into a funnel, sending them in a wide downward spiral three times before hitting another turn. To Trevor’s surprise, they didn’t lose speed as they went up the next incline. It was probably something magical or had to do with Sammie’s Power, but it brought them to a higher area.

They reached the top and fell downwards, hitting a loop on the way before crossing over the park’s entrance. There were some excited faces, mostly young, who were pointing up at them with wonder. The sight was gone in a flash as they hit a few hills while turning back towards where they started.

Driving had been nice, but this was lighting up all sorts of things for Trevor. He laughed as they soared down the track, the movements a little jerky but nothing they couldn’t handle. There had been a niggling worry in the back of his mind that his enhanced stats would somehow make it less fun, but he really had no complaints.

Klar and Griffin had really gone above and beyond when planning this trip.

The ride slowed as they entered the loading station again. He finally put his hands down, which prompted Adabelle to do the same. He took a moment to observe his wife.

Her cheeks were flushed, her eyes glowed bright, and her hair had been thoroughly tousled by the wind. There was an indescribable look on her face, caught between two emotions.

“Trevor,” she said.

“Yeah?”

A thrill-fueled grin blossomed on her lips. “I do believe I’d like to go again.”
