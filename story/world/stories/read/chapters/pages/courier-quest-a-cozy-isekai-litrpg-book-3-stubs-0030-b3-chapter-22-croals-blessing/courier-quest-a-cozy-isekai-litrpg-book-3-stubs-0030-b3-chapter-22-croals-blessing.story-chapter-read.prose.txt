The dragon continued to emerge from the sea for several seconds after Adabelle had gotten Trevor’s attention. Two dark antlers curled upwards from his forehead, and rows and upon rows of white spikes connected with sails grew out of his back. Water dripped from his scales, which were a glorious turquoise blue, and were the only source of disturbances in the ocean below.

After what Trevor guessed was about a hundred and twenty feet of dragon had passed, Croal was finally free. He had no wings to speak of, twisting and turning as he ascended. His four clawed limbs flowed gracefully as if allowing him to swim in the air as easily as the sea.

The shore-bound crowd’s cheers, claps, and whistles could be easily heard despite the distance. Trevor looked around the boat to find the stairs to the upper deck. They were close by, and he took Adabelle’s hand to lead her there. She didn’t hesitate to follow him.

From their new vantage point, they could see that most of the other boats were far off. None of them were as close to Mount Cruon and Croal as the Serendipity. High Priest Worth and some of his acolytes were on the sun deck, outside of the glass bubble, and appeared to be praying. A few of the more adventurous folks were out there, too.

Most of the spectators were already on the upper deck. There were hardly any of the reclining seats left, so Trevor summoned his own beach chairs and set them by the railing. They sat down together, lounging comfortably as Croal danced above them.

The blue dragon continued to swim through the air, twisting in on himself several times without turning into a knot. Water continued to fall from his scales like rain, landing on the Serendipity. The priests outside were going to be absolutely soaked.

Coming to a stop, Croal looked upon Paladona. His head slowly swept to the side, as if taking as much of it in as possible, before glancing down at the ship. Large, yellow eyes seemed to search the Serendipity for a moment before he started descending face-first.

Trevor stiffened, and Adabelle squeezed his hand. Croal didn’t come down on the ship, but near the sun deck. He disappeared into the ocean, once again leaving it so unnaturally flat that Trevor almost believed that he himself could walk across the water.

The dragon’s disappearance didn’t last long. He surfaced again on the other side of the Serendipity, then curved back into the ocean. Everyone could make out his spines moving, so they knew that he was still in motion. The water around them finally started to show some life once Croal emerged and dove again, now allowing two sections of his body to remain above water like a sea serpent.

Adabelle was quick to point out the changes. Small water spouts started spinning around the ship. They began around the size of a kobold, but soon grew as tall as the Serendipity’s glass dome. Every one of them circled Croal, who in turn was circling them.

“That is some phenomenal mana control,” the demonkin remarked with a whisper, her eyes darting as if to take in everything.

“You can say that again, the water is still really calm,” Trevor agreed, matching her volume. Some of the kids on board, including Siral, were loudly admiring Croal’s work, but most of the adults were watching in silence.

“It’s more than you can see,” Adabelle replied almost apologetically as she spoke normally again. Her tail gave his wrist a small squeeze. “The skill I picked after getting my Magic stat to ten was Mana Sight, so I can see the threads of power that Croal is weaving, and it’s unlike anything I have seen before. It’s almost more intricate than the Festival of Golden Lights, and he’s doing it all without the aid of a ritual circle.”

“That sounds like I’m really missing out,” he said, though he didn’t feel like he was. The show so far was already very fun.

Croal emerged from the water once more as the spouts he created started to deteriorate at the top. Droplets splashed everywhere, going far and wide, and created a cascade of rainbows above the Serendipity. The blue dragon went into the middle of this system, returning to their twisting, turning ways.

“They’re the true progenitors of magic, you know,” Adabelle said absently as she watched Croal work. “I know you’ve seen wizards cast spells before, but they learned how to mold the world with the help of dragons back in ancient times.”

“I actually didn’t know that,” Trevor replied. “But I assume wizards aren’t quick to bring that up when all they want to do is cool stuff. Most of the ones I’ve seen have pretty big heads, and I’m not talking about their brains.”

“That’s true,” she giggled.

“Can Glyrphizith do something like this?” he asked. “She’s supposed to be powerful since she’s a dragon older than Jackson was, right? I kind of remember that being a thing.”

The mood suddenly shifted before Adabelle could answer. Each of the water spouts suddenly grew an extra twenty feet, sending droplets even farther than they had before. Croal became more energetic as he evened out and created almost a perfect circle with his tail close to his face. His speed increased and the sails and spines on his back started to glow bright yellow.

Electricity coiled and arched around him. There was no rumble or sound as bolts struck the Serendipity’s glass dome. It appeared as though it had been built with this in mind because the priests on the sun deck were perfectly safe despite the lightning. The glass didn’t even darken as it dispersed the energy.

All at once, the sky turned white as a pillar of electricity met in the middle of Croal’s coil and shot upwards. Thunder cracked loudly, causing Adabelle to wince as it slowly rumbled away. The water spouts scattered, sending the last of their droplets into the mist where the rainbows could still be seen, and the dragon began to descend again.

[[Congratulations! You have received the Sea Dragon Croal’s blessing.

For the next year, treat your Stamina stat as two higher for the purposes of fighting sickness and disease.]]

“Oh, that’s cool,” Trevor laughed. “A whole year? If normal people get that blessing, then that’s huge! Paladona is a lucky place.”

“Indeed it is. We might have to return next year.”

“You won’t hear any arguments from me, lady.”

It had only taken a couple of minutes, but the courier had been impressed by the show. Croal’s control over the sea and sky had been absolute and awe inspiring. The waves didn’t even start back up again until the dragon was completely underneath the water. Cheers and applause came from the other watchers on the ship, and the newlyweds joined in.

Standing up, Trevor offered his wife help to her feet before sending his chairs away. Clay had become longer, changing from a stoat into something more draconic. They circled around their master’s shoulder in a recreation of what they had just witnessed.

He had expected the Serendipity to turn around and head back to the city, but it stayed where it was. A group had stood up and moved to the other side of the deck. They were pointing and speaking excitedly to themselves. With a gesture to Adabelle, Trevor walked over to join them.

“Croal’s so big!” Siral said. “Can we go down there and meet him, father? Can we?”

“No, that’s the priest’s job,” Mital replied gently. “We’ll be able to meet him later in the year. I’ll bring you then.”

Trevor leaned over the railing behind the crowd to get a better view around them, and he blinked when he saw the blue dragon’s massive head at the front of the ship. He was talking with High Priest Worth.

The moment he came into view, though, one of those enormous yellow eyes focused on Trevor specifically. Croal nodded, looking from the courier back to the priest. The kobold turned around and sought him out, and he stood up straight.

Taking Adabelle’s hand, he led her away from the crowd. “So, uh…”

“Did something happen?” she asked, concerned.

“I think the dragon knows us somehow,” Trevor said, also concerned.

The noblewoman gave him an affectionate look that still managed to clearly convey that the idea was an absurd one. “There’s no way Croal knows us, honey.”

“Will Lady Adabelle and Trevor please come to the sun deck,” a voice announced, coming through what had to have been invisible speakers set up throughout the ship. “I repeat, will Lady Adabelle and Trevor please come to the sun deck.”

The courier gave his wife a look, and she sighed. “I stand corrected. Let’s go see what Croal wants with us.”

“They get to go?” Siral loudly complained. “I’m going to need some shinier coins.”

People moved out of Trevor and Adabelle’s way as they made their way down from the upper deck and towards the front section of the ship that the dome didn’t cover. It was soaking wet from Croal’s show, but the water parted for them as they were welcomed onto the deck. The noblewoman held herself well, being used to dragons and high-intensity meetings, and her husband tried to match her.

Inside, though, he was freaking out. Not in a bad way, he just hadn’t been expecting to meet another Dragon so soon. Adabelle took his arm, wrapped her tail around his wrist, and stroked his skin. He started to calm under her touch.

Croal’s slitted eyes alone were almost Trevor’s height. Both of them focused on the newcomers as he breathed in, causing a noticeable wind. He opened his mouth, showing off teeth that would make any megalodon feel inadequate. They stopped next to High Priest Worth.

“Hello!” the dragon, with a head the size of a bus, greeted in a surprisingly normal voice.

Trevor blinked. Croal sounded like just some guy. He really shouldn’t have been surprised considering how delicate Glyrphizith’s voice was, but he couldn’t help himself. It boggled his mind that a creature that large could be so normal.

Then again, magic.

“We greet Lord Croal,” Adabelle replied as she curtsied, and Trevor bowed a half-second later.

“Oh, please, just Croal,” the dragon said, his lips curling up in a smile. “You’re Lady Adabelle and Trevor, right? Summoned from the north?”

“That’s right. Though if you insist on being called Croal, feel free to call me Adabelle in return,” she responded easily. “We are from Lucent, very far from the north, and are visiting on our honeymoon.”

“Sure, yeah, that’s fine,” he responded with a nod. The ship moved backwards slightly with the wave he caused. The dragon followed. “Congrats, by the way! That’s exciting. Love is awesome. How’d you like the ceremony?”

“It was amazing,” she answered honestly. “I would have you know that I can see the flow of mana that you had woven, and it was a wondrous thing. You show off your powers well.”

“Ah, well, that’s just stuff I learned from my mum,” Croal said, tilting his head back and forth as if shrugging. “Still, I can tell that you know your stuff. I can sense the storm in you, but also the calm before it. You’re no slouch yourself, are you?”

“Certainly not,” she said before smoothly changing the subject. “I must also apologize for my husband. He’s a big fan of dragons, and it seems as though he has been struck speechless by your majesty.”

Croal’s eyes shifted to Trevor and Clay, who was still mimicking his form, and he laughed. “Oh, you’ve got a little wyrmling around you! I didn’t notice him before. That’s adorable.”

“Oh, that’s, yeah, they’re Clay,” the courier managed to say. Adabelle reached out and rubbed his back gently, and he smiled at her as he got his bearings. “Sorry, this is Clay. They’re my earth elemental familiar. Usually they’re in the form of a stoat, but they were so impressed by your ceremony that they transformed and started circling around my head.”

Nodding, Clay did it again, completing a full lap around Trevor’s shoulders before stepping onto his head and curling up like a hat. Their master reached up and patted them gently.

“Yeah, they’re a little rambunctious today,” he said with a laugh.

“Not a little wyrmling, but still cute,” Croal said before looking between the two Summoned again. “So, this might be a little forward of me, but do you two—sorry, three—want to go on a little ride?”

The dragon’s request caught them both off guard. High Priest Worth responded while they were still trying to process the question.

“Lord Croal,” he protested. “Taking passengers is an undignified task for one such as yourself. Please allow us to take them where you would like them to go, should they be willing.”

“Thanks, but no thanks, Worth,” the dragon said. “I know you’re going to be busy with the rest of the ceremony, and it’ll be a lot faster if I take them. But don't worry! I'll return them to shore before showing up for the end.”

Adabelle cleared her throat. “May I ask what business you have with us?”

Croal leaned forward. “I’d, uh, rather not say,” he admitted in as close to a whisper as he could get. His eyes gestured behind them, and the pair of Summoned turned.

Everyone on the ship was watching their interaction with fascination. Some had even left the glass bubble in order to hear better. Once their presence was realized, the priests started shooing them off of the sun deck.

“But yeah. A little ride,” he repeated. “I’ll even do you one better. We’ll fly over Paladona, head back to the mountain, have a little chat, and then I’ll drop you off anywhere on the beach you want to go. Wherever you want.”

“Well, Trevor,” Adabelle started slowly as she looked at him. “You think getting to ride a dragon would be a great addition to our honeymoon? Because I wasn’t expecting it today and I most certainly do.”

An enthusiastic whine came out of the courier’s throat, forming something that most certainly were not words.

“Huh?” Croal asked, tilting his head.

“I believe that’s a yes,” Adabelle translated, trying not to giggle at her husband. “We would love a ride, Croal.”
