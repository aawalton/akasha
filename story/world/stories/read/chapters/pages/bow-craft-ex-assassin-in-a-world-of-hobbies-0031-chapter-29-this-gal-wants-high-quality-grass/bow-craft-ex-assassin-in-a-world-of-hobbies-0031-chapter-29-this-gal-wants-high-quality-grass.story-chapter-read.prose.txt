Chapter 29: This gal wants high-quality grass!

Craft ran through a field of broken trees. Stripped of leaves and withered, their trunks started to tilt forwards — towards the epicenter. This kind of devastation — he never thought he’d find it again. “Lei-rei!” he shouted. She didn’t reply. Just how fast was she?

Gradually, the temple’s slanted roof poked through the branches — and the ground ahead disappeared.

He swore as he dropped his knees and skidded to a stop just before a steep drop, and he found himself at the edge of a crater. It was like a bowl had been scooped out of the earth, but the flame of destruction had swerved around, leaving an apple core-shaped island standing in the middle. The temple itself was pristine and unburned on top, the grass around it as green as the day he’d arrived.

Good thing it survived. He wouldn’t just brush it off with a “it’s okay because it’s magically shielded.” No, he had long learned not to overestimate what magic could do. There was that one time the CAZ had tested how many nukes a similar-sized anomalous space could withstand. It was sturdy, but — five.

Also, just because a space was shielded didn’t mean its residents were as well. The earlier sustained shockwaves could have killed Nightshade — over and over again, each time she’d respawned. Who knew what that did to the mind?

He crossed a surviving ridge to the temple. Just as he reached the end, someone unexpected came out of the double doors.

“Mr. Bowen?” Solace said, just mildly surprised. She was in a white doctor’s robe. It surprised him she was without her hat: her hair was tied in a thick braid like a rope, extending far below her back. “I expected Lei-rei, but not you. At least, not so soon.”

Heart pounding out of his chest, he slowed to a stop and bent down, taking a moment to catch his breath. He raised a hand. “Sorry, I” —

“There’s no hurry.”

Just those words were enough for him to know that Nightshade was alright — or alright enough. Her wearing a doctor’s gown had to have meant something.

He righted himself and faced her squarely. “I just happened to be with Lei-rei.”

Solace began to turn around. “Yes, well” — she stopped briefly, looking around the smoking crater and showing him a pleased smile — “she did tell.”

She stood aside and gestured him to head inside. He chuckled heartily as he did. I wonder what penalty I have to pay.

***

Okay, okay — oh, there was a small crater in the middle of the corridor. Compared to the one outside, it was rather tame. It might’ve been some odd spot where all the shockwaves converged. Anyway, that was enough distracting himself. He walked around it and went right back to rehearsing the things he wanted to tell Nightshade — an apology, a more passionate apology, then a more tame apology, rephrased to sound less pitiful — standing in front of the guest room’s door. Two voices came from behind it. At least no one was hurt. He raised his hand to knock, but…

“I’ll never forgive the ground!” Lei-rei shouted from the other side of the door. He stopped mid-knock.

“Calm down!” Nightshade shouted back, albeit weakly.

Well, that just blew away everything he’d rehearsed. How weak her voice had been concerned him, but at least she still had energy — though, he ought to go inside before Lei-rei broke something else. Anyway, it wasn’t like he could be more prepared than he already wasn’t.

There was a dull noise like when you hit someone on the head with a glass bottle. Yep, too late.

He opened the door and poked his head in with a wry smile. Nightshade was on the bed, a pink mask over her mouth. Beside her, Lei-rei knelt beside the bed and buried her face into the sheets. Nightshade without her hat felt strange, as if half the person was missing. The same probably went for her HP if this world had that — but was she really sick? The trowel in her hand was smoking.

“Oh, you’re okay.” Craft pulled his head back. “Sorry to disturb.”

“Huh?” Nightshade looked at him with wide eyes. She quickly hid the trowel. “Craft! No, come back!”

“I’m kidding.” He stepped inside. Nightshade’s quiet voice reminded him that she wasn’t fully okay. “Lei-rei can run pretty fast,” he continued, looking at Lei-rei. He expected her to stir at the mention of her name, but she didn’t. “Is she okay?”

“Yep, she’s just…reflecting.”

A moment’s silence passed. Lei-rei still wasn’t moving. Considering she hadn’t turned into pixels yet, she was definitely alive…probably.

“So, what brings you here?” Nightshade asked. He shook off the extra thoughts.

“Well” — he walked over to the table in the middle of the room and dragged out a chair — “sorry about the crater, for starters.” He smiled again and sat down.

“Whoa, that was seriously you? Your magic’s pretty awesome!”

He shook his head and waved a hand. “Ah, no, it was a — er — team effort.”

Nightshade chuckled. “So the two of you became friends, huh?”

He looked away, feeling an itch toward the back of his ear…and a cushion in his chest. “That we did” — and he remembered another thing, looking at Nightshade. “Hey, I” — there was a ball in his throat — “I’m sorry. About last time.”

He waited for a reply, but she only nodded. I guess she wants me to continue. “I want to say I’m fully ready…but I know I’m not. But I also know that’s no good, so if you don’t mind, I’ll just take one step forward for now.” He nodded to his own words and looked at her. “I do want to be friends with you, even a little bit.”

She chuckled. “What’s a ‘little bit’?” She looked at him, smiling with her eyes instead of her teeth. “I don’t have a bad impression of you, you know? I was just worried.”

Worried, she says. “You’re too nice.” He really was beginning to think he’d scuffed his chances.

Her eyes drooped. “You’re somehow more worried than me.”

Craft touched his face, massaging his cheeks as if trying to push up the corners of his lips. “It’s hard not to be. I don’t know you well enough to know whether you’d change your mind.”

“Ehh.” She lazily waved. “I think I’m pretty open-minded about most things.”

That didn’t really give him any definite boundaries to work with. He shrugged. “If you say so.” He gestured to the whole deathbed setup. “Well, did the magical fallout give you a cold or something?”

She chuckled. “Don’t worry about this. I just got dizzy…at the exact same time that everything turned white and — oh boy — I thought I’d gotten sent right back to Enty’s place.” She lazily gestured towards her knee. “And I tripped.”

Craft made the connection between Nightshade, Lei-rei, and the crater outside the door. He upgraded his mental danger classification for Lei-rei, considering she had done superficial damage to a structure that deflected a tactical nuke-level attack.

A scrape wasn’t enough to chain someone to a bed. “Anima deficiency?” he said.

She shook her head. “It’s not that bad! Doc said I should just kick back and relax today.” She gave a thumbs up. “I’ll be good to go tomorrow!”

The door creaked open, and Solace came in with a sack slung over her shoulder. “No you’re not. Here.” She unceremoniously tossed the sack onto Nightshade, and it exploded into a pile of —

“Grass!” Nightshade raked it up and threw it around like confetti. She started to glow, and Craft finally made the connection to her Hobby.

“Touching grass?” he guessed.

Nightshade stopped and stared at him, only then realizing the thought process that must have led up to those strange words. “Gardening!” she complained.

“Oh.”

Solace pulled up a chair beside Craft. He noticed soil clinging to the hem of her gown.

“Down and dirty are we, ma’am?” he said with a chuckle, though she didn’t seem amused.

She sighed. “Her Anima deficiency isn’t terrible,” she said in a hushed voice, making sure only Craft heard, “but her collection efficiency is dropping.”

“Not sure what that is, but that sounds bad.” Craft kept his voice hushed too. “I didn’t think this ‘Anima’ stuff was complicated, though.”

Solace crossed her legs. “It’s not. She’s been marooned here for too long, that’s all. It’s one thing for her to do her Hobby, but she is, fortunately or unfortunately, the kind of person who also seeks some amount of novelty.”

Craft raised not just one, but both eyebrows: the left for Solace using the word ‘maroon,’ and the right for the fact that novelty was somehow connected to Hobbies and Anima. He decided this wasn’t good timing to tackle the first part, but the second had implications for him as well.

He leaned in. “ ‘Novelty’ ? Do I have to worry about that?”

She leaned in as well. “Answer me and answer you: when do you feel discontent towards your Hobby?”

That wasn’t something he had ever consciously considered before, but the answer came readily. “I’d have to do a really shitty job, I guess” — he shook his head — “no, it’s more of… ‘not doing all I can.’ ”

If he still had options, he didn’t have the right to stop and surrender. Should he be such a premature gentleman, he’d beat himself up more than if he’d just plain failed. Well, giving up wouldn’t stop being tempting.

“Then that’s all you have to pay attention to.” Solace tilted her head towards Nightshade. “In her case, she requires something ‘fresh’ every so often.” She turned towards the girl and watched her shower grass over Lei-rei with a big grin. “ ‘Every so often’ being once a month, give or take. It’s quite tame on the novelty scale, but even someone like that would start to feel tortured after three months of the same thing.”

Craft’s eyes went wide. “Three months? Why” — he held his tongue. He hadn’t wanted to prod this early.

Solace slowly turned and looked at him, appraising him with a steady, emotionless gaze. She looked away. “I know your kind,” she said. Alarm bells went off in Craft’s head, but her next words silenced them. “That’s why I must hammer into you the critical facts. The status quo is stable, and even with Nightshade’s worsening condition, there are many, many things I can do with my Animetrics license. As long as she is in my care, she is a hundred years away from any truly concernable state.”

She leaned closer to him. “Our investigation has ground to a halt. Now, you might think to offer aid, but a person who has just been born cannot be useful, no? Hence, conduct yourself in the appropriate order, and you can help her appropriately — but misplace your priorities, and you will hurt her by your own negligence.” She leaned away and looked at him. “Simple terms, no?”

There were threats, and then there was “honest advice masquerading as a threat.” It reminded him of Amacus — but that just made him frown. If he had just taken Amacus’ words properly, he might not have had to jump hoops on his first day.

On the other hand, if he never made that mistake with Amacus, he wouldn’t have taken Solace’s words here wisely.

Perhaps for a person like him, that was just what it took. Lei-rei had told him much the same things, but that had only left him at the cusp of beliving. With Solace’s verbal repackaging, he could better believe that the rational choice everyone talked about was the right choice, both for himself and the people around him.

“Simple enough.” He smirked and nodded. “Thanks,” he said.

“W-what,” Lei-rei muttered as she woke up. She shook her head, and the grass that had been piling like snow on her head flew off.

Nightshade pouted. “Aw.”

Lei-rei snapped towards her. “Don’t ‘aw’ me! You knocked me out with a trowel!”

“That said,” Solace continued, speaking hushed words to Craft, “opportunities to be useful are, themselves, fleeting.”

What’d she mean by that? He hadn’t even had the time to speak up when she stood up.

“Miss Lei-rei.”

She turned and stood to attention at record speed, her skirt flourishing in a spiral as she did, her hands behind her back. “Ma’am.”

Craft raised his eyebrows. There shouldn’t have been any real chain-of-command in town. Why was their relationship like that?

“Our patient has steep demands,” Solace said, eliciting an “I don’t!” from the patient. “We have officially exhausted the list of local grass species for her to examine. I’ll have you go further afield and search Monstera Village for those more…exotic.”

“Yes, ma’am.”

Solace turned towards Craft. “Mr. Bowen, what will you do?”

Oh, is this what she meant? Both Solace and Lei-rei were looking at him. The question had been so sudden that he couldn’t even register his options.

Nightshade coughed pitifully.

“What kind of place is Monstera?” he asked. He also recalled Dane saying something about spider silk coming from there, and it wouldn’t do not to know about a significant place so close to them.

Both Solace and Lei-rei stared into the distance. Solace was the first to look back at him. “Well, they certainly keep my office well-stocked with complaints.”

He raised an eyebrow, then looked at Lei-rei. She had a downcast gaze and a frown she struggled to hide.

Solace glanced at Lei-rei, then back to Craft. “It’s World’s Origin’s detached village for non-humans — ah, they live differently enough from us here that it’s simply the more comfortable arrangement for everyone. Dane would know more.”

He noted how Solace had taken over from Lei-rei when she couldn’t speak. He was concerned for her motions, and a growing part of him wanted her to rely on him, and that was a feeling he knew how to entertain.

Even when he couldn’t face himself, he would still face others. What do I have that they need? He had found it to be a master key to any relationship, whether with a cartel’s teniente, a demigoddess of the forest, or in front of him, an assassin who wished to remain silent.

Though he didn’t wish to rely on others, he didn’t hesitate when others relied on him. Whether he pursued this for salvation or self-destruction didn’t matter. All he needed was evidence that good lived on within him.

“I’ll go with Lei-rei and help her out,” he said. “Maybe go around, sightsee after that. Is that fine?”

“That’ll be fine,” Solace said. She tapped Lei-rei on the shoulder, bringing her out of her head. “Mr. Bowen will accompany you.”

“Ah — oh. Of course.” She looked at Craft and nodded. “It’ll be less lonely with you there.”

He didn’t expect the way she put it to be so embarrassing. He covered his mouth and shook his head, but he did smirk under there.

Solace chuckled with a timbre of amusement. “That reminds me” — she looked at Lei-rei — “let’s step outside.” She looked at Craft again. “I’m sure you have some things to say.”

Solace and Lei-rei stepped outside for their own secrets, with Lei-rei glancing at Craft and Nightshade just before she left. Now, it was just him and Nightshade together.

“Thanks,” Nightshade said.

“Yeah,” Craft said, bowing his head. “There’s not a lot I can do, but I do want to do something for you at least.”

Her eyes narrowed as she smiled, and a little silence passed between them. It wasn’t bad, but Craft took that as his signal to leave.

Before he turned, however, Nightshade hummed and tapped her cheek with the tip of a trowel, getting his attention. She pointed at him with the trowel. “Promise me one thing.” She paused. “Two” — she paused — “three things.”

He chuckled. “Greedy little shit, aren’t you?”

“Wh” —

“I’m kidding.”

She sighed in relief. “I really thought you were cussing me for a second there.”

He raised his hands. “My bad.”

“Let’s make it four things, then.”

He chuckled. Yeah, that’s on me. “Well, what is it?”

“First!” She raised her trowel to the sky! “…Souvenirs, please.”

“Oh, okay.”

“Second!” She raised her other hand to the sky — and put up a peace sign. “…When Lei-rei asks for your help, give it to her, please.”

“You sound like you’re sure she will.” He did notice something up earlier. “Can you tell me about it?”

She lowered her arms and shook her head. “Not for me to say.”

Figured.

“Third” — her gaze flittered around the room, but she was disciplining it to keep it on Craft — “when you get back…could you tell me your story?”

It was too sudden for him. No matter how even their footing was now, it just wasn’t even enough. He hadn’t even told Lei-rei.

He shook his head. Nightshade looked up at the ceiling.

“You’ll have to tell me yours first,” he continued, “then I’ll consider it.”

“Y-you!” she said with squinty eyes, throwing a tantrum on the bed. “So mean!”

She turned away with a fake pout. Even if they weren’t on even footing, he was finding more and more reasons to feel comfortable around her, and just the same, the idea that he could call her a genuine friend was becoming less unreal.

“Hey,” she continued. “I can leave Lei-rei in your hands, right?”

“You sound like you’re marrying off your daughter.”

She huffed. “I-it’s natural, isn’t it? She’s basically my family.” Sadness furrowed her brows. What’s that? Craft wondered.

“Well, I’m not sure if I’m just as worried as you are,” he said. “All I know is I’m more worried about her than I am about myself.”

That got her to look at him. “Hey. You’re a pretty classic worrier, aren’t you?”

Her eyes were bright — neutral, wondering. He raised his eyebrows. “I guess.”

“Well, if it’s so easy for you to worry” — she took a while to speak, looking around his feet, then back at him — “can you make it easy to count your blessings, too?”

His chest tightened. What blessings? he almost said, but when his gaze wandered upon the floor, “Touché,” he said. The grass that outlined Lei-rei’s footprints had him start counting at one.

***

Outside, Solace stood in the shadow of the temple while Lei-rei stood in the light.

“First, we don’t have an update on her amnesia,” Solace said.

Lei-rei bowed her head, sighing briefly. “I expected that” — and she doubted that was all.

“Orion picked up the head’s signal in Monstera. Investigate it while you’re there.”

Lei-rei perked up. Solace had said it so casually that she only understood it after a few seconds. “What?” She covered her mouth. “But that would involve Craft. We can’t” —

Solace raised a hand. “Yes and no. Whatever reasons Monstera might have to keep it from us, they are most likely playing a prank. In such a case, we can simply play along, but if, for whatever reason, they contra-insist… Well.”

She took a wooden coin out of her pocket. Lei-rei’s eyes widened, and when Solace flicked it into the air, she scrambled to catch it. She stared at it.

“I’ve never held this much in my hands before.”

“That’s not for you.”

“Of course, ma’am, but is it really okay for Monstera to” —

“And it’s not for Monstera. I’d rather take a vacation in the down_realm than owe them a favor.”

Lei-rei kept her palm open, unsure whether to accept it. “You want to use Craft as a middleman.”

“His bows, particularly.” Solace smirked. “Enough of them would raise a riot among Monstera’s rangers, and I do wonder if their council would be happy enough to make concessions regarding the head if that were to happen.” She dismissively waved one hand. “In that case, it would be cheap to owe Mr. Bowen.”

“Ma’am” — she tempered her voice — “regardless, he shouldn’t be involved. He’s still sensitive in some ways. I don’t think he will react appropriately if he finds out about this scheme.”

Solace glared. “And you’ll let him find out?”

Lei-rei shook her head. “I wouldn’t need to put in any effort; he’d piece everything together himself. Whatever the case, he is not in a state to play. It’s inappropriate to drag him along with our issues.”

Solace’s gaze traced Lei-rei’s face, observing her expressions. “You’re awfully stubborn about him, aren’t you? What’s gotten into my deputy? Do tell.”

Despite the apparent edge in Solace’s words, Lei-rei knew she was genuinely concerned. She averted her gaze. “He is who I was, even if just partly. The things that happened to me shouldn’t happen to him as well. That’s all.”

Solace observed her for a moment longer, but she ceased her appraisal, satisfied with whatever she’d found. “I’m surprised you’d advocate for someone other than Nightshade. Well, if you’re so interested in his wellbeing, consider this: when was the last time anyone had told him ‘thank you’ ?”

That got Lei-rei’s gaze to snap to her again. Solace momentarily smirked. “You say he can’t even help himself, but that’s precisely when helping others would help him the most. Isn’t that right?”

Lei-rei couldn’t find anything to say to that. She closed her hand and pocketed the coin. “Yes, ma’am.”

“Then that’s all. Prepare for your departure tomorrow morning. I’ll ensure Dane does not miss his schedule this time.”

Lei-rei chuckled. “I feel sorry for him.” She paused. “Ma’am?”

“Yes?”

“What do I do if Monstera refuses?”

Solace cracked her knuckles. “I’ll happily do the paperwork for a wager-war. Now, go. I still need to tell Mr. Bowen about his need-to-knows.”

Lei-rei sheepishly saluted to her new orders. She owed Solace far too much not to, but was it right what they were doing?

As she walked away from that place, she couldn’t help but feel that they were somehow tricking him. On the other hand, if she did a good enough job, then the wooden coin would stay in her pocket, and such a future would never come in the first place.

A little bit of wrong for a whole lot of good — that had always been the point of an assassin and her missions.
