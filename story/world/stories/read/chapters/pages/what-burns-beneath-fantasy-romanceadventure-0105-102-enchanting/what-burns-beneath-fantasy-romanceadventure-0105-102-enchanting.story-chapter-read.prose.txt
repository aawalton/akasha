“Brin, stop.”

I ignored him and tore towards the curtain. The ragged cry came again and I answered with my own shrill, “I’m com-mmph!”

Velidan’s hand closed over my mouth as I seized a fistful of heavy drapes. Fresh horror rose alongside the confused, frantic hope that he’d said violence wasn’t allowed, so surely if he saw someone being hurt he’d have to help-

Flickering candlelight illuminated a luxurious bedspread and two intertwined figures in the room ahead. Sweat-slicked skin and carved muscle moved atop a writhing feminine form that definitely did not need anyone’s help.

“Disobedient creature.” The little rumble of laughter vibrated through every inch of me. His grip tightened, drawing me flush against silk and iron.

A mortified squeal tore up my throat and was swallowed by his palm. I stood frozen, watching the scene ahead with wide eyes and burning cheeks.

“Hm.” Warmth and a slow, devilish chuckle brushed the shell of my ear. “I don’t know, Brin. You’re very lovely, but they seem quite taken with each other. I suggest you either wait til they’re finished or undress before charging in.”

Fierce heat raced through my veins as I realized I was still holding the curtain open. I dropped it with another smothered, embarrassed squeak.

Then I twisted around, elbowing my captor sharply in the process, and whisper-hissed, “What is… why are there… where are we?!”

He smiled. “A pleasure house.”

As if to accentuate the statement, feminine giggles and a breathy moan snaked from several curtains over.

Velidan bent his head to mine, eyes sparkling. “Not where I envisioned you’d bring me, but I can’t say I’m opposed. Were you hoping to peruse the wares or go straight to a room?”

Fire lanced up my throat. I opened my mouth to attempt a witty, scathing retort, but a series of rhythmic thumps from behind sent me fleeing towards the entrance.

Once back in the castle, I turned to catch my breath. “Saints, it’s just… right here?! Out in the middle of everything?!”

He chuckled again, head framed by the cascade of glimmering beads and fragrant petals. “In the realm’s sanctuary for species congregation? Where else would you have it?”

My blush deepened. “I just… I mean… in the human realm, places like this are… er, tucked away in cities. They’re not usually out in the open!”

“Ah. Then I’ll wager there wasn’t one in your quaint little village.”

“Certainly not! Saints, can you imagine a place like that in Fellbrook?!” A nervous giggle crept up my throat as the scandalous image took shape. And I thought the bathhouse idea was bad. Then I bit my lower lip and, face burning with the sheer madness of my curiosity, stammered out, “W-what… what, um… w-wares, exactly, do they… sell?”

He took in my expression with a wicked grin, then began a list that I quickly squeaked for him to stop. This earned another laugh. “There’s vendors deeper in and on the second floor, along with the more private rooms. Entertainment venues, as well.”

“O-oh. Saints. Ashes. Salt.” I spent a moment chasing my blush away, then squinted and said slyly, “You sure know a lot about the place. Do you visit often?”

Rather than any fragment of embarrassment, he responded with a chilling smirk and a smooth, “When something needs its spine ripped out.”

My little moment of witty, would-be triumph crumbled beneath a shiver. I pondered the statement in horror as a diminutive green figure burst through the beads.

“Thought I heard you two! Dawn, master, that place is wild!” Slip sagged against the wall, half-buried beneath a mass of cloth. She clutched blood-red velvet, ivory silk, sapphire-blue lace, slick black leather, and delicate silver chains. They were all bundled together in a chaotic skew. “Whew! They sure like their fancy perfumes!”

I goggled at the goblin as Velidan’s earlier command for her to ‘have fun’ took on an entirely new light.

“So I smell.” His nose wrinkled. “I’m surprised you’re still standing.”

“Hah! Good one!”’ She straightened, spiky teeth shining like little knives.

“And you came out with much more than requested. Sslipssinia,” he examined her bundle, brows raised, “is some of this for you?”

“Heh, no. The incubus selling clothes kept, y’know, smiling at me whenever I bought something.” The scaly patches of her cheeks and forehead darkened in what almost looked like a blush.

“How wicked of him. Have you been in there this whole time?”

“Nope! There’s a stalfos selling grog in one of the tents. Had a mug first.”

Velidan pondered that for a moment. “And?”

“Can’t recommend it.”

He snorted.

Slip’s luminous yellow eyes swiveled towards me. “Well, pet? What do you think of the market?”

“It’s incredible. And,” I bit down on a grin, gaze flicking towards the curtain, “um, full of surprises.”

She cackled. “That’s the spirit! You’re not jumping at every shadow again, right?”

My face grew warm. “W-well, no, but-”

“Good! Let’s go find something fun!” She held up her bundle to Velidan, who took it without complaint. Then her knobby, sharp-tipped fingers wound through mine and tugged me towards the field.

Watching Slip shop was a surreal experience. She bounded from stall to tent, deft and clever and utterly brash. Many vendors were praised, some were disparaged, and others received a disorientingly fast series of questions about their wares. She bought nothing, but scurried to and fro with all the vivacity of a child let loose in a sweet shop.

“I like her.” The words slipped out thoughtlessly, accompanied by a broad grin, as I watched the goblin scramble onto a table and poke an earthy-smelling pot.

Velidan stood beside me. He’d found a large sack at some point during Slip’s cavorting and her strange bundle, along with Teela’s cape, were inside. “I thought you would.”

That gave me pause. I examined him from the corner of one eye. “Why?”

The Fae inclined his head. Moonlight and lavender smoke traced the planes of his face, giving the already-fair skin a pearly glow. “You’re both… mm.” He thought for a moment, then cast a jarringly soft look in Slip’s direction. “Lively.”

I snorted a laugh, unsure if the statement was a compliment or yet another jab. “Lively? Heh. Okay. I guess I can’t argue with that. Is that why you picked her to keep an eye on me the other night? Because you thought we’d get along?”

“In part. She was very excited to meet you.”

“Really?” I blinked as the goblin hopped to another table and sniffed at strips of dried meat. “Why?”

He seemed to grow lost in thought, watching absently as Slip scurried further away from us. “Goblins are incredibly social creatures. They crave companionship. Sslipssinia feels safe in my presence and prefers my company to that of her clan. And,” he added dryly, “though I am exceptional company, she was eager to make a more… lively friend.”

I pursed my lips. “But why doesn’t her clan just come to the manor? It’s safe for them when the other High Fae aren’t around, right?”

“Mm. Tell them that.”

“What?”

He snorted. “Goblins are also cowards. Most Dreamweavers are terrified of me, despite how beneficial our bargain is for them. They won’t come near the house unless ordered, and terrorizing them just so Sslipssinia has company seems cruel.” A little growl followed the statement. “Though I’ve certainly been tempted.”

I stopped, parsing through all the information with a baffled shake of my head.

Velidan braced both hands against a table, lowered his voice, and gave me the most wry look I’d ever seen. “Her kin refer to her as Sslipssinia the Brave.”

“Really?” I watched the goblin raise a lump of cloth, lick it, then drop it with a startled squall. A charmed smile curved my mouth. “Really?”

He laughed.

She trotted onwards, growing ever closer to the field’s center and the rippling pavilions. They seemed to grow wider and taller as we approached. Near the outside of one, Slip scurried towards a series of iron bars with tools and weapons propped against them.

“What’s this? Gloomsteel? Pshaw, I’ve seen better!” she croaked at a flat-nosed, muscular creature. One little green hand clutched a sheathed knife.

The large Fae snorted at her before plucking the weapon away. He drew it, revealing a narrow dagger made of the same night-black, water-smooth metal Velidan bore. He tossed it high into the air, then snapped his fingers. The glassy metal glinted, winked into nothing, then reappeared in its sheath. He handed it back to her with a sneer.

Slip whistled. “Okay, berk, that’s pretty dawned slick.”

I gasped at the display before whirling towards Velidan, who took in my expression with a frown. “Can-”

“No.”

“Please! It’s perfect for-”

“I’m not buying you a weapon, Brin.”

“But-”

“You’ll probably skewer me with it the moment I turn around.”

That made me pause. Before I could stop myself, and probably sounding far too excited, I asked, “Would that hurt you?”

He gave me a withering glare. “No.”

“Well, then you don’t have anything to worry about! And it’s not for me, anyways, it’s for Renner.”

“You,” his tone grew as sharp as the blade in question, “are supposed to be shopping for your own pleasure. In our immediate vicinity I see two Fae selling jewelry, a gown made of spidersilk, and several very fine perfumes.” He turned on his heel, heading towards what did indeed appear to be a glass-wrought display of glittering gemstones wound through silver, gold, and other metals I didn’t recognize.

I trotted beside him. “But I don’t need any jewelry! And what in the realms would I do with a gown made of spider webs, anyways? That sounds terribly impractical, not to mention sticky.”

He paused midstep, giving me an icy look.

“And I’m sorry that I don’t smell very nice, but I promise I’ll take a bath later.”

Full, red lips curled.

Undeterred, I shifted in front of him. “You told me to enjoy myself. Finding gifts for people I care about is the best way I can do that.”

Glittering eyes narrowed.

“Please? Please? He’s had a really terrible couple of days. Really terrible.” I drew a deep breath, pleading up with rounded eyes. “I accidentally set him on fire and he had to fight a bunch of people who were trying to kill me and then he let himself get kidnapped so they wouldn’t kill me. Then he almost died trying to save me and he hasn’t been sleeping well and he’s going to be in trouble with his old friends and-”

I was prepared to keep going, until I was blue in the face if need be, but Velidan growled and stalked away. Several moments later he had turned over one of the belted pouches and was dropping the knife into his bag. He continued on after leveling an unreadable look at me.

I beamed back in response. My smile didn’t fade until we reached the colorful pavilions, at which point it was traded for open-mouthed awe.

Each silk-canopied space held obsidian tables and cascades of velvet lined by goods. But, as Velidan had promised, these wares were nothing like what we’d seen in the outer stalls. Pottery, tools, cloth, and food were replaced by a seemingly endless array of… well.

Magic.

Voices called out, touting bottles of liquid jewels and inky shadows, jars bubbling with laughter and good habits, swirling dreams trapped in crystals and girdles of starlight woven through gossamer. Vibrant potions that their sellers swore could bring luck, drown sorrow, incite love, and forge happy dreams. Gilded cages filled with smoke-wreathed songbirds and jewel-toned beetles. Dresses that danced on their own, quills that bled shimmering ink, books that spoke their pages, and lanterns aglow with ever-burning sparks. Mirrors that showed your heart’s desire and maps to long-lost secrets.

It went on and on, dreamlike and dizzying, and I found myself wandering breathlessly through the array.

It was just inside a dimly-lit, crimson canopy that I tripped into a table covered in black velvet and gilded trinkets. A palm-sized, pearl-rimmed mirror fell to the grass. I picked it up, moved to replace it on the table, and gasped.

My reflection was missing.

I tilted the glass, but it didn’t show me or anything else. Just empty silver. My frown deepened and I carried the object further in, towards the pavilion’s occupant; a barefoot, wisp-thin woman sitting cross-legged with her eyes closed. Her skin was so pale it was nearly translucent and her lips were hued like blood.

“Excuse me.”

Glassy red eyes fluttered open, settled on me, and widened. The Fae tilted her head with a little gasp. Wispy, colorless hair slid down one shoulder.

I held the mirror out. “I’m so sorry, but I think I broke this. I accidentally knocked it off the table.”

She rose on thin, shaking legs. Icy fingers grazed mine, then slid up to close around my wrist. She let out another breathy gasp and her skin trembled against mine.

I cast a nervous glance towards the entrance. Smoke and starlight curled outside amidst a sea of distant voices and music, but the space around me seemed oppressively empty. I swallowed, stepping back, and her grip tightened. “I’m here w-with someone. I’m sure he’ll be able to pay for it, I’ll go get-”

“Not broken.” Her voice was whisper-thin, so soft I barely heard it over the pounding of my own heart. She took a deep breath and, seemingly with some effort, released me. “L-look. Remember.”

“Remember?” I blinked. “Remember what?”

She gazed into my eyes, expression going unsettling vacant. Pale blue veins pulsed beneath her papery skin. “Faces. Loved ones. Or…” a shadow fell across her striking features. “Or… others.”

A familiar shape darkened the entrance, making my shoulders relax.

But only for a moment. Cloth rustled and then he was between me and the woman, voice thick with warning. “Careful, lethian. You’re practically drooling.”

Rather than flinch away, she offered a tremulous curtsy. “H-hungry. Yes. But I w-will not.” Her glassy red eyes turned to me, drinking in the features of my face. She swayed. “I will not.”

“Good.” Velidan stepped back, head cocked and brows lowered as he examined the shivering Fae. “You’re not hungry, you’re starving. Why?”

“I… w-wait. The lady Alora. She has a companion. A h-human. He is… kind. He visits. F-feeds me.”

“Alora.” He frowned. “And when were she and her mortal last here?”

“I…” her glazed gaze trailed to several round, empty glass bottles nestled in folds of cloth beneath a table. “I don’t remember.”

Velidan glowered around the darkened space. “Go to the Pit, then. There’s plenty of humans for you there. Well-restrained and for the taking, so long as you don’t kill them without permission.”

“No.”

The air seemed to grow still around him. Then, with a hint of that silky menace rounding each syllable, “Excuse me?”

She sank to her knees, head dipping low. Wispy hair grazed the grass with a soft hiss. “F-forgive me. But no. It’s… wrong.” The word rolled out very strangely; slow and lilting into a question at the end, as if she wasn’t quite sure of it. Then she shook her head and added more forcefully, “Wrong. Cruel. They are not… w-willing.”

Frost crept down the nape of my neck and my knuckles went white around the mirror. I stared at the woman, stuck between horror at their conversation and fearful, humbled admiration.

“Ah.” Velidan sneered down, clearly feeling nothing like admiration. “So your misery is self-imposed. And how does nobility suit you, lethian?”

She said nothing for a dozen heartbeats. Then, gaze lifting slowly back towards me, “I will not t-take. Not by f-force. But I will… trade.” The hunger in her eyes was unsettling enough to almost send me running back outside, but then she lowered her head and added raggedly, “Please. F-for anything.” One trembling hand lifted, palm-up, indicating the collection of trinkets.

I couldn’t seem to find my voice. My gaze turned helplessly to Velidan, but he offered nothing beyond another sneer.

“Remember.” She rose on unsteady feet, nodding towards the mirror again. “A f-face you know. L-love. Ask.”

My mouth went very dry. I looked at the shining glass and, before I could question the wisdom of obedience, said, “Durst.”

And there he was.

Deep-set eyes lined by shadows, heavy brows, and crow’s feet. A broad nose, bent in one place from a long-healed break. Thin, lined lips framed by a black beard. Solemn, still, and silent, but… warm. Gentle. He gazed unseeing until my vision blurred. When I’d wiped the tears away, only silver remained.

“M-Marion,” I choked out, and a rosy-cheeked smile framed by brown curls appeared.

“Y-yes!” I gasped, clutching the mirror to my chest and stepping forwards. “Yes, I’ll trade anything for this! What do you want?”

The Fae’s eyes grew bright and a joyful cry slipped from her lips. “Thank y-you. Oh. S-so hungry. Please.” She reached towards me, tears sliding down her cheeks. “Please. A memory.”
