“Is it working?” Gwen shouted from atop the wagon. Her hand was outstretched, a small spell circle hovering in front of her palm.

In the distance, Nasha was jogging, keeping pace with the wagon as it moved. From that distance, she looked like a small speck.

Nasha held up a hand.

“Is… is that a yes?” Gwen said, leaning forward and squinting.

“She’s holding a thumbs up,” Veronica said from below, also jogging alongside the wagon.

“You can see that far?” Gwen asked, breaking her spell and leaning over the edge.

Veronica tapped the side of her temple. “Improved eyesight.”

Gwen sighed. “That’s cheating, I tell you. Hey, is Sage able to, like, also upgrade us?”

Veronica kept pace as she pondered the question. She was inclined to say no, since Sage was integrated within her. The best she had been able to do was provide Gwen with research topics on the Path of Blooming and teaching her spells using Sage’s database.

Sage. Can you do that? Even though you’re only connected to just me?

[My current capabilities do not allow me to directly augment others. However, it may be possible to temporarily link your mind to another’s consciousness by connecting your mana core to them.]

“What? What would that accomplish?” she asked aloud.

Gwen leaned down closer, acting as if she could hear Sage if she got close enough.

[This would allow me to converse with them, while also allowing me to analyze their body’s status and inform them of what they could improve on.]

“Hm… that might be helpful.”

[However, the procedure is very strenuous and should only be used in safe settings. You will also temporarily lose access to all of your augment abilities.]

“Never mind, then,” she replied flatly. “I’ll do it once we get out of this Valley.”

“What?” Gwen asked from above.

Veronica glanced up and shook her head. “Nothing. The most Sage can do right now is talk to you, but it’ll make me extremely vulnerable.”

Gwen frowned. “Damn. I’d like to meet Sage one day.”

Nasha came over after a few moments, jogging alongside Veronica. “I can still feel your magic,” she said, glancing up at Gwen. “Looks like it works.”

Hearing this, Gwen pumped her fists. “Yes! Now I can apply my magic from super far away, as long as I can still see you.”

“Now your next task is to learn the purification spell I taught you,” Veronica said with a quick glance upward. “We’ll reach the village by then, so that’s your deadline.”

Gwen slouched, sitting up on top of the wagon. “Never a break, huh…”

“Don’t whine now. This is nothing compared to the studies students do in Valeborn,” Veronica said. “Your purification spell will be important if we run into the demon. It should help block any mind games it tries to play with us. Besides, you’ve already been learning curse-dispelling magic for a while.”

Gwen had mentioned upon their first meeting that she had been studying curse-dispelling magic. It was so she could eventually break free from Lorence’s curse, until Veronica showed up and killed him. Still, she had already put a great deal of time into that branch of magic. With a little insight from the world’s greatest mage, Veronica was confident Gwen would learn it in time.

“What about you?” Nasha asked, jogging beside Veronica. “How’s your little story time recital going?”

Veronica took a few breaths before answering. She couldn’t afford to slack on her morning jog routine. The good thing was that she was actually feeling the effects. After weeks of regular exercise, her body felt much nimbler and filled with energy.

“It’s going well enough,” Veronica replied. “I’m about three-quarters of the way there. The problem is that Sage keeps telling me to form more complicated spells as I go. Some of them are seventh-tier spells and higher.”

“You can cast those?”

“No, but I can form the foundational equations with my magic. It doesn’t take much mana to do that. It’s like… drawing the equation in sand. I could probably lay out the equation for a 10th-tier spell if you gave me a few hours.”

Nasha hummed. Veronica wasn’t sure if she fully understood that or not. After all, aura-users were different from mana-users. While Veronica could generate mana from her mana cores and also draw on ambient mana in the air, aura didn’t work that way.

Though both were sources of power, aura was tied directly to a person’s life force. The stronger a person’s life force, the more aura they could exert. Mana was magical in nature, but aura was far more physical. The philosophies were completely different.

Spells were formed by complex equations. Aura techniques, on the other hand, were more of an intuitive application. There were many techniques that aura-users could harness, but these were mainly learned from practice and all sorts of enlightenment. That was her understanding of it, at least.

Veronica hadn’t done much research on aura. The most she had learned was studying their technique of burning life force—the aura equivalent of activating her Exalted Form.

Veronica glanced over at her. Nasha was jogging almost perfectly. Steady form. Even breaths. Barely any sweat. She felt a bit envious. It would take her a while longer to match that physique.

After running for a few more minutes, Veronica stopped, and everyone took a short break.

Since the valley was completely barren, and the ground was practically infested with purple mushrooms and dead roots, there wasn’t any safe stream or river to bathe in. Luckily, Veronica could create a makeshift one. All she needed was a crater, stone-melting magic to smooth the walls to form a basin, some ice and a very large fireball.

Everyone took their turns bathing quickly, before moving on.

Four days passed as Dusk continued guiding them through the valley. According to him, some areas were filled with dangerous, poisonous plants. Ones that would even attack them.

The farther they went, the more uprooted land and shattered earth they found. Between the two large mountains, the sunlight had a difficult time reaching them. This resulted in an almost twilight-like atmosphere within the valley.

The three of them were riding atop the wagon. After traveling for so long, sitting inside had begun to feel stuffy. Dusk remained seated at the coach, refusing to join them.

Gwen had been able to practice her ranged healing much more and had gained confidence in herself. After that, she spent her time mastering the curse-dispelling magic she’d been studying. Luckily, Sage had the entire book Gwen had used recorded within its database.

Veronica, meanwhile, was close to completing Sage’s quest. She would be done with it in roughly a day, giving her plenty of time before they reached the sanctuary.

“Many people used to own farms around here. That’s why there are so many plant monsters,” Dusk said from below.

“Monster plants, huh? I guess in an area like this, anything can become a monster,” Gwen said.

“Perhaps even the ground beneath us is a monster,” Nasha added.

“I don’t think dirt can become a monster. It’s not even alive,” Gwen replied.

As her eyes wandered about, searching for any signs of dangerous plants, she frowned. “Hey… the ambient mana in this place is really low. I thought I was imagining things, but… there’s almost nothing here.”

Veronica arched an eyebrow. As she focused on her surroundings, she tried to absorb the ambient mana around them. To her surprise, Gwen was right. The ambient mana was pitifully low. Ambient mana could be found anywhere, and rarely could it be fully depleted, unless something or someone was siphoning it.

After traveling for a few more minutes, they all spotted something up ahead.

“We’re here,” Dusk announced. “We are at the outer village.”

Gwen raised a flat palm above her eyes, squinting and leaning forward. “That’s it, huh?”

“The sanctuary won’t be far from here,” Dusk added.

Veronica narrowed her eyes, frowning. “I think… something’s here.”

“Is it an enemy? A monster?” Nasha asked.

Gwen immediately began looking around them, suspecting an ambush.

Veronica shook her head. “No, it’s… not an enemy.”

[I am detecting a strange energy here in this village.]

An energy? Like the curse energy you sensed back in the settlement?

[No. It is different from that. I am unable to describe the phenomenon occurring. However, it is most similar to the abnormal fluctuations of power around Dusk.]

Veronica tried not to look down at him or make any sudden movements. Dusk? Then is he leading us to a trap or something?

[I am unable to confirm. However, the fluctuations do not seem dangerous. It appears to be mostly dormant]

Veronica frowned.

The Tears of Selene were said to grant some kind of unusual energy. The person who had claimed them in her past life had become incredibly powerful, far stronger than their tier suggested. If it wasn’t mana or aura, then it had to be some kind of third-party power. Perhaps there were clues to be found ahead.

“Sage says it’s similar to what it sensed back in the settlement.”

“A curse, then?” Nasha asked.

“…maybe,” Veronica answered. “I think we should check it out. We might find some clues about what happened to this valley.”

As they approached the edge of the village, Dusk slowed the wagon down. Once everyone jumped off, they all turned, waiting for Dusk.

“I’ll… stay here,” he said.

“Why?” Gwen asked. “Something the matter?”

He waited a few moments before answering. “I just… want to stay here.”

Gwen frowned slightly. But instead of pressing him, she backed down. “Alright. Then you can stay and watch the wagon. If anything happens, make sure to prioritize your safety, alright?”

Dusk gave the smallest nod, so faint it was almost imperceptible.

“Alright. Let’s see if there are any clues here,” Veronica said, turning toward the village.

Sage. Try to keep your senses on him, just in case anything happens.

[Very well.]

Dusk stayed behind as the three of them ventured forward toward the outer fence of the village.

“This place looks like it was blown up by a giant bomb,” Gwen commented.

Nasha stepped over a broken, ash-worn fence post lying cracked on the ground. “That’s what I was thinking too. These homes don’t just seem old. They all look damaged.”

Veronica went farther in with the two of them.

The buildings were all made of stone, though it was hard to tell what their natural color had once been. Everything was a pale gray, similar to the ground. None of them were in good condition. Each site had broken walls and collapsed roofs. Several homes were half-buried in the dirt, with shattered chairs and tables poking through ripped, tattered rugs. It looked more like ancient ruins than a town now.

“See if you guys can find anything about this place. I’m not really sure what we’re looking for, so take anything that seems suspicious,” Veronica said.

Both of them nodded, and they decided to split up.

Veronica moved toward one of the larger homes near the center of the ruined village. At least, she assumed it had once been a home. Half of the front wall had caved inward, and the doorway was almost completely buried beneath broken stone and debris.

She stepped over the remains of the threshold, careful not to put too much weight on the cracked floor beneath her.

There was no furniture left whole. A wooden table had collapsed in the center of the room, its legs splintered and half-rotted. Several clay bowls were scattered across the floor, most of them cracked into pieces. Against the far wall, a small shelf had fallen sideways, spilling a collection of ruined jars across the ground.

Veronica crouched down and picked up one of the larger shards. She held it between her fingers, but it didn’t last more than a second before it turned to powder, crumbling away into her palm.

“Well… a thousand years seems about right,” she murmured.

[I am detecting a faint fluctuation beneath the collapsed wall to your left.]

Her eyes shifted toward the left side of the room.

A large portion of the wall had collapsed inward, forming a heap of stone mixed with wooden support beams.

“Of course it’s buried under rubble,” she muttered.

Veronica cracked the ground beneath the wall, sending violent vibrations fracturing upward. Helped along by gravity, the wall sank downward, splitting and breaking into smaller chunks of stone. Dust billowed into the air, and she used a wind spell to clear the area.

Crouching, Veronica searched between the shattered stones. With Sage’s enhanced body upgrade, the task was much easier than it looked. Eventually, her fingers brushed against something strangely soft.

She grabbed hold and pulled it out.

Her eyes narrowed. “A… doll?” she murmured.

It was a small, cute doll. A girl in a blue dress with two button eyes. The doll itself didn’t look too remarkable, except for the fact that it was completely unmarred by damage or weathering. It was pristine. No blemishes, rips, or tears, nor did any dust linger on it after being buried.

“Is this the thing you’re detecting, Sage?”

[Correct. I am detecting a strange power coming from this doll. It may be one of many items causing the unusual fluctuations around this village.]

“Can you find out what it is?”

[Given some time, I may be able to find out. I will require you to keep this doll within close proximity as I begin analyzing it.]

“So keep the creepy cursed doll on me. Got it,” she said. “And… does this also give you the same feeling as Dusk, then? Like you mentioned earlier?”

[Correct. I believe the doll and Dusk have a connection.]

Veronica frowned. “…this doesn’t make any sense.”

The doll had been buried beneath a fallen stone wall, one that was clearly old and ancient. It had most likely fallen hundreds, if not thousands of years ago, as Dusk had mentioned—back when the village was still habitable. So then why… why was Sage saying that it felt similar to him? Was it one of the kid’s ancestors? Maybe he had some type of special connection to Selene?

“Hey!” a voice called out.

It was Nasha’s.

Veronica stood, turning in her direction. Nasha was waving, calling her over.

“You might want to come over and see this!”
