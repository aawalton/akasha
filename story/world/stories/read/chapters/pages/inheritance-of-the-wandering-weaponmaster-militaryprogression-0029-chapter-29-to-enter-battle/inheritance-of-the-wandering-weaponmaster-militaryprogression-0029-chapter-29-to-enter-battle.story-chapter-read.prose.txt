“Elites!” Georg roared, his voice somehow cutting through the tumult of a thousand soldiers preparing for battle. “To me!”

“Come here,” Stella said, giving more specific instructions to Ezra as he stumbled out of the tent. She was one of the two scouts that they’d reunited with as they’d joined the rest of the troops here, a tall woman nearly as tall as Ezra himself. Her straight dark hair was cut short, reaching her chin, and a thin scar on her throat explained the quiet, raspy voice. Ezra followed her, pulling his leather shirt on as she guided him toward their goal. Behind him, Boyd, Cord, and Leah followed, pulling their armor on as fast as they could manage. Once they got to an intersection, but still not with everyone else, Stella stopped.

“Finish dressing. Then we’ll move along.”

Ezra didn’t hesitate to do as she said, tying the straps along his sides to get the armor laying tight against his chest before lacing his boots. His fingers shook with nervousness, but he didn’t slow down. Instead, he finished and looked at the others, where Boyd was finishing with his boots and moving to his armor. Without a word, Ezra stepped close to the taller man and began tying the armor down.

“Thanks,” Boyd grunted and Ezra just nodded as his fingers ran up and down the chestpiece. By the time they were done, the rest of the elites were all ready and Cord was running his hand up and down the ties on Boyd’s armor. When he was convinced of his brother’s safety, Cord stepped to the side and picked his shield and spear up. Leah was ready as well, her equipment all settled as she looked at her taller fellows.

“Good,” Stella said as she looked everyone up and down. “Now keep up.”

They didn’t say anything as they followed her jogging pace through the thronging levies and soldiers. She moved between people with an almost supernatural grace, ducking behind and through people without slowing her path. Behind her, the rookie elites couldn’t nearly be so effective, but followed as best as they could manage. Then, when she danced through a squad of levies that were holding their spears and shaking, Ezra bumped hard into someone as he kept his eyes on Stella, knocking them down.

“I’m so sorry,” he said as he stopped and bent down to help them up. To his surprise, they had a nicer spear and a chestpiece. His throat swelled as he realized it was Adam he was helping, his eyes wide in terror.

“Ezra, I… I—” his voice cut out as the fear consumed him.

“I’m sorry, Adam,” Ezra said, gesturing for the rest of the elite recruits to continue following Stella. “I snapped at you, made an ass out of myself. Stay safe. Be careful. I’ll see you after, okay?”

Adam swallowed, but nodded. Ezra fetched out and fist bumped him before going and continuing to follow the rest of his team. Only a few seconds to possibly say goodbye to his friend for forever. He forced the thought from his mind, shaking his head as he refocused on the here and now.

Fortunately, Boyd was tall enough that Ezra could follow the back of his head and picked up the pace until he caught up. Stella’d slowed her pace a bit, and as Ezra got back into the front of the squad, he could see why.

Georg stood on a crate, his breastplate gleaming in the light of the rising sun. He wore a helmet, and his shield and spear were both in hand as the elite recruits fell in line beside the other elites. Petr, Troy, Amy, and Yoran stood there, their own equipment on. Every one of them except Petr wore leather armor, though it was segmented and layered over the parts of their bodies that didn’t need to bend. Petr wore a chainmail shirt with a belt over it. Under all of their armor and equipment, just like for the elite recruits, they wore their uniform coats. The gray wool bore only a small crest on the shoulders to show that it was a uniform under the armor, and those crests were that of the Count of Villy.

“We’ll be the last to leave from the Count’s army,” Georg announced as his squad lined up in front of him. “Our deployment will be near where the first Dragonkin appears, to be supported by Captain Basil, the Pseudo-Knight.”

Only at that moment did Ezra notice the other men who stood in Georg’s shadow. One stood before the rest, his leather armor was a dark brown, nearly black, and he didn’t hold a spear or sword, but had a massive longbow, still unstrung, in one hand. Basil, Ezra assumed, nodded at the acknowledgement and gestured for Georg to finish what he was saying while two women with spear and shield in hand slammed their fists to their plain breastplates as well.

“Our goal will be to hold the line while Basil exterminates the greatest threats,” Georg continued. “Count Svana’s elite squad has two Adepts, and they’ll be deploying to hold back any Dragonkin that are able to evade Captains Chloe and Basil’s initial assault alongside five other Adepts provided by Duke Brayton. Chloe will be engaging in strategic exterminations of high priority targets. I will be leading from the center of our squad, and we’ll be joined by two Adepts from Duke Brayton’s elites. Petr, I trust the formation to you. Get that sorted out, first contact will be in the next quarter hour, our assignment immediately thereafter so don’t slack off. Get some food in you, Yoran’s organized for a delivery to be made very soon.”

“Yes sir!” the company replied, and the two Adepts stepped forward while Georg started talking with Basil who idly played with the fletching on his arrows hanging on one of the quivers on his hip. He carried four quivers, one on each hip and one over each shoulder, for at least a hundred arrows.

“Petr?” one of the women asked as she stepped forward.

“That’s me,” he replied. “My thought is for you both to stand on opposite sides of Leah, since you’re closest in height to her. You’ll be on the left flank, so one of you can collapse in and react most easily, to be flexible.”

They looked at each other and the first who’d spoken nodded. “I’ll stand on the flank. Artemis will be on the other side of Leah.”

“And your name?” Ezra asked.

“I’m Helen,” she replied. “Nice to meet you. Now, let’s kill some goblinoids, shall we?”

Ezra chuckled as he stepped forward and offered his own hand. “I’m Ezra.”

Both of the two Adepts shook his hand without hesitation, then introduced themselves to the rest of the squad. They were polite, but focused on the task at hand, and as soon as they were done with introductions, they stepped back and waited for Petr to make the assignments.

“From the left flank,” he said, “we’ll have Helen, Leah, Artemis, Cord, Troy, Ezra, Lieutenant Georg, Amy, Boyd, Stella, Yoran, and myself on the far flank. Ezra, you, Amy, and the Lieutenant will form the center of the formation. Are you ready for that? If not, I will switch people around.”

That trust in him made Ezra’s heart thump harder, but he nodded. “Yes sir. I’ll hold the line.”

“Good man,” Petr said, and then, there were precious little commands to be given. Before they could stew in their anxieties, though, the promised delivery from the kitchens came. Warm biscuits with a hot sausage were delivered to each soldier, and they occupied themselves with eating. Ezra found the biscuit catching in his surprisingly dry throat as he swallowed, and took a few swallows from his waterskin while he was at it.

“Make sure you drink plenty,” Amy said as she finished her own meal. “Fighting’s hungry and thirsty work. Hungrier than anything you’ve done so far.”

“Can I refill my water before we go?” Ezra asked.

“Yes, and you should, but,” Amy cautioned, “don’t expect to be taking a drink in the middle of the fight. If you puke? Your mouth will taste like puke. You bleed? You’ll taste iron until the engagement’s done. The goblinoids don’t fight like people. They don’t have individual engagements, they throw themselves at the battle lines until they win or they break.”

“The captains will make sure that they break sooner rather than later,” Artemis cut in, her voice higher and sweeter than Helen’s. “When the Dragonkin and the stronger goblinoids are dead, the weaker ones will give up.”

Ezra listened to every piece of advice that came his way, losing a large part of his anxiety as their words comforted him. After all, if they were saying this, then he was pretty confident that they’d all be alright.

Then, horns sounded out again, and Ezra could hear that the battle’d begun. He had hardly noticed as the other squads moved out around them, hurrying to the battlefield while he stood there, eating breakfast. At that realization, the anxiety returned, writhing in his stomach as he looked around.

“It’s time to go,” Georg commanded as he took his place in the center of the squad and Basil fell in behind them. “March!”

The squad moved forward, their steps steady as they approached the battlefield. As they drew closer, the first thing that stood out to Ezra was the sound. Faintly, he could hear metal scraping on metal, shouts, and order, but over it all were constant screams. Screams of terror, of agony, of death. The hair on the back of Ezra’s neck stood straight up as the sound of battle washed over him, and his step faltered. And then he saw the battlefield.

Troops of archers stood nearby, loosing a volley as his squad crested the hill. Arrows fell like so many hornets on the enemy goblinoids, many striking home and forcing cries of suffering from their enemies. More were struck and continued without slowing, their rushing assault trampling over their fallen fellows. The screams didn’t come from the goblinoids, though, but from the soldiers and levies who stood against them. Ezra saw some spears strike home. Many others scraped off of the hide of the monsters they faced, thick purple blood flowing as they threw themselves at the human defenders. Their enemies’ weapons were crude clubs and blades, but they still cut through armor and flesh easily enough. Whenever a goblinoid was able to pass the shields and spears in front of it, they swung wildly, breaking bones and killing with ruthless efficiency.

Then, there were the monsters themselves. Most were just regular goblins, about 140 centimeters tall, with large eyes and bigger ears to compliment their green, warty skin. They wore furs and loincloths, and, just as Petr’d warned him, their skin could hold off a blow that wasn’t perfectly delivered. Ezra saw as one, with its huge eyes, took a spearhead straight into its skull. The blade didn’t reach deep enough, and it managed to push forward and knock a shield aside with its dying movements, opening the path up for another of its kin to rush forward and tear into the human defenders. Only a second passed before the shields slammed back together and the monster was dispatched, but similar scenes played out across the large battlefront.

Besides the goblins were others, orcs and bugbears. There were only a few of each that Ezra could see, and he didn’t have time to look specifically at them as their march took them through the battle lines. Instead of looking at the enemy army, he had to witness the results of losing focus or a single fight here.

Men and women screamed in pain as they were tended to, their bleeding staunched as they cried for friends and parents. The smell of blood choked the air, and Ezra’s stomach twisted further as he witnessed the greatest amount of human suffering he could imagine. That view only lasted for a few seconds as they were ushered forward, and then, Georg gave one last shouted command.

“Hold the shield wall! Contact in three, two, one!”
