The ‘Monster in the Mirror’, pockmarked with a thousand unhealing wounds, hid and skulked behind and between the great network of dark mirrors within her own realm. She was fighting a losing battle against an impossible invader. Her outrage had fueled a furious direct response, but the Sage of Man had grown more powerful than she could have possibly imagined. He countered her magics with soulless ease — contemptuously.

And he’d counterattacked with that foul power of his. A dark magic that should’ve never even touched her. Not only had it done so, but it was also stronger than anything she could summon. It consumed everything.

He’d touched her with it, too. It tore at her very soul, her essential being, so long undisturbed by any arts, as she was not truly flesh.

What is this twisted artifice he wields, and why isn’t it mine?! He holds annihilation itself in his hands! How have I not suffered worse? How am I not owed this power instead?! He is nothing, I am everything!

After her compound attack on the Sage’s family, and the ensuing strike on him in his weakness… she’d narrowly failed to end him or even capture him. He’d fled into apparent exile. It was enough that she’d conquered most of mankind’s territory.

The exile was something else, though. He’d delved deeper, past his own despair, and found a new power underneath it all. He’d returned with that power and first showcased it by obliterating his former family, which she’d twisted and used in her conquest, specifically of the Sage’s central territory.

She’d avoided him after this, hiding and seeking out information on what he’d developed, letting him capture and kill corrupted entity after entity so she could reverse-engineer his new weapon… but the advancement entirely eluded her. She couldn’t fathom it, much less develop it.

The voice deeper within her was no help. It had fallen back into melancholy not long after she’d corrupted the family, seemingly not approving.

She started all of this, and she abandons me in my greatest time of need? Worthless bitch!

The soul-pain and debilitation she felt from the wounds she’d suffered in the recent battle were crippling. Wroth and disgusted that she was to do so, it became clear that she’d need to abandon her realm to survive. If she were stealthy enough, she could slip away where he’d never find her.

Creating multiple mirror image decoys of herself as red herrings, she slinked through the network, ignoring the howls and agonized cries of the damned trapped within the mirror faces.

Unseen, unmolested, she made it to the transfer hollow and silently fell through it, casting her far, far away. Retreat was shameful, but she could live to fight another day.

On the other side, physically materializing in the pitch dark of ancient, buried catacombs, she saw the Sage’s face staring back at her right before the trap was sprung.

Agonizing spikes shot through her like lightning, driving her senseless, eliciting screams of combined pain and fury. She couldn’t hardly think, much less act, and she knew that she’d finally met her end…

The destructive energy running through her body and spirit impossibly stopped by the activation of secondary triggers, and those branching arcs like lightning running along every fiber froze themselves, and her along with it. It arrested her completely, and likewise arrested her annihilation, caught right at the brink. The agony was extreme, and she couldn’t so much as react to it, utterly spellbound and paralyzed. She could only hold herself conscious by long acquaintance with such extremes.

The Sage, entirely covered in his drab, brown robe, approached and leaned in, as if about to share a secret with a sister. His face was as expressionless as stone, his eyes cast down. “You must be wondering why I would do this,” he mused, “when you are the one who corrupted my wife and children, who tore apart everything I held dear and drove me to exile and madness. Why wouldn’t I destroy you, as I did the abominable husks you fashioned of what was precious to me?” He lifted his head slightly, showing an expression of contemplation. “Well, some mercies can’t be avoided.”

He walked behind her, so that he was unseen as he circled her monstrous form, perhaps six times larger than him. He walked widely around a barbed tentacle. “Others bear consideration, and you’re in the latter category. With your nature and predelections, no doubt you think I aim to torture you for what you’ve done. In truth, I should thank you, however much the flesh begs for my hatred. You absolved me of the lies of this world — peeled back a veil I never would’ve found, otherwise. Family. Loved ones. They are simply burdens. You know this, don’t you?”

The Sage left the question hanging, as She remained paralyzed in her agony and felt nothing but loathing — for him and for herself, to fall to such a pathetic creature.

He came back around, pausing in front of her with his hands behind his back, still looking down. “Not merely do I owe ultimate power to these events, I may be the first human to obtain true immortality thanks to them, and by extension, thanks to you.”

She wished she could speak, so she could reject his pitiful notion. Clearly, he was no mortal to begin with. It wasn’t possible to obtain what he did as one.

Oh, how he likes to talk like a human, though.

“So the question is begged: what other secrets do you hold? What secrets disappear with your destruction? I can already see much would disappear from the world instantly — many things your soul anchors here. Most of your constructs are just… playthings of little use, and abominable, but I could be mistaken in a few cases. Moreover, there’s far more to you than that. You’ve altered this entire reality, and that requires study.”

The Sage brought his hands around, hiding them in the robe’s arms, and finally lifted his eyes, so the disturbing, inhuman white ring could be seen. “I can think of no better homage to what you’ve done than to stay my hand against you. To make use of you, monster though you may be. In short, your preservation is… wise.” He lifted his hands and began a spell to transmute the circle she was already in. “How about a nap for now? For your sanity’s sake. You can rest here, in your little… retreat.”

The former Monster in the Mirror couldn’t scream as she felt herself pulled under. Nonetheless, she screamed internally, so that reality, in some way, could feel her ire.

I’ll see your end, Sage! No matter how long it takes, I’ll make this your error, as every other fool who dared to use me! I’ll never break — never! I’m unbreakable!

Azure awoke screaming and struggling, which caused her to almost take flight and launch herself, but Six grabbed her and brought her to the stone floor of the pillar in time.

Panting, Azure slowly regained her senses and relaxed, though the vision’s ramifications were hitting her like a sledgehammer.

She crawled over to the edge of the pillar and stared down at the broken area around what must have been a tomb. And she knew.

The Monster in the Mirror was contained here! She wasn’t killed! She was contained by the Sage to be used… including being harvested in some way for Matrazere… no… please…

The evidence before her was damning in its implications. Unease built up in her gut, anxious dread and revulsion cutting deeper and deeper.

Six put a hand on her shoulder. “Azure-”

“Let go of me!” Azure yelled, and ripped her shoulder away violently. She immediately flew down to the pit, inspecting, digging, touching stones, seeking, striving for more answers. She went fully crystalline as she tossed aside and kicked away stones. She sought to find doubt in a truth that was casting a shadow over her mind, drawing her deeper and deeper into darkness, even as she denied it.

“Show me!” she demanded of the stone. “I want to see, you motherfucker!” She had no idea who she was cursing. Reality, perhaps. “There’s another vision here, there has to fucking be!”

“There isn’t,” Six stated calmly from nearby. “I don’t think you need it to understand, though.”

“Hrraaugh!” Screaming, Azure punched a block of brick and shattered it, then began sobbing as she doubled over, desperately wanting to curl into a ball. “No! It can’t be true…”

Her resistance, her revulsion at the truth confronting her, could no longer hold back the damning lines of evidence driving into her brain.

The Mirror Demon wasn’t killed, but preserved over thousands of years, in some degraded physical state, likely transmutational for ‘storage’ by the twisted artifice of the Sage. That persona, mined of whatever knowledge she possessed many aeons ago, would likely not be spared a further degradation in memory, from the Sage’s curse. In the end, she was probably no more than a battery for Matrazere, deteriorated and forgotten, her mind left broken — ironically, for all her protestations.

“Someone broke her out,” Azure muttered amidst her sobbing.

“So the evidence suggests,” Six said gently.

The whole idea that she’d been ‘reborn’ as a Player on Calrenazzod was a complete farce. Lies she told herself to evade a truth embedded with mines of madness lurking and fit to destroy her. There was no way the System allowed someone like her to be there twice. Not even Samantha had been. It was against the spirit of the rules and almost certainly impossible.

“She’s not a past life,” Azure declared through clenched teeth. She forced her eyes open to stare at a destroyed place — at one of many destroyed places — that were so, so familiar.

I knew. All this time, I knew, and denied it; I let myself be convinced. “I am the Monster in the Mirror. All her sins… they’re entirely mine.”
