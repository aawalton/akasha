Chapter V.L (5.50) - Tournament Uniforms

“This is the equipment you will be using while fighting in the tournament.” Gulp the kokokyu, Arclight’s assistant, wobbled among them, passing out full body uniforms while Arclight spoke. “It is also easily the largest expense the academy pays for each year for individual students. So take care of it and show the clothing respect.”

Kizu lifted the uniform and examined it closely. Standing in their usual courtyard for Combat F, the fabric sparkled a bit in the sunlight.

Like their usual uniforms, it was a shade of sky blue. Unlike the usual outfits, this was all in one piece and made of a soft, almost spongy, material.

The entirety of Combat F accepted their uniforms from Gulp as he waddled amongst them. Some more gratefully than others.

“Ew,” one of his peers said. “This is nasty. Why do we need this? Our normal uniforms have enough enchantments on them.”

Arclight laughed. “You’ll want to wear this gear if you’re hoping to leave the tournament with your head still attached to your shoulders.”

“Are there safety protections instilled within them then?” Kizu asked. He turned over the outfit in his hands and examined it with his spellsense. While unable to decipher the unfamiliar enchantments, he could easily tell it was far more complex than the usual uniforms which were designed to keep them cool and comfortable in the summer sun.

“While we experienced a few hiccups, due to the border closure resulting in a bit of a delayed final tournament, we now have a crew of mages actively designing an enchanted tournament stage for your performance. So long as you do not exit the designated area, these outfits will effectively make you immortal!”

“Woah.” A boy next to Kizu held his new uniform at arm’s reach and gawked at it.

Arclight raised a finger. “However! They are only one-time use! And, again, only usable within the combat stage! Do not go running around blasting one another in the face with fireballs!”

Kizu thought the warning was a bit unnecessary. His peers in this class would be lucky if they managed to set off more than a few sparks. Well, with the exception of Evie. She was still a wildcard. She was also perhaps the student in the academy who required the warning the least. Her hands currently shook as her gloved hands clung to the new fabric, as if afraid it might wrap around her neck and suffocate her.

“After every duel, you will have your outfit exchanged with an identical new one,” Arclight continued. “When a lethal blow is dealt, the match will be called. The rules beyond that are simple. Do not torture. Do not involve the audience. Do not leave the stage.”

“How many fights are there?” the boy from earlier asked.

“An excellent question! Everyone will fight ten times. Single knock-out. However, there will also be a losers bracket, the winners of which will return to the standard bracket. So, don’t discount the idea of getting back into the fight after one loss! Never give in! Claw your way to victory!”

“Do we have to cast magic?” someone asked.

Arclight hesitated. “Technically, no. You do not. But we did just spend a year in class learning how to fight with combat spells, so I do hope you all will give it your all!”

Kizu ran a hand over the fabric. Unlike the normal uniform, it was stretchy with a bumpy texture.

Several other students asked questions that they should have already known, like whether or not parents would be allowed to spectate (they were). And what magic would be able to use all magic or just what they’d learned at the academy (everything was, of course, available). Meanwhile Kizu’s mind wandered over to Mort. His familiar was watching over Anata, who’d just returned home early from school. While Kizu was barred entry to Taroe’s home, Mort retained full access. A very minor concession Roba had fought to obtain from the ex-Elite.

As far as Kizu knew, Anata and Mae didn’t yet understand the extent of Kizu’s ban. They simply believed that he and Taroe had a minor disagreement and were giving one another space. He did his best to act entirely normal around them and he’d seen through Mort’s eyes that Taroe treated the two girls with more kindness than ever. In fact, last night the grizzled man even bought them a cherry pie from the local bakery. Throughout the night, Kizu had checked in occasionally from where he’d been hunched over a cauldron on his ship. He found it fascinating the girls simply accepted the pie with giddy thanks, not questioning the gift. Only Kon appeared suspicious. But the Kitsune boy was suspicious of nearly everything at all times, so that wasn’t abnormal.

He yawned as another student asked a question about bringing snacks into the stands while they spectated the other matches. At least through that query, Kizu learned that there would be vendors and food sellers present at the tournament. That would be fun to bring Anata too. He wondered if there would be pixie floss. When Ione made the mistake of teasing him about the candy in front of his niece, Anata had been incensed that he hadn’t thought to bring her back some of the fluffy candy from the race where he’d acquired Comet.

“Are the matches random?” Evie asked beside him.

That question broke Kizu’s thoughts and he tore his eyes away from the new uniform to watch Arclight as she responded. It had been something he’d been curious about as well. The chances of him being placed in the same first match as Ulric, a student who’d actively done his best to ruin his life, seemed extraordinarily unlikely. He suspected someone behind the scenes was pulling strings to artificially generate the match-up.

Arclight’s booming laughter shook the entirety of the courtyard. Gulp slipped and tumbled into the creek that ran along the garden’s edge.

“Sorry, little Gulp!” Arclight called over to her assistant. A moment later, a weave of water left the creek and deposited the blue furred creature back on the grass. Kizu noted that this was the first time Arclight had ever demonstrated water elemental magic in the class. From what he examined with his spellsense, it was competently formed as well. Far better than what he could cast, especially considering her current distance from the stream.

Kneeling down next to the blue creature, Arclight set a palm on his head and all the water burst off of him, instantly drying him like a dog shaking water off its fur to an impossibly efficient degree. Gulp’s jaw hung ajar, his tongue lulling out over his teeth. After Arclight finished drying him off, the kokokyu happily waddled away.

“Now!” The ground cracked as Arclight shot to her feet. Her single eye locked onto Evie. “I believe you had a question about the integrity of our match system!”

“I…wouldn’t phrase it—”

“I’m teasing! Yes! Our matches are rigged!”

That sent a ripple of murmurs through the class. Arclight simply laughed off the discomfort on display.

“We want to push you to your limits! This isn’t a tournament put together through chance and a lottery system. If you fear favoritism at play, you’re welcome to contact the faculty and voice your concern, but all 400 starting matches were carefully chosen by us. We discussed each of them and voiced our opinions on what we believed would serve you best as students. These will be challenges to push you to your limits in more than just a fist fight. To succeed at this tournament, you’ll need to concur far more than confronting a faceless enemy.”

Kizu thought the responses from his classmates interesting. A lot of them clearly suspected something like this already, judging by the snippets of whispered conversations he caught. He wondered just how much drama this would cause throughout the campus. Friends against friends, rivals facing one another. Grudges playing out with an audience to bear witness. Originally, he assumed that the first rounds would be boring lopsided battles with a dominant contender. He found he liked it this way far better.

Regardless, this settled Kizu’s skepticism about Ulric’s fight being random. They’d set this up. Likely they believed it an excellent opportunity to display growth from both parties. Kizu had never bested Ulric in an official match and Ulric needed to prove himself against Kizu.

“This will be our best two out of three,” Kizu said quietly. He grinned down at the uniform in his hands. So long as they wore these, nothing inflicted in the fight would be lethal or permanent. Maybe this time instead of removing Ulric’s kneecaps, he could go for a new joint. Shoulders? Elbows? So many choices.
