Using Shadowstride, I passed the parking lot in one step.

I crossed the door and went into the main building.

There were cameras, but all of them were already hacked. Instead of showing live footage, they showed footage from a week ago, in which everything was perfectly normal.

The Phoenix Guild building used to be a hotel, which went bankrupt. Farnes bought it and made it into his headquarters. Many areas that were essential to the hotel were no longer needed. Some were repurposed, some abandoned.

One such abandoned area was the laundry room.

It was flooded. As it wasn’t a hazard to the integrity of the building, it was abandoned and left as it was.

I used Shadowstride to zoom past the hallways. There was no one in these hallways most of the time, and today, with the party going on on the top floor, there wasn’t anyone here right now either.

The new, pristine-clean hallway changed completely into an old and dusty one.

LAUNDRY.

The broken plaque read near a barred door.

I pulled the wooden planks away and tossed them onto the floor.

I pushed the door open and was immediately greeted by a pungent smell. The smell of stagnant water mixed with amoeba and bacteria. It took everything I had just to not puke.

I took a step into the water. It was just a bit above my ankle. I took out my phone and turned on the flashlight. Floating detergent bottles, underwear, shoes, and pieces of plastic floated on the water. I moved with heavy steps and reached where I was supposed to. In the middle of the washing area.

“Aargh, I will have to touch this water. Blerrgh.”

Reluctantly, I bent down, looking for the hatch I was supposed to open. Cerise had said there was supposed to be a handle here that I needed to pull. Toby would have to do the same from the other side, and it would open.

Yet I found no such handle.

“Where is it?”

I wiped my hand on my coat, drying it, and opened the map on my phone. I made sure I was in the right spot.

I was.

“Hey, Cerise.”

—What?

“There is no hatch here.”

—Are you sure you are in the right place?

“Yeah, I checked.”

—Hey, Clark, where are you? I am here.

It was Toby this time.

“I am here, but there is no hatch here.”

—It shows you are one meter above where you are supposed to be. Cerise said.

“I am exactly where I am supposed to be,” I screamed into the intercom and looked up in confusion.

Shit.

“Cerise, check if the hotel had any floor changes.”

—I already did and didn’t find anything.

“Then check the ceiling height this room should have.”

—Seven metres.

“The ceiling doesn’t look that high. I suspect they did some work on the floor and covered the hatch in the process. They didn’t put it in their digital records either.”

—I am looking closer, and the hatch is welded shut.

—I hate it when people don’t keep their records properly. This one’s on me. I am sorry.

I could never have imagined Cerise was someone who would apologize. She looked like someone who would make you apologize even if the mistake was her own.

—So what now? Toby asked.

“Get back,” I told him through the intercom.

I channeled my Magika and focused on Shadowstride.

The shadows draped over me. My muscles bulged, the smell became nastier, and the water became colder.

I punched the floor.

It cracked but did not collapse.

I punched again and again until the smooth marble had turned into pieces big enough for me to pick up and toss aside.

I saw the iron hatch I was looking for.

As Toby said, it was welded shut. Water was leaking down through the fine cracks that had formed on its side.

I nudged it with my foot.

It was strong.

I focused on Shadowstride once again and kicked the iron with all my might.

As expected, the iron did not break, but the floor around it did.

I fell through. I was underwater, couldn’t see, hit my head and got dragged down. Couldn’t breathe, couldn’t focus.

Something grabbed my hand and pulled me out of the water.

“Clark, you crazy fuck! This isn’t a water park. Where are you sliding off to?”

I coughed the water out.

I was sure I had drunk some and hoped I wouldn’t catch some kind of disease.

I still couldn’t see. I reached for my face and pulled away whatever was blocking it.

It was a red knee-high sock.

“I am never working with you guys again,” I complained.

We waited until the water had stopped flowing down, then climbed out.

There were only puddles of water left. Several washing machines and tables had slid closer to the hole.

Toby dumped his bag on the ground and let out a loud breath.

“Time to get changed.”

The second part of the plan was to act as drunk party guests who had coincidentally stumbled their way into somewhere they shouldn’t have.

I decided against going alone with Shadowstride, as my last breakthrough had made it apparent that simply sensing shadows wasn’t enough to know if someone was actually there or not.

Toby and I would go to the secret floor together and then try sneaking in.

We ditched our old clothes and got dressed in neat pants, shirts, and coats. We dried ourselves, then used some cologne and mouth freshener to get rid of the smell.

“Shall we go, then?”

Toby didn’t look like anything less than a movie star. Mom always said I looked handsome and had never been conscious about my looks.

Toby made me.

“Cerise, can you take care of the cameras again?” Toby asked.

—Done, but you only have five minutes.

Cerise couldn’t play old footage on the cameras for more than five minutes, and there needed to be at least a thirty-second break between each hack. Something about how the firewall worked, how being in their system for too long would trigger a response, or how doing it one after another was bad, and how I wouldn’t understand.

“That is enough.” Toby ran. “You better be fast, Clark, or you will get caught by the cameras.”

I used Shadowstride to reach the door before him.

“That’s cheating,” he pouted.

I opened the door and motioned for him to go through. Toby did not move. Instead, he glared. Not at me. At what was beyond the door.

I turned and looked out.

A figure in a neat white coat and pants stood there with his stupid mug of a face.

“Ah, chauffeur. I was wondering where the noise came from. What are you and your friend doing here?” Palik said with a wide grin. “No matter what it is, I am sure Farnis won’t be happy to hear it.”

The hallway he was in had a single camera. It wouldn’t show us for five minutes. In that time, we needed to take care of Palik the Rank 88 Silver-rank Riftwalker.
