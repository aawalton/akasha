“Are you making a joke?” Lord Vaun’s jaw tightened. “You examined it for two days, and now you’re telling me you can’t refine it?”

The scholar gestured toward the golden lump resting on the table. “The resin has bonded too closely to the internal badge. According to our tests, ordinary flame will damage the badge inside and destroy the recording. Any solvent strong enough to dissolve the outer layer will do so unevenly, and may seep inward, corroding the badge itself. Physical removal is also out of the question. At this point, the entire structure has effectively become one piece. One crack, and the resin breaks along with the badge.”

Lord Vaun’s fingers curled against his palm. “Then what do you need?”

“A seventh-tier alchemist capable of using balefire,” another scholar said. “A sixth-tier specialist might suffice if they had access to a prepared balefire furnace, but even that would be uncertain.”

“And there is no one in Maftem capable of that?”

“There are a few,” the first scholar said carefully. “But those attached to the mage towers are already occupied, and the others are either commissioned or in seclusion due to auction season. Unless you are willing to wait several weeks, my lord, I’m afraid the refinement cannot be done.”

Lord Vaun fell silent. His expression did not break, but the disappointment was clear.

The first scholar allowed the silence to linger for a moment before speaking again. “If you give us a bit more time, we can hire an independent seventh-tier alchemist to come and melt the resin for you.”

Lord Vaun’s eyes blinked. He leaned forward on instinct, both hands gripping the edge of the table. “You can do that? Why didn’t you mention it earlier? Do it.”

The scholar smiled. “Unfortunately, that alchemist isn’t in Maftem. We can contact him, but he would need to use the transportation array in the square. And as you know, teleportation arrays are not cheap. Naturally, the archives would also take a fee for arranging such a service.”

“You…” Lord Vaun’s voice tightened. “…How much?”

“Roughly sixty thousand vix.”

“What?!”

“Forty thousand for the teleport itself. Luckily for you, he lives in another city not too far from here, so the cost is quite low. The remaining twenty thousand would cover his service and our arrangement fee.”

The other scholars beside him smiled as well.

“This, of course,” the first scholar added, “does not include the return teleportation fee. So the total would actually be closer to one hundred thousand vix.”

The color drained from Lord Vaun’s face.

A hundred thousand vix was a ludicrous amount. Even for a wealthy noble, it was not a sum one could simply spend and forget.

“Looks like that noble guy is being scammed,” Nasha said with little interest.

“Hmph. Serves him right. Isn’t that right, Veron—”

Veronica walked past both Gwen and Nasha, heading straight toward the roped-off appraisal area. “Are you in need of balefire?” she asked.

The four of them turned to face her.

Lord Vaun’s brow furrowed once he recognized her. “You… you were the one blocking my way in the street.”

Veronica looked at him with a raised brow. “I was crossing the street.”

His scowl deepened. “Is there something you want? I’m in the middle of something quite important.”

Veronica stopped just outside the roped section and rested one hand on the barrier line. Her gaze shifted past him, settling on the yellow-gold resin at the center of the refinement table. “I overheard you say you needed balefire,” she said. “Something about melting that piece of resin?”

“Excuse me, but who are you?” the mage at the front asked, a hint of pretension slipping into his voice.

The purple color of his robes marked him as a senior member of the Mage Association. Near the upper right side of his chest was a badge bearing three stars. That symbol meant he had connections and some degree of sway, most likely with junior mages working beneath him, similar to a professor.

That, however, did not mean he held equivalent authority. His followers were likely first or second year juniors at best.

“Veronica Everwells.”

The senior mage squinted, scrutinizing her. However, his eyes moved past her face and lingered a bit too long over her bare shoulders before drifting lower, perusing the rest of her body with open appraisal.

Part of this was Nasha’s fault. Her choice of outfit definitely left more skin showing than Veronica’s usual travel clothes. And although the set was casual, it was still flashy. Much more than her regular clothes or mage robes.

Veronica didn’t have a problem with the outfit. Not particularly. Dark elven clothing could be far more revealing by comparison. But she was not used to someone being so blatant about staring at her.

The man reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, round gray case. He opened it and withdrew a monocle, raising it to one eye as he stared at her face.

“A third-tier calamity mage?”

Veronica stared back.

A Core Appraisal Lens. A common magic artifact used to reveal a person’s pathways.

“Just a third-tier mage,” he said, standing a little taller as he tucked the monocle back into its case. “And yet you think you can come over here and interrupt us?”

The two mages beside him looked at her with open annoyance. Veronica rolled her eyes right in front of them, then turned back toward Lord Vaun. “Are you a noble?”

Lord Vaun stared at her, incredulous. “Of course I am. Do your eyes not work?”

Nasha and Gwen both walked up beside Veronica.

“Should I castrate him?” Nasha asked, sliding one hand toward her thigh pocket.

“I’ll heal him so you can do it again,” Gwen chimed in.

Lord Vaun immediately took a cautious step back. Although he was a noble, Veronica was fairly certain the man was only an ordinary man. No magic. No aura.

She lifted one palm, glancing between Nasha and Gwen to signal for both of them to stop.

“Hmph,” Gwen muttered, crossing her arms.

Nasha tilted her head, then shrugged and casually stepped back.

These two… they really feel that comfortable around me now, huh?

“Lord Vaun, is it?” Veronica said, returning her attention to him. “I couldn’t help but overhear your plight from across the archives.” Her eyes flicked toward the refinement table. “It seems you are in dire need of balefire to retrieve the item trapped inside that resin.”

Seeing that Veronica had stopped the two girls from approaching him, Lord Vaun stood a bit straighter and adjusted his outer coat. “I am. But what is it to you?”

Veronica clapped her hands together, tilting her head slightly with a pleasant smile. “That’s wonderful. You see, my friends and I are in dire need of access to the upper floors of the archives to find a certain book.”

She raised her hand, showing them the back of her palm. “I am a close friend of Viscount Leopold. But according to the rules here, unless the noble is present in person, even this signet representing his backing is useless.”

Their expressions changed upon seeing the ring and hearing the name of a viscount. Lord Vaun’s face relaxed somewhat, but the senior mage seemed to look at her with even greater spite, as if she were relying on outside authority to stand above her station.

“I see. I apologize for my rudeness, then, Miss Everwells,” Lord Vaun said slowly, his eyes studying her as though trying to gauge her intent. “You are asking if I can vouch for your entry to the upper floors, correct? I’m afraid I’m in a rather dire situation myself, so I won’t be able to—”

“You need that resin melted with balefire, correct?” Veronica asked. “Then how about a deal?”

“What?” he asked, slightly taken aback.

“I’ll melt that amber for you. In exchange, you grant us entry to the upper floors. Preferably up to the top floor, if your status reaches that far.”

“What?” the senior mage said. “Are you claiming to be a seventh-tier alchemist?”

“What a joke,” the man beside him muttered.

“Looks like her words are even more shameful than her appearance,” the other mage, a woman, added.

Veronica’s gaze sharpened as she looked at the three of them. “I’ll pretend I didn’t hear that. For your sake.”

She turned back toward the noble. “How about it? Do we have a deal? It is certainly cheaper than paying a hundred thousand vix to this swindler.”

“Swindler?” the senior mage seethed. “How dare you? Given your age and appearance, you’re nothing but a third-rate, third-tier mage, and yet you claim to be a seventh-tier alchemist? The appraisal lens does not lie.”

Veronica noticed that several people within the archives had begun to stare in their direction. They were certainly being loud. Luckily, unlike a regular library, the archives did not seem to enforce a strict quiet policy.

She glanced at the man for only a moment. “I never claimed to be a seventh-tier alchemist. Those were your words, junior.”

The man’s eyes turned furious.

Not only was he obviously a senior, shown clearly by his purple robes, but he was also several years older than Veronica. Early to mid-thirties, perhaps. And yet, hearing the defamatory words of the woman in front of him, he found it difficult to contain his anger.

“Junior?” the senior mage said, his voice turning cold. “You are a third-tier mage who knows nothing but how to destroy things. That is how all of you ruin mages are. Strength without sense. I am a senior here and a licensed scholar of the archives, yet you dare call me a junior?”

Veronica looked back at him, her face neutral. “I must thank you for repeating what I said. The archives seem rather loud today. Your students beside you might not have heard me the first time.”

The senior mage’s expression tightened. “You have some nerve, walking in here with a borrowed noble’s signet and relying on another house’s name to excuse your behavior. Confidence is admirable, but your delusion is embarrassing.”

The mage beside him chuckled, standing a little taller despite being shorter than everyone present. “Maybe all calamity mages are like this. Destructive mana. Destructive personalities.”

The woman on his other side crossed her arms and gave Veronica a sharp look. “Probably a mistress or something, thinking she can walk in here and disrupt us.”

Nasha’s smile disappeared.

Gwen glared back with a dagger-like stare of her own.

“I said I’d pretend I didn’t hear that,” Veronica said, her expression sharpening. “Don’t make me reconsider, if you know what’s good for you.”

The woman’s face stiffened.

The senior mage stepped forward slightly. “Or what?”

Before the tension could rise any further, Lord Vaun’s voice cut through it. “Enough.”

The senior mage stopped, though his eyes remained fixed on Veronica.

Lord Vaun looked between them, then returned his gaze to her. He still looked irritated, but the irritation was no longer simple dismissal. His eyes had become more measured, as if he were trying to decide whether she was a fool, a fraud, or something else entirely.

The appearance of Viscount Leopold’s signet had clearly shifted his evaluation of her.

“Are you saying that you can melt the resin?” Lord Vaun asked.

Veronica turned to him. “As long as balefire truly works the way they claim it will.”

The senior mage frowned. “What the hell is that supposed to mean?”

The shorter mage beside him quickly nudged him with his elbow. “Senior Harris, please watch your tone,” he whispered.

Harris finally noticed the citizens, scholars, and visitors scattered throughout the archives, all staring in their direction. He coughed into one hand and straightened, though the scowl on his face remained. “Elaborate on what you mean by that statement.”

“It means I’m not an alchemist,” Veronica said, looking back at him lazily. “I don’t know what that resin is, nor how deeply it has bonded to the badge. I don’t know what other tests you’ve run. Instead, I’m taking your words at face value: that balefire can melt the resin and allow the badge to be retrieved safely.”

The senior mage stared at her, unsure whether she was speaking honestly or setting some kind of trap.

Lord Vaun’s attention sharpened. “Do you have a method of creating balefire?”

Before Veronica could reply, Harris scoffed. “A third-tier creating balefire? She already admitted she isn’t an alchemist. Lord Vaun, please stop entertaining this rabble. As I said, most alchemists capable of creating balefire are currently busy or in seclusion. I’ll even give you a discount. Ninety thousand vix and—”

“I do know a method that will work,” Veronica interrupted.

Both Lord Vaun and Harris stared at her.

Nasha and Gwen exchanged a brief look, but neither of them spoke. They simply let Veronica handle the situation.

“I can’t create balefire,” Veronica said. “I don’t have an alchemist’s core or the proper comprehension. But I can mimic its effects for a short duration.”

The senior mage’s eyes narrowed. “Balefire is not ordinary elemental fire. It is an alchemical flame. Only high-tier alchemists with stable enough cores and refined chemical fire control can create it. A seventh-tier alchemist can do it directly. A sixth-tier can sometimes manage with the aid of a furnace. A third-tier calamity mage cannot replace it simply because she knows a flashy fire spell.”

Veronica’s gaze turned cold. “Did Lord Vaun give you permission to interrupt?” she asked, her voice carrying the chill of ice. Her stare held the pressure of a master looking down on an unruly student. “If I recall, he has not accepted your deal, and his questions have been directed toward me. Why are you answering?”

The senior mage’s mouth shut.

For a moment, the appraisal area went silent.

Nasha’s brows lifted slightly, surprised at how Veronica was acting. Gwen, on the other hand, recognized the mannerisms. It was noble-speak. The same way Veronica carried herself when speaking with Viscount Leopold or his son, Tomas.

Though right now—she was particularly abrasive.

Even Lord Vaun was taken aback for a few moments.

The woman in front of him was dressed casually. Not in noblewoman’s attire. If anything, she was showing a bit too much skin, an outfit more suitable for the hotter days of summer. And yet, she carried a viscount’s signet and spoke with the ease of someone accustomed to being obeyed.

“How confident are you in melting the resin?” he asked.

“Completely,” Veronica said. “If balefire is all that is needed, then I can retrieve that badge for you without damaging it.”

“Lord Vaun,” Harris said quickly, his voice taking on a more polite note. “If I may, this is all nonsense. There is nothing in her words that proves—”

“Alright. I agree,” Lord Vaun interrupted.

Harris’ face soured with disbelief. Even his students were stunned.

“If you can successfully retrieve the badge for me without damaging it, I’ll take you to the upper floors of the archives,” Lord Vaun continued.

Veronica smiled lightly. “Very well. That works for me.”

“What do you need?”

Veronica glanced toward the refinement table. “Nothing much. I can do it right here. However, I’ll need a strong enough barrier cast around me and the resin. Otherwise, my magic may uproot and destroy the lobby.”
