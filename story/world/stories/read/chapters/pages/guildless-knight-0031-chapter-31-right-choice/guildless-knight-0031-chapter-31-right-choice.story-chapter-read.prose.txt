Year 1427 - 502 years after the great conflict…

Two days passed since the fight in Arcek, a village that comes under the city Alcia. Reports from the fight made it into the local paper. The adventurer who decided to use an advanced spell on hostages—well, let’s just say he got quite a bit more famous than what he already was.

Ais Blackwell, vice guild master of the White Tiger Guild and the commander during the battle to save Arcek, had submitted her report to the Adventurer’s Association. Her report made it clear that the decision to use an advanced spell was a joint decision and not just Alan Blackwell’s impulsive judgement.

The head of the adventurer association remained silent publicly on the issue, despite internal murmurs revealing his displeasure with the White Tiger Guild’s methods.

And while all of this was happening, Alan Blackwell, well let's just say he was busy sleeping.

It was early morning, sun’s light filtered through the black curtains and fell on a motionless figure, enveloped in a layer of thick, furry grey blanket. The blue crystal that Bella had poured in with her mana stopped glowing, ceasing the cooling effect it produced as well.

Right after that, the blanket moved. The motionless figure that was earlier just rising and falling showed the first sign of him waking from his slumber.

Alan removed the blanket from his face, his eyes squeezed shut, adjusting to the brightness of the room. He brushed aside the black strands falling over his face. As his hand ran over his head, he felt the tangled mess his hair had become.

He kept lying on his back, his eyes finally adjusted to the brightness, looking toward the ceiling. He blinked twice and realised in a moment he was in Blackwell Mansion.

"Did I get a burnout?" he mumbled, propping himself up from the bed, his hand slightly trembled at his body’s weight.

"You sure did," a voice replied as the door to his room opened.

Alan looked in the voice’s direction, knowing full well who it belonged to. Ais walked inside the room. She was holding a large white mug in her left hand while she was sipping coffee from another identical mug.

"Want some?" Ais questioned, with a nonchalant expression.

"Well, I mean if you offer—" Alan began, but stopped midway. He took a moment to think before continuing in a loud voice. "Don’t you think you should tell me what happened to me instead!?"

Ais walked to the bed and sat in one corner. She handed out the other mug. "I am honestly surprised you need an explanation to understand what happened."

"I got a burnout after the fight with Goblin King," he muttered, then remembering the fight, his eyes lit up with curiosity. "Wait! Did he manage to heal from my attack?"

Ais took a small sip from her mug. "He didn't, your ability surely came handy," she said, then as if a sudden sadness spread on her face, she muttered, "If only I got my hand on Ferrust!"

Alan looked at her, his eyes narrowing with skepticism. "Don’t you think you are being greedy?" he questioned.

"Is being greedy wrong though?" Ais shot a question back.

Alan blinked quickly, not exactly having any answer to give to her immediately. He took a sip from the coffee mug. "I suppose not," he mumbled.

Ais nodded at Alan’s response, expecting the answer from him. "The adventurers association is at your throat, though," she said, her voice firm and filled with concern. "They believe you caused several casualties, despite there being method to avoid it."

"How many hostages died?"

"15 in total. 6 of them died because of the monster stampede, while 9 of them died because of your attack," Ais replied.

"15 people died because of me?" Alan questioned himself internally, his mind blank as he tried to process the information. After a brief pause, his lips parted, though no words came out of his mouth, and a brief silence followed.

He lowered the mug, placing it on the bed ahead of his crossed legs, not wanting to drink it anymore.

"Was it the wrong choice?" he questioned, guilt clear in his voice.

Ais didn’t answer immediately. She took a moment before speaking. "If I had to choose between someone who’s already broken or my comrades, the answer is clear. Didn’t you say something like this during the fight?" she questioned, her voice calm.

"I did, but—"

"Just don’t go into Hypotheticals about what would’ve happened and what not," Ais scolded. "There’s also the possibility that the death toll would’ve been greater had you not intervened," she said as she stood up from the bed. Walking towards Alan, she picked the mug placed in front of him. "Whatever happened was the ideal scenario, and it was also our choice, not just your choice."

"Sorry," Alan murmured. "Any chance I could get a moment?"

"Sure," she replied with a nod. Walking towards the door, she turned her gaze behind as she stood next to the door. With a slightly worried expression, she continued, "I will be here for one and a half hour. Come talk with me if needed and make sure to eat your breakfast before going out. I’ll send Noya when it’s ready."

"Understood," Alan replied, giving a quick look in Ais’s direction. The door shut as she walked out, leaving him alone with his thoughts. He stretched his legs and removed the blanket by shoving it to his left.

Lying on his back, he closed his eyes. Had I let Goblin close in, would Ais have let any of her team die? he questioned. He didn’t have any doubt in his or Blake’s team since both he and Blake were leading a group of A-ranking adventurers.

No, Ais is fast, but it still wouldn’t have been enough. I am pretty sure she wouldn’t have been able to use any of the destructive spells while fighting with her team, he mentally debated. There was also a Goblin King. It’s bound to take 2–3 minutes to kill it, he added. Now the real question is, how many from her team would’ve died in that 2–3 minutes? Taking into account Bella’s healing process, I guess four–five adventurers.

"Now the main question is whether my choice would differ had I not been in the battle," Alan mumbled, his eyes still closed. After a brief moment, he sat up on the bed and shifted to its corner. I would've made the same decision, regardless. Valuing the people I know over strangers is something anyone— no, what I would always do, he mentally added, rising to his feet.”
