Traced like a line drawn by a drunken sailor, Harry's sense of gravity was completely altered. Or was it just his sense of gravity?

As soon as the fight began, Plankton seemed to grab onto something invisible, then turned the camouflaged object over. The next moment, Harry and Madeline felt thrown about as if they were on a ship in a storm. Seeing that he and Madeline were heading towards the edge of the dome, Harry created a passage to the other side, trying to correct their course.

"The sea never sleeps!" the man shouted as he continued to turn the helm to his left.

His yellow teeth, as if contaminated, had small black spots at the tips. He then felt a sharp pain in his mouth, as if his own sins were gripping him. He ignored the pain and continued to move the invisible Timon.

The pair of thieves, falling horizontally to the left, decided to act quickly. Harry pulled Madeline into one of the doors he had created. Falling through them, they emerged on the other side, hoping their senses would return to normal. That didn't happen.

The range is the entire dome!? Harry added inwardly as he tried to understand his enemy's Emblem, which continued to grin with its hideous teeth.

"And right off the bat we have a radical maneuver from Plankton, who almost managed to make the couple crash into the barrier wall!" The announcer shouted, driving the audience wild. "Unfortunately, Locksmith's doors have as much range as style, taking them both to the other side."

Of course he wanted us to hit the wall and get a concussion, didn't he...? Harry gritted his teeth thinking about the audience and the need for games like these. He realized that Plankton's Emblem only worked inside the dome. He didn't know if that was a function of the barrier itself or if it was the pirate's maximum range. Never mind, we have to find a way to knock him down...

Although their gravity was still altered, causing them to fall horizontally to one side, the pull wasn't as strong as the first time. As if the entire arena were a "boat," what propelled them with such force was the breaking of inertia generated by Plankton's first movement.

It's impossible that rotating part of the entire arena wouldn't harm the man... Harry thought quickly. Even though he and his partner weren't affected... If we get there, he'll have no choice but to face us!

Realizing this, Harry, seeing the pirate refraining from such movements, looked into Madeline's eyes.

"We have to take him out of the game...!" Harry shouted, hoping Madeline would hear him. "It must be quite tiring for him to move everything in such a large area..."

Madeline then let go of Harry's hand that was guiding him through the door and almost broke her paintbrush with how tightly she gripped it. With her hair blowing in the wind, she turned to the man who was bent over and, in a smooth movement, created a barrier strong enough to support her weight and Harry's.

Before the man could change direction again, Madeline cut the air with her paintbrush, generating a corrosive paint slash. Like a force of nature, the precise attack collided with the pirate. Squeezing his eyes shut as if they had been burned, he grunted, and when he opened them again, he noticed another attack coming his way. This time, he dodged. With his furious eyes, a combination of white and red like a rotten strawberry, he turned to the boy, his partner, who was still standing there, unsure what to do.

"Attack them, you imbecile...!" He yelled as he struggled to dodge Monet's attacks.

Noticing the change in Fat Garry's attitude, Harry touched Madeline's shoulder and pointed to Plankton's partner, who seemed to be in a running position. The small but still large boy, in a mediocre attempt at running, moved his feet while panting.

...Seriously!? The pirate thought to himself, without time to argue with his partner.

Then, as if he had heard his partner's complaints and the judgmental looks of the audience, the boy made a rather... strange movement. Spinning vertically around his own axis, the boy seemed to want to vomit every time his face became visible. In a matter of seconds, like a gear being pushed to its limit, the boy spun wildly without moving from his spot. He seemed to float, but the pair of thieves soon realized that, between spins, a part of him, occasionally an arm or a foot, would throw him upwards.

"What the hell is this?" Harry looked as confused as Plankton, who looked disgusted. He knew, however, that it couldn't mean anything good. "Do whatever you can to keep him occupied... I'll deal with the fatass—Um, sorry, Fat Garry."

"...How's that any better? Hey!" Madeline retorted, then touched his shoulder. "Take care."

Harry simply nodded, reassuring her with a warm smile. The two then felt a sudden shift in gravity as Plankton decided to change course. Feeling an absurdly strong pull to the other side, Madeline and Harry barely had time to create platforms to withstand the impact.

Stabilizing himself, the Locksmith then stood up and lost sight of the boy with the wheels. What the...! He looked around but found nothing, when then, as if by a sixth sense, he turned to his left and saw a... blotch? It was difficult to describe. Perhaps a fluffy thorn, or a sharp cushion? In any case, something round was spinning at an astonishing speed, its only obstacle being a man in a tie and glasses that were about to be broken.

Just as a mosquito narrowly avoids a fatal attack from an ordinary person, Harry felt the air being sucked from his lungs as the sphere whizzed past, missing him by a hair's breadth. If I had been hit... He didn't even have time to think properly, as he lost his balance after the attack and ended up falling from his platform.

Instinctively, he summoned two doors in a vertical passage based on his center of gravity. The problem was that, even though the sphere missed him, he noticed it rapidly changing direction, going from left to right. Oh no... He then felt the effects of another gravity shift from Plankton's helm. When is this shit going to end!? I'm almost throwing up here... Thus, he had to stabilize himself again on a door, which began to upset his stomach.

Yes, it's strange to think of a person who constantly jumped from door to door, covering dozens of meters in a matter of moments, being hit by nausea like this. What defined it before, however, was that his center of gravity normally didn't change. Even when reversing vertical and horizontal directions, he still felt in control of his own body and knew that, despite everything, gravity only pulled in one direction.

He then saw the sphere returning towards him like a magnetic yo-yo and decided to redirect it. Now that he had a little more time, he waited for the right moment and... Wait, what? The sphere seemed to fall to the ground, as if gravity were following its normal course. He then dodged Harry's trap and, like a guided arrow, rose again.

The Locksmith didn't know how Fat Garry's ability worked, or if he had any control over his movement. He didn't want to risk creating a door too early just for him to dodge. He would wait, then, for the right moment to finally throw him away. What was supposed to be a sure thing changed when the directions pulling the boy seemed to detach from him.

The young thief saw, out of the corner of his eye, Plankton moving another, separate Timon with his other hand. Since Madeline had to get to the location, she hadn't had enough time to distract him while creating platforms.

He used a separate helm to change Fat Garry's directions... And he couldn't help but admire the technique the pirate had found with his Emblem. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw Madeline running and creating platforms at her feet, getting closer and closer to the helmsman. He could give up on the deadly sphere once and for all and go after Plankton again, ensuring he would reach the pirate faster than Madeline, but he didn't. Don't trip, Maddy. He added in the back of his mind as he felt the pressure the sphere brought with it.

He would be hit one way or another. He no longer had time to create traps. In a fraction of a second, the sphere broke through the door holding Harry like a wave crashing over a surfer. The Locksmith only had time to swerve to the side, letting the sphere collide directly with his shoulder. A familiar cracking sound roared and vibrated through every nerve cell in his body; something had broken. The next sensation, a new but no less violent one, took over; Part of his shoulder seemed to snap back.

Perhaps it was the adrenaline and excitement of the battle, but Harry didn't feel half the full pain, and yet, inadvertently, his eyes widened and he held back a scream. At that moment, realizing he had to at least be of some use, he grabbed the first thing he found on the boy's body and pulled with all his might.

For that fraction of a second while his shoulder still functioned before being ripped from his grasp, Harry strained to cut through Fat Garry's momentum. I hope I don't die in this... he added inwardly, feeling the pain coursing through his arm. He hadn't amputated it, but he certainly wouldn't have any use for this loop anymore.

Snap! He heard his shoulder being reattached, as if Fat Garry's strength was enough to put it back in place. The damage was done, however.

Breaking the myth of unstoppable force, the sphere's acceleration was halted as it lost its balance, revealing what lay beneath the swift image circling Garry. Despite losing his grip on Fat Garry, he had certainly caused some damage with that sudden halt in speed. He could see the boy suppressing another scream, possibly having dislocated or broken limbs.

Madeline, after seeing Harry's condition, momentarily considered turning back and helping him. I... But she quickly understood the sacrifice Harry had made; she needed to end this quickly. Ignoring her supposed lover, Madeline rushed towards Plankton with even more ferocity, leaving the pirate with a worried expression for the first time.

Creating preventative platforms in case the man altered gravity again, she ran and jumped between her creations. As she had predicted, Plankton then frantically changed directions, but Madeline was prepared.

From the outside, it looked like a greenish-purple tunnel that seemed to disintegrate rapidly in mid-air.

Like a hawk, Madeline then leaped towards the man who continued to smile, but she knew the answer lay in his eyes; he was terrified. With a series of corrosive cuts, Madeline attacked the man and punched him directly in the jaw. She kicked the pirate a few more times in the stomach and finally, to ensure he wouldn't get up again, she connected her knee directly to the criminal's head, causing him to fall completely; body and soul.

On the other side, lying next to an injured Fat Garry, Harry created some passages to facilitate Madeline's arrival. He had thought about doing this before, but with the way gravity was rapidly shifting, he believed Madeline could handle it better and with more stability than with his doors.

"N-No, please, I..." Madeline then kicked the boy in the face, causing him to fall straight down, without uttering a word.

Harry then looked up and, obscuring the sun, could only see hands that, even injured, seemed as delicate as a flower in spring. He accepted her help and, now standing, looked around, gently holding his shoulder with his other arm.

"...You did it. I can't believe it! I mean, I believe it, but—" And then he was silenced by Madeline's lips, who was now kissing him passionately.

Tasting the sweetness of love, Harry's mind seemed to enter a thermal shock of sensations, completely abandoning the throbbing pain that came from his shoulder. After a few seconds, Madeline broke away from the kiss.

"...I think they prefer it that way." And sure enough, the audience went wild.

"I know I do." Harry said in a dumb voice.

With the dome leaving the room, the screams that had been muffled before were clear and shrill. Madeline bent over alongside Harry, who groaned in pain as he adjusted his tie and glasses.

The first battle was won.
