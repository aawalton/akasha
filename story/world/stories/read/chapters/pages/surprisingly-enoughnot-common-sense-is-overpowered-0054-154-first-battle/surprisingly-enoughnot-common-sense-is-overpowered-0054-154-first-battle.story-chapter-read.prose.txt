The arena for this competition had only recently been completed. Even from a distance, it was impossible to miss. Massive stone platforms rose in layered tiers, reinforced with formation patterns etched deep into the walls.

After all, earth-attributed cultivators were also skilled construction workers, so building such a structure was not particularly difficult for them. In fact, considering how much damage cultivators regularly did to their surroundings, it was probably for the best that their construction skills were equally impressive.

Because of the competition, the entire pathway leading to the arena was packed.

Disciples streamed in from every direction, voices overlapping in excited chatter. Some were loudly discussing rankings, others were reviewing techniques one last time, while many had simply come to witness the spectacle.

Ning and the others didn't linger. After a bit of maneuvering through the crowd, they quickly found suitable seats.

The seats weren't so far away that subtle movements would be lost, nor so close that it became difficult to grasp the overall flow of the battle.

As far as Ning was concerned, it was a choice spot.

"This place is great," Zhang Feng said with a satisfied nod. "Your choice is pretty good."

"It's nothing worth mentioning," Ning replied modestly. After all, finding the right seat was a skill he'd honed in his previous life; lecture halls had a way of teaching that lesson early.

Lang Rulang sat down beside them without a word, arms crossed, her expression cold enough to chill the air. She stared straight ahead at the arena, her brows faintly knit.

Ning glanced at her, then leaned slightly toward Zhang Feng. "Did something happen to her? She seems grumpier than usual."

Zhang Feng followed his gaze, then suddenly leaned in and whispered, sounding far too confident, "Obviously."

"Obviously?" Ning echoed.

"She didn't make the top ten prediction ranking," Zhang Feng said with a solemn nod. "If I had to guess, that's definitely why she's mad."

Ning paused.

"...You sure about that?"

"Absolutely," Zhang Feng replied without hesitation.

Lang Rulang snorted.

"Keep running your mouth," she said coolly, her eyes still fixed on the arena. "If we end up in the same arena, I'll make sure you're the first one I knock out."

Zhang Feng stiffened instantly. "H-Hey, I was just analyzing the situation! Strategically!"

Ning leaned back slightly, the corner of his mouth twitching despite himself.

As they settled in, he let his gaze wander across the field. The arena was massive, far larger than it had appeared from the outside.

With such a huge crowd, chatter was inevitable.

"Did you see Qiu Han's profile? Peak sixth stage already!"

"They say Jin Su's wolf is almost as strong as she is."

"Hey, did you hear? There's a spiritual farmer ranked tenth."

That last comment made Zhang Feng choke.

"...That's you, right?" he whispered.

Ning nodded calmly.

Being placed tenth in the prediction rankings naturally made him one of the more talked-about contestants.

Strangely enough, Ning felt more amused than annoyed. If anything, it only reinforced his impression.

So I really am just a mob.

After all, a true dark horse wouldn't even appear on such a list.

Someone like Xiao Fan, for instance, was completely unranked.

As the murmurs continued, a subtle pressure swept across the stands, causing conversations to die down one after another. Ning turned his attention toward the central platform.

As expected, an old man stepped onto it. He wore plain sect robes, yet the moment he appeared, the air itself seemed to steady.

"I am Shu Jin," the elder said calmly. "I will be presiding over this competition."

His gaze swept across the arena before he continued, his voice neither loud nor forceful, yet carrying effortlessly to every corner.

"The Outer Sect Competition will now begin."

He paused briefly before continuing.

"The first round will follow a simple rule. Ten participants per arena. A free-for-all. Only the final person standing may advance to the next round."

A murmur rippled through the crowd, though most had already expected as much.

"Second," the elder added, his gaze sweeping across the seated disciples, "killing is strictly forbidden. Any disciple who deliberately takes a life will be immediately disqualified and punished according to sect law."

"Of course, we will intervene the moment a situation becomes truly dangerous. Your jade tokens are also linked to the arena formations. Should you suffer a serious injury, the formation will automatically eject you. Still, do not rely on this alone. Remain vigilant at all times."

That, at least, eased some of the tension.

Under normal circumstances, the arena served another purpose entirely. If two disciples harbored enmity or had grievances to settle, this was where they could fight openly under the sect's supervision.

Killing was forbidden there as well, although some young masters occasionally ignored that rule. After all, "accidental" deaths were a thing.

With an elder personally overseeing the competition and the formations ready to intervene, however, Ning wasn't particularly worried about his life.

"Third," the elder said, his tone sharpening slightly, "disciples with secondary professions must abide by additional restrictions."

Ning straightened slightly.

This was one of the points he had been most concerned about. After all, certain secondary professions could be outright unfair in a format like this if left completely unrestricted.

"Talisman makers may only use talismans they have personally crafted. Alchemists may only consume pills they have refined themselves. Artifact refiners who wish to use artifacts beyond the standard equipment permitted in the competition must use those they have personally refined."

The elder raised a finger.

"Furthermore, the number of such items will be limited. Do not think to overwhelm the arena through preparation alone."

That caused a more noticeable reaction from the crowd.

Ning's thoughts moved quickly.

The first restriction ensured that disciples with secondary professions were actually demonstrating their own skill rather than simply relying on their wealth or background. The item limit, meanwhile, prevented someone from walking into the arena with a storage bag full of talismans and burying everyone beneath them.

At the same time, it protected ordinary cultivators from being overwhelmed by consumables before they even had a chance to fight.

"Lastly," the elder said, his gaze sweeping across the arena, "I expect you all to give it everything you have."

With that, he swept his sleeve.

"When your jade token lights up, step into the assigned arena immediately. Any delay will be treated as a forfeit."

The elder stepped back as light began flaring throughout the stands.

One by one, jade tokens began to glow, each shining with a different hue as participants were silently assigned to their arenas.

Ning glanced down at his own token, already considering the implications of the rules, what parts of his preparation he should reveal, and how seriously he wanted to approach the opening round.

His thoughts were interrupted when his jade token suddenly flashed brightly.

"Well," Zhang Feng said with a grin, clapping him on the shoulder, "looks like you're lucky. First one from our association to go."

Lang Rulang glanced over, her expression cool as ever. "Don't embarrass us."

Wei Zhusang gave a small nod. "Do your best."

The other members offered their encouragement as well.

Ning rose to his feet.

"So soon," he murmured, his expression calm as he stepped forward.

The first round had begun.

...

High above the arena, on a raised viewing platform reserved for the sect's upper ranks, two elders sat overlooking the competition below.

Du Zhen frowned slightly as he watched the disciples enter their respective arenas.

"This year's competition has caused quite a stir. I've already received several complaints from the older outer sect disciples. Some of them have been waiting five years for their chance, only to be told they can't participate."

"That was inevitable," Elder Hu Sheng replied calmly. "But the decision wasn't made lightly. The order came directly from the Purple Mansion elder."

Du Zhen raised an eyebrow. "So it really is confirmed?"

Hu Sheng nodded. "Five years from now, the Flowing Light Secret Realm will open."

Du Zhen's expression shifted slightly as he looked back toward the arenas below, where groups of young disciples were entering as their jade tokens lit up one after another.

"I see... so that's the reason they restricted the competition to newcomers."

"Exactly. The secret realm only allows cultivators under thirty, and their cultivation cannot exceed Qi Condensation. The sect wants to identify suitable disciples now and spend the next five years preparing them properly."

That explained quite a lot. The older disciples might be dissatisfied, but the sect naturally cared more about an opportunity that came only once every few years.

"Then they'll just have to swallow their complaints," Du Zhen said. "Though I wish someone would tell them to complain to you instead."

Hu Sheng chuckled and picked up the cup of tea beside him. "Why would I want that?"

Du Zhen gave him a flat look before returning his attention to the competition.

"Still, this year's batch really is impressive. Xiao Qing alone would have made it memorable. A top-grade spiritual root hasn't appeared in the sect for quite some time, and she's already far ahead of everyone else."

"She's an exception," Hu Sheng said. "It would be unfair to use her as the standard."

"True. Even setting her aside, we still have four high-grade spiritual roots, several promising clan heirs, and quite a few others who've already distinguished themselves. That's better than what we've had in most recent generations."

Hu Sheng nodded. "The overall quality is good. What they'll look like five years from now is another matter."

Cultivation talent could give someone an excellent beginning, but five years was more than enough time for rankings to change. Some promising disciples would inevitably fall behind, while others who had gone unnoticed at first might suddenly distinguish themselves.

Du Zhen stroked his beard. "If even a third of the promising ones are ready by then, we'll have done well."

Just then, one of the arena formations dimmed.

Du Zhen's attention immediately shifted toward it.

"Hm?"

Shu Jin's voice carried faintly from below.

"Winner confirmed."

Du Zhen glanced toward the other arenas, where most of the first matches had barely gotten underway. "Already? It's only been a few minutes."

Hu Sheng lowered his cup and looked toward the arena as well. "That was quick. Who is it?"

Du Zhen checked the formation record beside his seat.

"Ji Ning. Fifth stage of Qi Condensation..." He paused for a moment before recognizing the name. "Ah, he's the one ranked tenth on that prediction list. The spiritual farmer."

"A fifth-stage spiritual farmer finished first?"

"Apparently."

That earned a second look from Hu Sheng. He watched the arena for a few moments, then reached for his tea again.

"Keep an eye on him."

Du Zhen glanced sideways at him. "Planning to steal another disciple?"

"I said keep an eye on him."

Du Zhen laughed. "That's usually how it starts."

Hu Sheng ignored him and took another sip of tea.

...

The light on Ning's jade token faded as he stepped into the arena.

Nine other disciples stood scattered across the stone platform. Ning swept his gaze over them once, quickly judging their cultivation. One was at the fifth stage of Qi Condensation, three were at the fourth, and the remaining five were at the third.

No obvious monsters. No top-ranked contenders. More importantly, no protagonist.

It seems my luck isn't that bad.

Considering how many monsters were participating in this competition, Ning was perfectly happy with this lineup.

At the same time, several gazes locked onto him.

It was obvious that the prediction rankings had circulated widely, and Ning had naturally become one of the more recognizable contestants. A fifth-stage cultivator with a secondary profession certificate who had somehow been ranked tenth was difficult to ignore, especially in a free-for-all where anyone who looked dangerous would naturally become a target.

The rankings didn't just list names either. They included brief descriptions, giving everyone enough information to identify the more notable competitors at a glance.

Ning was no exception. If anything, he stood out even more because of his appearance. Among the other contestants, he looked noticeably young.

Whether it was due to the advanced state of his Jade Skin refinement or simply his natural features, Ning appeared barely fifteen years old. He wasn't sure how to feel about that. At this stage, he looked more cute than handsome, which felt like a questionable return on all the resources he'd poured into body refinement.

Alas, this must be the price of power.

The joke helped settle what little nervousness remained.

The formation barrier sealed shut, and a sharp chime echoed across the arena, signaling the start of the match. Almost immediately, several contestants turned toward Ning.

"Take him out first," the lone fifth-stage Qi Condensation disciple said. "After that, we settle things among ourselves."

The others needed little convincing.

Ning wasn't surprised. Standing out naturally meant becoming everyone's first target.

Instead of retreating, he calmly tapped the ground with the butt of his spear and formed a hand seal. The instant spiritual fluctuations spread across the arena, cold qi burst outward.

[Hidden Ice Mist Technique: Great Accomplishment]

Dense white mist erupted in every direction, spreading across the stone platform and swallowing the contestants before they could close the distance.

"What?!"

"How did he cast that so quickly?"

"I can't see anything!"

"Watch where you're—"

A dull thud interrupted the complaint as two disciples stumbled into each other, and confusion spread almost immediately.

The Hidden Ice Mist wasn't merely a screen that blocked vision. Distance became difficult to judge inside it, sounds were distorted by the dense mist, and the ever-present cold gradually dulled the senses and body.

Within the sea of white, however, Ning remained calm. His breathing slowed as Turtle Breathing concealed his presence, suppressing the qi that might otherwise give away his position.

The moment he'd learned the rules of the competition, Ning had already decided on his strategy. A free-for-all was chaotic by nature, and the strongest-looking contestant would naturally draw attention first. Since he happened to be one of those targets, the simplest solution was to make sure nobody knew where he was.

Most disciples regarded Hidden Ice Mist as little more than a farming spell used to regulate temperature and protect certain spiritual plants. Ning had never shared that opinion. Anything capable of interfering with an opponent's senses could be useful in a fight.

After reaching Great Accomplishment and refining the technique countless times, its greatest weakness—the painfully slow casting time—had also been greatly reduced. Ning could now spread the mist across an area like this within a single breath.

There was still one problem: the mist blinded Ning as thoroughly as everyone else.

Fortunately, his Extreme Space-Time Sensitivity gave him a natural advantage when judging positions and movement around him. Combined with his familiarity with the technique and the sounds and vibrations produced by nine people stumbling around a confined arena, he would manage well enough.

A figure suddenly charged through the fog. Ning shifted aside before the disciple reached him, raised his bow, and released.

The bowstring hummed, followed almost immediately by a muffled cry and the sound of someone collapsing onto the stone floor.

One.

Before anyone could react, another disciple unleashed a broad flame technique in an attempt to burn the fog away. Flames surged through the arena, briefly carving a clear path through the mist, but it lasted less than a heartbeat.

That was more than enough.

Ning released another arrow through the opening, striking the disciple squarely in the shoulder and disrupting his concentration before he could maintain the technique. The flames collapsed along with him.

Two.

Nearby, another contestant panicked and began slashing wildly at anything that moved. Ning circled around him, stepped in from behind, and struck the back of his neck with the butt of his spear.

Three.

By now, the arena had descended into complete chaos. Some contestants retreated blindly, while others stayed where they were and focused entirely on defense. One even suppressed his qi and crouched near the edge of the arena, apparently hoping everyone else would forget about him.

Ning kept moving, firing whenever someone entered a clear line of attack. He aimed for shoulders, knees, wrists, and other places that could disable an opponent without causing serious injury. Whenever someone rushed toward the direction an arrow had come from, Ning had already changed position.

Another disciple fell to an arrow, while a fourth-stage cultivator managed to locate Ning through the sound of his bow and rushed toward him. Ning sidestepped the attack, swept his legs out from under him, and finished him with a strike from the butt of his spear.

Meanwhile, two of the remaining contestants encountered each other in the mist and apparently assumed the other was Ning. The resulting fight was brief but enthusiastic, and by the time they realized their mistake, one was already unconscious while the other had taken a sword strike across the arm.

Ning found the injured disciple shortly afterward and put him down with another arrow.

The temporary alliance had long since collapsed. Without any way to reliably distinguish friend from foe, cooperation became nearly impossible, and Ning had no intention of giving them enough time to reorganize.

Soon, only one opponent remained standing.

The fifth-stage disciple's face twisted with frustration as he realized what had happened.

"Wind Scythe!"

Violent qi erupted from his body, and a powerful gust swept across the arena, tearing through the surrounding mist and forcing it outward. For the first time since the battle began, the stone platform became visible again.

The mist was already beginning to gather once more, but the disciple finally had a clear view and immediately spotted Ning.

His pupils contracted as he raised his sword, but Ning was already moving.

He exploded forward, closing the remaining distance before the man could fully bring his sword around. Rather than using his spear, Ning stepped inside his guard and drove a body-refinement-enhanced punch directly into his abdomen.

The disciple folded around the blow. His feet left the ground for an instant before he crashed backward onto the stone platform and rolled twice. He tried to push himself back up, but his arms gave out beneath him, and he collapsed.

The arena fell silent as the remaining mist slowly dispersed, revealing nine unconscious or defeated disciples scattered across the stone platform. Of the ten who had entered, Ning was the only one still standing.

Shu Jin's voice echoed across the arena.

"Winner, Ji Ning."
