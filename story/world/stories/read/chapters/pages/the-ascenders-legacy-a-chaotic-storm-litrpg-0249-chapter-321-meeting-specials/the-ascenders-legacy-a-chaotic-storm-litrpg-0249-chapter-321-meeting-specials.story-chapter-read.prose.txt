It was evening when Crixis finally arrived at the special sect with Elder ThunderWind by his side. Eyes wide, he admired the beautiful mansions as they flew toward the last—a majestic dragon-themed mansion practically humming with power and the voices of multiple occupants.

"It seems Aodhán is off to a great start," Elder ThunderWind smiled as they approached the entrance. The door, however, opened before they could knock to reveal Aodhán, shirtless and sweating, a wide smile on his face.

"I'm so glad you accepted my offer, Crixis."

Crixis stuttered as he took in Aodhán's bloodline manifestation in its full glory, eyes tracing the golden lines up to his jaw before finding his voice. He bowed immediately. "Thank you, elder brother, for this opportunity. I won't let you down."

Aodhán's smile grew a little strained. "Okay, that's enough. You don't need to call me elder brother, Crixis. We are friends, remember?"

Crixis shook his head. "I know you couldn't have told me, but I wished you did all the same. A transmigrant. And I'd acted so casually."

Elder ThunderWind chuckled. "At the end of the day, Crixis, transmigrants are human. Just like the rest of us. I suspect Aodhán wouldn't have preferred you acted any differently around him."

"I wouldn't," Aodhán nodded. "And I'd prefer that doesn't change. We're friends—and now we're going to be brothers. There's no need for formality."

Crixis stuttered for a moment, then smiled. "I'll try my best."

Aodhán chuckled and waved him in before glancing toward Elder ThunderWind. "What about Rhetta and the others?"

Elder ThunderWind's expression sobered. "I fear they have chosen a different path, Aodhán. They asked me to convey their appreciation."

Aodhán's smile faded. "But this is a great opportunity for them."

"I'm sure they're aware of that," Elder ThunderWind said, his eyes carrying the quiet patience of someone who had seen such choices made many times before. "But they've decided on their path, and it's not up to any of us to tell them otherwise."

Aodhán was quiet for a moment, then nodded. It stung more than he'd expected. "It's alright." He exhaled slowly. "I'd been hoping to give Rhetta the sword I got from Gorvak. Can I still do that?"

Crixis nodded. "I can take it to her if you'd like—uh, Aodhán."

Aodhán glanced at him and nodded. "Okay, then it's settled." He turned to Elder ThunderWind. "Thank you, Elder, for everything."

Elder ThunderWind smiled. "You're welcome, Special Disciple."

Just then a loud crash echoed from above as someone was slammed hard into a wall. Crixis snapped his gaze upward in shock, but Aodhán just smiled. It seemed Darien was stronger than Seamus after all.

Elder ThunderWind raised an amused eyebrow. "I see you've begun training. I would think after this morning, you'd be requiring a bit more rest."

Aodhán smiled awkwardly. "I'll rest later."

"Make sure you do," Elder ThunderWind warned. "Spiritual strain is never something to be careless about." He turned to leave and then paused, his gaze settling on Crixis. "Be careful, young one. The most important job of any underling is to keep up with their special—but that doesn't mean you need to cripple yourself in the attempt. Run, but do not fall."

Crixis bowed gratefully. "I'll try my best, Elder ThunderWind. Thank you."

Elder ThunderWind smiled, and an instant later, he was gone, a gust swirling in his wake. Aodhán shut the door and turned to Crixis. "I'm happy to have you with me, Crixis."

Crixis smiled brightly. "Me too. This is a great opportunity you've given me—I won't waste it."

Another loud crash echoed from above, accompanied by multiple groans, and Aodhán chuckled. "The other specials and I are having a high-speed wrestling match. I'm obviously losing, if you couldn't tell, but it's fun. Come meet them."

Crixis nodded and followed Aodhán up the stairs, trying to wrap his mind around the fact that he was standing in the same building as not one but two transmigrants and a host of double inheritors.

They reached the training room a few seconds later—an expansive hall about thirty meters in breadth and double that in length. The space was clearly spatially expanded, with flowing runes covering every inch of wall and ceiling. A weapon rack spanned the length of the far wall, filled with an array of weapons associated with the storm.

At the center of the hall, people moved in a blur too fast for his eyes to follow, grunts and cries echoing as they clashed. He tried to track their numbers but failed. They weren't even manipulating lightning or using skills—just raw, high-speed movement.

It was amazing. Or at least, it was until his gaze landed on the massive dragon curled in the corner of the hall, pitch-black scales rippling with lightning. Even crouched, it towered over everyone else, its sinuous form utterly majestic.

Crixis froze as the familiar turned to stare at him, golden irises piercing.

"By the storm," he whispered, and Aodhán smiled. "Crixis, meet Varéc. Don't worry—he likes you."

"How do you know that?" Crixis asked disbelievingly, a chill running down his spine as the familiar bared its fangs—large, canine blades more than capable of ripping him to pieces.

Aodhán stepped into the training hall, chuckling. "Because he formed an opinion of you the first day I saw you. Trust me—if he didn't like you, you'd already know."

Crixis wasn't quite sure he believed that. If this was what the dragon looked like when he liked a person, he certainly didn't want to know what the alternative looked like. Before he could say anything to that effect, however, three figures slammed into the wall simultaneously with a thunderous crash.

A figure Crixis recognized stood at the center, a wide smile on his face as he called out to his fallen comrades. "I can do this all day, losers."

"It's cheating," Seamus groaned as he peeled himself off the floor. "You're several tiers stronger than we are."

"And you have numbers on your side," Sophran pointed out. "I think it's a fairly matched trade." He glanced at Aodhán, who was helping Darien up, and that was when he noticed Crixis.

Almost impossibly, Sophran's smile widened. "You must be Aodhán's new underling. Congratulations."

Crixis forced himself not to stutter. "Thank you, Special Disciple Sophran. It's an honor to meet you all."

"I bet it is," Seamus grinned, already back on his feet. "Nice to meet you too. You were at the trial mountain, weren't you?"

Crixis nodded. "Yes. I made it to the core sect."

"Very respectable," Sophran nodded. "You'll no doubt achieve great things."

Darien waved at him from across the room and smiled. "You know, if I'd known Aodhán would be choosing his underling today, I would have asked Rathis to come along."

Mirelle glanced at Aodhán. "We didn't bring our aides so as not to crowd your home on the first visit. But if you don't mind, we can call them now. They'll be more than excited to come."

Aodhán only hesitated a moment before nodding. "Sure—let's get all the introductions out of the way at once."

The specials called their underlings, and before long, the training hall was bursting with nearly a dozen people, all of whom quickly got to know each other as they sparred, talked, and laughed.

Mirelle's underling was a tall, muscled, blond boy named Su Tianan, who looked more like a soldier in his robes than a disciple. Seamus's underling was his younger brother, Kairn Calcirra—a more reserved version of his elder sibling.

Kairn was considerably stronger than Tianan and Crixis, already nearing the late forties in terms of advancement. When they sparred, he wiped the floor with them with barely any effort, only to be stumped the moment he dueled against Darien's underling—Rathis Cloudrend—a pale-looking boy with a bloodline manifestation so distinct it stood out even in a room full of them.

Rathis's skin was the color of the sky on a stormy day, and his hair—equally gray—almost seemed like vapor barely holding form. But the boy packed a considerable punch. At tier 47, Rathis was the most advanced underling in the room, though when Aodhán asked him for a spar, he promptly declined, blanching as his gaze darted to Varéc's looming form in the corner.

The last two underlings were Sophran's and despite being weaker than Rathis, they still put on a decent showing. One thing, however, was clear: Aodhán was the weakest special in the room, and Crixis the weakest underling at tier 15.

But neither of them minded. If anything, it only pushed them harder, and when Sophran called for another round of their high-speed match, Aodhán didn't hesitate to join, pushing himself faster with each loss. It had been a long time since Aodhán had lost consistently among friends, but he had never before had the chance to fight not just against, but alongside storm cultivators.

He was learning from every loss. Not just learning—refining, his mind searching for advantages at every turn, cataloguing movements and patterns even as his back repeatedly found the wall.

Their matches continued long into the night, underlings cheering from the sidelines. With the four of them against Sophran, the battles lasted longer, but even after hundreds of rounds, none of them had managed to make Sophran so much as stumble.

None of them contemplated stopping.

And perhaps, for the first time since his arrival in Gordon, Aodhán wasn't thinking of the war or of Lutia. He was present, and he was having fun.

Albeit with a great deal of groaning as, for the hundredth time in a row, his back slammed against the reinforced walls with a thunderous crash.

And boy, was it painful.

A rumble of thunder—distant but insistent—pulled Aodhán from sleep.

He blinked against the pre-dawn darkness of his master bedroom, disoriented for a moment before memory returned. Two days since his arrival in the special sect. Two days since the trial mountain. And his spirit was still far from full recovery.

Granted, he hadn't taken Elder ThunderWind's advice to rest, but he had refrained from channeling as much as possible. Yet his spirit still felt like a squeezed-out rag.

He sighed, rolling his shoulder where phantom aches lingered from his spar against Seamus the day before—another high-speed, skill-free match that shouldn't have strained his spirit further, but the sheer physical exertion was taking its toll on an already weakened spirit. At this rate, it seemed he might need complete bed rest if he was to recover before the week's end.

A growl rumbled from behind him, and Aodhán turned to see Varéc blinking sleepily.

"How'd you sleep, big guy?" he asked. Varéc yawned and flicked his tail in response. Aodhán took that to mean well enough and chuckled, pointing upward. "Imagine how much better it would be if you slept in your own room."

Varéc ignored him, and Aodhán shook his head at his familiar's growing petulance. He'd been behaving like an unruly teenager for the past few days. Not that he wasn't also a teenager, technically.

Aodhán smiled, remembering that his birthday was only five weeks away. Soon he would be a teenager no longer.

Smiling, he pushed himself off the bed and began making his way down the hall to his Aspect room for a few hours of meditation. It was the room he'd spent the most time in over the last two days, and for good reason, he thought, grimacing as a headache lanced through his mind.

He had pushed himself too much during the welcome party, and when it finally ended—barely minutes to midnight—rather than sleep, he and Crixis had stayed up talking for hours.

Crixis naturally had a ton of questions, and Aodhán had answered them as truthfully as he could. They'd covered his life before AEFLYM, his time in Lutia, and the circumstances that had brought him here. It hadn't been easy to be so open, but keeping things from Crixis would only hamper their bond rather than strengthen it.

In return, Crixis had told him about his life in the shelter, his parents, and his siblings. Nothing groundbreaking, but by the end of it, they knew each other better, and Crixis had finally dropped the elder brother nonsense.

Sleep had come eventually, but his spirit had decided to teach him a lesson regardless, and he'd woken the next morning with a mind-splitting migraine.

Crixis, determined to help, had offered to make his mother's special hangover tea—only to find the kitchen completely bare. And so, migraine and all, Aodhán had been forced to go to the market.

They had gone and come straight back, the noise and brightness of the market doing nothing for his head. Back home, he'd retreated immediately to the Aspect room while Crixis brewed the tea, and the combination had worked well enough. By evening, he'd felt sufficiently refreshed—refreshed enough, perhaps unwisely, to accept Seamus's offer for a spar when he and Darien had dropped in.

Had he declined, he might not be this miserable now. But Aodhán couldn't deny how much he'd gained from fighting people more powerful than himself.

But beyond the tier disadvantage, there was something else Aodhán was becoming more cognizant of.

Their paths.

With people wielding the same affinity, without their different and unique paths, it would be no different from dueling against oneself. These paths, he’d quickly realized, were the direction a person veered their understanding and skills toward.

And those directions, he was quickly realizing, were quite limitless.

From their spar yesterday, Aodhán had gained a little insight into Seamus’s path, and it wasn’t at all surprising. They hadn’t used any skills, but just from the way Seamus moved, it had been obvious he leaned very heavily into speed—not wind speed or surging momentum like him—but raw, chaotic lightning speed.

Darien, on the other hand, was harder to glean, and perhaps that was because he’d sparred against Crixis instead, which was likely why Crixis was still asleep, perhaps even aching far more than he was.

However, he wasn’t quite sure, but he suspected Darien leaned very heavily into pattern and perception, inverting the unpredictability of the storm to his advantage. He wasn’t quite as fast as Seamus, and yet, it was hard to catch him off-guard—a trait that had continuously stumped Crixis, whose path leaned more into overwhelming strength and power than subtlety or unpredictability.

But that could be tweaked to balance him out, and Aodhán had several ideas on just how to do so—the {Tempest of Transformation} being very high on that list.

He stepped into the Aspect room a moment later and shuddered, his breath catching at the density of essence in the air. Crystal-clear water lapped at his feet, and without hesitation, he dropped into it, exhaling in relief as a wave of cool mist spread across his spirit.

His Aspect hummed within him, deep and resonant, vibrating through his bones like a steady current. He followed its guidance without thought, several tendrils of water rising to revolve around him in slow, spiraling arcs.

So immersed he was in the process that he completely lost track of time, watery essence surging around him like a rising tide. By the time he finally surfaced from the meditative trance, it was already noon.

Fortunately, his spirit felt far more refreshed than it had been the evening before. Perhaps he didn’t quite need that bed rest.

Smiling, he stretched his core sense outward, finding Crixis already awake and meditating in the cultivation room. With his core sense as advanced as it was, reading what was happening inside another cultivator's core was almost effortless, and with eyes still closed, Aodhán watched as Crixis struggled to tame his willpower—a familiar battle.

The duty of underlings was to protect and serve as an ally to their special. But there was an equal and opposite responsibility: the special had to invest in their underling's growth. Watching Crixis now, Aodhán knew exactly where to start.

He pushed himself to his feet and made his way toward the cultivation room. He had barely gotten halfway down the hall, however, when his ring vibrated.

A text.

With a swift motion, he pressed the small button on its side, and an interface popped up, a single line of text crawling across it.
