The world stopped shifting, and Asher let out a weary sigh as he suddenly found himself back in his room at the palace. Rubbing at his eyes with his sole remaining hand, he opened his Personal Rift and allowed the rest of his team to join him back on the material realm.

“So… mission accomplished?” Moxy asked, throwing herself down onto the couch, not caring in the slightest that she was absolutely covered in demon blood. Based on the nasty slashes through her leather armor, a good chunk of that blood was probably her own as well. “I mean, we killed a greater demon, we slew a lich, and we robbed the place. That sounds like a win, right?”

“Sort of… the only thing is that while we killed a lich, it wasn’t the lich we’d originally set out to kill,” Asher admitted, sitting heavily on the opposite couch.

“Does that matter?” Rulfar asked, crossing his arms as he stood there, the slight fatigue Asher had seen on his face earlier nowhere to be seen. “We are intending to destroy the entirety of The Council of Death, are we not? One lich dead is as good as another.”

“Asher and I have personal business with this lich,” Samantha reminded them, frowning as she sat beside him. “It destroyed my village.”

“And we’ll rip out his bony spine for doing so,” Moxy agreed. “But I doubt it's all that pleased about us taking down one of its undead friends!”

“That’s the thing,” Asher said, wondering how on earth to explain this without sounding like a lunatic. “As far as I can tell… I think Zygrul is actually on our side. At least in the sense of taking down the rest of the Council.”

“The lich that burned your village to the ground?” Moxy asked, raising an eyebrow. “…You sure?”

“Asher, what do you mean?” Samantha asked, her frown deepening as she stared at him. “How could that monster possibly be on our side? You said the same thing back when you were grabbing all those journals, but I don’t understand. It killed hundreds of people!”

“Presumably far more than that, considering how long it’s been alive,” Rulfar added with a grunt. “You don’t actually trust that thing, do you?”

“I don’t trust it, but you don’t understand. The only reason I managed to kill one of the other liches was because Zygrul set me up perfectly. Here, let me just show you.” Placing a hand on Samantha’s forehead, Asher went to use Incorporate’s second-tier ability to share the memory of his discussion with Zygrul and how it had helped him kill Hylm. Only the moment he tried to push the memory into Samantha’s head, something went wrong.

Rather than being copied over, the memory started eating itself.

Asher jerked his hand back from Samantha’s forehead like it was a hot stove, staring at his fingers in horror as the sensation stopped just as suddenly as it had begun. He could still remember everything that had happened, but it was like looking at a photograph that someone had burned a small hole in the center. Only in this case, the hole was focused on Zygrul itself. Asher could recall the entire conversation, but unlike a few seconds ago, he couldn’t quite recall what Zygrul looked like in that moment.

This was even more jarring because he could still recall thinking that Zygrul looked exactly like how it had looked when Asher first arrived on this world, and that memory was fully intact. Yet try as he might, he wasn’t able to fill in the missing spot of this new memory with the appearance of Zygrul in the old.

“Asher?” Samantha asked, her annoyance nowhere to be found as she gave him a concerned look. “Are you okay?”

“Yeah… I think so,” he said, slowly giving up on attempting to restore his damaged memory. “So uh, no idea how Zygrul did this… but I think it booby trapped my memories of it somehow. The memory of our conversation just got damaged a bit when I tried to share it with you.”

“The lich has a Mind element?” Rulfar asked, his brow furrowing. “That’s not good.”

“No, I don’t think so. I mean, I’ve never encountered a Mind element anyway, so I might be wrong, but I’m pretty certain this is something else.”

“You sound like you have an idea,” Moxy pointed out. “Care to share?”

“I don’t know,” Asher said, looking around at his teammates as he hesitated. He didn’t want his idea to be right, but all the pieces fit together. “As far as theories go, it’s sort of out there. And if I'm right, it’s bad. I mean really bad.”

“You know you can tell us,” Samantha said, placing her hand over his and smiling at him. “Whatever it is, we’ll figure out a way to tackle it together.”

“Alright.” Taking in a deep breath, Asher let it out slowly, going straight to the heart of the matter. “…I think Zygrul has a Void element.”

The room was dead silent for about three seconds, before Moxy barked out a loud laugh. “A Void element? Man, that’s a good one.” Laughing for a few more seconds, Moxy finally realized none of the others were laughing with her, and she cleared her throat as she sat up, staring at him. “Wait, you’re serious?”

“If Zygrul truly had the Void element, nearly every country in the world would have devoted their full manpower to tracking the lich and taking it down,” Rulfar said, staring at Asher like he was crazy. “There’s no chance. You have to be mistaken.”

“I think it was hiding it,” Asher said, thinking back to what happened between Zygrul and Hylm. “I don’t have the faintest idea how it was capable of hiding its own elements from the rest of The Council of Death for so long, but Hylm, the lich I killed, clearly didn’t know Zygrul had the ability to drain enchantments. Zygrul shattered my Distortion by erasing the energy contained within the skill. It tore a hole through my Spatial Lock effortlessly! Its eyes even turn into the color of the void!”

“Is there a chance it was able to do that stuff because it’s a wizard?” Samantha asked, her face having gone pale. Despite her not having any real schooling, Asher knew that practically everyone across the world knew about the dangers associated with the void. Gostoria, the world’s most advanced and powerful city that existed nearly three thousand years ago, had been researching the void, even getting to the point where they announced that they’d managed to manifest the very first Void element.

The city, the continent, and the fifty million people living within, had vanished the very next day without a trace. All but wiped from existence as though they’d never existed.

“I don’t think so,” Asher said, scratching his head. “Wizards are able to duplicate skills with enough time, but the spells usually have to exist in the first place. Richard did say entirely new ones could be created if the wizard was talented enough, but I don’t know. When I traveled back from the wild realm to the material realm, I had to go through the void, even if only for a split second. In that brief moment, I felt the void eat away at the protective cocoon of tritessence my skill had woven around me. The sensation of what Zygrul did was nearly identical. The only real difference was rather than eating away at everything all at once, Zygrul’s attack was focused, consuming energy from a specific point.”

“If there’s an evil immortal running around with a Void element of all things… That is officially beyond the scope of what we’re doing here,” Rulfar said, shaking his head. “We need to notify the High Prince immediately so he can reach out to the other leaders of this world. They’ll send an army unlike anything the world has ever seen to take Zygrul down.”

“They can try… but they won’t be able to,” Asher said, putting the last pieces of the puzzle into place. “I bet it's gone to the one place nobody can follow. It doesn’t matter even if the world comes together with an army of realm wanderers. Zygrul’s retreated to the void.”

“I thought the whole reason why the Void element was scary was because the void realm is a giant, infinite plane of nothing, on account of the void eating anything that comes close?” Moxy asked. “If the void eats everything… how can Zygrul survive in there?”

“If it truly has a Void element, I bet it could survive,” Asher said, feeling more and more confident in his theory as he spoke. “Think about it. Everyone talks about how terrifying The Council of Death is as well, right? Even Hylm told Zygrul that the council would destroy it for betraying them. Yet Zygrul didn’t seem concerned in the slightest. I think that’s because it knows not even The Council of Death with all their power can follow it into the void. It’s the perfect escape plan.”

“Wow… this is actually starting to sound reasonable,” Moxy admitted. “So, what’s the plan then? Do we still tell people about this thing?”

“Of course we tell them!” Rulfar shouted, looking at Moxy like she was insane. “It’s an evil immortal with a Void element! There’s no telling what havoc such a being could cause!”

“We’ll definitely let everyone know what we think,” Asher said, realizing they had a pretty good piece of evidence to support their theory. “And even if I can’t share my memories of Zygrul, thanks to Moxy, we have this.”

Opening his Personal Rift, Asher pulled out the jet-black dagger that Moxy had found. Or at least, he tried to.

Because nothing came out.

Blinking in shock, Asher attempted to summon the dagger again, only for it to fail to appear yet again. Shoving his head into his own rift, he looked around, utterly flummoxed as to what was going on. As he scanned his rift, Secret Seeker drew his attention to one particular portion of the strange skill.

Part of the floor looked hazy and unnatural, as though it had been partially deconstructed and was still trying to repair itself.

Yanking his head out of his rift, Asher swore under his breath as he turned to look at the others once more. “Okay, that’s not good… The void dagger is gone.”

“Gone?” Samantha asked, at the same moment Rulfar shouted ‘Void dagger?!’

“I think it ate through my skill,” Asher admitted, groaning as he realized that had to be why he’d felt extra tired after his Recall. “I have no idea how Recall works, but the dagger must have ‘slipped out’ of my Personal Rift while I was moving between locations. It could be literally anywhere between here and the edge of the kingdom. Hell, it might even be in the astral realm right now, seeing as that’s where teleportation skills usually bring the user for a fraction of a second. If it hasn’t eaten its way out of that as well.”

“So not only do we have to deal with the rest of The Council of Death, we also have to worry about a lich we strongly suspect has powers associated with the void, along with a God damn void dagger having been tossed into some random person’s hands,” Rulfar said, placing his hands in his face as he muttered under his breath.

“We should tell the High Prince about the dagger,” Samantha said. “Maybe he can send out his truthseekers to hunt for it. If Asher’s skill has him travel in a straight line between points, that’s not too much ground to cover.”

“That’s a good idea,” Asher said, hoping Rulfar wasn’t going to have a full breakdown. It seemed the bloodhound was more concerned about the void than the rest of them for some reason, and the large man sucked in a deep breath as he glared at them.

“Great. Even if we don’t have to worry about the dagger, that still leaves Zygrul and the rest of the Council. If Zygrul was hiding a Void element, I don’t even want to know what the others are capable of.”

“Hylm had a Love element, so it’s probably safe to assume that we’ll encounter more forbidden elements,” Asher admitted, reaching into his Personal Rift again and actually pulling something out this time. Holding up one of the shining journals, he grinned as he shook the booklet.

“At the very least, if these journals hold what I think they do… We won’t be caught completely by surprise.”
