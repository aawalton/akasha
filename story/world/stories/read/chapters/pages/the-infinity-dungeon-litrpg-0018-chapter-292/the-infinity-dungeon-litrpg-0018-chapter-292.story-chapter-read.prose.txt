Chapter 292

The glowing orange gemstone slowly sunk back into the man’s chest, disappearing from view.

“Thank you Trevor,” Michael said. “You can go now.”

Icarus appeared behind him, already holding his hand, as soon as the man vanished beyond a bend of the underground corridor.

“I scheduled an Origin System update to make use of our improved understanding of the [Ghost Market] skill,” they said, their voice made of soft silk. “We still need to use portals, but it will lessen the strain on the veil between worlds.”

Michael nodded, but his mind was elsewhere. “We are only harnessing a fraction of the skill’s power despite it being available to the System like all others.”

“And it is not the only one with such a problem,” Icarus replied. They walked in front of Michael, and with their hands drew a diagram in the air. Since they were not letting go of Michael’s hand, he simply let it get pulled along, drawing lines and circles. “Of all the skills we have access to, only the common ones are fully integrated. I am currently allocating most of my resources to understanding [Ghost Market] and Adherence, if it’s alright with you.”

“You don’t need to ask me these things, Icarus, you know I trust you completely.”

He did not mention the fact that the AI was vastly smarter than he was, if anything for the simple reason that he refused to split himself up into instances while Icarus had no problems doing so. At least in the inner space.

The small elf boy seemed to get even smaller as they fidgeted for a moment. “I know…”

Michael patted their head, ruffling their hair and making them squirm a bit. “It’s alright. Come, we have a lot of work to do.”

Build log:

The veil between worlds shuddered. Spacetime was punctured, and Michael, Icarus and Johanne appeared in the middle of an empty field. Around them were only sheer rock faces, boulders, withered plants and random pools of water from the recent rain.

“Veil is stable,” Michael said. It had become a routine thing to check on the veil after spacetime magic was used, even in the absence of PAC energy. One never knew.

Before them, a massive dark aircraft took up most of the view.

They boarded, and the aircraft began to move, lifting vertically up without sound or vibration, shooting away toward the horizon. A sophisticated array of datascript, force elemental stones and other magic made it impossible to tell that they were moving, except by looking out the windows or by tracking their position using the Origin System’s surveillance network.

There was no sonic boom as they broke the sound barrier, air elemental stones taking care of that and much more, nullifying the atmospheric drag completely. They kept accelerating, their destination half a planet away, each minding their own business.

“Should we cloak once we approach the Vatican?” Johanne asked.

“No need,” Michael replied. Icarus was sitting in his lap, curiously watching the world from above, seeing it with real eyes rather than digitally. Michael thought there was no difference, but perhaps it was the experience that made it different. “We are beginning to shape the world narrative to prepare for a wider Origin System rollout. Events such as this are actually really helpful.”

Johanne hummed, adjusting her glasses and typing on a holographic keyboard. “Interesting,” she said.

“Travis has been hard at work together with the new hires and, of course, Icarus.”

This made the elf boy squirm a bit, although Michael was pretty sure they were doing it on purpose. He poked them in the back, tickling them. As they giggled, he kept speaking with Johanne, catching up on various things here and there.

“I got a notification that you rolled out a quest to gather huge quantities of raw materials,” she said. “Is it for your inner space?”

“Yep,” he said. Below them, the expanse of the ocean.

“Will I be able to see it, one day? I know you brought Travis in.”

It had not occurred to Michael that he had not offered a tour of the inner space to any of the others. In truth, even Travis had been limited to only the room with the System’s Central Dashboard. He said as much out loud, to which Johanne scoffed.

“My lord, you do not owe us anything, let alone a guided tour to your inner space. In fact, me asking to see it was rather out of line. I apologize for overstepping.”

Michael sighed. “Come on, there is no need for that. I am more than happy to show you.”

“The demands on your time must be many, my lord.”

“Yes, but now I have some free time so… want to see?”

Despite her attempts to remain stoic, he could see how much her eyes lit up when he offered. He began to get up, making Icarus pout that they had to relinquish their seat, but they eventually did and vanished in a poof of magic.

“How does the procedure work? What will happen to my physical body?” Johanne asked as she too got up.

Michael chuckled. “Watch,” he said, and slashed the air with his hand coated in magic.

As a portal opened, for a moment Johanne did not even notice the strange things on the other side. Instead, she walked around it, muttering to herself. “Fascinating, the portal travels with us, and there is no weakening of the veil at all!”

Then she finally looked inside, and her jaw dropped. Michael pushed her lightly, and she returned to herself and walked into the portal, with him following. As soon as he stepped through, his body split into two, with the one still on the plane looking like a powered-down automaton. He concentrated, and the body robotically walked toward the plush chair and plopped down on it.

“Yeah, as you can see controlling two bodies at once is not easy,” he said, noticing Johanne’s stare. Before she could speak, he continued. “I know I could use the Origin System to do as Icarus does and split myself into instances but… no.”

She just nodded, before turning around to take everything in.

“Truth be told, the inner space isn’t really made for people,” Michael said while giving her the tour, pointing at things and explaining their function. “Not yet, at least.”

She nodded again. She had felt his protection around her, and knew that without him she would probably die in less than a second due to the radiation from the spiral. The void, the automated defenses and of course, Icarus and Michael’s presence were also big threats to any intruder who dared invade the inner space.

As she watched the data lines, she noticed a strange tangle of them that she could not peer through.

“That’s Icarus’s main body, so to speak,” Michael explained. “It used to be a planetoid, now it’s all tangled up with data lines. As you can see, Icarus is currently… dealing with a few things.”

The lines were strange, chaotic, quite unlike the mathematical precision and perfection of everywhere else. She knew that all of them, together, made up the strange being that was Icarus, Michael and the Origin System, but she couldn’t help but feel like there, in the tangled mess of lines around Icarus’s main body, was the place where they were most alive.

She remembered Icarus’s strange behavior on the plane. She had seen glimpses of more such behavior, but only when Michael was present. She looked at the lines again.

The tour continued, but did not last long. A few minutes later, Icarus notified them that they had arrived and were currently hovering over the Vatican, and it was time to leave. As soon as they left the inner space and Michael merged with his real-world body, the AI elf femboy–for she knew Icarus’s true intentions with the form of their choosing thanks to many hours spent online–reappeared, already holding Michael tight.

It did not escape her notice just how different their demeanor had been when they announced that it was time to leave, compared to even just a few moments later when they manifested in the real world. And if she noticed, others had also certainly noticed. She debated with herself whether to voice her thoughts, but decided not to. They could read minds, of course they knew.

They landed in a big walled garden, where pope Damasus was already waiting for them. Members of the Church, ranging from priests to the newly minted bishops and cardinals, watched them from where they thought they wouldn’t be seen, not knowing that they all were inside a Domain. Some of them even had the System, and glowed to her eyes like beacons of Adherence and magic.

She and Michael ignored them, letting the pope lead the way inside the millennia-old buildings, through winding corridors and lavishly furnished rooms.

“The priests are wondering why I called them,” the man explained. “Most of them don’t suspect a thing. Soon, I shall show them the light of Origin as dictated by the Holy Quest.”

“Good. From what I’m seeing, you are handling the situation rather well,” Michael said.

“Thank you, Prophet,” the man said, stopping for a moment to quickly bow at Michael.

The action made him wince, heavy thoughts gathering in his mind. He powered through them and kept walking, overtaking the pope who fell in line behind them, stealing glances at Icarus. There was reverence in his eyes, much different from how he had looked at the little elf last time they saw each other.

“Why are you staring at Icarus like that?” Johanne asked the man, not reading the mood or perhaps choosing not to. Lately, Michael had begun to wonder if she did certain things on purpose.

“They are the Voice of Origin,” the pope said shakily, as if that should be enough to explain everything. “Even gazing upon them is a blessing I am undeserving of. I shall work myself to the bone to make sure–”

“We are here,” Michael said, interrupting him.

The sterile light of the overburdened shield at the center of the room greeted them. It turned the red and gold embroidery on the walls purple, and the gilded mirrors and chandeliers electric.

“Are you ready, pope Damasus?” he asked.

“Yes. May the Prophet and the Voice of Origin be witness, I relinquish the light so that I may carry my mission to the end.”

The man’s connection to the System was temporarily cut. Michael withdrew his Domain, specifically excluding him and nothing else. Meanwhile, he gathered his Faith, and approached the hexagon on the shield that had turned transparent. With a solemn sigh and a silent subvocalized prayer to the Grand Architecture, the Code which binds the Stars, and the Book of Origin, he thanked the Prophet and the Voice.

Michael had no time to ponder whether he had gone too far with the religious elements. The pope reached inside and plucked one of the shards from the mirror.
