Chapter II.XXXII (2.32)

Nearly every sculpt survived the loop and journeyed into the mountains. With Mitsuko on hand to repair damage to their melting bodies, none of them took permanent wounds and the only casualties were the few who’d passed before her arrival on the island.

And, as thanks for her assistance, King Lars bequeathed on her a guide as she split from his entourage. Jacquline once again led the way to the island’s ice tunnels.

The sculpt girl was quieter in this loop. Not in an unfriendly way, but rather, Mitsuko thought it might be some misplaced sense of reverence for Mitsuko. They also lacked Holly's relaxing presence in this loop.

Since they hadn’t reached the village settlement, Jacquline instead guided her to a different entrance into the island’s ice tunnels, one closer to their current position.

“You’re certain this is what you want?” the sculpt woman asked for the hundredth time. “These tunnels will be completely submerged. A pool of water builds up and freezes over after a single day in the sun. After this long in the heat, it’s likely to resemble a lake closer than a pond.”

“That’s fine,” Mitsuko reassured her. “I am a mage. I have a few tricks up my sleeve.”

“If you say so,” Jacquline responded, clearly still skeptic. She pulled her cloak closer and glared up at the ever present sun overhead. “If you wish, after this visit I can guide you to a different entrance further in the mountains that is less likely to be flooded.”

“I appreciate the offer. I don't think it will be necessary.”

Just as Jacquline had said, when they arrived at the tunnel site they were greeted by a massive body of water. Chunks of ice floated in it. Mitsuko knelt down and dipped a hand in the newly formed lake. The bite of the ice water made her hand tingle. She ignored the sensation and closed her eyes. A quick cast of Retrospection revealed the lake’s past. It had grown to cover a massive area over these last few days. She pulled her vision back into that of the present.

The tinkle of water from a snow melt stream adding to the lake was enough to remind her that this was a growing obstacle. Perhaps if she encountered it in another two or three days it might prove insurmountable. As it was, this was still a larger radius than anything she’d ever attempted to Mend in the past. It dwarfed her achievement in the clock tower. Not to mention, it would require her to reach far further back. Days, rather than minutes.

But Mitsuko was far from the same mage she’d been back when she reversed time in the clock tower.

She cast Mend.

Her mind went blank for a moment as the spell took hold of the water. Then she felt the water level dip, leaving her hand dry as it receded back. The edges of the water froze while streams retreated back up into the mountain to resolidify into snow above them.

The first hurdle was the most taxing to push through. As the area targeted by the spell shrank, so too did the difficulty to maintain the spell.

Mitsuko pulled back when she reached the night before the Prismatic Spiral had activated.

A layer of water remained, calf -high, but it had been there for years. With Sterling on her shoulder, Mitsuko hopped down and landed in the shallow water with a splash. There was no Mending this last bit of water out of the way. It was barely a temporary inconvenience anyway. A simple bitter chill that soaked her feet and bogged down her boots as she sloshed her way forward. That would disappear once she found somewhere dry to Mend herself. Her feet might sting for the moment but she wasn’t at risk of losing bits of flesh to frostbite.

“I don’t think I can follow,” Jacquline called behind her. “Sculpts can’t go near water. Not without special enchanted equipment.”

Mitsuko turned around. In her excitement to descend into the tunnels, she’d forgotten the sculpt.

“She won’t be much assistance anymore,” Sterling commented. “Simply a weight around your neck, as I am certain you’ll go out of your way to protect her should she follow you into danger.”

Mitsuko almost offered to carry Jacquline just to spite Sterling. But that would be reckless and endanger an innocent person’s life.

“It’s fine,” Mitsuko called back to her. “You can head up to your camp in the mountains, I’ve got it from here. Thanks for guiding me this far!”

With those final words, Mitsuko stepped over a lip that kept out most of the water, and ducked into the ice tunnels.

And immediately slipped.

Pinwheeling her arms, Mitsuko fell on her butt. Sterling yowled as they slid down an icy slope.

She could have cast Mend and forced herself backwards. She considered it but decided to instead ride it out and see where the tunnel led to.

By the time she finally skittered to a stop, Sterling’s claws had pierced through her shirt and torn into her skin. His ears remained pinned back even after they'd stopped.

He tried to play it off but Mitsuko could tell the sage was still frazzled as he jumped from her shoulder over to the ice. His glowing green eyes just barely illuminated his face. He groomed himself ferociously.

Other than Sterling, only the drip of the melting ceiling could be heard. That surprised her as she’d half expected the guardian to speak up immediately after her arrival.

After Mending the damage to her body and restoring her clothes to a dry state, Mitsuko blindly rummaged through her bag and withdrew a lamp and a pair of cleats to strap onto her boots.

A finger on the used wick, Mitsuko cast Mend and a second later it lit up, the wick restored to a time when it burned.

“Who needs bright illusions and fire magic to fight off the dark when you’ve got all of time at your beck and call?” Mitsuko smiled smugly at her accomplishment and raised the lantern.

“Well said,” Sterling nodded his head in approval.

The lantern's light only illuminated ten or so meters before darkness reclaimed the area. Mitsuko took a step up to the ice tunnel’s walls and knocked her knuckles against it. It was a vibrant shade of light blue and wet to the touch.

Mitsuko flicked her wet hands at Sterling who leaped back. He hissed at her, hackles raised.

“That was uncalled for!”

“You scratched me,” Mitsuko replied as she strapped on the cleats. She strode forward, lantern raised.

“Do you have a plan here?” Sterling chased after her. “Is it simply to wander and hope for the best?”

Mitsuko rolled her eyes. “Of course not. I have a rough idea where I am on the island based off where I entered. I figure the best thing to do is head to the center and see what’s there. One of the books I skimmed in the last loop mentioned the island's heart.”

“These tunnels wind and twist,” Sterling said. “You expect to home in on the location like a pigeon?”

“Sort of.” Mitsuko paused at the first fork in the tunnel and focused. It had been a while since she last cast this spell and so it took her a few seconds. Still, she’d never lost this ability. Her divination compass spell pinged her senses, directing her north.

She glanced at the direction she needed to go in to hit the island’s center. Of course that tunnel was only knee-height. She sighed and crouched down.

Pushing herself off the occasional stone jutting out of the ice, Mitsuko slid on her belly across the smooth slippery surface. Holding the lantern with one hand was awkward and the way she arched her back to see made her spine hurt.

“I would have loved this as a kid,” she muttered as she kicked off against the wall. “Maybe I should Mend my age down.”

Sterling didn’t reply.

Mitsuko twisted herself around to look back. The cat had slipped, all legs spread apart from his body. Mitsuko stared as he floundered in an attempt to regain his footing.

“You have claws,” Mitsuko called back to him. “Use them.”

“Your commentary is not appre—oof!”

After a moment of internal debate, Mitsuko sighed and Mended her clothes, dragging herself backwards across the ice over to where Sterling currently flailed about. She stuck a leg out, scooping him up with the back of her calf.

“Try not to scratch me,” she requested as she pushed off to get back to where she’d been.

Mitsuko wasn’t certain how long she was down there, sliding around on her stomach, but she knew that if she hadn’t been a temporal mage, her body would be aching for the next week from this awkward exercise.

Sterling continued to cling to her as she clambered to her feet. She stretched and Mended the soreness from her body.

You’ve come again, the guardian said. He sounded irritated. Earlier this loop. Well, it matters not. I need merely destroy you. Then all this will be complete.

The tunnels trembled and she heard a cocophamy of chitters echoing all around her.

“Thanks for waiting until I stood up,” Mitsuko said. “Very noble of you. It would have been bad news if you attacked while I was prone.”

The guardian didn’t respond. Instead, one of the furry critters she’d slain in the previous Porcelain Island loop emerged from down a tunnel. This time, she had light which let her make out its features. It looked like a rabid juhyo, only with far sharper claws, disproportionately sized to its body, and rows of sharpened teeth like a tiny shark. Its eyes glowed blue, similar to Sterling’s green eyes. An obvious sign of it being puppeted by an unknown force. Through context, Mitsuko felt confident in her assumption it was one of the guardian's minions.

Mitsuko attempted to cast Mend on the critter. The spell slipped right off it. It was the same as when she attempted to use the spell on the other guardian creations. Instead, she simply skewered it. However, it was only the beginning.

“Hey, Sterling,” she said as she darted down a tunnel.

“Now is hardly a favorable time in which to speak to me,” the cat commented as he clung to the back of her shirt.

Mitsuko punted one of the monsters, kicking it into another of its kin.

“Why can’t I cast Mend on these things? I was able to use the spell on Bupre back in the clocktower.”

“It will require one of your questions for me to explain why a child guardian lacked as much control over his surroundings as an experienced adult mage guardian."

Ignoring his snark, Mitsuko appreciated the answer. Bupre hadn’t been the same type of challenge that these other guardians were.

While the rabid juhyo swarmed in the hundreds, they were at least small and Mitsuko was able to continue smashing through their ranks with minimal damage to her body. She was considering herself pretty lucky.

Then the tunnel rumbled.

Mitsuko cursed. She managed to cast another compass spell which directed her over to the left fork.

As she made the turn, the ice at the junction exploded. Icy shrapnel pierced her skin and she felt the warm trickle of blood on the back of her head mixing in with the shards of ice.

Still, she continued to run. A glance over her shoulder revealed her fear.

That same massive ice worm that killed Jacquline in the previous loop. The thing was so huge that it took several more seconds for the entirety of its body to pass through the junction.

Mistuko gleaned above her. Then from side to side. There was no telling which direction the worm would emerge from. If she caught it in time, she might be able to Mend the walls to repel it and shove the worm back. Still, she wasn’t holding her breath and holding onto hope that she would  accomplish that task.

Instead, Mitsuko came up with a new strategy. She cast Deteriorate on the ice tunnel. In front of her, on her left, the ice shattered. She immediately let go of the spell and dropped to the ground, her momentum allowing her to slide across the ice, dodging to the  side of where the worm would appear. She flicked her wrist, creating a blade.

The ice worm burst out from the area indicated by her spell. As it erupted forward, Mistsuko passed under its girth.

She jabbed upwards, Deteriorating her blade to add extra umph to her stab. The blade pierced through the snowy flesh as it was ripped out of her hand. It left a sizable gash in the creature before the blade snapped as the monster passed through the tunnel’s other wall. Mitsuko noted that, like the sculpts, this was a magical creature made of ice and snow rather than flesh and blood like the juhyos.

Mitsuko was already on the other side of the worm, having completely slid under it.

“It really is quite fortunate that the guardian did not detect you while you were sliding about like a penguin,” Sterling commented from her shoulder. "I imagine that must be a result of the guardian's sheer quantity of minions. Difficult to track one specific soul with so many others active nearby.”

“Yes. I feel very fortunate right now.” Mitsuko darted down a different tunnel, then skittered to a halt and double backed. The feint worked. She heard the ice smash back down that cavern while she continued in the direction of her objective.

This was all just buying her minutes. She needed to end this worm for any hope of confronting the guardian.

For a second, she debated getting purposefully swallowed by the creature and cutting her way out of it from within like she had with the leviathan. However, this monster’s teeth completely dismissed this as an option. She’d get shredded by those layers of sharp teeth before getting to the throat. From the glances she’d seen of the worm’s mouth, the circle of glowing blue eyes at the rim were the friendliest part of the monster.

A thought occurred to her as she saw the ice crack up ahead of her from her Deteriorate spell. What is she wasn’t what it swallowed?

Whipping around her pack, she plunged a hand inside and ripped out a chunk of wood. She'd grabbed it on a whim at the start of this loop. It was the same wood as shed cast Retrosection on in a previous loop. Highly flammable wood.

Normally, this plan would take way too much time. However, the spells at her disposal happened to make time a nonissue. She shoved the wood into her flickering lantern and cast Deteriorate. The wood burst into flames, as if dosed in alcohol.

She ignored the pain of her flesh burning as her hand held the flaming wood. Pain was just an obstacle and she needed to be ready.

As the ice worm burst from the already broken wall, Mitsuko stuck out her arm, still holding the blazing fire.

The worm’s teeth snagged her arm and she slammed into the wall with force before the teeth severed the limb.

Gasping in pain, with blood gushing from where her missing arm was severed mid-bicep, Mitsuko fought to maintain consciousness. She kept reminding herself that pain was just an obstacle. She was stronger. But no matter how many times she repeated it to herself, it didn’t stop her vision from darkening.

Where matras failed, spells succeeded.

It took half a minute of struggle. Her arm slowly Mended back together. This was one of those times where she had to create the matter itself, rather than pull existing matter back into place. It was excruciatingly painful. Until, suddenly, it wasn’t. Only the echoes of pain remained.

She finally realized that Sterling was talking to her.

When she’d been thrown up against the wall, he’d still been on her back. While her body hadn’t broken from the impact, his smaller form had been crushed. His two back legs were bent in unnatural angles. She quickly cast Mend and his feline body snapped back into its normal form.

Back on her feet, Mitsuko cast Deteriorate on the tunnel around her. She scanned the floors, wall, and ceiling. Nothing.

“There’s no way just a bit of firewood killed that monster,” she said.

Stirling shuddered, his black hair on edge. “I agree it’s doubtful you dealt a fatal blow. However, do not underestimate the potency of fire against your current foe. Sculpts fear flames more than anything else and the creature you’re facing has similar anatomical and magical structure to the people of this island.”

“So… you think it fled? That doesn’t make any sense though. It shouldn’t have any natural instincts while being puppeted by the island’s guardian.”

Sterling shrugged. The guardian likewise remained silent.

Mitsuko picked Sterling up with her newly reformed hand and reclaimed her fallen lantern with the other. Then they continued down the tunnel. She periodically cast Deteriorate on the area around her, preparing herself for an ambush. Nothing came though. And she should feel the tunnel shake like before if the worm was nearby. She was starting to believe that maybe she actually had slain the monster.

To prepare herself, Mitsuko cinched the lantern to her belt. It might get in the way there but it was undoubtedly preferable if it meant keeping a hand free for whatever came next.

Then she rounded the corner and entered the heart of the island.
