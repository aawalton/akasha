Jia and Eui arrived late to the training field, and the other students were already engaged in sparring matches. Lee Jia was surprised to see a few new faces in the group of advanced martial arts disciples.

The tiger-headed boy who’s name she still didn’t know was present and sparring with Guan Yi— who must have benefited more than she realized from their sparring sessions last week—and Seong Eunae was clumsily practicing her basic forms under Takeda’s guidance. She briefly wondered how they had managed to awaken before Dae, who she had given the combat meditation technique to, but then realized that he’d been skipping martial arts training to tutor her.

Master Ienaga eyed the pair as they approached, frowning.

“I was willing to let your last absence go in light of the circumstances—congratulations, by the way—but I told you I don’t accept tardiness.”

Jia and Eui both bowed apologetically.

“We’re sorry! We got caught up in meditation and lost track of the time!”

Ienaga quirked an eyebrow at them curiously.

“Both of you?”

“Uh...yes?”

Ienaga pursed her lips as she stared at them, Lee Jia felt herself start to sweat as Ienaga tapped her wooden training sword meaningfully.

“Fine, I suppose it happens. Lee, from the results of your duel I can only assume you’ve been neglecting your martial arts in favor of magic. Your attempt to use ki to deflect Yan’s attack was an embarrassment.”

Lee Jia winced at the rebuke, rubbing her arm where the worst of her injuries from the duel was still healing.

“I didn’t realize you’d seen it...”

“Bring your foundations up to par before it’s time to start learning techniques. An, have some full contact spars with Lee to show her the importance of meditating on what she’s learned, then bring her up to speed with the rest of the class.”

Both girls bowed once again and chorused their compliance before finding a spot for their sparring.

Jia had sparred with Eui before, but mostly just to practice basic forms which usually felt more like a choreographed dance than an actual spar. Their few full contact spars had been when Jia was practicing magic as opposed to martial arts.

Eui was grinning as they took their stances across from each other. Jia wasn’t sure where Ienaga’s confidence in Eui came from—they both had the same amount of practice.

“Are you ready?”

“Of course!”

As their bout began, Jia initially found herself trying to keep a mid range position out of habit, before remembering that she didn’t have her spells. Eui took advantage of her hesitation to close the gap, striking at Jia’s face. She wasn’t holding back!

Jia slipped her head to the side to dodge the blow and attempted to counter with an open-palmed strike of her own, but Eui caught the blow and smoothly twisted Jia’s momentum against her, throwing her to the ground.

Jia blinked up at the sky, confused. She knew the throw—even knew that Eui might use it in that circumstance—but Eui had done it so quickly and cleanly that Jia barely had time to realize it was happening before she hit the ground. Eui helped Jia up and they set up for a second round.

“You’re thinking too much, Jia. You need to move with your ki and let your body do the remembering.”

Jia frowned—she knew that. She’d done that against Yan Zhihao. She thought she was doing it now, but apparently Eui disagreed.

Jia went on the offensive for their second bout, closing the distance aggressively and unleashing a flurry of claw swipes—without the claws—and spear hands. Eui calmly dodged and parried each blow, but Jia left no room for counterattacks. When Eui swung into an opening that Jia had left intentionally, she ducked under the attack and slipped past her defenses to—

Jia fell face first flat on the ground and shook her head in confusion. Looking back, she realized what had happened—Eui had tripped Jia with her tail! She was always better with her tail than Jia was, and Jia had completely forgotten she even had it!

“You’ve got your claws, but don’t forget that I’m a half-spirit, too.”

Jia blushed as Eui snickered at her. Eui’s tail was the only expression of her spiritual bloodline, and forgetting it was an unforgivable blunder. Jia huffed with frustration. She hated to admit it, but Eui was running circles around her.

“I don’t understand, I thought we’d practiced the same amount! Why are you so much better!?”

“Jia, when was the last time you even did ki meditation?”

Jia frowned as she thought about it. She couldn’t actually remember. She’d been so focused on improving her mana that she hadn’t given it much thought. She clutched at her hair and groaned, Takeda and Guan had even lectured them on the importance of meditating!

Eui smiled ruefully at Jia and shook her head.

“That’s what I thought. What do you think I’ve been doing while you were busy getting reading lessons from Hyeong?”

Jia understood what Ienaga had meant just now—she really had fallen behind in martial arts. She shook it off and got ready for another bout. She’d let her victory against Yan get to her head, but this had been a good wake-up call. She wasn’t going to start slacking just because of one duel.

Jia spent the rest of the lesson sparring with Eui. She never won, but she was determined to learn something from every bout in order to give herself something to meditate on later—and she promised herself she would meditate on her lessons this time.

After the lesson, Ienaga asked Jia and Eui to stay behind. They waited as the other students cleared the field, and Ienaga sent a few curious stragglers off with a glare before finally turning to Jia and Eui with a look of concern.

“Why were you really late this morning?”

Jia exchanged a look with Eui, uncertain how to respond. They hadn’t exactly lied.

“We really were meditating—”

“You didn’t see the looks on your faces when you arrived this morning, Lee. You both looked like soldiers returning from a war.”

Jia recalled the harrowing experience of Eui’s awakening. They had given themselves time to calm down and clean up afterwards, but even thinking about it now brought back an unpleasant tide of emotions.

“Yes, that expression. What happened to you two?”

Ienaga frowned down at them, a hand on her hip in an uncharacteristically matronly pose.

“Uh, that’s...kinda hard to explain.”

Lee Jia scratched her head as she tried to find the words. Ienaga’s eyes narrowed and her frown deepened.

“Try.”

Jia exchanged looks with Eui again, who simply shrugged. Jia grimaced before awkwardly trying to explain.

“W-well, I unlocked my qi the other day, but the way I did it was really dangerous so I was trying to help Eui do it a safer way, but then—”

“Hold on, I think I might understand what’s happening here. Were you two meditating together? I don’t mean at the same time in the same room, I mean—well if you were, you know exactly what I mean.”

Lee Jia’s eyes widened in surprise—she hadn’t expected Ienaga to understand so quickly. After sharing another confirming glance with Eui, both girls nodded. Ienaga pinched the bridge of her nose and sighed heavily.

“Oh, this is not a conversation I thought I’d be having today. Right, first explain everything from the beginning. I’m not going to judge you, but I need you to be completely honest with me.”

Lee Jia felt a bit confused, but she and Eui did their best to explain everything, starting from what happened during their first awakening, then their discovery of joint meditation, and finally the similar but much more intense experience of Eui’s awakening that morning.

Ienaga listened intently, only interrupting to ask for clarifications or details. When they finished their story, Ienaga was rubbing her chin and frowning contemplatively.

“That’s not quite what I expected. What you call joint meditation is more commonly known as dual cultivation, or at least it’s similar. It might be better to keep calling it joint meditation, dual cultivation has...certain connotations.”

Lee Jia tilted her head curiously.

“What kind of connotations? Is it a bad thing?”

Ienaga broke eye contact and pinched the bridge of her nose again. Lee Jia was surprised at seeing the normally implacable instructor looking so flustered.

“It’s—hmm, well I’ve never heard of mages dual cultivating, but it’s treated differently by Qin and Yamato. Both frown on it as a cultivation technique, but some martial artists do it...recreationally. It’s completely forbidden in Qin, though.”

Jia exchanged a confused look with Eui. This time it was Eui who voiced their confusion.

“Why? What part of meditation is recreational, and why would Qin forbid it? I’d expect them to be all about more efficient meditation techniques.”

Ienaga made an exasperated groaning sound and shook her head.

“Dual cultivation is accomplished by entangling one’s ki with someone else’s. For both spiritual and martial artists, this has traditionally been accomplished alongside a more intimately physical entanglement.”

Lee Jia stared blankly at Ienaga, blinking in confusion, but Eui’s eyes widened with understanding.

“Oh. Oh! You mean like sex?”

Jia’s head jerked up to stare at Eui in shock, her tail had gone rigid. Where did that come from? To her increasing surprise, Ienaga sighed and nodded.

“I had hoped to avoid putting it so crudely, but yes.”

“Yeah, but if I didn’t spell it out like that Jia would have never gotten it.”

Jia felt herself blushing as she glanced rapidly between Ienaga and Eui.

“Wait, what? Then when you asked—and then you thought that we—oh my ancestors!”

She tried to cover her face in embarrassment, but she felt like her blush was extending all the way through her hands. Eui’s cackling laughter was not helping, but Ienaga gave her a sympathetic look.

“I understand that it doesn’t seem to be that way for you two. I wasn’t aware that dual cultivation was possible without—well even at my age there’s always new lessons to learn. In any case, Qin forbids it for other reasons. Aside from being rather conservative as a culture, they believe that it’s a path to ruin.”

Eui scoffed dismissively.

“Why am I not surprised that Qin thinks that sex is evil?”

“They aren’t entirely wrong about dual cultivation. It’s generally a highly exploitative practice, with either the stronger partner stealing power from—or willingly giving power to—the weaker one. It’s only mutually beneficial among perfect equals, and even then there are dangers.”

Lee Jia’s ears perked up as she looked up urgently at Ienaga. Her blush still hadn’t faded, but this was more important than her embarrassment.

“Dangers? What kind of dangers?”

“A pair of equal partners who do all their cultivation together will slowly become dependent on each other. As one’s ki becomes increasingly indistinguishable from the other’s, they will eventually reach a point where they can’t cultivate at all without their partner anymore.”

Lee Jia let that sink in for a moment. That was quite a commitment.

“That’s not an immediate concern. At the very least, it should be impossible to develop that kind of dependence before your first bottleneck, but still, you should know the risks. I won’t tell you not to take this path, but I have to warn you that it’s not a decision you can make lightly.

“Before your next breakthrough you have to make a choice—never cultivate together again, or commit to exclusively cultivating together for the rest of your lives. And they don’t call us immortals for nothing—it will be a very long life. I might not look it, but I’m over a hundred years old, myself.”

Jia and Eui’s jaws dropped. Master Ienaga appeared to be the youngest of their three teachers and she didn’t look like she could be much older than thirty. Ienaga gave the two girls a rare smile, evidently guessing at their thoughts.

“Body cultivation has its perks. Bear in mind that nothing is stopping you from honing your crafts together as friends, even if you give up on dual—on joint meditation. Think it over carefully, but don’t stall for too long—it will be a much more painful decision to make later. Alright, I’ve said enough, you’re dismissed.”

As she finished, Ienaga turned to go, leaving Jia and Eui to consider the gravity of everything they had just discussed. Before she was out of earshot, Ienaga turned to call back over her shoulder.

“Oh! One more thing! You can, and probably should ask Magus Hwang about joint mana meditation—it’s the first I’ve ever heard of it—but try not to mention it to Qin Zhao. That’s a headache that I just don’t want to deal with.”

Jia stared after her flabbergasted. For the first time, she felt like she’d gotten a glimpse of Ienaga Yumi as a person, rather than the spartan instructor she’d come to know over the last few weeks.

Jia and Eui sat together in awkward silence. They had hardly spoken since Ienaga’s warning about joint meditation. The atmosphere was a little bit tense, and Jia found herself getting lost in her own thoughts.

She wasn’t sure if she was ready to commit to spending the rest of her life training alongside Eui. At the same time, even though they’d only known each other for a few weeks, she could hardly imagine a life without her. It was no exaggeration to say that Eui was the best friend Jia had ever had. While they both had their problems, Eui was the first person Jia felt comfortable sharing her trouble with, and she was happy to share Eui’s trouble in turn.

Sharing memories had been an intimate experience, and sharing their emotions had been even more so. They had gotten used to sharing memories, and had much better control now over what they shared with each other. Eui’s voice in Jia’s head had come to feel less and less like an intruder, and more like a gentle spirit on her shoulder. Though on the other hand, she had found the thoughts becoming harder and harder to distinguish from her own.

When Jia couldn’t bear the silence anymore, she looked up at Eui just in time to see her do the same.

“Um-”“So-”

They both spoke at the same time as their eyes met, interrupting each other and falling back into awkward silence as they each tried to let the other speak first. After a moment, Eui chuckled.

“Heh, this is so stupid.”

Jia couldn’t help but giggle herself.

“Yeah, you’re probably right.”

With the tension broken, the silence felt a little bit more comfortable. Eui was the first to break the silence, uninterrupted this time.

“It feels a bit awkward bringing this up now, but I had an idea about how I could help you catch up in martial arts. You picked up my knowledge of reading and writing when we meditated together, right? And I know pretty much everything about magic that you do, even though I didn’t join your tutoring.”

Jia nodded, she had a feeling she knew where Eui was going with this.

“So I figured, after what happened with my awakening, if joint qi meditation is possible then why not ki meditation? Of course, ironically, now that we’ve confirmed it’s possible we’ve also found out that we should probably stop with joint meditation…”

Is that what Eui thought? It hurt Jia more than she expected to hear that. As she blinked back the tears welling up in her eyes, Jia made her decision.

“I don’t want to stop! I like the idea of having someone I can share everything with! Coming to this place was the best thing that ever happened to me, but it’s not because of magic or immortality or any of that, as exciting as those things are. It’s because I got to meet you, and make the first real friend I’ve ever had. Maybe it’s childish or reckless or naive or all of the above, but if keeping that means we have to stick together for the rest of our lives, then that doesn’t sound so bad to me!”

Jia had to stop to catch her breath after her rant had come out in a torrent. She averted her eyes as she felt the blush creeping onto her face. She was suddenly embarrassed at her own rambling. As the silence stretched on, Jia felt her anxiety mounting—she was scared that she had pushed too hard, ruined the friendship they’d cultivated, what if—

“Oh, thank the ancestors!”

Eui’s voice broke Jia from her reverie.

“That is such a relief! I didn’t know how to say it without sounding weird and clingy. Thank the ancestors that you could be weird and clingy enough for both of us!”

Eui flopped over onto the couch as she sighed with relief, splaying her arms and legs out in a decidedly unladylike manner. Jia felt her face flush even redder as she puffed out her cheeks irritably.

“What the hell, Eui!? Why did you take so long to answer?”

“Ah, sorry. I just didn’t really know what to say. I’ve been freaking out about it since we talked to Ienaga. Plus, you should have seen the look on your face. It was seriously cu—oh fuck!”

Jia leapt over the tea table and attacked Eui, tickling her mercilessly until she was forced to beg for forgiveness.

“Ahahaha—sto—stop! Haha—ok, I giv—haha—I give up! I’m sorry!”

Jia granted Eui her forgiveness.

…

But not her mercy.

“Nooo!”

Eui learned a dark lesson about getting on Jia’s bad side that day as her desperate pleas for mercy fell on deaf ears.
