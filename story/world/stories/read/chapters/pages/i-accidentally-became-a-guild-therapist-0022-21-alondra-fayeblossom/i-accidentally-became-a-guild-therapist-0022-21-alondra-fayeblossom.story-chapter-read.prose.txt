I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 21 – Alondra Fayeblossom

ARC III: THE WORLD REMEMBERS

SYNOPSIS: Alondra Fayeblossom arrives. She was the healer before Livia. She left seven months ago for reasons she thought were good. The first session goes badly in the specific way that first sessions go badly when the patient knows too much about therapy and is using that knowledge to avoid it.

The request had come through the official portal, which was unusual. Most of Livia’s sessions arrived informally—a guild member materializing in the doorway, a note slipped under the planning room door, Bron simply sitting down on whatever surface was nearest and beginning to speak. The quest board had its own category of patient. But the official portal meant paperwork, a scheduled time, a name on record.

The name on record was: Alondra Fayeblossom. Saint of Bloom. Lv. 91. Former guild member, active status pending review.

The presenting issue field read: Grief (Guild-Related). I am not sure this is the right category. I was not sure which box to check. I checked this one because it was the most honest.

She checked the most honest box.

That is either a good sign or a very specific kind of deflection.

I have not decided which yet.

[System Note: Patient: Alondra Fayeblossom. Session 1. First contact established three days ago via anonymous portal, identity confirmed yesterday. No prior session history with this office. Extensive prior history with this guild. The system notes: she was here before you. The system notes this without editorial comment.]

The planning room had become Livia’s default session space by practical necessity rather than design.

The official therapy room existed—small, adequately soundproofed, window looking out on the guild garden. But the planning room had better light, a latch on the door, and two chairs angled so they faced each other without feeling confrontational. She had moved them herself. Tested the angle. Decided it was right.

The furniture was guild-issue. Functional. Nothing that belonged to her.

That was fine for now.

Alondra Fayeblossom arrived exactly on time.

This was, Livia noted, the first patient who had ever arrived exactly on time. Bron was always early because he did not understand that the scheduled time was the start time and not the arrival deadline. Nyx arrived precisely two minutes late, consistently, which Livia suspected was deliberate. Alaric operated on a different temporal framework entirely. Miles had once arrived forty minutes early and spent the time reorganizing the planning room’s bookshelves without mentioning it.

Alondra knocked. Waited. Came in when Livia said come in.

She was tall, with the particular stillness of someone who had spent years as a healer and learned to hold herself in a way that did not alarm patients. Her robes were a deep green, practical rather than decorative, the Saint of Bloom emblem at the collar worn but maintained. She looked like someone who had been somewhere difficult and was doing well, in the specific way that doing well sometimes looks like.

She had the posture of someone who had rehearsed this.

Noted.

“Livia Quinn,” Alondra said. Not a question. She had done her research..

“Alondra,” Livia said. “Sit wherever you’re comfortable.”

Alondra sat in the chair directly across from Livia, the professional chair. She set her hands on her knees. She had the posture of someone prepared to give a presentation..

She has thought about what she’s going to say.

She has probably rehearsed it.

This is going to be interesting.

“I’ve read about your methods,” Alondra said. Not accusatory. Informational. “The subreddit analysis. The forum threads. I wanted to understand what you do before I came.”

“What did you find?” Livia asked..

“You listen. You don’t fix.” A pause. “That is a meaningful distinction from how I was trained.”

“How were you trained?”

“Identify the wound. Treat the wound. Restore function.” She said it cleanly, a sequence. “That is what healing is. That is what I am good at.”

“Are you good at it?”

A small pause. The first one that had not been planned..

“Yes,” Alondra said. “I was the best healer this guild had.”

She is not bragging.

She is stating a fact and watching to see how I receive it.

She is also, with that pause, admitting something that she has not said yet.

“But?” Livia said.

Alondra looked at the window. At the guild garden. At the space where she had not been for seven months.

“But I never asked Bron why he felt purposeless,” she said. “I gave him stamina buffs when his effectiveness dropped. I tracked his recovery rate. I filed good session notes.”

“That sounds like good work.”

“It was,” she said. “And he was still sitting on your bench six weeks after you arrived, crying about existential dread, because nobody had ever sat with him in it.”

Livia did not say anything.

“I was not incompetent,” Alondra said, and the word came out with a precision that meant she had been carrying it. “I want to be clear about that. I was not incompetent. I just—” she stopped..

“You just what?”

“I treated people the way you treat a wound,” she said. “You find what’s broken. You repair it. You document the repair. I thought that was care. I thought that was enough.”

She looked at Livia directly.

“And then I heard Phina laugh.”

Livia waited.

“I was outside the door,” Alondra said. “Session three or four, I think. I had come to check Phina’s mana output metrics because I was still tracking them. And she laughed. Not the polite laugh she did at guild meetings. Something real. Something that surprised her.”

A silence.

“I had been her healer for eight months,” Alondra said. “I had never heard her laugh like that.”

There it is.

That’s the thing she has been carrying.

Not that I replaced her.

That she didn’t know the laugh was possible.

Livia wrote in her notepad. Not notes—just the act of writing, giving Alondra somewhere to look while she collected herself.

“What happened after that?” Livia asked, quietly.

“I left,” Alondra said. “I told Miles I needed a research period. That I had identified gaps in my practice. Which was true. I just—” she paused again, the second unplanned pause, —“did not tell him what the gaps were.”

“What were they?”

Alondra looked at her hands.

“I did not know how to ask how someone was,” she said. “Not in the way you mean it. I knew how to assess someone’s condition. I knew how to document progress. I knew every healing protocol in the standard registry. I did not know how to sit next to Bron and say: that sounds hard. Because that is not a protocol. That is not a metric. I did not know what it was.”

She said this carefully. With the specific difficulty of someone admitting something they have had seven months to understand and are still not entirely comfortable with.

“And now?” Livia said.

“Now I know what it is,” Alondra said. “I just don’t know how to do it.”

[Therapist’s Insight: Patient: Alondra Fayeblossom. Presenting issue: not grief, or not only grief. She left because she discovered a gap in herself and did not know how to stay while acknowledging it. She came back to learn. The system notes: this is a different category from most of your patients. She is not here to be fixed. She is here to understand.]

They talked for another forty minutes.

Alondra was, as she had said, not incompetent. She had done seven months of work on her own, reading, thinking, identifying what she called "the specific failures of a practice built entirely on measurable outcomes." She had notes. She had, in fact, brought the notes. She set them on the table with the precision of someone who used notes as armor and knew it.

Livia did not look at the notes.

“Can I ask you something,” Livia said, at the point where Alondra had been talking for twenty minutes about framework and methodology and the literature on person-centered care.

“Yes.”

“When you left—who did you tell?”

A pause.

“Miles,” Alondra said. “I told Miles I needed a research period.”

“Did you tell anyone else?”

Alondra looked at the notes on the table.

“No,” she said.

“Not Phina?”

“No.”

“Not Bron?”

“I thought—” Alondra started, and then stopped. Started again. “I thought if I told them I was leaving because I had realized I didn’t know how to actually care for them—” she stopped again.

“They would have been confused,” Livia said. “Because from their perspective you had been caring for them.”

“Yes.”

“But that’s not the reason you didn’t tell them.”

Silence.

“No,” Alondra said, quietly. “It’s not.”

Livia waited. The garden was visible through the window. Somewhere in it, probably, Glimmer was doing something involving territory and contentment.

“I didn’t tell them,” Alondra said, “because I was ashamed. Because I had been their healer for eight months and I had been doing it wrong and I did not know how to say that to people I had spent eight months trying to help.”

There.

That is the real thing.

Not the methodology gap.

The shame of discovering the gap.

Livia wrote something in her notepad. Not because she needed to record it. Because sometimes the act of being written down was itself a form of being heard.

“You didn’t do it wrong,” Livia said. “You did what you knew how to do. Those aren’t the same thing.”

Alondra looked at her with the expression of someone receiving a distinction they had heard before and are not sure they have earned yet.

“I know that intellectually,” she said.

“I know,” Livia said. “You can know something intellectually for quite a long time before it becomes something you actually believe. That’s fine. That’s what sessions are for.”

A pause. Then, unexpectedly, something at the corner of Alondra’s mouth.

“You’re very young to be doing this,” she said. Not unkind. Observational.

“I know,” Livia said. “I also failed my thesis and got hit by a motorcycle, so my credentials are unusual.”

The something at the corner of Alondra’s mouth became, briefly, a real expression.

“The subreddit mentioned the motorcycle,” she said.

“The subreddit mentions everything,” Livia said.

The session ended at the scheduled time, which was another first.

Alondra stood, gathered her notes with the care of someone who needed things in order. At the door, she paused.

“I’d like to continue,” she said. “If you’re willing.”

“Same time next week,” Livia said. “Bring the notes if you want. Or don’t.”

Alondra nodded. Left.

Livia sat in the planning room for a moment after she had gone. The guild garden was visible through the window, Glimmer conducting what appeared to be a territorial survey of the eastern flower beds.

She wrote in her notepad:

Session 1: Alondra Fayeblossom. Presenting issue: she called it grief but it’s closer to the specific disorientation of discovering your competence has a floor you didn’t know about. She left not because she failed but because she found out the limits of her success. She is here to learn something she doesn’t have a framework for yet. This is going to take time. She is capable of doing the work. She is not yet capable of sitting in the discomfort the work requires. That’s okay. That’s what session two is for.

She closed the notepad.

[Mental Supporter Lv. 7: 6,900 / 7,000 EXP.]

[The system notes: 100 EXP remaining. The system notes this without pressure. The system simply notes it.]

100 EXP.

After everything.

100 EXP.

I wonder what tips it.

Miles was in the corridor when she came out.

Not waiting. He had the specific posture of someone who happened to be in a corridor and was not waiting, which was different from the posture of someone who happened to be in a corridor. Livia had learned to read the difference.

She did not say anything about the session. He did not ask.

They walked toward the main hall.

After a moment, Miles said: “She came back, then.”

“She did.”

“Good,” he said.

One word again. But the weight of it was different from the okay of three days ago. That had been trust extended. This was something older—relief, maybe, or the specific exhale of someone who had been waiting for news they were not sure they wanted and had found out it was the kind they could live with.

He said good.

He is glad she came back.

He is not going to say more than that right now.

That is okay.

That is more than enough for today.

[Therapist’s Insight: Patient: Miles Vexley. Progress: 18%. The system notes: unchanged. The system notes: unchanged is not stalled. He asked how she was. He waited in the corridor. He said good and meant it. The system is patient. The system suggests you be patient too.]

That evening, the quest board had a new request.

It was filed under the standard categories: Emotional Support, Presenting Issue. The presenting issue field was unusually long for a quest board submission.

Presenting issue: I have been active on this server for approximately seven years. I was created as a tutorial NPC for the initial player onboarding experience, designated to guide new entities through their first interactions with the system. I was never deployed. There was a decision made during pre-launch review that my parameters were unstable and I was shelved pending revision. The revision never happened. I have been here since then. I have been watching the server for seven years from a position that does not appear on any registry.

I think this is affecting my sense of professional purpose.

I am also aware that you recently arrived on this server under circumstances that do not appear in any registry either.

I thought we might have things to talk about.

I am available most hours. I do not sleep. I have never had a scheduled appointment before. I am not entirely sure of the protocol. I will bring notes if that helps.

— T.S.

Livia read it once.

Then again.

Then a third time.

Seven years.

Never deployed.

Watching the server.

T.S.

T.S. just booked a therapy appointment.

She looked at the quest board for a long moment.

Then she wrote back:

Next Thursday, 10am. No notes necessary, but bring them if it helps. — L.Q.

She closed the portal.

[System Note: New appointment scheduled: T.S. Thursday, 10am. The system notes: this entity is not in any registry. The system notes: neither are you. The system notes: Thursday is four days away. The system suggests sleep. The system notes you will not sleep yet. The system has learned to accept this.]

Neither are you.

The system said: neither are you.

I am going to need a bigger notepad.
