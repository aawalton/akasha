Ryland was forced to [Translocate] away when Mavar decided the best course of action was vapourizing away the upper atmosphere of northern Rhel by turning everything into a star. It spoke of Mavar’s desperation, of the lengths he was willing to go prove his superiority. It was proof of a lot of other things, really, but Ryland didn’t need to deliberate on such things.

Rather, now that the plasma was far enough to not harm him, now that the liquified energy was far enough to only scratch his [Awakened Avatar] and [Animus Aegis] barriers, he could take a moment to plan his next moves.

Mavar was pulling on Living Mana for his incredible prowess. Even now, even when he was technically an Incarnation himself, he was drawing on a foreign strength.

The whys didn’t matter. The hows weren’t necessary for consideration.

For the fact of the matter remained that Mavar was tearing reality apart to assert the existence of the Overrealm where it really didn’t belong, but then asserting his will onto those existences to burn away their very essence as fuel for his home-made star core.

All through the links he had established through Living Mana. For, thanks to Acrypha’s interference before this later battle had started, Mavar’s ascension had been incomplete.

Only Living Mana had ascended, to some extent. Not the Demiurge. Not fully, not yet.

Obviously, Mavar intended to rectify that. The connection between him and the seemingly endless amount of Living Mana in the rest of the Accorded Realms was still firing on all cylinders. There still had to be more than enough left for him to continue ascending.

Ryland was settling on the right courses of action when a familiar interruption came in.

Acrypha. Again.

Ryland should have felt relief that she had reappeared. He had been intending to follow after and see where she had gone, but now she was back of her own accord, so that should have removed an important worry. Except, it did the opposite. He was only seeing her as an apparition through [Omniscience], but it was obvious that it was some sort of projection of Acrypha.

There was no way for Ryland to tell if that was the real her, not without further investigation and inspection.

How was she appearing in this manner? Ryland would have immediately gone in to secure her, but there was too much chaos going on. Too much energy being dispersed. Too much power at play.

She was already attacking Mavar. Whatever form she had come in had granted her several orders of magnitude more strength that couldn’t have been hers.

The clash between Mavar and Acrypha lasted only a few brief heartbeats.

Then Mavar screamed.

Ryland was actually amazed at seeing her accomplishment. She was tearing through Mavar’s connection with Living Mana. A direction of attack that had fully taken Mavar by surprise, which was partially why it had succeeded. For her connection with the Living Mana seemed even more integral, even stronger than the Demiurge’s own.

It was time.

“[Translocate],” Ryland incanted.

The severance of Mavar from his Living Mana power source was causing massive disruptions to his spell. So much so that the star core burning upon Rhel was now faltering, the plasma fading.

Of course, the residual energy and radiation left behind by that inconceivable blast of magic was still tremendously potent. Ryland felt his Incarnation form burning away at the sheer blaze of energy blasting him with no surcease. Still, he forged onwards. Acrypha, or whatever was posing as her, had created an opening. He had never needed one, but he wasn’t about to waste it either.

“You stupid girl!” Mavar was saying. Either he didn’t think much of Ryland re-entering the fray, or he was just that distracted. “All you managed to do was take away two Manifestations. All you did was cut my link to two strands of Living Mana. Nothing more!”

“[Entropic Ingestion],” Ryland incanted.

As Ryland’s spell bloomed with the strength of his Soul Glyph of Energy, he manipulated a Conceptual Glyph at the same time. All the energy wanted to emit. To radiate outwards. That was what kept bombarding Ryland’s barrier.

But by deleting the Glyph of Radiation entirely, Ryland had now left that energy standing harmlessly, even if it was potent enough to belong in the heart of a star.

And then his spell ate up the energy.

It condensed into a ball of power that could punch holes through stars. Ryland grabbed it, and Mavar finally looked over with gawking shock and alarm. A fingerbreadth. That was how close Ryland got to landing that compressed energy blast on Mavar. Maybe it was for the best he hadn’t landed the hit because he would surely have vapourized a quarter of Rhel too.

“I grow weary of your interference, Ryland!” Mavar shouted from where he had teleported to. “[Vestiges of Life].”

“Mere sorcery won’t work any longer, Mavar,” Ryland said.

He went on to prove it too. The air and light and other matter moulded into various creatures rushing towards Ryland to take him out, but he was done giving them any consideration. They weren’t worthy of such respect.

So, he focused on the Glyphs tying them together, and he simply cast his Soul Glyph of Sundering on top of them.

The stampede of wildebeests made of compressed air simply faded to a breeze. The dragons formed from storm clouds dissolved into sparking rainfall. The titanic behemoth of fire and slag crumbled to ash and dust.

Mavar looked around at their sudden disappearance. Then he took Ryland’s words to heart.

Instead of relying on spells to cast magic, he finally called upon his prowess as an Incarnation. It didn’t work either.

Mavar tried controlling space, but it didn’t work. Ryland didn’t even try to shut down the particular Glyphs that affected space to freeze him in place. Instead, he focused on a deeper level.

On the control that Mavar had on whatever he targeted.

“What in the—”

Mavar cursed and motioned his hand in a wide arc. Nothing happened. Whatever power he had been exerting had just disappeared entirely.

Of course, there were a great deal of other things for him to try. Both by using his direct control and by channelling sorcery that could shatter the world. More rents into Overrealm cracked open with the magic that he was calling upon, the mostly-destroyed atmosphere of northern Rhel groaning under the stress.

Ryland tutted. Not this again. He really didn’t care for it, so he took a very surgical approach to ending things for good.

Volatile energies crashed upon him with massive detonations. Pillars of light shattered upon his body. Great dragons grew out of sparking air and deadly storms, drakes coiling out of fiery smoke, wind whistling into shrieking cyclones.

Ryland’s barriers were taking a pummelling. But every spell that struck him hit him once, just once, and then disappeared to nothing.

The tornadoes brimming with destruction collapsed. The flying mountains fell back to the earth as smoky dust. Mavar’s magical creations all ground away until they were nothing, sparking, radiating, crackling to nonexistence. Every spell, every bit of mana, every incantation calling upon every sorcery, all struck Ryland and died. For good.

“This…” Mavar was getting more and more agitated. His body was shaking. His Incarnation form was stuttering, the energy creating it slowly dissipating. “What is this? What are you doing?”

Ryland couldn’t gloat that he had gone unaffected. By relying simply on his barriers, he had lost his cape and some of his robes had been destroyed utterly. They were still a part of his Incarnation form, so they weren’t lost entirely, but the fact that Mavar had damaged him enough to destroy them partially meant that he truly was a force to be reckoned with.

A force Ryland was shutting down. Permanently.

“We are vastly different Incarnations, Mavar,” Ryland said. “Not just in the domains we control, where I claim dominion over Sorcery and you possess the ability to Control all that you see. It’s also the fact that I have been an Incarnation for as long as you’ve known me.”

Mavar scowled. “That proves nothing. Your experience makes you conceited.”

“My experience reminds me that as long as I’ve known you, you’ve been a fool.”

Mavar roared in rage and attacked Ryland again. Except, nothing happened at first. No doubt, he had attempted to channel magic into the spells he had already flung at Ryland. He had sought to recreate the destruction from earlier just so he could slowly but surely grind Ryland to nothing.

But that was the thing.

“You can’t control what you cannot cast,” Ryland said. A bit of satisfaction slipped into his voice, which just enraged Mavar even further. “And you cannot cast what you cannot channel sorcery into.”

“Sorcery…” Mavar’s eyes widened. There wasn’t an easy way to tell, but Ryland got the reliable impression that he was looking inwards, in a sense. That he was observing his abilities to channel sorcery. “You couldn’t have! I am Incarnation. This sort of tampering is im—”

“Is incredibly possible when you know how and have been an Incarnation as long as I have.”

Ryland was, after all, deific.

For a second, Mavar froze. With many of his spells now completely gone, he was vastly neutered. A child playing at being a god.

Then he shrieked out in impotent rage and attacked once more. This time, it was purely his Control.

Space he might no longer be able to manipulate, but there was always time. And so much else besides. The pressure of the air, the temperature, the wide variety of particles that inhabited the atmosphere and the matter of the very world he had Inscribed with his Soul Glyph of Unity.

Mavar could control Rhel itself and all it contained, after all.

And so began a constant altercation where Ryland once again bent to deleting away the bits of Mavar’s power that allowed him to use whatever he wished. It felt inefficient at first. Just as with Mavar’s spells, he had to wait until a vector being controlled actually appeared before he could apply the Soul Glyph of Sundering.

Ryland couldn’t attack a singular link. Every time Mavar channelled his will into something or the other, his Incarnation form established a new link of control, one which it repeated when the Demiurge attempted to control that thing again.

So, Ryland had to remain determined. Even as strands of his hair got torn off. Even as bits of him were left cracked and spewing blood like glowing mana. Even if reality itself attempted to delete him several times. He remained focused. He continued concentrating. This wasn’t something he’d be able to stop if he didn’t use the full breadth of not just his knowledge, experience, and power, but his will as well.

The indomitable will that Mavar couldn’t even begin to grasp.

By the time they finally paused, by the time Mavar was forced to stop for the time being, the Demiurge’s form was practically melting. Rivulets of liquid mana, much like the “blood” blooming from Ryland’s “wounds”, were dripping off Mavar, raining down in devastation.

For there was little to nothing left of northern Rhel any longer. Just a hollowed out husk. A crater big enough to fit the former artificial moon and then some, where even the planet’s core now glowed soft and weak. Broken debris floated as gravity itself had been warped, the remnants of Rhel’s atmosphere sparking with residual energy while dark light turned the sky apocalyptic.

Ryland was hopeful the rest of Rhel was at least intact enough to let the last few people who had remained behind to stay safe enough. No doubt, the air had been poisoned, and the quaking had destroyed most architecture. But with magic on their side, they didn’t have to face annihilation.

“I hate you,” Mavar ground out. “I abhor you. I’ve detested you since the moment my people started fawning over you like you were some sort of saviour.”

“I know,” Ryland said. “It hasn’t taken me long to see that. Had your acts been driven by truly noble sentiment, then you wouldn’t have fallen as far as you have.”

“I haven’t fallen at all!” he snapped.

With a shriek, he slammed into Ryland. Depleted though he was of much of his capabilities, he was still an Incarnation in the end. The speed with which he reached Ryland was breathtaking, impossible to note with the naked eye even for Ryland almost. But that was fine.

Ryland didn’t even resist when the collision occurred, setting off a shockwave that broke the last of the northern Rhellian atmosphere.

Mavar pushed him back. They flashed together like a newborn meteor, bursting out of Rhel’s airspace to re-enter the cosmos, the sky turning dark and studded with twinkling stars.

The impact had further destroyed Ryland’s Incarnation form. Cracks now spread across his chest. For the first time in a long while, he could feel this body’s heart start pounding. For the first time in a long time, there might just have been some inkling of danger that he could have taken solace in.

He punched Mavar back.

The Demiurge growled as their momentum stopped. Ryland wasn’t quite certain what his intention had been. In fact, with so much destroyed and broken, he didn’t know what Mavar intended to do if he could even imagine succeeding against and defeating Ryland somehow.

But there was no point in considering any of that. Mavar wasn’t winning.

“Any last words, Mavar?” Ryland asked.

His eyes goggled with apoplectic rage. “How dare you assume that I will lose—”

“[World Node],” Ryland incanted. “[Burned at The Star Stake].”

As soon as Ryland’s spells started going off, Mavar sought to retreat. In the foolishness ensuing from his anger, he had mistakenly shoved himself and his opponent far from all the things he could reliably control. Still, Ryland allowed him to return, while returning himself to Rhel as well.

Except, the whole Realm of Rhel was now covered with tiny pockets of his Soul Glyph of Rift. The entirety of the planet was covered by periodic points that Ryland controlled.

“No!” Mavar shouted. “This much mana, to cover the entire planet…” He twisted around to face Ryland, whose form was quite literally fractured with lines of gold. “You’re breaking apart under the strain. But those cracks…” His eyes widened further. “No, you can’t be…”

“There is nothing surprising here, Mavar,” Ryland said. “I’ve already shown I can pull from the Overrealm when necessary.”

“But to do it so naturally, without any spell, means you’re… you’re ascending like me! How—how long—”

He was interrupted when the rest of Ryland’s spell took effect. Rhel disappeared. Instead, the World Nodes checked if there was any instance of Ryland or Mavar in the points they occupied, before transporting them to the sun itself.

And it wasn’t just their current physical forms either. Every bit of them, every bit of the liquid that had dripped off and the cracked splinters that had flown away.

They had all been transported to the sun.

At this closeness, the heat was excruciating. Even for Incarnations such as them. Where the overuse of their own power had cracked and molten them before, staying on the surface of the sun itself was now vapourizing them away entirely.

Mavar was too shocked by the change to note anything else. Their transportation had taken them too far away from Rhel for him to simply disappear with the help of his Inscriptions, so he was forced to bear the entire brunt of the sun’s devastation. If there had been any actual air, his screams might just have pierced through the roar of the sun fire.

And though Ryland himself had started suffering in much the same way, the last bit of his magic needed to be applied.

A spear of sparking red lightning burst from his hand, taller than mountains, a beam of fulgurous scarlet power that went on and on. Ryland aimed it like a javelin, then speared it through Mavar. In among the furious terror he was already experiencing, one more burst of pain was nothing.

But it was the end, the spear of crimson lightning staking him to the sun they had transported to.

The Demiurge was trying to say something as his consciousness finally pervaded the sheer annihilation Ryland had dragged him through. His words hardly came out, were not at all audible. And Ryland couldn’t help it. If he attempted to use [Oneself World], the sheer might of the sun wouldn’t allow it to last long. Even now, the atmosphere he had come here with was burning away.

But perhaps, since this was the end, Ryland could grant one small mercy. A spell to establish a line of conversation between them, securing the sound waves and barricading them against other forms of energy. “[Unbroken Link].”

“This… is…” Mavar said, his words magically reaching Ryland’s ears. The Demiurge coughed out glowing motes of mana, which combusted into nothingness under the sun’s deadly, endless flare.

“The end,” Ryland said. “I’ve severed your connections to anything and everything. There might come a day, should you survive, when you are able to Inscribe your Soul Glyph into a bit of the sun itself. Where you might slowly start to slow down the tremendous torture this has inflicted upon you. But till that day comes, in centuries, you will suffer for your hubris.”

Mavar shrieked. Even through his suffering, his rage was intense. Almost as hot as the sun itself. Almost. For when he channelled his Incarnation form to control the sun fire, he failed.

Ryland shook his head. It was pointless.

“I… I’ve…” Mavar took a deep fiery breath as he got over his shock. His eyes, now melting away as well, finally landed on Ryland. “The Rhellians…”

Ryland closed his eyes for a moment. “You need not worry for them. They are innocent of your crimes, and as such, will not suffer for them.”

Somehow, at the very end, Mavar had regained a last piece of whatever had allowed him to rule over Rhel for aeons.

Mavar finally fell back. At the same time, the disturbance caused by the lightning spear finally made the sun itself erupt in protest. Ryland rifted away just before the tidal wave of plasma that could have swallowed whole planets reached him.

When the notification of the Grand Frame arrived, he knew this was finally over.
