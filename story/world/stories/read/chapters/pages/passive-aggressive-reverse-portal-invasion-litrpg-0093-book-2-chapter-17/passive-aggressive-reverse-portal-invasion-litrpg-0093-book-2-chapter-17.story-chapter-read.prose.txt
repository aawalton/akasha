The Palace of the Telvan Outpost was, at the same time, both underwhelming and overwhelming. Overwhelming because of the art and the sheer size of it. I’d once done one of those virtual tours of the Sistine Chapel, and this place was both larger and more artistically in-your-face.

The building itself was simple. A perfect circle, entirely empty on the inside, except for a series of spiraling roof supports up high that climbed in an almost Escher-like experience. I felt fairly certain any architects nearby would want to have words with them.

That led to the underwhelming part of it. There was nothing inside. Nothing. No towering thrones, not a single piece of furniture, gold-embossed vase or decorative magical armor. Instead, there were just a number of Hiscariat standing in a perfect circle at the center of the building.

Oh, and the art? Where the outside was all about flowing cloth and colors, here on the inside, the colors were embodied by people. The inside of the walls held floor-to-ceiling portraits of what I assumed were important Hiscariat, dressed in differently colored robes. A stern, patriarchal Hiscariat with a groomed beard and folded tentacles looked imposingly down upon us, right next to a younger-looking female with her tentacles held up triumphantly and a wide smile on her drawn face. Ten colors total. Ten wildly different people, looking down upon us and, for the most part, clearly judging the hell out of us. Not imposing at all!

Mind-Aloft, ignoring any imposing sensations, led us straight to the edge of the circle.

As we approached, the circle automatically shuffled along to expand and leave room for the three of us. Mind-Aloft smiled, and left straight away.

They wasted no time. A female Hiscariat – this one particularly thin and severe-looking – straight across from us inclined her head graciously and spoke straight into our heads. “I am Drift-Ashore. The Hiscariat bids you welcome to the Telvan Outpost. We hope that your stay here will be fruitful, and your collective will form a true part of ours, for however long we may be joined.”

There was ceremony in there. And a shit ton of unspoken knowledge and undertones. What the fuck was I doing here?

Thankfully Steve reacted. “I am Steve Campbell. These are Liam Berg and Victoria Ramírez Méndez. On behalf of humankind, we appreciate the welcome. We also hope that our stay will be fruitful, and that we will find common ground between our species.”

Another mental voice appeared. This one was dry and heavily sarcastic. My gaze made the tour of the circle until I connected it with a short – for their kind, probably still a head taller than me – male with long, trailing braids from his beard who rolled his eyes as he sneered. “Agreed. I suppose it can only get better from here on. Trying to kill somebody as a first impression really lowers the expectations for the rest of us.”

Drift-Ashore closed her eyes, rubbing her temples. “Set-Alight, was it too much to ask of you to be professional for one minute?”

“Professional? I am not the one who panicked and flung half a mountain at a friendly. That’s on our lovely Duty-Abounds here.”

Another male, this one taller and more muscular with a stoic look and short hair, rocked slightly to the side, as if he’d been punched in the side. He snarled, but responded with impressive self-control. “I have stated this before. We need to focus more on training discipline than levels. Need I remind you all, that I voted against putting fresh Mages on the walls before they complied with my requirements?” While his face was impassive, the undertone of his mental message made no secret of just how unhappy he was with that fact. He continued, facing me directly. “On behalf of the defenders of the Telvan Outpost, I bestow upon you our most abject apologies. We erred, horribly, and hope that we may eventually make good on our mistake.”

I blinked. Jesus. Somehow, he even made an apology sound like a challenge. That dude was intense. “Eh. It’s no biggie, mate. What doesn’t kill me, makes me stronger, eh?” I wasn’t even kidding. In a world first for me, that single earthen missile gave me an increase of two to Blunt Resistance.

Set-Alight laughed out loud. “Oh, I think I like you. Want to team up and set fire to a few thousand Ceaseless?”

“We… could be up for that?” That was not how I expected this meeting to go.

“Set-Alight. You overstep yourself and ignore protocol. You do not have the requisite authority to order that. Besides, we should look out for the survival of the outpost first and foremost.” An soft-eyed elderly Hiscariat waved a tentacle at the bearded warmonger.

“Neither do you, Care-of-Ways. I handle military training and confirm when we are ready to launch any major offensives.” The stuffy, muscular dude interrupted the two, punctuating his speech with a frown that looked on the verge of becoming permanent. If he’d stayed with just that comment, the discussion might have remained civil. Instead he added. “Stick to administration. Real planning doesn’t suit you.”

From then on, the debate took a turn for the worse. Well. I’d say for the worse because nothing productive got decided. It was pretty damn entertaining, though. The ten Hiscariat bickered like residents at an old folks home, like they’d done this forever and didn’t expect it to change any time soon. Also, some of them, like Set-Alight, who I started quietly rooting for in my mind, wasn’t above sarcastic sniping or petty name-calling.

It was educational, too. The row showcased beyond a doubt that there was nobody in the city with absolute decision-making power. Even if I couldn’t tell how the actual power was divided, there were clearly some of those present who had less to say than others. That, or the discussion just didn’t touch on their areas of expertise. It was hard to tell.

Oh, it got confusing as hell too. When they spoke one at the time, I could follow along, barely. Their mental voices were different enough that you could tell them apart. But when they sped up, it became one muddled mess in my mind.

One thing I was able to figure out was, that there were several factions in the city.

The cautious and protocol-fixated Care-of-Ways spearheaded the defensive faction. They argued that they should remain defensive, fend off the Ceaseless and grow from the experience they’d gain there until they’d be able to dominate.

Duty-Abounds, watch captain meets gym teacher, did argue for offense. But he wanted everything in its place, everybody prepared to the utmost before embarking.

Drift-Ashore, who I’d at first taken to be in league with Care-of-Ways, turned out to be in favor of expansion. Cautiously tested expansion, with careful consideration of the options and the optimal approach.

Finally, Set-Alight seemed to be a bit of a loner, in that he argued for an attack. As in no-holds-barred, all-men-on-deck attack. He argued, and wasn’t afraid to point out, that they were effectively trapped in here, as long as the Ceaseless were allowed free reign of their surroundings.

I wasn’t entirely sure where we fit in all that. Clearly, the Hiscariat had enough firepower to shake the world. Also their levels, it appeared, were ridiculously high, on average. Apart from a few younger people in the outpost, I’d yet to be able to identify the level a single person, which either meant that they had high Secrets of the Self skills, were higher-leveled than myself, or both.

I was entirely sure of one thing, however. The Hiscariat were not hostile. Pedantic. Prone to tantrums. Very prone to being sidetracked. Yet, in the whole hour-long debate, not a one of the council, or whatever the assembly of people in the Palace were called, even considered the possibility that humankind and the Hiscariat should be enemies. And wasn’t that just a delightful tidbit?

At some point, Victoria started muttering under her breath. Her face went through a series of grimaces and expressions that were more than a little disconcerting.

I was just about to ask if she was having a breakdown, when she spoke up louder.

“Not like that. I think, perhaps-“

“LIKE THIS!” The mental shout burst through my head, shattering every grasp I had on the ongoing discussion.

For a second, the Palace was adrift on a sea of silence. Then the place exploded in mental chatter, punctuated by a few of them shouting out loud in surprise.

After a while, Drift-Ashore took the rein. “Victoria Ramírez Méndez.” Her mental voice pronounced her name perfectly, rolled r’s and all. “Today, you have risen to stand among the people. You have chosen to transcend the form which you were given, and address Ademia with the purest of touches.” The lofty, ceremonial words were momentarily replaced, as she continued wryly. “Maybe your volume could use a bit of work.” She made that clicking sound, before finishing. “Among the Hiscariat, you have earned the right to choose your true name. Consider it well, because it will mark your path for the entirety of your life as a His-Human.” That final mistake displayed beyond a doubt just how often a non-Hiscariat managed the feat.

Speech finished, Drift-Ashore spread both tentacles out wide. Her mental voice sounded weary as she concluded. “This was an enjoyable diversion to our debate. I apologize for the unseemly bickering. This is not something that you should be subjected to. As you can surmise, we are not entirely in agreement about the way to handle our current position.”

“We could-“ I started.

“However.” She interrupted. “The Hiscariat should agree on our approach before approaching any other species about possibly working together.” She looked at Victoria with true warmth behind her tired features. “Especially now that you have proved yourself capable of elevation.”

I bowed my head. Hard to argue with that.

“We welcome you to our new home away from home.” She continued, with an honesty that I didn’t expect, but found winning. “I hope that you will take this chance to get to know our city and our people. Then, we would love to talk to you again on the topic of the future of the Torn Divide, and learn more about humankind, their interests, strengths and goals.”

I’d had my share of foster parents. Most lessons I’d taken to heart from them were unintended. Yet, this one, at least, I’d internalized. ‘If you don’t have anything clever to say, don’t say anything at all.’

We bowed and left the Palace, unsurprised to see Mind-Aloft waiting for us.

She was entirely giddy, questioning Victoria mentally as we walked. “Tell me all about it. Who helped you? How did you figure it out? Are you okay? The breakthrough can be painful for some.”

Victoria rubbed her head, speaking out loud. “Yeah. I do have a minor debuff running for the next four hours. Mental strain. I probably should wait a bit before I try again.”

“Oh yes. Of course. That’s entirely understandable. In fact, I was already taking you to get some privacy and rest. How did you know, though?”

She rubbed her head, scratching the short black fuzz that had replaced her baldness. “Well, I just listened. Like, really paid attention, sabes? At some point, I realized that mental speech wasn’t a muscle as much as a… channel. You know, like a specific pitch.”

“I don’t know what kind of channel that would be.” Mind-Aloft happily buzzed. “Or why your thinking would associate it with rivers. But I have heard others think of it as casting your mind at a specific tone. Me, I think of it as a specific mood. As in, I need to attune my thoughts to that very special mindset, and there we go. But once you’ve rested, it will be a lot easier for you to learn it properly.” Her lip quirked up, and she finished. “Maybe shout a little lower next time. I think all of the Outpost heard you.”

“Did you actually earn a skill for that?” I asked, trying to distract Victoria from the fact that, yes, every single Hiscariat was currently staring at us, even worse than before.

She nodded and smiled.

You have earned a new general skill:

Mental Communication

Active skill

Why strain your voice attempting to make yourself heard, when you have the opportunity and capabilities to communicate right into the mind of the recipient? Every level in Mental Communication will marginally decrease the strain from and improve the control and reach of your mental speech.

“That’s amazing. I honestly think you should try for it too, Steve. I’m… pretty sure that one’s not going to be an option for me.”

Mind-Aloft agreed cheerfully. “Only a few Fighters have unlocked the skill that we know of.” She half-bowed to Steve. “The same applies to Scouts, to be fair. And then, almost always ones who have specialized in either Mental Control or Power. Or Perception, if I remember correctly.”

A while later, we reached our ‘privacy’ and she left us alone to rest and recuperate.

We waited for a bit until Mind-Aloft had left, before resuming our talk. Steve was the first to address the situation. He whispered. “How is this privacy? These people are weird!”

I couldn’t fault him for his comment. Our current resting place? A specific spot of dirt, set under a triangular brown shade sail that shielded us from the onslaught of the sun. Right out in the open of the fortress city, where everybody could continue staring at us. Which they… didn’t do? ”I… wow. What the fuck? Are they just ignoring us now? Sneaking looks at us? Is this privacy because they decide it’s so?”

“This is fucking weird. But hey, if the tall brainy folk have decided that these are no-staring zones? I’ll take it. Still, seems like pinche Pavlov would have an aneurysm if he met these people.”

“Why?” I asked. “Is he a Mage or something?”

Victoria stared at me. Then she shook her head in disbelief. “I think you just made my debuff worse.”

We rested for a while. It seemed like they actually did ignore us entirely under these shade sails, given that none of us got a single upgrade to Secrets of the Self after entering. After half an hour’s peace, we were discussing whether we should go find something to eat.

A Hiscariat paused right outside of the reach of our tent. He spoke out loud, instead of with mental speech. “Greetings. Can I enter your shelter? I believe that you would want to hear what is really happening here in the Telvan Outpost.”
