Chapter Six Hundred and Seven - WakeIt took a few trips to move all of the dust bunnies up and into the Beaver, but that was fine, I wanted the little ones to have time to settle in, and that meant making space for them.Unfortunately, the cargo-side of the ship was right out. There were too many dangerous things there. So, we had to keep them on the passenger side.I bravely donated my room to the dust bunnies, and laid out extra blankets and some of their favourite cushions. I did remove the spade and Weedbane from the room, just in case, but I left my hat collection behind after one of the dust bunnies managed to wear my old kettle helm (with the help of three of his little pals, because it was a smidge heavy for them).The core took longer to load. Amaryllis was very vocal about how dangerous it was to carry something giving off so much magical energy. That was pretty fair -- even just standing by it, I could feel magical energy suffusing the air. There was worry that it might interfere with the gravity engine.We opted to cut into the ship up at the very, very front, right inside the starboard prow. We fabricated a bracket and bracing to keep the core snugly in-place, as far from the rear where more delicate machinery was placed.Moving things around ended up eating a solid hour, and in that time, the keep continued to fall. There was a moment where the drop slowed down significantly, but that only lasted a few minutes. I saw Tristain leaving the keep soon after, looking exhausted, and I suspected that he had just poured a lot of magic into keeping their home afloat a little longer.Some of the black Avatars boarded the Beaver. Just three of them, but still. Mathew was the first aboard, the researcher and magical expert that had been in charge of the core. It only made sense that he'd come with it.As far as I could tell, the core was one of the most precious things that the Black Avatars had, but I also saw them moving some heavy crates around, and I suspected that they had decades, or centuries, worth of high-level magical items and ancient artefacts.A group who travelled the world like they did, righting wrongs and blowing up problems, would definitely end up collecting some weird and dangerous things.I think it was telling that they didn't mind us grabbing the core.The next on board was Percival. The big human man carried a trunk on his back as large as I was. It clunked with every motion, and I had an inkling that it was filled with armour and weapons.I greeted him, explaining (a bit bashfully) that since the Beaver had already been carrying nearly it's maximum number of people, and since we'd added the dust bunnies and the core on top of that, he would need to bunk with some others.He took it well and said that it wouldn't be the first time he slept in a hammock.Finally, our last passenger appeared. Guinevere.I had a suspicion that she would be coming aboard. The fox-lady hopped up onto the deck, carrying a small rucksack alongside her and looking a little less put-together than when I'd last seen her."Hey," I said. "Are you okay?""Never better, Captain Bunch," she said. "Is your ship nearly ready to depart?""Ah, yeah, it should be," I said. "Just a few more minutes to get everything together, and we'll be ready to hit the skies. I might go down and double-check our cargo hold, though, just in case."My Captaining sense was going off a little. It wasn't a warning, but it was more like... like wearing a shirt with a very itchy tag, maybe? It felt like something was off and it bothered me, so I wanted to go down there and give everything a once over, just in case."Are you the last one coming with us?" I asked.Guinevere nodded. "I am. We can depart at your leisure. The others have their places on our own ships."I walked up to the railing, looking down toward the ... remains ... of the Keep. The Avatars were systematically dismantling it as they cleared it out. Probably a third of it had already been cast into the sea."It's such a shame for it to end like this," I sighed.Guinevere laid a hand on my shoulder. "No need to mope. That's basically just a fancy pile of rocks. Don't give it power over you like I did."The narrative has been taken without authorization; if you see it on Amazon, report the incident.I nodded, slowly. "I guess ... I'm a bit more sentimental than I thought."She squeezed my shoulder. "There is nothing wrong with that," she said, her tails swishing behind her. "Do you wish to inspect the core with me? Before we depart?"I nodded, and we headed down into the hull. The core was fixed at the prow, and when we arrived, Awen and Amaryllis were there, standing well back."This thing is dangerous," Amaryllis said. "We're going to end up with mana-poisoning if we don't limit contact with it.""Oh," I said. The hold was usually a pretty dark space, though I kept it clean as a matter of course, so it wasn't mouldy or anything. "Maye we can have the crew sleep on deck? Or in a rotation?""That's not too big of a concern. Honestly, the Beaver is almost perfect for this one use-case," Amaryllis said. "We can have the crew spread across both hulls, one with lower exposure than the other. The wideness of the ship acts in our favour. I think that if we put a plan in place where everyone rotates which hull they're on and spends as much mana as possible at all times, we ought to avoid the worst of it, but that will require some effort."That would be a little tricky, but we could do it. If anything, there might be an upside to it. Mana regeneration was usually pretty slow. A few points a minute or so. I wasn't sure what the exact rate was. What I did know was that being close to a dungeon core created a massive surplus of mana.We could cast spells all day long and still have mana to spare. Heck, it had been enough mana to keep a castle afloat and create a storm around it at the same time. That was... a lot.If we used this right, my friends, the whole crew, might gain a lot of skill with our spellwork.I nodded, then looked at Amaryllis. "Can you do me a favour?" I asked her.Her eyes narrowed a little. "That depends," she said. "What sort of favour?""Can you prepare a lesson plan? Something to teach everyone a few useful spells. All of the crew, the scallywags, everyone. We need to spend a lot of mana, right? Might as well come out of it having mastered a few good spells?""That... actually, that's a surprisingly sensible idea.""Thanks! I try to be surprisingly sensible sometimes."We unhooked the Beaver from the keep a few minutes later.I don't know what it was, exactly, that I felt. Maybe some sort of melancholy? Empathetic melancholy. The Black Keep wasn't my home, but it was someone's home and it was a spectacular place. Now it was consigned to the bottom of the sea.Would it be lost forever?I couldn't imagine anyone being willing or able to retrieve it. Maybe in the far, far future, some archeologists would stumble upon it and wonder how it had ended up where it did, but otherwise, it was likely that the entire magical thing would be written off as nothing more than a passing myth.We could have left, but I chose to have us hover around. Natural rain clouds joined the last remnants of the storm, and we watched as the Black Avatars boarded their ships and took to the skies.Without anyone keeping the magic fed, the spells keeping the fortress afloat slowly weakened and gave out, and just like that, the Black Keep crashed into the waves of the Moonswept Sea and was swallowed up.It felt like we should've been at a funeral. In spite of Guinevere's words, I may've even been sniffling a little.Caprica spoke up, her voice solemn. "Bastion, let your report state that in the year 759, on the 5th of January, in the skies over the Moonstruck Sea west of Elkensteel, the Black Avatars' Keep came under attack. The enemy was destroyed, and all souls escaped unscathed, but by midafternoon, the Keep--a priceless example of magical engineering and historical import--was lost to the waves.""It will be done," Bastion said.I wiped my eyes. "Amaryllis, put that in the ship's log, too. And... and try to pin down the location better."My birdy friend nodded.The three avatars aboard the Beaver were silent, gazing at the churning waters with stony expressions."We'll get her for this," Mathew said."W-who?" I asked."The woman behind the Evil Roots. We'll see if she enjoys seeing her home drown. She made herself an enemy of the World, and now us as well."***
