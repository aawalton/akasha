Cellar-pests job day. We dressed for the occasion, meaning that Nadia was wearing her usual tactical-looking clothes she’d bought at a second-hand shop for our outings—and which made her look absolutely cute in that sharp, military sort of way. And I, on the other hand, was wearing stuff I wouldn’t be opposed to burning in case it got absolutely soaked with blood which, given the job description, was probably going to happen even if I tried my best to be careful.

Actually, it should not happen, I thought. The cellar pests we were about to hunt were like mutated, big rats that had swarmed into a tourist kitchen’s cellar through a busted sewer connection. Not dangerous by themselves, but according to the job description the cellar was crawling with them.

And thus, the plan was to “NEVER go inside unless time is stopped and you have plenty of juice left,” as Nadia put it.

We had measured the exact length of my time-stop and had bought a stopwatch I could carry with me to make sure I didn’t get caught misjudging time. If things went according to plan, any blood splashes should happen with me safe and chill on the other side of a door.

When we got there, after having picked up Vera at the CRC, the owner of the place was beyond ecstatic.

“Finally, someone is taking my jobs,” he said in a heavy accent. He wore all-white, like he was a chef working in the restaurant, which was impossible given the wooden board barring the window and the desolate state of the place inside.

Perhaps he owned other places?

His belly was massive, and he was a bit on the short side, but he had that Mediterranean look and savoir-faire that made him instantly likable.

“That’s right,” I said, introducing myself. “Adrian Hale, new D-tier hero reporting for duty.”

I had a tier now, since the Civic Response Center had started rolling out the letter tiers and my file was assigned a D.

“Good, good,” he said, shaking my hand nervously. “I am so beyond glad your talented and strategic manager suggested this job to you and that you had the foresight and insight and intelligence to pick it. Now come, come!”

He led us inside, with Nadia shooting me an amused look at his antics while Vera was blinking as if she’d just swallowed something that tasted much differently than what she expected. The cellar was at the bottom of a perfectly clean staircase, bare concrete painted white and only slightly smelling of mold.

“What’s a heavy steel door like that doing in a restaurant?” Nadia asked.

“It’s a temperature-sealed door,” the owner said. “The cellar is, well, used to be a massive freezer.”

“Did you serve frozen meals?” Vera asked. “So the kitchen, it was just to heat them up?”

“That would be right, yes,” the man said. “Cheap food for stingy tourists. Although I never skimp on quality or hygiene, of course. The food comes from my other restaurants, cooked in batches and then frozen as single portions. Come on, you can’t tell me you have never heard of Antonio’s Quick Kitchen?”

I shrugged. “Can’t say I have.”

The girls also shook their heads. “As for the hygiene…” Nadia began. “How did the rats even get in?”

“Ah,” the man said. “It was terrible. One day we heard a commotion from downstairs, went there and saw creatures swarming everywhere. Everything upside down! Food pallets open and leaking everywhere. Them munching on the frozen stuffs!”

“Is it still sub-zero there?” she asked. “Important to know before Adrian goes in.”

“Ah, no,” the man said. “It costs a lot to keep the system running, you know? And it wasn’t keeping the rats at bay either, so it was useless.”

“Alright,” I said, taking position near the door. The single incandescent light above provided warm tones to the bumpy white walls, paint over paint over paint that had stratified into strange relief patterns.

The space at the landing was cramped, but enough for the door to swing open if the girls crowded the far side and the man went up a step or two on the stairs. I had them move there, took out my stopwatch, and nodded.

Time warped. I clicked the stopwatch and saw the numbers tick up at their normal rate. It was everything else that was perfectly still. The two girls and the restaurant’s owner were now statues, and the smell of mold was replaced by the flatness of time-stop’s air. The hum of the light above was gone.

I opened the door and peeked inside, using a flashlight. Together with the stopwatch, and the axe I brought for the job, it completed today’s set of work tools.

I hummed and took in the situation. I walked among the frozen monstrous creatures, rats reaching all the way to my knees, easily one and a half meters long. The place was a mess, a disgusting rotted mess, and I was glad the smell didn’t reach me. The stopwatch reached 15 seconds, beeped, and I turned around and left, closing the door behind me.

When time resumed, the others all flinched at the sudden sounds echoing in the room.

“What was that?” the owner asked, alarmed. The smell of his sweat hit me, and I saw him dab the sides of his neck with a handkerchief.

“Frozen sound waves suddenly unfreezing,” Nadia said before turning to me. “Situation?”

I briefed her and Nadia about what I saw. The verdict was: doable, with caution. I’d go in, kill a few rats with the axe, and get out before the time stop ended, then wait outside for it to recharge.

The owner stuck around for the first two rounds, then told us he had stuff to do and left. Vera stopped him, made him sign an NDA, properly intimidated him about disclosing what he’d seen here and told him we’d call him when we were done so he could sign the rest of the paperwork.

“So,” Nadia began. She was sitting on the second to last step of the stairs. “Was intimidating the man part of the proper CRC procedure or is our manager feeling a bit protective over Adrian’s power?”

Vera shrugged. She was sitting on a stool, back against the wall in an attempt to relax while we waited for my power to come back online. “What if I were a bit protective?” she asked after a moment of silence.

“I’d say I approve,” Nadia said, massaging her temples. Yup, the mind privacy gear still hurt like hell after a while wearing it, and we were not getting used to it even after weeks of using it. “Adrian, love, how did it go with the stopwatch?”

“Very well,” I said. “Although maybe five seconds are too many. I think I can get out in three.”

“From the far end of the room, after swinging your axe at rats like a maniac?” she asked, cocking an eyebrow.

I’d done it twice and it hadn’t winded me to the point I couldn’t make it back. “I think so.”

“That’s going to be a no, dear,” she said, smiling. “I’d rather wait for you to do one or two more rounds than have you risk it just to get one more kill in. Deal?”

“Alright,” I conceded.

My power came back online, so I went in and out as planned. Killing the rats took two, sometimes three blows, which meant that I couldn’t do more than a few at a time, even while rushing. And I couldn’t rush too much now that the ground was literally flooded with their blood. It seemed like Nadia’s prediction was going to be correct, as I could already spot several bloodstains on my clothes.

I told her that, and she was very smug about it. “It was Vera’s idea, actually,” she said.

“Really?” I asked. “Well, thank you. I still feel bad about throwing away perfectly good clothes, but at least they barely even cost anything.”

“Burn’em,” Vera said, a rare show of passion as she glared at the stains. “What. They are second-hand, cheap, damaged, worn by who knows who, and now coated in filthy blood? Yeah, burnable, Adrian. I don’t feel bad for the environment. Not that it matters nowadays.”

“Right. The world kinda does whatever it wants, doesn’t it? Hey, how about we go outside to get some fresh air while we wait. It stinks here,” I offered.

We came back into the winter sunlight and basked in it for a while. It felt nice, the dual sensation of biting cold and warm morning sun. Above us, a shadow passed that wasn’t a bird or a plane.

“That must be Birdcall,” Vera said, annoyed. I guess it was a sort of bird, then, just a human one. “And he’s once again flying too low.”

“Are there regulations for flying Supes or something?” Nadia asked.

“I mean, yeah. There are regulations for everything. What matters is which regulation gets enforced. And flight restrictions are hard to impose on Supes.”

“Would you regulate me if I could fly?” I asked.

Nadia shot me a look.

“Theoretically,” I added.

“Well,” Vera said, thinking about it. She glanced at Nadia then shook her head. “Theoretically I should, yeah, but practically I probably would not.”

“Good answer,” Nadia said.

“Come on, let’s go back inside. Adrian’s time is almost up,” Vera said.

“Hey!” I protested.

Nadia chuckled. “You make it sound terminal.”

“Then let’s say,” our manager teased. “It’s time for you to go back inside the rotting room of mutated rats and chop them down and bathe in their entrails. Better?”

I didn’t bother to answer that. But I gave her a look that told her what I felt, and she grinned. Inwards, I tried to figure out when our relationship had evolved to teasing like this, and realized it was kind of blurry. Perhaps when we started inviting Vera over at our place semi-regularly?

I decided I didn’t care. More worryingly, I think I liked it.

In and out, plus three rats killed. Twenty-one point seven seconds, according to the stopwatch. I ran exactly 1.7 seconds too late and the girls noticed, because they saw the door close rather than seeing nothing but hearing the sound all at once when the time stop ended. They said nothing, mostly because I beat them to it, admitting my faults and promising never to do it again.

And then it was time to wait some more. Silence descended upon us, and I found myself unconsciously fiddling with my house key, taking it out from under the shirt and tugging at its chain around my neck. It was either that or doomscrolling in front of two hot women, one of whom was my girlfriend. I chose to fidget.

I saw Vera looking at the key, but it wasn’t like she hadn’t seen it before. She knew it was there, and what it was, and there was nobody else around anyway.

“So,” she said. “Heist as your next job?”

Nadia blinked at her. “Heist?”

“Right,” Vera said, hiding a chuckle. “Snatch-and-grab, as it were.”

“Adrian?”

“Sure,” I said. “Suspiciously lucrative jobs, my favorite. But if you said you checked and it’s clean…”

“I did check and I did say it.” She scribbled something down on her phone, then waited for a notification and nodded. “All done. And another thing. The security staff at the winter market said you are better than almost all of the Supes we send their way. I wonder why, given that most people hate these sorts of jobs. Say, did you also hate it?”

“No. It was actually rather relaxing.”

“Good!” she said, beaming. “Because here I have a standing contract to have you on-shift there weekly till March.”

“Oh my god!” Nadia cried.

“What?”

I was already hefting my axe.

“STABLE INCOME????”

I… I wanted to say something but I just laughed. We all did, and I took the job of course.

“How about beginning tonight, then?”

I nodded. Nadia took my hand as I did, no doubt recalling our chat a couple nights ago, and squeezed it. She nodded back.

When my power came back online, I tucked the key under my shirt where it belonged and went back to slaughtering.
