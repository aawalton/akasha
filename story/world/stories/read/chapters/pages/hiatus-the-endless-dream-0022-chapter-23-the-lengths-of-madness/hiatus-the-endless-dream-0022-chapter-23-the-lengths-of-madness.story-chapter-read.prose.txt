Staring down the barrel of a gun was more frightening than I could've imagined. It seemed like nothing would ever really go exactly to plan. I wanted to gloat, and with all the monsters and creeping cornfields, I had forgotten that guns existed. It was fine. I just needed to flip the switch, and unless he shot me in the head right away, I could do it.

My grin grew impossibly wide as I spoke, "You know, I thought you'd be taller."

The man in the lab coat's face darkened, and for the longest moment, neither of us said a word. I almost let a scowl touch my face. Luckily, the other man broke first and, with barely hidden disdain, spoke. "You seem awfully confident for one with no options."

I internally pumped my fist and silently thanked him for the opportunity to talk down to the him. After all I had gone through here, after all the bullshit and reading his disgusting notes, I wanted to take him down a peg.

I lazily waved my hand. "Oh, that. I wouldn't worry about it." It was a relief to be honest. The outcome was set in something even stronger than stone.

That seemed to rile him up as I saw his gun arm struggle not to lift again. "I've never met someone so delusional before."

"Out of curiosity," I interjected before he could go on a tirade, "Did you go to your house? If you did, did you open the oven?"

I saw a distinct lack of understanding in his eyes, so I just sighed. "That's why I feel like you're not a real scientist. You have no real curiosity. Like, 'why does my house smell like gasoline?' " I pointed to the window.

Hesitantly, he stepped out of the shed. He turned to look at his house and the pistol fell to his side.

I took the moment to stand and, in a swift motion, yanked the switch down, which resounded with a satisfying, deep 'shhhunk.' Power was back in the barn now, and an oil-soaked sheet would be igniting soon; that fire would then crawl along until it reached the second and final gas jug, but the madman didn't know that. I wanted to keep it that way until I was sure the fire took root. There was always the possibility... No. If you consider losing you can never win.

Now standing, I jerked my thumb at the window. "Sorry about your house."

Just like that the two most important locations in the Instance would be up in flames. I hadn't known exactly what the timeline would be when I left for the barn, so I had put the oven on a thirty minute timer when I slotted the gas canister in there. I kept the lid on which, combined with the heat, should've caused the pressure to build until it was too much. The explosion wouldn't be huge, but it would be enough to start a fire, and more importantly, it would piss the scientist off.

A snarl grew on the man's face, their skin growing red, and I could practically see the heat radiating off their skin. It had worked then, on both counts. Good.

He took a deep breathe in an attempt to steady himself. "You insist on fucking with me. Even at the end of your miserable little life? I'll have the Homo Fertili feed on you for years to come. Forget using you as material, you'll never see the light of day."

I shook my head, trying to convey as much mock disapproval as I could. "You still haven't realized that you've lost. There are no more of your stupidly named pets. I fucking killed them." Anger crept into my voice and took over. This isn't how I wanted it to go. I wanted him to be a puddle of tears and begging.

At that moment, what sounded like a rush of air and a building inferno sounded from the barn proper and I let go of a breathe I didn't know I was holding. I saw the scientist's eyes turn wide as he twisted his head away. I doubted the impatient man would notice anything. The fire just started.

He whipped back around, leveling the gun at me, clearly having expected me to rush forward, but I didn't. There was no need.

"That was your plan," he barked out, spittle launched across the shed. "Set the barn on fire and make the call as you're getting burnt? Did you just hope I wasn't going to chase you anyway? Did it not occur that I could just shoot you and watch as you immolate yourself? This? This is what the other Visitors died to? No wonder. A dullard dying to an idiot."

I laughed. I laughed in the man's face, all the frustration at the Instance worming its way into my voice. I remembered how it felt to get my skin peeled back in Kay's body. "So?" I spat out.

The pained, strangled sounds of yips crackled from the barn, and the sounds of roaring air grew louder.

"So. SO?" The man's voice was practically a scream at this point, "You've lost! You burned down your only chance to win and now I'll use you to replace what you took from me."

I tried to meet his gaze, but his eyes were wild. "You keep saying shit, and you still haven't gotten it through that skull of yours." Our eyes finally met. "I do not give a damn about what you or your Instance holder set as winning."

I continued laughing at him, "You think I was trying to make a phone call? If that's what I was trying, I would've been gone before Maggie even reached the barn. Long gone, but no. I wanted to make sure everything you've ever built was cinders before I killed the traitors and then you. Right now, I'm three for three. Let's make it an even four, shall we?"

The man seemed stunned for a moment, but finally found his words. "You're an idiot. This is just a setback. Nothing more. In a few weeks I'll be back where I started and you will have done nothing and wasted a precious reward. The Instance Holder will remake my house, and my notes as easy as it is to breathe, and I'll be back to my work and you'll be the first Visitor that I cut open." He thrust the gun forward, taking careful aim, but his finger was still off the trigger. I watched it intently, heart hammering in my chest.

He really could just shoot me here, and I would be ejected from the Instance only having accomplished three-fourths of my goals. That didn't sit right with me. I nodded, finally acknowledging the man's words. I had considered that giving up on the chance to become stronger for petty revenge seemed silly, but yet I am only human. "Right, sure, but answer me this. How soon can a Visitor rejoin the Instance?" His answer really could dictate if I went for Peeler, which I had hidden by the door, or go with my other plan.

"Hardly matters," he flicked the safety of the gun off, and I mentally cursed myself. I might've been able to charge him if I had known the safety was still on. He retrieved a length of cord and tossed it at my feet. "Tie yourself up."

I sighed and knelt down next to the rope. In my desperation to survive in the madman's house, I had taken two syringes from his basement, one I had used to meld with the werewolf—the other was waiting in my sleeve. I let the needle fall and land on my fingertips. The metal pricked my fingers, and it was all the self-control I had to stop my face from twitching in pain. My heart thrummed, and I imagined some of the cursed liquid reacting with my fingers.

Gesturing with my head towards the monstrous half-arm half-leg, I spoke, "This thing doesn't exactly move easily, or tie knots well. In case you didn't notice."

"Take a few steps forward, turn around, kneel, and put your hand behind your head," he commanded, shifting the gun from my head to my legs. His intent was clear, and his command irked me greatly.

Patience. I needed just a little more patience. It'd be worth it.

I kept the syringe cupped in my hands, hidden from view, and did as commanded. "Happy?" I asked.

He narrowed his eyes at me, "Why are you complying now?"

Great. For all the times for the man to grow a brain. I had thought I could skip over some of the lying for once.

I shrugged, "Because this won't stop me from killing you. Hell, I could do it with one hand."

The man scoffed, and I could feel him approaching through the hairs on the half-leg. I felt his gun lower as he reached for another set of ropes by his side, so I knew exactly when to strike.

I twisted, punching out with the syringe into his chest as deep as I could. I half stood, stepping into him to carry us both off balance. His gun flew off in between a set of crates. He was falling sideways, which would no doubt cause the syringe to come loose and shatter. I wrapped an arm around him and sank lower, balancing it so he would fall on his back instead. With one arm pinned underneath the man and the other effectively useless at fine motor skills, I had no choice but to push the syringe head down with my teeth.

The scientist flailed as pain began shooting through him.

"What did you do! WHAT DID YOU DO?!" he seemed to have lost all trace of me in his haze of panic.

"I won." I roared through a laugh of utter triumph. "You hear me. I WIN!". I untangled myself and rushed towards Peeler in a fit of euphoria. The man began to claw at his chest, as though if he were fast enough he'd be able to expel the chemicals already coursing through his system. The space just behind his ribs, where the lungs should've been, started to bulge out, splitting into three forearm-length feelers, trying to find anything organic to latch onto.

I grabbed my crowbar and rounded on the thrashing man. I swung down, aiming to put the tip through his teeth. His head bounced off the concrete of the shed in a bloodied haze of a shattered and frothing mouth. He was just dazed enough that I could use my feet to kick him onto his stomach. The feelers tried to reach out for me, but I didn't let my leg linger.

I proceeded to grab the madman by the collar and walk him towards the shed wall. "The thing is, unlike you I am curious. Curious by nature. Did you know that the organic material used as the base for combination doesn't need to be alive? It's how I got the fucked up arm. No. Of course you did. Let me ask you a better question. Did you know wood is organic?"

His hands clawed weakly at my grip. He tried to say something, but only blood and teeth fell from his mouth. I imagined it was begging before I shoved him into the wooden wall and watched as the tendrils latched onto the wood with glee. They pulled the rest of the man forward, breaking his body against the shed. It was as if a ghost was attempting to snatch him through. His neck bent at a dangerous angle. He yelled, kicked, and then a sickening crack begat a lolling skull. Even still, neck broken, the body moved, the hands pushed against splintered beams. The body tried to bring its knees up to its midsection to push, to gain distance, but it was all in vain.

The shed began to ripple and dance. The man's body and the small building – the barrier between the two – wavered. The windows started shaking, the door hummed. I saw the life drain from the man's body in equal parts to how many mouths of splintered and shattered teeth were forming on the walls. The eyes formed next, wide and bloodshot to the point I couldn't even see his original eye color, and that was my cue to leave.

I rushed out of the shed and watched the rest from a safe distance. By some twisted miracle, lungs must have formed in the amalgam, causing the creatures screams to resound through the night. They turned into wails that never stopped, and then—when the barn was an inferno and the flakes of embers leapt from one building to the other—their voices redoubled in volume, causing me to grip the sides of my head in pain despite the distance.

I hadn't seen my own transformation, but seeing this process from the outside, I didn't regret my decision to kill this man one bit.

Nate. Maggie. Those were a necessity. I didn't feel good about them. Especially how Maggie went out. This one. This one, though. My hands were steady, and I was smiling.

I continued to watch, between the house and barn, both large bonfires by now. The timer on [Stalker] ticked down and down until it reached 0.

I hoped it would just expel me. I really didn't want to kill myself.Everything was taken care of. My plans worked. I had won.My chest burned, and I couldn't feel a single thing.I began to laugh, the laugh turned into a yell, the yell to a cry, the cry to a sob, then back to a scream as I fell to the ground clutching Peeler.

I still had another Instance after this one.

Eventually, the moonlit sky began to fade, replaced by a marmalade-painted twilight. The sparse fields around me waved from side to side and out of existence, receding into a parking lot with no cars. All around me were the stunned figures of people. Some were already up and about, checking on others or calling out names in a panic.
