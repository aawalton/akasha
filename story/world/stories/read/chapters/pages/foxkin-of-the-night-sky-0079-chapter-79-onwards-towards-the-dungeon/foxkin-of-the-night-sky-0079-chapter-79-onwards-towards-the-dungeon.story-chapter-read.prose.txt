Thankfully, it only takes about twenty minutes for the gaping hole in my thigh to stop being a structural concern.

The flesh knits itself back together with quiet efficiency, the worst of the damage fading as if it had the decency to be embarrassed by its own existence. What remains is an aesthetically displeasing scar, jagged, uneven, and entirely unacceptable.

A quick glance confirms that Pride is already addressing the issue, smoothing the edges and pulling everything back toward my ideal with patient, inevitable correction.

Highly convenient.

My tail sways in quiet approval.

The motion is somewhat compromised by my current position—flat on my back in what used to be a forest, tail loosely wrapped around my waist like a very judgmental belt—but the sentiment remains intact.

Tragically, now that my wounds are healed and my resources are restored, I no longer have an excuse to remain horizontal.

Deeply.

Something about ranking dramatic landing techniques by joint damage.

The higher-tier ones are apparently the ones that do more damage.

Mildly concerning.

“Alright, I am going to go bully the monsters in that dungeon now.” I pause. “…Also, the no knee-bend landing should be moved up to A-tier at minimum. I know from experience.”

There’s a brief moment of silence—

Then a flood of responses.

A handful of genuine goodbyes, an image of a very serious-looking pigeon saluting me from the stars, and at least two separate voices dramatically collapsing into tears, begging me to stay like I’m about to vanish forever instead of walking a few hundred kilometers away.

I roll my eyes.

Then return the salute to the pigeon.

Professional courtesy.

My Garden folds shut as I let the tear in reality seal itself, the noise cutting off cleanly.

Peace at last.

I pull up my status menu and skim through it, isolating the base values with a practiced glance.

Twenty-three free stats per level.

I consider it for a moment.

Then nod.

Eight into Vitality. Every level.

The rest into Strength until I hit the next Milestone.

Dexterity and Perception can continue to spiral out of control on their own. My class and race seem more than happy to handle that for me.

I distribute the points accordingly.

On schedule.

Convenient.

I break into a run again, slipping back into motion with practiced ease as I weave through the forest. Sometimes I stay low, darting between trunks and roots; other times I simply jump, clearing the canopy entirely and skimming above it in long, controlled arcs before dropping back down.

The first stretch is quiet, almost peaceful, but that doesn’t last long. Gradually, the forest begins to change. The density thins in places, and faint paths begin to appear, eventually sharpening into proper roads that cut cleanly through the greenery.

Civilization.

At one point, I catch sight of a caravan and slow just enough to observe it properly.

It is… not what I expected.

There are wagons—technically—but calling them that feels misleading. Each one is closer to a small building mounted on reinforced wheels, large enough to comfortably house multiple people without feeling cramped.

It’s less a caravan and more a collection of mobile architecture.

More importantly, there isn’t a single horse in sight.

Instead, the entire convoy is pulled by massive, heavily built lizard-like creatures. They are thick-scaled, low to the ground, and built like they could drag a mountain if properly motivated.

Inspect identifies them as Ironhide Lizards, which feels entirely appropriate.

They’re also fast.

Very fast.

Nine out of ten.

Points deducted for not letting me ride one.

I pick up speed again, continuing toward my destination. As I move, I begin to pass more people, though most don’t even register my presence before I’m already gone. One moment I exist, the next I don’t. At best they spot a pale blur at the edge of perception.

Some do notice, though.

A grizzled Dogkin overseeing what are clearly new adventurers tracks me with sharp, practiced awareness.

A tired party returning from a hunt, dragging a makeshift wagon piled high with monster parts, their exhaustion visible even from a distance.

A massive Bearkin in full plate carrying a greatsword on his back that looks more like a structural beam than a weapon.

Tier two, mostly. One exception, a Catkin healer still at tier one, tucked safely within the group. Their classes are… acceptable. Rare at absolute best, nothing particularly noteworthy.

Tragic.

Briefly.

Free money.

Charming.
