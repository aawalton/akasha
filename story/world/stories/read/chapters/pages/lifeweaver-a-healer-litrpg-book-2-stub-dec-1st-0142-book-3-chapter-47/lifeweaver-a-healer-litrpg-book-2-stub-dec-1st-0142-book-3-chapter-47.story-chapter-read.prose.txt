Weaver's Perception found something interesting with the patterns along Hannah's arms, and the information trickled into Luke, who saw mana pulsing through them, almost as if they were another set of mana channels. It was similar, but at the same time, different. While it was mana working its way through the lines, it looked nothing like the regular, blue-tinted stuff he was used to. A darkness mingled with it, turning the essence darker, almost black like a shadow. Mixing with Hannah's own mana, it turned into a grayish mess that soon faded back into the regular blue.

"Shadow mana?" Luke mumbled to himself.

Ray walked up, wiping foul-smelling pus and ichor off his shirt. "What's up with the tattoos, Hannah? You look like an early aughts tough guy."

She shrugged, tracing a thick line going from her wrist to her elbow as it faded. "I'm not sure, but it made me feel strong, uh, eating that monster. Got a lot of experience from it too."

"That's," Luke cleared his throat. "Interesting."

"Dweller?" Curtis asked.

Hannah looked away, her cheeks reddening. "Nope. All me."

"Huh," Curtis said, turning toward where they'd find the boss. "Let's just go. I want to leave this dungeon ASAP. Another Intruder might show up."

"Could you give me a moment to prepare?" Lemira asked.

Curtis worked his jaw, his thoughts on the matter poorly hidden.

"Sure," Luke said, giving Curtis a look. "Of course."

Just like at the train station, Lemira sat down with the monster core held in front of her, eyes closed, and brow furrowed in concentration. Mana swirled through the core for a moment, and the innermost middle of it pulsed with white light, growing stronger by the second. Soon, she brought out the second orb and worked the two together. That light moved from one to the other and back again. Luke's eyes widened as the two cores began to merge. Pushed together, they disappeared into one another, the light growing strong enough that he had to look away for a moment. When his gaze returned to her working, it was a single core. Rather than becoming larger, it had condensed into a sphere the size of a bead.

"Is it vibrating?" Elijah asked, taking a tentative step toward Lemira.

"Humming," she said, wiping sweat off her brow. "The energy within is too great for the core to withstand."

Elijah stopped in his tracks and looked as if he contemplated backing away. "Is it dangerous?"

She stood, holding the small core in the palm of her hand, and smiled. "Oh, yes. Let us hurry to claim the King as well before it's too late."

"Too late for what?" Ray asked.

Lemira pointed to the thicket and started walking.

Ray raised his voice. "Too late for what, lady?!"

When she didn't answer this time either, he turned to Luke. "It's going to blow up, isn't it?"

Luke just chuckled and followed Lemira. Who was he to ruin her first attempt at teasing Ray? By all accounts, she had been successful, too.

The sound of the boss reached them before they even emerged from the small thicket.

"It's... snoring?" Curtis asked, his voice a low whisper.

"No way it slept through all its friends taking a beating like that," Ray murmured, shaking his head as he peered out from behind a thin, gnarled trunk. "Well, shit. It is sleeping."

The King of the Steppes rested on a small rise in the dirt, its head on its massive paws. Wind stirred the red-brown mane, but it didn't move. Slow, deep breaths made its flank move. As far as Luke could tell, it was indeed taking a nap. Good thing, too, because this lion was enormous.

When visiting a zoo as a kid, he'd stood mere feet away from the metal bars separating him from a male lion. Pictures don't do them justice, at least not regarding their size. You might think they're like large dogs. Height-wise, that might be somewhat true, at least if you compare them to the largest dog breeds at around four feet, but if you go by sheer mass, there's just no comparing the two. A lion's bulk is on an altogether different level. That's with a normal lion, and this was beyond what anyone could describe as normal. Even lying down, Luke could tell that much. Length-wise, a normal lion measures up to seven feet, which is just bonkers, but this one was twice that, if not more. Insane was the word for it, just insane.

While the lionesses were infused with different elements, this just looked like a lion. A massive, monstrous lion.

"Let me take it," Ray said.

"No," Luke said, shaking his head. "Remember the lioness? It knew you were coming, even with the stealth."

"I don't plan to use stealth."

Curtis shuffled awkwardly past a bush to reach them, whispering to not disturb the sleeping giant in the near distance. "Isn't stealth your whole thing?"

"At the start, yes, but I've trained a lot to fight in quick bursts. I'm not a one-hit wonder anymore." Ray must've seen the doubt on Luke's face, because when he spoke again, his tone had taken on a pleading character. "Let me do this. And hey, if it destroys me, you can just sew the pieces back together, right?"

Before Luke had a chance to answer, Ray stood and straightened to his full height, striding out of their lackluster hiding spot.

"Will he be alright?" Curtis asked.

Luke watched Ray go. "Guess we'll see. Be ready to jump in, I guess."

"Yeah," Curtis said, and together they stepped out of the brush, joined by the rest of their party.

Elijah looked like he wanted to join Ray, but held himself back while Hannah just watched with a wry smile. Lemira's focus was fixed on the boss, and it seemed she hadn't even noticed Ray walking up to it by himself.

Having crossed half the distance to The King, Ray disappeared into stealth. So much for a new strategy. A few seconds later, a shudder went through the lion, and it tilted its head a little, opening an enormous, yellow eye. Like the lioness, it turned to look over its shoulder and swiped at the air.

Ray appeared as the claws ripped into him, tearing deep grooves in his chest and throat. Blood fountained out with a gushing force that would mean death in mere seconds.

Elijah gasped and shifted his weight, about to break into a run.

"No," Luke said.

"What?"

Luke nodded toward the fight. "It's not him. Watch."

The decoy, or whatever you wanted to call it, slammed into the ground, kicking up dirt. Even as the lion stood to examine its victim, Ray appeared by its left hind leg, dragging the tip of his dagger across the monster's hide with little pressure, almost like he wanted to tickle it rather than do any real damage.

Disappearing into stealth again, he moved under the lion's belly as it rose to meet this pesky intruder. Luke saw it all through Weaver's Perception. Even in stealth, Ray's heart beat and mana swirled through his mana channels, appearing before Luke's senses. That was how he'd seen the fakeout, too, and known to hold Elijah back. If Ray wanted a fight for himself, he'd get one.

The Lion bellowed and lunged to the side, wanting to create distance between itself and the Assassin, but Luke saw it hadn't worked. It was weird. When the lion moved, Ray followed, as if he were connected to the monster somehow, and it allowed for another lackluster scratch of his opponent's side. The wounds were bleeding a little, but didn't look deep enough to do real damage.

Increasing its already impressive speed, the lion lashed out with a paw large enough to crush Ray's entire body. Luke winced as it connected to the still invisible Assassin, but then all he could do was frown. The blow had gone right through, and Ray followed the foreleg, again looking like he was connected to it. Another wound appeared, then another.

The King let out a mighty roar, and Luke froze for a brief moment, along with the rest of the party. Even at that distance, the skill had been enough to affect them. Ray, too, had felt the effect, and he appeared out of stealth, unmoving. Rather than play with its victim, the lion opened its massive jaws and crunched down on Ray, only the head and feet visible once the maw closed.

Hannah let out a short shriek as Ray's head thudded to the ground, but Luke shook his head, unsure of what he was seeing. "Not him. Again."

"Like Dirk?" Curtis asked.

Luke shook his head as Ray appeared by the lion's flank again. This time, he buried the dagger to the hilt before dancing back from a quick retort from the monster.

"Not like Dirk. Looks the same, but it's not."

Two Ray corpses bled in the dirt, but the real one moved like a viper, darting in and out, striking the boss and dodging back and away from any retaliation. Growing frustrated, the lion kicked and lashed with its tail, but Ray flowed along its body, slicing into it again and again. The movements weren't fast. Well, sure, they were fast, but not so fast that this boss shouldn't be able to keep up. There was no wasted effort, and Ray didn't even seem to need his feet while navigating his target's body. It was almost as if he swam in a river that was the boss, navigating the water like a fish. A fish with stingers.

No single attack did meaningful damage on its own, not even the ones where he buried the dagger to its hilt, and truth be told, they didn't seem to amount to much even put together. The lion's attacks were fierce and without mercy. If a single one struck Ray, Luke wasn't sure there would be much left to put back together.

After a while, though, the boss slowed. It was imperceptible at first, but soon became an undeniable fact. The monster didn't look tired or like it was flagging. But, despite its bulging muscles, it was as if it waded through mud, unable to push itself forward.

Ray was breathing hard. Sweat poured down his face, and his hair looked like it needed a good wash. Despite it all, he had a huge smile on his face when he stopped and pointed at the now prone lion. "See? That's what you get! I'm the King now, bitch!"

He sauntered up to the downed lion and leapt back a step, laughing, when it made one final attempt to crush Ray between its pointed teeth. Taking his time about it, Ray put the dagger to the lion's neck.

"Don't kill it!" Lemira shouted.

Ray blinked, then frowned. "Right. Forgot."

Never turning his back on the monster, Ray rejoined the rest of the group, and Lemira took his place by the lion's side.

"Poison?" Curtis asked.

"Paralyzing agent. Made it myself," Ray said, pointing to the lion's hind legs. "A few of the stabs were a different kind of poison. Makes it so whoever I cut can't cast spells."

"It didn't cast any spells," Elijah said.

Ray nodded. "Exactly. It would have if it could. Pretty sure it would, anyway. Since, you know, the girl lions were spellcasters."

"Not bad," Luke said, giving Ray a nod of approval.

"Right?" Ray said. "Who knew brewing potions would be worthwhile?"

It took Lemira the better part of an hour to extract its core. After that, she needed even more time to work on it, together with the ones she'd combined earlier.

The group eyed the countdown while she worked, but it turned out they were able to get out with plenty of time to spare. Lemira beamed, talking non-stop about what a remarkable pet they'd helped her acquire. All in all, it hadn't been a bad dungeon experience. Not at all.

As soon as they exited back through the portal, Curtis turned his face east, a grim expression on his face. "They're out there. Dammit, I knew I shouldn't have gone in."
