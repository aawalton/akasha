Satan Jr., Level 50 (Floor Boss)

Because of an ongoing challenge, Satan Jr.’s level is currently adjusted to Level 28 (Sector Boss.)

Holy shit. You madman, you actually did it. In the fifteen years since Satan Jr. came to the Gymnasium, we’ve given this quest to two-hundred and eighty-seven climbers, and you’re the first to tickle even one ball. There’s another liaison, Wayrod With A Rod Up His Ass, who has been giving it to every single advanced climber he’s assigned to. That’s his official name, by the way. We had a bet. I’m so happy. I’ll be even happier if you get the second ball, but I’ll settle for you staying alive.

Anyway, I didn’t think I’d ever get to write this guy’s battle description, but here goes:

Satan Jr. is an MMA superstar. He has an invincible streak of 152-0 in the Gymnasium’s battlecage. Before that, he was the forklift weight division champion of, uh, someplace. His skill is real. He can’t use most of his powers on this floor, but trust me, that won’t stop him from kicking your ass.

“Oh, come on,” Gary said.

A devil materialized in the center of the ring with a poof. It seemed like the same devil who’d refereed Gary’s fight against the boxing champion, only he was a lot more excited.

“Ladies and gentlemen," he yelled, the sound blasting from the microphone. “Welcome to the first ever Gymnasium title match, with the entire Satan Gym on the line, as well as 1% stakes on Satan Shakes LLC!”

The Shakes are on the line? Gary thought.

The announcer hadn’t stopped. “Because of the current invasion, we cannot make this battle to the death, but we can come close! In the blue corner, please welcome Gary Steel. In the red corner stands the reigning champion of the Gymnasium Association, the Prince of You-Know-Where, the Archduke of Progress and Development, the Corrupt Financier, the—”

Satan Jr. reached for the announcer’s neck and yanked out his windpipe. Fire spurted from the gap. The devil clawed for his neck as he dropped the microphone, his tail twisting wildly. Eyes pleading to Satan Jr., he collapsed, and his body flared into ash that remained in the center of the cage.

“Too long,” Satan Jr. said. “You insisted on your challenge, Gary Steel. A mistake no one made in the last fifteen years, you did it. Moreover, in what could only be a lapse in control of your lust, you dared to reach for my privates.”

The audience gasped.

“This shocks you? Not that he ripped the dude’s throat out?” Gary said to the crowd.

“Ring the bell,” Satan Jr. called, and a wrestler rushed to strike the nearby gong. Before the sound even reached him, Gary had drawn his spear and prepared himself. He was drawing big breaths. Satan Jr. may have been at the same level as him, but he was a sector boss. That was a massive step-up from the field boss he’d just faced.

Somehow, the thought excited him. This was a battle no one had accepted in fifteen years. Everyone had taken the bribe or succumbed to the threats, but not Gary. He was doing it. He was breaking their rules and playing his own game.

Fuck their corruption. I’ll prove it can be done the right way.

Something sparked inside him at the thought. Before he had time to consider it, Satan rushed him.

“Nail Gun!” Gary yelled, but the massive, red-skinned devil flowed between the near-instant strikes. He slapped the spearhead away, completely ignoring its black lightning, then slammed a right fist into Gary’s face. He lost the world. His back met the cage, which didn’t even budge, and he felt the steel tearing into his skin of his back. He ducked just in time to dodge the flying knee that rattled the cage where his head used to be.

“Thunder Step!” Gary yelled, retreating fast.

Watching from the sides, Asim felt a tug at his leg. Edgar was staring at him, pointing at his mouth. Asim sighed and waved his hand.

“He’s an idiot to mess with Mr. Satan Jr.,” Edgar said.

“He was already an idiot, Edgar. What do you mean?”

“Hell has agents everywhere. Becoming our enemy is suicidal.”

“He must have had his reasons,” Alicia said.

“I think he forgot to think,” Asim said.

All around, people were wondering the same thing.

“I bet he did something really stupid in there,” a girl said.

“Satan Jr. said Gary Steel reached for his privates,” another replied.

“Yeah, but don’t you think he’d like that? It’s Satan. I think that was a lie, and Gary actually slapped him.”

“Slapped Satan?”

“He’s Gary Steel, the guy who slapped a Branch Elder during the tournament.”

“Wait, that’s him!?”

“Yes! He’s so reckless…” the original girl said, blushing a little.

“I mean, he did dare to fight the boss that everyone else ran away from…” her friend replied, she, too, blushing.

Black lightning erupted all over Gary’s body, a prodigious amount, a power that should by no means be confined to this tiny cage. Strikes rained. Satan tried to infiltrate his guard again, but Gary played defense, shooting across the cage like a bolt of lightning. In the eyes of everyone watching, a black streak intertwined with a red one, their moves faster than most of the audience could perceive. Massive strikes met the cage, powered by Core Formation aura, but the steel didn’t even crack. All their power was perfectly contained.

Gary gritted his teeth as he fought. He didn’t dare blink. His perception was strained to the limit just to keep up with Satan. A red punch met the Golden Bolt right on the middle knuckle. Both fighters were pushed back, then returned, clashing head-on a dozen times.

Gary was stunned by Satan’s strength. He’d taken so much Muscle Sap and cultivated the first stage of the Starvein Immortal Body Manual, and he was still losing. His spear was pushed harder in every exchange until he started falling back.

Finally, Satan drove a punch into Gary’s guard, sending him flying into the steel mesh. “Too weak!” he yelled, diving into Gary, who barely thunderstepped in time to dodge the fist that nailed the cage. When Satan pulled it back, it was uninjured.

“To become the Gymnasium’s King of the Ring,” he said, “I had to master all four disciplines first. It was a simple matter, of course, but I was surprised by how useful physical techniques can be at this level. Come on, Gary Steel. Let me have my fun.”

Gary replied in the form of charged electricity. “Song of—” he tried to say, but the battlefield was too small. There was simply no time to unleash the Song of Lightning. Satan reached him instantly, forcing him to abandon the technique, and they restarted the battle dance. Gary tried to stay at range and poke Satan with his spear. Satan tried to come close.

Gary couldn’t get any hits in, but at least he slowed Satan’s advance. That annoyed the devil.

“Fight me like a man!” he bellowed, jumping up and falling like a meteor. An aura strike flew from his fist, smashing Gary into the mat, but he also received a thrust which tore a bloody line through his cheek.

As Gary smashed into the mat, he didn’t feel the softness he expected. Instead, there was pain, like something sharp sticking out of the mat and into him. As if he’d fallen on a bed of spikes. He yelled, hastily rolling away as blood dripped from his back. The wounds regenerated, but someone with less Vitality would have been seriously injured.

“What the hell?” he muttered, glancing at the bouncy, perfectly flat mat.

“You actually injured me!” Satan said, wiping the line on his cheek. His blood was on fire. “After you lose today, you can be proud.”

“I’ll do much more than that,” Gary replied.

They clashed again. The cheers of the audience were lost to Gary’s ears as he poured everything he had into the battle. Unfortunately, step by step, he was losing the advantage. He couldn’t match Satan head-on. He had to keep running, and the limited space of the cage made that harder.

I’m close though, he thought. Our strength isn’t too far apart. If only I had a little more…

Wait. A little more?

A fist punched his stomach in the moment of his distraction, sending Gary flying into the wall. He gasped. He saw Satan coming. “Thunder Ste—” he said, when the steel behind his legs came alive and wrapped around them. He couldn’t move. Satan’s fist clocked hard into the middle of Gary’s face, squeezing him against the steel mesh. His nose shattered. Blood filled his vision.

“Gah!” Gary said, stumbling as Satan retrieved his fist. The steel behind his foot had returned to normal. How? he thought, thunderstepping in a blur.

“There’s more where that came from,” Satan said, raising his bloodied fist. People cheered. Gary couldn’t see his own face, but it must have been a mess. A slowly regenerating mess.

At least, he could see.

A little more, he thought as his vision slowly cleared. I have that, don’t I? The stars…

A dreamy river crashed through his veins, frothing at every turn, begging for release. Gary gripped his spear and let it. The thrust blasted out, meeting Satan’s incoming punch. They clashed like in the start—only, this time, it was Gary who held a small advantage. Blue stars glittered in the air around his spearhead.

“That tickles!” Goldie whispered in Gary’s mind.

“Hmm?” Satan said, frowning. “Even if you’re a dual cultivator, it’s all the same!”

They clashed again. Gary’s wounds were healing, but he could feel the exhaustion settling in. His moves were slower. His mind, blurry. Suddenly, Satan disappeared. A pair of burly hands wrapped around Gary’s waist.

“Suplex!” came a roar, and Gary tipped over backward to land on his neck. Satan somehow ended up on Gary’s back, pulling an arm almost to the breaking point. Gary’s elbow was on fire. Panic rose. Meanwhile, flares of pain came from his abdomen that was pressed against the mat, as if daggers stabbed him through the floor.

“This is MMA, bitch!” Satan said.

Outside the cage, Asim frowned. “Where the hell is the bell?” he asked, looking to the side, where a wrestler was trying and failing to ring the bell.

“It’s broken!” he yelled.

“How can it be broken?” Asim said.

“Someone put wool behind it. It doesn’t make a sound!”

Asim’s eyes widened, and he rushed over to find an imp stuffing wool behind the bell. “Shhh,” it said, bringing a finger to its lips.

“What are you doing?” Asim thundered.

“I’m just stuffing wool to pass the time. I’ll ring the bell when the time comes. Look,” it said, showing a broken watch on its wrist.

“I’ll fucking stab you,” Asim said.

“That would incur the automatic hostility of every monster in the gym, since I’m the designated bell official,” the imp replied.

Meanwhile, Gary’s arm was still pulled back. He didn’t know whether tapping was a rule here, but he knew he wasn’t going to do it. He’d rather have his arm broken. And, since that was going to happen, he might as well do it himself.

He turned abruptly, snapping his own elbow and dislocating his shoulder with a  sickening crack. The audience recoiled. Gary’s mind was awash with pain, but he span in blind rage and smashed the shaft of his spear into Satan’s cheek. Hard. Black lightning and stars both erupted, and a pearly white tooth flew out as Satan was blasted into the cage wall. The entire cage shook. Gary stood, panting and huffing with bloodshot eyes wide. He’d lost himself for a moment there. All he knew was that he refused to quit.

He wasn’t expecting it. He must be injured, or even seriously injured. I can win, Gary thought.

The steel mesh didn’t entrap Satan. He fell flat on the mat, and from his vantage point, Gary saw a long, thin stinger emerge from the mat to inject a green liquid into Satan’s chest. The stinger was gone in moments—and Gary realized that, as Satan collapsed, his body had fallen in such a way that it blocked the stinger from everyone’s sight but Gary’s.

Satan jumped upright, bursting with energy. He didn’t seem injured. Instead, he was even more invigorated than before—even his tooth had grown back. This level of regeneration was impossible. Gary’s brows fell.

“What was that? Did a fly bite me?” Satan asked, laughing uproariously. “You’re stubborn, Gary, I’ll give you that. But you cannot defeat me. The more you resist, the more pain you’ll experience!”

Gary’s arm was regenerating, but it wasn’t functional yet. Waves of pain filled him from the shoulder and the chest where he’d been stabbed, somehow. With Satan in peak condition, Gary’s only hope was the bell. In the minute between rounds, he could regenerate. He had to.

I swear it’s been more than five minutes, he thought, glancing at the bell, where he saw Asim and a wrestler arguing against an imp. Wool stuck out from behind the bell.

Gary chuckled bitterly. Hatred rose inside him. The mat, the wall, the bell, the stinger… Of course he’s cheating, he even told me beforehand. He doesn’t give a shit about the rules. None of them do. They only apply to the weak and poor.

The whole tower is stacked against us, but watch me break it anyway, you corrupt motherfuckers.

The bell would never ring. His arm was broken. Even if he somehow struck Satan again, that stinger would just inject him with healing juice, and the cage itself worked against Gary.

If he faced Satan in a fair battle outside the cage, with a little more room to move, he might stand a chance. But not here. This wasn’t a battle he could win—so he just had to fight a different battle.

He won’t kill me, Gary realized. Fine. You can have the belt, but I’ll take something else.

He fell into a low stance, placing his good arm lower on the spear to protect it. He’d need it soon.
