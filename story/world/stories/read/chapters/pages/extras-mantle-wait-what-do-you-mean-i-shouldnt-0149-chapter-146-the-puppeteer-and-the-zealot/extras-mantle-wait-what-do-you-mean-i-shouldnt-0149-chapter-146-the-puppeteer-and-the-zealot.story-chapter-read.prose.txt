The underground garden smelled of wet loam and crushed limestone. Along the cavern walls, clusters of pale moss and glowing fungi cast a cold, blue-white glow over the damp earth. At the center of the chamber, two blind fish traced endless, unhurried circles in a shallow pond.

Hierarch Malachar rested his pale hands against the armrests of his dark throne made of bones. The cavern offered a rare commodity: competence. The blind fish swam exactly as their fate dictated. The fungi process decay into light. Nothing down here possessed the capacity to scheme, and consequently, nothing down here possessed the capacity to fail him.

His cult, unfortunately, suffered from the fatal flaw of ego and free will. Over the past twenty-four hours, his leadership caste had butchered their assignments with spectacular, almost insulting efficiency.

They had been handed victory on a silver platter, and they had managed to drop it.

A Child of Silence knelt on the damp earth before the throne. The gray-robed assassin kept his head bowed perfectly level with his knees. He radiated no aura, displaced no air, and offered no pretense of humanity.

He was just a tool awaiting application.

"Report," Malachar said. His voice barely rose above a whisper, yet the echoes in the cavern carried the syllables with absolute clarity.

"The outer perimeter has collapsed, Lord Throne," the assassin stated. "Outposts C1, C2, C3, and C19 have been purged by the enemy following our reclamation. Furthermore, Hand Vakan barely survived a failed gamble. Icas Vasht is confirmed dead. Eight favored ones have been slain by the Star Killer and the crimson-eyed doorman."

Malachar processed the casualty data. Eight Underlords. One Lord and One Hand are out of order. A catastrophic depletion of high-tier assets.

He felt no rage. Rage was a mortal indulgence, a flaw that clouded the purity of the Great Work. Instead, he felt a cold fascination.

He had already witnessed the butchery through his scrying sphere. He had watched Salvatore Silvers, the supposed crippled remnant of an Overlord—who still possessed more flames than what was expected of him—dismantle his elites.

The loss of the assets was an acceptable variable in warfare. What grated on Malachar’s nerves was the sheer, staggering arrogance of their deaths. Hand Vakan and Icas Vasht had spent twenty-four hours chasing a target that clearly desired pursuit. They had hunted a ghost through their own territory, congratulating themselves on springing a trap right up until the exact moment the prey locked the cage from the inside.

The survivors of the Bastion were no longer cowering behind their walls. They were actively culling his herd.

Malachar mentally reviewed the recent spike in essence signatures he felt from one of his outposts. A hundred untalented mortals, stringing their pathetic reserves together to chain a single, unified sorcery. It was an insult to the natural hierarchy of power. A termite chewing through the foundation of a divine temple.

"Withdraw the remaining forces from all perimeter positions," Malachar ordered. He shifted his weight, the blackened bones of his throne creaking under the slight movement. "Consolidate at the inner rings. Fortify the Tier 7 Convergence nodes. We will not squander more blood on meaningless skirmishes."

The Child of Silence remained perfectly still, the damp earth soaking into the knees of his gray robes. "And the Bastion's encroaching forces, Lord Throne?"

"Let them push inward," Malachar said. "The Convergence sequence is our singular priority. I will not tolerate further delays simply because my misguided flock insists on throwing themselves onto enemy blades."

He tapped a single, corpse-pale finger against his armrest. The sharp click of bone on bone echoed across the pond, causing the two blind fish to scatter into the darker water.

"Go to the aberrant alchemist. Inform Hand Vella that the observational testing period has concluded. She is to open her vaults and empty the menagerie."

The Child of Silence bowed lower, his forehead brushing the wet soil. A heartbeat later, he was gone. No spatial tear, no sudden rush of displaced air. Just the abrupt, sterile absence of a person.

Malachar remained on his throne for a time, watching his fishes.

The white fish swallowed a floating pellet of feed with a quiet plip. Below it, the black fish traced a lazy circle in the dark water.

Malachar stood. The heavy fabric of his robes dragged across the damp moss. He left the quiet sanctuary of the pond, walking into the far alcove of the cavern where the glowing fungi failed to take root.

Thick iron chains descended from the ceiling. Decades of damp air had welded the rusted metal directly into the limestone. Suspended at chest height within the iron links hung a mass of dark, glistening flesh. It was roughly the size of a man curled in on himself.

As Malachar approached, the meat expanded and contracted with a heavy, wet squelch.

This was the nascent seed of the Convergence. The engine driving his entire great work.

He stepped within arm's reach of the suspended mass and pressed his palm flat against the slick membrane. The tissue radiated a feverish heat, vibrating against his skin.

Malachar shifted his vocal cords. Flesh and cartilage reshaped internally without a sound. When he spoke, the scraping ancient was gone, and in its place the voice that emerged was a rich, warm baritone. It carried the heavy, undeniable texture of a proud father.

The kind of voice that made mortals desperately want to earn its approval.

"Withdraw, my son," Malachar murmured. He traced the pad of his thumb over a swollen, pulsing vein near the surface of the mass. "Consolidate our remaining forces. The Bastion pushes outward, blind to the underlying architecture of this conflict. Cease squandering our assets on territory that has already served its purpose."

The heart pulsed faster under his hand.

He wiped a smear of viscous fluid from his hand using the inside of his sleeve.

Turning away from the suspended meat, he retraced his steps to the edge of the shallow water.

The Great Work will proceed as planned.

Deep inside the Sanctum of the Cathedral Ward, Favored One Xerxes stood before a towering containment cylinder, his hands clasped behind his back. The heavy, thrumming vibration of the healing array made him nauseated.

He pressed a gaunt hand against the cold glass. Inside the pressurized tank, suspended in thick, bubbling dark blood, floated the ruined meat and bone that belonged to their leader, Vakan.

Waste.

The thought coiled in Xerxes' mind, sharp and venomous. They were pumping the Darkened One's most precious, concentrated essence into a failing vessel. The lower ranks trembled at Vakan’s name, terrified of the man's oppressive aura, but Xerxes felt only disgust. He knew the truth. Vakan was a politician wearing a zealot's robes. The man was completely deaf to the Lord's whispers, viewing him merely as a stepping stone for his own authority.

"Parasite," Xerxes rasped.

He struck the thick glass with his knuckles. The dull thud reverberated through the liquid. He desperately wanted to sever the essence feeds. To let the pretender choke on the sacred blood he didn't deserve. An Order V Lord wouldn't die immediately—his Spark would fight the suffocation for years—but the rot would claim him eventually.

Xerxes pulled his hand back, forcing his erratic breathing to slow. Devotion to his lord required discipline. Vakan, broken as he was, remained an Order V asset. They would need a Lord-ranked shield to weather the coming slaughter.

Command of the cult had defaulted to Xerxes in Vakan's absence. His first act had been cracking the seals on the Hand's private chambers to seize the intelligence Vakan so jealously hoarded.

Xerxes moved to the desk and dragged a scarred finger down a stack of transcripts.

"Pathetic," he murmured to the empty room. "The greatest flock of the Darkened One, reduced to cowering cattle by the incompetence of a few."

The reports, especially the casualty one, were staggering. Most of the reports pointed entirely to two anomalies: Starkiller and the Doorman.

These two individual were slaughtering their way through the perimeter outposts.

They posed a terminal threat to the ritual's apex. The Hierarch and his inner circle possessed the raw power to crush them, but the Hierarch was entirely consumed by the Convergence preparations and his own goals.

Xerxes needed a diversion. He needed to manipulate the board, to herd Starkiller and the Doorman directly into the path of the two-faced hypocrites' flock of the Hierarch. Let the false faithful of the city grind themselves to dust against those monsters. The Darkened One would feast on the resulting carnage regardless of who died.

Xerxes stepped away from the desk, pausing before Vakan’s massive scrying mirror. Deprived of its master’s essence, the artifact was dormant, reduced to ordinary silvered glass.

He stared at his reflection and let out a dry, rattling laugh. He had draped one of Vakan’s opulent, velvet-lined robes over his shoulders to signify his new authority. It felt ridiculously heavy. Beneath the expensive fabric, Xerxes wore his traditional, unadorned black garb, hanging loosely off a frame whittled down to wire and muscle from years of fanatical fasting. Where his skin was visible, it was a roadmap of jagged scar tissue—half from surviving enemy blades, the other half willingly carved in devotion to his Lord.

Heavy bootfalls hammered against the stone stairs outside the chamber.

Xerxes felt the fluctuating, panicked aura before the man even reached the threshold. He clicked his tongue in irritation. Another one from the Vasht clan. Another one of Vakan's weak-blooded sycophants.

"Xerxes!"

Boros Vasht shoved the heavy oak doors inward. The man's plate armor was streaked with grey ash and fresh gore from the outer wards. The coal-red manifestation of his Lord's Flame sputtered erratically at his shoulders, bleeding fear into the room's atmosphere.

Xerxes turned slowly. He kept his expression entirely devoid of the panic radiating from the newcomer.

"Brother Boros," Xerxes corrected, his voice a soft, raspy whisper that cut cleanly through the ringing echoes of the door. "Please. I am not a grasping bootlicker like the meat floating in that tank."

He tilted his head, studying the erratic spikes in Boros's essence signature with glee. "Your aura is bleeding terror across my floor. Tell me what happened that has you so agitated."

"The perimeter is collapsing!" Boros grated, his heavy plate armor clanking as he shifted his weight. "Without Lord Vakan and Lord Icas dead, the Initiates are breaking down. The Bastion forces just mobilized a major push. We need to counter-attack."

Xerxes tilted his head, his black eyes fixing on the larger man. "A counter-attack." He let the words hang in the air, examining them like a spoiled piece of meat.

"We are the Vanguard of the Darkened One," Boros insisted, the coal-red flames at his shoulders surging with agitated heat. "We do not cower while Bastion rats steal our sacred ground. We push outward and crush them."

"You intend to feed more of the faithful to the Star Killer," Xerxes stated. "You watched eight Underlords butcher themselves on their own arrogance in a single night. And your solution is to march more of the faithful onto his sword."

Boros's jaw tightened. The ambient temperature in the room spiked as his Lord's Flame flared, painting the stone walls in harsh crimson light. He dropped his center of gravity, a hand drifting toward his weapon. "I am the senior Favored One currently standing. I am giving you a direct—"

Xerxes didn't bother drawing a weapon. He raised his right hand and pressed two gaunt fingers directly into the jagged ritual scar carved over his own chest. He dug his nails deep into the scar tissue until thick, corrupted blood welled to the surface.

Boros choked.

The massive warrior’s hands snapped up to claw at his own throat. The intimidating red flames at his shoulders guttered and died with a pathetic hiss. Xerxes channeled the Lord's blessing through the open wound on his chest, seizing the corrupted essence flowing through Boros's veins.

He made the man's blood boil.

Boros collapsed, his heavy armor cracking against the masonry. Xerxes looked down at the thrashing man and felt absolutely nothing.

"You actually believe the Lord cares about your pride," Xerxes rasped. He stepped neatly over the convulsing warrior. "You treat his mission like a tavern brawl. A chance to prove your meager strength."

Boros seized violently, his chest heaving as blood flooded his lungs, before going completely limp.

"Worry not, Brother Boros. You will survive the experience," Xerxes said to the unconscious meat on the floor. He wiped his bloody fingers on the edge of Vakan's velvet mantle and walked toward the chamber's balcony, the heavy fabric dragging behind him.

He was halfway there when the whispers reached him.

« Withdraw, my son. »

The baritone voice vibrated through the marrow of his bones, rich and dripping with absolute authority.

« Consolidate our remaining forces. Do not let my children squander their lives on territory that has already served its purpose. »

Xerxes forgot how to breathe. Thick, viscous black tears welled in his eyes and spilled over his scarred cheeks, burning the skin. He slammed his forehead into the stone floor, desperate to show reverence.

His lord was telling him exactly what needed to be done. He was no longer a lost sheep.

"I hear you, my Lord," Xerxes wept, his blood-stained fingernails digging into the mortar of the stairs. "I am your vessel. Your will shall be done."

Far above, the Cathedral Ward was undoubtedly steeped in the smell of boiling blood and panic.

Down in the garden, the air tasted only of wet limestone and damp moss. Malachar settled back into his bone throne beside the shallow pond, resting his translucent hands in his lap. He withdrew his awareness from the heavy, oppressive connection he had just forced into the Underlord's mind.

Xerxes had folded exactly as calculated.

"Such is the nature of mankind: each believes himself the wisest soul beneath the heavens until fate acquaints them with their ignorance.” He said.

The new acting leader would execute the withdrawal, entirely convinced the shift was a matter of divine providence. With the Vanguard pulled back and repurposed as a physical barricade, the Tier 7 Convergence nodes were completely insulated from the Bastion's strike teams. The ritual sequence would trigger precisely on schedule.

A faint, dry smile stretched the skin tight across Malachar's face.

His thoughts shifted outward, toward the Bastion. The surviving mortals had proven to be a surprisingly resilient infestation, but the sheer scale of the fate variance rippling from their position was fascinating.

The board had fundamentally changed the moment that one piece escaped the primes’ design.

“But who would go against the dominant gods of Eldamar?” He sighed.

Whoever this person was, he needed to isolate that specific entity. To sit down and have a conversation with its head.

Malachar pinched a compressed pellet of feed between his pale fingers and let it drop.

The white fish broke the surface tension with a quiet plip, swallowing the morsel whole.

The Great Work proceeded.
