Hannah's retelling of her experience through the pink gate was like something out of a horror story. From the moment she crossed the boundary, her mind had been invaded by what she described as nightmares: Different visions of her, her friend's, and even her family dying in twisted ways where she was always the one responsible for their ultimate fates. Even after fighting through those mental attacks, she woke up to an almost empty health bar and a creature on top of her head with tendrils going into her brain. The smile on Hannah's face when she re-told of her ripping it off and squeezing the life out of it bordered on creepy, but Luke decided it was best not to comment.

While Luke's dungeon wasn't a dungeon per se, but an endless hospital, Hannah's wasn't even that. It was just nothingness, like an approximation of the void. She floated in there, unsure what to do. The quest told her she needed to survive and learn, but nothing about how. When she described the others, her voice took on a low, depressed quality. Yeah, there had been others, many others, in that void with her. As far as she could see, no one else had survived the first stage. They all succumbed to the brain-eaters, who, in the end, took control of them. Like zombies, they attacked, but unlike zombies, these weren't shambling, mindless monsters. They attacked with shadow, much like her own. Some were strong, others weak. If they had joined together in attack, she wouldn't have stood a chance, but they did not. Once they were all dead, Hannah found herself alone. So terribly alone. A week went by, or at least what felt like a week. It was then, after she had gone half-insane in that vast nothingness, that the voices started.

The Deep Dweller was dead. Hannah had known that much, but that didn't change what happened. It sang to her, crooned as it had done when they first became one, before the struggle. Sang to her of power and the horrible deeds of those in control of that which is, of genocide and terror, of division and strife. Wouldn't nothing be the superior choice? Wouldn't it be far better than the tyrants? Wouldn't it be... justice?

Affected by the words and ideas, Hannah couldn't help but feel. The Deep Dweller played her emotions like strings on a lyre, plucked until it found just the right melody to weaken her resolve. When she had no more defenses to erect against that insistent, grating voice, it struck, and in doing so, it revealed itself. Not The Deep Dweller. No, this was something much worse.

Humanoid, almost. Too tall and thin, too large a head. Eyes of light. Alive, almost. Not quite a monster, but powerful beyond any scope she could comprehend. A god, almost. No, not almost. A god. It had to be. What else could have that sort of power? It wanted her to join them, to become one of them, to merge with their ideas and take vengeance on... That's where her recollection became somewhat of a blur. This faction, whoever they were, wanted another way of things, to raze what was and build something new. Justice, they called it. A faction of shadows. The Others, they called themselves, and the being in front of her was their everything, godlike but, for some reason, unable to assert its will freely. It bestowed power and knowledge on those deemed worthy, those who joined the cause.

"What exactly is their cause?" Luke asked, cutting in because he needed a more thorough explanation. Hannah's talking had become stilted and monotonous, and the memories were disjointed at best, almost as if she'd dreamed it all.

She gave him a long, searching look while she put it in plain words, as if to gauge his reaction. "Those in control of the system aren't such great people. It showed me things, made me live through them. It's like I spent years in there, experiencing different lives."

"Not that I ever believed they are pure-souled pillars of virtue, I'm not even sure I figured there are leaders in that sense, but are you sure you can trust what this... thing told you?"

"This organisation, faction, whatever has its own agenda, but I don't think it lied."

Luke nodded. "So what happened next?"

Again, she scanned his face to gauge his expression. "I accepted."

Rather than offer some knee-jerk reaction, Luke nodded, taking his time to mull it over. If it were him, such a promise wouldn't pass the smell test. Consequences down the line would follow, and they didn't know enough about The Greater System to make an informed decision. But that was him. Hannah had made hers, and he couldn't fault her for it. That was neither his place nor his style.

"I see," he said. "Did it work?"

Her shoulders dropped a little, her breathing resumed, and some color appeared on her cheeks. That last one, he couldn't get a read on. Was she blushing?

"It gave me power, yes, in a way. But it was more like it showed me how to get it. Not like some god-creature reaching down and blessing me, or whatever."

"What do you mean?"

She swallowed hard and placed her free hand across her chest, half-hugging herself. "It showed me how to use the void. The Others draw strength from it in a simple but also kind of convoluted way. It's like they, or maybe I should say 'we', have an understanding with..."

"The void beings," Luke added.

"Yeah, the void beings. Not allies or anything like that," she was quick to add, "but... yeah. I don't know. The Greater System is a big place, I think."

"Yup," he said with a nod, before wrapping her arm around her shoulders to pull her close. "So none of the other Vespers made it?"

She put a hand to her mouth. "They were all Vespers... How didn't I realize that?"

"My dungeon had a whole bunch of Lifeweavers. Not sure why we got pink portals, and some others didn't, but I'm starting to think it doesn't have anything to do with our classes. Hold on a second."

Luke: "Hey, Ella. Sorry about our training session. I'll be with you soon, alright?"

Ella: "Do you know how long a day is for me when I'm this fast?"

Luke: "Uh, no?"

Ella: "It's the same as yours, slow-ass."

Ella: "Lucky for you, I can modulate how I experience it."

Luke: "Right. Well, I wanted to ask if you entered one of those pink portals a while back?"

Ella: "Yeah! It was weird as hell, let me tell you!"

Luke: "I'd love to hear all about it, but not this second. I'll be ready in a couple of minutes."

Ella: "Slow-ass asshole."

"I got to know someone else who sees what we see. I think the pink portals had something to do with those of us who can see the weave, or any version of it, I guess. As long as anyone with the class can, it pulls everyone into portals."

"You're saying all those Vesper Integrated are dead because of me?" she asked.

"Maybe," he said, shaking his head. "But that's not your fault. Maybe the system wanted to cull them. Or... something."

That settled on her shoulders and lingered there for a moment before she shook the weight off and seemed... fine. "I know it's not my fault. Poor luck on their part. Just wish so many didn't have to die. It's our new reality, but I don't have to like it. Maybe this is some of what The Others are working against."

"Maybe."

"There's a little more to what happened," she said, looking up at him from under her uneven bangs.

Luke brushed a strand out of her face. "Of course. Just a quick question first. Do you still feel like you have that mental block thing from Guide? The Singers broke it in our heads, but you weren't here at the time."

"It still hit me," Hannah said. "And it wasn't the best time for it either, I have to say. Almost killed me."

"Interesting. Well, let's go. Hit me with the rest. Let me guess, it was a lot of suffering?"

"Oh yeah. Lots of that good stuff."

Then she told him of a pact written in shadows with her blood as the paper - Luke was afraid to ask what that meant - and that of the leader of The Others. What followed could be considered training, in a way. It threw Hannah into the actual void and taught her how to begin holding off the creatures within using her will. As she described it, some things in there would attack, understanding or no, while the larger things that stirred in the void kept their distance. It was Hannah's job to keep them from killing her while that Others being infused her meta-heart with void mana, taking it straight from the source. There had been pain, lots of it in fact, during the process, and not just from learning to absorb and work that nothingness within herself, but from the creatures who pushed back, nibbling at her shadows before reaching close enough to consume whole chunks of her. The godlike leader of The Others didn't lift a hand to help, and the moment it deemed her successful enough, it disappeared, leaving her in there by herself.

"It was when I finally got away, after hours in the void, that I broke the moonlight wall, and I didn't know how to put it back together."

Luke leaned in. "Good thing I found you."

"Yeah," she said as she tilted her head up to meet him. "I'm glad you did."
