Chapter 17: Chosen

Stretched out vast before me was a labyrinth of black stone, touched with dark browns and crimson reds that seemed to pulse with their own malevolent life. I couldn't help but think it looked like a huge infected wound, weeping blood into the alien landscape of Crucible. The structure defied conventional architecture, with passages that twisted impossibly upon themselves and towers that seemed to exist in multiple dimensions simultaneously. Walls rose and fell in patterns that hurt to look at directly, as if the very geometry of the place was designed to unsettle the mind and break the spirit of those who would dare enter.

The air above the dungeon shimmered with barely contained Flux energy, creating distortions that made distant sections appear to shift and writhe like living tissue. Occasionally, flashes of purple and crimson lightning would arc between the spires, illuminating details that shouldn't exist in any sane reality. The sight made my teeth ache and my eyes water, yet I found myself unable to look away from the promised crucible of violence that awaited us all.

A massive crowd of initiates stood on a cliff overlooking the structure, hundreds of beings from dozens of worlds I couldn't name. The diversity was staggering and unsettling. Humanoids with skin like molten gold stood next to creatures that resembled walking crystals. Beings that looked like they were carved from living shadow conversed in whispered languages with others whose very presence seemed to bend light around them. Some initiates bore obvious marks of their homeworlds, scars from battles fought in environments I couldn't imagine, while others appeared deceptively normal until you caught sight of their eyes, glowing with the telltale signs of deep Flux integration.

The scent of ozone and something metallic hung heavy in the air, mixed with the alien musk of too many different species gathered in one place. Every breath tasted of anticipation and barely controlled violence. The red sun of Crucible cast everything in shades of blood and shadow, making even the most harmless looking initiates appear sinister and threatening.

An amphitheater had been erected before us, carved from the same black stone as the dungeon below. Its construction was flawless, each block fitted so perfectly that no seams were visible, as if the entire structure had been shaped from a single piece of living rock. Nyxen officials stood on its elevated platform, their dark robes rippling in the warm wind that carried the constant background hum of Flux energy.

Vash was there, her perfect features as cold and impassive as ever, violet eyes scanning the crowd with predatory interest. Beside her stood Drayven Vox, his obsidian skin seeming to absorb the crimson light rather than reflect it, those unsettling blood-red eyes fixed on his chosen initiate somewhere in the crowd.

Other Lords and Ladies I didn't recognize flanked them, each one radiating an aura of power that made the air around them shimmer like heat rising from hot pavement. Their very presence seemed to warp reality slightly, creating subtle distortions that spoke of connections to the Flux so deep they had transcended normal physical limitations. I found myself wondering how many initiates they had watched die over the years, how many promising candidates had been ground to nothing in dungeons just like the one spread out below us.

Initiates were muttering amongst themselves in dozens of languages, their voices creating a low susurrus that reminded me uncomfortably of insects preparing to swarm. I could already see potential alliances forming as groups clustered together based on species, faction allegiance, or simple mutual recognition of compatible fighting styles. Some initiates stood alone, radiating the kind of dangerous confidence that suggested they either possessed overwhelming power or were too unstable for anyone to risk approaching.

Near the edge of the crowd, a group of what appeared to be crystalline beings communicated through pulses of colored light that danced across their translucent surfaces. Not far from them, a cluster of reptilian creatures similar to Molech but with different scale patterns held what looked like a war council, their hissed conversations punctuated by aggressive gestures toward various sections of the crowd. A lone figure wrapped entirely in shadows stood apart from everyone else, and I noticed that other initiates unconsciously moved away whenever they got too close, as if proximity to whatever lurked beneath those dark wrappings carried some instinctive danger.

Kari wormed her way through the crowd, her lean frame allowing her to slip between larger initiates with practiced ease. When she reached my side, I caught the faint scent of the same metallic soap they provided in Vash's mansion, a small reminder of the alien nature of even the most mundane aspects of life on this world.

"Heya, Varus! Beautiful dreary red day, isn't it?" Her green eyes sparkled with humor, and despite everything, I found myself grudgingly impressed by her ability to maintain levity in the face of what was essentially a death sentence for most of us.

The crimson sky stretched overhead like a dome of dried blood, broken only by the occasional Flux storm that crackled with unnatural energies in the distance. Beautiful wasn't the word I would have chosen, but I supposed it was a matter of perspective. To someone who had grown up on Earth, where blue skies and white clouds were the norm, this alien landscape held a harsh beauty that was both compelling and deeply unsettling.

I didn't feel her humor, so I just grunted in response. The weight of what was coming had settled on my shoulders like a lead blanket, and I found it difficult to muster enthusiasm for small talk when I would likely be fighting for my life within the hour. Every moment of levity felt like a betrayal of the seriousness of our situation, though I recognized that Kari's approach was probably healthier than my own grim focus.

"Know much about what to expect?" I asked her, my voice lower than I had intended. The question felt inadequate given the circumstances, but I genuinely wanted to know if she had any intelligence about the dungeon's layout or the types of challenges we might face inside.

Before she could respond, a voice rang out across the assembled crowd, strong and sure. A Nyxen woman I hadn't met stepped forward on the platform, her skin like burnished copper catching the red light and throwing it back in patterns that seemed to shift and flow. Her swirling brown eyes held depths that suggested centuries of experience, and she wore the dark robes I had come to associate with high-ranking Nyxen officials. The fabric seemed to move independently of any wind, rippling with its own internal currents.

"Initiates!" Her voice was slightly high but carried across the vast crowd with impossible clarity, likely enhanced by the Flux to ensure every being present could hear her words regardless of their species' auditory capabilities.

As if someone had snipped a thread, everyone quieted instantly. The sudden silence was so complete it felt oppressive, broken only by the distant rumble of Flux storms and the soft whisper of wind across stone. Everyone here was likely used to what would happen should you disobey a direct command from Nyxen authority. I had learned that lesson myself during my time under Vash's tutelage, and the memory of her casual violence was enough to keep my mouth shut and my attention focused.

"You all know why you're here!" she continued, her voice carrying easily across the amphitheater. "The time has come to prove yourselves worthy of being Nyxen! Of being apprentices! In a moment, Lords and Ladies of the Nyxen will announce their chosen Initiates. Some will choose many, others will choose none or only one of you. These initiates will receive nothing for being chosen, they are merely favored!"

I found myself scowling at that revelation, my jaw clenching as the implications became clear. Being chosen was nothing more than a giant target painted on your back. Set out, marked as different from the masses, the other initiates would inevitably attempt to prove their own strength by seeking you out specifically. It was a calculated cruelty that I was beginning to recognize as typically Nyxen in nature, providing advantage and disadvantage in equal measure while forcing the recipients to prove their worth through violence.

The message was abundantly clear: Prove your strength or die trying.

"The rules of the games are simple!" the copper-skinned woman proclaimed, her voice taking on the tone of someone who had delivered this speech many times before. "Survive this Flux Dungeon! You collect Initiate Points for slaying Flux Beasts or each other. You may form alliances, but in the end, only one may succeed. At certain points a Flux Lord or Lady may give you an offer to exit the games, allowing you to live, should they believe you proved yourself worthy of such an offer. The games end when someone has collected four keys to open the cathedral that will be in the middle of the dungeon. Now, the choosing will commence, and then... the games will begin!"

Four keys. A cathedral in the center. Flux beasts and the very real possibility of having to kill other initiates to survive. The parameters were simple enough to understand but complex enough in their execution to ensure that only the strongest, smartest, and most ruthless would have any chance of emerging victorious. I found myself running calculations in my head, trying to estimate how many of the hundreds of initiates gathered here would still be alive by the time someone claimed those four keys.

The numbers weren't encouraging.

She stepped back with fluid grace, and a Lord stepped forward to take her place. His movement carried the same inhuman perfection I had observed in all high-ranking Nyxen, as if gravity and physical limitations were merely suggestions rather than immutable laws.

One by one, Lords and Ladies began choosing Initiates, their voices carrying clearly across the assembled crowd. Each announcement sent ripples of tension through different sections of the gathering as those chosen were marked for both opportunity and targeting. Some names I recognized from my brief time in the training hall, others belonged to initiates I had never seen before but whose reputations clearly preceded them based on the reactions they generated.

"Keth'val," announced a Lord whose skin appeared to be made of polished obsidian. A creature that looked like it was composed entirely of interwoven metal cables raised one appendage in acknowledgment, its form shifting and reconfiguring itself in ways that defied my understanding of anatomy.

"Sylas," called out a Lady whose hair seemed to be made of liquid shadow. A pale humanoid with eyes like chips of ice stepped forward slightly, his expression never changing despite the obvious honor of being chosen.

The selections continued, each one accompanied by the subtle shifting of alliances and the recalculation of threats among the crowd. I watched the process with growing tension, knowing that my own name would be called eventually but uncertain of when the ax would fall.

"Molech," Drayven Vox said finally, his voice carrying a note of satisfied anticipation. His eyes gleamed as they reflected the red light around them, and his perfectly white teeth showed in what might generously be called a smile. Hardly a surprise to me, given what I had witnessed of their relationship in the training hall.

The draxi stepped forward with predatory grace, his dark scales catching the light in ways that made him appear to be carved from living midnight. He shot me and Kari a glance across the crowd, his reptilian features arranging themselves into an expression that, while not human, conveyed malicious intent with perfect clarity. The promise of violence in those mercury eyes was unmistakable, and I found myself automatically checking the position of my resonance blade hilt.

Several more names were called, the tension in the crowd ratcheting higher with each announcement. The weight of anticipation pressed down on me like a physical force, and I could feel sweat beginning to bead on my forehead despite the relatively cool temperature of the crimson morning.

Then, finally, the moment I had been both dreading and anticipating arrived.

"Varus Thorne," Lady Vash said, her voice cutting through the murmur of the crowd like a blade through silk.

Her dark violet eyes found mine across the sea of initiates, and in those obsidian depths I found another message as clear as if she had spoken it aloud: Don't disappoint me. The weight of her expectations settled on my shoulders alongside everything else I was carrying. The memory of Lieutenant Zorin's bisected corpse flashed through my mind, a reminder of what happened to those who failed to meet her standards.

I felt the attention of hundreds of initiates focus on me like a physical weight. Some gazes carried curiosity, others calculation, and more than a few held the predatory assessment of those who saw opportunity in my newly marked status. I kept my expression neutral, refusing to show either pride or fear at being chosen, though my hand unconsciously drifted toward the hilt of my resonance blade.

Kari glanced at me sideways, and I caught a flicker of something in her green eyes that might have been sympathy or concern. She understood as well as I did what being chosen meant in practical terms. We were both targets now, marked for elimination by those who saw us as stepping stones to their own advancement.

The choosing went on for what felt like an eternity but was probably only minutes. More names were called, more initiates marked for special attention. Some received the announcement with visible pride, others with grim resignation. A few showed no reaction at all, which was perhaps the most unsettling response of all.

"Zara Keth," announced a Lady I didn't recognize. A woman with skin that appeared to be made of living starlight stepped forward, her form shimmering and shifting in ways that made my eyes water to look at directly.

"Thane Blackwood," called another Lord. A massive humanoid covered in what looked like natural armor plates acknowledged the selection with a grunt that somehow managed to convey both satisfaction and menace.

Each new selection added another layer of complexity to the deadly mathematics of what was to come. Alliances would form and break, temporary truces would be established and violated, and in the end, most of us would die in the bowels of that twisted labyrinth spread out below us.

It was some time before the choosing came to an end, the final selections announced to a crowd that had grown increasingly restless as the ceremony progressed. The air itself seemed to crackle with tension and barely suppressed violence, as if the assembled initiates were a powder keg waiting for the smallest spark to set them off.

The copper-skinned woman stepped forward once more, her robes billowing in a wind that seemed to blow from nowhere and everywhere at once. Her brown eyes swept across the assembled crowd with the satisfaction of someone who had orchestrated countless such events and knew exactly how they would unfold.

"Group by group you will start down the path," she proclaimed, her voice carrying easily across the amphitheater. "Each group will have an hour before the next departs. The first group consists of..." She began reading names from a list that materialized in her hands like smoke given form.

My name wasn't among the first group called, nor the second. As I listened to the roster being announced, I realized that the groupings weren't random. Each one seemed carefully constructed to maximize conflict and minimize the chances of any single faction gaining too much advantage. The Nyxen were master manipulators, and this was their greatest game.

Molech's name was called as part of the third group, along with several other initiates I recognized from the training hall. The draxi's mercury eyes found mine across the crowd once more, and he made a gesture with one clawed hand that needed no translation. He would be waiting for me in the dungeon's depths, ready to settle the score from our interrupted confrontation.

Kari squeezed my shoulder briefly as her name was called for the fourth group. "Try not to die before I catch up with you," she said with forced lightness, though I could see the genuine concern in her green eyes.

Finally, my own group was announced. The fifth wave, consisting of twenty initiates whose names I mostly didn't recognize. We would enter the dungeon with a four-hour head start on those who came after us, but that also meant we would face whatever guardians and challenges awaited with minimal intelligence about what lay ahead.

As the copper-skinned woman finished reading the final roster, the reality of what was about to happen settled over the assembled crowd like a funeral shroud. This was it. The games that would determine our fate, our future, our very survival, were about to begin.

"Good luck, initiates," the woman said, though her tone suggested she believed luck would have very little to do with who survived the coming ordeal. "The game... has begun!"

The first group of initiates began making their way down a narrow path carved into the cliff face, their forms quickly disappearing into the crimson haze that clung to the dungeon's entrance like a living thing. As I watched them go, I couldn't help but wonder how many would still be alive when the sun set on this blood-red world.

And whether I would be among them.
