Fauna snapped her staff upward, and the air thickened into a dense rolling shroud that swallowed the space between them and Sys.

[Mana Veil]

Fog exploded outward, luminous with crimson thread, not merely obscuring sight but warping the sense of distance. In the fog, the cyclone’s interior became a maze rather than a ring. Sys’s advantage — altitude, visibility, the ability to pick targets and punish them — began to blur at the edges.

And then Tara vanished.

[Blink]

She reappeared high above, near Sys’s left wing, not close enough to strike — yet — but close enough to force his attention upward. Her presence, sudden and audacious, was like a thrown knife. Sys’s eyes flicked to her instantly, tracking, calculating, anger spiking.

Klax took that attention and turned it into an opening.

He launched forward, not upward, closing ground distance in a straight line while the fog hid his approach. His fists flared, layered elements blooming around them — frost and fire and rot — all of it squeezed through Ethan’s Skill-pool like weapons being sharpened as they were swung.

“Dogma—” Klax began, voice low, and then he hit the crater’s lip and jumped, exploding upward with a force that cracked the scorched seabed beneath his feet.

“—STRIKES TRUE!”

[Flurry of Blows]

Klax’s first strike was pure monk — a piston punch aimed at Sys’s midsection, meant to disrupt breath and posture. But it carried Ethan’s law behind it, turning it from a mere hit into a kind of spiritual hammer. Sys tried to Skitter away, but the fog made the angle uncertain; he hesitated for the smallest fraction of a second, and that was enough.

Klax’s second strike carried [Winterbreath], frost blooming along his knuckles as he punched into the air beneath Sys’s wing. The spray in the cyclone froze into brittle shards, creating a sudden lattice that snagged feather and membrane and movement. Sys’s wing jerked as the ice clung, not enough to bind him yet, but enough to make the beat uneven.

Klax’s third strike carried [Ray of Putrefaction], a rot-laced jab designed not to kill but to sap, to make the target's muscle response sluggish. Sys’s Spirit Cores burned hot in protest, purging the effect almost immediately, but even the act of purging cost him micro-moments of focus.

He snarled, and the world around him pushed outward in a violent wave.

[Repulsor Shield]

The shockwave detonated through the fog, shredding it into tatters and hurling Klax backward. Tara flipped away at the edge of it, using her wings to ride the blast like a surfer rides a wave. Fauna staggered, staff digging into the seabed to keep her upright, but her face remained calm in that maddening rabbit-mage way — calm not because she wasn’t afraid, but because she had a plan and she was following it step by step.

But Sys wasn’t about to give up in the face of these odds.

His body split into multiple copies as he forced [Summon Illusion] through their assault.

[Mirror Image]

Five Sys’s bloomed into existence, wings overlapping like a halo of knives. Their green eyes all turned toward the Hybrids at once, and the air filled with the sick sensation of being targeted by a God with too many hands.

Tara laughed.

Not because it was funny.

Because Tara had always been the kind of creature who laughed hardest when the world tried to crush her.

“Cute,” she said, and then she made her own copies.

[Summon Illusion]

Three Taras appeared, each one grinning, each one brandishing daggers. The illusionary Taras weren’t perfect — not to [Eyes of Raziel], not to someone as experienced as Sys — but perfection wasn’t the point.

Noise was.

Confusion was.

Making Sys spend processing power on sorting was.

Klax, recovering midair from the Repulsor Shield blast, used [Skitter] to snap back into position faster than a body that large should have been able to move. He landed beside Fauna with a grunt, shoulders rolling, and the crimson threads along his arms brightened.

“Now?” he asked.

Fauna nodded once.

“Now.”

And they did something Sys hadn’t expected: they stopped trying to chase him.

Instead, they built a cage.

Fauna slammed her staff down and activated [Albionic Authority] — not as a single binding, but as a shaping force. Sigils burned into the seabed in a wide ring, forming a glowing circle around the crater. The symbols weren’t chains yet. They were rules, ancient and stubborn, setting the terms of what could and could not happen inside that ring.

The cyclone groaned as Albion’s law asserted itself, and Sys’s wings beat harder as if he could push the rules away through sheer arrogance.

Tara took the ring and turned it into a trap.

She Blinked to one point of the circle, drove a dagger into the scorched sand, then Blinked again, driving the second dagger into another point.

[Arms of the Vigil]

Spirit Swords erupted from the ground where her daggers struck, not aimed upward at Sys’s body, but angled inward, creating invisible “walls” that forced movement into lanes. Each Spirit Sword pulsed with Ethan’s law, bright and unyielding, and the air between them began to feel like a corridor rather than open space.

Klax filled those corridors with violence.

He launched upward again, not aiming for Sys’s torso, but for his wings. He struck in a rhythm that was monk-perfect: four hits, then a pause, then three hits, then a pivot. He wasn’t punching to hurt. He was punching to make Sys’s wings beat unevenly, to disrupt hover, to force fatigue into the Host body.

Sys responded with brutality.

He unleashed [Twilight Edge], and darkness became a wind that sliced through the cyclone’s interior like razors.

[Gale of Darkness]

The gale carried status effects like poison mist — confusion, paralysis, fear. Tara staggered midair as her mind tried to decide whether the daggers in her hands were meant for Sys or for Klax. Klax’s stance wobbled as his limbs momentarily forgot which muscles belonged to which movement. Fauna’s breath hitched as an icy spike of fear tried to clamp around her ribs.

But then Ethan’s voice — calm, quiet, unwavering — echoed through their shared connection.

Breathe. Anchor. Ignore. He’s trying to break your rhythm. Don’t let him!

Fauna’s eyes sharpened.

She layered Albionic Authority again. Meanwhile, Tara leaned into the confusion rather than resisting it. She made it part of her movement, switching targets unpredictably, her illusions darting in patterns that made no sense even to her — which was the whole point. She turned her rogue instincts into an anti-algorithm, something Sys couldn’t predict because it wasn’t predictable to begin with.

Klax did what monks did best: he endured.

He roared — not with Ethan’s Skill, but with his own stubborn animal soul — and punched through the gale until his fists found Sys’s wing-joint again. The moment he landed a clean hit, Fauna snapped her staff upward and condensed the cyclone’s spray into ice chains that wrapped around that wing.

[Winterbreath]

Sys hissed and tried to burn them away with a burst of Revok’s fire.

Fauna anticipated it.

She used [Shield of Aegis] not as a dome around herself, but as a timed plate in the space where Sys’s fire would have expanded. The flame struck denial, rebounded, and blew back into Sys’s own wing feathers, scorching them just enough to make the next beat ragged.

Sys’s expression finally cracked.

His eyes darted to Ethan.

To the hat.

To the one “Archon” he couldn’t finish.

He tried to reposition—skittering out of the corridor lanes, aiming to climb above the Albionic Authority ring and reset the battlefield again.

Tara saw it instantly.

And that’s when she coughed up a storm of exploding furballs.

It was ridiculous. It was disgusting. And it was perfectly Tara.

The furballs arced upward, wobbling comically in the gale like drunk little meteors, and Sys’s eyes narrowed in reflexive disdain.

“What is—”

They detonated with a wet whump, exploding into a cloud of sticky fluff that clung to Sys’s wings, his face, his sword arm, his eyes. It wasn’t damage. It was not holy. It was not elegant.

It was airflow denial.

And Sys, for all his power, for all his perfected strategies, was still fighting physics.

The fluff disrupted his wingbeats. It made one wing heavier than the other. It made hover uneven. It made Skitter corrections more frequent.

And those corrections — those small, constant adjustments — were exactly what Fauna’s Albionic Authority needed.

The Hopla's staff flared.

[Albionic Authority]

The ring on the seabed pulsed, and Sys felt it. You could see it in the way his wings jerked, in the way his posture tightened as gravity began to remember him.

“NO,” Sys snarled, and he forced [Angel Arm] again, firing downward, aiming to obliterate the ring itself.

But Klax and Tara moved as one.

Tara Blinked in front of the blast and threw up [Shield of Aegis] for a precise five seconds, letting the beam hit denial and rebound. Klax used that rebounding force like a springboard, launching upward and slamming into Sys’s chest with a monk’s palm strike that carried Ethan’s law and Revok’s fire together.

[Flurry of Blows]

Sys’s Host body cracked again, a spiderweb of fractures that glowed green as Spirit Cores tried to patch them instantly. Sys’s wings beat hard to regain altitude—and then Fauna tightened the cage.

She didn’t just keep Albionic Authority active.

She stacked it, pulsing it in sync with Tara’s movement lanes and Klax’s strikes. Every time Sys tried to rise, he felt weight increase. Every time he tried to reposition, he felt “permission” denied at the boundary lines.

Sys tried to summon more Mirror Images, desperate now to flood them with targets.

Fauna silenced him with a Mana Veil, cutting his rhythm.

Tara countered with her own illusions, turning the air into a hall of mirrors where even Sys’s [Eyes of Raziel] had to work overtime.

Klax continued punching, not into random spots, but into joints and wing anchors and muscle clusters, turning his monk discipline into a systematic dismantling of flight.

Sys’s rage became a physical thing, visible in the way the cyclone’s interior trembled. He unleashed [Gale of Darkness] again, bigger, harsher, trying to shred their coordination.

Fauna absorbed the gale’s edge with [Mana Veil], shaping the darkness into a funnel that forced Sys into narrower lanes. Tara danced at the funnel’s rim, carving it with daggers and Spirit Swords, guiding it like a shepherd guiding wolves. Klax drove through the center, fists blazing, enduring the status effects through sheer will and the grounding certainty of his Dogma.

Sys’s wings finally faltered.

Not because he ran out of power.

Because power wasn’t his problem.

Control was.

He had spent too long trying to dominate a planet through theft and mockery. He had never learned how to fight people who didn’t fight like lone predators.

And now three Hybrids — bound together by loyalty and desperation and shared Skill-pool — were fighting him like a pack that wanted to drag him down and tear him apart inch by inch.

Sys attempted one last reset.

He [Skittered] upward, wings beating violently, and then he tried to blast the entire arena with a quadruple [Angel Arm] volley aimed not at them but at the cyclone wall itself — as if collapsing the arena would drown them all and free him from the trap.

The moment he began charging it, Tara screamed.

“NOW!”

And the Hybrids committed to the ground.

Fauna slammed her staff down and made Albionic Authority manifest.

Sigils burned brighter. The ring’s lines snapped upward into spectral bands, not fully formed chains yet, but ropes of law that reached for Sys like hungry hands.

Tara drove both daggers into the seabed at the crater’s edge, and Spirit Swords erupted in a tight formation, forming anchor stakes that gave the spectral bands something to lock onto.

Klax launched upward one final time, his fists coated with a layered combination of Petrification and Poison — not to kill Sys, but to slow him, stiffen him, make his wings hesitate.

He struck wing-joint.

He struck shoulder.

He struck sternum.

Then he struck the wing again, right where Tara had scored it earlier.

The petrification bit.

Not fully.

But enough.

Sys’s wing stiffened mid-beat.

His altitude dipped.

Albionic Authority’s weight seized him.

And suddenly, in the space of half a second, Sys was no longer hovering like a God.

He was falling.

He hit the seabed with crater-making force, shockwaves rippling outward. The cyclone wall trembled. Steam and debris erupted into the air. The ring of sigils flared so bright it painted the entire storm crimson for a heartbeat, turning the fight into a cathedral-lit nightmare.

Sys tried to rise.

He couldn’t.

Spectral bands snapped around his wrists, his ankles, his wings, tightening with merciless patience. Spirit Swords pulsed at the anchor points, reinforcing the binding lines. Klax landed in the crater and dropped into a monk’s pin, using joint locks and pressure points to keep Sys’s Host body aligned with the “rules” Albion had imposed.

Tara hovered above, wings spread, breath coming hard, and she grinned down at Sys like a bat ready to drop on prey.

“Look at that,” she said, voice bright with adrenaline. “Turns out you do belong on your knees.”

Sys thrashed, wings spasming, eyes blazing green with a hatred so dense it felt like gravity of its own. His Spirit Cores surged, trying to brute-force their way out of the bindings, trying to overload the laws that held him.

Fauna stepped to the crater’s edge, staff leveled, her face pale with exertion but her gaze steady.

“Hold,” she said.

And the bindings held.

For the first time in what felt like a thousand lifetimes, Sys was still.

Not because he had chosen to be.

Because he had been made to be.

Ethan felt it immediately.

The invisible gag of Prophet’s Pacification loosened in his soul like a knot finally cut. His Skills returned in a distant rush — not all at once, not with comfort, but with the sharp clarity of a blade being placed back in his hand.

Fauna reached up with trembling fingers, carefully lifting the Demon Hat off her head. She cradled him with a tenderness that made Ethan’s thready little grin falter for a heartbeat, because even now — even here — she was still Fauna.

Below, Sys glared up at them, bound in Albionic Authority, pinned by Spirit Swords, pressed down by Klax’s monk weight and Tara’s hovering threat.

His eyes were wide.

Not with fear.

With disbelief.

Because for the first time, the world wasn’t bending to him.

It was bending against him.

Ethan’s single crimson iris stared down into those four green eyes, and the storm seemed to hush, waiting.

He spoke softly, without mockery now, without bravado, because this wasn’t a moment for performance.

This was a moment for an ending.

“Fauna,” he said, voice low and steady, “place me on him.”

Fauna swallowed, hands tightening around the brim, and then she leaned forward over the crater, lowering Ethan toward Sys’s face as the bindings held, as the Spirit Swords pulsed, as Klax’s fists clenched to keep the angel’s limbs from twitching even a fraction.

And Sys, still glaring, still raging, still trying to brute-force the impossible, finally seemed to understand what was about to happen.

But the Hopla did not move. Her hands quivered as they held Ethan, face torn between him and the dark eyes of the angel that were now fixed on her.

“Faun?”
