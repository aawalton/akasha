Jessica Gerald sat across from Terrel Young, the Portal Tyrant, in a conference room built from portal metal and stone. Real stone—granite, in fact. It felt almost medieval, except for the massive wall of TV screens competing silently with one another for attention. Fully half of them were tuned to different news companies covering the ongoing crisis in Surprise.

Ongoing crisis? Jessie snorted, then pulled herself together as the Portal Tyrant raised an eyebrow. “Sorry. It’s just funny.”

“That’s funny?” the big man asked. “I don’t get it.”

“Well, see, way back when Kade was an E-Ranker, he’d just gotten his Stormbreak merge finished. Actually, do you know about Kade’s build?”

The Portal Tyrant nodded slowly. “Kade and Ellen’s builds are both common knowledge with my research team, with the exception of their specific Laws and what they’re both calling Paths. They won’t say anything about those, and I won’t ask you to tell me.”

“You’re a good guy, Terrel,” Jessie said, forcing a smile and turning so her eyes didn’t have to watch the Desert Wind Building anymore. “Anyway, Kade got his merge done, and he went into overdrive. Everything became about the portal delves. Whichever ones he could get, working out or studying when he couldn’t. It was almost too much—especially when he started running up against Angelo Lawrence’s guild members.”

“Deborah Callahan and her secret team.”

“Right. So, meanwhile, I was stuck in school, and one day, I cornered him and begged him to let me join the Governing Council as a rep. There’s a rule where high-schoolers can work as GC reps, right? And I—“

The door opened. Jessie’s eyes flicked to Kade and Ellen—both looking shockingly clean, and shockingly good in Portal Tyrant uniforms. Her eyes narrowed, and her jaw snapped shut. “You showered before you got here?”

“Obviously,” Ellen said. “You should have seen us before. Disgusting.”

“And the uniforms?”

The Portal Tyrant answered before either Kade or Ellen could. “They’re the only thing we have in a wide enough variety of sizes to fit either of them well, and their clothes were lost causes without a long laundry cycle.”

Jessie squeezed her eyes shut and reached down to push her chair’s wheels, but Kade was there before she could get moving. “I’m sorry, Jessie. I didn’t expect all this.”

“You didn’t expect this? You didn’t expect that the Placid King would try to attack our base? Because I’m pretty sure we discussed the odds of the SS-Plus Paragons attacking Earth, and it was a very real concern,” Jessie said.

Kade sighed. Then he nodded. “We did. But I did mean to put you in danger.”

Oh. Oh! It wasn’t about the building, or Earth, at all.

It was about Kade, Dad, and that stupid promise!

“God dammit, Kade,” Jessie said.

The guild was safe.

That had been the first thing the Portal Tyrants’ gatewatchers had told me.

The second was that they’d moved the Desert Wind Guild into the North Wing, butted up against the 303 Wall. It was unused—the Portal Tyrants were always planning for the future and building to accommodate that vision, just like Phoenix had been with the 303 Loop before the Portal Blitz—and even with Jessie and three teams’ worth of delvers, it felt empty.

Ellen and I had grabbed the first two rooms we’d found, piled into the showers for the fastest, most furious scrub-down either of us had ever experienced, and pulled on the uniforms. Then we’d headed straight for Jessie.

I’d expected her to be upset. To be furious about being in danger from the Placid King’s assault.

Instead, she seemed more upset about the building.

“I’m going to leave you three to it,” the Portal Tyrant said. He stood, strode to the door, and turned. “Kade, I’d appreciate a debrief when you’re done with your guildmaster. Guildmaster Gerald, we’ll have to finish this conversation another time. I’m looking forward to learning more about the Stormblade.”

Jessie nodded stiffly. Her eyes drifted up to Ellen.

My partner took the hint, thank god. She nodded. “I’ll catch up with the two of you later.”

Then they were both gone, and Jessie couldn’t hold back the tears anymore. “I’m not mad at you, Kade. That’s the first thing. Well, a little mad. God dammit. I’ve been trying to figure out what I am since you killed that monster and disappeared into your portal world. Scared? Helpless? Overwhelmed? Dad would have told me to keep using my words and trying to find something, but I don’t know which word fits!”

She kept going. I braced myself, but even though her fists were balled tight, she didn’t try to punch me. That made me wince. She was really mad.

So, as she talked herself out, I waited. The urge to tap my foot or tell her to get on with it was almost overwhelming. I could be doing something. The God of Thunder’s time limit kept ticking down. But would an extra few levels on an ability matter? Tanat was hard at work repairing the Lightning Tower, and there were no A or S-Rank portals open in the Phoenix area. That left me with one move.

“So, Kade, what are we supposed to do? What’s the Desert Wind Guild supposed to do? I’m supposed to be in charge, but I’m so tired of trying to have the answers.” Jessie went quiet except for sniffles.

I waited. Sometimes, she’d take a break, then get going again. When that didn’t happen, I cleared my throat. “Right now, the only answer is the Traynor-Overholz Cannons. They got delivered to—“

“You want me to contact the Crone again? I was just talking to her, Kade, and she told me she couldn’t help, but she could clean up the aftermath. Do you know how hard that is to hear? Do you? I’m so tired, Kade. So tired.” Jessie lifted her elbow. It was swollen and red. “You heard what I was about to tell Terrel, didn’t you? About that night when you let me try to be a GC rep?”

My throat was dry. That feeling of needing to do something grew with every second, but I had nothing to do right now but this. I nodded.

“Well, you’ve been doing your best. So have I. So has Jeff—he’s waiting for you too, by the way—and everyone else.”

I closed my eyes and breathed. Counted. Jessie recognized it for what it was. We’d both learned the same techniques from the same man, after all. I counted and focused on my breathing, and that feeling—and the one of pressure that connected Ellen and me—both faded. Not by much. It wasn’t like I’d suddenly be free from either the pressure to advance my skills or the pressure to prepare for the God of Thunder.

But I’d misread Jessie, and I needed to reset. She wasn’t actually upset about the guild building. It was only a front.

I waited. Then I wrapped my arms around her shoulders and held her close. My purple and black uniform turned wet right away. She couldn’t stop crying, but she pushed herself up out of her chair and let me support her for a long time.

Sometimes, it was easy to forget that Jessie was only sixteen and that she could only handle so much. The Desert Wind Guild had pushed harder than any other guild, for longer than any other guild, and Ellen and I had both done things no delver had—ever. And all that pressure? It didn’t just affect us.

It weighed on my stupid kid guild leader, too.

So, instead of rushing off to my next battle, or focusing on repairing the Lightning Tower and prepping it for the Traynor-Olverholz Cannons, I put all my remaining energy into just…being there for her.

And when she finally pulled away, her face had gotten serious. “I’ll talk to the Crone and make that delivery. You want the rest, right?”

“You’re sure you’re up to talk about this?” I asked.

Jessie stared at me, brows slowly lowering into a half-pout, half-glare. Then her fist lashed out and hit me in the shoulder.

I groaned and fell over.

“Knock it off, Kade,” Jessie said. “And go see Jeff. He’s waiting for you.”

“Will do. I’ll be back again today, before we run out of time. And then I’ll be back again once it’s all over.”

“You’d better be.” Jessie’s eyes bored a hole in the back of my head as I left. When the door finally closed behind me, it was kind of a relief.

Jessie could be intense sometimes.

Jeff was waiting.

He’d been waiting a while, from the looks of it.

He held the null core in his hands, staring at it. Ice had formed on his fingers, but he didn’t make any more to shake it off. I’d given him that core during the Siege of Phoenix, right at the end. It had come from inside the Fallen Delvers Portal, and he’d been uncomfortable using it. What the God of Thunder had told me only confirmed that his feeling was correct; it’d strip his skills back down to nothing.

He didn’t look up, but he nodded his head slightly when I walked into his room. “Hi, Kade.”

“Hi, Jeff. What are you thinking about?”

“Well.”

Silence. I looked around his room as he pondered the null core. It was a pretty Spartan set-up, but that wasn’t surprising. A bed, where he was sitting. A chair, clearly for me. A desk and a dresser. That was it. Simple. Basic. Uniform. But that also meant there wasn’t anything to look at, and after only a few seconds, I had to refocus on Jeff.

“Well?” I asked.

He said something. Then he cleared his throat and said it again. “Your God of Thunder said this was a weapon, right? That you could use it against SS-Rank enemies?”

I nodded.

Jeff swallowed painfully. Then he held it out. “You need it more than I ever will, Kade.”

“What do you mean?”

“You’re up against—no, Kade, don’t give me that crap. I know that you know the stakes. I’ve been holding on to this thing, hoping for a good reason to use it, but if you don’t survive this, there won’t be a point in having it. The guild building didn’t survive thirty seconds of contact with the Placid King, and Eugene—“

“The God of Thunder,” I corrected automatically. I didn’t want to hear that name. Not right now.

“Sure. The God of Thunder’s going to be just as bad or worse.”

“He is. But he knows it’s a weapon, and he’s not going to fall for it.” I shook my head. “I can’t use this.”

“Please,” Jeff said. He stood up and lifted one hand from the core. A few bits of skin stuck to the frigid surface, but he didn’t even wince. “Please, take it. It’s the only thing I can do to help you.”

I took the null core—not because I wanted to, but because he was half-right. This was the only thing he could do to help me. But not the way he thought. “Jeff, remember the last time you visited my place before the portal break that I awakened in?”

“Yes. We spent most of it talking about my build and where I wanted it to go. You barely focused on yourself at all.” Jeff sighed as he collapsed onto the bed and stared at the ceiling. “You didn’t understand it, and I couldn’t explain it. The choices I was making were…they only made sense in my head.”

“Do you regret those choices?”

A long silence. The air conditioning blowing in was the only sound that broke it.

Then Jeff started talking. “I don’t. For a while, after Carlsbad, I did. And I still do, a little—but it’s more about Lamar and Nevaeh. They never found him. He’s dead, and that means two of my people died when I was supposed to be the one protecting them. The whole point of the C-Rank build was to get there as quickly as I could so I could find them, and it didn’t work. So I regret that.

“But I don’t regret the build as a whole. It was the best move I could make at the time, and it did get me to Carlsbad. More importantly, it got you and Ellen to Carlsbad. Without you two, Kent, Amanda, Angeline, Turner, and Nicholas wouldn’t have made it, because that trap Yalerox set would have worked. She’d have killed the Light of Dawn and his team, then finished taking over Carlsbad Fortress. You saved their lives.”

“No, you did, Jeff.”

“That’s what my parents said, too.” Jeff laughed mirthlessly. He looked up, eyes empty. “Most of the time, it helps. That’s why I don’t regret my build. But right now, it’s not helping at all. If I’d done things differently—if I’d known you were going to push to C-Rank so quickly from the time you started—I’d have built a proper build.”

“What would you have done?” I asked.

“More offense. Merge Retaliation with some other skills. It’s powerful, but it’s not like I couldn’t have done better. I just didn’t want to spend the time. Try for a 5-merge build, maybe ask Yasmin to be my Dual Skill Progression partner, but I’m not sure she’d want that. She’s a little more fiery than Ellen sometimes. But more offense. Try to build something with Retaliate that pushes out more damage. Then merge skills for evasion, shield-work, and armor. The final move would be a self-healing skill—something strong.”

“And that’d get you where you want to be?”

He nodded, sitting up slowly. “I want to be S-Rank, with you, Kade. You’ve been leaving me behind ever since you hit D-Rank, and it’s been especially hard since the Siege of Phoenix. First, you went running with the S-Rankers. Then you and Ellen disappeared into your fancy portal, and when you came out, you’d killed a god. It’s been hard, watching you do your best when I hit my best months ago.”

I grinned. Then I handed him the null core.

“What’s this about?” he asked, eyes narrowing.

“Simple. The God of Thunder’s timer runs out in eight hours. When it does, he’s coming for me. I doubt he’ll wait for me to get stronger and build up my defenses. That means I need an edge.” I took a breath. “The God of Thunder told me about this core. He said it was a weapon, but he also said…”

I left the rest unspoken. Jeff, sometimes, could fixate on things. He’d done that here—focused on the core as a trap to use against the God of Thunder. And he was right; we would be trapping him with it.

But first…

“I’ll send you with Jessie to talk to the Crone. She’ll know more about this than the God of Thunder did. We’ll have to move fast, but maybe we can find a solution that doesn’t leave you unawakened.”

He stood up. So did I. His massive arms pulled me in for an embrace, and I returned it. When he let go, I nodded. “If it works, you’re going to be the trap we spring for the God of Thunder. You and what that core will do to you. And then, when it’s over, you’ll get your second chance.”
