First things first: I needed to give them names.

With hundreds, maybe even thousands, of creatures potentially flocking to my castle to become citizens in the future, I knew I needed a proper naming system. It was for my own sanity. I wanted to look at a name and instantly know what race I was dealing with. Otherwise, things would get chaotic and confusing very quickly.

Technically, the most efficient method would be labeling them Goblin 1, Goblin 2, Goblin 3, and so on. But that felt too boring and lazy. These were my citizens now — I needed to at least put in some effort and make it feel personal.

In the end, I settled on the prefix "Gopu." From now on, every goblin citizen I acquired would be named "Gopu-[Something]."

The first two ended up as Gopuro (the male) and Gopuran (the female). Simple, nice, and kinda cool! I liked them.

With the naming ceremony out of the way, I finally moved on to test [Quest Creation].

I activated the skill and studied it for a quick moment.

Turns out, it was far more complicated and interesting than I had expected! The instructions were surprisingly clear and detailed.

This made me wonder… could it be that this System was actually created by multiple different entities?

After all, the one who gave me the [Citizenship Granting] skill was absolutely terrible at explaining anything, forcing me to figure everything out through painful trial and error.

However, the one who designed [Quest Creation] seemed to be very good at explaining things properly.

(Now that’s some interesting thought…) I mused.

****

Anyways, when I activated the [Quest Creation] skill, a blue form floated before my mind’s eye.

[Quest Form]

Quest Name

Prerequisite (Optional)

Quest Info

Quest Type

Objective

Reward

Penalty (Optional)

Quest Name, Prerequisite, and Quest Info were pretty self-explanatory. For Quest Type, I was presented with many useful options. It could be One-Time, Repeatable, Periodic, Chain, or even Group Quest.

If I set a quest to Repeatable, I would only need to fill the form once and have it run automatically forever. That was pretty neat!

I could also set up a chain of quests, just like in classic RPG adventure games.

Then, for Objectives, the System recommended I make them as specific as possible.

And that made sense.

If I set the objective too vague, like “Chop some wood,” the System might not recognize it as complete. But if I set it as “Chop 30 Units of Wood within 2 Hours,” the System could track it automatically, and the citizen would have a much better idea of exactly what I wanted.

And finally, the Reward section…

(Huh? What the hell is a Powerstone?)

I could also put things like meat, resources, or equipment as rewards. Basically, anything inside my inventory. But the System strongly recommended using something called “Powerstone” as the reward.

(But what the hell is a Powerstone? Do I even have a Powerstone?)

Just when I was wondering about that, another notification came.

[Ding!]

[Your Powerstone Balance is: 0]

[Would you like to convert Points into Powerstones?]

And with that, a conversion window opened.

[Conversion Menu]

You can convert your Points into Powerstones.

Powerstones can be exchanged for Points or vice versa at a fixed rate.

System Fee: 10% (Automatically taken off balance after every 100 Points converted).

Exchange Rate: 5 Points → 1 Powerstone (F).

The powerstone icon showed a small, glowing white crystal. And when I focused on it, the description popped up.

[Powerstone]

Description: The materialization of Points in the real world. Contains the essence of power and magic, with endless potential. Wanted by all beings and creatures in Azef.
