Chapter 111

The word problem settled on the bank between them, and Tama went still under it. Riley watched her for a moment. Tama looked like she was bracing herself for a knockout punch, this wasn't an overreaction, it was learned behavior. Korr-va had trained her to brace for the worst and Riley wasted no time breaking this cycle. Riley reached out gently and touched Tama's shoulder. Tama slightly pulled away then slowly looked up at Riley's face.

Tama thought the problem was the charcoal. She thought the problem was her.

It was not about the charcoal. Riley ran the count without meaning to, the way she always ran it. Valrik had the army and the weapon tests. Thorne had the field, the hunts, the two in the cells. Nikola had the queues and the build. Nami had the kiln line and had built it from bare ground into something that fed itself. Landryn had the mine, and the bulk house builds and the road south. Forty-one Hoshin had come through the gate two days ago, and the woman who had held every one of them together under Korr-va stood on the riverbank now with nothing but time. For someone to go from having the world on her shoulders to nothing, that was the problem. A person made to lead, handed nothing to lead, will reach for the nearest thing that is working and try to make it better. The clay pot line had been the nearest thing and Nami was familiar.

"How have you been settling in here?"

Tama's eyes moved off her, out to the drying racks, to the carts with their beds caked grey, and came back. "Good," she said. "Well." Her hands found each other and held still.

"That is what this place needs," Riley said. "Everyone finding their place in it. I'm glad you've found yours." A small pause. "You have, haven't you?"

"I do not know that I have found my place here."

Riley looked into her eyes and said nothing. She nodded, once, barely.

"I was leading them, before," Tama said. "All of them. Now I do not know what I am meant to be doing."

"You lead here too." Riley let that sit a beat. "There is more to do than there are people to do it, and I need leaders I can hand a thing to and not think about again. You. Nami. A few others."

"Help with what?" Tama's voice stayed low and even, but there was a frustration under it now, near the surface. "Everything here already runs. It runs without me, even you."

"It runs for today." Riley turned her head toward the pit, where the smoke went up straight into the cold air with no wind to bend it, and then back to Tama. "Today is not the problem. Tomorrow is. That was the trouble with Hoshin, it ran for today, it ran for someone else."

She kept her voice flat, no weight on the words it did not need. "What we have now was built to get us through today, at the size we were. It works. But we are not that size anymore, and it has to grow, and growing it is work, and the work needs people who can carry it. You are one of them now."

Tama looked at her for a long moment, the lines deeper at the corners of her eyes.

"Then tell me," she said. "I want to help."

"The wicks. You will head them. We can make the pots, Nami's line is making them now. We can make the powder. The wicks are the piece no one owns, and without the wicks the rest is a stack of clay." She let it land. "How fast a wick burns, that is a key part of all of this. Once we know the burn rate, we know how much to cut for a given throw, and how far a man can throw one before the fuse runs out short. That number sets the range of the weapon. Get me that number. Work with Valrik."

Tama nodded slowly.

"Second. The raider loot. Go through all of it, for two things. Every blanket, every length of cloth, anything that twists down into a wick, pull it and set it aside. That is your wick stock." She held up a finger before the next part. "And the glass. There are potion bottles in that loot. I want to know one thing about them. Do they break when they hit something, or do they bounce. Throw a few at a wall and watch. If the glass is thick and holds, it takes black powder and a wick, the same as the pots. If it is thin and shatters clean, we fill it and throw it to break on the target, wildfire, quicklime, whatever burns or blinds. The glass decides what goes in it. Find out which glass we have."

"I see," Tama said.

"Third." Riley paused on this one. "I want some of your people trained to fight."

Tama's eyes met Riley's, very directly.

"A few dozen, that are able-bodied. Valrik will pick them with you." She went on before Tama could fill the silence. "Your hunters know their way around spears, build on that. Bows, blades, with the spears."

Tama looked uncomfortable but continued to listen.

"Not for the wall, for behind it. If the walls are ever breached, there has to be someone standing between what comes through and the children. I want that to be your people, to protect those that cannot protect themselves."

Tama's eyes tightened in thought, and she nodded twice.

"Lastly, a trip to Rivermark. Goods we are short on, and healing potions. The soldiers, when they are hurt, the hospital takes them. It puts them back together. It will not do that for the Hoshin."

"If your people are in this when the fighting comes, and some of them will be, the potions are all they will have beyond the Hoshin healer. Let's get what we need now, and hope we don't need them."

Something changed in Tama's face, it softened then hardened and her eyes welled up with tears that she quickly wiped away.

Riley gave her the moment.

"No one has ever planned for the Hoshin beyond what they could take from them," Tama said.

"That isn't the case anymore," Riley said. "We take care of our own."

Riley and Tama let that moment hang before Riley continued.

"There is a second case for the potions," she said. "Thorne. Nikola. Landryn. I know that the hospital will not heal them the way it does the soldiers, and I do not know that the potions work on them at all, but it's worth a try."

"We take care of our own," Tama said. Riley nodded and continued.

"Sit with Zelgra before you go. She knows those markets better than anyone else here, who to deal with and who to walk past. She stays here, but she will set you up. Valrik gives you an escort. Cavalry, armed, but no battle plate. I do not want a war party riding into Rivermark. I want a trade party that can defend itself. He brings a horse for you and the saddlebags to carry it all home. It is your trip. If you want to take anyone else, that is yours to arrange."

Tama stood there wide-eyed, close to overwhelmed, the river going by behind her.

"Ah," she said. "All right."

"Not in a day or two." Riley's voice eased. "None of this is for tomorrow. Take the time you need. What I need is for someone to manage all this, and for one person to be responsible. Valrik, Thorne, Nami, the others are carrying their own. They will help where you ask. But these are yours to own, to start." She held her eyes. "There is more coming. For you, and for others too."

"I see." Tama's mouth turned, the first proper smile Riley had seen on her since the gate. "I will get started now."

"If you need me, ask."

Tama nodded, and turned, and went back along the bank toward the gate at a pace that had purpose, and Riley watched her go and disappear into the blur of the day.

Riley knew Tama needed something to fill the vacuum that was left from being unburdened of the Hoshin leadership. Going from being responsible for everything to nothing wasn't the path for her, she needed to find her purpose in this new world and Riley hoped she had given her the chance she needed.

***

The sentry on the wall above the gate straightened when he saw her coming and opened the gate so Riley didn't have to slow her stride to enter. As she passed through the gate she gave a nod as the guards snapped to attention, this was something Riley was still getting used to. As she entered the settlement the sound of the river fell behind her and the full noise of the settlement came up to meet it, hammers out of time, the well line, the doubled cookfires.

Landryn came across the yard at her before she had taken six steps inside the gate.

He was in his field coat, the heavy one, a ledger under one arm, and he came at her on a straight line through the moving yard the way a man crosses when he has been waiting for a face to appear and has finally got it.

"Landryn." Riley smiled. "How do you fare?"

"If there were only two of me." The corner of his mouth curled slightly. "I am glad I caught you, Warden."

"How can I be of service," she said.

"It is the mine." He stopped there for a beat. "There have been developments and we need you."

"We."

"Nikola and I, come down as soon as you can."
