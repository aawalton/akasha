“Well, at least it wasn't because of your Doom this time.” Said Kyera as she pushed herself up into a sitting position.

The apprentice Framer was only recently awoken and still in the cot where Alley and Darius had found her. The pair of young men had spent the better part of two hours floating around the medical tent, before Kyera had  come to.

“See, you see,”Darius said with a wild gesturing of his hands at Kyera.

“Huh?” Asked the brown-haired woman before wincing in pain as she stretched and yawned.

“That is exactly what I said, that at least this wasn’t because of the Doom Resonance, but Alley got mad and said I was being inconsiderate.”

Kyera dismissed the idea with a wave.

“I swear that was my last thought before the hydra dropped me.” Alley and Darius had heard some second- or third-hand accounts about what had happened to Kyera from the healers working in the tent at the northern stadium.

Alley let out a noise of vague disbelief.

“You two are ridiculous; if either of you died I promise I wouldn't be over here like ‘well at least it wasn't my fault, that sure makes me feel better’.”

“See, at least you don't have to feel guilty about my mauling by a giant shadow-lizard,” Kyera replied as though Alley had been entirely serious, and Darius let out a laugh.

“So, this Jakeson guy is as tough of a match as everyone says?” Asked the red-headed hunter.

“Jackson,” corrected Alley, causing Darius to shake his head.

“Weirdest name I have ever heard.” The Hunter muttered.

“He seemed like a nice guy honestly, but a real monster once the arena is up. He has a bunch of Cards that lower or get around the Vial cost of his Creatures so it was like playing against someone with a way higher Rank than he actually is.”

Alley had only seen Jackson play the once, but he had been equally impressed by the strength of the strange man's Library.

One of the men working as a healer came over to check on Kyera and almost fell over when Denderra appeared out of nowhere to pick the Framer’s brain.

“The hydra he played?” Asked the white-haired ghost. Without a moment's pause to greet Kyera or apologize to the stunned healer.

“Was it called Manducare?”

The brown-haired woman thought for a moment before nodding.

“Yeah…yes, that was the monster Manducare, Void Lost Hydra. Or something to that effect.”

Denderra let out a long sigh with her cheeks.

“I need to talk to this guy; no one should have the Cards he has in his Deck, they shouldn't even be Cards.”

Alley didn't know how to respond to that, and evidently he wasn't the only one, as the entire group just looked at The Oblivion Maiden as though waiting for her to elaborate. Denderra being Denderra, she didn't. Instead, the ghost just looked between the three of them for a few seconds before vanishing.

It was almost three hours later that Alley managed to get some answers out of Denderra. Now reunited with their very injured apprentice Framer, the pair of young men had alternated between doting on Kyera and mocking her roundly for being the only one of the trio to lose her match that day.

The truth was they all knew there was nothing to be ashamed of; Jackson was an obvious monster. Unless Alley’s memory was failing him, which was possible given he still got occasional flashes of what he assumed to be Anaten’s life, Jackson had also gained a Rank since the opening rounds of the Forstwing Trials. It should have been a little surprising that one of the competitors was gaining power at that kind of rate, but Alley himself had Ranked up twice in the preceding year so he knew that if you had the resources such explosive growth was far from impossible.

The trio had retreated back to the inn they were staying in to lick their wounds once the healers in the northern medical tent had confirmed that Kyera wasn’t about to drop dead at any moment, and her flow of Life Essence had grown strong enough that it should be able to handle most of her remaining injuries. Once back at the inn, which had a name but much like the multi-leveled one they had stayed in with the pair of princes, it was some combination of Frostlander words that Alley found utterly unpronounceable, and hadn’t bothered to try and remember.

Even after the huge stew he had consumed, Alley joined Darius and Kyera for dinner, which the trio ended up having to pay for since all three of them wanted multiple servings beyond the meal which was included in the price of their rooms. What really surprised Alley as he had sat with his friends in the warm common room at one of the five heavy darkwood tables wasn’t anything about today. It wasn’t that he was still hungry enough to eat multiple plates of cooked pork on thick, rich-tasting bread. It wasn’t even that Kyera had lost; while he respected her strategic play, even if he didn’t think much of her Cards themselves, Alley had on some level expected Jackson to defeat her. Truthfully, from what he had seen and heard, Alley wasn’t even certain that he could defeat the man with the weird name and clothes.

What really surprised Alley was just how muted and shy Darius was during the group’s extended meal. There wasn’t a Knots Alley had ever met that wasn’t anything resembling demure without a very strong reason, and nothing had actually happened to shunt his mood off its normal jovial track and onto…whatever this was. When it finally struck Alley what he was looking at he had to cover his mouth and pretend to cough to keep himself from laughing or looking smugly amused.

It was a combination of the current location and the pre-existing company. The most obvious clue to what was going on with the hunter had been his reaction when the youngest of the barmaids had brought them over the group's third helping of dinner. The woman was around the same age as Alley, at most a year or two older, and she was absolutely what one would call “buxom,” and yet Darius had simply thanked her like the other two, and made absolutely no effort to look down her top or flirt with her.

‘He's crushing on Kyera.’ Alley suddenly realized as the pieces of the minor mystery before him clicked together. With a little bit of hindsight, it seemed almost obvious. The pair of them had flirted a little in this very inn, but Alley hadn't thought much of it given that Darius had been pumped full of Pursuit Resonance for years and as such tended to flirt with any women with an active pulse. This was different; now that he knew what to look for, Alley was spotting little oddities in his best friend's behavior that were making it hard for him not to laugh.

Alley decided the only fair thing to do was to retreat upstairs early and do a little reading, so that Darius could make a fool of himself without an audience. Well, he admitted to himself he might try to convince Denderra to come spy on the hunter and the Framer like he had last time.

Inhaling a final chunk of pork Alley excused himself and headed upstairs to his rooms. The wood of the building was a deep brown, though not of the same darkness as the wood of the furniture, and covered in mirrored metal plates, or paintings of bearded men doing epic things. Alley liked this inn, and he was a little sad that he was going to be moving to a different one in a day or two. He knew in his heart that it was simply passing on the burden, but he felt the need to change locations every so often so as to avoid his Doom coming down upon the inn and causing some disaster in the bizarre Resonance’s attempts to destroy Alley.

Grabbing his bag along with a pair of books he’d left on the floor beside it, Alley sat on the edge of his bed. He pulled a trio of books from the bag before dropping it on the ground. He immediately winced and regretted how little care he’d shown. He was going to consider a few options for what he planned on studying tonight, but first Alley had something a little more pressing to do.

“Hey Denderra, you there?” He asked the empty room.

“Yeah?” replied the still empty room.

“I was hoping you’d be willing to tell me why you are so interested that Jackson guy’s Cards, the way you reacted to seeing that Queen of Nothing Card, I’d have to be deaf not to realize you know her.”

“Ugh, do I have to?”

“No, I’d appreciate it though.”

The white-haired ghost appeared next to the door of the room, and Alley was suddenly struck with a sense of deja vu he wasn’t his. One of the leftover memories from Anaten he had been dealing with the last few days. Denderra standing with the exact same pose, but actually alive, and in even more ancient clothing than what she already wore.

Shaking his head to clear it, Alley looked to the for once standing instead of floating Oblivion Maiden.

“I hate you for guilting me into it, but alright I’ll explain.” Alley didn’t quite think that was a fair description of what he had done, though he didn’t care that much since Denderra was once again proving to be a font of potentially useful information.

“You knew this was coming, as soon as you asked to speak to him.” Alley protested with a wry smile.

“Fine.” She said with a sigh.

“I don’t know if you know this, but before you bonded with my brother’s Deck, I, my brother, and the portions of his court that weren’t in the Cards had locked ourselves in Oblivion.”
