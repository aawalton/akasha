Bart picked up the card. It was true, his very first act of archival intake had been exceptionally primitive. He had simply shoved the greasy, crumpled poster into the tray because, at the time, proper provenance had seemed less urgent than having a chair. That poster had become the Infinite Archive's first new holding in centuries, and it had helped the creation of the Crime and Bounties section.

"We need the original," Bart said.

They walked back through the aisles to the old Crime section.

It was still there, sitting exactly where the system had first built it. It looked smaller than Bart remembered. Once, it had been almost the only functioning shelf in the entire building. Now, it was dwarfed by other rows of restored shelves as the archive health continued to improve. The yellow, greasy poster was still on a stand. Bart carefully removed it.

Gregor looked at the crumpled parchment. "That's it?"

"My first acquisition."

"Looks like trash."

"The System made a similar observation."

Gregor frowned. "What's it actually say?"

Bart hesitated,he realized, with a sudden, quiet clarity, that Gregor had never actually been able to read the document that brought him here.

Bart handed the poster to him. "Read it."

Gregor held the parchment close. His finger moved slowly beneath the first heavy, black word.

"Wan..." He frowned. "Want?"

"Wanted," Bart corrected gently.

"Wanted."

Gregor moved his finger to the next word. "Gregor." He stopped and looked up. "That's me."

"Yes, continue…"

His finger continued tracing the ink. "The... thief."

He skipped the crude charcoal drawing of his face and moved to the bottom line.

"Re... ward." Another pause. He squinted at the final two words. "Two pigs."

Gregor lowered the parchment and stared straight ahead.

"Two pigs?"

"Yeah, it seems that was offered in exchange for your capture," Bart said.

"That's insulting."

"The theft?"

"The price." Gregor held up the poster, genuinely offended. "Boss, I was worth at least three pigs."

"That is not the lesson I hoped you would take from this," Bart said.

Bart took the poster back and returned to the Cross-Reference desk. He placed the crumpled parchment on the indexing plate. He wanted it to correlate the original timestamp, the surviving portal transit record, and Gregor’s known origin point.

"Fairpoint."

It was mounted upright on a crude little stand built from stones, small bones, and silverware. One of the goblins was holding the pen aloft, aggressively waving it in the other's face. His words sounded like someone gargling rocks, but the Archive's translation field supplied the meaning automatically.

"MY TURN TALK. YOU LISTEN."
