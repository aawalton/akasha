Anxiety simmered in the pit of my stomach as I twisted the lock on the French windows before turning back towards my mom. She wasn’t even looking at me as she continued to walk towards the front door, and I was glad. I didn’t want her to know how hard I was thinking.

My thoughts were racing, though. I hadn’t expected Mom to want to come on the walk with Rex and me. Otherwise, I never would have texted Duncan and invited him to join us. I would have just waited until we got back before I checked my text from him, and that would have been better.

I needed to text Duncan to tell him something had changed, and we couldn’t meet up, that I’d see him later or another day, but I didn’t have time. I’d hoped that I’d have a chance to do it before we left, but Mom had insisted we go straight away. We were already almost out of my lounge, and Mom was moving too fast. I was running out of time. Soon, we’d be at the front door, and then, it would be too late.

My eyes darted towards Mom before my hand twitched towards the phone in my pocket. I could always pull it out and text him really quickly, I told myself, but I already knew that was a stupid idea. If Mom glanced back over her shoulder, she’d see me on my phone, and I knew she’d demand to know who I was texting. That would be too hard to explain.

I needed to get away from her. There had to be something I could think of that would give me an excuse to hide from her for just a couple of seconds to text Duncan and apologise. I knew there was. I just had to think of it.

My gaze flicked to the stairs as I walked through the doorway into the hall, and an idea gripped me.

“Did you put my bag upstairs?” I asked quickly, trying my hardest to sound normal.

I’d left it in the car, and Mom had said she’d bring it in for me. I couldn’t see it by the door, so either she would have dumped it on the landing at the top of the stairs, or it was in the kitchen, but I couldn’t see it in there. If it were upstairs, I could give Mom Rex’s lead for a second and run up to grab it. Then, whilst I was up there, I could text Duncan really quickly.

That could work.

“Yes. Why?” Mom said, glancing back at me.

Relief crashed into me, but I fought to keep it from my face.

“Oh, great. Thanks,” I replied. “I’ll just grab it quickly before we go, then.”

I held the lead out to Mom as she started to turn towards me, her lips pursed. She didn’t reach out to take Rex from me, and that made me nervous.

“Why?”

My heart thumped as I held her gaze, still holding Rex’s lead out to her. I couldn’t work out whether she was suspicious of me or if she was just still on the verge of becoming annoyed. I hadn’t done enough to mollify her after irritating her in the garden, so I had to be careful.

“For the walk,” I said, keeping my voice as steady as possible. “You know, for the poop bags. We have to bring those, right?”

I knew we did. Mom had mentioned them multiple times when we were shopping for supplies for Rex, and I could see the thin roll of bags on the windowsill by the front door.

“Yes, obviously. But you don’t need a bag to carry those. You’re not going camping,” Mom sneered as she crossed her arms over her chest. “You have pockets, don’t you?”

My hand holding Rex’s lead dropped slightly as I glanced down at my legs. I already knew I had pockets on the leggings I was wearing. I’d slipped my phone into one less than a minute ago, but I had to pretend I’d forgotten so Mom didn’t question me.

“Oh yeah. I guess I can always put them in there,” I said before sending Mom a smile.

She didn’t return it or reply to me at all. She just rolled her eyes as she turned away from me once more and continued towards the front door. I followed her slowly, watching as she grabbed her keys from the small table in the hallway before reaching towards the door handle.

My gaze flitted around the hallway as I tried desperately to think of something before landing on the bathroom door. A silent debate erupted in my head as I tried to work out whether I could get away with saying I needed to use the toilet before we went out. I hadn’t been in ages, not since we were out shopping, and that was hours ago. It was a pretty good excuse, but I was pretty sure Mom wouldn’t buy it. I’d already tried to get away from her once. If I tried again, it would make her suspicious.

Irritation spiked within me. That would have been a much better excuse than going upstairs to get my bag, and I was so annoyed that I hadn’t thought of it first. If I’d said I needed to use the toilet, I would have had way longer to text Duncan. I could have properly explained what had happened, but I’d lost that opportunity because I was stupid.

Mom held the roll of poop bags out to me, and a silent sigh of resignation slipped from my lips. It was too late. I wouldn’t have a chance to text Duncan before we left, so I’d just need to message him once we got back, and I could apologise then. That would probably be fine.

Still, I felt awful about it. Guilt pulled at me as I slipped the bags into my pocket, and I hoped that he wouldn’t be too annoyed or disappointed. He’d probably figure out what had happened, and he’d understand. Or, at least, I was hopeful that he would.

Mom glanced at Rex, checking to make sure I still had him on the lead, before opening the front door and striding out. I walked out after her and pulled the door shut behind me, pushing the handle to make sure it had locked itself.

We walked down the drive in silence before reaching the road at the end. Mom didn’t even hesitate. She just turned towards the centre of the village and began walking, but I paused. Anxiety nibbled at me as I looked around, trying to work out whether it would be weird for me to suggest we go the other way.

It would make sense, in a way. There would be fewer people around if we went away from the centre of the village. It was mostly farms and fields in that direction, and Lily had said Rex was fine around people. He didn’t seem to have any problems with them, but it still might have been safer not to risk it. Not until we knew what he was like.

That wasn’t the only reason I didn’t want to turn right, though. I had another, much more selfish, reason. Duncan’s house was that way. There was a chance, a very slim chance, that we’d go past it, and I wanted to avoid that if possible. It made it less likely that we’d run into Duncan, and things would be weird, or Mom would find out something was going on between us.

But Mom was walking too fast. She was already too far away from me, and it would have been weird to shout after her. I just had to go after her, and I was sure it would be fine. It probably would. Rex seemed well-behaved. There had been other people in the waiting room of the animal rescue centre when we’d come out of the room, and he’d been completely fine. He hadn’t even growled at anyone, so I doubted he’d have any problems on the walk.

I was still nervous about somehow seeing Duncan, but I tried to tell myself that it was unlikely as I hurried to catch up with Mom. We’d probably just stay on the path we were on until the bottom of the road and then do a loop around the village or something. That route made the most sense to me, but I needed to know for sure.

“So, where are we going?” I asked, staring intently at the back of Mom’s head.

“To the park,” Mom replied. “I used to take you there when we first moved to the area.”

Tentative relief began to build within me, and I was glad the path was so thin. It meant Mom had to walk in front of me rather than next to me, so she didn’t see the anxiety draining from my face.

“Oh, really?”

“Mmm,” she said. “It’s still the summer holidays, so there might be a few kids there, but I doubt there will be too many. They shouldn’t bother us too much, anyway.”

“Yeah, it’ll probably be fine.”

That was good. The park was good. We shouldn’t go past Duncan’s house to get to the park. Not unless Mom decided to take the shortcut there, but I doubted she’d do that. I wasn’t sure whether she even knew there was a path that cut through the graveyard to the field at the back of the park, and even if she did, she’d probably want to stick to the main roads anyway, so we were probably fine.

My anxiety had just begun to settle when, to my horror, Mom turned. My heart leapt into my throat as I watched her walk into the overgrown path that led right to Duncan’s street, and I froze.

Rex noticed I’d stopped moving and looked back at me, his expression strangely quizzical. Panic thumped within me, even as I tried to shoot him a reassuring but tight-lipped smile before looking at my mom again.

“Oh, are we going this way?” I called to Mom.

It was risky. Clearly, she intended to go down that path. After all, why would she have started to walk down it if not, but I hoped she’d change her mind because I wasn’t sure that I could do it. I could already see Duncan’s house at the end of the path in the distance, and I didn’t want to get any closer. Not whilst I was with Mom.

Mom looked back at me, her expression annoyed.

“Yes?” she said. “There’s another dog at the end of that road. I’m sure Rex will be completely fine with them, but it’s the other dog I don’t trust. Or their owner. I doubt they’re well-trained.”

She sniffed pointedly, and I glanced back along the road we were on. She was right. Someone had just turned onto the street, and they had a dog with them. It was a little thing, and it looked much smaller than Rex, even though he was just a puppy. I could already see it pulling at the lead in an attempt to get to Rex, and that worried me because Mom could have been right. Maybe Rex would be okay around it, but I had no idea how the dog would react to Rex. It could attack him.

“Good point,” I muttered as I hurriedly stepped off the pavement and onto the worn path.

My thoughts raced as we made our way past overgrown brambles and stinging nettles, oscillating wildly between worrying about something catching on Rex and hurting him and worrying about seeing Duncan.

How bad would it be if we did see Duncan? That was the question my mind kept going back to. He’d know that I hadn’t told my mom about us, wouldn’t he? Or would he walk right up to us and try to hug me?

It could be worse, though. What if he came straight up to us and kissed me? My stomach sank as horror welled within me. Surely, he wouldn’t do that. He knew what Mom was like. I was sure he did. He didn’t know everything, obviously. I hadn’t told anyone that much about her, but I’d known him for a long time.

Duncan had met her enough times to know what she was like, and some things had slipped out when I was drunk. I vaguely remember his horrified face when I’d accidentally mentioned her making me do a drug test a while ago, and that memory still filled me with shame. Even though I was drunk, I should have known better than to mention that.

“Oh, wait a second, boy,” I muttered, pulling on the lead to stop Rex.

He stopped immediately, looking back at me as I carefully pulled a thorn-covered vine away from the path before gesturing for him to continue.

“Good boy.”

Duncan was smart, I told myself firmly. And he probably wouldn’t even know we were there, anyway. I’d said I’d let him know when we were close, so it wasn’t like he’d be waiting at the door to see me. If he did happen to glance out the window at the exact moment we walked past, he’d see my mom, and I was sure he’d know it would be a bad idea to come out. Everything would be okay.

My gaze darted back and forth as we emerged out onto Duncan’s street, and I couldn’t stop myself from eyeing his house anxiously as we walked past. I kept my head low, trying to be as subtle as possible to ensure Mom didn’t realise what I was doing, but she didn’t say anything, and I didn’t see any movement from inside as we approached.

To my relief, he didn’t come out. Either he didn’t see us, or he did and decided it was a bad idea. I was pretty sure he would have texted me if he’d seen me, though, so he probably just didn’t notice us.

That was better, I thought as we neared the end of the street, and the gate leading into the graveyard came into view. It made me feel a little less guilty to know Duncan probably hadn’t noticed how close I’d been to his house without stopping in to see him, but it was fine. I could always see him later or another day, and that would be much better. Mom wouldn’t be there, so I’d be able to actually talk to him without being filled with anxiety. And I could do much more than just talk to him. I could hold his hand and even kiss him.

A smile started to pull at my lips as Mom reached for the latch on the gate, and I looked down quickly to hide it. I didn’t want her to know what or who was on my mind. That would be bad, and I shouldn’t have been thinking about that. Not with Mom there. It felt dangerous, and it was too hard not to smile when I thought about him.

We were so close. We were so close to safety. Mom had just pulled open the gate and stepped across the threshold into the graveyard when I heard a shout that made my heart sink. I wished I could ignore it, that I could hurry after Mom and pretend I didn’t hear the person who had called out for me, but Mom had already turned.

Anxiety vibrated within me as I forced myself to do the same, turning back towards Duncan as he jogged up to us. A pained smile pinned itself to my lips, and I met Duncan’s gaze before shaking my head, my eyes flicking towards my mom. The movement was small. I didn’t want Mom to realise I was trying to communicate something to him, but it might have been too subtle because he gave no indication that he’d seen it.

Rex stepped towards Duncan as he approached, his nose twitching as he strained to sniff him, and Duncan immediately reached down to stroke him before freezing. His hand was centimetres from Rex’s head, but he snatched it back quickly and looked back at me.

“Sorry,” he apologised. “Am I okay to stroke him?”

I glanced at Rex. His tail was wagging, and he pulled at the lead, trying to get closer to Duncan, so it seemed like he would be okay with it.

“Uhh, yeah. That should be fine,” I said, my eyes darting towards my mom again.

She watched, her eyes narrowed slightly, as Duncan bent down to stroke Rex.

“Who’s a good boy?” Duncan asked Rex, causing his tail to wag even faster. “Is it you? Are you a good boy? Aww, he’s so cute, isn’t he?”

Nervousness boiled frantically within my stomach, and I couldn’t help but glance at my mom again before replying to Duncan.

“Yeah, he’s pretty cute.”

Did she remember him? It must have been years since she’d seen him last. He used to come to my house fairly often. We were really close when we were kids, but that was a while ago. I was pretty sure she didn’t know we were still friends, and I hoped she didn’t recognise him. That would be better. It would be safer, at least.

Duncan straightened up, smiling at Rex one more time before laughing awkwardly.

“Sorry,” he said, looking at Mom and me. “Hi.”

My eyes darted towards my mom, watching her reaction closely.

“Hi,” I replied.

“Do you two know each other?” Mom asked, one of her eyebrows rising slowly.

She was suspicious, and she had every reason to be. I opened my mouth to reply, but Duncan got to it before I could.

“We used to go to school together,” he told her, before adding, “Still do, I think. You still go to Brookfield, right?”

I was impressed. That was a good response. He must have assumed Mom might remember we used to be friends, but he was making it seem like we weren’t anymore, like we hadn’t spoken in years, and that was clever.

“Yeah,” I replied.

Duncan’s smile grew wider.

“Yeah, I thought so, but I don’t really remember seeing you last year, so I wasn’t sure,” he said. “How have you been? Have you been in… Ireland for the summer?”

My lip twitched as I fought not to smile. Pretending he didn’t remember where I’d been was smart.

“Scotland,” I corrected him. “Yeah, we got back like last week. Have you been on holiday anywhere?”

“Not really. I went over to see my parents in France a couple of times, but I’ve mostly just been here. I’ve had training, so I couldn’t really go away too much, you know?”

“Training?” I repeated, my tone confused.

A smile pulled at the corner of Duncan’s lips, but he hid it quickly.

“Rugby,” he clarified.

“Oh.”

Silence stretched out between us, and my gaze flitted to Duncan before darting towards my Mom. She looked bored. Her eyes were roaming the street, and I could see the irritation on her face. She wanted to get away from Duncan and continue on our walk, and that was a good thing. It meant she probably didn’t suspect Duncan and I knew each other, and I was glad but not particularly surprised.

He’d done so well. I had no idea he was so good at acting, but he’d skilfully hidden our relationship from my mom, and I was impressed. I was glad I’d seen him too. Even though it filled me with anxiety, I was happy he’d come out to speak to us. At least it meant he knew I hadn’t just changed my mind about him coming on a walk with Rex and me and not told him.

“Well, we should probably be off,” Mom said after a few seconds. “Rex needs his walk.”

“Oh, of course!” Duncan replied quickly, glancing at Rex. “I gotta get on with my run, anyway. Have a good walk.”

“You too,” I said as Duncan began to jog away before wincing.

That was stupid, I couldn’t help but think. He wasn’t going on a walk. He was going on a run, so what I’d said made no sense, and it probably made me sound dumb. It was better than Mom, though. She didn’t even bother saying anything to him.

My eyes darted between Duncan and my mom as she watched his retreating figure, a slight frown on her face. I couldn’t work out what she was thinking or why she was frowning, though. I was pretty sure she hadn’t seen through our acting. She’d barely been paying any attention to the conversation, so it was unlikely, but why else would she be frowning?

“That boy was in your class at primary school, wasn’t he?” Mom asked after a couple of seconds. “Donald or something?”

“Duncan,” I said, making sure not to reply too quickly. “Yeah, he was.”

She shook her head, turning towards the graveyard again.

“Of course. Duncan,” she repeated, the sneer on her face audible in her voice. “Honestly, I have no idea how that boy got into a grammar school. They must have really lowered the entry requirements for him.”

The urge to argue with her rose within me. I longed to point out that he wasn’t stupid. He was smart. He was in the top set in science and maths, so I had no idea why Mom thought he didn’t deserve to be there.

It wasn’t worth it, though. If I did point that out, it would make it clear we did actually know each other, and that would be foolish. There was no point. Duncan was too far away to have heard what Mom said, so I just needed to ignore her. Then, she’d forget about him and inevitably move on to complaining about someone or something else.

As if on cue, Mom sighed loudly.

“Look at the state of those flowers!” she remarked loudly. “They’re so garish. Why would anyone put them on a grave?”
