Glae’s protections did not seem done. In fact, he began to chant even more intently, and his eyes went white, glowing with power. Resonance from his chanting and the mountains became so strong that it shook the ground with tremors.

The antimagic towers, giant orbs of eerie, ghostly white hovering above them, hummed like tuning forks even across the distance.

As the chant reached a fevered pitch, the eerie orbs began to shift. Like yawning maws, like open eyes revealing another eyelid, something deeper within them cracked open and spilled out, bled out, cascading like a rapidly falling cloud. Cloying fog saturated the area, but it was nothing so natural. The particulates glittered with the reflection of something greater.

It was difficult to fathom, but Samantha had the understanding suddenly click through her intense focus in observation, likely aided by her recent quest reward.

Special effect identified: “Supreme Entropy Suppression Field” — Greatly hinders the use of [Entropy] abilities or spellcraft within the area of effect.

Entropy effects are at -15 Effective Levels for all purposes, including caster level. No ‘automatic’ maluses can be enforced. Instead, compare at +4 Effective Levels as a separate contest. Success by the resisting party ignores any malus.

“Motherfucker, you do have a countermeasure for him,” Samantha muttered in wonder. “It wipes out the primary benefit! The unavoidable corruption.”

She was somewhat surprised to see that Glae had gone quiet and was eyeing her through the fog. He was panting slightly, and might’ve been sweating. She thought she also saw surprise. “He cannot overcome that, not against me, not outside the reaches of his domain. Moreover, his arranged spellcraft has absolutely no chance, and raw [Entropy] abilities shouldn’t behave like spells, to operate in lasting contingencies, being spiritually contradictory. These are the reasons I just played my hand. No doubt he senses all of this already.”

He took a deep breath and glanced around. With a gesture, Glae’s little bubble around the platform and him changed into a ‘cage-like’ format with wide gaps. “This is the most I can allow going forward, as the ritual must have majority open vectors to the air and ground. This will protect well against any unusual AoEs, but I’ll remain vulnerable in general. I’m trusting the rest of you to keep me from harm that can fit through these gaps. I can’t stop single-target attempts. Assassins in the back, for example. I must be absolutely focused on this ritual. I will do so even if stabbed or cut to ribbons, but suffice to say, it’s better avoided. We want the ritual completed, after all.”

Protect him from being stabbed in the back? Oh, the irony. “You got it. I’ve still got plenty of power, arcane-less or not.”

To her surprise, Glae had no arrogant retort. He nodded curtly, his eyes still focused on the First Page. “Conduct your final preparations in tactical consideration, and give me the signal to begin, Sam.”

Samantha nodded in turn and cast her mind to the team gestalt. She didn’t have Dax around, and she was always super busy, but fortunately, Michael was present. He was one of the top-tier tacticians and particularly specialized in small-scale things like this.

“Suggestions?” Samantha asked.

Michael was hot on the answer. “A team of seven of mixed specialties around that cage, as a final defensive bulwark: Gabriel, Athos, Gelmak, Bob, the warpriest, Revemort, the frogman shaman, Flugurt, and the elven druid, Aodhan. Some of our most powerful and mobile, like you and Azure, will float wherever they want rather than being confined. Same for most healers and other supports. The majority of Angels should be flying above, as striking cavalry, including me in the center.”

Azure was clearly pleased. “Aodhan is here?! He fights?! You’re disguised, aren't you?”

“Indeed, I am,” Aodhan replied. “I don’t care to risk my antlers in such situations. Other than that, we don’t like missing big events, let’s say.”

“Wait, Bob is here?” Samantha asked almost simultaneously during this conversation. “I thought he was in…” She trailed off, realizing there was no more ‘borderlands front’ now.

The likes of Carlisle, Galynth, Brynn, and Beikiar were integrated entirely into the greater army under Dax. The catfolk sufficed for at least half of the garrisons needed in the Borderlands, especially because many of them got weaker the further from their lands they went, anyway, namely the Servitors. Only the most powerful were the exception, there.

“You bet, Your Majesty,” Bob replied. “Estara is in Itha Ranon, after all, and I’m part of her royal guard, so there I was! I guess Jeeves felt he might as well utilize me.”

“Royal guard?”

“Well, she’s your daughter, so she’s a princess.”

“Wha-?! How in the world do you figure- gah. Nevermind. Fine. Ahem. Michael, please feel free to direct things tactically. Make it quick.”

Momentarily, all were directed to get in their assigned positions. Samantha and Azure were aerial, with Azure mounted on Deathsting and utilizing a handy backup alchemical potion she had for hovering purposes, though she wouldn’t be quite as fast as the spell she usually used. She’d be very inhibited in the fight without much use of her [Dark Mage] class, possibly inclined to hop into melee unless she could chunk destructive potions all day. Her stores were deeper than ever in that department, so it was quite possible if they proved effective offensively.

Samantha saw every reason to use [Sheath of Light] in this situation, especially to leverage its boost to Light-based attacks, which she’d be using quite a bit without arcane spell access — she’d activate the power in time with the beginning of the ritual. It had some disadvantages against stealth and illusion, but she could technically still get plenty of use out of [Conjure] beyond hiding herself. She made sure to protect Glae’s people with her enchantments against being blinded, to avoid the friendly fire situation of looking at the sheath.

Finally, the impatient but quiet Glae was given the nod, and he pulled out an alchemically enchanted, runic forge hammer. Thrusting it forward, he made signs with his other hand and began chanting in a more natural, sharp voice, words Samantha understood fell right in line with what she’d seen of the preparations so far.

The hammer head lit up with the ghostly runes, and the charge in the air picked up. In fact, it exploded outward, cascading far and wide, as the platform and the ritual itself began to extend, touch, and tap into the foundational matter and spirit of Calrenazzod as a whole. Reactions, she felt, were quite eminent.

Samantha prepared herself and triggered [Sheath of Light], encasing her body in raw, cackling energy. Simultaneously, the shaman, Flugurt, hit her with the delightful, beloved, glorious shamanistic Haste buff, stacking with her existing effect in the way only their spells could.

The combination made her faster than ever. ‘Light Speed’ wasn’t literal, but she was certainly closer to it.

I seriously need to keep a frog shaman in my back pocket.

The first thing that became obvious as Glae’s ritual dared to touch the soul of the world was a vast interlace of fizzling spells exploding in their face. She counted at least twenty, and there were likely more. The antimagic zone had probably saved dozens of lives at the least, and quite possibly the rest of the environment — everything other than the handful of individuals who might resist the contingency destruction effects, such as Samantha, Glae, Azure, and most of the minotaurs, but she was just guessing. Numerous spells would’ve sufficed to disrupt the ritual at the very least.

Crammed into a fraction of a second, but sequential to this, a small cluster of primarily destructive Entropic spells dropped and did not fizzle thanks to their own special rules of some kind — however, their heinously weakened levels, even coming from the mighty Sage of Man himself, made them easy pickings for Glae’s own additional layers protections to completely absorb and not allow them to see the light of day.

Haha! Yes! Suck it, Sage!

And then finally, Glae was proven wrong, as some singular ability spawned in a wide AoE, heavily weakened but totally unblocked, casting over the ritual and the day itself like a spiritual shadow. Samantha had little time or specialty to understand it, not being a spell, but she nonetheless got a general sense of the effects — it was designed to corrupt and degrade the focal point of a ritual.

That’s probably not good.

She had no way to counter it, being an ability. She wasn’t sure an anti-ability ability even existed.

Glae showed no signs of reacting, as if he didn’t even see it, such was he focused on what was in front of him, the ritual he was literally in the middle of starting.

A certain woman in white robes and a red mask floating in the air — one of Glae’s Negators — thrust out a strange staff with two rune-encrusted cubes on both ends. They lit up, and suddenly, six other Negators nearby jerked as their spirits seemed to be pulled and stretched out from their bodies, each glowing ghostly white. In obvious pain, they endured it stoically as their ‘soul-stuff’ was reduced and reduced to concentrated arcs of antimagic swirling into the staff’s cubes, three to each side.

Enormous networks of thread-like energetic nets flowed back out in hundreds of layers, and each of them seemed to absorb a portion of the resolving Entropic ability and slow it minutely. Again and again, the layers did so until the ability was completely frozen and still, unresolved and stopped, as though suspended in time.

She kept holding out the staff, and the six linked to it writhed and shook with effort. “We’re stuck like this!” the woman called in a thick Naugite accent. “We’ll need protection, too, as we’ve only paused it!”

“Glae was right about demons!” Azure called from atop her wyvern. “Heads up!”

All around, outside the stones of the ritual grounds, dozens of portals split the air apart, as twisted, eldritch things charged out of them.
