I’ve seen injuries before. Hell, I’ve caused worse injuries. But seeing them on a friend is a different level of horror entirely.

Swearing angrily, I summon my Apothecary. It barely fits in the tunnel, appearing over the top of the wyrm’s body and collapsing around it—I hear some glass break. I barely care, however, because I’m scrambling to find my health potions.

With an affinity like poison, my magic technically falls within the field of life arcana, which means I can brew Health potions. But poison is one of the weakest fields of healing (for obvious reasons) so the couple I’ve made took way too much effort and aren’t good for much more than healing cuts. I also have a couple more potent healing potions that I purchased a while back, but haven’t yet used because they cost me a small fortune. I don’t hesitate to grab them all, anyway.

My felis claws snag on the Apothecary as I fumble with the potions, and with a frustrated growl I whip the weapons off. My hands feel exposed and raw without them, but all that matters is getting the potions back to Cyros without breaking any.

Cyros is wincing, trying to fend off Álvaro when I return with the health potion. My invisibility is starting to rub off, now, but the floating potion bottle probably tells Cyros everything he needs to know, he grimaces, holding still, as I carefully pour the potion over the open wounds on his torso. The gashes hiss, and a tail of smoke rises as the potion makes contact.

Cyros’s frown becomes a little less pained, but the injuries don’t heal. Or maybe they do, and it’s just too little for me to notice. I focus on the wounds and give him a Check.

[HP: 62/100]

[Status: Hemorrhaging]

[Status: Acidic corrosion]

Crap. Why couldn’t it be something like getting poisoned? I was prepared for poison. I can do something about someone being poisoned! I return to my Inventory, scrambling to find the anti-lindwym acid antidote I’d prepared in advance. That should stop it from getting worse, but it won’t close the wound.

Gradually, like ringing clearing from one’s ears, I can start to hear Álvaro and Cyros.

“...have anything else?” Álvaro asks me, gesturing to Cyros. “More health potions?”

“Those were all I had,” I say, springing back to Cyros’s side with the antidote. I try not to panic as blood continues to pour from the wound. I swear the antidote over his wound.

[Acidic Corrosion status nullified.]

“Can’t you make more?” Álvaro asks.

“I can, but it’ll take a while,” I say, mind racing. “I’d need to start a pot of water—”

“It’s okay,” Cyros says, his labored breathing sounding anything but okay. “Sal, you need to complete the job for me. There’s someone I need you to take the adderstone to.”

His words shoot through me like ice. “No,” I say, stabbing a finger at him. “No, absolutely not! You’re not dying here. I’m going to fix this.”

Cyros tries to push himself up, then sinks back with a grimace. “It’s okay, Sal—”

“Like hell it is!” Álvaro cuts in. “She’s right, we’re not leaving you here to die.”

Cyros huffs out a pained laugh. “Not sure I’ve got much choice in the matter. If I don’t get these wounds closed, soon…”

No. No, no, no, no! I refuse. I won’t let this happen. I tear through my Apothecary, searching for anything that could help. I grab some leaves known for helping to coagulate cuts, and I shove them in Álvaro’s hands to press into Cyros’s wounds before returning to my supplies. Can I stitch him shut? I’ll have to hope any lingering acid doesn’t dissolve the thread. I’ve never stitched anyone closed before, but—

Something small clatters to the floor. I grab it automatically to throw back in with the rest of my supplies, then pause. It’s a dracid scale. Piria’s scale.

For a brief moment, defiant irritation bubbles up from within me. I hate being reliant on others. I hate bringing others into my problems. They always get hurt—always.

But Cyros needs help, and I care about him more than a bruised ego. I snap the scale in half.

I have no idea how long it will take for her to reach us. In the meantime, we need to get out of the danger of this den. If another one finds us, it’s going to be doubly hard to take it down while keeping Cyros safe. I start strapping everything back down in my Apothecary.

“We’re getting you out of here one way or another,” I say. I toss some more bandages at Álvaro while I continue to secure my supplies as quickly as I can manage. “First we need to move you out of this cave.”

That wakes Cyros up. “What? No! The lindwyrm—”

“Álvaro, grab the stupid wyrm already,” I snap.

“I can’t,” he admits. “We only knocked it out. It’s still alive.”

And they let me sit with my back to this thing the whole time?! “Fine—grab the one I killed, then. It’s back around the corner.”

Álvaro and Cyros both look at me in surprise.

“You killed one?” Álvaro asks, sounding impressed. “On your own?”

“Its hide,” Cyros says. “It needs to have an intact hide.”

“Oh my god!” I cry. I’d shake him if he wasn’t bleeding out. “Seriously? That’s what you’re worried about right now?”

“But—”

“I didn’t damage the stupid hide,” I hiss. “I poisoned it. Now Álvaro, hurry up already!”

He doesn’t need me to tell him a third time. Álvaro leaps to his feet and sprints down the tunnel. I turn back to Cyros.

He’s frowning at me. “You’re not strong enough to carry me out of here. Dryads are heavy.”

“I’m not just going to leave you to get eaten by dragons, idiot.” I Check his HP, and it’s lower than it was before I used the healing potions.

“Lindwyrms,” he can’t help but correct me. “Dragons have six limbs.”

I swallow down a tightness in my throat. “That’s not what’s important right now.”

He huffs out a pained laugh and closes his eyes.

“Hey!” I say loud enough to startle him. “Keep your eyes open.”

“Right,” he says. “Sorry.”

“You’re not the one who should be apologizing right now,” I grumble.

“No,” he says. “I am. I’m sorry I used you to get this job. I knew you wouldn’t say no.”

If I can keep him talking, maybe that will help keep him alert. “Why did you want it so bad, anyway?”

“The adderstone,” he says, wincing. “I need it. For my family.”

I blink. Cyros has never mentioned family before. I didn’t think he even had any. “Why can’t you get a healer?”

“Some things can’t be healed the conventional way.” He nods toward my hand, where I’m missing most of my fingers. “But adderstones are different. More powerful.”

“And random,” I add, recalling Echo’s description. “It’s a roll of the dice, isn’t it?” Which is why they’re not an S tier reward in the first place.

“It’s worth it,” he says. “Just for the chance.”

Álvaro comes jogging back down the tunnel, and Cyros falls silent.

“Got it,” Álvaro says, slowing as he reaches us. “Damn, Sal, how did you manage to kill that thing without a scratch on you?”

I glance down at the refiner, currently dormant and unassuming. I almost deflect, but it doesn’t really feel fair after Cyros has been vulnerable with me. “The remnant helped me. I’ll tell you about it later. Right now we need to get Cyros out of here.”

Álvaro winces, glancing around the tunnel. “Easier said than done.”

He’s right. Together we could probably lift and carry Cyros, but it won’t be with any degree of carefulness. Neither of us are particularly muscular. But we have to do something.

“We could make a stretcher with the tent poles,” I suggest. “Keep him rigid. Might be easier to carry—and won’t strain the wounds.”

Álvaro nods. “Good idea. Tell me what to do.”

Huh. No arguments from him right now—no jokes or flirting. I wonder how much of that is just a mask. Or maybe a coping mechanism.

Not that I would know anything about either of those.

Before I can start to lay out the tent poles, however, a breeze brushes against us. Álvaro and I both freeze as the wind becomes stronger, producing a low howl through the tunnels. The air has been so still since we got down here. What’s happening? Is something big coming our way?

I stand back up, glancing either way down the tunnel as I brandish my weapons and double check which potions I have left. I’m running low, but it will have to be enough. Something appears at the end of the tunnel, and the wind gusts forward, nearly knocking me off my feet.

I raise a hand before my face to block the wind as tears are pulled from my eyes. Then it stops, all at once, and I stumble forward. Clawed hands reach out to catch me.

“Oh dear,” Piria says. “Am I too late? I came as quickly as I could.”

Relief floods me; Piria is here to take control of the situation. And that same relief fills me with self disgust. I’m not a kid anymore. I’m not someone who should be waiting for an adult to step in and fix everything for me.

Yet, that disappointment evaporates in the face of concern when Piria starts tending to Cyros.

“I’ll be placing a spell over your body to keep you still,” she informs him. “You won’t be able to move, but it should prevent further injuries. Is that okay?”

He nods weakly, and the dracid gets to work. I Check what she’s doing, and Echo reports back that it’s some sort of spatial magic. Álvaro watches all this with wide eyes.

“She’s a champion?” he whispers to me unsubtly. He’s clearly Checked her as well.

“Yeah.”

“Woah,” he says, sounding impressed. “I’ve never met a champion before.”

I look at him in disbelief. “I’m a champion.”

“Well, yeah,” he says. “But you don’t count.”

“And those two champions of Widengra who attacked you last week?”

“You guys are all low level,” he says. “Nothing like her!”

I narrow my eyes, but he’s not paying attention. I mean, he’s not wrong; we’re all new champions and Piria’s been around for hundreds of years. Still, though. I deserve some sort of recognition.

“Okay,” Piria says, turning to us. “Ready?”

She doesn’t cast space arcana spells on us like she did on Cyros, instead spreading her hands as a spell circle lights up on the ground, surrounding all four of us.

“Hold on,” she says, and the spell activates.

It feels like someone grabbed my stomach and is pulling me inside-out like a worn t-shirt. The world warps into a spiral, then snaps back into clarity. Abruptly we’re outside.

I hiss, covering my eyes as the sunlight stabs into my heightening vision, and Álvaro also yelps, slapping his hands over his night vision goggles.

“Sorry!” Piria says. And then we warp again.

I stagger to my knees after the second warp. Álvaro leans over and throws up. My vision is spinning, and it takes all my willpower to not do the same. Piria doesn’t apologize this time, and just focuses on her spell.

It’s a tortuous trip back to the city. I’m glad she’s got Cyros suspended and immobile, because the trip would be rough for him if he could move. It takes another few minutes and twenty or so warps before shade abruptly falls over us, and I realize we’re back in the city.

With one final warp, Piria brings us to a healing house. I’ve never used one, because the Guild has its own more covert healing services to avoid scrutiny, but the Blackcloaks are the last thing on my mind right now. A goblin hurries into the room, and Piria and the healer quickly begin to talk. I’m briefly drilled on what caused the injuries and how long ago they occurred, then Piria, the healer, and Cyros all are whisked into a back room.

Álvaro and I are left panting and bewildered in the front room of the clinic. I look at Álvaro, at a loss for what to do next. He looks at me. Then he turns away and heaves out the window. I wrinkle my nose in disgust.

I sink to the floor in exhaustion, the action of the last hour finally catching up with me. Piria. Cyros. The lindwyrms.

And the remnant, of course.

Instead of sitting there and stewing about it, I first remove my Apothecary, which takes up nearly the entire room. Grabbing a handful of supplies, I pull them out, then vanish the Apothecary back into my Inventory. I pop a foul tasting weed in my mouth and begin to chew it, and when Álvaro has finished gathering himself, I hand him a rag to clean up.

“Thanks,” he says haggardly, sliding down the wall to sit by me. “That didn’t bother you at all?”

I continue to chew on the starweed, a licorice-like herb known for settling stomachs. “Nah, I’m fine. Want some tea?” I already got out a pot, some tea leaves, and a consumable heating spell scroll; I activate the circle to start boiling some water.

“Please,” he says, knocking his head against the wall. “God. What a day.”

I sigh. “Tell me about it.”

We sit there in silence for a minute as the water begins to simmer.

“How’s your Role Requirement?” I finally ask.

“Quest complete,” he says. “You?”

I gesture to the water. “This would have completed it if I hadn’t done so earlier.”

“Hm.” He frowns at the steaming water. “You’re pretty diligent about meeting your Requirement each day.”

“Yeah,” I say shortly. “Just takes missing it one time to never let that happen again.”

He nods along like he understands. And actually, it occurs to me that maybe he does. It’s kind of nice having someone else around who gets it.

I appreciate that he doesn’t ask for any elaboration. I take the water off the heat and start steeping the tea. Peppermint. It’s what Mom used to make for me whenever I felt nauseous.

Mom.

My throat tightens, and I busy myself with preparing the cups. I leave a thumbprint of grime as I do, and I wince at the filthy state of my… well, everything. I retrieve some clean rags and roll up my sleeves.

As I do, my sleeve snags on the refiner, which slips free from my wrist. I fumble to catch it, but it still goes rolling across the floor. Álvaro stops the black marble with a finger, then picks it up, curiously turning it around in his hand.

“You said the remnant helped you beat the lindwyrm?” he asks.

I eye him warily. He’d mentioned before how much he wanted a remnant of his own. Is he going to try to steal it from me?

Then, to my surprise, he holds it out. I cautiously take it from his hand.

“Yeah,” I say, suspicion bleeding into faint guilt. “Sort of by accident.”

“Tell me about it,” he says.

“Why?”

He gestures around the room. “Got anything better to do?”

I snort. Fair enough. I finish cleaning up, then pour out some tea for both of us. Better than sitting here and doing nothing other than worrying about Cyros, afterall.

So I tell him what happened with the lindwyrm. How I thought I’d be able to steal some mana from it to maybe feed to the remnant later—how I didn’t expect it to wake up, eat all the mana I stole, and then act on its own.

I roll the marble around in my fingers. “I can’t sense it now. Like it went back to sleep.”

Álvaro laughs. “Guess it ate too much. Think more mana would wake it back up?”

I hesitate. “I don’t know. Last time it woke up it infected a dragon with my poison mana and killed it.”

“Well I’d really appreciate not being killed,” Álvaro says, far too upbeat. “Can you ask it to not do that?”

I stare at the marble skeptically. “I don’t know. Can I?”

Álvaro shrugs. “Nye could. Well, they had to work past some things first, I guess, but after that they seemed to get along pretty well. Nye said their shield talked to them pretty regularly.”

“It does think,” I agree, feeling for the hairline crack in the refiner. “And it can certainly feel. I didn’t feel like it was trying to talk to me, though. It barely seemed to register I was there.”

“Huh,” Álvaro says. “They really do seem to have different kinds of personalities. The sword was an asshole.”

I snort. “Sword?”

“Oh yeah.” Álvaro grins. “I didn’t tell you? I was in this whole competition to try to win it, but this girl got to it first. Probably for the best, honestly, ‘cause it turns out the sword had anger issues.”

I grin, trying to imagine a sword with anger issues, and sip my tea. Álvaro uses the opportunity to launch into a retelling of the adventure he went on, and his interactions with the two remnants along the journey.

Gradually, I sink back against the wall, allowing myself to relax. I’m not sure if this was his intent, but it’s a good distraction from what’s going on in the other room with Cyros. I find listening to him talk is actually kind of nice, and as the afternoon drags on, I let the words wash over me.
