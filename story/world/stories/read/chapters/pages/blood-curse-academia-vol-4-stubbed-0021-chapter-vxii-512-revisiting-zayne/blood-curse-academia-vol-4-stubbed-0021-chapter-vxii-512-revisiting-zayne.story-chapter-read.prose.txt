Chapter V.XII (5.12) - Revisiting Zayne

Kizu swirled the potion in his hand. The silvery liquid churned in the glass vial. Not for the first time, he wondered if this was an idiotic idea. But his meeting with Vincent had lit something in him. A reminder that the answers and secrets of his sister were within reach. It was simply a matter of seizing those results for himself.

Kizu sat down in his hammock. “Better to not pass out on the floor,” he muttered to himself. Logically, he should tell Anata and Ione about this plan. Really, he should tell Taroe just to be safe. But instead he sat on Owl’s Respite alone, save for Mort watching him from on top of the table the skeletons played cards on. He justified the decision by telling himself they might talk him out of the task in front of him or even try to forbid this. But in truth, he was simply embarrassed. Embarrassed that he was too weak to reach his goal without this.

Without further thought, Kizu threw his head back and downed the potion. He closed his eyes and took in a deep breath. The smell of wet wood. The crash of the waterfall in the distance. The rock of the ship beneath him. He took it all in. Then he opened his eyes.

“Hello, Kizu.” A handsome dark, tanned man stood in front of him. He wore clothing that exposed a swathe of his chest and leaned on a spear.

“Zayne,” Kizu greeted his soul stowaway.

“Any particular reason for this social call? You don’t seem to be in trouble at the moment.”

“I need help. My sister is in the far west.”

“And you hope I can guide you to her?” Zayne frowned. “The world’s changed a great deal since I once lived. And my homeland is far. Even if I knew where we were right now….”

Kizu chuckled. “No. I don’t need you as a guide. Though I guess that’s an interesting thought for once I get over there. What I need is your help with astronomy.”

Zayne blinked and tilted his head. “As in…the subject you study at your academy? You want me to tutor you in astronomy?”

“No.” Kizu wet his dry lips and maintained eye contact with the soul spirit. “I want you to give me your memories.”

Zayne gave him a wry smile. “After thousands of years among the souls in the aurora, I hardly recall them myself. And you want me to grant them to you?”

“I want the same experience as what you did when you showed me those visions of your past. With the fallen star and the forging of the lamp. You mentioned in those past visions that you knew the star would fall. Not to mention how I originally found you among the souls in the stars above. You must have some grasp of astronomy.”

“A grasp of it,” Zayne admitted. “Though I do not know how well that knowledge translates into those paper tests you’re fond of taking.”

Kizu shook his head. “I don’t care. I’ve researched the other astronomy students in the ranks above me. To reach their level of competence using the textbooks and lessons they used would mean backtracking on my understanding of the stars from my lessons with the crone to firm my foundations in their methods. That’ll take months, if not years, of study. And I have too many other subjects that need my attention as well.”

“You see this as a short cut,” Zanye observed. “Do you understand that to burrow through a dune rather than climb over it requires far greater effort to reach the destination in a shorter route?”

“I just need to get there fast,” Kizu said. “If you’re willing to gift me the memories, I’ll be in your debt.”

“There is no debt between us,” Zayne replied. “I do not wish to begin to count favors to one my existence is tied to.”

“So you’ll do it?”

“I will do…what I can.” The phantom of a man lifted his spear and twirled it. It left his hand, swirling into the air above his head. It sliced through the open space, not creating even the slightest noise. As his fingers touched to reclaim the weapon. Kizu’s world shifted.

A thousand times over, he sat among star charts, watching as a man marred with thick burn scars explained in a raspy voice the differences in the skies and stars above. Then meanings derived by the movements and the minute details of what small changes symbolized. The man made Kizu…or was it Zayne? Regardless, the scarred man instructed how to use different scopes and even the technique to create the glasswork used to magnify the stars from above. Kizu wanted to ask why they didn’t just simply magnify their vision with enhancement spells or potions, but the words never left his lips in any of the thousand lessons taught by the man. The body Kizu existed in was in different conditions in every single lesson. Only the proximity of the experiences let Kizu be aware of the differences. Hunger, weariness, soreness, boredom, excitement. Those were simply emotions. But there was more than that too. The body itself grew and took shape. Muscles built. Nearly a meter of height gained. Hair grew only to suddenly be cut short again.

Years upon years of knowledge poured into Kizu’s mind.

And then it was over in an instant.

All save for a single memory, which began to play out. A continuation of the memories Kizu had experienced from Zayne before today.

“A lamp?” Lahel sounded both outraged and exasperated as she confronted Kizu. “This metal of the gods, the greatest boon ever granted to mankind since fire itself. And you used it to forge a lamp.”

Kizu set the offending piece of metalwork on the sandstone table between them. It was gorgeous, the golden metal glinted off the firelight burning in the braiser. At a glance, a fool might believe it to be a simple design. But anyone with a true eye for talent could see the skill and passion set into the gentle curves of the oil lamp.

“The blacksmith said this is what the metal longed to become. A bearer of light. An object to transform the evil essence from the dead into a servant of good.”

That last phrase gave Lahel pause. She stopped her berating of Kizu and leaned in closer to the golden oil lamp.

“Could this…do you think it could counter one of Sekai’s children?”

“What?

“Obviously it won’t do much against Dragons. But the Shades are creatures of darkness. It only makes sense that something like a lamp might counter their essence.”

Kizu stared at the oil lamp. “Counter…or contain? What if it could transform them into power?”

They both stayed silent for a few minutes, lost in thought. They had been granted an object from the stars. A similar gift granted all Dragons their wings. What sort of weapon could it serve as for humankind? Not for the first time, Kizu desperately wished that the blacksmith could have been successful with his attempt at the spearhead.

“Any ideas on how to test its effectiveness?” Kizu asked.

“No resistance group I know of has captured any Shades, if that’s what you’re asking.” Lahel barked a laugh. “How would they? There are stories of those who’ve slain the Shades, but how do you capture a shadow?”

Kizu gripped his spear and considered the problem. Was there another solution? What was he missing?

Lahel picked up the lamp and examined it, turning it over in her hands.

“What if instead of attacking Sekai’s descendants ourselves, we offer it to someone better equipped than us. Someone who has just as much reason to hate Sekai as we do. Use this creation as a bargaining chip for us to gain power.”

It took Kizu a moment to process her suggestion but when he did, he took a step back, his eyes widening. His grip tightened on his spear. He didn’t like the idea of handing over responsibility to some other entity. But if what Lahel proposed truly worked….

“You mean….” He licked his lips and glanced over his shoulder at the empty doorway. If word got out about this plan.

“Genie.”
