Never before had Leo heard such a lackluster introduction. It made him laugh.

“It seems that the Scarlet Vine spirit is still here,” she said when she returned. “This spirit was supposed to be the first, but was delayed. It will still go up for auction, so let me reintroduce it.

“This is the Clear Line spirit. As mentioned, it has no explicit power. You can inject raw cora into it to make its lines solid, but it’s not suitable for fighting walkers. Its first and second evolution requirements are simply to expand and refine one’s core, but the third is unknown.

“Bidding will start at one thousand credits.”

A collective murmur of annoyed patrons rippled across the room. Despite their annoyance, Hayden, disappointed, pressed the bid button to add one thousand.

Leo burst into piercing laughter. The fact that he broke Hayden so hard that he was bidding on a kiddie spirit was just too much. It begged the question: what next? Part of Leo wanted to let Hayden have it, just so Hayden would have to walk around with a training spirit. The other part was severely tempted to deprive him of even that much. Leo considered it, taking it down to the wire when Hayden was about to win it for 5,000 credits.

Screw it. Leo grinned and doubled the bid—but the transaction was denied. His eyes widened. What the…

A man in the back suddenly walked over, publicly—as if he wanted to humiliate Leo—and said, “You’ve been cut off, sir.”

“Cut off? Do you know who I am?”

“Yes, sir. We appreciate your patronage, but your father has frozen your bank account.”

“He seized my bank account. Why?”

“I’m not sure, sir. You’ll have to contact him. In the meantime, you will have to stop bidding.”

Leo pulled up his bank account as the man walked away. There was a pending transaction of 57,200,000 credits.

Leo’s world spun as he saw the number. No wonder… he thought. Somehow, Leo had gotten carried away and ended up spending fifty-seven million credits on useless spirits. Sure, there were a few Cs, but even if he sold them at auction, he’d only get ten million back in total. Maybe fifteen. What had he done…?

He turned to Hayden and found him wearing his classic apathetic expression. It was the expression he always had—no matter the situation. In fact, it was strange that he didn’t have an apathetic expression during the bidding. Was Hayden playing him?

No… he couldn’t have…

The bidding continued. The price was now to twenty thousand somehow. Hayden was still bidding on it, but the offers came in conservative increments of two thousand credits.

Who else is bidding on this spirit?

Leo looked into the crowd. Then, in a surreal moment, he heard Kayline whisper, “Tell Dad to stop,” to Lina. Lina glanced at Ritrix’s private box. “You tell him.”

Leo’s world spun. Ritrix Iska was the person bidding from the nearby VIP booth.

Down below, people were saying, “Not this again… what the hell’s happening?”

“A clown show. It’s probably a bunch of rich kids.”

The conservative bids were a marked change from Leo and Hayden’s behavior, and when the price reached one hundred thousand, speculation festered like an unwrapped wound. It only increased when the bid crested a quarter million for a training spirit. At half a million, there was a constant buzz of whispers, breaking any semblance of a silent auction.

Hayden kept bidding until the very end. For the first time, Leo saw real frustration—genuine frustration—on Hayden’s face. It wasn’t dramatic—it was subtle, eerie, and cold.

Kayline whispered, “Should I ask him to stop?” and that’s when Leo finally understood.

He really wants this… Leo looked at the stage. This wasn’t a mistake…

An insane puzzle of memories solved itself in Leo’s brain, giving him a moment of bizarre clarity. Hayden being in a skybox. Ritrix Iska showing up. The generous companion resources for the won bids. The Clear Line spirit coming out late—and Leo getting cut off right before it happened.

Leo felt demented for thinking it, but… was the entire auction for Hayden?

Hayden’s arrival. The sudden auction… He turned and realized that Hayden was sitting in the VIP seats of the general section. Such a concept was so nonsensical that he didn’t even notice.

There’s no way… Leo thought. He looked at the current bid: 980,000 credits.

Hayden placed a one-million-credit bid—face cut from marble—and the auction house fell silent.

Ten seconds passed, and then the auctioneer waited silently before saying, “Sold for one million credits!”

The house exploded with strange excitement. Hayden didn’t so much as breathe a sigh of relief. He just continued to watch dryly until the very end.

As much as Leo didn’t want to admit it, he had been played. He implied that he wouldn’t let Hayden buy a spirit, so Hayden goaded him into spending fifty-seven million credits before buying the one he wanted.

Anger simmered within Leo as he waited for the auction to end. He was almost hallucinating as he thought about all the wonderful threats and declarations he would make before he made them come true. He wanted to see Hayden’s face when he realized that Leo wasn’t just rich—but also connected.

It was going to be grand.

“You did that on purpose, didn’t you?” Kayline whispered to Hayden with a smirk.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” Hayden replied. He didn’t express any emotion—

—but Leo knew better. That’s why he snapped and strode up to them. “I think you do know what she’s talking about.”

“Hey, back off,” Lina snapped, stepping in front of him. Leo wanted to grab the bitch by the hair and throw her, but her father was in a suite thirty feet away. So Leo just sneered and said:

“I’m free to talk to guests. Now move.” He navigated around her and confronted Hayden. Hayden didn’t look the slightest bit concerned.

“You screwed me,” Leo declared. “You think I’m someone who can be screwed?”

“You screwed yourself,” Kayline said.

“No, I didn’t. I was led into a trap.”

Lina scoffed. “Do you think what you’re saying is remotely… normal?”

“Yes. If someone goads—”

“I think this isn’t a proper place to make a scene.” A cool voice spoke behind Leo, making his arm hairs prickle with frizzy white bumps. He turned and saw Ritrix Iska staring at him.

“Mr. Iska…” Leo said nervously. “I was just explaining—”

“How you made a fool of your father?” Ritrix asked. “I contacted your father, but Jeela beat me to it. Do you understand how shameful it is to have a galactic auction house’s owner contact your father?”

“S-She what?” Leo stammered. “Why did she do that?”

“Why? I’m unsure if this will surprise you, but people don’t like spending two days traveling to an auction, only for someone to buy every spirit there.”

“I…” He looked at Hayden. “He was also bidding toward a million!”

“A shrewd strategy, considering he got the spirit he wanted.” Ritrix studied Hayden with sharp eyes. He looked back at Leo. “You should call your father.”

Leo clenched his fists, dreading the fallout. He glanced at Nara. She looked away.

Ritrix walked past Leo but stopped in front of Hayden. Ritrix didn’t turn to him; he simply spoke while facing the door. “If you have secrets that could end the transportation strikes… I’d prepare them.”

“We’ll be in touch,” Hayden said.

Ritrix turned to his daughters. “I believe you’ve proven yourself cordial?”

“Yes, father,” Kayline said. She bowed slightly to Hayden; Lina did the same. Then, the Iska family gracefully exited the skybox, addressing no one as they left.

Leo boiled over with anger when he saw Nara smirking at him. He was going to kill Hayden.

Once the Iska family left, and no one was there to protect Hayden but Jora “soon-to-be-dead” Hedra, Leo turned to Hayden. “I swear to God, Hayden. I’m going to—”

The door opened, and Jora’s face paled when he saw who walked through it. It was a beautiful woman with golden, curled hair, wearing a red dress that matched the Phoenix Room theme. She had black and gold eyeliner and hazel eyes that glittered with wrath as she glowered at Leo.

Leo’s lips quivered. “Ms. Rian…”
