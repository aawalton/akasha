There were a hundred and ninety-three cultivators participating in the tournament. Therefore, one random person got a bye victory every day. On the second day, that turned out to be Gary. It was a pleasant and unexpected surprise, which didn’t particularly matter.

The pairings of each day were randomly decided at the end of the previous day’s fights. They weren’t announced, but every cultivator was given a two-hour timeframe in which they’d fight, so as not to force them to spend their entire morning in the arena. The person who wouldn’t fight was announced. Since Gary knew it from the day before, he spent the entire second day training at the clan. It was particularly peaceful—almost everyone was at the city.

His training routine stayed the same. The only difference was that he’d swapped Fan the Spear for the First Song. For the entire morning and afternoon, with a long break in the middle to practice aura control, Gary experimented with the aura intricacies of the Song of Mud.

Identifying which parts of his body to vibrate harder, and by how much, took him the entire morning. By the end of it, he knew how to enrich the part of his aura field where the spear would pass through in a thrust, while leaving the rest of his aura field at roughly the same intensity as before.

During the noon break, he practiced the aura control work-out given to him by Master Colorful. Surprisingly, that synergized well with the control and maneuvering required for the Song of Mud, making Gary glad he had a capable teacher at his side. This could have taken a lot longer otherwise.

He also didn’t forget to check how his friends had done.

Alicia: We both won!! It’s amazing, Gary. We’ve been training hard since we lost our spears, and it’s finally paying off! Is this how you feel every time?

Gary: Pretty much. It’s addictive, isn’t it?

Alicia: Yes! I’m just afraid of what will happen when I lose…

Gary: There’s always someone stronger. Just do your best.

Alicia: Yes!! Are you enjoying your bye day?

Gary: I used the time to train more. It was very helpful.

Alicia: Okay. I better leave you to it. Train hard!

Gary: I always do. Thanks, Alicia. Enjoy your lunch.

In the afternoon, Gary realized that while he’d figured out how to tune his aura field for the Song of Mud, actually implementing it was hard. His aura was all over the place during heated battle. Accurately vibrating certain parts of his body to certain patterns demanded not only precise control, but also great concentration. At present, he was only able to achieve it with time and preparation. An afternoon’s practice made it easier, the simplest vibrations becoming muscle memory, but there was still a lot of work to be done.

Gary worked deep into the night and only went to sleep when the moon was high in the sky. The next day, he awoke at the same time as Cog, and they met with Alicia before heading for the Shores of Glory. Gary hoped this day would be as uneventful as the previous ones.

However, while Gary thought the tournament was proceeding normally, the reality was anything but…

Gary Steel stepped into the spectator stands under the clamor of conversation. It wasn’t about him. It felt nice to be part of the crowd for a change.

“It sure is packed,” he said.

“It was the same yesterday. I think the people are really enjoying this year’s tournament,” Alicia said.

“Maybe it’s because of me,” Cog said.

“Hey, look! It’s the baldinator!” someone cried out. Fingers were pointed at Gary. Soon, a wide circle had formed around them, with people whispering all over.

“It was too good to be true…” Gary said with a sigh. Strange questions reached his ears as he waded through the crowd.

It’s not my fault my hair is fine! It was forged in lightning! Gary protested.

They reached their seats and Gary sank deep, hoping the crowd would forget about him. At least, he wished his battle would come quickly. He already knew he’d fight in the first two hours of the day.

“A quarter of the disciples have been eliminated already,” Cog said, seating down beside him. “The remaining people aren’t weak, though, so the battles will take longer. I think it will last six hours and a half.”

“Why didn’t you become a wizard, Cog? You’re clearly very intelligent,” Gary asked.

“But I don’t want to be a wizard. I want to have a spear!”

The crowd livened up once the four deacons entered the arena. They were the same as before, but they swapped who was the announcer every day.

“My name is Deacon Lily, and I will be announcing today’s pairings,” said the deacon. “Esgeth Wille versus George Copper; Optus versus Fay Rest; Pop Ear versus Greatest Disregard; Lin Wei versus Ole Carl. Disciples, please attend!”

Gary perked up at the mention of Pop Ear. He was one of the three who’d first approached him for Muscle Sap. The crowd, however, reacted to a different name.

“A family cultivator!” someone exclaimed. “I wonder if he’ll lose.”

“I remember this guy! He lost yesterday, too! If he loses again, he’ll be eliminated!”

“A family cultivator eliminated so early? This is news!”

“They deserve it, the pricks. Go, Ole Carl!”

“You got this, Ole Carl! Show him the power of hard work!”

As the disciples jumped into the arena, Lin Wei didn’t bear the usual arrogance of family cultivators. He still scoffed at the audience, but worry was etched into his eyes. Opposite him, Ole Carl raised a confused fist into the air. The crowd cheered.

“You got this, Ole Carl!”

Ole Carl was a rough young man, no more than eighteen years of age. He was wide-set and heavily-built, however. Gary could easily picture him plowing a field or making furniture. His spear, a brown one of heavy wood, hung on his back from a well-made, polished sheath.

Ordinarily, a match between this farmer-made-cultivator and a dignified descendant of the Lin family would have seemed like a joke. Now, however, the atmosphere was reversed. The crowd was supporting Ole Carl—it was Lin Wei who was suffering.

“Fight!” the deacon announced.

Ole Carl used strong and polished moves to clash against Lin Wei, who favored water techniques—similar to Sui Duang. They were equally matched. Even after the other three matches had ended, theirs was still ongoing. Finally, Ole Carl prevailed in a straight-forward clash, overpowering his opponent by just a hair. Lin Wei tumbled to the sand, soiled and humiliated.

“Serves you right!” the audience exclaimed.

“Go home to your mummy, loser!”

“Resources mean nothing if you don’t work hard!”

Lin Wei stood with a dark face and directly left the arena. The crowd’s booing turned into cheers.

“Was that Lin guy a bad person?” Gary asked.

“No idea,” Cog said.

“Helen gave a small speech against the families yesterday. Most importantly, the regular disciples are winning against the family ones—this hasn’t happened in years! The crowd doesn’t particularly care, I think, but everyone loves an underdog.”

“Either that or they’ve suffered their part of oppression by the families and clans,” Gary muttered. “There’s no way all the family disciples are losing, right?”

“Well, duh. But they’re losing more than usual. I don’t think I’ve ever seen a family disciple eliminated on the third day. Sure, maybe he was weak, but it’s not like Ole Carl is anyone special either. The family disciples were always better than the average disciple; now, that slight difference seems to have flipped. Decent disciples can fight them evenly.”

“I see,” Gary said.

“This is a great batch of disciples! Maybe the Heaven Reaching clan will rise again!” someone in the audience said.

“Gary!” a voice came from the side. Caramel walked over, bursting with excitement. “Are you seeing this? We’re winning!”

“This is the year we dominate, Caramel. Down with the families,” he replied.

“Hell yeah!” she exclaimed. She dragged him into a hug. “Thank you…” she whispered in his ear, then rushed to congratulate Pop Ear who’d just returned to his seat.

Gary watched her. Apprehension warred with excitement inside him. He opened his chat.

Gary: Are you seeing this?

Chloe: I’m not in the arena today, but I know what you’re talking about. We’re making a difference, Gary. A real difference.

Gary: Fuck yeah.

“Alicia Everglade versus Lord of Pot. Disciples, please attend!” came the announcer’s voice. Alicia stood.

“Good luck!” Gary and Cog told her as she jumped on-stage.

The Lord of Pot was a man in his early twenties, tall and lanky. He bore a green spear and had his hair in jives. While he looked like a lord of pot, that wasn’t the reason for his name—his grip on the spear was only one-handed, while the other held a small iron pot.

“Hey, that looks like my pot,” Asim said. Gary jumped.

“When the hell did you get behind me?”

“Just now. You had empty seats.”

“These are only for Heaven Reaching disciples.”

“So don’t tell on me. Chloe ditched me today, and I’m not watching the whole thing alone.”

“Hi Asim!” Goldie said.

“Hello Asim. Looking sharp!” Cog said.

“Huh. He really does,” Gary said. Asim wasn’t dressed in his usual attire today. He wore a set of short-sleeved black robes with purple trimmings, ending in pants underneath. His hair was tied back into a man bun, showcasing a jaw more angular than Gary remembered.

“I assume you chose the satanic circle,” Gary said.

“We held the initiation rites yesterday. Poisons were good, but summons won me over.”

“Summons?”

“And I can still stab people, of course.”

“Okay, but summons?”

“Shh. It’s starting.”

Gary turned to the arena sands just in time to see the Lord of Pot throwing his pot forward like a homemade comet. Alicia dashed aside to dodge, but he was already there, thrusting his spear. She leaned back. Her own thrust flew wide, and that’s when the pot inexplicably struck her hard at the back of the head.

Alicia tumbled forward, blood visible through her hair. The pot was already in the Lord’s hand, who shot it forward again. It crashed hard into the sand, filling the air with grains. Alicia was already moving, stumbling into a sideways rush towards the Lord of Pot. His spear came in a predictable slash. She leaned to dodge and dive into his guard.

“Behind you!” Gary roared. Everyone around him jumped, as he’d infused his voice with aura to make it heard, but he didn’t care.

Alicia tried to abandon her attack but there was no time. The pot struck her at the back of her head, exactly like the last time, with a dull ringing sound. She fell forward. Her face got buried in the sand.

“Winner, Lord of Pot!” the deacon exclaimed, flashing over the fallen Alicia. Gary and Cog had already dived onto the sands. While the two of them rushed to check her condition, Asim landed heavily in front of the Lord of Pot. Though he was shorter than the other man, Asim got in his face and glared calmly.

“You will not harm my friends,” he said.

“Chill it, little bro,” the Lord replied.

“Please vacate the arena,” the deacon said. The other three battles were still ongoing. People rushing in to take their fallen friends was common, but standing off like this was not.

“Where I come from, we stab people for less,” Asim said.

“Where’s that, bro? Knifeville?” The Lord of Pot chuckled alone. He made to grab his pot from the sands, but Asim kicked it away.

“Disciples!” the deacon’s voice rose alongside his aura, submerging them in a world of heavy water. Neither Asim nor the Lord moved their locked gazes.

“That’s enough, Asim,” Gary said, arriving beside him. His gaze landed heavily on the Lord of Pot. “You were much stronger than her. You could have taken it easier. You knew the first strike gave her a concussion.”

“It’s a fight to incapacitation or surrender, bro. She didn’t surrender. Which part don’t you understand?”

Gary gave him a long stare. “I hope you remember this.”

“Hey, fuck you, bro. We’re here to harm each other. If you have a problem with that, take it up with the deacons.”

Gary gave him a last stare and turned away. He and Asim jumped back to the stands, where Cog had already rested Alicia on an empty row of seats. A small crowd had formed around them—Gary spotted Caramel, Pop Ear, and Helen.

“Will she be okay?” Pop Ear asked.

“How should I know?” Cog replied.

“Let me,” Asim said. He leaned beside the unconscious Alicia and placed a hand on her forehead. Purple energy trembled around his hand, diving into her forehead like hungry tendrils.

“Um, you’re healing her, right?” someone asked.

“I’m gauging her condition,” Asim said. He opened his eyes and retrieved his aura. “She’ll be fine. The pot hit her hard, but everything is working. She’ll wake up in a few hours, dizzy but healthy.”

“Thank God,” Caramel said.

Meanwhile, the Lord of Pot was sitting much higher in the stands, not looking at them at all.

“I’m all for Alicia, but the rules did say incapacitation or surrender, right?” Cog asked. “I mean, he wasn’t wrong.”

“Who gives a shit? He harmed my friend, so we’re enemies,” Asim said.

“Rules or not, he was the stronger party. He really could have taken it lighter. You don’t see Feng Shen crippling his opponents,” Gary said.

“We could have a little chat with him at the clan,” Pop Ear said, smacking his fists together.

“Yes,” Asim said.

“What are you saying, guys? The guy’s an asshole, but we don’t beat people in alleys just because they’re assholes. We aren’t bullies,” Gary said.

“Are you serious, Gary? How many people have you challenged to Duels of Honor so far?” Asim said.

“That’s different. They were enemies. The Lord of Pot was just heavy-handed in a tournament. He’s an asshole, sure, but Alicia is fine. It happens. Just let it go.”

Cog crossed his arms. “I hope he runs into you and you fry his stupid jives.”

“I don’t fry hair, they fall,” Gary said.

“I bet I could make a lotion that makes hair grow faster,” Asim said. “I bet I could sell it.”
