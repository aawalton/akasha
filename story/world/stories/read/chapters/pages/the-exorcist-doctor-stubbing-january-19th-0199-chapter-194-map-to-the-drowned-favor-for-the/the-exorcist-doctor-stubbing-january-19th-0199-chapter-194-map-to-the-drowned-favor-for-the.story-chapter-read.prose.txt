One thing Gael learned about growing wings quickly was that nobody ever mentioned the back pain. The serrated, half-transparent wings themselves were cool as the pale, but nobody ever wrote poems about the hours beforehand, when entirely new biological structures were rearranging the flesh beneath one’s shoulder blades. The pain was a constant reminder that the human body had never been designed to undergo such mutations, so he’d been enduring it all afternoon—and it still wasn't finished. Unlike Maeve, who already sported a new beautiful pair jutting out the back of her dress, his wings were still growing.

Oh well. The pain wasn’t anything one more bottle of alcohol couldn’t fix, so he took another swig as he stood before his giant wheeled chalkboard in his surgical office, twirling a stick of chalk in his other hand.

The rest of the office behind him was absurdly crowded. Maeve, Jin, Vivi, Fergal, Evelyn, and Liorin were scattered among the furniture, while nearly two dozen children from Miss Umbrage’s clinic, upon reading his message, occupied everything else. They sat on crates, desks, cabinets, windowsills, and even the floor, filling the room with more fishy smells than any place containing scalpels ought to tolerate, but he did call them over. This matter concerned them. Over the last three months, some of the children got their friends and families back from being artificial Myrmur Hosts, but only some. For the most part, the rest of them were still waiting on him to deliver.

So after just one more swig, he tapped his chalk against the empty board and glanced back at everyone.

“All right, folks! We’ve now interrogated… er, interviewed our fifty-first artificial Myrmur Host, and he’s currently downstairs sleeping off the surgery,” he said, thumbing towards the windows. “But he’s given us yet another useful piece of the puzzle, so I think we can finally stop staring at individual pieces and start figuring out what miserable picture they belong to. Cara?”

The office door opened in the back, and Cara lumbered in carrying enough paperwork to make all the kids and teenagers visibly recoil. If it were just two cardboard boxes full of transcripts and documents, it wouldn’t be so scary, but she had four more on her shoulders and two more stacked on the tip of her toes, and somehow she was balancing all of them at the same time. She crossed the office without ceremony and dumped the boxes onto the surgical table, rubbing her shoulders in relief.

“This is every interrogation transcript—”

“Every interview transcript,” Gael corrected.

“That we have,” she finished, scowling at him as she patted the top of one of the boxes. “Everyone, start reading. Let’s get this done quickly.”

But Evelyn stared at her, then at Gael, looking confused. “Hold on. You called us all the way back here because you said you’d figured out where the bastard might be hidin’. I thought that meant you’d already done all the borin’ bits.”

“That’s not what I wrote in my letters,” Gael countered. “I said I had enough information to probably figure it out, but that there’s so many goddamned transcripts that I can’t possibly work through all of them without going insane. Therefore, you’re all my slaves for the next six hours. Go grab a transcript, each of you, and tell me anything remotely interesting.”

And that was enough asking from him. Cara began distributing the transcripts, and after only several more moments of grumbling, everyone found somewhere to sit and read. Even the children received pages, usually shared between two or three of them so the older ones could help the younger ones through the more difficult words. Soon the office settled into the scratching of paper, distant rain against the warehouse windows, and the muffled activities of the makeshift clinic downstairs.

It didn’t take long for the first useful detail to pop up.

“Host number thirty-five remembers the lighting,” Maeve said from the couch, holding her transcript in one hand and playing with her new wings in the other. “He said the room was mostly dark, but there were dim red lamps along the walls. They pulsed periodically. He compared them to alarm lamps used in some Vharnveil clinics.”

Gael immediately wrote that down on one end of the chalkboard. “Dim red lighting. That matches three of the earlier patients as well. Anything else?”

“Host number fourteenth said the room was cold, but not freezing,” Fergal said from the corner by the desk, right beside Cara. “He remembered being able to see condensation on some of the metal surfaces.”

“Cold surgical environment,” Gael murmured, adding another line. “Reasonable enough if the Breeder is keeping biological materials there for long periods. And?”

Vivi raised her transcript next. “This woman remembers the floor trembling every few minutes. She thought there was heavy machinery nearby at first, but… she said the movement wasn’t regular enough to be mechanical. Sometimes it happened repeatedly, and sometimes there were long gaps.”

Cara looked up from his own pages. “Heavy machinery doesn’t rule itself out, but irregular shaking sounds more environmental.”

“Or something large moving nearby,” Jin added. “Several Hosts already had Myrmurs inside them by the time they woke up. Their senses might’ve been distorted.”

Gael wrote both possibilities down instead of choosing, but more and more details began to emerge as everyone worked through the pages. Curved metal beams overhead. Chains hanging from rails. The constant smell of salt. A faint mechanical humming beneath the floor. Pressure in the ears. Strange groaning sounds that some Hosts mistook for whales. Quickly the chalkboard became covered to the brim with environmental descriptions, and Gael occasionally stopped to draw on the other end of the board, connecting all the details into one large, albeit very shoddy drawing.

At one point, the children of Miss Umbrage’s clinic even began to give him accounts of Myrmur Hosts who had vague recollections of how and where they were taken. Rebus claimed one Host remembered being approached by a sickly woman asking for help carrying something across a narrow bridge before he suddenly lost consciousness. Bran found another account of a man who’d heard a sickly woman begging for a physician, and was led away from the crowds into a dark alleyway. Then Nixy found a third account, and before long, they had over ten rough points of kidnapping scattered all across Wraithpier. Gael extended the side of the giant unfoldable chalkboard and began drawing a map of the northern ward from memory, though once again, it wasn’t a very good map. But as long as it worked…

An hour passed by with lots of chalk dust. Two hours passed by with lots of arguments and repeated readings. Three hours passed by with map corrections and several increasingly ugly versions of the lab’s drawing. By the end of it, Gael’s back somehow hurt worse than before, but his wings had finally pushed out from under the tail of his coat, and they started fluttering by themselves without his command.

Maybe they were just excited. Or bored. Either way, at the end of the fourth hour—and night was starting to fall outside—he stepped back from the giant chalkboard and planted his hands on his hips.

Everyone gathered behind him, looking up at the chalkboard expectantly.

On the left side of the board stood the rough drawing of the reconstructed laboratory: a large dome reinforced with metal ribs, glass spanning much of its upper structure, with the main surgical chamber sitting underneath the glass. Also, there was water—apparently a great deal of it—right beyond the glass dome.

On the right side of the board stood the rough map of Wraithpier, with most of the abduction marks in ‘X’ clustered near the northern edge of the ward.

All of them stared at the consolidated information for several seconds.

“... It’s underwater,” Gael muttered.

Maeve stepped closer, studying the drawing. “And if multiple Hosts saw giant fish swimming above the dome, then the entire lab must be deep underwater.”

“That explains why we haven’t found a damned thing on the surface, then,” Fergal grumbled, his expression darkening as he looked between both the drawing and the map.”We’ve searched just about every warehouse, abandoned building, sewer passage, dockyard, and old ruins in Wraithpier. If it’s deep underwater…”

Jin stepped forward and traced the abduction marks with a finger. Most of them were not only near the northern edge of the ward, they were also concentrated near the centermost northern edge of the ward. “But if it’s out under the open sea, how are we going to break in? Can any of our ships sail all the way out there?”

Gael was about to say there was nothing his floating fortress couldn’t handle, but Rebus, Bran, and Nixy shook their heads immediately.

“If ye mean past th’northern seawaters, that ain’t lake water anymore, Mister Jin,” Rebus said. “Yer barges ain’t goin’ out there. Matter of fact, none of ours can either.”

“Open sea’s different than lakes,” Bran explained. “Storms come down harder, currents drag ships sideways, an’ waves get tall enough t’roll little boats clean over. Ye take any barge beyond th’seawater in bad weather, ye’ll come back as driftwood.”

Cara frowned at them. “But people do fish out there, don’t they?”

“They do,” the three of them said simultaneously. “But th’only people who do, and have th’ability to not die out there, are…”

They didn’t finish their sentence, but when they exchanged a difficult look, Gael already knew what they were going to say.

Rubbing the beak of his mask, Gael glanced back at his chalkboard and considered the amount of time it’d take them to develop suits and ships capable of working out on the stormy open seas. But the answer was probably ‘a hell of a long time’—more time than he was willing to spend just sitting around Wraithpier waiting for Arnell to deliver the first working prototypes, anyways—so maybe it was too optimistic for him to have thought he could write them out of the equation so easily.

At the end of the day, he was operating in their ward.

“... Well then.” He sighed, turning to grin back at everyone. “It seems we do need the Fishermen’s help after all.”
