The Mission Hall was as busy as ever when Ning arrived, with disciples moving between the mission boards while several lines stretched out before the counters.

As usual, Ning headed toward Si Sihua's counter, only to slow when he noticed something different about her. She was sorting through a stack of mission slips with one hand while lazily supporting her cheek with the other, looking much the same as always, but the aura surrounding her had grown noticeably denser.

Ning examined her for a moment before smiling. "Congratulations on reaching the Seventh Stage, Senior Sister."

Si Sihua's hand stopped. She slowly raised her head and stared at him, and Ning quickly realized that there was remarkably little joy on the face of someone who had just achieved a significant breakthrough.

"Ning," she said, her voice carrying an ominous weight.

"Yes?" Ning blinked.

"Was it true?" Si Sihua asked, staring directly at him.

Ning hesitated. "Was what true?"

"Everything you told me before, that I couldn't remain a salted fish forever, that working hard now would let me relax properly later, and that reaching a higher cultivation would give me a longer life to spend doing absolutely nothing." Si Sihua leaned farther across the counter as she spoke, her expression growing increasingly aggrieved. "If all of that was true, why am I suffering so much now?"

Ning's smile became slightly strained. "Ah."

"My mother found out that I was finally taking cultivation seriously, and do you know what she did?" Si Sihua asked. Before Ning could answer, she slapped a hand against the counter and continued bitterly, "She became supportive."

Ning blinked.

Supportive parents in this economy?

"First, she bought me pills and cultivation resources. Then she started checking my progress every few days." Si Sihua began counting her grievances on her fingers. "Once I reached the Seventh Stage, she decided I needed a proper combat technique. Then came a movement art, and now she's arranging actual combat practice for me."

Her expression darkened as she reached what was apparently the greatest injustice of them all.

"Yesterday, I had to fight three people."

Ning quietly looked away. "Haha."

Si Sihua's eyes immediately narrowed. "Don't 'haha' me."

"Senior Sister, this is all for your future." Ning resorted to a line he himself had heard far too many times.

"I knew you would say that!" Si Sihua groaned and slumped over the counter, drawing a few curious glances from the nearby disciples. "I shouldn't have been lured in by your sweet words back then. I had a perfectly good life. I came here, worked at the counter, collected my contribution points, went home, ate, slept, and occasionally cultivated when I got bored." She sighed wistfully, as though remembering a paradise forever lost. "It was peaceful."

"At least you've reached the late stage of Qi Condensation," Ning said, trying to soothe the lazy girl.

"And now I'm constantly getting hit," Si Sihua countered, clearly unconvinced that this was an improvement.

Ning nodded sympathetically. "Progress often comes with sacrifices."

Si Sihua gave him a long look. "You're the sacrifice I'm considering."

Ning wisely decided that congratulating her any further might be dangerous, although inwardly, he couldn't help feeling somewhat vindicated. Senior Sister Si really had been slacking off. She hadn't done anything special; she had simply started cultivating seriously and reached the Seventh Stage remarkably quickly.

That alone said quite a lot about how little effort she must have been putting in before.

Naturally, Ning possessed enough survival instinct to keep that observation to himself.

Instead, he took out a small package and placed it on the counter. Si Sihua's attention immediately shifted toward it, and a considerable amount of resentment disappeared from her face when she peeked inside and discovered the familiar spiritual snacks.

"The usual," Ning explained.

Si Sihua quietly pulled the package toward herself.

Ning nodded approvingly. Snacks continued to demonstrate remarkable effectiveness in maintaining interpersonal relationships.

"Anyway, Senior Sister, I came to commission another information mission."

Si Sihua finally returned to something resembling professionalism and picked up her brush. "What are you looking for this time?"

"Anything related to the Sevenfold Pure Origin Art, particularly the First Fold. Personal cultivation insights, failed approaches, unusual interpretations, records left by people who cultivated it particularly far, anything along those lines."

Si Sihua nodded and began recording the request. There was no issue with commissioning information about a sect technique as long as the person posting the mission had already exchanged for it through the sect. The Mission Hall kept records of such exchanges and would verify them before accepting the commission.

Otherwise, disciples could simply pool their spirit stones, have one person purchase a technique, and then use information missions to pass its contents and cultivation insights around without everyone exchanging for it themselves. Naturally, the sect wasn't generous enough to leave such an obvious loophole open.

Ning wasn't expecting someone to uncover a miraculous secret manual that would instantly solve everything for him. What interested him were the smaller observations accumulated over years of practice: mistakes omitted from the official scripture, recurring bottlenecks, unusual reactions during circulation, and interpretations that might let him examine the First Fold from another perspective. Even an incorrect interpretation could be useful if he understood precisely why it was wrong.

Once Si Sihua finished recording the commission, she glanced up. "Anything else?"

"Actually, yes. Is there an easy way for me to learn the basics of spiritual medicine?"

Her brush stopped again.

Si Sihua looked at him for several seconds before slowly putting down her brush. "Aren't you already a farmer and an alchemist?"

"Yes." Ning nodded. Come to think of it, he could probably count himself as a spiritual chef as well.

There was technically another profession he could add to that list.

Ning chose not to.

"And now you want to become a spiritual doctor?"

"No. I only want the basics." Ning shook his head. "Anatomy, meridians, how different parts of the body respond to spiritual energy, basic diagnosis, that sort of thing. I have no intention of becoming a practicing spiritual doctor."

Si Sihua stared at him as though that somehow made the situation worse. "How much work do you intend to give yourself?"

Ning smiled. "I'm simply practicing what I preached to you."

Si Sihua wanted to retort, but she had no counterargument.

For all the suffering his advice had brought her, Ning himself was probably one of the hardest-working disciples she knew. If anyone had earned the right to tell her to work harder now so she could enjoy herself later, it was unfortunately him.

"That's different," she muttered eventually.

Ning's smile widened. "Is it?"

"Don't push your luck."

Ning continued, "This really isn't going to become another main profession. I just want enough foundational knowledge to understand the subject properly, and I'll study it whenever I have spare time."

That was mostly true. What Ning didn't mention was that the idea had occurred to him because of Know Thyself.

His talent allowed him to observe his own body in extraordinary detail. He could trace his meridians and qi circulation, examine his muscles and organs, and watch the minute changes caused by cultivation techniques. The problem was that seeing something didn't necessarily mean understanding it. His modern knowledge of anatomy helped, and years of cultivation had filled in some of the gaps concerning meridians and spiritual energy, but most of that knowledge had been picked up piecemeal.

A proper foundation in spiritual medicine would let him interpret what Know Thyself showed him far more effectively. Understanding meridian damage, internal injuries, abnormal qi reactions, and the interaction between spiritual energy and the body would help with everything from body cultivation and technique experiments to evaluating the effects of pills and spiritual food.

Considering how often Ning experimented on himself, learning the basics was just common sense at this point.

Si Sihua, unfortunately, knew none of this. From her perspective, Ning was simply trying to add another subject to an already ridiculous list.

"Just be careful," she eventually said. "You're already doing enough things. Don't keep adding more until even that ridiculous schedule of yours can't fit them."

"I know." Ning nodded. "That's why I said I'll study it in my spare time. I'm not changing my schedule for this."

Si Sihua stared at him for a moment.

"...You really do follow that thing, don't you?"

She had seen Ning's written schedule once. Every part of his day had been divided neatly, including designated breaks. At first, Si Sihua had thought it was some kind of punishment schedule.

Then Ning had proudly informed her that he'd made it himself.

Worse, he actually followed it.

"Of course." Ning looked mildly offended. "Why does nobody trust me?"

Si Sihua decided not to answer that. "The Medical Hall has introductory texts available to inner disciples. They're mostly the same materials apprentices study when they first begin learning. You can exchange spirit stones for copies, and they occasionally hold introductory lectures as well."

"That's perfect."

It was more than enough for Ning for now.

Then, he remembered his other purpose for the visit. "Right, Senior Sister. Has anyone responded to my mission for the White Sun Flame Crystal?"

"Oh, that." Si Sihua bent down and searched through one of the storage compartments beneath the counter. "You're probably one of the few disciples who uses the Mission Hall often enough that I remember half your commissions without checking. Someone completed that one this morning."

Ning's eyes immediately brightened, although the first part of her answer left him somewhat conflicted.

He had noticed for some time that most disciples seemed to underuse the Mission Hall. Plenty came here to accept missions and earn contribution points, but comparatively few posted commissions of their own unless they needed something very specific.

Ning couldn't understand why.

The sect had thousands of disciples spread across different halls, professions, cultivation levels, and areas of expertise. Somewhere among them, there was usually someone who possessed the information, material, or skill he needed. Finding that person himself could take days or weeks; posting a mission allowed him to offer a reasonable reward and put the problem in front of thousands of people who might be able to solve it.

Perhaps most cultivators were simply too accustomed to seeing the Mission Hall as somewhere they came to work rather than somewhere they could make other people work for them.

Well, Ning wasn't going to complain. If everyone else wanted to underuse one of the sect's most convenient systems, that was their loss.

Si Sihua soon straightened up with a small sealed box and placed it on the counter. "Here it is."

Ning immediately forgot about everyone else's questionable use of the Mission Hall.

She released the seal, revealing a walnut-sized crystal resting inside. It was almost entirely white except for thin veins of pale gold running through its interior, and the surrounding temperature rose slightly as concentrated fire-attributed spiritual energy escaped from the container.

It was the White Sun Flame Crystal Fenghuang needed to advance.

"The total reward was twelve hundred spirit stones," Si Sihua explained as she resealed the box. "You deposited four hundred when you posted the mission, so you still owe eight hundred."

Ning naturally knew that this was quite expensive.

Fortunately, he was not as poor as he had been a few days earlier. Elder Shen had worked surprisingly fast when recovering the spirit stones from Elder Shen Kuo.

Only a few days later, Elder Shen had appeared looking unbearably smug and handed Ning his share without explaining how he had obtained it.

Considering Elder Shen's personality, Ning suspected Elder Shen Kuo hadn't been outmaneuvered at all. The old man had acquired a legitimate reason to bother him relentlessly, so Elder Shen Kuo could have decided that several thousand spirit stones were a reasonable price for peace.

Ning could easily imagine it happening.

He counted out the remaining eight hundred spirit stones and handed them over. Si Sihua verified the amount before passing him the sealed box, which Ning carefully stored away.

Before leaving, Ning glanced at the package of snacks that Si Sihua had already positioned safely on her side of the counter and gave her an encouraging smile. "I hope you persevere a little further along this path, Senior Sister."

Si Sihua looked at him, then at the snacks, before sighing. "There isn't really a choice anymore. My mother knows I'm capable of working hard now."

Ning winced. That truly was irreversible. "Good luck, Senior Sister."

Si Sihua sighed. "I'll survive somehow."

Ning left the Mission Hall in an excellent mood.

When he returned to the farm, he found Fenghuang making his rounds above the fields. The raven flew low between the plots, releasing small bursts of white flame to burn away harmful weeds wherever they had begun to sprout.

Fenghuang noticed Ning almost immediately and flew over. His attention, however, shifted the moment Ning took the sealed box from his storage bag, his eyes locking onto it at once.

"Caw?"

"Good news."

Fenghuang perked up immediately, and when Ning opened the box enough for the fire-attributed aura of the White Sun Flame Crystal to escape, the raven nearly lunged for it.

"Calm down. Let me check it first."

Fenghuang stopped, although the way he kept inching closer made his patience somewhat unconvincing.

Ning inspected the crystal using his spiritual sense and compared its appearance and aura with the description attached to the mission. Everything matched what he had requested, so after confirming that the Mission Hall had already authenticated the material, he removed it from the box and held it out.

Fenghuang swallowed the entire crystal before Ning could say anything.

Ning stared at him. "I was going to explain how to use it."

"Caw."

"Of course you already know."

For a short while, Fenghuang simply stood there. Then his feathers trembled as white flames began appearing across his body without being consciously summoned, faint at first before gradually brightening as the spiritual energy within the White Sun Flame Crystal spread through him. His aura rose and fell several times, and Ning was preparing to intervene if something went wrong when Fenghuang's posture abruptly relaxed.

The raven swayed, lowered himself onto the ground, and let out a noticeably drowsier caw. Within a minute, he was asleep.

Ning crouched beside him and checked his condition with his spiritual sense. Fenghuang's breathing remained steady, his aura showed no sign of instability, and the white flames covering his feathers were gradually drawing inward rather than growing uncontrolled. Everything matched the information Ning had gathered beforehand regarding the advancement of White Flame Ravens.

Unlike human cultivators, spiritual beasts relied much more heavily on their bodies and inherited bloodlines when advancing. Humans deliberately refined spiritual energy through cultivation techniques and consciously developed their meridians and dantians, while a spiritual beast's natural growth was closer to the maturation of potential already encoded within its bloodline.

That didn't mean spiritual beasts couldn't cultivate deliberately. Intelligent beasts could learn techniques, train, and strengthen themselves just like cultivators. However, their natural advancement still depended heavily on accumulating enough energy and obtaining the right resources for their bodies to express more of their inherited potential. Stronger bloodlines often demanded more in return; rare beasts could remain stuck for years simply because the treasure needed for their next transformation was too difficult to obtain.

For Fenghuang, the White Sun Flame Crystal was one such resource. Its fire essence was highly compatible with the White Flame Raven bloodline, providing what his body needed for the next stage of maturation. Once consumed, he needed little conscious control. His bloodline guided the transformation while the deep sleep allowed his body to devote itself almost entirely to the process.

Ning stared at the sleeping raven with mixed feelings. He had spent countless hours cultivating, studying techniques, and trying to understand increasingly complicated cultivation principles just to advance one step at a time.

Fenghuang had eaten a rock and gone to sleep.

Ning couldn't deny feeling a little envious. "One day, I'll figure out how to cultivate like this too."

Considering his plans for automated cultivation, it wasn't entirely a joke.

Ning carefully lifted the sleeping raven and carried him inside. Fenghuang's body had already become noticeably warmer, with faint white flames occasionally appearing between his feathers as the crystal's essence continued to merge with him.

Since Ning had no idea how long the advancement would take, he prepared a safe place for Fenghuang to sleep and arranged a few simple protective measures to ensure nothing disturbed him. Only after checking everything once more did Ning finally relax.

Fenghuang looked remarkably peaceful for someone who had just swallowed twelve hundred spirit stones.

Ning sighed. "And somehow, I'm the one people call Young Master."

The raven continued sleeping, blissfully unconcerned with the financial consequences of his cultivation.

Ning rarely had reason to visit the main Alchemy Hall. Most of his alchemy practice took place under Ling Xin's supervision, and whatever materials or facilities he needed could usually be obtained without coming here personally.

Today, however, was different.

The main hall had been cleared for a gathering, with dozens of alchemists standing in small groups while elders and senior disciples occupied the seats closer to the front.

Ning followed Ling Xin inside and soon noticed something peculiar.

People were avoiding them.

It wasn't so blatant that anyone turned around and walked away the moment Ling Xin approached. Several alchemists still greeted her politely, and a few even exchanged brief words with her, but nobody lingered for long. Wherever she went, a small pocket of space seemed to form naturally around her.

Ning wasn't entirely sure whether that was a good thing, especially since he could feel quite a few gazes directed toward him as well. His performance in the Heavenly Origin Tower had made him more recognizable within the sect, and being Ling Xin's sole disciple naturally attracted additional curiosity inside the Alchemy Hall.

Fortunately, most people seemed more wary of approaching his master than they were interested in approaching him.

Ning glanced sideways. "Master, you seem rather unpopular here."

Ling Xin didn't look bothered in the slightest. "They're just jealous."

Before he could question that explanation, a woman approached from one of the nearby groups. She appeared somewhat older than Ling Xin and carried herself with a composed air, though the look she gave Ling Xin suggested this wouldn't be a particularly pleasant conversation.

"Ling Xin, I'm surprised you actually came this time. You usually ignore gatherings like this."

"Oh, Senior Sister Cai." Ling Xin nodded in greeting. "Today's a special occasion. On the other hand, I'm not surprised to see you here."

Cai Yun snorted. "You're still as arrogant as ever."

"Thank you." Ling Xin ducked her head slightly and rubbed the back of her neck as though receiving a compliment.

Cai Yun's expression flattened. "That wasn't praise."

Ling Xin looked up with an innocent smile.

Cai Yun's attention soon shifted toward Ning. "So this is the disciple you've taken in? I've been hearing quite a bit about him lately."

Ning cupped his hands. "Greetings, Senior Aunt Cai."

Ning already knew a little about Cai Yun from the information he had gathered beforehand. She and Ling Xin were both disciples of Fu Xuan, making Cai Yun his senior aunt according to sect seniority.

Fu Xuan had accepted several disciples over the years, with Ling Xin being the youngest among them and widely considered the most talented. Unfortunately, she was also, from what Ning had seen so far, probably the most troublesome.

That combination hadn't exactly made her popular with everyone.

An alchemical lineage had only so many resources to distribute among its members. Rare formulas, medicinal materials, spiritual flames, suitable furnaces, Fu Xuan's personal guidance, and eventually the resources needed for major breakthroughs were all limited. With Ling Xin's talent, she was naturally expected to receive a considerable share.

It wasn't difficult to understand why some of her fellow disciples had mixed feelings about her.

Cai Yun looked Ning over briefly before turning back toward Ling Xin. "My disciple has been progressing quite well recently. Yue Chan can already refine high-grade First-Tier pills consistently."

The young woman standing behind her stepped forward at the mention of her name and respectfully cupped her hands. Ling Xin smiled at her before looking Yue Chan over with genuine interest. "That's quite good."

Cai Yun's expression gained a trace of satisfaction. "She hasn't been learning for very long either. At this rate, she should be ready to attempt the next certification before long."

Ling Xin nodded thoughtfully. "Ning has only been learning alchemy for about a year as well. He hasn't reached that level yet, but he's already developed a pill of his own."

The trace of satisfaction disappeared from Cai Yun's face.

Ning looked at his master.

That had been completely unnecessary.

Cai Yun evidently thought so too. "Refining an existing formula reliably and altering a formula are entirely different things. Young alchemists should build a proper foundation before becoming obsessed with novelty."

"I know." Ling Xin smiled brightly. "That's why I'm so proud of him."

Cai Yun's eyebrow twitched.

"Senior Sister," Ling Xin continued, apparently having far too much fun now, "if you're worried about whose teaching method is better, we could always have an alchemy duel ourselves. It's been a while."

This time, Cai Yun actually took half a step back.

Ning's eyebrows rose slightly.

Judging from that reaction, there was history here.

Ling Xin's smile hadn't changed at all, but Cai Yun clearly knew her junior sister well enough to understand that she was entirely capable of turning a petty disagreement into an actual alchemy duel in the middle of the gathering.

Ning was beginning to understand why people kept their distance from his master.

Cai Yun wisely ignored the challenge and instead gave Ling Xin an irritated look before turning away. As she left, Ning happened to meet Yue Chan's gaze. Given the atmosphere between their masters, he half expected some hostility from her, but the young woman merely offered him a polite nod.

Ning returned it.

Apparently, she had decided that their masters' problems could remain their masters' problems.

How reasonable.

Once they were alone again, Ning glanced toward Ling Xin. "Master, did you deliberately provoke her?"

"Of course not. I was just allowing Senior Sister to prove me wrong." Ling Xin smiled innocently.

Ning stared at her for a moment before deciding that further investigation would probably only strengthen the case against her.

Not far away, Cai Yun joined another group and stopped beside a woman whose demeanor was noticeably calmer than either hers or Ling Xin's. Tang Feiyu listened to her complain for a short while before sighing.

"You know you didn't have to compete with her."

"She irritates me." Cai Yun clicked her tongue.

"It's mostly because you insist on competing with her," Tang Feiyu said calmly.

Cai Yun frowned. "Whose side are you on?"

"Nobody's. That's why I'm still sane."

Cai Yun gave her an unimpressed look, but Tang Feiyu had already turned her attention elsewhere. If nothing else, the exchange had drawn her gaze toward Ling Xin's disciple, and that reminded her of the young man standing quietly behind her.

"I heard you're acquainted with Ling Xin's disciple."

Su Fan followed her gaze and immediately spotted Ning. "Ning? Yes."

"What do you think of him?"

"He's good."

Tang Feiyu raised an eyebrow. Su Fan wasn't particularly generous with praise, so this was a surprise.

"His material handling is excellent, especially considering how long he's been learning. His perception is good too; he notices changes during refinement quickly." Su Fan paused, glancing toward Ning again. "Did Senior Aunt Ling say he already developed his own pill?"

"Apparently." Tang Feiyu couldn't help but feel that Ling Xin had acquired a good disciple.

Su Fan fell silent.

Across the hall, Ning happened to notice Su Fan and gave him a small nod. Su Fan returned the gesture, although Ning couldn't help noticing that he looked visibly more haggard than the last time they had met.

Tang Feiyu had noticed the change much earlier.

Su Fan had become her newest disciple only recently, but even in that short period she had learned enough about him to recognize his stubbornness. Failing the Heavenly Origin Tower's illusion trial had clearly affected him more than he was willing to admit, and instead of talking about whatever he had experienced, he had responded by throwing himself into alchemy. His progress had been impressive, but the hours he was keeping were becoming increasingly concerning.

"You should rest tonight," Tang Feiyu said.

Su Fan's expression tightened. "Master, I'm fine."

"At least finish early today," she said.

Su Fan hesitated before reluctantly nodding.

Tang Feiyu sighed at her disciple's stubbornness. She had initially accepted Su Fan partly out of respect for the Su Family and his talented elder sister, Su Xue. Since then, however, she had come to appreciate the young man's talent and determination for themselves, which only made watching him push himself like this more frustrating.

On the other side of the hall, Ning naturally hadn't heard their conversation, but what he had seen so far made Ling Xin's earlier explanation considerably easier to understand.

They're jealous was an oversimplification, but it wasn't entirely wrong.

Now that Ning thought about it, his master really was somewhat isolated within the Alchemy Hall. Very few alchemists visited her residence, and she rarely mentioned spending time with anyone from the hall.

Some of that was undoubtedly Ling Xin's own doing. She was impulsive, frequently disappeared to pursue whatever caught her interest, and was always ready to throw hands. Her talent, however, clearly played a part as well.

Ning studied his master. Ling Xin seemed completely unconcerned, occasionally greeting someone she knew before becoming distracted by something else. He doubted she cared much about being popular, but there was still something mildly unpleasant about realizing that success itself had made some of the people around her keep their distance.

Ning watched her for a moment before speaking. "Master, you know, if Senior Aunt Cai really is jealous, that means you're doing pretty well."

Ling Xin grinned. "Obviously."

"Besides, Grandmaster supports you, and I'm on your side. That's already two people. How many do you need?"

Ling Xin paused at that, and her grin softened into a genuine smile. "My disciple really is adorable."

Ning immediately sensed danger, but her hand was already on his head. "Master, I'm trying to comfort you."

"I know." Ling Xin happily messed up his hair.

Ning sighed. "Then stop ruining the moment."

"Hehe." Ling Xin continued to mess up Ning's hair.

The conversations throughout the hall began to fade before Ling Xin could torment him further. Ning followed the gazes of the surrounding alchemists and saw Fu Xuan walking toward the front of the chamber.

As a Purple Mansion cultivator and the head of their alchemical lineage, her presence alone was enough for conversations to stop one after another until the hall had quieted.

Ling Xin finally released Ning and straightened, becoming noticeably more attentive as her master took her place at the front. Ning used the opportunity to fix his thoroughly ruined hair.

Fu Xuan swept her gaze across the gathered alchemists before speaking. "Most of you have already heard about the recent events surrounding the Heavenly Origin Tower. I won't repeat what has already been discussed elsewhere, but those events have made one thing particularly clear: the sect will need to invest more seriously in the coming generation."

That immediately drew everyone's attention.

"The Alchemy Hall is no exception. We have no shortage of promising young alchemists, and several have already shown considerable talent. However, none has yet distinguished themselves so decisively from their peers that the hall can justify concentrating greater resources behind them."

A few expressions changed at that.

Ning could already guess where the speech was going.

Fu Xuan continued, "That doesn't mean we don't have such people. Alchemists develop differently, and the ordinary certification system is meant to judge whether an alchemist has reached the required standard, not compare an entire generation. Therefore, three months from today, the Alchemy Hall will hold a competition for all eligible young alchemists."

The hall immediately stirred.

Fu Xuan allowed the murmuring to continue for a moment before raising one hand.

"This will not simply be a contest to see who can refine the highest-tier pill. Material identification and preparation, flame control, refinement, adaptability, medicinal understanding, and the final product will all be assessed. Being able to reproduce a difficult formula is valuable, but an alchemist who does not understand why that formula works has only learned to follow instructions."

Ning's interest sharpened.

If the competition had simply rewarded whoever could refine the most advanced pill, Ning would have been at a major disadvantage against disciples who had studied alchemy for several years longer than him. A broader assessment, however, gave him room to make use of the strengths he had developed.

Fu Xuan moved on to the rewards.

"Those who perform well will receive contribution points and medicinal materials according to their placement, along with access to selected formulas from the Alchemy Hall. The highest-ranked participants will also receive Alchemist Jade corresponding to the level they demonstrate during the competition."

The reaction this time was noticeably stronger.

Alchemist Jade was formal recognition of an alchemist's standing and ability, making it much easier to accept certain commissions, access restricted materials and facilities, and establish one's credentials outside the sect.

Fu Xuan waited until the discussion quieted before adding, "The top three will also receive priority for their next spiritual alchemist certification assessment."

That was enough to send another wave of conversation through the hall.

The certification mattered even more, opening access to better formulas, more valuable commissions, and restricted resources. Priority assessment wouldn't guarantee success, but for someone already approaching the required standard, it could save considerable time.

Ning scanned the hall while everyone digested the announcement. Yue Chan's previously calm expression had become noticeably more focused, and several other young alchemists were already studying one another with newfound interest.

Then he looked toward Su Fan.

The moment Fu Xuan announced the competition, the young man's attention had fixed on her, and by the time she finished explaining the rewards, Ning could practically see the decision forming behind his eyes.

There was no chance Su Fan was sitting this one out.

Ning smiled faintly. He had been a little worried after seeing how down Su Fan seemed, but apparently the guy hadn't lost his spirit. He had simply been channeling all of it into alchemy.

Fu Xuan spent a little longer explaining the eligibility requirements and general rules before dismissing the gathering. The orderly atmosphere vanished almost immediately as masters called over their disciples, younger alchemists began discussing possible competition formats, and several groups formed around those already considered likely contenders.

Ning was still considering what he could accomplish with three months of preparation when Ling Xin tugged at his sleeve.

"Come on."

Ning glanced at her. "Where are we going?"

"My master wants to meet you."

That got his attention.

Ning looked toward Fu Xuan, who had already disappeared through one of the inner doors of the Alchemy Hall. Despite being Ling Xin's disciple for some time now, he had never properly spoken with the Purple Mansion cultivator who stood at the head of their entire alchemical lineage.

"All right."

Ling Xin started toward the inner hall, and Ning followed.

He had come here expecting an announcement about alchemy. A private meeting with a Purple Mansion cultivator hadn't been part of the plan.

Ning was beginning to suspect his day had just become far more complicated.
