Something was wrong. My gut was screaming at me that Chett was a problem, but was he a bigger one than whatever they were planning beyond the wall? Hadn’t I crafted need bubbles for people who’d needed protection from Lightning? A vest and an orb?

Up here, I was mostly just another hand on a crossbow, but I might be able to do something about Chett. Well… no, there was the cannon, but it was still mostly untested.

He’d looked… so sad. Still, I barely knew the man, and he’d clearly just confirmed the death of someone he cared about. His need bubble had morphed. Before, his need was somewhat similar to Burnom’s last one. He’d needed to fight himself.

Now, it had changed, and the new need was… chilling.

He needed something to remove a talent. Its cost boggled my mind, but the need itself scared me more: Talents could be removed?

Impossible. I’d never even heard of such a thing. They were… gifts. No, even that implied that they were physical things. They were a part of life. Air. Water. Sunlight. Talents! They couldn’t be fiddled with! Manipulated! At least, as far as I’d known, they couldn’t.

‘Why not? April’s world never had talents. Something out there must be causing them. The Divine? The planet? It certainly wasn’t biology…’ I thought.

“Sir, I want to go after Chett, too,” I said, putting complex thoughts aside for more immediate concerns.

“Need you up here, Elmerina,” came a voice to my left. The Lieutenant General and I turned to see General Neogon climbing the ladder. “Why in the heavens did you waste your damn free points on jumping? Told you Public Speaking should be your priority.”

Oh. He must have spotted me leaping back up to the top of the wall. I bristled a little at the comment. Even the Army couldn’t dictate where its soldiers placed their free points. It could certainly try, and encourage to a great extent, with incentives and the like, but it could not demand.

“I think Chett is part of her plan, General!” I said, ignoring the barb. I’d saved that man’s life and that had been worth it to me. I didn’t have Neogon’s cold pragmatism. I hoped I never did.

“How?” he asked simply.

“Did you hear what she said? She was talking about him. To him. I think she’s trying to… to…”

I didn’t know. What? Corrupt him? Make him go Hysteric?

I shivered at the thought.

“Aye. She probably plans to push him over the edge. Either to prevent him from helping us further, or for something more nefarious. Still, it would be ten times worse if everyone on this wall saw your red hair bouncing away like a coward.”

“I’m not a coward!” I shouted.

I wasn’t… right? I truly did think Freyna was planning something with Chett but—!”

“I know. Which is why you’re staying here. Trust in Private Umeldrei. She’ll sort Chett out,” he said.

I clenched my fist. This was wrong. I knew it was, but I couldn’t explain why. Networking? The nebulous skill gave me the occasional insight into people but I didn’t think that was at play this time. It was just a gut feeling.

Unfortunately, Neogon was right.  It felt somehow arrogant to say, but I had sway in the town. If they saw me running away from the fight, even if I wasn’t running at all, that might have a noticeable effect on morale.

Or maybe I was just a dumb girl with bright hair. I could put a helmet on, and no one would notice… still. I barely knew Chett. Even if he was part of the Eschal’s plan, I wanted to be here.

A lightbulb went off.

“Send Lancer Malken!” I shouted.

“Who?”

“One of Chett’s last friends! I think they’re squadmates!” I said.

“Nadir,” Neogon said.

“On it,” the second-in-command replied.

“Where is Vovel?” the general asked. He glanced up toward the flags, and the markings on them subtly changed the moment he grabbed the flagpole.

No one answered, but he seemed to receive information from somewhere before turning back to me.

“Elmerina, please remind the soldiers to regard the flags.”

I nodded.

“Soldiers. Mind the flags,” I said, my voice projecting far yet soft. My Public Speaking could reach the whole wall, but if Neogon wanted to issue orders to the West or North defenses, he’d need to use the flags or more mundane methods.

We didn’t have to wait long.

A line of men and women strode forth, but unlike the last company, these mages didn’t even come halfway to our furthest crossbow range. There, they lifted their hands.

In horror, I watched as a line of heavy boulders were launched from far beyond the tree line. Trebuchets. That’s what was taking them so long. They were setting up fucking siege equipment.

Normal trebuchets surely couldn’t have flung such large artillery, and for a moment, I thought that they had no chance of reaching us, until the earth mages acted.

The massive hunks of rock, already high, were buffeted further into the air, allowing them to travel the full five hundred meters instead of falling short.

Fireballs blasted up from the top of our battlements, and a few of the rocks exploded, sending small showers of debris in our direction. Edra summoned walls of water that slowed one of the rocks until it fell short.

Two more didn’t reach the wall even with the boost, landing harmlessly outside our walls. One overshot the wall and landed uncomfortably close to my farm.

One incredibly unfortunate boulder smashed directly into the wall, right beneath my feet.

I screamed as the entire wall wobbled, but Footwork kept me on my feet. The palisade was built behind the wall, and attached to it. The tree-sized logs that made up the wall held, but one of the logs was dislodged at the base. It careened forward, slamming into the ground and leaving an empty gap where a soldier standing on the parapet no longer had cover.

Mom’s style of fire magic would be a perfect defense against these.

I glanced over at my cannon, its barrel hanging over the wall, useless with the enemy still so far away, and doubly useless if I didn’t expose my Fire Magic.

Well. Fuck it. My secrets were damn near posted on billboards these days.

I had forty-one points in Fire Magic, and my Mana Pool and Mana Regeneration skills were sitting at a healthy thirteen and eleven, respectively.

Even after sinking 100 points into jumping, I still had 126 left. If now wasn’t the time to use them, I didn’t know when was.

I dropped 30 points into Mana Pool. 25 points into Mana Regeneration and another 40 into Fire Magic, bringing it up to 81. Higher than Mom’s had been when she fought Freyna in the skies near Mitoras. I felt my mana pool expand, unnerved by the massive influx of capacity. It was the first time I’d ever felt my mana pool shift.

Unfortunately, increasing the size of the pool didn’t increase the amount. I should’ve done this days ago, but at least I’d be gaining a little more than six hundred mana per hour now.

I ran down the wall until I reached my cannon. It was surrounded by soldiers on all sides, most of them barely paying it any mind. They would soon.

Hundreds of stones and small rocks were gathered to use as ammunition for the cannon, but it wasn’t loaded yet. Much as I wanted to attempt it, a line of twenty Earth Mages wasn’t worth showing the Eschal that trump card yet. If the damn thing even worked, it would only be useful for closer range infantry as it was now.

Irritatingly, one of those earth mages was using Berkenem’s sword. The witch had held it before, but I didn’t think she had the Earth Magic she’d need to use it. Probably just an attempt to piss me off.

A second volley of boulders launched into the sky, but this time I was ready. I suffused the oily fire magic into a pair of them that were close together. Then, before the Earth Mages could boost them, I lit the spark, spending about two hundred mana to set them aflame. Most of the cost had been due to the distance.

Two of the rocks exploded right over the Earth Mages’ heads, raining down debris and broken chunks of rock all over them. I couldn’t tell if it killed any of them, but they scattered like rats.

I  wouldn’t be able to keep doing that if I wanted any reserved mana, and I’d only stopped two of them. It seemed as if they’d adjusted their aim because many of the remaining rocks slammed into the wall, shaking us and sending us tumbling. Amazingly, the wall held, but at least one had impacted with such force that it embedded itself and remained lodged there.

“Who did that!?” Neogon shouted down the line, overjoyed, but also a little angry. “I swear soldiers hiding talents will be the death of me. Who and how!?”

No one answered. I decided that if they dared launch another volley, I’d do it one more time, but after that I’d need to conserve mana for whatever else they might try to pull.

I’d earned something like five hundred points since gaining my talent. One day, I truly would be a thing of legend. Not now, though.

Getting back to their feet warily, the soldiers and townsfolk began to cheer, but Neogon had a grim cast to his features.

“No riftspawn yet,” he muttered.

“General!” came a runner’s shout as he reached the top of the ladder and rushed to the command section.

“Report,” the general replied.

“The Eschal army is showing signs of mustering on the North and Western fronts.

The general thumbed his chin. “Fools. They always think the walls are the harder target. Those trenches will hold for months.”

“Wait, really?” I asked.

The general nodded. “If they had rushed us with light cavalry on the first day, there might’ve been very little we could do. Now that we have trenches along the north and west, the wall is actually our weakest point. If they breach it, we’ll need to burn it to the ground with our fire mages, so they don’t have a high ground overlooking the town.”

The words physically pained me. All that work. All that coin, just to burn it?

“Then we better not fucking lose it,” I told him. “I can’t afford to pay for another one, and I know a woodworker who's liable to murder me if we burn it.”

I managed to get a laugh out of the normally stoic general.

“Aye. I suppose we’d better n—!”

It came from above without warning. I screamed and instinctively threw myself onto the heavy wooden floor. It shook with my landing. White light, so close I could taste the burning air, and an explosion that temporarily deafened me. Bodies crashed on top of mine, and I wriggled, dazed and disoriented, but managed to push someone off of me, and get hurriedly back to my knees.

A few people had, unfortunately, misjudged where the back of the wall ended and had fallen off the tower.

When my eyes cleared, I trembled.

The section of bridge to my immediate right was simply gone. A massive gap where I could see the other people lay between our section and the other side of the wall. The logs that made out the outer edge of the wall had burst and flown forward into the open plain. The wall groaned and shuddered, not meant to stand with a major section of it no longer supporting. The timbers outside tumbled toward one another, forming a sort of arch.

Lightning. Chett had attacked the wall.

Distantly, I saw the earth mages reforming their line, but this time they weren’t alone. Soldiers were streaming out around them. They were committed to a full assault.

“Heavens help us,” I thought, terrified.
