The ancient oak tree groaned softly beneath Perberos's weight, its dead branches swaying in the freezing winter wind.

High above the corrupted valley, the elven scout was completely still. He had integrated his physical form so deeply with the surrounding shadows that he was practically invisible, a mere trick of the light. His breathing had slowed to an imperceptible, shallow rhythm. His luminous silver eyes were locked entirely on the towering, heavily armoured figure of the Bone General stationed two hundred yards below.

He raised his beautiful, curved elven longbow.

He smoothly drew the thick bowstring back to his cheek. The heavy, solid-steel bodkin rested perfectly against the riser. He calculated the severe crosswind sweeping through the valley. He measured the exact trajectory required to bypass the dense ring of Crypt Weavers and the towering Wight knights. He focused on the narrow, dark slit of the General's helmet, waiting for the sickly green runes on the monster's armour to pulse and temporarily blind the creature's own vision.

The shot was impossible for a human archer. For Perberos, it was simply a matter of physics and timing.

He exhaled slowly, letting the freezing air leave his lungs. His fingers began to relax their grip on the string.

A split second before the arrow released, a furred, clawed hand clamped violently onto the upper limb of the longbow, forcefully shoving the weapon downward.

Perberos reacted with lethal, honed instinct. He did not loose the arrow into the dirt. He smoothly absorbed the downward momentum, pivoting his body on the narrow branch, his right hand abandoning the bowstring and dropping instantly towards the curved blade at his hip.

"You arrogant, emotional idiot," a harsh, raspy whisper hissed from the shadows directly beside him.

Perberos froze, his hand hovering mere inches from his sword hilt.

Ronald was crouching on the thick branch next to him. The feline beastkin’s jade-green eyes were narrowed into furious, glowing slits. His muted green cloak blended perfectly with the dead pine needles, and his tail was lashing angrily against the bark.

"What are you doing?" Perberos demanded quietly, his voice a dangerous, icy whisper. He did not relax his posture. "I had the shot."

"You had a guaranteed death sentence," Ronald countered bluntly, not letting go of the elven bow. "Did you completely lose your mind the moment you saw that horde? Look at the target, Perberos. Truly look at it."

The beastkin pointed a sharp claw down into the valley.

"That is a high-tier dungeon boss wearing heavy, runic plate armour," Ronald explained, his voice thick with suppressed, furious logic. "The ambient dark magic radiating from its core is so dense it is physically warping the air. A single steel bodkin, even fired from an elven bow, is not going to instantly shatter that kind of shielding. It will undoubtedly strike true, but it will only wound the creature."

Ronald leaned closer, his fangs bared in a silent snarl.

"And the absolute second that arrow sparks against his helmet," Ronald continued, "those two hundred Wight knights will turn their heads. The Crypt Weavers will pinpoint our exact trajectory, and they will flood this ridge. You will die, I will die, and the General will simply regenerate his wounds and slaughter everyone anyway."

Perberos clenched his jaw tightly. The cold, unyielding logic of the beastkin's argument pierced entirely through the haze of his protective, desperate rage.

He looked away from Ronald, his silver eyes drifting across the massive, churning sea of the undead horde, tracking the horrific flow of rotting meat all the way to the distant timber walls of Ashenfall.

Even from two miles away, the sheer, chaotic desperation of the siege was visible. He saw the roaring, brilliant orange flames of the pitch trenches. He saw the tiny, fragile figures of the defenders scrambling along the parapets.

And then, he saw it. A brilliant, pulsing flash of pure, unmistakable golden light illuminating the swirling black smoke above the main gates.

Carcan. She was standing right on the edge of the abyss, weaving her healing magic into the very heart of the slaughter.

The sight of his twin sister standing so close to the jaws of death made his chest physically ache, but it also grounded him. Ronald was right. A failed assassination attempt would not save her. It would only guarantee that she died without her brother there to fight beside her.

Perberos slowly, deliberately lowered his hand away from his blade's hilt. He eased the tension off his bowstring, returning the heavy steel bodkin to his magical quiver.

He looked back at Ronald. Hanging from the lower branches of the massive oak tree, having silently climbed up behind the beastkin, were Garth and Silas. The two human rangers looked terrified, but their eyes were locked onto the elven scout with absolute, unwavering loyalty. They had followed him into the heart of the nightmare, and they had trusted him to lead.

Perberos closed his eyes for a fraction of a second, forcing the cold, detached persona of the elite scout to completely reassert control over his mind.

"I apologise," Perberos whispered softly, offering the squad a slow, solemn nod of contrition. "I allowed my fear for my sister to blind my judgement. You are correct, Ronald. A single shot is a fool's gamble."

Ronald let out a quiet, relieved breath, his ears swivelling forward as he released his grip on the elven bow.

"Apology accepted," the beastkin grunted, settling comfortably into the crook of the thick branch. "Now, what are we actually going to do? Because sitting in a tree while our city burns is not particularly appealing either."

"We formulate a coordinated strike," Perberos answered, his strategic mind finally engaging with the impossible mathematics of the assassination. "You are right about the runic shielding. A single impact will not shatter the barrier. We need to overwhelm the dark magic with simultaneous, compounding kinetic force."

He looked down at the three seasoned hunters. "We have four ranged fighters here. If we split our forces and surround the command group, we can establish a lethal crossfire."

"Shooting from four different angles," Garth noted quietly, resting his heavy crossbow across his knees. The grizzled tracker chewed his lip thoughtfully. "It divides their attention. If the Wights try to shield the General, they cannot block a simultaneous volley coming from a few different angles."

"But the timing has to be absolutely flawless," Silas added, his voice trembling slightly in the freezing wind. "If one of us fires even a half-second too early, the magical shielding will flare, and the rest of the volley will simply bounce off the hardened barrier."

"I will handle the timing," Ronald offered, a sharp, predatory grin touching his face. "I am not carrying a heavy bow. I can close the distance much faster than you three. I will move into the dense brush at the absolute edge of the valley floor, right behind their rearguard. When the General is sufficiently distracted by the siege, I will give the signal."

"What signal?" Perberos asked.

"An osprey," Ronald replied smoothly. "A sharp, three-note mountain osprey call. The pitch is high enough to cut cleanly through the low, grinding roar of the zombies, but natural enough that the mindless undead will completely ignore it. When you hear the third note, you loose your arrows."

The plan was incredibly dangerous, requiring them to operate independently behind enemy lines, but it was the only tactical option that offered a genuine chance of success.

They agreed to the strategy in hushed whispers. However, they could not move immediately. The chaotic, shifting lines of the lesser infantry were still pouring past their position, making safe traversal along the ridge entirely impossible.

They were forced to wait.

The four assassins sat silently in the freezing, dead branches of the ancient oak tree for hours, acting as helpless, distant spectators to the horrific meat grinder unfolding at the city walls.

They watched the sun arc slowly across the pale winter sky. They watched with bated breath as the massive, hollow towers of rotting zombies formed against the timber logs, threatening to spill over the parapets.

And they watched the spectacular, terrifying magical defence of Ashenfall.

Every time a tower reached critical mass, they saw a blinding, concentrated orb of pure white plasma erupt from the central gatehouse, obliterating the foundation and sending the rotting meat crashing back into the mud. They saw sudden, violent fissures open in the earth, swallowing hundreds of ghouls as the geomancers went to work.

"They are holding," Silas whispered reverently, watching a massive wave of pure elemental fire wash over the plains, incinerating a huge swath of the charging horde. "By the Light, they are actually holding the line."

"They are rotating their casters," Perberos observed, his sharp eyes catching the subtle shifts in the magical bombardment. He felt a profound sense of pride swell in his chest. Josh’s tactical mind was clearly at work. "They are managing their exhaustion. It is a brilliant defence."

They watched the brutal, grinding rhythm of the siege continue until the late afternoon. The sky began to turn a bruised, deepening purple, casting long, freezing shadows across the cratered plains.

Then, the static dynamic of the battlefield violently shifted.

Perberos felt the ambient temperature drop sharply. He looked down into the valley, his silver eyes locking onto the dense, quiet rear lines of the undead army.

The thousands of heavily armoured skeletal knights and the towering Bone Goliaths were finally moving.

"The General has called an end to the game," Ronald hissed, gripping the bark of the tree tightly.

They watched in absolute, frozen horror as the writhing ocean of zombies parted, creating wide avenues for the heavy vanguard. The sheer, physical vibration of the Goliaths marching towards the city walls shook the ancient oak tree they were hiding in. The elite phalanx of Wights surrounding the Bone General tightened their formation, their spectral blue eyes glowing brightly in the dimming twilight.

The assassins watched helplessly as the siege monsters reached the main iron portcullis.

The distance muted the sound, but they could see the massive, iron-banded oak trunks swinging backward and slamming into the gates. They saw the heavy timber walls of Ashenfall physically shudder under the apocalyptic impacts. They saw the desperate, frantic magical strikes from the parapets attempting to melt the Goliaths, only to be absorbed by their thick, rusted iron plating.

"The gates are going to shatter," Garth groaned, his scarred face pale. "They cannot stop those behemoths from the top of the wall. We are out of time."

Just as the tracker spoke the words, the dark, smoky sky directly above the main gatehouse erupted into a blinding, terrifying display of raw power.

A massive figure, clad entirely in heavy steel plate armour, leapt straight off the edge of the thirty-foot wall.

Perberos gasped, his silver eyes widening in absolute shock.

The falling figure did not simply drop. Mid-air, the heavy steel armour ignited with a raw, blinding aura of crackling blue lightning. The high-voltage static arced wildly against the darkening sky, illuminating the entire front line of the siege.

"Is that...?" Silas started, completely bewildered.

"It is Josh," Perberos confirmed, his voice filled with a mixture of profound awe and deep, sinking terror.

They watched the literal, falling comet of blue electricity slam directly into the skull of the central Bone Goliath. The impact was visible from two miles away. A massive, explosive shockwave of golden dust and shattered bone erupted outward, completely obliterating the towering monstrosity and cratering the freezing mud in front of the gates.

A second later, they saw the right-hand Goliath physically cleave in two, collapsing into a massive pile of smoking remains as a smaller, incredibly dense figure rode its axes down the creature's ribcage.

The sheer, suicidal bravery of the stunt left the assassins entirely speechless.

But the awe was extremely short-lived.

As the dust began to clear around the shattered remains of the siege engines, Perberos saw the terrifying reality of his friends' position. Josh and Bhel were now standing in the freezing mud, completely isolated outside the city walls. They were cut off from the defenders above, and the surrounding ocean of zombies was rapidly closing in.

Worse, the elite guard had noticed the breach.

Perberos watched the perfectly disciplined ranks of the skeletal knights step forward, their heavy greatswords lowered, forming a tight, impenetrable ring of dark steel completely surrounding the exhausted human and dwarf.

Josh and Bhel were entirely trapped.

Perberos turned his gaze sharply back to the Bone General. The massive boss monster was watching the isolated fighters near the gate, its glowing green eye sockets locked onto the unfolding slaughter. The General’s runic armour pulsed with dark, eager amusement.

The target was completely distracted.

"Ronald," Perberos said, his voice cold, sharp, and entirely devoid of hesitation. He pulled his elven longbow from his shoulder. "Give the signal."

The beastkin did not waste a single second with words. Ronald dropped silently from the oak branch, his muted green cloak snapping in the wind, and plummeted towards the shadows of the valley floor. Garth and Silas immediately followed, scrambling down the thick trunk to take up their crossfire positions on the adjacent ridges.

Perberos remained high in the ancient tree, standing smoothly on the thick branch. He drew a heavy, armour-piercing bodkin from his quiver, nocking it against the string. He raised the bow, locking his silver eyes onto the glowing green core burning within the Bone General's chest.

He slowed his breathing, completely ignoring the freezing wind and the distant, desperate fight for his friends' lives. He waited for the osprey's call.
