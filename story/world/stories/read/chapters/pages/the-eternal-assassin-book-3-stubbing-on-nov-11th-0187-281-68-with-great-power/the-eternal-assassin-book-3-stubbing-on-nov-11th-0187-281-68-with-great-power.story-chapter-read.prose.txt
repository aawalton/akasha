“Unlike most countries, our capital is our only real city here in Nunait,” Asher’s guide explained as she led him over to the city center. Shortly after they’d started walking, she’d officially introduced herself as Amanda. She’d tried to get him to call her Mandy, but Asher hadn’t liked just how close that was to Moxy, and decided to stick with her full name instead. Thankfully, Amanda didn’t mind in the slightest, acting like she was simply overjoyed that he’d followed her request so quickly and showed up at the capital. “Though while we’re small, we’re far from helpless. The Shining Order is filled with dozens of skilled fighters, all of whom have spent countless days honing themselves against the undead littering the Deadlands. Or the former Deadlands, I suppose I should get used to saying.”

Amanda laughed as though even just the concept was wild to her, and Asher couldn’t really blame her. He supposed if he’d become the leader of an order of knights who had been dedicated to a cause for the last thousand years, and that cause had suddenly been accomplished out of the blue, he’d be a bit erratic as well.

“It’s a nice city,” Asher admitted, taking in the usual sights of people selling food and wares in the nearby market as they walked by, or neighbors chatting with one another and simply going about their daily lives. The fact that he hadn’t seen so much as a single beggar as they walked was what really impressed him. Despite all that Donvath did to make his capital a wonderful place to live, not even he’d been able to eradicate poverty entirely.

“We do our best,” Amanda said, beaming proudly as she waved at a few smiling faces who called out to her. “As the bulwark against the Deadlands, we receive frequent shipments to supplement our defensive efforts. Food, equipment, even potions in some cases. We have our own farmers and workers of course, but with what little land we have, most of what we use is imported.”

“Wait, how is that going to work now that the Deadlands are gone?” Asher asked, suddenly realizing that he might very well be responsible for thousands of people having their lives uprooted as the two surrounding countries decided to take back the land they’d given away all those years ago. He didn’t regret taking the lich down of course, but he hadn’t considered that his actions might have any bad consequences to those who lived up against the zombie-infested lands.

“That is the million-shard question,” Amanda admitted. “Though it is one we have been prepared for for some time. Already, our people are working on restoring life to the dead soil that will one day hold our crops. Druids have been called in from the surrounding countries, favors that were owed are being cashed. Ideally, we’ll be able to start farming a significant portion of the Deadlands within the next few months, and we might even be able to eke in a harvest or two before winter hits. Of course, food is not our greatest concern. It is our neighbors.”

Rather than a magnificent castle, the center of Preaset appeared to contain a sprawling keep. As the two of them headed inside, Asher spotted a few clusters of warriors wearing armor with similar suns emblazoned on them. Their armor was clearly basic steel, as opposed to whatever white material had been utilized to make Amanda’s. They were all too engrossed in their training to even notice their presence, and Asher allowed himself to be led deeper into the keep.

“You think they’re going to try and take back their lands?” Asher asked, reading into Amanda’s silence as he carefully examined each and every one of the paladins in training. Not many of them had all that impressive auras, but there were plenty of instructors leading the training sessions who did. Asher happily accepted the free workout for his lagging Judgement skill, making mental notes of any auras that stood out from the rest.

“While we hope that won’t be the case, we would be fools not to prepare for it,” Amanda said, her voice taking on a hint of steel for the first time. “Our country may have begun as nothing more than a defensive bulwark against the Deadlands, but we have grown into so much more over countless generations. We are our own people now, and we will not allow ourselves to be wiped out after accomplishing the goal we’ve worked toward for so long.”

“About that…” Asher said, rubbing the back of his neck sheepishly. “I’m sort of figuring this isn’t the case, but just to confirm… your people don’t all secretly hate me or anything for swooping in and dealing with the problem you’ve spent so many generations fighting against, right? I wasn’t getting that vibe, but I figured it couldn’t hurt to ask.”

“Of course not!” Amanda said, actually coming to a halt as she turned to look at him like he was insane. “Why would we have even set up the bounty in the first place if outside help was something we didn’t want? No, we are not bothered in the slightest. After all these centuries, we genuinely thought eradicating the Deadlands for good was impossible. Our Order’s mission was to simply contain the undead threat. Every few generations one of our leaders would grow bolder and attempt to launch an attack into the heart of the Deadlands, and every time it resulted in nothing but loss. My own father gave his life on such a mission thirty years back, and I will never forget the sadness on his face as he bid my mother and I goodbye, all but knowing what was waiting in store for him. It is for that reason I never attempted to launch such an assault after taking my position as head of the Order.”

“Sorry about your father,” Asher said, getting a dismissive wave from Amanda as she turned and continued walking.

“It is in the past, and I am who I am today because of his sacrifice. Now then, that’s enough about us. Shall we discuss how this evening’s ceremony is going to go?”

“It’s still happening today?” Asher asked, taken aback by the revelation. “I got here so late I sort of thought I missed my window. Don’t these things take time to plan?”

“I started the preparations yesterday the moment I got back from investigating the Deadlands,” Amanda laughed. “One of the benefits of having such a small capital is that there isn’t too much we need to work around. And as I am the leader of our country, I can easily make it happen. Would you prefer we wait until tomorrow?”

“No, today would be perfect,” Asher said, blinking at the obvious. “Hold on, you’re the leader of the country?”

“The Order leads the country, and I lead the Order,” Amanda confirmed. “It is how things have always been. This surprises you?”

“A little bit, to be honest. I only know one other leader of an entire kingdom, and he’s not really the combat type.”

“Not many leaders are,” Amanda shrugged, resting a hand on the hilt of her sword. “Many nobles obtain powerful elements and skills for themselves purely for talking points and never learn how to actually use any of them. While Nunait might be small and have less resources, I would bet on any one of the officers in my Order being able to take down any similar noble. We train from birth to fight and grow strong, and I believe it shows. Still, numbers are the great equalizer. Which brings me to a question I’ve been preparing to ask of you.”

To Asher’s surprise, Amanda got down on one knee before him, her golden eyes boring straight into his own.

“We would ask that you, The Reaper, join our people’s cause. To enter into an alliance with our people and come to our aid if necessary.”

“Wait, hold on,” Asher said, laughing nervously as he held up his hands. “What? Sure, I’m pretty good at what I do, but I’m just one guy. Hell, I’m pretty certain you’re further along in elemental advancement than I am, based on the fact that you seem to be literally glowing. Why me?”

“Modesty is unbecoming of a warrior,” Amanda frowned. “You killed the monster who created the Deadlands and hid within its center for over a thousand years. If the rumors are true, you’ve slain countless master assassins, and even greater demons have fallen to your blade. While armies and the number of warriors one kingdom can bring to bear are important, it is the individual greater powers that give leaders pause when they are debating how to move their pieces. Even if you are not yet in the Stage of Power, which I will admit is astonishing to hear, you have already made quite the name for yourself. So much so that I fear some will act to remove you from the board before you become a thorn in their side. Unlike most greater powers, as a new Spatial user, it is also known that you don’t have any true allegiance just yet. Before you can fall into the hands of any one nation, there are those who would rather see you dead.”

“A little late on the warning,” Asher snorted. “I was attacked by a Shadow Squad last night who had equipment specially designed to take down a Spatial element user. No clue who sent them, but they won’t be going after anyone else.”

“You survived a Shadow Squad sent specifically for your head?” Amanda blinked, getting to her feet as she stared at him. “I know you have experience fighting assassins, but still…”

“I’m pretty hard to kill,” Asher said, deciding to leave it at that. “You seriously think me declaring to the world that, what, you and I are friends is going to make that much of a difference?”

“Yes,” Amanda said, surprising him with her certainty. “At the moment, while my Order is filled with a number of impressive warriors, I am the only greater power within our lands. Though I doubt you would have heard of me, as you come from the other side of the continent and my name has had no reason to spread as yours has. My people call me Falling Sun.”

As if to prove her point, Amanda’s body lit up even brighter, radiating light that was powerful enough Asher had to squint and look away. Blinking away the spots once she was done with her demonstration, Asher shook his head.

“So what, are these greater powers just people who have made it into the Stage of Power?”

“No. As I touched on earlier, any noble with access to enough shards and time can reach the Stage of Power,” Amanda explained. “Greater powers are those who have shown they deserve to be there. They are not necessarily warriors, but they are people who have done tremendous things with their elements. For myself, I pushed deeper into the Deadlands than anyone else had in centuries, slaying a few powerful undead even capable of speech before getting back out again. That alone was enough to grant me my status.”

Huh… Guess that explains what killed the last few Servants the King of Death had tried to build.

“That makes sense,” Asher said, tilting his head as he thought. “Though I’m sort of surprised I haven’t heard more about this in the past.”

“Like you just assumed, most people think others are just talking about people within the Stage of Power when they hear anyone discuss greater powers,” Amanda shrugged. “It’s not something that really comes up much except in discussions between leaders of nations. You are currently living within the Noala Kingdom, correct? Have you heard of Gran?”

“Wait, Gran?” Asher laughed. “Are you serious? I’m not surprised that she’s one of these special, greater powers, but is that actually her moniker?”

“Despite its small size, the Noala Kingdom is decently well known for hosting three greater powers,” Amanda nodded. “After Gran, there’s the High Prince, also known as The Immortal, and Winged Death. Each one is famous for their own reasons.”

“I’m not surprised to hear Donvath is that well known either,” Asher admitted. “But ‘Winged Death’? Who is that?”

“One of the heads of the noble houses. That is all I know,” Amanda shrugged. “She is said to hold the Dragon element. She earned her moniker a few decades back when the Lambit Republic tried to send a small army through the Noala Kingdom’s borders in order to launch a surprise attack on the Eight Crests. According to the rumors, Winged Death took it upon herself to personally destroy the entire army, flying overhead and raining dragon fire down on their heads. Those tend to be the sorts of things that grant one the title of a greater power.”

“No kidding,” Asher muttered, realizing he was a little more closely connected to that person than he thought. He’d only heard of one person in the Noala Kingdom having a Dragon element.

And he’d all but killed her sister and tricked her son into befriending him before immediately betraying that trust.

Figures Theorn’s mom and Leighann’s sister is one of these super-special people… Just my luck.

“But enough about the powers that be of this world,” Amanda said, ripping him from his thoughts as she smiled. “I do not expect an answer just yet to my request. It will take a few hours to finish preparations for the ceremony, you have plenty of time to think about it. And I should clarify, your five-million-shard reward is entirely unrelated. Regardless of whether you wish to throw in with me and my people, after tonight, you will be a wealthy man.”
