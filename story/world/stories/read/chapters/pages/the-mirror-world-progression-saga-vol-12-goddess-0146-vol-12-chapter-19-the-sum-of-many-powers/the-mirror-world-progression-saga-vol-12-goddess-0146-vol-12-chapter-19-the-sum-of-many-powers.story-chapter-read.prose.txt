As the elite groups organized and began moving out, Samantha moved to the general center of her army and used her ability obtained in Ozra’s Challenge Room, [Wreath of the Clouds]. She reached upward with a force that enshrouded a rather significant ‘cut’ of the planet and pulled down a mass of the visible cloud spectrum. The cloud matter transmuted into pneumanic magic that formed around her entire army.

Every soldier had the cloud magic swirl and cover them in a shimmering, misty sheath, mostly transparent except at a distance. It still had a nice, subtle aesthetic to it, especially seen in bulk.

Pretty. I’ve got little time for such admiration, though.

As cheers went out under a suddenly brighter day, Samantha zipped across the battlefield, getting her target pointed out in mind as she went. Just to be safe and to please the Brain Trust, she kept [Conceptual Body: Body Like a Relic] up, forming her skin into steel, and thus regenerative armor. On the other hand, thanks to [Create], she had no need for it to be advertised blatantly. She kept the appearance of skin — it was just ‘adamantine skin,’ essentially.

She’d already destroyed a few towers with ease, though they had caught on to her attacks and started getting more defensive if they detected her. All this served was to consume more of her time, as nothing stopped her, just delayed her.

Ironically, it did make her pivot to sneaking around a bit more for a while, less to hide, more to effectively ambush.

She approached her target fortress heavily cloaked from detection, flying not far over the heads of the enemy soldiers' groups. As she got in range, the tower was just performing a spell — one of their go-tos was an AoE force pillar similar to Samantha’s own version, and they could target it over the heads of their enemies, allowing good range compared to most things.

Samantha unleashed a rapid sequence of spells, first a divine dispel, followed by a wide detonation via Seal of Detonation which blew up inner stone layers and exposed various routes inward. Obtaining more information as she went, she located the inner alcove of the wizards, dispelled the protections over it, and proceeded to layer multiple FE-based attacks inside while they were still scrambling to adjust and defend.

Her levels were vastly tyrannical over theirs, especially as their ritual circle was disrupted by damage. They didn’t last long, getting massacred to the man, and Samantha had conserved most of her MP after the first needed bit.

Soldier marksmen began to respond, peppering her with arrows and bolts that just bounced off of her skin. She barely noticed them.

One last wave of her hand via simple telekinesis collapsed the rest of the tower down on itself. Samantha zipped away and re-cloaked to stealth over to her next target. She left a hyper-realistic [Create] double behind and under independence, to distract and cause chaos, artistically tweaked to be even a bit more terrifying than she was on the battlefield. However, as it began waging warfare with faux-magic, it caused more than distraction and terror. It was scoring heinous casualties on the surrounding soldiers.

I think that’s a great new addition to my strategy, then. But how quickly will they counter?

As she had the extra available, along the way, she formed more terrorizing instances of [Create] as her doubles, softening up ranks as the soldiers struggled to defend themselves, much less find a way to counter. Wizards futilely attempted dispels to no avail, and anti-conjuration effects didn’t have the levels, even though they probably had high bonuses. Samantha sensed a few contests fail against her that would’ve worked a week ago.

Gee, I guess those days are done, you motherfuckers. Eat it!

She moved on to the next target hard point, debating internally whether she could continue to use MP like she was.

As if on cue, Dax sent a mental message. “Keep using those Seals. It fucks ‘em up too efficiently. I’ll make sure you are fueled with MP from rotating items.”

Samantha winced. “Dax, those could go to others, too. I know they could.”

“Do you really think anyone compares to you in efficiently destroying shit? It may be expensive, but it almost annihilates a fortress in two seconds! And you move from target to target almost as fast, practically undetected. No one can compete with this, not in combination. Just keep doing it, Sam. This is our best use of the resources.”

“Mmph. Fine.”

Aiming to repeat her success, Samantha flashed across the space to the next tower. Not far off, she saw another one in the corner of her eye fall, collapsing into rubble. She didn’t need to see who did it to recognize the handiwork. She smiled.

Six Moons Falling. Elsewhere, Gelmak is doing the same, and at another, Bast; others, Canmore, Palthennius, Michael, Zadkiel, Redberry, Keinauz… on and on. We can do this. No matter how many towers they erect, no matter how many walls they put up, we’ll break them all.

A curiosity did pop into her head. As soon as she made contact with Dax to ask, the other interrupted her.

“Oh yes,” Dax declared. “They’re being let loose, too. Most are rather slow, but one, or sometimes two, to a tower is all that’s needed.”

Damn. Nice. I wish I had time to see it.

Summoned as one to the battlefield, brought together under the ever-changing, ever-merging, and therefore ever all-encompassing banner of Sir Arthur, the Knights of the Round acted as one. No matter how far apart, no matter what they were doing, when the whole was complete, it was an unbroken chain.

Sir Galahad and Sir Gawain coordinated to precision-cut through a hole in a fortress. Galahad held off the reinforcements as Gawain went within, laughing as spells tore into him from the wizards, and the green stone of his body regenerated the damage invariably, faster and faster with each wound. Just as invariably, he cut down the panicking wizards with glee, soon ending their efforts forever.

The great duo of Lamorak and Bedwyr did something similar, with Lamorak causing damage and distraction on the outside, while Bedwyr went within. But the one-handed prodigy made no incision, by some mysterious means finding his way in. The wizards inside died no less in terror, but they barely ever saw their assailant at all. A blur in the shadows was their executor.

Sir Bors needed no assistance, nor guile. Ignoring attacks of the enemy like annoying gnats, he stomped up to the tower and put his arms and shoulders against it. With strain, with a great bellow, he toppled it, and the ants inside went scattering. Rather slow, Sir Bors only caught a few before they fled, but the tower’s functionality was ended nonetheless.

Sir Kay and Sir Percival coordinated, as they often did. They walked past the soldiers unmolested, by the uncanny power and aura of Percival. He also knocked on the door and was let in to tell a story. The wizards sat down, transfixed to listen, as Sir Kay went around to each and whispered in their ear. One by one, they fell over dead.

Sir Lucan’s targeted tower endured several charges as an odd, solo hit-and-run that caused nothing but confusion. Each time, he soundly hit a section of the base of the tower. On the fifth, though, the tower groaned once and then collapsed down on itself.

The targeted tower of Sir Tristan also fell apart. In this case, though, little could be said but that those nearby heard a lovely harp playing for a while. At the end of a song, the tower toppled.

Sir Lancelot, in contrast to the rest, ripped apart tower and wizard alike three times as fast, and nothing, absolutely nothing, could stop his great blade from cutting through any of it.

But the blade was slowed in part by its own will, for it appeared in many places second by second, all over the battlefield. Once, Galahad needed it for a moment, and it was there, wielded and cutting through soldiers gumming him up. Elsewhere, a random soldier was menaced by an enemy, left defenseless on the ground, and suddenly the great blade was in his hand, and he stabbed it clean through his foe… and then was baffled, as the sword disappeared yet again, to help another in need.

Sir Arthur could not be said to be any slower than Lancelot. He charged through enemies with a continuous industrious sprint, his aura knocking the lesser cannon fodder aside, until finally he leapt atop the walls surrounding a tower. Then, he raised Excalibur high and swung it down almost casually, causing massive destruction propagated more as if a true titan’s blade had just descended. So broad was the ‘cut’ that it was more like the titan’s boot had come down, obliterating the fortress and making a pit into the ground.

So the golem knights did their duty, feeling and knowing each other’s success intrinsically, as they had countless times before. It was a job to them; it was work to be done, though one of honorable duty. Tirelessly, implacably, they continued, like rote, their inborn experience unmatched in the world.

They’d been around long enough to know a warm-up when they engaged in it. The final act was far from over, and, as ever, they were eager to meet it head-on. To underestimate them as a whole, to fail in understanding the parts and the sum of their unity?

That was damning indeed.
