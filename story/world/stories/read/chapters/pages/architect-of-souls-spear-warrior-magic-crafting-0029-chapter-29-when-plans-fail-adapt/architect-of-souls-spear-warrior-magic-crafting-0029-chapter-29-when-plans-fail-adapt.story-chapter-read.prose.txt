The next morning, Ferris awoke happy. He’d made it an entire day without the oath statue being destroyed. Not that there is any doubt, of course, he decided.

After breakfast, Ferris and Jade took a packed lunch and set out. Mother Lily had given him directions to a stationary shop near the library, and Ferris’ first order of business was to acquire paper.

Entering the store, Ferris could see why it was next to the library. It catered specifically to the researchers and library patrons. He was able to find a good-sized journal—complete with writing pencil—costing him only a moderate price; well below what it would have cost back home. It was more expensive than the simple meals and necessities he had been buying, but he was able to afford it thanks to the mundane money Uncle Winton had given him.

Armed with his new journal, Ferris wanted to test Jade’s memory. He had thought of a couple more things to try after Cyrus’ advice last night. They found a nearby park and sat down, enjoying the morning. Open space in a bastion was always valuable, so the park was not large. It was carefully maintained, with plenty of spots for people to linger placed along a walking path.

First, Ferris wanted to evaluate Jade’s memory in general. Ferris had tried to keep his lists of names to something that he could remember, but now he wanted to try something more repeatable.

With each passing day, Ferris and Jade’s got better at communicating over the bond, but that often only showed how far they had to go. Some of the slime’s thoughts were an indecipherable mess, while others were just strange.

When Ferris had listed out names of famous delvers back home, he remembered them based on their stories. But Jade… didn’t. It was hard for Ferris to explain, but the slime appeared to have no problem directly memorizing them.

It was similar to when Jade tried to share their perspective of the world. Ferris would remember what was in a room by seeing a visual image in his mind. Jade somehow knew more details, but they weren’t part of a cohesive whole. More of a list of facts the slime could reference at will.

It was all confusing to experience, but it worked for the slime. At least, until they forgot it all the next day.

Ferris took out the journal, opened it to a new page and wrote down twenty random numbers. He then told them to Jade over the bond, waited a couple minutes, and asked the slime to repeat them back. Jade remembered them all easily. Ferris didn’t think he could memorize them that fast, but at this point he wasn’t surprised at all that the slime could. If he could figure out the long-term recollection problems, Jade would have a much better memory than him.

Next, he tried fifty numbers, and then one hundred, and then five hundred. Amazingly, the slime still had perfect memory, able to recite them all with no trouble. Now Ferris was a bit shocked. For him, remembering fifty numbers was impossible, one hundred silly… and five hundred was scary.

It had taken a long time to recite five hundred numbers. Jade was even able to easily recite numbers from the earlier tests. The experience, while a bit uncanny, reminded Ferris of his disagreement with Foreman Carter’s opinion on slime intelligence. Slimes weren’t ‘dumb as rocks.’ They just thought differently. Jade had taken some time to learn to understand visual pictures, as eyesight was a foreign concept. They had excellent spacial awareness… and apparently unreasonably good short-term memory.

They tried a similar experiment with names, and Jade had similarly perfect recall. He didn’t go to the same extreme as five hundred numbers, but the slime showed no sign of hesitation reciting words.

The strange wonder Ferris felt as Jade kept perfectly recalling each name and number reminded him of Cyrus’ description of unknown biases. Ferris had known Jade could directly memorize things well, but he’d assumed there was a natural limit. He’d never really tested it. Despite the foreign feeling to Jade’s thoughts, he’d still applied human logic to the slime.

As Ferris recovered from his surprise, he started getting excited. Jade could do something Ferris had never even imagined, and it was fun to discover. Last night he’d been afraid of Cyrus’ unknown bias, but now actually learning something new…

It was thrilling.

Ferris eventually filled a page with numbers to test later that day. He figured with more numbers he could test much more regularly, marking them off as he went.

Next, he gave Jade the dozen names of fish he had used two days ago. He wanted to see if repeating the same name several days in a row affected memory. Would the slime be able to remember the fish longer than the numbers?

His last idea was to experiment with the bond’s mental communication. Yesterday, Jade had recalled the names of famous delvers much longer than previous tests. Ferris had fondly remembered the delvers, even telling Jade a bit of their stories. Afterwards, he’d wondered if this added additional weight through the bond, making the memory last longer. Thus, he told more stories, sending as much imagery and feeling over the bond as he could.

Even if the last experiment failed… it was fun, and Jade liked it.

For lunch, Ferris and Jade ate Mother Lily’s packed lunch. On a whim, Ferris started a new journal page to record the food ratings. The game had been a consistent memory that Jade never lost, perhaps because the slime played it at every meal. Ferris wanted to know if the ratings would change day to day for similar foods, or if there was a pattern to the slime’s preferences. Plus, it was fun.

That inspired a new test, to see if Jade could ‘keep’ something in memory by thinking about it regularly.

“Jade,” Ferris said over the bond, “my family name is Charla, named after the pepper. When I ask, can you remember that?” He added a mental image to the word, as well as his memory of how it tasted.

“Yes,” the slime responded, “Charla, like the pepper.”

Ferris smiled. He would ask Jade what his family name was many times throughout the day, and see how long they could keep this memory alive.

After lunch, it was time for Ferris and Jade’s appointment with Serena. They had agreed to meet at the fourth district’s training hall and use one of the practice rooms for their experimentation. Ferris had used one in the fifth district—although the attack he tested was a failure—but this was his first time visiting the training hall closest to his residence.

Serena had arrived early and got them a room. An attendant showed him the way when he mentioned his name. The room had the same sandy floor and beat-up walls, although there was a table, chairs, and a cot provided. A rather vicious looking knife lay on the table. Serena likely expected Ferris to meditate on the cot, but his mental body was still recovering.

“Ah,” Serena stood up as he entered the room, “Ferris and Jade, welcome.”

“Thank you, Serena.”

“I am eager to get started. Now, I have studied the regenerative nature of slimes several times recently…” Serena stopped, noticing something was wrong from Ferris’ expression.

“I apologize, but my cultivation has had some… difficulty. I won’t be able to participate in our planned tests today.”

Serena’s excitement deflated, and she sat back down.

“I see. I am sorry to hear that,” she said with a resigned expression, “I understand how difficult that can be.”

“While we cannot proceed with our plan today,” Ferris began, a little hesitant, “I was hoping to receive some related advice.”

Serena, who had started gazing off to the side, lost in thought, looked back at Ferris.

“You have read the observation journal on Janice Landsworn’s,” Ferris asked, to which Serena nodded. “In it she mentions Gumly’s natural ability, Adaptation.”

“Yes,” Serena said, eyes darting to the side. “If I recall correctly, she praised the ability, claiming it to be the reason there are so many variations of slimes in the rifts.”

“I have hopes that Adaptation will help with acquiring Acid Reconstruction, but…” Ferris said slowly. He was working his way up to discussing very personal information for a cultivator. His contract with Hollowfield required him to share some details, and he would need to work together with Serena on such topics, but… he had just met her. It had been embarrassing enough to admit to having problems cultivating, what would she think of him after this?

Despite his hesitation—or perhaps due to it—Serena caught on.

“An ability like Adaptation would likely be more… complicated than typical,” she said diplomatically.

“Yes,” Ferris sighed, and committed to admitting his weakness, “to be honest, I am having trouble understanding how it works.” He had searched the entire area of the slimespace after bonding Jade, but seen nothing that appeared to be the ability. First Steps of the Soul had taught him how to feel the presence of an ability, which worked on the Consume Pool and Digest River. But he hadn’t seen any sign of Adaptability.

“That can be more common than you think.” Rather than berate him, she grimaced in memory. “You are forging a new path, don’t expect to get it right immediately. Instead, we should make a plan to test and understand the ability.”

Serena motioned to Ferris to sit at the table with her. He had been caught up apologizing and asking for help and had neglected to move from the entrance of the door. Flushing with a little embarrassment, he set Jade down to explore the room and took a seat.

“Now, the standard method of learning from a manual involves creating a very specific set of spiritual circumstances that allow the ability to appear. We will discuss that more when you learn Acid Resistance. In contrast, the description from the journal of Adaptation sounds like it works through reaction to physical conditions.”

Serena lifted the knife she had left on the table, deftly flourishing it in her solitary hand. She had likely intended to use the blade on Jade. The slime wouldn’t feel any pain from such a weapon—at least if she didn’t hit the core—but it made Ferris pale slightly.

“I cannot say that I have experience with abilities that generate or affect creation of other abilities,” Serena shrugged, and sheathed the knife at her waist, “but I would recommend you investigate any and all interaction with the growth of core fruit.”

Ferris noticed she said she ‘cannot say,’ rather than directly stating she had no experience. Was she trying to tell him she had experience, without directly stating it? It was a little odd that she didn’t just deny knowledge, as this was exactly the kind of double-speak an oath contract like Ferris’ should prevent, although he had no idea if the clan bound their own members similarly.

Or maybe it meant nothing, and she actually didn’t have any experience with such abilities.

Considering the advice, Ferris thought about his Core Tree and the fruit that was growing. There were odd yellow pillars around the top rim of the tree’s tower, but they were all over the slimespace as well. The dark green crystal beneath the tree matched Uncle Winton’s description of a core, so that likely wasn’t it. The crystal and the tree were surrounded by black rock ground only present in that one place… but it was rock; how could that be a representation of Adaptability? The grey ants and birds were everywhere; either might be related to the ability in the same way the clams were part of Digest. Ferris wasn’t sure why the ants would throw themselves in acid though. He hadn’t seen them—or anything—directly touch the tree, but he wasn’t always watching.

“Beyond that, I would suggest you recreate the situation that Janice described in the journal. She claimed that the Gumly would acquire abilities that overcome specific problems the slime had,” she spread her arms out. “Thus, we give Jade a problem, and watch to see if an ability develops that solves it.”

“So, I would ask you, Ferris.”

She leaned forward, getting into it.

“What is a problem you need solved?”
