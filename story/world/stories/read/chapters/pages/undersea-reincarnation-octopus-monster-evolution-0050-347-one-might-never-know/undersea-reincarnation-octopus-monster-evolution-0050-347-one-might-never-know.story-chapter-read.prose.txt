It was like an explosion had gone off in this relatively shallow section of the ocean. Flares of telekinetic purple mixed with the white of churned water while Henry and his manifested Octominds poured all of their focus into locking down the wiry, pale-yellow Stinglance. Henry and Maurice were ready to attack, having channeled a decent chunk of their own mana into a desperate, reckless attempt at bringing the creature down, and for all of their effort, Henry could almost taste the sneer the seahorse was feeling at their attempt.

It was a powerful specimen. It was fast. And it stood in its own domain. Why would it need to fear these two upstarts that were about to become its lunch?

The seahorse’s arrogance only made what followed more enjoyable.

Henry had the pleasure of sensing its smugness dissolve away when it found itself unable to break free from his grasp, just as one of Henry’s arms crept towards it while Maurice’s Domain activated right atop its head.

The abilities activated all at once, and everything around shuddered. Lightning Discharge burned through Henry’s arm and flew out of his limb, illuminating the sea for a fraction of a second with a blinding blue-white flare. Water sizzled and boiled, and what remained of Henry’s arm was then shredded and torn as Maurice’s own dark, ominous Imploding Bubble detonated, sucking in the blood, algae, water, and even the light of day into its inky blackness.

Henry’s Telekinesis was, of course, torn asunder, and his Octominds reeled from the backlash, with a of them taking so much of it that they lost their corporeal form. But Henry had sensed it. It wasn’t just his and Maurice’s attacks that had broken his skill. The seahorse had done something. And as the blood and disturbed water cleared, he saw it wafting off the form of the Stinglance like a corona of purple, ominous flames that grew realer by the second.

The Stinglance hadn’t revealed everything it was capable off. Some of its leaves had been shredded, and Henry could see quite a few burns marring its skin. But it wasn’t anywhere close to dead. If fact, it looked even more pissed off, if that was even possible.

“[Shit. Second form. Plan failed. We run,]” Henry tersely sent.

“[Second what?]”

Rarely did his senses scream at him like they were doing now, and he knew this wasn’t aura or a fear effect. That purple magic was bad news, and through his mana sight, he could see it literally eat at the ambient mana.

Then a little bit of greed slipped through the weariness. Was that a venom?

Henry cursed, shoving aside the distracting thought and began hopping away in short teleports through his Shadows. He still had upwards of fifty of them assigned to this fight, so it wasn’t hard to keep an eye on the monster and see it tear through the water after them.

It wasn’t willing to let them go unpunished and frankly, Henry didn’t want to leave, either. But he didn’t have that many options to take it down. They’d just thrown everything they had at it, and it hadn’t been that effective.

Unless I compress a big attack, he thought, skin clammy as he continued hopping away while the creature tore his clones to shred. He wasn’t sure what was more scary. The monster at his heels or the damage a powerful compressed skill could do to him. But so long as he didn’t die, Henry could bounce back.

He didn’t really want to run.

“[It’s pursuing,]” he sent to Maurice. “[Buy us some time, I’m gonna try something else.]”

“[Okay!]” the crab shot out, with no hesitation whatsoever. Immediately detonations began ringing in the path of the Stinglance, forcing it to weave and dodge, and when Maurice sent the faster but smaller varieties of the bubbles to save on mana, the seahorse just ignored them, barreling through them without slowing. Henry watched with a wince as the bubbles just… fizzled out and dissolved, the magic itself corroding under the liquid burning out of the seahorse. Because now that it was moving, it was clear that it wasn’t fire but a substance, and it was leaving a dark trail in its wake, coloring the water and wiping away the mana in its vicinity.

Henry really wanted to get that ability.

As his Octominds supported his effort to work on two simultaneous skills—three if you count the Leviathan fang being set at the tip of his arm—he wished he could chuck the monster in his storage but, he didn’t know what a hostile, powerful enemy could do in his Maw, and he didn’t want to try with this guy.

“[It’s almost here!]” Maurice shouted, though Henry was very much aware. He could see it. The implosions weren’t slowing the monster down whatsoever, no matter how much they turned and twisted around the rocky reefs to lose it. And while his tooth-capped arm was ready, the other two skills weren’t.

He needed time to compress them a little more. Only a few more seconds. But he didn’t know if he had those seconds with how fast the creepy little bastard was coming at them.

They really had pissed it off. Though that might work in their favor.

“[This is probably gonna hurt me, Maurice. You want to come with or do you want me to send you up top?]”

Henry would rather not have Maurice too close, but he also didn’t want to force him away. For a moment, he considered telling Velistraine about the seahorse… but he didn’t want to run back to the ship with his tail tucked between his legs. He didn’t work so hard to just call for help when things got tough.

He didn’t want to run. And from what he was sensing off of the crab, neither did Maurice. “[No… I’ll stay. I’ll at least help when we’re trying to get away. If things don’t go well.]”

On that note, Henry stopped talking and focused up on the approaching Stinglance. His Octominds were busy keeping the two skills stable, and Henry hoped the damage wouldn’t be too bad. The two skills have been condensed to occupy nearly half their usual size, which made them feel at least twice as intense, if not more. Both instances of Dispelling Pulse and Lightning Discharge were becoming pretty uncomfortable within his soul, like two pressurized, burning coals that ached and threatened to tear him from the inside out.

That’s the limit, I think. I can’t afford to push it more, Henry realized. His little compression exercises were most likely not enough to prep for this. Over weeks, they might have tempered his soul enough to handle this, but so far, the progress would be meager, at best. But just like that Riptide fight. Just like taking the fight to the Trickster and just like everything else, Henry wouldn’t move forward if he didn’t take risks. Though knowing the danger of what he was doing, he pulled on the essence of Lifeweaving, surrounding the area in his soul where both skills had collected, then he turned his gaze toward the unrelenting, algae-shaped demon that was bearing down on them.

No risks. No rewards.

Henry suddenly twisted around. Facing the monster, he blurred toward it, meeting the creature right above a particularly high reef and surprising it with his sudden change of heart. But the surprise barely lasted, and the corona of mana-dissolving toxins spread wider as Henry cut through the water separating them.

Right then and without stopping, Henry stuck Maurice to a clone and left him behind. Just so he wouldn’t get hit with the lightning.

Henry wasn’t using anything to speed himself up. He would have accelerated with a Lightning Dash or a quick teleport, but he really believed that casting any other skill risked tearing his spirit wide open. He was already under such strain that he was barely holding everything together, even with the help of the Octominds. But that was fine. The Stinglance wasn’t dodging, and Henry just needed to get close enough.

Moment of truth, Henry grimly thought as he came with arm’s reach of the Stinglance. Accepting the dart that flew at his eye, he braced, ignoring the pain as his eye melted away while a compressed Dispelling Pulse exploded out of him, engulfing himself and the seahorse. The A-rank’s movement stuttered, and the purple corona lost most of its color, but Henry didn’t doubt that it’d be back within moments. And that’ll be too late for the Stinglance.

Ignore the gaping ache he felt from the compressed Dispel and because Henry couldn’t afford to lose, he nudged the remaining skill a little more, adding some Predation to the Lightning Discharge. The skill nexus had been like a hot, painful coal only a moment ago. Now it was agonizing to just hold on to though before he let it out, the Leviathan fang blurred through the water, aiming to punch a thigh-wide hole into the A-rank’s chest.

Thankfully, the seahorse wasn’t having much of a good time, either.

Pressurizing a skill made it hit nearly as hard as if it were being empowered by a decent amount of essence. So his pressurized Dispelling Pulse managed to match the Stinglance’s ability, considering it had been too confident to use essence itself. But when Henry both pressurized and channeled Predation into Lightning Discharge?

The result would have had him completely awestruck if he hadn’t been in so much damn pain.

When the bolt left Henry’s arm, it lit up the world with a blinding shade of red. The water around his arm and around the seahorse turned to plasma, right before a sharp, whining clap rang out. Scalding, superheated and pressured water deep-cooked his extended limb and thanks to the improved cognition afforded to him by his Octominds, he could feel every fraction of it. And that was nothing compared to the twisting ache in his soul.

To say the double-empowered Lightning Discharge had been effective would be selling it short.

Henry was about to step into A-rank himself, and while being near an attack of this intensity would have vaporized him before, the physical damage was nothing he couldn’t heal in a few minutes. So ignoring the pain, he pushed mana into the Leviathan’s tooth and nearly passed out from the effort of moving his mana, but he had no time to waste.

The Stinglance was floating limply in front of him, fins twitching. Its dark brown eyes were barely visible under the swollen and crisped ridges, and its veil of caustic toxins was stuttering, as if it was trying to get back to work but its whole body was unresponsive, and while he saw its gaze flick to the oncoming stab, the only reaction it could manage was a half-hearted shot of another envenomed dart that didn’t even get close to hitting Henry.

The fang tore through its chest, and Henry’s vision blurred further. One of his eyes was still burning from the earlier envenomed shot he’d taken, and even more toxins were coursing through his veins. He almost didn’t notice the notification come through, as he was focusing all of his being on not passing out from the pain that pulsed through his whole body and soul. His Octominds—those that hadn’t been knocked out by the backlash—were all working on healing him, even the one keeping an eye on their surrounding, but every pulse of mana or skill usage was excruciating.

“[Henry! Henry, can you hear me? I put its corpse in my storage, I’ll take us up, okay? Don’t sleep!]”

Henry hazily felt a tug on his arm as he pushed through the pain and took stock of the state of his soul. It was hard to focus in this state but what he felt immediately made his blood run cold. It was as if all of it had become… cracked. The once smooth surface was all full of hairline cracks that pulsed with pain whenever any mana moved around them. He could channel his mana and everything, but it was exceedingly painful.

Sparing his attention for a second to tap Maurice on the claw in response—so he didn’t need to activate Telepathy—Henry gently channeled the essence of Lifeweaving through his body and by extension, through his spirit, and it was like he had just poured cold water over a fresh burn. It was painful, but after a few seconds, he could feel the healing energies take the edge off the pain. After all, the upgraded Life Surge did say that it accelerated soul healing, but this might take a couple of days. The soul did not heal as fast as his flesh. It wasn’t anywhere close to that. On the other hand, with Life Surge being so effective on his flesh, Henry was slowly becoming more aware of his surroundings and soon enough, he had enough focus and energy to look through his clones.

Less than a minute ago, Henry could swim by himself, which surprised Maurice. The crap took his usual spot and chattered to Henry, relieved, but there was no mistaking the tension still hovering above them. That had been close. That had been dangerous.

But Henry couldn’t help but re-think of that bolt of lightning he’d unleashed. He had to make this new technique his own. He had to master it.

One might never know when he’d need it.
