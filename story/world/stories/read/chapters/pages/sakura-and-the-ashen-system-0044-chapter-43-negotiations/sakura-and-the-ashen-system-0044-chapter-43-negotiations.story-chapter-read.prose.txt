The man asked with obvious hostility, "Gilbert, who did you drag to us?"

Sakura immediately understood two things. First, this man really did know Gilbert well, and she could hear it in his tone. Second, he had about as much joy at the meeting as a man who had found a scorpion in his boot.

Gilbert stopped in front of him completely calmly, which, admittedly, was typical of him. After everything they had gone through just on the descent into the canyon, his composure seemed almost indecent.

"Marven," he said first.

Sakura glanced at the man again. So this was Marven. The name Gilbert had mentioned before, the one that had made the scouts stop shooting first and asking questions later.

Only then did Gilbert answer the actual question.

"Sakura."

"I heard the name, let’s say. Now try answering the question."

Sakura, still angry because of the trap and with remnants of sticky filth on her clothes, held back from saying anything unnecessary only because armed mages were standing around them.

"She’s with me," Gilbert said.

Marven looked at him with tired irritation and folded his arms across his chest.

"I already understood that too. You’re always this laconic when you don’t want to explain anything properly."

"No. I’m simply saving us time."

"It doesn’t help. It only pisses off the people who, for example, are deciding whether to let you into the camp or not."

Gilbert did not answer. He only moved one shoulder slightly, as if acknowledging the argument.

Marven shifted his gaze to Sakura. She noticed again that although he did not look old, his eyes were too stern, almost ancient. The man was sturdy, grim, with close-cropped hair and an extremely wary gaze. Sakura immediately disliked that gaze because it was too attentive and literally burned right through her. As if Marven had already seen much, much more than he was saying.

"Are you a mage?" he asked.

"Seems like it," Sakura answered.

"Seems like it?"

Sakura could have lied and said that yes, she really was a mage. But she remembered how Gilbert had reacted to her during their very first meeting, back in the dungeon. He had immediately sensed that something was wrong with her. Based on that, Sakura decided that other mages like Gilbert could probably sense it too. Which meant there was no point in lying. That was why she answered Marven.

"I’m still getting familiar with the local terminology."

Someone among the scouts behind him snorted quietly, but Marven did not even smile. He kept looking at her more and more closely, and Sakura felt unpleasant tension crawl under her skin.

"Show me your hand."

Sakura froze.

"My hand?"

"Your left one."

At that point, even pretending was useless. Sakura automatically pressed her left arm closer to her body. Gilbert turned his head slightly toward Marven and said with a hint of warning, "Don’t push."

"I haven’t started yet," Marven said dryly. "She reeks like someone hid a bonfire in a sack and hoped no one would notice the smoke. If you brought her here, I want to know exactly what you brought."

"She’s not a threat to your camp."

"That’s not for you to decide."

Sakura inhaled slowly. People were watching them from all around: tired, wary, armed. Adults, teenagers, a couple of children near a distant awning. They were not a crowd of murderers or terrifying enemies like some she had already met on the road. Like Sakura and Gilbert, these people were also fugitives, only with different experience and probably stronger paranoia. But not without reason.

She did not want to show the mark, but she wanted even less to let this turn into a fight because of her. Especially when she saw how tense Gilbert was. Sakura almost felt pleased that he was standing up for her like this, and still, it was unnecessary. The people in front of them were not enemies.

"All right," she said quietly.

And rolled up her left sleeve. The mark opened to the cold canyon air: dark petals, an ash-obsidian pattern with a scarlet trace, as if something living slept inside her arm. Several people in the camp immediately tensed. One of the scouts with a bow stepped half a pace to the side, taking a more convenient position.

Marven went a little pale, and Sakura noticed it.

"So it’s true," he said.

Gilbert did not move.

"What exactly?"

"Don’t play with me. A scout from Ogrom brought rumors about a girl with an obsidian beast. And about the escape from the arena. This is an Obsidian Hierophant. I decided most of those rumors were just city nonsense."

"Unfortunately, not all of them," Sakura said and lowered her sleeve again. "Though personally, I would have preferred to turn out to be just city nonsense."

Marven looked at her differently. Not more warmly, but more attentively.

"Do you understand what is coming after you?"

"We had a couple of opportunities to notice."

"Then why are you here?"

"Because we don’t have any good options."

Marven grunted and turned back to Gilbert.

"You disappear when we need hands. Then you come back when you’re neck-deep in trouble yourself. And you bring someone here who is being searched for everywhere."

"I didn’t come to hide behind you."

"And how is that supposed to look from the outside?"

"Like a bad idea," Gilbert said. "But not like a betrayal. We will leave soon. And we were careful not to bring a tail behind us."

Marven looked at him for several long seconds. Then he exhaled through his nose.

"Until dawn. No longer."

Sakura blinked.

"What?"

"You can stay until dawn," Marven said irritably, this time to her. "Not because I like you so much, but because we don’t hand fugitive mages over to the authorities. Even very inconvenient fugitive mages."

"Thank you," Sakura said carefully.

"Don’t thank me. If they come after you, we won’t die to protect your secret."

"Still, thank you. We’re grateful. Truly," Sakura insisted.

Marven did not appreciate her stubborn gratitude, or only pretended not to appreciate it.

Meanwhile, Gilbert said, "Marven, we need to talk. The three of us."

By that, Gilbert was carefully implying that they needed a conversation without extra eyes and ears.

Marven looked wearily at the sky, which from here was visible only in fragments between the canyon walls.

"Of course. Because all of this was only the introduction."

"Yes."

He waved for the scouts to disperse. Then he led Gilbert and Sakura behind a stone outcrop at the edge of the camp. There, the noise grew duller: the crackling of fires, quiet voices, the rustle of cloth in the wind. Far enough that they would not be heard, but not so far that Sakura could forget they were next to a camp of fugitive mages.

Marven stopped first.

"Talk."

Gilbert was silent for a couple of seconds, and that was already a bad sign. Gilbert always knew what to say. Not now. Now he was choosing his words.

"We need a fresh core of a dead mage."

Marven’s face changed so quickly that Sakura tensed despite herself.

"Are you completely sick?"

"No."

"I strongly doubt that."

"I understand how it sounds."

"No, you don’t." Marven stepped closer. "You are standing in a camp of mages and asking me for the same thing hunters cut out of our people after death. You truly had enough nerve to come here with that request?"

Sakura felt everything inside her tighten unpleasantly. Even after all her conversations with Gilbert, it sounded worse than she had wanted to hope. Marven was right. Cutting into someone’s flesh, even a dead person’s, for your own benefit was ugly. But when she thought about it too much, her thoughts always returned to Lir. They had to do this for a reason. They had to do this for him.

Gilbert did not look away.

"You know I don’t need a prize or a trophy. I need a way to pass the inspection in the Castle of Justice."

Marven went still.

"In the Castle of Justice?"

"Yes."

"Wait. You want to go to the Castle of Justice? Voluntarily?"

"Yes."

"Then your first answer was definitely a lie. You are sick."

"Maybe."

"Gilbert." Marven frowned.

"They’re holding a boy there," the mage said. "Lir. He was taken as bait for her. If I get inside, we’ll have a chance to pull him out."

Marven shifted his gaze to Sakura. She did not know what exactly he saw on her face, but the man asked a little more quietly, "Who is he to you?"

"A friend," she said. Then she corrected herself, because that was too little. "His family helped me when I understood almost nothing and could have died. And now he’s there because of me."

"Do you know what they’ll do to him in the Castle of Justice?"

"I know. That’s exactly why we have to get him out. Please."

Marven turned away and cursed quietly.

"We don’t have a fresh core," he said at last. "And if the gods of Veyr suddenly decide to be kind, one won’t appear."

Sakura did not even immediately understand how strongly she had been hoping for a different answer. Hoping, even though she herself understood what a vile kind of hope that was.

Gilbert nodded as if he had already expected failure.

"And places where one might be? Anything at all."

"There were some. Not anymore. The last contact disappeared a week ago. Two of ours who knew the bypass routes were taken by hunters. Another died crossing the salt marshes. Don’t ask what was left of him. It won’t help."

"I see."

"No, you don’t," Marven said, looking at him harshly again. "We have a couple of graves where the earth hasn’t even had time to settle yet. And I can see that thought already moving in your head. So crush it right now. I will not dig up my own people for your plan."

Gilbert did not look away.

"I know. By the timing, they most likely wouldn’t work anyway."

"Then why did you come?"

"Because this was the nearest place where there might have been a chance."

Marven smiled without any humor.

"You always did know how to dress up desperation as calculation."

Gilbert did not answer that.

Sakura suddenly caught herself wanting to defend him too, even though Marven was not entirely wrong. That made it even more irritating. Everyone had their own truth, and there was definitely no single answer right now for what they should do.

"Can we at least spend the night?" she asked. "We can’t climb back up now. Not after that horrible descent."

Marven shifted his gaze from Gilbert to her, and irritation flickered in it.

"Until dawn. I already said that. Stay under the southern awning. Don’t wander around the camp much."

"Understood," Sakura said.

"And one more thing. If anyone asks why you came, you were looking for shelter. That’s all."

"No one here needs to know the rest," Gilbert said.

"Exactly."

When they returned, Sakura felt as if she had been wrung out from the inside. The Mages’ Refuge did not look like a truly safe place. It consisted of scattered tents, rolled packs, and small fires.

At the southern awning, Sakura and Gilbert were given a place. Not especially comfortable, but after the canyon, even a plain stone platform already felt like a gift. Sakura sat beside Gilbert and spent some time simply looking at the camp.

People were trying not to stare at the strangers. They were doing a poor job.

"So it was all for nothing," she said quietly.

"No."

"Gilbert, we came for the core. It isn’t here."

"This is not the end of our search."

She turned her head toward him.

"And where are we going to search next?"

Gilbert once again looked as if he knew more than he was saying.

"We’ll think about that tomorrow. Rest."

Sakura exhaled tiredly and leaned the back of her head against the stone.

"Sometimes I really understand why Marven gets angry with you."

"He isn’t angry because of that."

"Yes, I already noticed there’s a whole collection of reasons."

Gilbert glanced at her from the corner of his eye, but did not argue.

Sakura felt too cramped sitting in one place. After the conversation about the core, the camp around her seemed even more foreign. Outcasts lived here, people who others wanted either captured or destroyed. But even they looked at Sakura as a threat.

She stood up.

"I’ll walk a little. Not far."

"Marven said not to wander through the camp."

"Just a little. And not far."

Gilbert sighed wearily.

"Fine. Just don’t go past the fires."

Sakura nodded.

"And don’t test traps with your feet."

"I accidentally got caught in a net one time, and now you’re going to remind me of that for the rest of my life?"

"That’s usually how reputations are formed."

Sakura gave him a look that said his own reputation was not flawless either, and walked away along the edge of the camp. She moved slowly, trying not to draw attention. The ground and stones were cold. Dust hung in the air. The canyon walls rose upward in almost black planes.

At the far edge of the camp, Sakura stopped.

Off to the side, high above her head, something flickered.

It was dark and fast. A creature ran along the stone wall far ahead, almost dissolving into the shadows, and disappeared behind an outcrop. Sakura only managed to see the movement.

She frowned.

Most likely a scavenger. Or the canyon had decided to add free hallucinations to her exhaustion.

Sakura stood there for a few more seconds, peering into the darkness, but saw nothing else. Then she slowly turned back.

And heard a thin, frightened inhale.

Sakura immediately turned toward it. On the stone wall at the edge of the camp stood a boy of about eight or nine. He was skinny, wearing a jacket too large for him, with dark hair sticking out in every direction. The child had not climbed very high, but high enough that a fall would be unpleasant. With one hand, he was reaching for something stuck in a crack between the stones. A toy? Sakura did not have time to see.

The ledge under the boy’s foot cracked treacherously.

"Don’t move!" she said sharply.

The child froze. Sakura did not even have time to exhale before the stone suddenly crumbled, and the boy fell.

Sakura rushed toward him. She understood at once that she would not make it, because everything was happening too quickly. But her body was already searching for a solution on its own, and her hand struck the air, opening [Inventory].

The case with the scrolls appeared in her palm. Sakura almost at random pulled out the one she had placed closer after the last sorting, the one she recognized by the shape of the sealed energy. Sakura unrolled the paper and directed the magic upward.

[AIR CUSHION]

The air beneath the boy suddenly thickened, like an invisible cushion shoved between him and the ground almost too late. His fall slowed, his body turned in the air, and in the next moment he still crashed straight into Sakura.

They hit the stones together.

The air was knocked out of Sakura. Her elbow struck so hard that sparks flashed in her eyes, and someone’s small knee drove painfully into her side.

"Ow..." she forced out. "Alive?"

The boy lay on top of her in such terrified silence that it seemed he still had not decided whether he should immediately start screaming and go into hysterics.

"Hey," Sakura rasped. "How are you?"

The boy finally nodded.

"Alive."

"Great. Then get off. You’re small, but heavy."

He rolled to the side, frightened but whole. Sakura sat up, holding her side.

Almost immediately, a young, pale, terrified woman ran up to them.

"Neil!" She grabbed the boy by the shoulders. "What are you doing? I told you not to climb there!"

"There was..."

"I don’t care what was there!"

She quickly felt over the child, checking whether anything was broken. Then she raised her eyes to Sakura.

For one moment, Sakura thought the woman might actually say thank you.

But she only pulled her son closer.

"Come on," she said sharply. "Now."

"But she helped..."

"Come on, Neil."

And she led him away almost by force.

Sakura remained sitting on the ground. Then she slowly exhaled and stood up, feeling the pain in her elbow and side.

"You’re welcome," she muttered after them. "Always happy to have your children fall on me..."

Sakura thought she should not be too critical. The woman had been frightened. And she probably had the right to be frightened. To her, Sakura was not a savior, but a strange mage with an obsidian mark who had appeared in their camp together with Gilbert and new problems. But understanding that was easier than not feeling it.

When Sakura returned to Gilbert, he was standing and looked as if he either already knew or had definitely guessed what had happened.

"What happened?"

"A child decided the canyon walls weren’t dangerous enough for him."

"Are you hurt?"

"Humiliated. Only my elbow suffered. A little."

"Is the child unharmed?"

"Yes."

Gilbert gave a short nod.

"Good."

Sakura smiled without much amusement. She was just about to sit down again when a dry click sounded in the darkness. A small stone came loose somewhere high on the wall and rolled down, bouncing from ledge to ledge.

That was normal, because things were always falling from the canyon walls.

But then a second sound came, exactly the same. Sakura lifted her head and narrowed her eyes, trying to find the source. Meanwhile, the sound repeated a third time, then a fourth, then a fifth. By the tenth, Sakura lost count.

The camp changed instantly. Conversations cut off. One of the scouts grabbed his bow. Marven, by the central fire, sharply turned toward the canyon wall. Gilbert tensed too and looked ready to cast at any moment.

Sakura felt cold run over her skin.

On the upper ledge, between the stones, enormous black eyes gleamed. Sakura recognized the dry, long-legged body. She had seen a creature like that during the descent. A scavenger. Back then Gilbert had said that if they did not go after them first, they would not come after them either.

Apparently, someone had forgotten to tell the creature itself.

Canyon scavengers began crawling out of cracks, side ledges, and narrow stone gaps. There were many of them. They moved quickly and came from different sides, so Sakura could not count them. They moved low, almost silently, clinging to the stone with long legs. Their gray bodies looked dry and too light, and there was not even a hint of fear in their eyes.

Marven sharply raised his hand.

"Children to the back wall! Weapons!"

People burst into motion without unnecessary questions. Too quickly for this to be the first attack of their lives.

The scavengers were not supposed to do this. Sakura understood that from the mages’ faces. They knew these creatures. They expected trouble from them, maybe, but not a direct attack on the camp.

So something had stirred them up. Or driven them out of their shelter.

The first scavenger dropped from the wall.

And behind it, the entire dark canyon wall began to move.
