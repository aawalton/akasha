Ten thousand repulsor lifts shrieked at once, a mechanical chorus that rattled the teeth.

Revenant-class hover carriers dropped from the twilight sky. Their blocky hulls cast long shadows over the northern plains of Aerthos. They hit the designated landing zones hard, kicking up grey ash that hadn't settled in four years. Ramps slammed into the dirt.

Mark IV infantry automata marched out. Their optical sensors cut a uniform blue line through the gloom. Aegis Legionaries followed, their dark armor absorbing the little light that remained. The Dark Elves barked coordinates, establishing perimeters with ruthless speed. Every chest plate bore the silver crest of the roaring lion.

Heavier variants of the Revenants maneuvered into position. They lowered massive pallets covered in thick canvas. Mark III-B Engineering units swarmed the drops, ripping the tarps away. The modular components of heavy automated turrets lay exposed.

The engineers moved fast. Sparks showered the landscape as plasma welders fused adamantium base plates to the bedrock. Three distinct layers of defense took shape. The frontline would anchor the Fortress Breakers—cannons built to punch through enchanted siege armor. Behind them, rapid-fire suppression turrets were assembled, their rotating barrels ready to unleash walls of plasma. Interspersed throughout the lines, the snub-nosed domes of C-RAM units swept the sky, their tracking algorithms hunting for aerial threats.

Further out, specialized engineering squads tore into the earth. Localized gravitic pulses cut trenches, displacing tons of soil in seconds. Automata laid down razor-wire and kinetic mines. They carved a kill-zone into the bones of the continent.

...

The Hydra and the Kraken made landfall.

The two kilometer-long nuclear submarines drove themselves out of the Maelstrom Sea. They slammed straight onto the jagged northern shoreline. Their radar-absorbent hulls ground against the rocks. The screech of tortured metal echoed for miles. They settled heavily into the mud and sand.

Inside their bellies, the Genesis Forges roared to life with a new directive. The production of torpedoes halted. The automated factories began mass fabrication of structural components.

Heavy, insulated power cables were dragged from the submarines' reactor housings by teams of heavy-lift automata. The cables snaked across the scorched earth, glowing faintly with channeled energy. They led directly to two cleared tracts of land situated several miles inland, right behind the trench lines.

The build sites for the Colossus-class Crawlers were ready to receive their Parts.

...

The sky above the build sites darkened with the sweeping wingspans of the Centurion C-52 bombers.

They flew low and tight. Their eight-engine configurations rattled the engineers on the ground. The bay doors hissed open.

Hundreds of silver, rune-etched crates plummeted from their bellies. Specialized gravitic dampeners slowed their descent. They hit the ground with precise thuds.

These crates held a volume of refined star-iron, adamantium plating, and complex magitech circuitry that defied their physical dimensions. A single C-52 delivered the raw materials of an entire supply convoy.

The engineering units descended upon the crates immediately. They unpacked the compressed materials, the spatial enchantments unraveling with sharp pops of displaced air. Scaffolding rose around the two three-kilometer-wide sites. A skeletal framework of steel reached for the sky.

Zirhad coughed. He waved a hand to clear the dust and exhaust fumes billowing from a departing Revenant carrier. The air tasted like dirt and ozone. He preferred the sterile environment of the aerospace hangars aboard The Aegis, where the variables were known. But the sheer scale of this project demanded his presence on the ground.

He adjusted the collar of his uniform. He looked at the long, disciplined rows of Aegis Academy graduates standing at attention before him. Their faces were marked by exhaustion and focus.

"Alright, listen up," Zirhad barked, pacing the length of the formation. "The physical construction of the Crawler chassis is being handled by the automated systems. Your job is the nervous system. The mana conduits, the sensor arrays, the integration of the localized Forges. A single misaligned rune in the primary power transfer matrix, and we build a very large, very expensive crater."

He pointed a finger at a section of the rising scaffolding. "Squad Alpha, you're on the port-side thermal exhaust routing. Squad Beta, begin calibration of the kinetic shield emitters. The Lord Commander wants these operational before the Hegemony vanguard reaches the valley. We do not have the luxury of delays."

The young elves saluted, a crisp, unified motion. They broke off toward their assigned sectors.

High above the construction, the shriek of jet engines echoed across the sky. A squadron of W-29 Wyverns banked hard through the clouds, their thrusters burning bright. They flew routine combat air patrols, ensuring the airspace above the build sites remained uncontested.

...

Miles out to sea, the water was dark and choppy.

Theramore stood on the bridge of The Axiom. His good eye was fixed on the tactical display. He had arranged the 3rd Fleet into a tight, overlapping defensive arc. He was the rearguard, the iron wall protecting the vulnerable construction sites on the shore.

His destroyers formed the outer picket. Their radar arrays spun, searching the horizon for any sign of a Cinderfall counter-attack. The two Crusader-class carriers, The Templar and The Paladin, flanked his flagship. Their flight decks buzzed with the constant launch and recovery of the combat air patrols.

"Maintain the perimeter," Theramore grunted to Trisa. He tapped the display where the blue icons of his ships formed a solid line. "I want eyes on every wave between here and the horizon. If a seagull looks at us funny, I want a firing solution on it."

He swiped the screen, checking the subsurface sensors. Seven nuclear attack submarines prowled the deep waters behind his main line. Usually, the nuclear boats were under Valen's direct command. But with the young general reassigned to oversee the critical shore defenses, Theramore had absorbed the remaining boats into his tactical net.

He didn't like it. He preferred ships he could see and shoot with a railgun, not silent ghosts lurking in the dark. But he understood the necessity.

The initial rush of the coastal bombardment had faded. Now, they faced the grinding reality of holding a position. They had kicked the hornet's nest. The Hegemony was bleeding, but it wasn't dead.

Theramore gripped the railing. He stared out at the grey, unforgiving sea. They were waiting for the inevitable retaliation.

…

The Cinderfall infantry marched. It was a slow, grinding slog. After three days of relentless harassment, they had finally found a rhythm. A brutal, bloody rhythm, but a rhythm nonetheless.

They knew the tactics now.

Whenever the low, thunderous roar echoed from the cloud layer—the sound of the metal birds, the Wyverns and C-130s—the order would go out. The columns would break formation instantly. They scrambled toward the defensive runes etched hurriedly into the frozen dirt by the combat mages.

The mages would pour raw mana into the arrays, raising shimmering, golden domes of force. The air would ignite with plasma fire. The ground would shake as the aerial bombardment struck the shields. It was an inelegant solution to a terrifying problem. They were burning through their reserves of magical crystals like water pouring through a sieve.

And still, the shields weren't perfect.

A stray plasma bolt would punch through a weakened sector. A cluster of incendiary munitions would catch a squad that hadn't moved fast enough. There were always casualties. The smell of ozone and roasted meat was a constant companion on the march.

The strategy was working, but it was slowing their advance to a crawl. They would weather the storm, reform their battered ranks, and begin the winding march again, only to repeat the process hours later.

But the real terror, the problem the Cinderfall leadership couldn't solve, was the silent killers.

The Phantoms.

They dropped explosives from the stratosphere like ghosts. There was no warning roar, no glint of sunlight on metal. Just a sudden, localized apocalypse. It forced the commanders and supply lines to travel under constant, heavy magical shielding, draining even more resources.

By the time this army reached the enemy lines, the generals feared they wouldn't have enough magical crystals left to power the siege cannons or sustain a frontline shield wall.

The only sliver of hope was the Mother Phoenix. Legends said the progenitor was capable of reviving an entire army during battle, its golden fire burning away death itself.

First, the Silver Lion Cub must die, the captains muttered to their men, trying to keep morale from shattering completely. Then, they would turn their wrath on the traitorous bastards of House Black.

I stood in the CIC of The Aegis, watching the live feed from The Oracle.

We had a problem.

The Hegemony was dealing with my whittling attacks through sheer, brute force. I had hoped to dwindle their numbers significantly before they reached the kill-zone General Goliath was preparing in the valley. But I had underestimated the reserves of a kingdom that was centuries old. Their coffers were deep, filled with generations of hoarded magical stones. They were simply absorbing the losses and marching on.

I zoomed the holographic projection out, studying the terrain they were currently navigating. It was a rugged area full of steep hills and deep valleys.

I could deploy kinetic mines along their path. But the Cinderfall commanders weren't stupid. They would send smaller, expendable groups of penal infantry—the Inquisition forces—ahead to detonate them.

No. I needed remote-controlled mines. Explosives I could detonate at the exact, opportune moment to cause maximum devastation, bypassing their meat shields.

Even then, I was looking at several million soldiers. Mines wouldn't stop them; they would only delay the inevitable. I needed something that would buy me significant time. Time for the Colossus-class Crawlers to finish construction.

I switched the display from a topographical view to a deep-scan geological survey of the mountain range ahead of them.

I was looking for a waterway. A river I could quickly redirect to stall the enemy forces for an extended period.

My eyes scanned the glowing blue lines beneath the earth's surface.

I found one. A massive, underground river, flowing just a few kilometers ahead of the Cinderfall vanguard. It was buried deep beneath a layer of porous limestone.

A cold, calculating satisfaction settled over me.

"Tes," I said, my voice echoing in the quiet command center. "Calculate firing solutions for the C-52 bomber wings. Target coordinates: Grid Seven-Alpha."

[Calculating. Target acquired.]

"I don't want a surface bombardment," I instructed. "I want deep-penetrating bunker busters. We are going to crack the roof of that cavern."

A few runs of strategic bombing, and we could create an artificial river, a raging torrent of freezing, subterranean water blocking their march completely. It would turn the valley into a muddy, impassable swamp.

That would give me the time I needed to set up the remote minefields on the far banks.

"Reroute the bomber squadrons," I ordered, watching the red icons of the enemy army plodding relentlessly forward. "Let's give them something else to worry about besides the sky."

...

The air above Grid Seven-Alpha thrummed. Three C-52 bombers broke the cloud cover, their massive, swept-back wings slicing through the bruised twilight. They flew in a tight, disciplined line, aligning perfectly with the subterranean river mapped by The Oracle.

The bomb bay doors snapped open.

They dropped bunker busters—heavy, tungsten-tipped cylinders designed to penetrate solid rock before detonating. The munitions plummeted, striking the limestone crust of the valley floor.

The earth buckled.

Deep, muffled detonations thumped through the bedrock. The ground heaved upward, then collapsed inward, creating a chain of massive sinkholes that rapidly linked together into a jagged, mile-long trench.

A heartbeat later, the water breached.

A geyser of freezing, black, subterranean water erupted into the twilight air, shooting fifty feet high. The pressurized underground river, suddenly freed from its limestone prison, roared to the surface. It flooded the newly created trench, violently overflowing the banks and turning the valley floor into a churning, impassable torrent of mud and ice-cold rapids.

I watched the Cinderfall vanguard grind to a halt on the tactical feed. The sudden, churning rapids severed their advance completely. They were boxed in. Behind them stretched miles of scorched earth. Ahead lay a freezing, impassable swamp. They couldn't see the House Black Mage Towers from their current elevation in the valley, but their commanders knew the topography. Any detour they attempted around the new river would force their marching columns directly into the firing range of those spires. Every alternate route would be paid for in blood.
