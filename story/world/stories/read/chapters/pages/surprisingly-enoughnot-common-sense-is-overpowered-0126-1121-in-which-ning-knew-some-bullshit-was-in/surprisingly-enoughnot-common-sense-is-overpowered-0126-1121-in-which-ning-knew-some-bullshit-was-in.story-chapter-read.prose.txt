Yue Chan's table was among the cleanest on the field. Several completed preparations had already been placed into their jade containers, and there was very little wasted material around her furnace. Her methods weren't particularly unusual, but they didn't need to be. Every movement was precise, and once she chose how to handle a material, she carried the process through without unnecessary hesitation.

"My disciple isn't doing badly either," Cai Yun said, the satisfaction in her voice difficult to miss.

Ling Xin watched Yue Chan work for several moments before nodding. "She's good."

Cai Yun glanced over, expecting something else to follow.

"What?" Ling Xin asked.

"I was waiting for you to find a problem."

"Why would I? She's good." Ling Xin paused, then smiled. "Surprisingly different from you, Senior Sister."

There it was.

Cai Yun gave her a flat look before turning back toward the competition. "Your disciple is different from you as well."

"Thank you."

"It wasn't a compliment."

Ling Xin's smile only widened, while Ling Yinran, seated on her other side, continued watching the competition and wisely left the two sisters to themselves.

Cai Yun's gaze eventually returned to Ning. "I still find it strange that you ended up with him. You refused disciples for all those years, and then somehow settled on an outer sect spiritual farmer."

There was just enough emphasis on the last few words to make her opinion clear.

Ling Xin's lack of disciples had never been due to a lack of talent. If anything, the problem was the opposite. She was one of Fu Xuan's most gifted disciples, possessed a Pure Fire Spirit Body, and had enough accomplishments in alchemy that even the people who found her unbearable couldn't dismiss her ability.

Unfortunately, her reputation had grown alongside that talent.

Too many experiments had ended with damaged furnaces, strange pills, or unexpected explosions, and Ling Xin's habit of challenging anyone who irritated her to an alchemy duel hadn't exactly helped. Plenty of younger disciples admired her. Most preferred to do so without becoming personally involved with her.

Ning had been overlooked for the opposite reason: an ordinary outer disciple with no family and no money to burn through herbs.

On paper, neither of them had been the obvious choice.

Somehow, they'd ended up finding each other anyway.

Ling Xin looked entirely pleased with how that had turned out. "See? This is just fate."

"You got lucky."

"Senior Sister, luck is also part of ability."

"That's what people say when they get lucky."

Before Ling Xin could defend this profound cultivation principle, another voice came from behind them.

"Could the two of you at least wait until the first stage is over before turning it into another competition?"

Tang Feiyu approached with a cup of tea in hand and settled into the remaining seat. She had heard enough on the way over to understand exactly what was happening.

When Ling Xin claimed they had merely been having a peaceful conversation, Tang Feiyu didn't bother answering. She simply looked toward Ling Yinran.

Ling Yinran shook her head.

"I see."

Tang Feiyu sighed and let it go. Then, her attention moved to Station Thirty-One.

Su Fan was still working steadily despite the exhaustion visible on his face. One material rested beside his furnace under carefully maintained heat while he processed another, and when he returned to the first, he inspected it before making a small adjustment rather than immediately forcing it back toward whatever state he wanted.

Tang Feiyu watched him for several breaths, and a faint smile gradually appeared.

"He really has improved."

She knew better than anyone how much work had gone into that improvement. Su Fan had always taken alchemy seriously, but after returning from the Heavenly Origin Tower, that focus had become almost excessive. Tang Feiyu had spent nearly as much effort convincing him to rest as she had teaching him, and the dark circles beneath his eyes suggested that particular lesson still hadn't fully taken hold.

At least the rest of her teaching apparently had.

"He is much more patient. He used to interfere the moment something moved outside what he expected," she continued, mostly to herself.

Ling Xin followed her gaze and nodded. "That is indeed an improvement."

Tang Feiyu watched Su Fan finish another preparation before finally turning her attention toward Station Seventeen. By then, Ning had almost completed the Green Tendril Vine.

She watched his hands for several moments.

"That's almost the Hundred Herb Subtle Essence-Separating Hand."

Ling Xin burst out laughing.

Tang Feiyu stopped and looked at her.

"Sorry." Ling Xin waved one hand, though the grin on her face suggested she wasn't particularly sorry. "You just reminded me of when I first gave Ning a proper collection of alchemy techniques. He spent more time complaining about the names than learning them."

Tang Feiyu looked genuinely puzzled. "What's wrong with the name?"

That only made Ling Xin laugh harder.

Cai Yun's mouth twitched.

Ling Xin noticed immediately. "See? Senior Sister understands."

"I understand your disciple," Cai Yun corrected. "I'm questioning how someone raised by you developed that much common sense."

"I have common sense, okay?" Ling Xin felt she was being slandered.

Tang Feiyu, meanwhile, still seemed unconvinced. "Hundred Herb Subtle Essence-Separating Hand describes exactly what the technique does. It separates medicinal essence from different plant structures without damaging the portions you want to preserve."

"That's what I told him." Ling Xin quickly recovered. "Then he asked why squeezing medicinal juice out of a plant without crushing the wrong part needed such a grand name."

Tang Feiyu fell silent.

Below them, Ning drew another thin strand of medicinal fiber free without damaging the softer portion wrapped around it.

Tang Feiyu pointed toward him. "That's practically what he's doing."

Ling Xin looked down and became strangely solemn. "So Ning was right."

"He was not."

"He was," Cai Yun said. "The name is excessive."

Tang Feiyu took a slow drink of tea and decided to move on.

For a while, their attention returned to the competition.

The differences between the contestants had become increasingly clear.

One disciple had scorched the edge of his Silvercap Mushroom and was cutting away the damaged portion. Another had cracked an Ironheart Seed too deeply and was now trying to keep fragments of shell from contaminating the oil.

A woman farther down the field used the Flowing Jade Filament Art to peel the frost-veins from her Moondew Leaves one at a time, while another contestant had chosen the more forceful Red Furnace Nine-Turn Method to soften his Crimson Vein Root before pounding it into paste.

At Station Sixteen, Feng Yue was staring into her furnace with such absolute confidence that, from this distance, one might have assumed everything was proceeding exactly according to plan.

The slightly blackened Silvercap Mushroom beside her suggested otherwise.

Ling Xin glanced between Feng Yue and the mushroom. "Interesting technique."

Cai Yun's lips twitched. "Perhaps it's the Ancient Charred Mushroom Art."

Tang Feiyu nearly inhaled her tea, while even Ling Yinran failed to hide her smile.

The laughter faded quickly, however, as Tang Feiyu's attention moved across the field again. A number of contestants were already approaching completion.

Then she looked at the hourglass.

More than half the allotted time remained. Her expression changed slightly.

Cai Yun noticed. "What is it?"

Tang Feiyu didn't answer immediately. Her gaze moved from the contestants to Fu Xuan, who had been sitting quietly with the other judges throughout the stage.

She had studied under that woman for far too many years to believe this was all there was to it.

Tang Feiyu lowered her cup. "Master gave them an entire hour."

Cai Yun looked toward Fu Xuan as well.

Ling Xin's eyes immediately brightened. She knew that look on her master's face.

"Oh. This will be interesting."

Sure enough, Fu Xuan rose from her seat a few moments later, drawing the contestants' attention back toward the center of the competition grounds.

"Everything you have done until this point has already been recorded. Your handling, choices, mistakes, and completed preparations will make up the first portion of this stage."

...

First portion?

Ning's bullshit detector had been right.

Fu Xuan continued. "The materials in front of you were never intended to remain yours for the entire stage. Your jade talisman will now indicate the neighboring station you have been paired with. Each pair will exchange their materials when the formation activates."

The competition grounds immediately erupted into murmurs.

Fu Xuan waited for them to settle before continuing. "You will not receive fresh materials. Whatever state they arrive in is what you will work with. Your task is to assess what remains usable, adapt to what the previous contestant has done, and complete as many of the required preparations as possible."

Now Ning understood why the first half had seemed too straightforward.

Knowing how to prepare a medicinal material from beginning to end was one thing. In practice, however, an alchemist wouldn't always be working with ingredients they had personally grown, harvested, and processed. Farmers, apprentices, merchants, and other alchemists could all have handled them first.

If his solution whenever something differed from the textbook was to throw it away and start again, he was going to become poor very quickly.

His jade talisman lit up, and an arrow appeared pointing to the station on his left.

Ning turned his head.

Feng Yue noticed at almost the same moment and gave him a bright smile.

"Oh. We're exchanging."

Ning smiled at that.

Feng Yue had been extremely confident from the moment he'd met her. She even planned to one day refine the Pill of Immortality.

Surely someone with that much confidence had done a decent job.

The formation beneath their stations activated. Both preparation tables sank several inches before sliding sideways beneath the stone partitions. A moment later, Ning's work disappeared, and Feng Yue's table rose into its place.

Ning looked down, and his thoughts stopped.

The Crimson Vein Root paste had separated into three distinct layers, none of which looked particularly interested in becoming acquainted again. Two of the Ironheart Seeds had been crushed outright, while one side of the Silvercap Mushroom was black.

The Green Tendril Vine had become a sticky knot, and one Moondew Leaf was floating in a bowl of cloudy blue liquid that Ning was almost certain had not existed among the provided tools.

And the Violet Dusk Flower...

Ning stared at its mangled petals for several seconds. He had the unsettling feeling that the flower was staring right back at him, straight into the depths of his soul.

What is this? A biblically accurate flower?
