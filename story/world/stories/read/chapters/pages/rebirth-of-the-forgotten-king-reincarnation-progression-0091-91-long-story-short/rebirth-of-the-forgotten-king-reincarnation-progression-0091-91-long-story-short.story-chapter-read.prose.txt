"And that's what I've been going through for the last… few—" Adrian paused mid-sentence, his brows furrowing. A sluggish haze still clung to the edges of his mind, like he was waking from a dream he couldn't quite remember. "Wait. How long was I out?"

Zuri chuckled softly, resting her elbow on the table as she propped her chin against her palm. "Just over a day."

"A day?" Adrian blinked, momentarily stunned. Honestly? That’s… not as bad as I thought.

Alisha, however, did not share his indifference. Her fork clattered onto her plate with a sharp clink, and she straightened so fast it nearly knocked over her drink. "Over a day?! And no one thought to tell us? That’s insane! I’m going to have a word with the principal—"

Xenon tried his best to calm down. "Princess Alisha, please calm—"

"No! That’s completely unacceptable!" Her fist slammed onto the table, rattling plates and drawing the attention of a few students at nearby tables. "What kind of school subjects a student to something like that?!"

Adrian shifted uncomfortably, throwing a glance around the cafeteria. He could feel eyes on them now, curiosity buzzing in the air. Lowering his voice, he tried to placate her. "Alisha, it’s fine."

"It’s not fine," she shot back, arms crossing tightly over her chest. "They didn’t even tell us where you were! We thought something had happened! What if—"

She cut herself off abruptly, pressing her lips into a thin, hard line. Her hands clenched against her sleeves, and Adrian could see the way her fingers trembled slightly before she inhaled sharply through her nose, forcing herself to calm down.

A lump formed in Adrian’s throat. I… didn't think she'd feel so strongly about this. But it's probably still best if I try to diffuse the situation.

With a sigh, he lifted his tray slightly, as if to make a point. "Well, I’m here now. Eating. Alive." He took an exaggerated bite of food for good measure.

Alisha glared at him, unimpressed. "That doesn’t make it right."

Kael, who had been quietly observing the entire exchange with an amused tilt of his head, finally chimed in. "So, are you mad at her?"

Adrian frowned, glancing at him. "Who?"

"The principal," Kael clarified, lazily stabbing a piece of meat with his fork. "You know, for throwing you into whatever the hell that was."

Adrian hesitated, his gaze drifting toward the far end of the cafeteria, where the staff bustled about, refilling trays and keeping the endless flow of students moving. It would be incorrect to say I'm not angry, but, at the same time, I'm not furious about what happened either. But in the future, I'll definitely be more careful when it comes to dealing with the Principal.

His grip tightened slightly on his fork before he exhaled, shaking his head. "Yeah. I’m mad, but I also know she only put me up for it because she knew I could handle it."

Kael huffed a quiet laugh, smirking. "That’s one way to look at it."

"That’s a terrible way to look at it," Alisha muttered.

"You’re not wrong, though," Zuri mused, tapping a finger against her chin. "Myrael Faesbane isn’t the type to waste her time on people she considers beneath her. If she put you through that test, she must have seen something worth evaluating."

"Yeah, well, she also gave me this." Adrian reached into his pocket and pulled out the ring Myrael had given him. He placed it on the table with a slight clunk.

Xenon leaned in to get a better look. "That’s magic-infused."

"Obviously," Adrian muttered, resting his chin on his palm. "But she wouldn’t tell me what it actually does."

Kael huffed a short laugh. "Figures."

Zuri picked up the ring, rolling it between her fingers with a scrutinizing gaze. "Very interesting, it’s not cursed at least."

"Well, that’s comforting," Adrian deadpanned.

Alisha, having finally settled down from her earlier outburst, leaned closer. "You haven’t tried it on?"

Adrian hesitated, his fingers twitching slightly. "Not yet. Didn’t want to take any chances." If Myrael had been vague about its purpose, there was probably a reason for that.

"Smart," Xenon murmured.

"But don't you want to know what it does? What if the ring doesn't do anything at all?" Kael asked.

Zuri placed the ring back onto the table with a soft tap of metal against wood. "Well, there’s only one way to find out."

Adrian stared at it. His gut twisted, a quiet warning whispering at the back of his mind. But curiosity gnawed at him just as fiercely. With a sigh, he picked it up, running his thumb over the smooth band before slipping it onto his finger.

For a moment, nothing. Then, he felt warmth.

A pulse spread from the ring, a steady thrum beneath his skin, like a heartbeat not quite his own. It wasn’t painful, nor unpleasant. More accurately, it was just sort of there.

Adrian flexed his fingers, frowning. Doesn't really feel like much has changed.

"Well?" Kael asked, leaning forward.

"It feels... warm," Adrian muttered, still adjusting to the odd sensation. Aside from this warmth, I can't detect anything else that would make this ring special. It's an odd thing to be sure.

Zuri arched a brow. "Warm?"

"Yeah, that's sort of it" The sensation settled into something just shy of comforting, like slipping into a sun-warmed coat after a long winter night.

"Maybe it’s a focus enhancer," Xenon suggested thoughtfully.

"Or a trap," Alisha muttered, arms crossing.

"Or something completely useless," Kael added with a smirk.

Adrian sighed, rubbing his temples. "Funny thing is, I'm inclined to agree with you, to an extent."

Zuri chuckled, but her gaze remained fixed on the ring. "I’d keep an eye on it, if I were you."

"Yeah, no kidding," Adrian muttered under his breath.

The rest of the day blurred into a sluggish haze. Adrian, Zuri, Xenon, Kael, and Alisha all split off toward their respective classes, leaving him to trudge through lectures that felt more like slow torture than education. By the time the last class ended, even the simple act of pushing his chair back felt like an insurmountable effort.

He rose with a groan, stretching his arms overhead, his joints popping in protest. Alisha, already gathering her things, shot him a knowing glance.

"Still alive?" she teased, slinging her bag over one shoulder.

"Barely," Adrian muttered, pressing a hand against his temple. If I had to listen to one more sentence about Mistrallian History, I think I would’ve thrown myself out the window.

Alisha smirked. "Well, at least you stayed awake this time."

"That’s debatable," he said, adjusting the strap of his bag.

As they stepped into the corridor, the flow of students surged around them. Adrian let the noise wash over him, his brain too fried to process more than the occasional stray word. When they finally pushed through the doors and into the open air, he drew in a deep breath, savoring the crisp freshness that replaced the stale classroom air.

Alisha adjusted her bag’s strap, pausing at the crossroads where their paths split. "I’ll see you later," she said, flicking a quick wave before heading in the opposite direction.

Adrian lifted a hand in return. "Yeah, see you."

He lingered for a moment, watching as she melted into the crowd, her vibrant energy swallowed by the ever-moving tide of students. A sigh escaped his lips. Right. Now what? The thought of returning to his dorm drifted through his mind, tempting in theory, but the idea of being boxed in after hours of fighting sleep at his desk was unbearable.

Instead, his feet carried him along the quieter pathways of the academy, where the noise of the student body faded to a murmur. Before long, he reached the edge of the academy’s park: a small, open space nestled between the sprawling buildings.

His gaze swept over the park, pausing at a lone figure lounging on one of the wooden benches. Zuri reclined effortlessly, one leg crossed over the other. A small piece of bread rested between her fingers, and with absent grace, she tossed crumbs to the flock of pigeons huddled at her feet.

For a moment, Adrian simply watched. The way the sunlight skimmed the edges of her hair, how her posture exuded a lazy elegance, the rhythmic flutter of wings as the pigeons darted for their meal. Then, without so much as a glance in his direction, Zuri spoke, her voice drifting across the open space. "Are you just going to stand there, or are you going to say hello?"

Adrian shook his head, stepping forward with a smirk. "Didn’t want to interrupt your important diplomatic relations with the pigeons."

Zuri’s lips curved in amusement as she finally turned to face him, her sharp violet gaze locking onto his. "They’re much better conversationalists than some of the people I’ve met."

Adrian snorted. "That bad, huh?"

She tore off another piece of bread, tossing it into the frenzy of fluttering wings. "You have no idea."

Adrian watched as Zuri idly tore off another piece of bread, scattering the crumbs to the pigeons at her feet. The birds scrambled, wings flaring, beady eyes sharp with hunger, yet she barely acknowledged them. Her focus remained elsewhere—on something unseen, something unreadable.

With a quiet hum, she leaned back against the bench, draping one arm over its back in a posture of effortless ease. "I need a favor."

Adrian arched a brow. "Should I be concerned?"

Zuri’s lips curled slightly, the amusement in her expression subtle but unmistakable. "Not unless you’re afraid of a little sparring match."

Adrian blinked. Huh? Where did that come from? He crossed his arms, studying her. "That’s pretty sudden. Why the interest?"

She tilted her head slightly, considering her words. "Word’s gotten around," she said after a beat. Another piece of bread crumbled between her fingers before she flicked it away, watching as the pigeons dove for the scraps. "People are saying you’ll be put onto the Disciplinary Committee soon. Since I’ll be joining as well, I figured I should see if you’re actually up for the job."

"Ah, so the Principal also invited you to join the committee?"

"Precisely, she did it while you were… occupied."

Adrian exhaled through his nose, letting the silence stretch between them. There was something deliberate in the way she said it, too measured, too detached.

His eyes narrowed slightly. "That’s not the whole truth, is it?"

Zuri’s fingers stilled for the briefest moment before she let out a quiet chuckle, shaking her head. "Terrible liar, aren’t I?"

Adrian gave her a flat look. "Awful."

She sighed, tilting her head back to gaze at the sky as if weighing whether to speak. "Alright," she admitted, "what I said was a half-truth."

Adrian remained silent, waiting. Let's see what she has to say for herself.

Zuri flicked a stray lock of hair over her shoulder before leveling him with a gaze sharper than before. "I also want to see if you can protect Alisha."

"Xenon is her bodyguard. I don’t see why you need to test me for that."

"Xenon is her bodyguard, yes, but everyone knows Alisha likes you."

"Excuse me?"

"You two spend a lot of time together. People talk."

Adrian ran a hand through his hair, resisting the urge to groan. "That’s just because we have the same classes."

"Mm." Zuri’s hum carried the weight of someone who clearly didn’t believe him. "That’s what you tell yourself, but people have noticed you always walk out together."

Adrian stared at her. "That—" He stopped, running through the past several weeks in his head. Okay. Maybe that’s true.

Zuri leaned back, satisfaction glinting in her eyes as she observed his growing exasperation. "I don’t particularly care what the truth of your relationship is, but what I do care about is whether you can handle yourself. Alisha is someone I respect, and if she keeps gravitating toward you, that means you’ll be in situations where her safety becomes your responsibility. Whether you like it or not."

Adrian let out a slow breath, rubbing the bridge of his nose. Of course.

"You’re really not going to let this go, are you?"

"Nope."

Adrian sighed, shaking his head. "Fine, if you must insist on sparring against me, then I'm more than up for the task. We could do it now, unless you'd prefer another time."

Zuri’s smirk widened slightly as she rose to her feet. "That's fine, I’d hate to waste my time."
