“Eighty-five thousand!”

“Ninety-five thousand!”

“One hundred twenty thousand!”

The bidding for the second manuscript immediately shot upward. With Archmage Ballamus backing the lot, there was little doubt left about the theory. If even he considered the notes worthy of attention, then they weren’t merely valuable.

They were legitimate.

Varetus hadn’t spoken since the three mages left. He hadn’t even bid on the manuscript. Instead, he sat in his balcony, watching the auction with a stern expression.

Just looking at him, it was obvious he wasn’t pleased.

“One twenty-five!”

“One thirty!”

“Wow. They’re really going all in on this, aren’t they?” Gwen asked.

“Looks like you caught a lucky break,” Nasha added.

Veronica sighed with relief and leaned back against the couch. She turned toward Nasha. “Hand me your cake.”

Nasha reached over and passed her the plate of half-eaten cake. Apparently, it had been a little too sweet for her.

Veronica didn’t mind in the slightest.

The bidding continued for another minute until finally—

“Sold! For one hundred fifty-five thousand vix!”

The winning bid came from a woman in one of the VIP balconies. According to the auction servant, she was from the Jasmine Mage Tower.

With the manuscript sold, Veronica finally had plenty of money for the remaining Avalon materials and the demonic energy Sage needed.

Well, plenty might have been an over-exaggeration.

After subtracting what she had already spent and accounting for the auction house’s fees, she estimated they had a little over one hundred thousand vix left. Far better than where their funds had been a few minutes ago, but not enough to spend carelessly.

Still, it was better than being broke.

The next several lots passed quickly. Veronica ignored most of them, only raising her paddle when something useful appeared. She won a set of corrupted monster cores for fourteen thousand vix, a voidglass shard for fifty-one thousand, and, after another stretch of waiting, a dryad’s seed for forty thousand.

The monster cores contained enough demonic energy for Sage. The voidglass and dryad’s seed were the final materials she needed from the auction for Avalon. Everything else could be obtained through regular markets.

There was no longer much reason to stay. Their funds were nearly exhausted, and none of the remaining catalogue items interested her.

“We’re leaving?” Gwen asked, perking up.

She had been slouched on the couch for nearly half an hour, completely bored.

“Yeah. That dryad seed should suffice for a plant with spirit energy.” Veronica said.

Nasha stretched one arm lazily across the back of the couch. “Sitting here this long made me stiff. I can’t wait to get home and sleep.”

Before any of them could stand, the servant stationed behind them stepped forward. “My ladies,” she said with a small bow. “Forgive the interruption, but before you leave, I should mention that the final portion of tonight’s auction includes several special lots not listed in the public catalogue.”

Veronica paused.

Gwen looked over. “Special lots?”

“Yes. The final five items are mystery lots. Their details are withheld until presentation.”

Nasha raised an eyebrow. “Sounds like a good way to make people desperate and overpay.”

The servant wisely said nothing.

Gwen turned toward Veronica. “We’re still leaving, right? Because we don’t have enough vix for anything else.”

Veronica glanced down at the catalogue again.

The final listed lot was a concentrated vitality potion made from dragon heart. It could serve as ascension material for Gwen, but the starting bid alone was one million vix. And as Gwen had pointed out, they were essentially broke.

“Let’s stay,” Veronica said after a moment.

Gwen stared at her, shoulders slumping. “What? Why? I’m sooooo tired already. And we barely have five thousand vix left.”

“It doesn’t hurt to see what the items are. A few more minutes won’t kill you.”

Gwen sighed and sank lower into the couch.

“I’m taking a short nap,” Nasha said. She closed her eyes, leaned over, and rested her head directly in Veronica’s lap.

Veronica looked down at her, hands hovering awkwardly above Nasha’s head. After a moment, she slowly lowered one hand onto Nasha’s hair and left it there.

The auction continued, and as expected, the prices grew increasingly absurd.

Enchanted armor sold for three hundred thousand. A sealed beast core from a two-headed vexbeast passed half a million. A set of formation spell formulas recovered from an old ruin sold for over four million.

It had long since reached the point where most of the lower section couldn’t even place a meaningful bid. Only the richest families and organizations could afford the final lots.

Veronica leaned back in her seat, resting her head against the soft cushion of the couch. By then, she was simply a spectator, all the way until the vitality potion sold to the Caroline Commerce Group for twenty-eight million vix.

“Oh? Is it finally over?” Gwen asked, sitting up as the auctioneer walked offstage, replaced by the man from the earlier half of the auction.

Nasha stirred but otherwise didn’t care, nuzzling her head deeper into Veronica’s lap. Veronica couldn’t help blushing and stroking Nasha’s hair with how vulnerable she looked. Then Gwen gave her a disgusted side-eye, and she promptly composed herself.

“Honored guests,” the man said, his voice carrying smoothly through the hall. “We have reached the final portion of tonight’s auction. As many of you may already know, we have five lots that are not listed in the public catalogue.”

Several people perked up. Most of the VIPs were still in their balconies, though a few had left. The three commerce groups remained, along with Varetus and Harris. Lord Vaun had left nearly an hour ago. Most of the lower section was gone as well, leaving rows of empty seats behind.

“These five mystery lots have been personally reviewed and approved by the senior appraisers of the Pelotema Grand Auction. Seeing as the night has dragged on, we won’t waste your time and will begin immediately.”

The first mystery lot was a damaged blade nearly six hundred years old, recovered from an abandoned fortress near Vitian’s old eastern border. Though too fragile for combat, it still carried traces of martial intent from its former wielder. For aura users, it was a treasure they could learn from to accelerate their growth.

Veronica’s interest faded almost immediately. She wasn’t an aura user, and even Nasha didn’t use swords. It was useless to them.

Trailing Star bought it for fourteen million.

The second lot was a pale-blue monster core from a young sky leviathan, large enough to fill its glass case with slow gusts of wind. It went to one of Maftem’s great noble families for fifteen and a half million.

The third was an old formation pillar covered in worn spell formulas from the Path of Ascension. Varetus bought it for seven million.

The fourth lot came in a small glass box: a black flame burning atop nothing.

“A captured remnant of an abyssal flame,” the announcer said. “Suitable for curse research or crafting flame-resistant potions and artifacts.”

Willow Mage Tower purchased it for eight million.

“They’re all spending money I’ll probably never see in five lifetimes…” Gwen muttered, still slumped on the couch.

So far, nothing had appeared that Veronica needed. Some of the materials were impressive, but none were worth regretting. Perhaps she should have left an hour ago.

“Well, there’s still one more lot,” she said. “Might as well see what it is.”

With a snap of his fingers, the fourth lot disappeared, but nothing took its place. Instead, the announcer straightened, clasped his hands behind his back, and looked toward the ceiling.

“Now,” he said, his confident smile slowly returning, “for the fifth and final mystery lot of the evening.”

A low murmur moved through the room. Gwen sat up straighter, knowing this was the last lot of the night. Nasha finally opened both eyes, though she made no effort to lift her head from Veronica’s lap.

“This final lot is somewhat unusual. Not because it is strange or lacking in any way, but because it is simply too large to present within the hall.”

That drew even more murmurs.

“For this,” he continued, “we contracted several veil mages tonight to help reveal the final item of the auction.”

Several robed mages stepped out from the guarded doors behind the stage and spread across the edges of the platform. One by one, they raised their hands.

The room dimmed in an instant, and the ceiling vanished.

Gwen gasped, her eyes locked onto the view above.

For a moment, it truly looked as though the ceiling had disappeared. The night sky slowly shifted, growing brighter and brighter like an illusionary mirage. Soon, it became a pale morning sky, endless and blue, with soft clouds drifting lazily in the distance.

Then the sky blurred. A shape sharpened within it, layer by layer. A long hull. Sweeping side fins. Three large circular engines beneath the base. Reinforced glass windows along the deck. As the illusion continued to form, the top became visible as well: a wide, polished platform large enough to hold a dozen people comfortably.

The image solidified until it hung over the hall, vast and elegant, nearly the size of a small mansion.

Veronica’s gaze locked onto the giant ship, her body completely still.

The announcer spread his arms. “A pair of personal airships, each sold separately,” he said. “Vessels commissioned for private travel and long-distance expeditions. Each ship contains five private cabins, a reinforced storage hold, two small workshop chambers, and a mana-shield generator capable of blocking C-rank monster attacks with ease. With three engines, each has a cruising speed of around thirty kilometers per hour. And with no roads or terrain to slow them down, they can cover far more distance than any magitech carriage.”

Gwen’s mouth slowly opened.

Nasha finally lifted her head.

Veronica stared at the illusion above the stage, her earlier exhaustion disappearing in an instant.

A mobile base.

Completely furnished with proper utilities. Workshops, bedrooms, storage—everything she could need. Reaching the sixth tier, when she would finally be capable of flight, was still a long way off. But even then, flying herself would be far less convenient than having a mobile base that could travel without tiring.

The announcer’s smile widened, as though he could feel every ambitious thought spreading through the room.

“The two vessels will be sold separately under identical terms. Of course, each airship has already been approved for travel throughout Vitian and bears the seal of the Royal Family.”

Another murmur passed through the hall. Having the Royal Family’s seal was significant. It meant the ships were permitted to travel almost anywhere within the kingdom’s borders.

“Excuse me, but does that mean the Royal Family are the consignors?” a voice called.

Veronica turned and spotted Corwin from Trailing Star. He would eventually become the leader of the commerce group within a few years. She remembered speaking with him several times in the future.

The announcer nodded, pleased by the question. “Excellent question, Mr. Corwin. These airships were sponsored by the Royal Family as gifts from the Arkavail Empire. His Highness wishes to distribute them throughout Vitian to determine how well they fit within our kingdom. We were fortunate enough to be chosen as one of the first auctions to offer them.”

“Does that mean these airships will become obsolete in the future?” another voice called. This time, it came from Selka of the Caroline Commerce Group.

The announcer chuckled. “Of course not. Owners will receive yearly maintenance and approved upgrades to the latest magitech models. The only requirement is that they provide honest feedback to the engineer assigned to their ship.”

The murmurs grew louder. People spoke freely among themselves now, turning toward their aides or pulling out communication crystals.

“Damn, that sounds so awesome,” Gwen said, still staring upward in awe.

“Must be nice to fly anywhere you want,” Nasha added, looking at the ship with an equal amount of curiosity.

Once the questions settled, the announcer continued. “For the first airship, the starting bid is ten million vix! Minimum bid increment: one million!”

“Sixteen million!” someone shouted, raising their paddle immediately.

“Eighteen million!” another called, driving the price even higher.

The bidding had barely begun, and people were already shouting absurd numbers. For non-mages, flight was a luxury—a dream for those who wanted to experience the sky. Some aura users could learn to fly, but such people were few and far between. Others possessed flying mounts like wyverns or gryphons, but they weren’t always comfortable for long-distance travel, and their carrying capacity was limited.

“Thirty-six million!” shouted a voice from one of the further balconies.

Veronica hadn’t paid much attention to the bidder before. He was a younger man, perhaps in his mid-twenties. According to the servant, he wasn’t affiliated with any of Maftem’s major mage towers or commerce groups. A traveling noble, perhaps.

After several long minutes of anticipation-filled waiting, the bids eventually stopped.

“Going once! Twice! And… sold!” the announcer declared.

Most of the other VIPs had stopped bidding around the twenty-million mark. It wasn’t surprising. An airship was more of a personal luxury than an asset that would directly strengthen their organizations.

But now that the first had been sold, everyone turned their attention to the second.

“It’s time for the second of the pair! The final airship and the last item of tonight’s auction! Same terms as before!”

The bids came quickly.

“Twelve million!”

“Fifteen!”

“Sixteen!”

“Nineteen!”

“Twenty-two million!” Varetus called from the Ivy Mage Tower’s balcony.

“Are mage towers really that rich?” Nasha asked, looking toward him.

Varetus sat with a hard, stoic expression. He hadn’t bid on the first airship, but apparently, he wanted this one.

“Damn, no one else is bidding,” Gwen murmured. “Looks like they’re going to get it.”

Veronica had been silent since the airships were first revealed. Then she waved one hand in front of herself.

Gwen and Nasha both looked over.

“Huh? Veronica? What are you—” Gwen’s question was cut off as Veronica’s amplified voice echoed throughout the auction hall.

“Lucian. Are you still here?”

The remaining guests, mostly nobles and people of considerable wealth and influence, all turned their attention toward their balcony. Even the announcer stared at her in surprise.

For several moments, no one answered.

Varetus looked toward Veronica with a displeased expression. Harris, standing beside him, appeared even less pleased. He leaned down and whispered something into the old man’s ear.

A moment later, Varetus waved a hand, amplifying his own voice. With the second airship nearly his, he clearly had no intention of allowing an interruption.

“Who are you? What is the meaning of—”

A gust of wind swept through the auction hall.

Everyone recognized it immediately.

Three cloaked mages appeared before Veronica’s balcony, floating in the air—the same three who had intervened earlier.

Archmage Ballamus’s representatives.

The central mage faced Veronica, the eight stars on his badge gleaming beneath the artificial sunlight overhead.

Although Veronica couldn’t see his face, she knew it was him. Sage had long since matched his voice to the one Veronica remembered from the future.

Lucian Dane.

A seventh-tier Path of Ascension mage and one of the representatives of the Arcanum Spire.

“Hello, Lucian,” Veronica said with a pleasant smile.
