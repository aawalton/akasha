The town was in full swing by the time Jane left the library. She felt a pang of jealousy at seeing all the people who were able to go about their normal work without odd magical interruptions. As of the next day, she would be back in their ranks: a productive member of a nice town doing normal, productive, ultimately nice things.

For now, though, she had errands to run.

Her first stop was the cobbler's shop. Jane had been there before, filling out her footwear after arriving in Glenfall in a pair of fashionable city boots that had barely survived one long bout of travel. The walking shoes she had bought on that occasion fit like a dream of comfort, and the boots were sturdy enough to handle rough terrain without complaint. Both were lasting beautifully. They hardly showed any wear beyond what she could fix herself with a quick oil and polish every few weeks.

She wasn’t looking to replace them. What she didn't have was something in between the two configurations, and she aimed to fix the deficit.

The trip to Trailor had made that need clear. Her heavy boots had been overkill for that purpose, designed as they were for climbing over boulders and wading through streams. The sheer weight of them had nearly worn out her legs. What she needed was something lighter than the boots but more substantial than her everyday shoes, something meant for endurance travel over maintained roads.

Jane entered the shop and exchanged greetings with the cobbler. It didn’t take her long to explain what she was looking for and why. The shop’s owner listened without interrupting, nodding occasionally as if she had heard it all before. Jane reflected that she probably had. The cobbler had been at her business for decades.

When Jane finished, the woman asked her to sit on a low stool near the workbench. She then pulled out a measuring kit, knelt down, and began taking precise measurements of Jane's feet.

Jane had been through this process on her first visit. As before, there were more required measurements than she expected, but she didn’t question it. The results spoke for themselves. Whatever information the cobbler was gathering was useful and necessary in some way.

When the measurements were finally complete, the cobbler quoted a price and a timeline of two weeks. Jane agreed to both without haggling. Good work deserved fair payment, and this work was worth more than double the price.

She tried to ignore the fact that she was working with what amounted to an unlimited budget. An archmage could draw as much money from the royal treasury as they needed for living expenses and the accomplishment of their tasks. As grateful as she was for these resources, and how they had eased her work in Trailor, Jane preferred to think of herself as simply a baker.

Her next stops were quicker affairs. Today, she had decided, was a great day of restocking. Now that Bella and the other girls she’d befriended had brought her up-to-date on cosmetics, Jane found there were several little consumable items she just didn’t want to do without, not to mention the normal, everyday types of things that every person needed. She bought liberally, putting everything into a cloth sack and hefting it along with her as she went.

She didn't mind. These were the ordinary expenses of ordinary life. Just buying them made her feel ordinary again, more in-step with the people around her.

The food market was her next destination. It was also the most important one of the day. Her shop had been closed for nearly a week, and her supplies had been running low even before she left. She had some large orders to place if she wanted to reopen tomorrow.

The baking supplies were routine enough, at least. She knew exactly what she needed and exactly where to get it, having made the same purchases dozens of times over the past several months. She visited familiar stands to order butter, flour, salt, and sugar, knowing that anything she managed to buy too much of would fit nicely under her front counter until she needed it.

The vendors knew her by now. Several of them greeted her by name, asking about her trip and expressing pleasure at her safe return. Jane answered their questions as quickly as she could while still being polite. She had a lot of work left to do, and not a lot of time for conversation.

Once the baking supplies were handled, Jane turned her attention to a part of the market she usually visited only as an afterthought, or on a separate trip for her own groceries. She needed to buy meat. A lot of it, by her standards.

Hattie's meat buns had planted an idea in her head, one Jane couldn't seem to shake loose without at least giving it a try. She had been making small improvements to her offerings for a while, trying to introduce some variety in her day-to-day stock. By now, though, enough time had passed that even the ‘new’ products had all started to feel more or less the same. She was bored. This Hattie-inspired idea seemed just different enough to snap her out of that boredom.

Jane wandered down one of the narrow lanes of the meat market and perused the stalls on both sides. Each one displayed a variety of cuts from different animal sources, some of them prepared in ways Jane couldn’t imagine duplicating herself. There were steaks, strips, whole birds, and sausages everywhere she looked.

She bought a variety, compensating in mass for her lack of expertise. Jane felt like she was absolute garbage at the non-baking forms of cooking, but she decided not to worry about that problem yet. Not when she was almost sure she had a solution to it.

Her final purchase was a selection of game birds, small ones that reminded her of what Hattie had used in one of her own variations on the dish. These were wrapped separately and added to her increasingly heavy cloth bag.

The produce market was her next stop, and every bit as daunting. Jane steeled herself and forged through, buying various leafy greens and crunchy vegetables meant for dicing and throwing in stews. She skipped potatoes, for now. They would be too heavy for what she had in mind.

By the time she finished, her cloth bag was so full that she could barely lift it. She had purchased far more than she could carry home comfortably. When the produce vendor offered delivery for a reasonable price, Jane gratefully accepted, arranging for the load to be brought to her door that evening.

And evening was coming on quick. As she hurried to Gurther’s shop to buy a variety of herbs and spices, Jane could already tell she was going to get no more than a few hours of sleep that night.

But that was fine. More than fine, in fact. She was excited.

This was a project, and Jane had always liked having projects.

"So why have you dragged me here again?" Bella looked around the kitchen, taking in the piles of meat and vegetables covering every available surface. "It looks like a butcher's shop, Jane."

"Hattie taught me more about bread than I expected.” Jane unwrapped one of the packages of chicken and stared at it. “But when we were making the meat buns, she handled most of the fillings on her own.”

“She gave us a lot of tips,” Bella said.

“I know. But we talked about so many things that night.” Sighing, Jane poked the chicken. “I have no idea how to cook most of this."

"And you want me to show you?"

"You're a genius at this kind of thing. You know you are. If you can teach me enough to get by, I’ll be able to do the rest of the experimenting on my own. Please, Bella,” Jane pleaded, hanging on her friend’s arm. “I need you for this.”

Bella laughed.

"Fine. Butter me up like that, and I can't say no. Let's see what you've got."

They started with the birds. Bella took over Jane's cutting board and demonstrated how to separate meat from bone while explaining what to expect from each cut.

"For what you're doing, you want to cut everything thin or dice it into small chunks." She worked as she talked, reducing a chicken breast to uniform pieces in less than a minute. "Then you’ll brown them in a hot pan, like Hattie did. Get some color on the outside, but don't cook them through. The baking does the rest."

"Like this?" Jane tried to copy Bella's cutting technique on a second piece of chicken. Her cuts were less even, but not embarrassingly so.

"Close enough. You'll get better with practice." Bella moved on to the rabbit. "This one's leaner. You'll want to add some fat when you cook it, or it'll dry out."

They worked through the different meats one by one. The game birds were the trickiest, as they were small and fiddly things to work on. Jane wasted one or two of them with awkward, irregular cuts that rendered the meat all but unusable. But Bella was a good teacher, and patient without being condescending. She was the kind of person Jane could learn from without getting self-conscious, and that sped things up quite a bit.

By the time they finished with the last bird, Jane felt like she at least understood the basics. From there, the various ground meats and steak cuts were much easier to figure out.

"Now vegetables." Bella wiped down her knife and reached for an onion. "Don’t worry. These are easier."

Even after weeks of traveling together, the time spent with Bella in the kitchen felt like something Jane needed. She had wanted to be home, and home wasn’t home without Bella peeking in from time to time. Now everything felt right in a way that had still been missing over her first day back in town.

"How's Dave settling in?" Jane asked, feeling much more in her own wheelhouse as she diced carrots. “Is he doing well?”

Bella's knife moved through a head of lettuce, reducing it to strips with incredible speed and precision. "He's completely out of his element. But he's trying hard. I've introduced him to a few people who might have need of his services. He has a lot of work ahead of him before he finds his place, but I think his father sent him with a fat enough purse to buy that time.”

"That's good. He's young enough and hard-working enough that I can't imagine him not doing well, given time."

"That's what I figured. He just needs opportunities. This town has plenty of those. I’m sure he’ll get there."

They moved on to the herbs and spices, which Jane already knew something of. This part was more of a discussion. Jane wanted to invent her own signature blend, rather than just copying Hattie’s. She and Bella looked at various meat and vegetable combinations while talking about what flavors might complement them best.

"The trick with fillings like this," Bella said, "is balance. You want enough seasoning to make it interesting, but much more than that is going to screw it up. Just use a light hand, and you’ll be fine. I think you have enough to go on with, anyway. I’d best leave you to it.”

"You're leaving?"

"I think so. It’s been a whole day, and if I don’t go see Brit eventually, he’s liable to get mad at me."

Jane raised an eyebrow. "He gets mad at you? I didn't think he had it in him."

"He doesn't." Bella grinned as she gathered her things. "But it's still not fair to bully him. Besides, I've missed him. He's a good guy, when it comes down to it."

"He is." Jane walked her to the door. "Thank you for this. I know it wasn't how you planned to spend your evening."

"It was fine. I like teaching people things." Bella paused in the doorway. "Don't stay up too late. You've got a shop to open tomorrow."

"I know. I won't."

Bella squinted at her. “You’re going to be up all night experimenting, aren’t you?”

“Not all night.” Jane waved her friend away with a laugh. “Now go see Brit. I’ll get some rest tonight. I promise.”
