Three Weeks Later

User: Jeffery CarlsonE-RankStamina: 200/200, Mana: 50/50

Jeff didn’t bother looking at the rest of his stats. He’d spent three weeks in the library, building the perfect tank build with Kade’s help. A mix of Bernard the Wall, Luke Walker—the tank he’d most dreaded fighting in the Fallen Delvers Tournament, Deborah Callahan, and his own fighting style. For three weeks, all he’d been able to do was research the build, fine-tune the merges he’d need, and wait for his core to finish resetting.

But now, he was finally back.

The Governing Council rep at the bright green portal hanging in an abandoned parking lot in Surprise went through his build. “You and your team are clear. Looks like a guild run, so we don’t need to dig too deep. I’m curious, though. Partial carry? That’s a beefy healer for an E-Rank portal, and that support?” He whistled.

“Half-right. That support’s my girlfriend,” Jeff said. “But the three of us are holding a try-out for the guild. I know what I’m doing.”

A skeptical eyebrow went up, but the rep nodded. “Alright, you say so. Head on in.”

Jeff shook his head. It was so weird to be E-Rank again. Weird, but also strangely liberating. He wasn’t bound by his bad decisions anymore, but he was restricted by the oppression of rank. Outside of the Desert Wind Guild, most people didn’t know who he was or the experience he had, so they underestimated him. Fortunately, this was a guild clear.

“What do we have today?” he asked Yazzie as she waved from just outside of the portal.

“Portal opened about two hours ago and was picked up twenty minutes later. It looks like a standard E-Rank, but no one’s been inside to confirm. I thought about it—I could probably handle the first room of a trap portal by myself—but that guy didn’t seem too happy when I suggested it.” She pointed a thumb over her shoulder at the suit-clad GC rep. “The team’s you, me, Sophia on healer, and three absolute beginners. They’re almost as weak as you, Jeff.”

“Shut up,” he said jokingly.

“Sure, sure, low blow, my bad. Anyway, we’ve got a mage, a fighter, and an archer. Looks like a pretty well-balanced team composition.”

“Other than us,” Sophia said softly. She grinned, but behind it, Jeff could still see the strain. “Make sure everyone knows.”

“I will,” he said softly, then cleared his throat. “Desert Wind delvers and would-be delvers, today’s job’s a simple one. We’re in and out in two hours. Maybe three. E-Rank monsters, a D-Rank-equivalent boss, nothing out of the ordinary. But in case something does go wrong, Sophia and Yasmin here are serious, powerful delvers, and I’ve got a lot more experience than it seems.”

“The biggest danger’s going to be a trap portal. If we get one of those, we move slow and don’t worry about a portal break. The rest of the Desert Wind knows about this portal. They’ll have the area secure long before there’s any danger of breaking.”

“Will the Stormblade be there?” the fighter asked. She’d asked about him during her initial application, too, if Jeff remembered correctly.

Jeff shook his head. “No. Kade’s got obligations. But the B and C team hasn’t been called into any portals. They’ll cover us. So, nice and easy. Keep the monsters off Sophia, and she’ll keep all of us in one piece. Yasmin, you’re up.”

“Formation. I want Jeff up front. Cindy, Alejandro, you two are center with Sophia, since you can’t take a hit. Gianna, off to Jeff’s left, covering his shield side. And I’ll make sure nothing stabs us in the back.”

“When can I meet the Stormblade?”

Jeff stared the fighter down. “Kade operates on his own schedule. If you handle yourselves well today, we’ll bring you to the guildhouse to get registered and armbanded. After that? Who knows? Any other questions?”

The fighter’s hand went up.

“Any questions that don’t have to do with Kade?”

She lowered her hand, looking sheepish. “No. I’m just a big fan.”

“I bet. Ignore Kade for the next few hours, and let’s get this job done.” Jeff hooked his new, E-Rank shield—the best his considerable fortune could buy—around his arm, drew his sword, and stepped through the portal.

He arrived in a cavern filled with stalactites, stalagmites, and the dripping sound of water. In the distance, screaming echoed up and down the Warren world’s halls, and he readied himself. It was time to start catching up with his friends so that one day he could stand beside Kade again. He snorted: in that regard, he wasn’t so different from the overly eager fighter.

Jessie Gerald kicked her feet up on an ancient-looking wooden desk, shoes still on. The motion hurt, but once it was done, it stretched out her quads beautifully, and the pose didn’t hurt her joints as much as just sitting normally. It also made her look cool.

“I could get used to this,” she said.

In the three weeks since the God of Thunder had—briefly—arrived in Phoenix, she’d come to terms with the reality that the Desert Wind Guild’s building wasn’t getting rebuilt anytime soon. Maybe ever. The fight against the Placid King’s assassins had been devastating, but the real damage had come from a thousand Overholz Cannons’ worth of shrapnel raining down on the wreckage. There was simply nothing left to rebuild.

Between that and the damage the city had taken—dozens of buildings had been completely leveled, and thousands had taken at least serious damage—fixing her guild hall was simply not a priority.

That was fine, though. She had this swanky new place to monitor her guild’s teams from, and to house them.

“Hey, Ellen, did you know this place has—“

“An entire wing of guest bedrooms,” Ellen said from the couch they’d had Kade carry up here. “I lived here for most of my lift, Jessie. I know all about the guest wing—and that he never even furnished them until the Traynor Guild moved in.”

“Right. Uh, anyway, the E-Team just pushed into that portal in old Sun City. I’ve got a few delvers from the C-Team keeping an eye on things there, and with Sophia and Yasmin on board, they should be fine. Everything else is quiet, so…” she trailed off, looking at Ellen for the first time. “You going somewhere?”

“Oh, yeah, Kade and I have a super-hot date tonight.”

Jessie rolled her eyes. “I don’t need to hear that. Also, you’re dressed for a fight, not for a date.”

Ellen didn’t say anything for a few seconds. That was long enough. “What’s Kade hiding from me, Ellen?”

“He was going to tell you before we left, but fine. Look, you remember early in the three-day countdown, when Kade and I went to the Soaring Sabers’ world? He left some unfinished business there regarding Tanat.”

“Oh. The alien woman.”

“She’s not an alien. Just from a world where humans grew a little differently,” Ellen said. “Anyway, we sort of kidnapped her, then abandoned the rest of the world after exposing the SS-Ranker in charge’s secret about ascension. He has no idea how things have gone down there, and—“

“And that’s a problem for him, because he’s convinced that Retan’s a bully who needs to be put in his place,” Jessie finished. “Blah blah blah, back to the playground, nothing ever changes.”

Ellen grinned sheepishly and shrugged. “How are you holding up?” she asked as she walked over to stare at the monitors. Jessie touched a few keys, and the chat she’d been following with User295 and Yreanne vanished just before Ellen could see it. Those two hadn’t wanted to join up with the Desert Winds—not that Jessie hadn’t offered very lucrative terms—but they’d agreed to keep working with her as a favor. Still, they wanted their privacy, and she needed to respect that.

How was Jessie holding up? “Well, the new building’s fantastic.”

“It’s temporary. The GC’s gonna take it over when they win the dissolution case against Bob. They’ll let us stay until the Desert Wind has a new home, but…”

“Think we could buy it? I love the pool.” That was true. Jessie had started getting a pretty good tan going just by virtue of floating for hours without having to control her body, letting the current move her around the pool in the garden. “I think I could get used to this place.”

Ellen laughed. “I could to, but…”

“But what?”

“Well, we’d need to do some serious remodeling. Anyway, Kade’ll be here any minute. We’ll be back in two hours.”

“That fast?”

“Oh, most of that’s to say goodbye to Tanat. Kade won’t need ten minutes to deal with Retan, and I’m just along to play tourist,” Ellen said, still smiling. “Bye!”

Jessie rolled her eyes and waved as Ellen left, and it was only ten minutes later that she realized she hadn’t really answered Kade’s partner’s question.

“I am doing just fine,” she said to the empty air in the office that had belonged to Bob Traynor. Then she wheeled her chair off toward the room where her swimsuit dried on a rack to go for another float. Whatever Kade and Ellen were up to, it wasn’t her problem. They could handle themselves. She had a whole guild to worry about, and for the first time in…a while…everything was running perfectly smoothly. “Yep. Just fine.”

Tanat expected to arrive back in her old home high above the sect and have to take the long, winding pathway down. Every time she’d left by any means save her Lonely Gate, she’d always had to climb or descend, a journey that, even with her A-Rank body and speed, had taken a long, long time.

Instead, she’d arrived in the center of the dueling grounds, stepping out right in between the rebuilt treasure house and the sect’s wooden, cliff-shaded building. The stone platform and sand-filled dueling circles hadn’t changed much, but the people in them? That was a very different story.

Things had obviously changed a lot in the last few weeks—and not for the better.

For one thing, there were no S-Rankers. Everywhere she looked, all she could find were B-Rank disciples and below. A handful of A-Ranks stood in a knot near the dueling grounds’ center, watching the others and occasionally pointing at one. No one else made a noise, save grunts of effort, choked-back cries of pain, and the sound of steel on steel or feet on sand.

And Retan…Retan stalked the silent trainees, occasionally ‘correcting’ one with a quick blow and a shouted command. In the weeks since Kade and Ellen had arrived, the Soaring Saber Sect had shifted from a raucous, chaotic mess of would-be swordsmen to one silent, afraid, and obviously under the control of a single man.

Retan took three steps before he turned to face the portal, light sword erupting in his hand. “Tanat. You dare show your face here after—“

He didn’t finish his sentence.

The Stormblade erupted from the portal, lightning surging all around him as he moved faster than Tanat could even imagine. A wave of static rippled across the dueling ground, and electricity arced out from it, touching each of the combatants in turn before they could so much as react. The low-rankers hit the ground almost as one; the A-Rankers managed to resist for a handful of seconds before the chains of lightning connecting them to Kade redoubled in strength. They joined the low-rankers littering the battlefield.

Then Kade’s sword met Retan’s light blade.

Tanat was a brilliant swordswoman. She didn’t say that out of a misplaced sense of self-worth, or of ego. After all, she’d resisted the God of Thunder himself longer than anyone—including Tanat herself—would have hoped. But as Kade flickered and danced around Retan, lightning and rainwater slicing through the master of the Soaring Saber’s armor and flesh with each blow, she could hardly follow. A beam of pure lightning hammered into Retan from behind him even as Kade repositioned and attacked from the front. Chains of electricity pulled the master of auras back and forth, into and out of pools of Darkness that had to be Ellen’s doing.

The fight lasted less than twenty seconds before Kade landed a lethal blow, then another, and then a third. Retan hit the sand, blood pouring from his neck and staining it red. And Kade unsummoned his sword.

It had been one of the most one-sided fights between ‘equals’ that Tanat had ever witnessed. It had been utterly horrifying to watch. And, not for the first time, Tanat regretted that the Ellen woman had become Kade Noelstra’s Dual Skill Progression partner.

Oh, the things she’d have done with that man!

Then Kade cleared his throat as the disciples he’d taken out in the opening quarter-second of the fight slowly recovered. “You all should know what Retan, the Paragon of this world and leader of the Soaring Saber Sect, was using you for.”

As he explained exactly what the treasure room had been and the danger their former master’s plot had put them in, eyes started to shift and narrow. Tanat couldn’t blame them. To their eyes, this looked a lot like exchanging one SS-Rank master they knew for a new one they didn’t.

Then Kade pointed at Tanat. “This one aided me in my fight against a power much higher than Retan. For that, I owe her my life. In partial payment of that debt, I’ve brought her home. This is her world now. There’s only one rule. Neither you, nor anyone else here, can attempt to push past S-Rank. There will be no ascension.”

Kade’s sword flicked out. It cut through Retan’s core, and Mana erupted from the cut, spewing just like blood had poured from his throat. It dissipated into the air before anyone could react. “Tanat, you’ll do the same with the Mana stone in the treasure room.”

“Of course, Kade. And thank you.”

Kade nodded, a smile on his face, as he and Ellen walked back through the portal at the center of the dueling grounds. It closed, and Tanat stared at the A-Rankers. “Well? Are you going to stand there, or are we following the Path of the Soaring Saber?”
