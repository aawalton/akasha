Noah turned to the fountain. It was rounded at the base, an overlarge scarab shell forming a platform for a skeletal serpent's bones, over which water ran in an uneven ripple that almost looked like silver shimmering scales.

Oh. said the Devourer. I see.

Noah stepped forward and placed one hand on the skull of the serpent, staring into its empty eye holes.

Memory rippled into him in pieces, fragments that flashed by almost faster than he could perceive them. Four people—gods? shadows? souls?—arguing. Ritual circles, simple at first and then more and more complex. A mental map of himself, carefully split along specific lines, each piece with its purpose. The shock of the division when he actually followed through—

Noah snapped back into himself more violently than any of the previous visions. Where those had been neatly packaged memories, this was more like a random grab-bag of barely-coherent reject pieces.

But though none of the specifics of the memory cascade were strong enough to settle into clarity, the whole vision did impart one absolute fact.

"This is yours." The Reaper placed her hand on the snake's back, looking through Noah's eyes again at the Aspect within. "Your memories and power were scattered, your soul banished, and your Aspect dispelled, but your body, you entrusted to me before you left."

"When the Devourer died," Noah murmured. "At his own hand. But why? If he was so powerful, why not fight this war himself? Why leave it to someone like me?"

"I do not know. Those memories were sealed before his death, so they were never part of my domain."

That's not something I can remember either, the Devourer said with a mental shrug. Guess we'll find out once we grab the last three Shards. But first... if you don't mind.

Noah accepted the nudge against his control, and the Devourer did what he did best. The entire skeleton, the scarab shell, and the fountain underneath for good measure, all disappeared into Noah's endless hunger.

Unlike the sparking fuzz of the dying souls outside, the Devourer's skeleton tasted solid and cold, like an ice cream cake for his soul, delicious and comforting. And with the skeleton came the imprint of its movements, its shape, its myriad hungry mouths, all burned directly into Noah's soul.

He flexed his shoulders, feeling exactly how easily he could do away with those silly human limbs and become hunger incarnate, wreak havoc upon his enemies—

"Be careful, little god," the Reaper said, before Noah's imagination could get too far away from him. "Until you have Ascended, you are still a mortal like any other. You can still be bound, broken, scattered again, and I doubt your enemies will be so careless as to overlook your fragments another time. Immortality is not always a gift."

Noah nodded, but he couldn't stop grinning. He had a dragon for a body and a scarab-serpent swarm for a soul! Sure, that made him target number one for every cultist on Erandir, but it was just too cool. He couldn't bring himself to worry.

The Devourer was as eager as him, and he found himself already facing back toward the persistent tug of his Shadow Heart, showing him the path back to the Reaper's Gate and reality.

"You're welcome," the Reaper said with a laugh. "I release you from my custody. Go, live well, and may our final meeting come only when all is accomplished."

Noah bowed in thanks, then turned and stepped into the shadow ring guarding the Reaper's courtyard. By the time he reached the other side, he was running, Shadow a glowing beacon pulling him onward.

Noah burst out into the Underworld proper and was hit in the face with the density of the Watcher's oppressive atmosphere. He was too excited to let it slow him down, though, and recovered from his stumble and kept running.

"ENOUGH."

The air shook and silver light surrounded Noah, melting and dripping down in a bizarre cage. Vor Ashen strode forward, his expression flat and his movement unnatural. He no longer carried his weapon but Noah found that no comfort whatsoever.

"You are coming with me."

Gravity lurched, and while Shadow's faint tug back toward reality remained, the whole rest of the Underworld tilted to orient toward the silver glow on the horizon. Rather than standing on a flat plane, Noah was perched precariously on the side of a mountain, flailing for balance. Dylan reached out and grabbed the ground, anchoring him just enough for him to avoid falling backward into the abyssal slope.

Moonlight sizzled in pools around him, the dripping cage of light around them constantly shifting.

The Watcher's power assaulted his mind from all sides in unsteady surges.

Noah didn't know how to move, how to escape, but he knew he couldn't stand here. He took one staggering step forward up the slope, then another. The power around him pulsed and screamed. Broken mirrors flashed in his mind, and the adversary stalking toward him raised a hand glowing with white power.

We must escape.

With the Reaper’s information, his survival had gone from a personally important thing to something essential for the survival of the entire future he wanted to create. As the sole weapon capable of destroying the dark Primordial gods, Noah's freedom was of absolute paramount importance.

This isn't going to work. We need to do something different.

"Be ready," Noah whispered, and felt inward assent.

Vor reached him and Noah exploded into a whole new form. For a moment he hovered in place, then he scattered in all directions.

It would have been disorienting for anyone else, but Noah was used to steering a swarm. It felt more familiar than his single human body at this point, given the lack of visibility without his Paths. At least having a thousand hungry eyes to see from gave him the surround view that he needed to build up a picture of the area.

Vor snagged one scarab out of the swarm and crushed it to dust. Noah was already escaping the cage in every direction, dodging the liquid moonlight as he flew at full speed toward the edge of the Underworld.

He needed to get back to the escape before it was too late. Before he lost too many of himself.

He could feel them being picked off one by one, some by falling meteors of moonlight, some by Vor's hand, and the man was far too fast.

But Noah was myriad, and he had a desperation that no slow-thinking patient deity would be able to conjure.

Vor wanted Noah destroyed, or captured, or whatever it was the Watcher wanted to do with his mortal adversary. Ironically, the Watcher may have been able to bring more power to bear by possessing his representative, but where a mortal agent would have been able to match Noah's desperation with zeal and fervor, a god worked on a different scale entirely.

Noah needed to escape.

Not every collision ended in Noah's destruction. Some of the mercury-dripping moonlight was too small to destroy what it landed on, and the hungry scarabs devoured it whole. For every one that was destroyed, another was replicating itself from the vulnerable moondrops.

Noah found himself consuming the mental attacks as they neared him, almost reflexively, and his frantic flight slowed.

He wasn't the same person he'd been before entering the Reaper's storm. And he had the Devourer on his side, stronger and more solid than ever.

He coalesced his swarm, shells interlocking into the serpent's spine, as he reared up and opened a vast hissing maw as big as the remaining scarabs could manage. His own internal gravity fought the Watcher's externally imposed weight.

He wasn't strong enough to just throw off the effect, but he was strong enough to drag all the dripping moonlight in. It was enough that soul wisps he hadn't even seen were yanked in near-instantly.

Where void consumption destroyed, Noah's Devourer aspect just kept growing. The more power it ate, the stronger the draw, and the Watcher's power disappeared into his many hissing mouths faster and faster.

Then, everything stilled. The Watcher's power stopped falling. Vor Ashen stopped swatting at Noah's scarabs.

"You were right all along. You are nothing but a failure."

The tilt of the world abruptly snapped back into normal alignment. The atmospheric pressure relented and the glow around Vor Ashen went out. Before the man could more than gasp in disbelief, whatever gravity had been anchoring him disappeared.

Noah's gravity was stronger. His devouring serpent swarm reared up, snapped down on the trembling Watcher cultist, and swallowed him whole.

Noah coalesced back into his human-wisp form, staring out at the sliver of moonlight on the distant horizon. Then, a moment later, even that blinked out, leaving Noah in uniform dark greyness.

The Watcher had retreated from his own territory in the Underworld, rather than keep feeding his power into Noah's Devourer.

"This is why everyone's so afraid. Once we get started, we can't be stopped." His grin was back, broader than ever. He pointed to the horizon where the Watcher's light had disappeared. "You can hide from us now, but I'll be coming for you soon enough."

The walk back to the exit was long, interrupted twice by encountering groups of maddened souls who fled as Noah approached, and once by crossing over the river. He didn't need to jump across souls on the way back, simply dispersing into his swarm and reforming himself on the other side.

The dim darkness of the Watcherless sky felt comfortable rather than ominous, and by the time they reached the Reaper's Gate, Noah was feeling both relaxed and excited.

He couldn't wait to see what the status messages for all this would look like.

The gate itself was hidden by the same deceptive distance perceptions as the wall and valley, unseen until he walked right up next to it. Someone could wander the Underworld for years without ever finding it. If he'd been even a few steps to one side or the other, without the Shadow Heart pointing him directly there, he'd have gone right past it.

The portal itself was a swirling ball of light that warped the world around it, making the bizarre geography of the Underworld look like a globe around him.

Been nice talking with you again, Shadow said. Take care of yourself out there.

Noah paused at the threshold. “You say that like we’re parting ways.”

I’m still around, but out there, I’m not distinct enough to make it past the noise. Just a tiny part of who you are.

“Is there something I should do about it? Summon you or…?”

Nothing to summon. I’m already you. Only here is the distinction notable. Or maybe you just needed someone to talk to.

“Either way, thank you. Without you, I wouldn’t be standing here.”

Well, I’ll be back soon enough, the Devourer said, rather smugly. Just get those last three shards, and then we can really get to work. One is with the Crimson Cult, the White Bloom has the other two.

“I’m on it,” Noah said with a nod. How would he pull that off? No idea. But when had that ever been enough to stop him? He’d already infiltrated one cult and stopped another’s massive ritual. May as well round out the collection.

Don’t underestimate the cults. They’ll be ready for us now.

“Concerned for me?”

Purely transactional! I’d have a hard time finding a new vessel for my rebirth.

“Sure, keep telling yourself that,” Noah said, and stepped into the portal.

For a moment he was both places at once, the empty underworld and the overwhelmingly tangible physical reality he’d been absent from. His body was distractingly present, his heartbeats warm and loud, and even breathing felt alien.

It took a long disorienting moment to settle back into himself. The weight of existing was strange enough on its own, without the improvements Tony had been working on the whole time he was gone. He needed to test them out properly once he acclimated. But for now, a smile spread across his face as he opened his eyes, alive again and stronger than ever, and a whole cascade of notifications greeted him.
