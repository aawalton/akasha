How I wanted to leap and jump for joy, to grab one of my airships and set out today to see what was over the horizon, to see what would come, we'd seen it, land, real land, actual land so far out! However that would be suicide. If we were going to go we needed to go right.

Right meant adding more ships, larger ships, supplies, people who knew this might be a one way trip, organization, preparedness, and all the boring work that went with it.

“You know you still have a kingdom to run,” Chien chided as he saw the glint in my eyes.

“Don't kill my joy Chien. How often do we see something truly new.”“We? All the damn time, you make it yourself. You, I suspect much less, since you make it. Guess you're seeing it from the outside though huh? Wow this is weird.”

“I have to go!” I declared.

“What will your wife say though.”I gasped, looking at him in shock and slight anger. “You leave her out of this, what she doesn't know...” As I spoke I looked behind me.

For once in my life it seemed I hadn't fallen into the trap, she wasn't here, watching angrily.

“Will have to be told to her later.” I finished, earning a long laugh from my longtime assistant.

As Chien was trying not to support himself on a table I began running the numbers in my head. A month, it was a bit of a back of the napkin kind of math, but that was around where it came to, one month. Even that was pushing it, one and a half was probably a better estimate, maybe two or more depending on winds, and if we ran into any issues, which was almost certain. I sighed as I thought through it.

“You realize you won't be able to go on the first one,” he said as he finally calmed himself.

“Yes, I'm aware,” I moped.

“Hey, you're the one always saying how he wants other people to do things, are you?”“Still, I did want to do this. Will you be going?”“Absolutely not,” Chien replied with a shake of his head.

“Who else could I trust?”“Someone who doesn't find the idea of taking a flying ship across an ocean repellent. You really keep using that one, but it tells more to your failure of bringing people up to speed.”Ouch, that hurt, he wasn't wrong, but it hurt.

“I'll have to see if we have any good candidates somewhere, who knows, maybe we'll find someone who's excited about it,” I grumbled.

After bouncing a few things back and forth I went to see me wife and tell her of the discovered land. She was doing some small task, administering our home, which by now was the size of a palace and had enough staff that an administrator for it was actually needed.

“This coming from the man who always hated being taken from his shop. You'd be going mad on that ship in a week, and we both know you cannot stand to travel,” she said after I told her of the discovery and the fact that I wouldn't be able to lead the expedition.

“I know but...”“But you are needed here, to keep expanding society and to bring your dozens of projects at a time to fruition. Clean out your to do list and I might be willing to hear you on this. And what of me? Were you going to leave me behind?” she accused.

“No, no, I was going to see if you would want to come with me. It's been so long since we had some time away from all of this.”“To where? Some barren land half a world away where there's likely to be nowhere near enough food, enough comfort, or any shelter for us that we didn't make?”

“Oh, between the two of us there would be no problem, you know I could build a basic house in minutes,” I grumbled.

“One that was much like where we grew up. I appreciate that, and the idea, but I've grown to rather like some of the newest additions to homes, like carpets, and good floors.”

There were still a few things that were older in construction, like beds. Some had taken up the idea of rope beds, but some, my wife included, preferred the old ones, made of tree branches and soft leaves, grown, though now in a slightly different manner than before. We'd gone through several different iterations since we'd married, some more modern, but every few years she'd replace it with one grown by a healer.

“Well, whatever they find I'll have to send instructions to build somewhere to stay, maybe in a few years...”“To do list,” my wife retorted.

“Very well, let's try not to add anything to it shall we?” I laughed, I was almost always the one adding to it myself.

Decision made, and the promise of a reward for cleaning out my backlog I retreated to my workroom. In my mind I ran through whom I might send, what man or woman might make the best one to take that job. There were a number of candidates, several people who might do well. I might even send my own daughter, no, she'd like the trip but she liked people too much for the first one, a shame.

As I considered my magic flowed with old practice. I had items to make, plans to form. On one paper I began to write and draw out basic designs, ready to send off to trusted men who might clean them up and put them into practice. On another a sheet of synthetic sapphire began to form from nearby elements, pulled from several kinds of rocks to form a window that would hold up to even heavy damage, part of the airships we were already making. Metal warped, heated, and cooled in practiced rhythms in another, ,controlled to give a proper temper, forming a globe that would be installed in one of the southern cities.

As I let things flow I found my wife was right, I really did enjoy my work.
