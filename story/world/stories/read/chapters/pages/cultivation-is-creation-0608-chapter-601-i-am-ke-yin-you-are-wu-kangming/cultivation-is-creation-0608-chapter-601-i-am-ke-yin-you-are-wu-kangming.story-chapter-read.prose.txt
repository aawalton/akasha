The tension between me and Wu Kangming was clear. Xie Ling sensing it, manifested a massive hand made of frost to grab a hold of Li Na and began to walk off towards the far edge of the step to give us privacy.

“I can walk by myself!” the young girl shrieked. “I swore a Heavenly Oath! I’m not going to try anything. I don’t need to be treated like a prisoner!”

Her protests were ignored as the ice princess continued walking, the ice construct following behind like an obedient pet carrying its cargo. At first Li Na tried to struggle against the ice grip but quickly gave up when she realised no matter how much she twisted or pushed, it wouldn’t make a difference. When they were moved far enough that they wouldn’t be able to overhear, I turned my attention back on Wu Kangming, and for the next thirty seconds or so, we just stood there staring at each other.

“How long have you known?”

It was the sword cultivator who broke the silence, which was probably for the best since I'd been struggling to find the right words anyway.

"What I said before was true," I replied, wanting to be completely honest about this because the situation was already complicated enough without adding half-truths. "When I sacrificed myself on that cliff to save you from the demon, I later woke up with no memory of being Hou at all. Instead, I remembered my life in the cultivation world, my experiences at Azure Peak Sect, and that I was actually Ke Yin."

Wu Kangming nodded slowly but didn't interrupt, which fit what I knew of him.

"It was only after I started cultivating dream qi that Hou's memories began returning," I continued. "In the beginning it came as fragments and impressions that didn't make much sense, but the more my cultivation advanced, the more I continued to remember. And by the time I reached Oneiric Sovereign, I had most of my memories, or at least enough to understand what had actually happened."

Despite being the truth, the explanation felt inadequate since I couldn’t capture the confusion of having two complete lives worth of experiences existing simultaneously in my head. My worry that I hadn’t done a good enough job grew as Wu Kangming stood there thinking about my answer for a full minute.

"We are opponents,” he finally said after taking a deep breath. “You had no obligation to tell me you were Ke Yin just as I had no obligation to tell you I was Wu Kangming. That is how tournaments work. And that is how the cultivation world works. But then…why does it feel so wrong?"

His face seemed just as confused as his words, and I could understand why. This was a person who'd built his entire worldview around understanding rules and following them consistently. So, when there was an internal conflict between what the rules enforced and what his emotions cried for, he didn’t know how to deal with it.

"It feels wrong because we became friends as Hou and Tian and then brothers," I said simply. "That's what makes all this so complicated. The rules say we should be trying to kill each other, and the tournament demands it, but the relationship we built over five years of traveling together says something different."

Wu Kangming's eyes closed briefly. When he opened them again some of the confusion had been replaced by reluctant acceptance.

"I…I thought I was you for a while," he admitted quietly. "But the Hall of Shattered Reflections showed me the truth about my identity, that I was neither Ke Yin or Tian, but Wu Kangming.”

I smiled slightly at that admission but didn't tease him about it. As funny as it sounded that my opponent had spent part of his life believing he was me, the reality behind that confusion was actually pretty messed up. Wu Kangming had been through multiple identity crises one after another.

First living as Tian and feeling like he was missing a part of himself. Then thinking he was Ke Yin and that Wu Kangming was his enemy. Only to then find out that he was in fact Wu Kangming and that Ke Yin was his enemy. And finally finding out that Hou, the one that Wu Kangming saw as a brother in this life, was actually Ke Yin. The psychological toll of that kind of repeated upheaval would be enormous for anyone. The poor guy definitely hadn’t had it easy in the Dream World.

"Now that the truth is out," I began, "what do you want to do? Fight me right here right now and finish the tournament, or wait until after the trial is done?"

As Wu Kangming considered his options, his hand tightened slightly on his sword hilt and for a few moments I thought we were going to have the final clash right here right now, but then he spoke.

“I would rather we wait until the trial is done.”

Seeing my questioning look, he continued.

“I know that this Tian identity was something created for the trial, but Tianming was more of a father to me than my own ever was. The least I can do before leaving this world is pay him back for the sacrifices he made."

The reasoning made sense to me. Just because we were here temporarily didn’t mean that the relationships we'd formed didn’t matter. Walking away without honoring those connections felt wrong on a moral level.

"That’s a relief because I was thinking the same thing," I admitted with a smile. "We work together to reach the Chamber of the Sleeper King, then we get the Celestial Dream Lotus and share it so we can both save the people we care about. Then after all that's done and we've fulfilled our obligations to the lives we lived in this world, we have the final battle that everyone's been waiting for."

Wu Kangming's expression relaxed slightly, and for a second I thought I would see a smile.

"That works," he said simply.

We fell into silence again and I found myself not quite sure what else needed to be said to properly acknowledge the strange situation we'd found ourselves in, and Wu Kangming seemed to be struggling with the same uncertainty because he opened his mouth like he was going to say something, but then closed it again and looked away toward where Xie Ling and Li Na were standing at the far edge of the step.

"Thank you for not ambushing me and ending the battle before I even figured out Hou was my opponent," he whispered. "That is what most cultivators would do in this situation."

"I'm not like most cultivators," I said, because it was true and because I wanted him to understand that our friendship had meant something real to me even if the circumstances surrounding it had been constructed by the workings of the Dream World.

A faint smile finally appeared on Wu Kangming's face, and he looked at me with something that might have been respect or might have been relief that we were on the same page about how to handle this complicated situation.

"I'm beginning to see that," he said.

We stood there after that exchange without knowing what else to say, the conversation having reached its natural conclusion but neither of us quite ready to move on to the next phase of the trial, and the awkwardness was starting to build again when movement from the other side of the step caught my attention. The other two must have sensed that we were done because Xie Ling had released Li Na from the ice hand and both of them were walking back toward our position. It was good timing because I could feel spiritual pressure beginning to gather at the center of step ninety-one, which meant the next battle was beginning.

A figure materialized where the pressure had been building, taking form from nothing in the way all the manifestations did, and I studied him carefully to get a sense of what we'd be dealing with.

He appeared young, maybe early thirties in physical age, with an unlined face and bright curious eyes that suggested an intellectual bent rather than a martial one. His robes were simple and practical without any of the elaborate decorations or sect symbols that many of the previous manifestations had worn, just plain white fabric that looked clean and well-maintained but unremarkable.

The spiritual pressure radiating from him was significant though, Peak Lucid Lawbearer level based on the way the air around him seemed to shimmer and distort slightly, and I tensed in preparation for whatever technique he might use to attack us. But instead of attacking us immediately, the figure studied us for a long moment without speaking. His gaze moved from me to Wu Kangming to Xie Ling and finally to Li Na before cycling back to focus on me specifically.

"I am Yuan Shi," he smiled. "I walked the path of cultivation forty-seven thousand years ago. I studied under the Sect of Eternal Cycles and achieved Lucid Lawbearer at age three hundred and twelve. I died attempting to comprehend the Dao of Beginning while meditating in the Palace of First Principles."

The introduction was formal in a way that none of the other manifestations had bothered with. Most of them had either attacked immediately or offered brief explanations before engaging in combat. Yuan Shi seemed content to just stand there and talk, making no aggressive moves despite the spiritual pressure that continued building around his form.

"Tell me," he said, his smile widening, "what can you tell me about the Dao of Beginning?"
