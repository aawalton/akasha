Interlude II - Harvey

So far, Harvey’s performance in the tournament challenges had been… lackluster. On one hand, he found it a bit distressing to see his combat ranking stagnate around 100 despite all of the hard work he’d put into the weekend challenges. He’d been middle of the pack for the first challenge and quite a bit better in the second, though it also ended in failure. However, a part of him struggled to care. It was hard to muster motivation when he knew that at the end of the semester he’d be kicked from the academy for not being capable of paying their tuition fees. With his home in turmoil from the Dragon invasion, it seemed silly to care about something like a ranking system. Real people died.

It would probably be different if he was in a position like Weston, who had a dozen family members killed. That had seemed to light a fire under his Tainted friend. His anger and violence had only grown as the months went on. At one point he’d even tried beating up Harvey after taking offense to his lackadaisical attitude. That hadn’t gone well for him. While Harvey had hardly left the altercation without bruises, Weston had been far worse off.

That had been weeks ago.

Harvey wandered through the forest outside the academy grounds. Little furry critters fled from his footfalls. If anything more dangerous than a rabbit appeared, he was more than ready to tackle the challenge. Sadly, that rarely occurred this close to the academy. Their first semester had a lot more magical beasts sniffing around but, for some unknown reason, they’d all fled in recent months.

“Maybe I should go apologize to Weston,” Harvey mused out loud. The old him would be on his knees, desperate for the older student’s approval. Now though, not so much. He barely spoke to others these days, instead finding a lot more pleasure in solitude.

As he walked, Harvey stared up through the forest canopy at birds overhead. There was a small temptation to practice leaping into the air with magically enhanced power to smack the fowls down to earth. But, while the challenge might be fun, that wouldn’t be fair to the poor birds.

“Wish one of you would try attacking,” he told the feathered beasts above. “Then I could getcha guilt-free.”

The forest was so peaceful as he stepped through foliage. He knew that Kizu had been dedicating a great deal of time to spatial magic ever since they stopped speaking. It made no sense to Harvey. Well, back when Kizu had that bummed leg, it made a bit of sense to want to put less stress on it and rely on other means of transport. But still, why would anyone want to skip over the beauty around them? Every moment was a blessing. Why spend more time than necessary in a dimly lit corner of the library reading books on theory when you could be out here in the wild?

Harvey’s path through the forest likely would seem meandering wanderings to anyone attempting to track him. That was on purpose. He doubted anyone cared about him enough to try to follow or check-up on him, but, if they did, it would just seem like a thoughtless jaunt through the woods. Which was certainly part of it. Just enjoying nature and the atmosphere.

However, he did have a destination in mind.

Music swelled all around him, a beautiful voice filled his heart and brought him extraordinary peace.

He stepped out of the forest at the seaside cliff he’d discovered chasing Mort months back.

On the edge of the cliff, facing the endless stretch of sea beyond, sang a transparent woman. Harvey didn’t know if the words she spoke were a foreign language or just pure, melodious, music. It mattered not at all to him.

He sat down on a tree stump, closed his eyes, and took in a deep breath of the sea’s air as the melody washed over him. Somehow, she blended the noises of the world into her music. The crash of waves breaking on the rocks of the cliff, the squawk of birds in the jungle, the buzz of distant cicadas, the subtle summer breeze rippling over the forest. They all worked into a chorus, blended together by her voice. She was the great conductor, taking noise and transforming it into transcendent beauty.

“Harvey,” the pleasant voice said as her music drifted away.

Harvey cracked an eye and smiled. “Ray,” he greeted her in return.

“I did not see you arrive, why did you not announce yourself?”

“And interrupt?” Harvey laughed joyously. “Never.”

He stood and dusted himself off. He resisted the urge to comb his fingers through his hair. He’d started letting it grow back out again and was under constant threat of the wind blowing it into a chaotic nest.

Ray hummed happily as he strolled up to her.

“Did you bring your flute?” she asked.

“Of course.” It had taken him weeks of listening to her sing before he’d gained enough courage to mention that he was also a musician, albeit far inferior to her in the art. She’d insisted he join her playing ever since. He would harmonize while she focused on the song’s melody.

He still turned red whenever he thought about his first time playing for her. His shaking hands had fumbled dozens of notes in the short tune. And yet, Ray had been delighted.

Harvey took a moment to admire the sheer scope of her beauty. It went far beyond her voice. Her soft features, eyes filled with care, a gentle nature, her attitude toward life. He viewed her as the pinnacle of goodness in the world. And Harvey would do anything to protect her.

Already, he was making plans to build a cabin out in the forest to live in after he was doubtlessly removed from Shinzou Academy at the end of the semester. While his family would miss him back in Edgeland, he’d already received letters from his mother begging him to find a way to stay away from the nation.

There was a bit of a legal gray area as to whether or not he could stay on the island. But there weren’t a whole lot of other options. Harvey certainly wasn’t about to join the Tainted refugee camps in Tross. The only other option would be to join a mercenary crew and work for Tross’s revolutionaries.

No, Harvey had decided the quiet life was for him. Just him, the forest, and Ray. He knew enough about combat now to fend for himself and he’d been studying the local flora to gain a better grasp on how to be self-sufficient on the island.

“You enjoy fighting,” Ray said, shattering the silence of his peaceful musings.

“I do,” Harvey admitted.

“Can you explain it to me?”

Harvey mulled over the question. He raised a fist and the tattoos on his arm glowed. Those had been Tip’s idea. A shortcut to strength that streamlined enhancement spells. The risk had been minimal since Tip’s sister was a master tattoo enchanter. It did complicate casting other branches of magic, since his arms innately wanted to channel into physical strength. Healing himself was the one other exception, he found it likewise was easier to repair damage dealt to his body, though healing others was a chore. So far, out of everything he’d studied at the academy, Harvey had found most of the difficulty was with elemental spells. Still, giving up easy channeling with his hands was worth the trade in his opinion. He preferred the close up nature of enhancement battles.

“I like the challenge,” he finally said. “There’s a thrill to besting an opponent. I know some people really get into sports but this is better for me. It’s like dancing with a partner. You gain a level of understanding and a sense of who your partner is, far beyond what you get from a simple conversation. It’s intimate.”

“Do you wish to fight me?” Ray asked. She cocked her head at him, curious.

Harvey laughed again. “Never.”

“But you will never know me otherwise,” she said sadly.

“Fighting is just one way of learning about someone. Music is another.” He reached up his sleeve and pulled out his flute. A bit battered, but still functional. An old gift. He raised it to his lips and blew a few notes. They drifted out into the world. Unlike Ray, he lacked the complete command of sound she wielded with her songs so his notes simply merged with the world’s noise. A slight blip of organization in the constant cacophony of chaos.

He glanced at Ray. He’d expected her to join in and sing. Normally they spent hours here, creating music together. But something was different about her today.

“My father stirs,” she said.

Harvey blinked. “You still have family alive?” He’d assumed that since she was a spirit that any mortal connections had long since been severed. But he’d also never pried into her life from before.

“Alive,” Ray said the word, as if testing its taste in her mouth. “Not quite. Death must be a threat to directly experience life. Like how I simply observe mortality from my perch here. I am a spectator of life, rather than one who truly lives.”

“So, your father’s also a ghost?” Harvey asked.

She turned and faced him. Her transparent face scrunched up in confusion. Harvey’s heart melted at the cute, quizzical expression.

“I’m not a ghost.”

“You’re… not a ghost?” he repeated.

“No. Not literally, at least. Father created my existence and I’ve always been who I am.”

“I… don’t understand,” Harvey said honestly. “You look like a ghost. And you’re bound to a specific location like a lot of common stories of spirits. You even have a gravestone.” He gestured at the stone slab that protruded the ground at the cliff edge.

“My father’s friend created that. He designed it to help keep me safe. All of my siblings have a similar link to the island.”

“Who is your father’s friend?” Harvey had a hundred jumbled questions in his head as he processed what she was telling him, but that one came out first.

“Very short. Less than half your height. Undead followed in his wake wherever he traveled. I was not fond of him. He had cruel eyes.”

“Short.” Harvey mused. “A gnome then? I guess that makes sense.” He vaguely recalled Professor Krimpit mentioning gnome occupation of Shinzou Island in one of the History F lectures last semesters, but that had been at least several hundred years ago.

“Perhaps.” Ray drifted up to him. She reached a hand out in the direction of the sea. “Ever since my sister’s chance meeting, I worry about my father’s arrival.”

“You mean the girl that Kizu watches over? The one who was here with the fox Kemon girl when I first met you?” Harvey vaguely knew about Kizu’s ward. He’d seen her several times over the last few months, though he wasn’t certain exactly why Kizu was watching her. Money obviously wasn’t an issue for someone like Kizu’s family. At first, Harvey assumed it must mean Kizu was receiving specialized training, but he heard the girl was Professor Taroe’s daughter and his old friend still ranked extremely low in Rejuvenation and Restoration.

“Yes. My sister,” Ray said the words wistfully.

Harvey frowned and scratched his head. “Your father obviously isn’t Professor Taroe. Do you mean a half-sister? Who is your mother?”

Instead of answering, Ray turned her attention to the sea. She stared down at the waves as they crashed into the coast. Each surge of the sea  sprayed salty mist into the air.

“I have no mother. I was created solely by my father. A failure who will never accomplish his dreams. At best, I linger in this place in hopes of offering meager assistance when breaches his confinement below and inevitably comes once again into the land of mortals. I hope to perhaps soften his hand as he grips his territory. I am not certain whether it is possible.”

“That means you and Kizu’s ward share a father?” Harvey asked. His heart beat a bit quicker at the notion. These were secrets. And likely they were kept for good reason.

“Yes. My sister’s mother was a mortal. You can see it written in her flesh. I know not who.”

Harvey’s mind raced. Finally tearing his eyes away from the brilliant visage of Ray, he paced along the cliff edge. He loathed studying as much as the next student, but that hardly meant he was dense by any measure. The clues were all there from this conversation and his friendship with Kizu all those months ago. He felt them all click into place, creating a picture. Perhaps a flawed one. And yet… it would all make sense.

“Her mother is Kizu’s sister,” he said. “Kizu was dead-set on finding his sister when he arrived on campus. And he only really settled into his other studies after that girl turned up. He even made an effort in History F at the end of the semester. If Kizu met his sister and she handed off her daughter to him, then that would make sense.”

“I know not,” Ray repeated. “But my sister is so precious and small. Imprisoned in state half-mortality. Please show kindness to her.”

“Ray. Of course I’ll be kind to a child. I’d hope you know me well enough for that.”

“Of course,” she relented. “I did not intend the request as a slight against your character. I simply worry for her. I know you to be a kind man.”

She drifted closer to him. There was a sad smile on her face. She reached out, her translucent hand reflected slightly in the sunlight.

Harvey extended his hand. The swirling tattoo on the back of his hand turned down as he accepted her. Their fingertips touched. Then she passed through him. The only sensory signifier that they’d held one for a brief moment was a slight numb tingling.

He sighed. Despite their lack of true contact, a proud joy rippled through him. She longed for that moment of contact as much as he did. That knowledge was enough for him to be content.

Sitting, legs dangling over the lip of the cliff, they chatted. He spoke about his classes and family while Ray listened. Sometimes she offered advice or knowledge and other days they often sang together. But she rarely offered any true details about her background.

When a moment of silence struck, where they simply listened to the nature around them, Harvey decided to ask a question that pried into the heart of her existence.

“Who is your father?” Harvey asked. After all these months of knowing one another, he’d never pressed Ray on that question.

Her eyes flashed scarlet as they met his.

“His name is Otochi. A Calamity and the son of Sekai. And he stirs.”
