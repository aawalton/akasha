The illusion of safety provided by the high timber walls evaporated the very second the heavy vanguard began its march.

The thousands of skeletal knights and the two dozen towering Bone Goliaths did not charge with the chaotic, shrieking frenzy of the lesser zombies. They moved with a terrifying, mechanical inevitability. The sheer physical weight of their advance caused the freezing mud of the plains to tremble, a deep, rhythmic vibration that travelled straight up the solid oak logs of the city walls and rattled the teeth of the defenders standing on the parapets.

"All casters to the front lines!" Caistina Tharistrar’s voice boomed across the ramparts, magically amplified to cut clearly through the rising panic. "Recall the resting shifts! Every available mage on the wall, right now!"

The frantic, highly disciplined rotation system that Josh had implemented was instantly abandoned. This was no longer a matter of managing stamina. It was a desperate bid to stop the city from being physically flattened.

Dozens of mages, elementalists, and geomancers poured out from the inner barricades and the upper dormitories, rushing back up the steep wooden stairs. They crowded onto the walkways, their hands and magical focuses flaring to life with raw, volatile energy.

Brett stepped up to the very edge of the central platform, his pale eyes locked onto the closest Bone Goliath. The monstrosity was easily twenty feet tall, a horrifying amalgamation of hundreds of human and beast skeletons fused together with thick, pulsing dark magic. It wore crude, rusted iron plating bolted directly into its massive bones. It dragged an ancient, iron-banded oak trunk behind it, treating the massive siege ram as though it weighed nothing at all.

"Target the joints!" Brett screamed to the casters forming up around him. "Do not aim for the thickest bone! Melt the connective magic!"

Brett did not bother with a sweeping, continuous beam of plasma. He needed absolute, pinpoint destruction. He drew deeply upon the reserves funnelled through his crimson Ember-silk cloak, compressing the thermal energy between his palms until it formed a blinding, searing lance of pure white heat.

He thrust his hands forward, unleashing the compressed plasma directly at the right knee joint of the approaching Goliath.

The elemental strike was flawless. The white-hot lance pierced the thick, swirling necrotic fog and struck the exact point where the massive femur met the tibia. The intense thermal shock vaporised the dark magic holding the joint together. The iron plating melted instantly, and the fused bones shattered under the immense, sudden heat.

The Goliath let out a deafening, hollow roar of structural failure. Deprived of its right leg, the massive behemoth pitched violently sideways. The sheer momentum of its advance carried it forward, sending it crashing heavily into the freezing mud. Its colossal weight crushed dozens of heavily armoured skeletal knights marching beside it, snapping their rusted halberds like dry twigs.

Fifty yards to Brett's right, Caistina unleashed her own terrifying display of arcane mastery.

The elven mage raised her pristine white staff high above her head. She did not use fire or lightning. She manipulated the very fabric of gravity and arcane pressure. A massive, shimmering violet ring of pure energy manifested directly above the skull of a second Bone Goliath.

Caistina brought her staff down in a sharp, sweeping motion.

The violet ring contracted violently, descending like a magical guillotine. It sheared cleanly through the rusted iron plating protecting the Goliath's neck, completely decapitating the towering construct. The massive, horned skull tumbled off its shoulders, crashing into the mud, and the dark magic anchoring the body instantly dissipated. The headless Goliath collapsed into a mountain of loose, brittle bones.

A ragged, desperate cheer erupted from the archers and spearmen stationed near the mages.

But the triumph lasted for only a fraction of a second. Two Goliaths had fallen, but twenty-two more continued their relentless, earth-shaking march. They stepped over the ruined remains of their fallen brethren, entirely unbothered by the losses.

The heavy vanguard finally reached the base of the timber walls, and the true horror of the Bone General's strategy became apparent.

The Goliaths did not need to wait for the zombie ramps to be rebuilt. They simply used the remaining lesser infantry as a living carpet. The massive constructs stepped directly onto the writhing sea of ghouls and zombies currently clawing at the timber logs. The sheer weight of their iron-plated boots crushed the lesser undead into a thick, horrific paste, providing the titans with an elevated, solid foundation to stand upon.

A Goliath stationed near the eastern watchtower raised its massive, iron-banded oak trunk and swung it brutally against the solid timber wall.

The impact sounded like a thunderclap.

The entire section of the wooden rampart groaned and buckled under the immense kinetic force. Several thick oak logs splintered instantly, sending massive, jagged shards of wood flying inward. Two militiamen standing directly above the impact zone lost their footing as the walkway violently shook, tumbling backward over the inner railing and falling into the city streets below.

"They are trying to shatter the wall supports!" Captain Aris yelled, drawing his broadsword and charging down the walkway to rally the panicked defenders. "Drop the heavy masonry! Crush their skulls!"

The heavy infantry rushed to the parapets, hoisting the massive chunks of limestone and shattered paving stones they had been using against the zombie ramps. They hurled the heavy boulders straight down, aiming for the glowing green eye sockets of the towering behemoths.

A massive chunk of stone struck a Goliath squarely on its iron-plated shoulder. The impact dented the rusted metal and chipped the bone beneath, but it did not fell the creature.

The Goliath stopped swinging its battering ram. It slowly tilted its massive, horned skull upwards, locking its glowing green gaze onto the terrified heavy fighters standing on the parapet.

Then, it reached down into the mud.

The Goliath wrapped its massive, skeletal fingers around the very same jagged boulder that had just been dropped on it. The creature hoisted the heavy limestone chunk with effortless, terrifying strength. It leaned back, its fused spine popping loudly, and hurled the boulder directly back up at the timber wall.

"Incoming!" Josh roared, his storm-grey eyes widening in horror as he saw the massive shadow arcing through the pale sky.

He lunged forward, throwing his heavy, steel-clad bulk into a group of frozen, staring militiamen, tackling them violently to the wooden floorboards just as the boulder crested the parapet.

The jagged limestone crashed down onto the walkway with catastrophic force. It completely obliterated the wooden railing and smashed directly through the thick floorboards, creating a massive, gaping hole in the rampart. A seasoned city guardsman was caught directly in the centre of the impact, his screams cut brutally short as the stone crushed him flat against the timber.

The psychological devastation was absolute. The defenders had relied on their high elevation for safety, using gravity as their primary weapon. Now, the monsters were actively returning their fire with terrifying, lethal accuracy.

All along the wall, the Goliaths began picking up dropped anvils, heavy stones, and even the shattered bodies of their own skeletal knights, hurling them violently up onto the walkways. The ramparts devolved into a chaotic, bloody nightmare of flying debris and splintering wood.

"Bring down that giant!" Bhel bellowed, picking himself up from the floorboards near Josh.

The dwarven fighter pointed his throwing axe down at a Goliath that was currently reaching into the mud for another boulder.

The defenders near the eastern watchtower rallied with desperate, frantic courage. A heavy ballista crew swung their massive weapon around, ignoring the flying debris. They cranked the thick torsion springs to maximum tension and pulled the release lever.

A massive, iron-tipped bolt the size of a small tree trunk shot out over the parapet. It struck the Goliath directly in the centre of its massive chest cavity. The incredible kinetic force of the ballista punched cleanly through the rusted iron plating, shattering the fused ribs and destroying the swirling core of dark magic hidden within.

The beast collapsed, its massive arms flailing wildly as it fell backward into the sea of bone.

At the same time, a squad of city guardsmen managed to tip a massive, boiling cauldron of alchemical pitch directly over the edge of the parapet. The liquid fire cascaded down, completely engulfing a second Goliath. The intense, clinging heat melted the dark magic binding its joints, causing the titan to slowly crumble into a burning, thrashing heap of charred bone.

They were killing them, but the cost in blood and shattered timber was incredibly high, and the most terrifying threat was currently converging on the absolute centre of the defensive line.

Josh pushed himself up from the wooden deck, pulling a terrified baker to his feet. He looked towards the main gatehouse, and his breath caught painfully in his throat.

Three of the largest, most heavily armoured Bone Goliaths had completely ignored the flanking walls. They had marched in a tight, unstoppable wedge directly through the smothered pitch trenches. They were now standing right in front of the massive, sealed iron portcullis.

They raised their iron-banded oak trunks simultaneously.

BOOM.

The first strike hit the thick iron bars of the portcullis. The deafening, metallic crash echoed across the entire valley. The reinforced wooden platform where Josh was standing physically jumped beneath his steel boots. The vibration was so intense it rattled his teeth and caused a sharp, blinding spike of agony to shoot through his cracked ribs.

BOOM.

The second strike followed perfectly in sync. The thick, solid oak logs bracing the gates on the inside groaned loudly, a terrifying sound of stressed timber reaching its absolute breaking point. Dust and loose mortar rained down from the stone archway surrounding the gates.

BOOM.

The third strike caused the central iron bars of the portcullis to visibly bend inward.

The terrified shrieks of the civilian militia on the ramparts were entirely drowned out by the rhythmic, apocalyptic pounding. The three Goliaths were operating like a flawless, mindless demolition crew. They drew their battering rams back and swung them forward with mechanical, relentless precision.

Josh looked down at the thick wooden floorboards beneath his feet. The planks were jumping and splintering with every single impact. The gatehouse was not designed to withstand the concentrated, simultaneous pounding of three high-tier siege engines.

If those gates shattered, the sheer, crushing tide of ten thousand skeletal knights, zombies, and Crypt Ghouls would pour directly into the main plaza. The crude, secondary rubble wall Captain Aris had built would not hold back a unified flood of that magnitude for more than five minutes. The residential streets of Ashenfall would become a slaughterhouse.

Josh turned his head, his storm-grey eyes searching through the thick smoke and flying debris.

He found Carcan standing near the inner barricades. The elven healer was surrounded by a dozen bleeding, groaning guardsmen who had been struck by flying masonry. Her silver-white hair was illuminated by the intense, pulsing golden light of her amber staff as she desperately tried to seal their wounds. She was doing exactly what she had promised, keeping the line breathing, but she was entirely exposed.

He looked at Brett. The elemental mage was leaning heavily over the parapet, firing bolt after bolt of searing plasma down at the Goliaths battering the gates, but the thick, rusted iron plating on their shoulders was absorbing the worst of the heat. Brett was gasping for air, his face pale and slick with sweat, rapidly approaching the very dangerous limits of magical exhaustion.

The strategy of holding the high ground had failed. Raining arrows and magic from above was no longer sufficient. The structural integrity of the city was failing directly beneath their boots.

Josh felt the heavy, uncompromising anchor of his Guardian class settle deep into his bones. It was a cold, absolute certainty. He could not stop the gates from shattering by standing on top of the wall.

He unhooked his heavy dark steel heater shield, sliding it firmly onto his left arm and securing the thick leather straps. He drew his master-crafted longsword, the steel hissing sharply over the deafening noise of the battering rams.

He looked across the violently shaking wooden platform.

Bhel was standing near the top of the steep wooden stairs leading down into the city. The dwarven fighter was fully clad in his overlapping iron plates and boiled leather once more, though he was leaning heavily on one of his axes. He was panting heavily, watching the floorboards splinter beneath them.

Josh caught the dwarf's eye.

He did not need to shout over the roar of the battle. He did not need to explain his sudden, suicidal change in tactics. He simply looked at the heavy, shaking gates below, and then looked back at the fierce, unyielding warrior who had stood beside him through countless horrors.

Bhel wiped a smear of blood and soot from his intricate beard. The dwarf looked down at the shaking stairs, understood exactly what the towering human was silently proposing, and offered a slow, grim, fiercely determined nod.

Josh turned towards the parapet, his heavy steel boots thudding loudly against the shaking timber as he marched directly towards the edge. Bhel fell into step right beside him, gripping his axes tightly.
