“I should be able to fix that,” she assures me. “Don’t use your emergency skills—we need to be delicate to prevent permanent damage. Thank you, by the way.”

“For saving me,” she clarifies. “Using the blankets to muffle the sound was quick thinking, I might have been out of the fight if not for that.”

I look away and shrug. “That was Vi,” I murmur. “I didn’t do anything.”

“Oh, I see,” she replies, in that neutral tone she uses when she’s trying to pretend not to judge us. “It was a bit hard to tell during the fight, but that was definitely you at the end, right? What was that spell?”

It was hard to tell. I think we switched multiple times, but sometimes it felt like our body was moving according to Vi’s will or mine almost before we actually switched. It’s a feeling that’s happened before, but I don’t particularly want to delve into that memory just now.

“Not a spell,” I correct her. “And before you ask, not chaos magic, either. Just a boring old skill.”

“The fire controlling aspect of your [Pyrothaumaturgy]? How did you manage that?”

I smile proudly and snap my fingers, conjuring a little heatless flame burning at the tip of my index finger like a little candle. The first time I tried that, I burned myself, but not now.

“I’ve been practicing,” I inform her. “Just like you told me to.”

It’s all stuff I’ve done before, even as far back as the convergence point. Controlling fire means more than just moving it around—even if the skill that let me do it was originally [Pyrokinesis]. Shape, size, heat, light, color—Engie doesn’t like me doing it, but I can do whatever I want with it. If I go deep enough, it’s probably even possible to change even more fundamental properties, like Vi’s conversion spell. In theory, the only actual limit is how much energy is in it, but even then I can absorb any extra energy or push more in from my own reserves. So the real limit is my own focus and how precisely I can control the energy involved. [Pyrothaumaturgy] gives me a nice starting point, and Talla’s advice—simple as it was—has been priceless for slowly expanding my abilities from there.

“Impressive,” she admits. “I didn’t realize you’d taken to it so much. I thought you were focused on Nipper.”

“It’s the same thing,” I say, snapping my fingers again.

With a little exertion of will, I alter the magical signature of the flickering flame on my index finger and hold it up to my shoulder. Nipper looks up from where he was already getting a head start nibbling on the dead monster and then wriggles through a gap in the air to emerge next to my head and happily snap the little treat off of my finger. His teeth gently graze my skin as he does, but he’s careful not to hurt me. Such a good boy.

“He only perceives magic,” I explain. “So communicating with him has been a great way to refine my control. I wouldn’t have been able to pull you out of that tent without him.”

“Well then thank you too, Nipper,” she says, smiling at the worm on my shoulder.

Pretty pointless, since he can’t hear or understand her, but I appreciate the gesture anyway and some echo of the sentiment probably translates through our bond. Speaking of which, did he get any gains of his own?
