Like the rest of the city, the Night Market is vertical. Unlike the rest of the city, it’s extremely narrow. From one wall to another it can’t be wider than a house; just enough room for a business to exist on either side and the catwalks that cross up, down, and between. Of course, there aren’t actual stacked houses here, like in the main city, but there are hundreds of platforms of various sizes where maintenance spell circles are carved into the ground. They’re probably designed that way to make it easy for workers to move between all the spell circles to repair and replenish them with ease. As a result, the slapdash shops that have set up on the various platforms are open on all sides, both encouraging sticky fingers, and making it easy to track down anyone who might get caught.

A handful of maintenance spells light the larger platforms, scattered above and below me like a field of red stars, but it’s still far darker than the rest of the city. Lacking the usual accommodations for species without dark vision, I pull out my low-light potion and drink it down. Similarly, Cyros slips a pair of artificed goggles over his eyes.

[You have been subjected to a Night Vision potion,] Echo needlessly reports. [Time Remaining: 7 hours, 32 minutes.]

I’ve got a second potion ready to go just in case, but seven hours should already be more than enough time.

“Where do you need to go for your jobs?” Cyros asks in a hushed voice. The Market isn’t silent by any means, but it is noticeably quieter than normal squares, with everyone speaking in low, conspiratorial tones. Very few people are here to browse, but rather have specific destinations in mind.

“Artificer,” I say, not seeing any point in keeping my jobs from Cyros. He’s already here, and if he wants to help, maybe we can get everything done even faster. “Restocking mind wipe scrolls for the Guild. Then retrieving a package for The Dragon’s Hoard.” I don’t mention the intelligence job, as saying that out loud would be dumb, and Echo is the one who will help me complete that one, anyway.

“My job is also for scrolls,” Cyros says. “Summoning. Let’s find that first.”

Fine by me.

It’s not that certain affinities are illegal, per say—you can’t really make it illegal to have an affinity for something when lots of people are born with them—but the city does have a list of forbidden spells. Types that involve some sort of mind control magic are banned for obvious reasons. Summoning scrolls sound harmless on the surface, but depending on what kind of creature you summon, and where, the effects can be devastating, so Moonfall has a blanket ban on those, too. A lot of the magical items in the Night Market are forbidden for similar reasons, deemed too dangerous to own or use in the city, for one reason or another. Which, of course, means the Blackcloaks really want them.

Despite having intel on where the Night market would take place, the Guild didn’t provide any info on where the specific shops would be—just who to look for. I suspect this is because they all set up at random, anyway. As a result, Cyros and I start climbing to the top of the Market and decide to work our way down, so if or when the Guards do bust the market, we’ll be in a better position to flee.

Luckily, my ability to Check people’s names comes in exceptionally handy for finding the merchant we’re looking for. We’re most of the way to the top when I find the person on my job sheet: a dhampyr named Creed. She’s seated on a blanket on a smaller level, a series of bound scrolls arrayed in front of her. I Check the scrolls, the woman, and the blanket; as expected, there’s a trap in the blanket spelled to trigger if anyone tries to pinch one of the scrolls. What exactly that trap might do is beyond me, but I’m quite interested in seeing if I can find anything like that in the Guild that I could adapt for my own purposes.

“That’s her,” I tell Cyros, leading us over to her ledge.

“Really?” He squints at the woman, then shakes his head. “How do you keep doing that?”

I grin, switching catwalks to make my way over to her shop. “Magic.”

Cyros and I sit down across from the dhampyr, who raises an eyebrow as she looks us over.

“They start them young, these days,” Creed remarks.

I try not to narrow my eyes. Come on, I’m eighteen, now! A legal adult—especially by this world’s standards. Cyros doesn’t seem bothered, however.

“Helps us go unnoticed. The Guards tend to overlook minors,” Cyros says, taking out a pouch that he sets on the blanket between them. “Payment for Lord Oscur’s package.”

As far as I know, there is no Lord Oscur in the guild, or any of the city’s nobility, but the Guild often uses false names and codes for delivery jobs.

Creed only grunts in response, taking the pouch without even opening it to look at the contents. Rather than selecting any of the scrolls on display, she withdraws a wrapped bundle from behind her and sets it before Cyros. He takes it as Creed turns to look at me.

My job didn’t come with a payment for Creed; probably something they’d been paid for in advance, or by some other means. I also won’t be taking the package back to the Guild, but leaving it at a separate drop-off location, not scheduled until two days from now. I’d be paranoid about keeping a job’s package for so long, except it will be guaranteed safe once I can get it into my Apothecary.

I hand over a folded and sealed paper I’d been given. “Here for delivery.”

This time Creed does open the letter, her crimson eyes flickering over the words. Then she refolds it, slips it into her cloak, and produces a second bundle of scrolls for me.

“Anything else?” the dhampyr asks.

Cyros shakes his head, and I also decline—though it is tempting to buy some of those scrolls for myself. I’m on a job, however, and I’ll already have enough things to carry; I can come back to do some personal shopping at a future Market.

Cyros’s job is done, but unsurprisingly he sticks with me as I start searching for my second delivery contact. I Check as many people as I can as we maneuver the tight catwalks, but mostly I keep my focus on the scrolls, ensuring they don’t get pinched along the way. The way we have to squeeze by each other certainly doesn’t make this a convenient location for the Night Market, but the layout is just begging for me to climb and jump around like a jungle gym. I’ll have to come back here for some parkour practice at a later date.

If I’m lucky, I’ll find the target for the intel job at some point as we wander around. It sure would be easier to do without having to worry about anyone stealing the scrolls—maybe once I pick up my second delivery item I can store both in my Inventory so I can go hunting with less things to think about.

As I’m casually scanning the crowds, one name in particular pops out, and I stop suddenly to do a double take, Cyros nearly running into me. A person behind us grumbles, and I keep moving, hurrying off the catwalk and onto the nearest platform, looking down at a level beneath us.

“What is it?” Cyros asks.

I let out an annoyed sigh. “No one important.”

Álvaro is standing at the center of a small crowd, foot planted on the carcass of the scorpid. He’s laughing and gesturing to different people in attendance, clearly haggling out some sort of deal. Dammit, that should have been my sell! I’ve half a mind to alert the Guards just to break up the Market early and lose him his business.

But he’d probably just stick it back in his Inventory and slip out scot free. And besides, I’ve still got two jobs to finish. I turn away from the Monster Hunter and keep searching for my next contact.

“Do you know him?” Cyros asks.

“Yeah, he’s a jerk,” I grumble, still scanning the crowds.

There’s a hint of caution in Cyros’s tone. “How’d you meet?”

I roll my eyes. “What are you, my mom?” But the second I’ve said it, my stomach turns with regret. “Sorry. I ran into him a couple days ago. He stole my kill.”

A flicker of amusement passes over Cyros’s face. “I’m surprised you let him get away with that.”

“I didn’t let him,” I snap.

“What, you just stood there and watched as he dragged that whole beast away?” Cyros teases.

“No.” I lower my voice. “He had an Inventory. Like me.”

Cyros’s smile is gone. “You mean he’s from…”

“Yeah.” We press through a particular thick part of the crowd, and both of us go silent, tightening our grips on the packages beneath our cloaks. Once we emerge and start down a less crowded staircase, Cyros moves back to my side.

“What does that mean?” he asks. “Should we… I mean, does he know?”

“Yeah,” I say. “He even said he knew someone else from back home. A sibling I think.”

Cryros frowns. “How many more like you are out there?”

I can only shake my head. I’d have no way of knowing—not without asking Shirasil, and I definitely won’t be doing that.

“So are you going to talk to him again?” Cyros asks.

I finally catch sight of a name I recognize among Echo’s Checks; the contact for the second delivery job. I start to zig-zag my way over and down.

“Why would I?” I ask. “He was a jerk, like I said.”

“Well, yeah,” Cyros says. “But what if he knows something about how you got here? Or about… you know.”

The gods, though he’s too chicken to say that out loud. In case they might be listening.

Okay, well, that’s totally plausible with Shirasil’s unannounced drop-ins.

“It couldn’t hurt to know more about what’s going on,” he says. “More information is never a bad thing.”

A favorite saying of his—and he’s not wrong. My goal is to take on the gods. Widengra, specifically. And if Álvaro might know anything that can help…

I wrinkle my nose in distaste. “Fine. I’ll go track him down after the Market. Happy?”

“I mean, you don’t have to,” Cyros says. “It was just a suggestion.”

I shoot him a bewildered look. “Seriously?”

He blinks. “What?”

“You just talked me into learning more about him, and now you want to talk me out of it?” I ask. “Why do you care, anyway?”

“I don’t!” Cyros objects. “I mean, I care about you. Your wellbeing. I just want to help, okay?”

I narrow my eyes at an increasingly uncomfortable-looking Cyros. By then, however, we’ve nearly reached my target.

Unlike Creed, who’d set up a makeshift shop, my second contact appears to be a patron of the Market. Tika is a goblin, which I hadn’t expected, and I’m briefly thrown back to Iski’s stern but motherly demeanor.

Then a couple humans very nearly run the goblin over, and she drunkenly stumbles out of the way, shaking a sloshing gourd at them as she shouts an impressive string of obscenities, and the comparison is utterly shattered.

I cross the stream of people and crouch down at the goblin’s side. “Are you alright?”

Tika shoots me a glare that could have curdled milk. “Who are you? What do you want? Don’t touch me!”

I’m not touching her, in fact, but I am a bit thrown by her reaction. Am I sure I have the right person? I Check her name again, this time focusing on her level, profession, and whatever other personal information I can nab. She fits all the criteria I was given. With little else to do, I try offering the passphrase.

“I heard you were looking for a helper.”

Something glints in the goblin’s eyes, and I abruptly find myself questioning how much of her drunken demeanor is actually authentic.

“Not someone like you,” she says, slurring her words, even as she replies with the correct response. “I need someone more subtle. Heh.” She abruptly grins. “They should make them less generic, don’t you think?”

This last part, obviously, was not part of the passphrase, and I don’t even know how to react to her so brazenly shattering the illusion of secrecy. Still, she’s definitely the person I’m looking for, so I withdraw her payment. From the weight and wooden sounds of movement inside the bag, it’s not filled with coins, but most of the people in the Night Market don’t pay with money, anyway.

Tika snatches the pouch from me, then tips her head back, pouring the rest of the gourd’s contents down her throat. She shakes out the last few drops, then thrusts the gourd into my hands. “Here, hold this for me, won’t you?”

I take hold of the gourd automatically, watching her in bafflement as she then stumbles away.

“Er.” Cyros stands behind me. “Are you sure that was the right person?”

As I stand, I feel something inside the gourd shift. I turn the gourd on its side, and again feel something roll around the bottom of the container.

I grin, tucking the gourd beneath my cloak as well. “Pretty sure it was.”

Two jobs down, one job to go.

The intel job is actually less about gathering information on the actual target I’m after, but trying to identify one of their contacts. Echo is exceptionally useful for this, when I can afford her my full attention. I nod for Cyros to follow me.

“Let’s get out of the way,” I say to him. “I want to store some things before I keep going.”

With a curious look, he follows as we move toward the periphery of the Market.

“So what have you got?” he asks once we’re out of the earshot of others.

“Dunno,” I admit, handing the gourd to him to examine. “Whatever is in there is too big to fit out the nozzle.”

This is a bit of a problem, because it’s loose enough that I’m not sure I’ll be able to add it to my Inventory. Since everything has to be packed snug enough to be considered one item, anything that is loose often ends up being counted as two items, and therefore only one of them can go into the Inventory. In this case, I suspect the gourd, once attached to my Apothecary, would be stored unhindered, but whatever is inside the gourd would fall out and be left on the ground outside. Not the end of the world, as I could then pack it away by itself, but I don’t know if I’m supposed to deliver the object still inside the gourd as a way of proving it hasn’t been tampered with.

Cyros passes it back with a shrug. “You could just store the scrolls and hang onto the gourd. I doubt anyone would try to steal an old drinking flask, anyway.”

Probably not, but I don’t want to risk it. Curious, I tip the gourd over, allowing a couple drops of alcohol to dribble out, then stick my finger through the opening. I might be able to stuff packaging in here if—

My finger brushes the concealed object. It’s cold and hard, like metal, and an electric jolt seems to course through me. Something else passes through me—an idea, or a memory—I yank my hand away, very nearly throwing the gourd at the ground.

“Are you okay?” Cyros asks, alarmed.

“Yeah,” I say, that electric feeling already gone, as if I’d never felt it at all. Still, my heart is beating fast in my chest. “It shocked me or something.”

Cyros grimaces. “Probably best not to touch the mystery cursed relic.”

“Probably a good idea,” I agree, taking the cork tied to the flask and stuffing it in the end. What was it I had felt at that moment my finger had made contact? It feels like a word that’s just on the tip of your tongue. Something that’s slipped your mind, but is only barely out of reach. It felt… familiar, maybe?

That’s as much as I can get.

In a secluded maintenance tunnel, I remove my Apothecary, add the scrolls, add it all back to my Inventory, and then tie the gourd to my belt alongside all my potions, hidden away beneath my cloak. Once everything is properly secured, the unease from the object in the gourd has passed, and I can almost chalk up what I felt to an overactive imagination. Once I deliver it back to the Guild, it won’t be my problem anymore. Now, just one last job to complete.

Cyros and I make it back out to one of the main thoroughfairs, and I once more return to scanning the crowd. I’m just about to move down to a different level, hoping to find the target I’m looking for there, when someone grabs the back of my scarf, pulling me to a stop by my neck. A bubble of irrational rage surfaces within me—like when your headphones catch on something and get ripped from your ears. Cyros knows I hate that! But when I turn around, it’s not him that’s taken hold of one end of my scarf.

A scrawny, lizard-like girl is standing there, her scales a pale sheen of purple and green. She’s a dracid, I realize, though I’ve never actually met the dragon-like species before; they’re rare in Dunmora, doubly so in the desert region of Moonfall. I can only stare at her in surprise as she snaps her hand back, letting go of my scarf like it burned her.

“Sorry!” she squeaks, eyes darting nervously around. “Um, you are Sal, right? You look like her. What I’ve been told, anyway. A young human traveling with a dryad, they said. Dark clothes. Pale skin. Easily angered.”

“What?” I snap. “I am not!”

The dracid flinches. “Oh, sorry, that must have been a mistake. But you are Sal, aren’t you?”

I exchange a suspicious glance with Cyros. He seems as baffled as me.

“Who’s asking?”

The dracid wrings her hands. “Well, um, my name is Piria. Has Lisari mentioned me?”

I freeze, a chill going through me at the name. “How do you know her?”

Piria attempts to smile, but it more comes out as a grimace of fear. “She’s my god. Well, ours, I suppose. I’m her champion, you see.”

Piria, Champion of Chaos, offers out her hand. “Nice to meet you!”
