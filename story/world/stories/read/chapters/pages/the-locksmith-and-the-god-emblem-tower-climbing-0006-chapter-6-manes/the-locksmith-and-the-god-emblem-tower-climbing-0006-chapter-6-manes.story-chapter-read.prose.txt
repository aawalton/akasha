Who would've thought that cabins surrounding a gigantic flame and a church at the end would house crazy cultists? Well, Harry certainly didn't think about that.

"Who are they pleading to?" The Locksmith asked as the group hid in the shadows. "The flame?"

"...No." Brun whispered in reply, watching a few figures dancing around the fire. "I think it's the Tower..."

The Logos Tower, although being a fairy tale for most people, held a significant number of devotees. Many believe that the Astronomer had, unwittingly, found a creature or a being that hid from the universe. The story goes that the discovery had unleashed upon the world a great many disasters, with the Emblems being the last of the catastrophes. Either way, the faith on the Tower led the government to hide away most of the information about the subject, which in turn made the story even more believable.

Following this faith, it's clear a few of them would plead to the tower whilst inside of it; for the devotees, it was a dream come true.

"Why are the cabins shaped like that?" Harry asked, again. "Look, I'm getting freaked out by all that, okay? I've made a promise to myself to never rob a cult before."

"What?" Prism turned to him, curious. "Why? Did you accidentally end up worshiping it?"

"They get crazy, okay?" The young thief replied, looking around while scratching his head. "...And the prize isn't that worth it anyways."

"Oh, that makes sense."

"What? Are you going to judge me now? I never said I loved robbing. But it had to be done—"

"The fire sure seems worth the devotion." Vela said, scaring the group with the volume of his voice.

"Vela!" Brun grabbed his shoulder. "Shit... Don't say it like that..."

"You know what?" Prism got up, much to Harry and Brun's displeasure. "Let's separate. I can go around and check on the individual cabins. With my Emblem, they won't be able to detect me."

"No way I'm letting you go on your own!" Brun still said in a low voice. The old woman then sighed and added. "I'll come with you. You said earlier that you can cover both of us, right?"

"What?" Harry also got up. "What about me and Vela?"

"You two can go on the right side," Brun said. "Me and Prism can check upon those on the left, and if we don't find them, we can converge on the church. My bet is that they'll be there, if they're still alive. Still, we need to be sure."

As much as Harry hated to admit, it was logical. Harry and Vela alone could probably hold their ground for a while and even win most battles because of the versatility of their Emblems. And even though the other two didn't have any defensive or offensive Emblems, Prism's invisibility and blinding would prove to be disorientating enough for even the toughest enemies. He just had to keep Vela away from the flame.

"...Fuckin' hell." He adjusted his tie. "Okay then. Vela, are you with me?"

Blinking rapidly, the candle man then looked at Harry and gave him a reassuring smile. "Of course, Mr. Locksmith. I just get carried away by bright lights."

"Are you a moth or something?" Harry asked with a chuckle.

"I just don't like being in the dark." Vela said in a calm demeanor, but Harry could feel the weight of his words. "That's all."

"...I got you." Harry patted his shoulder as they each said goodbye and parted their ways to the cabins, hoping not to catch the attention of the hooded figures in the center.

Harry then conjured a door and quickly went through it. Jumping dozens of meters, the Locksmith was happily surprised when he saw that Vela could keep up with him, even in the night.

Stopping amidst bushes, near the first cabin, he turned to Vela. "Are you sure you aren't feeling nauseated or just feeling bad? With the doors and changes of directions, I mean."

“All set, Mr. Locksmith.” Vela answered calmly, “During my time in the monastery, I worked with many balance and resistance exercises against sickness.”

“Monastery?” Harry asked, frowning, “You were a monk?”

"I was many things..." Vela replied with a weak smile. "But yes, I was also a monk."

"Shit..." The Locksmith leaned his back against a tree trunk. "Took me a couple months to understand what I could do and a few years to master it. You would think the hardest part would be the multitasking, but it wasn't. It was the free fall."

"...You're afraid of heights?" The former monk said. "Doesn't seem like a small fear for an acrobat."

"You damn right it isn't..." He chuckled. "But you keep on living, right? You face your fears long enough that they turn into obstacles; simple hurdles you can jump through."

“You’ve been doing this for a long time, haven’t you?” Vela replied. “I could tell by your movement.”

“Ah, yes…” He laughed embarrassed, “Anyone can do it, to be honest. It’s even hard to imagine how I moved before.”

“You must like it, I imagine.” The former monk said with a smile.

"What's weird was that, after a few years, that fear turned into an addiction. It was harder for me to resist the feeling than give in to it. It's so... liberating, you know? For a few, brief moments, you feel like the wind; just gliding through the capital in search of yourself. In the end, that's what we're all doing, right? Just chasing identity."

Before replying, Harry looked up and saw himself under the presence of the stars. Confident in their light and indifferent about the universe; they shone for their spectators but could shine twice as much even if they were alone.

How much had he already told Vela? Perhaps it was because of the prospects of the mission, with the odds being against their favor, but Harry felt compelled to tell someone, anyone, about his life. At the same time, though, as he stargazed, he felt vulnerable; weak. The only person he would regularly interact with was Madeline, a thief whose relationship with him was built on rivalry. Even Matthews, Harry's brother, was out of reach.

In his experience, sharing this much wasn't practical nor wise. But Harry would be lying if he said he didn't appreciate the conversation.

“I did not mean for you to share this much.” Vela gave a small laugh, sensing Harry's discomfort. “We are here on a mission, not to question our souls. Introspection has no place in a world that only views results, yes?”

Harry then regained a smile and said with excitement, “Of course, Vela. Anyway, we need first to see the insides of these cabins. If we're lucky enough, we can hit the jackpot.”

“Right.”

Let’s do a quick sweep and get out of here as fast as possible. I would hate to run into a group full of people with Emblems or even those cultists. What madness…

Still crouched, they slowly approached one of the cabins. Scanning the surroundings, they noticed zero signs of guards or traps. Were they really that confident, or was the lack of traps itself a trap? Thoughts like these sprouted not only in Harry, but also in Vela.

The strong smell of alcohol was already apparent as they leaned on the horizontal logs of the structure. Lowering themselves beneath the windows, everything was surprisingly quiet around them, even inside the cabin. Also, for some reason, it looked like the windows were barricaded from the inside, only a sliver of vision slipping from one of the windows.

According to Harry's experience, there should be guards in each of the entrances or at least inside the structures, but the sound of heavy metal was nonexistent; even the smallest movement would already produce a metallic noise.

“Get ready.” Harry whispered, “I’m going to open a passage through the view I have from the window, which would be on top of that bed, see? I’ll connect that passage with a door in front of you, and, without making noise, you’re going to open it and throw a jet of fire. Can you control the power so you don’t burn the cabin completely?”

"Of course. Although I can't guarantee the flame won't scare away anyone inside the cabin."

"It's fine; the mother flame is far enough that we can take out the person before it makes a scandal."

With a view from the window, Harry generated a passage inside and connected the two doors. Vela, therefore, pointed his candelabrum the same way a warrior points his spear, and then stoked the flames that sat on the tips of the candles. As if the fire itself obeyed his orders, not a single extra spark was thrown and the wood remained intact, only the glow bathing the room.

“…I don’t see anything, Mr. Locksmith.” He said to the young man, “Perhaps we can go inside and take a closer look—”

Pulled like a fish on a hook, Vela was sucked through the door by a strip darker than the night. His fire, in the same way, was swallowed by a darkness as if the very concept of gloom rested inside the cabin.

Thinking fast, Harry grabbed his hand. Desperately pushing him to the outside, it appeared as the stripe was stronger. Pushed along by the shadows that engulfed the cabin, the young thief fell alongside Vela and saw dense strips that covered his mouth and nose. Suddenly then, they quickly dispersed as soon as the Locksmith fell in with the former monk. No. It wasn't out of nowhere; Vela had unconsciously activated his Emblem, scaring the dark away.

The shadows are afraid of it...!

“Vela, are you okay!?” Harry supported him while Vela grabbed his candelabrum, “Use the fire, it repels it!”

Without answering, he stood up and increased the flames of his candelabrum. A demonic scream then reverberated inside the cabin as some black strips returned to the darkness. Vela kept pushing the light toward the corners, but the creature was faster and jumped between the shadows faster than the flames.

“What the fuck is that?” Threatened, the Locksmith turned, searching for the living shadow, “I imagined the demons that lived in the tower were also metaphorical! This thing—”

“Not a thing,” A distorted voice came from one of the shadows, “I have a name, you know?”

When Vela turned to illuminate the shadow, he saw the strips gathering to form a humanoid figure, dark as a black hole. Its white eyes added contrast to its body while deformed strips still followed beneath its feet.

“I’m Manes,” the figure spoke again, “And I was fucking dreaming.”
