They ended up making three new machines, and changed enough about the ironrollers that it probably counted as a fourth.

The first was what Alyssa insisted on calling The Crushy. It was a... simple was the wrong word for it, but it was a straightforward rock crusher. Large stones, either Jungle Rock or, far more commonly, the tremendous amount of slag they had building up around their Volcanic Crucible, was fed into the top of a steel box. Inside the steel box, it fell into what could best be described as a funnel made of two rough steel plates. One steel plate was fixed entirely in place, while the other was attached to a fairly hefty flywheel slightly off-angle, and as a result effectively wiggled in place. Not that the wiggling was to be underestimated. The Crushy was powered by a clockwork heart nearly as big as the train's, and it was kind of intimidating to see Jacob toss a boulder twice his own size into the mouth of the machine and then watch it just be... broken down, as the two steel plates pushed together.

The bottom of the 'funnel' was open to about the size they wanted their gravel, so once the rocks were properly crushed, they landed on a motion slide conveyor belt that carried them up and away from the intake box, dumping them in a gravel pile that initially spilled half outside First Tower until Oliver managed to extend the wards to include it fully. He'd also created a new type of gripper for his spider-bots, and started making their method of interaction modular, such that new bots could be easily outfitted with either grippy claws, or with a metal scoop that would have ironically made digging up dirt to insert railroad ties quite a bit easier, had they gone that route.

They'd also reworked the ironrollers into a contraption that only required a steel beam to pass through exactly once to turn it into railway track, and cut the output rate from once per twenty minutes to once per five, and that was before a ton of optimizations they knew they could make. Their steel beam production would be their new bottleneck before long, but that wasn't going to improve until the Factory train line to Ironworks was complete.

Which would be...

Right now, they had eight transport carts making the trek from Ironworks to First Tower, each managing about one delivery per day of roughly five thousand kilos of hematite. Those were then refined to three thousand kilos of steel in their Volcanic Crucible, which was a lot, but even twenty four thousand kilos of steel per day vanished quite quickly when each one of their steel beams, corresponding to a single length of track, weighed a hundred and sixty kilos. So at full tilt, they could produce up to two hundred and fifty steel beams per day, corresponding to five hundred individual tracks, and with each track being four meters long, cut in half because they needed two per section, that was a maximum of five hundred meters of rail per day, or a minimum of three to four weeks of production.

It would probably take longer than that, because the tracks weren't the only thing they were using iron and steel for, but a month wasn't bad at all. It also had the possibility of taking less time, thanks to their stockpiles of hematite, but... a month probably wasn't the worst estimate for the track-laying itself. Bridges would complicate that, though.

In any case, at the end of the ironrollers was their second new machine, a new drop hammer. This one had a slot cut into its striking face, where a clockwork arm could insert a railway spike - currently being made in the Metalpress, but Henrietta had her eye on making a dedicated machine for its production - that would be rammed into glowing-hot metal with tremendous force at very predictable intervals.

Except for when the machine got off-track, of course. Then anything could happen, but all of those paths ended with the steel in question being tossed back into the fires whence it was forged to destroy it. The track that was recognizable as such was spit out rather unceremoniously next to the First Forge, where some spider-bots would pick it up and bring it to a holding area where it could cool, then be loaded into the Factory train when the traincar was back in the station.

The railway ties themselves were being made by an adjusted Lumbermill, which had gotten a second set of saws installed to allow for cutting square beams fifteen centimeters to a side instead of just flat boards. The wood was still dried all the same in the wood kiln, though Jacob had needed to adjust the intensity of the heating enchantment there to ensure the outside didn't scorch while the inside was still wet. He'd managed to do so just by tweaking some of the gearing ratios, which Henrietta found to be a tremendous sign that they were heading in a less Oliver-dependent direction.

Once dried, the beams were fed into the third new machine - a chop saw - which cut off the beams into two-meter lengths, while simultaneously drilling holes for the railway spikes to fill. They weren't worried about holding the railroad down, after all. It just needed to not move side to side.

All in all... having the Metalpress was absolutely fantastic, and the spider-bots were getting better and better at automatically moving parts and materials from one location to the next. Where once they needed to spend weeks hand-making every single gear, every single rod, every single plate needed for any contraption, now they just needed to ask Oliver to make some designs and send his familiar to the Metalpress for an hour or two, and then they had everything they'd need to make something new.

Of course, there were still plenty of problems to be solved. Issues with the road being too high of a grade in some places, with spider-bots grabbing important pieces and running into the river for no apparent reason, with running eternally short on clockwork hearts to make... and they hadn't even gotten to their first bridge with the Factory train.

But hey, they were really going places now.
