Don gave the satchel from Matthew's corpse to Charlie. The belt was bloody and the girl took it gingerly. “What do you want me to do with this?”

“They’re useful. You can store food or potions you find,” Don said.

“Oh…” Charlie said. “How come you’re so good at this?”

Don chuckled. “That’s just common sense.”

“No I mean like this whole thing,” Charlie said as they walked through the forest.

Don didn’t answer immediately. The sun was rising, it was a beautiful start of a morning. Don looked at it wistfully and smiled. He didn’t remember when he had last enjoyed a sunrise this much. He was tired and his muscles ached. Despite his improved physique, he would need rest soon.

Additionally, for some reason his Survival Instincts were acting up. There was no imminent danger, but it felt like it was closing in. Don looked around in the forest but saw no movement. He could do nothing but stay watchful. Don looked at the blonde girl. She was still shocked, but her eyes looked like they were more and more focused.

“You’re in high school right?” Don asked.

“Yeah?”

“You know when you just don’t question what the teachers make you do?”

“I do,” Charlie said and smiled. “They’re assholes half the time.”

Don laughed. “They are, but that’s not what I mean. I mean you don’t question having to learn English or Biology. You just attend the classes.”

“Yeah, I guess I do. Or I did…”

“It’s like that. We got a new teacher, the System. And it sure as hell is an asshole. But I just decided to play by its rules.”

“What rules are those?” Charlie asked.

“Kill or be killed,” Don said quietly.

“I’m not sure I can play that game,” she said after a pause. “I think not everyone can be a fighter. That’s why I’m happy there’s the camp. You’ll join right? You’ll fight for us?”

“We’ll see. But I do want to see this camp of yours.”

“Yeah,” Charlie said, a bit more energized.

They walked for a bit, but something kept bothering Don. It was his Survival Instincts. Don focused on the feeling, trying to understand it. Then he realized— something was following them. He darted to the side and crouched behind a tree.

“Get to cover,” Don hissed.

Charlie went behind a boulder and they waited. Soon a young man in a school uniform emerged from the bushes. He was wearing loose jeans and a black hoodie and a white cap.

“Charlie that you?” he called in an unsure voice.

“Jimmy?” Charlie called back.

“Yeah,” Jimmy said. “You okay?”

“You asshole!” Charlie hissed. “You left us!”

“What was I supposed to do?” Jimmy called as he came into view. He spread his hands and scowled.

“Oh, I don’t know dude,” Charlie said as she got up. “Get help?”

“Well you got some,” Jimmy said and gestured at Don.

“Yeah?” Charlie said and drew in a breath. “Michael, your friend, is dead.”

Jimmy’s indignance deflated like a popped balloon. “Shit. That’s crazy… no cap.”

“Uhhuh,” Charlie muttered.

“You two can continue bickering while we move towards the camp,” Don said.

“Yeah, right,” Charlie said and nodded. “This way.”

“Who’s you?” Jimmy asked as he caught up to them.

“Name’s Don,” Don said without looking at the kid.

“I’m Jimmy. Kinda fucked up what’s happening isn’t it? I figure it’s like a story or a game. Lot less fun when it’s really happening though. But fortunately there’s strong people like you. How did you beat that boar?”

Don gave a suffering look at the kid. He was smiling brightly, like his friend hadn’t just died and like this was some kind of school excursion. Jimmy didn’t take the hint. He just continued.

“You saw like every animal turned into monsters? That boar must have been like a regular old pig or something. You know it's pretty fire that you could fight it. Are you like high level or something? Did you see other people die? There’s a lot of dead people. Did you cap anyone yet?”

“Jimmy,” Charlie muttered. “Stop it.”

“What?” Jimmy said. “I’m just trying to brighten the mood. Right, Don?”

“Kid,” Don said quietly. “Just shut up.”

“Right, right,” Jimmy said.

For maybe twenty seconds, Don could just enjoy the nice stroll in the woods. His Survival Instincts were still pestering him. A quiet red throb in the background of his mind. He was keeping an alert eye for any monsters. If they faced any, he would have to avoid using his claws. As long as they didn’t run into a monster-boar or a monster-bear, Don figured it’d be fine. Soon enough Jimmy started talking again.

“What’s up with this System though? Like what is it? Why is it doing all this? What is this all for?”

“Doesn’t matter, kid,” Don muttered.

“I wanna know. How am I supposed to go to sleep at night if I don’t know what is this shit. Is it like a simulation or something?”

“Who cares, Jimmy,” Charlie said. “Don’s right. It doesn’t matter.”

“How can you not care? It’s like THE question or something.”

“Look,” Don said. “The Earth we had? Might have been a simulation or a dream or whatever just the same. Maybe it was and this is the real reality.”

“Whoah dude,” Jimmy said. “That’s like super deep and shit.”

“Point is that it doesn’t matter. You just have to assume things are what they seem. And what it seems like is that we have to get levels. Survive and get stronger.”

“Right, right,” Jimmy said. “I’m level 3, yo. But it’s scary to fight.”

“I think it’s scarier to not be able to fight,” Don said simply.

“Word…” Jimmy said and fell into a blessed silence.

The silence lingered. Only sound Don could hear was the tweet of birds in the cool dawn. But something was off. It was too quiet. Or the quietness had a heavy quality to it that just felt wrong. He couldn’t explain it. It was a feeling. Then suddenly it snapped into place. Don’s Survival Instincts flared up like a red hot iron in his mind. Danger. Immediate danger. Without even thinking, he tackled Charlie to the ground.

"What the—" she started, but her words were cut short by the whistling sound of an arrow flying through the air where she had been standing a moment ago.

THOCK!

The arrow bit into a nearby pine.

"Run!" Don shouted, pulling Charlie to her feet.

They took off, weaving through the trees. Jimmy was right behind them, his eyes wide with terror. Don's mind was racing. Another arrow flew past his head and hit the nearby tree, sending bits of bark exploding. It was that psycho. The Hunter.

Another arrow whistled through the air. Don heard Charlie scream. She tumbled. Don turned to see her clutching her leg, an arrow protruding through her thigh. Blood was already soaking her tattered uniform dress.

"Keep going!" Don shouted at Jimmy, who had frozen in place. Don scooped Charlie up in his arms. Her weight was nothing to him with his enhanced attributes.

“Bro…” Jimmy said, still standing still and staring at Charlie’s wounded leg.

Don pushed the kid down just as an arrow flew at him. Then he ripped him up and pushed him forward.

“MOVE!” Don shouted. “Towards the camp!”

"I can't—" Charlie sobbed, her face contorted in pain.

"You'll be fine," Don said through gritted teeth. "Just hold on."

He sprinted forward, Jimmy scrambling to keep up. Don was faced with a cruel reality. He didn’t have to outrun the Hunter. He just had to outrun Jimmy.

The red sense of danger was still blaring in his mind. The Hunter was pursuing them, moving through the forest with uncanny silence. Only a faint twang could be heard before an arrow shot at them. The Hunter had a Stealth skill too. So that’s how the System worked when you were on the receiving end…

"How far to camp?" Don hissed.

"Half a mile," Charlie gasped, her face pale. "That way."

Don adjusted his course, weaving between trees. He sped up, leveraging his Sprinting skill. He heard Jimmy's ragged breathing behind him.

"Dude, I can't—" Jimmy wheezed.

"Keep going!" Don snapped. He knew the kid wouldn’t make it.

Don kept sprinting as hard as he could. He wasn’t quite at his top speed, with Charlie in his lap, but he was still speeding forward faster than any athlete.

[General Skill increased: Sprinting!]

[Sprinting Level 4]

Another arrow whistled through the air. Don heard a sickening thud behind him, followed by a gurgling sound that made Don turn. Jimmy stood frozen, an arrow protruding from his throat. Blood bubbled from his lips as he tried to speak. Then his eyes rolled back, and he collapsed.

"Jimmy!" Charlie screamed.

Don turned and kept running.

"We can't help him," Don said, pushing himself to move faster. The camp had to be close.

Sharp pain exploded in Don's shoulder as an arrow punched through his flesh.

[Health at 86%][Energy at 63%][Debuff inflicted: Mild bleeding]

Don stumbled but didn't fall. His vision blurred momentarily from the pain, but he pushed through it. Charlie was crying against his chest, her hands gripping his torn shirt.

"We're almost there," Don said, though he wasn't sure. The arrow in his shoulder sent waves of burning pain down his arm with each step.

Then he saw it. A clearing ahead. Makeshift barriers of branches and rocks. People moving about.

"There! That's it!" Charlie cried.

Another arrow struck. This one pierced Don’s leg.

[Health at 66%][Energy at 49%][Debuff inflicted: Moderate bleeding]

Don tumbled and Charlie fell with a yelp. He quickly picked her up and hoisted her on his shoulder. He gritted his teeth and kept running. His strength was waning and his Energy was falling rapidly. Now it was at 40%.

Don sprinted the last hundred yards, his legs burning with exertion. The pain in his shoulder and leg was flaring up. His Survival Instincts were screaming. Behind him, he heard movement in the brush. The Hunter was still following.

"Help!" Charlie screamed. "Let us in!"

People at the barrier turned. There was shouting. Faces appeared, looking out at them with a mixture of fear and concern.

"Open up! He's hurt!" Charlie yelled.

Don glanced over his shoulder. A figure in green was standing at the edge of the trees, bow in hand. Their eyes met across the distance. The Hunter nocked another arrow, aimed... then lowered his bow. He nodded once at Don, a small smile playing on his lips. A promise that this wasn't over.

Then he melted back into the forest.

Don stumbled through the opening in the barrier as people pulled them inside. He gently set Charlie down and then just collapsed on his hands and knees as his lungs fought for air.

"What happened?" a tall man asked, looking at the arrows lodged in Don.

"The Hunter," Don said, panting on the ground. “He hunts people.”

The tall man nodded grimly. “I know who you’re talking about.”

Then he got up and shouted at the camp. “Fighters! Secure the perimeter. Do NOT go out wandering about. We stay huddled inside.”

Don chugged down a healing potion. It healed most of the damage. His leg and shoulder were still wounded, but this was manageable. He emptied one of his waterskins and saw the tall guy look at his stash of potions with interest.

“You look like you could use a rest, friend,” the tall man said and offered a hand to pull Don up.

Don grabbed it and hid a wince.

“I’m Vance,” the tall man said. He was handsome with a streak of gray in his black hair and trimmed beard. He was wearing a loose sweater that hid bulging muscles. “Thank you for bringing her back to safety. What about the other two?”

“Dead,” Charlie said as she clutched her leg.

“Here,” Don said and gave her a healing potion. Vance’s eyes followed the transaction.

“Thanks…” Charlie said. She chugged it down and the wound in her thigh started knitting together.

“Please take him in, chief,” Charlie then said, looking up at Vance. “He can fight. He’s really good. We need fighters, right?”

“We do,” Vance said, looking Don up and down. “If they can behave…”

Don gave him a tired smile. “I’ll try to.”

Vance laughed and clapped him on shoulder. “Good man. You look like you could use a rest. There’s a little cot with some grass and tattered clothes as bedding. Go get some shut-eye. We’ll talk business once you wake up.”

“Don’t mind if I do,” Don muttered. He thanked the man and told Charlie he’d talk to her later. For now he could really use some rest. It had been a hell of a day and night to boot.
