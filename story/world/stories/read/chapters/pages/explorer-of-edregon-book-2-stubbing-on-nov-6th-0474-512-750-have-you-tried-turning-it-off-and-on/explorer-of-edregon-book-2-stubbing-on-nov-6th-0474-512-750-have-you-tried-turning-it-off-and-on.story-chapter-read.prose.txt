Despite the fact that Vin confirmed rather quickly that the beastkin Druids didn’t have any magic to offer him that he couldn’t just learn from Shia or any of the other Druids of the Sacred Forest, getting to watch them work as a unit to regrow an entire swath of the forest was an incredible sight. Once the beastkin’s God settled down for the evening and brought them all closer to the ground, the Druids got to work.

Like an elite combat squad rushing off to battle, the robed beastkin slid down the side of their God before leaping and rolling as they hit the ground. From there, they split up into different groups. Half of them made their way back over to where the forest had been devoured and got to work smoothing over upturned dirt and filling in holes, while the other half ran over to the largest pile of droppings Vin had seen in his life and got to work collecting it in large wheelbarrows to transport it. After that, the teams met back up. Fertilizer was spread around the forest floor, fallen organic matter from the God’s meal was collected and rapidly composted through some new nature spell Vin hadn’t seen before, and seeds were scattered and imbued with magic to cause them to erupt into new tree growth in the blink of an eye. In less than an hour, the small army of Druids had completely regrown the eaten forest, before retreating to the side of their God and scrambling their way back onto its back using its rough skin for handholds.

Vin had watched the entire process from up high, sitting on one of his clouds as he followed along with the many different steps. He couldn’t shake the feeling like he’d just witnessed the magical equivalent of a racing pit crew leap into action, with each person knowing exactly what they needed to do and having shaved mere seconds off their total time through decades of practice. Once it was all over and the Druids had returned to God’s back, Vin cloud stepped back himself, shaking his head as he wondered if Terra would ever have any squads of people capable of moving with that level of teamwork.

With Alka out and exploring God’s back, he had been left alone in what he’d come to learn was functionally Fritz’s storage closet. Despite space being a premium up on God’s back, as God’s Tender, the beastkin was allotted certain privileges that few others got to enjoy, such as a building just to house all his stuff related to carrying out his duties. Still, Vin had slept in far worse locations over the years spent wandering around back on Earth, and he was simply happy to have a roof over his head for the time being.

As curious as he was to learn more about these people and their society, he’d taken one look at the throng of people waiting for him or Alka to appear again before deciding he just wasn’t up for that at the moment. One of the best parts about adventuring with Shia was that she functioned wonderfully as a social magnet, always happy to gather people up and somehow managing to befriend people everywhere they went. Vin was not the biggest fan of crowds even when he wasn’t the center of attention, so once the Druids finished up with their cycle of regrowth, he decided to spend the rest of his evening working on his magic within Fritz’ storage closet.

He had a couple of spells he was currently working on, and he wanted to finish up at least one of them before heading back to Terra.

Lumel probably already met with the Airwalker, which means I really need to put the pedal to the metal and get this one figured out, he decided, turning his focus back to the spell he was designing with the eventual trip back to Lumel’s fragment in mind. Ovord had given him the idea originally, but Vin had long since made it his own.

Now he just needed to make it a reality.

Sinking into Introspection, he nodded toward Ovord who was off meditating to the side before he focused on the task at hand. He’d already spent a significant number of hours working on this spell here and there, both while he’d been awake and asleep, and he had the feeling he was close. Provided he didn’t trigger any additional runic backlashes, one more lengthy session of spell work was probably all he needed.

Vin let his already iffy sense of time drift away as he worked to crack his latest tier-3 spell and finish up the runic formation. Combining Aqua Sphere, Create Air, and Magic Lock all into one spell was no easy feat, but he was far more skilled than he’d been even just a few weeks ago. Runes blurred by one after another as he worked and tweaked the connections here and there, venting minor runic backlashes all the while and relying on Spell Builder to keep him from doing anything too stupid in his eagerness to finish the spell.

Just as he was debating whether or not he should take a break and check to see how much time had passed, everything clicked. Vin blinked and took a step back, letting out a bark of laughter while he took in the glowing runic formation before him.

“Very impressive,” Ovord said from behind him, causing him to flinch when he remembered yet again that he wasn’t alone within his mana pool any longer. “I believe that spell will do exactly what you need it to during your future travels.”

“That was sort of the idea,” Vin said, eager to leave Introspection and look at his newest notifications. “Do you need something?”

“I just thought it wise to suggest we take a look at your vassal’s unique soul scenario before wasting my three minutes for the day,” Ovord said calmly.

“We already did that this morning,” Vin said, before realizing what Ovord was getting at. “...It’s tomorrow, isn’t it? How much time has passed?”

“You were working on your spell for a little over a full day,” Ovord confirmed. “It is now late evening, if my count is correct.”

“Of course it is,” Vin sighed, before shaking his head. “No. You know what? That was a day well spent. Tier-3 spells aren’t exactly easy, and I’ve been working on finishing this one for some time. If anything I’m more surprised Fritz didn’t kick me out of his supply room.”

“Perhaps he knows that moving a mage while they are meditating can be quite dangerous,” Ovord offered. “Regardless, your vassal?”

“She’s not my vassal,” Vin drawled. “Though we should get you those three minutes before they reset. Give me a minute.”

“I am not planning on going anywhere,” Ovord joked, and Vin snorted as he opened his eyes.

New spell learned! Tier-3 Air Spell (Air Prison). 15,000 exp gained.

Level up! Adventurer of the Arcane lvl 57!

+3 Attribute points to spend.

“Air Prison?” Vin muttered, frowning at the name of his new spell. While that was technically a fairly apt description, he couldn’t say he was that big on the name the System had gone with for it. Unfortunately there wasn’t much he could do about that. Shaking his head, he tossed his new attribute points into focus, clicking his tongue at the realization his focus was now sitting at a tantalizing ninety-nine. On the plus side that meant he wouldn’t have to devote any more of his free points to it as it would naturally tick up to one hundred upon hitting level 58, but on the down side, he’d have to hold onto his passive point for an entire additional level just to see what was offered.

He had debated whether or not he should just hit up the floating library again and see if he couldn’t find the answers there for himself, but he’d decided against it in the end. There was no telling what the Gods had chosen to change with the creation of their new System, and he didn’t want to take the chance and miss out on something amazing while going off of outdated information.

One of the side benefits of spending an entire day working on his magic was that the throng of beastkin outside of the storage room were no more, no doubt having grown tired of waiting for him to emerge after the first couple of hours. Based on the lack of swaying of the God underfoot, the magical beast had already settled down for the night, and Vin took advantage of the darkness to trigger his newest artifact and fly up into the air. A cast of Sense Magic located Alka, and he rolled his eyes as he cloud stepped over to her. He could have continued flying, but after spending a full day sitting cross legged, it didn’t hurt to stretch a bit.

“I’m surprised Fritz hasn’t kicked you off their God yet,” Vin admitted, touching down beside Alka on the top of the giant beast’s head. “They seriously don’t mind you being out here?”

“Nope. Apparently, whatever creatures these things are, their head and back are ridiculously thick. It’s how they can put up buildings and foundations without hurting the magical beast. From what I gathered, it sounds like you have to go in through the underbelly to truly hurt them, so chilling on the head is completely fine.”

“If you say so,” Vin said, before turning to smile at the small, slime-based replica of himself. “Hi, Epli. How’s it going?”

“Good. Happy,” Epli said, waving at him with his own golem hand as it returned his smile with his own face.

“Ovord wants to take a look at your soul again,” Vin said. He ignored Alka’s groan when the Slayer flopped down onto her back in response.

“It’s been forever and he still hasn’t even done anything! This is the worst!”

“Alka, it’s been four days. You’re telling me you’d seriously rather he just dive in there and experiment with your soul of all things rather than take his time? I know you’re impatient, but this is ridiculous.”

“You’re right,” Alka sighed, motioning for him to get on with it. “Let’s do this thing.”

With a nod, Vin made sure Ovord knew what was about to happen before turning control over to the soul mage. Just like he had the last four days, Ovord spent his first two minutes doing nothing more than staring at Alka’s core, not even blinking as he looked at something only he could see with Vin’s Beyond the Veil. But as the third minute began to tick away, the former Arch Mage did something entirely new.

His finger turned ethereal, like that of Alka’s former ghost body. Before Vin could even ask what he was doing, Ovord leaned forward and carefully poked into the center of Alka’s golem body.

Alka’s eyes flared a brilliant, powerful green that blasted light up into the night sky like a beacon as she gasped, and her body began shaking uncontrollably. Seconds passed and the shaking showed no sign of stopping, and Ovord hummed with Vin’s body.

“I see. I was afraid of that.”

“Afraid of what?! What did you do to her?!”

“I do not know,” Ovord said calmly, peering more closely at Alka’s core even as she continued shaking. “Do not fret. I am skilled enough to undo what I did. Here.”

As promised, with only a few seconds of control left to him, Ovord tapped a finger into Alka’s core once again, and her body went limp while her eyes dimmed almost to nothing. Slowly, they built back up to their regular state, and Alka groaned as she held a hand against her head.

“What the hell… I thought I wasn’t supposed to be able to feel pain any more.”

“Alka, are you alright?” Vin asked, blinking when he found himself in control of his body once more.

“Yeah… I’m okay,” she said. Her voice quivered slightly, and she reached out to scoop Epli up, allowing the schlime to flow back into her helmet. “Maybe you were right about the whole not rushing things. Let’s… Let’s take our time with this.”

Before Vin could say anything, Alka turned and walked back toward the town, her eyes more dim than usual as she held her arms against her chest. Vin waited for her to get further away before he frowned and looked down at his own chest.

“What happened?”

“The first step of many,” Ovord answered, the soul mage not sounding put out in the least. “I am the greatest soul mage from my own world, but I am still not on the level of the Gods. Your Beyond the Veil provides me with the ability to see their great workings, but understanding it is something that is going to take time. Along with much trial and error. Do not fret. As I have already demonstrated, I have the ability to undo any mistakes I make. We will figure this out eventually.”

“Eventually isn’t good enough. And you saw how impatient Alka is,” Vin said, watching her walk off. “Now that she knows this is a possibility, she’s desperate to get her class and levels back.”

“Well, unless you have someone skilled with deciphering the Gods’ great workings of magic who is willing to assist us, your vassal will have to learn patience. Otherwise trial and error is all we have.”

“Someone who can decipher divine magic?” Vin repeated. A smile slowly stretched across his face, and he nodded. “You know what, I think you might be in luck.”
