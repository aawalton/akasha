Duvas watched another loaded wheelbarrow pass through the gate.

"The smokehouse finished its previous batch of meat yesterday, and we have already transferred it to the storeroom in the manor,” the majordomo said. "That was fortunate. Even empty, however, the smokehouse won't hold all the usable meat from five massive beasts. The hunters will also continue bringing in their usual catches. We are already expecting Yufim’s hunting group to return by evening, and his group usually brings the largest amount of meat among those five groups.”

"We will cook whatever can't be salted or smoked in time," Kivamus said. "Send it to the manor and each of the four blocks."

"I have already arranged for that," Duvas replied, anticipating his orders in advance as usual. "A sizable amount will be used for lunch. The news has spread about it and the villagers are planning another gathering in the market block this evening. Apparently, eating a delicacy usually served to nobles deserves its own celebration."

Kivamus chuckled.

After the fear of the past few nights, he had no objection to a second celebration so soon.

Duvas continued watching the meat being carried away. "But the cured adzee meat would be a different matter. We shouldn't consume that simply because we have it. Once properly salted and smoked, the better cuts will remain usable for months, and hunters are already bringing in good amounts to meet the village’s needs. We could keep enough smoked meat for the village’s emergency food reserves and sell the remainder in Cinran."

Trevalo turned toward him with immediate interest. "Adzee meat sells easily there. Nobles, wealthy merchants, and the better inns will all pay well for it, especially if it has been smoked properly. I could carry a few barrels of smoked meat with each caravan without taking meaningful space away from the coal. Or if you want, I can dedicate a full wagon to it next week, since it should be cured by then.”

“No, small amounts would be better," Duvas said. "Sending too much at once would encourage people to ask how Tiranat managed to kill so many adzees together. A barrel or two each week would look like the result of ordinary hunting or the occasional beast coming too close to the village. We can continue selling the full adzee meat stock slowly for a few months at that rate. That wouldn't raise questions we aren’t ready to answer.”

“That makes sense.” Trevalo nodded. "I know several buyers who won't ask where each cut came from as long as the quality is good. The nobles of Cinran, as well as those visiting the town for other purposes—like other barons in the domain of the Count—will buy it easily to give feasts in their own villages and manors to boast of their hunting prowess.”

Hudan snorted. “Typical nobles. Taking credit for others’ work. I don't think they'll be giving it to the commoners, like we are doing. Probably just their extended families, if that.”

Trevalo just shrugged in response. “Does it even matter what they do with the meat as long as they pay for it? Anyway, selling it gradually will also prevent the price from dropping."

Kivamus looked at the majordomo. "Then work out how much the village can spare after the smoking is done. Plan to fully stock up our emergency food stores with this bounty—I want to keep our reserves full whenever possible—but we can keep replacing it with the regular meat from hunting parties after it is cured, and remove the adzee meat for selling. Trevalo can sell it in small amounts every week."

Duvas inclined his head. "I will handle it."

Trevalo smiled. "And I will find the buyers."

"Come to the manor for lunch," Kivamus told him. "We will be serving some of the fresh meat, which will taste much better than the cured meat. You should also know what you'll be selling."

Trevalo's smile widened. "I would be glad to."

Another wheelbarrow passed them, piled with cuts destined for the smokehouse.

The smokehouse was close enough that Kivamus could see workers carrying the first prepared pieces inside. Others rubbed salt over meat that would have to wait for hanging space.

But even with all the manpower Duvas had provided, the work would continue for the rest of the day.

By late night, all usable meat would be cut, salted, cooked, or placed inside the smokehouse. The smoking itself would continue for the next few days.

There would still be some waste though, especially if Yufim returned with a large amount of meat today.

The current smokehouse hadn't been built to process five enormous beasts at once while also handling the meat normally brought back by the hunters. Some cuts would have to be cooked immediately simply because there was nowhere else to preserve them. The village will be having a real adzee-meat feast today.

Kivamus watched the workers lifting another cut onto a wheelbarrow.

"We need a second smokehouse built soon," he said. “An attack by a full pack of adzees is rare, but we’ll probably have to send more hunting parties in the coming months.”

Duvas looked toward him. "We can't build anything else right now—all three construction teams are all occupied these days. Finn has already started on the first tenement, Taniok is still working on…” He glanced at the merchant. “That project, and the third team is building the barracks.”

"I know. Once one of them finishes its current assignment, have them build another smokehouse in the southeast of the village."

"Good idea. That would also protect our stores if one smokehouse caught fire or was damaged during an attack," Duvas said.

"Exactly."

The population was growing every month. Hunting, livestock, and trade would grow with it. He knew that even if they sent ten times more of the current number of hunting parties, there would still be no shortage of game, since the southern forests were huge, and apart from Tiranat, there was no other place which hunted meat in this area. Kirnos was the only other sizable settlement in this region, and they mainly relied on fishing from the ocean.

That meant the village would need the extra smoking capacity even if another pack never came near Tiranat. Or perhaps, it would be better if a pack of adzee did come in the future. Last night had proven that the village could handle it, so any attack in the future would give the guards valuable fighting experience, more shooting practice to the women handling the scorpions, and a liberal amount of pricey meat to the village stores.

A win-win for every side.

Kivamus snorted. Except for the pack of beasts.

One of the butchers called for more hands.

A few men who had just finished with another beast jogged to him and helped move a heavy section away from the carcass.

Kivamus remained there for a while longer, watching the steady work. The sight itself was unpleasant. But the bodies also showed how much the village had changed.

A full pack of adzees had attacked Tiranat.

Five of them were now being turned into food, preserved stores, and trade goods.

Apart from that leg injury of Maran, not one villager had died.

That mattered more than anything else.

Kivamus looked toward the children.

Lucem was crouched near the marked line, trying to see around one of the guards. Clarisa had come back to stand beside Syrene and was now asking questions about the exposed ribs.

"Come on kiddos. We are heading back," Kivamus called, not wanting the two kids to run around unsupervised. "I want to inspect the rest of the village before the day gets hotter."

Lucem straightened immediately, his eyes not leaving the butchers’ work. "But we haven’t even seen them remove the fangs yet!”

Syrene walked over and caught her brother lightly by the shoulder.

"You've been staring at those fangs long enough. Trust me, you won't get a chance to steal one of them with the guards watching.”

Lucem looked thoroughly surprised that his secret plan had been found out so easily.

Syrene laughed. “Come on."

Clarisa pointed toward the partly skinned carcass. “Can’t we stay longer, pleeease? I also want to see how they separate the shoulders."

"You can ask me about it later," Syrene told her. "We're leaving now."

Lucem opened his mouth again.

Syrene raised an eyebrow at him. "Keep complaining and I won't bring you with me the next time something interesting happens. Ma might not even let you leave the manor for a few days if you don't get up to leave right now.”

Lucem stopped any protest immediately.

Clarisa gave the carcass one last reluctant look, then followed the others toward the gate.

Kigeir also seemed to have decided that it was time to return so the merchant called up his kids as well.

Hudan chuckled and fell back into position beside Kivamus, with Duvas, Syrene and the two kids following them. The four guards continued in formation around them.

Trevalo stayed back, saying he had to go to speak with his wagon drivers about making preparations for leaving tomorrow.

Kivamus passed through the northeast gate. The road ahead led deeper into the village.

The day was already growing hotter, but there were several construction sites and work crews he hadn't visited recently.

Since he was already away from the manor, he might as well see how the rest of the projects in Tiranat were progressing.

***

They returned through the northeast gate and continued south along the main road.

The four manor guards moved around Kivamus in a loose box, leaving enough space for Duvas and Hudan to walk on either side of him. Syrene, Lucem, and Clarisa walked nearby, with the two kids talking enthusiastically about the adzee carcasses at first, but the excitement of seeing the bodies was already beginning to fade into complaints about the heat.

It was not yet midday, but the morning was growing warmer with every passing minute.

Kivamus looked toward the longhouse blocks as they approached them.

The first two had been built in a hurry during the winter, when Tiranat had needed shelter more than anything else. They had been made with unseasoned logs and simpler construction, enough to keep people alive through the cold months but not enough for the long term. Finn had already warned him that both blocks might not last another winter without serious repairs.

Repairs would only delay the problem.

Once one of the construction teams became free, the fifth longhouse block would have to be built west of the fourth. It would be a proper two-story block, like the newer ones. After that, the residents of the first block could be moved there while the old structure was dismantled and rebuilt properly in the same place. The villagers would still call it the first longhouse block, because changing names only created confusion.

Then the second block residents would be shifted into the new first block, dismantled, and rebuilt in the same way.

It would take months though.

But at least Tiranat now had three well-trained construction teams, and every week of work made them more experienced. The men who had barely known how to raise walls last winter were now learning foundations, framing, planking, roof work, and all the small tasks that turned a pile of timber into a building fit for people to live in.

Kivamus slowed near the longhouse road.

“Syrene,” he said. “Take Lucem and Clarisa back to the manor.”

Lucem turned at once. “Why?”

“Because I’m going to inspect workshops and construction sites, not another adzee.”

“Oh. That sounds… less interesting.”

“That it will be,” Kivamus chuckled.

Clarisa looked toward Syrene. “Can we go past the kitchen first?”

Syrene gave her a knowing look. “You want to see if Madam Nerida has started cooking the adzee meat.”

“No, uh… She might need help?”

Lucem snorted. “You want to taste it.”

“Like you don’t!” Clarisa countered immediately. “And you eat much more than me!”

Syrene pointed toward the manor road before the argument could grow. “Come on, both of you. If Madam Nerida lets us anywhere near the kitchen, you can ask her about it there.”

Lucem looked as though he wanted to object again, but one glance at his sister made him reconsider.

The children followed Syrene toward the manor, still talking as they went.

Kivamus watched them for a moment before turning west with Duvas, Hudan, and the guards. The new market block lay to his left, still carrying the signs of last night’s celebration. A few blackened patches marked where fires had burned, and one of the stalls had a stack of empty baskets beside it. On his right, the third and fourth longhouse blocks stood with proper drains surrounding them, newer and stronger than the first two.

Farther ahead, the steady sound of hammering and sawing came from Darora’s workshop.

The workshop stood beneath a wide roof built more for shade than shelter. Its sides were open in several places to let air pass through, though the heat still gathered beneath the roof as the sun climbed higher. Half a dozen carpenters were working inside, each focused on a different task.

One man shaped a long beam rigorously, sending curls of wood falling around his boots. Two others were smoothing planks on trestles. Another worker was boring holes through a heavy frame, stopping every few turns to clear the shavings from the tool. A fifth was fitting a wooden guide rail into place and muttering under his breath each time it resisted him.

Near the back, Layita stood near a younger worker’s piece, pointing at the joint he had cut.

“No,” she said. “If you leave it like that, it’ll shift the moment weight comes on it. Cut the shoulder straighter.”

The man frowned at the wood. “It looks straight to me.”

Layita took the chisel from his hand, adjusted the angle, and shaved a thin strip from the joint. “Now it is.”

The younger man rubbed the back of his head.

Darora stood nearby with his arms folded, watching her with obvious satisfaction.
