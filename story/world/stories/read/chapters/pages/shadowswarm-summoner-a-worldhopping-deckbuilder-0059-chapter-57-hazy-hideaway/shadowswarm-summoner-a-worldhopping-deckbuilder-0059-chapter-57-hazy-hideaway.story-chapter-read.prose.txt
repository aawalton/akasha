Chapter 57: Hazy Hideaway

It would’ve been really easy to resent being the group’s beast of burden, but Raeleq had grown up used to it. It didn’t matter that he was a prince of a once-proud people. He was big, so he carried heavy things for smaller people. It was the way of the world, and he’d made peace with it long ago.

That said, the Levi-Box was weightier than it looked. Especially when he had to drag it up and down a few flights of stairs. If there were lifts anywhere, they weren’t obvious or available to the public.

“It’s just a little further now,” Dexi’Lynn said, as if she could read his mind. “But you might want to watch your head, big-man.”

It was true, the further down they went, the more cramped and compact the buildings became. Of the four major intelligent races of Stonskarr, the two subjugated groups tended towards shortness. There were a few tall molekin, but the majority had been about five feet tall or under. He wondered if it was weird for Jessica to suddenly be surrounded by people her own size, instead of dwarfed by everyone around her.

The heat was worse, this far down. He didn’t know how many more levels there were below them, but the sweat was starting to make it hard to carry anything. Raeleq shifted the wrapped box to his other shoulder and ducked beneath a metal pipe, steam hissing from a crack.

On top of being more cramped, the area looked worse and worse. All of Rentan Academy had been relatively safe, but he could remember traveling to other cities. This gave off the feeling of an impending stabbing, though from the way the kobolds scattered at his approach, they might not have been brave enough to try on their own.

“I’m beginning to second-guess this,” Jessica muttered as they turned down an alley. The place was a maze, and only Dexi knew her way around.

“Don’t be silly, we’re here!” The bronze kobold ducked inside a doorway.

Jessica cursed, then followed, leaving Raeleq the difficult choice of whether to stay out there by himself with an expensive piece of machinery, or to crouch and try to enter. After ten seconds of debating with himself, he sighed and stooped low, back starting to complain already. The second he was in, he coughed and sputtered.

Maybe it was different for kobolds, who had an easier time seeing in the dark, but the inside of the building was so hazy with thick, stinking smoke that it was impossible to make out more than outlines of people, looming in the fog.

“Easy, you don’t want them to think you’re a soft-shell,” Dexi’s voice came from somewhere vaguely in front of him. “We’ll be in and out in an hour or two, no big deal.”

“W-wait,” said Jessica, “that’s not what we agreed to!”

Raeleq stood, but had to stoop to avoid bonking his head on the ceiling. He blinked, and more of the pub, for that’s what it was, came into view. The clientele were exclusively kobolds, who drank, smoked, and played cards. One, bigger than others and a striking red and black, hissed in excitement before embracing Dexi’Lynn.

“Sister! To what do I owe the honor? You here to borrow money, or smoke my leaf?”

“Sohx! Both!” Dexi cackled. Once they parted, she motioned for Jessica and him to follow. The place wasn’t big, but there were still rooms in the back.

“Jess,” Raeleq said.

“I know, I know,” she said, looking close to panic. “We’ll get her some leaf to take with her, and then we’ll meet back up with the others. She gets cranky without smoking.”

He blinked, incredulous. Raeleq was not the kind of person to deny someone their creature comforts, but putting everything off, risking themselves like this? Raeva would’ve put her foot down and dragged them both out. Or maybe just left on her own, telling them to catch up when they were done being stupid.

But he didn’t know the way around. The entire time he’d carried the Box, he’d followed them closely. He knew how far they went, and how many stairs he would have to climb, but the actual steps would elude him. Cursing with more force than usual, he lugged the box along to a private room.

Inside, there were no chairs. Instead, the floor was covered in soft, plush, ratty old pillows and cushions. The two kobolds made themselves comfortable on their knees around the table in the middle. On the table was an intricate glass pipe, with half a dozen mouthpieces connected by hoses, and a brass cup on top where he assumed they burned whatever they were going to smoke. The act had never called to him, and this detour wasn’t making it any more appealing.

Jessica, on the other hand, sat next to Dexi’Lynn with a bounce. “Is this stuff safe for humans to smoke?”

The two kobolds shared a look. “Probably?” said Dexi, shrugging. “I’ve smoked with some humans before. They can’t handle it as well as us dragonblooded, but hey, who can?” They burst out laughing.

“Here,” said Sohx, pulling out a leather bag and slapping it on the table. “Help yourself. I know it’s been a bit since you’ve got any of the good shit. Hell, I’m surprised they let you out on leave this early. Don’t you have another month to go?”

Alarms sounded off in Raeleq’s head, but Dexi’Lynn made a dismissive sound and reached for the bag, taking a pinch of crushed green and purple herbs and packing the bowl. She then took the glowing end of a stick and pressed it against the mix. “My team faced some difficulties, I’m afraid. Everyone except for me died horribly in a crash, and alas, I probably did too.”

“I’ll smoke to your memory,” he snorted, grabbing one of the hoses.

“So you’re siblings?” Jess tried to change the subject, with all the grace of a newborn lamb. “How big’s your family?”

“Not that big,” Dexi shrugged. “About eight of us kids, and then there’s our moms and dad. And that’s the survivors, naturally.” She brought a mouthpiece up to her snout and inhaled deeply. When she exhaled, the smoke danced in circles, scattering into a hundred chaotic swirls.

“I miss Otiz,” Sohx sighed, taking a hit as well. “He was a good kid. Way too kind to be one of us.”

“What happened to him?” Raeleq asked, unable to help himself. He waved a hand in front of his face. The smoke was starting to make his eyes water and head swim already. He didn’t like it.

“Got eaten by a rock wyrm. He was about ten. Good kid, but kinda slow. I mean, the rest of us did get away because of it, but I would’ve much rathered it be Stiv.”

“Right? Fuck Stiv.” The two laughed, and puffed away again.

Raeleq nudged Jessica, trying to convey everything he felt in one single, powerful frown. She nodded and turned back to the two. “Hey, Dexi? I know we said we’d smoke, but we’re a bit nervous about carrying this around and -- “

“That? Oh, no problem,” Dexi waved her off. “No one here’s got any use for a Levi-Box. Not even for stealing. I mean, who would we even sell it to? Not like any of us are leaving the area without the Empire’s permission. Well, mostly not any of us.” She cackled again.

Sohx’s slitted eyes went thin. “You’re serious, then? You’re ‘dead’ and are going to fuck off forever? Where will you even go? Not like there’s any outrunning the bastards. They have the biggest piece of infinity out there.”

Jessica bit her lip. “It’s not the point. It’s just, we’ll feel better when we…”

“I won’t deny that,” Dexi said, ignoring her entirely. “But that doesn’t mean we can’t find a bit of peace before they catch up with us and either kill or enslave us, right? This is my best bet. So I had to come see you.”

Raeleq understood, and wished he hadn’t. It made it harder to push for leaving this cursed, stinking place. Jessica, red-faced, opened her mouth to probably lose her temper. This time, it was his turn to keep her from speaking.

Sohx scratched the tip of his snout with his middle talon. “Yeah, I get it. You wanted one last smoke. I’m glad you did, Dexi. Even if it means I’m going to have to get all sappy about it.” He sighed, as if completely put out.

“What do you mean?”

He tapped the bag full of herbs. It wasn’t an especially large bag, but it bulged. “Take it with you. Remember me fondly, and if you ever find a place free from the Hermanic jerks, come get me. I’m gettin’ pretty damned sick of it here too. You want me to tell anything to the family?”

“Naw. Just let them think I’m dead. Maybe one day I’ll come back and we can laugh it all off.”

“Pffft. Dad would lay an egg.”

Raeleq and Jessica shared an entire silent conversation in seconds. It was mostly a bunch of confusion, incredulity, and the awkward question of whether or not they should even be here for this. They had so many bigger problems than this, but it would’ve been wrong to deprive Dexi of this opportunity, he supposed.

“Hey, lookit us, being rude,” said Dexi’Lynn. “You gotta try this stuff, Jess, it’s great. Don’t mind what I said earlier, it’s definitely safe, and it’s also a lot of fun. A couple of quick puffs and you’ll be less high strung. That’s a promise from me to you.” She grinned.

Jessica hesitantly took a hose and brought the mouthpiece up to her lips. Their new friend took that lit stick and ignited the mix once more. The mind mage took a puff, and then choked immediately, to the uproarious laughter of the kobolds.

“You never forget your first time, right?” Sohx laughed. “Harsh, stinky, and you think you’re about to die. It gets better.”

“Why, though?” Raeleq finally spoke up and asked. “Why smoke at all, if it’s harsh, stinks, and makes you feel like death?”

They shared a look.

“I’unno,” said Dexi. “Boredom?”

Watery-eyed, Jessica took another hit, and handled this one better. She blew out smoke, sighing at the end. An oddly peaceful smile washed over her. “Oh. Huh. You sure you don’t want to try, Raeleq?”

He shook his head resolutely. “One of us has to keep our heads on while we’re in danger. I thought it would be you. Come outside when you’re done. And please don’t forget, we have to keep moving. Don’t throw it away just because you want to try something new.”

Without waiting for an answer, Raeleq fought his way to his feet and dragged the Levi-Box with him. The rest of the establishment paid him no mind, save to watch him maneuver out the front door on unsteady legs.

The air of Bedrock Spire still tasted a bit like soiled laundry, but it was sweeter and cooler than it was in that damned room. He didn’t like to be the type to judge, but he thought he understood why the kobolds smoked.

The moles weren’t allowed to drink, while it seemed like the kobolds were encouraged to. Both were forms of control, meant to curb or predict their behaviors. Maybe they’d still smoke without the incentive, but it would be their choice and not a coping mechanism.

He wrinkled his nose in distaste. This entire detour was a pain in the ass, and he desperately wished to get back to everyone else. He thought of his sister, and her end of the plan. She had to have been doing better than him.

Right?
