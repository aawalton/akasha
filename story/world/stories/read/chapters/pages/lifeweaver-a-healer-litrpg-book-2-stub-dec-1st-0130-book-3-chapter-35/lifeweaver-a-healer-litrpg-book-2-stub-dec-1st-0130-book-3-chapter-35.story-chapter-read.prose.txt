They encountered four more monsters on the way to their destination. Elijah dealt with the first three much as he'd done with the one near ISG headquarters. With the fourth and last one, a wolf the size of a van, Lemira wanted to have her turn. Despite her assurances that she'd be fine on her own, Luke couldn't help but feel a slight tinge of worry. Something about the way Ezezal said he'd reach out and punish Luke if something happened to his daughter made him think there was something behind the threat, despite the demon being dead. He'd prove himself a rather shitty guardian, too, if she ended up dying to the first monster she engaged on Earth.

Even so, Luke stood back and let her do her thing. Building trust was important, and if she said she could deal with it on her own, he trusted her.

"Are you sure about this?" Elijah asked. He'd explained that the weapon he used was called a glaive, and he now stood right next to Luke, resting the weapon over his shoulder.

"She'll be fine," Luke said. "I think."

In the end, she was never in any danger. As she made her way toward the wolf monster, it growled at her, jumped side-to-side, but didn't pounce on the half-demon.

Luke craned his neck, trying to make out what was happening. She wasn't far away, but she faced away from them, her front toward the monster. "Is she talking to it?"

"I think so," Elijah said, shaking his head.

A moment later, Lemira raised a hand over her head, and the wolf stopped moving. It struggled, as if wanting to either flee or attack, but it remained in place until the one directing it, Lemira, lowered her hand, forcing it to lie down on its stomach in the middle of the street. Soon, the monster began whimpering as if in pain.

Elijah took a step forward, but Luke held out a hand. "Wait."

Just as Elijah was about to say something back, the wolf's chest bulged outward, and the wolf's head fell to the ground, signaling its death. Lemira reached forward with her hand and formed a fist before making a pulling motion. Something pushed through the corpse and flew in a straight line toward Elmira, who caught it. With that, she turned and started walking toward Luke and Elijah.

"That was gruesome," Luke said, even as he looked over the monster core in Lemira's cupped hands.

Elijah looked a little pale. "Can't condone hurting animals."

"Monster, not animal," she said.

A thought struck Luke, who had to ask, "How did you gain levels over in Lili-land? Did you get portals opening there, too?"

Lemira put the core into her inventory before answering. "Fought against some of Leminal's undead. We got some monster spawns, but no portals. My father would task the believers with summoning monsters for me to bring under my control, and sometimes to fight. It... It wasn't easy."

"Well, good job with that wolf," Luke said. "Some real Sith stuff, that force-grip."

Elijah pursed his lips. "That name rings a bell. Is it a class?"

"Movie," Luke said.

Lemira looked on with interest. "What's a movie?"

"I'll show you later. Why don't you, you know... summon that monster?"

Lemira brought it out again and held the core up to her face, squinting at it. Rather than being a broken, uneven thing, like the ones Luke pulled out of monsters, this one was a perfect circle. It was sky blue with white patterns swirling within, almost like dancing clouds, and it emitted a faint hum, as if vibrating.

"Claiming a monster's core is one thing, but bringing it back as a companion is quite another. I must meditate over the orb to manifest it, then use a few skills to improve its power, or the companion will come out a weak and pitiful thing."

Luke nodded. A pet class that used monster cores, improved said cores, and even leveled the monsters once they were out and fighting sounded like a fun option, and like it could be strong. He wanted to see what she could do.

Checking his watch, Luke pointed in the direction they needed to go. "We have plenty of time. Once we get to where we're going, you'll be able to raise that little pup."

She gave him an appreciative nod and put the core away as they resumed their walk, hearing more monsters in the distance.

Ray: "Hey, Luke-boy! We're heading into another dungeon. Let's go!"

Luke: "Hey. Done with the TV marathon? We're supposed to meet Milla in two hours, Ray. I've got a new friend for you to meet."

Ray: "Completed season three. Back to it tonight after we've gained a few more levels. We've got plenty of time. Bring the demon-chick and the new friend!"

Luke: "My sister will be pissed if you're late and you're not her favorite person to begin with, dude."

Ray: "Pfft. Milla loves me."

Luke: "...Remember the hot sauce incident?"

Ray: "Oh, come on! I was ten years old!"

Luke: "You were seventeen. And what about her friend's bikini that time?"

Ray: "Ages ago! Anyway, I'm taking Curtis. Haven't seen Hannah around, have you?"

Luke: "Hannah is in Seattle. She won't be late. Neither will you."

Ray: "Man, I wish I'd taken her profession. What a time-saver."

Luke: "What profession did you end up picking?"

Ray: "Alchemist. Poisons, you know?"

Luke: "Fair enough."

Luke: "Don't be late."

Ray: "Geez, enough with the nagging, Mom."

Luke: "Milla will put you on babysitting duty."

Ray: "Relax. We'll speedrun this place."

That made Luke think of something. "Hey, Elijah. Do you have a communications gem?"

Elijah looked through his inventory and came away with one, nodding. "Seems like it."

Luke: "Good."

Elijah: "This is strange."

"It is," Luke said, pointing into the distance. "More monsters."

"I want that one too," Lemira said, hurrying her steps a little. She looked excited. "Elijah, would you please exterminate all but one?"

"Sure thing. Might even level up."

Luke watched them go. It dawned on him why she might want one of those specific monsters. They looked a lot like llamas, only their necks were shorter and, if he wasn't mistaken, their teeth were long, their muzzles covered in blood.

"You're not aiming to ride one of those, are you?" he shouted after her. Lemira turned around and gave him a happy wave. She was so going to ride one of them.

Elijah made quick work of the group of monsters, and Luke thought he saw the man start glowing a little after the second kill, and perhaps move a little faster, but then the fight was over, and a monster core exploded from the chest of the last surviving llama-monster.

After that walk, it was only a bit longer until they arrived at their destination: New York Penn Station. The place wasn't quite as deserted as the rest of the city, even if no trains were running. From the look of things, they'd turned it into a place to congregate and seek shelter. Food was being distributed from a number of places, and Luke saw several tents erected inside, and even more people in sleeping bags right on the floor. The entrances and inside were guarded by police officers who eyed Luke, but didn't stop him. Since they didn't arrest him on sight, he figured he was safe. The cops had better things to do than arrest him for... murder. At least he hoped that was the case.

"What is this place?" Lemira asked, looking up at the high ceilings and the many, many closed storefronts. Luke had been there once before, in high school, and the difference was startling.

"It's a train station," Luke said. "We're meeting my sister and a bunch of other people here later."

"But the trains aren't running," Elijah pointed out.

"It'll be fine," Luke said.

They found an empty spot, and Luke drew quite a few looks when he placed down his shelter and ushered his two companions inside. Glancing around and seeing quite a few unsavory-looking types, he hoped the little rectangle that was his new home would stand up against break-in attempts. Leaning inside, he called out. "I'll be right back."

Closing the door, he headed back out to the main hall, where he approached an empty table with the Department of Integrated Affairs insignia. A pair of men in their forties sat behind it, not doing much of anything. Two Integrated guards flanked them. All four followed Luke's approach with interest.

"What's going on here?" Luke asked.

The one sitting on the left, who was perhaps a little older than his colleague, cleared his throat, then spoke in an even tone. "What do you mean?"

"This city. It's falling apart. What are you doing to stop it?"

"Everything we can."

"I see," Luke said. "What about the military? Can't they come?"

"Who are you?"

Luke didn't answer that particular question. "Never mind. What about you guys? There are DIA teams of Integrated, aren't there?"

"We are doing anything we can," the agent repeated. "We can provide you with food and blankets, and you're safe as long as you stay in here or in any of our other designated locations. At this time, you must follow the curfew. If you are Integrated and want to help, you can talk to our associate over there." He pointed to a different table by the far wall. "And if you are not Integrated but wish to become one, go to that group gathering over there," he pointed to yet another table. "They will leave for the orb in about an hour."

"You're talking people into becoming Integrated?"

The agent gave a slight shrug. "It allows you to eat food from the Integrated Shop, if nothing else. As long as you stay in the safe zones, you should be relatively safe. You can even stay in the Tutorial Dungeon as long as you clear it out first."

Luke nodded. He wasn't sure why he'd walked over to these people. Dealing with the situation couldn't be easy, and it sounded like they were giving it their best effort, but it wasn't enough. Earth was going to shit. This particular city might be circling the drain a little faster than most, but soon every other place would follow. There had to be more the Integrated could do to stop it.

"I'm Integrated. What can I do to help? other than finishing dungeons and closing portals?"

The agent gave him a long look, then pursed his lips. "Nothing."

"Nothing?"

"Help your fellow Integrated whenever possible. Finish dungeons, like you said, and grow stronger."

"That's it?" Luke asked.

"This is the world we live in now, and it won't be getting any easier. If you want to help more, join the DIA teams and deploy with them. If not, get stronger. More conflict is coming, and I'm not only referring to monsters and portals."

"What do you mean?"

The agent who hadn't spoken yet put a hand on the older one's arm, as if to stop him from speaking, but that did little to dissuade his sharing of information. "Our military is converting to Integrated. Every single enlisted is going through the Tutorial Dungeon or has already done so. With our most powerful strategic weapons and long-range capabilities reduced to nothing, more powerful individuals will need to step forward to take their place. Other countries are infringing on our territory as we speak. For now, it is not on US soil, but it is only a matter of time."

"That's enough, Burke," the younger agent said, gesturing to Luke. "There's no need to share that much with a civilian who just walked up to us. Are you looking to get written up again?"

The older agent glared at the younger one, but stopped talking. Instead, he waved for Luke to go. "Get stronger, Integrated."
