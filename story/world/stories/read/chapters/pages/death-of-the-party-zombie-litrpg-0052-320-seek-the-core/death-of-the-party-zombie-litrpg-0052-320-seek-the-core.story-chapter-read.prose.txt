Ren slowly lowered her sniper rifle, but kept a focused glare on the Radoch corpse. “So these are the assholes who are coming to your world?”

“Yeah.” Theo looked away from the glow of the monitor. “They’re usually a little more sinister and lively, however.”

The control room had been safe enough for the two magicians to join him. While the dead shell of the cockroach-alien was a surprise, it looked as if they had long passed. The pair didn’t seem too taken aback by the creature, although from some of the stories they had told him of the things they had been through, it wasn’t a shock they were acclimated.

Max was rubbing his chin, his brow furrowed in thought. “Dead body aside, this whole room is rather strange. Very modern. More like something from Earth than how high fantasy this world is.”

At first, Theo just offered a distracted shrug while he read the monitors. He had some basic knowledge, but Chuck had kept him away from the inner workings of their own System. “Hmm? Oh, I suppose it is odd. The underlying nature of this universe seems to have a healthy dose of technology, although most worlds are indeed traditional fantasy.” His eyes left the screen briefly to look at the elf’s long rifle.

She caught his glance and held it up. “I just wave this all off as sufficiently advanced magic.”

Max opened his mouth as if he were going to make a quip about her statement, but reeled it back in. “One might assume that this Radoch was sent to destroy the world.”

“Generally, they are collectors.” The vampire raised an eyebrow. “But… who’s to say they didn’t get what they wanted? I can’t determine the cause of death, as the body has been dead too long. Way before either of you arrived on this planet.”

The magicians exchanged a look.

“We tried asking around,” Ren began, “to see if anyone knew when the partial deletion happened. No luck.”

Max nodded. “Either there were no Players here, or the ones who witnessed it are also long dead.”

Theo leaned back in the chair and sighed. He turned to face the two with a glum expression on his face. “I can’t make head nor tail of this. We can probably connect to this and it will help transfer you over… once you’ve agreed, of course.”

It seemed like a given at this point, but he didn’t want to push them.

Ren scowled and worked her jaw as she looked around the room again. “We’re essentially in a small bubble within the void right now, yeah?”

“Correct.”

“Do you think you could find the person who was meant to be stationed in this room?”

The vampire pushed his glasses up, and his pointed fangs reflected the glow of the monitors. “I guess we’ll see.”

Sally opened her eyes. “I can’t even power nap. What a drag.”

The sky was now overcast from the dissipated clouds caused by the wreckage. A dull, smoky haze hung in the air, irritating her nostrils. She itched for battle, but wanted nothing more than to curl up in a warm bed.

“Do you always complain this much?” Hunter turned his head to look at her, a slight smile on his shadowed face.

“Usually more.” She sighed and sat up. “I’m gradually becoming less of a manic yapper. So sad.”

The man grunted, but had nothing to add. After turning his eyes back to the treeline, he shook his head. “You know, I could go down there myself and take care of them.”

“Probably.” Sally rubbed her eyes. “But if they have some tech for trapping you or something, then we’ll lose the ace in our hand.”

“Seems unlikely.”

“Jackie says the survivors are basically whoever was closest to the last ship’s commander. That bug had a protective force field strong enough to withstand the collision and explosion.” She clicked her tongue. “And people tell me I have plot armor.”

Hunter nodded. “Whatever they are, they’re woefully unprepared for what awaits them here.”

“That’s the spirit.” Sally closed her eyes and looked to the sky as she smiled. “We’re missing a few familiar faces, but I can’t think of a tougher obstacle than the Outsiders. Ironic how most of us look like villains, but we use our power for good.” Her brow furrowed as she recalled all the things she’d been through. Eaten through. “Or at least good-adjacent.”

“Sometimes that’s what it takes to get the job done.” The man glanced at the zombie before pulling his revolver out to observe it. After a moment, he sighed. “Memory loss is a bitch, huh?”

“You’ll be in good company on Sanctuary. Most people there don’t remember their old life, or even that they had one, sometimes.” Sally opened her eyes again and grimaced. “It’s pretty sad if you think too much about it. I never did like the SOMA situation.”

Being a tech-magic reincarnation of the Earth Sally wasn’t something she considered often. If the old her had truly died, then she was just the extension of that life, even if something akin to being digital. Maybe it was being merged with a zombie that gave her the dull view of her former and current humanity. It felt too real to be a game or virtual reality.

She held out her hand and looked down at it. Pale-green undead flesh, covered with dirt, ink, and dried gore. Her muscles twitched slightly as her hand took the brunt of the damage from casting so many skills lately.

“Monster is just a title,” she said, mostly to herself. “What really matters is being true to yourself and doing what you can to prop up those who need help.”

“Sometimes the road to salvation is difficult and bloody.” Hunter lowered his weapon. “I may not be one of you, or belong to this weird universe, but I certainly feel more like… myself when around you. Whatever that really means.”

Sally grinned and pushed herself up to her feet. “Must mean your goal is noble, if not morally complicated as well. Once we repel the Radoch attack on Sanctuary, I can’t imagine wanting to retire. We’re all driven to fulfill a purpose, aren’t we?”

Hunter smiled and stood up as well, holstering the large revolver. “It doesn’t get boring at least, right?”

Theo drummed his fingers on the desk idly as he stared unfocused at the monitor.

Chuck had started interfacing with this computer-like device, but any information was a long way off. He hadn't been able to work anything out from the various screens himself. Max was practicing sleight of hand over on another chair, while Ren stood glaring at the Radoch corpse as if her piercing blue eyes could bring it back from the dead to answer their burning questions.

Eventually she tired and clicked her tongue. She instead turned herself to face the vampire. “So, have you ever drunk elven blood?”

“Hmm?” Theo blinked and looked her way. “Yeah. I mean, only System-created elves.”

“Does it taste different from human blood?”

“Sure.” He shrugged. “It has a more refined, woodsy flavor.”

Max continued twirling around a dagger that occasionally switched to something else. “Really? It didn’t seem any different from my own.”

Theo raised an eyebrow and allowed that statement to build in silence. The magician stopped twirling the blade.

“We got stuck in hell for a long time.” Ren shook her head. “It’s not a place many can survive with their minds intact. The less detail we go into about our exploits during that time, the better.”

“For both our reputation and our current mental state,” Max agreed, nodding sheepishly. “That said, it is what forged us into what we are today.”

“You mentioned you’re technically the rulers of Hell now?” Theo asked. “Interesting, as even though Sanctuary has plenty of demons, we have no hell. If nothing else, it would be quite the feat if Chuck could absorb that part of this world.”

He smiled to himself. Edward was always pestering him about bringing home a souvenir from any world he went to. Bringing home an entire realm solely for the demon and his kind would be difficult to top.

“As long as we get to relinquish our titles.” Max flared his nostrils. “The power granted by the position is nice, but if we are to assume the role of mercenaries for your world…”

“Assuming we agree to your plan,” Ren added. “Not that we’re seeing a reason to decline at the moment. Just between us, we are waiting for a few of our allies to ask around to determine the general appetite for the idea.”

The purple-clad magician nodded. “Not that unlimited lives and a stable world are a bad thing, but the pair of us can’t exactly decide the fate of a whole world. You know, ignoring all we went through to save it in the first place.”

Theo tilted his head to the side. “It’s likely yours won’t be the last planet we save, nor you be the only powerful heroes we recruit to the cause. You certainly wouldn’t be sitting idle if you joined us, although I am forbidden from saying anything further.” His fangs flashed as he grinned. “Something to sink your teeth into.”

As the pair exchanged glances, a message came in through the STAR.

//Chuck: I have enough information to talk.//Chuck: Do you want to…//Chuck: Hang on, this might be the time to test something.//Theo: What could go wrong?//Chuck: …//Chuck: Just be ready.

“Looks like we have some news.” Theo stood up from his chair and brushed his jacket down. “Plus, to show you how much we appreciate you, we have a little debut of our own to reveal.”

He turned to place his hand on top of the nearest monitor. A ring of blue light bloomed into life around his wrist, gradually spiraling and brightening. After several seconds, there was a crackle in the air.

Beside the rows of magical technology, a flash of blue light illuminated the dim chamber. Blue wireframe flickered around the space, deforming and waving until they condensed into a familiar figure.

Chuck blinked several times before checking his hands and arms. “Ah. Good news - it worked.”

Theo crossed his arms and leaned up against the wall. “How does it feel?”

“Like I’m astral projecting, which is an odd feeling considering I’m an incorporeal being already.” He shot the vampire a dry smile before turning to the others present. “The Russets. Pleasure to make your acquaintance.”

Max stood up and gave a bow, taking the top hat from his head along the way. “I won’t make things awkward by trying to shake your hand. You must be the Architect.”

“Pleasure is all ours,” Ren added. “You don’t usually greet your… prospective allies like this?”

“No.” Chuck shook his head before turning his attention to the Radoch corpse. “Previously impossible. Breakthroughs lately have worked to our advantage, but they often do in times of stress.”

While made of dense wireframe and blue light, the stress was clearly visible on the man’s face. Theo was no stranger to seeing him this way, but since there were so many spinning plates at the moment, it could only point to one thing.

Trouble on the horizon.

“It’s concerning seeing them here,” Chuck continued, pointing at the body. “While it would be foolish to believe Sanctuary was the only planet being scouted, we initially presumed it was just an agent operating independently. A hobby that just so happened to step on our toes.”

“But now you’ve kicked the nest, and the swarm is out for you.” Max returned the hat to his head. “Or more accurately, after what your world can provide. Maybe there was something here they thought was worth stealing?”

“Almost certainly.” Chuck waved his hand, and a thin blue screen appeared in the air. Data feeds ran down one side, while the main portion showed a topography layout of the world. “While not unique, so to speak, the Guardian powers you have here would be figuratively worth their weight in gold if acquired.”

Ren gave a brief shrug. “The ones Max, Wolf, and I absorbed certainly made us incredibly powerful.”

Chuck nodded politely. “Some planets have a condensed amount of acquirable power. Usually, this is shared out amongst all the Monsters, Players, and other entities living within the world. If a single individual inherits a huge share of this energy, they become… well, like Theo.”

“An Aspect,” the vampire explained. “A walking embodiment of a particular force or theme. We’re not allowed to call it being a demi-god officially, lest our egos grow too large.”

The Architect flickered, his form fading in and out slightly. “I must return soon until we can work out a better way of sending me here.” He furrowed his brow, distracted by the thought. “Maybe clones with a temporary life? I can’t duplicate within Sanctuary, but across other worlds…”

Theo cleared his throat. “The Guardians, Chuck.”

“Ah, yes. Where was I?” He regained his composure and gave the magicians a tired smile. “The power of the Guardians lost from the deleted half of the world was not erased. That energy still exists and could be claimed.”

Max raised his eyebrows. “You are saying one of us could become an Aspect.”

“Data says possibly two of you. It just works out that way.”

The elf deflated slightly. “You’re going to tell us where this power now resides, right? It’s not going to be easy, is it?”

On the map showing the world, the screen zoomed in to a section of the empty void. A circle highlighted an otherwise plain space containing nothing visible, almost three times as far away from the continent as this control room was.

“A space of reality much like this one,” Chuck confirmed. “And yes, it won’t be easy.”

Theo grinned and pushed his glasses up his nose. He knew exactly what it was. It could only be one thing.

A dungeon with a Boss to kill.
