Chapter 5.24 (V.XXIV) - Clearing

Taroe was not amused by the appearance of a baby hippogriff in his home. Comet was swiftly banished from the building and restricted to the forest outside town.

Anata, however, was predictably ecstatic about the magical creature. She spent hours combing the hippogriff’s feathers and brushing the fur.

The one good thing to come of this was that Vincent’s promised ‘additional gift’ turned out to be a pocket stable. It could be placed anywhere with enough flat ground and would expand into a small, but functionable, stable. But it still required flat ground. And a place out of the way of curious neighbors susceptible to losing fingers to Comet’s beak.

The cove where Owl’s Respite rested wasn’t ideal for an eagle-lion-horse hybrid beast. Dog, the capybara, didn’t mind the darkness and had the ability to swim out beyond the waterfall to the outside end of the cove to sunbathe whenever he pleased. Comet, however, was not a capybara.

And so, Kizu, Ione, Basil, and Aoi got to work clearing a space in the nearby forest for their new resident.

“This is one of your failed experiments?” Aoi asked, shaking a green potion.

“You wanted something that could speed up the aging process,” Kizu said. “That in your hand accelerates aging rapidly, but it doesn’t ever stop. So, yeah, a failure. But splash it on some trees and they should die quickly.”

Rather than uncork the potion, Aoi threw it at a nearby tree and the glass shattered against its trunk. In the span of a minute, the healthy tree withered and died. The leaves fell as the moisture was sucked out of it.

Ione approached and tapped the wizened trunk with a stick. “Is it safe?”

“Once exposed to air, the potion’s potency decreases quickly,” Kizu said. “It should be completely safe now. But also I have antiserum on hand, just in case.”

“Great!” Ione stepped aside and let her massive, multi-headed bear charge at the tree with a roar.

Kizu winced as the trunk snapped. It slammed into some other trees and got caught in their branches. They had a lot of work ahead of them if they wanted this clearing open for Comet.

“Can you control dead plants?” Kizu curiously asked Aoi as she splashed another tree with the aging potion. “Do they have a death signature like animals?”

Aoi shook her head. “No. Maybe if I specifically pursued that study I could learn how after a few years. I’ve only read one record of a necromancer ever commanding dead plants and she was an ex-druid. Plants are fundamentally different from humans.”

Basil stepped forward and raised a forearm that was edged with metal from pinky to elbow. He sliced down and chopped at the base of a tree. It took a few swipes but the tree fell, knocking its shriveled companion out of the branches and they both fell to the forest floor.

“Whew.” Basil wiped sweat from his brow with the back of his flesh hand. “This new transformation is tough. The edge is sharper than any mundane blade but it still requires a lot of strength.”

Kizu eyed the blade on his friend’s arm. It glinted in the sunlight. The artifact Emperor Sasaki had gifted Basil granted the shapeshifter more flexibility over his shape. Recently, Kizu had seen Basil take on forms of Kemon and Tainted more and more frequently as well as increase his height and mass. But this was the first time Kizu had witnessed anything like metal.

“Is this spell in preparation for the tournament challenges?” Kizu asked.

“Partially,” Basil admitted. “But I want to test out more elemental based combinations in general. There are certain spells that only I can master and command. You’ve seen me create explosions with my expelled flesh? That’s a pretty simple one and an effective offensive. But it also costs me a bit of my mass every time I cast that spell.”

“But a spell like this costs you a lot less,” Kizu said. “And I imagine it can also be used as a defensive measure to protect your body in steel armor.”

With his flesh hand Basil pointed at Kizu and snapped his fingers. “Bingo! I’ve always liked elemental spells. Another one I’m testing out expels my sweat into an outline of my body with water spells then allows me to craft a mannequin of ice. But that one costs me a bit of my mass in the process. Once I master it though, I’ll have a near endless supply of mannequins at my disposal!”

“The blade arm spell looks more useful. Stronger than the bone one you showed me in the Hon Basin.”

“Psh. You’re making me blush!”

“Just an observation.”

“Better save some of those flattering observations for Ione. That silver tongue of yours might get you in trouble!” Basil got to work chopping up the fallen trees into more manageable chunks.

It was now Kizu’s turn to take down a tree. He approached one and examined it up and down. Then he placed both hands on it to feel. The rough bark pressed against his palms as he focused. Then he jumped.

Not far, only a few meters away, but he dragged the entirety of the tree with him. It crashed into the forest floor and threatened to flatten him as it fell. Steadying his weight on his monster leg and enhancing his human body with magic, Kizu shoved the tree back.

It smashed into the forest beyond him.

Kizu brushed his hands off, pleased with the result and turned back to the growing clearing behind him. The tree he had removed had left behind a smooth, ankle high stump while stumps left behind by Basil and Aoi’s trees were waist high and jagged.

Ione examined one of the remaining trees, her massive bear by her side. “I could summon an ice tortoise from the Tross tundra. Maybe freezing a tree could make it easier to knock over?”

“You don’t have any summons who can slice things?” he asked.

“Nope! I suppose I could burn it all down with controlled flames. I think that will leave behind a scorched patch of forest though. Probably not what we want for Comet.”

The bear had claws but he supposed it would be akin to trying to cut down a tree with kitchen knives. Not the most effective solution. Still, its strength was nothing to scoff at. The smaller trees could still be uprooted by that summon alone.

After a couple hours of work, they had a patch of forest cleared for Comet to frolic around in. The next step was a bit trickier. Kizu used the fallen tree trunks to create a wooden barrier around the clearing. And then they got to work adding to that fence with elemental magic, heaving up stone and dirt to create a wall. It should contain the hippogriff until Comet learned to fly. Which was only a few weeks off, from Vincent’s explanation. Hopefully by then they’d have a more permanent solution.

Kizu was drained after the effort, but they emerged triumphant in their endeavor.

“How about we go get some drinks in town?” Basil suggested. “It’s been a while since we all last went out.”

Ione stretched and yawned. Unable to assist with elemental magic to build the wall, she’d been napping in the sun for the last hour. “Yeah, I could go for some grub and booze.”

“Excellent! How about I get in touch with a few more—”

“No!” Kizu interjected forcefully. “If we want to invite more people out, I’ll contact them. The last thing we need is fifty students flooding a pub thinking Basil is putting on another party.”

Basil grumbled but relented. As they walked back to Owl’s Respite, Kizu pulled out his scrying orb and sent out a few messages to friends he thought might be free for the night. He chose a pub that was usually pretty empty and had cheap, but edible, food.

They all sat down at a table off to the side. Ione and Aoi were chatting about the cards Ione had converted into a bestiary and Aoi’s attempt to do the same by copying bits of her grimoire into different undead cards. They were comparing notes and showing one another cards. Meanwhile, Basil eyed the different outfits worn by others in the room. Kizu overheard him muttering something about wool shortages.

“What can I get you?” A waitress leaned in to put her face in his line of sight.

“Oh.” Kizu’s brain stuttered. He hadn’t even looked at the menu.

“Two servings of tomato basil soup,” Ione said. “Also, a few slices of that fresh bread those guys over there are chowing down on.”

The waitress’s eyes glanced over to Ione. Then she nodded and moved onto Basil and Aoi’s orders.

“You should shave half your head,” Ione said after the waitress departed. “Right down the middle.”

Kizu blinked. “What?”

“Kizu’s green hair is iconic though,” Basil said across the table. “Wouldn’t be the same person without it.”

“Hm.” Ione nodded, eyes fixed on Kizu’s face. “Good point. Maybe remove a chunk of your nose? That might work too.”

“What are you—”

“The waitress was staring, Kizu,” Aoi helpfully pointed out. “Ione is jealous and tossing out wild suggestions to deter people from pursuing you. Likely because she believes you’re too innocent to even notice.”

“The waitress was not interested—” Kizu started to say.

“Is ‘staring’ even the right word when she spent their one sentence conversation batting her eyelashes like a hummingbird’s wings?” Ione said.

Kizu frowned. Now that she pointed it out, maybe the waitress had been weirdly fixated on him.

“You know what happened the last time I noticed a random girl couldn’t take her eyes off me?” he asked. “She tore off her flesh and revealed herself to be an undead necromancer wearing a poor girl as a skin suit.”

Aoi jerked in her seat and her attention darted over to where the waitress was taking another table’s orders. There was a hunger in her eyes. And not for food. “You think that maybe….”

“The odds of that happening twice is entirely unlikely,” Basil said. Then he hesitated and glanced at Kizu. “Well, I suppose when he’s involved… it might not be impossible.”
