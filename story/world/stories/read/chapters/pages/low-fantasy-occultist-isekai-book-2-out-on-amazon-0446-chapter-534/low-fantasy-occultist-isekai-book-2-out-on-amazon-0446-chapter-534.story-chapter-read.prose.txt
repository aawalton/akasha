Hopefully, the sword would help him through his trials.

He stepped away. He needed no horse for this journey. The trade roads were safe enough with all the new soldiers being put to work, but the sky was far faster.

Eugene offered a crisp military salute.

It had taken some time, but he was back to where he’d been, able to direct every mote of mana that made up his body. Or even better, given how much more there was.

With a crack like a whip, Nick launched himself into the air.

He tore through the freezing morning sky, climbing rapidly until the sprawling stone walls of Floria looked like a scatter of gray pebbles in the grass. He banked eastward, the wind roaring against the smooth curve of his shield.

The borderlands fell away beneath him, and the vast grassland stretched out, a patchwork of rolling golden fields, dense thickets, winding silver rivers, and creeping brown arteries of the trade routes.

Once Floria’s expansion slowed, even those would change, replaced by paved pathways that would make trade even faster.

Flying at this altitude offered a majestic view, but he’d chosen to go so high for another reason. His esoteric senses expanded, sweeping across the terrain below. Compared to the shadow he cast, the world’s lesser creatures burned like tiny, insignificant candles.

An hour into the flight, near the rocky foothills bordering a merchant pass, Nick spotted a disturbance. A flock of Gale Eagles circled a caravan, their broad wings churning the air into dangerous, unnatural crosswinds.

Nick altered his trajectory and dove toward the cliffs. The largest of the flock, around level seventy, had razor-sharp plumage and folded its wings to dive toward the panicked draft horses below.

A mage was taking potshots at them, keeping them from swooping down, but his mana was dwindling. Soon, only the archers would remain to defend, and their arrows lacked the piercing power to injure the beasts.

Nick intercepted the matriarch as it prepared another pass, though he didn't bother summoning the devastating firestorms or vacuum spheres he would use against a real threat. Killing a mortal beast was no longer the challenge; restraint was. If he unleashed his true power, he would vaporize the eagle, the caravan, and a fair chunk of the trade road.

Thus, he wove a hair-thin thread of kinetic force, infusing it with spiritual intent, then snapped it outward like a whip. The invisible lash wrapped around the eagle’s throat, and Nick pulled sharply.

The beast’s neck snapped with a clean, audible crack.

And just like that, it went limp, tumbling harmlessly into the brush a hundred feet from the merchants.

A couple more applications brought the rest of the flock low, and Nick banked upward, leaving the caravan guards cheering at the empty sky.

Even from miles away, Nick could taste the sheer density of the warding arrays humming in the ether, a complex lattice of defensive magic designed to repel both sieges and scrying.

Nick landed half a mile outside the main gates and dismissed his kinetic sphere. Walking the final stretch was protocol; the city’s anti-air defenses were notoriously lethal, and the Ducal Guard punished arrivals by air.

He joined the throng of merchants, sellswords, and travelers bottlenecked at the grand archway. The smells of the metropolis rushed over him—roasting meats, open sewers, the tang of metal from the artificer districts, and, surprisingly, the exotic perfumes of the Free Cities.
