[New Evolution Acquired: Ursine Vitality (F-grade)]

Your cellular regeneration and muscular density have adapted to mirror the resilience of apex ursine predators. You can absorb damage that would cripple others, your body refusing to go down easily.

+4 Constitution

+1 Power

[Passive]: Reduced bleeding, increased pain tolerance, and minor damage resistance.

[Active]: When dropped below 10% Health, go into berserk mode, increasing all stats by 20%. Resistance to damage increased while in berserk mode by 50% After berserk mode expires, all stats reduced by 90% for a duration

Scales With: Constitution

Don felt a strange tingling on his skin. It felt like it was tightening and thickening. There wasn’t much of a visual change, but he definitely felt different. He flicked his claws open and slowly pushed them into his arm. It took a while before he drew blood. His skin was now easily two, three times thicker than a normal pre-System human’s. But it still looked the same, although almost all of his body was caked with grime.

Don was also very pleased with the Constitution increase. This evolution alone had almost doubled his ability to withstand damage. Sure, he was still lacking in the active skill department for both defense and offense. Don wondered if he needed a class to really start getting active abilities.

The berserk-mode seemed amazing. In dire situations, into which Don was clearly wont to run into, he could get a power boost to finish off a tight fight. However, it came with a severe drawback. Losing 90% of his stats temporarily would put him back to basically level 1. He wasn’t sure how long the condition would last and how to remove it. Hopefully Devour would help him out.

Before Don was ready to move, he considered his two Attribute Points from the level up. More power never hurt, but considering the beatings Don had endured lately with the bears, he put one point into Constitution, putting it at a nice round 10. The other point he put into Agility, just to give him a slightly better edge with his hit and run tactics with the bears.

However, when Don got back on the move, for the next hour he didn’t find much prey. He found one level 8 Grizzly and killed it easily. He even let the bear take a swipe at him, to see how his new evolution would work.

The damage was significantly lower because of the Trait and his higher Constitution.

After the bear was dead, Don searched around for bears for some time before he decided he needed to change things up. So he climbed a tree to see if he could see any movement from higher ground. He kept jumping from tree to tree as he watched the ground. He did see another group of those thugs at one point. Having no reason to reveal himself, he just watched them pass as they talked to each other with low voices and poked at bushes with their spears.

“Why the boss sent us here? This is Reggie’s territory,” one of them said.

“Reggie’s dead,” the lead thug said.

“Huh. I just saw him this morning. What happened?”

“They ran into something?”

“Like a monster?”

“Who knows. Dave, the scout found his party in a forest opening. Said all the bodies were… drained.”

“Drained? What the hell? There’s only bears here.”

“Maybe there’s another kind of monster.”

“And they sent us here? Fuck’s sake.”

“Just shut up,” the lead thug said. “If we see something off, we’re to report back to boss and not fight whatever it is.”

Don smiled to himself as he watched the thugs bicker. In some juvenile way he enjoyed being a mythical monster in the woods. It reminded him of the Mexican folktale, Chupacabra. It was also elusive and drank the blood of its prey. Whereas Don drank pure life essence, he found the similarity funny.

He considered killing the thugs. They were clearly part of a faction that could only be likened to a criminal organization. He checked them and found they were slightly higher level than the last batch. These were levels five and six. The lead thug was level seven. Don was fairly sure he could take them on, especially if he ambushed them.

Then he remembered the feverish eyes of Jacques and shook his head. He wasn’t a goddamn murderer. There were enough monsters out here killing people, so that Don didn’t need to turn into one.

Nothing interesting happened, until Don jumped on the branch of an absolutely massive tree. This was no spruce or pine or any kind of normal tree. The trunk was light yellow and extremely thick. Like grain silo thick. The branches were all at ninety degree angles and thick as logs, making it very easy to walk on them.

There’s something weird about this tree.

It was definitely not any kind of tree that grew on earth. Don looked at the bark. It was smooth and almost seemed to shimmer slightly. This was some kind of System bullshit.

Carefully Don examined the tree moving from branch to branch. Sometimes he stopped to listen and watch, but there was no movement around the massive tree.

He was truly surprised when he rounded to proverbial corner of the tree, making a large jump to a lower branch. What he found was a balcony.

It was carved into the tree with delicate round shapes. It was the size of a jacuzzi, with a thin railing carved from the same yellow wood. It was only six feet away from the branch Don was perched on. Don jumped on the balcony, landing lightly.

The surface under his feet was perfectly smooth—almost unnaturally so. No tool marks. No seams. Just flawless curves etched into golden bark.

It didn’t feel like it had been built. It felt like the tree had grown it this way.

The railing was waist high and patterned with strange swirling symbols. There was no roof above the balcony, but the branches above formed a partial canopy, letting in shafts of light through thick green leaves. It was quiet up here. No birds. No bugs. Just wind.

This place was… different. Sacred, almost. Or maybe just important.

Don took a few slow steps forward. His eyes tracked the symbols carved into the railing. They weren’t decorative. Not exactly. They pulsed faintly—not with light, but with presence. Like the air around them hummed at a frequency just beyond hearing.

Then he saw it.

At the far end of the balcony, directly against the tree trunk, there was a circular shape. It hadn’t been there a moment ago. It wasn’t a door, or at least not any kind of door Don had ever seen. It looked like… a ripple.

A slow, swirling distortion hung in the air, about six feet tall and perfectly round. It shimmered with faint green and amber hues, like sunlight through a leaf. The bark around it bent inward in a perfect circle, the wood grown back like peeled fruit. No cracks. No edges. Just this… opening. A portal grown, not built.

What the…

This was weird. Don had done his best to adjust his mind to just… accept the weird in this new reality. But it seemed like the weird just kept piling up faster than Don could cope. He shrugged and stepped closer. This new reality did not reward timidity.

As he neared it, the air grew thicker—warmer. His instincts prickled, but there was no danger here. The surface of the portal shifted when he moved, like it was watching him. Waiting.

Then the System pinged.

[You have discovered: Verdant Heart – Tutorial Dungeon] Warning: Dungeon recommended for levels 10–15. First-clear rewards available. Optional: Solo Instance – Entering alone will adjust dungeon to single combat parameters. Would you like to enter? Y/N

Don stared at the prompt. Then back at the tree. Back at the swirl.

Yep. Definitely System bullshit.

It wasn’t just a tree. It was a gateway.

And it had been waiting.

Don considered his options. In video games, dungeons were a great place to acquire resources, usually equipment. Don had scant little equipment, so getting some would be a great idea. There was also the first-clear reward, which would be a distinct advantage nobody else could have.

The level requirement did worry him, but he was apparently strong for his level. Jacques had said Don was stronger than him, and the wackjob had had two levels on him.

Don considered his options. The answer was simple. The new reality did not reward timidity. It rewarded risk taking and pushing forward. This might just be the advantage Don needed to push himself ahead to win the race against Jacques.

Nodding to himself, Don steeled his resolve and entered the portal.
