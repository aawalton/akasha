I looked away from the worn markings on the dilapidated inner walls of the ruin and glanced out the broken window toward the sky. Stephen had been gone for more than an hour, and I was starting to get worried.

The markings, which I was almost certain were actually enchantments of some sort, had been enough of a distraction to keep me from fretting too much, but even they could only do so much.

Stephen had wanted to scout the area a bit more, and his mirror falcon form really was the best when it came to such things. Not only did it have natural camouflage, but it was also highly mobile and nondescript.

I’d decided to explore what remained of the old stone building while he was away, and the more I looked around, the more I felt the structure had once served as a place of worship.

Between the obvious quality of the materials used and the layout of the structure, it seemed the most logical explanation. If the engravings on the walls actually were this realm’s version of enchantments, that would only reinforce that conclusion.

I wasn’t going to get my hopes up, though. Besides, it wasn’t like I’d be able to use such an enchanting system, even if it did use engravings instead of intent-based enchantments. Still, the possibility was interesting enough to hold my attention while I waited for Stephen to return.

Wandering away from the broken window and deeper into the dilapidated building, I searched for any additional markings. Most appeared to be near windows and doorways, though I’d noticed a few carefully carved symbols scattered in other locations as well.

With the lighting growing dimmer as I went deeper into the structure, I removed a basic light enchantment – one of the first I’d crafted – along with one of the weakest essence cores I had in storage.

Since the enchantment wasn’t designed to draw essence from a core, I made a tiny scratch on the core, which would cause the essence within to slowly seep out. It was a bit wasteful, really, but since I could just put the core into stasis when it wasn’t in use, I figured it wasn’t too bad.

Thankfully, my attempt to artificially provide ambient essence worked, and the small device activated, casting a gentle light. I had to keep the core and the light charm in the same hand, but that was hardly an issue.

The light was just starting to flicker when I felt Stephen’s presence. It wasn’t a skill that let me know he was there. Rather, it was just sort of a sixth sense. Unfortunately, it didn’t come with any special perception skill offers.

“You wandered pretty deep while I was gone,” he commented. “How’d you manage to get the light to work?”

“I scratched a weak core to get it to release essence,” I answered. “How did your scouting go?”

“Good. Nobody paid me any attention at all. The area where we arrived is down to a skeleton crew, but it looks like they are mostly just guarding the remaining supplies. The incursion site itself seems to be hidden by some kind of enchantment, much like the fortress was in our realm.”

That seemed a bit odd. It implied, at a minimum, that there were factions in this realm, which wasn’t what I expected.

Then again, I hadn’t really put much thought into what kind of society the demons might have. I’d only seen them as aggressors set on killing as many Dians as possible. Of course, that could still be true, even if the demons of this realm were not unified.

“There is a city not very far away,” Stephen continued. “They use vehicles that remind me of those used in your old world back before it was integrated. The style is obviously different from what you showed me, but I think the way they function is probably similar.”

When practicing with Minor Illusion, I’d managed to create the equivalent of silent movies to show Stephen, Cora, and Mike what Earth had been like before magic had appeared. That had led to some pretty in-depth discussions about how pre-magic technology had worked, so I took Stephen at his word when he said he suspected this realm used something similar.

“Don’t they have magic here?” I asked. “The demons who attack our realm seem to have magic.”

“Almost certainly, but it’s not like people walk around overtly using magic, even in Dia.”

He made a very good point.

“Do you think we’ll have a hard time entering the city? What kind of defenses does it have?” I asked.

“As far as I can tell, there are none,” he replied, surprising me. “We shouldn’t have a hard time getting inside, but we’ll need to be careful. Though there appear to be several different types of demons living there, I didn’t see a lot of hooded figures. Walking around completely hidden might actually draw more attention to us.”

“So, we need some kind of actual disguise.”

Stephen nodded. “I actually think I might be able to push my shapeshifting skill to let me partially shift. I haven’t really had much reason to push it in that direction with the way we fight, but Zeta mentioned it was possible.”

“Oh, that might work well!” I said excitedly.

I’d seen several types of demons during our surveillance that looked like they could be some kind of humanoid beast-creature. And it wasn’t like he had to get it exactly right. As long as he was close, that would be enough.

However, two small issues immediately came to mind.

“What about inspection skills?” I asked, mentioning the first. “Wouldn’t they out you as a Dian?”

Stephen shrugged. “It’s hard to know without trying. We have no idea if they even have such skills in this realm. And if they do, we won’t know how much information is shared until we encounter it.”

“What about communication? How are you going to understand and communicate with the natives?”

“That’s the really interesting part,” Stephen said with a sly smile. “When I got close enough to hear the demons talking, I realized I understood everything they were saying.”

It took a moment for the implications to settle in. “Does that mean the demons have been able to understand everything we say when they attack?”

“It seems that way. It’s probably something that happens when you travel between realms. You already know that Travelers are a real thing. Have you ever heard of one appearing who couldn’t communicate?”

Now that he’d mentioned it, I hadn’t heard of anyone like that. I’d always just assumed Dia had granted Travelers the ability to communicate. But if it was something that happened automatically upon traversing realms… well, that was something that the people of Dia should probably know.

Still, that was good news for us since it would make blending in that much easier.

“You’re right,” I said. “How long do you think you’ll need to figure out how to partially shift?”

“I honestly have no idea. I’m not sure how much the system is functioning here, nor whether this realm will hinder or help me. I was able to partially shift before I ascended, so as long as this realm doesn’t overtly restrict me, I don’t think it’ll take too long.”

Looking a little hesitant, Stephen added, “I’d actually like to visit the city without you once I manage to partially shift. After seeing it myself, I’m not sure it would be safe for you to enter looking like a Dian, even if we did find a way to disguise you.”

“Why is that?”

Stephen shifted uncomfortably, but before he could answer, the light in my hand rapidly started dimming.

“We should probably talk in the sanctuary, anyway,” I said, reaching for him.

Though I could pull him inside easily enough without contact, I wanted to do everything I could to minimize my essence expenditure. Once our hands made contact, we shifted from the ruins to the comfort of our sanctuary home.

I took a moment to absorb the last bit of essence from the failing core before sitting down on the couch. A second later, Stephen joined me.

“Okay. To answer your question, it looks like the city uses Dians as slaves.”

I gasped. I’d never heard of people being kidnapped from Dia during attacks before. However, it was certainly true that people regularly went missing during attacks. However, I’d always understood that to be because they’d been eaten or something. There were also unidentified remains after nearly every attack.

But if they’d actually been kidnapping some people and bringing them back to the demon realm as slaves… that changed things.

“You’re sure they are Dians?”

“Well, I don’t have Identify, so I can only go off of the system’s basic identification, but they look exactly like Dians. There were also a couple of other races being held as slaves. If I had to guess, they’re probably from other realms being infiltrated. And even if they aren’t from Dia, they are close enough that leaving them here as slaves doesn’t quite sit right with me.”

Slavery, in general, was something neither of us agreed with. It was one thing for criminals to be forced into indentured servitude because of their crimes, but actual slavery – and especially hereditary slavery where people could be born slaves – I could never agree with.

“And you’re certain they’re actual slaves?” I asked.

“Yes. Absolutely certain. They even have metal collars.”

My mind flashed back to a time in my life when I’d worn such a collar. It had only been for a short time – hours, at most. But the feeling of despair and the way those caged with me had completely given up hope had always stuck with me.

“Yeah. I’m not all that inclined to let that stand, either.”

Frowning, Stephen said, “Well, unless we can find a way to hide our backgrounds, we’re going to have a very hard time saving anyone. And we still need to figure out how to get home.”

“I might be able to use my healing skill to perform cosmetic surgery,” I suggested. “I’ve pushed the skill quite a bit, and it is a core skill, so I should be able to use it without too much effort.”

“I’d honestly rather you didn’t,” Stephen said with a wince. “At least let me do some more preliminary reconnaissance before you do something that extreme.”

I couldn’t help but pout a bit at his comment. I understood that he didn’t want me to put myself at risk, but I felt the same way about him. The thought of having him enter the city alone honestly scared me.

“Oh, there’s something else I wanted to mention,” Stephen said a bit more brightly. “I don’t think they have dungeons like we’re used to. It seems more like they have rifts like what we had in the lower realms.”

“Do you think this is a lower realm?” I asked.

“No,” he replied, shaking his head. “I don’t think they are actually the same thing, just similar. They mostly seem to appear in populated areas, and they disappear as soon as they are cleared. I watched as a party left one and it disappeared behind them. Nobody seemed surprised by it, so that’s likely the norm here.”

That was interesting information, and if we happened upon one outside the city, it might provide a source of essence.

I wasn’t necessarily opposed to killing demons to replenish my lost essence, but doing so would put us at greater risk. Also, I didn’t think it was right to kill people when they hadn’t done anything to me.

It was one thing when they’d invaded our realm with the intent to slaughter people, but it was quite another when I was technically the invader. Besides, it wasn’t like the normal citizens were likely involved in such things.

“Did you see any outside the city?” I asked.

“A few, but they were already being monitored by demons. If you’re thinking we might be able to use them to progress, it’s probably not going to happen. I think they have some sort of technology that lets them know when a new rift appears.”

“Well, that’s disappointing,” I said. “What about wild beasts or creatures that escape from rift breaks?”

Stephen shook his head. “I didn’t see anything like that. That doesn’t mean they don’t exist, but I didn’t see any while I was out. With how quick the demons are to respond, I doubt many rifts end up breaking in the first place.”

I huffed out a sigh.

“On the bright side, if I can actually pass myself off as a demon, I might be able to enter one and learn more. I think I’ll also be able to acquire any supplies we might need, including potentially purchasing some Dian slaves. I know that’s not what you want to hear, but it’s the best idea I have at the moment.”

Obtaining slaves legally would undoubtedly be the safest option if we could pull it off. It would also allow us to gather some information directly from the slaves instead of making a bunch of assumptions.

Of course, we could also just wait around for the next time the incursion breach opened and sneak through. But now that I knew there were people who needed saving, I just couldn’t bring myself to abandon them.

“I don’t like it, but I understand,” I said. “I’m not sure what I can do to keep myself occupied since I can’t waste essence, though.”

“I saw you looking at the engravings earlier. If you want, I can see if I can find anything related to enchantments. I’m not sure you’ll be able to do anything with them, all things considered, but if you’re curious…”

The offer lifted my spirits slightly, and I agreed. Even if I couldn’t use this realm’s form of enchanting, learning about it would give me something to do with my time.

That was, of course, provided I had the ability to read and comprehend this realm’s language. Since I certainly didn’t understand the symbols carved into the ruin, I really couldn’t be sure that was the case.

Maybe it only applied to the spoken word, or maybe I missed out on whatever granted the ability to understand. Whatever the case, I needed to find something useful to do with my time.
