Aura converged. Pulses of it arrived from various directions, intermingling, combining into areas of higher and lower density. A will shaped them. There were rings upon rings, arranged in a row as if expecting a rapid hedgehog. A spear stabbed through. It gathered the rings—reaped them. Their power overlapped on its tip, each ring its own, then were flung forward. As they struck an obstacle, the first ring stopped, and was quickly crashed into by the second, then by the third. Their powers fused until, finally, the obstacle shattered, and the remaining rings flew through into the distance.

“I did it!” Gary cried out.

“Indeed, disciple,” Master Colorful said with a smile, watching the green strike fly over the garden wall and dissipate shortly after.

“It really was the angle, just like you said,” Gary said.

“Mm. You’d gotten it right already—all you needed was a little adjustment. I have to admit I underestimated you, disciple. I expected you to learn the second song towards the end of the tournament. With two days left in the first phase and then the entire finals, we might have time…”

“To learn the third song?” Gary asked hopefully.

“Yes, that for sure…” Colorful replied, his voice thoughtful. Calculating. “I will tell you something, disciple. I never thought you’d make it in time, so I never revealed this before. I have a way for you to beat Sui Duang.”

“What!?” Gary exclaimed.

“What’s the name of each song in the Nine Songs of the Spear?”

“Song of Mud, Song of Life… That’s as far as I know, Master.”

“It’s all one sequence. First comes mud. Life is born in it, rising to the sky and becoming rain. That’s the third song. Then, it descends again with woeful menace—the fourth song, the Song of Lightning.”

“Lightning? As in, my vibration?”

“Precisely. The power of this technique rises exponentially with each song, but a song’s power is even further increased if you can use its element. For you, the fourth song will be a double power spike—both because it’s a new song, and also because you specialize in lightning. You will be able to utilize its true potential. Moreover, lightning counters water. If you can learn the fourth song in time for your fight against Sui Duang, I believe you will have a good chance of defeating him. At the very least, you’ll give a good battle before you die.”

“Does this mean that without the fourth song, I don’t really stand a chance?”

“Sui Duang has trained much more than you. There is always a chance, but if you fought as you are now, I wouldn’t favor you.”

“That’s not what you said before!”

“I didn’t want to discourage you. You were aware of the situation. How can the number two disciple be easy to defeat?”

Gary crossed his arms. His gaze played dark. “I won’t lose,” he said.

“Resolve can only carry you so far, disciple. You need the skills to couple it, and to your honor, you really have worked hard. Even for you, however, learning the fourth song in time will be near impossible. You need to train even harder. Can you do it?”

“Of course I can. I just won’t sleep.”

“Well said. You probably won’t meet Sui Duang until the finals. The schedule will be announced in advance, in brackets, so we’ll know at which day you’ll meet Sui Duang. Until that day, you must overwork yourself, even if it means facing other opponents at less than your peak. As long as your mind can train, you will train. That is your only shot of making this work.”

“Yes, Master.”

“The songs become exponentially more complex as they become more powerful. Thankfully, you already understand lightning, so learning the fourth song won’t be too difficult. The real obstacle is the third song—the Song of Rain. It’s much more challenging than the Song of Life, which took you five days to learn, but you must learn it even faster. From now on, you will train here, in my courtyard, and I will oversee you to accelerate your progress. With the Tower’s blessings, we just might succeed…”

“I’m ready, Master!”

“Then watch.”

A stick flew from the ground to Master Colorful’s hands. He focused for a moment. Blue light shimmered on its surface, a collection of little aura streams that mingled but never fused. Suddenly, Colorful whipped the stick forward. The aura split into multiple drops like he’d thrown a handful of water, each drop clearly separate from the rest. They splashed on a bush. There came the sound of arrows. Holes were drilled into the bush and the dirt beneath, dozens of them, each as thin as a hair.

“I thought we weren’t targeting garden decoration,” Gary said.

“Who cares about bushes? The Sui are willy and powerful—anything less than our full strength will fail to tame them. Did you watch my strike?”

“I did.”

“What did you see?”

“Your aura was shaped as drops. It was like a dozen different thrusts launched at once.”

“Correct. The Song of Rain is a technique similar in spirit to your Nail Gun, though vastly different in application. In Nail Gun, you use lightning to stimulate your muscles, thrusting a dozen times in an instant. Those strikes are powerful, but there is still a delay between them. Competent opponents can deal with each thrust individually. The Song of Rain uses the concept of water to nullify that disadvantage. Aura is split into drops, each a miniature thrust, which are unleashed at precisely the same time.”

“Yeah. Good luck blocking that,” Gary said, eyeing the torn bush.

“The Song of Rain is best against fast opponents whom you can’t catch otherwise or defensive opponents whose guard you struggle to penetrate. However, it’s more than just speed or being unblockable. Each drop is not too sharp, but when they’re combined, they create a strike even mightier than the Song of Life.”

“I got it, Master. Please teach me!”

“Very well. Draw your spear. I want you to first use the Song of Mud and Song of Life—the songs are designed so they’re naturally used in sequence. As your spear returns from the Song of Life, place it where you’d formed the rings, reform them, and gather them around your spear while it’s sitting still. If done correctly, the rings will be drawn to your spear’s high aura density and stick to it. That’s the first step to using the Song of Rain.”

Gary did as he was told. “Nine Songs of the Spear: Song of Mud!” he roared. He vibrated his body in the precise way he’d learned to gather aura in his thrust’s trajectory, then stabbed his spear through it. It felt like splitting mud. The slow thrust erupted, shooting a brown projectile at the bush and annihilating it.

“Song of Life!”

He drew back his spear, sharpening his body’s vibration. The cloud of dense aura formed into rings—not perfectly shaped, but well enough—which the spear gathered on its way out. It took a greenish hue. The resulting aura strike seemed homogenous, but it was really made of successive rings moving at the same speed, guided by an aura thrust through their centers. It drilled into the ground where the bush used to stand, each successive ring burrowing deeper and shooting dirt everywhere. When it finally dissipated, a deep, spear-shaped hole was left in the ground.

Gary’s spear was already in position. This time, he didn’t pull it all the way back to the thrusting position, but instead kept it semi-extended in the area where the rings had formed. He channeled more aura to reform them. The process was different now. The rings formed wobbly, then collapsed into clouds the instant he tried pulling them towards the spear.

In that instant, as Gary was left with just a misformed Song of Mud, he realized where he’d gone wrong. His face turned ashen. He knew what he had to do, but to actually achieve it…

Crap.

He’d rushed the previous songs. He’d managed to use them consistently, sure, but the approaching duel had made him focus on their battle-readiness instead of perfecting them. For example, the rings he formed for the Song of Life weren’t perfect—some were ovals, others were warped, and most were wobbly and unsteady. They could get the job done without significant loss of power, and Gary had assumed that was fine.

But the songs built on top of each other. Structural weaknesses in one song were amplified in the next. To learn the third song, Gary had to first perfect the first and second songs, which meant that even more work had been added into the few days he had available.

“Master…”

“So you see,” Colorful said. “There is a vast difference between learning and mastering a song, and each song is used as the foundation of the next. No building can stand on shaky ground.”

“Is that why you said only geniuses can learn the fifth song?”

Colorful chuckled and shook his head. “The Nine Songs of the Spear are a core technique of our clan—how could it be easy? Your lightning vibration allows for some shortcuts in the Song of Lightning, so you don’t need to master the previous songs completely, but you still need a high degree of proficiency. What you’ve achieved is just the tip of the iceberg. You can only use the songs with a thrust. Ordinarily, I would have asked you to adapt each song to all the spear moves before moving on, but we don’t have time for that. The thrust will have to do.

“Of course, you’ll still have to master all the moves if you ever want to learn the fifth song, which is another massive spike in difficulty compared to the fourth. But that’s far in the future, and it requires a degree of aura control that you’ll probably fail to achieve during Birth of Aura. For now, we’ll master using the first two songs with the thrust, then do our best to make the third song even barely usable. That’s the bare minimum to start learning the fourth song, which is where your true hope lies.”

“Then, why didn’t you tell me this before? You knew I’d rushed the songs.”

“Some lessons, disciple, are better taught through experience.”

Gary sighed. “I understand. I’ll do whatever you tell me, Master, and work as hard as humanly possible.”

Colorful nodded. “Let’s get started.”

***

The moon hung high in the sky when Gary dragged himself back to his cabin. His eyes struggled to focus. Mental exhaustion was hitting him hard, and he had a fight tomorrow, but at least they’d made some progress. He was close to mastering the first two songs enough to start work on the third. The training required was grueling, but he was confident. He would do nothing else until his fight with Sui Duang. If he poured every available second into training, it would work. It had to.

Gary opened the cabin door and supported himself on its frame to enter. His bed seemed sweeter than ever. Cog’s steam-snoring was music to his ears. As soon as Gary closed the door, however, something was wrong. The air carried tension. He turned to find a man standing in the center of the cabin, tall and wide, with white hair and sharp green eyes. A trenchcoat covered most of his body, while his hands wore gloves. Below the trenchcoat hid a nice, sharp suit.

Gary tried to yell but found himself frozen. The very air constricted him. He couldn’t move an inch, and when he tried to summon his aura, it dissipated in the face of a vibration unimaginably more powerful than his own. He couldn’t even breathe.

To the side, Cog snored like nothing had happened.

“Greetings, Gary Steel. I’m Senior Inspector Whitefang from the Enforcement Hall of the Heaven Reaching clan’s main branch on the sixty-fifth floor. I’m investigating the suspected existence of a Muscle Sap distribution network in this branch. May I ask you some questions?”
