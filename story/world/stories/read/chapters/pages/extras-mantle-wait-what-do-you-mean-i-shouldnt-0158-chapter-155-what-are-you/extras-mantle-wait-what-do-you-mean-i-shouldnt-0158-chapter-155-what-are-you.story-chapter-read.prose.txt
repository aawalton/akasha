Jin lay back on the mattress and let the status panel hang over him like a mobile over a crib. At the thought of a mobile, he remembered playing Clash of Clans, reading novels, and watching streams.

Jin sighed. It's been almost a month since he magically reincarnated or transmigrated, whatever, from his world. Those memories of Ryujin Amane were getting weaker and weaker the stronger he got as Jin Winters.

It was probably for the best, considering that in his past life he was nothing but a cancer-stricken young man who would have died...

« Jin. »

I know, I know!

He chuckled and focused back on his skills.

“Four skills, two tokens, and one empty slot I kept forgetting I have available.” He mumbled.

« That’s cuz you keep ignoring me! »

“Right,” Jin said, and then smiled. “So basically your fault.”

« …Listen here, you litt—»

"Okay! Okay!” Jin interrupted. “Which two options do we choose to make me a monster?"

« Stuck inside an idiot. Before that, the empty slot. »

“Ah-huh,” Jin nodded. “What about it?”

« You really want me to do all the work, don’t you? »

“Now you're talking like a smart guide!”

Angel sighed. « I guess it goes without saying, [Essence Construct] is the top contender. With it being a marked skill, we would have more freedom with the skill. »

“See, that wasn’t so hard,” Jin said. “And yeah, I agree [Essence Construct] is the skill that I need.”

He'd been leaning harder and harder on his constructs to act as his defense—the sacrificial barriers, the spear-points, the flimsy little squares he fed to the Engine like kindling—every single time he gets into a fight.

« There are some more options. A dedicated shield skill, for starters… You will be losing on versatility, but the shield won’t be something that shatters the instant it does its one job.» Angel said. « Or an illusion suite, which, given you have just inherited a full set of mind arts off a conveniently dead ORDER IV priest, it would be criminally easy to build right now.»

“Possibly,” Jin nodded.

« Or a pure movement art… but—»

“But?”

«—but nothing beats constructs,» Angel admitted. « They are a shield when you need a shield, and a wall when you don't. A weapon, a launch pad, a battery you can throw at someone's head. The single most useful skill you own. I was all set to argue for a nice balanced loadout, Jin, and I have talked myself directly out of it in real time. It's very humbling. »

“Wonderful talks,”

« I do this so you feel involved in your own decisions. »

"Whatever," Jin said, and rolled off the bed.

He cleared the floor with a sweep of his arm. A mug and yesterday's shirt went skidding into the corner. Then he sat on the cold tile and drew his tempad, scrolling through the available skill cores.

« Could’ve done the same on bed… »

“Could’ve, but I would have to move away sooner or later,” Jin shrugged. “Besides, I like the cold, anyway.”

Soon a list containing all the available skill cores opened on the screen.

«—not be a problem anymore. You have gotten way stronger and experienced, Jin. Have faith in yourself. »

“If you say so…” Jin sighed. “Do I have these cores or?”

« You do. »

“Aight.” He nodded and looked through his spatial ring. And pulled out the cores one at a time, setting them in front of him.

He sat back on his heels and looked at the little constellation he'd made on the floor.

"Third time doing this," he murmured. "Still feels like a reckless thing to do."

« You do have a gift for reassuring imagery. »

"Pfft~" He chuckled. Then crossed his legs, set his breathing into the Eternal Sovereign's pattern, and went down his soul.

◈◈◈

His soul-space was quieter than it had been in weeks.

The raging storm was gone, and his soul space was like a settled sky. Three eternal sovereign stars burned in their fixed constellation, and at the centre was his mantle heart and essence heart.

It had cracks of gold running across its surface, pulsing like veins ever since the assimilation.

He didn't reach for his heart directly.

His chains rose off him—silver and burning cold—and Jin fed them his intent. He needed to be very specific about what he was doing; after all, extracting an acquired skill was unheard of, and so he gently probed and prodded for the skill he wanted over a long period. For once, he actually cursed his ever-growing list of skills.

This one.

The vague, half-formed thing on the heart’s southern face, the little constellation of runes, was the formation of the essence construct skill. The chains found the shape and closed on it, delicate as a jeweller's tweezers going after a splinter.

“Easy… Got it!”

He peeled.

It was a deeply etched formation, and he had to put far more effort and concentration into peeling it out because it turned out he'd used constructs far, far more than he'd ever be caught admitting out loud.

He held it suspended.

“Angel!” He said.

The cores in the real world melted into him one by one, and Jin in the soul space halted and redirected their flow before they took the marked skill’s slot. He brought the core's essence up to meet his essence contract’s formation.

For a long moment, the skill fought him like they always did. One thing he had realized: skills didn't want to become other skills; they wanted to stay exactly what they were, small and safe and known, and say thank you very much to the one using them. Alas for them, Jin wanted them to merge.

He held the shape steady and insisted, aligning his will to his mantle, the boon whispering underneath it all—the harvest blade learns new domains—and the fragments stopped fighting and started accepting thier fate.

The formation flared, drank the cores whole, and finally settled after a long, agonizing while.

He surfaced with a gasp, sweat gone cold on his neck, and the status rewrote itself in the air in front of him.

•••

▸ [Elite] Thermokinetic Engine (Growth) (135)

▸ [Elite] Spell Weaver's Matrix (125)

▸ [Adept] Construct (Growth) (51)

"Phew~ and a growth type. Damn!" Jin said, and grinned like an idiot at the empty room. It was ridiculous how good it felt just to see the bracket read full.

« The skill will climb fast. You'll feed it every fight without thinking, same as you always have. »

“Now that’s done, I remember Joe and Salvatore had been trying to tell me something about the tokens.” He mumbled. “Something like You can't just slap them on whatever you fancy, Jin.”

He'd never gotten the whole sentence out of them before other pressing matters that needed his attention.

“Angel. Do the tokens have conditions? Beyond the ones printed on the screen.” He asked.

« Not that I can see.» Angel's tone sharpened into the careful register he used when he suspected he was about to be proven wrong. « The token text says it bypasses thresholds and progression conditions. On paper it should eat anything you point it at... »

"Only one way to find out," Jin said.

« And it's your favourite way apparently. Being reckless. »

He stuck his tongue out at Angel and then fished a token out of his ring.

“Okay, let’s see…” He pressed it flat against the brand-new Construct skill in the panel.

A prompt bloomed.

[Expend Ability Upgrade Token ×2 to advance [Construct] from [Adept] to [Master]?]

Then, half a heartbeat behind it, a second box in hard, unfriendly red.

[Request denied. Skill [Construct] has not attained sufficient mastery and understanding. This skill may advance to [Adept] only. Achieve adequate understandings before pursuing [Master] rank.]

Jin stared at it.

"Ahhh," he said, long and low, and let his head tip back. "There it is..." He shook it. "Good to know. Great."

« Fascinating,» Angel said, and the maddening thing was that he actually meant it. « So the token cannot conjure a Master or the rank beyond it… out of nothing. »

"Which put us back to our original discussion," Jin's eyes tracked back up the status. Doom at one-twenty-five. Spellshot at one-oh-nine. The Matrix at one-twenty-five. The Engine sitting fat and dangerous at one-thirty-five.

He tapped a knuckle against his knee. "All four are peak Elite, and I believe all four should qualify." He blew out a breath. "And I've got two tokens."

« Indeed, that brings us back to square one. »

“It’s not a good habit to repeat stuff, Angel,” He chuckled.

« … »

Jin leaned back and thought back to the question Joe had asked him. Who are you, Jin? What are you actually striving to be?

Jin used guns, but he wasn't a gunman. He used magic, but in a roundabout way, which made him disqualified as a mage.

The only thing he had done with consistency was harvesting and reaping his enemies.

Jin closed his eyes.

He reached for the grey. Not to fight anything this time… just to look at himself without immediately flinching away. [Sovereign's Indifference] came down over him cool and even, and he stepped back into the dark behind his own eyes and let the question rise up to meet him.

What are you?

It didn't come as words. It came as forms… shapes he could reach out and try on like coats off a rack.

Guns… Iron Howl in his fist. He shrugged it on. It fit across the shoulders and pulled tight at the chest, because for all the noise he made, he had never once won a fight with the gun and the gun alone.

He let it fall.

A mage. The circles he'd never finished, the grimoire he still hadn't opened, the elegant, proper, respectable spell theory he kept promising himself he'd sit down and learn the moment the world stopped trying to end. It hung off him like a coat bought for a slimmer man he intended to become someday. A lie he told about later.

He let it fall.

A harvester. That one clung. Chains and yield, the thing his own titles kept carving over his door in letters too big to ignore. One Who Harvested Divinity. The Harvester. It fit better than the other two, and it still wasn't the whole of him, because he had never once harvested for the harvesting's sake—only ever to get his hands on the next thing he needed to survive the next thing that wanted him dead—

And there it was. Under the grey, down in the dark, the plain shape of the answer, sitting where it had probably been sitting the whole time.

He didn't reach for another coat. He looked down at his own hands instead.

“I'm not the gun. I'm not the magic or the circle. I'm not even the chains.”

The words came out flat and certain, without a scrap of the panic they'd have carried a month ago.

“I'm the man who picks up whatever the moment hands him and makes it kill. Bullet, spell, bomb, borrowed power… doesn't matter what's in reach. I just become whatever the fight needs me to be.”

The dark didn't answer him.

It was his eternal sovereign stars that agreed.

Jin opened his eyes and let out a breath.

"Okay… The Engine and the Matrix," he said. "Not Doom. Not Spellshot. For now it’s them."

« Your reasons, please, » Angel said. «So that I know you didn't simply point at the two biggest numbers and call it wisdom.»

"The Engine's the load-bearing wall of everything I do and have been doing lately. It’s safe to say that is the core part of my skillset." Jin climbed to his feet, joints popping, and started to pace the little room out of pure nervous energy. "And in the engine everything runs on charges… kinetic and thermal charges… my mobility, my burst, my powers, so pushing it to master is the most optimized answer." He rolled his shoulders. "But… you were the one who spent a week convinced my body couldn't handle the Engine going full-song. That much throughput, even at the Adept rank, was cooking me from the inside out. You said so. Repeatedly. With diagrams, if I may mention."

« I did. It would. The diagrams were very good. »

"Which is exactly why the Matrix and why it becomes master rank first." He almost smiled, and there wasn't much humour in it, just the clean satisfaction of a plan clicking together. "Better foundation, less of me getting cooked.”

« That is a genuinely good plan.»

"Noted and treasured." Jin pulled another token from his ring, held one in each hand, and drew a slow breath that shook only a little on the way in. "Matrix first.”

He pressed the first token flat over his heart.

◈◈◈

The token melted.

There was honestly no better word for it, same as the skill cores.

The metal went soft against his skin, then sank through it, and a heartbeat later it struck his mantle heart like a nail driven clean into the centre of him.

Pain… but not the soul-wrenching agony he'd braced for.

His new foundation and stats caught most of the pain, spread it wide and thin across his body. It hurt the way straightening a badly set bone hurts, and he was okay with that.

He felt the runic formation of the Matrix take charge and slowly unfold.

It didn't just stay in his mantle, heart... It raced to every part of his body, roots of the matrix flowing along his circulatory system and carving a new place for themselves.

Master-rank skills left a mark on the body, Joe had told him, and only now did Jin understand that some of those marks were like tattoos that sat on the outside where people could see them, and some of them were deep inside.

For the matrix, it went straight inward, down every essence channel he owned, rewriting them as it went. Old crooked pathways he'd worn by accident and never fixed straightened and widened. Dead-end capillaries he hadn't known were choking him opened up into arteries. The whole muddled, improvised mess he'd been shoving power reknit itself into something clean, dense and deliberate, a lattice… a formation… a system with real intent behind every junction.

Jin arched up off the floor, making a low sound through his teeth at the deep discomfort this brought him. He wanted to scratch an itch. He wanted to bite down on that, but couldn’t.

That went on and on for almost twenty minutes before it settled, and his system told him of the success.
