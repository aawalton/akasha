Chapter 82 - Forfeit

The world snapped into motion.

Zephyr lunged first.

Kaelin barely had time to register the blur of movement before a wall of thorned vines erupted from the ground, splitting the plane between them in two. She dove left, rolling across the uneven earth as the vines lashed the space where her torso had been a heartbeat earlier.

He wasn’t holding back.

Zephyr landed on a flat pillar of stone that surged upward beneath him, giving him instant high ground. Mud slid from his boots as he stabilized, one hand raised, one foot braced. His Nature threads pulsed bright green around his shoulders; steady, controlled and terrifyingly efficient.

“Kaelin,” he called down, “don’t make this harder than it needs to be, please.”

She wiped mud from her palms, breath steady. “What was the point in asking? You know I’m not the type to give in.”

A ghost of a smile touched his mouth. Then he moved.

Kaelin reacted instantly.

The world slowed around her, moving like warm honey. She felt the rush slam into her veins—a clear-cut clarity—and she sprinted toward the next ridge, her feet striking the ground in near-silence.

Zephyr’s arm lifted, curling his fingers. He didn’t move quite as quickly as Kaelin, but it wasn’t too far off. But unlike Zephyr, she was also working her time threads elsewhere, to the future.

She stared off into the space behind him. Though careful not to lose her footing, she focused on what was to come: five, ten seconds ahead. Spikes forming. A burst of vines. A rolling wave of earth.

She dodged before he’d even tried to cast a spell.

Her body blurred, slipping through the gaps in unfolding futures, twisting past a boulder that hadn’t yet risen. She sprinted up the angled stone, her speed multiplied tenfold.

Zephyr’s eyes widened. He hadn’t seen that coming.

But Kaelin had.

She leapt, fist glowing with the bite of accelerated momentum—

—and then pain blossomed through her ribs as something slammed into her side, but despite seeing nothing, she knew the pain was real.

“An illusion…” she muttered quietly.

“Oh, how’d you guess.” Zephyr asked sarcastically, a smug grin spreading across his face.

Her vision snapped back to real-time. The threads gifting her a speed enhancement slipped from her mind as the pain took the forefront of her thoughts, and she crashed onto the ground, skidding until her palms burned.

“Dammit—” she gasped, rolling to her feet.

Zephyr stood on a new pedestal, breath slightly uneven. “I bet you didn’t see that in your little predictions.”

She spat dirt from her mouth. “Stop being so patronising, no shit I didn’t. How do you expect me to if you hide it behind one of your illusions. Fucking dual-affinity crap.”

"Tri, I’ve got a third, or did you forget in all your… basic… ness.”

“just stop Zephyr, you’re embarrassing yourself.”

He thrust both hands forward.

Roots exploded from the dirt, twisting together into a spear of bramble. Zephyr hurled it with brutal force. Kaelin ducked, letting it splinter against a tree behind her.

I’ve got to work to my strengths.

She cut the air with her hand. A bubble of pale yellow time distortion rippled as it surrounded Zephyr, bending light around it. The world slowed, his movements dragging like he was wading through syrup.

Kaelin didn’t wait for him to catch up and use his own. She surged forward, her speed enhancement coming back into action crackling around her ankles, every step a blurred streak in the forest.

She reached him in seconds, threads humming dangerously, then she froze mid-strike.

Something shimmered at the edge of her vision. A silver flicker. A whisper of movement.

He was an illusion.

Zephyr’s silhouette split into three.

“All right,” he called from somewhere behind her, his voice echoing wrong. “I might be pretty bad at Mind magic, but I guess I don’t need much.”

She clenched her jaw. “You coward.”

"Woah, chill, stop getting pissed just cause you’re losing. I mean, It’s just meant to be a friendly match”

Her future visions fractured, the illusions messing with her predictions. Three Zephyrs, three sets of potential attacks, all overlapping in some part. She clenched her fists, trying to filter the noise.

One vision showed a wall of earth rising beneath her.

Another showed a vine whip aimed at her legs.

The last showed a stone shard flying at her head.

She tried to choose—tried to pick the right future—but her thoughts clashed, tripping over themselves. Pain spiked behind her eyes.

“Dammit, just stop!”

She dodged left.

Wrong choice.

A stone column shot upward and clipped her jaw. Stars burst behind her eyelids as she spun backward, collapsing onto one elbow.

Zephyr’s real figure stepped through the illusions, the fakes dissolving like smoke. He panted lightly, sweat dripping from his brow. His boots sank several inches into fresh mud—and Kaelin realized why.

He had armored himself.

Earth coated his limbs in hardened plates, his shoulders thick with living bark, his fists encased in jagged stone. Roots wrapped his ankles like greaves.

It was just like what he’d done against Daniel way back then, albeit a little different.

He looked like the forest had swallowed him and decided to keep him.

“Still think you can take me?” he asked, voice steady but strained.

Kaelin wiped blood from her lip and rose slowly. Her legs trembled, but she forced them still.

“Anytime.”

She snapped her fingers, and once again, the world slowed to a crawl.

Kaelin inhaled sharply and activated her full Time Sight. Hoping that maybe this time she might be able to notice the illusions before she gets jumped.

The futures unfolded like scrolls in her mind—every angle, every dodge, every possible combination. Twelve seconds ahead. Twelve seconds of clarity, twelve seconds of brutal, unforgiving precision.

She sprinted.

Zephyr tried to counter, his Time threads flaring pale yellow as he finally managed to get up to speed with his own enhancement. But despite everything, he only managed two thirds of the speed which Kaelin had. It wasn’t enough.

Their fists collided.

She ducked a vine lash. Slid beneath a stone fist. Vaulted over a crack splitting the earth.

She reached him with her fist drawn back, moving at almost triple her normal speed.

As she went in for a strike to Zephyr’s face, Kaelin noticed something which she hadn’t realised before: In her visions of the future, one spot seemed imperfect, it didn’t quite match the rest of the forest.

Then it clicked.

It was A future she couldn’t avoid, not now, she’d noticed it too late.

But at least now she knew that Zephyr’s illusions weren’t perfect, and she knew what to look out for.

Kaelin kept her sight trained on Zephyr as something hit her on the right side of her face.

She wouldn’t miss it next time.

The impact sent her flying to the side. She flipped, air rushing past her ears, and hit the ground hard enough to see all sorts of shapes and colours whizzing around.

She lay there on her side, chest heaving, mud sticking to her cheek.

Zephyr stood over her, breathing ragged, armor crumbling around him.

“I guess you’re not as good as I thought. I really expected more of a challenge from you.”

Kaelin groaned, rolling onto her back. “I could’ve won if I wanted, I just didn’t feel like embarrassing you today.”

“Get up.”

She laughed once, weakly. “Or what.”

“Just get up, and fight me for real. I don’t want to waste my time and energy if you’re not gonna go all out.”

Thunder cracked high above the arena.

Both of them froze.

A voice echoed magically across the battlefield:

“Two combatants remaining.”

Kaelin froze.

Zephyr froze.

The arena went silent except for the faint ringing in Kaelin’s ear which had been yet to fade.

Kaelin blinked once. “Two?”

Zephyr let out a slow breath. “That’s… fast.”

Kaelin snorted. “Guess they all took each other out. At least now you won’t have to pummel another poor soul.”

Zephyr shook his head, lips twitching. “You know you deserve it. What would be the point in going easy on you. You can take it.”

“Either way…” She cracked her neck, bending her knees into a ready stance. “looks like it’s just us.”

Zephyr’s expression hardened, the easy banter evaporating.

“For top spot,” he said.

"Hah, hell no. I don’t care for the top spot, I’m just doing this cause I know you want it so badly”

Then they moved.

No hesitation this time, and no restraint, no more holding back. Their threads snapped alive in the same instant, time bending and twisting in a chaotic braid around them.

Kaelin flashed forwards and Zephyr countered with a stone wall, rising from the earth.

Kaelin vaulted it, causing Zephyr to shatter it beneath her, shards of rock flying in all directions.

She saw five seconds ahead: a vine whip aimed at her calves.

Kaelin twisted to the side in an attempt to avoid it, but she didn’t see the second attack.

Zephyr’s fist slammed into the ground. A shockwave burst outward, tearing the dirt apart and forcing her backward. She caught herself on a knee, breath ripping from her lungs.

I might not be able to hold out much longer, Kaelin thought to herself

Then—

“Kaelin!”

A voice, hoarse and terrified.

It was Aria.

She stumbled into the arena from the north entrance sprinting through the artificial forest. Mud sat in streaks down her arms, sweat plastering her hair to her face. She ran straight toward Kaelin, grabbing her wrist before Kaelin could do more than blink.

Her voice dropped to a whisper, urgent and trembling.

“It’s Lena. Something’s wrong.”

Kaelin’s heart stopped.

Zephyr stared, confusion and caution mixing on his face. “What’s—what happened?”

Kaelin didn’t respond.

She pulled her hand out of Aria’s grip, eyes wide, threaded with rising panic.

“Oh shit.”

Zephyr flinched at the tone alone. “Kaelin—?”

She shouted across the arena, “I’m gonna have to forfeit this one. But I’ll get you back next time!”

Then she ran, bolted out of the arena. Faster than she had moved the entire fight. Threads snapped awake around her feet as she vanished through the archway of the arena’s eastern exit.

“Kaelin!” Zephyr’s voice cracked, fear slipping past his usual control. “Kaelin, wait!”

But she was gone. Already out of sight, just around the corner.

Zephyr didn’t stop to think. He leapt off the platform he’d formed, sprinting after her.

By the time he reached the exit, the entire courtyard was empty.

Kaelin was nowhere to be found.

And dread curled cold and sharp under his ribs.

Finally, it hit him, what Aria had said just before they’d left.

“Lena…”
