Hope tasted like stale air and copper, but it was hope nonetheless.

The subtle change in the tunnel’s descent told Cade what his eyes couldn’t yet confirm—they were approaching the heart of this nightmare labyrinth. Each step carried them deeper into the ancient construct’s bowels, the walls no longer merely stone but something organic, something alive with malevolent patience. The crypt’s heartbeat pulsed through the floor beneath their feet, no longer distant but immediate, visceral.

They were close. So fucking close.

“The air’s changing,” Orro observed, his voice barely above a whisper as he helped Rayka navigate a particularly treacherous section of the passage. The assassin’s wounds had been hastily dressed during their brief rest, but fresh blood seeped through makeshift bandages with each careful movement. “Heavier. More metallic.”

“Like a storm about to break,” Gav agreed, his heightened senses alert for dangers lurking in the gloom. The telepath’s partial shift had receded, but amber still ringed his pupils, a wolf lurking just beneath the aristocrat’s veneer.

Cade nodded, tasting that same electrical tension on his tongue. His silver core hummed beneath his skin, responding to whatever energy permeated this place, drawn forward like a compass needle toward a lodestone. The destruction magic within him practically sang with anticipation, eager for what awaited at their journey’s end.

“We need to be ready for anything,” he warned, though the words felt hollow against the vastness of what they’d already survived. How do you prepare for the unimaginable? How do you steel yourself against horrors not meant for mortal comprehension?

Easy answer: you don’t. You just keep moving, one foot in front of the other, dragging what remains of your sanity along for the ride.

Evie drifted closer to Nora, their shadows merging in the phosphorescent glow that guided their descent. The siren’s injured hand remained cradled against her chest, but her eyes had regained their focus, determination replacing pain. Guildford had faded to little more than a translucent outline around her staff, conserving what energy remained after his dramatic entrance and equally dramatic battle performance.

“It feels like we’re being watched,” Elena murmured, her whip-blade coiled tight in her fist. Blood still stained her leathers where the pegasi had torn through flesh, but her steps remained sure, her gaze constantly scanning for new threats. “Not just by the crypt itself, but by something else. Something... waiting.”

“I smell old stone and new blood,” Jer quipped, though his usual levity seemed forced, strained at the edges. “And opportunities for heroic glory, of course. Can’t forget those.”

Cade caught Orro shooting the acrobat a look that contained something he’d never seen there before—not the assassin’s usual tolerant disdain for Jer’s antics, but something closer to genuine respect, perhaps even affection. Whatever they’d faced together while separated from the rest of the crew had forged a connection Cade wouldn’t have thought possible twenty-four hours earlier.

Chubbs rode on Cade’s shoulders, her tiny hands clutching his hair with surprising gentleness. The toddler goddess had been uncharacteristically quiet since their reunion, her usual endless stream of observations reduced to occasional murmurs too soft for even Cade’s silver-ranked hearing to decipher.

“You okay up there, squirt?” he asked, reaching up to adjust her position as they navigated a particularly steep section.

“The dark is listening,” she replied, her small voice carrying a certainty that sent shivers racing along Cade’s spine. “It knows our names.”

Cade exchanged a glance with Rayka, whose concern for the child was mirrored in the tight set of her lips. “The dark can listen all it wants,” he told Chubbs with more confidence than he felt. “We’re just passing through. No need to introduce ourselves.”

The tunnel widened suddenly, opening into a rectangular chamber that brought their procession to a halt. Unlike the organic, flowing architecture they’d encountered thus far, this space was rigid, deliberate—a perfect oblong crafted from smooth stone blocks fitted with supernatural precision. The ceiling soared at least thirty feet above their heads, disappearing into shadows despite the bioluminescent fungi that clung to the walls.

Across from them stood a single door, massive and imposing, carved from what appeared to be a solid slab of obsidian. Above it, an inscription had been chiseled into the stone in characters that shifted and reformed as Cade watched, rearranging themselves from incomprehensible glyphs into something almost readable.

But what drew his attention—what filled his gut with immediate, instinctive dread—were the manacles. Seven sets of them, embedded in the left wall at regular intervals, heavy iron chains anchoring them to the stone. They hung at different heights, as if designed to accommodate various sizes of captive, from adult to child.

“I don’t like this,” Nora stated cautiously, her hand automatically dropping to her greatsword’s hilt.

Before any of them could respond, the entrance behind them slammed shut with the finality of a headsman’s axe. Stone ground against stone, sealing their only retreat with seamless precision. The sound echoed through the chamber, a death knell that resonated with the crypt’s persistent heartbeat.

“That’s new,” Jer observed dryly. “Usually places wait until we’ve stolen something before dramatically locking us in.”

“Oh gods,” Elena hissed, her attention fixed on the floor. “Look.”

Water.

A thin rivulet at first, seeping from nearly invisible seams where wall met floor. Then more—tiny waterfalls emerging from hidden channels in the ceiling, cascading down in sheets that quickly transformed from trickle to torrent.

“Oh, that’s just perfect,” Cade growled, already calculating the chamber’s volume and their remaining time. “The one time we could use a water mage, and all I’ve got is magic that can burn and blow stuff.” He turned to Nora. “You know some water magic, right?”

Nora shook her head once. “It was Wavebreaker, my sect’s sword. I know the sword techniques, but without that blade, I’m useless here. Even my dagger can’t control water it hasn’t conjured.”

The water rose with unnatural speed, already ankle-deep and climbing rapidly. Within minutes, judging by the rate of flow, they’d be completely submerged. Drowning in darkness, their journey ended not by monsters or traps, but by the simplest and most fundamental of elements.

“The inscription,” Rayka called, her voice nearly drowned out by the thunderous cascade. “Can anyone read it?”

Cade passed Chubbs to Gav, then waded toward the far door, fighting against the current that threatened to sweep his feet from under him. The water had already reached mid-calf, cold enough to make his muscles cramp on contact. He squinted up at the shifting characters, trying to make sense of the constantly rearranging glyphs.

“It’s not static,” he called back, bracing himself against the rising torrent. “The language keeps changing—wait. There’s a pattern. It’s cycling through different scripts.”

Nora joined him, her greater height offering better leverage against the rising waters. “Those are shifting scrits. I think they’re moving from Progenitor to Ancient Sylvan to Elder Scrollhand,” she observed, her paladin training, combined with her years of secretly being a huge bookworm, allowing her to recognize what Cade could not. “Then it repeats.”

Together they watched the cycle, waiting for the moment when the characters formed something they could interpret. Water surged around their waists now, the temperature dropping further as the chamber continued to fill. Behind them, the others had gathered in a tight circle, Gav holding Chubbs above his head while Rayka supported Elena, whose injured leg had begun to buckle in the powerful current.

“There,” Cade pointed as the glyphs momentarily coalesced into recognizable script. “That segment reads ‘to leave’ in Elder Scrollhand.”

“And that portion,” Nora indicated a different section, “means ‘one must stay’ in Ancient Sylvan.”

The message repeated, shifting through languages but maintaining its core meaning: To leave, one must stay.

“What the hell does that mean?” Elena demanded, her voice strained as she fought to maintain her footing in the rising water.

The answer came not in words but in a metallic clinking from the left wall. The manacles rattled against stone, as if animated by invisible hands, chains stretching toward them with hungry purpose.

Cold realization washed over Cade, more chilling than the water that now reached his chest.

“It’s a sacrifice chamber,” he said, the words falling like stones into the chaos of rushing water. “One person has to stay behind, chained to the wall, for the rest to escape.”

Silence fell, broken only by the roar of water and the persistent clinking of empty manacles. The horror of it—the cruel simplicity—struck them all at once. One life traded for many. A single sacrifice to save the group.

“No,” Rayka was the first to break the silence, her voice cracking with emotion. “No, absolutely fucking not. There has to be another way.”

“Maybe we can break the door,” Gav suggested, desperation edging his normally cultured tones as he struggled to keep Chubbs above the rising waterline. “Combined strength, focused on a single point—”

“It’s sealed with the same magic as the rest of the crypt,” Nora countered, her tactical mind already assessing the situation with brutal honesty. “We don’t have the tools or the time.”

The water climbed higher, now at neck level for the shorter members of their crew. Elena had been forced to swim, her injured leg no longer able to support her against the current. Jer kept one arm around his twin, helping her stay afloat as the chamber continued to fill with merciless efficiency.

“I’ll do it,” Cade announced, the decision crystallizing in his mind with perfect clarity. Leaders protect their people. This was his crew, his responsibility. If someone had to pay for passage, it should be him. “Get to the other side as soon as the door opens. Get the Crown, get out.”

“Absolutely not,” Orro’s voice cut through the chaos like one of his blades. “You’re the only one who can match Bernard’s power. Without you, the mission fails.”

“This isn’t a democracy,” Cade snapped, already pushing through the water toward the manacles, intent on his purpose.

“You’re right, it’s not,” Nora agreed, and Cade felt a momentary surge of relief that someone understood—until she continued. “It should be me. My fear magic has proven inconsistent in these depths, and my sword can be wielded by others.”

“Fuck that,” Orro snarled, his usual composure shattered by the dire circumstances. “If anyone stays, it’s me. I’ve been living on borrowed time since Grassblade exiled me. This was always how it was going to end for me.”

“How dare you?” Rayka’s voice broke, rage and terror fusing into something primal as she rounded on Orro. “After everything—after all of it—you think you get to just throw your life away? Like it means nothing? Like we mean nothing?”

“I didn’t say—” Orro began, but Rayka cut him off with a vicious slash of her hand.

“You didn’t have to say it. I can see it in your eyes—that same gods-damned martyr complex you’ve had since the day I met you. Well, I won’t let you. Not this time.”

“By that logic, it should be me,” Gav argued, his voice rising over their exchange as he passed Chubbs to Evie despite the rising water. “The beast is too volatile, too dangerous. I’m the greatest liability.” His laugh held no humor, only bitter resignation. “I’ve already lost myself to the wolf once down here. It’s only a matter of time before I lose the battle permanently.”

“Ridiculous,” Rayka shot back, tears mingling with the water streaming down her face. “If anyone stays, it should be someone without combat abilities. I’m the least magically gifted—”

“Are you actually fucking serious right now?” Elena’s voice cracked through the argument like a whip, raw with fury. “My brother risks his life to save you from undead Arbiters, and this is how you repay him? By volunteering to die because you think you’re expendable?”

“That’s not what I—” Rayka tried to explain, but Elena was beyond reason, beyond consolation.

“None of you get to die,” she snarled, her grief transforming into something feral and desperate. “None of you. We came in together, we go out together.”

“Stop it,” Cade commanded, his voice cutting through the cacophony of arguments. “All of you. This isn’t helping. We need to make a decision before the water makes it for us.”

The water had reached chin level for most of them now, forcing them to tread water to keep their heads above the surface. Evie struggled to keep both herself and Chubbs afloat, the toddler goddess clinging to her neck with wide, solemn eyes that reflected the chamber’s dim light.

“I won’t let anyone sacrifice themselves,” Cade declared, his voice rough with emotion. “That’s not who we are. That’s not what this family does.”

“Then what’s your brilliant solution?” Orro demanded, his dark eyes burning with an intensity Cade had never seen before. “Because in about two minutes, we all drown. Is that a better outcome? All of us dead instead of one?”

“There has to be another option,” Rayka pleaded, her composure completely shattered now, her voice breaking on every word. “Please, there has to be. I can’t—I can’t lose any of you. Not after everything we’ve survived.”

The manacles rattled again, more insistent this time, chains stretching toward them with predatory intent. The message was clear: choose, or the choice would be made for them by the rising waters.

“I’ll do it,” Jer announced, his voice carrying a calm certainty that silenced all argument. Gone was the jester, the prankster, the eternal optimist. In his place stood someone Cade barely recognized—a man who had faced death once already today and found himself unafraid.

“No,” Elena’s voice broke, the syllable raw with horror. “No, Jer. Don’t you dare. Don’t you fucking dare.”

“It makes the most sense,” he replied, a strange peace settling over his features as he treaded water. “I’m not a mage. I’m not a warrior. I’m an acrobat who got lucky enough to find a family when I thought I’d lost everything.”

“That’s exactly why it can’t be you,” Cade argued, fighting against the current to reach him. “We need you, Jer. Your optimism, your heart—”

“My decision,” he cut Cade off, already swimming toward the wall of manacles. “You’d all do the same. Any one of you would sacrifice everything for the others. Hell, you were all just arguing over who gets to die, like it’s some kind of prize.” He laughed, though the sound held no mirth. “Let me have this one win, okay?”

“Jer, please,” Elena begged, struggling against Gav’s restraining arm as she tried to follow her twin. “Don’t do this. I can’t—I can’t lose you. We’re supposed to go together. Always together. You promised me, you bastard, you promised after mom died—”

“And I’m keeping that promise,” Jer replied, his voice steady despite the tears streaming down his face. “Two halves of the same whole, remember? I’ll always be with you, El. What’s a little thing like death compared to that?”

“I swear to all the gods,” Cade shouted, using every ounce of authority he possessed, “if you click those manacles shut, I will personally drag you back from whatever afterlife claims you and murder you again myself.”

“That’d be something to see,” Jer replied, his smile never wavering as he reached the wall. The manacles seemed to sense his approach, chains rattling with anticipation. “Think of this as my final performance. Take care of my sister, would you?”

“Stop him,” Elena begged, turning desperate eyes to Cade. “Please. Use your magic. Do something. Anything. I can’t lose him, Cade. He’s all I have left.”

“You have us,” Rayka tried to comfort her, but Elena shoved her away with surprising force.

“It’s not the same,” she screamed, raw grief transforming her face into something barely recognizable. “He’s my twin. My heart. Without him—” Her voice broke, the words dissolving into a sob that seemed torn from the deepest part of her soul.

Cade knew the truth—this wasn’t something he could stop with destruction magic or clever words. Jer had made his choice, and in the depths of his soul, beneath the grief and rage, Cade recognized the same determination he’d have shown in his position.

No! Cade thought as he gritted his teeth through the tears that blinded his vision. I refuse!

The Son of Ruin began to swim, forcing his destruction magic to form tight beams of condensed wind. They propelled him forward, but the water had already shoved him to the opposite side of the large room.

He pushed harder.

The water had reached the ceiling now, leaving only a pocket of air that shrank with each passing second. They treaded water frantically, gasping for breath in the diminishing space. The manacles clinked against stone, waiting for their volunteer.

“I love you guys,” Jer said simply, his eyes meeting each of theirs in turn. “Best damn family a thief could ask for. Now get ready to swim.”

“NO!” Cade shouted, lunging through the water toward him, rage and desperation fueling his movements. “It should be me! Please, let it be me. I’m the leader—it’s my job to protect all of you!”

Jer looked back at him, love for his family written plainly in his eyes and sad smile. “Sorry, Cade, but I get to choose how I go, and what better way than for those I love?”

Before anyone could stop him, Jer slapped the manacles around his wrists. The iron cuffs snapped shut with a sound that seemed to vibrate through the water itself. For one breathless moment, nothing happened—just Jer, chained to the wall, water lapping at his chin as he stared back at them with that unshakable smile.

Then the obsidian door across the chamber shuddered and began to rise.

Water rushed toward the opening with catastrophic force, creating a current so powerful it tore them from their positions. Cade caught a final glimpse of Jer’s face—peaceful, determined, his smile never wavering—before the torrent swept him away, tumbling through darkness toward whatever waited beyond.

He fought against the current, trying to swim back, to reach Jer, to tear those damned manacles from the wall through sheer force of will if necessary. But the water’s power was absolute, carrying them helplessly forward into the unknown.

“JER!” Elena’s scream tore through the chaos, raw with grief, before water filled Cade’s mouth, silencing further protest.

They surfaced in another chamber, smaller than the first, the water draining away through unseen channels with the same unnatural speed with which it had risen. Cade gasped, lungs burning as he sprawled across cold stone, every muscle screaming from the effort of staying alive.

“Everyone check in,” he managed between ragged breaths, forcing himself to his knees. “Rayka?”

“Here,” came her soft reply, her voice thick with unshed tears.

“Nora? Evie?”

“Present,” Nora confirmed, already helping a shivering Evie to her feet. Chubbs clung to the siren, golden eyes wide with shock but physically unharmed.

“Gav?” Cade continued, dread building as he tallied their survivors.

“I’m alright,” the telepath replied, though his voice suggested the opposite.

“Elena?” His heart sank as he spotted her, curled into herself at the chamber’s edge, shoulders shaking with silent sobs.

But it was the final name that stuck in his throat, that refused to emerge despite his desperate wish to hear a familiar voice respond with sardonic humor.

“Orro?” Cade called, scanning the chamber with increasingly frantic intensity. “Orro, report!”

Silence answered. Terrible, absolute silence.

“He was right behind me,” Rayka whispered, horror dawning across her features as she too searched for the assassin. “When the door opened. He was right there.”

They scoured the chamber, calling Orro’s name with increasing desperation, but the truth became inescapable with each passing second. Orro was gone—not with them, but not left behind with Jer either. Just... gone, vanished in the chaos of their near-drowning.

And as if to mock their loss, a black orb rolled across the stone floor—identical in size and design to the blue and green spheres they’d already collected, but pulsing with obsidian light that drank the chamber’s meager illumination. The crypt’s final trial completed, its prize delivered as promised, paid for with Jer’s life and possibly Orro’s as well.

Cade stared at the cursed thing, rage building in his chest until he thought he might shatter from the force of it. Two of his crew. Two of his family. Gone, taken by this nightmare labyrinth in the span of heartbeats.

Elena looked up from her grief-stricken huddle, her eyes finding the orb with pure hatred. “We didn’t want it,” she whispered, voice raw with tears. “None of this was worth his life. None of it.” She met the young thief’s eyes. “I blame you. You should’ve done something, you bastard.”

Cade couldn’t argue. Couldn’t find words to justify what had just happened. Couldn’t explain how they were supposed to continue after this, how they were supposed to function with these gaping wounds in their collective soul.

Elena O’Keefe sniffed and then dropped the orb unceremoniously, turning her back on Cade.

No one moved.

His hands trembled as he collected the black orb, its weight far heavier than its size should allow. Three orbs now, each paid for in blood and pain. Each bringing them closer to Bernard, to the Crown, to whatever waited at the crypt’s heart.

But at what cost? What price would be demanded for the final stretch of their journey? What more could they possibly lose?

“We keep moving,” Cade finally said, the words tasting like ash in his mouth. “We make sure it wasn’t for nothing. We find Bernard, we get the Crown, we get out.”

“And then?” Rayka asked, her eyes hollow with grief.

Cade didn’t have an answer. Couldn’t see beyond the next step, the next breath, the next moment of putting one foot in front of the other despite the weight crushing his chest.

The way forward lay through another doorway, this one standing open as if inviting them to continue their descent into the crypt’s final secrets. Light spilled from beyond—not the eerie phosphorescence that had guided them thus far, but something warmer, almost inviting.

Another trap, Cade thought bitterly. Another test. Another sacrifice demanded by this place that had already taken too much.

But they had no choice. Nowhere to go but forward, carrying their grief like armor, their rage like weapons.

“For Jer,” he said, his voice barely audible even to his own ears. “For Orro. We finish this.”

And as they moved toward that beckoning light, Cade felt something harden within him—not determination, not courage, but something colder, more primal. A promise written in blood and sealed with loss.

This crypt, this Crown, whatever forces manipulated them from the shadows—they had taken his family. Before this day ended, he would make them regret it.
