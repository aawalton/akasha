It seemed Donvath’s idea to get his face out there and reveal that the Shadow Squad sent after him had failed miserably had been a good one, because as one day passed after another, Asher didn’t see hide nor hair of any additional attacks. As much as he wanted to strike while the iron was hot and head straight for The Lost Lady, there were a good couple of things he had to check off his to-do list before that.

Not to mention Rulfar needed a few days for his blended skill to recover, so unless they wanted to be down their best chance of actually finding the lich hidden away within the complex cave system, they had to wait regardless.

After returning from the ceremony and getting to experience just what clever ideas Samantha had come up with to get around Brian’s ‘no kissing’ rule that night, Asher headed out the next morning on his own, bouncing around the kingdom like only he could.

First, he Recalled over to Dormaul, ignoring the box on Helpha’s counter for just a moment as he ran over to Polly’s Potions and put in a new order for Lesser Restoration potions. Even if he was going to wait for the team to come up with what they wanted for the more unique ones to store in his rift, it was never a bad idea to have a dozen or so healing potions waiting on standby should they be needed. Poly also happened to have the materials on hand for a single potion of Restoration, so he snagged that as well. Ninety-six thousand shards later, Asher headed back to the Helpha’s weapon’s shop while Polly worked on making his potions.

The last he’d spoken to the ferrum he’d given her both his void dagger they’d taken from Zygrul’s base, as well as a number of mysterious scales taken from the abyssal monster guarding said base. His hope had been that she would be able to construct a sheath that prevented the terrifying knife from draining his own energy while on his person or eating through the walls of his Personal Rift and falling out again.

Based on the note she’d left him, it sounded like she’d succeeded.

Asher,

Managed to get the scales to work for me, though it wasn’t easy. As a thank you for giving me a new material to work with for the first time in centuries, no need to worry about any sort of payment. Even if the blade is secured while inside the sheath, don’t do anything stupid after taking it out. I’d hate to see one of the few people I deem worthy of my work lose their life from something like this.

-Helpha

Asher tentatively opened the box like it was Christmas morning, beaming in excitement as he spotted the dark purple sheath lying inside. Gingerly removing the sheath from the box, he pulled the dagger free, wincing as the blade of pure void instantly began draining his energy from him. As useful as such a weapon was no doubt going to be, Helpha hadn’t been exaggerating. He had to be beyond careful, and similarly to his masterwork mithral dagger, Asher preferred not to overly rely on his equipment anyway.

Returning the dagger to its abyssal sheath once more, Asher stored it carefully into his Personal Rift before making his way back to Polly’s. He still had a few important errands to take care of, but he’d experiment more with the dagger later. Thanking Polly for her good work as always, Asher headed over to his next stop of the day.

Where he proceeded to get his butt handed to him by a short man who loved pointing out Asher’s every mistake.

“Come on, I thought these lessons were supposed to be making you better at fighting with daggers!” Mornamol laughed, nimbly parrying each thrust Asher aimed at his chest as he tried desperately to attack the man. Asher figured most instructors would probably be a tad worried if their students came at them with a genuine intention of trying to kill them, but Mornamol actively encouraged it.

Not that it mattered in the slightest. Asher had yet to so much as put a scrape on the former slayer in all their lessons.

“So. Did. I!” Asher grumbled, backing off and yanking out the steel dagger that Mornamol had somehow managed to slip past his guard and lodge within his ribcage. “…You have a Dagger element, don’t you? If I tried to bash your head in with a hammer, would I have better luck?”

“Oh absolutely, no doubt about that,” Mornamol chuckled, not even denying the claim to one of his elements. “Those nobles and their uppity circles always look down on the simpler elements like Hammer or Sword. But oftentimes it is the simple elements that can become the most deadly. Rather than having the crutch of a powerful skill at their beck and call, one has to work much harder and become truly skilled when their element is simpler. By the way, I heard about your recent expedition to the Deadlands. Good job on that.”

“How in the world did you hear about that?” Asher asked, caught off guard by the news and wincing at the second steel dagger that stabbed him in the literal back. He’d learned a while back that Mornamol could somehow throw his daggers and curve their trajectories like a boomerang, but he hadn’t even seen the retired slayer throw that one.

“I might not be one of those nobles I dislike so much, but that doesn’t mean I don’t have my sources,” Mornamol chuckled, holding his palms face out as their signal for ‘pause the fight.’ “What was really in the center of the Deadlands? I’ve got a pool going down at the guild with some of the slayers.”

“It was a lich,” Asher said, using Distortion to extend his reach as he yanked the dagger from his back. “One of the members of the Council of Death.”

“The Council is real?” Mornamol blinked, scratching at his chin with a dagger he hadn’t been holding a second ago. “Well damn, didn’t see that coming. You’re not worried the others are going to go after you in retaliation?”

“Trust me, not something new to worry about,” Asher drawled, deciding he didn’t want to get into it. While Mornamol knew he had a powerful healing skill, he hadn’t told the old warrior about his Eternity element or his history with the Council. He liked Mornamol, but they’d only really known one another for a few days. And while the old warrior was almost as bloodthirsty as Moxy when they really got going, he was far more calculating and cunning when they weren’t trying to stab one another. “What about you? Worried they’ll go after you for teaching me how to stab better?”

“Are you kidding? I’m small fry, there would be no point in coming after me,” Mornamol chuckled. “I may have lived long enough to retire, but I was never anyone important. Not like The Reaper.”

“Yeah, yeah, I know you think the name is as dumb as I do,” Asher said, shaking his head. “Though now that I’m hearing about some of these other names that the supposed greater powers have, I suppose I can’t complain. Gran doesn’t exactly strike fear into the hearts of others.”

“Greater powers,” Mornamol snorted, rolling his eyes at the idea. “Buncha gassed-up idiots if you ask me. You want to make it to retirement age? Spreading your name out there like that is just about the worst way to do it. I can count on one finger the number of ‘greater powers’ I’m aware of who have lived to be my age or older, and that’s just because everybody loves Gran. I’m pretty certain if you sent an assassin after her she’d have them sitting on a cushion and sipping tea with her in seconds.”

“You might not be that far off on that assumption,” Asher admitted, recalling how that was precisely what he had ended up doing with the woman. Granted, he hadn’t intended to kill her from the start, but even so. “Do you know her personally? I was thinking about dropping by and saying hello, but I don’t know how to find her.”

“Gran is always roaming about, never staying in any one place for long,” Mornamol shrugged. “Half the time she’s not even in the Noala kingdom. She likes it here from what I heard, but she’s not from here. Realms, if you believe the rumors, some say she’s older than the kingdom itself. Either way, I think she treats it more like her favorite vacation home.”

“Makes sense,” Asher agreed, noting the exact moment Mornamol dropped his hands. In that second, he lashed out, compressing a tunnel of space and attempting to slam his dagger straight into the man’s chest.

With a flicker of sparks, his blade scraped along the edge of Mornamol’s dagger as his strike was deflected like all the rest, the former slayer grinning at his exasperated expression.

“Not bad. But not good either. We’ve got some work to do.”

After another few hours of being stabbed, slashed, ridiculed, and then stabbed some more, Asher headed off to his third and final appointment of the day. The one he’d been dreading even more than his lessons with Mornamol.

His experiments with the second-tier effect of Permanence.

Once it was late enough that the library would be largely deserted, Asher Recalled over to Whikoga. After giving Rosh a thorough apology for scaring the hell out of him the other day when he was desperate to get Smiles over to Draken and his Death Aura, he headed to the library in the third ring. With his heart racing, Asher stepped into the library, catching Wilna’s eye almost instantly.

“Asher,” she smiled, her white, porcelain skin shining as always as the cracks in her face shifted with her expression. After a quick look around to confirm there wasn’t anyone nearby, she turned back toward him. “Ready to discuss the experiment?”

“Ready as I’ll ever be,” he admitted, swallowing hard as he followed her over into one of the back rooms. There, on the table, sat a box. Of course, it was what was inside that box that filled Asher with dread.

About a week ago, he’d placed a simple apple on the table and directed his second-tier Permanence ability on it, commanding the world to keep the age of said apple stable. Since then, he’d teleported across the continent, fought plenty of battles, briefly ventured into another realm of existence, and even died at one point. If Wilna confirmed that the apple still had the exact same age as it did when he triggered his skill a week prior…

He’d have solved the first hurdle in his own isolating immortality.

“Are you prepared?” Wilna asked, placing her hands on either side of the box as she looked at him.

“You’re really drawing this out, aren’t you?” Asher asked, frowning at the smiling craston. “You told me you were going to check it every day, so you should already know whether this worked or not. Can we skip the theatrics? I feel like I’m going to pass out.”

“Apologies,” Wilna said, picking up the box and revealing an apple that looked the same as it had when he’d placed it there. “I thought a dramatic reveal was appropriate given the situation, but I see how tense you are. In which case, I’ll get to the point. You did it, Asher. Your skill works.”

“I did it…?” he asked, gingerly picking up the apple and staring at it, unable to spot any changes to its red skin. “It’s seriously the same age it was last week?”

“To the second,” Wilna nodded, her smile only growing. “A small part of me is sad that an actual solution to the problem of immortality was found centuries after my creator passed, but I have already come to terms with his passing. Like yourself, the apple’s age is now static. You did it.”

Asher fell back into one of the chairs lining the table, laughing in relief as he spun the apple around in his hands and admired it. Now all he needed to do was work up the courage to admit to Samantha he’d been hiding the secret of his own immortality for a few weeks before popping the question as to whether she’d like to remain young forever with him.

Easy enough.

“Thank you again for all your help,” Asher said, laying the apple down and smiling at Wilna. “I quite literally couldn’t have done it without you.”

“Helping people is what brings me joy. It is half the reason I became a librarian,” Wilna admitted. “However, I don’t believe we should stop here. This second-tier effect of yours is brand-new, and currently, you don’t know what else it can do beyond halt aging and freeze a portion of the wild realm. If you have time, I would suggest we run some additional experiments to see its limitations.”

“That’s a very good point,” Asher nodded, thankful not for the first time he was acquainted with such an intelligent person. The last thing he wanted was to discover that removing the effect from the piece of fruit caused all the time it should have aged to catch up with it all at once, or any other horrific surprises. If he was going to use this skill on Samantha, he needed to know exactly what he was dealing with. “And you’re still offering to help?”

“I told you before. As fellow immortals, it is in our best interest to work together and establish good relations,” Wilna explained with a soft smile. “Grudges are powerful things, and I would much rather have a new friend to look forward to speaking with every so often than a new enemy I have to keep an eye on as the centuries pass.”

“I suppose I’m going to have to get used to that sort of mindset as well,” Asher said, struggling to comprehend someone angry enough to hold onto a grudge for hundreds of years. Not even Kroult had managed to maintain his anger for that long, and he’d been sealed away for over six hundred.

“There is no need to rush. It will come to you in time,” Wilna said, reaching into a different box and pulling out a second apple. “Now then, no time like the present. See if you can’t extend your skill to the second apple as well. If you could extend your immortality to multiple people, that would probably be a good thing to know.”
