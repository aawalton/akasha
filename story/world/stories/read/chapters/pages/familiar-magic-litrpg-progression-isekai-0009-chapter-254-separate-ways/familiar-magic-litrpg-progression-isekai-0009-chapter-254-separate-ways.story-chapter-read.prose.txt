The singular Master’s Tower that appeared on Earth should have been the most miraculous place on the planet. It was as large as a city block at its base, and rose up high enough to disappear into the clouds. It glowed with an eerie silver light, was home to a true master of all things magic, and no one knew what else. And yet its neighbor received all the attention.

For nearly half a year, the no-longer-lost city of Atlantis sat directly beside the tower. Master Ulric’s protection had kept the city–also the body of a god–safe, already having repelled several attacks by the Followers. Atlantis was no longer a ghost town, with Awakened from all over the globe flocking to the city of steel. Twilight was their god returned, and the Church of the Fractured Covenant proved they hadn’t forgotten their faith.

Ethan had moved into one of the finished buildings, along with most of the refugees from Corvale. Each day he marveled at the progress they were making. Gone were the broken windows, the debris, and wreckage of caretakers, the robotic stewards of this place. Now the city shone brightly in the afternoon sun, had streets busy with vehicles, and construction going on all day long.

It had been slow going, initially. There was so much damage, and the priority had been reconstructing the defenses around Twilight’s core. But as soon as they had one of the primary caretaker facilities up and running, things really took off. The facility started by repairing any caretakers that were salvageable, which in turn prioritized getting the rest of the caretaker facilities back online.

After less than a month, the helpful constructs were scrambling across Atlantis, restoring and reconstructing everything in sight. The twins, Maria and Miguel–two of the “new gods” who would stay behind after the originals left–remained in the city’s main tower. They spoke for Twilight–who they considered to be their father–and guided efforts to bring the god back to consciousness.

Unfortunately, the war on the Followers had not been progressing so cleanly.

Ethan and the group that infiltrated Atlantis had discovered a final plan of Twilight’s, meant to protect the people of this world. The Followers’ only means of reproduction was to assimilate other species through a combination of science and magic. Each planet they attacked was a new opportunity to replenish their troops, and they’d done the same with humans.

Frightening and dangerous, both the magical forces and governments of the world had been fighting desperately to keep their people out of Follower hands. But Twilight had planned for this problem, and left a solution. Ethan and his team had managed to activate an ancient mechanism which immunized the populace against that terrible fate. No human who came in contact with the so-called “blood rain”, even indirectly, could be transformed.

They celebrated when the plan succeeded. But those were early days, and no one had yet considered the consequences.

At first, it worked the way everyone hoped, and the conversion slowed to a crawl over a period of months. During this time, Earth’s forces had begun pushing the Followers back. It had been a turning point in the war. But it left the invaders with few options.

When they could no longer recruit, they shifted to wholesale slaughter.

Through it all, Ethan had been there. He was severely weakened after taking back Atlantis, and it had been weeks before his dimension and flare Bonds had been restored. He’d fought alongside the others, though his powers felt uneven. It had been a full additional month before Tomo, his demon Familiar, was back by his side, but Ethan had finally been able to spend some time purely dedicated to fighting the war.

After so long devoted to finding Twilight, it was a relief to turn his full power against the invaders. Sitting out of wars wasn’t something Bishops did, and Ethan had needed to constantly remind himself that what he’d been doing was just as important. Of course, nothing had felt right once the Followers began attacking civilians.

The fighting became extraordinarily brutal from that point, and even months later, it showed no signs of slowing. Thankfully, between Ethan, King Rothavaro, and the Banshee–who had finally unlocked Twilight’s gift–enemy generals had been dropping almost as quickly as they popped up. Meanwhile, the CFC, government, and even the growing forces from Nexum–thanks to the continued Dimensional Bleeds–had been hard at work.

But while they’d done well, and Follower installations were falling, they weren’t alone. A mysterious force had been quietly eliminating targets on its own, and rumors abound as to who they could be. Some believed it might be the remains of the Broken Covenant, secret societies of Bonded, or–to those in the know–possibly even the work of the Old Masters.

Ethan was frustratingly aware that it wasn’t the final group. While he’d managed to stay in contact with Noctem, fourth god and supposed leader of the Vitus Domini, the Masters themselves hadn’t been seen since their god was healed. They’d been in seclusion, and Ethan was forced to wonder if, being so old, they had forgotten how much could happen in a manner of months.

Today, he’d received a summons from Noctem himself, and hoped he would finally get an answer about what the group planned to do.

Ethan walked through what ancient dwellers of Atlantis had referred to as city hall. It was a large building positioned at the front of the city–using the orientation that the city’s severed legs had once provided. The building was shaped like a sphere, though with much of the shape concealed underground. It served as the central location for control of the city’s function, and acted as a convenient place to meet up for VIPs. Ethan used it for this purpose today, as he had someone to say goodbye to.

Prince Calevaro–a prince with no kingdom in this world, and a nearly destroyed capital city–stood leaning against a steel statue of a beaded man. Some kind of hero once upon a time, now just a convenient landmark.

The prince had returned to wearing well-tailored suits, though he didn’t bother with the mask anymore. His silver hair and half-elven features were on full display as he chatted easily with Selina, Runemisstress and Nator. She’d long-since recovered from the injuries she received when Corvale was struck down by the Follower-controlled Atlantis, and seemed to be telling a story about…laundry?

“Anyways, whatever you put in there comes out clean, and that’s not natural,” she said, shaking her head.

“Thank you for your wisdom, Runemisstress,” Calevaro said, smirking slightly.

Selina seemed to be searching his words for sarcasm, then gave up when she spotted Ethan. “He’s here!” she shouted, then ran over and threw her arms around Ethan.

He returned the hug, dodging horns as he did so. It was good to see Selina seeming like her old self again. She’d blamed herself for Corvale’s defeat, and near-destruction, and had been uncharacteristically quiet for some time. Then again, perhaps she was putting on a brave face for the prince.

“Bishop,” Cal said, extending a hand. Ethan grasped it, happy to see the prince also looking better. Since Ethan reached Twilight Rank while his friends were only Dusk, and they were at war on so many fronts, he rarely crossed paths with his friends these days. Although…

“You’re right on the edge,” Ethan said.

Calevaro’s smile widened. “Twilight will soon be mine,” he said. “Just a few more insights into Kent, and I’ll have caught up.” He referred to his third and final Familiar, a lava serpent named for his brother.

“Not quite caught up, I haven’t stopped progressing just because you’re slow.”

Cal chuckled. “As always, my incompetence shames me,” he said.

Selina rolled her eyes. “Is this really how the two of you say goodbye?” she demanded.

Ethan shrugged. “Technically it’s hello, and goodbye. So this feels about right.”

“The prince is going off to do something Ethan-worthy!” Selina said, arms flailing in emphasis. “He’s likely doomed–sorry your majesty–so you two should at least hug.”

“My mission is not so dangerous,” Cal insisted. “It’s merely isolated.”

That wasn’t entirely accurate. The CFC had discovered that the Followers weren’t content to simply be whittled slowly away, and had taken a rather horrifying step in central America. Spies had managed to discover a series of tunnels leading to massive underground caverns. The invaders had begun stockpiling humans down there, those who’d yet to be exposed to Twilight’s cure.

It appeared they meant to begin their own breeding pens for new Followers.

“Be careful down there,” Ethan said. He hated that he wasn’t going personally, but his own business lay elsewhere.

“I’ll do my best,” Cal said. “If I’m not back before you leave, I’ll try to find a way to join you.”

“Don’t rush!” Selina insisted. “Take your time, stay with your team, and watch your back. I hate that you’re going down into those tunnels without a healer.”

“Then come with me,” Cal said with a smile.

“Unfair!” Selina said. “You know why I have to go with Ethan. And it’s definitely not because I like him better; he’s objectively awful.”

“True,” Ethan said.

“I understand,” Cal agreed, “and I jest. You go to liberate your ancestral home. I respect that greatly.”

“He’ll probably hit Twilight while he’s down there, Selina, have faith in him.”

Selina sighed. “I do. We’ve all been separated so many times since those days on the road, and it just feels wrong. I miss Cara.”

“And I miss Valanor,” Ethan said with a smile. “Even if the sentiment is one-sided,” he added with a laugh.

“Oh, he loves you,” Selina insisted. “He’s just too uptight to ever use those words. Or other nice words. But you can see it in his glares!”

“I’ll take your word for it,” Ethan said.

“Any sense of when you leave?” Calevaro asked, growing serious once more.

“Soon, I hope,” Ethan said. “The dimensional tides are shifting, because of the Union, but in the wrong way. It used to be impossible not to get to Potentia, now we’re more likely to end up on Nexum.”

“Is that really a problem?” Selina asked. “I’m sure there’s resources there that could be helpful. Just make a quick stop then complete the journey.”

“I asked Noctem about that–he knows rifts better than anyone, after all. But he said we’d be unlikely to find the dimensional energy we need on Nexum. Maybe we could get a few people through, but not the Fable train with all the enhancements we’ve made.”

Selina nodded. “I figured as much, I just want us to have the best chance.”

“We cannot be certain of Nexum’s potential support, regardless,” Calevaro said. “Our world has been stripped, piece by piece over the last two years. Yet they still have a population to feed, and demons to fight, it may be they’re in worse shape than we are.”

“Hard to imagine,” Selina said. “Where in the Goddess’ name are all the Champions?”

“I’ve wondered that myself,” Ethan said. “I heard so many stories about them on Nexum, how they essentially shaped the world and kept it at peace. But in the end, the only ones I was really aware of were the Chosen.”

Cal ran a hand across his hairless chin as he spoke. “The Champions have always been reclusive,” he said. “Even those who were considered ‘active’ were only thought of as such because they would appear to give a gift to their home kingdom, or prevent a war. But we grew used to their absence, convinced ourselves they were always out there somewhere, doing Champion business and waiting until they were needed. I think that may have always been a lie.”

As they considered his words, the sounds of a pleasant bell rang out, indicating that the next CFC transport would soon be departing. Calevaro would leave with them. “It’s time for me to go,” he announced.

Ethan nodded, reaching out to shake the man’s hand again, then being surprised when the prince did pull him in for a hug. Ethan laughed as Selina clapped rapidly. “Good luck, Cal. If you do hit Twilight, I hope the fusion is a success.”

“I trust you,” Calevaro said. They’d worked together to recreate what Ethan had inadvertently done with his techwork golem Familiar. While that Bond was meant to be temporary, the creature had been fused to his soul thanks to unusual circumstances. Cal had an unusual temporary Bond of his own to Snowbear, the Guardian Beast of Ice, and the two had agreed to try to make it permanent.

It wouldn’t grant Cal new abilities–that they knew of–but it was a symbolic joining for the two who had grown close.

Calevaro turned and initiated a hug with Selina, much to her surprise and delight. When they broke apart, the prince turned back to Ethan. “Where are you off to next?” he asked.

Ethan frowned. “Master’s Sanctuary to speak with Noctem. I hope there’s some actual news this time. I’m getting sick of waiting on the Vitus Domini for some kind of meaningful contribution.”

Cal smiled. “Give them hell for me,” he said, then turned and strode away.
