Chapter 30Burnt Bridges

Craft used to be the unofficial psychologist in the anomaly dive teams he’d joined. No, not a therapist — playing bomb tech for a person’s feelings had never been a strong suit — but when survival hinged on everyone’s sanity staying above a certain level, he’d quickly developed a knack for picking out the weakest link in the team. Even the smallest signs of restlessness, irritation, or ennui would tip him off, and his finger would land on the next poor bastard.

Teams who had him had lower mortality rates. Still, no one wanted him on theirs.

***

It was the next day, and he and Lei-rei were in Dane’s carriage, just one bridge away from Monstera. They’d scrounged up enough Hobby supplies to last the trip, but not a Hobby nor the scenery could distract him from how Lei-rei had been listless since yesterday.

He was sitting on a bench opposite to her. Nightshade had told him Lei-rei would be the one to reach out, but she hadn’t, and he was a need-to-know sort of guy. The best he could do was distract her.

He turned towards the driver. “Hey, Dane, when’d your ride get so smooth?” The suspension was leagues better than most luxury cars he’d ridden.

“Keep singin’ me praises!” Dane shouted from the driver’s seat. “Air springs are expensive! ”

“Took you long enough. You could’ve afforded it any time,” Lei-rei said.

Craft looked at her. I’m glad you’ve still got some energy.

Dane solemnly shook his head. “Lady, one day you’ll reach the point when money can’t buy time.”

“Stop pretending like you’re suffering from success. You said that ten years ago. ”

“Shush.”

Craft cracked up. He’d been feeling lighter on his feet after that chat with Nightshade, and he appreciated this moment a little better. He’d even forgotten about the impostor, and dare he thought: things might be looking up.

“Well, you’re lively,” Lei-rei said.

He hoped she would be, too. “Yeah, well” — he smiled — “things aren’t bad.”

“I heard from Nightshade.” She gave a thumbs up. “You’re doing a good job.”

“Wow.”

“What?”

“I wish I had you as a boss.”

They darkly chuckled, and they realized they’d both been worked to the bone by terrible superiors — a memory that had become just another joke to share.

“Y’all making me sad, somehow. Y’all okay?” Dane said.

““Don’t worry about it,”” they said at the same time. They looked at each other at that, and they chuckled normally this time.

“I’m happy you’re on this trip,” Lei-rei said. She let out an exasperated sigh. “I wouldn’t survive Monstera’s atmosphere on my own.”

Craft half-imagined a gang running the place. In Amatoria flavoring, they’d say things like, “Ehh? What’chya looking at? Y’gonna cry? You need a therapist?! I know a guy!” …That couldn’t be it.

“Is it actually that bad?”

She glared at him like he’d committed speech-crime. “It’s terrible.”

“She just doesn’t know how to mingle!” Dane said.

Craft looked at him then Lei-rei with raised eyebrows. She shook her head. “The locals only speak in either money or fights,” she explained. “Dane speaks money. I speak fights. Difference is I hate speaking.”

Her shoulders slumped, and she leaned forward, resting her chin on her hands. “If it weren’t for Nightshade, I wouldn’t even be going all the way out here.”

Craft couldn’t imagine having to fight everyone all the time just to get around. “That’s rough, huh.”

She squinted at him. “It’s your problem too, you know.”

“Oh.” He covered his mouth with both hands. As the newcomer, it wouldn’t be surprising for him to walk the coals of isekai hazing: he’d walk into an official establishment, trigger an encounter with the local untouchable thug, then either get dragged into a bar fight or an ever-deepening conspiracy.

…And he hadn’t needed to leave Earth to have experienced both. He’d rather abide by a third option. “Couldn’t we just pay them off?”

She shook her head. “Last time I tried, my wallet got drawn and quartered. They’d noticed me when I’d paid off my first challenger. Next thing I knew, I was surrounded, and” — she shivered and bowed her head, shadows veiling her face — “never again.”

Dane laughed. “You paid off half the village!”

The fact that she could, though. Back in town, they’d easily gathered supplies just by asking around, but here she was saying she’d bribed people out of involving her in their Hobbies.

“Sounds like money’s more powerful than I thought,” Craft said.

“Well, it’s—”

“Damn straight it’s important!” Dane shouted.

Lei-rei shook her head. “Here we go,” she muttered.

“Hobbies are life. Between my Hobby and yours, unless it ain’t a bother or you’re in serious trouble, ‘course I’m gonna choose my own — so when the birds are still chirpin’ and you wanna hassle someone, how do ya get ’em to go out of their way for ya?”

There was the ping of a coin flip, and Craft caught a silvery object before it flew over his head. He held it up, and on one face was an embossing of a spider, and on the other, an embossing of a…pudding-like thing. It had two lines for eyes. What is that?

“That’s a Monstera coin,” Dane continued. “Every place issues their own, and sometimes individuals do, too. When you do favors for someone, that’s what they give ya, and the coin’s value depends on who ya get it from.”

“Yeah? And Monstera coin?”

“Pretty high up there.” Dane chuckled. “Keep the change.”

Craft whistled. He remembered Dane mentioning exotic materials coming from Monstera. If he wanted some, local products were best bought with the local currency, and he pocketed the one in his hand. “Sounds like I ought to earn some.”

“You’ll have to trade,” Dane said.

“Or fight,” Lei-rei added.

Craft stared out the back of the carriage. “It really comes down to that, huh? Think I can trade bows for coin?”

“Oh, definitely.” Lei-rei nodded vigorously. “They don’t have any bowyers. The archer types will swarm you the moment they find out.”

“So we really can’t avoid getting swarmed…”

“Don’t take them the wrong way. They may be overbearing, but they’re also warm. Unfortunately, I’m someone who prefers cold drinks during winter.”

Dane laughed. “You’ve always been a cold one” — the scratching of a knife against its sheathe shut him up real quick — “oh boy, am I a sad driver! Gotta keep my eyes on the road else a wheel’s gettin’ caught in the gutter!”

Craft wanted to chuckle, but he didn’t want to join Dane on Lei-rei’s shitlist.

“That aside,” she continued. She gazed at the carriage’s floor, huffing and looking up. “We do have an in. If we approach a certain someone, we wouldn’t have to fight at all.”

“Oh?” Craft perked up, but Lei-rei had a conflicted expression. Well, that’s not right. “What’s the catch?”

She closed her eyes and clenched her fists. She stayed like that for a while until she sighed and relaxed.

“He’s a former work acquaintance,” she continued. “Amicable, good work ethic, but he’s…impulsive.”

Craft raised an eyebrow. “Doesn’t sound like the end of the story.”

She rested her arms across her knees, clasping her hands together. “He’s…fundamentally not bad.”

Did she really have to emphasize that?

She continued, “He once asked, ‘Can I triple-wield?’ It was a half-joke coming out of a two-handed swordsman’s mouth. The newer half of the team laughed, but me and the other half held our breaths.

“At the time, he didn’t even know how to dual-wield, so he learned that first. He became proficient in an hour.”

Wow.

“He started practicing various kinesis magics on a sword after that. His control was impressive as always, but ‘controlling a third sword using magic’ was a suspiciously predictable idea for him to come up with.”

She shook her head. “ ‘Too easy,’ he’d complained. Of course. Why wouldn’t he? So he started making it harder for himself. Instead of using magic directly, he purchased linked kinesis stones and attached them around all his swords, making it so that he would be controlling the free sword by gesture using two other swords like magical chopsticks.”

“Magical chopsticks…” Craft muttered.

“He managed to learn how to manually control it. I was truly impressed by how it extended his fighting range, but it was unwieldy, even for him” — she sighed through the nose — “so he decided it was ‘too hard,’ and he started preprogramming attack and defense patterns into the kinesis stones.”

To make things even harder just to loop back to being lazy — Craft was starting to figure out that guy’s character.

“Come the second day, he seemed pleased with the new equipment. He could switch between manual and programmed modes whenever he wanted, and there were plenty among the team who challenged him, but I don’t know what they were expecting. Even without the new gear, they wouldn’t win against him.”

“I’m guessing it was a massacre.”

She nodded. “When I took him on myself, it felt like fighting two of him. I can’t even handle one.” She rubbed her forehead. “He didn’t stop there.”

She took on a grim expression. “He and I had a parting session. He’d said he wanted to ‘try something out,’ but I already knew he just wanted to experiment on a live subject.”

Going by the flow… “And you agreed—”

“How can I not! He was going to lend me a Limited Edition Abyssal Pressure Cooker!—”

She paused, realizing she’d thrown her arms out and knee-walked closer to Craft. She pulled away and sat back on the bench. “Well” — she cleared her throat — “that was the deal. We ventured to the outskirts for the experiment, and I was fifty meters away, rather close.”

“That’s close?”

She raised an eyebrow, then realized the source of his confusion. “Considering we could close that distance in a second, yes.”

“Oh, wow.” Craft politely nodded to that.

“He told me he’d shoot something at me.”

“Oh.”

“I didn’t expect he’d use lightning stones on all three swords. I don’t understand the principle, but he pointed the two swords in his hands at me, placed the third on top of those, and with a lightning discharge, launched it at me faster than I could unsheathe my own.”

“…Wow.” He genuinely could not comment. By Lei-rei’s description, that guy had created a railgun with swords…to launch another sword: a sword-railed railgun. I guess the ammo would be called a “sword-railed railgun sword.”

There was that, though: it was a railgun. Only someone with modern knowledge could have thought of that, and that piqued his curiosity about this person’s origins.

“I absolutely detest getting dragged into his shenanigans, but I got an Abyssal Pressure Cooker out of it.” She groaned. “Maybe what I actually hate is how he knows exactly what to say to convince me. It might actually be end-tier social skills that are the most overpowered of all.” She shook her head.

“I’m not sure what I expected, but he doesn’t sound so bad,” Craft said.

She looked at him in disbelief. He shrugged.

“I mean, fundamenta— Alright, I get why you used that word.”

She chuckled, but sighed and looked away with wide eyes. She shook her head. “I hated dealing with him.”

The guy was certainly on the pushy side, but it wasn’t like such people couldn’t be convinced. “I’m sure he would’ve backed off if you’d told him.”

“But, the pressure cooker…”

“Could’ve haggled him for it.”

“That stubborn fool?”

“Just be twice as stubborn but less dumb.”

She narrowed her eyes, about to say something, but she stopped. “No, I see where you’re going.” She covered her mouth and looked down. “Why didn’t I think of that…” She looked at him again. “Craft, are you actually secretly competent at these things?”

“I had to be secret and competent in my last job, yeah.”

“Touché.”

“A railgun, though… Do I have a chance to talk to him?”

“You know about it? Well, we’ll be finding him first thing anyway. He’ll convince the other monsters not to swarm us. Maybe you can befriend him on the side, then I can squeeze out another pressure cooker out of him.”

“Befriend him? I thought he was a bigshot.” Someone who could reel in Monstera’s people ought to be one.

“Despite everything, he’s not difficult to speak to,” Lei-rei replied, but her words and her frown didn’t correspond.

“Hey, you alright?” Craft asked.

She leaned against the bench and looked down. “Do you think you’re doing better?”

The sudden turn of the question surprised him. Why’s she looking like that? Doubtless, there was something simmering under her facade, but he didn’t know enough to ask; only to answer.

“I think so.” He nodded. “Feels like it’s been ages since I’ve felt this way. I don’t know how much further I have to go, but I’m going in a good direction, and that’s what counts.” He rested his chin on his hands. “I wonder if I’m doing enough, though.”

Lei-rei leaned closer — and flicked his forehead.

“Ow!” He pulled his head back and rubbed it. “Why?”

“ ‘What you should do’ and ‘what you can do’ are different things. Focus on the second and the first follows on its own.”

“Well” — he rubbed his head — “I can’t argue.”

“And I’ll flick you in the head every time you forget.”

He chuckled — but she didn’t join him. She’d leaned back again and furrowed her brows, content to stare out of the back of the carriage, fiddling her thumbs.

Craft narrowed his eyes. Ennui, restlessness — he couldn’t ignore her symptoms. There was something else about Monstera wearing away at her nerves, and though this was no anomalous space, he was afraid she might lose it before they even got there.

He might have been a need-to-know kind of guy, but right now, she was on his team — and he really needed to know.

“What you ‘should’ do and what you ‘can’ do” — he asked — “which one’s been bothering you more?”

She shook her head at the question, but not much else. She’d expected it.

“That person and I aren’t on good terms,” she finally said. “It’s been a while. I didn’t think we would be meeting again this soon.”

It surprised him, but he was relieved. Her worries had a name. “Is that why you didn’t want to go?” he continued to ask.

She furrowed her brows. “Mm” — was all the noise she afforded. Her frown was soft and fleeting; there, but also not. Whatever had happened between them, she had long accepted the fact of it. She’d just never known what to make of it.

He knew that feeling. Even now, he didn’t know what meaning Rafflesia’s death was supposed to have.

“Hey, I’ve got a what-if for you,” he said. Lei-rei shifted in her seat. He continued, “If, somehow, you knew this was going to be the last chance for you to make up with that person, wouldn’t you do it?”

She canted her head back, looking at him half-amused and half-annoyed. “Are you trying to guilt trip me?”

He shifted in his seat. Compared to him, she was lucky. “It’s just a chance I’d never gotten.”

***

His words had tipped her off about what he’d gone through, and looking at him, it was as if she’d seen her own future. He was reassuring, composed — and burdened by regret. Would she turn out that way too?

Apologetically, at his expense, he reminded her of the true shape of her shame: a chain that bound her from taking neither risks nor gains. It was the status quo, the lack of control, and the undeservedness of her natural freedom.

No one wanted to carry that. Craft surely hadn’t. In front of someone more unfortunate than her, she found herself robbed of the right to continue lamenting. She couldn’t pull him up if she, herself, couldn’t follow the light.

***

“Well, it’d be foolish of me not to take your advice,” she said.

He perked up. “So you’re thinking of making up with him?”

She inhaled and slowly exhaled. “I don’t care what happens.”

She’s still nervous. “Come on, I’ll back you up,” he added. “From far away. With a sniper rifle.”

She chuckled. “Coward.”

He chuckled with her. The silence after that helped Lei-rei make up her mind.

“You’re right,” she said. “I’ve been avoiding this for a while. I can’t do that anymore.”

He smiled, and she returned the gesture. The conversation had died, but they both felt they’d said enough. If only smiles could last forever, then it’d be perfect.

“Come to think of it, you never mentioned his name,” Craft said.

“Oh, it’s Ri—”

“Whoa!” Dane blurted. The carriage slowed down.

“What’s going on out there?” Craft called out.

“It’s — I dunno!” He showed his face in the carriage. “There’s someone ahead! I’m pulling over!”

“But what’s going on?” Lei-rei said.

“It’s faster if y’all just take a look!”

Dane returned his eyes to the road. Craft and Lei-rei scooted to the front and pulled away the flaps.

There was smoke ahead, and there were fires burning on the ends of splintered trusses on either side of a gorge.

“Oi!” Dane called out. He waved at a green-skinned humanoid, its muscles bulging like it drank steroids for water.

It pointed a machine gun skywards and started shooting. “Boss! Boss!”
