I deliberately walked slower to match Filki’s pace. Her cane tapped against the stone in a rhythm that kept me from losing my mind in the identical hallways.

We walked in silence. I was letting my mind catch up with everything that had happened in the last hour. Zoldyck and his curse. Lania’s power. Rifts closing. And somewhere in that mess, I was here, trying to save a little girl from an immortal priest who’d spent decades grooming her like a garden.

“Wait,” I paused, the rhythmic tapping of the cane did too. “When your mother escaped, wasn’t Aram there too.”

“Yeah,” she answered.

“Then why does he look so young? Shouldn’t he be older or dead?”

“Good observation. The answer is,” She took a pause for dramatic effect. “I don’t know.”

“You don’t know?”

“I mean…I am not sure but there are some possibilities.”

“Which are?” I pressed harder to get the answers.

“It might be his superpower,” I was about to interrupt but she said what I was thinking just after. “Which doesn’t make sense because if his powers let him not age, he would have been the one getting experimented on not the one conducting one.”

Hands crossed, I nodded in agreement.

“However, there’s one other possibility for his power. Have you seen his eye?”

“Yeah, it’s made of the same black sludge others seem to have been made of. On that note why does the kids see them like normal people.”

“It’s Mahilka’s superpower. Something to do with hypnosis. It’s better for them. Don’t want them to see the abominations…Getting back on track, I suspect Aram’s power somehow suppresses the black sludge. You know what the black sludge does?”

“I can guess, it gives immortality while letting you keep your powers, but makes an abomination out of you in the process.”

“That’s right.”

“Hmm…I see, so Aram’s superpower might have something to do with negation. I did see him flying. I suspect he can negate gravity, and many more things.”

“Could be, I have no clue.”

“You aren’t that good for a master sleuth.”

“The brain has rusted with the age.” She said with a smile, letting me see her missing molars.

“Now it’s my turn to ask questions,” Her smile faded away. “What’s the plan?”

“We can’t do this alone,”

“Obviously, did you think I dragged this old body up out of that chair to watch you die heroically by yourself?"

"I mean the kids. The others in the facility. There has to be more of them than just you, Lania and Pyiari.”

“You want to arm the children?” She asked in astonishment.

“Not arm them. I don’t want them to pick up spears and swords to fight. I want to give them a choice. The same choice I gave to Pyiari and Lania, the choice to fight for their freedom or dance here on Aram’s whims.”

Filki studied me for a long moment, her old eyes peeling me one at a time. Trying to find what was hidden deep within. Whatever she found was apparently good enough.

“There’s a children’s quarter here, older children. They have seen more than Lania and Pyiari. If anyone’s going to listen to a stranger with a stupid plan, its them.”

"Great vote of confidence."

"I call them like I see them."
