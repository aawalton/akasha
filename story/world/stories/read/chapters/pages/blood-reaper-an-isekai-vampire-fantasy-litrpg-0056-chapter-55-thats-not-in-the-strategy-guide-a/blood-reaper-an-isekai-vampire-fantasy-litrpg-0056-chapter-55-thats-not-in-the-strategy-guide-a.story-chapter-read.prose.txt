Midday sunlight filtered through the forest canopy in dusty spotlights. The woodland clearing had fallen into unnatural silence—no birds called, no insects buzzed. Only the soft, persistent hiss of acid eating through stone broke the stillness.

Puddles of iridescent goo dotted the rocky clearing like toxic constellations, each pool smoking gently as it consumed whatever it touched. The dissolved remains of slugs formed rainbow oil slicks across the gray stone, still bubbling with residual chemical reactions. The stench hung thick in the air—a caustic blend of ammonia and copper that burned the back of Ace’s throat and made his eyes water involuntarily.

Ace stood at the center of the carnage as he scanned the perimeter for threats. His squad formed a loose circle around him—Tara reclaiming her arrows, Olivia cleaning acidic residue from her staff with methodical precision, and Marcus dabbing blood from his nose with a tattered sleeve.

Their labored breathing echoed in the tomblike silence. The aftermath of their violence had transformed the clearing into an alien landscape—one where even the ground beneath their feet couldn't be trusted. They had won this skirmish, but the forest itself seemed to be holding its breath…

…as if waiting for something worse to arrive.

"Clear," Ace reported, scanning the empty clearing for stragglers. "Are there any more nearby, Olivia?"

“Nothing,” she confirmed, the last to rejoin the group.

"Good?" Ace asked, scanning his team for injuries. “Everyone alright?”

Tara grinned, her expression exhilarated. "You guys, that was perfect. Not a single acid burn. Marcus, help me harvest samples before they fully dissolve. I have a feeling they might be useful."

“For what?” Marcus asked, his face scrunching with disgust.

“I have some ideas,” Tara said with a mischievous smirk.

Ace raised one eyebrow at her, wondering what she was up to, but she just shrugged.

“I’ll tell you later,” she said.

"That…" Marcus ran the back of his hand across his brow to wipe away the sweat. "That was almost too easy."

Hmm.

The man had a point.

Ace nodded, but something nagged at him. The slugs had been dispatched with minimal resistance—exactly the kind of confidence-building exercise they needed.

It really did feel too easy.

“Maybe we’re just that good.” Olivia set a hand on her waist and leaned her weight onto one hip. Her staff rested on her other shoulder, giving her the air of a seasoned warrior.

This girl was a mystery, and one Ace needed to figure out one way or another.

Gray sparks splintered over the oozing slug carcasses—which meant they only had mundane loot, if Ace recalled correctly.

Still, loot was loot.

He extended his hand toward the nearest slug corpse, and thankfully, the familiar spectral interface of his loot list materialized before he actually had to touch the acidic pile of bubbling slime.

The thought of a screen popping into existence right in front of his face didn’t even faze him. Each time the notification appeared, it felt less alien. Less wrong. Which, in its own way, felt like a warning sign.

He scanned the screen, but the loot he had gotten was mostly Iron Chits and something called Corrosive Oil.

Similar notifications appeared over the other carcasses as his teammates collected their spoils. The coins felt unnaturally heavy in Ace's pocket, as though weighted with more than just metal.

“Oooh, everyone trade me that oil, please,” Tara said as she tapped her screen. A vial of gray goop materialized in the air before her, and she smiled as she inspected it.

“Girl, what the hell are you up to?” Marcus asked dubiously.

“Mischief,” Tara replied with a devious little grin.

A familiar pressure built behind Ace's eyes—the sensation of approaching a Level Threshold. It wasn't pain exactly, more like the feeling just before a sneeze that never comes. He was close to leveling up again, tantalizingly close, but still short. The slugs simply didn't provide enough experience to push him over the edge. Each kill added a fractional increase to the invisible gauge in his mind, but the bar remained incomplete.

“What’s everyone’s Level?” he asked.

They glanced between each other uncertainly, as though no one wanted to go first, but Tara eventually gave in.

“Level 21,” she said.

“Level 23,” Olivia added.

Marcus never took his eyes from the loot list in front of him. “Level 20.”

Hmm.

It looked like the System had been telling him the truth, then, about everyone else leveling higher than he had thus far.

“What about you?” Olivia asked.

“Level 18,” Ace admitted.

Everyone paused and studied his face, as though in shock.

“But you’re a legendary class,” Tara pointed out.

His eyes briefly narrowed. “Rub it in.”

She blushed. “Sorry.”

“We need more kills to get strong enough for the Crucible,” Marcus interjected. “These slugs aren’t worth shit for experience."

“That’s why it’s called grinding,” Tara said tersely. “It’s not supposed to be fun.”

“Um, guys…” Olivia said, her voice trailing off as she stared down at her hands.

"We could continue hunting," Tara suggested, as if she hadn’t heard Olivia speak. “There are likely more clusters nearby."

"I'd rather find something that doesn't try to melt my face off," Marcus quipped.

“Guys!” Olivia shouted.

It was the loudest Ace had ever heard her speak.

Everyone paused, and their gazes shifted toward the Blood Dancer on the fringe of their group.

“What’s wrong?” Tara asked.

“Check yourselves,” Olivia ordered.

“Check… what do you mean?” Marcus snapped. “We didn’t do anything offensive.”

“Not what I meant.” Olivia’s gaze never left her hands, and she lifted them in the air as she examined them. “Check your skin.”

Ace glanced down at his fingertips. His body had taken on a faint, opalescent quality, like oil on water. When he moved them under direct sunlight, rainbow patterns rippled across the surface—the same iridescence the slugs had displayed. He flexed his fingers and felt something new: a substance that wasn't quite acid but wasn't normal sweat either.

"Shit," he muttered, wiping his fingers on his pants. The fabric sizzled slightly where he touched it.

Marcus was frantically rubbing at his forehead. "I can... feel everything. Too much." His eyes darted in multiple directions simultaneously, focusing on different movements around him without turning his head. "It's like my brain is processing everything at once. Background. Foreground. All equally important."

Everyone stared down at their hands or tried to rub the goo off by running their palms against their clothes. Small tendrils of painless smoke wafted through the air from the tinged cloth.

"I can feel more," Olivia added softly, her eyes wide with wonder. "As if my body is learning to flow rather than move."

“Why do you all get the pretty changes?!” Marcus stared down at his arms, which now radiated with a soft iridescent glow and a slick layer of slime. “I told you we shouldn’t have—”

The first subtle vibration traveled up through Ace's boots, so faint that he almost dismissed it as imagination. Then came another—a distinct tremor that resonated through the specialized inlays of his Phantom Step Resonance Boots. He froze, his head tilting slightly as he tried to figure out what was causing that.

The others continued chatting, oblivious to what his gear had detected through the damp forest floor.

“Quiet,” Ace ordered. He lifted one hand, palm flat as he tried to get everyone to be quiet, and he quickly scanned the empty forest around them.

Instantly, everyone went silent. They all glanced at the trees surrounding them in silent bafflement.

Huh. Apparently, they hadn’t sensed anything. If not for his gear, whatever this was would’ve snuck up on them all.

Ace pressed his boot more firmly against the ground, focusing on the sensory feedback he was detecting through the soles of his shoes. The vibrations formed patterns he couldn’t quite follow, yet.

"There’s movement underground," he whispered, his brow furrowing in concentration. "Big. Coordinated. Headed this way."

There—another rumble. Another wave of tremors began as whispers in the earth, almost imperceptible vibrations that shot through Ace’s boots. After a few seconds, they grew, intensifying into a guttural growl that emanated from deep within the forest floor. Eventually, loose stones skittered across the ground. The pebbles jumped and bounced in chaotic patterns, clacking against each other as the thunder built.

“Uh, guys?” Marcus said

“Shh,” Ace and Tara said in unison.

Ace shifted his weight, changing his foot position to scan for ways out. The tremors responded immediately, intensifying beneath his heels. He pivoted, checking another potential escape route, and another, and another. Each direction yielded the same result—vibrations drawing closer, converging on their position from all sides.

They were surrounded.

The color drained from his face. These weren't random tremors that he had detected too late—they were a calculated attack that had remained just out of range. Whatever was hunting them had deliberately encircled them, staying far enough away that they’d had no chance of noticing until it was too late.

“We need to get the hell out of here,” Marcus hissed under his breath. “Like, now.”

“We can’t,” Ace said darkly. “There’s no safe route out of here.”

He shifted his gaze toward his teammates, and he caught each person’s eye, one by one. Before he could say anything else, however, the loose soil just beyond the treeline rippled like water. The surface rose and fell in shallow waves that ballooned outward from some unseen epicenter. The nearby ancient trees swayed, their massive trunks creaking in protest as their roots strained against the suddenly unstable ground. Dust motes and dried leaves kicked into the air, only to hang suspended in the sunbeams.

“Get ready,” he ordered.

The four of them pivoted until they were all in a tight circle, back to back. Olivia raised her makeshift staff. Ace summoned a Duskblade. Tara grabbed her bow and notched an arrow, while Marcus raised his hands in preparation.

Instantly aware. Immediately unified.

Despite their situation, Ace allowed himself a small smile of pride.

A wet slither snaked through the air, like someone dragging a water-soaked carpet across stone. Ace aimed his Duskblade in that direction, dread pooling in his stomach with the cold certainty that their fight was about to continue.

Then, it went painfully silent.

The quiet that followed wasn't merely an absence of noise—it was a presence unto itself, thick and suffocating. Every muscle in Ace's body coiled with anticipation as his enhanced senses strained against the stillness, searching for any hint of movement in the forest. The forest itself seemed to hold its breath, with not even leaves daring to rustle in the deathly quiet. The next few seconds stretched on painfully, suspended in that terrible moment between knowing danger was nearby and the inevitable attack to come.

A piercing scream filled the air. Everyone winced at the deafening shriek, and seconds later, the forest exploded with movement.

Dozens of slugs burst from the ground, this time as large as horses. The dirt shivered as they emerged from whatever tunnels they’d used to approach unseen, and in an instant, Ace’s team was surrounded. Their skin pulsed with more intense iridescence than the first batch of slugs. Acid trails smoked where they skidded across the ground, faster even than the first wave of creatures Ace had killed. In mere moments, the once-green underbrush shriveled to ash and collapsed into piles of dust, corroded to nothing from the sheer power of the slugs’ acid.

It was a living, undulating wall of acidic death.

"What's happening?" Marcus asked, his voice tight with tension.

“Uh, guys…” Tara gulped and glanced at Ace over her shoulder. “I think what we killed were babies.”

Oh.

Oh, fuck.

“So we just raided a nursery?!” Marcus snapped.

“Focus,” Ace chided. “Can anyone tell how many of them there are?”

"At least forty," Tara’s voice cracked. "I can't get a precise count because they keep emerging. It's like the whole forest floor is alive with them."

Great.

Just great.

‘Death by slugs’ wasn’t exactly how he wanted to go out, but it looked like they might not have much say in the matter.

A massive slug, far larger than the others, oozed from a thick crack in the ground and towered over them. Not a creature, really, but more of a monument, its massive form blocking out the dappled sunlight that had illuminated the clearing moments before. It was twenty feet of iridescent nightmare, and its body pulsed with waves of bioluminescent light that traveled through its translucent flesh. The light created a sickening rhythm—bright, dim, bright, dim—almost hypnotic. Its hide wasn't merely wet but actively secreting something that hissed and popped as it contacted the air.

But the stench—that was the worst thing of all.

The vile odor hit them like a physical force—an impossible blend of sickly sweet rot and industrial solvent.

Ace gagged.

Two glowing red eyes protruded from what passed for its head, though to call them eyes was kind of generous. They extruded from stalks of goop that swiveled independently of each other, taking in everything around it without so much as moving its head.

Its underside left destruction in its wake—not a simple trail but an advancing chemical warzone where moss, stone, and soil alike bubbled and transformed into smoking sludge. Every movement unleashed a grating, wet, almost screeching scrape of burning stone and slop. When it lurched forward, the entire clearing trembled, the vibrations of its bulky body carrying through stone and soil loud enough to rattle bones.

The Identify screen popped to life in front of him.

———

Alpha Corrosive Slug

Core Racial Trait: Acidic secretion network - interconnected primitive hive mind capable of dissolving organic matter and some metals.

———

“You’ve got to be fucking kidding me,” Marcus said under his breath.

The alpha slug roared.

Its scream echoed through the forest, the sheer weight of its voice bending trees as it concentrated its full power on Ace’s squad.

“Alright gang,” Ace said as he raised his Duskblade. “Time for the real test.”

“Ready,” Olivia said, her voice dark and even.”

“Let’s do this,” Tara agreed.

“Damn my rotten luck,” Marcus muttered. “Fine.”

“That’s the spirit,” Ace said dryly.

Marcus let out a mirthless laugh.

They were surrounded, they had no way out, and they were vastly outnumbered by creatures far larger than them. If the babies were any indication of what the team was up against, these things could probably burn a vampire alive just by falling on them.

But if the four of them went down, they would go down swinging.

As a team.
