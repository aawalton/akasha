The mangrove-lined living islands in the not-quite-morass world slowly gave way to a more defined coastline, with trees that looked like they might not be the tops of monsters. I eyed it warily, then strode through the stinking mud and climbed up. Solid. Unmoving. It didn’t feel like a monster’s body, and it didn’t attack me. “Looks safe. Come on up.”

“Don’t have to tell me twice,” Ellen said. She scrambled to the top of what turned out to be a long, narrow berm or dike, maybe ten feet wide at the base and no more than three or four where we stood. It was made of churned earth that gave slightly with every step, but that was a definite improvement from the saltwater swamp we’d been fighting through.

It did come with a drawback, though.

Syringe Beast: B-Rank

They flew, and they were roughly mosquito-shaped. Beyond that, the similarities to anything I’d seen on Earth ended. Each was the size of my head; they reminded me less of insects and more of flying lobster monstrosities, with long, pointed spikes sticking from their tails. They came in packs, and after the first wave, I let Ellen manage them with Shadow Box and her other spells.

After fifteen minutes, she was getting sick of swatting them, though.

“Let’s get back in the water,” I said. “You’re not gaining anything from murdering those. We need to find challenges for you.”

“Great,” she groaned, but she dropped into the water on the dike’s far side. Then she stopped and turned. “This side’s not saltwater.”

I joined her. Sure enough, the water felt warmer and more still than the gently pulsing ocean beyond the dike had. It lacked the taste of salt, too—I only knew that because I’d splashed some into my mouth. Instead, it carried a different taste.

Blood.

“Be on your guard.”

Ellen nodded. I readied the Storm’s Blade, but before I’d even taken a step, the water erupted, and a massive monstrosity lunged toward me. Its jaw opened in a vertical slit, and blood poured from between hundreds of sharp, yellowed teeth as a crocodile the size of an elephant grabbed me by both legs and rolled.

I went under, jerked off my feet by the sudden attack. Teeth punched through my skin everywhere my armor had gaps, gouging into muscle and scraping against bone. It hurt, but the muscles didn’t tear, and the bone didn’t break.

The croc didn’t care, though.

It just kept rolling, and I rolled with it. The Storm’s Blade cut into its flesh, and its teeth ripped into mine. There was every possibility that an A-Ranked monster could kill me, and if one could, it’d be an ambush predator that had gotten me off-balance. I hacked away awkwardly, unable to shift into any of my stances while underwater and entangled.

Then fear slammed into me. The Stormkin Avatar started to push it back, but I let it happen. This monster was killing me. I was in mortal danger. Every second underwater was one more second that I could die. Adrenaline poured into my veins and core, and I redoubled my efforts even as Ellen’s aura of fear washed over me and filled my veins. The Storm’s Blade sliced into flesh, and blood filled the water. More blood than the Storm’s Blade or the croc’s jaws could have made.

The sensation of fangs sliding out of my thighs sent shivers down my spine, and I pushed myself back to my feet, erupting sword-first from the water. The croc surfaced next to me—or at least, what was left of it did.

Ellen had layered multiple Shadow Boxes, and the monstrous croc’s armored scales hadn’t been up to the challenge. It bled from dozens of wounds, the red, copper-scented liquid mixing with the stagnant swampwater around it. “Are you okay?” she asked.

I looked at my wounds, then nodded. They were bleeding, and they hurt, but my delver’s quick healing and skills were already patching them up, and my Stamina flowed into them, dulling the pain. “Yes. That was embarrassing.”

“Be careful.” Ellen pulled me back up onto the dike, her aura slipping back into her core. “We can’t afford to slow down; this place is going to be crawling with Guardians soon.”

She was right. “We’ll hunt from the top. If we don’t get anything, though…well, I’m fine with being bait. You can keep me alive, right?”

Ellen’s face shifted between concern, horror, and a pensive, questioning expression. Then she nodded. “Not every woman gets to fish for crocs using their boyfriend as bait. I’m in.”

I shivered. “We’ll see if we can get anything from up top first.”

My Mana never went over thirty percent.

Every spare point siphoned off into Ellen’s core through our connection, a constant flow from Cheddar to me and into her. It was, in its own way, a heady feeling; I’d first felt it back in the Arboreal world where we’d discovered the writing that had led to the Crone, helping Ellen much like I was now. The difference was that back then, I’d been doing it to clear the portal.

This time, we had no intention of finding this world’s boss.

And this time, I was nothing but a glorified escort, covering for Ellen so she could concentrate on doing as much damage as possible. On gaining as many levels as possible. On ranking up.

She had some new tricks up her sleeve, too.

Darkwalker, an A-Ranked spell that solidified her shadow into a clone that repeated her casts.

Blackslick, which covered the ground around her in clinging shadows that slowed her enemies. Coupled with her fear aura, it was shockingly devastating.

Eclipse Bolt, a single, massive bolt of lightning—the green-black, shadow-infused kind—that left behind a patch of disorienting Darkness around its target.

And, with Mana pouring into her, it was only a matter of time before something gave.

I felt it at the same time she did.

Static. Static, in my core. It spread along the portal metal and lightninggold bindings holding my core in place, rippling through my Mana and crackling across the desert. I breathed deeply. So did Ellen. “You’re ready for one of your Laws.”

It wasn’t a question, but Ellen nodded. “Let’s keep pushing. I have more to learn.”

“Got it.”

The next fight—against a burrowing snake that left behind a berm just like the one we were following—went quickly, and Ellen stuck to Shadow Box and a handful of carefully-selected spells. The snake died in seconds.

And on and on we pushed.

It was…working with Ellen again felt like I’d been fighting with half of my skills, then being given the rest all at once. She complimented my abilities perfectly; I’d built to cover her weakness against single enemies, and she’d built to take out swarms so I could focus. And even though it wasn’t fighting except defensively, I could feel that synergy as we re-learned each other’s preferences. She was no Phoenix Reborn or Portal Tyrant. They were stronger, faster, more…destructive.

But she was close. And more importantly, she was mine. My partner.

Her skills kept leveling up, and one by one, we pushed closer to her push to S-Rank.

Then, after one final crocodile kill, she did it.

“I’m ready. Tell me everything I need to know,” she said, sitting down and breathing heavily. Her hands rested in her lap. It wasn’t a meditation pose, and she wasn’t about to push to S-Rank right here.

“Alright. We should be in deep enough to avoid the Guardians. I’ll give you the overview, then walk you through my process in detail once we’re back home and this guild war’s over. There’s one step I’m not as sure about, but you may be able to skip it. You have no Path, right?”

“Right.”

“Good. The first thing I did was go through all my Laws—not only the consolidated versions, but every single one I’d learned. Every Law you learn moves you further down a Path. Understanding that Path will be critical later in the process.”

“But I just said I didn’t have a Path,” Ellen interrupted.

I nodded. “You have no known Path. No Paragon has become your teacher. You could have reached out to the Crone, but I’m glad you didn’t. Becoming beholden to an SS-Plus-Ranked monster like the God of Thunder…well, it comes with some consequences.”

“So I see,” Ellen said. She smirked.

“Anyway, you’ll go through your Laws, make sure you understand each of them. The next step is the one you may be able to skip. You need to find a unifying shape of your Laws and move that Path forward. In my case, I created something called the Thesis of the Stormblade. It had a lot to do with anger, and with promises. My rage and commitments drive me, and they would have driven me down the Stormsteel Path.”

“But?”

“But there’s another step you can choose to take, especially if you’ve deviated your core into unknown territory,” I said. “For me, that involved taking the Stormsteel Path’s mental world, twisting and reforging it, abandoning parts I wouldn’t follow, and adding in new ones. Then, when I was finished, it involved taking over the Black Tower and turning it into my own private portal world. I didn’t know exactly what I was doing, but I think I can coach you a little more than the God of Thunder did me.”

“I won’t have a portal world, though,” Ellen said. She scratched her head. “Not unless we find an A or S-Ranked world to take over, and that’s not likely, is it?”

“No. I’ll see if I can think of a solution. If not, we’ll…”

“We’ll figure it out,” Ellen finished. “We always do.”

“Right. Now, are you ready to keep going? We need to make this look as good as…”

I trailed off. Something had shifted in the Wickenberg Portal. A presence had arrived—something that wasn’t the boss, but was more powerful than anything outside of it. I closed my eyes, grip tightening on the Storm’s Blade, and stood. “There’s a Paragon here.”

“You’re sure?” Ellen stood, too, readying herself for battle.

I nodded once, staring at the ocean to our left and the freshwater cove to our right. Then I braced myself. The presence wasn’t as strong as mine, but as I’d learned from the croc, raw strength didn’t always matter. This was the Paragon’s home, and we were interlopers.

And there was also a sense of…familiarity. Not like I knew the Paragon, but like I had some connection to it.

That probably wasn’t a good sign.

Sarah Cullman hated guild wars.

She wasn’t associated with any guild because any guild that got a hold of her would, inevitably, look at the S-Rank healer as the key to victory over the others, and every other guild would look at her like a military target to be removed as quickly as possible. Between that and her general disdain for the guilds as a whole, the Spark of Life had maintained a careful neutrality, only aligning with the Governing Council when it was necessary.

Tonight, it was necessary.

Sarah had been drinking. She’d had a couple of shots…then a couple more. And one or two after that. She’d been out with a couple of her unawakened friends, from before she’d gotten her system…before she’d become the Spark of Life. Sarah liked spending time with her less powerful friends. It helped keep her grounded in what mattered to her. In why she did what she did. And it also helped her keep her mind off what she’d had to do…and who she’d lost.

Fortunately, her S-Rank body and skills processed alcohol at variable speeds, and that meant that she could be as drunk or sober as she wanted to be.

Unfortunately, it reacted to her desires, not her needs.

So, Sarah was a little drunk as the guild war kicked off. A little drunk, and a lot pissed off.

Guild wars were strange, because—aside from her, if she ever took a side that wasn’t ‘The Governing Council’—no one actually wanted anyone to die. Every delver was necessary for the defense of Phoenix, and that point had only been driven home further by the siege and its aftermath. But that wouldn’t stop the A and S-Rankers on each side from going all-out, and that meant it was up to her to pick up the pieces when they did.

A half-dozen Guardians and as many Portal Tyrants lay on the asphalt where she’d set up a temporary field hospital, along with a few of her awakened friends. The GC had a team nearby to protect them, but for the first time, Sarah was a military target. That wasn’t stopping her from healing everyone, though.

And one of those someones was a familiar face. She’d seen Ezekiel Elwood at GC meetings, usually tagging along with his partner, the Portal Tyrant. Everyone knew they were an item. They didn’t make it hard to see.

The A-Rank support had taken a hit from Vern Kellen himself in the opening moments of the guild war. He’d bought Terrel Young a moment or two to react, though—and that had turned the operation from a surgical strike that would have decapitated the Tyrants completely into a slugfest in Phoenix’s streets.

Ezekiel’s arms and legs were covered with bruises and half-healed puncture wounds. The S-Ranker’s thorned-vine assault had been brutal, efficient, and quick; another handful of seconds, and the Portal Tyrant’s recruiter would have been killed.

But Sarah wasn’t the Spark of Life for nothing, and none of his wounds were anything but that—wounds, not a maiming injury that’d take her time and effort to fix.

He coughed pink fluid. “The…the Desert Wind. They’re supposed to be our allies, right? Where’s Kade?”

“The Unbroken Storm?” Sarah asked. She hadn’t seen him or any of his guild members since the fighting started. Not one of them—with the exception of their B-Rank healer, Sophia Walker. She’d insisted on tagging along with Sarah, and right now, she had her hands in the chest cavity of a woman who’d taken a warhammer to the sternum. Her eyes were closed, but Sarah could see the tears. Her level of drunk went down a little, and her level of fury went up a lot. Sophia didn’t need this right now.

“Yeah. Where is he?” Ezekiel pressed.

“Haven’t seen him.”

Ezekiel started to say something, then stopped. He scratched his head. She glared, and he lowered his arm. “Sorry. These thorn-wounds itch like a real bugger.”

“That’d be the toxins,” Sarah deadpanned.

“Come to think of it, I haven’t seen Vern Kellen since the start of the fight, either. Where’d he and the Portal Tyrant end up?”

The Spark of Life had no answer to that, and she had dozens of injured delvers to work on. “Ezekiel, stay still and let the healing do its thing. You’re going to be fine.” She stood and walked to the next wounded delver—a woman with a leg that was still attached, but barely. Then she got to work.

Guild wars made no sense for anyone, and the sooner this one was over, the better.
