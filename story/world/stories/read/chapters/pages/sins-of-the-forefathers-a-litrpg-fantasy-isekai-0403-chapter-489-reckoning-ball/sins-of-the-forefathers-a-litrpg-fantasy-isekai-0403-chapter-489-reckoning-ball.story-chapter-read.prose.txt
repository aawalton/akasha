I stabbed the Breaker down into the replica of the Heartspire. The Mithril splines of the mystical instrument pierced right into the darkened, blood soaked stone of the tiny tower.

I thumbed the activation switch, and in an instant, an injection of wild Aether from the Monster Core within the handle of the Breaker was transferred into the oddly shaped Ward Stone. Logically, what I knew to be happening was that the Aether was wreaking havoc on the delicate Aether channels created by the runes that defined it’s existence. Functions overloaded, misfired, or flat out broke in a cascade that destabilized the entire padlock Ward.

In real life, though, what that meant was that the sliver of stone meant to replicate the Heartspire visibly heated up. It glowed a fiery orange in color for a moment, flash evaporating all of the blood that coated it, and then…

Exploded with a small, weak bang. People all around it were showered in a fine mist of gravel, but that was about it.

Physically, at least.

Mystically, though…

I think our gamble had paid off.

We heard it before we saw it. Somewhere deep below us, I heard what sounded like the grinding of massive stone gears as they rumbled to life. The gnashing of the teeth as they awakened shook the ground around us, and the stone on either side of the plinth began to split vertically beneath it. Everyone had to take several hasty steps backward as the gap only widened with every passing seconds. As it did, the plinth and the Ward Stone I had destroyed began to descend into the pit that had formed where it stood only moments before.

Despite it’s shattered nature, that seemed to be the catalyst for what came next.

A shaped began to arise from the blood of the lake. Foul crimson liquid poured off the sides of it like small waterfalls, frothing and cascading down the sides of it as it appeared from the depths. Long and wide, it spanned the radius of the entire lake, crossing the distance from the shore where we stood…

To the Heartspire in the distance, the limp form of the Calamity I suspected was Nehustan upon the tip.

It was a bridge. A literal bridge at that, hewn from the same dark stone as the rest of this Godforsaken Hold. Smooth and neatly paved, it had stopped its ascent several feel above the waterline of the blood lake. It had no guardrails, and bore no markings. It didn’t need it.

Just the sight of it rising above the blood was enough to disquiet. There was a sense to that bridge. An essence that screamed, ‘step foot upon me, ye mighty, and be tested’.

Because on the other end of that bridge…was our goal.

One way or another, this would all end once we crossed it.

The captives of Karag Varla, the Korvok’s ‘sacrifices’.

Our own captives, including Olag, Maria, and possibly even Sena.

The mysterious Korvokhan, and whatever Rendarchs that might have survived the collapse.

And of course…

Both Azarus…and Nehushtan.

I hadn’t forgotten why we were here. Somehow, someway, this snake had a way for us to bypass the War Wards of Smaragd. He was our last, best hope to get around them, because within those Wards lay our true goal. What I, and every other member of the expedition-of both expeditions had suffered so much for.

The deliverance of Vereden.

The first step towards that was conquering this Hold, though. It remained to be seen just what other horrors it had to throw at us.

Speaking of horrors…

While everyone else was busy contemplating the oddly intimidating bridge to the Heartspire, I considered the living torso of Draskul at our feet. The crippled Rendarch stared back up at me hatefully, now that his desperate little gambit to keep us from our destination had failed. In the end, he hadn’t turned out to be very useful after all. Whatever passkey he might have once had, had been buried in the rubble of his own tower, but we hadn’t needed it in the end.

Now he was just…worthless.

Out of a nowhere, while I was contemplating the best way to dispose of someone who had ended up being just another Korvok, a heavy boot appeared in my field of view. It drove itself down heavily onto the thickly muscled neck of the Rendarch, and a sharp crack filled the air. Draskul’s eyes only had enough time to widen in shock…before he lay still forever.

I blinked, and followed the leg up to it’s own. Bleddyn stared back at me with a purposeful stare. “Let’s go get back our people, Nate.”

I nodded slowly, and turned back around to consider the bridge once more. I raised my right arm into the air, catching the attention of all of our combined forces…

And lowered it, pointing directly at the Heartspire.

“Move out,” I announced heavily. “We’re finishing this.”

………………………………………….

It was an odd thing to consider, but the foot on the bridge to the Heartspire was pretty treacherous. In retrospect, it made sense. Only moments ago, this thing had been submerged beneath a lake of actual blood. For as much of the liquid that had oozed off the sides, it wasn’t actually water. The thick fluid still clung to the stone, threatening to send your feet flying out from underneath you. This was only made worse by the fact that there were no guiderails on the sides of the bridge.

Nobody had actually slipped and fallen into the lake, thank God. But there had been a few close calls, only stopped because the potential victim of the lake had been grabbed by his fellow.

But, eventually, we found ourselves standing at the base of the Heartspire. With the way Nehushtan was draped over the peak of the pointed tower, his long tail was actually only a few dozen feet away from us, trailing in the lake of his own blood.

There was a problem, though. The bridge may have led us here…

But I didn’t see a way in. There wasn’t a door. As far as I could see, there was nothing more than a tall, blank expanse of blood soaked stone where the bridge ended.

In the darkness before the spire, I hadn’t been able to see across the length of it, before we reached the end. Sure, I hadn’t been able to see a warm, open doorway with a cozy fireplace waiting within, but I hadn’t expected that. I’d expected something, anything.

But no. There was nothing here.

I wasn’t the only one to stare at the unexpected dead end for a few silent seconds.

I also wasn’t the one to break that silence. “So…” Renauld trailed off uneasily. “Now what?”

I exchanged a look with Bleddyn. “Well,” He said roughly. “It’s just stone, right?”

“Presumably,” I heard Meia mutter off to the side.

“Stone can’t stand up to the kind of strength we got,” Bleddyn continued, ignoring her. “I figure, if we wail on this wall for long enough, it’s gotta give eventually. If there ain’t a door for us to walk through…”

“Then we’ll make one,” I finished for him, instantly understanding him. I smiled slightly. “If there isn’t a path forward, then it’s our responsibility to create it. I agree. Bleddyn, in our transformed states, I bet we can do it ourselves.”

Sometimes I forgot the sheer amount of physical force that I alone could bring to bear. I so often fought people that were either just as powerful as I was, or even equivalent to me, that I forgot my strength was literal superhuman. Most of the time, I was barely keeping up with the forces I needed to confront. But Bleddyn was right. At the height of our strength, and if we struck at the same time, the level of sheer impact we could impart together was likely on the level of a Terran wrecking ball swinging at full tilt.

More than enough to punch a hole through any structure. And if one blow wasn’t enough?

Well.

Nothing stopping us from trying again.

“Way ahead of you, Nate,” Bleddyn shifted into his Taran-blessed, full werewolf state halfway through speaking.

Have you ever seen a nearly nine foot tall lycanthrope deliberately crack his knuckles, one after another on both hands?

I have.

And I joined him, limbering up for the exertions ahead. I triggered Vis Maledicta Exactoris, exploding to my own scaled, massive height, joining Bleddyn in towering over the. Then, I pushed Might of the Wyrdwood as high as I could possibly manage.

Right now? That was a full eighty percent.

I was so close to mastery of one of my most important Skills, I could tell. Just a little more time, and I could do it.

Something in me knew there was a reward waiting at the end of that path.

Ghostly crimson vines snapped into being, crawling over my entire Rhazal cursed form. But only for a moment. Once they’d reached the correct position, they suddenly hardened, transforming into a nearly full set of pseudo plate mail, greaves, and gauntlets, and a full breastplate now layered itself over my scales, visible beneath my coat. The only thing missing was a helmet, at this point.

To my right, I saw Bleddyn slide into a stance I’d never seen from him before. He slid into a vaguely reminiscent horse stance, left leg leading in front of the other, and cupped his right fist to his side.

“Everyone back!” He suddenly barked, eyes fixed on the wall ahead. “Don’t stand behind us, of ye’ll get caught in the blow back.”

Obligingly, the gathering of our forces did as he bid, retreated well over a dozen feet back down the bridge we’d just advanced upon. Once they were a safe distance behind us…

A spark of bright, butter yellow lightning appeared in his cupped left hand, held over his right. In moments, it had rapidly expanded out behind him in a brilliant, almost blinding display of dancing, Aetherial plasma. The shape of it almost remind me of a spider’s web…

Or, a sudden epiphany struck me, the visible veins within the wings of a bat.

Not unlike my own.

I snorted in amusement at the thought, but sobered up quickly. We had a job to do. Copying Bleddyn, I sank into a mirrored stance to his left. Right leg in front, left fist clenched tightly at the ready.

It was probably for the best I led with my left, anyway. Even transformed as I was, my Primordium fist was still harder than the scales on my right.

I took a deep breath and steadied myself. “We go on three,” I grit out, breathing through the strain of maintaining both of my Skills at such a high level. I wouldn’t dare to fight in a state like this. It was all I could do just to draw breath.

But a single punch?

That, I could do.

Bleddyn grunted an assent, and I saw the arcing veins of lighting behind him start to draw inwards, towards his cupped fist. As the bright plasma began to be shaped by his will, an inkling started to occur to me through my strain.

I had never pushed Might of the Wyrdwood this hard before. It was a surprisingly deep Skill, for something that was nominally only meant to enhance my physical strength. The ethereal thorns and vines it manifested were something I had never fully understood. The first time they had formed themselves into full-on armor, I’d been terribly surprised. But…here at eighty percent…

I thought I could actually do something with them.

Carefully, I mentally reached out to the Skill, and…coaxed it somehow. Not to try and draw out more strength.

But more projections.

They oozed out of the gauntlet over my left hand, layering themselves in ever more armor, forming true spikes. Under my will, thorns as long as my destroyed daggers grew from the knuckles of the conjured armor, and…

I reached my limit. That was all the manipulation the Skill would allow me, at eighty percent.

Still, this would be enough. My fist now glowed brighter than all the rest of my Skill-borne armor combined.

I breathed in. “One.”

Bleddyn shifted, his plasma forming into the shape of a spike.

“Two…”

I tensed every muscle in my body, ready to spring forward like a bullet from a gun.

“Three!”

Both Bleddyn and I launched ourselves forward as hard as we possibly could. Enough that the stone beneath our starting positions cracked from the force of it, the noise echoing out over the lake like the crack of a whip. In a fraction of a second we appeared before the wall between us and our goal, and in a nearly synchronized movement, we drew back our fists…

And drove them into the wall.
