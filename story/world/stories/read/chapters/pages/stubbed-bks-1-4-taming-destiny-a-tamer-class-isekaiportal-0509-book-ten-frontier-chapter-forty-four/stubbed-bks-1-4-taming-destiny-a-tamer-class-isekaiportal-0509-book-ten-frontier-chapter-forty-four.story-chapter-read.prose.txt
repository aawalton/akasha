I feel a wave of outrage flood over the Bond from Romeo a moment before he pounces on me. Though I could easily match his attack with a wall of earth or fire, I decide to use this sparring match to work on something else.

Pulling my spear from my Inventory, I do my best to match him strike for strike.

Romeo’s claws slide off the metallic surface of the weapon Nicholas gave me from the family treasury as I use the spear to defend as much as attack. Few Titanbends have been spear-users so there wasn’t much choice. This spear is certainly not one I would have chosen for myself, as good as others would consider it. Only my Metal-Shaping can interact with it, and even that struggles due to the enchantments imbued in it for durability and resistance to damage.

Still, perhaps because of that, it forces me to improve my spear-wielding Skill rather than ‘cheating’ with magic.

It takes me a bit of time to get into the groove, and I feel proud satisfaction prick at me through the Bond as Romeo’s claws evade my spear to rip into me time after time. But the injuries are hardly a problem for me – Sensation Management dulls the pain so it doesn't distract me, and Flesh-Shaping easily fixes the damage.

The satisfaction Romeo started the fight feeling fades into increasing frustration as time wears on and I continue fighting just as hard as I was at the start – and begin winning. This fight is exactly what I need – enough danger to maintain my concentration, but not so much that I need to throw technique out of the window in order just to win.

Finally, Romeo disengages with a snarl of deep aggravation as, in the last five moves, his claws have been deflected twice, his mouth once, and he’s earned two new injuries to paint his feathers in red.

You’re playing with me! he snarls.

I shrug.

“I’m just taking advantage of a sparring session.”

A play fight?! Romeo screeches, making a noise I’ve never heard from Bastet’s throat, one that’s more bird than cat even as he puffs up his feathers to make himself look bigger in his outrage. Evidently ‘sparring’ mistranslated a little.

Before I can do more than blink, he sends blades of air flying at me. I don’t have time to pull up an earthen shield, but I activate the shields in my tattoo and armour. The power that Romeo put into the attack is made evident by the marks left on my armour’s chestplate, despite both shields being active.

A moment later, I’m knocked over as Romeo throws himself bodily on me, snarling crazily. I manage to get my spear between us, keeping his teeth at bay by acting as a bar against his throat, though his claws still tear at me, shredding my armour little by little. Lightning that sparks off his body slams into me, depleting my shields rapidly. If I don’t want to be cooked from the inside out, I’d better finish this quickly.

With him so close and also one of my Bonded, sending my mana into his system is as easy as breathing.

First, I stiffen the joints of his limbs. The the claws tearing at me slow and then stop as Romeo finds himself unable to bend them in the way he was expecting to. Then the wings buffeting me freeze too.

Romeo turns his head to look at them, and I feel both confusion and helpless frustration surge on his side of the Bond.

What’s happening? he demands.

“I am,” I tell him casually. “Do you surrender?”

I feel the hesitance on his side of the Bond, but he continues struggling as much as he can within the limits of his own body.

I decide to up the ante a little and freeze the muscles of his diaphragm and ribs.

Romeo’s eyes go wide in shock and fear as his breath lessens to a faint wheeze – enough oxygen allowed in so that he doesn’t die, but not enough to be comfortable.

What. Are. You. Doing? he asks. I find it interesting that his mental voice is affected, even though it doesn’t rely on air to be produced.

“Do you surrender?” I ask again.

For a moment, he eyes me. He’s still, apart from the very faint fluttering of his chest as he gasps for what little breath I’m allowing him.

Then he slumps. His body doesn’t go far – his frozen joints see to that – but the muscles I’m not controlling all relax.

I surrender, he sighs, sounding annoyed, but, if I had to guess, I would say it’s more directed at himself than at me.

Controlling my mana still sitting in his body. I relax the joints and my hold over his breathing. I keep an eye on him just in case he decides to restart the fight, but he just eyes me warily.

When he’s breathing easily again and his limbs are his own, I push him off me, standing and using Water-Shaping to clean myself off – blood and dirt from the forest floor don’t make the nicest of armour paints.

I notice him eyeing me thoughtfully.

“What?”

He hesitates for a moment, before evidently deciding to go for it.

Did you ever feel like you had to fight me seriously? Or was this all a play fight to you?

“I wasn’t playing,” I shrug.

You told me you were play-fighting, he accuses.

“Sparring, not play-fighting,” I correct. “Sparring is where we practise fighting together to get better at it. Even adults do it – Bastet does too,” I point out before becoming more thoughtful. “Though with everything happening recently, we haven’t done much of it since we arrived. Something else to organise,” I murmur.

But…this was a fight to determine who could mate with the females, not a ‘spar’, Romeo says, sounding a little lost.

I lift an eyebrow.

“I’m not interested in mating with Bastet,” I reply, keeping my communication over the Bond purely to amusement and not letting on any of the deep discomfort that I feel at the thought. On so many levels that would just be wrong.

Then…will you drive me away? he asks, his ears sinking on his head.

My second eyebrow rises to join the first.

“Why would I do that?” Frustration mixed with self-reproaching shame drifts over the Bond between us. Romeo looks away. I draw my own conclusions based on what he said before the fight. “Because that’s what males do?”

Romeo is silent for a moment, then huffs out a sigh, looking back at me, though his eyes don’t quite meet mine.

You are more powerful, he admits. I lost.

“And that’s fine,” I tell him – an idea that apparently never occurred to Romeo before, judging by the way he jerks and the surprise over the Bond. “We’ve all lost fights before – but learning from them is how we get better. Which is why we spar in the first place.”

I sense him testing that idea, the sensation coming across the Bond giving me the distinct impression of a raptorcat cub prodding at something new and jumping back immediately in case it lashes out, then, when it doesn’t, prodding at it again.

He’ll probably need some more time to consider that, but there’s an answer I need.

“You started the fight because you thought you were stronger than I am; that you would win a fight between us. Correct?”

Romeo pulls himself out of his thoughts and sends me a sense of assent.

Powerful males mate with females, he reiterates. I wanted to prove to the beautiful female that I am more powerful than you are.

“And that’s fine – today. But you now have a choice.”

A choice?

“My companions, my pack are beings I rely on,” I tell him bluntly. “I can turn my back to them without fear that they will take advantage of perceived weakness. I can be injured and know that they will seek to heal me, not kill me. So yes, a choice. Either you stay and become a proper part of the pack – acknowledging me as the leader, in perpetuity, even if you become stronger – or you leave.”

I give him some time to think. Because I’d much rather lose the capabilities of a Tier three, than risk that he’ll turn on me at some key moment of a battle.

But even if he decides to go, I don’t regret this fight. Honestly, the experience has been quite interesting.

Tier threes were once an existence that I could only hope of defeating with the help of several Tier two Bonded, and even then it was risky. Now? Even without my Bond with Romeo, I could have defeated him at several points of the fight.

I remember how dangerous the fight with Raven was – how his acidic magic, powerful physique, and ability to fly meant that several of my Bonded died trying to kill him. And in the end, he did most of the damage to himself by using an acid-bomb ability that sent him flying without control into the trees.

The fight with Romeo proves that the tables have changed. And sure, Romeo doesn’t have acid jets, but lightning is just as dangerous and, capable of flying, he could have hit us from above just like Raven did. Plus, his air-claws and air-teeth were invisible and highly dangerous. And no, he doesn’t have an acid bomb, but that tornado really was impressive – until it hit my earthen spikes and fell apart. Really, Romeo’s weakest area was his use of physical claws and teeth – or, rather, his lack of strategy when frustration made him mindless.

But even if I consider the fight with Raven, I know I would win. I can fly, so his ability to dominate us from the air would be nullified. I could heal the wounds from his matrix-dissolving acid relatively easily now, and I have so many more strings to my bow.

I could now control the wind around his wings, forcing him into the ground. My control over water could dilute his acid and make it less powerful. I could pierce his scales with a spike of earth tipped with metal as he swept in to attack us. And I strongly suspect that I could beat him in a Battle of Wills now that my Willpower is so much higher than it was at that point.

By my reckoning, Romeo isn’t that far off Raven in power levels. But the difference between the result of the two battles just proves how far I’ve come. And so I can afford to set boundaries now that I couldn’t back then.

Finally, I feel Romeo come to a conclusion.

Fine, he agrees. You are stronger. You are packleader. Then he eyes me with that same light in his eyes from before the fight, a light I now recognise as competitive challenge rather than threat. But we will spar lots, yes?

“Winning in one of those won’t make you packleader,” I remind him warningly.

He sends me a sense of acknowledgement.

I know. It is enough to win. Then he flashes me a mischievous look. And if you do not wish to mate with the beautiful female – Bastet – then she might look at me even if you did win.

I consider it for a moment, then shrug.

“I always encourage my companions to seek to improve themselves, in whatever way they want.” Then I eye the rumpled and broken feathers from when he slammed into the earth pillar, and the matted and blood-soaked feathers around injuries from my spear. “Let me heal you,” I insist, though I don’t move until he gives me wordless permission.

Though my mana has invaded his body before, the first time was to quickly remove the traces of the parasite’s influence, and this second time was with the objective of attacking him – I didn’t spend much time actually looking at his body. Now, since I’m healing him, I do. And I find some interesting differences.

For all that he and Bastet look very similar from the outside, there are some key differences inside. Their digestive tracts aren’t exactly the same. If I had to guess, Romeo is actually more omnivorous than carnivorous – meat is the main part of his diet, but he’s capable of eating fruits, berries, and fungus. Bastet, I know from experience, is a pure carnivore. She doesn’t even like cooked meat, though Trouble and Ninja do from time to time – probably because I introduced them to it as cubs.

Bastet also has an extra organ – one that manages a type of hormone that is important for the health of her feathers and claws. Romeo doesn’t have that at all, though he has some glands in his skin that Bastet doesn’t have.

Those aren’t the only differences between them, but they are the most notable. I heal Romeo almost absent-mindedly as I consider what those differences mean.

I had wondered how there could be raptorcats in both this world and the samurans’. I had wondered whether there had been a rift or something in the past that allowed the ancestors to spread from one world to the other. But now, I’m leaning more towards the theory that, somehow, winged panther-like creatures with bird-like talons have developed independently of the other on two different worlds.

Unlikely, certainly, but possible. After all, this world seems to have humans, much like those on my world, and there is remarkably little difference between my body and, say, Loran’s – apart from those caused by my Class and his Evolution. What explanation there could be for that I have no idea.

But that doesn’t mean that procreation is possible between Bastet and Romeo – or between me and a potential partner, either.

I’ll have to see how things go between the larnatis and raptorcat – if they procreate without a problem, it bodes well for me and a potential partner. If not…well. Maybe magic will have the solution. My Flesh-Shaping is able to affect DNA, I muse, though redirect my thoughts fairly swiftly – thoughts of children are more than a little disturbing right now, for all that I’d like to have a family some day.

You said you wished to speak to me, Romeo prompts me as I withdraw my magic and my hands from his healed body. He sits up and begins to groom himself, removing the traces of blood and rearranging misaligned feathers.

“Yes, I did,” I remember. The whole fight and aftermath had briefly knocked it out of my mind. “I was wondering something. Well, now, two things. First of all, you’ve travelled pretty far, haven’t you?”

I have roamed this side of the mountains, yes, he agrees, tilting his head to the side curiously.

“Well, can you tell me what you noticed – places of particular power, beasts of particular power, that kind of thing. We’re trying to build up more of a map of our surrounding area.”

I can, he replies after a pause. Though I do not know how useful it will be for you. Territories change lords frequently.

I wave that away.

“Knowing that a territory is in high dispute would actually be quite useful.” After all, that means there’s something worth having in it.

Very well. And the other thing?

I look at him with excitement rising. Could this be how I have a breakthrough?

“I want to learn how to control lightning – can you help me?”
