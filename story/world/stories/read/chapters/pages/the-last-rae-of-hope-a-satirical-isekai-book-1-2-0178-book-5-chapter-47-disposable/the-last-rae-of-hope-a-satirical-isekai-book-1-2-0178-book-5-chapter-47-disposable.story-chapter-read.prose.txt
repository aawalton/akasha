Immediately upon finishing our translocation, I noted that the Inner Sanctum of Chairo was dry. It wasn’t just somehow sheltered from the snow; it was dry-dry, the lack of moisture on anything raising my hackles. I reached up to push wet hair out of my face and found it wasn’t wet, either. Looking down at my boots and finding no mud sent a shiver up and down my spine.

“Cleansing ward?” Nora asked before I even finished the thought. “Mother Liora, probably. She thought you stunk the first time you showed up in Chairo.” She pulled at her cloak as if irritated. “Though it seems like a massive waste of energy, if you ask me.”

I grunted, still unnerved. “Maybe.”

Then I glanced at Vernie, who was most decidedly not refreshed. Not only was she still wet, but she also carried a complex odor, half of which I could attribute to hanging out with skreethi and the other half to the sewers she must have run through.

Chester sat at attention on her shoulder, and his dark glow suggested that whatever cleansing Nora and I were given had been blocked by his presence.

Maybe she foolishly thought she could wash the blood of all the murders she committed by becoming a neat freak?

“Hmm…” was all I could come up with to dismiss the matter.

The forum stretched ahead of us, lit up against the night sky. There were lamps lit with small flames, fully encased in glass-like crystal, a sharp contrast to what they illuminated.

Upscale buildings had rotting rubbish piling at their bases. Decorative planters had gone to weed. It was in all ways wrong, a place that had been carefully maintained for a long time only to have decay overtake it in the final moments.

It was the crystalline statues that were the real horror.

They were scattered through the forum in no particular arrangement. They were certainly not displayed or arranged with intent. They were bulky, human-sized crystals, almost appearing encased in rocky armor, their flat facets catching and throwing back the lamplight in small fragments.

Some were mid-step. Some had their arms raised. A few were frozen with their angled mouths open, caught in the middle of saying something they never finished. In their stilled faces, I could see the beginnings of fear, as if they had understood what had happened in real time.

None of us recognized any of them in particular, though most of their features had been obliterated by sharp, glassy angles.

We exchanged several long looks of horror, shaking our heads furiously when one of us was about to call too much attention to ourselves by shouting about the implications of what lay around us. With burning and indignant resolve, we stormed forward, none of us trying to look at anything in particular too closely.

Olethros twinged, pulling his cloak around himself as he fell in step next to me.

“Are you alright?” I whispered.

“I’m fine,” he replied, his eyes shut.

“Actually, you’re a bit transparent.”

He seemed to shrink a bit. “Perhaps we’re getting close to Sparkles.”

I looked at Chester, or the dim shadow that was all that Chester could manifest, then at the increasing number of statues glowing a little too brightly. “Perhaps it’s something else…”

Eventually, we came across a sign.

Harden your resolve against demons. The faithful who surrendered their form still give, and their continued Purpose sustains our protection.

Gave their form? Willingly?

Or had it been taken away simply for asking questions?

Were they people reduced to some cheap holy barrier, prepared for the moments after the main crystals fell?

The worst part was the ambiguity—whether they’d actually chosen this, or whether Relias had framed it as a choice.

And then he just twisted the whole thing like it was the people’s fault this happened?!

Nora whispered into her staff as we walked, her voice catching as she described our situation to Galenus.

The high temple rose at the far end of the forum, still lit from within, the golden light coming off it too bright and too saturated, especially since the outer holy crystals were down. The pools lining the way had been drained since our last visit, their basins discolored by greenish algae and dirt. The statues of the past Chosen Ones still presided over them, with one new addition.

A statue of me.

She was smaller than the rest, cowering on her knees as her right hand was held upward in desperate supplication. For a moment, I couldn’t breathe.

The mockery of it! The Chosen One, betrayed and broken, on her knees, begging.

Is his whole Purpose in life to continually piss me off?

Because he’s more than fulfilled it!

The really insulting part, everyone, was that after all his bluster and lies, he’d still gotten my nose wrong.

I rubbed at my own sullenly before Nora nudged me to continue.

Before I could take more than a few steps, however, a silver magic circle manifested between us and the temple.

Mother Liora, shimmering into view in gold and white robes, threw up her hands with a victorious laugh. “Welcome! Thank you for coming to see us!”

Nora held up her staff. “Us?”

“Yes. Master Landon here has been dying to see you.”

She stepped out of the middle of the circle, allowing his dark robes to form unhampered. Long, dirty strands of silver hair hung limply from his cowled hood, the entire top half of his face obscured by darkness. He strode forward, pulsing with black vapor from shoulders to sleeves.

Nora went very still beside me. Tetora coiled tightly, waiting for the right moment. Vernie and Chester both hissed.

Olethros cracked his eyes open slightly, fixing them on the dark mage directly in front of us. “Hmm…” was all I heard him murmur.

Mother Liora stepped back into the circle, the wards flaring around her feet. “You cannot run from your destiny. You cannot hide! There is no Paradise for those who turn away from the Eternal Covenant! There is no place for those who turn away from their Purpose—”

Something tightened in Landon’s posture just before he twirled around, his hand swiftly dipping into his robes.

Shnnk!

No use of animus. Nothing magical that would have registered as a threat to a priestess. Just a sword, drawn and stabbed all in one go.

Mother Liora looked down at the sword embedded in her chest, deep red blood blooming across her clothes. Her aura flared and faltered as her face twisted in horror.

Then she fell.

Landon pushed his hood up and plucked a sliver of ereborite from his forehead.

“It never occurred to you that I would have safeguards for working with such an accursed substance?” he asked her with only a hint of curiosity.

She gurgled and clawed at the ground, blood pouring from her mouth.

“I’ll take that as a no,” he replied, putting the black stone he had plucked against her forehead with wispy fingers. “A precaution, you understand. I don’t want you healing yourself.”

Her eyes rolled back as the jewel disappeared, her spluttering ceasing.

“Master Landon!” Nora cried, peeling her eyes off the dead woman. She ran toward him, but he held up his left hand, a crystal ring glittering in warning.

“A safe distance, if you don’t mind.”

Nora gasped, her aura turning blue. “Let me try to remove that ring—”

“You’ve broken one of my tools, Old Wolf.” Relias’s voice, almost conversational, came from somewhere in Mother Liora’s robes. “Now I find I must dispose of even another.”

“Master Landon!” Nora reached out to grab him, but he turned away, even as Vernie pulled her back.

“Not now,” he said dismissively, crystallization overtaking Landon’s left fingers. “Do not give him any more information than necessary.” He then gestured with his right hand, and a small communication orb floated up from the dead woman’s pocket. With a snap, the dark mage shattered the orb. “His last orders to me were to find weakness in your powers, Lady Nora. I would think you interacting with such a crystal would serve him more than you wish.” He then glanced at me, his eyes hard. “And I do not consent to your attempts to save me, either.”

Nora’s face twisted even as I stiffened. “But—”

“Don’t mourn me.” He didn’t look up from his hands. “I understood the long-term risks long before I accepted this work. I simply chose to believe the benefits outweighed them.” The crystal crept past his elbow. “I told myself it was necessary, believing the demons had stolen away my star pupil. But the moment he turned it against humanity… I understood precisely what I built, and what that had made me in return.”

“He lied to you—everyone!” Nora cried.

Master Landon nodded. “And I excused it every time he did. But you did not.” He paused. “You surpassed me long ago, Nora. I never found the right moment to say so—until now.”

It was painful to watch. His skin turned translucent first, then opaque, then hard, facets popping out in erratic eruptions. Yet he watched it happen to himself with a strange detachment, until he could no longer see.

“Master!”

His transformation was complete.

I turned to Nora.

She was staring at him, her eyes fixed open but most likely not seeing. Her hand trembled as it reached toward him, then fell.

“Nora.” I reached for her hand.

“Not now,” she shouted, waving her arms. “I can’t accept this now!”

The others kept watch as I tried to reach for her again.

She pushed me away. Then her jaw set. Her eyes moved from Landon to the high temple at the end of the forum, to Paradise far above it, to the cloud still hanging between them. She looked back once at the statue of me, and her expression hardened.

“Not now,” she said again, softly.

“Alright…” I agreed, stepping back. “We should… We should see if Ragnerus can join us. I promised I would—”

“Not now,” Tetora echoed, his ears going flat as he stared at the high temple. “We have one more to deal with.”

“One more?”

“Just wait,” was all he growled.

The forum itself was very quiet. Beyond were the distant sounds of battle, but even those seemed to be settling, only the occasional blood-curdling scream being carried on the wind.

Then the high temple doors creaked open, and Aleph the Ox-like stepped out.

He appeared shorter than before, or maybe it was just that he’d lost weight, his frame gaunt beneath the set of very plain white robes he wore. His brown hair and beard, now streaked with gray, had grown wild. The ox horns curving back from his temples looked dull and more pronounced against the hollows of his face.

He had no weapon nor entourage.

It was just Aleph, stone-faced and exhausted, with circles upon circles under his eyes. His hands crossed in front of him, steady and calm, as if he had accepted all that was and all that would be.

His gaze swept across us—Nora, me, Olethros, Vernie and Chester. Then it slid past Tetora as if he’d made the choice not to see him at all.

The temple doors shut behind him with a hollow and sickly boom.

He raised his chin and inhaled deeply before shouting, “Leave this place. The high temple is forever closed to you.”
