Within the central tower of Matrazere, inside the busy central ritual chambers full of multiple teams of wizards and endless errand runners, Unobus sat on the command throne with his eyes closed as he flicked through mental viewpoints via clairvoyance directed through the access orbs.

The tower suddenly got extra charged. Immediately, he got a report on a spirit world vector, a ‘danger,’ as something was attacking the rune there. He felt only derision and amusement at that.

Scoffing outloud, Unobus shook his head. “Attacking the rune? A danger? Yes, let’s get right on that, protecting the invulnerable against gnat bites.”

A few laughs from the elders cut through the air.

Various signs rapidly intensified — the tower shook, and the rune itself began to flare so bright with magic and energy, many wizards went slack-jawed as their perceptions turned to it. Everyone could feel the dimensionalities warping in the spirit world. Actual change was occurring, and far too close for comfort.

Slowly, Unobus rose out of his seat, feeling increasingly disturbed by the ongoing anomaly.

By the Thousand Forefathers… this level of alteration this quickly is unthinkable… that should take years…

“Counterattack in the spirit vectors now!” He commanded, flinging his staff. “Find the source!”

“Your Ascendancy,” said an aide reluctantly, “We haven’t bothered with operatives of that nature here since… ever. Because of the rune. Our scryers are already looking remotely, and the source isn’t clear. The effect seems to use a strange methodology. Sympathetic information is alien and garbled; not actionable.”

“Garbled? What are you talking about, garbled?! It’s coming from somewhere! There must be a bloody target!”

But as the aide opened his mouth, the intensity ramped up even more, where the rune’s absorption of energy lit up the world outside, and a high-pitched whine could be heard.

Thousands of breaths sucked in and held as the rune flickered.

Unobus found himself inundated with mental exclamations, questions, and requests, and heard nothing terribly different in the physical chambers he was in, as wizards panicked. Several began running — to where, he didn’t know. He ignored them all like white noise. His inner eye was fixated on the rune, seemingly getting… unstable.

It’s not possible. We’re being tricked. It must be a hallucination!

Horror repeated itself as the rune flickered a second time. In the spirit world, the aura of Matrazere began to fray and come undone.

Unobus found his eyes gravitating to the source of a strange sound in the chamber.

It was a wizard sobbing into his hands in the corner as if damnation had come for him.

We’re dead if we succumb to fear. Don’t they get that?

It succeeded in shocking him out of his stupor and his denial. Steeling himself, suppressing his own fear for the sake of survival, and projecting calm, Unobus sat back down in the command throne.

He called, “Prepare for secondary defense! All priority to the runic tower.” Even as he spoke, the rune was flickering and dying, and the high-pitched whine was high enough to be painful. It was obvious that it was close to being disabled. Sabotaged. “Attrition remains our victory, and they still have an army and a fortress in their way.”

Many wild, wide eyes swept over to him, as the gravitas and the insanity of that statement were not lost on them at all. But they soon steeled themselves as he did. They turned and began their announcements, communications, and spellcraft, as the engine of the Dominion moved into action once more.

In the spirit world, the area of Matrazere was flooded with intense, chaotic energy trying to flee outward, but such was the endless and extreme flow of it from ‘beyond,’ that it soaked into the environment and twisted it. Thus, the underlying structure supporting the rune was being undermined.

Samantha was vaguely aware, such was her work in simply holding the connection open in the final moments of expenditure, together with Azure, and, to some extent, Bast. He was no longer cackling, and his grip on her hand was tense. Sweaty. The ‘vomit stage’ had finally hit him. Technically, it was easier near the end, but they were already drained, and the sense of nausea was intensifying.

Just a little longer…

As soon as the weakness of the spirit realm spread into the tower, which was its primary anchor of reality there, the rune was being undermined even as it was flooded with energy.

It didn’t take much. As soon as the warping reached it, in mere moments, the extreme perfection got corrupted. Marred.

The energy blasting into it flowed through improper channels, and a section of the rune went unstable. The rune burst with a tremendous blast, merging with the energy flooding the zone for a brief moment before it was gone.

In the real world, there was a sharp cracking sound that echoed, and then everything just died. Switching to a mirror with part of her mind, Samantha viewed the tower — there was no apparent physical damage. The rune had simply gone out as its spirit world analog was annihilated, robbing it of any meaning. It still existed, it was just… flaccid. Futile and unable to enforce itself.

It was a deceptively miraculous moment: the tower was no longer invulnerable, no longer unassailable or inaccessible. The impossible had just been accomplished; a mystical sabotage for the ages.

Samantha didn’t need to give the order. Dax’s excited voice broke over various other protestations in the gestalt, getting the greater machine of the army back into focus. Quickly, contingencies were triggered. Exceptionally long-range attacks started immediately, as magic blasts, enhanced arrows, and even hurled catapults from the backlines started up in earnest.

Even now, though, the Dominion had not completely panicked. The spells were countered easily, and the projectiles hit magical force fields and shattered.

Great. This isn’t over by a long shot.

When the energy had finally completely exited the dimensional enclosure and the great collaborative act was complete, Bast gasped and went limp, and the other two had to catch him and lower him to the ground.

Sweating profusely, eyes half-closed, he mumbled a question weakly. “We did it… right?”

“Indeed,” Samantha said. “Your part is done, Bast. Thank you.”

Samantha was suddenly struck by the fact that she had still not opened her eyes. She’d simply moved mirrors over and split her perceptions, while still keeping her Third Eye as her main viewpoint for the world.

Nodding vaguely, Bast passed out.

Okay, and now we gotta figure out how we-

“Goddess?” Dax chimed in the primary gestalt. “Are you seeing this? The sky…”

Samantha looked back through many mirrors to see the battlefield. The general environment was cast in hazy darkness, as before, but the intensity of it was fading rapidly; light was making progress. It felt like morning was hidden somewhere beyond, and destined to arrive.

The sky held a more amazing sight. Six bright stars were shining, and even as she watched, they seemed to be intensifying. One was more intense than the others and bigger. Really big.

“What the hell?” Samantha muttered.

A familiar voice rang loudly in her head, now hyper-excited. “Undefeatable, indestructible, unbreakable! Just words and protestations!” The voice of Endless Templar. “So I told you. So I told you also that I’d see you here, after the beginning of the end. You are my herald… and I am yours!”

Shockingly, Samantha realized the voice was connected to the star, but it was not just a light in the sky… nor was it distant.

The tail appeared briefly behind it as it raced down and down with increasingly amplified speed. It was a falling star — a meteor.

The leading, glowing orb came down toward the tower and exploded into a force field, in the most violent display Samantha had ever seen. The magic could not contain this violence, which spilled out into the realm. A fireball engulfed the whole fortress, and the shockwave cascaded outward, knocking nearly every combatant to the ground or into each other, most getting physically stunned in the process. The very walls Samantha was behind, on the backlines, cracked from the impact. The sound shook her to the bones and would have deafened her if not for being an Avatar. Others didn’t appear so lucky.

The fortress walls of Matrazere were blown out from multiple angles, shredded into pieces by the unstoppable force of a hard meteor impact. It sent chucks spinning over the field, crushing thousands of zombies with each rolling piece.

One of the secondary towers fell. The central tower was scorched but stood tall.

A second meteor came down, as the voice in her head spoke again, “On Calrenazzod, you must give me a new name, Goddess.” The meteor was unstopped, shredding through the top of the primary tower and impacting into the fortress courtyard, where another terrible explosion bloomed like a wide, wild flower of flame and dirt and ripped apart stone and concrete structure. The ground rippled with tremors; another tower was gone. Stone and earth became a rain over the battlefield, and her own armies began to cast protection spells just to avoid collateral harm. “For you are whom I am ultimately called down for, and by.”

Samantha remained utterly spellbound, as another star became a meteor and dropped, and then another, and another — boom, boom, boom! The greater fortress of Matrazere was engulfed in fire and torn apart by earth and cacophonous raw destruction. The central tower was the last thing seen, still tall above it all, but the fifth meteor impacted it right at the base.

The final tower of Matrazere fell; it disappeared into the billowing fire.

The sixth and final meteor dropped dead center, and its impact was an insult to injury, as it was the greatest mass of them all. It was tantamount to a small nuke going off, and the protections already flaring up in full and complete defense by her armies were well-reasoned, as the secondary fireball and billowing smoke came for them too. Instead, the army-wide network of protections caused it to warp around, following paths of least resistance, with those inside comparatively shielded.

But Samantha knew even without seeing — in the great billowing cloud of surrounding destruction — that at the center, at the impact point of the meteor, there was nothing left but a crater.

Matrazere had been turned to dust.

“Six Moons Falling,” the Goddess whispered.
