Ellen opened her eyes.

She and Kade lay on the cold, stone floor of the Lightning Tower’s lowest floor, shivering as she untangled her limbs from his. The purple and black Portal Tyrant’s uniform hadn’t helped one bit. Somehow, the cold rain and freezing wind they’d experienced while metaphysically naked to the storm had cooled their physical bodies instantly.

Not that she wanted Kade to untangle too quickly. The core-bonding, skill-ranking process had been far more intimate than their previous attempts. It hadn’t just been the nudity—though he wasn’t bad to look at. It had been…vulnerability on his part.

Yeah, that was it.

He’d been vulnerable before, but outside of the few weeks his core had shattered, he’d never been completely helpless. This was a new experience for Ellen.

And the way he’d handled himself had been…enlightening, to say the least.

As Kade gently pulled his hip out of her stomach and their impromptu game of Twister ended, she sighed. “That’s that, I guess.”

“What do you mean?” Kade asked.

“Well, we got the rank-up. The amount of Mana flowing between us seems to have increased, and your core’s more open to me. And I wouldn’t trade that for the world—it feels better than anything else I’ve experienced. But it’s also over. Until we push Shadowstorm Battery to A-Rank, or learn how to do a core bond without ranking up, we won’t have that kind of connection again.”

Kade leaned back on his elbows. His dark hair had gotten messed up in their attempts to escape the tangle, and he stared at the ceiling. His chest rose and fell. As it did, Ellen found her own breathing synchronizing with his. Then, after a moment, he sighed heavily. “I know what you mean, and I think I can help.”

He started unbuttoning his uniform, and Ellen found her fingers working her own shirt’s fastenings. “We have time for this?” she asked, hoping to hear the right answer.

“I made time for Jeff, Jessie, and the Portal Tyrant. I’d better be able to make time for you.”

Half an hour.

Half an hour was all the time I could spare for Ellen.

That left enough time to get dressed and, as we climbed the Lightning Tower again, have a quick conversation about Bob. He was a problem—an incredibly minor one, but a problem nonetheless. And neither of us had a truly good answer to him. But I had an idea.

“If we manage to survive the next day or so, it’s time to leverage the Governing Council against him,” I said. “They might be the only organization in Phoenix more powerful than his company.”

“Or we just go scorched earth.”

I squeezed Ellen’s hand. “I’m not sure you mean that.”

“Why wouldn’t I?” She stared at me, and I found myself stuck on her piercing gaze. “I didn’t want to do it before because it’d make a mess for all of Phoenix. The Traynor Corporation’s like a gigantic squid—and yes, I know we just fought one of those. Its limbs are all tangled up in everything.”

“Also like something we just did?”

“Shut up. The point is that it’d be a disaster for Phoenix if Bob died. But so what? Phoenix rose from the ashes of the Portal Blitz. It can survive the loss of a CEO and his too-big-to-fail corporation, and if it can’t, maybe it deserves to die.”

We debated the merits of various strategies as we climbed the Lightning Tower and arrived back in the throne room. Tanat opened her eyes, took one look at us, and smirked. I found myself growing red, but before I could say anything to defend myself, she raised a hand. “We have a visitor.”

“What?” My hand let go of Ellen’s, and a moment later, the Storm’s Blade manifested in it. “Where?”

“She’s not in the tower. She entered through a glowing gold portal, and at first, I thought we were under attack. But the portal closed as soon as she went through. She’s been out there for…fifteen minutes? Maybe longer. I decided not to interrupt your core-bonding, since you two so clearly needed it.” Tanat’s smirk grew wider for a second, and her eyes flicked between Ellen and me.

I ignored her and peered out into the maze. It was a tangle of paths and half-built ramparts. Tanat had finished the outline, and now she was working on the trenches and walls. And, out in the distance, surrounded by a barrier of lightning rods that crackled with lightning as it poured down into them, was a familiar, eel-like monster.

“Magda?” Ellen asked. “What’s she doing here?”

“I don’t know, but I’m going to find out.”

The two of us hurried through the maze, closing the gap as quickly as our S-Rank bodies could carry us—which was about the speed of Deimos on a busy highway. We got to her in only a minute or two. I cast a single Ariette’s Thunderhead, and Ellen shifted into her Voidwalker form. The draw on my Mana increased noticeably as our bond and Shadowstorm Battery reacted.

The eel looked terrible. In just the last day, she seemed to have lost an unhealthy amount of weight. Ribs showed through her scarred, but still smooth, skin, and something about her eyes seemed sunken. When they met mine, a spark of something traveled through them, and she surged to the edge of her lightning prison, careful not to get too close to the rods in the ground.

“Kade Stormblade,” she said, voice dripping with seduction—and with fear. “I’ve come to bargain with you.”

I didn’t trust her. The A-Rank Paragon had already tried to betray us in her portal world, then attacked my sister. But we had all the power right now, so I waited a few seconds and nodded. “Go on.”

“When you killed the Placid King, it broke something in my relationship with him. I found myself feeling emotions I hadn’t felt in a long time. Fear, mostly. Fear toward the God of Thunder.”

“Don’t care,” Ellen said.

“Let me talk. Please.” Magda’s gigantic eyes washed over Ellen, then back to me. Lightning struck, and she flinched. “I am…was…his core-bond partner. I know more about the God of Thunder than anyone else. Yes, more than you, too, Kade Stormblade.”

“And I assume you’re going to share out of the goodness of your heart,” Ellen asked. I nodded. Magda couldn’t be trusted. She was about to lie to us.

The eel’s head shook, eyes wide. “No, absolutely not. But I would be willing to trade that knowledge for assurances.”

“What knowledge?” I asked.

“The Halls of Thunder.”

“I have no idea what you’re talking about,” I said after a brief few seconds.

“Oh, I think you do.” The Paragon of the Sliding Surge moved, leaning back almost casually. The motion was incredibly smooth—almost to the point that I couldn’t tell she was still missing a fin from our previous encounters. “If you don’t, Eleanor Traynor would.”

I closed my eyes. “Ellen? What do you know?”

“Nothing about the God of Thunder, but…” she trailed off.

“Exactly,” Magda said. She waited. “Go on. Tell him.”

“The Ghostdream portal we cleared. The one that connected us to the Crone. You think the God of Thunder has one?” Ellen asked.

My eyes opened wide before I could stop myself. Magda noticed. She smiled even wider. “No. He doesn’t have a Ghostdream. He has a nightmare. See, a long time ago, the God of Thunder wasn’t a god. I don’t know his title—I haven’t entered the Halls of Thunder myself, and I wouldn’t know anything about it at all if it weren’t for a dream he had while we were core-bonding. But I know enough to piece it together.

“The God of Thunder was a servant to a master of his own, millennia ago. A Stormlord. An elf. A powerful lightning mage. Something happened—I don’t know what, so don’t ask—and he ended up with his master’s power, or at least some of it.” Magda paused dramatically. “I believe the secret is inside the Halls of Thunder.”

“And you think this matters to us why, exactly?” My heart pounded in my chest. This was an opportunity.

“I don’t think. I know. The God of Thunder has been very clear. When his offer of amnesty expires, he goes to war with you. You’ve never seen him at war. I have. As you are now, your victory is impossible. But if you explore the Halls of Thunder and uncover the storm dragon’s past, you may find a weapon against him, or a tactic to bring him to your level.”

I breathed deep. We had tactics. The Traynor-Overholz Cannons were on their way. The null core stood ready if Jeff could safely use it. The entirety of our shared portal world was slowly being turned into a trap for any monster foolish enough to attack it. It’d be ready for the God of Thunder when he arrived, even if he arrived a second after his reprieve ended.

But Magda didn’t think it’d be enough.

And I believed her.

A long time ago, my first year of high school, I’d found myself up against an asshole who’d been picking on a group of girls. Anime nerds—the goofy, socially awkward types. This guy really seemed to have it out for them, and after giving him a few chances to stop and the school a few chances to intervene, I’d taken care of the problem. Or so I’d thought.

He’d been one of the rare bullies who had a handful of friends, and they’d jumped me after school three days later. It had gotten ugly, and the next day, I’d brought Jeff. Everything kept escalating, over and over, until it was totally out of control; I’d underestimated Fred’s ability to find allies, and he’d underestimated my need to win.

Eventually, we’d both backed off by unspoken, mutual agreement. We’d still fought three more times over the next two years, but neither of us wanted another all-out war after school every day.

That wouldn’t happen with the God of Thunder. He and I would escalate, but he wouldn’t stop. He couldn’t stop, because that feeling of pressure wouldn’t let him. Ascension, at the end of the day, mattered more than his relationships with his allies, enemies, or even the universe as a whole.

He really was just like Bob Traynor.

I shook my head slowly. “And I suppose you know how to access the Halls of Thunder?”

Magda smiled widely. “Of course I do. Why would I come to you with this information freely if I didn’t have something worth holding over you two?”

Ellen squeezed my hand tightly. I squeezed back, not ignoring her unspoken worries, but putting them on hold for now. We waited. The seconds slipped by, every one of them less and less replaceable.

Then Magda spoke again. “I will give you the path to the Halls of Thunder. In return, you will allow me to return to the portal connected to the outside of your city and will not pursue me into it. I will return to my old life, without the God of Thunder, and the two of you will pretend you’ve forgotten I even exist.”

Another pause. “Do we have a deal?”

A half-hour later—we’d moved impossibly quickly—our team was assembled.

Me, of course. I wouldn’t send anyone else into the God of Thunder’s hidden portal world if I wouldn’t go myself.

Ellen. She wouldn’t let me go without her. I didn’t blame her, even if I really wanted her helping Tanat with the Lightning Tower. Her help would be critical, anyway. She’d been instrumental in clearing the Crone’s Ghostdream.

The Portal Tyrant. He was available. As far as S-Rankers went, that was a glowing recommendation.

And, to my surprise, Sarah Cullman, the Spark of Life.

We’d assembled in the Portal Tyrant’s castle, inside a fortified sparring room—with the Paragon of the Sliding Surge in attendance.

“Ghostdream portals are extremely rare,” Ellen said. She was giving the briefing today.

“Not a Ghostdream,” Magda interrupted. I shot her a glare.

Ellen nodded slowly, obviously annoyed. “This is not a Ghostdream. This is something more unique. We’re not sure what to call the typing, but it seems to behave similarly to a Ghostdream, based on what Magda here said. It’s tied directly to its owner’s past—to memories it considers core to that monster’s very being.

“Magda believes the Halls of Thunder have something to do with the God of Thunder’s rank-up from S to SS-Rank, which triggered a pressure that has forced him along a single path for the last twenty thousand years or so. This gives us two opportunities.”

I cleared my throat. “First, it gives us the chance to level our skills a bit more, in a likely S-Rank portal. That alone would be critical right now.”

“Assuming it doesn’t kill you, Kade,” the Spark of Life said. “If you die on my watch, your sister’s gonna kill me.”

“Har har. But the other opportunity is to learn something we can weaponize against the God of Thunder himself. If we can do that, we may be able to use it to defend either Earth, my portal world, or both—assuming he attacks them both.” I grinned. There was no joy in it. “We’re almost out of time. Assuming he’s a being of his word, we have…six hours? Less? We need to move quickly.”

“Great. What are we waiting for, then?” the Portal Tyrant asked. He had his massive hammer over his shoulder and a pair of hand axes hung from his belt. The four of us were…well, not ready, but as ready as we’d ever be.

I nodded at Magda. “Open up the Halls of Thunder.”

“You’re aware that this is probably a trap portal, correct?” she asked.

“I’d be shocked if it wasn’t.” Who knew what we’d find there? A trap? Impossible monsters? A waste of time that ended any chance we had of killing the SS-Plus Paragon? Or maybe, just maybe…that weapon we were still holding out for.

Magda nodded. A single fin held out a keystone core, and from it, a portal opened.

At first, it was jet-black. Then, gradually, shapes took form. Clouds. Only clouds. Then lightning. A familiar lightning.

Red-orange lightning.

It wreathed the portal, crackling and arcing to the sparring room’s walls as the lights blew out around us. I drew the Storm’s Blade; blue-white lightning ran down the blade and cracked in the air around me.

Then I threw myself into the Halls of Thunder, my ad-hoc team of S-Rankers right behind me.
