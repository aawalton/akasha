The morning came soft and dim, the shutters still catching only the earliest edges of light.

Elynara blinked against the pillow. Apparently still working on farm time, her body was already awake at this hour. But she didn’t mind.

She felt better now. Sleep had done its job, and the bruises clinging to her ribs and legs had finally faded to faint reminders. A stretch told her everything else was moving properly again. The stiffness of the days prior almost completely gone.

And then…

A squeak.

Then a bounce.

Then absolute chaos.

Starpuff landed on her cheek with the force of a smug apology, tumbled across her shoulder, flicked her hair, and rammed into her belly with what could only be described as enthusiasm unburdened by self-awareness.

“Wh–stop–!”

She rolled with mock-groan, more surprised than hurt, arms flailing through the covers just in time for the puff to loop back and nudge her nose again.

“Gentle. We talked about this. You’re supposed to be gentle.”

Starpuff pulsed cheerfully and rammed her cheek again. She sighed.

“Every morning with you is a trust exercise.”

Still, she was up. She swung her legs over the side of the bed and rubbed her eyes, feeling the leftover haze peel off her thoughts one breath at a time.

Yesterday had gone well. Fire magic felt less slippery now, and more like a tool she could trust to do exactly what she wanted. To a degree. The shaping practice had paid off. And soon, in due time, she would finally get to test her surprise spell properly.

Not yet, though.

The puff seemed satisfied with her vertical status and zoomed off in a celebratory spin, trailing faint motes like it had won something.

She didn’t argue. Letting her arms rise in a slow, spine-stretching arc, she hummed out the last of her grogginess and dropped lightly back onto the mattress.

Time for training.

But first she fed the puff some mana, lest the obnoxious spirit make trouble for her, again.

Her hands fell into her lap. Breath slowed. The staff rested on her knees, letting her draw mana a little faster. Capacity training was muscle memory now. She would draw the mana in, almost like breathing, fill her core up, push its limits, and then ‘breathe out’. Mana flowed into her chest with familiar rhythm, held and released, shaped and stored. And so her cycling went.

And it grew. Bit by bit.

It took longer now, the cycles needed increasing. But that was the price of progress. She pushed anyway, through a dozen sets before her core buzzed with the familiar low hum of near-limit strain.

The edge hit just as the first footsteps echoed in the hallway outside.

Seventy-five.

She smiled faintly, breath still even, and let the sensation settle.

A nice round number. And definitely an improvement.

She held still a moment longer, letting her mind drift just slightly far enough to wonder. Could she double her progress? Perhaps if she trained twice a day, and kept the sessions smaller? Hm. Worth testing. She would add it to the plan.

But before she could think further, her stomach made a loud, opinionated declaration about its current emptiness.

Elynara blinked once, hand on her belly.

“Okay, okay. Message received.”

With a quick stretch, she stood and padded toward the hall, Starpuff zipping ahead with all the grace of a self-declared breakfast scout.

Time to eat.

Breakfast passed without much incident, aside from Mina snatching Elynara the moment she stepped into the hallway.

“Come on,” the younger girl whispered, tugging her hand. “You’re not skipping prep today.”

“I wasn’t planning to?” Elynara replied, already matching pace.

They passed Father Alaric mid-discussion with two of the nuns near the storeroom shelves. He didn’t say anything, only offered them a glance and a gentle smile. The kind that said he saw everything, especially with the way Mina had looped her arm tighter as if claiming her spot.

Elynara raised an eyebrow. Mina didn’t notice. Or pretended not to.

Either way, she was all too happy to declare to anyone nearby how much better Elynara looked today and that helping was good for recovery, not bad, and besides, she’s already moving better so obviously it’s working!

Elynara didn’t argue. Just helped clean up, set the tables, and carry some stuff Mina had trouble moving alone.

After breakfast, she stayed. Not because she had to; but because it felt nice.

Helping with laundry baskets, sweeping corners that had somehow collected even more dust, and letting herself get pulled into a mild tug-of-war with the younger kids over a missing broom.

Mina and Pufflet joined in, of course, the latter contributing moral support by headbutting furniture at strategic intervals. Starpuff made itself entirely unhelpful by bouncing between shelves and trying to knock over precisely folded washcloths. And failing, thankfully.

The puff sparkled proudly regardless.

By midmorning, one of the kids asked if she would show off her fire magic again.

“Will do, but later. Evening is better for that.”

They pouted. But a few sticks of chalk and pencils distracted them fast enough.

Elynara leaned back against the stair post and exhaled slowly.

She could head to the guild... but Alicia had very specifically told her to take things easy. She had already done plenty for the morning.

Her gaze drifted upward.

Library?

Yes. That sounded right.

She had books half-finished, old notes scribbled out to check on, and there just might even be something she missed before… Maybe.

She made it halfway to the library before pausing again.

No. She had a better idea.

Why not just… ask?

The quiet library nun was sitting near the entrance as usual, surrounded by what appeared to be three different ledgers and a stack of papers. Elynara cleared her throat gently.

“Um. Hi. Could I ask about... well, books on magic? Or spirits? Anything like that?”

The nun blinked once, looked up… and then smiled.

“Of course, dear. Though I’m afraid we don’t have much on that. Most of what we have is local records, traveler notes, and the like. No tomes of spells, I’m afraid.”

Elynara figured as much. But still, she followed along as the nun pointed out the relevant shelf rows and offered commentary here and there.

“There’s a bit on shrine traditions... mostly local customs. The spirits are treated as minor guardians in our region. Still worshipped by some of the forest folk.”

“Nothing else?” Elynara tilted her head.

The nun nodded thoughtfully.

“There are some older accounts, though, from distant regions: places where spirits are honored under totems instead. But nothing detailed.”

Thinking back, it looked like Father Alaric likely told her everything there was to learn here, after all.

Still, hearing it from someone else helped. And well, she didn’t know about the totem thingies before!

…Not that what she learned now was much to go by either.

But it was still good to know. You never knew what would end up being useful, after all.

The nun moved on to the subject of magic, with a faint chuckle.

“Now, that’s the slippery one.”

Elynara raised an eyebrow.

“Really?”

“I’m afraid so. There are some mentions of it in travelogues we have around here. Mostly brief and secondhand. If you’re looking for anything more specific however, there are none to be found here. Just brief mentions and explanations here and there.”

“Huh.”

“I can help you find the books where magic is mentioned. Would you like that?”

Elynara nodded eagerly at that.

“Yes, please!”

At that, the nun went around the library, efficiently picking up the titles. There might not have been that many books here, but it was still impressive how she seemed to know where everything was.

“Those few are travelogues, but their authors had the penchant or interest in magic. They noted down a lot of what they have seen, and likely even more they just imagined. But neither of them had any magical training on their own, it seems. Those two books are a little more concrete, talk about elemental magic, of sorts. And this one here mentions seeing a mage using healing magic once.”

“Healing?”

“I’m actually not sure if it’s magic. The book talks about it possibly being some sort of divine providence. It could just be another form of magic though. And there is one account in Old Sites and Lost Lines that mentions it, but it’s vague.”

“So... no cantrips. No spells, advice, or anything? Just... hearsay?”

“I’m afraid so.”

The nun smiled apologetically, then bid her good reading before returning to her ledgers.

Elynara stood there for a bit longer, clutching a few more vague collections in her arms. She read for a while. Flipped pages. Took notes. But it was all more of the same. Hints. Fragments. Nothing actionable.

Still...

Elemental magic existed. Her own hunch confirmed. So did healing. Probably. Maybe. At least it seemed that somewhere out there, people were doing more than just tossing fireballs and stuff.

She just had to figure out how.

Eventually, she slumped back into the cushioned reading chair by the window and stared out at the garden beyond the courtyard wall.

Her cheeks puffed up slightly in frustration.

Which, of course, was when Starpuff returned from who-knows-where and immediately pounced her cheek with a happy boop, surprising her.

“You–!”

The puff spun smugly and settled in her lap.

“Troublemaker,” she muttered.

But her hand still rose to pet the little menace anyway.

It was a strange little thing today. Not particularly helpful. And yet, somehow... comforting.

She hadn’t found any proper answers.

But at least she now knew some things for sure. And a little bit of trivia here and there.

Deciding enough was enough, Elynara noted she still had some time to kill. Not much, but enough.

Which meant it was snack time!

Saying her goodbyes to the nun, she was soon outside and not heading towards the guild for once.

The market stalls had shifted into their afternoon rhythm, bright cloths catching the wind just a little slower, and the sellers lounging with that halfway-between-lazy-and-ready posture only long practice could teach. It made browsing easy. And more importantly, it made impulse buying far too simple.

First came sweets. A pouch of honeyed nuts. Two bundles of pressed jamrolls wrapped in wax leaf. Three sticks of something crunchy coated in what claimed to be sugar, though Starpuff’s tentative nibble suggested it was mostly optimism.

“Your fault for trying.”

Elynara murmured, watching it whirl in protest and then dive into her collar to sulk. She still didn’t know if it actually tasted things, or if it was only interested in them because she was. Or because they just looked interesting.

She continued her slow stride through the market, turning towards the fruit stalls next. One in particular catching her eyes.

She hadn’t seen this kind before; it was small, green, and speckled with purple near the top, like someone had painted it halfway then got distracted. The merchant gave her a deal when she asked about flavor.

“They’re a little tart. But not too much. Best eaten cold.”

She bought four. And ate two while walking.

“Mm. Not bad.”

She wandered, watching her shadow stretch longer across the cobbles, occasionally batting Starpuff away from someone else’s hanging fabric. Her thoughts, of course, had turned elsewhere by now.

The show.

She did promise the kids something big. And sure, she could make fire. Flash it. Spin it. Maybe even draw a few shapes. But it needed flair. Presentation. Something more... dramatic.

Something throwable.

She chewed thoughtfully.

And then smiled.

She found what she needed at a spice vendor’s stall, half-hidden behind a curtain of dried chilies and sun-dulled roots. The woman running it squinted at her request, then raised a brow when Elynara explained.

Following that she went to get some other things she noticed from two other stalls.

A few minutes, a suggestion, and one minor smoke-related mishap later, she had what she needed.

Probably.

Possibly.

Hopefully.

It would work. She was mostly sure.

But she had everything she needed now, and so her steps took her slowly back to church.

The church’s courtyard air carried the smell of simmering grains and open window herbs. Her baggage hung a little heavier now with the faint clinking of pouches, one an odd-shaped bundle tucked carefully into the side.

Time to prepare.

First, she wanted to put some training in.

She dropped her bags off in her room, tied her hair up, and headed for the back patch of stonework near the wall; the same place she always practiced.

She didn’t go for anything flashy. Just practicing the moves to show later. Fire shaping, mana compression, the usual balancing act of too dim and too bright. She didn’t want to show off something unstable. Not around the kids.

Starpuff watched from a windowsill like a pompous judge, puffed up and glowing slightly.

“Don’t you start,” she muttered, reshaping a curl of fire between her fingers.

It took a while, but Elynara was eventually satisfied once she made sure everything would go well. Even then she still double checked, just in case. It was better to be safe than sorry.

Next, she had some organizing to do!

After a quick rinse and a fruitless attempt to convince Starpuff to help corral the kids, which left it merely spinning in a mocking loop, she managed to wrangle two of the more energetic boys and whispered her plan.

Their eyes lit up like lanterns.

And that was that. Half the courtyard was soon involved. Tasks were assigned. A few sneakier kids went to find Mina. Others gathered stools. One tried to make a sign and got very offended when Elynara said it looked upside down.

Mina blinked once at the scene when she arrived, accompanied by conspirators, of course.

“What... exactly is happening?”

“Preparations,” Elynara said simply.

“For what?”

She grinned.

“You’ll see.”

That got a long sigh. And a reluctant smile. And then Mina rolled up her sleeves and joined in.

By the time the sun dipped toward late afternoon, the courtyard had been cleared, swept, and roughly organized into a ring of crates and benches. There was even an overturned bucket that Elynara definitely did not approve of, but had no time to fix.

The audience was ready.

And so was she.

Well, almost.

Even the nuns had stepped outside to watch, chatting softly near the church door. Father Alaric stood just a little to the side, hands folded, the ghost of a smile behind his usual quiet eyes.

The children, of course, were already buzzing like spring bees. Half of them had taken to chanting “Fire Queen!” again, much to Elynara’s dismay.

Starpuff floated above the crowd, flashing excitedly in pinks and greens like a festival lantern gone rogue.

She stood at the center of the courtyard, palms slightly damp, breath caught somewhere between a laugh and a held note. Her nerves pressed tight behind her ribs.

This is silly, she told herself. You have fought a big bad scary monster. There is nothing to fear here.

Just an audience.

Of children.

And adults.

And people who fed her.

Her stomach flipped once.

But she straightened anyway. Raised her staff lightly in both hands and gave a small bow.

“I promised I would show you some magic,” she began, voice a touch softer than usual. “So I will.”

A cheer rose from the crowd. A pair of claps. Someone whispered something about sparks. Starpuff did a celebratory spin and then pretended to faint dramatically onto the roof’s edge.

Elynara smiled.

And began.

The first flame appeared above her palm: small, steady, and warm. She let it hover gently, then nudged it sideways. It bounced once into her other hand. Then back.

A few of the children gasped.

Next, she made it grow, then shrink. Stretched it out like string, then curled it inward like a sleeping cat. The flame flickered obligingly, each motion measured and smooth.

Then a second flame joined.

And the game began.

They chased each other. Swirled around her arms. Danced from shoulder to wrist. Her fingers moved like a conductor’s, coaxing shapes and flow. She ‘caught’ them between loops. Tossed one above her head and caught it on the return.

Laughter followed. The kind that didn’t care about elegance, only wonder.

A third flame came next. That one juggled in irregular loops, and her rhythm was lost for a moment before she regained it with a sheepish smile that only made the crowd cheer harder.

She even managed to coax all three flames to ‘juggle’ properly, brightening at the top of their arcs.

The sky slowly darkened behind her.

And the flames only brightened as a result. With a bit of a nudge from her.

Then she felt the shift.

Not in the air, but in herself. Her nerves faded beneath the rhythm of focus and flame. The courtyard fell quiet, expectant.

She drew a slow circle in the air with her hand.

The flames followed, forming a ring that pulsed gently in the dimming light. She gave up control over them, and as they started to disperse, shaped another spell, then directed it inward, toward the center.

And as it entered, she changed it.

Then, with a little over the top flare a bird emerged.

It was small at first, then brightened, its wings fanned in flame and tail trailing sparks. It flapped once and soared above her, arcing just close enough to make one child shriek with laughter as it looped by. Another tried to catch it and fell over backwards with a grin.

The firebird circled her twice.

Then she raised her staff.

And the bird became a butterfly.

The wings curled wider. Softer. Flame spun slow, controlled, and utterly enchanting. As the butterfly slowly moved toward her open palm, she created a second, smaller one that flickered faintly gold.

Together, they flew.

They beat their glowing wings across the courtyard, circling once above the heads of the children. A pair of little hands reached up. One nun gasped softly. Starpuff spun twice in place, its glow flickering like an echo of the butterfly's wings.

Elynara breathed slowly.

And gently brought one butterfly to rest at the tip of her staff.

She raised it. Turned it.

The butterfly trailed behind like a ribbon of light.

The second landed on the opposite end.

And now both ends of the staff shimmered.

She spun, slow at first. Once. Twice.

Circles of light swept through the courtyard, soft and warm, flickering across the walls.

Then she stopped.

Let the butterflies dissolve into flames, the last circle still dispersing light.

The courtyard held its breath.

She didn’t speak.

Instead, she reached into her satchel with one hand while keeping the other steady on her staff.

The fire pulsed.

And burst.

Not from her hands, but from the air in front of her, where a single compressed shape took form.

A phoenix.

Its wings unfurled with a roarless rush, its flames long and bright. And just as it ignited into full shape, she tossed the small bundle she had prepared into its path.

It burst mid-air, igniting with color.

Shavings, threads, petals, bits of wax-slicked ribbon. Each one caught the light as it fell, spiraling through the wake of the phoenix like stardust.

The courtyard gasped.

The phoenix circled once, wide and high.

Then rose.

Higher.

A single beat of wings.

And it flared once, sharply, before exploding in a quiet scatter of flame and embers.

Their pitter patter the proof of its beauty.

A soft shower of heatless sparks rained down.

The courtyard lit with awe.

Even Father Alaric’s eyes widened faintly, lips parted just so.

Starpuff had practically fainted mid-air.

Elynara bowed again, deeply this time, one hand pressed to her chest.

“Thank you for watching,” she said, breathless and warm.

Applause came next. Scattered at first, then louder. The children cheering. The adults clapping. Someone whistled. Mina just smiled, arms crossed, a faint pink in her cheeks.

Elynara stood at the center of it all, grinning like she had swallowed sunlight.

That had gone... very well.

Too well, maybe.

But she would think about that later.

For now, she simply basked in it.

And then everything exploded.

Not literally, thankfully. But close enough.

The moment she stepped down from the courtyard’s center, the crowd surged forward.

First to rush in were the children with their faces bright, arms wide, and voices boisterous. A dozen tiny hands pulled at her sleeves and cloak edges, their voices tumbling over each other in a mess of joy.

“That was amazing!”

“Make the bird again!”

“No, the circle part–how did you do that?!”

“Teach me how to do fire! I wanna explode!”

“You’re the Fire Queen forever now!”

Elynara held up both hands, already flushed. “It was just–just a little practice! I’m not even that good yet–”

That didn’t matter.

She was the Fire Queen. That’s what they had decided.

Even Mina looked far too pleased about that name, arms crossed, one eyebrow raised like she dared her to try and deny it now.

The crowd parted briefly as Father Alaric stepped through, quiet pride visible plainly on his face.

He reached for her hands, still a little warm from the casting, and clasped them gently between his own.

“You’ve come so far,” he said. “And we’re all better for it.”

Her mouth opened. Then closed. Then opened again.

No words came.

Her cheeks burned.

She was fairly certain her ears were on fire. Or maybe her soul. Either way, she looked anywhere but at him and mumbled her thanks, trying to convince the giggling kids her blush was “just a trick of light.”

No one believed her.

Dinner came next, and she hoped the excitement would die down.

It did not.

The entire meal was more energetic than usual. Kids still mimed her spellcasting with wild hand gestures. A few tried to imitate the butterfly flaps with napkins. One declared loudly he was starting a “fire squad,” and another immediately followed with “I’m gonna be Fire King then!”

Elynara had no idea how to respond to that one.

She sat hunched over her plate, spoon in hand, trying to vanish between bites of stew and sips of tea. Every time someone passed, they clapped her shoulder, or said something like “still glowing strong, huh?” or “are you hiding phoenixes in your sleeves?”

She nearly choked on her bread twice.

Eventually, Mina took pity on her. Or perhaps she just got annoyed on her behalf.

“Alright, alright,” the younger girl said, rising with her arms crossed. “Fire Queen’s not taking more questions tonight.”

“But–”

“She needs time to recharge. Fire doesn’t sparkle itself, you know?”

It worked. Somehow.

Elynara gave her a very weak thumbs-up from behind her mug.

The rest of the evening passed with slightly less fanfare. She helped dry dishes, slowly winding down from the day. Sat by the window for a bit with Mina and puffed some mana into Starpuff, who was still floating like it had just won an award.

And when the place settled into calm and the lanterns dimmed, Elynara curled beneath her blanket with her head still too warm to sleep.

But not in a bad way.

In the soft, fluttery way that came with being seen. Truly seen.

Fire Queen?

Maybe not.

But tonight?

She didn’t mind the title too much.

The lantern glow in her room flickered low, shadows stretching long across the floorboards.

Deciding she couldn’t sleep yet, still too heated from the day, Elynara sat cross-legged on the bed as her staff rested against her knees, chest humming with a faint warmth.

She might as well practice what she planned this morning.

The house was quiet now. Most of the little ones were already asleep, their laughter tucked into dreams.

And even Starpuff calmed down, curled near the window, its glow dimmed to a soft jade-like pulse that almost seemed to rise and fall in rhythm with her breath.

She focused inward.

The cycles came easy tonight. Mana pulled, held, released. Then again. And again. Smooth, steady, and she didn’t feel the strain yet. The rest really had helped.

Another draw.

Her chest filled with the familiar buzz of power. Just enough to press at her edges, just enough to test the walls of her capacity.

And when she pushed one more time she felt it shift a little again.

Seventy-eight.

A quiet smile tugged at the corner of her lips.

Progress.

She leaned back, letting the mana settle. Letting her thoughts wander.

Her mind flicked back to that morning. To the theory she had brushed past in her notes. A simple one, really.

All this time, she had been training at her limit. Drawing in mana only when near full. Letting it stretch, push, and slightly overflow. That tension built capacity. Like stretching fabric.

But… what if that wasn’t the only path? What if just the motion itself had some effect too?

Breathing, not bracing.

Inhale. Exhale. A rhythm. A habit. Could that work? Could smaller, frequent motions still yield some growth?

It was worth testing.

Not now, though.

She yawned into her sleeve, eyes already half-lidded. Starpuff drifted over like it felt the drowsiness too, flopping into the crook of her arm without complaint.

“Tomorrow,” she whispered, tucking the blanket up over her shoulder. “We’ll try tomorrow.”

The spirit chirped once. Soft. Sleepy.

And Elynara let herself drift, the warmth of progress still clinging gently to her chest.

She fell asleep smiling.
