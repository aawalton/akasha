The outer edges of the Deadlands weren’t too bad, all things considered. The Death Aura was weak enough that Asher’s Complete Resilience was able to shrug it off completely, and he figured anyone with an evolved death resistance would probably be able to do the same. There were certainly the occasional roaming band of zombies that he either had to avoid or cut his way through, but they were about as exciting to fight as monsters were. With no skills, no thoughts, and no desire for self-preservation, all the zombies he encountered would stop their aimless shambling and start sprinting at him in a straight line the moment they saw him. Killing them was as simple as stabbing out with Distortion and his living wood dagger once they got close, the sharpened blade cutting clean through their rotting flesh and weakened bones with ease. It was efficient. It was fast.

It was boring as hell.

Asher groaned as yet another trio of zombies sensed him from afar, the three of their heads snapping over in his direction as they began loping across the barren ground toward him. All he wanted to do was keep going in a straight line toward the center of the Deadlands, but if there was one admirable thing about zombies, it was their persistence. If he didn’t stop and personally put them down, they would chase after him endlessly. And seeing as he had to make frequent stops to recover his energy from the repeated uses of Distortion to help him cover ground faster, it was always better to deal with the zombies now rather than when he was tired.

While waiting for the zombies to reach him, he briefly focused on Samantha’s Eternal Mark and scried her location. Closing his eyes in the Deadlands when he knew there were a few zombies running directly at him might not have been the best idea, but he’d been practicing with Eternal Mark ever since his stint in the wild realm, and he’d gotten better at taking quick peeks using the skill’s second-tier effect.

His mind shot back a few miles to the edge of the Deadlands, and Asher smiled as he saw Samantha yanking her living wood arrows out of a few zombie corpses, the hunter looking entirely unfazed by the rot and gore. Moxy was standing close enough to her that he was able to catch the left half of her body as well, and Asher could only shake his head as he spotted the brownish blood drying on her hands. Only Moxy would want to try actually boxing with a zombie when she could easily kill them from afar with her air blasts.

Letting his mind snap back to his own body, Asher checked on the zombie’s progress. Now only a few hundred feet away, he nodded, watching them as they drew closer.

One of the stranger things he’d noticed rather quickly about the Deadlands was the sheer variety of zombies that wandered around. Men and women, old souls and young warriors, it appeared as if the King of Death didn’t discriminate when it came to adding to his ranks of the undead. Even now, each of the three zombies running his way appeared to come from entirely different eras.

One was a man covered in rotting leathers with a helmet loosely secured to his head. There were three horns going down the center of the helmet, which Asher could only assume meant something, and he had some strange, wooden carvings strung across his neck on twine that Asher had no idea how was still secured. To his left was a woman in a flowing gown that had been largely torn to tatters. Once upon a time it must have been magnificent, and a few tiny gems still dotted it here and there, but now it was rotting away and stained black and brown with blood and dirt. The third zombie was so decayed that Asher couldn’t even tell if it had once been a man or a woman. Little more than a skeleton with scraps of flesh hanging off of it, it let out a near-constant groan as it ran toward him, keeping pace with its more muscular zombies with relative ease.

Asher didn’t know why these three zombies seemed to be wandering around in a group when they each looked so drastically different from one another, and it wasn’t exactly like he was going to get the chance to ask. As the first of them got within twenty feet of him, Asher formed a tunnel of compressed space and thrust outward, ensuring his stance was stable and his grip correct as Mornamol’s lessons drifted through his mind. The zombie with the tri-horned helmet received a living wood dagger straight to the throat, severing the twine holding his necklace together and lopping off the zombie’s head effortlessly. Without pausing, Asher withdrew his blade, shifted the compressed space, and thrust out again, this time killing the zombie that was little more than a moving pile of decayed flesh. Finally, he finished off the gown-wearing zombie, whose attire had slowed her down a little compared to her comrades.

Wiping the old, brown blood off of his dagger, Asher stored it back into his Personal Rift before continuing on.

That was about how the first couple of miles went. Run in a straight line, pause to kill zombies, stop to recover energy, and run a bit more. Thankfully, on top of all the sparring his team had been doing over the past few weeks, Asher had finally added cardio to his daily routine. That combined with his Belt of Strength and the ability to shorten twenty feet to a single step allowed him to fly through the Deadlands far quicker than any normal person could ever hope to match, only having to stop here and there to fight undead monsters and recover energy. But despite how quickly he moved, a few dozen miles weren’t something even he could traverse in only a few hours.

Damn, I miss having Samantha fly us wherever we needed to go, Asher thought, panting as he stopped to collect his energy yet again. The downside of having a tritessence-filled gem from the wild realm tucked away within his rift was that now, every time he ran low on energy, he also had to resist the urge to crush the gem and receive a chunk of it back, rather than waiting for it to restore naturally. Obviously, he knew he needed to save the gem for an actual emergency, but running one’s energy low wasn’t exactly the most pleasant feeling in the world. It was almost like exhaustion and starvation rolled up into one, and only the knowledge that the sensation would abate within a couple of minutes allowed him to tough it out every time.

It was at about the ten mile mark that Asher began to notice a difference in the Death Aura that permeated the Deadlands. The weak, icy aura that he’d been able to pretty much ignore began to grow more dense, the sensation seeping down into his very bones and causing him to shiver every now and again as he felt the magic get to work. The Death Aura was officially stronger than what Complete Resilience was capable of protecting against, and he could actually feel his body ever so slowly dying. Thankfully, Eternal Regeneration was actually strong enough to counteract this, in no small part thanks to his Amulet of Improved Healing, but the sensation of his body constantly decaying and regenerating wasn’t all that fantastic.

And if that wasn’t bad enough, the zombies were starting to grow a bit more worrisome.

Just as Rulfar had predicted, the stronger the Death Aura grew, the more empowered the undead became. That meant they ran faster, they screamed louder, and even their decaying bodies became more durable. His living wood dagger was still capable of decapitating them fairly easily thanks to the unique properties of the magical wood, but with their speed picking up, encountering roving bands of undead became more and more precarious the further into the Deadlands he went.

“Nasty… zombies!” he shouted, narrowly leaping out of the way of a rotting woman’s teeth as she threw herself nearly ten feet forward while he was distracted with four other zombies all trying to swarm him. The groups were also beginning to grow in size, and Asher had only managed to kill half of this group from afar before the rest got too close to him.

Ducking under an errant swipe from a man whose hands ended in claws for whatever reason, Asher thrust his dagger up under his chin, slicing through his spine and putting him to rest once and for all. He slipped into the astral to dodge two zombies who attempted to lunge at him from either side, watching as they collided and went down in a tangle of limbs. Returning to the material realm a few feet away, he thrust down twice in quick succession, using Distortion to reach their heads easily enough and take them both out. That just left the woman who was desperately trying to take a bite out of his flesh, and Asher twisted out of the way as he caught her lunging at him yet again from the side. Prepared this time, he slashed out with his dagger, the blade sliding straight into her open mouth and slicing clean through her rotting head. As the last zombie went down, Asher paused, catching his breath as he walked over and crouched down to better look at one of the zombies.

“Another oddity,” he muttered, lifting up the clawed hands of the zombie that had nearly torn his throat out. Where his yellow and blackened nails should have been, the zombie’s fingers seemed to end in jagged bone claws instead. It wasn’t the first strange zombie he’d seen, and the number of zombies that had weird add-ons was growing the deeper into the Deadlands he went. Before bone-claws, he’d also witnessed an undead woman with two additional arms grafted onto her torso, a man that had spat some sort of acidic sludge at him from his rotting mouth, and even another zombie decayed past the point of recognition that scrambled around on all fours like some sort of freaky spider.

Asher didn’t know if the zombies were naturally evolving due to the intensity of the Death Aura or if someone was purposefully creating them to be unusual, but either way, they were definitely more dangerous than their regular counterparts. The surprise mouthful of acid had been the first actual injury he’d taken, melting partway through his shoulder before he’d washed it off with some water and let Eternal Regeneration take care of the rest.

Checking the angle of the sun, Asher stood up, wondering just how long he’d been running for at this point. Based on the map he was using, and monitoring the distance he’d traveled from his Eternal Mark on Samantha, he could tell he was roughly a dozen miles into the Deadlands. The fact that he was only about a third of the way to the middle really put into perspective just how massive the Deadlands truly were, and simultaneously, just how powerful the King of Death he was attempting to kill was as well for his skill to cover such a vast distance.

As he waited for his energy to replenish, Asher stood there, contemplating how he was actually going to launch his surprise attack. Until he knew what the King of Death’s lair actually looked like, there was only so much he could really do. But that didn’t stop him from fantasizing about slipping into some giant, blackened castle and making his way unseen to the throne room. He imagined shoving his dagger into the lich from behind while it lazed about on its throne, and he grinned at the thought of dispatching such a powerful enemy before they even knew what had hit them. As battle-hungry as Asher was, the different members of the Council of Death were far too powerful for him to even risk messing around with. Wizards were capable of utilizing hundreds of different skills, which meant playing around with his food would be far too risky. No, for once, he’d have to put his opponents down hard, and put them down—

A gleaming black sword pierced through Asher’s stomach from behind, tearing him from his daydreams as his eyes widened in shock. Stunned from the blow coming out of literally nowhere and the pain radiating from his gut, Asher didn’t even move as a pale face leaned over his shoulder, turning to stare curiously at his shocked expression. The face looked like it belonged to a young man, though his eyes were beyond bloodshot, stained completely red as though every blood vessel in them had burst long ago. The man opened his mouth, and Asher recoiled at the points his teeth had been filed into.

“So you’re the one killing all the troops,” the young man said, laughing as though he’d just won some sort of game he’d been playing. “I knew I’d find you first!”
