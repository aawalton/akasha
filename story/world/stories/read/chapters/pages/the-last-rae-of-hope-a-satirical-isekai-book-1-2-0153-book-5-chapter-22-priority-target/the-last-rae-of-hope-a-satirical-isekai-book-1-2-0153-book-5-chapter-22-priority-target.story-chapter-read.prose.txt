Nora did absolutely nothing to hide her suspicious stare. “Well?”

I straightened, folding my arms back behind me in a mock display of innocence. “Well, what?”

“You’re not going to tell me what you did to get promoted to pink?”

“I… Reached out to a family friend?”

“Maura has friends?!”

“Hey now,” I chided. “She gets along with Mama, doesn’t she?”

Nora’s eyes narrowed, making it clear my argument was not accepted. “Well, what’s this friend of your family like?”

“Um… I don’t think I know her very well, but…” I paused, searching for the right phrasing. “She’s known to be quite the titan in some circles?”

“That doesn’t mean jack sh—”

“So when are you going to scan me, anyway?”

Nora blinked. “What?”

“Or do you just open the app? Come on now, I want to see if my numbers went up!”

“Oh!” she exclaimed, fumbling for her phone. After a few feisty taps, her fingers froze.

“…Huh?”

“What's wrong?” I asked, instantly filled with dread.

“One of your titles changed—not just one, your main designation!” She turned the screen toward me.

Rachel Emily Smith

The One Who Chooses

“The One Who Chooses?” I read aloud just to be sure.

Nora had already pulled back the phone to scroll through the app. “You were The Chosen One yesterday. What else is different?”

I waited, more impatiently than anything, since it was clear she was not going to let me look over her shoulder.

“Your conviction’s up,” she noted. “Way up. I’m a bit impressed! But then…”

“Then what?”

Was she punishing me for not telling her about Rhea right away? I mean, come on—give me a little time to sort out my free trial religion, will you?

“Your aggregate Faith and Trust levels are down…”

“By a town’s worth or two?” I asked lightly, hoping my face would pull off the terrible joke.

In all honesty, what had happened in Northwick and Southwick might not have made any difference, since I doubted they ever saw me as their hero.

“Tetora’s recruitment should be worth more!” she shouted at the phone. “If I were in charge, you’d better believe I’d change the weighting on this thing’s algorithm!”

“Thank you,” I said with an awkward half-smile, knowing her antics were meant to commiserate.

She muttered something under her breath as she swiped right. Then she started to pale. “We have a problem!”

Vernie was at the top of the Contacts window. She had changed from yellow to red, and her entire entry was flashing irregularly at the top of the list, tagged “Priority”.

“Where is she?” I demanded even as Nora clicked on her.

“Red Mountain.”

“I’ll get Olethros, you get Tetora!”

Nora was already running for a mirror portal. “Galenus will meet us in the reception room!”

***

“Something’s wrong with Vernie!” I blurted as my greeting to Olethros. “Please—huh?”

He was locked in a close-eyed stare with the stick Father Baram had blessed. As soon as he heard my voice, however, he had flung the stick across the room as if it had bitten him.

“Rachel?” he asked, startled. “Is that you?”

“Yeah, we need to—”

His robes flared outward, wisps of purple shadow trailing from their hems. “What did you call me back when I was trapped?”

Was he still upset about that?

“Um… The second time, right? Snek boi. And look, whatever Nora told you that means, it's not true!”

He exhaled slowly, dismissing his aura. “Pardon my reaction. I can’t hear your thoughts anymore.”

I blinked. “Huh? Oh… I’m sorry for… um…”

“Startling me,” he supplied as he approached. “Yet it was accidental.”

“You just said you can’t hear my—”

He shook his head. “I believe this is an improvement. Our enemy may share my talents, so it is a blessing you can now block them.”

“Funny you should say it like that…”

“I chose the wording deliberately. I can’t confirm yet, but I think you made the right patron choice.” He straightened. “Now what’s this about Vernie?”

After quickly catching him up, we met up with Nora, Tetora, and Galenus.

“And where is Red Mountain?” Galenus asked.

The rest of us shared an uneasy glance.

“Don’t you know?” Nora asked. “Check the maps!”

“I am well acquainted with all of our maps,” Galenus replied, bristling slightly. “Red Mountain is not marked on any of them.”

Nora gritted her teeth. “How can it not be marked?”

“It’s a hideout for thieves,” Tetora declared, locking eyes with Nora. “Who would advertise the location?!”

I coughed, quirking an eyebrow. “What’s going on between you two?”

“They’re all wrong!” he said, shaking his now electric-blue steel claws. “The weight doesn’t make sense! They’re too light for what they are, and it's throwing me off!”

Nora huffed. “You’ll get used to them.”

“So you keep saying,” Tetora growled, his tail frizzing.

“Red Mountain,” I repeated, cutting through them. “Vernie sent her henchmen there after she joined us. Do you recall her saying where that was?”

They both paused and turned away from each other to reflect on the past.

“I can’t remember much,” Tetora admitted. “I wasn’t feeling well…” He then turned back to Nora. “And you still owe me a new shirt!”

“I see nothing wrong with the one you’re wearing,” Olethros observed. “Aleph always was good at coordinating outfits for you.”

Tetora felt rigidly silent as Olethros turned to me, saying, “I’ll ask Beigaldi if he knows.”

He shimmered for a moment, then gestured for Galenus to pass him one of the floating maps.

“He’s not certain, since they were ignoring most of the humans and smaller hybrids, but there were pockets of hidden settlements along this mountain range,” Olethros explained, running his index finger along the lower west edge of Turri. “There is also an abundance of iron-rich sedimentary rock in the area, with three different peaks worthy of the title Red Mountain.”

“I don’t think we have time to search them all,” I said. “Maybe we start with the largest? I can't see them calling it a mountain if it’s a hill by comparison to the others.”

“Unless you want to throw people off, which they do,” Nora disagreed, turning to Galenus. “Can you look for human-based animus spikes around those areas?”

“I can,” Galenus replied, his brow furrowing as the map adjusted beneath his hands, “but the region has a significant amount of ambient animus already. It may be difficult to isolate any single human source of animus… And some of those areas may be populated, which would further complicate tracing her signal.”

Nora frowned, then pulled out her phone. “Her name is flashing with a certain cadence… I wonder…” Forbidden from simply sharing the screen, she began tapping her staff against the floor. She struck once, twice, paused, then three quick taps. “Anything out there that matches?”

“That’s a very strange tempo,” Galenus mused as he drew several runes in the air. “Animus from humans is usually far more regular with a stable pulse.”

Olethros scowled knowingly. “The pattern suggests she is currently inebriated beyond comprehension.”

Galenus paused to look up. “I should have considered that… My apologies, Your Majesty.”

Even Olethros seemed momentarily startled at the sudden address. “None are necessary.”

“I will continue to scan for—I found it?”

Nora nodded her head in self-congratulation. “Let’s go!”

***

Red Mountain, of course, was the middle-sized of the three iron giants, though one could argue it was the most mountain-shaped, being strangely unattached to its neighbors. On one side was a firmly sealed cabin, the steps leading up to it covered in dusty, oddly angled footprints.

“She’s alone,” Olethros confirmed, pulling his hand off one of the timbers that framed the cabin’s doorway. “I could not make any sense of what she is thinking—only that she is failing to do so almost spectacularly.”

“That might be to our benefit,” Nora hedged. “Wifty over swiftly?”

“Careful,” I warned, even as she was already moving.

“Yeah, yeah,” she said, tapping the door with her staff. “No traps. Just locks…”

Several soft clicks followed as she magically picked them all. The door then swung open on squeaky hinges.

Inside, Vernie was sprawled across a battered chair, her boots hooked over the edge of a dirty table. Empty bottles littered the floor, some intact, others shattered beyond recognition.

Tetora walked in first, shielding us with his bulky form. “Vernie?”

Vernie lifted her head slowly, her once-green eyes now dull gray. “I was wondering how long it would take you to show up this time…” She shook herself several times, then feebly tried to extricate herself from the chair. “No, I don’t like the blue glow at all. It’s weird.”

"Don't bother trying to explain anything to her," Tetora remarked over his shoulder as we filed in. "She's going to need to sober up first."

As I turned to Nora to see if she could speed up the process, Vernie's armor rippled with a bright gold aura. She snapped taut and crossed the cabin in a blink, dodging around Tetora with inhuman speed as something flashed from her hand.

Olethros reacted immediately, summoning a layered barrier between himself and her, but the ereborite jewel passed through each one as if they didn’t exist.

He was mid-phase when it struck, the jewel correcting its trajectory to embed itself between his eyes. He staggered as shadows flared, then folded tightly around him.

“Olethros!” I shouted.

Vernie collapsed as well, the light around her dying. “Woulda been nice,” she slurred into the floor, “to have enough for all the demons with you.”
