Chapter LXV (65)

Both other times Mitsuko had killed the guardians, she’d died shortly after and never seen the bodies of her opposition. This time, however, Mitsuko watched in fascination as Anemone’s decapitated corpse slowly dissolved. It didn’t happen as quickly as what she’d seen when Sterling had been silenced in a previous loop, but the guardian’s body did similarly flake away into nothingness. Until, finally, only empty slots in the stone where her hooves had once been sealed.

The icy sword Mitsuko had skewered the guardian with clattered down and rested amongst the rubble and cut up vines from Anemone’s construct’s remains.

Mitsuko turned around, aware that the guardian’s acolyte remained in the room with them and was still a hostile entity, likely now possessed by the guardian’s spirit. Raising her fists, Mitsuko prepared herself to attempt a nonlethal takedown on the girl.

But Hallie was slumped on the floor, face down. She appeared to be unconscious.

Mitsuko kept an eye on the girl, just in case the sage plotted some sort of deception, as she moved over to Sterling’s crumpled form off in the corner of the room. The cat’s body was broken and beaten from Anemone’s earlier strike. Only a bloody stump with scraggily hair remained of his tail, the rest blasted into smithereens. The rest of his body wasn’t much better.

“Don’t waste your blood healing me,” Sterling said as she crouched down next to him.

“Should I carry you? The sage is up on the pyramid’s sixth floor, right?”

Sterling paused for a moment. His eyes flicked over to the stairs on the other side of the room. “I…suppose you should carry me. As your guide it is my duty to escort you.”

Mitsuko scooped the cat into her arms. Sterling hung, broken legs sprawled out in awkward angles.

“Do not fear for my well-being. My spine shattered in my earlier confrontation and I subsequently have felt no pain.”

Stumbling as she walked, Mistuko crossed the large room to the staircase.

“Actually, upon further consideration, perhaps leaving me down here might be better for your concentration. It would hardly do for me to distract you from the sage above. Listening to her is vital in your next steps forward and I serve only as an obstacle and distraction of that.”

“You’re babbling,” Mitsuko observed, continuing to carry Sterling as she ascended the stairs. “Why? Is there something dangerous about this sage? Anything I should know?”

“Of course she’s dangerous! She’s a sage. Each of us is a nightmare of power in our own way. Only…Juniper is a nightmare in other regards in equal measure.”

“Pilroth,” a sweet voice said from above. “Stop poisoning this young flower’s ears with your dangerous pollen. Silence may serve you well, should you ever acquaint yourself with the term.”

Mitsuko ascended the final steps and entered the treasure trove of the pyramid.

Unlike Sterling’s secret room which had been filled with opulent wealth and gold, Verdant Island’s room bursted with greenery. Plants upon plants grew up the walls and onto the ceiling overhead. Interwoven amid the plants grew more of the bioluminescent moss.

“A…garden?” Mitsuko asked in bewilderment. She reached out and touched a peach the size of a watermelon.

“Yes, poppet, welcome to my garden.”

A woman flickered into existence in the center of the nursery. Her skin was dark like bark and ears pointed. Where Sterling’s eyes glowed green, this sage’s emitted a soft brown, like fresh soil. A glance down at her legs revealed her to also be a faun, the same species as Anemone. Mitsuko hoped this one wouldn’t prove to be another child obsessed, egomaniac.

The faun raised a hand and brushed it against one of her many plants. The vine shivered under her touch, as if pleasured, then expanded and grew. A flower blossomed, then bulged out into a blue fruit. The sage cradled the fruit and whispered something to the vine before plucking it.

“Eat this,” the sage commanded, handing Mitsuko the blue fruit.

Mitsuko took it and looked down at Sterling in her arms, waiting to see what he would have to say about the plant. But instead of him responding, the new sage fulfilled his role as guide.

“I am certain you have heard stories of blood fruit? They grew like weeds in my day, I doubt they’ve gone extinct in the years I slumbered. They’re well known for helping mages recover blood at a faster speed. This species of plant is related to blood fruit, but far more potent. With my magic within it, I believe it will be fast acting enough to restore your weakened body. Please, partake of my bounty.”

Sterling remained mute in Mitsuko’s arms, not disagreeing with the new sage, so Mitsuko shifted his weight and set his limp body on the ground before raising the fruit to her mouth.

Her teeth punctured the soft skin and sank into the meat of the fruit. Its juice ran down her chin, incredibly sweet. Addictively so. Mitsuko did her best to savor the taste, but the fruit disappeared into her stomach far faster than she would have liked. She licked her fingers clean of the juice, desperate to reclaim the taste.

“Poppet,” the sage called to her gently. “Heal yourself now. Use Pilroth’s power to remove those unsightly injuries.”

Mitsuko blinked. Now that the sage mentioned it, the brain fog from earlier had faded and she felt surprisingly energetic. She reached into her pocket and brought out her severed ears. Placing the slabs of cartilage against the sides of her head. Then she cast Mend.

The constant thrum of noise faded in an instant and a warm heat sealed her ears back in place. Mitsuko let out a sigh as the throbbing pain disappeared. Then smiled at the sound of her own sigh. All around her, Mitsuko could hear the noises of the jungle. The chirps of insects, the cries of birds, the rustling of leaves. The sounds filled her with nostalgia for her once carefree childhood.

“That’s a pleasant look you now wear on your face, poppet,” the sage commented. The faun beamed at her. “Our noble Champion, you’ve done well to come so far. Even despite your dubious guidance. My name is Juniper. I am the Sage of Deterioration.”

“That’s—” Mitsuko cut herself off and cleared her throat, not used to hearing her own child-like voice. “That’s a violent sounding spell.”

Mitsuko liked it. Unlike the utility based Mend or Retrospection spells, Deterioration sounded like an offensive ability.

“It can be,” Juniper admitted. “But not always. I offer you a passage into the future.” She placed her hand on a small, green sprout. Then, before their eyes, it accelerated upwards. A bud formed and bloomed into a gorgeous yellow flower. “Deterioration is growth. Eventually, everything returns to the dirt to renew the cycle, but that does not mean the moment of beauty never existed.”

“Your spell can accelerate an object’s time,” Mitsuko commented. “The opposite of Sterling’s Mend spell.”

“Yes, Pilroth and I have opposing abilities. His looks to the past, mine to the future.” Juniper crouched down to examine the crumpled cat at Mitsuko’s feet.

Only then did Mitsuko realize she’d used Mend on herself but not Sterling. “Shit,” she muttered to herself before casting the spell on the cat’s body. She heard a popping as Sterling’s bones positioned themselves back into their normal skeletal structure.

Sterling slowly rose to his feet and stretched. “Hello, Juniper. Pleasure to see you again.”

“Pilroth,” Juniper said, voice harsher than when she spoke to Mitsuko. “I admit, you’ve done well bringing our Champion to me. Far better than your performance last time.”

“Thank you,” Sterling said stiffly. “I appreciate the acknowledgement of my efforts.”

“Not efforts, Pilroth. I am acknowledging results. But you accomplished what you needed to. Now you can rest. Enjoy your time in Tempus. Trust and worry not, you’ve established a passable foundation and left the Champion in good hands.”

Mitsuko felt awkwardly spoken around as Juniper continued to serve back handed compliments in Sterling’s direction. And the sage just…took it straight on.

“I would have never made it as far as I have without Sterling,” Mitsuko said, inserting herself in the conversation. “He’s been loyal and knowledgeable. But, more than that, he is a friend to me.”

“Friendship with Pilroth?” Juniper raised an eyebrow. Her warm smile shifted to an amused smirk. “Really n—”

“The biggest hiccup in all of the timeloops so far was you taking him from me,” Mitsuko said. She barreled on, cutting off the Sage of Deterioration. She lifted a hand and pointed it at the faun. “You all are the reason I lost nearly a quarter of all the available loops. Don’t you dare even consider taking him from me again.”

Sterling blinked and looked up at her, his mouth agape but forming no words.

“Poppet,” Juniper said placatingly. “Please, I know Pilroth has done well so far. You heard me state as much.”

“I heard you say you want him to ‘rest’ which I am interpreting as you sages all ganging up on him again and forcing him back to his cell on Ashen Island.”

“Often it is difficult to move on. I know change is scary—”

“Ha! I look like a child so you speak to me as one? Fine. I’ll play the role and act like one. Send him there and I will go back to Ashen Island for Sterling’s guidance. Every. Single. Loop.” Mitsuko crossed her arms and leaned back against the room’s foliage. She meant every word of her threat. She refused to be strong armed into removing someone she cared about from her life.

“Pilroth, please talk some sense into our Champion,” Juniper requested. She had the gall to appeal to the person she’d just been berating moments earlier.

Sterling hesitated. “Juniper is correct,” he finally said. “Only one sage may act as your guide at a time. And it is wise to hear from multiple sources of information.”

Juniper opened her arms, smiling as if Sterling’s words settled the matter.

“I am not leaving my friend behind,” Mitsuko said. “I refuse to let him rot in that underground cell in a city frozen in time.”

“Tempus City was the result of his previous efforts,” Juniper said darkly.

“Careful,” Mitsuko said. “I have it on good authority that sages who reveal information that I don’t expend questions on have a habit of bursting into a plume of dust.” Then she paused. She cocked her head and tapped her chin. “Oh, wait. Do the rules not apply anymore when you all deem it necessary to manipulate me?”

Juniper closed her brown eyes and took a deep breath, clearly attempting to bury her irritation.

“I misspoke. Please forgive my slip of the tongue. Is there no talking you out of this course of action?”

“I won’t let you all steal Sterling from me. He is my companion.”

“How about a…compromise?” Juniper sounded like she forced that last word out. As if it caused her actual pain.

Mitsuko shrugged. “I’ll listen. But I’m not promising to agree to something without the details.”

“There are six sages. I believe we should distribute the weeks evenly between all of us so as to not let you stagnate in the other spells Sterling is not as adept in.”

“So, right now with my three spells on hand, you only get one out of every three weeks with me?” Mitsuko said.

“Since Escara cannot properly communicate—”

“Sterling gets his position,” Mitsuko cut-in. “I’ll agree to let you all split the role of guide so long as Escara’s spot goes to Sterling.”

Juniper wanted to argue. Mitsuko could see it on the faun’s face. She looked to Sterling for help, but the cat’s green eyes were instead locked on Mitsuko. He said nothing.

“Fine. For the time being, I relent to these conditions.” Juniper raised a hand and rubbed her temples. “With the stipulation that we can alter this deal, should you change your mind later.”

“I won’t,” Mitsuko said confidently. “Well, unless it means more loops with Sterling.”

“This is not how I wanted our first meeting to proceed,” Juniper said, exasperated.

“Then you should have spoken to my friend with more respect.”

Juniper apparently had no rebuttal to that. Instead, the sage shifted topics. “Rather than continue bickering. Why don’t we resume this discussion in the next loop.”

“That’s fine with me,” Mitsuko agreed. Then another thought flitted across her mind. “Though don’t assume you can steal Sterling’s body either. I don’t want to hear any words out of that cat’s mouth unless they belong to him.”

“I would never sully my—” Juniper cleared her throat. “I mean, I would not even consider disrespecting him by stepping into that body after he’s claimed it for his own. There are plenty of other fine bodies and I agree it is best for each sage to find their own.”

Good enough. Mitsuko considered telling her about the menagerie she’d discovered in the city, but decided against assisting the sage. She could find her own body without Mitsuko’s help. It was time to end this conversation and move on.

“I need to round up all the children below and get them back to their town,” Mitsuko said.

A bit of Juniper’s earlier smile returned. “In that case, allow me to supply some of my bounty to serve your efforts.” She motioned at the plants behind her. “I have a few different fruits that will not only satisfy their stomachs, but disguise them from the jungle’s dangers.”

“Anything to remove Anemone’s influence from her acolyte’s?”

“Sadly, that treads too closely to the matter of the Prismatic Spiral for me to answer without an official question on hand.”

Mitsuko clenched her teeth in frustration and then forced herself to relax. “Fine. How about something to harmlessly lull children to sleep?”

“That, I can supply.”

Barely more than a minute later, Mitsuko held an arm-full of multi-colored fruits. Now off of the subject of Sterling, Juniper’s entire demeanor shifted and every word she spoke held a chipper note as she explained the different effects of the fruits. She completely ignored the cat skulking under the drooping ivy.

Sterling remained silent, cleaning his fur while watching them out of the side of his eyes. Mitsuko considered mentioning to him that Mend had reverted his body back to the way it was, cleansing him as a side effect, but decided against it. He seemed to be cleaning himself more out of habit and to keep himself busy rather than for any practical reason.

“Anything else you need before your departure?” Juniper asked once finished with her explanation. The sage hovered, as if ready to jump into action at the slightest prompting.

“No,” Mitsuko replied stiffly. “I think it’s time for us to go. There are a lot of confused children that need help finding their way home.”

“Are you planning to return the way you came?” the sage asked.

“Unless you have another method to get out of here.” The return journey should be easier. Mitsuko had triggered most of the temple’s traps and knew about most of the ones that still remained.

“You didn’t think that Anemone strolled through the entire pyramid to enter the chamber below?” Juniper raised a hand to her mouth and tittered. “There is an exit on the fifth floor. Simply approach the corner of the chamber and press up against it.”

“Thanks.”

Mitsuko turned and started down the stairs with Sterling at her heels, leaving behind the lush bountiful garden.

“Good luck, Champion,” Juniper called out behind her. “We’re all so proud of you!”
