They examined the room once they stepped inside.

It was quite ordinary, if not a little poor in condition. There was a nightstand, a bed, close-blinded windows, and a small sink basin. Staple amenities in any inn.

The bedsheets were still pristine, except for a few creases as if someone had sat down on them a few times. However, there wasn’t any sign of the bed being actually used.

Veronica raised her hand and fed mana into the ring again. The pull now shifted downward.

She crouched beside the bed and reached underneath.

A moment later, she stood, holding a white crystal orb afixed to a small bronze base.

“Is that it?” Gwen asked. “I’ve never used a personal communication crystal before.”

Veronica nodded. “Yeah. It looks old though. Probably one made nearly a decade or two ago, judging by its design. I can see why someone like Lorence would use it. It’s outdated enough to be untraceable by modern methods.”

She set the crystal on the nightstand.

“Okay. Let’s see if we can find anything in this place,” Veronica said, turning around. “He must have something hidden somewhere.”

Gwen nodded and immediately began to move. She began searching beneath the bed and between the sheets.

Veronica turned around and opened the drawers.

The top drawer had a few documents inside.

“Two days ago… four… seven…” she murmured, reading the paper. There were various dates, all written by hand. Next to them were amounts. Some totalling hundreds, others totaling tens of thousands.

Vix? But some of these days, the amount is so little…

“Hey. You said that he came by nearly every day to harvest crystals from Rocky, right?” she asked, eyes still scanning over the papers.

“Yeah, he did,” Gwen replied from behind her. “He would usually take one or two large crystals and then be done. Very rarely did he come back more than one or skip a day.”

Veronica’s face soured. She couldn’t really make anything off from the sheet. Perhaps it had been some type of hidden encryption that only he knew about. Otherwise—to her—the numbers were just completely random.

Sage. Any idea what any of these mean?

[There is no immediate pattern that I recognize based on the information shown. Computational power is too low to accurately run simulations to decrypt.]

Too weak, again? What tier would I have to hit for you to decipher it?

[Once you reach Tier-7, I can begin running more complex equations and simulations, which include deciphering code or even gauging the likelihood of complex immediate outcomes.]

A quick sigh escaped her lips. That was of no help to her right now.

She placed the papers on top of the stand next to the crystal and searched the lower drawers.

There was nothing in them besides a short steel dagger and empty pieces of paper. She crouched, checking beneath the stand and behind it; both locations were completely empty.

Turning, she, spotted Gwen moving over to check the basin, lifting it and checking around the base.

Veronica put her hands on her hips and scanned the rest of the room in its entirety. Her eyes moved from the ceiling, down to the floorboards.

She squinted, focusing her eyes and enhancing her vision with Sage’s sensory upgrade.

The floorboards became clearer, and she could almost make out each individual lines of bark on the planks.

However, this didn’t actually help in her searching. She could see the small little details on the floor, but that didn’t assist her in finding anything out of place. Her scope of vision became narrow; in her current state, she was essentially looking through the hole of a straw.

[Fourth floorboard from the wall to your right. Seventeen inches from the bed.]

She turned, counting the floorboards. Once her gaze passed over it, she noticed the slight difference there immediately.

The spacing between that board and the rest was slightly wider, so little that one would have to look there specifically just to make it out.

Veronica stooped, trying to dig her fingers in between the boards.

It was still too tight; her fingernails were also too short to gain enough leverage.

“Oh, screw it,” she murmured.

Veronica’s right arm tensed, filling with a well of mana that soaked through her skin and bones. She was going to take Sage’s advice this time.

In one punch, she slammed her fist down, shattering the board and leaving a hole straight through, splinters uprooting into jagged edges. With her right arm’s enhanced physique, she felt no pain, not from the punch, nor the splinters gouging at her arm. She simply swatted it all away.

Veronica reached in and retrieved a small sack. Looking inside, there was maybe two or three thousand vix. A hefty sum, but nothing significant. Not for a person in Lorence's field of work.

“Find anything?” Gwen asked, walking over.

Veronica stood, patting her hands on her lap and shaking off any rogue wood chips.

“Just a bag of vix. Not enough that would be reasonable for someone selling mana crystals,” she replied. “You?”

Gwen shook her head. “Nothing.”

Veronica sighed. “There’s a chance this might just be one of his hideouts. His real stash might be somewhere else.”

Walking to the night stand, her gaze fixated on the communication crystal. The real purpose for why she had come was this very artifact.

She pulled out the replica ring Chelsea had made and held it near the base. A faint buzzing hum filled the room as the enchantments attempted to link.

She glanced at Gwen. “Go watch the door. Let me know if anyone comes.”

Gwen nodded quickly. “Right. Okay.”

She stepped carefully over the splintered wood and took position in the doorway, watching both ends of the hall.

Veronica stood, watching the crystal.

She had discussed this situation with Sage on her way here.

Although she had access to Lorence’s communication crystal—she couldn’t just activate it. There would be no point. Whoever it was at the other end of the line would immediately realize it wasn’t Lorence. It would completely ruin her chances at finding out who his buyers were.

Sage. You can do this, right? Because if you can’t, this’ll be pretty awkward.

[Yes. I will begin modifying your voice.]

Veronica felt a strange sensation within her body. Her throat strained as Sage began manipulating her voice, shifting tone and pitch. It wasn’t painful, but the sensation was strange.

“Ah. Ah. Ah.” With each test, her voice became lower and lower.

“You’re courting death,” she said, testing, voice now completely mimicking Lorence’s.

Veronica reached up, cupping her throat with her hand. “My throat feels scratchy. It’s weird hearing his voice come from my mouth.”

After roughly a minute of waiting, the buzzing noise from the crystal stopped with a soft click.

Someone had connected.

Veronica’s brows shot up once she heard the voice come through.

“Hey! Took you long enough! Where the hell have you been?”

She recognized the voice immediately. It was the same person who had informed her about Galland’s case.

Dain.

“Sorry,” Veronica said.

The moment the word left her mouth, she regretted it. Someone like Lorence wouldn’t apologize. That wasn’t how he carried himself. She needed to correct that mistake—fast.

“Just—goddamnit!” she snapped, slamming her fist onto the nightstand for emphasis.

Gwen, who had been watching from the doorway, immediately glanced back in.

Veronica turned, lifting a finger to her lips, signaling her.

She understood immediately and returned to watching the hall.

“What? Is there an issue? The hell’s going on?” Dain asked.

Veronica turned back, letting a growl slip into her voice. “There’s… been a problem. That’s why I haven’t been in contact.”

“A problem?” Dain echoed.

“Something… troublesome has been on my tail lately. Been affecting my work.”

She kept her words deliberately vague. She couldn’t be sure if Dain was actually Lorence’s buyer or not. He could just as easily be nothing more than a drinking partner.

She couldn’t afford to slip up now.

“You too?” he replied. “We’ve been running into an issue the past couple days as well. Someone’s been nosing around.”

“What?” she asked.

A grunt came through the crystal. “Some woman’s been snooping around an old contract from a few years ago. Nothing pressing, but she might be looking into us.”

“Just one problem after another…” Veronica muttered. “How are you handling it? This better not affect anything.”

It was obvious Dain was talking about her. Still, there wasn’t enough to piece anything together yet.

“Relax. We’re monitoring her. Keeping everyone tight-lipped. She wants one of the boots to make a deal with her. Some expensive-sounding contract. But I don’t think she knows anything else.”

Boots? Was that a code word? A title?

“But we’ll handle it. Nothing will come of it,” he continued, his voice rough. “The boots are there as insurance anyway. We can get rid of them anytime. But you—the drops. How long until the next one? Or is this problem of yours something you need help with?”

Galland is expendable? What exactly were they using him for?

“It’s an annoying bug that’s been following me around. Can’t get anything done safely. It’ll take some time to clean things up myself,” she replied.

Then she coughed.

Veronica pressed a hand to her throat. Her mouth had gone dry. The pressure sat deeper in her throat now, the irritation steadily worsening.

“Are you injured? You sound different,” Dain asked.

She paused, weighing her next response.

“Worry about yourself.”

“Hah.” Dain’s voice boomed through the crystal. “Did this ‘bug’ actually get you? I can hear the strain in your voice.”

Veronica scowled. He hadn’t figured it out yet, but she wouldn’t be able to keep this up much longer.

“But our next monthly shipment is in two days,” he continued. “We still need to fill the cargo. But if you’re having trouble…” A brief pause followed. “We can send some men over to take care of the problem.”

“What’s the catch?” she asked.

“The next two drops. Double the amount. Do that, and we’ll help swat your annoying fly.”

Veronica let a moment pass before answering.

“Fine. I don’t have the time to deal with it myself,” she said. “But send the loyal ones. Only those who know what’s happening. I can’t tell if I’m being hunted by a mosquito or a bear.”

“That’ll be extra, Lorence.”

“Fine.”

Dain clapped from the other side of the crystal. “Perfect. Meet at the usual spot.”

Usual spot? Where the hell is that?

“I can’t do that. Let’s change locations,” she said. “Meet me ten kilometers north of town. I’ll set up a meeting point.”

“All of a sudden? Why?” he asked.

“I’m being chased by someone elusive. You think they’re normal if they can injure me? I have plenty of ways to stop them, and yet I couldn’t,” she replied, her voice rougher than intended. “I can’t risk them tracking my location. Somewhere open would be safer. Just in case.”

“North, huh?”

A low hum came through the crystal. “Mm… Well… if you’re being chased, it’s better to stay cautious. I’ll send Park and Blair to meet you. Two Tier-4s should be enough.”

“Good,” she said.

A faint grin crept across her face.

Things were falling into place.

[Disturbance detected. Several intruders have entered the building.]

What?

Veronica’s ears twitched as she tried to sharpen her hearing. It was faint, but she could make out a low rumble below. The quiet hum of the crystal had masked it.

Her brows creased. “Hey. What are you doing to keep track of the pest snooping around? Just so I know who to avoid or get rid of.”

“She’s just some filthy mage snooping around, so you don’t need to worry,” Dain said. “I’ve got a pair of Tier-3 aura users tracking her from afar. They're a bit new, but they should be enough. If she does anything suspicious, they’ll deal with it discreetly.”

Veronica turned her head.

Gwen was still in the hallway, watching in both directions. And yet, she didn’t see anything.

But Veronica could hear it. The footsteps coming up the stairs. They should have been visible by now.

Unless…

Veronica turned back to the crystal. “Thanks for the information,” she said quickly. “I’ll hire some myself. I’ll speak to you next time. Send those two to meet me tomorrow at noon.”

She grabbed the ring from the stand, cutting the connection before Dain could respond. Then she raised her palm and detonated the air in front of her.

The crystal shattered, fragments blasting into the wall behind it and carving a jagged dent into the wood.

Gwen screamed, turning back into the room.

“Wha—what’s going on? Why did you—”

She didn’t answer. Instead, she bolted out of the room, seizing Gwen’s hand and dragging her along.

“H-hey! Where are we going?” Gwen shouted.

Veronica cast a glance over her shoulder.

The hallway was empty. At least, that's how it appeared.

But Veronica knew better.

She could hear them. Her ears twitched at the faint sounds that echoed across the walls. Multiple sets of footsteps reaching the top of the stairs. Even the shadows along the corridor shifted, just slightly out of place.

“She’s running! Go!” a man’s voice barked from behind them.

Gwen ran, stumbling as she glanced back—yet she still saw nothing.

Veronica pulled her toward the window at the end of the hall.

“Alright. You’re going to have to trust me,” Veronica said, eyes flicking once more to the empty corridor.

She raised her hand, mana flooding into her palm. Three white wings unfolded, glowing with a faint, ethereal light.

“Wait—what are you doing? A Tier-3 spell in here will—”

Veronica released it.

A violent surge of energy erupted forward, a blazing mass of fire roaring down the hallway.

She didn’t hesitate. Turning immediately, she scooped Gwen off her feet.

“What is—aah!” Gwen cried out.

Veronica reinforced her body and leapt, crashing through the second-floor window in a burst of shattered glass.

Midair, the explosion thundered behind her.

Heat surged through the hall before spilling out the window. Flames licked all across the broken frame as shards scattered into the open air.

Veronica landed with a heavy thud, strain running through her body, but her enhancement held. The flames lit up the sky above her as they roared from the second floor.

She set Gwen down and immediately seized her hand once more. “Come on, we have to keep moving!” she said sharply.

Gwen stumbled beside her, forcing her legs to move despite the chaos.

“What… what is happening?!” she cried.
