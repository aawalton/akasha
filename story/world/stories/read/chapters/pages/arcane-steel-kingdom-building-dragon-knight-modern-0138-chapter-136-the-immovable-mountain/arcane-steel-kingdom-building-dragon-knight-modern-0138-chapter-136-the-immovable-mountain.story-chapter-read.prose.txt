The Throne Room of Khaz'Modan was a cavern of impossible dimensions, hollowed from the living bedrock of the world’s tallest mountains. It was a place designed to impress, to show the raw, enduring strength of the earth. But for a thousand years, it had felt empty.

At the very center of the grand chamber, directly before the High King's obsidian throne, lay a massive, circular depression in the stone. It was charred black, its edges smoothed by ancient heat. This was the hearth that had once held the First Flame. It used to illuminate the entire cavern, casting a warm, divine light that filled every dwarf with pride. Now, it was a gaping, physical scar. Cinderfall had taken the flame, using it to hatch their monsters and forge an empire, leaving Khaz'Modan in the dark, forced to rely on flickering torches and localized magma flows that cast harsh, dancing shadows across the faces of the assembled throng.

This was not a standard council meeting. Every elder, every clan chief, and every guild master from across the Adamant Forge-Kingdom stood shoulder-to-shoulder on the tiered stone platforms. The murmurs of the crowd were a low, grinding rumble, the sound of tectonic plates shifting before a quake.

In the center of the vast floor, Ambassador Hedric unrolled a heavy, wax-sealed parchment. He cleared his throat. The sound echoed off the vaulted ceiling, silencing the assembly.

"The first missive is from the Cinderfall Hegemony," Hedric announced, his voice carrying the deep, resonant timbre characteristic of his people. He began to read.

"To the craftsmen of the Adamant Forge-Kingdom of Khaz'Modan, and to High King Valdric Ironhand. A blasphemer has soiled the name of your craft. This child wishes to challenge dwarven craftsmanship. He makes dark and terrible weapons, crafts containing no soul. Join us in our noble cause as allies forged in the fires of the First Flame. Join us in righting this wrong. Cinderfall requires not your soldiers, but your craftsmen."

A ripple of low, dangerous growls swept through the hall. Mentioning the stolen First Flame in a demand for servitude was an insult bordering on madness. Hedric raised a hand, demanding silence, and produced a second scroll. This one was bound in green silk.

"The second is from the Verdant Conclave," he stated, breaking the delicate binding.

"The Queen of the Elves proclaims that Aerthos now rightfully belongs to Cinderfall. No usurper shall be tolerated. Furthermore, this house has committed untold atrocities against the neutral elven faction. They must be brought to justice. High King Valdric Ironhand, open your doors. Let us pass through the mountains. Supply us and Cinderfall with metal, that we might right the wrongs that have been committed."

Hedric let the parchment roll shut with a sharp snap. "That is all we have received, my King." He bowed stiffly and stepped back into the ranks of the elders.

For a long moment, the only sound was the crackle of the torches.

Then, Master Craftsman Aldric, standing near the foot of the throne, let out a bark of laughter that sounded like a hammer striking an anvil.

"What in the Maker's name are Cinderfall and Sylvanheim smoking?" Aldric bellowed, turning to face the assembly. "Did they find a new, mind-altering weed in their precious ancient forest? Have they both lost their collective minds?"

He pointed a thick, calloused finger at the discarded scrolls. "Alarion Wight is my student! What he makes is an evolution, a continuation of dwarven craftsmanship! He took what we taught him and pushed it further! And did they forget who started this war? They burn the boy's home, slaughter his family, and now they want us to fund their revenge out of our own pockets? They want us to arm them so they can kill a boy defending his own lands?"

A gnome guild master, perched on a stool to see over the balustrade, chimed in, adjusting his thick spectacles. "It is always like this. They start a pointless, arrogant squabble over borders or pride, and we are forced to foot the bill in healing potions and steel. They bleed, and they expect us to supply the bandages and the blades."

Another clan chief, his beard braided with gold rings, crossed his massive arms. "I say let them choke on it. Three great nations against a mere boy. How much more humiliation can they take? First, he feeds their starving peasants. Then, he shatters their fleets. And now, they beg us for help to cross a mountain because they are too afraid to face his flying machines."

The hall erupted in agreement. The dwarven council found the entire situation utterly absurd. The arrogance of the surface-dwellers, demanding submission and supplies while simultaneously insulting the greatest technological leap their race had ever witnessed, fueled their long-simmering discontent.

High King Valdric Ironhand rose from his throne. The obsidian seat groaned slightly under his massive frame. He raised his rune-etched warhammer, bringing it down on the stone dais with a single, deafening CRACK.

The silence was instant.

Valdric’s eyes, the color of molten steel, swept over his people.

"Aye," the High King rumbled, his voice carrying the authority of millennia. "I am of the same thought. This situation... it is a testing ground. A testament to how much power the boy truly holds."

He stepped down from the dais, pacing before the empty, scarred hearth. "For aeons, we have wanted freedom from this servitude. We want to build what we wish, to craft wonders of our own design, not simply fuel the greedy, bloody ambitions of the surface nations."

He stopped, looking at Aldric. "We went to him, hoping to offer our forges, to be the hands that built his army. We thought we had the advantage of production." He let out a low, rumbling chuckle. "He showed us machines that build themselves. He doesn't need our hammers for his war."

Valdric turned back to the crowd, his voice rising, filling the cavernous hall. "But he offered us something better. He offered us the soul of craft. He will build the soulless machines of death, and he has asked us to build the life that comes after. To build the homes, the tools, the beauty of a new world."

He hoisted his warhammer high into the air.

"But a new world cannot be built while the old tyrants demand our sweat and our steel! This is our perfect opportunity. Fate has handed us the hammer, and Cinderfall has offered us the anvil of their arrogance!"

The low rumble of the crowd began to build, a vibration of absolute agreement.

"Khaz'Modan has stayed silent for far too long!" Valdric roared. "For a thousand years, we toiled in the dark, staring at the scar where our sacred fire used to burn! We received nothing but demands and scorn! Let us ring the war bells! Let us show the world what an immovable mountain truly looks like! I say we raise our banner for war, then meet our new ally on equal footing! This is the time to show our mettle, men! This is the time to strike the anvil!"

The room erupted. It was a roar that shook dust from the vaulted ceiling. Ten thousand dwarven and gnomish voices screaming their defiance, their heavy boots stamping the stone floor in a rhythm that echoed the beating heart of their mountain.

High King Valdric turned to his ambassador. "Hedric! Prepare a declaration of war. We are siding with Aerthos. We declare war against Cinderfall and Sylvanheim. We are done bowing to their whims and their arrogance."

A nation of dwarves was picking up arms for the first time in a millennium. They were the most fortified, defensive nation in the world. They were oppressed, forced into economic servitude. But seeing Alarion’s crafts hold up against the might of Cinderfall motivated them. The boy had offered them a future of true craftsmanship, and they were willing to fight to secure it.

They had crafted impenetrable walls and defense networks for centuries. Now, they saw weapons that could cut through the enemy's salvation.

Khaz'Modan was not a country of open plains and vulnerable cities. It was a heavily fortified region, a network of magical shields and heavy metal defenses built deep within the mountains themselves. It was an impenetrable, geological wall that separated the eastern half of the continent from the west.

The cities and ruins of the mountains were connected by extensive pathways and massive, hollowed-out caves crafted for transit. The only way to pass these mountains safely, to move an army from Sylvanheim to Cinderfall without spending months navigating treacherous, freezing peaks, was through the huge gates hollowed out by the dwarves. If the gates were open, an army could pass. If the dwarves closed them, the continent was effectively split in two.

The dwarves maintained a few structures on the surface, military outposts and strict, fortified checkpoints. But the main population lived in the cavernous, sprawling cities underground.

Each of these massive subterranean settlements possessed a gong located in the central square. They were artifacts of a bygone era. They rang for one reason, and one reason only: the mountain was under attack, or the mountain was going to war.

The last time these gongs rang, the First King of Cinderfall had attacked, stealing their precious First Flame.

Now, after a thousand years, the silence was broken.

The sound started in the capital city, from the High King's palace. It echoed through the cavernous walls, a deep, bone-rattling vibration.

THUNG. THUNG. THUNG.

It spread outward, a chain reaction of sound. One city after another joined the chorus. The gongs of war were ringing through the caves of the dwarven nation.

The massive, hundred-ton iron-and-obsidian doors that allowed passage from the east to the west began to tremble. With a screech of ancient gears and the shutter of immense stones, they began to close, sealing the tunnels tight.

On the surface, the forts along the walls were being manned. Heavily armored dwarves, carrying weapons they had crafted for themselves, not for export, poured into the defensive lines. They powered up the magical defense ballistae and the heavy, rune-etched cannons.

The rhythmic thump of their march was backed by the clatter of heavy armor and the sharp, metallic snap of loading mechanisms. The Sylvanheim army, assembling near the border and soon to begin their march, expected open gates and subservient suppliers, was about to face the most heavily armored and fortified nation on the planet. They were about to learn what happened when you demanded tribute from an immovable mountain.

The scent of pine sap hung heavy on the balcony of the Great Banyan. The Headmaster leaned his weight onto his gnarled staff. He stared out across the sea of clouds.

His joints ached. A deep, persistent throb in his bones warned of a coming storm. This wasn't weather. The geopolitical fault lines of Aethelgard were grinding together. Cinderfall. Aerthos. Sylvanheim. Three major powers locked in a death spiral.

Footsteps slapped against the wooden walkway behind him.

An academy aide burst onto the balcony. The young man gasped for breath, his robes askew. He held out a rolled parchment stamped with a heavy iron seal.

"Official diplomatic channels, Headmaster," the aide choked out. "Sorry for disturbing you headmaster but this is important."

The Headmaster took the heavy vellum. He dismissed the boy with a flick of his wrist. The aide bowed hastily and retreated, his footsteps fading back down the winding branches.

Alone again, the Headmaster cracked the iron-grey wax. He unrolled the scroll. His eyes scanned the blocky, dwarven script.

The parchment slipped from his fingers. It hit the wooden decking with a dry clatter.

Khaz'Modan had entered the fray.

He leaned against the railing, his breath coming shallow and fast. Four. Four of the seven great nations were now plunging into total war. His hand drifted to his side, fingers tracing the outline of an old, faded scar beneath his green robes. He had earned that wound centuries ago. The Beastkin Wars. The last time the continent had fractured on this scale, rivers ran red for generations.

Fools, he thought, his jaw clicking shut, the taste of old, bitter magic flooding the back of his throat. They thought they could bypass the rules. They thought they could assassinate an entire ruling family in one fell swoop and face no consequences.

He stared out at the churning storm clouds over the Maelstrom Sea.

They thought they had found a loophole in the ancient treaties. They thought they could simply annex Aerthos before anyone could object.

Dragon Valley existed to prevent this. The Great Nations did not go to war anymore because the cost was absolute annihilation. The balance of power was a fragile, terrifying construct.

And now they pay the price, he mused, a cold dread settling in his stomach. Who could have anticipated the boy? Who could have guessed a grieving child would forge machines of war capable of elevating a ruined nation back to the status of a Great Kingdom in four short years?

He turned his gaze downward, toward the hidden depths of the floating continent.

He is a True King. This situation... it goes far beyond the squabbles of mortal men. The world itself will react to this shift in power.

He grabbed his staff. He needed counsel. He needed the one entity older than the treaties, older than the wars.

I must meet with the Mother of Dragons. The ramifications are no longer just political.

He turned his back on the clouds and hurried toward the spiraling staircase leading to the inner sanctum.

I sat in the CIC, my mind still processing the massive, convoluted document Nyxia and I had received. Before I could even issue the next set of orders regarding the troop deployments, the heavy blast doors slid open.

A Legionary officer, designated for emergency missives, practically sprinted into the room. He carried a sealed parchment.

"Lady Nyxia," the officer gasped, halting before her and holding out the scroll. "This arrived by magical beast courier moments ago."

Nyxia took the parchment. She broke the seal and unrolled it. I watched her crimson eyes scan the text. She blinked, She looked baffled.

She handed the parchment to me without a word.

I took it, my eyes dropping to the heavy, blocky script.

"The Adamant Forge-Kingdom of Khaz'Modan is done playing your games. We are joining this war. We proclaim that Alarion Wight is the rightful sovereign of Aerthos and its lands. We decree ourselves his allies and will aid him in any way possible. Hence, we are picking up arms against tyranny. We declare war against Cinderfall and Sylvanheim. We shall not stand for this oppression any longer. This binds us and our allies."

I stared at the paper. I read it again.

"Well," I said, a slow, genuine smile spreading across my face. "That was unexpected. I didn't expect an answer to my offer from back then so soon."

Nyxia looked at me, recovering her composure. "which offer?"

"I told them they needed to decide which side of the wall they wanted to be on when the storm hit," I replied, handing the parchment back to the officer.

I turned back to the tactical map. The eastern border of the Cinderfall Hegemony, the mountain passes leading to Sylvanheim, were suddenly flashing with new, friendly blue icons. The Dwarves had closed the gates.

"It seems," I said, the smile turning predatory, "that Sylvanheim is going to have a very difficult time reaching the front lines."
