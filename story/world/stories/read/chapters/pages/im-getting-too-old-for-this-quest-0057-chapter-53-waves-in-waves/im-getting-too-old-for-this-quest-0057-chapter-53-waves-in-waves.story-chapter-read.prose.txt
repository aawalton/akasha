The tentacle struck faster than Tad could react.

One moment the oft-oblivious otherworlder was raising his open hands, and the next, a massive appendage had coiled around his torso, lifting him off the ground. His face went blue almost immediately as the pressure squeezed the air from his lungs.

“Ah crap, not again!” he wheezed.

“You’ve messed up!” Puffin’s shrill little girl voice pierced down from the lighthouse. “You’ve messed up big time!”

Garrick watched Pickles the Kraken rise fully from the water, its colossal body blocking out the afternoon sun. Seawater cascaded off its gray-green hide in sheets. The creature’s eyes—each the size of a wagon wheel—fixed on them with what could only be described as irritation. Its mantle burned orange—Low Tower. A Sphere above Tad, and considerably farther above Mr. Montrose.

Well. His little disciples would need to work together.

Fortunately, they had brought an old man.

Problematic, but not impossible.

Dashiell turned to Garrick, his face drained of color.

“Sir…” he whispered, voice cracking. “What do we do?”

Garrick let his posture relax, as though he were observing nothing more pressing than an interesting cloud formation. A hint of a smile touched his lips.

“Well now, Mr. Montrose, that’s entirely up to you.”

“Me?” Dashiell’s gaze ping-ponged between Tad—whose face was now transitioning from blue to an alarming shade of purple—and the unnervingly calm old man. “Sir Garrick, what do you mean?”

“You’re at the helm of the Golden Lion, are you not?” Garrick’s voice remained measured, almost conversational. “The premiere Guardian unit in the Montrose company’s endeavors. In your expert opinion, what course of action does this predicament demand?”

“Now is—” Dashiell’s voice climbed an octave, “—NOT the time for a lesson!”

Tad made a strangled sound that might have been agreement.

“Ah, but life is always about lessons,” Garrick said, watching a bead of sweat roll down Dashiell’s temple. “So. What is our course?”

Dashiell squeezed his eyes shut, trying to concentrate. His breathing came fast and shallow.

“Come now, Mr. Montrose.” Garrick’s tone held the patience of a grandfather helping with basic maths. “You’ve been decisive in situations worse than this.”

“There are only three of us!”

“Three, two, seventy…” Garrick offered gently. “There is always a way forward, no matter your number.”

“How ya just gonna stand there?!” Puffin screeched from above, her voice cracking. “You’re gonna get clobbered!”

Garrick’s eyes flickered to the little girl in the lighthouse window, then back to Dashiell. The young man was clearly wrestling with something internal.

Ah.

A pulse of warmth.

Garrick felt it emanate from within Dashiell’s mantle. The Marked Sigil, that damnable node of destiny, was responding to the boy’s elevated state.

Not yet, Garrick thought, suppressing a frown. Not like this.

“Dashiell.” His voice took on an edge, dropping the usual amiability. “Focus. Block out the distractions. What do you see?”

Tad’s strangled wheeze had gone quiet, which was not a great indicator of his longevity.

Dashiell sucked in a breath. Garrick followed as the young man’s eyes tracked across the scene: the thrashing kraken, their imperiled comrade, the positioning of the remaining tentacles.

“I… I see…” Dashiel’s voice steadied. “The kraken is focused on Tad, but watching us too. The other tentacles are spread wide and leaving more exposed. If we could reach them…”

He trailed off, looking uncertain.

“Good,” Garrick nodded, ignoring another pulse from the Sigil. “Continue.”

Garrick had to think the effects of the Rejuvenation potion were coursing through him and making this a much more intricate balance than it ought. However, the young Montrose's eyes seemed to spy the shoreline.

“Its attention is divided. We can use that.” He straightened slightly. “We need to free Tad first. I have my sarissa—but I cannot use such a weapon and swim at the same time. If I can get closer, I can harass it, keep it focused on me. And you…”

He met Garrick’s eyes.

“Will you use your strength?”

“Mr. Montrose.” Garrick’s expression remained neutral. “We may be on leave of duty, but you are still the leader of my Guardian unit. You decide what I do.”

Dashiell’s shoulders squared and his bearing suddenly shifted.

“Right. Then yes, Sir—erm—Garrick. You extract Tad. I’ll distract the beast using the rocks as cover.”

Garrick’s eyes gleamed. “A sound plan. Let’s move.”

Dashiell sprinted for the nearest outcropping and ducked behind it. Then, as though a blue bolt of inspiration had ignited in his brain, Garrick watched as he grabbed a rock the size of his fist, waited for his moment, then hurled it at Pickles’ bulbous head.

PLONK!

The kraken’s roar shook the air. Its massive head swiveled toward Dashiell, one eye narrowing with what looked distinctly like annoyance.

“Now, Sir!” Dashiell shouted, waving his sarissa. “Over here, menace. Pay attention to—”

Garrick was already moving. He reached Tad in three long strides, grabbed the tentacle wrapped around the younger man’s torso, and began to pry.

A little stronger than I would’ve thought for Low Tower. Fair enough, I guess no phoning it in at the moment.

The appendage resisted for perhaps half a second before Garrick’s fingers sank into the rubbery flesh and simply pulled it apart. Tad tumbled free, gasping like a landed fish.

“There we are, Mr. Tadanius,” Garrick said as he hauled him upright, patting his back perhaps harder than necessary.

Tad coughed up what looked like a concerning amount of seawater.

“Thanks… I knew you’d…” cough “…get me out!”

Garrick held the still-writhing tentacle between thumb and forefinger, studying it with mild interest.

“I’m sure you did, Mr. Tadanius, but now is not the time to—”

Pickles thrashed.

The kraken’s attention had shifted. No longer focused on Dashiell’s rock-throwing, it was now locked onto the two of them. Specifically, onto its captured tentacle.

A low rumble emanated from the creature’s throat.

“Ah,” Garrick said.

The tentacle snapped, and a rolling column moved back toward them like a whip crack. Garrick released it and sidestepped. The appendage struck the ground where he’d been standing, leaving a crater in the rocky shore.

“Nasty to be caught in that, eh?” Garrick observed with a chuckle.

Tad stared at the fresh wound in the earth, then at Garrick, then back at the crater. Garrick, however, caught movement in his peripheral vision.

“Mr. Tadanius. Duck.”

Tad dropped like a stone as another tentacle screamed through the air above him, close enough that the wind of its passage flattened his hair.

Garrick caught this one mid-strike and redirected it with a casual gesture, sending it splashing harmlessly into the surf.

Dashiell, meanwhile, continued his barrage from behind the rocks. His aim was improving—one stone caught Pickles directly on top of its head with a hollow BONK.

The kraken’s roar turned from annoyed to genuine rage, and its entire body rotated toward Dashiell.

“Get to the lighthouse,” Garrick told Tad. “We’ll regroup there and—”

He didn’t get to finish. A tentacle shot out straight for Dashiell’s position. It moved with terrifying accuracy, snaking around the rock the young man had been using for cover.

Time seemed to slow.

Garrick saw Dashiell’s eyes widen as the appendage wrapped around his waist and saw the young man’s mouth open to shout as his hands scrabbled uselessly at the slick, rubbery surface.

Then Pickles the kraken wound up.

The tentacle coiled back like the arm of a trebuchet, with Dashiell dangling from it like a caught fish. For one absurd moment, man and kraken made eye contact.

Then the tentacle snapped forward and Dashiell launched.

He pinwheeled through the air, his scream dopplering as he arced up, up, and away, his coat flapping behind him like a flag. One of his boots came off. Garrick tracked the young Montrose’s trajectory with calm calculations—roughly forty-five degrees, initial velocity approximately sixty miles per hour, projected distance…

A few seconds later, a distant SPLASH echoed across the water.

Garrick and Tad stood in silence.

“Did he just—” Tad started.

“Yes.”

“Should we—”

“Likely.”

Pickles slipped beneath the waves with the satisfied air of a creature who’d just taken out the trash. Ripples spread across the water’s surface, marking the kraken’s descent.

Tad’s hands came up, astara beginning to flicker around his fingers. The telltale hum of portal energy started to build.

Garrick caught his wrist before the Chant could form.

“No.”

Tad deflated. “Aw, man. Still?”

“It’s not yet safe, Mr. Tadanius.”

“But without my magic stuff, I’m not really useful.” The words came out quieter than usual.

“That is not true.” Garrick’s tone was gentle but firm. “I saw you when you fought the Treasure Guard—”

Another distant splash interrupted him. Much larger than the first. Pickles had evidently retrieved his thrown quarry and was now, presumably, descending to drown him at leisure.

Garrick pinched the bridge of his nose. “Should have dealt with this myself. But no. I wanted this to be a teaching moment.”

He glanced at Tad, who was already striding toward the shore with determination.

Seems he got over his bout of uselessness quickly.

With a sigh—quieter and more resigned than the first—Garrick followed.

Above them, Puffin’s voice rang out with bloodthirsty glee. “Get ’em, Pickles! Show ‘em what for!”

Tad reached the water's edge and turned back. "Here!" He pulled something from his belt. "You'll do better with this than I will!"

He tossed it in a smooth arc.

Garrick saw the glint of steel—a dagger, still in its sheath—and his body reacted before his mind caught up.

Panic flooded through him, sudden and overwhelming. His breath caught. A flash of memory—blood, so much blood, the copper-salt smell of it thick in his throat, pooling around his hands—

"No!" The word came out harsher than intended. He jerked aside.

The dagger clattered onto the stones.

Tad froze mid-step, blinking. His expression cycled through confusion, concern, and bewilderment in rapid succession.

Garrick closed his eyes. Breathed. The phantom smell faded.

"It's nothing," he said, though his voice sounded strained even to his own ears. "Carry on."

Tad gave him a long look, but finally shrugged and splashed into the water. His butterfly stroke was enthusiastic if not particularly efficient.

Garrick remained on the shore for a moment longer, his gaze fixed on the dagger. Then he drew a deep breath, letting the old ghosts settle.

Finally, he turned away from the blade and bent down to remove his satchel and pack. From within, a small warm creature peeked out.

Ember.

He smiled despite himself. Her bright eyes sparkled with recognition.

"Watch our things, please, Ember."

The little fox puffed out her chest and stood as tall as her frame would allow, every inch the dutiful soldier. Garrick gave her a quick pat.

His attention shifted to the ocean. Then he closed his eyes and felt outward.

There—sixty feet down, moving fast. Ten miles per hour, heading deeper. Pickles had Dashiell and was making for the bottom.

Garrick positioned himself at the water's edge. His hand found the tortoise pendant around his neck.

One touch, one flicker of energy and he winked out of existence.

The world became cold and dark and pressure.

Garrick materialized beside Pickles in the murky depths, close enough to see individual scars on the kraken's hide. The creature was massive down here—far larger than it had seemed on the surface. Tentacles as thick as tree trunks undulated through the water with languid grace.

Pickles was carrying Dashiell toward the bottom, apparently in no particular hurry. The young man was struggling weakly, his air clearly running out.

Garrick reached out and grabbed two of the nearest tentacles. The kraken suddenly stopped mid-swim with an audible “blub?”

Slowly—almost comically—its enormous head rotated back toward Garrick. Both wagon-wheel eyes blinked in the dim light filtering down from above.

Garrick, tentacles in hand, managed an apologetic grimace.

Sorry about this next bit.

The kraken's eyes widened.

Garrick pulled.

The tentacles went rigid in his grip as Pickles suddenly realized this was not, in fact, going to be an easy meal. The creature tried to yank them back but Garrick held firm, his feet finding purchase on the rocky bottom of the sea.

Pickles opened its beak—a massive thing that could have bitten a rowboat in half—and released a stream of bubbles that Garrick assumed was some sort of underwater roar.

Then it fought.

A third tentacle whipped toward Garrick's head. He ducked, still holding the first two, and a fourth came from below, trying to wrap around his legs. He shifted his stance and trapped it under one boot.

The water around them churned with displaced silt and thrashing limbs.

Pickles released Dashiell.

Tad came down through the cloud of silt, cheeks puffed out and eyes wide. He caught the young Montrose under the arms and kicked hard toward the light.

Good lad.

Garrick watched them rise. At the surface, Tad rolled onto his back, keeping Dashiell’s face clear of the water as he began the swim toward shore.

Something enormous tightened around Garrick’s boot.

Right. My turn.

All remaining tentacles converged on Garrick at once.

He let go of the two he'd been holding and pivoted. The tentacles crashed into the space where he'd been standing, cratering the rocky bottom in an explosion of silt. Garrick had moved three feet to the left, his movements underwater somehow maintaining the same unhurried quality they had on land.

Pickles thrashed, trying to locate him in the murky cloud.

Garrick reached out and grabbed the main body of the creature with both hands. His fingers sank slightly into the rubbery flesh. Pickles' eyes, both now visible and focused on him, went wider than seemed physically possible.

Time to go, Garrick thought.

He bent his knees, set his stance, and pushed upward while simultaneously twisting.

The kraken's massive body rotated in the water—slowly at first, then faster as momentum built. Garrick's feet left the bottom as he swung the creature in a full rotation, using the water resistance itself to build speed.

One full rotation. Two rotations. Three… On the sixth spin, Garrick released.

Pickles shot upward through the water like a breaching whale, except much, much faster. The displaced water created a momentary vortex that pulled Garrick up in its wake.

Both of them broke the surface at nearly the same moment—Pickles launching into the air in a graceless tumble of flailing tentacles, Garrick rising more sedately behind.

The kraken pinwheeled through the sky, its beak opening and closing soundlessly, until it became a distant speck against the clouds.

Garrick floated in the gentle surf, treading water. He turned toward the lighthouse and raised one hand in a casual wave.

Puffin had watched the whole thing from her lighthouse window.

She'd seen Pickles descend on the old man in the water. Seen them both disappear beneath the surface. For a few glorious seconds, she'd been certain her protector was drowning the old fool, teaching him a lesson about respecting a young lady's property rights.

Then Pickles had launched from the water like a stone from a catapult, tumbling end over end until he was nothing more than a twinkle on the horizon.

Puffin's jaw hung open. Her mind was blank.

The old man's head emerged from the surf. He looked directly at her and… was he waving?

Puffin found her own hand raising in numb response—though she had no idea why—moving as though pulled by invisible strings. A horrible, sinking realization settled over her.

High above, a crow cawed.

The old man was still floating in the water, looking up at her with that same infuriatingly calm expression. His voice carried across the distance with perfect clarity.

"I believe, young miss, we should have a conversation about the proper treatment of travelers."

Puffin's hand slowly lowered.

She was in so much trouble.
