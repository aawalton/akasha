Chapter 382

Noon in Gimleh was usually a lovely time of day.

It was a city that had been moved several times in the past, both in order to accommodate an ever-increasing scale, but also to avoid being swallowed up by the abyssal Chasm in the center of the Unified Mass, which steadily pulled all worlds into its maw.

It was a city with more history than there was space for in the history books. Powerful Awakened could live for many years, but not nearly long enough for anybody to even have traceable ancestors who had been there at its founding.

Even on a cosmic scale, the city was incredibly old. And it had changed its skin many times.

As a matter of fact, there was a not insignificant movement pushing rather adamantly for a name change. They argued that, since nothing was as it had been originally, that it could not rightfully be called the same city.

Like a ship that had sailed the ocean for years to the point where it had been repaired again and again, until every piece of timber had been replaced with new materials. In such a situation, could that ship still be called the same ship it had been when it was originally built?

According to the members of this movement, the answer to that was no.

After a good number of relocations, the folks of Gimleh, regardless of their stance on other city-related issues, had realized that they preferred sun and clear skies over rain and hurricanes.

And thus, after a long, convoluted history, noon in Gimleh was usually a lovely time of day.

Today happened to be one such day.

Especially in the many parks around Gimleh, stuffed full of wonderful vegetation and vibrant flowers, the day was ripe for enjoyment.

“Dad, can I have just one more?”

“We already talked about this, Junna,” her mother said, but then cracked a smile. “But I guess I can let you have one more. But only one, all right?”

“Yeah!” Junna exclaimed, lunging for another cookie.

Mom sent Dad a soft smile. No, a weak smile. They were afraid.

It hadn’t been long since a large portion of the people of Yanglith had been wiped out in a blitz invasion. And there was no guarantee that their home wouldn’t be the next. There was not a single person in Gimleh who wasn’t fearing what might happen.

But they couldn’t show that fear to their kids.

There was a desperation permeating Gimleh, and likely all other worlds in the Alliance. A desperation to hold on to a sense of normalcy. And that had resulted in a whole lot of people coming out for picnics in the parks.

“Then I can also have more, Dad!” Junna’s brother, Jann insisted.

Their father shot him one of those looks. “You heard what Mom said. Only one more for each of you.”

“And more sweet tea?” Junna tried.

“All right, now you’re pushing it!” their father said and went in for a tickle assault.

The siblings laughed so hard that they spat crumbs everywhere.

Birdsong chimed from the leaf cover of the trees, making for a perfect background for a perfect day.

And then the world stopped.

At first there really wasn’t anything particularly noticeable. A few people here and there seemed to realize that the birdsong had stopped.

But nobody missed when everything exploded into chaos.

The very earth abruptly shifted as a massive eruption tore the ground apart in the eastern mountains, close to the outskirts of Gimleh, and a beast of leviathan size broke free from the depths. Projectiles from Gimleh’s defense systems were immediately launched and cracked into the massive chunks of falling debris, shattering it in the air.

Nonetheless, it rained down upon the city to impact the multi-layered dome barriers, pulses of many different colors jetting across the surfaces of the usually invisible shields at points of contact. That was fine to defend against falling rocks and other such dangers, but they wouldn’t last if a genuine attack by peak-rank monsters happened.

They just couldn’t be built to that level of strength.

Pale white skin shone against the bright sun above, stretched thinly across visible bones, as the titanic beast rose up to its full height, revealing its true form.

A row of bizarrely crooked tusks jutted out from its lower jaw, appearing dangerously close to piercing its own face in place. As far as the shape of its body went, a thick, humanoid bulk would leave Earthlings with the impression of the ogres from myths and legends. Massive arms and shoulders sat on a barrel chest that made it very obvious how it had been able to dig itself out of the ground.

And the ferocious aura that it emitted was only survivable thanks to the combination of barriers and distance. Unfortunately, none of those would last long if the giant charged.

“M-Mom? Dad?” Junna stammered, the leviathan creature towering above the rooftops clearly visible even when she was sitting down. “What is th—”

“Grab the kids!” her father shouted, following his own words and hauling both her and her brother along by the wrists. Her father was stronger than her mother and Junna could see him desperately attempt to control his speed to not pull to far ahead of her.

“Dad! Don’t leave Mom behind!” she screamed.

“I’m no— I won’t, I promise!” but they were still pulling away.

“Just take them and go!” their mother shrieked, her voice shrill with fear in a way Junna had only heard once before when her brother had gotten both of his legs crushed under the heavy wheels of a cart in an accident. It was the worst sound she, in her young life, could imagine.

Her wrist hurt terribly, and she was afraid it would snap like a twig in her father’s steel grip. Even when she shouted for him to slow down and loosed his hand, he didn’t appear to hear her. His eyes were wild and frantic, and that frightened her more than she could describe with words.

Jann was crying, and judging by the wails and screams all around them, so were most others. In only a few moments, her mother had disappeared in the wave of terrified people fleeing from the giant on the horizon, and Junna found herself wondering if she would ever see her again.

When they made it out of the park, their father threw the both of them over his shoulders and came to a skidding stop just before they entered a narrow alley between two buildings. Breath ragged, he stared into the panicked crowd as the titanic beast let out a howl that shook the earth like a quake.

“Come on, come on,” Junna heard her father mutter under his breath, the sound of his voice trembling. “Where are you?”

After a couple of seconds, their mother came stumbling out of the crowd before tumbling to the ground, clutching her leg in pain.

“Mirall!” Dad shouted and shot forth to haul his wife to her feet. Along her shin was a nasty discoloration, so he took her in his arms and told his kids to hold on tight. Then he set off.

Hanging across her father’s shoulder, Junna could see the giant monster clearly, and it felt as if the monster was looked at her too.

When it took its first step, it covered a day’s travel through the deep jungle at once. That single step pulverized the edge of the mountain range that bordered the wilderness surrounding the outskirts of Gimleh, and sent dust billowing up into the air and over the city, obscuring the view of all but the creature’s thick neck and head.

But the dust hadn’t hung for five seconds before a gust of wind tore across the treetops and onto the city, blowing it all away in a moment. While the dust had been raining down through the city-wide barriers unobstructed, the gust was apparently recognized as a hostile event and caused the shield to activate again, the sand, soil, and fragmented wood crackling against the multi-colored surfaces.

“Daddy…” Junna muttered meekly through the cacophony, her father only hearing her because her lips were right by his ear. He grunted but made no other indication that he had heard. “Daddy!” she shouted, startling him.

“What is it?”

“Look!” she said, pointing. “He’s flying in the air.”

It was a risk to stop now, but so was being blind to what was happening. He turned around.

Where the dust had obscured the sky just a second before, an army now hung in the air around and in front of the mountainous ogre. There were thousands upon thousands of them, all with the same pale flesh that looked like thin, fragile parchment paper pulled tightly over bones.

And their course was set directly for Gimleh.

Junna’s father stood frozen, his lips bobbing open and closed like a beached fish gasping for air as his eyes remained glued to the swarm. Even though they weren’t releasing any aura in earnest, he could still feel the combined passive pressure of their spirits from here. It was suffocating.

“Dad?” Junna tried, but he didn’t reply. “Dad?”

“Dear?” Mom chimed in. “Allarn, we have to go. Now,” she insisted when he remained still.

“I can’t… I can’t breathe,” he gasped, terrible shivers invading his body. “Air is ref—… refusing to come…”

“Dad!” Junna screamed, battering her father’s back with her little fists, but to no avail. Suddenly speeding up, one of the horrific, white creatures rocketed forward, heading straight for their little family. A terrifying, murderous grin split its face from ear to ear. “Run! Dad, run!”

That final shriek of hers seemed to finally hook something in him, and he took a few steps backward as the monster neared. Opening its mouth to the point where the skin looked ready to rip, it let out a discordant giggle that sent pins down their spines.

With a gasp of horror, Allarn finally turned and ran at full speed, but before he could make it two steps, an invisible force carved a deep groove in the ground and sent him tumbling to the ground, his three passengers rolling to a stop.

“Go, go, go!” he cried, tears streaming down his cheeks. “Mirall, please take the kids,” he begged. “I’ll try to slow this thing down, somehow!” Before he spun to face the creature, his eyes caught his wife and children one final time, his heart breaking at the realization that it would be the last time he saw them.

“Go!” he yelled, putting up his fists in defiance. His fear had evaporated in favor of determination.

“No, no!” Junna shrieked as the horror descended upon her father.

Allarn’s entire lower body practically exploded as the pale monster crushed him. Completely unable to put up any resistance, the man couldn’t even scream. The face of the fiend filled his vision, but in his mind’s eye, he saw only his wife and children. That wasn’t such a bad thing to see in the final moment of life.

Sticky ichor splattered across his face as the grinning face was abruptly vaporized.

Through eyelids fluttering into unconsciousness, Allarn saw a man step over him. Something green flowed, a cool and soothing sensation filling his entire lower body. The person crouched down to look him over.

“That doesn’t look pleasant. The trick is to kill these ugly bastards before they can hurt you. Well, now you know for next time, I suppose.”

“Wh… Who?”

“Me?” the person asked. Allarn’s vision was growing clearer and clearer by the second. It was a man grinning down at him. “I’m Eik.”
