Sakura lunged for the scroll case as fast as she could. She could already physically feel that she was out of time. And when she glanced back for a second, Sakura finally noticed it: the obsidian creatures were trying to get to her. From the very beginning.

Gilbert and Pampli had only been holding them back, throwing them off, keeping them from reaching their true target. Again and again, the obsidian creatures’ attention snapped back to her. Their heads kept turning in her direction. Even while Gilbert was hacking at one of them with his sword and Pampli was sinking his fangs into the black plates of the other, there was still the sense that both of them were only angrily fighting off obstacles and trying to push through toward her.

Toward Sakura.

She felt herself going short of breath from that realization. They had come for her.

Sakura dropped to her knees beside the case, yanked it open, and started sorting through the scrolls with trembling fingers. There was too much, and none of it was right. One scroll was marked with a water and irrigation sign. Another called up small daggers, but Sakura threw that idea away after one glance. Daggers against creatures like these? They would not help. The third looked almost utilitarian, with some kind of minor light seal.

Behind her came a sharp, ugly scrape. Sakura turned. Gilbert was still holding on. Holding on through speed, technique, and cold calculation. Even without magic, he filled Sakura with admiration. But he was being pushed back. The obsidian creature was pressing him hard, coming at him from different angles, trying to break through his defense. And another one, the one Pampli had been holding off a moment ago, was already driving toward Sakura despite the beast’s fangs.

“Faster!” Gilbert shouted, deflecting another strike. “I won’t hold her much longer!”

“I’m trying!”

Sakura grabbed at the case again. She needed the right scroll. One that would work, not turn into another problem or make her look like an idiot.

Her fingers stopped on a scroll made of darker, heavier paper. The magic inside it was more complex, and even through the material she could feel the tension in it. Sakura was not sure about any of this, but certainty was a luxury she did not have.

After quickly reading the information on the scroll one more time, she snatched it up and rose to her feet. The idea spinning in her head had already become obsessive.

“Gilbert!” Sakura shouted. “Let her go!”

He did not even turn around. He only smirked at the corner of his mouth, as if he had already understood where she was going with this.

“I’m ready,” he threw back shortly. “On your signal.”

Sakura gave a sharp whistle as she turned toward Pampli.

“Pampli! Fall back!”

Pampli heard her immediately. The beast jerked back unwillingly, with obvious irritation, but obeyed anyway. In that same second, Gilbert moved aside, stepping out of the creature’s line of attack.

And both creatures, as if they had been waiting for that exact moment, launched themselves forward.

And in that instant, Sakura finally knew for certain that all this time they had truly been hunting her.

The two black, multi-headed masses rushed straight at her. The ground shook under their many legs. Their heads stretched forward. And their red eyes burned with a horrifying, hungry focus.

Sakura ran. Not toward the caves, but in the exact opposite direction. Toward the place where a raging river churned behind the tall trees. She ran there, feeling how lightly the new set sat on her body, how little it hindered her movement. The forest blurred into dark streaks around her. Behind her, already gaining, came the bloodthirsty creatures.

The river appeared suddenly. It crashed against the rocks with such fury that it seemed like another living thing eager to swallow someone whole. Sakura burst right up to the edge.

The creatures were already too close.

She spun around, breathing hard, and froze for one short instant. From the outside, it probably looked like madness. As if she had decided to take the attack head-on. But Sakura was waiting. Waiting for that tiny opening when both creatures would lunge at the same time and would no longer be able to change direction fast enough.

One of the heads of the first creature opened its jaws. The second came in a little farther to the right, trying to take her from the side.

Now.

Sakura sprang to the side so fast and so easily that she surprised herself. Her feet found the right footing on their own. She slipped out of the strike, shot past the front segments, and in the next instant was already behind the creatures.

They did not have time to stop.

Sakura threw up the scroll.

“Come on!”

The paper trembled under her fingers and flared with silver light, and this time Sakura did not try to force the structure open. She linked her will to the magic of the scroll quickly and smoothly.

[RESTRAINING NET]

Something like a burst of shadow-silk erupted from the scroll. First a narrow stream, then a net. It spread through the air instantly and crashed down over both creatures at once, binding them tight.

The creatures screamed. They lunged in opposite directions, but only tangled themselves further. Their legs locked together. Their heads slammed into one another. The black bodies lost their balance. And then it was not magic anymore, but momentum.

Both creatures went down with a crash, straight into the raging river. The net still held them together, tightening even more. The creatures writhed, trying to tear free, but the river was stronger. The current seized them at once and dragged them over the rocks.

One of the heads sank its teeth into the other. The second creature answered in kind. Bound too tightly together, blind with rage and pain, they began tearing into each other right there in the water. The river spun them, smashed them against the rocks, dragged them farther away, and they still kept mauling each other. It was terrifying and almost hypnotically beautiful at the same time.

Sakura let out a breath and nearly collapsed after them. The pain woke again in her shoulder and side. Her hands, still clutching the spent scroll, weakened. Sakura took a step back from the edge and finally braced one hand on her knee, dragging in air.

“It worked...” she breathed, hardly believing it.

But she was not allowed to relax.

Something changed on the far side of the river. At first, it was just the feeling of something. As if some outside observer had been watching the beautiful scene.

Sakura slowly straightened and stared into the darkness on the other bank.

Mist was rising there. It looked as though it were gathering itself out of obsidian ash threaded through with scarlet sparks. The mist did not creep along the ground or curl randomly. It moved with purpose, drifting closer to the water.

Alarm rose inside Sakura at once. She could not move, as though her feet had been nailed to the earth. All she could do was watch as the mist thickened and grew denser.

And at some point, it began to change shape.

At first, it was only a tall column. Then shoulders, arms, a head. A few seconds later, a human silhouette was standing on the far side of the river, made entirely of the same ashen blackness with scarlet flecks. It was not quite human. Too distorted. And yet the shape was still human.

And the silhouette stretched out a hand toward her.

Sakura felt terror rising in her chest. No longer just anxiety.

A voice drifted over the river. Otherworldly. Hoarse.

“I have waited so long...”

An icy shudder ran over Sakura’s skin.

“And at last you have come.”

The figure stood motionless with its hand extended. That was much worse than a direct attack, because Sakura did not know what would happen next.

“You must come with me,” the voice said. Ancient power pulsed through it. “It is already decided.”

Sakura felt that power before she fully understood the words. A sinister, crushing energy rolled off the figure.

At last Sakura jerked and stumbled backward.

“No,” she whispered.

And the figure slowly lowered its hand, as if disappointed by the answer.

Then in the next instant, the silhouette moved. It shot at her straight across the river, flying over it in a swift, mist-like rush. Black-red ash surged behind it like wings or smoke from an explosion. Sakura felt anger. Pure force. Power hurtling toward her so fast that her body could not react in time.

It was already almost on top of her.

And in that moment, Gilbert suddenly appeared in front of her. Sakura did not even see where he came from. Only the flash of the sword with the emerald hilt, and one short swing.

In the next second, the blade cut straight through the mist-figure. The obsidian silhouette split apart, or rather scattered like smoke, black ash flying outward as the scarlet sparks went out. A heartbeat later, nothing remained above the river..

Sakura stood there, breathing hard, unable to feel either her legs or her fingers. Pampli and Gilbert had gone still beside her as well, just as tense. Pampli growled low, staring into the emptiness above the river. Gilbert did not lower his sword. And Sakura still stared at the place where the ashen figure had been standing just seconds ago.

“W-what was that?”
