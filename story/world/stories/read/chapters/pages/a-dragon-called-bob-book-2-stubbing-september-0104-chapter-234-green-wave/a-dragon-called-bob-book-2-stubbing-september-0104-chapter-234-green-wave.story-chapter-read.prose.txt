The soldiers leaned out of the way, ducked, or threw themselves to the ground with their hands over their heads whenever my dagger of righteous smiting swung past them.  When it wasn’t pointed directly at them, they crept slowly forward, moving to surround me.

I took the opportunity to dump the rest of Dimpleworn’s body into my pocket dimension.  No point leaving good loot lying around.

“I think this is where you disperse and go back to whoever inherited that douchebags title?”

Silence.  Even the birds seemed to have gone quiet.  I palmed three more portal stones and considered my options.  If they didn’t run, I could unleash the ready teams of bunnyborgs, hose them down with more sewer water from the city, or make my escape.

The city authorities were like to get a wee bit tetchy if I kept using the river water of Ankmapak as a weapon.  I scanned across the seried ranks of knights and mages and concluded I would need to mobilise a sizable chunk of my army to deal with this lot, which would take time.

Finally, as a dragon, the thought of fleeing from puny mammals disgusted me.  Vanity and Wrath high-fived in my mind and set about subduing the other aspects of my psyche.

“You will surrender to us for the assassination of Duke Dimpleworn,” called an officer towards the front.  He wasn’t right at the front, sensible chap, and three soldiers dived to the sides as I swung the dagger in his direction.

“That was a duel.”

“Do you have any witnesses that will support your claim?” he sneered back at me.  There’s always one bloody guard who just won’t take the hint.

I pointed the dagger at a young woman in mage robes who, for some reason, had not stayed well back behind the armoured meatshields like a good wizard ought to.  “You heard it all right?”

She slowly sidestepped behind a knight covered in plate mail and crouched down.

“What about you?”  Again, I pointed the dagger, got to briefly enjoy the flash of terror on their face, then they too slipped back into the throng without a word.  “As if you’ve got a case anyway,” I grumbled.  “I’m a friend of the Emperor.  Your biggest ally at court is now in stasis in my possum pouch!”

“Possum pouch?  We all saw you drop out of the sky and attack our lord unprovoked, with none of the usual diplomatic rites before a battle, and kill him in cold blood.”  That bloody officer was a hair's breadth from being added to the menu, but I was already full and needed to evolve again.

“That’s not… You idiots think you can get away with this?”  I was almost completely surrounded now, but that didn’t worry me too much.  Transform and fly away, or open a portal and disappear; both options remained open to me.

“And you say you’re close to the Emperor?  A known rival to our beloved and foully slain lord?  When our report reaches the Small Council, you and your tyrant friend will face a whole new level of strife!”

“That’s not…”  I was repeating myself.  This guy was infuriating.  “Look, you lot should just bugger off, ok?”

“I don’t think so, Baron.  Your unauthorised use of Pamletta-Tier siege weaponry in an assassination should also be included in the report, I think.  That’s a war crime.”

“This?”  I waved the dagger back and forth.  They weren’t ducking and diving anymore.

“That.  And it seems you aren’t willing to just slaughter us, are you?  Some curse of the gods perhaps?  Maybe it’s out of charge?  But you are talking and not slaughtering us, so maybe you don’t have the power to follow through on your threats.”

Perspicacious.  It’s a good word, and in this case, it applied to an annoying prick.

“Yeah, well, that’s just like your opinion, man.”

“Baron Bob, I suggest you surrender to us peacefully so you can be tried for your crimes.  Fighting will only make it worse.”

“Get boned, fuck face.”  I opened a portal beneath my feet and fell into it as mage fire flashed through where I’d been standing.  I smiled as I heard the screams of pain as they caught each other in the crossfire.  Never surround someone, then fire into the centre of a circle.  Odds are you’ll kill some of your mates.

The portal snapped closed before anyone could follow me, and I landed with a thump outside a derelict-looking tent.  Those guys had pissed me off, so I was going to take off the gloves and stop playing Mr Nice Dragon.

“GEEKU!”

There was a thump from inside the tent, and the tall orlic stumbled out of the flap a second later.  The entire camp had turned at my yell and stood frozen.  Hands reached for axes and swords, but as soon as Geeku swept me up into a hug, they all relaxed.

I gently freed myself from the smelly embrace and looked up at the war boss.

“Bob!  Been long time!  Time fer more killin’?”

“You bet your green buttocks it is.  Gather the tribe, or clan or whatever you call it.  I want them ready to go in ten minutes.  I’ll drop you around the enemy via portal.”

“I’ll pop back and do a spot of aerial recon, then I’ll open portals.  You guys just swarm through, you’ll be aimed right at the bastards.”

“Enemy?”

“Hoomies, Geeku.  Hoomies.”

He grinned broadly and started swelling in size.  Fur began to spread across his skin.  Seems I’d left him too long without anything to kill, and his battle lust was getting the better of him.

I dropped into another portal, emerging half a kilometre above where I’d been a moment before.  Shifting back into my true form in mid-air was a new one for me, and I briefly regretted that I didn’t have the camera crew watching me remotely.  I must have looked cinematic as fuck.

My mammal suit dissolved as I fell, replaced by draconic glory.  My midnight wings snapped open and caught the wind.  I bellowed and spat fire into the sky.

Pigeons-Making-Love-On-The-Wing sent me spinning to avoid the bolts and blasts of magic that soared up at me.  A few bounced off my armoured hide, but I dodged almost all of them.

As soon as I started opening portals, green fury spilled from blue discs and began destroying any human is sight.  I spaced them widely around the Nineteenth, with the first going down in front of the confused soldiers.

Geeku, bless his evil green soul, led the charge.  He had swollen into his full Super Cyan form and began using knights as cudgels, snatching them up and laying waste to anyone nearby.  He had to grab new weapons every few swings as the force of the impacts reduced the bodies to mush.

This served to grab the attention of the humans, rather effectively, I’d like to add.  As they reacted and charged to meet the threat, I began the next phase of my improvised plan.

More portals opened—whoever it was that made all these portal stones needed a raise, I hastily amended that thought to a pat on the back—and the ready teams of bunnyborgs and BB-209s emerged right in the middle of the enemy formation.  Crimson blasts flared, and body part sculptures began to take shape.

The next wave of the attack was different.  I wasn’t averse to taking someone from behind, as Esme could attest.  More orlics burst out just behind the rear ranks of Dimpleworn’s now terrified army and charged into the vulnerable mages and support classes.

I am a very clever dragon, and bloody mammals should learn to be respectful.  The fact that I had a load of unusual options available, which basically amounted to cheats, was not relevant when contemplating my genius.

Circling lower, I began putting groups of the enemy to sleep.  Neither orlic nor robobunnies gave one whit of care to the fact that they were stabbing and chopping at sleeping, defenceless people.  I nodded in satisfaction.  Ignoring the anti-air fire from below was no problem now.  The mages and archers had greener things to worry about.

The orlic were almost as savage as the bunnies.  If I’d had any idea about the malevolent force my cyborgs would become on the battlefield… I’d probably have done it anyway.  Let’s be honest, terror troops just work.  Not good for the old karma, but I could work around that.

I could imagine the grim expressions on their faces, hidden beneath their fancy visors, as half-naked, seven-foot-tall green dudes charged at them screaming.
