Searing Sands Corespace, Earth Core #17, Fourth Floor

Elyx was not having a good day. The former lead assistant to the primary ritualist had recently received a promotion she very much neither expected nor desired. This, of course, had been by virtue of commander Zertrolk decapitating the lead ritualist. The red-skinned lugren did not mourn her superior's demise, she had been a conceited, arrogant woman, but rather her unexpected elevation into a position of prominence. Gone were the days Elyx could simply toil away, refining ritual diagrams and fixing the numerous mistakes of her colleagues. No, now she had to report to the commander, a demon whose approach to problem solving appeared to start and end with killing the messenger. And, even worse, the Lady Ira knew her name.

It's okay. Elyx reassured herself, reorganizing a stack of parchment on her desk. The Dark Angel is in another dimension. I'm safe from her here.

Never mind the fact that the entire purpose of her job was to unseal that dimension, unleashing hordes of her kin into the Labyrinth. This was really starting to feel more like a lose-lose situation. Either she failed and the commander would execute her for incompetence, or she succeeded and would be required to have an audience with the Lady Ira herself. The Dark Angel was infamous for her impossibly high standards, as well as the fiendish torments she inflicted upon any who failed to live up to them. The first corespace incursion had been a disaster, they still didn't possess a single one of the supposed 'Earth Artifacts' that were the key to the entire operation and, to top it all off, the powerful forces of the Labyrinth were now actively searching for them.

Just yesterday, Elyx overheard a conversation about tek'kalla scout fliers scanning two of the adjacent quadrants this corespace was hidden within. She had no doubt that the tek'kalla airships' sensors would detect the extremely anomalous readings radiating from their corespace aperture the moment they drew near. Now that the ritual was partially active and the Core was coming online, they stuck out more than a succubus in a convent of nuns. For the fourth time that day Elyx cursed herself for performing so well on the aptitude tests that the greater demons had seen her ferried out of the home world and into the Labyrinth. She would have been better off flunking them and living out her life in the Scorched Wastes with the rest of the lugren caretakers.

At least there the greatest danger was boredom.

The tent flap rustled, and a shadow darkened the canvas. Elyx didn't need to look up from her work to feel the commander's presence seeping into what was generously called her office. She sighed, grabbing another piece of parchment and scrawling over the existing calculations with new, updated figures.

"Stop looming over my doorstep and come in."

Sand crunched under commander Zertrolk's boots as he ducked inside the small tent, stepping out of the scorching desert sun. The greater demon dwarfed her slender figure, his midnight skin a stark contrast to the reds and oranges most common among their kind. Elyx felt his gaze boring into the top of her head, but she didn't flinch, continuing to work.

"What is it?" She asked tersely.

"You would do well to speak with respect, ritualist. My patience only extends so far."

Zertrolk's words were backed by the frigid, icy feel of his aura. It filled the room, and Elyx suppressed a shudder.

"I'm too busy to waste time with little things like speaking the right way, commander. Just yesterday, the dimensional resonance of this entire region of the Labyrinth underwent a significant shift. It came out of nowhere, and now we have to recalculate-"

"How much of a delay?" Zertrolk said, cutting her off.

"We won't be ready for at least another two weeks." Elyx said, finally looking up. She met Zertrolk's glare defiantly, a little surprised at her own brazenness. "And that's assuming nothing else goes wrong. And that we get one of those Earth artifacts in time to test it."

"You will have them soon."

"I keep hearing that, but I'm not seeing them."

The commander's eyes narrowed and Elyx flinched, wondering if she had finally gone too far. To her immense surprise, however, Zertrolk smiled. It was a subtle, brief expression, but she was sure she saw it. How she kept getting away with speaking so disrespectfully to a greater demon was beyond her. As was her apparent lack of anything resembling survival instincts.

"It took longer to get our forces in place than anticipated, but we will have them in hand soon."

"Will we be able to get them back to both ritual sites with half the Labyrinth looking for us? The process must begin at both Cores simultaneously if we want the rift to have any semblance of stability."

"We will. Vylix and some of my other agents are busy removing certain troublesome individuals from the opposition. That should be enough of a distraction to allow at least some of our forces to return with the artifacts. We do not need many."

Just hearing the assassin's name sent a shudder down Elyx's spine. Vylix was the only wraith demon she had ever met, and she fervently hoped that never changed. She hadn't been the only one relieved when the commander dispatched her on a mission that took her far away from the corespace."We don't need many if the artifacts do what we were told they did." Elyx sniffed. "Until I see one for myself, we won't be sure of their true capabilities."

"Nevertheless." Zertrolk said, tapping a clawed finger on her desk. It punched through a handful of papers, pinning them to the wooden surface with a menacing click. "You will find a way to make them work. Our people have waited long enough. We will not be denied our birthright any longer."

This time, Zertrolk did cow her into silence. Elyx swallowed, then nodded her head once.

"One week." The greater demon said, turning to exit the tent. "You will have the devices in hand within the week. Once you do, we will proceed."

When Zertrolk was gone Elyx slumped forward in her chair, banging her head on the desktop. Her horns knocked a stack of parchment over, sending them spilling across the sandy floor. She was too tired to care. One week? What was she supposed to do in one week? She didn't even have the artifacts yet. If they didn't do what they were supposed to, none of this would work. Even if they did, how was she supposed to factor in their real-world performance without testing?

It must be nice, making impossible demands and not actually having to do anything to try and meet them.

Maybe she would have been better off working under Malice. Elyx shook her head, immediately dismissing that thought. No, as dire as her current circumstances were, Zertrolk was at least a known quantity. There wasn't a creature in the cosmos more capricious and recklessly unpredictable than a succubus.

It's probably all the hormones. Elyx thought, finally getting up to gather her papers. Maybe I'll find a way to test that someday.

But for now, she had her task. Find a way to make her ritual do something by the end of the week. Would it be anywhere near as successful as it would be it she were given the proper time? Absolutely not. But if she managed to make it at least partially effective, then maybe she'd be able to convince her executioners to make it quick.

With a weary sigh, Elyx left to gather her assistants. With any luck, one or two of them would show signs of competence today. A girl could dream.

Jade hefted the dark, glossy orb, examining it while the rest of the raid group exited the corespace portal ahead of her. The dimensional anchor was much as Braston's information promised, a crystalline sphere filled with faint, shifting colors. She and Tara had extracted it from the Core, where it had been encased deep within the crystal structure. After the events of the previous corespace Jade was wary of breaking the large gem apart, but it turned out massive explosions were not the norm when it came to deactivating Labyrinth Cores. Instability only occurred when outside forces introduced unusual energies into the mix, like when demon infiltrators tried to adapt the powerful cosmic relics for use as batteries for ill-conceived dimensional rituals. In this case, once the mages had finished draining the Core extracting the dimensional anchor was relatively straightforward.

"This is weird…" Jade muttered, turning the relic over in her hands. It was heavier than it looked, but that wasn't what was throwing her off. The orb emanated a tangible… permanence… that made it feel like the relic existed more than everything around it.

"Are you still looking at that thing?" Naomi asked, pausing in front of the portal. "Just put it in your bag, it's time to go."

"No!" Tara practically jumped on top of Jade, placing a protective hand on the relic before Jade could reply. "Do not put a dimension anchor inside a dimensional bag!"

Jade raised her eyebrows at the tek'kalla, suddenly curious.

"Why? What happens?"

"Just… trust me, okay? Nothing good."

"Alright then." Jade shrugged. When the party mage warned that doing something would be a bad idea it was best to take heed. Or at least, that was her experience when playing the tabletop roleplaying games her brother had introduced her to. "Luis, you can carry it."

She tossed the softball sized relic to him, grinning as he nearly dropped it.

"Woah, this is heavy." He said, holding it up so he could peer into it. Then he shot her a look. "Why am I the one carrying it?"

"You have the highest strength score. If any of us had it, it'd slow us down."

"Fair enough. But you get to carry it through the portal."

"The portal?"

"Yeah, amiga. Crossing a dimensional boundary with a dimensional anchor sounds dangerous. That's more your area of expertise."

Jade glanced back at Tara.

"This thing is safe to take through that portal, right?"

"Hmm… Now that you mention it, I'm not sure."

"You're not sure? We were about to walk out with it!" Jade said, running a hand over her face.

"It's probably fine. I took some readings after we extracted it. It's pretty stable."

"Pretty?"

The debate continued, taking long enough that Fenrin came back through the aperture to check on them. Fortunately, he had a solution.

"Why not just tie a rope to it?"

So it was that Jade exited the corespace behind her friends, holding a rope tied to the suspicious relic. When they were all back in the Labyrinth proper and standing on the small island that housed the portal arch Jade took a deep breath. She braced herself, then pulled the orb through after her. It made a heavy 'plonk' sound as it dropped to the stone at her feet, a few blue sparks crackling along its surface. They faded after a moment, and Jade cautiously picked it up.

"Huh, I guess it was fine."

The corespace portal winked out, vanishing in the blink of an eye. The dark portal arch was left vacant and inert, the glowing sigils lining its surface fading away.

"Uh, was that because we all left the space?" Jade asked nobody in particular. She cast a wary glance at the orb, nudging it with her foot. "Or because of this?"

"Let's just get going." Tara said quickly, picking up the relic and handing it to Luis. "See? Nothing to worry about."

With no more dimensional boundaries to navigate, the group wasted no time setting off back to Illysport. It was a two-day journey back to the city, and if they hurried, they could get to the edge of the swamp quadrant before nightfall. None of them relished the idea of settling in for the night amidst the pungent, muddy bog filled with ambush monsters. Well, no one aside from Urgraan, whose people preferred such environments.

Jade fell into step behind Elise and Aylin, adopting an elven form for its superior night vision as evening shifted to twilight. Her mind drifted back to the recent battle, discomfort churning in her stomach as she remembered unleashing all the violent urges her new form concealed. Not just releasing them, but reveling in them. They were locked away, now, isolated and secure. But did she have the discipline to keep them there? She didn't like how uncertain she felt about the answer.

That wasn't the only thing bothering her, either. This boss had been different than others they'd faced. Seraphine knew what she was, a challenge for wayfarers to overcome. How long had she been stuck in that role, fighting and killing an unending series of adventurers? There had been no small amount of good in her, Jade felt it when draining the angel. Hope, mercy, valor, and justice. All that and more had infused her spirit. But it had been tainted, poisoned by doubt, regret, and a seething hatred aimed directly at her. Or at least, at demons.

"You look troubled."

Jade started, looking up to realize Fenrin was now walking beside her. The half-elf gave her a tired smile, his half-light, half-dark hair contrasting starkly in the twilit gloom. She sighed, shaking her head.

"It's just been a long day. I need a break. Something longer than a night's rest."

"We all do."

He didn't say anything more, just walking beside her as they trudged through the swamp with the rest of the raid group. There was little conversation, everyone too drained to do much more than put one foot in front of the other. There was too much on her mind, though, and eventually, Jade spoke again.

"Thanks again, for coming back for me.""None of us would leave you. I just happened to be able to make it to you first."

"That doesn't make what you did any less brave."

"Says the person who stayed behind to make sure we could get away." He said, looking a little embarrassed. Then his face grew more serious. "That boss was a dangerous one. I hope the others aren't like her."

"That's part of what's been bothering me. Not how dangerous she was, but what she was like.""You mean, the things she said?"

"Yeah. I can't help but wonder how long she's been here. The Labyrinth is thousands of years old, right? Has she been fighting here that whole time?"

"I don't think anyone knows how it really works." Fenrin said, shaking his head. "Most Labyrinth creatures are spirits. I was taught that when we slay a monster, its spirit returns to the Labyrinth and is reborn somewhere else.""She seemed different than other monsters. Even the people we fought in the stronghold near Lysara weren't like her. I don't know what it means, and I don't like that I don't know."

"Maybe we could try to find another sanctuary guide. Sometimes they're willing to answer questions like that, although when it gets to the deeper mechanisms of the Labyrinth, they usually won't."

"Maybe…"

Jade sighed. There'd be time to dwell on mysteries later. Their group was finally emerging from the swamp, entering the highlands that bordered the massive lake Illysport was in the center of. They'd be setting up camp, soon, and she'd have a chance to get some rest. They even had some proper food, as dimensional bags and enchanted containers eased many of the logistical issues that came with camping. The blinking icon in her interface reminded her that she had new messages to sort through. The summary page indicated that there were multiple level ups and quest completions, but nothing that required immediate attention. Drained as she was, Jade didn't feel up to paging through class feature options or to assigning stat points. Tonight, she and her friends could celebrate another safe return from a harrowing mission.

"Ah, crap." Luis said, shaking his head as he and Jade worked to assemble one of their tents. It didn't take long; the enchanted canvas did most of the work for them. "I knew I forgot something.""What?" Jade asked, alarmed at his tone. "What is it?"

"It's that quest! I forgot to say a cool catch phrase. Those templars would've been perfect for it!"

"Oh, you…" Jade snorted, punching him in the shoulder. "Don't you do that to me."

The burdens of the future could wait, at least for a short while.
