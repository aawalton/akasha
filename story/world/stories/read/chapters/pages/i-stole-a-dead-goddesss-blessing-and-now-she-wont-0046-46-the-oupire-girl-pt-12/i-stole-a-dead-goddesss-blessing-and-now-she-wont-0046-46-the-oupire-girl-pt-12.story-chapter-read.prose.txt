Anadis sat on a chair, already bathed and changed out of his torn pajamas. He’d miss them. Once he bought a new one, he’d make sure to ask Aelia to add her artistic touch to it. For now, he watched the girl as she peacefully slept on her side in his bed. She was drooling all over his pillow, but she was breathing peacefully. That was all that mattered. Relief settled in his chest, knowing no permanent harm had come to her.

He was having some trouble keeping his eyes away from her. Why? Well, she now wore one of Nora’s tunics, and Nora was short—pretty short, the shortest in the family. The girl, meanwhile, was tall, nearly Anadis’s height, maybe even a touch taller.

So, simple math: short tunic plus tall girl equaled problems.

Every time she moved, the hem shifted, and his eyes betrayed him. He’d look. Then he’d mentally slap himself.

Stop looking, you idiot. You’re being a creep. Stop it!

And… then he’d look again.

Her hair was cut in a wolf style, thick and glossy, the kind that made you want to touch it. Her bronze, sun-kissed skin glowed under the moonlight streaming through the open shutters. Even half-asleep, it complemented her taut, well-defined physique—the kind earned through strict discipline. Thanks to Father’s training, Anadis recognized that immediately.

However, that wasn’t what had intrigued him the most. What drew his eyes were her slightly elongated fingers. Each one looked as if it had been coated in matte dark ink from the tips to the knuckles. The blackness was absolute, as if it swallowed every trace of light—except her nails, which gleamed like honed obsidian blades.

Her ears were also strikingly pointed, resembling an elf’s, but much smaller, about the size of a human’s ear, although their pointed ends marked her as a daemon.

“How long are you going to keep staring at her?” Hel asked, appearing in a ribbon of light.

“Eh? I’m not staring,” he protested.

She settled onto his shoulder. “Of course not, vessel,” she said, her voice carrying an unsettlingly pleasant edge. “You’re practically trying to bore a hole through the poor girl.”

Anadis brushed her off his shoulder carefully. “You’re distracting. Go somewhere.” He wasn’t in the mood for her antics. After everything that had happened today, fatigue weighed heavily on him. All he wanted was to crawl into his bed and curl up to rest.

She gasped, wings flaring. “How dare you? I was comfortable there,” she said, thoroughly indignant and smoothing her robes with exaggerated dignity. “You’re becoming far too comfortable forgetting that I am a goddess.”

When will she wake up? Anadis thought. If she weren’t sleeping there, I’d be asleep already. Should I sleep next to a girl… no, no, no, I won’t. Whose great idea was it to put her in my bed anyway?

“Why don’t you wake her up?” Hel asked, this time choosing to sit atop his head. His hair barely stirred beneath her featherlight weight.

“Well, she just went through a rough battle. She needs to rest more,” he said, letting out a yawn.

“Use a little Soul Drain. It should wake her up.”

His breath caught, and he gave her a dubious look. “Are you cra—” She glared at him, and he harrumphed. “I mean… I’m not draining an unconscious girl just because I’m tired.”

Hel smiled toothily, her pearly teeth gleaming against his frown. “It won’t do her any real harm. She’s already mostly healed, and you’ll recover some soul essence in the process.” She gave him a once-over. “Considering you still look like a ghost, I’d say it’s worth it.”

“How are you so sure of that?”

She sighed. “I can see the condition of people’s souls. When they’re hurt, their souls are too,” she explained. “Their core gets tangled and uneven.”

“How come only you can see that and not me?” He let out a soft huff. “I thought your Blessing was my Blessing as well—whatever you can do, I could too, right?”

“As I told you before, don’t you dare compare a puddle to an ocean. Even given centuries, you would struggle to approach my level,” Hel said, lifting her chin as she placed a hand to her chest.

“I’m going to assume that I’m the puddle.” He furrowed his brows.

“But of course you are.”

“One question?” he suddenly said, glancing at her always-glowing eyes—the same as his when using Soul Sight. “Don’t you get tired of using Soul Sight all the time?”

“Naive. Again, we are not the same,” she said, looking at her long, shiny black nails. “I don’t need to use any abilities. They’re just part of me, as natural as breathing air is to you. Grow stronger, and perhaps you’ll manage half of what I can do.” Anadis caught her reflection in the corner mirror. She had crossed her legs, hands planted firmly on her hips as though she were seated upon a throne.

He felt an uncontrollable impulse to flick her away like a bug, but held back. The fallout wasn’t worth it.

“So what are you going to do?” Hel asked, her eyes drifting toward the sleeping girl, then back at him.

“I don’t really want to hurt her.”

A dramatic sigh. “Fine, I’ll wake her up.”

“Wait, what?” Anadis reached an arm toward her, but it was too late. She had already dashed away, leaving behind her usual trail of golden and violet dust—like a miniature sandstorm—and slipped inside the girl’s body.

No!? Don’t tell me she’s going to do the same thing she did to the Luminous back in Eljudnir.

His blood ran cold at the thought of what that meant. If it had frozen a Luminous, what would it do to a mere Blessen? Hel could kill her.

The girl groaned, her face wrinkling into a frown. Then she moved slowly, but soon her movements grew more agitated as she thrashed in the bed. In a second, she shuddered, eyes wide and huffing as if she had just woken from the worst of nightmares. Hel popped out of her body, exhaling a puff of air and wiping her brow as if sweating… she obviously wasn’t sweating. It was all an act.

The girl’s eyes darted all around the room, clearly frightened by the unfamiliar surroundings. She looked like someone trying to remember where she was.

“Hey,” Anadis began in a soft tone, hands placatingly raised, trying not to spook her any more than she already was. “How are you feeling?”

The mattress bounced as she suddenly sat up in an aggressive stance, the too-small tunic riding up her thighs as she did so. Anadis felt his cheeks flush as he caught himself peeking before glancing away. Her breathing hitched as her gaze locked onto him. She tightened her posture instantly, sliding one leg back as if ready to kick him or vault off the bed.

Anadis squeezed his eyes shut and raised his arms, bracing for the impact that never came.

“Where am I?” she spoke, her voice sharp yet quiet.

“My house,” he said, keeping his tone quiet. “You’re safe.”

“Why am I here?”

“I stepped in and tried to save you from that man… Well, in the end, you were the one who saved me with your Blessing.” Anadis didn’t know where to look as he rambled on. “Your fire Blessing was really amazing, by the way. But then again, I brought you here, so I guess we saved one another?” He laughed nervously.

Her ruby eyes narrowed to slits, concentrating hard on him. Then they widened, and she gasped audibly before dropping her aggressiveness.

“It seems you finally recognized me,” he said, showing a small smile. “A bit late if you ask me, but what matters is that you’re okay now.” He paused, then added, “Selene…”

Yep. This girl was Selene. Anadis had recognized her the moment she fainted earlier that day and her cowl had loosened from her head. All sorts of questions flooded his head in that moment. What was the daughter of Roger the blacksmith doing in the forest alone? Especially fighting an Emanant?

A look of astonishment crossed Selene’s face, then her brows furrowed. “Anadis?”

“It’s been a while. Three weeks since you disappeared on me,” he laughed awkwardly. “But now I understand why you always wore those thin leather gloves and that veil around your head.”

Immediately, her gaze snapped to her hands. No gloves. Then to the sides of her head. Nothing covered her ears. She cursed under her breath.

“You are an Oupire,” Anadis said matter-of-factly. Out of all the questions that flooded him while escaping, that one shook him the most. Selene wasn’t even human. She was an Oupire, one of the daemon races, born far from Aura Cay in the easternmost reaches of the continent of Igindor, where humans rarely ever went.

Had she been hiding her identity for her entire life?

Must have been hard…

Her hand curled into a fist, her knuckles turning white. “Are you going to report me?”

“What? No, why would I do that?” he answered honestly.

She bit her lip, uncertainty flickering in her eyes. “Then…” Her voice cracked. “Then you’re planning to have me killed?”

“Oh, relax,” he said. “Selene, you’re my friend, aren’t you? Why would I want to kill you or report you? Why would I have bothered carrying you uphill for almost two hours if I wanted any of that?”

“Then what do you want?” She glared at him, though he could tell she didn’t truly mean it. Daemons weren’t exactly welcome in human lands no matter how much authority said otherwise. “Why involve yourself?”

Anadis pinched the bridge of his nose and let out a slow grunt, not understanding how to deal with this situation. How do I make her understand I just wanted to help her? I guess she’s not someone who trusts people right away.

“Listen, I don’t know the details of why exactly you were fighting an Emanant,” he said, running a hand through his hair. “But I couldn’t just leave you passed out on the ground, so I brought you here—to my house, my bedroom.” He gestured at his room, and she looked around briefly. Then he pointed at her. “That tunic you’re wearing is my sister Nora’s, and those bandages on your arms are courtesy of my mom and grandma, who spent half an hour patching you up.” He sighed, frustrated. “So maybe stop assuming I dragged you here for some weird reason.”

She blinked and looked down at herself, noticing the small tunic, then looked at him and blinked again. Immediately she pulled the tunic down to hide her legs. “Turn around!” she yelled, her face flushed. “Don’t look at me!”

After a minute, Selene settled down on the bed, the sheets covering her legs and her face still a bit flushed. Anadis now wanted answers; he needed them. “So,” he said, staring into her scarlet eyes, framed by thick, dark lashes, “why were you fighting an Emanant?”

“That’s…” She seemed to hesitate for a second. “It’s not your business, so don’t worry about it.”

Anadis had half expected this response. He would have to break through her walls—but how? Should he be demanding? Should he be slow and gentle? He didn’t know her that well, yet she seemed stubborn enough to make it a difficult task. “I remember you asking him something. If I’m not mistaken, you asked him, ‘Where’d you have him?’ I need to know what you meant by that.”

Selene refused to meet his gaze. He could see how uncomfortable she was with this conversation.

“Listen, Anadis,” she said, shaking her head, auburn hair swinging. “This isn’t anything you have to worry about.” Her eyes, as red as wine, met his for just a moment before darting away.

“Selene!” Anadis’s tone was a bit loud and firm. “I want to help, but I can’t if you don’t let me. Tell me who that man is. I know he’s part of the Blessen Division. Is he one of the Blessens that were at your door the day of the registration?”

Her breath caught, and her blackened fingers shook suddenly.

“What did he do? Tell me, and I’ll help you stop him. I’m a Blessen. My twin, my sister, and my father are too. Surely, we can bring that man down to justice.”

“No! You can’t,” she snapped at him. “He’s a very powerful man, an Emanant. No matter how many Blessens you bring, he’ll still be above them.”

He rubbed his temple. She obviously doesn’t know Dad is a Luminous and could deal with him easily. Still, he needed to understand exactly what the man had done to her.

She gripped the bedsheets and ground her teeth. “I’m leaving,” she said, getting off the bed. “I appreciate what you did, but please keep away from me at the Learning Hall. Let’s just forget this ever happened.” She sounded… defeated.

“Wait,” Anadis jerked up from his seat. “Don’t leave—not yet. I need to know the reason. I want to help.”

“Why?” she asked loudly. “You don’t owe me anything, and I thank you for saving me. But I don’t want your help. Don’t feel obligated to help me. We only met each other briefly, and it’s best if you act like you don’t know me. Just keep on living your life and leave me alone.” She let out a weary sigh. Then she gestured with an open palm as if asking for something.

Anadis tilted his head, confused.

“My clothes and my gloves. My cowl. I’m not leaving my things here. I’ve been hiding who I am for long enough—I’m not about to throw it all away by walking around showing my features.”

He nodded, discouraged. “Fine.” He motioned toward the corner stone wardrobe, where her clothes sat neatly folded. Selene moved stiffly and grabbed them, pulling down on Nora’s short tunic.

Hel had been hovering near her for a while. She fixed Anadis a sidelong glance and nodded toward Selene. “Are you giving up already?”

What could he do? She didn’t want his help—no one’s help, for that matter.

Selene walked past him, her shoulder brushing his. He didn’t know what compelled him, but her skin felt warm as he grabbed her arm. Selene snarled, her cuspids glinting ever so slightly as she leered at him, a hint of fear in her ruby eyes. “Don’t touch me.”

“S-sorry.” Anadis released her arm quickly. “Selene, it’s dark out. Don’t leave,” he begged. “Please spend the night—not here, of course—but in my sisters’ room. It’s too dangerous to go out now. We’ll talk about everything tomorrow.”

“I’m a Blessen. Nothing will happen to me.”

A loud thud shook the wall as someone slammed the door open. A huge, muscular figure stood in the doorway, his sisters and mother behind him.

“Dad?” Anadis blinked.
