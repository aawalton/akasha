Elise and Charlotte looked out at the city in silence. It looked like something out of a zombie apocalypse movie, except if that zombie apocalypse had been infected with an unhealthy dose of eldritch horror. Some of the draugr looked almost human. Others barely looked human at all anymore.

A few were even flying around. They had wing-like extensions made of corrupted flesh, covered in eyes and tentacles, and while they were nowhere near big enough to allow flight, they were still somehow airborne. She thought she saw one or two more powerful-looking draugr as well. But no matter where she looked, there was nothing that looked like it hadn’t been human at some point.

Why is it so different? she wondered as she descended back to the ground.

After not seeing a single one of these draugr for an entire year in Erun’s cave, suddenly there were thousands of them. She wasn’t sure if Erun’s cave was the odd one, or if this one was. She would have to check out the third city to be sure. She had a suspicion she knew what the difference was, though.

Unlike in Erun’s cave, where all the monsters knew to avoid the castle, the ones here did not seem to care. She had seen many of them milling about right in front of the gate. So either the owner of that castle did not care to clear them out… or they were dead. Or they had turned into one of these. Or they were a lich more like the ones she had heard about in Jelor, and had deliberately created all these draugr and culled everything else.

The last possibility made her nervous, but after thinking about it more, she decided it was extremely unlikely. The draugr were too disorganized. Too random. Too purposeless. Even if they had been created intentionally, they clearly weren’t being controlled anymore. Otherwise they would likely have invaded Erun’s cave at some point in the past 10,000 years.

“Let’s head to the castle,” said Elise.

Charlotte nodded, and the two started moving.

Almost immediately, a pair of draugr noticed them. One had evidently been a child. Elise once again made quick work of them, but grimaced when she saw the System notifications.

[ You have slain Abyssal Draugr, Tier V, lvl 2 ]

[ You have slain Abyssal Draugr, Tier V, lvl 1 ]

[ You have leveled up! 13 -> 14 ]

They had a long way to go to get to the castle, and there were a lot of draugr between there and where they were now. If she kept fighting through them like this, she would level up much faster than she wanted to. The fact she was even getting level-up notifications was also a firm reminder of how dependent she still was on the System.

Sure, she could kind of handle herself without it now. Her magic had progressed to the point where, if she was careful, she could take care of monsters without needing to touch the System at all. But her most powerful tools were still locked down within the System. {Power Word Kill} and {Don’t Look At Me, Peasants!} specifically were abilities she knew she was far from being able to replicate on her own.

But the aether pulses that Erun had showed her would help. With just Aya’Rhun’s, it was impossible, but Erun had given her the other pieces of the puzzle. She saw how it could be possible in the future. She just needed practice.

“Charlotte,” she said, turning to the arachne. “What level are you right now?”

“Five,” said Charlotte, tearing her gaze away from the corpses. “Why?”

“And you’re Tier 7?”

“Yeah.”

“Okay, from now on, you’re going to kill all of them.”

“I am?” she asked, looking excited.

“They give me too many experience points,” said Elise. “If I keep going, I’ll be forced to evolve, which would be bad. So I need you to handle all the monsters for now.”

“Okay!”

“You can’t eat them.”

“What if I need to bite them to kill them?”

“Spit it out afterward.”

Charlotte crossed her arms, pouting. “Why?”

“We don’t eat people, remember?”

“Why not?”

“We just don’t.”

“I don’t understand.”

Elise wanted to just put her foot down, but seeing Charlotte’s expression, she realized that the arachne might never understand if she didn’t get a proper explanation. Charlotte was very child-like at times, but she was not actually a child. She had spent decades as a spider monster, and while she lacked experience with humanoids, her mind was that of an adult’s. She deserved a proper explanation.

“Charlotte, if I died, would you eat me?”

Charlotte thought about it for long enough that it made Elise uncomfortable, then shook her head.

“No. We are friends. I would not eat you. But these are not our friends. Why can’t I eat them?”

“They are not our friends, but they were still people once.”

“So?”

Elise struggled to find the words for a few seconds.

“Do you know what the word ‘respect’ means?”

“Like when you think something is strong?”

Elise furrowed her brows. “Elaborate.”

“Elaborate?”

“‘Elaborate’ means to explain further. Talk more about what you mean.”

“Ohhhh. Well, Tyral always said to be careful of other monsters and people. He told me to respect their strength, and not let my guard down.”

“So basically acknowledgement. You are acknowledging their strength, and treating it seriously.”

“Yeah,” said Charlotte, nodding.

“Okay, so that is just one kind of respect. There are multiple other kinds of respect. For example, you can respect someone’s accomplishments. If I kill a really powerful monster, you would respect that. Or you can respect someone’s opinion. Even if you disagree with them, you can still acknowledge that it is a valid opinion, and that they are not crazy.”

Charlotte nodded again, but her expression said she didn’t quite understand. Elise debated trying to re-explain, but she didn’t think she could do it any better than she already had.

“In humanoid societies, we also have something called ‘respect for the dead.’ It’s like– It’s like respect for someone’s existence. No matter what they did, or what they look like, or who they were, they still existed. They were a person. And we respect that. That means that when they die, we don’t disrespect them. We don’t hurt the corpse, and we definitely don’t eat it.”

Charlotte frowned, still looking confused.

“Think about it like this. Humanoids eat the meat of animals and monsters. Animals and monsters can’t think the same as we and the humanoids do. They only have their instincts, like you used to. And humanoids eat them. So if they also eat other humanoids, it’s like saying that those humanoids are no different from the instinct-driven animals and monsters. You’re not respecting their existence as a person. A person that is more than just a beast.

“It’s a complicated thing. You probably won’t understand it immediately. But that’s how it is, and if you want to make friends with more people, you need to respect humanoid customs. Hey, there’s another form of respect. Even if you don’t understand it, you still acknowledge it, and you agree to abide by it when you’re in a humanoid place. Understand?”

“I think so…” said Charlotte, rubbing her chin. “So I don’t eat people… because they could have been friends?”

How the heck did she get that out of what I said?

“Yes, kind of.”

“Okay. I get it now. No eating draugr.”

“Right. But you still need to kill them. And then we’re going to destroy the bodies, because the System is not respecting the dead. It’s turning them into monsters.”

“The System is not a friend.”

“Exactly. It’s not a friend. And it’s being mean to people who could have been friends. So we’re going to kill the draugr, but then we’re going to make sure that the System can’t disrespect their bodies anymore. And then maybe in the next life, we can be friends.”

Charlotte nodded enthusiastically. “Okay, let’s go!”

“One last thing. I’m going to be practicing some things while you fight, so if you see any of the draugr acting weird, don’t attack them immediately.”

“Okay. Now let’s go?”

“Yes, let’s.”

Charlotte set an excited pace as they fought their way through the city to the castle. The moment they finished handling the first corpses, she basically sprinted toward the next group of draugr and gleefully tore into them. They did not stand a chance against her, and her acid-coated hands cut through them like butter. This happened three times before Elise had to ask her to slow down a bit, because she was killing them too quickly for Elise to get any practice in.

“Maybe use some of the Skills you don’t usually use,” she suggested. “Use this as an opportunity to level them up.”

“Oh, good idea!”

Elise regretted making this suggestion only a few seconds later. As it turned out, the Skills that Charlotte didn’t often use were somehow more gruesome than her usual fighting method of tearing things apart with her hands.

When they got to the next group of draugr, Charlotte opened the slaughter by standing up on her back four legs, bending her spider abdomen so that it was facing forward and firing a line of webbing at the nearest draugr. Except instead of tangling it up or encasing it, the webbing just stabbed straight through, creating a sizzling hole, then expanded behind it before getting pulled backward. The draugr got jerked forward as Charlotte fell back down, and the eldritch zombie flew straight into her waiting spider maw.

Elise watched in horror as the spider fangs sank into its body, pumping it full of acid while the corpse melted from the inside out. The smell was horrible, and it was made worse by the fact that the draugr looked like it had once been an elderly woman, her wrinkled face wrinkling further as the skull melted beneath it.

Elise decided not to watch any more after that, keeping Charlotte exclusively in her periphery while she fought. She focused her attention on one of the draugr in the back, finding the aetheric link between them almost instantly as she began her work.

Erun’s aether pulse drills were different from Aya’Rhun’s in many ways. From Elise’s side, the way she performed them felt different, and the effects were very different. Aya’Rhun’s did almost nothing when used on other living creatures, while causing changes in mana when used on her mana cores. Erun’s did basically nothing to her mana cores, while causing changes in other living creatures.

All his drills seemed to be the building blocks of the aether Skills that Elise was often so dependent on. It made her re-think how those Skills worked as well. She definitely had a firm understanding of {Charm}, and one of Erun’s aether pulse variations was actually just the same manual version of {Charm} that Elise had discovered.

{Suggest}, though, she realized she was completely wrong about. The Skill still did roughly what she thought it did, but the way it did it was completely different. She thought she had been close to replicating it by infusing some of the same emotions and thoughts into her aether pulses, but that was only imitating the effect, rather than the cause.

{Suggest} worked by literally transferring a packet of information from one to another. That information could be literal words, like how she had used it at the beginning, or emotions, as she later learned, or even more abstract things like “enemy to my right.” The reason that all of those worked within a single Skill had nothing to do with those aether pulses she was experimenting with, and everything to do with the fact that the Skill literally created a copy of those thoughts and feelings and sent them to the target.

It suddenly made complete sense why she hadn’t been able to replicate all of the Skill’s functions, despite being quite adept at imparting emotions through System-free aether pulses. The emotion-imparting pulse was just one of many, when it came to aether pulses, and it was not the one that {Suggest} was built upon.

While Charlotte committed horrific war crimes against most of the draugr, Elise worked on trying to actually replicate {Suggest} on her one single target in the back. It was still a combination of multiple different aether pulse variations, but she had gotten a basic level of mastery of them already, so combining them wasn’t completely beyond her.

She started by doing the variation that made a temporary expansion in the thread. An ordinary aether thread was not thick enough to accommodate information like that. Erun told her to think about it like a bubble traveling along the line. It wasn’t entirely accurate, since it didn’t exist in the physical world, and the “bubble” arrived instantaneously, but it was good enough for visualization.

This was the basis of many aetheric abilities, including not only {Suggest}, but also {Whisper}. The hardest part about getting it to work was encoding the necessary information within. Aether vaguely coded with a simple emotion was easy, and it was what she started with. A healthy dose of fear to stop the draugr in its tracks and separate it from the group.

With it temporarily immobilized, that was when she really started working. Coding anything beyond a simple emotion into aether was difficult, but she didn’t feel it was beyond her. It was similar to the constructs she was already making in her soul, but a little more abstract. She couldn’t send the actual constructs. She had tried once with Charlotte, and it would always get left behind when she tried sending the bubble. Erun said it had something to do with proper constructs being full of her essence, and requiring a lot more effort to take out of her soul.

But she could still structure the aether somewhat. Something between raw aether and a construct. It was not easy work, but in the two weeks since Erun taught the variation to her, she had discovered a few methods that had tentatively worked on Charlotte.

The second bubble she sent carried a more complex version of fear. Instead of just primal fear, it was fear with the connotation of something about to kill her. The fear she felt when she had first faced the warg, as opposed to the fear she felt when she thought Hallbjorn was going to die.

That had no visible effect on the draugr beyond what the first one did. It was still stunned, frozen in place. That was just the first baseline test, though. She hadn’t expected much. The third one, however, caused a notable change. This one was the fear of being chased, and the effect was immediate. The draugr took off running directly at her. She could tell it wasn’t actually after her, but rather running away from an imaginary pursuer. She followed this up quickly by sending the fear of suddenly stumbling upon something dangerous, and it once again stopped in its tracks before it could get too close.

Elise continued trying increasingly complex variations of fear, while the draugr stuttered and stumbled around. Charlotte finished up her own battle and stopped to watch, visibly confused at what was going on. Elise barely gave her any mind, instead fully focusing on her own efforts.

The more complex the emotion, the harder it was to package it into the bubble, but with each variation, she got a better understanding of how it worked. Not every attempt worked. Especially not the later ones. Her final attempts revolved around replicating the {Suggest} trick that Hans had taught her, which tricked the target into thinking an attack was coming from a specific direction, or that their enemy had moved a different way than they had. None of these were successful, but she did cause some interesting anomalies.

While trying to make the draugr think there was an attack coming from the right, it didn’t completely work, but it did turn slightly to the right and twitch a tentacle-infested arm as if it was going to attack or try to block. There was something happening for certain. It just wasn’t happening with enough clarity or intensity to cause the effects that she wanted.

After a couple minutes, the draugr seemed to start building up a resistance. She had to expend more aether to cause the same effects, and it was breaking out of them more quickly. It wasn’t at all sustainable, so she decided to end it, using yet another type of aether pulse that she had not gotten enough practice with.

Erun called this one an “aether spike” and it was more or less a direct attack on the opponent’s soul. Elise used one with as much power as she could muster, hoping to kill the draugr in a single attack. It failed, but it did a lot of damage, forcing the draugr to its knees, holding its head as it let out a disturbingly human scream. The second aether spike finished it off, ending its suffering and leaving Elise feeling a little gross.

The aether spikes were nowhere near as efficient as {Power Word Kill}, unfortunately, but she hoped to build up her skill with them to the point that she wouldn’t be as dependent on the System to kill with aether. And despite her new proficiency with magic, she still didn’t have anywhere near as much mana as she had aether. She enjoyed learning magic, but unless she drastically changed her evolution path, she would always be aether-first, and having a reliable, go-to attack would be good.

It was better than trying to scare monsters to death with {Suggest} or {Forced Feeling}, at least. And while the scream of the draugr lingered in her head, she felt good about the progress she was making.

The scream brought the attention of a few more draugr over to them, including two of the flying variety, so before they could melt the corpses down, they were fighting again. Elise practiced more with her aether pulses, making minor changes and improvements, and when she finished, she worked on supercharging her aether spike as much as possible. It was stronger than before, but still not strong enough to instakill the draugr.

They then melted the corpses down, and the next few hours passed in a repetitive fashion as they fought their way toward the castle. They did not make it that day. By the time the soul storm was approaching, they were not quite halfway, so they decided to turn around and make a camp in the tunnel for the night.
