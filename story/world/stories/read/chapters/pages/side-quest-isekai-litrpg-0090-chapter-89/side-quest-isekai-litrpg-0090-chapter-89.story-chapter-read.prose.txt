The box was not empty, like it was supposed to be, but Logan didn’t have time to register that.

Thirty-some monks, with more falling in behind their ranks, were entering the temple, and Logan was as exposed where he was wedged behind the glass as a bear hiding behind a sapling.

Despite being on the opposite side of the display, Nugget must have felt Logan’s panic through their bond, because Logan suddenly felt a wave of boldness come from the other end and the dragon disappeared.

Luck presents opportunity, they said. Well, luck was doing him dirty right now because his butt was squeezed up against a massive display glass, and one wrong glance from the monks would set off alarms and paint Logan as a thief.

Even if he explained he had only ended up here because of Lambert’s job, it was just one more tally mark next to the list of things Logan was standing out for when he was supposed to stay under the radar. All that assumed they didn’t use some special monk skills to kill him first.

Then came the noise Logan expected as every suddenly gasped and shouted.

Logan’s stomach knotted. Yeah, I really messed this up.

Then he heard the words actually coming out of their mouths.

“It must be a sign! Look how the ceiling is shaking above the altar of Razan!”

“Our prayers have been heard!”

“Quick! Everyone, that way!”

With his cheek squished against the window, Logan could only see a bit of the entryway. The monks all ran deeper into the temple, but on the opposite side from the display case. None had glanced his way yet.

Stealth is level 28!

Reassurance came to him through the bond again.

Whatever you’re doing, Nugget, thank you! But be careful!

If word got out that a dragon had been spotted, that would be far worse than Logan getting discovered. The reavers had to have spies covering the area, and if they caught wind, reaver informants would swarm the place.

And once they got hold of Nugget… Logan couldn’t bring himself to think like that.

As Logan pressed his feet against the ribbed interior wall of the display and hurled himself up, he opened his storage ripple. Without thinking twice, he touched the Casket Box of The Book of the Living Shrine.

He only wanted its contents, but there wasn’t even enough room in the window display to open the box. And there was no way he was passing up the chance of getting this now while he was right next to it. And so, into his ripple went the entire thing, casket box and contents alike.

The glass squeaked loudly in Logan’s ear, and he feared the noise might draw attention at any moment. But the only cries were coming from the far end of the temple, where monks were calling on their fellow monks to tug on the pullcords and charge the temple beneath the ceiling panel where the commotion was happening.

Bit by bit, Logan squeezed his way up, past the height of the display area, and then hauled himself once again onto the wooden scaffolds.

Above the ceiling grid, Logan saw Nugget jumping on a ceiling panel. Traces of residual golden questlight diminished atop the spot. Nugget must have seen that light just as Logan had been drawn to the guiding light in the display case and chosen that section to sow his chaos.

“Psst! Nugget!” He kept his voice low and relied more on his bond with Nugget than anything else, but the dragon responded and flew to Logan.

Below, the monks kept calling out to Razan, and the bright tinkle of handheld bells drifted up to the rafters.

Logan felt like he recognized the name Razan, but he couldn’t quite remember why. Right now, though, he was just focused on getting out of here.

He ran along the rafters toward the end of the scaffolding, which would put him closer to the temple’s main entryway as opposed to heading deeper into the center. He wanted to be able to bolt right out the door. As he ran, he passed several of the service openings, each of which had its own blue ladder. His foot struck the lip of one ladder, and he slipped.

Falling headfirst, Logan gasped as he tumbled through the opening.

Meditate!

He reached for the lip of the opening, but he activated his slowed-down perception skill too late. His fingers only caught air.

That is, until Nugget tucked his wings in and, in a golden blur, darted beneath Logan.

Right under Logan, Nugget rotated so his four paws faced upward, and cupped them together at the center of his chest to create a foothold. When Nugget caught Logan, the dragon spread his wings wide and beat them once with a mighty flap. Together with how the dragon pushed against Logan, the force was enough to reverse Logan’s fall. It propelled him several feet up.

Logan hadn’t expected such strength from Nugget, but then he remembered that Nugget now had Logan’s heritage boons too, including his Measured Touch trait. Before he could dwell on it, the lip of the opening was back in reach, and Logan closed his fingers around it. The Vicelock perk steadied him as he hung there motionless, which gave him time to regain his balance and eventually pull himself up.

Nugget darted into the opening, too, and back to the safety of the closed ceiling grid.

Logan drew Nugget into a hug before he even realized it. From over Nugget’s shoulder, Logan had a clear view of the dizzying drop to the floor. “Thank you,” he whispered into the dragon’s ear.

Below, the monks sounded worried. It seemed like they were trying to figure out why the ceiling panel above Razan’s shrine had stopped shaking. They shouted out desperate cries to Razan to hear them and give them a sign that their prayers were heard.

Logan didn’t know what those prayers were, but he didn’t plan on sticking around to find out. “We can’t stay here,” said Logan to Nugget. “Ready?”

The dragon, still wrapped in Logan’s arms, gave a firm nod.

Logan let him go, and Nugget trailed behind him as they continued to the far end of the rafter.

At the end, he scanned the network of drainage pipes leading out to the exterior gargoyle statues. He hoped to find a hole or open seam to take him outside that wouldn’t require him to drop down to the temple’s main level, where he would be in clear view.

The only one he found wasn’t even big enough for Nugget to squeeze through.

He glanced at the final service opening that was wedged up against the back wall. The expected bright blue ladder descended down. These things really are going to be the death of me.

He wondered, though, if he might make one more use out of his Vicelock perk. If he grabbed the side rails rather than the rungs, could he slide his way down?

As he wondered this, the monks’ cries to Razan grew quieter. He needed to act now, and the only other option he saw was an outright jump, and he already knew that wasn’t going to work.

Letting out a reluctant breath, Logan lowered himself onto the ladder, gripping the wooden rafters and walking down with his feet as far as he could. When he finally put his two hands on the ladder, he grabbed the side railings. Here goes nothing.

At first, he didn’t move. Damn it. I should have figured. My Vicelock perk isn’t going to let me slide down. The whole point is that it makes my grip secure.

But he was already a few rungs down now. If he tried to climb up, he would be at that point where he would lose his balance. Then that would send him in a complete plunge to the bottom.

Instead, he loosened his grip just a little. Enough that his skin still made contact with the vertical ladder rails, but loose enough that he wasn’t actually holding it.

That worked, and he began to slide down.

As his momentum picked up, Logan clamped his hands to the sides to act as brakes. Unfortunately, though, he had made yet another miscalculation. The fact he was moving kept the perk from kicking in. How did I not expect that!

His momentum only picked up faster and faster. Even gripping the ladder, he would smash into the floor, and he would not survive that.

He sent a quick burst of urgency to Nugget, who was in a nosedive matching Logan’s increasing speed.

Nugget sent back warmth over the bond and reminded Logan of something Logan had once tried back in Gnashridge Heights.

Brilliant, Nugget!

A mere twenty feet away from the temple floor, Logan completely released his hold on the ladder, and instead reached into his storage. He put on the Amulet of Bleed Immunity, and quickly accepted the prompt to change to the Life affinity.

Eldertread!

Right after he activated the skill, his descent slowed just the smallest bit. Just above him, Nugget slowed too and shifted into a more horizontal glide. Logan reached up and clung to the dragon’s talon, just like.

He felt Nugget’s stress at carrying Logan’s weight, but the dragon pushed through the strain. Just like Logan had once tried to do with a chicken at Gnashridge Heights, he did with Nugget now and slowly drifted to the floor. Nugget wheeled one wing higher to pivot their descent into a semi-spiral, and they slipped right through the temple’s main door, which was still propped open.

A few feet from the ground, though, and Nugget’s wings collapsed above him, sending Logan and the dragon into a cartwheeling tumble as they bumped down the front stairs and landed in bushes.

At the end of the fall, Logan dizzily got to his feet.

Shrubbery branches snapped as he moved and scratched his face and hands. Plenty of people were milling about, but not a single person was looking their way.

Okay, that, I can call luck.

In the brush, he rested until his stamina refilled, and he and Nugget could both catch their breath. Then, he wrapped Nugget in his cloak and, when he saw a break in the foot traffic, casually stepped out of the bushes as if nothing had happened and strolled down the temple’s front staircase.

He wanted to put as much distance between himself and the temple right now to avoid any of the monks linking him to the strange things they had just experienced. For that reason, he directed himself toward the Mages’ Guild.

Some other time, he would complete his pending quests with the temple, like charging the prism, charging Lambert’s tool, and… he paused just before reaching the last step.

Crap.

He had already finished the device-replacement quest for Lambert. The monks would find out Logan had been here! At least, they would if they tried to use the charging device. Apparently, it wasn’t only used for the prism, since Lambert used it for his tools. There might be other people who used it too.

But how risky would it be for Logan to show his face there now? Should he pretend like the hadn’t made the device change yet? He still had the old one in his storage.

He just needed a little more time to think up a strategy.

As his foot hovered over the last step, he didn’t realize who had come down to stand beside him.

“Logan? Is that you?”

Logan slowly turned his head and let out an awkward smile at the confused person standing next to him.

“Oh. Hi Brother Timmons.”

“I hope the commotion inside didn’t deter you from entering the temple,” the monk said.

Before Logan could say no, though, the monk’s eyes slid to the neckline of Logan’s cloak.

The cloak that concealed Nugget.

The blood drained from the monk’s face as he stepped back with a gasp. He turned and seized Logan’s wrist with surprising force and pulled Logan alongside him as he raced up the stairs and back into the temple, and cried out. “Brethren! Quickly!”
