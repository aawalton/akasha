Face is not some foreign, alien concept. It is not some bland interpretation of respect or childish arrogance. “Face” is a term denoting a blend of a dozen different traits, ranging from honor to reliability to trustworthiness. Face is the word we use to establish one’s entire reputation in the world. To “give face” is to show respect, earned and accurate, to another’s reputation and stance in society. Someone who decides that face is meaningless is someone who has thrown away the fundamentals of social rules in a world where might makes right, and thus, can never be trusted, nor expect to be able to trust anyone else.

Someone without face cannot be trusted to deal properly with guest or host rights. They cannot be trusted to deal fairly and honestly. They cannot be relied upon if given a command or fulfilling a trade. So many reduce the concept of face to something childish, to a simplification that describes it as arrogant masters making demands of their “lessers”, but in truth, when so much of the relationship between cultivators and sects is determined by mutually assured destruction of varying degrees, a social marker of who, and how, to trust, is essential. Losing face is more than looking foolish, it is losing access to markets, to safe havens, to the social norms and rules that allow living people to interact with words rather than blades. To reduce this complex social tool to something so banal as whether or not someone is treated as superior or inferior is the work of outsiders to our culture, or to idiots who cannot see beneath the surface of what’s right in front of them

Conversely, an over-reliance on face is just as much a guarantee of idiocy as its opposite. Sometimes, one must strike first, lest they fall into a well-prepared trap. Sometimes, to betray, or cheat, or abandon another is not just the better option but the only option that keeps one from death. In times like these, an obsession with social reputation can be a shackle, keeping one from thinking clearly and acting in their best interests.

Like any other social phenomenon, the mathematics of face are a complex one, with myriad depths to explore and understand before it can be casually tossed aside. It can just as easily destroy a life as enshrine it, and which way it will go depends on those involved.

-The last recorded words between The Honorable Tian Yang and his son, accused of unjust killings and demonic cultivation.

Unfortunately for her balance and organic integrity, the cultivator did not, in fact, want any improvements. Neither did the next one, or the three after that.

And yet, as she divides her attention between crafting rooms and tunnels for those wandering below, she does eventually find someone who takes her up on her offer.

“Yes! I accept!”

She smiles, clapping politely over the sound of clashing weapons. “Excellent! What are we thinking? You haven’t lost any particular pieces yet, but we can always do a manual removal. Or are we thinking a general overhaul?”

“How dare you call on outside assistance!” roars the cultivator’s opponent, their spear clashing constantly against his shield and gladius. “You would taint this conflict with your cowardice?”

“No need to be like that!” Raika says, coiling to lean over the spear-wielding cultivator, their Qi flowing like radiant illusions and flashing glass, their skin a brilliant gold-yellow with flaming red hair. “I’m happy to modify the both of you! Honestly, if you want to call a break, we could-”

“A truce! Until we speak to the spirit!” begs the shield-wielder, the ground around him turning as ashy as he is, brittle and cracking but doing little against the flashing colors and sharpness of his opponent. “You wouldn’t cost us both such an opportunity!”

“After what you tried? You dared to steal from me, then accuse another of the crime, casting them beneath my spear instead of admitting your crime? I’ll die before I let you rest even a single moment!”

“Well, that’s not very productive. Maybe we can have a conversation about this first? It wouldn’t do to have both of you coming apart over that vow.”

Raika makes the ground dip slightly beneath the spear-wielder, sending their next hit off-center and allowing her to spawn a tendril with which to hoist the shield-wielder off the ground. He panics a moment, then exhales in relief, then spasms back to readiness as the spear comes for his throat from out of another, invisible angle-

All of the blood in the spear wielder’s body suddenly moves slightly slower than the rest of them.

As their body suffers a stroke, seizure, heart attack, and a few thousand micro-tears at once, Raika sets down her volunteer, who mostly just stares at his opponent, now vomiting violently and barely staying on their knees. She feels the way his brain tickles at the thought of running, but a few eyes growing out of the tendril holding him help to quash that instinct quickly.

“You… you dare-” They’re interrupted by spitting up a bit more blood, gagging on their own words. Still, they’re a tough one, their cultivation a good four or five steps into Core Formation- a little catastrophic flesh damage isn’t enough to keep them down for long.

“Well, yes. I don’t really care about your stuff, I just want to stretch and work out some ideas. If interrupting your fight after I got permission does that, then that’s fine. Plus, this way, you also get a chance to get improved!

“And speaking of!”

She turns, a coil of flesh acting as her lower torso as she partially wraps herself the shield-bearer.

“Got any suggestions? If not, I’m just going to experiment a little, but I promise it’ll be beneficial either way. It’s honestly not that hard to improve things in a baseline body, bodies are an incredibly well balanced mess.”

“Uh… perhaps… my bloodline?” he says, visibly a bit overwhelmed.

“I have no idea how to improve that. It’ll be fun to try!”

And before he can protest, she’s moved 20% of the air in his blood out of his brain, and just like that, he’s out.

It’s actually really easy to modify people when she can feel and see and be inside their bodies. They’re inside hers, so it’s only reasonable that the inverse is just as true, but even without that, there’s that wider understanding, that sort of awareness of what is. They are Flesh, and they Live, and she has an intimate connection into both of these concepts, one that overrides their own.

It has a word. Starts with a D, she thinks.

The shield-bearer’s body sinks beneath the flesh that is the ground while his opponent spits, crawling back up to their knees. Their spear comes up to point at Raika’s body, its tip glowing with incandescent glass and perspective-shifting illusion.

“Spirit! You interfere with an honor-bound affair. I will not surrender him.”

“Even though I can just kill you pretty easily if you try and stop me?”

“I dare to defy the Heavens. I won’t shame myself by failing to defy you, even if it should lead to my death.”

Her head hurts.

A dozen safeguards or more cycle around each other, slamming shut like gates on her psyche, over and over. Triggered defenses try to lobotomize her entirely, revealing themselves in time for her to grab them and neutralize them, removing protein blocks and pre-programmed cancers before they have time to spread.

Still, blood dribbles down her face as she smiles, her gaze vacant for a moment.

Then it snaps back to the spear-wielder.

“Thank you. That was lovely. You remind me of someone, maybe of me, and that’s lovely. That attitude will get you killed or let you go far, and one is a lot less likely. I can help with that.”

The spear-wielder tries to rise- but the parts of her brain that govern conscious thought no longer have the bloodflow to do so. They fall to the ground, bright gold skin a lovely contrast to the soft, fleshy tones of their surroundings- and then the ground swallows them up, and they’re gone.

Two for the price of one. She’s going to give these two such gifts.

But that can be another of her- this version in particular is focused on finding new subjects.

Ironically, her next one is the first one she appeared to.

She feels someone carving into the ground, making large, sloppy letters, and reappears next to the young woman with the missing arm, a sloppy tourniquet proving entirely inadequate to stemming the blood flowing from it.

She smells of medicinal herbs and alchemical pills, which explains some of how she’s lasted this long, but her face is pale, her breathing shallow.

“Spirit… I…”

She’s not very good at talking. Blood loss will do that to most people.

Raika leans over her, growing out of the wall of the pore-like hole the disciple of the Jianghu has found herself in.

“Hello again. Have you changed your mind about the improvements?”

“I… offer… I don’t…”

“If you consent to treatment and modification, just say yes.”

Still, she hesitates. She almost does so for long enough that Raika considers fixing up some of her blood flow just to make sure she survives.

But then.

“Please.”

Raika smiles. “Of course!”

The process is smooth after that. As she develops, she finds herself relying on tools less and less, her understanding and control over Flesh and living tissue blending with her will directly inside her own body.

Blood vessels are rerouted to circulate back into the woman’s torso while Raika works, giving her a bit less of a headache as she keeps the cultivator alive. Threads of nervous tissue, tendon and bone all sprout from the socket at her command, pulling from out of Raika’s own near-indefinite reserves, blending seamlessly between herself and Flesh as a whole.

The new limb spirals into place, each joint gaining a few extra degrees of mobility as additional ligaments are woven into them, the muscle fibers denser and brighter than the rest of her body. She makes sure that the nervous system is on a hair-trigger, twitch-reflexes heightened to the maximum with enhanced sheaths and internal conductivity between her nerves. A blooming coat of keratinous, iron-based scales form over a thin layer of fat, a second layer padding above them before skin regrows over that in turn.

The spiritual organs are more difficult, as always. Raika takes some tissue from near the woman’s Dantian and around the flesh “inside” her meridians. Both organ systems exist in the same space, but not the same dimension, and the overlap lets her harvest tissue that is more-or-less useful for flavoring new pathways.

Deciding to experiment a bit, Raika crystallizes some proteins and keratin structures, making the pathways out of that crystalline architecture act as replacements for missing spiritual organs. It’s a sort of prosthetic, but should store Qi, and she’s curious to see if the different style makes any difference.

And then she gives the arm a dagger.

Not a big one, just a little added joint and muscle system which can flex and release a sharp-edged version of the material she used to build the Qi-pathways in the arm. She has no idea what this woman’s cultivation is (honestly, she just hasn’t checked, too focused on more interesting things), but chances are that her being a sword-user means it can do things with sharp edges. An added gift.

And then she re-weaves the nervous system, veins, and muscle-fibers to the rest of her body.

There’s a moment where her blood pressure gets dangerously low, suddenly having to fill up a whole limb that wasn’t there a moment ago, but Raika fixes that. A little extra blood added into the system, transformed to match the body it’s appeared in, fixes that right up.

The face of the woman relaxes in her sleep, then scrunches again as her arm starts twitching.

Raika may have overtuned the nerves. Just a bit.

It’ll be fine.

And this was lovely.

She devours the body of the dead disciple the woman killed, leaving the ring, clothing, and other artifacts, and then moves on once she’s sure that her experiment isn’t going to die in her sleep.

There are so many more to visit, and there are definitely some people in her body that have something more interesting than a missing arm.

A few more rejections come and go, some more polite than others. By this point most people don’t instinctively try and cut her apart the moment she appears, which is nice, but there are always exceptions. Before long, she’s made her way through most of the remaining groups, leaving just a few individuals she can sense.

Raika and Raika and Raika rise up out of the soil, turning to speak to more of the talented youth flitting about her interior-

“Hi there! Are you interested in gaining some improvements?"

And all freeze.

All but one of her on the surface collapses back into the flesh.

The one that remains keeps looking at the scene before her, her face slack. The animation has gone out of it, leaving it a mask of flesh, a thing to emote which fails to do so atop engines of sight and mastication.

She spends a moment just staring.

A figure in blue-black-purple robes sits atop a rope of calcified intestine, the trunk-like organ rising and sinking into the landscape in many coils. It’s as bloody as any part of this place, except where the disciple of the Peak of the Azure Serpent sits, where it is bloodier.

“I may well be, if you are the spirit of this hidden realm,” he responds, smiling. “Apologies for my unseemly appearance- though perhaps it lends me some semblance of my surroundings. This lowly disciple greets the honored being before him.”

He gets up, bowing properly and politely. Not too deep, not too shallow.

His bow puts his gaze directly in line with the body on the floor, but she gets the impression that he doesn’t even notice it. Part of the background to him.

His mind feels quiet. Aware and wary, satisfied, on edge, eager, ambitious, self-assured, but quiet.

No guilt screams in him.

She looks at the body on the ground.

All of the cultivators here are so young. Core Formation is nothing to the generations of Morae, it seems. Those in sects or associated with the Peaks are even younger than most, peerless talents fed by peerless resources.

“What happened here?” she asks instead of following that thought.

“Apologies for the display, honored one. I did not know that I would be graced by your presence, and a disagreement between myself and the one you see before you has led to an unsavory outcome. If their remains displease you, it would be an honor to remove them from our dialogue.”

He raises a hand to do just that.

He finds that his hand is no longer a hand.

A sound like gelatin hitting water comes as what was a hand sloughs off his body and falls into the ground.

There is no pain.

To his credit, he doesn’t scream- though he does immediately attempt to run away, his core spinning up an impressive amount of Qi for his realm as he launches himself away. A stream of pressurized water, shaped to a long snake, manifests around him, carrying him away like a fish in a stream.

When he next wakes up, she’s already brought him back.

It wasn’t even hard. His brain is just as much Flesh as all the others, and his body, while heavy, altered by some technique or physique, is not so heavy that she cannot drag him.

She can feel it as he comes back awake. The stutter in his consciousness resolves itself as confusion, memories of running and of where he is now connecting with no space in between. It’s interesting to watch.

She’s mostly staring at the body, but she watches him anyway. She sees when he becomes properly worried, properly afraid. She feels when his remaining hand goes to the jade comb on his belt, infused with more Qi than he has in his whole body- and cracked in half.

He checks a half-dozen more life saving artifacts, fear getting louder with every broken trinket. She lets him.

“You know, I think I should be angrier about this,” she tells him. Her voice sounds loud in comparison to how he’s breathing, but it’s no louder than before. “There is a version of me that’s furious, maybe. I’m mostly just surprised. I did not see this coming, and the mechanics of it surprised me. It’s like finding a roach inside a sealed jar, at least to me. There’s a disgust, especially when you stop to think about it, but mostly, you’re wondering- how did this happen? What events made that a thing?”

He, very wisely, isn’t talking. Less wisely, his Qi senses are drifting over her body, tickling ever so slightly as he gets a feel for his surroundings, looking for his sword, for an escape route.

“I’m really curious, actually. I want to understand how this happened. What were you thinking when you killed her?”

He’s still saying nothing. His breathing is tight, his Qi moving in steady ripples that speak of clear and conscious control. He’s afraid, yes, but not entirely panicking.

“That was a direct question.”

He rolls over so he’s on his belly, orienting his body towards her, pressing his head to the ground.

“Apologies, honored one. I-”

“What were you thinking when you killed her?”

“...I was thinking that she had made herself my enemy, one far too dangerous to underestimate. I thought that the face it would cost to let her live would not be worth the harm it would cause my family should she leave this place and spread word of the dialogue that passed between us.”

“What dialogue did you have with this junior?”

“We spoke of familial matters, and political arrangements between our elders, honored one.”

She nods, still looking over every inch of the corpse. A woman, younger than him, not by much, but they’re all young, these are adolescents, they’re competent and old enough to kill and hunt and do all the terrible things one can do to another, but they’re young. They’re young.

There are a few safeguards she can’t quite root out working very, very hard to keep her thoughts from moving past that. From drawing connections. From comparing the corpses and the other cultivators to other young people, to people in her past and in a maybe-present that-

They’re working very hard.

“Were you to be married?” she asks instead.

“That was a potential outcome of the ongoing negotiations, honored one.”

He’s not guilty about what he did to her. Even as his fear fluctuates, treating her like a dangerous animal rather than something with teeth already around his throat, he doesn’t feel guilty. Even as he prepares to say he is, his brain chemistry lighting up the pathways connecting the thoughts to his lips.

But he doesn’t speak. He learned his lesson cheap, though he might disagree. He’ll probably miss the hand. And the artifacts that tried to help him escape her, but mostly the hand, she assumes.

“Did she disagree with being wedded to you?”

“Yes, honored one.”

“So you killed her?”

“...Yes, honored one.”

“But not before you did violence on her.”

Now the fear is there. Loud and bright, tasting of adrenal sourness, hot and cold and slimy.

He doesn’t answer, but it wasn’t a question. He learns quickly.

He’s not even twenty. Already deep in Core Formation. A genuinely promising talent, one protected by at least some sorts of interests, going by his protective talismans. He has centuries ahead of him, bright with promise and possibility.

She looks at the body.

She can feel the Flesh of it. The Flesh of them both. Every part of their biology, and track a pattern of events from the wounds and the chemical effects of the remains. It’s easy to compare it to his still-functioning system.

“I think I don’t like someone who would do this violence. I think you assumed no one would care, or that you would be protected in your actions. I think you took their denial of you rather poorly, and I think you’ve added quite a bit of distaste to an awakening I was thoroughly enjoying.”

He keeps not saying anything, but sweat drips from him to land on the floor, flowing from where his forehead kowtows against her Flesh.

“This is nice, in a way. Reminds me a lot of where I am, you know? And it helps me center myself. This is just what I wanted, things that made me really think.”

Finally, she turns to look at the promising young man.

“Do you think you should live?”

“I will spend the rest of my life making amends for my acts. I shall ensure her family, mortal and otherwise, is provided for for ten generations, that her elders are honored, and that her ancestors are enshrined forever in any home I ever have. I regret my actions and the foolish haste in which I took them, as I acted out of fear and arrogance. I apologize for sullying your domain with my mistakes, honored one, and beg you to spare me. My master shall provide anything you wish, even enshrine this place as a holy land and-”

“Do you think you should live?”

She tracks the saliva he swallows.

The air is crushing him. Every part of the realm has turned its gaze upon him, and the weight of it makes his lungs constrict, his eyes bleed, his heart strain against impossible pressure.

Still, he speaks.

“Yes.”

She nods.

“I agree.”

All across the hidden realm, dozens of Raikas turn back to their work, re-manifesting or refocusing on their tasks.

One of them turns all of her focus on a series of changes, precise and vast, that she enacts on a piece of Flesh, which tries in vain to scream.
