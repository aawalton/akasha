Ash tilted her head back and looked up at the giant trees stretching toward the sky.

A tiny part of her mind still refused to accept the sight. It made no sense. Nothing in her early tutoring or in the books she’d managed to get her mitts on said this was possible. There wasn’t even an island, so how were trees just growing out of the water, in the middle of the ocean? It boggled the mind. As she stared up, she could pick up some movement as well. People walking about and traveling from one structure to the next.

“Bridges” she heard herself mumble. It was impressive, but honestly? The shock and awe were already fading out. If this foray into the Pillar had done anything for her, it was pushing the boundaries of what she could adapt to.

It was just one more crazy sight to add to the list. Though she was still looking forward to see how they lived up there.

The boat groaned under her feet, and while her heart jumped in her chest, she had no visible reaction to the stimulus. This last week had been… something. Even though she had still managed to get some sleep, the attacks had been relentless, and pretty often—especially the first few days—Ash had to wonder: was this going to be it?

Curious sharks. Enraged drakes. Serpents. Fish of all kinds and forms. Even a damn eagle had tried to nab Fabian once. But thankfully, most encounters had only been with B-ranks, and the captain could scare those away easy enough and if they happened to be weak enough, then Velistraine had them take shots at it to get some extra fragments.

Suffice it to say, this has done wonders to her levels. But while she could minimally contribute to a low B-rank fight, she couldn’t say the same about A-ranks. Yesterday, they’d encountered the first one, a giant turtle thing that Velistraine had to battle for nearly a full hour before the stubborn beast decided to call it quits, and earlier today, it had been a territorial barracuda that had forced the crew to make some emergency repairs lest they sink.

Ash’s gaze slid away from the giant canopy toward the crow’s nest. Velistraine was still up there, watching for threats. She had mentioned something about the frequency of the attacks, but Ash still struggled to believe it.

Ash had thought the monster waves were unique to the Reach. She hadn’t known they could happen out here as well… Then again, this area of the world was also bathed in the lights of the Pillars, so the similarities weren’t completely out of left field. Back when she was a child, the subject had fascinated her. While the event didn’t really come in waves per se, the monsters still grew wilder and bolder a few weeks every year. It was one of the main reasons why establishing a foothold in the Reach was so difficult. Considering the five Pillars that constantly nourished its wilderness and it having much less living space than the ocean where the other Pillar came through, things could get hectic out there, especially considering how much monsters like their living space and didn’t like sharing it.

Ash’s musings were suddenly cut off when from the corner of her eyes, she saw the captain stiffen, and instantly, adrenaline shot through her body as her eyes flicked about, looking for movement. What was it now?

Before Ash could figure out what caused Velistraine’s reaction, the captain jumped over the ledge of the bird’s nest and came down in one smooth motion. All around, the crew went quiet, waiting for orders. Or for any information, really. After a few seconds of staring past the port at the deceptively calm waters, Velistraine grinned, showing her teeth, like a predator having caught the scent of something juicy. “Kraken,” Velistraine said. “Watching us. And I’m almost sure it’s not even here in person. It’s a clone that’s peeping on us…”

Clones? Ash swallowed. Did that mean they’d finally found a Trickster?

With one swift movement, Velistraine took off her coat and boots, leaving her with her white blouse and pants as she turned to Ray, who was frowning at her wiggling toes in disapproval. “Ray-Ray. I need something to—”

Velistraine’s voice died in her throat as a wild, heavy presence fell over the ship. All of the sudden, Ash didn’t feel like she was standing on the ship with her captain, instead, she’d intruded on something’s domain. Or someone. And whomever they were, they were staring down at her, but she couldn’t tell where they were. But she knew. As surely as her heart was trying to escape her ribcage, she knew she wasn’t supposed to be here.

Ash stared fearfully up at the looming trees. Where was it?

Ash felt the instinctive part of brain screech at her to run, but no muscle would respond to her will. Then she finally saw it. The shape detached from a branch, falling down toward them to doom them. Even though her eyes told her it was maybe not that much bigger than her, her soul screamed in fear as if a god was coming to smite her down. Still, through the sheer panic running through her mind, a fresh memory popped up and gave her pause.

Didn’t Ray say something about a guardian to her and Fabian?

Before her thoughts could catch up with what was happening, Ash saw Velistraine jump up, sword drawn to meet the oncoming ape. The attacker already had a gnarled club raised above its head, and as Velistraine approached, it swung down. The club met the sword, and as much as her instincts screamed at her to get away from the ape, she still expected Velistraine to cut through the wooden weapon, or at least to parry it.

Instead, there was a massive cracking sound, like a slammed fist on a wooden table, and next thing Ash saw was the sprout of water resulting from Velistraine hitting the waves. Hard.

Ash whirled toward Ray, hoping for an order or something. Anything. But instead, the man was smiling as he looked up at the still-hovering monster.

How is he smiling?

Wait, why am I afraid?

Ash blinked, suddenly concerned at the sudden change in her mood. She’d just been terrified for her life but… as if she’d just awoke from a dream, the dread was slipping away, like from a nightmare she couldn’t remember.

Velistraine burst out of the water and landed on the prow, hair and clothes dripping as a trail of blood streaked down the side of her face as she bared her teeth at the figure hanging from the side of the perch.

“Is that how you say hello to your favorite student, you old bastard?”

Ash’s mouth jaw loosened. Student?

“Favorite?” the ape-like humanoid jeered, still looking down at them. “You haven’t visited in three years. And you were never my favorite to begin with, you little shit.”

Next to her, Ray spoke, while a few of the crew were alternating between looking up at the guardian they had never met, and then at their invincible captain who, well, just got smacked down. “Kids, this is Zerathstra. The guardian of Thalis, and likely one of the few individuals that could still instill some humility in our dear captain.”

“I’ll fire you, Ray,” Velistraine shot back at her first mate, only for him to completely ignore her. Ash and Fabian stared at the first mate for a second, then looked up at the now hovering ape. Having let go of his perch, he floated down until he gently landed on the prow of the ship, during which Ash took an unconscious step back before glancing toward Velistraine. The captain was still rubbing at the painful but already healing wound near her brow. Looking down at her bloody hand, she grimaced before wiping it on her pants. “Well, as much as I’d love to catch up, there’s a Trickster kraken I’d like to get my hands on. Give me a second and I’ll come on up, alright? You can guilt-trip me all you want, then.”

The ape tilted his head, then his smile widened while Ash came to the oddly-timed realization that he was barely a foot taller than her.

“What? Why are you smiling like that?” Velistraine asked, eyes narrowed, and Ash took a moment to look the guardian up. The humanoid ape wore studded leather and loose, billowing brown pants that tapered at the ankles, and no shoes. Chuckling at the captain, Zerasthra shrugged. “If you’re after a Trickster, then you’ll need to look elsewhere. Because the one watching us is a guest.”

Velistraine was about to jump back in the water, but slowly, she turned to the ape. And so did everyone else and as Ash glanced around, she could see the same question flash in everyone’s gaze as they shared looks back and forth.

A kraken as a guest?

Henry watched from a distance as Zerathstra and the woman clashed in the air, and he shivered at the explosion of power he sensed through his spirit. It wasn’t just about raw power, either. It all happened in a fraction of a second, but Henry had noticed how the woman had twisted mid-air and mid-swing to change the angle of her attack, only for Zerathstra to react so fast Henry hadn’t even seen him move. He only saw the club smack her down—which she barely parried from caving her skull in, and a fraction of a second later, she was under water.

Which she had used to look straight toward him while blood billowed out of her brow. You’d think she’d be too busy defending from Zerathstra if he’d continue to attack, instead, she had turned her gaze to him. Not at his clone, no. But where his real body was… nearly 500 feet away.

It just confirmed something Henry knew already. A-ranks were freaking monsters.

Eyeing the red-haired captain, Henry narrowed his eyes. He knew she was one. His Identify wouldn’t even show him the rank, which meant she was A-rank, though clearly, Zerathstra was still a bit stronger than she was.

Was she an enemy, though?

Peeking above water through his closest clone, he saw the two talking. The woman was standing on the prow of the ship, though from this angle, he couldn’t see her features or hear what the two were saying. But they weren’t fighting. Which was good.

A moment later, the familiar figure of Zerathstra peered over the water and waved. At Henry.

Is he telling me to come over? Shit. What should I do?

As much as it was fine to head into the city and talk to people, this somehow felt more embarrassing. A whole crew was going to be staring at him. Sizing him up.

Don’t overthink it, he told himself. Still… should he show up as he was? Should he get bigger for the intimidation factor? Should he take human form?

Dammit. He was over-thinking it!

Taking a deep inhale, Henry closed his eyes for a moment, then shoved his anxieties aside. This was just like stepping into a room for an interview. Or presenting a school project. Just take it slow and answer the questions calmly and so, Henry began making his way toward the ship with his real body. Clearly, these were guests. Even if they got weird, Zerathstra was around.

The guardian would have his back. Probably.

Ash watched as Velistraine put her boots back on. The captain was giving the ape a look.  She wasn’t quite glaring, nor was she narrowing her eyes or frowning. But her gaze was intent. As if she was trying to figure something out. As if she was trying to read between the lines. But her target kept grinning at her, twirling his gnarled club around. It looked like regular wood to Ash’s eyes, but as far as she could tell, there wasn’t even a scratch from Velistraine’s sword, which was… extraordinary.

“You’re telling me this Trickster kraken has a name, and it’s friendly? Fully sapient. Are you sure, Zer?”

“Yup! Very friendly. He came here a few weeks ago with a couple of other awakened beasts. I’m sure you two will get along, once you get to know him. Give him a chance.”

Velistraine rubbed her ears vigorously, trying to get some of the water out. “That sounds just… crazy. You know krakens as much as I do. They’re not all bastards, sure. Some keep to themselves. But not Tricksters. You know how weird this is. Weirder still, it’s not even B or A-rank to display this level of intelligence. What am I missing here?”

In response, the ape just grinned wider and made his eyebrows dance. “You’ll have to figure it out.”

Velistraine groaned in her throat and cracked her back. “Fine. But let’s keep going. I want to hide the ship and take a break. The wave’s coming early this year, and I haven’t had a good night's sleep in a week.”

Zerathstra looked to the side, down toward the water, and nodded. “I’m glad you got here in time.”

Velistraine hopped and landed next to the ape, then gave him a one hand hug and a gentle nudge as both stared down in the water. “Don’t mention it. Now, let’s see this mysterious kraken.”
