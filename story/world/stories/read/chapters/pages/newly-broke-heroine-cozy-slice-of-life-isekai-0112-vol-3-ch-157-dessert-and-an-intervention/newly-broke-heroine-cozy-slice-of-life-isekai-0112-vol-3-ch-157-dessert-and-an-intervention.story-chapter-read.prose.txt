Missy stared at her for a good ten seconds. Her face scrunched; she opened her mouth to speak, then thought better of it. And that repeated twice before she mustered a response.

“Who?”

“The Merchant of Fortune?” Fiona repeated, feeling a muscle tic on her brow. Seriously, was this woman the one person on Cepalune who didn't know who she was? That was a switch.

Missy continued to draw a blank. “Repeating that title did not help at all.”

Fiona could hear angry arguing down the hall. The two dragons were hardly being subtle this time around, and big Folk have big voices. Nope. I don’t have time for theatrics. Fiona let the illusion fade, and Missy gasped and recoiled like she’d seen a ghost.

“You?!”

Well, I guess she must recognize me from Karlin’s description. “Yep. That’s me. Ring a bell now?”

“We–we checked–”

A little sleight-of-tail from our Kitsune,” she answered calmly as mousy brown hair gave way to bright red waves, and Missy’s hands went to her side. “And my name is Fiona. I came here to stop Karlin from hurting a lot of people.”

Then she made the connection, and her fury hit a boiling point. “The forge went up in flames because of you! You’re the reason I’m stuck hiding in this hole in the ground, why Karlin went off the deep end of the cavern, why he’s trying to–”

Fiona returned the finger shushing, leaning in. Missy let out a sound of disgust, but otherwise, took the hint before she made her case. “Okay, we can talk like adults here. The fact that you haven’t tattled on me indicates you have some reservations about Karlin’s conduct. The fact that I haven’t taken a comically oversized mallet to his skull is indicative of my willingness to entertain a better way. I don’t really want to, for what he did to his brother, Douglas, my–you know what, screw it, my boyfriend.”

It felt a little weird saying that out loud, but it was the case.

“You lied to me.” The accusation from Missy stung, even if somewhat earned.

“The only thing I lied about was the name and the face. The rest was the truth. I put my heart out on my sleeve in a very literal way,” she added, with Wingding stirring and making enthusiastic flaps on her wrist. Missy’s face scrunched, and she looked intrigued.

“Does your mark animate?”

“She does way more than that,” Fiona answered. “I still am tempted to go with plan A, and bulldoze this place as payback. But that hasn’t served me very well of late.”

“So what changed?”

“To make a long story short? You and Norbert.” Nearby, she heard the dragons going unusually quiet. They weren’t coming to blows, at least. “Look. You know what he has stashed here, don’t you?”

“The pyrogel? I told him repeatedly that whatever scheme he had planned to use it for was going to end badly for everyone!” Her eyes carried a spark of fury.

“Yep. I need to neutralize it. And Karlin has to answer for what he nearly did to the entirety of Fiefdala; he could have wrecked a whole nation, Missy! No matter what foolish idea he’s pursuing.”

Missy scoffed. “And what do you think you know, exactly?”

“That he’s trying to bring back Feo’thari. But he’s missing pieces of the story.” Fiona kept an ear out for anyone approaching, but it was still relatively silent–except for a few heated words between Karlin and Norbert. Something about being a disappointing son and an overbearing father was exchanged, followed by long pauses.

Missy blanched. “You know something he doesn’t–”

Fiona waved for silence. “Before I go any further, I want to make something clear. Fiefdala is my home, and I will protect it from anything that tries to ruin it. That includes your boyfriend, who is no philanthropist. Secondly, I have more than just myself here, and if anything were to happen to me–unlikely, because I’m kinda built differently–then rest assured, the not-good outcome goes into play.”

After a moment of hesitation, Missy let out a reluctant sigh. “I guess I don’t have much reason not to go along with this, do I?”

“Any scenario that ends without anything going ka-boom is a best-case in my books.” Fiona could see her thinking about it. “Hey, you know how I got here, to Cepalune?”

“No.”

“I went down fighting an eldritch nightmare dragon. And not before I took down hundreds more of its twitchy, toothy cannon fodder. But not before someone yanked me from certain death in the form of thirty tons of tanker truck thrown at me. So trust me when I say this: Karlin is making a big mistake.”

“Gods, Earth is–”

“Earth might be okay.” It was the first time she truly believed it, after thinking about Bianca’s words. “But things are stirring here on Cepalune. I’m just a retired heroine trying to run a magical shop and live a simple life of friends, found family, and occasional pilferage of sweet rolls.”

Missy rolled her eyes. “Did you really make those sweet rolls?”

“You betcha!” She couldn’t help but grin impishly at that–all that sampling had given her ideas for improving on the formula. “Okay, we’re pressed for time, Missy. Karlin’s alchemical explosives are degrading. If they go off, it'll put a crater in the world. We didn’t know we’d find him down here until a chance encounter with some smugglers, but I’m still dealing with it because it’s personal for me.”

“Why?”

“Because he’d probably blow up my shop. Which technically his brother has a share in,” she explained. She went pale at that. “Tell me he isn’t this crazy or this dumb! What is his target?”

“I don’t know. He’s obsessed with getting Aurelium. We tried to gather enough of it, but look at how that turned out.” Fiona winced–that gold likely had been cut into chunks and made its way back to the slave houses of Vale in ill-gotten gains, or Vale’s government itself. Missy squinted at her. “So what’s with the funky title?”

“Okay, call me crazy–”

“You are.”

Fiona rolled her eyes–she had one coming for a while, probably. “Look, you might think I’m nuts, and on a normal day, you would be right. I kinda had a crashout a few months back when the novelty of being flung across the cosmos and my last days on Earth came back with a wave of bad memories. But Fiefdala and Cepalune are my world of consequence right now. And Karlin is likely putting more than just Fiefdala in trouble.”

“Why?”

“Because I think someone wants him to succeed in bringing back Feo’thari. Then finish the job they started, five thousand years ago,” she added in a low tone.

It made sense. If this had been Glado's plan all along, lies hiding within lies, or the mystery third party who had put Bianca on Cepalune, too, then it presented a one-time opportunity to strike when the presumed dead goddess was vulnerable.

Except for one problem: Feo’thari wasn’t interested in coming back willingly, and Wingding was her heir.

She really needed to give her surrogate daughter a better name, all things considered.

“Look, just focus on the immediate problem: Do you want thousands of people to go up in a plume of alchemical smoke? Do you want to take that risk?”

“But he–” She let out a frustrated groan. “He converted it.”

Fiona blinked. “To do what?”

“To make an incendiary. Shelf-stable. That stupid otter got him the wrong stuff, and after we left the pop-up cafe, he spent all day with alchemists fixing the mess. Then he moved it. I don’t know where, and he didn’t tell me.”

Stupid otter…wait, Santino’s black sheep? Fiona nodded. “Okay, go on. What else was he working on?”

“I don’t know. He had a coin press in the basement, too, along with some regular gold ingots plus a smelter. And some other alchemical stuff.”

She tapped her relay. “Nick, do me a favor? Anything else in that basement, like a smelter?”

“And a coin-making machine. It looks like Fiefdalian currency, and I think it’s real gold. But…there’s residue on it. Looks like the pyrogel, but dried and crumbled.”

Fiona had a theory. “What exactly is below the Fiefdala treasury?”

Nick let out a grumble of frustration. “Rock and dirt, and maybe some connecting tunnels of Underlune. Why?”

“Yes, why, indeed?” Missy fumed. “He never told me the full plan. He kept people out of the loop and compartmentalized. Where are you going with this, Fiona?”

Maybe nowhere. Maybe Karlin has a scheme crazier than any of mine.

“Is that…the chick from dinner?” Nick said in an edgy tone.

“Yes, this is Missy,” she answered as Fiona waved for her to talk. “I would point accusingly at your sneakiness, but there’s one other glaring issue.”

“What’s that?”

“Fiona, you might be the vessel. Or there’s a good chance of it.” Missy pointed at Wingding. “Yeah, if Karlin sees that, you’re a dead woman. Because he hasn’t stated what, exactly, happens to the vessel during this whole procedure. My guess? It doesn’t end with ‘happily ever after’ if you get my drift.”

“What does ‘vessel’ mean exactly?” I didn’t have Wingding with me until after I got to Cepalune.” Fiona squinted. “Also, nice of you to finally open up a bit. You need a girls' night out and a lot of time away from Karlin after this. Depending on how politely he surrenders after tonight, He may get conjugal visits.”

“What’s a conjugal visit?”

Fiona groaned; there was no time for this. “Never mind. Why and how did he hear about a vessel?”

“Translations from something his father had, who in turn got from the late Missus Fierkraag, though I’m not sure where she got from, either. What it says, according to him, is that the vessel is a catalyst or something to condense all the power of the aurelium, and theoretically, if we have enough? Feo’thari will revive.”

Then Missy’s eyes narrowed. “Then I hope she wipes Vale off the map, and then Aegortin sinks into the sea, along with everyone on that island, drowning for what they did to me. And so many others.”

Fiona let out a soft sigh. Two thoughts cropped up. The first was that those translations were likely derived from that maddening tablet in the other room. How the dragon didn’t go insane doing it, she didn’t know.

Secondly, if she believed Glados’ words from before, she reacted very differently to the ‘benevolence’ of Aegortin. “Do you really want that? You think Feo’thari would want that? Destroy parts of the world she fought and bled for so long ago? Do you like the idea of a vengeful goddess killing plenty of innocent people in the process? Is that why you’re helping Karlin, despite your personal reservations?”

Missy’s silence said more than words ever could. “I’m putting a stop to Karlin, and you can choose to do the right thing and help me, Missy. I can’t give you back your past. I can’t bring you back to Earth, back to your mom and dad. I don’t even know if there’s an Earth to return to, but I hope that my last stand moved the needle somewhere in the cosmic universe. What I can do is give you a chance at a better present and a bright view of the future.”

Fiona edged the door open, glancing out. “I need to know the rest of Karlin’s plan before I bring down Hammertime. And also, why would you go to his father, of all people, for help?”

“Because he’s hurting. He’s estranged from both his sons, and his wife. I just...was hoping to maybe get them together. Just not like this."

Called it. “So it's in our best interest to heal this rift for good, because Karlin’s brother is on his way. And as a backup? Help me find several tons of magical thermite and turn it harmless.”

Missy stared at her. “You are crazy.” She grasped her hands in the air as if strangling someone and let out a frustrated growl. “Fair warning, if you don’t beat the hell out of Karlin? I just might first. I think he might have the answers we need on his datapad. And how is Douglas getting inside?”

“My rogue of a bird will take care of that. Think you can swipe his datapad?”

Missy thrust out a hand, looking confident. “Doubt I could stop you if you really wanted to charge in like a manic elven war machine.”

Fiona grinned as she shook hands. “Great. We’ve got less than twenty minutes. Let’s get moving.”

Missy peeked out the door and waved her over. The dragons were arguing about bad choices, wasted potential and–did Norbert actually throw the claw down and say that Doug was the better son?

Missy waved her over to the door. “You know the best way to get a dragon’s attention? Treasure and history. And pride,” she added with a smirk, before thrusting the door open.
