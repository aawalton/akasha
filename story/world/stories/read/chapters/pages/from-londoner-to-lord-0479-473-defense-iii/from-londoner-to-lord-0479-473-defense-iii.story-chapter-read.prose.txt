The howl was long and deep, and far too close.

Every conversation in the yard stopped.

Then another adzee’s howl answered from somewhere beyond the wall.

The guards who had been sitting with bowls in their hands rose at once. Some set their food down. Others quickly scooped it all in their mouths, and grabbed their weapons as they stood up. Shields came up from the ground. Spears were pulled from walls. A few of the newer guards looked around in nervousness, unsure where to go.

Hudan’s voice cut through the first few moments of confusion.

“Northeast gate group, with me! Southwest gate, to Levalas! Crossbow teams, with Feroy! New recruits stay in pairs and follow the guard assigned to you!”

Kerel was already shouting from the other side of the yard, leading his group toward the manor gate. The half-burnt instructor Nesser grabbed one recruit by the shoulder and shoved him toward the right group before barking at two others to pick up their shields properly. Feroy pointed with two fingers, sending crossbowmen into teams of three and four, each group moving toward the place they had been assigned earlier.

For a few moments it looked chaotic. Too many guards, too many weapons, too many orders at once.

Then slowly, the training settled over them.

Men and women formed into small groups. Spears lined up. Shields were checked. Crossbows were loaded carefully. Guards began running out through the manor gate in steady jogs, each group heading toward its post.

Kivamus watched them, feeling the tightness in his chest ease just a little.

A year ago, some of these same guards had not even thought it necessary to remain at their posts for a full watch, if they got hungry or were bored. Now they moved immediately under orders, tired and afraid, but disciplined. Hudan and Nesser had done well. Kerel too. Hopefully it would be enough for tonight.

Beside him, Duvas raised his head to the sky and whispered a prayer to the Goddess for the village’s safety.

Another howl rose beyond the walls, and then a horn was blown from the southwest.

This time, the whole manor heard it clearly.

Hudan ran toward the gate with a group of guards behind him. Levalas and his group exited after that, while Feroy took another group toward the south. Kerel stayed near the manor gate long enough to make sure the last of the assigned guards were moving, then turned to secure the manor itself.

Kivamus stood in the middle of the brazier-lit yard, watching the guards go out to meet whatever waited in the dark. He wanted to go with them, to do something more useful than stand behind the manor wall, but he knew that was not his task tonight. His task was to stay here, keep his face confident, and make sure everyone who remained behind did not lose their nerve.

For now, he had to trust Hudan, Feroy, Levalas, Kerel and the rest of the guards to keep Tiranat safe tonight.

~ Hyola ~

Hyola stood on the northeastern watchtower and tried not to grin too much.

It was already dark, and the night was still hot enough that sweat kept gathering under the collar of her padded armor. The worst of the day’s heat had passed, but summer had settled over Tiranat properly now. At least up here on the tower, there was some wind. Not much, but enough to make it better than standing on the ground near the gate with the men and their shields.

The fire Feroy had ordered lit outside the walls burned around 50 meters away from the tower, throwing light over the open ground near the palisade. It did not reach all the way to the forest, but it made the ground close to the wall clear enough to see. That was where the adzees would have to come if they wanted to attack.

And if they did, Hyola was ready.

The scorpion sat mounted on the outer edge of the watchtower platform, facing the open ground beyond the palisade. It looked like a crossbow made for a giant, with a heavy frame, thick arms, steel fittings, and a groove long enough for a bolt nearly three feet long. She loved her crossbow, but this was different. With this thing, she did not need to get close to an enemy. She did not even need to be on the ground. She could stand up here, turn the sights toward whatever beast or bastard thought it could threaten Tiranat, and shoot.

It was beautiful.

She had first seen the scorpion yesterday, when Lord Kivamus and the captain had come up to this watchtower for another round of testing. At first she had only stared at it, wondering if it was really as dangerous as everyone said. Then Darora had shown them how to load it, how to turn the side gears, how to set the bolt, and how to use the sights.

Her first shot had gone way too wide.

That had annoyed her enough that she fixed the mistake immediately, after asking about what she was doing wrong.

After that, she had not missed a single shot. Not one. The captain had looked surprised. Lord Kivamus had looked pleased. Hyola had spent the rest of the evening pretending it was something ordinary for her, even though she had wanted to grin until her face hurt.

She could practice with her crossbow whenever she wanted in the manor training ground. The scorpion was different. It was too powerful to shoot carelessly, and villagers worked outside the walls most days. They also had to keep its details hidden from outsiders for as long as possible. So she had barely gotten to use the scorpion.

Now, within a day, she might get another chance.

Let the beasts come close if they dared.

She would show them what it meant to threaten her home.

There were two other female guards on the tower with her. Only one of them had a crossbow, because many of the others had been handed to the guards on the ground for tonight. So Hyola had given her own crossbow to the second guard, then taken the scorpion for herself. For tonight anyway. The other two women had also been shown how to use it and had practiced with it, but the captain had placed Hyola in charge of shooting tonight because she had the best aim. By far.

One female guard would keep watch for other threats. The other would help her reload when needed. Hyola would handle the targeting. At least, that was the plan.

There were dozens of scorpion bolts in the storage compartment on the side of the platform. Iron-tipped bolts, mostly.

Below the tower, several male guards waited near the gate with spears and shields ready. Their armor and spearheads caught the light from a nearby lantern now and then as they shifted. Most of the other guards were still back at the manor though, likely finishing their early dinner before anything began. Hyola hoped they ate fast.

Not that she needed them up here.

She had the scorpion!

Still, she knew better than to take the night lightly. The forest tree line was around 500 meters away, near the farthest distance the scorpion could reach. Hitting anything that far away in the dark would be mostly luck. The fire lit the ground near the wall, but beyond that everything faded into darkness in the faint moonlight. If something moved in the trees, she might only see a vague shape.

She leaned slightly into the scorpion’s sights and imagined a beast standing near the fire. Then she visualized the bolt striking it through the neck, or chest, or head if the Goddess felt generous.

That made her grin again.

“Are you actually enjoying this?” the older of the two women asked quietly.

Hyola glanced at her and shrugged. “I am.”

The woman stared at her for a moment, then shook her head. “Of course you are.”

The younger guard said nothing. She was new, recruited only recently, and she had been looking between the fire, the wall, and the forest ever since taking her place on the tower. Her hands kept shifting on her weapon. She was trying to hide her fear and failing.

Hyola noticed but did not say anything yet.

Instead, she let herself think briefly of Calubo. He would have laughed if he saw her standing here with the scorpion like it belonged to her personally.

They had tried to arrange their watch duties together when they could, just so they would have more free time at the same hours. Since the market square had started opening up again, they had gone there a few times together. Nothing fancy. A meat bun. Fresh bread. A walk past the little stalls. Sometimes they stopped near Juhibi’s makeshift clothing stall and looked at the ready-made clothes brought from Kirnos.

Hyola had not bought anything yet.

Not because she did not want to.

She still remembered what it felt like to look at things and know she could never have them. Before Tiranat, before Lord Kivamus, before the guards came to the limestone quarry, she had lived as a slave long enough that most of her childhood was only bits and pieces in her mind. She remembered hunger. She remembered the cold. She remembered never having coin. At the quarry, she had worked, eaten what she was given, and slept in huts that barely kept her from freezing in the winters.

Then she had come to Tiranat.

At first, it had meant food, shelter, and a place in the upper floor of the servants’ hall. That alone had been more than she’d had before. But there had still been no coin in her hand. No real choice about what to buy. No small thing she could point at and decide, I want that.

But now the manor paid wages.

Now she could walk through the market square with Calubo and think about buying something because she liked it, not because someone allowed it.

And then that day had happened. Calubo had proposed to her in the alehouse, in front of other guards, like an idiot who somehow still managed to make her happier than anyone else ever had.

She smiled despite herself.

Since then, they had spent whatever time they could together. He would probably be sent to the massive city of Ulriga with Pydaso when the merchant returned, and she hated that, but it was fine. He would come back. She knew he would. And he would tell her all kinds of stories about the things he had seen there. One day she hoped she would get to visit that city along with him. Until then, she would take whatever time they got.

A faint movement near the tree line made her smile vanish.

Hyola straightened.

For a few moments she stared into the dark beyond the firelight.

Nothing.

Or maybe not nothing.

The forest was too far away to see clearly. The fire burned close to the wall, and its light did not reach the trees properly. But she thought she had seen something shift there. Something large.

“Be ready,” she said quietly.

Both women turned at once.

“What is it?” the older guard whispered.

“I thought I saw movement near the tree line.”

The younger guard’s face tightened. Her eyes went wide, and she stepped closer to the parapet as if that would help her see better. Hyola saw the fear clearly now.

For one irritated moment, she wondered why someone so new had been posted up here tonight. Then she pushed that thought away. It did not matter. One day, Hyola had been new too.

Well, perhaps not exactly like her.

Hyola had always been ready to stab anyone who even looked at her wrong.

But she had still been one of the first women to join the manor guards. She remembered the doubts, the uncertain looks, and the way some men had expected them to quit after a few hard days. That day had never come. And the captain had said more than once that the female guards with experience, like Hyola, Isomi, and Savomi, would have to help steady the newer ones.

So she looked at the younger guard and kept her voice firm.

“Hey, it’ll be okay. We’re on the watchtower. We’re safe up here. Even if an adzee somehow breaks the gate and gets inside, it is not climbing up here to eat us. And I don't think those gates were built to break so easily.”

The younger guard swallowed and nodded.

Hyola pointed toward the wall below. “But keeping ourselves safe is not enough. It’s our job to keep the village safe, and the other guards alive. So we do not let the beasts come close if we can stop them.”

The younger woman nodded again, this time a little steadier.

Then a horn sounded from the southwest.

All three of them froze for a heartbeat.

Hyola turned sharply toward the sound.

The horn meant a confirmed sighting. An adzee had been seen on that side.

So the pack really was near the village tonight.
