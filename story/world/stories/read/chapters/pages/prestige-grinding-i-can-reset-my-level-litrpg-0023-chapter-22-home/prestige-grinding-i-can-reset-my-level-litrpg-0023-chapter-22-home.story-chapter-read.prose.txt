In their perfected form, the effects increased twofold, and new options appeared.

‘Speed Surge is now benefiting from reduced air resistance, and other kinds of pressures I’ll be under. The effect is amazing.’ Sora moved around to test his skill, and enjoyed the reduced pressure.

[Mana Shield] and [Amplify] also benefited from the upgrade. The first skill was continuously fed mana through his surroundings, without Sora doing anything for that. Even the number of layers he could cast increased from the basic five to twenty. The second skill changed in nature, as Sora could cast it on multiple targets without any penalty.

‘I can use the same amount of mana to help others cast their skills. It’s like paying once and having a few times the reward.’ He smiled as he felt the new effects were related to his [Mana Evolution] supreme skill.

Each skill was listed as perfect, and Sora was more than happy with the result. These three perfected skills were much better than expected.

'Let's start running.'

It was late, and he had spent a day without sleep but he could still keep going. Hiyoko needed rest, so Sora transferred the little bird to the [Familiar Bubble] he had bought in Barcelona.

The bubble was a protective space that allowed familiars to rest in comfort, isolated from the outside world. From the outside, Sora could see Hiyoko sleeping soundly on a small bed inside the transparent bubble, oblivious to the journey. If he wanted, Sora could make the bubble opaque, hiding his familiar from onlookers. The bubble floated above his head, bobbing slightly in the wind as Sora activated [Speed Surge] and sprinted towards France.

He quickly passed the border, the Pyrenees mountain range fading behind him as he looked back at the land that had welcomed him for a month.

"Goodbye, Spain! Thank you for everything…" he muttered under his breath as he started running again.

As he sprinted toward his home, he activated his monster radar from time to time, ensuring he always knew where monsters hid. From zone to zone, he would often stop to kill monsters.

His new class, [Martial Titan], leveled up rapidly, but he ignored the new skills for now. There would be time for that later. By the time he reached the vicinity of his city, it had been 1 and a half days since he left Barcelona. He was already level 38, much stronger than when he had been just a day earlier as a level 24 player.

He knew the main reason for his progress was the combined use of his perfect skills, and the ability it gave him : he could kill monsters that were supposed to be unkillable for his level.

His new class, [Martial Titan], had already reached his own level, but unlike other players who would be excited with every new skill, Sora hadn't even opened a single skill description.

Standing outside the small city of Ales, Sora pondered whether to gain more power before entering. But then, he realized—he wasn't here to fight. He was here to see his parents. And besides, no one from his past would recognize him now. Even if someone who disliked him crossed paths with him, how could they know who he was?

The real challenge would be staying calm if he encountered Rachel or one of her accomplices. Who knew how much stronger she had become in the past two months? She wasn't even level 50 when they last met. She couldn't have improved that much, right?

"It doesn't matter," Sora thought. "I'll ignore her for now. Our time will come."

He decided to enter the city and observe his old home first. Heading to the living area on the city's outskirts, Sora found a bench close from his parents' house. Activating [Mana Eyes], he peered inside. The house was empty.

‘What happened? Why is no one home?’ He thought, feeling restless. ‘Did they move? Are they working?’

Without anything better to do, he had to wait.

Twenty minutes passed but to Sora, it felt like an eternity. Eventually, he saw two figures at the end of the street. He recognized them before he even entered their eyesight. However, what he saw didn’t please him at all. His heart started aching.

His parents were walking home, but they had changed. His father looked twenty years older, and his mother was supporting him as they walked. She tried her best to cheer him up, but the pain in her eyes was unmistakable. Sora felt a lump in his throat, his eyes moistening. He wanted to run up to them, tell them he was back, that everything would be okay—but he couldn't. Not like this. He wasn't Sora Hashino anymore.

‘They had changed so much in just two months.’ he thought, before looking down to find Hiyoko climbing on his chest and stopping on his shoulder to hug him.

He patted her head before he observed his parents closely, noting their levels.

‘They are still level 9, the same as when I left. Thank god.’ He sighed in relief.

At least Rachel hadn't targeted them. Not yet.

Sora deactivated [Mana Eyes] and waited, watching as his parents entered their home. After calming himself for another hour, he finally stood up.

‘I’ll do it. I don’t care about.’ Having decided something he might regret in the future, he walked toward the door and knocked.

After knocking, he waited, his heart pounding in his chest. His mind raced, filled with questions about how to approach this. What would he even say? He was alive—yes, but he wasn't the same person they once knew. He had changed, inside and out, and his journey had taken him far from who he once was.

A few moments later, the door creaked open, revealing his mother. Her eyes were tired, carrying the weight of the last two months, but she smiled gently, more out of habit than true happiness.

"Good evening," she greeted, her voice soft and polite.

Sora hesitated, suddenly unsure. His throat was dry. "Hi… I'm…" He trailed off, his own words catching him off guard. The usual confidence he had built over these weeks seemed to vanish in front of her, this woman who had raised him, cared for him.

‘Should I leave?’ He wondered, scared.

"Are you here to see someone?" she asked, noticing the awkward pause, her eyes scanning him as if trying to place his face. Despite her facing a stranger, she smiled gently when she saw the doubt in his eyes.

Sora took a deep breath, deciding to rip off the bandage. "It's me, Mom. It's Sora."

Her smile faltered, confusion flashing in her eyes. "Sora? What are you—?" She shook her head, dismissing the idea immediately, as if the notion was too painful to entertain. "That's impossible. My Sora is dead. Please, don't joke about something like this."

His heart squeezed painfully in his chest. He had expected this, but hearing the words still hurt more than he anticipated. When he saw the pain in her eyes, he knew he had to convince her before she was hurt.

"I'm not dead, Mom," he said, stepping forward slowly. "It's really me. I know I look different, and I know it's hard to believe, but…" He trailed off again, uncertain how to convince her.

Tears welled up in her eyes, and her hand gripped the doorframe tightly. "This isn't funny," she whispered, her voice breaking. "If this is some kind of cruel joke, please stop. We've already lost too much."

Sora clenched his fists, before stopping the door that was about to close. He couldn't stand seeing his mother so fragile, so worn down. "It's me, I swear. I can prove it." He racked his brain for something only he would know, something she couldn't deny. "When I was little, I used to call you maman étoile because I thought you were like the stars in the sky. You were always watching over me. No one else knows that."

Her eyes widened, her breath hitching as the familiar words washed over her. She hadn't heard that name in years.  As he continued, giving more and more details, the light in her eyes slowly returned. As he spoke, disbelief gave way to hope.

"S-Sora?" After a while, she stopped him, her voice barely audible. "How… how is this possible?"

Before he could respond, his father appeared behind her, frowning at the conversation. "What's going on here?" he asked, his voice gruff but weakened by age. "Who is this?"

Sora turned to face him. "Dad… it's me. I'm back."

His father's eyes narrowed, taking in the sight of the young man standing before him. He looked so different, yet there was something familiar in his posture, the way he stood.

"You look nothing like him," his father said, his voice harsh. "You sound nothing like him either."

His mother was the first to react, stepping aside to let him in. "Come inside," she whispered, still shaking, her hands trembling. "We need to… talk."

Sora followed them inside, his steps uneven. The living room was the same as he remembered. It was familiar, comforting, yet overshadowed by the sorrow that had filled it in his absence.

Sora sat quietly, staring at his parents. He could feel the tension in the room, the weight of disbelief hanging heavy in the air. His mother's hand still rested on his arm, as though she feared letting go would make him vanish.

Just like he convinced his mother, he quickly explained to his father who, though trying to hold back emotions, was clearly on the edge of breaking.

"How?" she finally asked, her voice small. "How are you here?"

He knew he couldn't keep this hidden from them. They deserved to know the truth. Everything.

"There's something you need to know," Sora said, his voice soft but steady. He locked eyes with his parents. "Something I haven't told anyone."

His mother's grip on his arm tightened slightly, her brows furrowing in concern. "What is it?"

Sora took a deep breath, preparing himself for their reactions. "I didn't just survive by chance. I… have an ability that saved me. Every time I die, I can reset. Start over from the beginning. I lose many things, but I come back stronger each time. That's how I'm still here."

His father blinked, his frown deepening. "Reset? What are you talking about?"

Sora hesitated, then continued. "When I die, I start from the beginning. My body and appearance can change. I can even take on a new identity. That's why I look different now. It's still me… but I've been through this process."

The silence was loud. His mother stared at him, confused. "Sora, I don't understand… you've… died and came back? That's why you look like this?"

He nodded slowly. "Yes. I can start over. It's part of this new reality. It's not just me—i'm sure there are others with incredible abilities."

His father leaned forward, his expression hard to read. "And why didn't you come back earlier? If you can reset… why now? Why not when you first… disappeared?"

Sora felt his father’s anger. "I couldn't." He paused, gathering his thoughts. "The first month after I died, everything changed, I wasn't reborn yet. I had to wait for a month before coming back and the place i appeared in was extremely far from here … On top of that, I was afraid of coming back as … myself.  My killer could kill me again. "

"Your killer?" His father was shocked at those words. “You weren’t killed by monsters?”

"Yes, someone killed me. It was Rachel. And I didn't want to meet her at my weakest", his eyes turned red as he explained.

Both his parents froze at the mention of Rachel's name. The room grew even quieter. His father's fists clenched.

"Rachel?" his mother whispered, her voice barely audible. "Rachel… killed you? But she said you died at the hands of monsters…"

Sora nodded, eyes dark. "She lied. She betrayed me and killed all my friends. Even worse, she told me she killed Nathan."

His father stood up, furious. "You’re saying Nathan didn’t die from an accident?!"

"No," Sora interrupted gently but firmly. "It wasn't an accident. Rachel killed Nathan. She confessed it to me right before she killed me. That's why I've been gone. I didn't come back because I was dead."

His mother covered her mouth, her eyes wide in disbelief. She shook her head as if trying to process everything. "I can't… believe this. Rachel… how could she…"
