The plan was good. Simple, but good—as the best plans often were in Eola’s experience.

By the time Eola was ready to fight the Winterstalk, Colin had convinced the villagers of Shimmer Grove to loan them three goats—he’d had to promise to pay twice their value if any got eaten—and to bring their herds inside for the night. Then they’d set off, with Colin guiding a recalcitrant-sounding Belanna and Roth driving the bleating goats out into the chilly valley. He kept looking back at the jagged mountain and the snow-choked couloir, a worried expression on his face.

Eola was sure she wore a matching one. The plan was a good one, but it put most of the responsibility squarely on her.

They set up in a snow-covered meadow a mile from the village. It was almost perfect for their ambush, and after Colin and Roth holed up in a copse of firs and she found a boulder to lurk behind, the three goats’ bleating made for perfect bait.

Then, there was nothing to do but wait—and they didn’t have to wait long. Colin had picked up on the Winterstalk’s presence even before they’d left the village, and it wasted no time in going for the goats. Before it could, Eola burst out from behind the freezing rock, smallsword in a high guard, and sprinted toward the monster.

And what a monster it was.

True to the description, it was as long as any of the village’s houses, but moved low to the ground on so many legs it reminded Eola uncomfortably of millipede season in Greenarbor—the two weeks a year when there were dozens in the streets and the town took on a vaguely…unpleasant, for lack of a better word…odor. Its fur so perfectly matched the snow that she could only keep track of it by its swift movement and churning limbs. A half-dozen eyes in a long, thin line and a similar line for a wide jaw broke its head into almost even thirds. If it weren’t for the eyes, it would have looked a lot like Nera the weasel, albeit with far too many legs.

The smell was overwhelming, though. Nera had a similar scent, but unlike the yellow familiar, the Winterstalk’s musk overwhelmed even the goats’ terrified stink. The sweet undertone made Eola’s eyes water, the tears freezing to her cheeks.

But the plan had to go on.

“Yah!” Eola shouted, feeling incredibly stupid that she couldn’t think of anything better to yell. Her sword flicked out to cut across the monster’s flank.

The Winterstalk stopped and roared as it circled back, jaw coming around to face Eola.

The goats, predictably, scattered. Fine. That was fine. It wasn’t Eola’s problem; Roth was on goat-wrangling duty. All she had to do was keep the monster occupied while Colin—

Colin stepped out from behind a tree and started sketching his flame ritual.

—did that. Eola thrust again as the Winterstalk’s maw hammered toward her. Her feet danced back across the snow, and she spun to the right. Its momentum carried it past her, legs churning the snow into slush. It splashed across her cloak and breastplate, half-frozen water trickling down her front.

She shivered and thrust again, even as her wand hand started sketching the rune-chain for Spark Shot. It took too many bars of Mana to cast, but it’d be effective against the Winterstalk. Her smallsword hadn’t done more than enrage the beast.

She needed more than that.

Then a passing leg hooked her ankle, and down she went.

The rune-chain fizzled out as her dad’s wand slipped out of her fingers. Eola reached for it, fingers scrabbling in the snow, and took a half-dozen kicks to the stomach and chest. Her breastplate dulled the worst of it, but Y’aer, it still hurt! Worse, the impact drove the air from her lungs, and she sucked in a new breath reflexively—a breath full of snow. Freezing water filled her mouth. She coughed, but no air came out.

And as she did, the Winterstalk came around again. She saw it coming, forced herself to her feet even as her lungs screamed for air, and tried to block it. She might as well have tried to block a mountain, though.

The Winterstalk’s jaws closed around Eola’s left leg and ripped her off her feet again. Pain ripped up her thigh from her knee as it twisted and teeth punched into muscle and sinew. She slammed back down into the snow, screaming; the monster dragged her as she cut awkwardly at it, trying to get it to—

Heat.

An incredible amount of heat, all at once.

Colin’s ritual went off. The Winterstalk roared in agony, and Eola rolled as it let go of her leg. Shards of its teeth stayed trapped in her skin, and slush splashed through the gashes its fangs had torn in her woolen pants. Y’aer, it was cold! It seeped in through the gaps in her cloak and coat, running down her chest and back in rivulets. She forced herself to her feet—her left leg shook with every step—and jammed her sword into the wounded monster’s neck, right below its chin.

Then she leaned on the blade and let her weight sink it into the beast.

It kicked with dozens of legs.

Then it went still.

“Y’aer’s curse, that was close,” Colin said as he hurried across the slush-filled field. “I picked up a few skills in my Siege Mage course that fired more quickly, but—uh, Eola? You alright?”

Eola didn’t respond. Instead, she dropped to one knee, then to her hands, as her body shivered in her cold, wet cloak and coat.

By the time Roth came back with the three goats and looped their leads around a nearby tree, Eola couldn’t stop shaking. He took one look at her, frowned—a long, deep crease forming in his brow—and said, “Hypothermia. That’s bad luck.”

“We should head back to Shimmer Grove,” Colin said. Eola looked up hopefully.

“No time. It’s a good hour away the way she’d walk. With the storm rolling in, it’d be a death sentence. And your fire magic won’t do trogs droppings to help. We’ll have to warm her up carefully.”

“So what do we do with her?” Colin asked.

Eola didn’t much like being talked about like she’d talk about a sack of potatoes. She tried to stand back up, but only made it to her hands and right knee—her left leg didn’t seem to want to take her weight.

“Y’aer curse us,” Roth muttered, and he got to work setting up the tent.

As he did, Colin pulled his mittens off, stripped Eola’s gloves from her fingers, and shoved them into his own warm ones. Then he paused, hesitating. “I’m going to have to…”

“Do it,” Eola said. She tried not to blush as Colin worked the clasp on her sodden cloak, then undid the buttons on her coat and slid it off her shoulders. The wind ripped through her woolen shirt as Colin worked her breastplate’s straps, and when it fell free, she shivered violently until he wrapped his arms around her. He was also wet, but strangely, didn’t feel anywhere near as cold as her warm clothes had.

Come to think of it, she’d probably never been this cold in her life.

Something was very, very wrong here. Eola had been cold before. Greenarbor wasn’t exactly one of the big cities to the south, and it had rough winter storms that blew in from the sea. And then there’d been the snowball fight last term. She’d been soaked through after that battle in the academy’s hedge maze, and so had her friends. Granted, the wind was howling, and night was close, but even so…even so.

“I think it’s the Winterstalk. Something broke off in the wound,” she mumbled through teeth that wouldn’t stop chattering. Roth looked at her, eyes narrowing, and pushed her gently toward the tent.

Somehow, Eola wasn’t quite sure how, she ended up on her back, her head in Colin’s lap and Atta lying on her chest. Oh, how she wanted to crawl into her bedroll and go to sleep, but with the three of them and their familiars, there wasn’t really room to move. Even if she’d been able to escape from Colin, she couldn’t possibly have gotten settled without kicking Roth in the face.

He pulled a small penknife from his belt, unfolded it, and started cutting at her pants, high on her left thigh. She stiffened, blood rushing to her face, and he stopped. “We need to get a good look at the wound before Colin casts his spell. If that thing’s teeth are in there, the last thing you want is for the scab to heal over them.”

Eola nodded slowly, still unsure.

“For what it’s worth, I have no interest in what’s under your clothes.” Roth went to work, slicing through the wool in small, inch-long cuts. The blade’s icy tip scrabbled across Eola’s leg. She shivered again, but the angry-eyed boy worked smoothly and carefully, and before long, the wound was fully exposed.

Roth sucked in a gasp, going pale.

“It’s that bad?” Eola asked.

“This is going to hurt. A lot. And it’s going to leave a scar, even with Colin’s ritual. You might as well start casting that now.”

As Eola watched, Colin started to sketch out the rune for his First Order healing ritual. It was always mesmerizing watching him work; he was so incredibly precise, so methodical, that it was almost trance-like. But tonight, Roth didn’t let her sit back and watch. He flicked her calf a few inches below the lowest puncture wound, then grimaced. “Yeah, this is going to hurt. I’m so sorry. Now, listen carefully, Eola.

He put a finger and his thumb into the rough cut the Winterstalk had ripped into Eola’s thigh, and she bit back a scream. Roth nodded in sympathy. “My dad always said it was okay to scream as long as I didn’t cry when he did this for me. That’s trog’s dung, though. Go ahead and cry as much as you want. It hurts. The village I’m from is called Norwall. It’s right up where the Spine Mountains spread out, just below Mount Blue Point. Mostly humans there. The gnomes don’t do well in snow that deep, and the elves…well, they stay away.

“Anyway, it’s an ugly place, with ugly people. You have to be tough to survive in Norwall, and there’s not much room for pretty things. The only exception was Nathien. Nathien Sleetbud. He had eyes the color of fresh snow, and fingers thin and quick enough to make mine look clumsy, but a worse sense of rhythm and melody than an ox.” Roth smiled. As he did, he yanked on something, and Eola’s eyes filled with tears. “One out, a dozen or so to go.”

“Y’aer, help me.”

“Y’aer isn’t going to help you here. That’s something my dad used to say, too. Anyway, Nathien loved my music, the blessed fool. He and I grew…close.”

“What do you…Oh,” Eola said, mouth closing as she figured it out.

“Yes. I prefer men. Shut up and listen,” Roth snapped. “Nathien wasn’t a boyfriend or a fling, no matter what my dad thought. We were closer than that—closer than what you’ve got with Colin. Have the two of you even had a proper kiss yet?” Colin shook his head, and Roth nodded. “Didn’t think so. Nathien and I…I loved him like I’ve never loved anyone before or since. He made that flute for me. It was all I had left of…

“I’m getting distracted. The point is that Norwall’s a hard place to live, and Goethe was a hard dad to have when I couldn’t meet his expectations. He wanted a tough boy—a knife fighter and a hunter. He didn’t want a bard. And the bigger point is that when we’re finished here, and I’m using my body heat to warm you up, you don’t have to worry about anything.” Roth went quiet, then snorted. “Well, almost anything. You have a spare set of trousers, right?”

Eola nodded, then screamed a little as Roth rooted around in the next puncture wound up on her leg. Colin’s ritual activated, filling the tent with a soft emerald light.

“Good. Now, keep listening. I’ve got stories to tell, and you can’t go passing out while I’m in a telling mood, okay?”

She wasn’t sure, but Eola could’ve sworn she heard something catch in his voice.

For the next…Eola had no idea how long. It could have been ten minutes, or it could have been hours. Either way, all Eola could do was struggle feebly against Colin’s embrace while Roth’s fingers dug through the jagged, torn lacerations in her calf and thigh. Every minute or so, he’d stop, working something out of the wound, and Eola couldn’t always stop herself from screaming. It was nothing like getting healed in Varin’s Academy’s infirmary. And the whole time, Roth kept talking, talking, talking about the adventures he’d had in Norwall with Nathien Sleetbud.

This was, as Roth muttered every so often, frontier healing—and frontier healing was rough, painful, and left scars. Eola remembered the scars covering Roth’s body, but couldn’t focus enough to ask about them. Not that he would have answered. He was busy.

Eola woke up too warm, sandwiched between Colin—who’d pressed up behind her and wrapped her in his arms—and Roth’s back.

She started to struggle free, only to stop as Colin moved first. “You’re awake. That’s good. Roth said you’d be out until midmorning.”

“Did he?” Eola felt…not great. Her head pounded, and she reached for a waterskin and gently sat up to pour some into her mouth. The tent was too small for much more, but Roth had been right; they’d stayed cozy and warm inside of it.

“Yeah. He worked on your leg until you passed out, then kept going.” Colin’s brow furrowed into a slight glare. “He was right. You weren’t going to make it to Shimmer Grove. But he didn’t have to be so rough about it.”

Eola paused. Then she undid the fasteners on her wool-and-down bag and gingerly pulled it off her leg.

They’d wrapped it in bandages from mid-thigh down to near her ankle, but even through that—and with Colin’s healing ritual—blood had still seeped out. Luckily, it looked like it had dried sometime in the night. She went to poke at it.

“I wouldn’t. We’ll be here a few more hours to let you heal,” Colin said as Roth snored. He reached to one side of the tent and handed over a small leather pouch. Inside were close to a dozen shattered teeth. Each was no more than a half-inch long and maybe a quarter as wide, with edges that were serrated in reverse.

Eola winced as she held one of the blue-white fangs in her palm. “It’s cold.”

“They all are. Winterstalk fangs. Roth pulled them out of your leg and rinsed away the blue pus that’d filled the punctures. He worked on you for most of the night. It was my idea to keep the fangs, though. They might be useful alchemically.”

Alchemically. Alchemically…

It hit Eola like a brick to the face. “The musk sac! I need it frozen for Ensorcellment.” She tried to push herself to her feet, only for Colin to push her down by the shoulder.

He nodded. “Roth took care of that, too. He’s been busy, but it’s all packed up and on Bellana.”

Roth had taken care of it. He’d not only remembered the musk sac from Eola’s list, but he’d gone out in the storm to cut it out of the Winterstalk’s throat, left it in the snow to freeze, and wrapped it up for her. That almost made up for the hours of torture she’d endured at his hands last night. She closed her eyes, relaxing, and fell asleep again.

When she woke up, the tent was mostly empty, and her change of clothes lay on its waxed cloth floor next to her bedroll. She gingerly dressed herself, pulled on her breastplate, and threw her coat and cloak on—someone had dried them out while she slept, probably with Wring Wetness and a fire cantrip.

The whole way back to Shimmer Grove, then to Varin’s Academy, she couldn’t help but think about Roth, though. The dozens of cuts along his chest and arms. Every one of them had healed without magic—and, according to him, every one of them had been treated by his father.

But he hadn’t explained where they’d all come from, and Eola knew he wouldn’t even if she asked. He’d already told them more about himself last night than he’d shared in all the months they’d known him, and while the southern cities—and even towns like Varin’s Town or Greenarbor—had no problems with men loving men or women loving women, he hadn’t seemed like his father had been particularly happy with him. It’d been a hard thing for him to talk about, either way.

Roth was a mystery. Prickly, angry, and short-tempered, he’d pushed himself almost to death’s door during the Library Exam, then done everything he could to save Eola’s life last night. She didn’t understand him, but she was glad he was her friend.
