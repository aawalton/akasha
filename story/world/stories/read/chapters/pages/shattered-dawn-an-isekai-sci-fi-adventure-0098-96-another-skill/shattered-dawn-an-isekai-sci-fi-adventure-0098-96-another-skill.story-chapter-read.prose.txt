Unlike the other Aurelian altars Elion had seen, this one was immaculate. Intricately carved and expertly painted, the tall statue of Aurelia looked lifelike. Out of the corner of his eye, she seemed to move.

A basin full of clear water rested on a pedestal before the Sentinel. Zev knelt at the altar, head bowed.

Elion produced the bag of ascendency stones that Zev had given to him. He poured them out into his hand, counting the shimmering gems. Most of them were Aurelian tears, but there were also Artefin skillstones, Heranan seeds, Zelian shards, and others that Elion did not recognize.

In total there were 38 of them.

“A wealth of stones,” Yaret murmured, gazing at what Elion held in his hands. “The advantage of being a Starholder, I guess.” A hint of bitterness colored his tone.

“I know they are valuable,” Elion noted, holding them delicately. He lifted one and held it up, allowing it to sparkle in the light.

“A man’s wages for a year might be enough to buy one of these stones,” Yaret said. “Even in my service to an Aurelian knight, I’ve never seen so many of them in one place.”

Elion glanced at his uncle, who still knelt at the altar.

“Yaret,” he whispered. “You wish to remain my squire? We go into great danger. You could leave us now, and I wouldn’t blame you.”

Yaret looked hurt. “I am committed to serve,” he said. “Since Aurelia pulled me from my darkest moment, I have never wavered from this path.”

“I do not doubt you, Yaret. I’m not trying to get rid of you. I want you with us.”

Yaret nodded, smiling.

“As your knight, I’m supposed to care for your needs, as you see to mine?”

“A bit of food, some water,” Yaret said with a shrug. “Nothing you wouldn’t need yourself.”

“I also want you to live for more,” Elion said. “You have had a difficult life.” He sighed. “I am afraid that I might not life through this. I don’t want that to ruin you again, like the last time your knight died.”

Yaret stared at the ground. Elion selected five Aurelian tears from his collection and pressed them into Yaret’s hand. He closed Yaret’s fingers around the gift.

Slowly, Yaret uncurled his fingers to stare at the gems. “You need these more than I do,” he said.

Elion shook his head. “Levels matter more than experience. These five won’t get me another level. And I may never get a chance to level up again. Take them. Use them to build something, in the future.”

“I won’t outlive you,” Yaret said, trying to give the gems back. “I’ll protect your life, or die trying.”

“Please, Yaret,” Elion said. “Just take them. Think of it as holding on to them for me for later if you have to.” He pushed Yaret’s hand away.

The man hesitated, then slipped the gems into a pouch. A single tear ran down his cheek.

“Go ahead, Elion,” Zev said, rising from his knee. “It’s time to level up.”

“This is a serious amount of experience,” Elion said, cupping the gems in his hand.

“It’s your birthright,” Zev said. “And you’re going to need it for the fight ahead.”

Elion placed the stones on the altar. The sun shone through the small heap, light shining through the facets. A rainbow of color surrounded the gems.

> << Offer Ascendency Stone to the Path of Dawn? >>

“Zev,” Elion said. “I have a question. About the oaths. I want to understand more before I take this next step.”

“What is it?”

“Does power come from the oaths? You said that Dorian’s Aurelian power was stripped from him by a council of the Path of Dawn. Why doesn’t Praxis simply take power from oathbreakers?”

“The power doesn’t come from the oaths. The ascendency paths were created by the sentinels, working with Praxis, as a means to ordering power. Praxis allows each order to police itself.”

“But… Who is really in charge then? Why couldn’t you strip Amburn of power. Or why couldn’t he strip yours? That doesn’t make sense to me.”

Zev sighed. “It’s Dorian, again,” he said. “The Golden Throne is the means by which men can engage with Praxis. Without it, our orders have fragmented.”

Aircraft engines rumbled in the distance.

“We don’t have much time here,” Elion,” Zev said. “I sense through Aurelia that your sister is trying to use the power of the throne. Probably under Dorian’s duress. If she succeeds, then Dorian will certainly win, destroying all the Praxian paths and making himself supreme. But I fear she will not be strong enough to handle it, and the throne will kill her.”

“I see,” Elion said. “The Path of Dawn is created by Aurelia, but empowered by Praxis?”

“Yes,” Zev said. “And overseen by a council of Knights. But without access to the throne, we are powerless to enforce our actions. Breaking oaths means nothing to Praxis.”

“What is Praxis?” Elion asked.

“That’s enough questioning,” Zev said. “We don’t have all day to talk about theory and history.”

Elion sighed heavily. He turned his attention back to the notification.

> << Offer Ascendency Stone to the Path of Dawn? >>

“Yes,” he said, instinctively closing his eyes.

The bright flare of rainbow light shone red through his eyelids for a moment.

He swelled with strength and power.

> << 3976 XP gained >>> << New level: 12 >>> << You have 5 ability points to assign >>> << 10th level reached >>> << New ability selection available >>> << Know The Truth >>> << Grants the ability to pierce illusions, uncover hidden dangers, and perceive concealed realities. >>> << Empower >>> << Send your own strength to strengthen an ally, enhancing their natural abilities. >>> << Protect Your People >>> << Over an area, redirect incoming assaults onto yourself. >>

He looked at the “Protect Your People” skill, and nearly selected it. He’d nearly chosen it the last time he leveled up. He desperately wanted the ability to protect his people. How many times in the last few weeks would that have been a valuable ability?

But before he made his selection, he decided to take advantage of something he’d never had before.

“Zev,” he said. “I’ve gained a new skill. What should I select? I’ve got Know the Truth, Empower, and Protect Your People. Which one is good?”

“What other skills do you have? Alandar’s Beacon?”

“Yes. And Save a Friend, and Manifest Armaments.”

“It seems like you’ve been leaning into a support build. So Empower would be the best one for that. Unless you prefer to tank, and then it could be Protect your People, but I’d lean away from that if I were you. There aren’t going to be a lot of people to protect going forward.”

“So you think Empower, then? What about Know the Truth?”

“I’ve got that already,” Zev said. “So you don’t need it.”

“So Empower, then?”

“It’s up to you,” Zev said. “Your build, your decision.”

Elion turned the options over in his mind. He trusted Zev. He could tell that the man wanted him to take Empower. That way, maybe he could just lend his strength to Zev, who was the better fighter already.

He selected Empower.

> << Would you like to improve an ability or respec? >>

“How should I spec out?”

“A good distribution, favoring Empower and Manifest Armaments,” Zev suggested. “You probably don’t need any in Alandar’s beacon. If we end up fighting tephalians, we’ve lost already and there won’t be any allies to call.”

That made sense, so Elion distributed the points accordingly.

> << Name: Elion James Walker >>> << House: Starhold >>> << Ascendency: Aurelian Path of Dawn >>> << Level/XP: 12/4151 >>> << Abilities (Level): Manifest Armaments (4), Save a Friend (3), Alandar’s Beacon (0), Empower (5) >>> << Boons: Translation >>> << Quests: None >>

Elion wondered if he’d put too much into Save a Friend. Winning mattered right now. If he didn’t have enough strength to win, then nobody would be saved.

Maybe he should have put more points into Empower.

But he’d already locked in the respec, and it was going to take a lot of experience to get him to the next level and another chance to change those decisions.

“Okay,” Elion said, wiping his brow. “I’m done.”

“How do you feel?” Zev asked, clapping Elion on the shoulder.

“I feel…” he windmilled his arms around, and flexed his muscles. He felt strong. Powerful. Ready for a fight. “I feel good,” he said.”

Then, in an instant, all power disappeared from him. He gasped, and fell to one knee. Zev clutched his chest. Yaret’s rifle fell to pieces.

Elion thought he was about to die.

The moment passed, and they all climbed to their feet.

Yaret cursed, dropping the scrambled pieces of his rifle to the ground.

> << Reconnecting to Praxis >>

> << Please stand by, temporary suppression detected >>

Elion rubbed his head.

“That was close,” Zev said. “It isn’t normally that strong.”

“Tephalians?” Elion asked. “Here?”

“They’re all over Erod,” Zev said. “Dorian can’t deal with them effectively, because he still insists that they’re a fiction, invented by our ancestors to oppress us. It should affect you less now that you’re at a higher level.”

He turned to look at Yaret, who was collecting the pieces of his rifle and stashing them in the parachute bag he still wore. The parachute was missing. He pulled some grey bricks from the bag as he arranged things.

“Keyla can probably fix it, when she gets here,” Yaret said. “She’s good with this stuff.”

“Where did you get those explosives?” Zev asked, indicating the grey bricks.

“At the wreck of the heli-jet,” Yaret said. “Part of it crashed down near me. I thought they might come in handy at some point.”

Zev cringed. “Just… Be careful not to set them off. Especially not when you’re near us.”

Yaret grinned. “I’ll be careful. I know what I’m doing.”

“Let’s go then,” Zev said as Yaret finished packing up his bag.

“Yessir,” Yaret replied. “The others were pinned down, not far from where the jet crashed. I think we should be able to reach them in under a half hour.”

“Why didn’t you say something sooner?” Elion exclaimed, helping Yaret to his feet.

“I got distracted by that guy,” Yaret said, pointing to Amburn’s body, lying where Elion had killed him.

“Lead the way,” Elion said. “Let’s go find them.”

“We’re not going back,” Zev said, a distant look in his eye. He faced east, toward the sun, which was now well above the horizon. “We have to push on.”

“What? But this was the meeting place. They need our help! We can go help them right now!”

“They’re not at the meeting place, and the time has passed. We can’t wait any longer. They’ll understand. They have two Ascended and Isalyn. They’ll be fine, as long as we can complete our mission.”

“No!” Elion said.

“No? That’s all you have to say? Don’t be a petulant child, Elion. We need to get on with things.”

“They’ll be useful. We need them,” he protested, but even as he spoke he felt the wisdom of Zev’s words. Liora was in danger. Dorian was trying to use her to unlock incredible power.

What good would it do to save his friends now, and lose the world?

“Fine,” he muttered, turning toward the sun. “Can we at least leave a message for them?”

Zev produced a small, portable recorder from his pocket. He used it to create a message, urging their friends to press on to the palace, and catch up with them there.

“If the Black Wall finds this… well, I suppose it’s not hard to guess our intentions. It won’t hurt,” Zev said, placing the recorder at the base of the altar.

With that, they jogged out of the park, pushing back into the wild streets of the once glorious city of Erod.
