I felt brazen, wild, and utterly fierce when I went to my bedroom.

Dinner had been a talkative affair. Renner and I had joined the others at a long table near a crackling hearth. The innkeeper served everyone bowls of stew and slightly stale bread topped by wedges of hard yellow cheese. The two Thalessians had spent most of the meal discussing what we’d seen in the barrow.

They had apparently found similar markings on the floor of a cave to the far east. Joselia said that about six months ago the port city of Saint’s Harbor had called out to the Order for aid. They’d had breathless roaming outside the city walls and rumors of people performing dark magic at night. Joselia had been one of the paladins sent to investigate, but by the time her troop reached the city there were no more breathless and any suspicious activity had died down.

The only strange thing- and a very strange thing it was, indeed- they’d found was a cavern beneath some cliffs. The cave had similar- though much smaller- markings etched deep into its stone floor, all of which were stained in dried blood. Joselia said the cave had been otherwise empty, save for the remnants of a few campfires. They’d investigated the area for several weeks, waiting for a resurgence in anything dangerous, but nothing else had happened. The scene remained a mystery.

Renner, Royce, and Teela had chimed in with questions and comments. I had sat quietly, wanting to participate in the conversation, but… well.

I found myself quite distracted.

Not just by Renner, though any glance in the man’s direction left me with a fluttering pulse and a helpless little smile. My gaze kept lingering, quite unbidden, on the set of his shoulders, on the thoughtful press of his lips and the way firelight made shadows dance across his face.

But it wasn’t just him that commanded my attention. It was her. My mom. The sheer, stunning reality of her existence.

I found myself wondering if she’d seen this. Me, sitting at a table in Sedgewick Vale, surrounded by warmth. Saints, if she’d had visions of Joselia walking along a roadside and of Renner as a young man… what else had she seen? What else had she done? What other things had she set in motion, deliberately or otherwise?

The questions spiraled and swam, too overwhelming and far too big for me to ever possibly answer. But, inevitably, they all circled back to one tangible thing; runes.

My magic. The defining feature of my life. One I’d been delightedly mystified by during my younger years. But now…

Now, I found myself craving answers.

My mother had seen me use each rune. She’d said as much in her letter. And even though the future was malleable, even though her visions could have changed at some point or gone off-track… she’d written each rune down knowing what they did, and wanting me to use them.

For most of my life, I hadn’t really questioned that fact. It simply was. I could make beautiful little lights, and of course that was amazing, but there was no need to question why such a thing was necessary. As for the magic to flavor food and create rainshowers… tricks. Fun, delightful things to brighten someone’s day or help a dying plant.

But now… now, the questions came.

Veth was dangerous. Veth could, and had, defended me.

Luridel had urged life into dead witchwood. Luridel had given Royce’s bracelet the strength to fight Baltha, to distract her for just a moment.

And in that moment… I’d used Velidan.

Velidan. The looming, baffling centerpiece of this bizarre puzzle. The rune she’d written down despite surely knowing whom it would summon.

Had my mother seen him in the cave? Had she watched him rip Baltha apart, then turn on me? And still, even knowing all that, had she wanted it to happen?

My heart raced as I followed the logical trail of that thought. He hadn’t hurt me. He’d terrified me more than once, had threatened my life and the lives of those I loved… but he hadn’t actually hurt any of us.

And then he’d been there. This morning, when I’d reforged two pieces of a divine sword with yet another rune and the act had left me unconscious and choking on a throat full of blood… he’d been right there, able to heal me. His presence had been unwanted and frightening, but… something good had come out of it.

I’d felt quite afraid of my newest rune this morning. And then in the marketplace, surrounded by screaming innocents and the shadows of dark magic, I’d been too afraid to wield my own.

I didn’t feel afraid right now.

Right now I felt strong and bold and brazen. Right now I was looking at Renner and thinking about Joselia on the side of a road twenty five years ago and trusting my mother with all my heart, and with a final bite of my stew and a madly pounding pulse I bid everyone goodnight and hurried to my room.

It was as I’d left it; hazy moonlight spilled through the single glass-paned window, illuminating a rustic space. The quilt was still rumpled and bunched from my nap and the blue shadeblossom drooped in its little vase on the nightstand.

There were candles, but I didn’t light them. I twisted my fingers into the shape of vir and golden light smoldered just above my palm. I watched the glow, savoring its strength and beauty and letting the triumphant sight of my magic flood my veins with fierce heat.

Then, still cupping the light in one hand, I lifted my other.

“Sillar.” My fingers carved the air, tracing a pattern of lacy tendrils. I watched with baited breath as a pale green glow shivered into being… then drifted like mist to settle on the floorboards between my feet.

I blinked down, waiting expectantly, and for a moment all my fierce boldness faltered into confusion. Nothing was happening. The mist seemed to flicker, as if fading, and I opened my mouth to try again.

Then several things happened at once. My nostrils filled with the smell of damp earth and crushed grass, the room filled with the soft rustle of leaves, and the pool of mist hardened into a solid, writhing shape.

I bit down on a scream, throwing myself backwards and covering my face as the green thing lunged for me. It looked quite like a long snake and I braced myself for the sting of fangs. They didn’t come. After a panicked, frozen moment I peeked down through my fingers at… a vine.

It was limp, leafy, and… rooted into the floorboards. As if it had grown there. I gaped, head tilting at the sheer impossibility of a plant being able to grow out of a bedroom floor- nevermind the sheer impossibility of the entire situation- and when I lifted one hand to my mouth the vine moved. Its head, for lack of a better word, raised in tandem with my hand. I froze at that. Then, after several breathless moments, I stretched my fingertips out.

And the plant reached right for me.

Helpless, hysterical giggles fell from my lips as the vine wound its way around my wrist. Very gently, as gently as a beloved witchwood branch, though with the wiry, coiled kind of sensation that indicated withheld strength.

But the notion of withheld strength was a very frightening thought, so I quickly chased it away.

“You’re beautiful,” I breathed after some time, pulling my hand back.

And the vine drew back, perfectly in tandem.

This led to several minutes of tentative experimentation. I snaked my fingers to and fro, watching in baffled delight as the plant mirrored each gesture of my left hand- the hand that had traced sillar. Movements from any other part of my body seemed inconsequential.

I puzzled over the miracle, gently touching the waxy leaves, until with a shiver and a glow the vine shifted to mist. Then… to nothing. Even its roots faded.

My heart throbbed, as though something precious had been lost. I gave my head a little shake and chided myself for being silly even as tears pricked behind my eyes.

I suppose I could always make another one. Although… salt, what if it was alive? Even more than a normal plant- like how a witchwood is alive? And what if I… what if, wherever it went, what if it’s dead now?

That question also felt much too big for me to ever possibly answer. But the thought that I’d possibly coaxed a plant into existence only to then kill it was disturbing enough that I didn’t quite want to try the rune again just yet.

Although…

I murmured vir again. Examined the glow, waiting for the spots, the nausea, the dancing black behind my eyes and the throb in my chest…

Nothing. I felt tired, but that was all.

My teeth sank into my lower lip as that brazen fire flooded my belly once more. The boldness that Joselia’s tale of my mother had instilled.

And, before another flare of nervousness could stop me, I traced the next rune.

“Zellen.”

My crooked little line hung for a moment, shivering with pale yellow light. Just as I felt certain it would fade to nothing, a curious hum sounded from beyond. Quiet at first, but quickly growing louder.

My brows knit together as the sound crescendoed. There was something very familiar about it, though I couldn’t quite put my finger on what it was. Just a droning hum, with the occasional sharp rise in pitch. As I watched the light grew brighter, the crack stretched and widened, and the hum grew to a buzz as I peered closer.

My jaw dropped open and I fell back with a yelp as a thousand tiny, scrabbling black legs and shimmering gossamer wings appeared within the light. Or not within, exactly, but… beyond; as if I was looking through a window at a very bright space. I almost thought I saw emerald-green leaves amidst the mass of insects, but my glimpse was cut short as I frantically dropped my hands.

The widening sliver of light faded, like sunlight falling to shadow, but not before there was a buzz and a rasp and the flutter of rapid wings, and then-

I stared, open-mouthed, wide-eyed, and stunned perfectly still, as a glinting yellow honeybee zipped past the fading rune and hovered just before my eyes. It was a large bee, nearly the length of my thumb and quite fat, with wings shimmering like liquid silver. Its body was a mass of thick black fuzz streaked by buttercup-yellow.

It made a bee. It made a bee. It made… what in the realms… why did it make a bee?!

I dazedly pictured the writhing mass; what had looked like an entire hive of the creatures coming straight towards me. Had I let the rune hang there, would more bees have come in?

Come in. It came in. It wasn’t… I didn’t make it. Surely I didn’t. I can’t make bees! No one can make… I… I brought it here.

The thought was so strange that it sent me sinking onto the mattress, both hands clasped at my throat. My visitor drifted lazily through the bedroom. The insect seemed curious, but unconcerned, about its environment.

I brought Velidan here. He said I ripped him out of his realm.

Did I rip a vine from the Night Court? Did I rip a bee from the Night Court?

I gaped and blinked and shook my head, eyeing the little creature in utter bewilderment. Spots danced across my vision and I sank back against the pillow.

The insect hummed its way along, wings vibrating the air, until it reached the chicken-shaped vase and drooping shadeblossom. It wiggled up to the flower’s center, legs feeling for pollen, and I noticed that it had no stinger.

Well, that’s probably a good thing. A sting from a bee that size would be awful.

I squinted, shifting forwards to examine the creature more closely. Aside from the missing stinger, its unusual size, and the sheer vibrancy of its color, it truly looked just like a honeybee.

“Did I take you away from your home?” I murmured, head cocked to one side and feeling suddenly quite guilty. Saints, would this creature, this obviously living thing, fade to nothing in a few moments just like the vine? Had I condemned it to death? My throat thickened. “I’m so sorry.”

The insect turned and looked at me.

My heart stopped. Every muscle locked into place as beady, shining black eyes met mine.

And I felt for all the world that the creature… perhaps not understood me, exactly, but knew that I was addressing it. It met my gaze and held it.

And bees didn’t do that.

Ashes, what if it isn’t actually a bee? What if it is from another realm, and it’s… saints, some kind of Fae?! My heart slammed against my chest. The thought made me want to laugh and scream at the same time, but I couldn’t manage a single sound.

The bee continued to stare. Its wings buzzed a little tune.

I stared back, sucking in ragged breaths. It’s not… it’s… it’s not a monster. It’s not a Fae! It can’t be. It would be mad to think… It’s just looking at me, that doesn’t mean anything! I’m… I’m just being… Saints, I think I’m going to faint.

More spots danced as the insect turned back to nuzzling the shadeblossom. It continued for a moment before, perhaps dissatisfied, it flew directly to the windowsill. A dark, needle-thin leg lifted flush to the glass, tapped once… and those black eyes turned towards me again.

My world narrowed to two clicking mandibles, two twitching antennas, and the indisputable intelligence in that glistening little gaze. I sat stunned, shaking my head in disbelief.

It tapped the glass again. Stared at me, wings flicking impatiently.

Numb legs carried me to the window. Trembling hands slid the glass open, and rounded eyes watched as the bee disappeared into the darkness.

I collapsed in a daze atop the mattress, still in my clothes and boots, with blackness pressing at the edges of my vision. I lay staring at the ceiling, more bewildered than I’d ever been in my life, as sleep quickly claimed me.
