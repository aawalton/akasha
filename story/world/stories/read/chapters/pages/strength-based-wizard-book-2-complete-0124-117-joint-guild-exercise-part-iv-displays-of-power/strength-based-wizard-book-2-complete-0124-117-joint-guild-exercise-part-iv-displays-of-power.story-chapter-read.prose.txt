Chapter 117

Joint Guild Exercise, Part IV (Displays of Power 2)

POV: Veronica Sampietro

August 3rd: 00 days, 02 hours, 34 minutes until Phase 2 of the Gods Game commences…

Veronica walked under strings of warm lights that sagged between the buildings of East Fourth Street like lazy constellations, her heels clicking on the pavement loud enough to sound rude. Usually crowds of people would be packed into the patios and outdoor seating areas lining the street, filling the street with the sound of life. Tonight, it was a quiet shadow of its usual self.  The restaurants were still open, technically, but half the tables were empty. A couple of servers lingered near doorways with that look people got when they were trying not to stare at their phones for updates about the world ending tomorrow. Evacuation ahead of the monster outbreak had thinned downtown to a hush and other than Veronica and her date, it seemed as though no one was particularly happy to be there on the eve of disaster.

Which, honestly, made the date better.

Damien had been… good. Better than good, if she was being honest with herself (which, more often than not, she wasn’t). He was funny without trying too hard, sharp without being cruel. And he listened—really listened—like he wanted to learn the shape of her mind, not just get to the part where she agreed to go home with him. Overall, things just felt easy with him. They’d had dinner, then a drink, and then another drink, and the conversation didn’t die once. No awkward lulls, no weird power games (as was, unfortunately, often the case with men who had access to the System). For once, Veronica felt like she could breathe.

They walked closer together, his arm around her now, warm and easy against her shoulders. She let herself lean into him, just a little, and felt him tighten his hold like he liked the weight of her there.

“So,” Damien said, voice low, smooth. “Do you want to come back to my place and have another drink? I don’t want this conversation to end yet.”

Veronica smiled, the kind of smile that didn’t need permission. Perhaps it was that third after-dinner cocktail… Perhaps it was finally getting a moment away from all the stress of training, Guilds, and all the bullshit that came with being a System User, but she let the next words come willingly.

“Yeah,” she said. “I’m in.” She pushed a little closer, and he laughed quietly, pleased, the sound disappearing into the mostly empty street.

“I live in the apartments above Tower City,” he said. “In Terminal Tower.”

They headed toward Public Square, the lights behind them fading into a warm blur. The closer they got, the more the silence thickened. Downtown Cleveland at night was supposed to hum with the sound of light crowds milling about. Tonight it felt like a stage after the audience left—props still set, lights still on, but no one around to watch the silent, lifeless stage.

Veronica glanced around, uneasy in a way she didn’t want to admit. Something in the back of her mind itched.

“It’s so weird,” she said. “Being this quiet. Like… the city’s haunted.” She instantly regretted saying something so stupid.

Damien squeezed her shoulder. “Better quiet than chaos. I’m sure things will be hectic tomorrow.”

“When does your building need to be evacuated by?”

“Tomorrow at Noon.”

“Where are you going to go?”

Damien shrugged. “I was planning to head to Philadelphia for a few days for work. Already packed and everything. I might just head out a day early.”

She made a noncommittal sound, eyes scanning the shadows between buildings.

Then, she felt it. A little prickle at the base of her skull. A tug at her awareness, like a finger flicking the edge of a glass. Her [Perception] was picking up on something. It was still a relatively low-leveled Skill and hadn’t upgraded since she first received it. The result was her often attempting to read the tea leaves her System-granted heightened awareness presented to her.

[Perception] had changed the world for her in small, unfair ways—turning “maybe” into “there.” When she read the tea leaves right, it turned instinct into certainty. And right now, it screamed at her.

Veronica stopped dead.

Damien slowed too, confused. “What—”

“Stay back,” she said, sharp enough that he did without thinking.

She shifted her stance automatically, weight lowering, hands ready. Her eyes cut into the dark. An alley tucked away in shadow beside a closed storefront whose awning had blinked out. Movement from the darkness.

Three shapes burst from the shadows like thrown knives—small, fast, ugly. Imps. Three imps, her mind corrected, taking in the figures dashing towards them. Their bodies were wrong proportions, all elbows and claws and grinning teeth, eyes like hot coals. They leapt straight at her.

Veronica didn’t flinch.

[Summon Shield]

Ability: [Alter Shield]

Alteration Selected: [Invisible Fortress]

She snapped up a shield—an invisible wall of force that shimmered for a split second like heat off asphalt. The imps slammed into it with wet, spiteful thuds and bounced off, screeching, tumbling back like they’d hit a windshield at highway speed.

“Seriously?” Veronica muttered, disgusted. “What the fuck?”

Was there a Gate nearby? Had it gone unnoticed by local Guilds, letting monsters slip out into their world?

No time to chase the answers to those questions! Her [Perception] was still firing.

A second tug on her mental attention, this time at her flank. Three more imps.

She didn’t even turn. She felt them coming, felt their trajectory like she could see it drawn in thin silver lines.

She activated [Defiance].

The Skill lit inside her like a match struck in her chest. A recent addition, she’d only had the chance to test it a few times. It had a heavy Mana cost, but returned all attacks to their source two fold.

At the same time, with a quick mental command her armor equipped with a familiar flicker of pixelated light. Plates slid into place over her back, shoulders, spine—enchanted System-produced steel that altered itself to fit her like a glove, all while giving her absolute flexibility and freedom of movement.

Claws swiped at her from behind. They skittered uselessly against the armor with a sound like nails on a metal countertop. Her Mana plummeted as [Defiance] was triggered.

Veronica’s lips curled.

“Nice try.”

Then she heard Damien grunt in pain. Real pain. Her pulse jumped.

More imps? Her mind raced. Did one slip past her Perception and attack Damien?

She spun around, ready to throw another shield, ready to tear into anything that touched him—and froze.

Damien stood there, chest heaving, looking down at himself. Claw marks raked across his torso. Fresh, angry red lines as the blood soaked through his button-down shirt. The imps were still trying their best to claw through the armor covering her shoulder blades. Clearly

But the imps behind her were the ones that had swiped—she’d felt it. She’d heard it scrape against her armor, clearly undeterred from the effects of [Defiance]. The dots quickly connected in her mind.

The claw marks across Damien’s chest weren’t from an imp’s attack.

They were the result of [Defiance]. He—Damien—was the source of the imps’ attacks.

His face twisted, fury boiling up so fast it looked like it surprised even him. He lifted his head, eyes no longer warm. They were hard and cold now. Bright with what Veronica recognized was pure, unadulterated hatred.

The imps were latched onto her, trying to pry their claws into her armor. She ignored them, too stunned by the realization of what her eyes and mind were telling her.

“You dirty little cunt!” he barked. “How dare you!” The words hit the air like a slap.

Veronica bristled at the word. “Damien… What are you doing?” she choked out.

“Look what you did to me!” he exclaimed, staring down at his blood soaked shirt.

“You… You attacked me…?” she muttered.

Damien glanced up at her, his face twisted like a mask slipping. Warmth fell off him in chunks, revealing something pale and greasy underneath—rage that had been simmering the whole time, just waiting for a woman to do something unforgivable like protect herself.

Veronica didn’t flinch. She couldn’t afford to. Her mind was still reeling—how fast it had turned, how smoothly he’d worn the “nice guy” costume—but tapping into her Skills snapped her focus into place like a seatbelt locking before a crash. The System poured cool focus through her veins, dampening the waves of emotion crashing against her ribcage.

Damien raised one hand and hissed something under his breath that wasn’t quite a spell and wasn’t quite a prayer. The air between them rippled.

Seven more imps popped into existence around him in quick, wet flashes—like someone had pulled them from a bag of filth and flung them into the street. Their eyes glowed, their claws flexed, and their mouths split into eager grins. Two of them—bigger than the others, with longer limbs—grabbed Damien by the shoulders and lifted, pulling the man into the air.

Veronica’s instincts came alive, screaming at her to move, to act. She summoned a round shield behind her, and pulled it towards her, slamming it into her back and crushing the two imps latched there like bugs. She could hear their blood hissing in the air as the shield pulled away from her armor.

Damien was being pulled away from her, clearing distance to let his imps do the dirty work. She lunged anyway, weapon already in motion. Her hammer appeared in her hands with a familiar weight—solid, comforting, and honest, in a way a man could never be. She swung for Damien’s ribs with full commitment.

At the same time, she triggered [Reach].

The shaft of her hammer extended mid-swing with a sharp, metallic shink, stretching forward like it had suddenly remembered the System wasn’t bound by normal physics. It should’ve caught him. It would’ve caught a normal man.

But the imps yanked him up hard in response, just quickly enough. The hammer whistled through the space where his chest had been, passing under his feet by inches.

Damien laughed from above her, breathless and nasty. The sound made her skin crawl.

Veronica bared her teeth.

“You have got to be kidding me,” she snapped, pivoting as an imp dove at her face.

[Summon Shield]

Her left forearm shield flared—hard light hugging her arm like a bracer—and she batted the imp away with a sharp backhand. It cartwheeled through the air and slammed into a brick wall, leaving a wet smear before it slumped, twitching.

Tapping her Skills focused her mind even more, each activation sharpening her, stripping away panic and replacing it with a clean, furious clarity.

She’d grown stronger. And she wouldn’t be an easy target. She would never be an easy target.

“Why is it so hard,” Veronica shouted, voice ringing down the empty street, “to just meet a nice guy?!”

An imp lunged low for her legs. She hopped back, armor plates clinking softly, and slammed the hammer down. The head of it cracked into the pavement with a bright bang, and the creature flattened like a bug under a boot.

Damien hovered above, supported by those two imps like he was some pathetic demon prince in a clearance rack cape.

His face contorted again, spit flecking at the corner of his mouth. “You’re filth,” he snarled. “Filthy—filthy little—” He seemed to choke on his own hatred, then found the words he’d been saving like a sacrament. “Just a filthy bitch,” he barked, “who should accept her place as sacrifice. You should be happy to die in the name of Chernobog! Accept it, and it will make you clean!”

The name hit Veronica like ice water poured down her spine.

Chernobog.

The thing mentioned in her Trait. It was related to whatever had touched her soul and left a stain that whispered doom into her life like a slow, patient curse. She knew it would haunt her one day, and one day soon. The time, apparently, was now.

Damien wasn’t just some creep with imps. He was Soul-touched too. Another one of the marked. The Quest had hinted this might happen eventually—violence, escalation, the System, or Chernobog, or whatever, tightening its grip… But this? This was insidious. This was something even more. Even worse… The System Killer, she thought, finally seeing the full picture. Someone who had access to the System themselves and was only targeting female System Users.

Veronica’s fear burned off and left something hotter behind. She lifted her chin and smiled, sharp and mean. “Oh,” she said loudly, projecting her voice so it carried. “So, you’re a weird cultist and an incel. That’s… honestly kind of impressive. Like, you collected the whole set of being a fucking loser. I hope your god, or whatever, likes it when you get your ass kicked by some filthy bitch!”

Damien’s face twitched.

The insult landed. Not because it was clever—though it was—but because it hit what he was: fragile, entitled, with a self-inflated narcissistic ego.

His eyes flashed.

“What did you say?” he spat. He tilted his head to the side, mumbling something she didn’t catch.

Veronica leaned into it, because she could feel him wobbling in the air, emotionally off-balance, and she knew her Skill was there—waiting. She’d take this fucker down!

“Let me guess,” she called, voice dripping with contempt, “you tell yourself you’re ‘chosen’ because it’s easier than admitting you’re just a little man who couldn’t handle being told no.”

She felt it then—the subtle shift and pulsing sensation in her mind as the System recognized the effect. The invisible thread of her [Taunt] Skill catching and pulling. She kept an eye on her Mana bar, which was getting dangerously low. She kept a good mana potion on her person at all times, just in case. But it was only one, and she’d rather end things as quickly as possible.

To her surprise, the taunt worked. Damien’s lips peeled back from his teeth like a snarling dog. His composure—what little he had—cracked clean in half.

“Shut up!” he screamed, voice breaking ugly. “SHUT UP!”

He thrust his hand downward and the air tore open beside him like a wound in reality.

Something bigger crawled out. A lot bigger.

It looked like one of the imps, but distorted… wrong. Wrong in the way a child’s drawing may have tried to capture one of the smaller imps she had previously crushed. It was about four feet tall, shoulders bulging with muscle like someone had pumped it full of air. Its arms were too thick for its frame, veins raised, skin stretched tight. It moved with a gorilla’s fury, knuckles dragging, eyes blazing. The wings on its back were clearly too small to take its bloated body into the air.

It roared, high and shrill, and charged Veronica like a living battering ram.

Veronica planted her feet, summoning three more shields that slammed into place in front of her and readying her hammer.

The creature hit them with a brutal WHAM.

The shields dented inward under the impact, vibrating, edges flickering as the force buckled them. The street shook and cracked beneath their feet. Veronica’s arms jolted with the feedback and her Stamina and Health bars flashed, recognizing the impact.

But they held. They didn’t break. Veronica gritted her teeth, mentally commanding the floating shields to push back. The bloated imp was difficult, and heavy. It dug into the smashed cement beneath its clawed feet, holding its ground.

The beast snarled inches from her face, its breath hot and foul against her armor.

Veronica tightened her grip on her hammer, eyes blazing back.

Ping!

Monster Identified: Vagrant Imp, Level 45

“Okay,” she murmured, voice low and deadly. “Now we’re getting somewhere.”

The muscled imp bellowed again, spittle and heat and hate, and Veronica braced to punish it.

Then the air behind her went cold.

Her [Perception] flared—too late this time—and something small and fast ripped upward from her own shadow like it had been waiting there the whole time.

Three of them.

The little imps launched like darts. These ones were only about the size of her palm.

They latched onto her armor with clawed hands, scrambling over her shoulders and neck. One clamped onto her helmeted head, its fingers digging into the seams. Another slapped itself across her visor like a wet rag. The third crawled over her faceplate, screeching, wings beating her cheeks with frantic little gusts.

Her vision vanished into flailing black limbs and toothy grins.

“Get—off—” she snarled, voice muffled behind metal. She kept her mental focus on the shields fending off the Vagrant Imp.

The miniature imps’ claws scraped, their weight tugging her head down, and for half a second she was blind and furious and moving on instinct alone.

Then the muscled imp slammed into her shields again. This time the dented panes buckled, collapsing inward with a violent shimmer. The impact hit her like a car. Veronica’s boots skidded and her spine jolted. The world snapped sideways and she went down hard, the pavement rushing up to meet her shoulder and hip with a punishing crack.

Pain flared bright in her ribs.

She tasted blood—metallic, hot. Must have bit my cheek, she thought, glancing down at the Health bar suspended in the bottom corner of her HUD. It was still practically full. Her large reserves held, boosting her physical regeneration. In the span of a breath, she was as good as new. But she had other problems.

“Shit,” she hissed under her breath, trying to wrench her head free, trying to rip the imps off, trying to breathe through the sudden crush of chaos.

The muscled imp loomed over her, raising its grotesque arms above its head like a hammer for another smash.

The small imps clung tighter, shrieking in her ear like living static.

Veronica stopped trying to play fair.

She went very still for a fraction of a second—just long enough to focus.

Then she triggered her newest Skill.

[Royal Guard]

It lit inside her like a detonator.

And she felt the cost immediately—her Mana guttered, drained in a single violent pull like someone yanked a plug from a bathtub. All of it. Gone.

Her armor exploded off her body. Plates tore away in a burst of force, shredding outward like shrapnel made of enchanted steel. The imps on her—those little parasites—were ripped apart mid-screech, torn into wet fragments that spat against the pavement.

The muscled imp didn’t even get a second to understand what was happening. The flying metal slammed into it from every angle, crushing and slicing and pulverizing until the inflated thing became a smear of meat and mist.

Then the blade-like armor plates soared through the air like a flock of angry birds, converging on her real target. Damien. The metal wrapped around him midair in a brutal, perfect compression. A coffin made of her own armor snapped shut around his body with a deep, satisfying CLANG.

For a heartbeat, the street went quiet.

A few of the small imps still flitted about overhead, circling, confused, screeching, waiting for orders.

Veronica sucked in a hard breath, pain blooming through her side, and equipped her second set of armor—pixelated light snapping new plates into place over her shoulders and chest. Lighter, not as strong, but still functional. She also withdrew her mana potion, throwing it back. Her Mana bar rose to about half full.

She got to her feet and approached the metal coffin containing Damien.

Step by step.

Hammer in hand. Her visor cleared now, the world sharpening into focus again. She wanted to crush him, but she wasn’t a coldhearted killer. He could answer for his crimes.

The coffin twitched faintly.

Veronica leaned down, peering at the seams where the plates had fused, leaving only the narrowest of gaps. She wanted to see the look on the asshole’s face, realizing he’d been captured by his own supposed victim. When the hunted became the huntress…!

Instead, she saw a gleam, and then an imp-like face poking out through a narrow crack, with its too wide a grin, too many teeth, and eyes gleaming with a nasty intelligence.

A hiss of anger curled through her. What happened? Did he turn into an imp, or…

From an alleyway off to the side, Damien’s voice spat out like poison.

“YOU THINK THAT’S ENOUGH?” he screamed.

Veronica snapped her head toward the sound.

Damien stood in the mouth of the alley, unscathed. That confirmed it. He must have a Skill that allows him to switch places with his imps.

His eyes burned with righteous madness.

“You have no other choice!” he snarled. “Accept your fate!”

He lifted both hands, and blood poured from his hands and his chest, pooling on the ground at this feet.

“This is my [Sacrifice]!” he barked.

The alley floor drank it in and the air thickened with the feeling of magic being worked.

And then the shadows above the buildings moved. Not one or three, this time. An entire flock. Countless flying imps erupted into the night like a living storm, pouring out of the darkness in a screaming wave—wings beating, claws flexing, mouths open.

So many that the lights of nearby lampposts and the buildings in Public Square seemed to dim beneath their swarm.

Veronica’s stomach clenched.

She was good. She was strong. But she wasn’t built to handle a crowd like this alone. She could draw their attention, soak up as much damage as was necessary, but not dispatch a hundred or more monsters.

She needed support, and didn’t have it. And Damien knew it. His eyes gleamed as he let his mouth quirk into a victorious smile.

Veronica’s jaw tightened.

She backed up a step, eyes tracking the swarm as it began to wheel toward her like a hurricane choosing its landing point.

Then she did the only smart thing. She pulled out her phone with shaking hands—not fear, not panic, just adrenaline—and thumbed a message into the group chat.

HELP! TROUBLE! SYSTEM KILLER!

She hit send, just before needing to drop her phone and adjust the grip on her hammer, just as the imps descended, smashing into her.
