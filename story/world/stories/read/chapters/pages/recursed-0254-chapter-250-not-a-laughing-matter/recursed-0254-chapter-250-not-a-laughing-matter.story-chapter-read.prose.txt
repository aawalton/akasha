Euphoria had just enough time to grin and take in his… unfortunate situation before the ritual’s second phase triggered.

There were no hymns for this. Nyxalraith’s preparations made sure that everything was complete the moment the god manifested before her. If she hadn’t, she knew without a doubt that the laughing god would slip away.

The flickering flames that echoed the pink of her stomach rolled free from the fulfilled runes and reached for the distorted ceiling. There was so much of it. Far more than she’d been expecting. What had previously been an intentionally dark chamber was now awash with the radiant light of fire that whirled above.

“You see my name now,” Euphoria chuckled, but his usual chorus of giggles was notably silent. “That is… not a pleasant surprise.”

He laughed again as his ethereal, ever-present grin tilted up. Nyxalraith was pleased to hear it sounded nervous. It was obvious he’d noticed what was going on overhead.

“You are alive. Good to see!” There was another unsettled chuckle and Euphoria’s primary smile flickered to another place in the black storm that constituted his body. “And you have grown so much…” Despite his lack of eyes, the glance to his surroundings was obvious. “And in such a short time, too. Would have hated to see the Chasm end you before your time.”

Nyxalraith’s glare didn’t falter at the god’s idle chattering. The brilliant pink flames swirled above. They spiralled until the form of an iris took shape, identical, if not for the colour, to that which hung over Coral. Little God’s influence. Whether it was intentional or not, she had no way to know.

“I see Little God is back.” Euphoria let out a roll of snickers as he emphasised her personal term for the Elder God. “I told you it was only a matter of time.”

She should have expected Euphoria of all beings to laugh off such a situation, but that he was still annoyed her. “G̣͈̻̥̖͒̎͂h͍̖͖͕̯͎̹̟ͤȍ̤͙̗͕͔̓͛r̯̖̰̮̝̆̇͑ͦ̿ṷ̳̺̩̙̩̬͖͔͌̍͆͐̑̋k̥̲̠̟ͣͦ̀ͥͭ͒͛̾͋̐i̙̩̹͈̜̮̝͖ͭͬͮl̗̗̞̼͋͂, I hope that claw was worth it.”

Nyxalraith could have sworn he winced at the mention of his name. “Hey, you’re mad. I get it-”

The pseudo accretion disk burning in the inconsistent space of the ritual chamber suddenly unleashed a wave of pink light, much like the solar winds that had been pelting Coral’s surface for months. Nyxalraith’s colour effortlessly ate through the dark cloud. In all but a few seconds, the obscurement was peeled back and Euphoria’s white grins were revealed in their entirety without their usual backdrop.

“Is this really necessary?” Euphoria all but whined. “All over a single finger? Look! You even regrew it!”

Nyxalraith glared at those unreal teeth. Without the haze, she could freely see how the smirk grew from nothing. There were no roots at the base of those teeth. The white grin was a distortion in a reality that couldn’t fully express his form, not enamel.

Not that she had any intent on leaving Euphoria to exist as he was: beyond her reach.

“I do not enjoy being betrayed.”

“And yet you left, accepting my offering as payment without fulfilling your end of the deal.”

Another giggle, this one somewhere between mocking and jittery. “That was the-”

“I know that was the fault of my ritual. The binding contract was loose. I assumed,” she stressed the word, “that we had an understanding that didn’t require something to hold the other accountable. I see now I was naive.” She paused, lowering her eyes from Little God’s imagery bleeding through to below their feet. “But you are right that I have grown. This binding is far beyond the last.”

With that, Nyxalraith flooded the ground below her with corruption. It skimmed past the multi-dimensional pentagram and filtered into a voided sphere her earlier ritual had prepared. Immediately, a Dark Star began to form.

Nyxalraith had taken note during her battles against Sekhhath’Ra and Solan. She may not have had a comprehensive knowledge of rituals then, but her experiences against the people who had succeeded in suppressing her Dark Stars made significant strides in helping her understand how to replicate that in a ritual.

And so, when the sphere of blackness bloated to fill its container, it struck hard, fast, and with enough power she had to worry that cultists on the other side of Coral would feel the reverberation. But most meaningfully, the Dark Star was contained. Power continued to accumulate in that dozen metre orb, but as dense as the Dark Star grew, it never breached the active ritual.

“You don’t want to do this,” Euphoria said, apparently knowing exactly what she was planning. “I would rather not put down a promising kid like yourself.” He grinned, but she knew it wasn’t genuine.

“Would you dare?” Nyxalraith didn’t waver under the threats of this god. “Are you willing to take the risk?”

Nyxalraith had spent a long time in the past months simply reviewing her memory of Solan’s fight. She had challenged the Fleshsmiths’ god, and he had backed down. Why? Her first thoughts laid in her connection to the Eidolons. But that theory fell apart by the fact that the god acknowledged her as a Seed before deciding to flee.

So then was it her — at the time — new eyes, or the death pact? Or both? Nyxalraith hadn’t been sure of anything at the time. All she had… was a sort of instinctual certainty that the god would back down. Like a predator backing down from the apex.

Now though, Nyxalraith didn’t need to rely on instinct.

Euphoria had lost his chance at freedom the moment he’d been baited into accepting the summons. Her confidence in her ritual, even against something nearly a hundred evolutions higher than her, was absolute.

Really, the only option he did have to escape was to kill her. Given his fear of the Eidolons, she doubted he would make that choice, but that wasn’t to say she hadn’t prepared for the possibility. Euphoria couldn’t touch her without first striking Little God. And, well, he’d already fled from one Elder God.

As the weight of the suppressed Dark Star continued to mount only meters below her feet, Euphoria grew increasingly apprehensive.

“Do you seriously believe you can contain me?” Another hollow laugh. “How many of your forebearers have tried, and failed to control a god? Did your birth kind not find their deaths doing just that?”

“You are no Soulchasm,” she said. “The Bodytwisters have succeeded. And another cult has as well, if the northern Abyssal Fog is anything like the first.” There was no actual known origin point for that, so she didn’t know which cult was related. “And besides, my rituals are better.” At least those she’d given as much time and effort towards as this.

Finally, the Dark Star was filled with as much corruption as she was willing to strain her containment. Nyxalraith ceased the flow from her core. Instantly, the ground began to warp. The sphere of metal twisted and rippled through the already bent pathways of space all around them until wherever they looked, the containment could be seen anywhere.

While Nyxalraith indulged in the satisfaction of an extremely complex process of manipulating corruption coming to fruition in just the way she’d planned, Euphoria was less enthused. There hadn’t been so much as an impulsive giggle from him in a minute. It wasn’t until that corruptive control extended to the pink solar rain, stretching that across the inner sphere rapidly being shoved into place, that Euphoria was finally pushed to act.

He tried to escape.

Instead of attacking her, he tried to flee. Again.

A dozen smiling fissures cut through regions of especially high ritual activity, only for the runes and lines to crumble away perfectly useless. The greatest achievement of her ritual was that she’d managed to remove any actual source to strike. Her ritual was more of a living beast than a mechanical process. As soon as the hymn was over, it was too late to stop.

Nyxalraith was sure the other cults — especially the Scriptures — had found ways to make their rituals impossible to disable by striking the underlying runes and pentagrams, but she doubted any were like this. It was as if she’d breathed life into a function.

Still, Euphoria wasn’t a god for nothing. He found the cracks in the corruption. The gaps in the flow of reality that would allow him to slip back to wherever he usually resided.

Unfortunately for him, the ritual was prepared. Before Nyxalraith had even noticed the less-than-physical entity trying to squirm out of her clutches, the ritual was already upon him. Calling on a nature of a specific entity, darkness pierced through the falling light and wrapped around every one of the hundred present grins. They tugged at him. Without pulling in any specific direction, they tugged him away from the escape he planned to slip though, and held him locked within the prison of a Dark Star warped into a cage.

It didn’t stop there. The darkness continued to pull, and unable to resist, all of Euphoria was forced to manifest. His entire soul was pulled from the furthest reaches of what her eyes could see, and tamped down into this narrow, inescapable prison.

The sensation was incredibly strange. Using T͑s̟͂tl̬̐te̞pͦ without having the presence of mind to do it manually… well, even after having proven it worked a dozen times before she’d implemented the ritual, she still couldn’t get over the weight.

It had started with the curiosity of her pink ritual flames. Different ritual types or relying on particular sources could alter the colour of candle fire or even make it a different state entirely. But for a while now, Nyxalraith’s flames defaulted to pink. Just like her Pink. It was odd, but it led her down a line of thought that had created this latest aspect of the ritual.

If you could call upon a god’s nature with a ritual, could you do the same for a person’s?

The answer, as it turned out, was yes.

Nyxalraith had successfully created a connection between the ritual, and T͑s̟͂tl̬̐te̞pͦ. Of course, because the name was only seventh evolution — only? How far she’d come — she felt when it activated like it was a thick rope tied around her chest, pulling her to the other end of Coral. She could refuse the call if she wanted. Easily. But the fact that could use her name through a ritual in this way meant she could apply a level of precision in its use that she frankly didn’t possess.

Especially when combined with Little God’s aspects.

There was, surprisingly, more body to Euphoria than Nyxalraith had expected. It was neither fleshy nor mechanical, and she could barely understand it as anything more than a web of nerves connecting the bodiless mouths that really had no place to exist in this world. It was entirely alien. Nyxalraith instantly knew that she couldn’t let anyone without significant mental defences view it, lest their mind bubble and grow unable to form a single thought that wasn’t humour, amusement, or the need to laugh.

Euphoria had finally realised that there was no getting out. Instead of roaring in a rage at Nyxalraith, or growing fearful of what would happen, he broke the silence with a quiet laugh.

It was probably the most honest ripple of humour she’d heard from him. Not followed by that eerie giggling, or the thunderous, booming guffaws.

“I hate that you know my name… and, I guess, that I’m the subject of this jewel of a ritual,” he said between chuckles as he looked around at the Dark Star that surrounded them without actually placing them inside the Dark Star. “But I can appreciate the comedy of the moment. Now that I’m stuck with you, there’s nothing to do but enjoy the moment.”

As Euphoria’s hundred smaller grins joined in with the laughter, Nyxalraith tried not to look annoyed. She’d gone through all this effort to get him back, and now he acted as if it didn’t bother him in the slightest.

Well, whatever. Nyxalraith now had a god bound to her whims and she wasn’t about to let him go to waste.
