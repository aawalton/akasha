Without even getting as much as a moment to catch his breath, it was time to head over to the Singer system. It was way ahead of the tentative schedule, and even if he hadn't imagined coming up with some master plan, an impromptu rescue mission was far from the best way of staging your own mini-invasion. Not that it mattered, though. Luke wasn't about to leave Ray to his own devices. Execution wasn't a great end after all. Gathering up the stray thoughts and emotions took some time, but once he'd calmed himself, Luke settled in for a brief meditation session, just so he could get at Ray's echo thread.

Ray: "One more thing."

Luke sighed.

Luke: "I'm trying to come rescue you, asshole. Shut up."

Ray: "Fine. Sure, whatever. Guess I won't share the information you'll need to stay out of their trap."

Luke: "...Trap?"

Ray: "No, no. Don't let me distract you."

Ray: "We'll both go down. That's fine."

Luke: "..."

Ray: "Just fucking with you."

Ray: "So, I told you about them draining my mana, right?"

Luke: "You didn't."

Ray: "Well, they did. Stamina too. It's uncomfortable, let me tell you."

Ray: "So, yeah. Look out for that."

Luke: "How are they doing it?"

Ray: "Dunno."

Ray: "Ah, crap. Get your meditation on, bro. They're coming for me."

Ray: "I'm hearing steps."

Ray: "No rush, though. Take your time."

Ray: "The Singers who just entered my cell have hoods on, covering their faces. That's a good sign, right?"

Luke: "How many of them are there?"

Ray: "Three. One is holding a device of some kind."

Ray: "Aw, shit."

Luke: "Ray?"

A couple of seconds passed without Ray replying.

Luke: "Hey, Ray!"

All silence. Not good. Them having a mana-drain device was bad news all around, but Luke saw no other choice than to risk it. Letting them execute Ray was out of the question. The echo thread appeared in the weave, and Luke grabbed hold of it, wishing he could yank on the thing to bring people to him instead, but no such luck. Weavestepping through the weave and then into The Greater Weave, blasting through to the Singer system, Luke readied himself for the moment he appeared.

Ray was in a bare, all-white room. The walls, ceiling, and floor all glowed white as well. It was a stark, cold white that was far from inviting. Two Singers held guard positions by the single door leading into the room, and four more stood at the corners of a deep black execution block in the middle of the room. Ray was on top of it, arms and legs bound with white rope. He wore a white hood over his head, matching the executioner standing next to the block, a white-bladed axe held high over his head. These damn Singers didn't mess around. They'd just brought Ray to this place to take his head off.

Taking in the room and the enemies took no more than half a second, but the executioner recovered in that time and, rather than hack Ray's neck, he swung the axe at Luke, who intercepted it with several threads of mana as he ducked, changing its trajectory so it took another Singer in the leg, shearing it off with almost no resistance. Luke then launched himself forward at the executioner as the injured Singer fell with a scream and the others started moving. To his surprise, they fled out of the room.

"Trap," Luke spat as he rose from the now dead executioner. "Of course it's a damn trap."

The door closed, leaving Luke, Ray, and the bleeding Singer alone. A new sound reached his ears: the telltale hissing of gas being pumped into the now closed-off area. As that happened, all light disappeared as well, leaving them in pitch-black darkness.

"Get me off this damn thing!" Ray shouted.

The Singer on the floor groaned.

Luke drew in a breath, reached down with a thread, killed the Singer, and freed Ray with another. Weaver's Perception meant he didn't need to see with his eyes to get a good idea of his surroundings. He didn't know what sort of gas the Singers were pumping into the room, but he wasn't about to stick around to find out. Ray sat up and pulled the hood off just as Luke grabbed hold of him with a thread of mana.

"Wait!" Ray said, but it was too late.

While Luke's senses were primed to sense just about anything biological, Weaver's Perception didn't act like some infallible information-gathering skill that told him everything that happened around him. With how fast everything had happened from the second he appeared, dazed and hurt by weavestepping through his own system weave, The Greater System Weave, and then into the Singer weave, it was understandable that he missed the device strapped to Ray's chest. The moment he touched him with a thread of mana, the thing whirred to life. It released a shockwave before Luke had a chance to react, but it wasn't a physical one. Rather than being pushed back, Luke got to experience every ounce of mana being forcibly pushed through his body and away from it. Stumbling to the floor, unable to move or even think, he couldn't help but imagine his mana slamming into the wall before dissipating into the air.

Having reached a mana regeneration rate that couldn't be described as anything but impressive, Luke's pool regenerated at a rate that would make most Integrated green with envy, but it wasn't fast enough. Losing all mana had been a blow, but he realized it was just a way to immobilize him long enough that the gas could reach critical mass in the room. The darkness spun around him as he struggled, trying to will the trickling of mana into becoming a raging river, but it was no use. Every breath he took brought more of that invisible substance into his lungs. It was as if it took up all the space, meaning no air could make it into his system. Fading fast, Luke grappled with what little mana he had, trying to use it to destroy the gas. The Healer's Moment would have helped, but he didn't have the mana for it.

All the lights came on at the same time. Rather than stay on, they strobed on and off, growing brighter and brighter until it seemed they were trying to flash him to death. Ray was unconscious but alive. A shrill sound came on, like the Singers had started DJing, but were only allowed to use samples of different makes of fire alarms. As if that wasn't enough, a pungent smell spread through the room. The combined assault on his senses made for a horrific experience, and he had no way of getting out. When the device on Ray's chest whirred again, releasing a second pulse of mana-killing energy, Luke was almost glad to be away from it all, and he embraced the sweet release of unconsciousness. Except it didn't come. His body fought against it. At first, he didn't realize what'd happened, because more mana had poured into him out of nowhere. Then, when he realized what'd happened, it didn't come with relief or a renewed will to fight. All it brought was despair.

Weaveguard. Several of his internal organs were wrapped with mana to create protective weaves. They did nothing against the onslaught on his senses, but they'd kept themselves in place against the first wave. After the second, they faltered. One by one, they unraveled, and the retrieved mana went straight into his meta-heart. His total mana rose to almost a third of his maximum. If he'd been in a better position, maybe that would have been enough to get out of the terrible situation, if it wasn't for the terrible fact that one of those weaveguards held the speck of void mana in check.

The void mana broke free, and the pain from last time seemed a fond memory compared to what he experienced in that moment. Screaming, Luke went down, trying to re-form the weaveguard to isolate the void mana again. Even as he worked, the whirring from Ray's chest sounded again, and Luke knew it was over.

A third wave slammed into him, and this time there was no resisting it. Nothing left. Thrust out of his system, all that mana disappeared into thin air, all except the void mana. That little part of him was unaffected, and the last thing Luke perceived before all went dark was that void mana growing, almost as if it was taking root inside his meta-heart.

Luke woke screaming at some point later, feeling like his chest was being torn apart.

"Luke!" Ray shouted from right next to him, but there was no replying, not through that.

All went dark again. Screaming. A different white room. Restraints. Singers. No mana. Darkness. Next time he woke, Luke stayed conscious a little longer. The pain was not becoming more bearable. If anything, it was getting worse. The brightness of the room made his head hurt, on top of everything, so Luke kept his eyes closed, looking inward. What he saw made him pass out again. Void mana. So much of it.

"Luke!"

Darkness.

"Just wake up, dammit!" Ray shouted.

Gritting his teeth, Luke came to. The void mana had spread even more. If nothing changed, it would eat him whole, and somehow he knew that would be his end. That was unacceptable. Luke wasn't ready to give up just yet. Forcing himself to stay conscious, he focused inward. Concentrating was almost impossible without mana. The Singers kept him drained somehow, just like with Ray. Cowards.

Dimly, he realized his inventory was empty, and he was no longer wearing the robe he'd come to love so much. None of that mattered. Smudge was still in there, snug in his Stable but unable to get out. Items didn't matter. In that moment, nothing from his inventory would have been much help. Even the Mana Orb wouldn't do much, since whatever mana Luke drew in would just be taken away. No, this time he would have to do without. He would have to combat and defeat the void within himself without any mana of his own.

This time, Luke would have to fight against the void with nothing but the power of his will.

"So be it," he mumbled, fixing the invading mana in his meta-heart with an internal glare. It was like stepping up to be the challenger in his own body. That was all well and good. It was time to show that void mana who was the boss in there once and for all.
