A trembling fae woman pressed her face against the bars of her cell, her features gaunt and hollow. Her long blond hair hung in matted clumps around her face as greasy strands clung to her thin cheeks. The skin around her eyes had sunk into dark, bruise-colored pits, making her pale green irises appear unnaturally large under the hanging fungal light. But those eyes still held a spark of desperate life even as the rest of her wasted away.

Her body called out to us for help. Patches of her scalp showed through where hair had been torn out, and dried blood crusted along a jagged cut across her forehead. Her lips were cracked and bleeding, split from dehydration or perhaps from screaming. When she opened her mouth to plead again, I saw several teeth were missing, and her gums had receded.

“Help me,” she whispered, stretching a skeletal hand through the bars. “I beg you, please help me….”

Nolan didn’t respond to her, the gunslinger tense. His jaw clenched so tight I could hear his teeth grinding.

‘Nolan, we need to get her out of here!’ Again, no response. He just glared at her. ‘Nolan?!’ I shouted, and then I hopped off his shoulder and slapped his leg with my tail. ‘How about you snap out of it already!’

He jerked his head and looked down at me.

‘I don’t know what’s gotten into you, but you need to get it together!’ I retorted.

He blinked rapidly, seeming to come back to himself. ‘We can’t help her yet,’ he whispered. ‘Not without exposing ourselves and losing any chance of finding that girl.’

‘But we can’t just—’

‘We will come back for her,’ he said firmly, his gaze holding mine with an intensity that put a stop to my protest. I glanced back at the fae woman who had slumped against the bars, her strength spent. ‘Right now, we need to m—’

“She’s still in decent condition,” a loud deep voice boomed from the other end of the hallway. “A working liver, an adequate heart—she’d do just fine for a man of your taste. You know the shelf-life on these fae are top grade. And the magic in their blood keeps the severed organs going for at least a week!”

Nolan yanked me backward into the shadows, his hand covering my muzzle. Two figures appeared at the far end of the corridor, their shadows on the ground giving them away before they appeared at the corner of my eyes. One was unmistakably an orc—broad-shouldered and hulking. The other was slender, probably beastkin or human, and was much shorter, too.

“I don’t care about the quality of her internal organs, or lack thereof,” the slender one said, his voice raspy and high-pitched. “I just want her skin!”

Nolan pulled us into an alcove, pressing his back against the damp stone. I tried to make myself as small as possible as the prisoner fae dodged looking in our direction.

“You truly are a filthy rodent,” the orc snorted and chuckled. “Your trash disposal services would facilitate King Khunum’s projects, but your taste in merchandise leaves much to be desired. Skin rots too quickly without proper preservation.”

He snickered, clicking his fingertips together as he eyed the woman in the cell. “One man’s trash!” As they drew closer, I could now make out the rat-like features of the beastkin. He had a very large head for a body that small, his whiskers twitching as he sniffed the air. “Besides, fae skin makes the finest canvases. So smooth, so... resilient.”

“There’s more where she came from,” the orc grunted, pulling out a ring of what looked like keys. “Healing potions have stopped working on the vessels used for mass production. This one is spent. At this rate, she won’t last very long. All she is now is a meatbag of wasted space. But the boss just brought in fresh stock. Mostly fae, and one runt human girl.” he shook his head. “That feisty bitch nearly bit my fucking pinky off.”

My heart jumped. They were talking about Klepto Girl!

“Speaking of intact,” the rat continued, rubbing his hands together, “when do I get my turn with this one? I want to take my time with my tools!”

“Egh….” the orc grunted.

“She was all mine just a second ago, wasn’t she? If you harvest what’s left of her for the king, then she won’t last long at all for my experiments!”

“Experiments?”

“I like to skin them alive, but slowly!” the ratkin cackled. “It’s art!”

“You’re one sick little shit,” the orc grumbled, jangling his keys as he unlocked the cell. “You can take your little experiments outback. I just washed the floors.”

“Perfect!” the rat squealed, practically bouncing with glee. “I’ll make her last for days. My new preservation techniques are quite remarkable.”

The fae woman’s eyes went wide as the cell door creaked open. She scrambled backward, pressing herself against the far wall, but she was too weak to fight or run. When the rat grabbed her by the arm, she didn’t even scream. Instead, she made a small, broken sound like a dying animal.

“Don’t worry, dear! I’ll make your last days worth whil—” Squelch! That overactive voice box of his was suddenly split two ways after a stiletto dagger sliced through his throat. Blood spurted across the fae woman’s face as the ratkin’s struggled to talk. The orc spun around, but Nolan was already behind him, the healer’s sword the shaman had given me finding its mark between the brute’s ribs.

“Die, you piece of shit,” Nolan grunted under his breath, before he twisted the blade, silencing the orc permanently.

My feet scurried out of our hiding spot before taking slow steps near the pools of blood that began to spread on the ground. I stared in shock at the corpses, thrown off guard by Nolan’s hasty move. ‘You killed them,’ I whispered through our link.

‘Your point?’

…What… was my point? I guess this was my first time seeing Nolan kill something, or someone, that wasn’t a monster. Even though he’d admitted to doing such things, seeing him do it in front of me hit differently. I wasn’t sure if I should feel disturbed or relieved. These men were monsters in their own right, after all. They deserved what they got. And besides, it wasn’t like I hadn’t taken a life the first few minutes I’d been reincarnated.

The fae woman had pressed herself into the corner of her cell, her eyes so wide that I thought they’d pop out of her head. She watched speechlessly as Nolan wiped his blade clean on the orc’s clothes, before walking over to the rat and doing the same.

“We’re not here to hurt you,” he said as he stepped over the beastkin and approached her slowly. She stared at me for a long while, and then her gaze turned to Nolan before she leaped to his feet.

She clung onto him and wept. “Thank you!” Her eyes were bloodshot and dry as tears struggled to stream down her face. “The gods have answered my prayers,” she repeated, her voice hoarse and scratchy.

“There are no gods.” Nolan replied bitterly, and then carefully disentangled himself from her. “Can you walk?”

She nodded weakly, though I doubted she could manage more than a few steps. Her legs were like twigs, knees bulging unnaturally against skin stretched too tight.

“They said…t-they said that I… don’t have long….” She sobbed. “I would like to go back to my town, where I can die in peace.”

Nolan and I exchanged tense looks before turning back to her, where he finally asked, “What did he do to you?” I had a feeling the gunfighter already knew the answer to the question.

The frail fae replied by lifting the bottom of her torn tunic halfway, where a large lateral scar was inches above her navel. “He took it all, and continued to farm my organs until I became… unsatisfactory.”

I felt sick to my stomach. Farming organs? This side business sounded a lot worse than that douchebag’s drug operation!

“How many others?” Nolan asked, his voice tight with controlled rage.

She swayed, leaning against the wall for support. “Dozens. Maybe more. They bring us in batches.” Her hollow eyes drifted down the corridor. “He keeps us in these chambers. Rows and rows of us. The orcs keep us alive, barely, while he harvests what he needs. He uses a blood ritual to… revive organs in places where they are missing inside of us to… mass produce his merchandise.”

“And this newest batch,” he pressed. “Where is he keeping them?"

Her eyes lost focus for a moment, before she whispered, “Lower levels. The new ones... always go to the preparation chambers first.” She gasped, her breaths becoming shallower. “Down the spiral stairs, past the alchemical lab.” She pointed a trembling finger toward the end of the corridor.

Nolan noticed she could barely stand as her body collapsed against the wall. He proceeded to loot the dead, the orc and the ratkin. He pulled out everything he could from their inventory and laid out all of the items in front of her—bread with berry jam, mana restoration vials, healing potions, a canteen of water, and a short blade. He then unfastened the orc’s cloak and draped it around her bony shoulders. “How often do they check this level?” he asked as he offered her the bread and water. Without hesitation, she wolfed down the bread, the poor woman clearly starved. “Is it only that jackass over there paying you visits regularly?” Nolan said, pointing his head to the orc he’d just killed.

She nodded as she chewed her food in a hurry.

“All right. How often? Every three hours? Twice a day? Three times a day?”

She nodded again.

“What is it? Three a day?”

Her head shook more vigorously, the girl engrossed in her food and the canteen of water.

“Does anyone else come down here for rounds? To check up on him or the captives?”

She shook her head no.

“Then, you can wait here.” As soon as he said that, she stopped chewing. “The guard is dead, and you confirmed that there’s no one else who comes down here. Give us a few minutes. Any longer than that, and they might wonder where he is. We’ll be back.” She looked at him worried. The fae didn’t seem too confident that Nolan would come back for her.

“You see this?” He pulled out the Ulmah, the ladybug instantly spreading its wings again on Nolan’s open palm. “It’s a powerful item that’ll lead us to where we need to go. It’s how we found you, and it’ll help us get out of this spare parts factory.”

The woman’s eyes widened at the sight of the Ulmah, its iridescent wings casting tiny rainbows against the damp cell walls. “All I’m asking is for a little time. The truth of the matter is, if we take you, you’ll slow us down. I need you to eat and drink up, find a dark place to hide, and stay here until we come back.”

She swallowed the wet food in her mouth, her bottom lip trembling as she stared at Nolan. “I… am afraid.”

“What’s your name?”

“Elara,” she said softly.

Nolan smirked, offering her his hand. “Nolan Black. Pleasure.”

Elara stared at his extended hand for a moment before taking it in her own trembling one.

“I understand you’re in rough shape, but fae, they are strong people. You have my word, and I will come back for you and take you back home.”

She took the mana restoration vial on the ground and the healing potion and offered them to Nolan. “Please, take them. They’ll be of no use to me.”
