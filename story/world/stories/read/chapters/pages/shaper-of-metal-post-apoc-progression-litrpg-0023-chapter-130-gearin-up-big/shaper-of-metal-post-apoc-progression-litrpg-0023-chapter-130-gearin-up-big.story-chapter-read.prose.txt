Chapter 130: Gearin' Up Big

“Jack.” His uncle said it over the line like he dropped a sack of weights he’d been holding. “Wasn’t sure I’d ever hear from you again.”

Jack avoided reacting to that statement. “Does that mean you’re willing to meet?”

“Ah, well, sure! Of course, son. I can get free in a couple of hours if you-”

“Perfect. I’ll see you then.”

“Wait! Jack, hold up, please. You must know I’ve been worried about ya. And others have asked, you know. Are you alright? You’re not in trouble again?”

“Again,” Jack repeated with bitterness he couldn’t hide. Then, he laughed it off, hoping it didn’t sound hollow. “No, Uncle. There's no trouble at all. I’m doing fabulous, actually. Never better.”

“Good, good. Okay, then! Glad to hear it; that’s a load off, alright. Two hours. I look forward to it, son.”

“You bet. I’ll see you, Terrance.” He hung up and took a deep breath. Rolled his neck.

Try as he might, he couldn’t bring himself to like seeing his uncle again. It wasn’t like he’d ever been a pleasant person to begin with. The combination of various resentments — including the recent ‘betrayal’ element, however understandable it might be — combined with guilt for the same situation, combined with a sense of almost required gratitude for taking him in back in the day… Well, it was exactly the sort of thing a man just avoided having to deal with his entire life, if he could. Fight or flight on your own oozing wound? The choice was pretty obvious.

We gotta do what we gotta do. I hope he actually knows something.

With two hours to kill and desiring to do something more than pace back and forth and worry about his father, Jack first contacted the guy who’d been building his netgun to see if it was ready. Yes, it was — or the parts were all ready, that is. Jack was still going to do his magic on them. He got directions to Samoflange’s workshop and headed over immediately.

It was tucked away in the exceptionally nondescript hallways of inner tower rooms with numbers, not unlike a crowded Origin or downtown office building might be if it housed umpteen small businesses for newly practicing lawyers, web designers, ‘digital creators,’ and marketing whatevers. Samoflange had a decent-sized allocation of space, however.

The door unlocked remotely and opened for him to walk through, at which point he quickly saw that every single bit of the space allocation was accounted for. It was like a miniature warehouse right in front of the door, and there were clear signs of a knocked-out wall that told him Samoflange had once redecorated it to make it like this. Shelves full of parts, tools, tubs of parts, and things broken down into parts were everywhere, as were tables full of things either in the middle of being broken down or assembled. It smelled of grease, sawdust, sweat, and chemically-sanitized shop rags.

The last was probably because there was a huge pallet of chemically-sanitized shop rags right inside the doorway, with one plastic bag of them ripped open and spilling the red fabric onto the floor. It wasn’t a bad smell, per se, even to his enhanced senses. It mitigated the sweat.

Hrm. Oil, grease, hydraulic fluid, cleaning solvents, heavy metals. Shit, I think my Nonian senses actually like that stuff in particular now. Good thing, I guess!

A grinning young man strode up, clad in well-worn and stained overalls, a long-sleeved shirt, and oversized goggles as a hat, currently wiping smudged hands on a shop rag. That hadn’t helped his clothing or his face, clearly. “Ferronaut? Nice to meet you.” He offered a fist-bump — the common, even instinctive consideration dirty-working folk paid to the obviously much-cleaner while they were on-the-job.

Jack offered his hand instead and said, “My hands are getting dirty today, so no worries. And call me Jack.”

Samoflange nodded respectfully and converted the fistbump into a handshake. “You got it. Big Iron is good, too.”

Jack chuckled. “I’ll think about it.”

“Anyway, right this way.” He headed in the opposite direction he’d come from, at a brisk pace. “I got all the pieces in one place, aside from a few it’s best not to screw with.”

Indeed, he was brought to a work table with a bunch of mostly metal pieces splayed out in an organized way, lying on the polished wood. It looked something like a disassembled gun to Jack, though with way more parts than usual. At least, he could see hints of the final shape. Perhaps what a laser would look like this way? He wasn’t sure about that one. There was more material there than he expected. Big frames.

He also saw many, many instances of the payload that would go in the cartridges — that is, the metal nets. Improving their default properties, particularly for tensile strength, would shortcut his follow-up control powers. Wonderful!

All he could do was wait to see the final piece to have it all make sense.

“Alright, this won’t take long,” Jack said as he channeled his memorite and collapsed it into the first piece. “Maybe a few minutes.” Memorizing the piece itself, Jack then subtly reorganized the structure, adding in the unique strengthening his powers could apply, through interlacing branches with anchors at extradimensional angles. Absorption was also added for additional impact strength.

And then it was done, and he moved on to the next piece.

“Can you keep track of what’s altered?” Samoflange asked. His goggles were down, and he was watching closely. “I can’t tell at a glance.”

“Oh, I can tell, don’t worry.”

“It’s too bad I didn’t know the performance improvement. I maybe could’ve translated that to the weapon.”

“Eh, no biggie. Room for the Mark 2 version one day, right? After all, you don’t want to go into debt with nothing to equalize eventually.”

“Heeey, good point! Yeah, we can do that. But after testing out the toy for a while, so you can give me more useful feedback.”

“Roger, roger."

Jack went piece by piece swiftly, adding in durability to what would be his assembled future netgun. On the plus side, he was also memorizing the pieces if he had to replace something.

Once he was done, Samoflange exclaimed, “Great!” and rolled up a tall adjustable stool to the table, throwing down what looked like a soldering kit. “I’ll put this together in a jiffy. Most are fitted or screwed together, but I wanted strength on a few things that need a little more custom effort.” He paused and popped his goggles up. “Uh, how much time do you have? Can you, I dunno, improve generically every part on that table right there?” He pointed to a table, perhaps a meter away, with various metal parts on it.

Jack took a look and brought his hand up for a thumbs-up. “Piece of cake. Makes us even, then?”

“Considering it's damn near priceless, yeah, I’d say so. Even if you got just a fraction done.”

“Works for me.”

Jack walked up to the other table and took a look at what was there. He mostly had no clue what he was looking at, but he saw some obvious impact-testing plates. Varieties of metal pieces, like copper and gold. There were also some cables and chains, which would particularly benefit from his Tensile technique. Quite a bit of this would be to test, he realized.

He smiled as he considered the work and the netgun. It was oddly satisfying to pay this way.

A labor trade. Yep, yep, ahhhh… Is there anything more manly? Might regrow some hair on my chest from this.

Amused at the thought — hopeless as it was with his customized physiology — Jack got to it, starting on the bigger pieces and moving progressively smaller, memorizing, adding in basic durability, not thinking about it much, and moving on to the next. Ritualized, steady, even work, quickly turning instinctual. It was part-mental, part-physical, with all the wonderful feel of working with one’s hands. Control and Augment use was just like that. Transmute was a little weirder, but even more satisfying, because it felt like magic.

It was enough work to break a sweat. Halfway through, Samoflange put down an Infused energy drink on his table, which Jack thanked him for and happily partook of.

Augment: Absorption has improved to 0.7!

When the last pieces on the table were finished — four identical parts of some sort of load-bearing elbow joint — Jack wiped his brow and checked the time. 45 minutes, on something like 300 pieces. Not bad considering the varying sizes and complexities, and the need for memorization.

He turned around to see Samoflange already tinkering with some other contraption with its guts out, and sitting on a display stand was the netgun in all its glory. There was more there than he expected.

It was a thick-barreled beauty of gunmetal and blue steel highlights, now a bit longer in the tooth than the original schematic, enough that a non-ANP or very strong-wristed fellow would find that thing heavy and annoying to swing around one-handed. It also had a very bulky load under the barrel, immediately outside the trigger guard.

“You two-shotted it?!” Jack exclaimed excitedly. He crossed the space quickly to inspect it more closely.

“Yup!” Samoflange hopped up, pushed his goggles up onto his head, and grinned between Jack and the weapon, hands on his hips. “What do you think? Can’t get more than a second load with the bulk needed, but as you can see…”

“There’s a second option.” Jack looked at the second part of the equation: a massive drum, fitted with a forward handle that would be close to the barrel, to help with bracing with the added weight and bulk.

“That’s right! Take out the magazine, and it fits right in there. Side note: the CO2 cartridge is housed in the grip, and you’ll get max power for around twenty shots at tweaked-up power. I could modify it for lower power to get more, but I doubt you want that.”

Nodding, Jack picked up the pistol from the stand and inspected it. It had a safety switch, but Jack still asked, “Is it loaded?”

“Nope! Point it wherever you want.”

Jack held it out, two-handed and one-handed. It was fine. Heavy but not quite as bad as it looked, and just as he thought, his general improved strength had no trouble at all with it.

“I figured you should load it yourself to get a feel,” Samoflange continued. “Oh, and, ah, if you’re reloading in the field, I have it where you can just shove a firing cartridge down the barrel for speed. It gives a little click. Attaching a new magazine for reloading would be pointless, sadly, as it would be one shot. Otherwise, to reload two shots, you have to remove the magazine, insert a cartridge by either opening, and also load the magazine.”

“Noted,” Jack said, grinning happily at his new toy. “The initial two shots thing is worth all the costs, I think.”

“That’s what I figured. It’s fast to cycle, too, but watch out, because there is a teensy bit of a delay with the sheer bulk aspect of this shit. Relevant for ANP perceptions.”

“Gotcha.”

“Anyway! Let me show you how to load this puppy, and you can test it out at my tragically short firing range. And don’t worry! Almost everything here is entirely legal and authorized, and that definitely includes almost everything done at the firing range, including most definitely using this net launcher.”

Chuckling at ‘almost everything,’ Jack replied, “Hell yeah, brother — let’s do it!”

After learning all the little minor details of operating the gun, including loading and detaching the magazine, Jack and Samoflange proceeded to the range, which was separated by a heavy curtain and only the length of the facility, which wasn’t terribly great. Nonetheless, it was clearly surrounded by a reinforced structure. The target was a stand with a gelatin figure on top.

Jack fired net after net at it and watched it work smashingly without any manipulation. By design, the cartridge broke away past the barrel, unleashing the net to carry most of the momentum forward. Being metal nets, the wire-like fibers didn’t look like they would feel too gentle, so their use against softie targets was quite questionable. Not the hugest deal, because Jack could simply grab it with his power and mitigate the danger where necessary.

For the purposes of quick-clearing his 2-meter ‘limited zone’ with metal, it was fantastic, and the net was great as something to control for entangling purposes, because it was going to be damned hard to avoid.

Samoflange was enthusiastic and insisted on being a guinea pig as well, offering up his body to get netted up. Jack reluctantly agreed, on the condition that he faced away from the net.

It worked without a hitch, wrapping him up completely. Jack did a bit of powers follow-up, especially considering he was ready to do so just for the safety concerns. The net was super durable, and his ‘victim’s’ attempts to tear or get out were futile. Jack could even lift and levitate him in the damn thing. Samoflange got a kick out of all of it, presumably because he was seeing his ingenuity in action.

It made him think these were actually great if someone were ever falling, too. Wrap ‘em up and reduce their speed as slowly as you could. Nice, even distribution on tap.

All-in-all, the netgun was more effective than he’d hoped. A net was a devastating thing to have flying at you, but normally a pain to drag around and even slow to unspool and toss. The gun simplified it. It was a bit awkward to get used to manipulating with Control, but he’d definitely be practicing.

Step into my parlor, said the spider to the fly. Damn. Could’ve dropped that line during the last battle if I had this thing then. Hehe.

Jack finished up by watching Samoflange reassemble a cartridge with the net tucked inside. He watched this done half-a-dozen times, and even then, when he tried it himself, it wasn’t right. It was a complex contraption in itself, and a sophisticated physical engineering feat on the part of Samoflange. It would take way more practice doing it conventionally, so he planned on replicating it with his powers and their special memorization capacity. At least he’d gained a lot of basic understanding of the mechanical process, which, without a doubt, would help the replication, cheat-code-assisted or not.

In any case, Samoflange was fine keeping him stocked, even for additional training fires — probably because that would mean guaranteed contact and access to his material improvement capabilities. They parted with a promise for a case of fifty delivered as soon as tomorrow. Jack told him to wait for his message. He didn’t tell Samoflange details, but he wasn’t sure where he’d actually be living by tomorrow. His prior quarters were soon to be obsolete, after all.

A bulky contraption in a holster at his hip, carrying the rest in a duffel bag, Jack walked and whistled his way back to his quarters. He soon found himself humming the words to that ‘Big Iron’ song.

Oh, shit. This seriously contributes to that nickname, doesn’t it?

He laughed at himself at how slow he’d realized it. Samoflange had even mentioned it, seeing the reality far sooner. It hadn’t even been on his mind when choosing his intranet handle on that message board. Well, wasn’t that clueless of him?

Shrugging it off, he stowed the weapon for the time being, took a shower, grabbed a quick snack, and otherwise got ready to head to Operations. It was time to lose his precious, beautiful abs and go back to the old Jack for a little while.

He sobered up quickly. The gun had been a great distraction from his troubles, but the issue ahead was now looming again. The far darker shadow of his father cast over him, as it had so many times before.

The farm was looking better than ever, as was the veritable mansion that now sat upon it, where a destroyed manor had once been. It was perhaps half again the size previously, and looked like it had been built on a sturdy concrete foundation. Nonetheless, it had the same general look as before, as if it were a new and improved version of what Jack’s grandfather had built.

One thing was new — a bona fide watchtower just outside the fields, and apparently manned by a guard.

He doesn’t want to get taken by surprise again, I see.

Jack generously tipped his cabby and exited the vehicle. It took off soon thereafter. He’d been advised to take one instead of any other pretense. People might suspect he was connected to something, but it was logical he might not want to advertise it on Eden, at least. He was wearing some old clothes as an ‘undercover’ outfit, an old, but well-cleaned jacket over it. He had faux-flesh added here and there, sculpting him with a ‘dad bod,’ as promised.

His uncle was outside to meet him, button-up shirt on and wearing his typical hat, all smiles as he walked up and had his arms out wide. His beard was newly trimmed. They embraced, and Terrance muttered, “By the stars, you bulked up! Been working out, eh? Shit, you even look taller!”

Jack planted on a smile and shrugged as they parted from the hug. “I get that. I think it’s just a perception thing. But yeah, I decided to get into shape again. Still working on the shape, mind. Damn protein regimens. Still figuring it out.”

His uncle nodded vaguely and amiably. “Well, come on in! Just you and me, but I got some grub, if you want.”

“Sure thing.”

Passing through the front door, Jack saw the steel gun room was still there, now extra bolted to a reinforced floor.

The place is almost a fortress now. Guess I can’t blame him. People have died here twice.

The dining table had fried chicken and various sides on it, obviously from a 'place,' due to the paper receptacles. But it was more of a home-cooked type of place, and a bit familiar. Once he got close, he recognized it.

“Oh shit, Maybelles!” Jack exclaimed. “It’s been ages. Are they running somewhere? I tried eating there a year ago, and…” He trailed off and shook his head.

His uncle grinned and nodded as he hung his hat on a chair and started rolling up his sleeves. “Newly reopened. Same recipes, same everything. Maybelle recovered from the cancer, and now she’s sworn to teach her niece every little trick, who’ll take over after a while. And can you believe it, her name is May!”

Jack laughed. “Somehow, I can.”

“Anyway, pop a squat and have at it, son, for old times' sake. I’m damn sure about to do the same. I kept my appetite, but I ain’t waitin’ no more, ya hear me?”

“I do, Uncle. I definitely do.”

Soon, they were both digging in, keeping their silence for a spell as they satiated their nostalgia for greasy chicken, mashed potatoes, coleslaw, and other such timeless staples. For drinks, they had cold beers.

Eventually, his uncle leaned back, wiping his hands on a napkin, and asked, “So, tell me what you can, son — what the hell have you been up to? Other than musclin’ up.”

Jack chewed, swallowed, and washed his food down with a swig of beer. He sighed and shrugged. “I have to be vague, I’m afraid. I hope you understand.”
