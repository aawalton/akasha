Stomach-swooping fear goes through me as the serpent approaches faster than we can ready our attacks.

Then, a second before it's due to strike the ship, it sinuously twists almost completely back on itself, grabs its hatchling, and dives deep. Its silvery scaled form fades out of sight in the murky water almost as quickly as it had struck towards us.

We stare at the water, tension thrumming through the air despite the sudden calm. The same question no doubt echoes in all of our minds. Where has it gone – and will it be back?

Josiah comes to stand beside me, staring into the water, his expression tight, his fingers clenching the railing hard enough that I’m concerned he'll leave indentations when he releases it.

"That thing has proven itself a danger," he says quietly, his voice no less intense for its lack of volume, the flash of something that looks awfully like betrayal in his eyes. "And yet you handed our best bargaining chip away to it."

"It hasn't turned around and attacked us yet," I point out, though I know it's a weak argument, as his snort acknowledges. The key word in it – yet. Feeling driven to continue, I ask with irritation colouring my voice, "What do you think would have happened, Josiah, if we'd threatened the hatchling and then it managed to get free by itself, or died because of the unstable Energy within it? What kind of bargaining chip would we have had then?"

"Why would it have died?" Josiah demands. "A hostage is only as good as its health. We would have taken reasonable care of it – and ensured it could not escape."

I stare flatly at him.

"Did you not see the way Energy was exploding out of it? That was uncontrolled – either an issue with the hatchling itself or a consequence of the manastorm. And it was only getting worse with its stress and fear. I strongly suspect that if we’d tried to keep it captive, it would have either died, or killed someone soon enough. And then its parent would most likely have sought revenge against us regardless, even if we weren't the true cause of its death."

"And now, do you think the situation is any better?" Josiah challenges angrily. "What stops it from wanting to take revenge for what it sees as our capture of it – however unknowing on our part that may have been? At least holding it hostage would have given us something in the meantime."

"Returning its hatchling to it may have bought us goodwill that we can use," I point out.

Josiah twists his head to stare at me directly.

"You can't intend to make an ally of this beast," he says incredulously. "It's attacked us twice."

"Only in defence of its hatchling the second time," I reply, gesturing at the suddenly calm water beside the ship. "If it had been attacking us for any other reason, why would it have simply disappeared?" I hold his gaze steadily. "If I can make an ally of a Tier three who clearly considers this bay to be its hunting grounds, then I will – because I believe our people as a whole will be far better off for it."

He looks about to respond when shouts from the quarterdeck draw our attention to the stern. No words need to pass between us before we're both moving. There, Josiah takes an audible breath and my heart rises into my mouth – the serpent has surfaced again, its head and part of its body visible above the water.

"Captain–" Josiah begins, his voice tense, clearly intending to order some sort of defensive or offensive measure.

I place my hand on his arm with controlled strength.

"Wait," I say, with more confidence than I feel. "Look – it's not moving to attack us."

And it isn't. If anything, its body language is watchful, but surprisingly non-confrontational.

Josiah folds his arms, his expression discontented.

“We should attack it before it can attack us – again,” he argues, but I can tell that he knows his attempts are doomed to failure. He knows me well enough by now to know that I won’t fire on a target that’s not attacking us – not without significantly more evidence of a threat than I’ve had here.

“I want to negotiate with it,” I tell him. The soft sigh that leaves his mouth indicates that I’ve just confirmed what he suspected – but wished wouldn’t happen. “Trust me,” I urge, holding his gaze with mine.

I see his internal struggle, his reluctance to do it, not because I haven’t proven myself to him, but because it’s the lives of his crew at stake here.

Finally, I see his shoulders soften.

“You’d better be right,” he warns me, and though there’s an attempt at levity in his tone, it falls flat due to just how serious his eyes are.

“I will do what I can to make sure I am,” I promise. “Still, there’s no point in taking chances. Ready the defensive measures all the same, just in case the negotiation goes badly."

Josiah looks far happier with that; perhaps a little too much so. "Only activate them on my word, though," I urge him. "If you act too quickly, you might sour a negotiation that could otherwise have succeeded."

Josiah is quiet for a moment, then gives me a nod that finally looks accepting rather than mutinous. I’ll take it. I return the nod, then spring up into the air.

I'm not sure if I’ll be able to maintain my flight during a Battle of Wills, I say, reaching out mentally to Bastet. It’s not actually something I’ve tested – perhaps that’s an oversight. Fortunately, I have back-up. Would you be willing to catch me if I begin to fall?

I could carry you from the start, she suggests.

I don't want the serpent to think I'm trying to attack it, and having you too close probably wouldn't be very diplomatic. Bastet is a Tier three, after all. The serpent might not be a good match up for her, but that doesn’t mean she’s not a valid threat for it.

Bastet acknowledges that wordlessly and hangs back as I fly closer to the sea beast. Still in my storage tattoo, Lathani and Ninja send a questioning wave down our Bonds, clearly wondering what's happening. I direct them to Bastet for an explanation and turn my full attention to the serpent, closing the distance.

I trigger the Battle of Wills as soon as I'm within range, trusting Bastet to catch me if I suddenly start falling. To my surprise, the torrent of resistance pressing against me is not nearly as powerful as I was expecting. I remember the last time I faced Kalanthia in this space – it was like meltwater rushing down a sheer cliff face, blasting me away. I'd had to hold on with every inch of concentration, digging my metaphorical fingers into whatever passes for the floor in this space just to avoid being thrown out immediately.

But that was before I even reached Moriax and I’ve levelled up several times since then. I’ve also gone through another challenge in my soul space, and formed a Dominate Bond with Nicholas which, even if he wasn't resisting me at the time, was still a significant undertaking. I've grown considerably in Willpower, and only now is it becoming clear just how much effect that has on my use of my main Class Skill.

In fact, I’m almost surprised to realise that I’m genuinely confident I could force the Dominate Bond on this sea serpent even without its agreement. And if it proves an ongoing threat to my settlement, that may be something I have to do. But hopefully the serpent will be reasonable.

As I press forward, it becomes clear this isn't the serpent's first Battle of Wills – or, more likely, that domain battle skills translate well to this arena. Unlike the single flow of resistance I'm used to facing, this acts more like the serpent itself, coiling and shifting, striking when I least expect it with both speed and precision. I'm forced to take more than one step back when a strike catches me off guard.

But I'm not the only one who has learned to fight with metaphysical senses. Concentrating, I imagine a large, rectangular, convex shape before me to act like the prow of the ship I’ve spent so much time sailing upon. It takes me a few attempts to force my imagination into reality – well, the soul version of reality – but it’s not long before I manage to construct a clumsy shield in front of me.

My shield falters almost immediately when struck by one of the serpent’s hits, but I just refocus, the shield reforming more quickly than it appeared the first time. This version lasts two blows before it disintegrates, a calculated strike bursting through one of the weak points in the top corner. Learning from the distracted focus, I firm up that weakness and reform it.

Little by little, I begin making more steps forward for every step I'm pushed back, my shield lasting longer and longer. Fortunately, the serpent has nothing like the power its physical body wielded against the ship.

Finally, I reach halfway between us and for the first time become able to feel the emotions of the beast opposite me. As is usual at this point, there's a good deal of fear. But not only that. Calculation, wariness, and even a hint of gratitude are also tangible. The last is a little surprising – since I doubt the serpent is grateful for me initiating a Battle of Wills, I can only conclude it's grateful that I released its hatchling.

As I press step by step towards the Tier three, I decide that's probably a reasonable way to open the conversation.

“Is your hatchling all right?”

The serpent's emotions coil and congeal into caution. I feel it hesitate on its next strike and take two quick steps forward while the resistance is light.

What do you care for my hatchlings? the sea serpent asks, warily.

“It didn't look well when I saw it. It was caught in the manastorm, wasn't it?”

The emotions around me briefly twist with both fury and fear. The serpent stays silent, but its emotions tell me everything I need to know.

“Perhaps I could help,” I offer. “I have skills in healing, and I've worked considerably with Energy and with those who carry too much of it.”

That familiarity I'd noticed earlier – when I'd sensed the snakeling’s Energy through my mana sight – makes sense to me now I’ve had a little time to think about it. The way the Energy saturated its body was somewhat reminiscent of looking at Noir and Ivor just after they'd hatched, before I'd Bonded with them. The question will be whether this hatchling is more like Ivor, who maintained his internal matrix, or Noir, who lost his.

On the plus side, the baby serpent doesn't seem to have suffered obvious physical mutations – though internal mutations I couldn't see are another matter.

The Tier three's brief flicker of hope is quickly masked with suspicion.

And what would you want in return for this generous offer?

I'm mildly surprised to discover this beast is capable of sarcasm. None of the others have evidenced the same, though they’ve certainly shown humour.

“I would need a Bond with the hatchling. A Bond like the one we will have.”

The serpent's presence coils in agitation, the resistance against me rising and falling rhythmically. Even at its strongest, braced as I am, it doesn't push me back – and I make steady headway each time the resistance drops away.

A Bond. An entangling weed, it practically hisses. And you think we will simply agree to one? With you?

Its incredulity is clear. By this point I'm well into the final third of the distance between us, and each detail of the serpent's presence is sharp. Fortunately, its form here is considerably smaller than its physical body – its head only slightly larger than my own, frozen in a semi-coiled position that must mirror its posture outside.

“You've attacked us twice,” I point out. “I recognise the second time was likely to retrieve your hatchling, but I'm sure you can understand why I wouldn't want this situation repeated. Unless you wish to leave this area entirely, or to be killed as we defend ourselves, a Bond between us is the best option. Surely you can see that.”

The fluctuation of emotions around me is even more incredulous than before. I can understand why — from its perspective, it's hard to see what benefit it's getting. I shrug and take another step forward.

“The majority of beasts who have Bonded with me have either chosen to stay or left better than they arrived. Given the hostility of our initial meetings, I simply want certainty that you'll hold to your side of the agreement. If we'd met under different circumstances I might have offered a different kind of Bond. But this is the way it is.”

I take two more steps and draw level with the serpent, close enough to touch it.

“Besides,” I add firmly, “you should be able to feel that I could force this on you if I wished. The fact that I'm not – that I'm giving you the choice – doesn't that mean something?”

A choice between death, losing my home, or entanglement, the sea serpent scoffs. Not much of a choice.

“Yet, a choice nonetheless. Consider this too: I had a choice earlier,” I point out. “A choice to hold your hatchling hostage, to kill it – or to free it.”

I let the silence do the rest of the work. From the way the serpent's emotions suddenly freeze and then begin shifting in a more thoughtful rhythm, my point, only partially verbalised as it was, has still hit home.

My sense of the space around us tells me my time here isn't unlimited.

“Make your choice quickly, please,” I urge. “Bond with me and remain in your home, and we can seek a way of living that benefits us both – or choose no Bond and face either exile or death at our hands. And also remember – if you Bond with me and allow me to Bond with your hatchling, even temporarily in its case, then I might be able to see – and heal – what the manastorm did to it.”

I wait, tense despite myself, uncertain even now about what the serpent will choose.
