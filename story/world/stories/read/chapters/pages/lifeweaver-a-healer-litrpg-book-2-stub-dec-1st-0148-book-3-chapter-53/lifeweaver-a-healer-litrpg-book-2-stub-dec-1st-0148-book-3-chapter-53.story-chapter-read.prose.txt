Luke's former party, with Nash at the helm, was headed to another portal, their fourth for the day, so they parted ways after comparing their spoils from the masses of dead goblins. Some credits, a little XP in Luke's case, and an interesting tidbit of information. Their discussion soon showed that while Luke and Curtis received the same Goblin Slayer title as a reward for their efforts, the others did not. Instead, they got the standard five thousand credits. It was the first time that had happened, at least to Luke, who figured it had to be a result of his and Curtis coming face to face with goblins before.

"Anyone there you can teleport to?" Curtis asked, pulling something new from his inventory.

They were running in the general direction of the building where they'd found the object,

"Milla is there," Luke said, taking a closer look at what appeared to be a scooter. "You got the same upgrade as me and Hannah?"

Without stopping, Curtis unfurled the thing and showed that it was indeed a scooter, but it looked like something out of a sci-fi movie, all white, sleek curves, and futuristic lights. "Hm? Oh. No. This is from the shop."

He got on it mid-stride and kept pace with Luke's run. "Nice, isn't it?"

"Didn't know you could buy those."

"Me neither. You couldn't even, not until this morning."

"The items change?"

Curtis shrugged. "Guess the system provides what we need. This one runs on monster cores. See you there."

With that, Curtis sped off at a speed for which he really should've used a helmet. That couldn't be safe. Luke stopped and shook his head at the marvel of core-powered transportation, mumbling, "The system provides."

Even at that speed, Curtis couldn't compete with Luke's mode of transportation. Unfortunately, the particular method Luke used meant arriving like he'd just been run over by a car, or worse. Shuddering at the thought of pain to come, he settled himself and closed his eyes, preparing to fold his mind in on itself to reach the needed meditative trance. Then he thought better of it.

Things were in motion. Terrible things. He wouldn't always get the chance to find a quiet spot and settle down for the ride. Better to start practicing for when he needed to get away from or travel to someone while in duress.

Settling into a run, Luke healed some lingering damage in his chest, extremities, and even his head. With that done, he set off at a full run. Curtis was long gone, but catching up was not the idea here. Luke planned to jump ahead, but not so much in a physical sense.

The threads that make up the system weave were always there, like a nagging sensation that he couldn't scratch. Even so, the weave eluded him most of the time, unless he concentrated. Concentration in this case was a little more involved than brute-forcing the issue with focus. Improving Focus and Willpower, or even Intelligence and Wisdom, hadn't done a thing to make this particular task easier. No, this was all Luke and his ability to control his mental state. Perhaps he should have attended that mindfulness seminar back in school. Alas, he had to work with what he had.

It was a pain. Not in the physical sense. Trying to calm himself while running and extending Threads of Mana for practice was just hard. Real hard. Starting out with a more achievable goal and a lighter challenge would perhaps have been a more optimal way to go about things, but that had never been his way. Luke had to admit, at least to himself, that he didn't always choose the smartest route and make the most thought-through decisions. That was fine. It was. Sometimes you just need to fly by the seat of your pants and trust your gut. In this instance, it was a pain, though.

In the end, after several failed attempts, he found his mind calming down a little. It was enough to glimpse the weave. Even then, it swayed, and the image faltered. After another few grueling attempts, Luke got it back. This time, he held on to it. That was the first hurdle, the easy part. When, after a lot of cursing and having to re-do the entire process twice more, he found the concentration to pick out Milla's thread among the infinite number running in all directions.

By that time, he'd arrived at the building on foot. Luke stopped, panting not from physical exhaustion but from the mental strain. Why did everything have to be so difficult? Sighing, he entered the building. No use weavestepping at that point. The robe had just about stitched itself back together again after the goblins. More blood? No thanks.

The room was just about full. Milla and Curtis talked to Peter from ISG and someone new Luke hadn't met before, a woman in her early thirties with the sort of bob-cut you didn't see much past the roaring twenties flapper culture. She wore shock-red colored lipstick to contrast the black-dyed hair and dark makeup around her eyes. Eye shadow? Luke could never remember what the different types were called. To add to the eye-catching appearance, she wore a tight, sleek black dress that showed generous cleavage. It was almost like staring into a colorful flytrap.

There were plenty of other people in the room, both those he knew and those he didn't, but his eyes kept going back to the woman with Peter. He got an inkling.

Integrated Lara. Architect. Level 18.

It was her. He'd seen a lot of what she'd accomplished, like The Gauntlet and the training portals. This was a class, er, person he wanted to meet.

"You're late," Curtis said.

"There's still a lot of time left on that thing, isn't there?" Luke said, pointing at the hovering little present left behind by the Singers.

"This is Lara," Peter said, introducing the Architect.

Luke took her hand, small and a little limp, and shook it. "I saw. Why did you dungeonize this place? I'm Luke, by the way."

"Lara," she said. "Charmed." Her voice had a purring quality to it that made him shiver. He'd best be on his guard with this one. Still, she was polite enough when she explained that it gave her knowledge of everything within the space and that it allowed her to manipulate objects within it.

"Also, please don't call it dungeonize," she said after answering the question.

"Dungeoinification?"

She shuddered, which did interesting things to her cleavage. No, stop it. Bonk!

"It is called System Architecture Rebalancing. I call it SAR:ing."

"If you two are done," Milla interrupted, "perhaps you can tell us why you brought us here, Peter?"

Peter, who'd been talking to a man in a dark suit and sunglasses, turned back to the conversation. "Yes, of course. Lara, why don't you share your findings with everyone? We figured it was best to tell everyone at the same time, so there would be no accusations of favoritism."

Everyone in the room turned toward Lara, and she began to speak as Luke spotted Nick off in the corner of the room, together with the rest of his party. The nun was missing. A bright-eyed young woman had taken her place. She was bald. Strange.

"We know this object, and we can suppose all others like it, were left by those we now call the Singers. I have found that this is a crafted object made by someone with a type of engineer-like class."

"It's a bomb, isn't it?" someone in the crowd asked, his voice trembling a little.

Lara considered the question for long enough that a lot of people started fidgeting, but in the end, she shook her head. "Not in the sense that it will explode like a conventional bomb, no. I believe that it will cause some sort of widespread effect that will cover an area larger than the city itself."

"What effect?" Milla asked.

"I don't know. What I do know is that it won't be a beneficial one."

Curtis snorted. "No shit."

"Can you tell us anything more?" Luke asked.

She gave him a look. "One more thing. I've been able to measure its decay rate. It's stable."

"Can you explain that like I'm five years old?" that same man asked. "I might be dumb as shit, but I don't understand a word coming out of your mouth."

"My apologies," Lara said. "What that means in this context is two-fold. First, the countdown is a lie. By my calculation, the activation time is two days earlier than what the description tells us. Second, it can be triggered manually at any time."

"Shit," Milla said.

"Shit," Luke agreed.

"There is something else. I'm afraid my readings tell me that Singers didn't create this object themselves."

Curtis's voice was even and cold. "Someone helped them? Who?"

"I cannot say specifically what individual did, but it was a human Integrated. Something about pairing the frequencies of whatever the object is set to release to human-specific effects."

The room fell silent. In that silence, Milla stepped forward. "A human? Are you sure?"

Lara looked at her as if the question was an insult. "Yes, I'm sure. More than one, if I were to hazard a guess."

Luke didn't know what to think. How would anyone betray their own kind to these Intruders? It boggled the mind. A sound reached through the once-again silent room, one Luke, at first, didn't recognize. Then, he turned to Curtis, who was grinding his teeth so hard they might pop right out of his skull.

"I. Will. Make. Them. Pay," Curtis growled, each word clipped and dangerous. "They. Will. All. Pay."
