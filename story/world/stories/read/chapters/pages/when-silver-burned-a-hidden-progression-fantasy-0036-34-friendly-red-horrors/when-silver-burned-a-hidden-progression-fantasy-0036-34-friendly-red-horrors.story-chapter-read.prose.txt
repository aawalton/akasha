The pain wasn't even the worst. No, what made Kalsin miserable for the following days was going through the everyday motions, unable to focus on anything but the sharp agony whenever he touched a thing.

Though he'd tried to go slow and careful, his punishment had resulted in four torn fingernails and most of his fingers completely without skin around the tips. The bleeding was short lived, but at least the fluids had made the mud a little more pliable. Orders or not, he had stopped well before midnight because, while he knew the lieutenant wanted to make an example out of him, he also valued his fingers remaining firmly attached to his body. His one enjoyment during that night was listening to Fals' voice waft through the evening, cheers, laughter reaching his ears as time had passed and the lights dimmed.

Though he had finished early, he hadn't joined the tail end of the celebration afterwards. Turning in for the night seemed like a better idea, and yet it was a long while until he had fallen asleep with both hands pulsing in pain. Shutter, who'd sat out the party for his own reasons, helped him clean and wrap his hands the next morning. What Kalsin appreciated even more was the silence from Shutter as he sat there, bandaging. No words, no veiled judgment behind kind words nor pretense of sympathy came from him. It made his injuries just a touch more bearable.

Nobody gave Kalsin a hard time about his little stunt, at least not to his face. He quickly got tired keeping track of how people reacted to what Brownweather deemed justice well served; he'd seen everything from speculative glances thrown at him, to a spectrum of smiles of pity ranging from fake to real ones, and a fan favorite, jokes about a revolutionary 'mitten sword stance'. Kalsin had smiled along, but found himself withdrawing from people when given the chance.

His chafed mood must've shown, because Crawl, Littleguy, and the recruits he usually spent time with quickly stopped bringing up the incident. It was considerate of them. If it wasn't for the constant companion of dull sting in his hands, Kalsin might've made an effort to show gratitude. Instead, he'd just become similarly stingy.

They continued their journey toward the Old the next morning. The atmosphere among the recruits was an odd mixture of relief, anticipation, and uncertainty. There was no shortage of stupid stories born the previous night and told in a dozen different variations, some of which wildly unlikely to have actually happened. Like, how four cups of ale deep, Uggo had started giving free massages on an improvised bench they'd covered with blankets when he'd revealed his hidden talent. Fals had, allegedly, bedded both the drummer who led the percussion for her songs and the settlement's major. Both at the same time, according to her. Bonds had been forged, pent up tension had been released, and now they had nothing left but to walk into the mouth of the waiting beast.

Lukewarm, desolate winds accompanied their advance through the flatlands stretching between the two lines of defense, a dark tundra with rustic highlights going on and on whatever direction they turned. Abandoned trenches, bits of piled up and broken earth spoke of the people that had fought and died before them, remnants from old battles as the border between the Ember Republic and the Eastern Expanse shifted back and forth throughout the decades.

"Lookout spots," one of the soldiers escorting them called from the back when they passed a lone, circular earthwork, rising a good five feet above the ground like a small island protruding from a sea of red grass. "Those manning the lookouts keep watch, alert the older boys if they see movement. Sneaky fuckers, the Veiled are. We keep the grass trimmed around the lookouts so they can't blend in. But the front has moved up since. You'll see."

The recruits spent the walk in silence, listening to the occasional bit of wisdom shared by some of the more talkative soldiers over the whistling wind. It was late afternoon when they stopped by a small spring hidden in the shade of a thin, white copse of trees. Fractures aplenty, their horses never had a shortage of water, but human appetite still held a preference for crystalline fresh drinks after a long and sweaty day. And so it came as welcome news that, rather than pushing through the last stretch toward their destination, Brownweather ordered them to set up camp for the night.

Kalsin took care of Sap as the group split up, some clearing spots for the tents while others went to wash out the dirt that had snuck past the folds of their clothes. The man handling their mounts was decent enough at his job, gentle with the animals, and he always gave Kalsin a friendly nod when he checked on Sap. He never questioned why Kalsin was there so often. A recruit brushing off his bahten, using the little free time he had for extra labor, was a picture hard to imagine. And yet. And yet.

Maybe there's a universal understanding between people who, at some point during their lives, had formed a connection with an animal that was more than simply pet and owner. Kalsin liked to think so. A pinch of honesty in a dishonest world.

When Kalsin finished up, he joined Pike and Crawl sitting some distance away from Littleguy, who was busy hammering in the last few pins around their tent. The camp was loud and lively all around them. Kalsin sat next to Pike, looking from a fidgeting Crawl to a distant Littleguy stomping his heel at a pin with more force than necessary. Or it could've been a regular stomp. It was hard to tell with someone like Littleguy.

"There goes another," Crawl said, one leg bouncing to an unheard rhythm, his eyes following something in the distance. "Damn, that was— okay, does anyone actually know if she's hitting the targets? Can barely see it land from here."

Following his gaze, Kalsin saw a figure at the edge of the camp as she put another arrow on the string of her bow, drew, held for a breath, then released. Several groups were enjoying the show of marksmanship, though the blonde gave no outward sign that she was aware of being the center of attention.

"Sure she is," Kalsin said, humoring him. He gave Pike a questioning glance.

Pike made a large spoon of jégberry porridge disappear in his mouth, chewed thoughtfully, then shrugged in response.

"Something off about that one, Crawl," Littleguy said. He had finished setting up their tent, and after securing the flaps to remain open, he joined their group with a blanket held around his neck like a cape. "When the lieutenant offers you a choice between your real name and whatevershit he cooks up, you better well choose your name. Sh'then went with Wardoll instead. Hells."

"János. János. János. Was that enough, or do you need some more to fill your imaginary quota? Stick the label 'Littleguy's real name reservoir' on it, may his ego feast upon the contents on rainy days." Crawl said. "No, but really. You're just jealous Wardoll got a name better than you did."

"Defending her won't make her fuck you, Crawl."

"I'm not... now that's just uncalled for."

"I can point you to the end of the line," Pike spoke up, scraping another spoonful from the bottom of the bowl. "Consensus around the camp is that she's a real looker. You're not the only one who might be aiming to, mh, take their shot."

"Ah," Kalsin said. "I get it. She's an archer. Take a shot. Hah. A shot. That's funny, because archers shoot arrows, and—"

Pike's spoon bounced off Kalsin's head mid-speech, who pressed a hand against the aching spot on reflex, then cursed when his wrapped fingers protested upon contact. He gave Pike a dirty look and settled with mentally flipping him off, since physically he was unable to do so.

"Uh-oh" Crawl said with a weary tone. "Trouble personified approaching from the south."

Kalsin looked, and saw Ringtail enter the camp, her energetic gait eating up distance as she went. When she caught sight of Kalsin, a strange expression passed over her face, but Kalsin quickly turned away. He didn't want to give her a reason to approach.

Unfortunately, judging from Crawl's widening eyes, the opposite was about to happen.

"How's the food today, Pike?" Kalsin asked, standing.

Pike hummed contentedly. "'Fish knows his spices."

"Go," Littleguy said. "We'll deal with her."

As Kalsin hurried off in search of Cyanfish manning the pot, Littleguy's brisk yet meaningful words brought to him an oddly soothing sensation. The camaraderie of his companions caught him off guard. They understood him more than he gave them credit for, he smiled. He then frowned. They understood him more than he was comfortable with.

Cyanfish ended up giving Kalsin one, then another steaming bowl of porridge, and both portions smelled almost as good as they tasted. Kalsin savored the jégberry's sweet-and-sour tang as he chatted with the young recruit. Though Kalsin had no natural disposition for flapping his mouth, he'd found that Cyanfish was easy to talk to, even if the topic of conversation was a hollow cavern of nothing-at-all.

Charisma. You're either born with it, or you learn to pretend you were born with it.

Kalsin was good at pretending.

It was much later when he returned to his group, late enough that the sun had almost disappeared below the horizon, casting orange streaks between the shadows of many tents. Pike had also disappeared somewhere.

Kalsin retook his spot on the ground. He didn't ask what Ringtail wanted. But Crawl remarked on it anyway.

"If I said she actually looked sorry, would you believe me?"

"Not really."

"Well. I guess there's no point in saying 'she actually looked sorry', then. I think she wanted to apologize. We told her off, but... dunno. Hard to predict what 'Tail has got going on in her head." Crawl glanced at him as if expecting an answer.

"I don't much want to hear it. Easier to leave it alone," Kalsin said.

He didn't understand Ringtail and those who harbored animosity for no apparent reason. There was no logic to it—she had been obsessed with making his life more difficult from the start. Always sneaking glances at him with that frown on her upturned nose, brows perpetually furrowed.

No, it was wiser to avoid people like that rather than keep trying to bridge the chasm. They were going to fight the Veiled together, that was true enough. But they didn't have to like each other.

Night crept up on the camp like a satin cloak. Littleguy asked Kalsin how his fingers were healing at some point, but they remained quiet for the most part. Recruits slowly disappeared inside their tents and a hush fell over the evening air, the soft whistle of wind and the ruffle of fabric taking over. Crawl and Littleguy went inside to their beds soon after. Purposefully or not, the big man left his blanket on the ground, and Kalsin wrapped it around himself when he felt the cool air worm past his clothes.

An owl hooted with an irregular pattern. Rustic petals drifted above the grass. The evening chorus, the way the slowly rising half-moon illuminated the rolling red plains; everything appeared so different from what Kalsin was accustomed to back in the Republic proper.

Jack probably loved this. Kalsin imagined her large form picking through the vegetation, hoping to find a patch of brickleaf hidden in the red undergrowth.

Jack. His friend, his once bodyguard. Would he ever get the chance to reunite with her? Because no matter how much Kalsin wanted to ignore what had happened—things had changed, and they had changed irreversibly. Both for him and for her.

Jack was now Clenn. She had been given a clean page, and what kind of friend would Kalsin be if he attached himself to her, dragged her down now that she had a chance to finally make something of herself? Creating another Cinder... please not that. Anything but that.

He worried that his path was different than hers, and he didn't know where it led. He could almost see Jack talking to him, fingers dancing.

‘Stop fucking around, Kalsin. You're not a soldier. When'd you ever let others mix your palette and determine the curves of the lines you draw? Do what you're good at, cobble up some plan, and run with it. Even if it means running away to live as a hermit in the wastes, or infiltrating the Veiled with a broken axle, or taking on the scars of every shit-faced recruit around you. Who cares which. Who cares, as long as you make. It. Yours.’

The inside of their tent was dark when Kalsin entered, and after his eyes adjusted, he saw the gently breathing piles that were his companions nestled to one side. They were pressed close, with Crawl's arm hanging over Littleguy's forehead in that telltale and somewhat amusing manner of the unconscious. Theirs was a strange relationship. There was history between the two. Kalsin hoped he wouldn't be there to see the final chapter of it.

He watched them for another second before turning to his own spot.

But someone was already there.

The young woman was sitting on his bedroll, staring at him through a wall of red hair the color of clotted blood. The lily patterns on her dress were faded and almost seemed to move and crawl about in the gloom. Her features were obscured to him, but Kalsin somehow felt that her eyes were unblinking and her mouth an upturned gash stretching from ear to ear.

Kalsin didn't move. He couldn't. His veins had turned to ice and he trembled. He had seen her before. Back in Atlas' office, when he was about to receive his sentence.

The girl made no sound. Her wall of red hair remained turned toward him as if she could see him through it. Then, without warning, a lock of hair dripped to the ground.

Her scalp started melting in small spots at first, then faster, turning the veil of red hair into a waterfall of blood that pooled on his bedroll. Pale arms turned to gray and stretchy rivulets, lilies elongated like mold washing down the drain.

Her figure collapsed in on itself, disintegrating like candle wax left out in the hot summer sun until there was nothing left but a red layer on the ground.

Kalsin's breaths came fast, and though he wanted to run and escape, his legs didn't obey him. He wanted to turn away, but it was like his body was forced to watch the grotesque scene unfold.

Distantly, thanks to a part of him that was immune to panic and knew only cold reasoning, he noticed how strange the position of the pool was. The red liquid stayed within the boundaries of his bedroll, perfectly aligning with the edge of the haphazard fabric. There was not a single drop going past the sides and touching the ground. And then, as if sentient enough to know a point had been made, as if knowing his very thoughts—the red layer rolled away like smoke in the air.

And the red girl was gone.

The tent was silent. It was dark, with just enough light to see Littleguy and Crawl sound asleep. A restful evening. Everything was the same when Kalsin had entered, and nothing remained, not even a trace of the girl having been there.

Kalsin went to his knees with a gasp, bracing himself on all fours—though he had enough presence of mind to avoid falling on his bedroll.

He was seeing ghosts. He was seeing ghosts.

Insane. Delirious. Hallucinating things that were not real and no one else saw. Sanity scratched and looked for a handhold, and found none. Kalsin trembled, aware and unable to do anything about the fact that he was fucking losing his mind.

His hand shook as he poked at his bedroll. Of course, nothing happened. What else was he expecting? Palms sweating under the layer of bandages, he grabbed the edge of the fabric and pulled on it, smoothing out the wrinkles and looking inside.

That's when he saw the thorns.

In the darkness, he almost missed the small shapes scattered around and caught on the inside of the cloth of his bedroll. They were small, half the size of his fingernails. He would've never seen them if it wasn't for... for whatever it was he had seen.

The red girl, she had… warned him?

Kalsin let out a shaky breath, trying to gain back control. He plucked a thorn between his bandaged thumb and index finger, careful to only touch the wide base. The tip wasn't long, but it was sharp, and it glinted with a strange sheen when caught in the filtered moonlight. He squinted. There was definitely some kind of coating around the tip.

Kalsin's thoughts raced. A ghost was haunting him, and he had to accept that as a fact. That was enough to mess with the brain in and of itself. But the ghost saved him from some sort of poison that, most likely, would've been fatal. There was no way to test it, but what other purpose would poison serve? The disturbing way the ghost fell apart—maybe there was a reason for it. Maybe it was showing him what would've happened if the coating entered his bloodstream.

Which meant that someone close—with the possibility ranging from recruits he'd come to know to soldiers he'd barely met—just tried to kill him.

Kalsin shivered again, and not because of the ghost or the cold winds of night. But because maybe someone knew about his curse, about being more than just another heretic. About what he could do.

Maybe the Civil House wasn't done with him at all.
