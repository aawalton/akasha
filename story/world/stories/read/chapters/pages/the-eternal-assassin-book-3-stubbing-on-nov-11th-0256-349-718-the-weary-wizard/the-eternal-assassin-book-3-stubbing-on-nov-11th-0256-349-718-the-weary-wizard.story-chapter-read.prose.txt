“Realms… I must look even worse than I feel if that’s the expression you’re greeting me with,” Lanra whispered, chuckling weakly as she smiled at Asher. “Richard won’t let me see a mirror, so I figured it has to be bad.”

“Bad is certainly… a word,” Asher said, struggling to lie to the wizard’s face as he took in her current condition. He’d thought Richard had looked like death during his time spent in tritessence debt, and that Lanra had looked pretty bad when he’d dropped her off with Richard to take care of.

Compared to her current appearance, in those previous memories, she’d been at the pinnacle of health.

Lanra lay on one of Richard’s couches with a small mountain of blankets piled on top of her shivering frame. Her face wasn’t just white or pale. It was partially translucent. It almost looked as if her physical form was in the middle of fading away, like it simply didn’t have the strength left to continue existing on this realm any longer. Asher tried not to stare at the veins and muscles that were visible through the stretched and wrinkled skin on her face. He knew Lanra was old, as all wizards pretty much had to be, but she’d never really looked her age. Now…

“Did Richard say if you’d manage to recover from this?” Asher asked, taking a careful seat on the couch beside her. The rest of the Eight Crests’ realm wandering team had visited before him, and while he wasn’t actively trying to spot them, Secret Seeker drew his eyes to the wet splotches along the couch that he assumed were fallen tears. As much as Mako and the others ragged on Lanra, the fact of the matter was they were a team that had spent the last couple of years delving into deadly and unpredictable realms of existence with one another. He couldn’t imagine not bonding with one another through such activities.

“Richard has danced around the topic, which has been quite entertaining to see,” Lanra admitted, chuckling again as she shook her head. Even as she did so, a few strands of her pure-white hair fell from her head, and Asher’s heart reached out to the poor woman. “One of the first things I asked him after regaining consciousness was why he was spending so much time and energy trying to save my life if I wasn’t going to be able to cast magic any more, because I wouldn’t be able to repay him regardless of his demands. You know what he said to me? He told me if he wasn’t able to get me back to better shape than I was in before going into tritessence debt, it was a sign he wasn’t a skilled-enough wizard, and that I wouldn’t owe him anything in the first place. I’d never pegged the abrasive, annoying wizard of the Noala Kingdom as a big softy.”

“He definitely has some surprising sides to him,” Asher admitted, giving Lanra a sad smile as he struggled to choose his next words. “Lanra—”

“If the next words out of your mouth are some form of apology, I swear I’ll find the strength in these old bones to cast one more spell, if only to set your pants on fire,” Lanra declared, narrowing her eyes at him. “We all knew what we were getting ourselves into going after the Council of Death. You were completely out of tritessence and that magical explosion was about to sweep over all of us. My quick thinking resulted in saving all of our lives. If I deserve anything for putting myself in this state, it’s not an apology, but a medal.”

“I can’t argue with you there,” Asher chuckled, nodding his thanks as he kept his apology to himself. Despite the fact that part of him still felt like it was somehow his fault, Lanra’s words rang true. He’d been completely spent and unable to do anything in the face of almost certain death. Despite him shouting at her to give him her tritessence, if she hadn’t, they almost certainly all would have died. “This is probably a stupid question, but I’m assuming stuff like potions or skills don’t really help with what you’re currently going through? I’ve got an Amulet of Improved Healing I’d be more than happy to lend you if you said it would help.”

“I do appreciate the offer, but no, it wouldn’t do me any good,” she sighed, resting her head back on the couch and closing her eyes for a moment. Asher didn’t miss how he could still somewhat see her eyeballs through her translucent eyelids, and he shuddered at the fact. “Soul damage is far more complicated than physical damage or even mental trauma. The human body simply isn’t equipped to heal from such a thing. To be honest, it’s a miracle Richard even managed to restore me to this state. When I tried to get him to reveal how he pulled off bringing me back from how deep I placed myself in tritessence debt, he told me I could thank you of all people if I wanted to thank anyone. Something about experimenting on a greater demon corpse?”

“Oh!” Asher said, blinking in surprise at the admittance. “Yeah, rather than sell a greater demon corpse at the Grand Auction, I handed it over to Richard for his studies in exchange for some shards and favors. He said he wanted to study the strange weight that they seemed to have. I suppose it must have something to do with the soul.”

“Well, that decision of yours inadvertently gave me the chance to say my goodbyes,” Lanra smiled, opening her eyes again as she looked at him. “Not that I’m giving up on recovering just yet, mind you. That said, I’m a realist at heart. Even if I was brought back from the brink of death, I’m smart enough to know that my days of wizardry are over. If I do manage to pull through, I’ll probably recover enough that I won’t make small children scream just from looking at me, but my soul is far too damaged to harness my tritessence to recreate skills like I spent my entire life training for. No matter what the future holds, I am a wizard no longer.”

Asher sat there beside her in silence, unsure of what to actually say in response to such a declaration. It was good that Lanra seemed to be taking her new future in stride, but he could tell she was still hurting inside at the revelation. From how tired and frail her body looked, he was more than a little worried that she’d simply give up the fight to stay alive now that she knew she’d never be able to go back to how things were before.

From his time spent as a mortician’s apprentice, Asher knew that one’s mental state played a surprisingly important part in one’s health, especially when it came to the elderly. He’d lost track of the number of times their funeral home would receive someone in their late eighties or nineties, only to then receive their spouse a few weeks or sometimes even days later. Something about giving up the person or thing one loved most occasionally signaled the end for an otherwise healthy person.

And that was just a person’s mental state. Asher didn’t even want to think about how critical having a solid soul state had to be for physical health.

“How is it that you look more troubled than I do?” Lanra teased, snapping Asher out of his worrying. “I told you, I don’t fault you for a moment. Your quick actions saved not only my life, but the life of my team. I am old and have already lived a full life, but they are young and have many more adventures ahead of them. My death will be a significant blow to the Eight Crests whenever it does happen, be it from this or some other venture into another realm, but there are other wizards capable of taking my place. Replacing me will be a far simpler task than replacing the entire team.”

“I feel like you’re taking this all far too easily,” Asher said. He was surprised to find himself frowning down at the woman who looked like she was already long since passed through death’s door and was now chilling on death’s couch. “Yeah, I won’t lie, it looks pretty bad. But you’re not dead yet! You didn’t expect Richard to be able to save your life in the first place, and yet here you are. You probably never thought we stood a real chance at taking down the Council of Death, and yet they’re now down eight of their original fourteen members. It’s too soon to throw in the towel and talk about the Eight Crests losing their best wizard when you’re still on the mend! You can’t give up yet!”

“I appreciate the pep talk, but your words are wasted on me,” Lanra said, giving him a sad smile. “You are talented beyond belief, there is no question there. But you do not understand soul damage as I do. There are limits to what even the most skilled healers are capable of. Superficial soul damage can eventually be ‘healed’ through enough rest, but damage of the magnitude I put myself through? There is nothing that can be done.”

“I might be able to heal you!” Asher all but shouted, practically panting as he finally came out with his greatest secret. Not including the members of the Council of Death, he could count the number of people who knew about his Eternity element on both hands. “I can’t tell you specifics, but I’m capable of healing my own soul damage. Maybe I can get that skill placed into an artifact somehow and then share it with you. It should be able to heal you!”

“Oh, Asher…” Lanra said. Her face strained for a moment as her blankets shifted and a weak, frail arm ever so slowly dragged its way out of them. Even doing this much looked like it was costing Lanra what little life she had left, and Asher hurriedly reached out and grabbed her hand to stop her from moving. Nodding her thanks, she sighed. “…I already know about your strange element.”

“You do?” he asked, recoiling at the news. “How? Did Richard tell you?”

“No. When you opened your soul and allowed me to flood it with my tritessence, I got a good look at you,” she admitted. “In all the decades I’ve spent learning skills from others, never have I ever seen anything even close to whatever that element is within your soul. I understand why the Council of Death fears you so much, as well as why Donvath and you have decided to keep it a secret. The sensation of… foreverness, that I got from your soul… I have no words to describe it.”

“If you saw what I’m capable of, then you know I might be able to help you,” Asher said, gripping her hand tighter for a moment before wincing at the pained look on her face as he felt her frail bones all but creak under his gentle grip. “I just need time to find an enchanter and to figure out what kind of gemstone will even work with my element. You just need to hang in there.”

“How many people know of your element?” Lanra asked, raising a shaking eyebrow up at him. “Donvath… your team… maybe one or two others? If keeping it a secret is that important, do you really believe turning one of its skills into an artifact is the best idea? Not to mention the trouble that would come from such a thing. Wars would be fought over an artifact capable of healing soul damage. The coalition would shatter and the peace we are working so hard toward achieving would be a thing of the past. I do not want any part in such a future.”

“But without it…” Asher couldn’t bring himself to say the words he knew to be true. The two of them kept dancing around the subject and treating it as only a hypothetical, but Asher was pretty certain at heart, Lanra knew the same thing he did. He didn’t need his limited first-aid knowledge to know that Lanra was living on borrowed time, courtesy of Richard and all his hard work. Richard may have bought her the chance to say goodbye, but her soul was far too gone at this point. As if she could read his mind, Lanra somehow found the strength to squeeze his hand in her own, her entire body relaxing afterward as though it had been the hardest thing she’d ever done.

“Without it, I’ll die,” she finished for him, giving him a weary smile. “That is life, Asher. People grow old, and eventually, they die. I am ninety-six years old, and I have accomplished more in those ninety-six years than many could in a dozen lifetimes. Having seen your unique element, I have a feeling you may not have to worry about growing old and dying, but I have had my entire life to prepare for this. I’ll admit I always thought my time would come from Mako screwing up and getting the rest of us killed on one of our missions, so to be frank, this is a pleasant surprise. I actually get the chance to have one last conversation with those I care about.”

Asher simply sat there, unable to accept Lanra’s calming words. No matter how ready for death she seemed, or how tired she looked, he struggled to understand how one could just give up when there might be a possible alternative. As he warred with himself, Lanra let out one final sigh before pulling her frail fingers from his hand.

“I’m glad we were able to go on at least one trip to the wild realm together,” she said, giving him one last, shaky smile. “You are going to be a wonderful realm wanderer, Asher. I just know it. Now, could you please send Jameston back in? He’s holding onto my sending stone for me, and I would like the opportunity to speak with my family back in the Eight Crests while I still have the strength.”
