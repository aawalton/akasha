Dorian

All of us stared at each other, and I almost gave the command to pack up and leave. A stray Visitor I could deal with. It might be messy and dangerous, but I knew I'd come out on top. A creature akin to a minor God?

I didn't know.

Maybe—if I had more information and time. I lacked both.

The other problem was the more important one. If I left now, I couldn't ensure four Survivors made it for the rest of the time limit. There was no good outcome if that happened. Four needed to live.

Worse still, I was back to not knowing if the third Visitor was an ally or an enemy again. If Rend killed Talin, the last Visitor could've had nothing to do with it.

I spoke, my voice rushed, "Is Rend sentient. Capable of thought and reasoning? Making deals?" Krevith nodded, and I cursed. The loyalty, or lack thereof, of this third Visitor suddenly became a lot more important.

"Jeren. Turn me down." I spoke softly, hoping that Jeren had kept the stones' audio quiet.

His voice was tense on the other line, "Yeah?"

"In the looped Instance. Did you confirm everyone was from this Instance."

"Everyone knew at least someone. Even among those dead." He responded, clearly unsure of where I was going. Honestly, I didn't either. I was throwing things at a wall and trying to find out what felt right. From that, though, the Visitor was probably from this world. Which meant—I looked from Krevith to Maerin—that the Visitor would've been part of the upper class, and very much not our ally.

"Alder, Nico, and Orlan." I began asking, "Is there anyone else here that would know about the Dream?"

Maerin shook her head. Jeren matched the information from over the stone.

Finally, I had some good news. I had an actual reason to help Maerin kill them.

I lowered my voice even further, fearful that someone in the cells might hear from over the stone.

"Jeren, [Wayfarer], do you have more charge?"

"Yeah."

"Can you pulse it? Rend might be in there, and if the Visitor is an enemy it could reveal them."

"I don't think that's a good idea."

I frowned, but Jeren knew more about his Boon than I did. "Why?"

He took a deep breath, and the tension didn't leave him. "There are two big questions about the Dream when it comes to Boons and Restrictions. What defines 'you' and what defines an 'enemy'?" Maerin leaned in, still slightly out of it, but distinctly interested in whatever Jeren was saying.

Jeren continued, "We know that 'you' includes the clothes you wear when entering an Instance, but they stop being 'you' when you take them off. Enemies. They're the same in a lot of ways. [Wayfarer] won't work unless I see the danger, or it tries to attack when it's already active."

And Jeren only had so much charge. It seemed good news came with bad, but never the other way around.

"We have to go underground. It won't be safe otherwise. We have to split up. We'll take one of the Survivors. The Visitor can't be anyone in my group, so it's best if I only take one. If anyone with me dies I'll know who it is."

All of my group held their breaths. There was silence on the other line. "Does this have to do with whatever deal you made?" Jeren asked.

"It doesn't matter. Just. I can promise that they won't die. That should be enough."

A sigh. "I guess it'll have to be. Who."

I looked towards Maerin, and she hesitated. I should've told her to pick another Survivor. Not someone that she wanted revenge on. The chances that one of the non-upper-class Survivors was a Visitor were small. I wanted to tell her to make the choice that would give us a better chance to survive.

I looked away.

"Alder." She finally spoke, and my head snapped towards her.

I gave her a smile, "Okay. Alder it is. Jeren, we'll be there in a sec."

The line went dead without a response, and I turned to my group of Survivors. "We get Alder and we book it. Paltra, Krevith, can you two begin gathering light sources, wood for torches, the like? We'll be underground for a while, and light is the only thing I know that will keep that thing down there at bay.

Maerin squeezed Paltra's hand, and only then did the Huntress and Gladiator stand to do as I requested. I pulled out the Fiend statue, concealing it in my palm. "You ready? You can wait out here if you'd like."

She shook her head, "No. I want to be there."

"We can't do it in front of everyone. The rest of the Survivors will panic. We'll have to take him a bit away first, or at least to one of the rooms."

She said nothing, just controlled her breathing. With that, we headed to the back of the ship, towards the cells. We opened the door, and all idle chatter from the Survivors died. Jeren still sat in the center of the room and tilted his head to one of the cells.

I took a step forward, glanced in the cell, and frowned. Maerin just had to pick the guy with the gun. Whatever. It didn't matter. "Alder?" I questioned.

The man was middle-aged, somewhere in his mid-thirties, with a closely shaved head and wide eyes. He hugged his gun as I directed the question to him. "What is it, Fiend?"

"You'll be coming with me." I kept my voice even, trying my best to not inject any emotion that [Untrustworthy] could use against me.

Jeren tried to help, "It will be safe. He won't let you die. I can confirm that."

"You're in league with him!" a woman's voice from the opposite cell called. I rounded on her; she stood in the corner, and—at my gaze—contorted herself further into it.

"Krevith has been acting weird ever since he went with the Fiend!" It was a man in circular spectacles this time.

"It's the witch!" The voice was shaky and came from Alder.

"It's the witch!" a third voice, this time it came from a nearby cell with an older man. He was in the same cell as a young boy, not older than ten. "She made a deal with the Fiend! The Prophet was talking about it!"

All the noise gave Alder enough courage to level his gun at me. My stomach dropped, my heart beat in double time, and pinpricks traveled down my spine as I stared down the barrel. This was the second time I had a gun pointed at me. I didn't even bother waiting for his itchy trigger finger; I just activated the Boon, and as the Veil spread over my hand, the Survivors began screaming.

"He is. He is a Fiend! He uses Nothing. He uses the Void!"

The sound of a rifle firing rang in my ears, and the bullet flew from the barrel and teleported to the other side of the Void, where it continued traveling. I watched with a sinking heart as I traced its soon-to-be path, and closed my eyes as the bullet embedded itself right into the woman in the opposite cell.

She shrieked.

A growl sounded in the room.

All hell broke loose.

I didn't think, just twisted on my heel and dashed up to Maerin, interposing the Veil between ourselves and everyone else. I could barely see out of it. Jeren would have to take care of everyone else. I had my people to deal with. "Sorry. Just don't touch the Veil. It will teleport you." She was shocked enough that she didn't immediately struggle and managed to hear my words through her growing panic. It stopped her from thrashing, but the hyperventilating and murder in her eyes told me I needed to give her a little more space.

I turned my attention to what was happening through the Veil. I couldn't really tell what was happening. Jeren had activated [Wayfarer], which looked like a translucent, shimmering golden sphere centered on the Visitor. The cells were open. People were running. There was a large hole in the ship's side where red light was spilling in. Another deafening howl. Yelling. Arguing, but I couldn't make out a single thing. I kept an eye out for Alder. He broke away from his cell. He stood next to Jeren, aiming down the sights, shooting outside the hole at something beyond. Someone small broke for the door and slipped out. I turned back to Maerin and motioned, "Let's go."

She was staring at Alder, her eyes then shifted to another person with spectacles, who was breaking for the door.

"Maybe the one without the gun," I suggested. Maerin stared at me wide-eyed, as if I had appeared out of nowhere, before nodding.

"Dorian!" Jeren shouted as I began stepping for the door. He wasn't facing me—still looking out of the hole.

I cursed and looked between Jeren and Maerin. "Find Krevith and Paltra. Then get that guy with the glasses. I'll get Alder." I broke from her and spoke into the stone. I was careful in my approach. "If Alder shoots at me again, I'm killing him."

"Now's not the time!" Jeren shouted back. He was hearing me through the stone but yelling loudly in the space.

"It was a warning. Nothing more." I slid next to my fellow Visitor, putting him between myself and Alder. My skin began burning and itching as the light touched it. Jeren had let his barrier fall, only having activated it for a few seconds. Alder kept the gun leveled at a stone creature I refused to call a wolf. The head was upside down. Its mouth was ape-like with saber-tooth teeth. Its chest bulged out like a tumor growth, but its stomach was sunken into the bones of its spine. It lifted its head and howled, one low, constant hum, and—overlayed on that—another higher-pitched yipping sound.

On its stone skin were two burn marks with the barest amount of material chipped away. Likely where Alder's bullets had hit.

"No way we kill that thing," I said, my voice full of certainty. Neither Jeren nor I had combat Boons. I shifted my vision to the cells. I had seen some people run out. It appeared the only one left was the woman who got shot. "Where'd it come from?"

"I don't know." Jeren stared down the creature.

The only thing that came to mind was collapsing the ship on the creature. It might not kill it, but it would stall it, hopefully for long enough for us to get underground.

"I can kill it," Jeren replied, and I thought I didn't hear him correctly at first.

"What?"

"I can kill it." Jeren affirmed.

It was dumb. Our best option was cutting our losses, splitting up, and making a break for it, but as I looked at its sharp teeth and clawed paws, I knew that even if it was the smart option, I would never go for it. I wanted the thing dead.

I had a hundred questions, but really, only one was relevant. "What do you need?"

"I need it below me and still."

I stared down at the wolf-like creature. "I'll see what I can do."

I leapt out of the side of the ship.

Moonlight

Nico sprinted through the halls of the ship, eyes wide and heart racing. Of course, the Witch was here. It made sense why she was here. But him? It made no sense that he was here. He had been loyal his entire life. Despite knowing about the truth of the world, about the Dream, and their God, he was faithful. He fabricated texts, prophecies, created culture for the masses to follow. Designed everything to make them content and obedient with just enough fear. It was working too. It had worked for the past two hundred years. He couldn't figure out why he was here.

He had seen the way Maerin had looked at him, the way her eyes were filled with hate directed at himself, Alder, and Orlan. Hate so deep it couldn't be satisfied even if she got what she wanted. How she broke out of the Joy charm in the first place was a mystery, and how she even managed to kill the High Priest was a mystery further. But if she could do that when he was one blessed with powers by God, she could certainly kill him, and he didn't want to die.

He threw himself into a room, barricading it with a large wooden crate.

Rend was here too now. He couldn't see a way out. He couldn't see anything at all.

Then the answer came.

"I've been waiting for you." A voice said.

Nico jumped, shouting a small yell as they scrambled against the wall. "You? What are … why are you here?"

"God sent me. Sent me to these trials to punish the brother of the Dreamspawn."

Nico wanted to step further away, but he had already blocked his exit and had nowhere else to go.

"What do you want?" he couldn't keep the quiver from his voice.

"It's what you want. You're probably confused about why you're here. I'll tell you why. To help me. Alder. Orlan. Yourself. You are owed better. Being sent here is not punishment. It is trust."

Nico's breathing relaxed ever so slightly. This. This sounded right. This is what it should've been. No way he was being punished. Not after everything he did, not after everything he was owed. "What have I been entrusted with? C-can I go back after this?"

"Of course." The person said. "The lose condition is four out of ten people survive the night. Why do you think that is? Because only three Survivors were ever intended to make it. Alder. Orlan, and you."

Euphoria filled the man's bones as all the weight, worry, and pressure of the past few hours melted away. He felt like he could collapse into a heap and sleep for the next week. "I'm at your disposal, as an instrument of God. Just tell me what needs to be done."

"Simple. Just kill the other Survivors."
