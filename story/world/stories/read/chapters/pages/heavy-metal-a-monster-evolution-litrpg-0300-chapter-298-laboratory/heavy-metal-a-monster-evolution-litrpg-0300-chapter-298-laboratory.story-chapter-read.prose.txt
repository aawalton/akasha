“Wait… how is this possible? No, stay back!”

The warden revealed himself to be just a regular human mage of some sort. Rusty wasn’t entirely sure, as he didn’t seem to have much offensive capability. The strange mage he had seen back in his own world had been quite powerful, but this one could barely cast a simple mana bolt, which Rusty could easily avoid or even smack away with his metallic hand.

“Your atrocities end today!”

Teral, on the other hand, seemed more driven than ever before. The elf lifted his staff, channeling his remaining mana to order two of his lesser constructs to grab the human mage and restrain him. Rusty was there as well. He had pulled the man back when he tried to escape, but before he could yank some kind of suspicious lever, Rusty pounced on him in his wraith form, which seemed to terrify humanoids.

“Atrocities?”

Rusty wondered, remembering the strange monster they had just battled. There seemed to be more to this mission than the last one, so he continued analyzing everything around him, trying to fully understand what he was supposed to do here.

“You’ll get nothing out of me, elf! Your kind should be honored that it can be sacrificed to advance humanity!”

The warden shouted, spitting as he spoke. His words remained arrogant even though he was completely restrained. It was possible that he still believed his people would arrive and save him.

“Oh, is this the outside?”

While Teral was questioning the warden, which mostly consisted of his constructs holding the man down while the two of them shouted at each other, Rusty looked at the display. It was somewhat similar to the one back at the Soul Forge, showing what was happening outside.

There, he saw elves battling the guards they had encountered earlier, along with others evacuating the emancipated prisoners. From what Rusty could see, the group of elves was winning, and they weren’t being gentle about it. He watched as they speared human guards and used their summoned beasts to tear them apart. They clearly had no intention of taking prisoners from the people who had been trying to massacre them.

“Shut your mouth. Now tell me where you put them, you monster.”

‘Oh…’

Rusty looked away from the screen, which showed a somewhat blurry image of the outside of the compound, and turned his attention back to Teral. The elf was in the process of ordering one of his constructs to strike the captured mage.

The construct hit him again. Blood flowed from the warden’s wounds, which was somewhat surprising. The old Teral, even when consumed by the urge for vengeance, usually wasn’t someone who would attack or torment a person who was incapable of fighting back.

“Teral, I don’t think he’ll be able to answer in that state…”

Rusty finally called out to him. Teral froze at the sound of Rusty’s voice. His raised hand trembled slightly, hovering over the bloodied warden, who slumped against the stone wall and coughed weakly.

The young elf slowly stepped back, looking at the bloodied knuckles of his living armor summon. For a brief second, the fierce hatred in his eyes flickered, replaced by a momentary shadow of shame as he looked up at his metallic summon.

The human mage coughed out a red spatter, letting out a wheezing laugh despite his swollen jaw. He spat onto the cold stone floor, glaring at Teral with bitter defiance.

“Once the guardians of the kingdom arrive, all of you will die, purified like the lowly creatures that you…”

The man taunted them, still confident that someone would come to save him. However, before he could continue, an arrow shot through his forehead, killing him instantly and making Teral flinch.

“Why did you…”

He called out to the woman who had done it, but she simply shrugged as she approached.

“He wasn’t going to say anything. It will be faster if we just search this palace for an entrance.”

Lethira lowered her bow and stepped past the motionless body of the warden with complete indifference. Teral stared at the lifeless human for a few seconds, his chest rising and falling heavily as the anger in his eyes slowly subsided. He opened his mouth to protest her sudden act, but the truth of her words could not be denied. They were running out of time, and interrogating the man would have yielded nothing but further delay.

“So, I’m assuming we have limited time, and those guardians will eventually come to chase us down?”

He asked both of them. Luckily, the elves could understand his speech thanks to their magic.

“It’s the great spirit. Luck is on our side, I see!”

“Yes, it might be, but…”

Teral nodded, then looked at the spot where the defeated creature had been. Rusty, meanwhile, was examining the console the mage had been using. It was much simpler than the ones in the summoning chamber or the training chamber. There were only a few knobs and levers, making it relatively easy to figure out.

‘He was about to pull this one, so these ones should be safe…’

There wasn’t much to the console, so Rusty pressed one of the buttons and pulled a lever that looked safe. Soon, something clicked, and a section of the wall began sliding to the side.

Beyond it was another set of stairs leading downward. As Rusty stepped closer, his life detection skill picked up something below.

“Hey, what’s that?”

Teral and Lethira became aware of the heavy grinding sound as the secret doorway fully receded into the wall. Cool air rose from the dark stairwell, thick with the scent of concentrated mana. However, there was something else in the air as well. Something was rotting down there.

“Stay behind me. There could be things in there…”

Rusty moved forward, knowing that this was Teral’s objective. If they failed to complete it, Teral would probably never be able to leave the prison. Soon, the three of them descended the stairs, moving at a quick pace as the threat of the warden’s allies still lingered.

Without being asked, Rusty moved ahead, using his light element to illuminate the dark tunnel. He returned to his base form, the halberd in his hands glowing brightly as they continued forward without saying a word.

It was clear that Teral wanted to ask him something, but for now, this was more important. Soon, they arrived at the place the warden had tried so desperately to hide.

“Ugh… that smell… how could they do this… those bastards…”

When they arrived, the female elf had a visceral reaction to the stench filling the room, immediately returning whatever she had eaten into a corner. Rusty lit up the area with his halberd, its light casting long shadows across the damp stone walls.

The light revealed a sprawling underground laboratory, far larger than the prison cells above. Iron racks lined the walls, holding glass jars filled with body parts. Many belonged to humanoids, elves in particular. Rusty could tell from some of the heads and their pointed ears floating in the murky liquid. Yet somehow, this was not the worst part.

Along the sides of the room stood massive glass containers filled with the same liquid the creature that had attacked Rusty had emerged from. Inside were monsters in various stages of development. Each one appeared to have been assembled from elven parts and pieces taken from other creatures. They were some kind of chimera, with an elf as the foundation and elven magic woven into their creation.

“Why would those humanoids do this…”

Rusty placed his hand against one of the containers, staring at the monstrosity inside. Teral stepped up beside Rusty, his fists clenched tightly at his sides. The young elf stared at the distorted faces submerged within the murky liquid. Tears welled in his eyes, but they were quickly burned away by pure fury. He wanted to shatter every glass vessel in the room, but Rusty raised a metallic hand to stop him.

“Wait.”

Rusty spoke calmly.

“My life detection skill detected living creatures here. Maybe someone could still be alive.”

He activated his skill again. The monsters were gone, but every life signal he detected could somehow be attributed to a living species, and a few of them still seemed to be elven.

“There might be someone alive over there.”

He pointed toward a corridor on one side of the laboratory. Teral and Lethira, who had managed to recover, exchanged a nod before continuing down the corridor. Further inside, they discovered more prison cells. Some were still occupied, holding a few survivors who had either endured the experiments or had not yet been subjected to them.

“Don’t worry, we’ll get you out of here. Just wait!”

Teral’s eyes shone with a mixture of hope and anger as he ordered his constructs to shatter the locks. The people inside resembled those they had rescued from the upper levels. They were pale, emaciated, and weak, yet they were alive.

Some of them looked strange, though. Some had more limbs than Rusty remembered humanoids having. Others had tails or strange orbs attached to their bodies. Regardless of how they looked, they were alive, and his summoner wanted to save them. Rusty would do everything he could to help.

Rusty went along, smacking the locks apart while Teral summoned as many living armors as possible to carry the prisoners out. They made sure no one was left behind, and once the cells had been cleared, Rusty noticed something else: a map.

‘This could come in handy.’

His summoner was too preoccupied with everything else to notice, but Rusty had learned by now that gathering information was just as important as rescuing people.

The map was not the only thing of interest. There was also a large book containing information that could prove useful. Rusty only flipped through it briefly, but he noticed several names and locations that might be important later.

He tucked the map and the book inside his armor. His storage system only worked for his own items, so he could not store them properly. Still, hiding them inside his armor would have to do. Soon, they were ready to leave.

“Okay, everyone is clear. Get back!”

Lethira shouted before pulling out a wooden item with a length of rope attached to the end. She used some low-level flame magic to light it before throwing it inside. Rusty watched with interest as it flew toward the laboratory and the creatures within.

*BOOM*

The item was small, but it packed quite a punch. It exploded with a huge bang, flooding the area with flames. Fire and shockwaves tore down the stone hallway, blasting outward in a cloud of dust and heat. Heavy stone fragments rained down, sealing the dark laboratory beneath tons of rubble. The remaining abominations, along with all evidence of those cruel experiments, were buried forever beneath the collapsed ceiling.

Rusty raised his shield to protect them from the shockwave, but they were already far enough away that no one was hurt. When the dust finally settled, only a blocked tunnel filled with smoke remained.

“Well, that’s one way to do it. You have to give me the recipe for that enchanted tool!”

The explosive power was similar to the blast he could produce when overloading both the light and dark energies in his twilight armor. It was much more controlled, though, and perhaps he could even sell something like it for a profit in his own world if he figured out how to make it.

“The great spirit likes the ogre fire.”

Lethira smiled, but Teral quickly tapped her on the shoulder.

“We need to get out of here. We’ve already spent too much time.”

They both nodded at each other before looking toward Rusty, who simply followed after them while carrying two people over his shoulders. It had been quite the hectic introduction to his old summoner, but it was also an interesting turn of events.

Soon, they moved upward, returning to the place where he had initially been summoned. Now that they were back, the blocked passage had been cleared by some elves. Rusty did not recognize most of them, but a few faces seemed familiar.

“We have everyone. Let’s go. Tell everyone to retreat into the forest!”

Once the elves appeared, Teral began giving orders. It became clear that he was indeed the leader here. Even though he was young, his responsibilities seemed to have increased, and the other elves listened to him without hesitation. They nodded to one another before retrieving wooden whistles and blowing them.

‘Must be the signal to retreat.’

Rusty thought as he continued moving with the group. They did not seem to realize that he was a different summon from the lesser armors Teral had created. Otherwise, they probably would have started calling him a great spirit again.

Once outside, Rusty saw the same scene he had witnessed through the panel as they arrived in the main courtyard of what appeared to be a prison compound. Smoke curled into the gray sky from shattered guard towers and burning wooden barricades. The battle in the open courtyard was already coming to an end.

Liberated prisoners were being guided toward the smashed outer gate, where the edge of the ancient forest offered immediate cover. Elven warriors stood guard around the perimeter, finishing off the last remaining defenses and delivering justice to the human guards.

Rusty watched as some of the humans pleaded for their lives, but the elves weren’t having any of it. They used their swords to pierce their hearts, their bows to send arrows through their bodies, and even spears taken from the guards to finish them off. Just as he had expected, this was true war, with no prisoners being taken. Perhaps it was also a statement from Teral, a warning about what happened to those who tried to subjugate elvenkind.

Rusty stepped over a shattered iron beam, carrying the two weakened prisoners with ease. The group made their way through the ruined gates and finally crossed into the cool shade of the dense forest. Behind them, the prison compound came into view, resembling a fortress that was now completely engulfed in flames.

In the distance, heavy horns echoed across the valley behind them. Human reinforcements were arriving, but they were far too late to stop the escape. Lethira cast one last glance toward the horizon, adjusting the bow on her shoulder before fading into the thick foliage alongside the other rear guards.

They pushed deep into the woodland until the sounds of the courtyard vanished completely. There was no time to rest. They had many weakened noncombatants with them who could barely move, let alone keep up with the others. Their escape was not over yet, and Rusty was certain that Teral would have something to say about it.

“Rusty…”

“You don’t have to tell me. Let’s stay behind and hold them off.”

He replied as he slung his halberd over his shoulder. He was the strongest among them, while the others needed to continue the evacuation. Within this forest, there were countless places to hide and plenty of opportunities to strike from the shadows.

The battle was far from over. Now it was time to become the hunter.
