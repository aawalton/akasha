James and Zhang Chāo made brief excuses to James’s entourage.

James managed to slip Zhang Chāo’s note into Mina’s hand, confident that she would interpret it correctly.

Then they resumed circulating the room, though this time with a more definite direction.

James had visually identified the people who had been gathered around the Assassin King. Now he needed to figure out what faction they belonged to and why they were trying to have him killed.

The secondary mission in his mind was to speak with the Assassin King, let him know that they knew he was being retained, and try to give him reason not to take on one of the stronger Rulers. Perhaps the enemy could be outbid.

There were still people who approached James unsolicited, though. Now he had to force himself to be more polite than he felt like being; had to slow down and greet each person as if his top concern at the Summit was still meeting and greeting rather than foiling an assassination plot against himself.

“Hello, we represent the Confederation of Allied Republics,” said a man with little presence in terms of aura. “I’m Sora Carpenter.” Beside him were three other men and a woman, all of whom were dressed relatively simply compared with the opulence of some of the Rulers.

From this and the introduction, James guessed these were individuals who aspired to lead in a more quasi-democratic fashion.

Just like Dean, he thought. Well-intentioned but misguided. I don’t want to be a tyrant, but this isn’t an era of popular rule.

“A pleasure to meet you all,” James said, giving them a slight nod and a smile.

“The honor is ours,” began one of the men before the woman elbowed him in the side and frowned.

“What Frasier here means to say is that despite the fact that you have declared yourself an Emperor, we appreciated the sentiments underlying your statements up on the stage,” said one of the other men. “You seem like a kindred spirit.”

“It’s a shame you have to put caveats on saying hello,” James replied, scowling. “Being that concerned about appearances and your internal politics all the time must be exhausting. And how are you conducting diplomacy with other royal Rulers if you have such a disdain for these labels?”

All five of them looked a bit taken aback.

“Truth be told,” said Carpenter, “we have stuck a bit to ourselves and the handful of other non-monarchical Rulers.”

“That seems like a mistake,” James said. “I’m glad you decided to stop and speak with me, though. You’re making the right choice, circulating the room.”

“Cavorting with a bunch of want-to-be dictators,” muttered the woman under her breath, clearly intending to be heard.

“Well, good luck,” James said. He stepped to the side and began to move past them.

“Wait,” said Carpenter, but James did not alter his pace.

“He’s not interested in befriending us, Sora, I told you before,” said the man who hadn’t yet spoken.

“None of these people would piss on us if we were on fire,” said the woman. “A bunch of megalomaniacs, gifted power by a System that worships brute force.”

Now that James had overheard all of their voices, he recognized their accents. These people were all from Western Europe, except for one American.

They still think the rules-based international order could be reestablished, he thought. I should wish they were right, but I can’t bring myself to care. Rules-based order only worked because they had economic power and the United States backed them with military power. Same as the Pax Romana. That was never going to last forever.

But he still stopped in his tracks, forcing Zhang Chāo to come to a sudden halt beside him.

The Taiwanese leader looked at him curiously, but James just gave him a sheepish smile.

I’ll only be a moment.

He turned back to face the group, who were still arguing among themselves, although they kept the volume to a polite library conversation level.

“Look,” James said, just loudly enough for them to focus on him. “No one is going to care about the group of you unless you prove you’re more than just talk. You chose weaker Jobs, positions that every Ruler here knows make you inferior to the rest of us in power. You did it out of principle, I guess, so I ought to admire it, but I can’t make myself do it. The truth is, you brought your irrelevance on yourselves. What have you done in the last few months? You need to show you’re strong enough to matter. Make a name for yourselves. Restore democracy or republican rule or whatever it is you believe in to some areas that one of these other guys conquered. I’m sure you can find some person who’s actually the tyrant you imagine you’re taking a moral stance against.”

“Easy for you to say, warrior king,” said one of the men. “Some of us have families and people who depend on us staying alive—”

“Do they depend on you being weak or strong?” James asked. “Because my wife, my children, my mother, my sister, and my subjects—” For the first time, he allowed himself to use that word out loud instead of the more democratic “citizens”—“they all want me to be strong enough to protect them. If I can’t do that, I’m no more valuable to them alive than I would be dead. That’s what being a Ruler is. I can tell that you all have been lazy. That woman over there—” He gestured to a Latina Ruler—“she has more aura than the lot of you, and I can tell she started as a Healer. What enemies have you destroyed? What liberty have you brought since you came to power?”

Probably an interesting story there, actually.

“You’re beginning to resemble Pú Tiě Shān,” said the female member of the group softly, but in a tone of vindication.

“No, I’ll resemble Pú Tiě Shān when I have my boot on your throats!” James snarled. “Or do you all have your heads so far up your own asses that you can’t tell the difference between someone who’s trying to tell you to get in the game and someone who wants to crush you? Because that’s what Pú Tiě Shān wants. He said it with soaring rhetoric and flowery justifications, but he just wants everyone to bow to one authority. I want people to be able to stand on their own. This world and its people aren’t made to kneel. But you’re making me wonder if he isn’t secretly right about humans.”

James realized, in the back of his mind, that his emotional control Skills would normally have kept him from getting anywhere near this point. He needed to rein it in. This was the most frustrated he had ever been with other humans since the System appeared.  His voice had risen to an uncomfortable pitch by the end of his rant, and he was beginning to draw unwanted attention.

He took a few deep breaths, tried to force himself to calm down.

Was I ever this angry before I had power? That was the crux of it for him, though. The fact that he and these other men and women had power, but this little group of them seemed so weak and complacent. It was contemptible. It was infuriating. It was an object lesson in all that James did not want to be, the opposite end of the spectrum from Pú Tiě Shān.

Perhaps that was his projection, but the Rulers before him gave no indication that he was wrong. They were stunned into silence for several seconds before the first man spoke again.

“It’s not easy,” said Sora. “We… we aren’t as strong as you. Maybe you’re right that we chose the wrong path. Hopefully, together, we can have the strength in numbers that we don’t have separately.”

“Maybe,” James said. “I know it’s scary, and I sound like a crazy American, but a little crazy is what you need right now. If you don’t take risks, you can’t take back what you lost. The world that we all miss!”.

I guess I do identify with these morons, far more than I meant to, James thought. All of them, and Dean, and me… we’re just a bunch of dumbass idealists in our own ways. I’m just lucky I punch a bit harder. Maybe that’s why I’m being a bit hard on them in my head. This Confederation.

He started to turn away, then froze. He looked back at them.

“The Federation,” James said. “Your name. It should be something simple like that. It’s too long right now. The Confederation of something-republics. Banana Republic? Whatever. You know the Confederacy lost, right? And the Articles of Confederation failed? Don’t pick a name that connotes failure.”

The face of the American member of the group lit up with a flicker of recognition, while the Europeans looked slightly confused.

“Fix the name” James said again. “Take some aggressive action. That’s how you get yourselves taken seriously, instead of getting gone. And after you do, feel free to send an envoy my way.”

There. Carrot and stick. Although I guess I’m more speaking loudly and carrying a big stick instead of doing it Teddy Roosevelt’s way. Maybe I gave them bad advice, and they’ll all get killed. I wasted too much time on those five anyway.

He began walking away again. He could hear the group muttering among themselves again, although the Fisher Emperor could not make out the words. He glanced over at Zhang Chāo, who had fallen in step beside him again.

“You have a rather brusque style, James,” Zhang Chāo said, looking as if he was trying not to smile.

“And?” James asked.

“You’ve given them something to think about.”

Fine, don’t tell me what they’re saying. High perception prick…

James smiled slightly and shook his head, and the duo moved on.

Then he saw a familiar shape approaching. The figure was a part of a Ruler’s entourage, rather than a Ruler herself. All the members of the group were dressed in cloth face masks and loose-fitting, all-covering desert attire.

How do I know you?

But the familiar figure was beautiful and delicate even with her face covering and slightly baggy clothing concealing much of her appearance. It only took James a moment to place her.

“Sierra,” he said at just the moment that they were about to pass each other.

She froze in mid-stride, then seemed to forcefully compose herself. She turned to face James, and he saw her smile through the cloth over her mouth.

“I’m touched that you remembered me after all this time and recognized me despite the mask,” Sierra said.

“You have a distinctive appearance, and you have some rather unique abilities, too,” James replied. “You and…” He realized he was about to mention her twin brother, and he quickly decided to pivot away. “And well, that’s not even mentioning your personality! After traveling with you for weeks, how could I possibly forget you?”

“You know the Fisher Emperor?” said the tall masked figure beside her. He had a jovial, lightly accented male voice. “What a noteworthy figure to simply never mention!”

“Forgive my rudeness,” James said, turning to face the masked figure who he recognized must be the Ruler Sierra had accompanied.

“Not at all,” Sierra said. “The fault lies entirely with me. If I had informed the Undying King that I knew you, he would have undoubtedly wanted me to make introductions. Instead, I left it entirely to you to make the first move. You always do take the initiative, James.” She spoke softly, at first in a formal tone and then with a touch of apparent affection toward James.

“Only when someone lets me,” James replied. “You know I’m not that proactive.”

Sierra squinted up at him. “Are you sure you’re the same person I met before?”

“Maybe not. It could always be an illusion.”

She snorted. “Yeah. Right.”

“How did you two come to meet?” the Undying King asked. He spoke in the tone of the idly curious, and James had the strange feeling that he was listening to the voice of a man who had inherited wealth and power rather than one who had been forced to kill others to obtain it. He ignored that impression and answered.

“We had Orientation together,” James said. “I wasn’t the Fisher Emperor back then. Just a humble aspiring leader.”

Sierra looked like she wanted to laugh but was trying hard to control herself.

“What brought the two of you together?” James asked, looking at the Undying King.

“I think you mean the three of us,” the King began.

“Wait, are you the same Undying King who monopolized the market for undead creation artifacts?” asked Zhang Chāo as if suddenly realizing who was there.

“Well, I would hardly call it a monopoly,” the other man replied.

Then he and Zhang Chāo were engrossed in a tedious-sounding business discussion. Only a wink from the Taiwanese Ruler told James that the Undying King was now the subject of a deliberate distraction. This left only James, Sierra, and a third party, a man in full plate armor who had been mysteriously silent this whole time, left out of the conversation.

I can’t see an inch of that guy’s skin, James noticed. That was strange. The gaps in the armor were just dark, like it was void inside. Maybe this additional member of their party wasn’t human.

James turned his attention back to Sierra.

“How did things work out for you?” he asked. “You had plans of your own after Orientation.”

He was only vaguely aware of the existence of plans as an abstraction, but it was obvious Sierra had gone on a journey, so he could at least piece together that she had been searching for someone or something.

“They worked out well. The Undying King helped us out. That was part of why we ended up in his group here.”

“Us?” James said. “Hm.”

He looked the armored man up and down again, and he got a strange intuition. James extended his hand, and after a moment, the armored figure shook.

Yeah, I think that is him.

“Congratulations,” James said. “I don’t know how you did it.”

“A method that wouldn’t work for anyone else, if you’re wondering how you could accomplish it yourself,” Sierra said.

He shook his head. That kills all my curiosity.

“I have to go,” James said. “I’m glad I got to see you, but I need to figure out who wants to kill me, before this conference ends.”

“I hope it’s just one person coming for you this time,” Sierra said, half-smirking. She almost looked genuinely concerned. But it was hard to be certain with her. It might have been just a trick of the light.
