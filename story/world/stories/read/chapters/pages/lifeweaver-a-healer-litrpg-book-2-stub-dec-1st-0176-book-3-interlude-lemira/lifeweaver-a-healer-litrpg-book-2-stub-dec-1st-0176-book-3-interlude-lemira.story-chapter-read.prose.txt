While Lemira was used to following commands from those above her in authority, doing so this time felt wrong. Milla Quinn, the guildmistress, was kind and forgiving, perhaps a little too much so, whereas Lemira's father had been a stern parental figure who asserted control in the domineering manner of demon kind. A stark difference, certainly, and Lemira considered it as she rode past the guild headquarters of The Band. The clacking of hooves from under her echoed on the hard-paved ground.

Ignoring the guildmistress's order made her insides twist, almost akin to physical pain, but this time she followed the path she knew offered Earth and all the new friends she had made at least a slim chance to survive. Seeking refuge inside headquarters was a stalling effort at best, and one through which humanity would not come out victorious. The demonic horde never stopped increasing its numbers. The rends in the earth opening all over the city they called New York were portals only to the higher-ranking demons, the ones Luke had called greater demons. Lesser demons were birthed from them, not transported, and they would never, ever stop coming.

Fighting the lesser demons was an exercise in futility, and standing up to the greater demons was foolhardy at best. Even so, Lemira had chosen to do just that. If this system event was her doing, she didn't know - perhaps she would never know - but it was her responsibility to see it through. The infernal core inside her chest had ached ever since the first demon appeared, and a little while ago, that ache had supplied her with a direction. No, not one direction, but many. Far too many. Having selected one at random, Lemira rode for all she was worth.

Milla Quinn had listened to Lemira making her case, but ruled against pitting all resources of the guild against the demonic scourge. Another failing on Lemira's part was not being convincing enough for the guildmistress to see the threat. So, she rode on, hoping against hope that the humans of New York would see the truth before their eyes before it was too late. If this city fell, the kingdom would be next, and by that point, it would be too late.

The Singers' arrival at this point in time was no coincidence. Capitalizing on the chaos was a tried-and-true strategy when taking land. Her father had taught her that lesson well, but what they had accomplished was beyond a new system's capabilities. If not for the system event, not even the denizens of Infernal Flux, the world of her father's, would have been able to pass through the veil between systems. The Singers had not launched the invasion without outside interference. None of that mattered, however, not in the face of the challenge that had now befallen Earth. The demons.

And here was one now.

Lemira sent a quiet order to her steed, and it skidded to a halt on the wet ground. Rain was coming down hard now, and the skies thundered in the distance. Flashes of lightning produced harsh shadows in the dim light of early summer's afternoon. The sky was truly dark, despite the early hour.

"Lemira," the demon said.

Names hold power among demonkind. This creature knowing hers did not inspire much confidence in her, but she didn't have much in the way of options. No one had listened to her repeated pleas, but the greater demons needed to fall, and the crystals gathered. That was the only way to stop this.

"You know my father, then?" Lemira asked, raising her voice so it would carry through the thunder and across the road where a single demon waited for her.

It was male, tall, and lithe. Rather than the long hair worn by most demons, this one had grown a crown of horns atop his head. Clothed in resplendent cloth and fur, holding a scepter in one hand, this demon saw itself as one of great importance, perhaps even nobility. That made sense if he knew her father.

"Of course, he was my uncle after all, cousin."

A list of names scrolled through her mind, her horror growing with each one. Her father had many siblings, each of them terrible in their own way.

"My name is Xerventes, cousin. First son of Xertentes."

Lemira let out a low, unladylike curse and considered her options, even though she knew there were none. This would not be a battle favoring her, but it was a battle she would come through victorious nonetheless. Somehow.

"Return to Infernal Flux," Lemira said, buying time to make sure her skills and companions reached their optimal states.

Xerventes took a step forward, speaking without dropping his pleasant demeanor. It was all a front, of course. "You know I cannot do such a thing. A demon must serve its purpose. It is in our makeup. You must know that as well as I, despite your half-breed nature."

Raising his scepter, Xerventes continued. "I was surprised to hear of your father's demise. Even in his exile, my father had hoped to be the one standing over his corpse in the end. Tell me, half-breed cousin, how did he perish? Was it you?"

"What difference does it make?" Lemira asked as the ground rumbled. Her gaze shot to the scepter, and, just as she'd feared, it glowed a dull orange.

Before she could do something, anything, to prevent it, the ground yawned open, and a lesser demon spawned, full of rage and hate from the moment it was born. More followed behind it, and then more still. This was just what she had been afraid of. Her own companions were ill-suited to battle against scores of lesser creatures, and they grew more numerous by the second. Lemira needed to end this before it was too late. Perhaps it already was.

First, she brought out every companion, straining her will to keep so many active.

Closing her eyes, Lemira gave her steed free rein to keep her away from the lesser demons. Those swift, strong, and mindless little beasts would be even more dangerous now with Xerventes directing them.

Each ferocious elemental lurker was as good as two or three lesser demons, with The King of The Steppes taking on twice as many, and the wolf tore monsters apart. The glowing life force leaking from every wound sizzled in the rain as, for a moment, it looked like Lemira's companions would come out on top.

Then, Xerventes grinned and thrust the scepter into the air. Another rend in the earth opened, and then another, and the greater demon did not look the least bit burdened by the strain. It was as Lemira had feared. She was outclassed. Companions versus monsters was a losing battle, which meant she needed to find another avenue of attack.

Commanding the wolf to sweep itself in a shroud of shadows, Lemira ordered it to step away from the fight with a thought, directing it to instead circle around and strike at the one demon that mattered, Xerventes. To her surprise, the wolf managed its task, jaw clamping down on the greater demon's arm, and Xerventes yelped in pain, the grin slipping, even if it was just for a moment. Despite the massive wolf biting down, the demon didn't drop his means of commanding the lesser demons. Instead, Lemira's cousin grabbed the wolf by its neck. Lemira winced in pain and shut herself off from her companion, mourning the loss as it began glowing a dull orange. A moment later, the wolf burst apart in a spray of life force, its biological matter utterly incompatible with that which made up all living creatures borne of Infernal Flux.

The street was pure madness and chaos. Lemira's companions were buried under a writhing mass of lesser demons, and still more crept up through the rents in the street. Standing tall against the endless demonic horde was not something one did lightly, but she wouldn't bend or break. This was her mission, and she would see it through.

Her King roared, stunning those around it before following that up with a taunt that forced all monsters to focus their attention on it. A brave and noble use of its skill and toughness, but not one that would see Xerventes dead. Her companions didn't have much longer, nor would Lemira be able to keep them active, and even her steed was tiring, having fled and jumped out of the jaws of lesser demons several times since the battle began.

Lemira had to admit, if only to herself, that she stood no chance of coming away from this with her life intact. The simple act of accepting her fate lifted a weight off her shoulders, and a clear picture of what she needed to do appeared before her inner eye. If she were about to meet her infernal end, then she would at least take Xerventes with her.

Gathering her strength, she scoured through her list of skills, finding the one she was after. Just as Lemira was about to activate her trump card, she received a message from someone she didn't know.

Penny: "Heyyy! Better put those lions back in their cages if you want them to live. Do it now!"

Unsure what to make of it, Lemira returned her attention to the skill, reading its description. Were the sounds of thunder and flashes of lightning coming closer? The rain was getting overpowering, which turned the battle into even more of a mess. Hundreds of lesser demons had crawled into the street. Soon, they would number in the thousands.

Penny: "Not joking! Also, you'll want to head inside that building! Last chance, girlie!"

Lemira cursed, but decided to trust this stranger.

Storm, answer me!

Thunder, break the sky!

I am the lightning, and I have come!

Directing her steed, they both threw themselves through the open doors and into what appeared to be an indoor marketplace of some sort. The floor just inside was slick with water, and they slipped and fell in a heap just as Lemira dismissed her steed and all other pets who were still outside and alive.

It was just in time, too, because lightning cracked and slammed into the ground just outside the building, but not before several lesser demons made their way inside, having followed Lemira's retreat. Lightning struck again and again with enough force to shatter the windows. It was almost pitch black now, and Lemira scrambled to her feet, unsure how to meet the threat of the lesser demons already inside. Bringing her companions back out was impossible, and without them, there wasn't much she could accomplish.

Backing away from the snarling beasts, her back struck something hard, and she let out a yelp as hands grabbed her shoulders, pushing her aside.

"Do not worry, mi amiga. You are safe now, no reason to fret."

A man stepped around her and put himself between Lemira and the lesser demons, a man wielding a spear. He bent at the knees, bounced a few times as if testing his footing, then charged forward and through the demons with a battle cry, killing them all without breaking a sweat.

Lemira was about to give her thanks to this mysterious stranger, but the spearman kept going, heading out into the worst thunderstorm she'd ever seen.

"Wait!" she shouted, but was drowned out by a booming thunderclap strong enough to force her back a step.

Afraid she might see his corpse on the ground just outside, Lemira walked toward the now blown-apart doors. The outside was almost pure darkness now, except for the constant flashing of lightning. In those flashes, she saw the falling rain and the back of a man dancing among the beasts, a deadly dance of the spear that left anything drawing near dead in its wake.

Lemira stood there, stunned, watching the scene, before she realized that the rain no longer pattered on the ground before her. So what was it she saw falling around the man? Not drops of rain, she realized. They were spears, an uncountable number of spears, all of them falling almost too fast for her eyes to catch them, mere streaks in the flashing lights, like a beautiful rain of death.

A figure appeared, descending through the air, her hair billowing in all directions, lightning crackling among the strands. Her eyes glowed white like those of a goddess, and when she spoke, it was as if they carried thunder within them.

I have come on thunder,

I have come in lightning!

And none shall take my friends from me!

The soaked short-sleeved upper garment with an explicit word printed on the front detracted a little from the image of a goddess, but the way she reached a hand out to point at Xerventes seemed to affirm her authority.  Lightning leapt from the tip of her finger, striking the greater demon's still-raised scepter. It broke, shattering into pieces. Lemira had never seen something like it before, and she stood there in awe.

Xerventes still lived, and a ritual circle appeared above his head, an infernal spell forming. A spear slammed through the demon's chest before it finished, and another followed, piercing Xerventes's skull, then many more, and more still, until the greater demon fell face-first on the ground. Dead. he was dead. Lemira could hardly believe it. With him, the rends in the earth closed back up, stopping the lesser demons.

The young woman landed next to the spearman, and the two of them turned to face Lemira.

"Lemira, right?" said the young woman. "I'm Penny!"

The spearman straightened and flashed a kind smile. "Mateo. Pleased to make your acquaintance, mi amiga!"

"W-who are you?" Lemira asked.

Penny flashed a smile of her own. "Luke mentioned some trouble over here, so we figured we'd pop in and help." She glanced up at the sky and got a faraway look on her face. "Good thing we did, too, because this place is going right to hell." She looked over at Lemira. "Heh, 'hell'. Get it?"
