Fatigue weighed heavily on Jade's body, and she wasn't the only one. Her friends were wounded and exhausted, it was plainly visible in their sluggish responses to Vastrozun's emergence. They weren't prepared for another fight. But they didn't have a choice in the matter. Even if they had, this wasn't something they could walk away from. The wounded demon lord lurched closer to them, barely keeping himself upright. Jade seized on that hope, clinging to it as she rallied her companions.

"He's weak…" Jade panted, wiping a line of blood from her forehead. She forced herself to stand up straight and project an air of confidence. "We can do this."

"Together." Luis nodded. He stepped forward, interposing himself between Vastrozun and the rest of the party, armed only with his curved sword. His splintered shield lay discarded on the ground nearby, reduced to a pile of slag.

The demon lord took another step closer. His gaze swept across Ira's limp body and he stopped, his expression growing thunderous.

"Now," Vastrozun growled, thrusting one palm forward, "reap the rewards for your defiance."

Darkness seeped from his skin, flowing like tar to congeal in an orb that floated over his palm. It grew larger, swelling to the size of a bowling ball, until-

"Aargh!"

The orb erupted in a shower of golden energy, and Jade felt a magical backlash rip through the demon lord's arm. More radiant essence wafted from the welts on his body with a sizzling hiss. Luis surged forward, closing the distance between them as quickly as his exhausted frame could carry him. His sword flashed in a silver arc, but Vastrozun knocked it aside with a massive palm. Luis wasn't done. He pressed the demon back, weaving a web of golden threads in his wake as his sword cut through the air in a flurry of attacks.

"Everyone!"

All of them: Jade, Luis, Naomi, and the others, leapt into the fray. Fenrin discarded his empty quiver, drawing a shortsword and joining Luis and Jade in the melee. Naomi, one of the few of them still okay on mana, continued to pour fortifying energy into the air, infusing her friends with strength and speed. Even Siora joined in, firing conjured spikes of ice as quickly as her cooldowns allowed. Only Tara stayed back, still too wounded to contribute directly. Instead, Jade heard her through her earpiece, calling for Seris to be ready to pick them up. It took Jade a moment to understand the urgency in her voice, it wasn't like Vastrozun would just let them walk away, even if they wanted to. Then, she caught a glimpse of the sky.

The conjured fog was rapidly fading, releasing shafts of crimson sunlight back onto the surrounding wasteland. Far above it in the distance, approaching in too vast a number for Jade to count, were the silhouettes of large, winged figures.

"Your gamble failed." Vastrozun grunted, defending against the myriad of incoming attacks with unyielding fortitude. "I yet live, and my children will soon arrive to dispose of you."

"We're finishing this before that." Naomi challenged. She paused he spell, drawing a weapon from her belt. "Luis!"

Luis disengaged long enough to glance over his shoulder.

"Now we're talkin'!"

Naomi flung Ira's silver blade across the battlefield. The flaming weapon landed several feet short of Luis, bounced once, and then slid to a stop at his feet. Luis flicked it into the air with a quick kick and snatched it, dropping his former weapon and charging back into the fight. Not a moment too soon. Jade and Fenrin were almost completely drained of magic, forced to rely almost exclusively on raw swordsmanship as they dueled a demon lord more ancient than they could fathom. Consuming the divine relic seemed to have put him in a similar situation, fortunately, but when it came to combat experience, they were simply outclassed. Vastrozun had drawn an enormous, rusted greatsword from a hook on his back. It didn't look like much, but as Jade soon discovered, even an inferior weapon was a lethal tool in the hands of a master.

We can't give him time to recover. Jade thought, fighting through her fatigue with dogged determination. We can do this!

There! Vastrozun stumbled on a loose piece of stony debris. Jade seized the opening, striking twice with her daggers, driving them into a pair of large cracks that split the back of the demon lord's armor. Before she could follow up with a more devastating hit she was forced to jump away from Vastrozun's counter, barely managing to cross her blades and deflect his enormous weapon. The force of the blow sent Jade spinning towards the ground, but she wasn't done yet. Jade twisted in the air, planting her palms on the flagstones and flipping back to her feet before kicking off a slanted slab of stone and charging back in.

"Ah!"

Jade's left leg spasmed and she stumbled, almost falling. The sensation was different than her other wounds, biting deeper. She fought through the pain with grim determination, rejoining Luis and Fenrin to surround Vastrozun and pressure him from all sides. The demon lord was immensely strong, but his wounds were slowing him. With Naomi's fleetfoot melody bolstering them, they managed to stay ahead of his powerful strikes.

Smash!

Vastrozun's greatsword slammed into the ground between Fenrin and Jade, impacting so forcefully that the flagstone exploded into razor-like splinters. With her armor's defenses gone, even these near misses chipped away at Jade's remaining stamina. She seized the opening anyway, ramming Fang into a gap in the demon lord's armor just beneath the arm. The draining effect of her blade triggered, sending a small trickle of life energy back into her beleaguered body, but at this point it barely moved the needle. She lurched away from his retaliatory strike, ragged breaths escaping her heaving chest.

Dodge. Strike. Jump back. Dash in.

The battle continued, silent save for the clash of weapons and grunts of pain. This was no climactic clash between heroes and villains, as stories so often praised. There were no dramatic speeches, impassioned words, nor ultimate spells at play. Despite the stakes, this final confrontation felt little different than any of the other desperate struggles Jade and her friends had survived over the past months. Two opposing sides, limbs heavy with exhaustion, wearing each other down one blow at a time. It was a grueling war of attrition, where every inch gained exacted its toll in blood. All that mattered now was which side had more to give. How long the fight carried on in this manner, Jade couldn't say. All she knew was that, at some point, her body simply reached its limit.

Jade saw the attack coming. She had just raked another savage slash across the demon lord's side. He spun towards her, heaving his greatsword in a massive arc. She ducked the blade, letting it whistle over her head, and then lunged back in to continue the attack. Or rather, she tried to. Her legs trembled, barely keeping her upright. She tried again, but they still just wouldn't move.

No. No! They were almost there. She couldn't. She… couldn't…

The demon lord didn't look so great himself. His breaths came in short, ragged bursts, and they were accompanied by a sickening squelch that definitely hadn't been there before. But even in his current state, he couldn't miss an opportunity like that. Eschewing anything more sophisticated, Vastrozun slammed one of his giant fists into Jade's chest.

CRACK.

For the second time in the past half hour, Jade felt something, or multiple somethings, crunch in her chest. The subsequent wave of pain was so all encompassing that it washed away everything else. When she regained her senses, Jade found herself landing in a pile of cracked and broken stones, her body curled in on itself. Tears stung at her eyes and each shallow breath was accompanied by a fresh bolt of lightning in her chest. She was vaguely aware of the distant clash of metal on metal, but she couldn't bring herself to turn her head to see it. It was just too much. She had done all she could… right? No one… no one could have expected anything more from her. That's right… that was it. She'd… done well.

Darkness encroached on the edges of her vision. Everything began to fade, even the pain. Jade didn't fight it. If anything, she welcomed the escape. Her life had been nothing but fear, pain, and battle for so long. Why should she torture herself further? She deserved to rest.

Her eyelids fluttered. They were so… heavy…

Jade…

Someone was calling her. She ignored it, what did it matter? But the speaker persisted.

Jade!

Alan…?

Then, she remembered.

I'm not doing this for myself.

It had never been about her. The reason she was pushing herself, the reason she'd endured so much and yet continued to press on was for them. Her family. Her friends. The people who relied on her, whether they knew it or not. Maybe she had reached the point where she was willing to give up on herself, but she sure as hell wasn't about to give up on them.

Jade opened her eyes. Never before had such a simple act taxed her willpower to such an extreme, but she did it. A red sky stretched above, still partially obscured by the remnant of the oppressive dark cloud. In the distance, the demon host drew ever closer. Her companions doggedly fought on a short distance away. Tara was on the ground now, too, Siora kneeling beside her. Naomi's music still blasted across the desolate courtyard, although it was faltering now, the magic beginning to waver.

"Jade…"

She heard her name again, louder this time. Wait, she hadn't been imagining that? Turning her head, Jade found herself almost face to face with Ira. The angel was lying in a pool of silver blood, her dark wings spread limply beside her. But despite the mortal wound punching through her chest, faint, ragged breaths still slipped between her bloodied lips. It took all of Jade's remaining mental capacity just to process the sight of her. She wasn't dead…? And wait, how did she even know her name? Actually, she didn't care. Of all the things to worry about, that was the least pressing.

"What?" Jade croaked, meeting the angel's unfocused gaze as best she could. She didn't possess the requisite strength to be hostile. It's not like they could do anything to each other, at this point.

"Don't… don't let it take me.""Take… you?""The Labyrinth."

Jade realized then that she could feel it. A mental force lightly tugging on the angel's subconscious. She recalled Ira mentioning something like that earlier. Some kind of compulsion from the Labyrinth to… what… integrate with it? Jade couldn't remember everything Ira had said but, judging by the state Seraphine had been in when they fought her, it wasn't a pleasant experience.

"How…?"

That single word was the only response Jade could muster. She coughed, spraying something red and sticky across the stones in front of her. The taste of copper lingered in her mouth. Jade's gaze slipped from Ira's to examine the blood.

Hmm… a distant, semi-delirious part of her mind thought, that doesn't look good.

"Succubus." Ira murmured, the light in her eyes fading. "Drain."

In her current state, Jade didn't have the capacity to discern between allies and enemies. All she knew was that beside her lay a scared, dying woman, who faced what she understood to be a terrible fate. Moreover, there was something she could do about it. She didn't think. Jade dragged herself closer, leaving a red smear on the rough stones in her wake. Only a few feet separated them, but it felt so, so far. She pushed through the pain, heaving herself closer. Everything was fading again, and this time, no mystical voice called to push back the darkness. But Jade didn't let go. She had a mission, now, and she wouldn't give up on it. That had always been the case. Once she had her mind set on something, she wouldn't budge on it. That mindset had propelled her to the top of the athletic world… and had also been her undoing. In this instant, however, that sheer refusal to give in to the inevitable served her once again.

With one final heave, Jade reached the angel. Then, as everything began to fade away, she pressed their lips together.
