Chapter 8

Angels

Of course, something immediately goes wrong, because why wouldn’t it?

It’s not so much a problem as it is just a massive, glaring red flag, but we hardly start out before we encounter the first sign that the dungeon has fundamentally changed.

Dungeons are a sort of non-Euclidean space where the natural laws, or at least your perceptions of them, get a lot more wibbly-wobbly. Concepts like gravity, thermodynamics, the square-cube law, and even distance and time are influenced a lot more by the whims of the dungeon core than by those of Einstein. The more powerful the core, the more these things can be bent.

That is to say that the actual internal size of a dungeon isn’t necessarily going to match the size of the outside world that it occupies. However, every dungeon also has an exterior domain, a kind of zone of control that serves as an external barrier between it and the rest of existence, where it wields less influence than within the dungeon proper. This domain is also the space that the dungeon will swallow the next time it expands.

However, calling the domain a barrier isn’t a coincidental choice of words. See, dungeons really don’t seem to like visitors, so each one gussies itself up a little differently to discourage approach. The domain might be a barren, bone-strewn path leading to an ominous entrance, or it might be a snippet of ocean waters washing up against a stone wall of water-worn divots and grottos, only one of which is the actual dungeon entrance.

This is actually a rather weird habit, because every indication is that, in some manner, the presence of higher lifeforms like humans feeds something back to the dungeon, supplementing it with vital energies it can’t get from its arcane font or as some sort of data feed. So a dungeon wants, in so much as it can be personified to have wants, to keep us close without letting us in. If we’re close, it gets those resources, but if we get in, we start disrupting its power build-up. The most active dungeons, then, have a domain that offers some benefit to venturing into it while still discouraging actual entrance.

The Forest Cavern had accomplished this by misdirection. Its domain was an unsullied expanse of green, untouched by boot or blade, and if you got off course, mists would envelop you, and you would find yourself back where you came in at. It wasn’t flashy, but it did the job, and it was probably a fairly low-energy solution. A perfect fit for a low-power dungeon out in the middle of nowhere.

That’s why I can’t seem to bring myself to do anything but stare dumbly at the neat, pristine, quality road nestled between perfectly manicured ditches and a charming tree line. It’s not that much different from the main imperial highway, or the road curling up to my estate that connects it to the village. The air is refreshing, the sun is pleasant, and we’re feeling entirely too welcomed.

“This feels way too bass ackwards,” I note.

“You know full well that didn't translate,” Ayre flatly snipes back at me.

“Otsui.”

Ah, there's that red in his cheeks! “Shut up!”

I give him a break for now and turn to look over the group. There's four of us here, and all of them are staring at the road like I was. Unfortunately, the fourth isn't Yorin, and it's not anyone from the guild, either, but I don't feel jipped. After all, we've got an actual front-liner for this one, and he's the world's strongest horned rabbit.

I really thought I'd have a harder time convincing him to join. When I asked him, I stressed the threat to the village and mentioned the benefits a dungeon would bring to his training. I even pointed out that he would be the first wild monster to clear a dungeon. And yet I expected it would take much more than that - bribes, promises, even special den structures for the estate. But, after some expected hesitancy, he volunteered right away. Maybe he's more serious about protecting this place than I gave him credit for.

… Not serious enough to walk on his own four feet, though, when Korrigan remains happy to carry him around like a baby.

“What do you think, Kyuuga?” I ask. After all, the way he's watching it, even he knows this isn't normal. Which wouldn't surprise me; he probably knows the dungeon's boundary very well from his own patrols. “They're rolling out the red carpet for us. Do we accept it?”

He glances to me for a moment, then looks back to the road. With a brief effort to get his feet under him, he hops from Korrigan’s arms and to the ground. The rabbit takes a few tentative hops forward, then looks back to us once more.

And then he bolts at full sprint down the lane!

“Ah! Hey! Don’t leave us behind,” the oni child calls, starting forward after him. “Kyuuga!”

“Kyuuga, don’t be reckless,” Ayre shouts as well. He’s faster than Korrigan, but not fast enough to catch up with Kyuuga.

I chuckle at the scene, then cheat by engaging Empower and keeping up with the lot of them with a light jog.

This is nice, running along with friends on such a pleasant day. It almost feels like home.

* * *

“Wow, they really pulled out all of the stops with the remodel, didn’t they?” I ask as we stand before the entrance to the Forest Cavern Dungeon. “You think I could hire the core to consult on my house for the estate?”

But Ayre gives me a flat, semi-revolted look. “First the crystals, now this … I’m starting to think maybe you and this dungeon share the same kitsch taste.”

Before, the entrance was a simple cave, literally a hole in the wall of the cliff face. After the interesting way the domain filtered visitors, the actual doorway had been a disappointment. I’d rated it a six out of ten.

But now, the cave entrance has been rounded into a smooth half-oval that looks deliberately crafted, and mirrored winged figures that I can only describe as angelic are carved into the cliff face to either side, as if beckoning visitors in. Their figures are a bit ambiguous, so I can’t really tell if they’re pretty men or androgynous women, but that’s probably the point.

When I turn to Ayre, I decide to ignore his comment about taste as I point to the carvings, themselves. “Hey, Ayre, does the Tolestean faith have angels, or are these supposed to be harpies?”

The archer gives a put-upon sigh as he crosses his arms and his ears droop. “I feel like you wouldn’t have to ask such questions if you listened to Sacred Yorin as much as you should.”

He raises his head to meet my gaze as he moves to answer me anyway, though. “To be honest, that first word didn’t translate, so no, it’s safe to say we don’t have that concept. Harpies are often considered to have a stronger tie to the Heavens than the human races that are bound to the ground, though, because we’re closer to the Arcana, while they’re further removed from it and closer to the Essence. Their connection to the Essence, then, is stronger and purer than ours.”

Ayre crosses his arms again with a bit of a scowl. “Nowadays, that’s generally considered to be mythology, as the Essence is so far away, and the Arcana so close, that the difference in exposure by altitude is irrelevant. It also gets uncomfortably close to echoing the kind of sentiment that led to the use of Arcana being branded as heresy and grounds for violence in olden times, so you’re unlikely to hear even the harpies themselves make such a claim anymore. A lot of them still go into the clergy, though.”

I turn back toward the statues, pinching my chin in thought as I look up at them. “So that’s how they’re seen here, huh? Kind of weird I didn’t see any in the capital, then. Though I guess I didn’t really see much of the High Temple.”

My friend nods back. “If you were going to see them anywhere in the capital, that’s where they’d be. They’re more common in mountainous or wide-open regions, and are usually assigned there. They’re agoraphilic by nature, so they don’t really care for dense urban sprawls, forests or valleys.”

I bob my head firmly at that. I can definitely relate. City life has conveniences, to be sure, but wide open spaces are the places to live.

… Maybe I should have chosen to be a harpy during calibration. Flight through open skies, aerial superiority as I shoot down at my foes. But then, depending on how their wings work, it might have gotten in the way of the fine motor control necessary to operate a firearm, and who knows how much of a pain it might be to get gunpowder soot out of feathers. Not to mention all the time I spend in caves like the one in front of us.

Nah, nevermind, sticking with Human was the right way to go.

In slides Ayre’s critical, plush-lipped face to my field of vision, bottom lip jutted out judgmentally. “You’re thinking about something weird again, I can tell.”

I snap a bright smile onto my face with practiced efficiency. “Anyway, let’s head in, shall we?”

“That's not an answer!”

Fortunately, I’m saved from having to give my friend one by the appearance of the dungeon’s entry notice. Unfortunately, it’s because it doesn’t bear good news.

You are entering “Forest Cavern Dungeon”

Arcana Level: CRITICAL

Point Multiplier: 500%

WARNING: This site’s energy levels have reached a critical state. It will soon undergo an expansion event. If this is not an approved domain expansion, emergency procedures should be enacted accordingly.

If suitable action plans are unavailable, immediate cascade neutralization should be prioritized. If neutralization is impossible, evacuate all sapient life from the affected area until expansion is complete.

Estimated time remaining until expansion event:

11:42:33

“I’ve never seen an arcana category that high, or an active countdown,” Ayre observes first.

Korrigan groans and rubs at her eyebrow. “Trying to understand this box is making my head hurt …”

“Don’t worry,” I assure her, serious again as I draw my pistol. “It’s just saying to do what we came here to do. Heads on a swivel, everyone. If the dungeon is unstable, there’s no telling what we’re going to run into in there.”

Ayre gives a firm nod, unfolds his compound bow and pulls an arrow from its quiver to hold at the ready. That gives Korrigan focus, too, who grips her bone-white staff with conviction. At her feet, Kyuuga’s hackles raise as he curves himself into the fluffiest, most hostile U-shape imaginable.

“Alright, boys and girls,” I declare as I give the slide a sharp pull. “It’s showtime!”

We all pass through the tunnel entrance and leave the sunshine behind us. Like our first time here, it transitions into a curving, dimly lit, downward-sloping tunnel. Unlike before, it’s got a much more finished look to it. Instead of the natural look, each section of the floor is leveled off, and the walls and ceiling are smooth. Instead of sudden drops we have to work our way down, there’s very comfortably arranged steps to take us down to each new platform. Crystals still provide the illumination, but they’re mounted in brass fixtures to the walls at regular intervals, and they glow with the same yellow light as a Holy Arcana Crystal.

Still, we move slow and steady, our eyes peeled and our best ears open for so much as a pin to drop. Each step is made as silently as possible, and I keep my gun constantly trained on that ever-rotating passageway ahead of us.

Just like the last time, however, nothing comes at us, and we eventually exit into a large, open chamber. Unlike last time … it’s not empty.

The entire room is centered around a glistening fountain. Crystals light the ceiling far overhead as if we’ve stepped back into the daylight. Soft spring grass and flowers cover the floor and fill the air with pleasant scents. All assortments of chairs and benches circle the courtyard, providing plenty of seating for the nobody that’s here, and long tables fill the spaces between, piled with all sorts of fruits, berries and fresh vegetables.

It feels like we've stepped into some sort of fair at the park, and we just got lucky enough to be the first to arrive.

All of us have begun to break our tight formation as we try to take it all in, though I’m the first to voice our no doubt shared thought. “What in the world did we walk into down here?”

“Oh my gosh, hi! Welcome to the Forest Cavern!”

Clearly, our nerves aren’t entirely sedated, because as one collective movement, we all wheel on the high-pitched, sunny voice in an instant. I’ve got my trigger half-depressed, Ayre’s arrow is fully drawn back and spiraling with green energy by the time he’s whipped around, Korrigan’s staff is flickering with lightning, and Kyuuga jumps, spins in mid-air, and comes down ready to lunge.

But if we were shocked by the park, we’re definitely not ready for what we see before us.
