They all stared at each other with the same questioning look. In other circumstances, it could have been pretty funny, but this was serious.

Thomas spoke first. "You know, I half expected a video game prompt or something to pop up in front of us. Like, 'Well done, adventurer! Here's your reward!'"

Jo looked at Zach. "What do you say, Mr. System Encyclopedia? Did anyone in your creepy family ever get a quest like this?"

Zach shook his head vehemently. "No way. But from what it sounds like, they're working against what the System wants, so that tracks. I don't know how you resolve quests."

"It's not that complicated," Twitch said. "The scroll told us that we need to return this whole place to humanity, right? What've we done so far?" He started checking off points on his fingers. "Kicked out the guards, destroyed the portal so no one gets in from the elf's world—"

"Aether," Jo corrected.

"Aether, right, right. Now we've looted the vault or inventory room or whatever. But that can't be everything. After we're gone, nothing stops Zach's creepy-ass family from coming back and just restarting operations with a whole new group of prisoners they bought from somewhere. So the quest ain't done yet."

Ugh. He had a point.

Jo didn't look happy either. She crossed her arms over her chest. "So what, we're supposed to guard this place from elves and Zach's creepy family—"

"I left my creepy family years ago," Zach muttered.

Jo shrugged, then restated her objection, "So we're supposed to guard this place until the end of time?"

"Probably until System Integration Day," Thomas muttered, closing his eyes. Suddenly that 12-hour boon from the System didn't seem nearly enough. "Then it will be a free-for-all."

She shook her head. "No, I'm not willing to do that. Maybe we should just bring this mountain down around the dungeon, cut it off from everybody, and leave. Zach, you got those magic fireworks? You think we can set them off on a timer? We could blow up this whole place and seal the dungeon in."

Thomas felt a spike of alarm. "We can't do that. Eventually the dungeon needs to be dived or else it will overflow, and monsters will come out after the Integration."

"I have a hard time imagining those wisps getting out if the whole mountain was brought down over it," Zach said, though he sounded a little doubtful about the whole idea. "The bosses might have a chance if they worked long enough on it, though."

Thomas shook his head. "We can't risk it. Eventually something will dig out and then attack nearby communities who had nothing to do with this place." He took in a breath. "I think we need to go the other way."

Zach gave him a sideways glance. "What are you talking about, Mana Man? Turn it over to the National Guard?"

"Fuck the National Guard," Twitch practically spat.

Thomas had barely an idea, but it was still better than destroying this place and making the overflow someone else's problem in a few months. "No, not the government if we can help it. We need to open up this place to everybody. Remember that enchanter guy who upgraded my halberd back in the LA marketplace? He actually gave me hints about this dungeon. People know about it because it's probably one of the few Light mana-centered dungeons on Earth. The System's right that it should belong to us. Human beings, I mean." As he talked, he started warming up to his idea. "A level three Light, Electric, and Nature dungeon? Hell, all we need to do is just let people know about it, and they're going to come here on their own."

"But you're not talking about bringing in the National Guard, right?" Twitch sounded alarmed. Considering what they put him through, Thomas didn't blame him.

"No, they aren't even guarding dungeons in remote places. I meant we make this place a public dungeon. I have a — hold on, let me get it." He started digging through his pack, which, despite its massive extra spatial size, was actually starting to fill up from all the sheer amount of loot. Finally, he pulled out the guide that he and Zach purchased at the LA Marketplace. "This is a list of dungeons the National Guard doesn't know about or doesn't bother to block off. There's a whole bunch of them in the Western states, and a lot are level three, like this." He waved the thick booklet around. Amused, Jo took it from him, and so Thomas waved his hands around as he spoke, instead. "Look, I know my idea sounds dumb, but I think people who want exotic drops will go for it. We pass out flyers and maps of this place. Let as many people know exactly what this is. It'll be impossible for the branch family to keep them all out. If the Guard eventually takes over... well, whatever. At least it will still belong to humanity."

"We can't trust that," Twitch said, looking around with eyes narrowed. Clearly, he was thinking. "Eventually, someone with power's gonna step in and take advantage of this place. It's just a money maker waiting to happen. It would be real easy to remove the bars and convert the cells to rooms. Make it a hotel or hostel or something, and charge a fee for entrance."

Thomas shrugged. "Sure, some asshole will try it, but as long as it's not the elves and as long as prisoners aren't being forced in and out to mine this place, who cares?"

"'Cause we could be that asshole charging a fee for entrance," Twitch said.

Thomas started to laugh. Being a business owner was not in his blood whatsoever, but then after a moment, he realized that Twitch was not joking.

Jo was clearly thinking along the same lines. "What, really?"

Twitch shrugged. "I ain't running it myself; I'll just get a good crew under me, you know what I'm saying? Pay them in mana to fix up, manage the place, and take a cut of the profits. That way, we keep an eye on things here."

"You really think that would work?" Zach asked, cocking his head.

"Hell yeah. People are looking for work, you know how it is out there," Twitch said. "I got my contacts and people who want to get out of the gang. People who know construction and shit. Give me a few days, and I can get something sketched out." He nodded back at Zach. "You bus me and Jo in every once in a while to make sure things are running the way they should and enforce the message if they aren't. But we pay our people enough to make sure they're loyal—that's the key. Thomas takes care of that. He's the money guy and maybe does some healing if people get in trouble. Meanwhile, we all get the rewards."

He was serious about this, and it didn't sound like too bad of a plan. Thomas was happy to be the money and occasional healing guy. Zach would be the transportation, and from the stern look on Jo's face, clearly, she didn't mind being an enforcer to make sure no one else tried to take over this dungeon.

"Yeah... that could work," Thomas said slowly.

They waited a beat, and though nobody said anything out loud, Thomas was certain that he wasn't the only one who half-expected a video game prompt to finally appear in front of their faces. Just planning out management of the dungeon's facility wasn't going to be enough. It looked like they actually had to do it.

Then he was struck by a sudden realization. "No, wait, I just thought of something. The System contacted us through the dungeon. Maybe that's what we need to do again."

"What, rerun the dungeon and go to the reward room and hope there's a scroll?" Now Twitch sounded like he wanted to laugh at Thomas.

"Yeah, exactly," Thomas said. "That seems to be how people get messages from the System. Through the dungeons."

"Huh, you know, you might have a point," Jo said, "but I don't really want to rerun that dungeon until we at least get this set up."

They were in agreement, and after some back and forth, decided to split up the duties in several ways. Jo and Twitch offered to check out the rest of the facility to make sure that there weren't any rogue guards still hiding out down a tunnel somewhere. Twitch said he was going to give another electric jolt to the actual portal itself, and not just the control panel, just to make sure that it was completely dead.

They were also going to mark points to clear out equipment that they could sell. Thomas insisted on stripping this place down to the nuts and bolts.

Twitch claimed that he knew people who could take out the prison doors and make the former cells into "alcoves" that could serve as sleeping areas or common rooms. The whole place would definitely be more hostel than a luxury hotel, but as Twitch pointed out, it would cater to adventurer types, not people who were glamping.

Meanwhile, Thomas and Zach got the enviable (in Thomas's opinion) job of moving out the loot from the inventory room.

Since Thomas was free for Zach to transport, thanks to his Ability, he was the pack mule. That was no problem for him.

After a little discussion, they decided to use three separate locations for their storage units. The first would be back up in Tahoe. Thomas and Jo were a little worried that they might be recognized, but the law enforcement who had come after them had not seemed to be local. Plus, Zach had a stupid amount of fake driver's licenses he could use to sign a rental agreement.

The second storage unit would be in Reno to make it easy to move mana through the Dark Elf's portal. Light mana was one of the main currencies, and it would be a great way to convert level three crystals into gear and higher-leveled mana.

The last unit would be in LA, nearest to Griffith Park. Unsurprisingly, out of the three, the LA unit was by far the most expensive, but they actually took mana as payment alongside dollars, so that easily became a moot point.

There were probably better options to hide all the goods, like a remote cave in the middle of the forest, but nobody knew the location of a convenient cave. The storage units weren't a thousand percent safe, but as long as Zach's teleportation couldn't be tracked, it was the best idea they had.

Cleaning out the rest of the inventory room didn't take nearly as long as Thomas thought. After putting the last of the mana and items in the LA storage unit, Thomas turned to Zach.

"How are you doing on Teleportation mana?"

Zach chuckled. "Dude, now you're carrying all the magical weight, and you weigh nothing to me at all, my mana's going a lot further than it used to. Why? Got something in mind?"

"Yeah, a new house," Thomas said. "The front door of my apartment was kicked in a couple of days ago. I still got to figure out what to do with my nephews," and my brother, he mentally added. He had left his cell phone back in Arizona because he wasn't risking any location tracking to the storage units, but there hadn't been so much as a missed call from Derek. "They're going to need a new place to live, Earth-side. Plus, Twitch is basically homeless, and I doubt Jo and her daughter are going to feel safe at her house anytime soon. Even if they're not interested, we need a gathering point now we're running a business."

"You thinking a team house?" Zach asked, then nodded before Thomas had time to reply. "Yeah, that'll be a good place to crash and stash some stuff outside the storage units. If the yard's big enough and you don't mind, I'd like to pitch my tent out there."

"Sure." Thomas grinned. "You're welcome to the couch, too, once it starts snowing."

"That'll be good for Kevin," Zach said seriously, "I don't think he likes the snow. But, dude, are you sure you still want to live in Tahoe? You know my teleportation range is pretty far. The team could be anywhere."

"Lake Tahoe's my home," Thomas said, "I'm not letting anyone drive me away from it."

Zach thought about it for a moment then nodded. "There are worse places to ride out the Integration."

Thomas privately agreed. Tiny was insane for wanting to go back to New York. If things fell apart the way he feared, he didn't want to be surrounded by literal millions of panicking people who now had magical powers while all social order broke down.

With a shudder, he turned his mind from that scenario.

"You know that luxury vacation place on Stateline, right on the California side?"

Zach grinned and, in answer, laid a hand on Thomas's shoulder.

A moment later they were there. Normally, in the height of summer, the road that went from the touristy part of California to the touristy and casino part of Nevada would be absolutely choked with tourists. Now only a couple of cars were parked on the block. People all around the world were holding their breath for the System Integration in a few short months, and no one felt like vacationing.

That was probably why, when Thomas and Zach walked into the luxury property management office, the lady manning the desk was very enthusiastic to see them. She barely asked for references.

After a quick tour, Thomas had signed a lease and walked out feeling a little like he'd been hit over the head. He was now the new renter of a ridiculous lakeside log house. It had five bedrooms and six baths.

Why there were more baths than bedrooms, he had no clue, except that the place was meant to be an Airbnb for the rich and wealthy. Now that those jerks were all hiding out in their bunkers or whatever, property management places were interested in long-term rentals.

The monthly rent would have once made him clutch his heart, keel over, and die, but for once Thomas's bank account wasn't doing badly. If currency switched fully over to mana, he'd be doing even better.

The log cabin house was fully furnished, of course, and because it catered to people of means (workaholics), it had a basic desktop setup with a little business station, including a scanner and printer.

The first thing Thomas did, after raiding the kitchen for some of the goods the previous renters had left behind, was fire up a basic word processor and start making simple flyers with a map.

Things were looking up.

They'd go to the LA Free Marketplace, sell off more of their mana and some of the weapons, and start spreading the word about a new dungeon for the right entrance fee. Zach was talking about looking into marketplaces in Phoenix and Tempe, too. The dungeon was within driving distance of LA, and those people did love to drive, but the more locals who knew about it, the better. Hopefully, the National Guard wouldn't be too interested in the place. It helped that it was out in the middle of basically nowhere.

As soon as they got their first customers in, they'd make a dungeon run for themselves and see what the System thought about how they had completed their quest.
