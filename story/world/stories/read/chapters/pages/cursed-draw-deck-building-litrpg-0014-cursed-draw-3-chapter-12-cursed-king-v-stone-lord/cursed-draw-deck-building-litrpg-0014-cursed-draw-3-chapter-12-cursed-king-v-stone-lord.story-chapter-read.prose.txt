​

Alley winced as he saw which of his Cards was being offered up as the ante in his match. You could always approach someone after a loss and offer some sort of trade, or simply pay them. It had happened at least once to Kyera, and was how she'd ended up with her Bell of The Burrows Card. A Card, he thought ruefully, that she'd had time to register with the tournament officials and thus make use of in this upcoming round without penalty. That wasn't the case for Alley’s newly acquired pair of zombies, so they remained in his collection for the upcoming matches. If he wanted that collection to keep featuring Saoirse, he was going to have to win this match.

​

​

“Ninety Cards and it’s one of my uniques,” Alley muttered with a rueful shake of his head. He didn’t know if there was some magic at play or if it was just a run of bad luck. Whatever the reason, Alley could swear he never got to ante one of the Cards in his library that he was trying to replace; it was always something he considered a powerful or useful portion of his Deck.

The Card his opponent was risking didn’t have much use for Alley; he was still, however, confident the old white-bearded man would be more than a little frustrated to lose it. Naturally, that was true of any time anyone lost a Card, but from what he’d seen, this was one of the linchpins of Vern’s strategy.

​

​

Tilting his head, Alley thought for a few seconds about the ante phase. He didn’t really care what Card he won off the old man from The Shattered Peninsula, especially not when compared to how much he did not want to lose Saoirse. He already knew a little about what he was up against, but the more he knew, the better chance he had of winning.

“The Dead Man’s Hand.” The ancient linked item was powerful and versatile. It couldn’t actually be activated during a Challenge match, but one of its abilities could be used during the ante phase before a match began. Rather than surging forward in a powerful strike as it so often did, the enormous skeletal hand that had been crafted from Anaten’s own limb slowly emerged from the teal colored portal that housed it and reached a finger out to gently tap the image of Hall Of The Underlord that hovered above Vern.

​

​

Alley felt a gentle warmth settle over his eyes, and he assumed they were glowing the classic blue that always overtook someone’s eyes when they were looking through a Library or collection. The world around Alley was suddenly replaced by swirling blue energy, and the images of five Cards floated before him. ‘Evolved Avalanchomancer, Terracotta Warriors, Hordebound Mystic, Shard of The Living Mountain, Tectonic Hammer’.

This sort of effect had the added benefit of slowing down time…or speeding up his perception of it? Alley wasn’t actually sure how it worked, but when an effect allowed you to look through a Library or the like, very little time outside of the little reality you perceived passed.

‘I wish I could read the effects of the creature Cards, but Denderra isn’t in here with-’. He paused and considered that maybe she was. The Oblivion Maiden had been his host when he first touched his Deck, and she seemed to exist in a sort of limbo between his Dealer and reality.

“Dendi, can you hear me?” He asked aloud, hoping that he wasn’t embarrassing himself by talking to no one outside of the swirling blue that was the barrier of what he could see.

“Yeah.” Replied the pale ghost who appeared next to him. “You want to know what these monsters do, I take it?”

“If you don’t mind.”

Only three of the five Cards were Creatures, though their effects it seemed were fairly wordy. It took a few minutes, but eventually Alley had a good idea what he was dealing with. Even with the slower time, he was starting to feel a little guilty for making his opponent wait for an effect that didn’t even alter the match itself to finish, so he opted for the Relic.

“Shard of The Living Mountain.” He declared as his eyes ceased glowing and Alley’s perception returned to the real world.

A second after Alley had made his choice, the Hall of The Underlord Card faded away and was replaced with Shard of The Living Mountain, much to the obvious shock of Alley’s opponent.

“Fwar.” Grunted the old man. “ You sneaky litt’l git, you have an item that changes my ante? That's some dirty shit right there.” Alley grinned and raised his hands to chest height with his palms facing upwards in a fake display of helplessness.

“It’s just the ante, you still get to use the Card…even if it is for the last time.”

Vern snorted and shook his head.

“Yeah, laugh it up, after I win that banshee, I’m going to tear it half right in front of ya.”

Alley had to admit he enjoyed the old man’s attitude and had a feeling he was going to enjoy the match even more.

The pair of them turned and walked away from each other as the ethereal blue walls that formed the sides and middle of the Challenge Arena rose from the ground around them. After taking his customary spot near the back of the Arena Alley turned to face his opponent and awaited the images of his opening hand to appear in front of him.

Across the center barrier, Alley saw Vern doing the same. The white-bearded elder’s five floating Cards floated out from a bronze torc of finely twisted metal that sat around his right bicep. The backs of each Card bore the image of a shining diamond.

“Nice custom backs,” Alley called as his own Card images began to appear.

“Thanks, I hate yours,” Vern called back. “ A skull chalice is just creepy.”  Multiple times so far, Alley had been made fun of for having custom Card backs for a Cursed Deck; no one had ever said they just didn’t like them before. It wasn’t worth defending his Cards when he hadn’t even been the one to pick their backs anyway.

Atop Alley’s head, the invisible crown Manifested, but the Vials themselves were visible, forming what appeared to be a floating crown of their own at this point. Vern’s vials, on the other hand, began floating in a series of circles around the arm on which he wore his Dealer.

“Alright, what are we playing with?” Alley muttered to himself and began inspecting his own hand in earnest.
