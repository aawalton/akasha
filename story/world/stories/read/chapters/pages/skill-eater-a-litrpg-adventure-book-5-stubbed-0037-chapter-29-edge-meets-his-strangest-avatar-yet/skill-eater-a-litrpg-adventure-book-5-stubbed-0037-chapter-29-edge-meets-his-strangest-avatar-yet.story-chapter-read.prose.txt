Two days later, Edge found himself hiking across the Lakelands.

He had left World’s End early in the afternoon and was heading toward the wooded sub-biome where he’d battled the nanex—terrain that would offer some privacy as he prepared for the exhibition match scheduled for tomorrow night.

Edge had been watching the clock all day, which had the unfortunate side effect of making the seconds tick by at an agonizingly glacial crawl. He didn’t know exactly when he’d stolen the Epic skill from the stage-four lobster on the monsters’ homeworld, but he would hit the two-week mark sometime within the next few hours. His trip was uneventful, and he arrived at his destination without any surprises, heading deep into the forest while refining his strategy to win the Duos’ Clash.

All such thoughts were driven from his mind when he felt pressure building inside his core, a sure sign that the skill was almost ready to hatch. Adrenaline flooded his veins as an exquisite thrill of anticipation electrified every fiber of his being. Picking up a new Epic power was a major accomplishment, and he was eager to meet the avatar and find out what its magic could do.

As exciting as that was, it was only half the story. Edge was even more amped up about what would happen after the avatar bonded with the empty skill socket Skill-Eater had provided and advanced it to rank two—the final condition he needed to clear before evolving to stage three.

Judging by what had happened after reaching stage two, he would pick up significant gains across the board—upgrades to all his core’s ultimate abilities, possibly a new ultimate too. Not to mention improvements to his reactor, reservoir, skill village, and Disruption diffusor, and of course, nine additional skill slots, two of which the ravenous lord had manifested early.

He sat down on a boulder, savoring the prospect of taking his biggest step yet down his personal path to power. Soon, the rising tension told him the skill was only moments away from hatching. Grinning from ear to ear, he stepped into his inner world, where Rue and his avatars were gathered around the egg-shaped manifestation that represented the digesting skill.

The bright-green shell was covered with latticework of cracks. As he came to a stop in front of it, the surface shattered in a burst of warmth.

Tri-State Sentinel (Epic) has finished digesting.

Would you like to slot in Tri-State Sentinel now?

“Hell yes, I would.”

When the floating screen faded away, Edge bent down to examine the avatar. His eyes went wide when he realized what he was looking at.

He expected his new Epic avatar to look human, or at least humanoid. Chibime had turned into a stylized representation of himself when he stole Roar from the garax, and Sea looked like a miniature version of the Gardener—the spirit of the land that had given him Fear the Seasons after banishing the Ancient possessing her body. That avatar hadn’t changed form, but the skill had joined his collection willingly and hadn’t been subdued, digested, and reborn.

Sentinel’s avatar was human too, so that part wasn’t a surprise. The skill’s manifestation had three aspects, just like Elemental Blade, so Edge wasn’t astonished when three different shapes emerged from the egg. What shocked him to the marrow of his bones were the forms they had taken.

Instead of looking like himself or the monster he’d stolen the skill from, each aspect wore the appearance of one of his closest friends. The first was a statue of Sasha carved from pristine white stone. The second was fashioned from clay and looked just like Mel, and the third was a cloud of swirling ash bearing Lilly’s features and form.

The avatars must be able to tap into my subconscious. This is really fucking weird. I’m glad none of them look like my mother. While the trio of tiny women raced over to the pillar representing his skill slot and began binding to the socket, he pulled up his Guide and read the skill’s description in full.

Tri-state Sentinel <earth, water, wind> (Epic): Rank 1.

This skill has two components. When cast, the user will become out of phase with this dimension, entering a state where only creatures capable of crossing dimensions and incredibly powerful magic can interact with them in any way. The user will be unable to move until the skill is manually deactivated, runs out of mana, the time limit expires, or all three sentinels are destroyed.

Additionally, three construct clones will be created. One is made of solid stone, the second from thick clay, and the third from ash and air. They must remain within 100 feet of the user, or they will cease to exist. The user may control one of the sentinels freely and issue the other simple orders.

No other skills may be used while Tri-State Sentinel is active, although existing skills will not be canceled. Auras and passive powers will not affect the clones, and their attributes will scale with the user’s Amplification. The skill has a time limit of 5 minutes and a cooldown period of 24 hours.

Edge’s jaw dropped as he reached the end of the description. From the brief vision that accompanied it, he knew all three sentinels were earth-aspected. The ash clone was air-aspected as well, and the clay construct was water-aspected. The wind bonus from Fear the Seasons would boost the ash clone, although that would change when winter rolled around and the seasonal enhancement shifted to ice.

His mind was whirling with the implications of what Sentinel could do. It would provide an amazing form of defense, letting him guard a location while impervious to most attacks, and that was only the tip of the iceberg.

The five-minute time limit, hundred-foot range, and twenty-four-hour cooldown were all significant limitations, but those kinds of restrictions meant the magic was usually strong. I should be able to mitigate them with upgrades when I rank the skill up. He would find out as soon as he stole a single skill. Sentinel had been highly ranked when he claimed it and arrived with its cup filled nearly to the brim.

Edge was so eager to try it that his hands were shaking, but he needed to introduce himself to the avatar and welcome the trio of tiny women to the team. No matter how much he wanted to take Sentinel for a test drive, building a bond with his newest skill was vitally important, and you only got one chance to make a first impression.

He walked over to the marble pillar, where the triple avatar was chittering at Chibime and Sea. The Epics seemed to like each other, which meant he wouldn’t have to deal with a personality clash like what had happened when those two met. He walked through the crowd of excited avatars and came to a stop.

Stone Sasha, Ash Lilly, and Clay Mel turned to look at him, and he felt curiosity and rambunctiousness flowing through their newly forged bond.

“Hello there, new friend... err, friends. My name is Edge, and I am delighted to welcome you to the team. The rest of the gang are excited to meet you too, and I hope you enjoy living in my village. On that note, feel free to pick out an empty plot and make yourselves at home. We should come up with something to call you. Sea and Chibime chose their own names, so I figured you might like to as well.”

The women nodded their agreement, and he spent the next few minutes going through the ideas that popped into his head. After they shot down his other suggestions, they picked a name that was a shortened version of their skill’s.

“Got it. Going forward, your name is Tris, which is a lot easier to say than Tri-State Sentinel. We’re going to be seeing a lot of each other going forward. I spend some time in here every day, so we can get to know each other better a little later. I need to run a quick errand, but I’ll be back soon. Why don’t you introduce yourselves to the rest of the team in the meantime? I know they’re eager to make your acquaintance.”

With that, he shook each aspect’s hand in turn, which was tricky since they were only the size of his forearm, chuckling as they hopped off the pillar to interact with the other avatars. When he was certain the ladies felt welcome, he was ready to move on to the main event. He debated whether he wanted to try the skill before or after upgrading it but quickly concluded that using it first would help inform his decision.

Edge stepped back into his body and looked for a good spot to take Tri-State Sentinel on its maiden voyage. He found a nice clearing with plenty of space, made sure nothing nasty was lurking nearby, then stepped into the center of the glade.

Cackling like a maniac as his reactor burst into flames, he gathered his will and activated his Epic skill.

After an intense lurching sensation, he found himself frozen in place—the world washed out like what he’d experienced before Shadow Step became Shadow Play. Whatever being incorporeal entailed, this state was deeper and more profound. In shadow form, he could still be harmed by hostile mana, and when he encountered other ethereal creatures, they seemed solid to each other.

This was something else entirely.

After his field trip to the monsters’ dimension, Edge could sense that he’d taken a half step in that direction, leaving him out of phase with this plane of reality. His instincts told him that barring extraordinary circumstances, nothing could affect him until the spell ended. He wasn’t breathing, and his heart wasn’t beating, which meant his body was in a state of suspended animation, although the magic had cast his consciousness beyond the boundary of his brain.

This strange form of existence was interesting in and of itself. He planned to study it when he had time to spare, as it might yield some valuable insights. However, he only had five minutes to find out what Sentinel could do, and he needed to practice if he wanted to make the most of it during tomorrow's exhibition match.

He could sense the clones standing nearby, each with their own distinct properties. The constructs didn’t seem to have a personality like Sentinel’s avatar or Manifest Chain. They felt more like machines that could obey simple orders. With an effort of will, he cast his consciousness into the stone clone while asking the others to patrol the area. He could sense their location without looking and knew they would signal him if they found anything of note.

The ash and clay sentinels split up and circled the perimeter of their range, transmitting a vague impression of what they encountered along the way. It was nice that they could communicate to some extent, but right now, he was more interested in finding out what the clones could do while under his direct control.

Guiding the stone sentinel felt like moving his own body. It was slow, and his senses were dulled, but his precision wasn’t too bad. The stone clone was heavier than his flesh-and-blood frame, and he could already tell that it was considerably more powerful.

That’s enough standing around. Let’s find out how much stronger I really am.
