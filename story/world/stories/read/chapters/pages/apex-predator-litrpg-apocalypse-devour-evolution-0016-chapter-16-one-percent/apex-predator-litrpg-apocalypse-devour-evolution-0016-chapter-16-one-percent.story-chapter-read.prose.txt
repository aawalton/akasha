Only sheer willpower and Don’s System enhanced body allowed him to move. He scrambled sideways from the bear’s charge. It swiped at Don, delivering a grazing blow to his shoulder. The claws tore flesh, but the wound was shallow.

[Health at 60%][Energy at 19%]

Don ran.

He sprinted through the branches and bushes, jumped over the roots and rocks and just tried to create distance. This was not a fight he could take right now. The bear roared behind him, as Don pumped his legs. His energy was dwindling down, but he leveraged his Sprinting skill.

He kept running and a sense of urgent worry filled him, as the Energy meter dropped down to 15%. But he had to keep running, keep pushing through the pain. With his lungs burning, muscles screaming, he pressed forward. Don didn’t look back—he could hear the grizzly behind him, crashing through underbrush, snarling with bloodlust.

Every step jolted his ribs. Every breath was like dragging fire into his chest. But Don kept moving.

He leapt over a fallen tree and ducked under a low-hanging branch. His hand brushed the bark of a spruce, and he turned sharply, scrambling up its trunk in a single motion. He snapped out his claws partially— just enough to let him climb easily.

The bark splintered under his claws as he pulled himself up. The tree swayed under his weight—but held.

Behind him, the Blackmaw Grizzly crashed into the clearing, nostrils flaring. It spotted him immediately.

“Shit,” Don muttered. He knew bears were good climbers. He was on a timer.

The bear stood on its hind legs and roared. Then it crashed its front paws into the spruce. The whole damn tree shook and Don’s other hand slipped. His feet kicked air as he hung onto a single claw-hold. He inhaled sharply and pulled himself back up into a balanced position. Fortunately his improved physique was reliable even in a state of weakness.

The bear started to climb. Claws dug into bark. Thick limbs heaved upward with terrifying strength. For such a monstrously large bear, it climbed smoothly. Not fast, but with the steady pace of inevitability.

If Don didn’t do something, he was fucked.

He looked around frantically. There were no weapons or tricks to play. He could jump down and hope his physique could handle a five meter fall. But the bear would follow and he would be back to square one.

Then Don got an idea. He could leverage his monkey physique, and do monkey shit.

The bear snarled below and the tree shook as it climbed. It lunged upwards and tried to take a swipe at Don. It missed, but only barely.

Don pushed hesitation from his mind. He ran down a branch and jumped. His arms and legs waved in the air as he crossed four meters. He crashed into the next tree beside the spruce. He slid down but he sank his claws into the bark, slowing his descent enough to grab a branch.

The bear looked at Don like he had just cast long lost magic. Don almost wanted to laugh at the flabbergasted bear’s blinking face. But he was too exhausted and in too much pain to do that. He glanced at his Energy.

9%

The jump was a maximum effort maneuver. And his body was already beat up. It had taken a chunk out of his reserves.

And the bear was clearly a seasoned hunter. It quickly snapped out of its confusion. It slid down the trunk and immediately went to the tree Don was in.

“You’ve got to be kidding me…”

The bear rammed into the new tree. Bark splintered. Don clung to a branch with one arm and winced. He wasn’t in shape for another chase. He climbed up, one branch at a time, dragging his aching body through sheer grit. Every muscle screamed. His shoulder was leaking again. His leg felt like dead weight.

Still, he climbed.

He needed to get higher. Needed options.

As he scaled past the middle canopy, the light shifted—more sky, fewer leaves. Don paused, crouching in the crook of a branch, panting. Below, he heard the steady thud of the bear pacing the base of the tree. Then silence.

Don peered down through the branches.

The Blackmaw Grizzly was there, sniffing the trunk. Its head swayed slowly side to side, like it was deciding how best to climb.

Don didn’t wait.

He moved again, not climbing—leaping. From tree to tree, using every ounce of improved muscles from his Simian Physique. His body moved on instinct now. There was no grace, just momentum and survival. Branch, jump, branch. Grab, breathe, jump again.

But then the System flared up a warning.

[Energy critically low. Please rest to avoid incapacitation.]

Don could certainly feel it. His lungs burned with every breath, yet he felt like he wasn’t getting enough air in. The last landing left him sprawling on a thick limb. He crouched there, pressed flat against the trunk, trying not to make a sound.

He didn’t move.

Didn’t breathe too deep.

The bear was confused now. He heard it return to the first tree, then backtrack. It was sniffing the air. Trying to track him.

Don flattened himself tighter to the bark, trying to steady his heart and still his breathing. His own blood thudded in his ears in rhythm with his hammering heartbeat. He just had to trust his Stealth skill.

The grizzly had returned. It stood near the base of the very tree Don was now in. Head tilted. Sniffing again.

It knew. It sensed something. But it didn’t know where. It seemed like Don’s Stealth was confusing the monster’s sense of smell. But it wasn’t mindless. It seemed to know Don should be in the tree, but it wasn’t sure.

Don stayed still. Muscles trembling. Breaths shallow.

Seconds passed like minutes.

The bear leaned against the tree and let out a questioning groan.

Then Don heard something whistling through the air.

Tsok.

An arrow struck the tree right next to the bear’s paw. It reared back and roared. Another arrow struck it in its back. With a rage fueled charge it sprinted off into the forest. Don let out a breath he didn’t realize he had been holding. He looked at his Energy.

1%

That one percent was what was keeping his body from a total shutdown. He knew instinctively through the knowledge the System imparted, that he would pass out when the bar hit zero. And passing out in a tree was a bad idea. Hell, passing out in a forest full of monstrous beasts was also not a great notion.

Don made himself as comfortable against the tree branch as possible and just exhaled. He had never been so sore, tired and just overall fucked up as he was now. Despite it all a goofy grin spread on his face. He was alive. He had somehow scrambled out of the jaws of defeat and death and lived to fight another day.

And damn it, if that wasn’t the best thrill he knew in this crazy new reality.

He wanted more of it. But his body was spent. Don just breathed slowly and tried to relax. He felt surprisingly at ease ten meters in the air, dangling off a branch. Maybe it was his levels, his race or his Simian Physique, or his high Agility, but something was making it very natural for him to balance on the thin branch without much thinking about it.

Don felt elation when his Energy ticked up to 2%.

That had taken maybe two minutes. He’d wait a couple more and then try to drink another health potion to ease his pain and wounds.

“Hold on…” he muttered. “Maybe I can…”

Don fished a crumpled plastic wrapped bar from one of his pouches. The rations. Maybe they would give him Energy.

He peeled back the wrapper and a thick brown bar that smelled faintly nutty emerged. Don shrugged and started chewing. The taste was bland, and the bar was hard and chewy. But hey, it was food. Don realized he hadn’t eaten ever since coming to the tutorial. But it didn’t seem to be a problem with his Devour ability. Apparently his metabolism was now under System magic bullshit. Eh, worked for him, made life easier.

However, Don did learn that the rations definitely were useful, and he was glad he hadn’t thrown or traded them away. Just one bar had recovered him back up to 22%

Excited by this, Don picked another bar. He peeled open the wrapper and took a bite. Just a little one. Then he watched his Energy.

27%

With a grin he shoved the bar in his mouth and was suddenly feeling a lot better. He was still beat up and hurt, but at least he wasn’t lethargic.

It’s time to get moving again. My energy is at 42. That’s enough to kill something and Devour it.

But as much as Don enjoyed fighting and wanted to take on another Blackmaw Grizzly, he wasn’t sure his body was up for it. It would be ideal to find something between the gibbons and the grizzlies. Maybe one of those boars?

Don jumped down from the tree. His legs held and when Don rolled into a jog, it felt good again. It was time to continue the hunt.
