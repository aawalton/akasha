"It's... you." Harry said after a long pause. "Again."

"Indeed." The Masked Man replied, staring at Harry.

Shit. I didn't remember him being so off-putting... The Locksmith looked away as he gave a sheepish smile. He swept his gaze over the frozen figures, each and everyone with their emotions running high. Is he the one behind this?

"Is any of this real, or we're just inside my mind?" Harry asked, reluctantly turning to meet the entity's stare.

"Is anything that happens in the Tower truly real, Locksmith?" He jerked his head to the left as he seemed to float closer. "We're all just old ghosts."

"Don't be semantic. You know what I meant." Harry said with a harsh tone.

"Yes." He replied. "I understood the first time."

A few seconds passed as Harry looked around then stared at the man again. "Okay, yeah. So which is it—"

"It is all real." The figure said, maintaining eye contact through his mask. "I figured it would be nice for the two of us to chat. Don't fear for them; they can't experience this. Besides, I think I just took you out of a... pickle. That's the expression, correct?"

It'll be a hard talk, that's for sure... Harry added inwardly. "Y-Yeah, it is... How exactly are you doing this? I can't even use my Emblem while things are in this state."

"Yes, stasis. During this period, your Emblem won't work, nor will you be able to inflict damage on anyone. You can relax, for this is a safe space. Remember the start of every loop, where you couldn't summon any of your doors? Since the Tower takes a few seconds to tune in with each of its floors, this phenomenon happens. This area effect uses the same principles."

Realizing the man was spewing what seemed to be important information, Harry straightened his back and listened closely to the mysterious figure. Yes, it could be dangerous, and he could be lying. But what else was he suppose to do? Like a chef without its knife, Harry felt naked without his Emblem. The safety and flexibility his passages produced was well worth the hassle.

Harry, now getting near Madeline while eyeing the man, who seemed to be following him through the air, used her shoulders to rest his arm.

"And you can control this phenomenon, I suppose?" He asked, careful not to cross any lines.

"Negative." The man replied as he defied gravity, his tone solemn. "I merely redirect the priorities of the Tower towards other areas. Fiddling with these aspects is burdensome but imperative to my escape."

"Escape?" Harry regretted asking this as soon as he said it. A curious and compulsory question that may have gone too far. Luckily, he didn't seem to bothered.

"I remind telling you about it on the first floor. Did I not?" He jerked his head to the right once again. "Truth be told, as long as I enjoy watching your petty fights and the laughable lies you use to justify your own sins, I feel tired."

Are you really tired, or are you lonely? Harry held himself from asking, not risking further annoying the figure who could literally stop time. He then remembered what the Masked Man had said to him at the start of the climb, way back on the first floor: The oldest of the prisoners. Was he one of the people who could've reached the last floor? His knowledge of the Tower's structure told Harry he definitely knew more. Was this knowledge attained along the climb, or... Is he the Astronomer? The oldest of the prisoners?

"What should I call you?" Harry asked. "I feel uncomfortable not knowing your name while you seem to know a lot about me."

"Ra...ven. Raven." The figure said after a while. "Yes, it is better this way. Now let us proceed."

"Yeah." Harry replied, frowning, as he moved closer to the edge and placed one foot on the tip. "What do you want?"

"I want nothing but to help you reach the last floor, Harry—"

"Are you serious?" Harry replied, not regretting cutting Raven off. "I know that you contacted the knuckleheads on this 4th floor just to rob me. Prism stole my journal and gave it to you. Now tell me: why should I even listen to you?"

"You're in no place to negotiate, Locksmith." Raven replied sternly, slowly floating towards Harry. "If I hadn't brought you here, you'd be dead, or at the very least, unable to defend your so called friends. As I've told you already, I do not wish your harm. If it serves you anything, your journal is safe with me."

"Why did you steal it!?" Harry asked once again. "God! T-There couldn't have been anything remotely useful for... whatever you are. It's not even that important, it only has a couple doodles and—"

As if he was just shot, Harry's eyes widened as he closely remembered the content's of the journal. Even though most notes were nothing special, except for emotional value, one of them could be proven important. Back when Harry participated in the creation of his first Emblem, he used the Lock Symbol. Breathe. It brought him unpleasant memories, seeing as the procedure, somehow, backfired and resulted in Devon's death. He never understood how could such a simple and specific Emblem could've caused such an explosion, but the content's of the procedure never left his mind.

The kid and the Astronomer; the telescope; the timid moon who dreaded attention. They were all there. A fragment of the truth. For months, every dream was somehow related to that event. If only I could've seen what was drawn on that blackboard. Or maybe what was written on those documents... As always, he blamed himself for not having enough tenacity; not being strong enough to at least honor Devon's death.

Either way, after that faithful day, he used an old notebook he stole from the library to pore over the meaning of the Symbol in question. His search for important documents — usually hidden in the mansions of nobles — was what eventually led him to the Logos Tower. Certain that the documents had only ornamental value for the nobles, the Locksmith began his career. At worst, he might find a clue about the Symbol. When he realized that none of that mattered, the Logos Tower proved to be the last, but most dangerous, option.

The diary, with dozens of depictions of the Lock Symbol, were in the satchel.

"Why do you want it?" Harry tried to sound intimidating, but the shakiness in his voice was apparent. "Is this about—"

"The God Emblem." Raven interrupted as he was now standing mid air. "You are correct in your assumption."

Harry froze as he contemplated the man's words. God Emblem!? Dracno also reacted when I brought up the subject... so it is true. Although I had no proof that he told the truth, something was deeply wrong with the tyrant, who claimed to have climbed the tower once.

"T-That's what Dracno said..." Harry muttered to himself, but Raven heard it nonetheless.

For a moment, the figure's gaze seemed to divert from Harry. Through the eyeholes of his mask, which shared an impenetrable darkness, Raven seemed to recall something at the mention of Dracno.

"Ah, yes..." he finally replied, almost cheerfully. "Dracno, the lunatic who got even more lost by observing... Funny how even through the armor he could see the beauty." He paused for a moment. "Anyway, I remember him. He was the first and only one to reach the top floor. Often, danger lies in curiosity, you know? I remember helping the man get back to the second floor, where he could rest. Although there is no rest for a man as disturbed as that one."

"So he saw the God Emblem?" Harry asked, trying to milk as many answers out of Raven as possible. "That's why he went mad? And you helped him get down? If you can do that, then you surely can help us reach the last floor, correct?"

"For as much as I'd like for your trip to be made shorter," He replied. "I can't interfere in it. It's too dangerous even for me to be here. There's a path that must be taken, and events that must happen."

"Events like hiring a thug to steal my journal?" The Locksmith asked with a grin as he clenched his fists.

"You're upset, I can see it. But yes, events such as that one. I needed to be sure, you see."

"Then why didn't you steal it yourself!?" Harry asked, filled with anger. "Because you're too noble for such an action? Can't bother to get your hands dirty?"

"What a weird question. My hands are clean, Locksmith." Raven replied, not understanding the expression. "I can't stand too long next to you, otherwise things may get... muddy. I do like the loneliness, but not the incarceration. I was made to stare at the stars, and that's what I wish to do. Negotiating with Prism and Alexander was proven difficult but fruitful. At the end of this predicament, we'll both be free. Isn't that what you want?"

My freedom and yours can be two completely different things, though... Harry added inwardly as he looked around at the frozen faces.

"But we're chatting now, aren't we? Its been a couple minutes since we started." Harry said. "What makes this different from the other time? Is it because of the frozen time?"

"...Yes." The figure replied after a few moments, then added. "And in actuality, it is time for you to get back."

"W-Wait!" Harry raised his hands. "Um, will I get back right before the explosion? Can't you give me a few seconds of advantage? Otherwise I'll — you know — be brutally ripped apart by the force of the explosion."

"Oh..." The mysterious figure sincerely chuckled for the first time. "This state we're in now, is just temporary. It was only made so you and I could chat a bit. Time is not frozen in its entirety; causality and entropy continue to dictate the flow of our destiny. From their perspective, you simply disappeared."

"Are you fucking serious?" Harry frowned, getting ready to absorb what might have happened in these last few minutes. "What is happening right now? Is anyone dead? Please don't tell me someone died while I was out chatting with the Tower's doorman. Besides, didn't you just lie to me?"

"Like you humans say." The figure replied. "I've never lied, only spiced the truth. It is an interesting concept, though; telling untruths for own gain."

"That's what you're going to do with Alexander?"

Raven kept quiet, but Harry could sense a smile behind the mask. "Just so you know: the cost of this tiny trick that I made in order to save your life will be discounted from the loop's duration. Prepare—"

Harry straightened his tie once again as he looked around. As the words finally sunk in, he widened his eyes and stared at the man.

"Wait! Why? What do you mean!—"

And Raven disappeared.

Things couldn't have gone that bad after only a few minutes, right...?
