The team used the final hours at their disposal as wisely as they could—gathering information, purchasing gear, and advancing as rapidly as possible while remaining near the free city.

Everyone but Edge spent some of Mortium they had earned from repelling the monsters’ invasion to upgrade their implants to tier two… then used the gobium they had rerecovered from the dolomite geode to take the devices straight to tier three, dramatically increasing their potency and the size of their aether wells.

He had asked Eddy to prepare the upgrade kits ahead of time. The man thought Edge was an elite up-and-coming harvester after their prior interactions, and he had cleared the surgeon’s schedule to prioritize the crew’s needs.

Sasha, Melenia, and Jumo upgraded the various devices that shielded them from a worst-case scenario. Going from tier one to tier three in a single afternoon was a big boost to their defenses, and they were looking forward to purchasing new implants when they evolved and had more points to work with.

Sakura installed an Organ-Guard to protect her vitals and a second device that sealed shorn vessels to prevent blood loss. She also took her compounding pharmacy from tier two to three, expanding the range and increasing the quantity of the substances it produced.

Trapper upgraded her implant that neutralized poisons and installed a new device that countered hostile mana, supplementing the implant that shielded her from physical attacks. They wouldn’t hold out against a sustained barrage, but the devices would help her survive an occasional heavy hit that slipped past her guard.

While life as a hunter would never be safe, the crew could breathe a little easier with the extra insurance in place, especially after what had happened in the Haunted Vale.

Two months after finding the invitation posted inside the kaiju’s core, the day of their fateful meeting arrived.

After checking in with Lanna and Astil and letting the women know they should be back sometime tomorrow, the crew rented a floating wagon and departed the free city. Trapper had accepted a simple resource-collection job as cover, and Edge and Sakura were hiding in the conveyance, obscured by every concealment power at the team’s disposal.

They would arrive at the coordinates they’d been given early that evening, and the discussion he had been dreading for eight long weeks would ensue at last. He hoped he didn’t run into Corpse inside the Coalition’s base. Even if he resisted the urge to throttle the woman on the spot, he doubted he could reign in his killing intent after encountering the scion responsible for Momo’s death.

Eight hours later, the hunters passed beneath a massive stone arch. The scions left the wagon and hid in the shadows while the rest of the crew continued toward a sub-biome another twenty miles ahead. The deception served two purposes. It would prevent the Coalition from associating Edge and Sakura with the other members of the team while helping the duo arrive at the meeting without being observed enroute.

There was a chance that their precautions wouldn’t do any good—that the faction already knew everything about them and their recent activities—but it was better than nothing. The crew was doing everything in their power to limit the information at the Coalition’s disposal, minimizing potential leverage points during these deliberations.

Edge wished he had been able to evolve before meeting King, but his requirements were stringent, and he was lucky that he was on track to clear them at all. At least Sakura and Trapper were able to pull it off, and the rest of the crew is well past the halfway mark after the marathon melee to defend the portal.

Skill-Eater was asleep, but he planned rouse her once they entered the base so the ravenous lord could observe through his eyes. While their primary goal was to buy enough time for him to hit stage three, this was an invaluable opportunity to study the faction that had put them in this precarious position—their only chance to evaluate King and the Coalition’s forces before they were forced to commit to a course of action that would irrevocably change the fate of everyone living in Puppet Town.

“How long will it take to reach the coordinates?” he asked Sakura when the crew was out of sight.

“Another hour or so should do the trick. What do you think will happen when we arrive?”

“Based on the rumors I’ve heard, the Coalition has a secret base where they’ve been hiding from the Wardens. I expect they will take us there, make a show of force to intimidate us, then introduce us to King and pressure us into sealing the deal.”

They waited a while longer and then set out together, reviewing their strategy along the way. Sakura was pretending to be Edge’s liaison to Puppet Town. She would leave the talking up to him while using her magic to analyze everything in sight.

He would stick to the unvarnished truth to defeat any magic that could sense lies, which was a common skill used in all manners of diplomatic interactions. He expected King to employ a combination of bluster, bribery, and flattery, and would act like he was on the verge of succumbing to the pressure, make a few demands that would be easy for the faction to meet, then ask for some time to consider the proposal before signing on the dotted line.

The only critical pieces of information he needed to keep to himself at all costs were Sakura’s status as a scion and the existence of Puppet Town’s core manufactory. He had no idea if he could talk his way out of this alliance without putting a target on his back and endangering the people he loved, but he would worry about the ultimate resolution of this situation after making it through today’s meeting.

As they mulled the matter over, the scions arrived at the borderlands of a particularly treacherous sub-biome known as the Toxic Marsh. The wetlands were filled with poison gas, deadly quicksand, and a host of lethal predators, but their instructions included a route that would see them safely through the region’s myriad threats. They put on the magitech gasmasks they had purchased the day before, which had enough aether to protect their lungs for a full twelve hours.

Edge switched on All-Seeing Gaze and Warlord’s Mantle, at which point, all that was left was to proceed with the plan and hope for the best.

While a constant thrum of danger filled the poisoned air, it seemed the intel was good. Nothing attacked them as they made their way along fingers of dry land running between the fetid marshlands. While there was plenty of hazardous terrain to be found, the ground beneath their feet remained blessedly solid.

They came to a stop as they drew near the coordinates, and Sakura scouted with Remote Viewing. She returned to her body a few minutes later. “There’s a man waiting for us a half-mile ahead. He has sharp energetic senses. He noticed my projection and waved.”

“Stay behind me and be ready to run at the first sign of trouble. If this goes south, retreat the way we came while I buy you time to get away.”

His pulse pounded and adrenaline electrified his veins, but nothing untoward happened as the duo completed the final leg of their journey. Like Sakura said, there was a man sitting on a boulder beside the path, lounging like he didn’t have a care in the world.

Edge recognized the warrior. “I’ve seen him before. I ran into him when Mel and I were exploring the silver ring. He was arguing with a girl, who must have been Corpse, given what we’ve learned about the Coalition.”

He suppressed the surge of anger that realization evoked. There would be time to indulge his rage later, but this situation called for calculation and control. As they approached, Edge took the man’s features and tasted the magic wafting from his core.

At his best guess, the warrior was peak stage-three. The dense mana smoldering in his reactor belonged to an Epic core or something even rarer. The man had flawless brown skin and amber eyes that seemed to take in everything at once.

He was wearing a hunters’ kit over his chiseled physique. Instead of killing tools designed to battle men or beasts, the warrior had a kite shield strapped to one arm and another secured to his back. Edge had learned the hard way not to inspect them with Dragon’s Greed, as he had almost blinded himself the last time he examined the extraordinary gear with his treasure-sensing sight.

Everything he had seen so far indicated the man was powerful, but what really drove it home was how relaxed he was. He was surrounded by deadly predators and lethal environmental hazards, but the warrior was so unperturbed that he could have been soaking in a spa, making no move to defend himself as the duo crossed the final few feet.

“Be on guard.” Skill-Eater opened one eye. “That man is a scion. I can’t tell which lord he bears unless he draws upon the power we provide. He doesn’t seem to have sensed my presence, or that of Lore-Weaver.”

Thanks for the heads up. He flashed a subtle hand sign to warn Sakura. The duo came to a stop in front of the boulder and waited for the man to rise to his feet.

“We’re here,” Edge said. “I assume this isn’t our final destination given the circumstances.”

“Indeed,” the warrior replied. “My name is Brennen, although everyone calls me Shield. I’ll be taking you the rest of the way. I’m going to use a device that will prevent you from learning our base’s location, but you are under my protection, and I swear that I will die before I let you come to any harm.

“I know it won’t mean much, but before we head out, I wanted to apologize for the series of events that led up to this moment. I didn’t have any say in what happened, and neither did King for that matter, but I don’t approve of needless bloodshed or violence as a means of coercion.”

After speaking his piece, Shield activated a device attached to his belt. From Edge’s perspective, a dome of impenetrable blackness extended ten feet from the man’s position. The sunlight shone straight through it, so he could still see the ground, Sakura, and his own body, which made it far better than wearing an old-fashioned blindfold.

Brennen didn’t seem to expect a reply. He motioned for the duo to follow and set out at a brisk pace that was effortless for a cored individual to maintain. They walked behind Shield for hours, crossing a half-dozen sub-biomes before entering a network of stone tunnels. The cloaking device was powerful. Edge couldn’t see through it with Gaze, and judging by the frustration on Sakura’s face, her magic couldn’t penetrate it either.

He wasn’t about to trust anyone from the Coalition, but he didn’t detect any hostility from Shield as the scion led them deeper underground. Every sense Edge had told him the warrior was dangerous, but he was laser focused on his duty as bodyguard and guide, and Edge honestly believed Shield would protect them with his life while the duo was in his care.

“We’re almost there,” Brennen said several hours later. “Don’t do anything stupid, and I promise you will walk out of here in one piece, no matter how the conversation goes.”

It was cold comfort, but far better than nothing. Shield seemed level-headed and reasonable, which was a pleasant surprise after Corpse’s invitation. It made Edge hopeful that their business would be concluded without bloodshed.

When they stepped out of the tunnel, Shield deactivated the device, revealing a massive caldera bordered by natural stone. The area was open to the sky, although it was covered by a potent magical barrier. A fortress loomed a thousand feet ahead, ringed by a cluster of auxiliary structures. Top-grade turrets were embedded along the rocky perimeter. Hundreds of people were going about one errand or another, along with dozens of high-stage beasts that looked incredibly tough.

Most surprising of all, Edge caught sight of ten different vehicles parked in the corner, including a pair of two-man airplanes with menacing cannons protruding from the undercarriage. Magitech transports wouldn’t work while the planet was on lockdown, but it was a show of wealth and martial might unlike anything he’d seen since arriving on Ord—clear evidence that he was gazing upon a stronghold owned by one of the planet’s most powerful factions.

“King is waiting for you inside.” Shield gestured toward the fortress. “I’ll walk you through the front door, stand guard until you return, and escort you to the Toxic Marsh when your business is concluded.”

Side by side, Edge and Sakura followed the scion into the sprawling structure, knowing that whatever happened next, this meeting would have grave repercussions for their collective future.
