White light faded from Archie’s vision just as the slight jolt of him suddenly being teleported halfway across the Multiverse sent a tingle up his spine that somewhat disoriented him for a couple of seconds.

However, the second his feet hit the polished blackwood flooring of the Tavern, and unlike Archie, who was somewhat disoriented, Volos and Arsenic sprang from his arms like coiled springs let loose.

“Wait–!” he shouted, but it was too late.

Both spirit centipedes hit the ground with barely audible thuds and instantly scattered in opposite directions, becoming twin streaks of motion that zigzagged across the lounge with reckless glee, uncaring of anything and anyone in their way.

They darted between tables, dove in between the legs of a server, which caused said server to raise an eyebrow not only at their behavior, but also at their levels. Seeing E-Grades suddenly enter the tavern was something that was incredibly rare, especially seeing four of them in the span of a month.

“Well, at least no one’s threatened to skin them yet,” Archie muttered to himself as he adjusted his coat.

Unlike the guild members he’d seen in the tavern on Fractal, who, while unbothered by his relatively lower level, still regarded him with interest veiled in caution, whether due to him being a fellow guild member in E-Grade or for reasons unknown to him, these people seemed far more relaxed. Within seconds of his arrival, he had only felt a total of twelve Identifies cast on him out of the fifty or so people in the tavern.

In contrast, on Fractal, nearly everyone had Identified him the moment he stepped through the tavern door.

Another thing Archie noticed was that instead of wearing armor and having their weapons at their side, ready at a moment’s notice, these people wore open coats, wide sashes, and soft fabrics that hinted at them being people on vacation rather than adventurers planning out their next quest or expedition.

“I got twenty on the green one!” one man yelped as Volos zipped between his boots, Arsenic hot on his tail. The chase escalated as the two spiraled up a nearby chair, vaulted from the backrest to a table, launched off a half-finished sandwich, and landed in a perfectly synchronized roll on the next tabletop over.

“I got thirty on the other!” Another shouted, which caused a chain reaction of everyone in the tavern to join in, betting on which one would catch the other and win the tussle.

However, just as Volos and Arsenic leapt into the air once more, a shimmering, hand caught the both of them mid-air, catching them like flies in amber and stopping them from landing into a mug full of beer, judging by the trajectory of their jump.

They twitched, wriggled, and hissed in minor indignation at how they were forced to cease their race, and suspended a few feet above the ground. Their disgruntled hisses were soon joined by the groans of guild members who had been shouting and betting on the outcome – frustrated now that their unexpected source of entertainment had come to an abrupt end.

“Excuse me,” came a clear, calm voice from across the room, cutting through the Tavern’s ambiance like a practiced blade. “Could you kindly control your spirit beasts before they dismantle our lobby?”

Archie looked up.

At the far end of the room stood a woman in a medieval tavern outfit, positioned behind a desk. From her posture, no-nonsense expression, and the faint trace of danger radiating off her, Deacon presumed she was the secretary.

Archie raised a hand sheepishly and rubbed the back of his head, slowly approaching the telekinetically caught forms of Volos and Arsenic. “Right. Sorry. They’re only a couple of weeks old, and whenever they enter a new place, they become very excitable.”

The secretary gave a curt nod and made a dismissive flick of her wrist. The magic field lowered gently, depositing the two spirit beasts onto the floor before dissipating entirely.

“I swear,” Archie muttered under his breath to them both, reaching down to scoop them back up, “If you guys keep on acting like you’re on permanent sugar highs, you’ll leave me with no choice other than to purchase a couple of leashes and I don’t think you want me to do that do you?”

As he turned toward the secretary, she gestured toward the elevator shaft behind her. “If you’re here for the Taverns' client services or if you have an appointment, please proceed to the second floor. If you’re waiting for someone, there’s seating available here on the first floor. Refreshments are complimentary until the end of this month in celebration of a successful God-Tier expedition into the Unknown Depths.”

A what and in… where? Archie blinked. “Uh... right. Thank you.”

He glanced around again and took in that the people here really did seem more at ease compared to the ones in Fractal. Not just physically relaxed, but... emotionally as well. The bits and pieces of conversation Archie picked up as he walked toward the secretary were filled with jokes, quick-witted quips, stories, laughing, and even the occasional singing.

The vibe of this tavern was completely different compared to the one on Fractal.

Volos and Arsenic both shifted inside his arms, poking their heads out from in between his fingers, and trying their best to pout at him, and trying to get him to let them free.

“Yeah,” Archie muttered, half to himself. “If everyone on this planet is like this, I can see why Henry decided that coming here would be nice.”

But I do wonder, what had happened on the planet he was previously on, the random one the System teleported him to, that caused him to want to come to this one? Was it that bad that he had no choice but to leave it?

From behind the front desk, the secretary raised a brow as her book flipped pages on its own. She watched him a moment longer as he continued to approach her desk, which caused her to close her books and slide them under the desk top and onto one of the shelves that was near various other buttons and switches.

The centipedes resumed talking with one another via sign language, using nothing but their antennae, and from what Archie could understand, they were talking shit about him.

Archie sighed. “Okay, I’m definitely buying the two of you leashes and preventing you both from leaving my side until you both become adults,” he sighed, much to Volos and Arsenic’s nonchalant attitude.

They had never heard the word leash before, nor was it mentioned in any way within the USL language crystal.

As Archie neared the secretary’s desk, he slipped a hand into his Spatial Storage and pulled out the Compass of Desire, something that he’d noticed right away then he’d first accessed his Spatial Storages, which he assumed Bralmir had done when putting his mana bike back together after he’d taken out his Power Core to power up the teleporter.

The moment the brass lid clicked open, the compass flared to life, as it latched onto his desire to locate his brother. The compass's needle trembled for a moment before spinning a full circle, before it then abruptly jerked to a halt, glowing faintly crimson as it pointed true: northeast.

Archie’s lips curled into a smirk. Finally.

That subtle hum from the artifact was unmistakable; his brother was close, or at least relatively nearby.

His brother.

His newly born niece.

The only pieces of family he had left were scattered across the Multiverse after the destruction of their Universe, and they were now on the same planet as him.

The thought of Henry and his niece being the only members of his family that he would be able to see and that it would remain that way for two thousand years, felt like a shot in the gut.

There were still two thousand years until he’d be able to see his Mother and Father again.

Reaching the secretary’s desk, he offered a polite nod. She looked up, her expression was curt and just a hint more amiable than before, though that could have just been a trick of the light.

“Welcome to Omniversal Solution’s Veyrun branch,” she said smoothly, voice polished by countless repetitions. “How may I be of assistance, Iron Trekker Archie Gracefield?”

Archie offered a small smile in return. “Sorry again for the trouble these two caused… I just wanted to ask a couple of things before the three of us get out of your hair. Could you tell me how to leave the city? I need to head northeast, so if there’s an eastern or northern gate nearby, I’d appreciate directions.”

The secretary gave a measured nod. “You’re in the city of Veyrum,” she said, pulling a thin silver tablet from under the desk. It lit up with a quick gesture and produced a holographic map of the city that was labeled as Veyrum above it. “The Eastern Gate is your best option. It leads directly to the Ascendant Bluffs, and from there, the trade routes head north through the Cloudspire Hills.”

“However, right now, I suggest you head through the Eastern Gate as the Northern Gate is currently crowded as the city of Veyrum is currently having its annual Verdir Festival – a festival the city celebrates in order to honor their revolution of their previous democracy that brought their city to ruin.”

Huh? Archie thought before shaking his head and pushing aside the thoughts that were budding in his head. No, focus on trying to get to Henry and your niece instead of trying to dig more into politics that don’t involve you.

“Perfect,” Archie said, flicking a single silver coin onto the desk. It spun in a lazy circle before settling with a soft clink. “Thank you for your help, and sorry again for how these two knuckleheads were acting.”

She raised an eyebrow slightly but accepted it with a nod of thanks. “Safe travels, Iron Trekker Archie Gracefield.”

Turning her gaze towards the two centipedes who were “sneakily” climbing up Archie’s chestpiece and back to Archie’s eyes, she said, “I would recommend you stop feeding them sugar or anything containing sugar, in order to prevent more situations such as this from happening again.”

Volos and Arsenic, now comfortably coiled around Archie’s shoulders like scaled scarves, wiggled their antennae mockingly at him, believing that they had escaped from his grasp unnoticed.

Archie sighed in response. “… I’ve never once fed them sugar. They were just born with this much energy.”

He let out another sigh as the human secretary watched him with visible concern, then turned and headed for the tavern door. Pushing it open, he stepped out into the cool, open air of the city.

The streets of Veyrum were a curious blend of Victorian Gothic architecture and mixed with the same type of magical transportation that the City of Neo-Eden had strewn about.

The streets of Veyrum stretched out before him like an old school Gaslamp Fantasy novel brought to life, imposing blackstone buildings with gilded trim, wrought-iron balconies dripping with flowering vines, and magical streetlamps that glowed softly as the sun began to set. Cobblestone walkways bridged across between buildings a few stories high, forming a slender web of bridges and suspended plazas above.

Mana-powered vehicles of all shapes and sizes hummed as they glided a couple of feet above the streets around him. Street performers juggled and performed various tricks, some with mana and some without, while children chased enchanted soap bubbles that refused to pop unless caught with two hands.

And despite it all, even with the hum of ambient mana, laughter, and distant music from the ongoing Verdir Festival to the north, the compass in Archie’s hand tugged his attention relentlessly northeast.

The red-tinted needle quivered as if eager to be followed.

Archie paused at the edge of a large roundabout plaza flanked by arched transit gates and directional signs glowing in the five dominant system languages. The Eastern Gate was clearly marked – an elegant arch inscribed with words in USL: “To the Bluffs Beyond, Glory Walks Beside You.”
