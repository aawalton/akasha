“Citizens from Toronto to Windsor to Buffalo and beyond are reeling after the massive explosion at Niagara Falls and the following battle,” the radio jockey said, sounding excited. “Rumors have spread, but there is no confirmation on the identities of who has the Tax Guild Stone, only that it has been seen, found, and taken. No regional System notification has been sent of the new holder. All citizens are advised to STAY CLEAR of the structure where the battle ensued. Dozens of people have fallen into the portals or gone catatonic from touching the…energy fence.”

A pause, and then his voice came back, clearly a bit further from the mic and much grumpier.

“Fence? What the hell do you mean an energy fence?”

Another pause, and the voice came back, excitable once again.

“The early death toll for this Hunt, while unconfirmed, is being reported as high as 50,000 people across the entire region of Toronto,”

The radio jockey sighed, suddenly sounding exhausted.

“Now please, I beg you people and monsters listening in. Stop the madness. It’s been grabbed, soon to be claimed, so there’s no reason to—”

His voice cut out, replaced by that week’s favored tune.

Regina Renfrew, who was just a normal citizen, flicked her radio off. It was the only tech she could afford.

She rushed over to her only window, got on her tippy toes, and peered around her closed blinds with shaking hands. The afternoon light panged against her migraine and squinted her eyes, but she made herself scan the street.

Her baby howled from his crib.

“Shhh,” She shushed without looking back. “I think its alright now. No more booms. It’s all alright. It’s done.”

Outside, cars lay gutted and marred. Body parts and their owners lay spilled. Smoldering Skill energy sparked and crackled in the air. But the people had finally stopped killing. Her small basement was safe. Her baby was safe.

After the giant bang, and not ten minutes later, the crashing noise of fighting and death stopped. A gentle wind was already washing it all away.

She spun, feeling the weight of the world lift off her shoulders, and padded over to her child.

His screams calmed as she approached, and scooping him up, Regina hugged tight and cried from relief. Immediately she began planning what she could scrounge up for them to eat and hummed just the way he liked.

A voice boomed from the street, igniting her fears momentarily. Thankfully it was a voice she’d grown familiar with.

“Hoooo-ey!” The affable voice whistled. “What a mess! But Dorge say we can do, friends. This gooood pickings. Good pickings for all. Get to working, Garbage Garmen!”

Her baby giggled in her shoulder.

And Dorge got to work.

Mr. Mystical laughed so hard the mocha pot he possessed clanked violently against itself. It was hilarious, really. No one who’d reached their level and influence could possibly be so stupid.

“Ah! You two got me good,” a miasma tear dripped from the spout, and he reached from the bottom with his non-physical hand to wipe it away. He wheezed, letting the joke sputter out.

“Really, I shouldn’t be so gullible. Get the Tax Guild Stone for us? A three-part gold grade Lich becoming the holder? Ridiculous! The System itself would smash us to bits for offsetting the balance it maintains! Why, we’d be attacked on all fronts! People, monsters, the System itself would bend back and spank us! It would be chaos! No one can hold that much power! Should wield that much power! Hehehehe!”

His laughter died out as he set himself atop the fridge.

Sauce bubbled lazily on the stove, Francessca hid upstairs for ‘protection,’, and the front of house was empty. There was little interest in eating during the Hunt.

The bloody part of the Hunt was over. With the boom passing not ten minutes ago, surely the phone would be ringing off the hook shortly. They’d already agreed to cancel deliveries for the rest of the day. No point in overworking Francesca while Alex was on an important task.

Nino and Nina grinned back at Mr. Mystical from the kitchen of the original Nino’s pizzeria location. The sweet-looking grandmother was practically draped over her husband, with her eyes full of white frost. Nino stood casually, muscles bulging beneath his worn flannel.

The powerful Lich couple looked at each other and spoke without words. Nina giggled, smacking Nino playfully on the cheek, and Nino growled a laugh back before pulling his wife in tighter.

The couple truly had no qualms about public displays of affection.

“You think-ah we joke, eh?” Nino said, breaking his look on Nina to grin at Mr. Mystical.

“It’s hilarious!” Mr. Mystical chirped back. He was still getting used to their extended bits meant to instill fear before breaking apart into booming laughter.

“Nina, I know you have deep knowledge of the System and its ways. You and your matriarchal bloodline have known for centuries that upsetting the balance of all things one way or the other whips it back hard. When karma ruled in slow, meandering ways, it was easier! It took decades, and you could plan across generations. Now, the System does it way more violently! It doesn’t happen over years. We would be crushed in weeks. Perhaps days! Days I tell you!”

Nina grinned to herself, remembering the days of her mother and grandmother. Their constant worry masked behind serene mischief. Their worry for the sick neighbour. Worry for the demon infecting the neighbouring village fields. Worry to not bend things too much and get a backhand from the karmic balance that once governed.

Before the System came and changed the rules, that is.

If only they could help Nina now. If only they could yell at and deal with her sister.

Fatima the Bitch, Nina thought to Nino. Questa stronza è sempre stata una mela marcia. Certe persone sono fatte così. This bitch has always been a bad apple. Some people are just like that.

But what was done was done. It was her problem to solve now. Now that she had her scent, it constantly tickled the back of her throat. She would deal with her sister. And from her progress and vitality, she was on the exact right track.

She pressed her bosom into her husband to whisper in his ear. These days, she didn’t look a day past 40 years.

Mr. Mystical giggled again, worry brewing within as he watched them from his pot. He was connected to them now. Part of the Lich. He knew they jested, but he also felt the exact same desires they did.

Under his mocha pot, and underneath the shells the old couple wore, they were all undead beings tied to their Domains and with the unsatiable urge to do one thing.

“We no joke, Bartholomew,” Nina spoke, calling Mr. Mystical by his real name.

“You worry about System backlash? Fuck-ah the System,” She continued. “We twist and bend rule, as always. It will allow us to expand. To grow our Domain. Grow our level. We are Lich, first and foremost, and we must always go and grow ourselves.”

The last two words gusted out of her, frosting the kitchen surfaces. It didn’t affect Mr. Mystical. He was part of them. Three parts and three souls summed into a whole, making them far more powerful than they ought to be. He wasn’t privy to all their thoughts and feelings all the time. Only when they wanted him to be, and right now, they wanted him to understand.

Information washed over from Nina and Nino into Mr. Mystical. Family drama, old slights, all of it. Passed on from generation to generation, and then sister to sister, finally landing them here. A tale as old as time itself.

Some people really were just bad apples, rotten to the core.

He started laughing again, far louder than before as he came to understand. It was a cold mania propped up by centuries of Italian voodoo and the unrelenting drive of a family that never gave in. No matter the will or way. A dousing of Mr. Mystical’s relenting drive topped it all, flavouring the personality of the Lich.

“You never play by the rule, Barty. And we never, ever play by the rule.” Nino sniffed, popping a cig from nowhere to huff on in the cold kitchen. “It is why you such good addition. We do what we need to. Cheat everyone and everything, bend it all, take and do what we need. And break rule. Use rule. Twist rule. We never listen to no one. We only listen to ourself, and—”

Mr. Mystical finished Nino’s sentence for him. He was part of the Lich, after all, and now he understood the true game they played.

“—And only give a flying shit about us and our friends…and our…,” He sputtered out, wondering on something that went unanswered.

“But what about Alex?”

“Alex?” Nina said sweetly. “He is the key to the whole thing. From the start, this we know. You think he find ad to work here by chance? By luck? I skilled, Barty, but think on thoughts we share.”

Mr. Mystical gulped, the information he’d received settling in. It made sense. When you really got down to it, Italian voodoo, Lichdom, and food magic all shared one aspect.

It was all just different sides of life magic, and in the old days, the most powerful version had a far more nefarious and feared name.

Blood magic.

“Does he know?”

The old couple just grinned back.

“You think he need to?” Nino asked.

Nina laughed, the frost lifting in the kitchen, “I no want to know that bitch if I no have to.”

Emilio balanced easily on the cloud and watched all that he could.

A gentle breeze of essence rustled his whiskers. The sound of the explosion finally reached him. Emilio purred at the signal.

That gnome, Acorn Turtleshell, deserved to be lauded in rewards. The one with the gun too. He’d let his Steward know afterwards. Once his [Meditative Nap], a truly delightful Skill, and his essence cycling was complete.

Stretching to crack his back and crossing his paws, he got more comfortable, unworried about falling the thousands of feet below.

For him, maintaining balance was easy.

It came naturally to him. He was a cat, after all. A large cat by frame and a fat cat by weight. He hid his formidable muscle under his paunch and his uncontestable power in his claws, but truth be told, with his leveling and ascension, his hankering for birds, snacks, and when the mood struck him, softened butter, wasn’t enough to maintain him girth. Not for the most powerful cat on Earth.

He needed more. Food was delicious, naps was marvelous, and his area of control was comfortable.

Still, he needed and deserved more. While he had no clue what the words said beneath his [Hidden Demigod of Felines] said, he felt it’s guidance.

The Title drove him to ascend higher. It was a test of intellect, power, and splendor, that much he knew. Like the ideal version of Feline was calling from the System, it urged him to sniff outside his boundaries and claim a larger stake.

He flexed his [Murder Mittens], feeling the essence of the sky cycle through them, and delighted in what lay ahead.

Checking in on Alex, Emilio felt a swelling pride within at his Companion’s development. The demands of the [Heroic Hawk Core] he’d provided Alex, the only bird Emilio would ever respect, was working out to be a fantastic path.

Flicking over his stead, Emilio scanned the rest of his base of power.

The hawks themselves he’d brought into his pack watched and circled the sky perimeter. His new partner, and underling Boss, [Runs with Wind], scouted the deep woods and counseled Emilio while remaining under his command. The Garden Gnomes fornicated in the tunnels, multiplying ceaselessly, and trained new porcelain soldiers in the claimed Dungeon within the city. He caught a snippet of Marzipan ordering another round of push ups before pulling himself toward the end of his claim.

On the front lines in the swampy woods, his most fiercely violent followers watched the Northern regional boundary of Toronto and waited for his order. Right at the edge, the forest turned rotten and dark.

While his eyes saw far, it was limited to what was his. All of Toronto he could see. His vision simply ended at the edge.

And [Hidden Demigod of Felines], his prized Title with so many words under it, urged him to claim it. The destroy the dark that hid within. It was what would push him further down his own path.

The occasional wandering, attacking beast from the region of Algonquin had stopped. What came out from the Blightwood north was horrifying, even to him. Inedible, with rotten, twisted, unusable Cores unsuitable for Cultivation. Worse than rabid beasts the monster dungeon hybrids fought the Gnomes holding the line for all of Toronto.

But they were apparently smart enough to cease their probing, manic attacks.

This Emilio his prime target was onto him, and planning something large.

He’d never seen his enemy. The one that scared the powerful and intelligent [Runs with Wind]. The one that twisted animal and dungeon spawn to form what the Gnomes called [Skinwalkers]. The one that thought Toronto was weak, and its protector, and true ruler, was skittish.

The [Beast of Blightwood]. How he longed to sink his claws into this pest. How he longed to cast forth his [Righteous Lightning Gut]…after he had his fun.

Emilio felt Alex’s emotions rolling through their connection, getting the gist of what was happening, and felt self satisfaction warm his ears.

No worry crested through his girth. Even when he’d been weak and wet, a mere street cat with a knack for hunting and want of growth, he’d still chosen his Companion correctly.

He purred, and hoped the [Best of Blightwood]’s Core would be tasty. Hoped too that his enemy understood Emilio’s pack was coming soon.

Acorn Turtleshell awoke to pain.

He was flung in an arc against the howling wind, making him want to scream out from the hurt.

With some effort, he cracked open his eyes.

Panic beat within as darkness welcomed him.

He couldn’t see. Looking left and right, ignoring his aching body, everywhere looked like a deep shadow.

He blinked, squeezing his eyes, thankful now that his eyes were still attached to him, but frightened at what had happened.

Have I died? Is this what death is? Pain and darkness and wind?

The wind was the only thing he could hear.

He reached with his remaining hand around his body. The shield was still strapped to it, his face still had a giant hole where his mouth should be, his chest remained, his ears were still attached…

Even the plant rope from Dandelion was tied to his waist remained.

Where the hell am—

Laughter crested above the wind and made his heart soar.

Acorn Turtleshell loved that woman’s laugh, and felt his stomach flexing from his own silent giggle.

“Did you see THAT?” Dandelion cried above the wind, the only thing he could always hear.

It was a secret shared only with him. A Skill she hid from everyone. [Leader’s Laughter]. Strange that no one asked why she could always be heard above the dins of battle.

Little did they know, she kept it secret for a reason. She wanted to be a Battle Warden one day. With Acorn as her second. A secret weapon between them.

“DO YOU SEE THE MUSHROOM CLOUD! IT’S BLOODY NIGHTTIME NOW! SWEET APPLESAUCE! HE’S VAPORIZED THE BIRDS AND COLLAPSED HALF THE FALLS! WOOO!” Dandelion yelled over the wind.

Acorn looked up towards his voice, and finally he could see.

She stood atop The Expediter and pointed forward, holding the rope that held him, a giant mushroom cloud of smoke shading the sky. Wind whipped her hair as she cackled and yelled orders.

Acorn couldn’t hear Alex’s call back, but he could certainly hear hers.

“IF TANK SAYS IT’S THERE, THEN INTO THE BATTLE WE GO! LAND US AND LET THESE HUNTERS LEARN TO FEAR THE VERY SIGHT OF PORCELAIN! WE WILL GET THE STONE!”

It was at that moment that Dandelion Datura looked down towards him.

He could barely see the details of her face from his movement, but he could certainly hear the whisper meant only for him.

“See? I knew you’d handle it.”
