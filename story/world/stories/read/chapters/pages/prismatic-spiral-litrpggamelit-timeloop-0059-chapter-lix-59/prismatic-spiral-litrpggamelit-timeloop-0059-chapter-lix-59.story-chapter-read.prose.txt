Chapter LIX (59)

Dozens of children shuffled around the ruins. They swayed slightly, moving along with the melody of the music. But while clearly affected by the music, they also still seemed conscious of their surroundings, which was a huge relief. They chatted and ran around playing games with each other.

Mitsuko slipped in and did her best to blend in with them all. Thankfully, she wasn’t the only scrappy looking child among their number. Quite a few of the children wore torn clothing and a few even had cracked and hardened blood staining their clothes. Likely from their recent journey into the jungle. Mitsuko frowned at that and wondered how many children had suffered more than just scratches. She’d encountered monsters in this jungle that could swallow a seven-year-old with a single bite.

Green foliage sprouted across the entirety of the ruins. The ancient stone was barely visible beneath the vines and flowers sprouting across its surface.

Mitsuko tapped her finger against one of the ancient building foundations. There  was certainly more left behind than what she’d seen down at the southern ruins. She wondered if in a few more levels she would have the capacity to Mend even structures like this. Or perhaps once she maxed out Retrospection she could witness these ruins being built and used by the ancient civilization here.

The music radiated off of the buildings, it called to her. Beckoning her and demanding that she relax, lower her guard, and be at peace. But she continued to resist its pull.

The other children lacked her willpower. They hummed along to the music while oblivious to the fact they’d been plucked from their lives and families. Everyone at the ruins seemed utterly content.

She approached a half-goblin boy lounging on a vine-covered boulder. His head bobbed along to the melody while his feet kicked the air.

“Hello,” she called up to him, her voice a bit squeaky.

“Hello!” he replied, a bit dreamy.

“I’m new here. My ship just pulled into the harbor yesterday. What’s happening here?”

“You heard Mother’s call?”

“Yes?” She didn’t know who Mother was but the context was enough for her to assume it to be the island’s guardian. Best to play along.

“That’s wonderful!” he declared, his face split in pure happiness for her. “Welcome home! Mother will take care of you now.”

“A gender helps me narrow down the suspects even further,” Sterling said. “I believe I have enough information to take a gander at this sage’s identity. Should you ask. If it is who I believe it to be, then she is quite a piece of work.”

Mitsuko ignored the sage. “Where is Mother?” she asked the goblin boy. “Can I meet her?”

“No,” he replied sadly. “She went into the temple after the first day. She said a hunter will come for her. The hunter wants to assassess….assassinses…um. To kill her.” The goblin boy frowned and continued to quietly try to pronounce the word ‘assassinate.’

“Dammit,” Mitsuko cursed. Inside a temple? That meant traps and complex puzzles.

“That’s not very nice,” the goblin boy said, his smile now missing. “You shouldn’t say those kinds of words. Not about Mother.”

“Sorry. I’m just upset. I wanted to meet Mother.”

“Well you could talk to Zoe. She’s one of Mother’s Trusted!”

“Trusted to do what?”

“Carry out her word, of course!”

So an acolyte. Mitsuko filed away the information. “Who are Mother’s Trusted?”

“Zoe, Hallie, Quinn, and Gavin. But Zoe is the one to talk to. She’s a human with blond curly hair.”

Four acolytes? That was a lot. “Why did Mother choose them?”

“I don’t know. I wish she chose me instead. But Mother’s Trusted are amazing. I like them too. They’re like big brothers and sisters. Even though Quinn’s smaller than me.”

Mitsuko said goodbye to the boy and wandered off in the direction of the largest building amongst the ancient ruins buried in jungle foliage. A pyramid with a flat, plateaued, top. That would likely be the temple in question.

“Hello!”

“Hello!”

“Ahh, cute kitty!”

“Hey, you!”

“Hello!”

Children greeted her cheerily as she passed them. All of them lounged about completely carefree, but they remained conscious of their surroundings. Mitsuko stopped once to try asking one about her parents at home and the girl’s face had twisted in distaste at the question. It honestly reminded Mitsuko of how she felt whenever people asked about her own father. Mitsuko let that form of integration drop. She wasn’t getting anywhere with it. And she knew why the children had left, so the answers were rather pointless.

“You’re the newbie?” a girl with blond curls bounded up to her. “I’m Zoe! I’m so happy you’re here to embrace Mother’s fold! She’s going to protect us and keep us all safe forever!”

“Thanks, Zoe,” Mitsuko said. “I’m Mi…a.” She stumbled over her name, realizing mid sentence that she shouldn’t just blurt out her actual name for these people. Just in case the guardians somehow had a means to communicate to one another like the sages seemed to have.

Mitsuko glanced over at Sterling. In the past, Rodrick had used his acolytes as mouthpieces, implying he was granted sight through them. Could this sage see through Zoe’s eyes? But Sterling looked unperturbed, so Mitsuko decided not to worry for the moment.

“Mia?” Zoe tilted her head. “That’s a Hon name?”

“That’s right.” Mitsuko snapped her attention back to the acolyte girl. “Sorry, I’m just a bit tired and easily distracted.”

“That’s alright! Listen to Mother’s song! It’ll give you lots of rest without even sleeping!”

“Wow. That’s neat. What about food? Is there a…snack place?”

“Oh! There’s the garden! Want to see the garden?”

“Yes!” Mitsuko tried to match the girl’s enthusiasm. She felt so awkward trying to pretend to be an eleven year-old.

Zoe ran off and Mitsuko chased after her. Other children called to them as they passed by. They all repeated the same happy empty greetings. A few pointed out Sterling and cooed, but none of them approached the sage.

A wooden gate swung open, revealing an orchard. Baskets of fruit sat beneath the trees. Along the perimeter grew dozens of different vegetable plants. Unlike the rest of the ruins, this fenced off area looked freshly built and the trees neatly organized.

Sterling approached one of the nearby baskets and pawed at it, tipping it over and spilling nuts across the dirt.

“Fascinating,” Sterling said, examining the nuts.

“Bad kitty!” Zoe dashed over and attempted to shoo Sterling from the food.

The cat gave her an unimpressed look then lazily strolled back over to Mitsuko, flicking his tail at the girl.

“Care to elaborate?” Mitsuko muttered to the sage.

“Not without an official question.” Sterling paused. “However, you should ask her where this food came from. Just to confirm my suspicion.”

Mitsuko almost didn’t ask Zoe out of spite. But then she realized this was Sterling’s way of giving her a hint. The question would grant her information just as it would him.

“Did this food grow here naturally?” Mitsuko asked the Trusted girl.

“Of course not! It’s part of Mother’s bounty! She gave us this all. It’s for me and you!”

“She grew it? In a matter of days?” Mitsuko turned and took in the hundreds of trees lined up in the orchard. “That seems…remarkable. Does Mother practice druidic spellcraft?”

Zoe frowned and Mitsuko realized she’d slipped back into her normal speech patterns.

“Amazing!” Mitsuko said suddenly, changing her tone to simulate wonder. “C-can she make flowers grow too? I love blue ones!”

“Might be coming off a bit too thick now,” Sterling commented. “Your acting is very stiff too.”

But Zoe took the shift in stride. “She can! She made a flower meadow! Want to see it too? She uses beautiful songs to grow plants super fast.”

“My, um, other mother told me stories about druids,” Mitsuko said. “That’s what I meant earlier. They grow plants too! There’s one at the circus on Mauve Island.”

“You went to the circus?” Zoe asked, completely immune to Mitsuko’s attempts to press for information about druids. “I wanted to go! Gavin told me the clowns dance and throw pies at each other.”

“Yes. They did that. But I liked the trapezists the best. The clowns are, um, stinky and weirdos.”

“Unless I am mistaken, your vernacular is striking along the ages of a five year-old with word choices like ‘stinky’ and ‘weirdo,’” Sterling said. “Though, carry on. Perhaps my sense of modern language is inaccurate despite our efforts to emulate it throughout the Prismatic Spiral.”

Mitsuko blinked and glanced at the cat. He was making snarky commentary, but also just let slip that the Prismatic Spiral granted him access to her language. Which made sense. The odds of him speaking the same language after a thousand years was unlikely. She quickly developed a hypothesis. When they first met, Sterling mentioned that the Prismatic Spiral was linked to her soul. Could that grant them some level of access to her knowledge? It might be a line of thought to pursue later.

“I wish I knew a song to grow flowers,” Mitsuko said wistfully. She really wanted this girl to take the bait and talk more about the guardian’s magic.

“That’s okay! After this week we will have eternity with Mother! She said she’d teach us magic to be just like her!”

“Why after this week?” Mitsuko asked, attempting to press tactfully. “I want to learn now.”

Zoe sighed dramatically. “Me too. She taught Hallie and Gaven how to sprout seeds. But me and Quinn didn’t get to learn anything.”

Mitsuko crouched and plucked one of the nuts off the ground and wiped it with her sleeve. She tossed it in her mouth and immediately regretted it as she attempted to chew while missing molars. But it did taste like a normal nut. Nothing remarkable. She handed one over to Zoe who chewed it happily.

“So yummy,” Mitsuko said. “Maybe if we ask nicely she’ll teach us too?”

Zoe swallowed. “Maybe if we ask Hallie…she’s the oldest. She still sometimes talks to Mother while outside.”

That sounded like the exact person that Mitsuko needed to avoid. And judging by Sterling’s sharp look in her direction, he likely agreed with that assumption. Zoe might not be connected too closely to the guardian, but this other acolyte sounded far more dangerous.

“You really think she’ll listen? What if we just went in the pyramid instead?”

“Mother said that’s dangerous! It’s her special place.”

Mitsuko debated pressing the girl, trying to convince her to explore and adventure. But she reminded herself that not every child possessed the same wanderlust and curiosity she had at their age. In the end, Mitsuko decided to take a step back.

Plus, it wasn’t like she wanted to risk the child’s life inside the temple. After tasting several fruits Zoe shoved into her hands, Mitsuko allowed the girl to guide her back to the main ruins where the other children loitered.

“What happens after this week?” Mitsuko asked the girl. “Is there a reason Mother is waiting that long to come out of the temple?”

“Oh! Did I not tell you?” Zoe beamed at her. “Next week is the beginning of paradise! We’ll be protected forever and the bad person hunting Mother will be destroyed!”

“Protected forever,” Mitsuko repeated.

Her gaze swept over all the children in the area. Despite the warm sun and humidity, she felt a shiver run through her body as she recalled a city protected forever from the tides of time and change.
