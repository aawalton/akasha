“What did you make me?” I ask Morgan. She floats over to a table that has a bundle of cloth on it. I see can whatever lies beneath the cover is burning with Potentia.

She pulls the cover off with a flourish, and picks up the massive cloak from the table. She has to use telekinesis to be able to hold the whole thing up. It’s taller than her and wider than her arm span.

“I made you a cloak! You’ve got a distinct lack of style, and it’s not like normal clothes are really your thing. It’s enchanted too, you’ll love it.”

I take it, and feel the thick cloth between my fingers. It’s gunmetal gray, the same color as my armor. Hundreds of miniscule runes are embroidered in the magical cloth with blue metallic thread.

“The cloth used to be Kevlar before I gave it the titanium-to-mithril Exalting treatment. I’m calling it Adaweave. The embroidery thread is starmetal. It’s from the small scale experiments I did with platinum. It’s even better for enchanting than electrum is.”

The cloak is thick, though still flexible. Two heavy silvery clasps made of mithril sit around the collar.

“The clasps are enchanted to stay on, though they do have a quick release functionality.”

“So something can’t haul me around by the cloak,” I guess.

Morgan laughs. “Nope! It’s so you can tear off it dramatically during a fight. You know, when it’s time to get serious.”

She takes the huge cloak from my hands and places the clasps on my gorget. They stick with a soft click. The cloak wraps around me, almost like a living thing. It hides my bulk and softens the hard edges of my armor, reaching down to just below my midcalf. It’s warm, too. An odd thing for me to notice. I haven’t truly thought about the temperature since I Ascended.

I reach up to touch the collar, and the cloak smoothly flows out the way.

“One of the enchantments is to stay out of your way. You won’t trip on it, and you don’t have to worry about getting tangled up in it during combat. There’s more of course. Durability, self-cleaning, self-repair. But there’s one you’ll really love. Look down.”

A soft blue glow is underneath my boots. Tiny grasping tendrils of energy leak out from underneath them. It reminds of a plasma globe in a way.

“What is it?”

“It’s a reinforcement enchantment, but targeting everything you step on. You can go indoors now!”

I take a tentative step, the blue glow vanishing as my boot leaves the ground. It come backs as I place my boot on the ground.

“Look, try this!” Morgan points to a plank of wood floating in the air. “It’s just a regular piece of wood. Just levitating.”

Normally I’d crush something like this into splinters, even if it was resting on the ground. I place my weight on it. It holds, not even flexing a little.

“You can turn it off and on. Just think about not floating anymore. Same goes for the quick release functionality.”

With a flash of intent, the blue glow vanishes and the wood plank shatters with a crack. I flick it back on, and the reinforcement enchantment activates before I hit the ground. A pulse of blue light radiates a few inches in all directions, crackling along the floor.

In an instant I turn around and pull Morgan in for a hug.

“Thank you, thank you!“

I let her go after a moment and turn away. “Ah, I’m sorry. It’s just…”

“No, it’s alright. I can’t imagine what it’s like. Not fitting in the world, being forced to stay outside. Sometimes in the literal rain. A cargo aircraft is not a home,” Morgan says.

There was a reason I spent so much time in the Pineapple Express while I was blitzing. Even during the little downtime we had while the crew was sleeping. Where else was I supposed to go?

“Bad weather doesn’t bother me.”

“It’s not about whether it bothers you or not, Mach,” she says. “It’s about the principle of it. You shouldn’t be left out in the cold, not after all you’ve done, and everything you continue to do. It just wouldn’t be right.”

Both of us pause for a moment, and I look away.

“Thank you, Morgan.”

“Always, Mach. Now, try the quick release! Think about the clasps detaching.”

When I try to activate the function, nothing happens.

“Did I break it?” I ask, and Morgan laughs.

“No, not at all. Pull it off or shrug dramatically, dealers choice.”

I exaggerate a shrug, and the clasps come loose with a click. The cloak flies backwards, as if tossed away.

“Enchanted for melodrama,” Morgan says. “Though really it’s just a spell designed to enhance motion. It goes farther than it normally would. Isn’t magic awesome?”

“Yeah, it is,” I say with a tinge of awe. “It really is.”

I pick up the cloak and marvel at it. It’s construction is immaculate the materials used were extremely expensive and rare.

“There’s no way you did this in the time I was gone, Morgan. You’re good, but this is… incredible.”

“Nah, I’ve been working on it for a few weeks while you weren’t around,” she says while looking down. “I wanted it to be a surprise. I’m glad you like it.”

“I brought a few things too, but on second thought, I’m not sure how I could give you all of them.”

“Oh?” Morgan perks up. She’s always interested in magic stuff.

“Yeah. A few chunks of some magical uranium. I’ve taken to calling it Saturnium, but it’s not official or anything. A pieces of scrap metal, all slightly red colored. I don’t recognize it. It’s all highly contaminated, though. Not sure how you’d safely deal with it.”

“You brought me magical radioactive material?” Morgan says.

“Yes? I thought you might like it. I’m sure it’ll be weird and magical or whatever.”

She blinks for a moment. “I’m not sure if I should yell at you or thank you. Both, maybe.” She hums for a moment. “I should be able to whip up a containment area for it, but later. What else did you bring?”

“It’s for the both of us, actually. General Guan Yu gave it to me after I finished with the Fracture there.”

“The Ascended Chinese general?”

“Yeah,” I confirm. I pull out the thick letter. “He gave me this and a spiel about council of peers. Said it was for the both of us.”

She takes the letter from me and opens it. She shakes out a hand written letter, and a small palm sized pitch-black slate. More runes are engraved on the slate, and a tiny silver of some kind of glowing crystal is embedded in the black stone. The stone itself drinks in the light, looking almost like a hole in space.

She unfolds the letter, and we read it together.

Machina, I would like to once again humbly thank you for your timely assistance with our Fracture. You went above and beyond any expectations we had of you. Thank you. What we learn from what you gave us will be released publicly, you have my word. You saved many, many lives today. More than anyone will ever be really able to count, and most will never know. But we—I—will never forget that.

This missive is more than a thank you note, though I’m sure you’ll receive many in the days to come. It is also an invitation to both you and Morgan, who I assume is also reading this message.

I would like to invite you both to the first Council of the Ascended. It is clear at this point that given our unique abilities and the power we all wield it is imperative we meet and discuss the way we must interact with the world. It is not meant to be a governing body. It is not meant to rule the world. Merely to offer to ability to contact each other in times of great emergency, or help deescalate tense situations where we may disagree.

Whether we want to admit it or not, every single one of us is weapon of mass destruction. We must tread carefully in everything that we do. Even the smallest of missteps could lead to untold damage. No one wants to find out what happens if two of us come to blows.

I have proposed we meet on neutral grounds to the others, free from any government control or requests. This will be for us, and us alone. Those of us who have responded already have suggested the North Pole. The slate provided is a portal anchor. It will allow a portal ritual to target a location the caster has never been to. I hope to see you there, as this is only a request. I would never attempt to order either of you.

Honor and Glory,

General Guan Yu

There’s a date and time on the bottom of the letter, as well as coordinates that I assume refer to the meeting location. As expected, they are at the North Pole.

“Well, that’s a lot.” Morgan says. “Firstly, what did you do for them? I’m not going to admonish you for it, I’m just curious,” she adds.

“I helped them out with a Fracture they weren’t confident in being able to clear without losses.” I say.

“Unusually vague for you, but I won’t push. Alright, what do you think? I personally think we should go. It’ll be a good chance to mingle and schmooze. Get a lay of the land, so to speak.”

“I agree, I think it’d be worth going. But I am concerned.”

“About what? I’m sure no one would be stupid enough to start a fight.”

“…I might be. If Berhane shows up. That monster does not deserve diplomacy. Not after everything he’s done!”

Morgan places a hand on my shoulder and gives me a complicated look before finally settling on something vaguely sad.

“You’ll have to learn to. Make deals with monsters like that, I mean. Because General Guan has a point. We are weapons of mass destruction. Greater or less, maybe, but at a certain point the scale stops mattering. Once we get into city-flattening territory, everything beyond that is meaningless. How many people are you willing to hurt or kill to fight him?”

Part of me wants to give her the answer of ‘How many people will die if I don’t?’ but that’s just as bad. If I have to choose between the lesser of two evils, then I haven’t tried hard enough. If both paths are lined with thorns, I shall blaze a new one.

I fall silent, and Morgan moves on.

“Look, the message says that some people didn’t respond. Maybe he won’t show up.”

“But he does, and there’s no risk of collateral damage, then maybe I can—”

I cut myself off, partially horrified at what I nearly proposed.

Kill him.

I haven’t killed anyone. I don’t want to kill anyone. But when it comes to monsters like that, ones that can’t be contained or jailed or truly punished, what else can I do?

Do I set aside my own morals and do what must be done? Do I commit an act of violence—I’d hesitate to call it murder, but still—in the name of the greater good? Or do I stand by own morals and refuse to stoop to his level. Isn’t that condoning the use of violence, if I refuse to defend others who can’t defend themselves?

Do I accept that some people in the world just need to die? Do I decide that I’m the one who gets the right to decide that?

A warm hand on my shoulder pulls me from my dark thoughts.

“Mach? You alright there?”

I shut down the previous thread and move onto a better train of thought.

“Yeah,” I lie.

“…If you say so. You don’t have to go. I’m sure I can be enough for both of us.” She gives me a strained smile.

“No, we should go to together. We’ll see what happens, I guess. It’s not for a few days anyway.”

“True, true. In the meantime, I can take a look at this little nifty piece of runework,” Morgan says, looking carefully at the portal anchor.

“I didn’t know something like that possible.”

“I didn’t either, though I have been very busy working on other projects,” she says dryly, looking around at her gallery full of half-built prototypes. “It’ll be interesting to see it in action, though. I’m very curious to see how it works.”
