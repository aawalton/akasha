Luna reeled in surprise as the shadow that had appeared behind Samuel struck—only for the blow to fall on the man that she and the alligator had been fighting, rather than Samuel himself.

What? Did we have another ally nearby? Did James summon this man? How did he get through Samuel’s barrier? Why did he choose to jump in now, when we had already all but won?

The questions kept proliferating, with no obvious answers in easy reach.

Fortunately, the wolf did not need to decide what to do about the newcomer yet. The blow of his axe fell heavily on the flaming crown wearer, almost severing the man’s arm from his shoulder in a single strike. Samuel released his hold on the human, and the new arrival continued attacking the fiery crown Ruler.

The victim of that assault attempted to resist while also clutching at the partially crushed throat Samuel had left him. He danced backward, parried strikes that should have been lethal, and generally attempted to hold his own despite looking like he deeply regretted the life choices that had led him to this moment.

Samuel backed away from that fight, perhaps sensing that the new arrival—a human whose extremely pale visage reminded Luna of a white snake—might not be on their side just because he was enemies with the flaming crown wearer.

“Do you know who he is?” the alligator asked in a low rumble as the pale man fully severed one of the fiery crown wearer’s limbs.

The flaming Ruler let out a muffled roar of pain and then released a blast of flame at the pale man, but it felt weak and unfocused compared with the deadly attacks he had been using against Luna and Samuel. The two monsters had radically weakened the fiery crown wearer with their last few attacks, and the wolf guessed that his ability to use his powers was also still recovering from her Bite of Sealing.

Luna shook her three heads in unison, then sniffed the air. It was almost a reflex for her when she wanted information. Though her olfactory sense wasn’t always useful in the complex world they lived in, it didn’t often provide her with bad information.

Three sets of eyes widened as the disturbing odor hit her.

“Samuel, that human… I smell traces of the Panther Queen’s blood on him!”

At the same moment that she spoke, the pale figure rushed through a curtain of flames and swung the axe he wielded down at the flaming Ruler. The victim plucked the crown from his own head and used it like an improvised shield to deflect the blade of the axe.

As the fiery crown wielder blocked blows, he steadily gave ground, retreating toward the barrier of swamp water Samuel had erected.

“Is the Panther Queen still alive?” Samuel asked as they watched the fight. “Either way, I only need another minute to recover from my injuries and replenish the resources that the flame Ruler forced me to use.”

“We might not have another minute,” the wolf whined. She wished her mate, Romulus, was here. Or the Fisher Emperor. If the newcomer had defeated the Panther Queen—and Luna could not sense the Panther Queen still alive anywhere—and was stronger than the fiery crown wielder, too, then the wolf could easily imagine herself and Samuel being overcome. They had barely turned the tables on the Ruler they’d been fighting.

She looked up, but it was hard to see the barrier area where James was fighting with Samuel’s curtain of swamp water blocking the way.

“Should I dispel my swamp-water veil, so we can try to avoid this fight, or even get help?” Samuel asked.

“I don’t think the Emperor is free yet,” Luna said. “Hard to say if the spider is done with her fight with the veil in place. But I don’t think we can run. If our Ruler exits his fight and comes out to find the pale man confronting him, that’s a failure on our part. He summoned us to help him, but you and I have yet to defeat a single opponent.”

“Because they’re fighting each other now,” Samuel replied in a slightly amused tone.

Luna gave him a look, her leftmost head tilted.

“What, you missed that this fellow was the same one who the Panther Queen went off to face earlier?”

“I just assumed he showed up after,” Luna replied, eyes widening again. “He looks completely different!”

At that same moment, near the swamp water veil, the flame Ruler made a stand.

Blood streaming from a dozen wounds across his body, he smiled.

“Finally got my powers working all the way again,” he muttered.

The pale man lunged for him, but the fiery crown wielder disappeared in a flash of bright light and reappeared on the other side of his attacker.

“Time to die,” the fiery crown wielder said. “You fucking traitor!”

His flame crown glowed the brightest Luna had yet seen, and he unleashed a blast of intense, concentrated light that forced her to avert her eyes. The last she saw, the energy was about to strike the other man in the back, though he was also standing extremely close to the swamp water. Perhaps that would mitigate some of the damage from the flame type attack.

She could not have predicted what happened next.

The flames continued to rush forward, the longest and most powerful blast of the fight, completely obscuring the person being attacked from view, until finally the fiery crown wielder’s crown dimmed and then faded to black. The flow of energy abruptly cut off, and the flame Ruler slumped his shoulders, visibly exhausted.

Luna could not see what remained of the one he had been attacking, because the body was obscured behind a thick cloud of foul-smelling swamp vapor where the energy attack had collided with Samuel’s wall of swamp water. Doubtless his body must be horrendously charred.

The fiery crown wielder turned back to Luna and Samuel with a sheepish look.

“You two had me already,” he said. “And I’m certainly in no shape to fight you off now, let alone the Fisher Emperor. We can call this your win, but I may come back another day for a friendly—”

A heavy presence fell across the entire area within Luna’s perception. At the same time, the flame crown wearer fell silent, a Mana-infused claw jutting out of the side of his neck.

The color of that Mana, Luna thought, panic rising inside her. It’s the Emperor’s Soul Magic. Or rather, this person can use Soul Magic, too.

If the pale man truly was a person at all.

The presence that Luna felt seemed to originate from him, and it felt less like a human man’s Ruler aura and more like a god had descended into the melee.

The pale man wore a creepy, twisted expression that seemed to mix amusement and annoyance. His hand, which seemed to have mutated into a sort of monstrous claw, twitched within the flame Ruler’s neck and then pulled backward, out of it.

The fiery crown wearer let out a pained gurgle, then a horrendous scream as his soul shattered and his body disintegrated.

“I didn’t want to have to waste that power on him,” said the pale Ruler in a voice that left no doubt about the divine energy the man had to be channeling. Every syllable made Luna’s bones rattle, made her want to fall on her knees or turn and flee. “He was unworthy of it. Just because some stupid Sun God liked him, he thought he was special. Really, I thought the two of you would have weakened him enough for that to be an easy kill. Guess I should have gone for the head.”

He grinned and licked the dead man’s blood off his claw-hand.

“I hope the two of you can entertain me until the Fisher Emperor comes,” he said. He drew a coin from his pocket and tossed it in the air.

When it landed in his outstretched hand—the one that was still human-shaped—he scowled down at it. Then he shrugged, grinned, and looked back at the two monsters.

“It looks like it’s your lucky day, servants of the Fisher Emperor. My divine backer has a sense of humor. Even after the two of you witnessed me completely end your previous opponent, I’m going to spare you from suffering a similar fate. That is, you’ll probably both die here, but the coin toss decided I won’t destroy your souls.” He chuckled and winked. “You may rejoice at the mercy of the great god Apophis.”

That name… Is it one that the Emperor has mentioned before?

“I’ll lower the veil, so we can run,” Samuel said in a low voice, almost a groan, as if he was in pain. Luna began to protest, but he continued, “Not immediately. We’ll do our best. Maybe between the two of us, we can run down the time on this power-up he’s using. Surely he can’t channel the energy of a god for long.”

The wolf nodded. They would do their best.

Samuel had recovered his trident at some point while Luna wasn’t paying attention. Now he twirled it in one mighty claw, and the swamp water veil started to move again. It pulled back from the dome it had formed, beginning to concentrate in the area Samuel and Luna occupied, floating in the air above and behind them.

“Your tactics are cute,” their opponent said, chuckling. “Utterly useless, but cute.”

Then he disappeared.

Luna had a moment to recognize that the enemy was gone and to begin charging her lightning breath.

Then the pale, snake-like man stood in between her and Samuel, grinning.

A whip-like tentacle sliced through the air beside Luna’s leftmost head. It moved slowly to her perception, almost without a sense of urgency, but the terrible pressure in the air prevented her from reacting as quickly as she normally might have to the attack. The tentacle sliced her front left leg to the bone, and as it pulled back, the tendril seemed to stimulate all the nerve endings in that region with more pain than such an injury would normally inflict.

With a single blow, the Wolf Lady was reduced to crying out in pain.

“Luna,” Samuel growled.

His massive tail whipped forward from one side, trident swinging from the other, as he tried to retaliate against the one who had just injured his companion.

The pale man reached out with one hand on one side and a single tendril on the other and stopped the trident and tail respectively in mid-swing.

“That—” Samuel grunted with effort. “That can’t… be…”

“You can’t believe a human is stronger than you?” The man giggled. “Dumb brute. Now you stand opposed, not just to me, but to a god who shares my skin. How could you possibly overpower even the Partial Incarnation of Apophis?”

And Luna saw exactly what he meant. In a moment of clarity, she recognized something she hadn’t been able to see before. As he fought, the pale man’s skin and clothing were slowly boiling or rotting away, depending on where she looked on his body, only to be regenerated just as quickly.

This was the price of sharing one’s vessel with a divine being. In addition to whatever formal cost the Skill imposed, it was overtaxing the enemy’s healing factor, possibly to its limits. The Skill was more unsustainable than Luna had guessed.

Perhaps not enough to save her and Samuel, though.

The wolf let loose her burst of lightning breath at the same moment that there was a distracting noise from up above.

The Apophis worshiper looked up at exactly the same moment that Luna launched her breath attack, so he took the attack full in the side.

Luna had one set of eyes on what was happening above as well. The barrier had broken. The Fisher Emperor had destroyed the souls of the two men within it besides himself.

Now he exited, turned his head to glance to each side, and then looked down at where Luna and Samuel struggled with the Chosen One of a dark god.

Run, James! Luna sent through their bond. The fight is unwinnable. Just run!

The enemy had said outright that he was not going to use Soul Magic to destroy her and Samuel’s souls, but he had made no such pledge against James, and the Apophis worshiper’s eyes lit up greedily as he took in the figure of the freshly available Fisher Emperor.

The lightning breath that Luna had fired seemed not to have had enough impact even to distract him.

James looked down at Luna and Samuel, struggling with the Chosen of Apophis, and Luna could see he sensed the insurmountable power of the god that their opponent was channeling—a power that none of them could possibly defeat.

The Fisher Emperor gritted his teeth and disappeared from view.
