The creature commonly known as Dylan was not, as would be traditionally defined, spying on Noah at all times. Except when his holder was directly interacting with the Astral Script to upgrade or evolve something, Noah's thoughts and actions were entirely unknown to the Void creature in charge of monitoring the man.

What Dylan did have full access to was Noah's Astral Script, including several layers of it that were never shown to the user. Insignificant flags being triggered, potential Paths being opened, requirements being fulfilled for things Noah would never think to look for. He was two rescued children away from unlocking a Childcare path, for instance, and one rare creature away from unlocking the Trophy Hunter path. Dylan knew full well that Noah had no interest in choosing either, and would probably filter them out sight unseen for not being rare enough even if he did end up earning access to them.

However, while this bland list of actions and unlocks may be useless to Noah himself and irrelevant to his actual Path progression, it did give his supervisor a constant running update list of what his wayward charge was up to out in the physical world.

Dylan could not in truth lounge back on a spinny chair with his feet on a desk, sipping a warm drink while a list of Noah's actions scrolled steadily by, but if he had been, he'd have spat out his drink in shock and jumped to his feet when the latest feat came through.

Revolving Doors Are My Jam. Requirement: Died at least three times. Died in more than one realm.

It was in this exact moment of shocked disbelief that Dylan found himself abruptly ejected from the cozy comfort of Noah's Astral Script and spat out into a blank, empty, and very unfriendly terrain.

"I just have to figure out how to use any of it without the system showing me what to do,” Noah thought loudly. “How hard could that be?"

"Quite hard, you absolute idiot," Dylan retorted irritably. He'd lost control over his physical form and ended up stuck half human half space octopus. “You’re making a terrible mistake, just so you know. This is your last chance to save your self before it’s too late.”

"Dylan?” Noah turned to him with surprise. “What are you doing here? I thought you only showed up in my evolution screen. How did you get out here if the Astral Script is inaccessible?"

"I am bound to your Void pact and manifest through the script barring extreme circumstances, and these circumstances are beyond extreme. Where you go, I will be present, and right now, that appears to be death.” Cold started to spread through Dylan's fingers and tentacles, the hostile environment doing its best to eliminate him before he could escape.

At the same time, Noah winced and put a semi-transparent hand to his semi-transparent chest where the Void pact was anchored. "No pain resistance here. Damn it."

Right now, there was a tiny thread of connection holding him to the Abyss through Noah's void pact. If Dylan gave up on his duty and fled for his life, he could probably make it through before the pressure of this place broke it down entirely.

Though Dylan may have been many things, a quitter wasn't one of them.

"Take us back right now." Dylan floated a little higher as indignance got the better of him. “What were you thinking? If I'd known you were suicidal I wouldn't have put so much effort into you. I assumed you were just the usual brand of delusionally reckless. It hasn’t even been a decade! The only thing that's holding you together is about to disappear. If we don't get out of here now…"

"I think I'll take my chances, thanks," Noah said dryly. He then winced and put a hand to his chest, where the black diamond that anchored Dylan, the pact, and all its associated benefits was slowly shrinking, leaving cracks through his soul as it went.

"Look," Dylan said hastily. "I don’t know what will happen when your spirit stops being shielded, but it won’t be good. Right now, no one knows where you are; that's what the Void has been doing for you this whole time, shielding you from the other primordials. But as soon as that shield is gone, you'll become a really tasty target for all of them."

"What makes you think they haven't already been coming after me?" Noah looked confused by this revelation. "I've been fighting off cultists from every cult this whole time."

"Exactly," Dylan said with full emphasis. "Cultists, not Ascendants, not deity avatars."

"Avatars? Don't you mean Aspects?"

"No. The point is, if you want to stay you, you need to act now. The only thing keeping you together right now is that every layer of yourself is contained within the Void pact. You break out of that, you'll come apart at the seams. You don't need to get rid of the pact, you need to get out of here."

"I came here for answers and I'm not leaving until I have them."

"You can get answers all kinds of other places, you don't need to get us both killed! Why won’t yo—" Dylan felt the moment the Incarnation of Void reclaimed his voice, his body going stiff and tone flat. "Human. You owe us an explanation."

Noah’s posture stiffened as well. "I don't owe you anything."

"You have repeatedly pushed the limits of our tolerance. You swore not to oppose us and yet have fought our servants at every turn. You swore yourself to our enemy. You have not safeguarded the pact as you ought. There is penalty due to us as a result."

"The pact never broke, did it? I've remained perfectly within the boundaries. Acting against your cultists isn't the same as acting against you directly."

"You have sworn to destroy everything we oversee, yet we still protect you."

"I can't help but wonder what part of that protection is benefitting you."

"You have died. Your time on Erandir is at an end. Do not play games with us, mortal. Give us the seal."

"I refuse." Noah almost smiled.

Dylan, or the Void speaking through him, did not. "Our pact has reached its natural conclusion. You were given power and freedom to wield it as you saw fit. Now you have died, it is time for us to part ways. Give us what was promised."

"I didn't promise anything. I said I wouldn't rise against you, and I haven't."

"You have fought our servants at every turn, and taken personal action toward becoming a god yourself. We are tired of waiting, mortal."

The cracks spreading out through Noah’s core pulsed with voidlight, sudden and sharp enough he cried out and doubled over, but he straightened a moment later though his voice was strained. "I said I refuse. You have no authority here."

"Insolent creature. There is no need for us to—"

"Did you forget?” Noah grinned shakily. “We've been through this dance before. If you could have done anything else, you would have by now. Don't go making threats you can't follow through on. It only makes you look bad."

"It is to both our benefit if you retain autonomy and remain alive. Throwing yourself into the hands of the Watcher or the Reaper is harmful to us both."

"Or maybe it's only harmful to you." Noah shrugged, reached into his chest, and grabbed what was left of the Pact in one hand. "I lived, I died, pact over."

"You would rather destroy yourself than be parted from the seal? Why? Do you truly wish to be enemies?"

"I wish to be free."

"And yet you bind yourself to everyone you meet. Where is the freedom in that?"

"Because those bonds are ones I choose. Aurelia, Tony, Erwest, Zax, Bun Bun, Snow, Vion, Kaelan, Seraphina, my nightshade…" With one more sharp tug, the crystal lattice woven through his soul finally came free in Noah's hand. The black diamond with its gooey web of accompanying strands had only been superficially grafted in, for all the Void’s attempts to the contrary.

"Why would you choose to do such a thing? It is incomprehensible. You flee from every attempt at restraint, yet take on the burden of caring for people you do not know, accepting their restraints freely without hesitation. Why? What value do you see in these creatures? They destroy themselves and each other so readily, you would blame us for our part in their own actions?"

"You may not be responsible for every atrocity that exists, but you are responsible for every life you have helped to destroy." Noah closed his translucent hand around the Void pact.

For a long moment, neither of them moved.

"We respect your defiance, human, but it is misplaced. You have put us off long enough."

"I'm not done with Erandir," Noah gritted out, "but I am done with you." Then Noah crushed the diamond into powder.

Abruptly, the Incarnation of Void disappeared from Dylan's body, leaving him free to act on his own behalf again. He waited a moment for the Abyss to drag him back, but nothing happened.

"Wait, wait—" Dylan spun around and reached out helplessly, already feeling the atmosphere of the place eat away at him. "Don't leave me here!"

"Too late. Looks like your boss is gone. Hope you have a good severance package."

“What have you done?” Dylan turned to Noah, frantic now. "I do not understand. I've been with you through your whole life on Erandir, watched you stumble through events you should never have survived. I've given you every opportunity possible to make the right choice and return what was stolen. I've shown you the paths to power and domination and you turn away. Now you have destroyed the Pact, your only protection is gone." Dylan was coming apart faster now, his form fraying into shapeless shadow, the hostile Underworld biting at him from every angle.

"I’ll survive. Or not. But what about you?" Noah looked concerned. "Will you be alright?"

"There's no way out any more,” Dylan murmured, resigned. “Even if you ran at full speed, we wouldn't escape in time. Not now that you've broken that." He gestured to the handful of gooey shadow strands hanging from Noah's hand, all that remained of the pact.

"What will happen to you, without the pact?"

"I do not know," Dylan whispered. He’d never considered dying before. It wasn't really a thing, as far as he knew. You either ate others or were summoned out into the world to play until your summoner ran out of life for you. "This is nowhere I should have ever been."

"For what it's worth, I'm sorry. I came here for answers and for freedom, but I never wanted to hurt anyone."

Dylan gave a raspy, disbelieving laugh. "Now I'm an anyone, am I? Just because I'm dying slowly and pitifully in your hand? I guess I never will understand you."

Noah looked down at the gooey tangle in his spectral hand. "This is you?"

"It is the connection between you and me, which should have been mediated through the Void, but now..." Dylan trailed off. Just like him, the last traces of the pact would fade away within moments.

“Now the Void is gone from this, yeah?” Noah gathered up all the thin trailing threads in his hand. Put together, it was barely the size of a fortune cookie. "All that's left is you and me?"

"Yes. And I'm not going to be here much longer." Dylan's shadowy huddled form had shrunk to the size of a child and continued coming apart at the edges.

"Would you want to?"

Dylan didn't know how to answer. He never had been an anyone before, not really. He was a communal projection between this human and the Incarnation of Void.

“If I could keep you with me without the Void in the picture, would you want that?” Noah held up the gathered handful of solid shadow, connected by faint lines back to Noah's chest and down to Dylan's fading form. It would take only the slightest tug to tear it free entirely. "Or would you rather take your chances and try to find your way back home on your own?"

"There is no home for me to return to, even if I knew a way out of this place. You..." Dylan trailed off, confused. The thought had never occurred to him before, but now the question was posed… "Your Astral Script has been the closest thing to a home I ever had."

“Good enough for me.” And before Dylan could ask what the madman was going to do, Noah popped the entire thing into his mouth.

—

Noah had no Astral Script here to narrate the results of his actions, but he'd been devouring things long enough to know what it felt like when his Aspect integrated something.

Dylan's form stopped fading and coalesced instead, into a tiny creature that vaguely resembled the form it'd been in at the start of their conversation. Dylan's head was perfectly spherical now, with only two red points of light for eyes, and it connected directly to a lower body of oily-iridescent eldritch shadow tentacles. He was small enough to fit in one hand, and looked aggressively displeased about this.

He stared down at himself, then up at Noah with reproach. "Couldn't have done that before I lost ninety percent of my soul mass, huh?" His usual tone was back, as if he hadn't been pleading and dying ten seconds ago. "I shouldn't have expected anything better."

“Whatever you are, you’re my responsibility now.” Noah raised an eyebrow. "Unless you'd rather I cut you loose..."

"No need to get personal about it," Dylan grumbled. He shook out each of his tentacles, bounced from one side of the ground to the other, his intense focus looking cute enough that Noah couldn't help but laugh. "This is your fault," Dylan snapped, looking up and pointing one tentacle at Noah's face. Then he froze, his tentacle turning to point behind Noah instead. "Uh... who's that?"

Noah turned to look.

He'd become perfectly accustomed to seeing himself from the outside by now, having enough shadow copies, astral projections, clones, and personal manifestations of various natures that everything from a Noah-exclusive strategy session to a Noah vs Noah free for all would have felt ordinary and comfortable.

But the Noah standing behind him looked distinctly not like himself. He couldn't pin anything down as being different; the eyes were exactly the same, his mother's nose, his own slightly scruffy chin, the almost forgotten scars of his adventures on Erandir…

The other Noah rolled his shoulders and stretched his neck, his soft smile looking wrong on Noah's normally determined face. "Finally. Some privacy."

"Hey, I'm right here!" Dylan snapped.

“What are you?” Noah asked. “I don’t feel any connection between us like with my spectre clones.”

“Of course you don’t. Connection would imply differentiation. Those copies are incomplete, partial. You connect with the pieces of you they are. I am not a piece, not a partial. Though there are some rather troubling gaps in my memory, I trust we will remedy that promptly.” The copy spread his arms. “I am the Devourer.”
