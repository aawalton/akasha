What? asks Romeo curiously, though there’s tension there too.

I lean against a tree, wordlessly indicating that I mean no threat. I suppose tension is natural – we haven’t actually got to know each other much. Perhaps it’s about time to spend some time with my newest Tier three Bonded.

Bastet, the mind-reader, glances between me and the larnatis that everyone can see she’ll eventually accept as her mate – except the larnatis himself – and a sense of approval drifts down the Bond between us.

I’ll go hunt, she announces, pulling away from my scratches and turning to head into the forest.

Romeo immediately shifts to her side.

I’ll come.

No, Bastet quickly responds, whirling on him with a quiet snarl.

But–

You speak to Markus.

Romeo’s ear tufts droop and I feel a sense of disappointment down the Bond, though it’s directed at Bastet.

Why? he asks, literally whining like an unhappy cub.

Because Markus is Packleader. If you want to be my mate, you must accept my Packleader while you are here.

His head swings around to gaze at me thoughtfully, a strange light in his eyes. It’s not quite threat, but it’s close.

I hold his gaze with equanimity – I’ve faced far more threatening things than a Tier three larnatis, by this point.

Fine, he replies to Bastet, his previous disappointment replaced by…anticipation?

Good, Bastet chirps, then leans in for a quick head nuzzle before trotting away.

Romeo looks frozen in place. Unless something has happened while I’m not around, that head nuzzle might have been the first time Bastet has offered him affection. I know that, though she recognised his value as a fighter, she hasn’t wanted to make things easy on him.

I asked once why she was making his life so difficult. She explained that since her kind’s males rarely stick around after mating enough for the females to catch, the females need to be sure that they were worth bearing cubs for. While I’m sure that for lower-Tier raptorcats, all of that is prompted by instincts, for Bastet, it’s a clear thought process. And I’m not certain that she’s entirely right – Romeo’s behaviour doesn’t seem like that of a male planning on disappearing after mating.

But, ultimately, I have no desire to be a matchmaker. And if it doesn’t work out or Romeo wants to go, I won’t stop him. The Bond between us mostly serves for communication and as a guarantee of behaviour while he’s in and around the settlement. Humans, especially non-Classers, look like prey to him.

Since Romeo is still statue-like, gazing wistfully after the departing raptorcat matriarch, I stay still too – he can make the first move.

Finally, long after I’ve stopped being able to detect Bastet through anything but the Bond, Romeo swings his head towards me. I tense a little – his gaze is the definition of challenging.

Beautiful female says you are Packleader. But if I defeat you, then maybe I will be Packleader. She will see I am most powerful – and she will let me mate with her, he muses and starts stalking towards me, his wings opening widely – in threat, or in a peacock-like display, I can’t be sure.

I shake my head incredulously.

“Yeah, that’s not going to work, mate,” I tell him, completely unthreatened.

Why not? he asks, tilting his head to one side. It is the way things are. Powerful males mate with the females; weaker males are driven away. I see static electricity flicker over him as the wind begins to pick up around us.

Eyeing him, spoiling for a fight, I sigh silently. First Tilathis, now Romeo? Apparently challenging me is becoming a rite of passage for powerful male feline-types.

Well, if it’s a fight Romeo wants, I’m willing to give it to him. To be fair, I wouldn’t mind testing him a little, and this is as balanced as it could be between safety and danger. After all, even if I ended up losing, I can stop the fight with a single flex of the Bond between us.

“Alright. I’m game,” I say lazily. “How about a bet – if you win, I’ll put in a good word with Bastet. If I win, you’ll make more of an effort to properly join the pack. Deal?”

If I win, beautiful female will mate with me, Romeo insists.

I shake my head.

“That’s Bastet’s choice, not mine,” I tell him with finality. “And her name is Bastet.”

Romeo growls.

Human noises, he dismisses.

“Human noises that she likes,” I tell him pointedly. “Anyway, like I said, the best I can do is sing your praises to her as a mighty hunter.”

He considers that then makes a pleased rumble.

Acceptable.

Without any more warning, he leaps at me, teeth and claws bared.

And promptly finds I’m not there.

He looks searchingly around himself, even whirling around as if he thinks I’ve gone behind him. But he only looks in two dimensions. For a creature who can fly, he’s surprisingly limited in his outlook.

I pull a chunk of wood out of my storage space – there just in case I need to start a fire where there is no wood, but otherwise worthless – and drop it.

My aim was perfect. It lands directly on his head.

He snarls in surprise, but finally looks up.

I wave mockingly at him from where my wings, grown during our negotiation, are holding me in the air.

Romeo snarls at me, mantling his wings.

Better give me a good fight for that, human.

I smile at him.

“Come and get it, then.”

Most-powerful-beast-and-best-hunter-and-best-mate-and-I’ll-prove-it-go-on-and-try-me – or, in short, ‘Best’, snarls in surprise at the two-legger floating in the air above him. For a species that seems almost entirely land-bound, the human looks surprisingly comfortable up there in the air.

But the male doubts he has anything like Best’s manoeuvrability. Still, he feels a kindling of his competitive spirit. This male seems so confident. Time to see whether he has reason to be.

Extending his wings, Best leaps up into the sky. The wind recognises his dominion, supporting his wings and streamlining his body.

Wrapping his body in the wind, he darts for the two-legger in the air faster even than birds can match. Best’s snarl becomes anticipatory – surely the two-legger cannot dodge this.

He opens his mouth and bites down, preparing for hot, delicious blood to spill into his mouth.

Yet his jaws snap closed on empty air; his claws lash out, meeting nothing.

For a moment, he struggles to understand that his attack failed, and then Best looks around himself wildly, once more unable to see the two-legger.

Then, remembering his earlier error, he looks above and then below.

There! The two-legger dropped down, successfully avoiding him. Good reflexes. Best will give him that. But reflexes alone aren’t enough to win.

Best drops towards him, pushing himself to move even faster than before, and this time he reaches out with claws and jaws of wind, invisible to the eye, but deadly.

Yet, again to his surprise, the two-legger survives an attack that has killed many before him. He deflects the air-claws, and, with a beat of his own wings, shifts far out of the way of Best’s dive.

For the first moment, frustration intrudes on Best’s focus. It felt like the wind had turned against him. But how could that be the case? He had dominion. And this two-legger’s focus is not wind – he knows that.

Sure that he had just somehow made a mistake, he breathes in, summoning the wind to his call, and then out, shaping it to his desires. He feels its fluid power flow over his feathers, and through his very being. As he breathes in, more wind joins the whirlwind he’s summoning; as he breathes out, its speed increases.

He half-expects the two-legger to attempt to interfere with his summoning – that’s what he would have tried against another male trying this. Yet the two-legger has just drifted down and just waits to one side. Best might have guessed him to be frozen in fear, except that the Bond between them indicates nothing but curiosity.

For a moment, Best actually puffs himself up a bit. The only explanation he can think of is that the other male wants to see his display of power and ability.

If so, Best will show him an attack like none other before. And then the male will be amazed, and the beautiful female will be amazed, and then she will agree to mate with him.

With that happy thought, he poured more and more of himself into his attack.

Forks of white fire begin to mix with the wind, flickering in and out of view. More and more, until the whirlwind around him is as much white fire as it is thick, almost opaque wind. It takes him his full white fire Core’s mana, and one of his two Wind Cores to power the attack, but Best does not regret the expense. Instead, he pours even more of his determination and willpower into the display. It’s bright enough that the beautiful female can probably see it from her hunt.

It’s ready! he purrs proudly as he beholds his creation, better than anything he’s ever made before. Who could resist a male capable of this? A pillar of whirling wind that reaches up towards the heavens, outlined by forks of white fire. He is at its base; its creator, its wielder.

Lifting it a little with his will so he can ensure his aim is good, he blinks as he doesn’t see the two-legger where he was a moment ago.

For a moment wondering whether the two-legger’s courage has broken at the magnificent sight before him, and has simply run away with his tail between his legs, Best is relieved when he sees that his target has merely drifted down to stand on the ground once more. Dangerous beauty such as this deserves an observer. And a target.

Yowling with a mix of glee and excitement, Best dives through the air right at the two-legger, the tornado screaming as it propels him faster than he’s ever flown before.

I hope beautiful female is watching! he thinks a moment before he crashes into something hard, solid, and utterly unyielding.

Stunned by the impact, he loses control of the white fire and whines in pain as, for some reason, it decides to go through his body all at once.

Some of it returns to his white fire Core, refilling it a little, but most drives through his body painfully before vanishing from his sight and grasp.

The wind of the tornado has also been disrupted – somehow parts of it are being used against other parts. The smooth circular flow of it is no more – instead, the tornado fights with itself, wobbles, and, before Best can take control of it again, falls apart.

Best lands heavily on the ground, completely confused as he looks upwards.

Where did those come from? he wonders as he stares at the thick limbs of oddly shaped rock looming above him that have just ruined his attack entirely.

The sound of crunching leaves near his head sends him rolling to his feet, snarling instinctively at the beast that dares to approach while he’s recovering.

No beast is this – it’s only the two-legger. His body language is hard to read, but the connection between them expresses…disappointment?

Is that all you have to throw at me? the two-legger rumbles, his mental communication sounding utterly unimpressed.

For a moment, Best stands gaping at him. And then outrage wells up inside him. Did the two-legger not see that glorious meld of white fire and wind that could have swept the whole area clear of trees?

Best’s vision goes red.

Without thought, caring nothing for his injuries, he throws himself at the two-legger, his teeth and claws bared and enhanced with as much mana as he can pull from his half-drained Cores. He will make this two-legger recognise his magnificence.
