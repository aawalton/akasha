Mettius jumped down from the tree landing in a roll.

“There’s a group coming this way, more archers,” Mettius said.

“Let me handle them, stay back,” Caleb said.

He ran to each fallen archer and using his Imperium drew their arrows to him. Then he ran in the direction Mettius had indicated, activating his Iron Vortex as he ran and throwing the bundle of arrows out to be taken up by the magic.

The expected darkness bubble appeared the instant after Caleb spotted the oncoming group of archers. He felt as arrows entered his vortex, getting caught up by the wind and joining his tornado of death. He ran out, arrow after arrow getting added to his vortex until suddenly they stopped. The archers began to back away from him, but he didn’t let them.

Caleb used Jump to leap forward and at the apex of his flight he pulled on the armor and buckles of the five men in front of him. Suddenly pulled back by their own equipment all five stumbled and Caleb came soaring down on them like a ringfallen chunk of steel. Before he reached them his arrow storm had already lacerated them badly on any exposed surface.

One died as he landed from an arrow to the neck while a second died when Caleb landed on him. The third and forth died by Caleb’s hammer. With the death of the fourth, the darkness vanished and Caleb saw an adult man in his forties with a long graying beard holding a sword up to defend against him.

Caleb swung, breaking through the guard, but pulled the blow at the last instant so that the hammer would knock the man unconscious—he hoped.

Mettius and Kelia ran over after Caleb gave them the all clear and together they tied the man up.

“We need to get some answers from him,” Mettius said, pulling another vial out of his pack.

“How can we trust anything he says? Will he even speak?” Kelia asked.

“I can make him speak,” Mettius said confidently. “And prevent him from lying.”

They fed the man the potion and he woke up with a start of coughing that turned into vomiting.

Once he’d settled down and Kelia had dumped his own water skin over him to clean him up, Mettius rolled him onto his back and stood over him. He adjusted something on his pauldrons, touching each, and then spoke.

“How many initialized are in the village.”

Caleb could feel the power of a skill in the words.

Part of Caleb wanted to answer despite not knowing.

“More than you can handle!” the man said maniacally though his face showed surprise at his own answering tongue.

“A number if you will,” Mettius said.

The man waged an internal battle before grunting out a word.

“D-d-dozens,”

“How are there so many initialized?”

The man didn’t even fight to answer that.

“The Benefactor has come and he will be the end of your pretender empire! The true heir to the First Empire is here and he grants classes to those faithful to him! You may have defeated us, but we are just pawns beneath—”

Mettius turned to Caleb and Kelia. “The problem with this skill is that when they have a topic they want to talk about they won’t shut up.

He continued on for a little while, speaking of their inevitable doom and destruction, speaking so loud he couldn’t hear Mettius’ follow up questions. Then Mettius punched him in the mouth and asked.

“How can we sneak into the village? Is there a passphrase?”

More grunts as he tried to stop himself from speaking, but eventually he gasped out, “First empire!”

“How did you get your class,” Mettius asked.

The man fought hard to keep the words in.

“Cas—” he began but then he bit down hard on his tongue, the tip of which fell out of his mouth. Blood began pouring out.

He fell back let the blood pool in his mouth and then inhaled. Despite his fanaticism, that was a step too far for his body to allow and his natural reactions took over. He began to cough the blood up while still bleeding profusely from his tongue.

“Fanatics,” Mettius said, shaking his head. “We won’t get anything out of him now.”

“Mettius,” Caleb said. “This is more than fanaticism. The empire is not popular here, but we have little reason to hate it that much. They mostly leave us alone and my village gets the worst of the attention.”

Mettius’ look of irritation turned to one of concern, and Caleb wondered briefly how bad the empire was if this was the level of fanaticism he was accustomed to.

“You’ve seen it this bad before?” Kelia asked, speaking Caleb’s thoughts for him.

Mettius shook his head.

“Not personally, but I’ve heard stories, seen riots, read accounts. This wouldn’t be out of line for the rebels.”

“There are no rebels out this far,” Caleb said. “No one cares enough to rebel.”

“Consider yourselves lucky,” Mettius said, acid in his tone. “This Benefactor concerns me. He can grant classes. That’s something only Mythic classes can do.”

“What about this error?” Caleb asked. “Maybe its not mythic, but some exploit of the failing system.”

“I don’t know what’s worse. Someone finding an exploit that drives people to suicidal manias or that there’s a mythic individual running around outside of the known entities.”

“How many known are there?” Kelia asked.

“Not many, but they are all heads of state of one sort or the other.”

“What do we do then?” Kelia asked. “If there’s a mythic rarity class in there we stand no chance.”

“If there was a mythic rarity entity in there, Magnus wouldn’t have escaped.,” Mettius said. “This rebellion was sparked months ago. Whoever this ‘Benefactor’ is, he has better things to do than wait around in the armpit of the empire.”

Caleb let the dig against his home go as he sensed no malice in it and had more important things on his mind.

“We can try to sneak in with the gate phrase—hopefully there wasn’t more to it than he said.”

“Let’s take their clothing,” Kelia suggested.

The legion issue clothing was a mix of shades of red and white, while the villagers all wore hides.

“Good idea, some of them have to be free of blood,” Mettius said, looking at the now dead man in their midst who was very much not free of blood.
