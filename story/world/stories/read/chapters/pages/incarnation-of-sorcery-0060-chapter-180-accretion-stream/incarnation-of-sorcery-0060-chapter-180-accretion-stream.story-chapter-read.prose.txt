Ryland checked up on Viren’s attempts with the Inscription about a couple of days later. He needed the break to regain his strength after the exertions. It allowed him to recover after taking on the Great God, and it had also granted Viren enough time to make some decent progress with the new task that Ryland had set him.

“I think it’s coming along well,” Ryland said after he had inspected Viren’s attempts from multiple angles for a few minutes. “Good work, Viren.”

“You really think so, sir?” Viren asked sceptically.

He looked dubious about Ryland’s praise, which was fair. After all, there wasn’t a whole lot. Viren had managed to set down a few of the base Glyphs of the Inscription because they were the ones he knew, but they weren’t very High-Tier.

“Have you broken down the task into different components?” Ryland asked.

Unlike with the Tier [7] Glyph task he had set Viren across an entire semester, this time, Ryland wasn’t being very hands-on with the thing. That last one hadn’t just been an exercise in casting powerful magic. He had also been teaching them how to conduct the process of learning more powerful Glyphs all by themselves.

“I started the first step, sir,” Viren said, proving that he at least remembered the steps of the entire process. He was hanging his head, though. “But I got a little overwhelmed, so I decided I’d bolster my confidence by just setting out the few Glyphs I was familiar with.”

Well, Ryland couldn’t blame him for it. It wasn’t ideal, but it was far from a failure. He hadn’t given up, after all, which was the most important matter in the end.

“And the next step is…?” Ryland prompted.

Viren took a quick, deep breath. “Now I need to note down all the Glyphs I don’t know and can’t cast. After which, I will be learning those Glyphs. And then I can start casting them.”

“You still seem concerned about it all.”

“Well, sir, your Glyphs are at Tier [Quintillion] or something insane like that?”

Ryland blinked, then laughed. “Are you worried that your Glyphs’ Tiers are going to be low, especially for the ones you’re learning from scratch?”

“Yes, sir, exactly.”

“Well, don’t worry about it.”

Ryland pulled out some of the Deification Prints he had picked up from one of his achievement rewards. He cast them onto Viren’s Glyphs, and after some time, they started glowing with familiar iridescence.

Viren’s eyes were glowing and reflecting the rainbow lights edging the Glyphs he himself had placed. “You just turned them into Tier [Quintillion] Glyphs!”

Ryland chuckled again. “No. Just Tier [25] ones. But yes, still far more powerful than you could have probably managed on your own.”

“That’s very, very true, sir…”

With Viren now convinced that he didn’t need to worry about the exact level of strength, he focused more readily on learning the exact Glyphs he’d need, which would be a difficult task on its own. Now that Viren’s intent going forward was settled, Ryland could turn his focus onto other matters.

“The next thing I’ll be searching for is condensed accretion stream matter.”

Viren blinked. “What, sir?”

“Hmm. I think it would be better if I could show you. That said, I need you to keep practicing the new Glyphs so you can cast at least half of them when we return. Is that understood?”

“Absolutely, sir,” Viren said with a vigorous shake of his head. The boy was determined not to let go of this opportunity. “I won’t waste a single moment.”

Ryland didn’t mind letting Viren take more time. He had gifted Viren a free spot on the ritual Inscription for him to practice his fortification process. Therefore, if they were attacked or someone tried messing with the Inscription, none of the so-far important locations would be harmed.

Plus, Ryland was leaving behind a slew of alarms and wards to alert him in case something nefarious occurred while he was gone.

With another [Translocate], they visited another star. The difference was that they didn’t need to get as dangerously close as he had gotten last time. At least, not yet anyway. Plus, it wasn’t actually a single star this time. Instead, Ryland had appeared next to something that was almost a binary star system, where it was a star orbiting a black hole with a huge accretion disk.

Viren was properly awed yet again, for Ryland didn’t hear a single peep from him. Instead, the boy was staring agog at what most people didn’t even know about, much less actually get to see up close in this fashion.

“This is an accretion stream,” Ryland explained to a stupefied Viren. “Essentially, it’s the energy and matter of a star being dragged towards a black hole, whose immense gravity doesn’t let anything escape.”

That explanation did wonders to break Viren out of his little awed stupor. “Except for you, apparently, sir.”

“Indeed!”

Ryland started showing Viren just what he meant by gathering the accretion matter. It was actually rather simple. He cast somewhat complicated magic again, one that had the same bones as the fortification spells he was using. While there was less need to learn and adapt in this scenario, it certainly wasn’t unhelpful.

“You’ve already seen the basic principles, Viren,” Ryland said. “Can you tell me why we might need any sort of adaptation in this procedure?”

“Uh…” Viren looked around desperately like the stars were going to spell out the answer he needed. Then he blinked. “Oh, right. It’s because everything is moving, right? When we were—well, when you were moving the star, I remembered that even enormous cosmic bodies move. Even if it doesn’t look like they do.”

“Exactly!” Ryland beamed at the boy. “Since both the black hole and the sun are going to move, I need to ensure that whatever spell I set here moves along with them. I could of course use Subglyphs to modify the exact coordinates to match the accretion stream’s position, but I thought I’d show you that sort of adaptation spell yet again.”

Viren nodded and silently watched. Just as Ryland finished up the spell, he spoke again. “Wait, did you just call that a sun, sir?”

“Oh, yes,” Ryland said. “The actual reason I brought you here was because I had a task for you. A task that’s on the planet orbiting this combined system of a sun and a black hole.”

“Sir, how come a black hole can tear away matter from the star but not pull away the planet orbiting it?”

“Magic. How else?”

Viren blinked several times, before his eyes went wide again. It seemed he was starting to grasp the sheer level of magic one had to cast for something of this calibre to take effect. Magic that could stop planets from falling into black holes had to be tremendously impressive, and the work of a mage—or something else along those lines—like Ryland.

Of course, Ryland knew whose exact work it was, but it would be fun to see if Viren could discover it himself in the task he was about to set the boy.

“What is the task, sir?” Viren asked.

“Simple,” Ryland said. “I wish for you to meet some representatives on Abarosk, that little planet over yonder.”

“Uh…”

Viren was probably concerned about being sent to an entirely foreign Realm all on his own. Even aside from worrying whether he’d survive the atmosphere on it—which he should, thanks to Ryland’s [Oneself World]—there were a lot of other concerns to take into account. Such as how he’d communicate, where he was supposed to go, and so on.

Oh, and whether the locals would consider him a rare delicacy or not.

“Don’t worry,” Ryland said. “I’ll grant you what you need to succeed.”

True to his word, Ryland popped his hand into his rift to pluck out an important item from his personal inventory pocket Realm. It was an amulet with an amber bead stuck to a leather string.

“This amulet has an Inscribed spell circle that will translate whatever you hear into a language you understand,” Ryland said. “And in turn, it will translate whatever you speak into a language those who hear you understand.”

“Oh!” Viren said. “Very neat.”

“Just make sure you aren’t close to too many animals and monsters. They get very confused when they suddenly understand sapient languages with perfect clarity.”

“Hmm, not as neat, I suppose.”

“And once you reach Abarosk, just mention Lord Esper. That should get you to the Ivail Trade Guild.”

Viren looked like he was trying to absorb that information. He nodded resolutely. “I’m ready, sir.”

“Alright, then. Good luck out there. And don’t be afraid. Even had I not sent you with the capability of communicating and with the necessary directions, you aren’t heading to an uncivilized world. The Mastitians are very nice, all things told.”

“That’s comforting to hear, sir.”

“Good. Now off you go. [Translocate].”

A rift swallowed up Viren, taking him to the Realm of Abarosk.

Viren blinked at the city around him. The architecture and layout of the structures everywhere felt so foreign, he might as well have been placed into the wilderness outside of the city to get his bearings better.

Tall spires made of glass and ceramic gave him the vague impression of being back in Arcoryx City, but these ones were warped and twisted in an almost unnatural way. The streets were sinuous and wet, despite the green sky showing no sign of clouds or recent rainfall. And if it had rained, surely the structures would have been wet too.

The whole place was also a giant hive. Viren wasn’t sure how else to think of it. The roads wended into each other with flyovers and underpasses, the structures arranged on top and around each other like unruly hexagons left by haphazard bees. If Viren was supposed to navigate this whole place by himself, he was already feeling intimidated.

Especially since Ryland’s handy little amulet didn’t translate signs. He was going to have to speak with the locals.

That proved a daunting task at first just because of how alien they seemed to him to begin with. He spotted the people before long, after slipping and sliding his way through the city. It really didn’t help when the wet streets were also inclined. But their construction soon made sense.

The locals were, for lack of a better term, snake-people.

Actually, there was a better term. Ryland had mentioned it. What had it been…? Viren wracked his memories for a few moments before the word came to him. Mastitians.

Essentially, they were half humanoid, and half serpentine. The upper half had limbs that resembled Viren’s own, just usually bluish or greenish or some shade in between. Their flatter heads had long, spiky hair most of the time, and they wore accessories made of bright metals and polished gems.

He approached the first one hesitantly. This one wore a dress made of scales, with the skirt parting as the serpentine lower half swished along the wet road.

The being appeared to be browsing some sort of shop, except it was the size of a small closet with the entirety of its offerings locked behind glass. The creature placed a small number of coins in a slot, before a mechanical arm placed a bottle of liquid into a pocket with an opening, through which the Mastitian obtained their purchase.

The tiny shop also spoke. “Thank you for the purchase!”

Viren was too intrigued by whatever sort of advanced Glyphtech this was supposed to be to fully appreciate that his translation amulet was working perfectly.

The Mastitian wasn’t as alarmed to see Viren as one might have thought. “Ah, a human. And a child at that. Where did you come from, if I may ask? Oh, I suppose you don’t understand Mastitian, do you? It’s alright, I can get you a translation—oh, would you look at that. You do understand me.”

Viren just stared. The Mastitian had spoken so much and so quickly, he was a little bit overwhelmed. If Ryland’s translation amulet hadn’t deciphered every word for his mind to understand, he’d have been in big trouble.

“Um, yes, hello,” Viren said, trying to regain the confidence that Ryland had sent him here with. He couldn’t allow himself to get so wrapped up that he failed his task. “I came from Lord Esper.”

His offering was met with a blank stare from the Mastitian’s solid black eye. “…and?”

“And I came here to find the Iv—”

There was a flash from the shop. The glass glowed, and the items behind the encasing disappeared to be replaced by the figure of a well-dressed Mastitian.

“Ah, how lovely to meet you, Emissary.” His strongly masculine voice was directed at Viren, and his smile was so radiant, Viren was starting to wonder if the Mastitian had metal for teeth. “I know who you are and where you need to go. Listen closely to my directions, please, and tell them Soltivo sent you, along with your lord, of course.”

Viren nodded. These Mastitians seemed nice, but they were also a bit too overbearing, guiding Viren wherever he was supposed to go without actually waiting for him to tell them anything much about it all.

That said, Viren did appreciate the adroitness of it all. The directions were very crisp and clear, emphasized to help an absolute newcomer to the city such as himself, so that even he had no trouble navigating the web of structures and strange roads.

A lot of people stared at him. Viren ducked his head and tried not to stick out too much. He did note that Mastitian society seemed quite homogenous. While there was some variation in skin tone and hair-and-scale colours, there weren’t any other races he recognized. No dragonkin or golemites or forgeans. Nor any elvhenans or kuldoreans either.

It was just scaly, snake-people everywhere.

Eventually, Viren arrived at a large structure with grey metal sheets stretched across glimmering pylons of glass and ceramic. There, as soon as he mentioned the name of Lord Esper, he was guided inside.

The longer this went on, the more Viren was intrigued by just what exactly Ryland’s connection was to this place.

“Greetings, Emissary,” said the Mastitian who was clearly the owner of whatever this Trade Guild was, going by how he was dressed in rich white robes and jewellery of actual gold. “Now, would you kindly tell us where Lord Esper is?”

Straight to the point. Mastitians clearly weren’t people who beat around the bush.

Viren pointed straight up. “He’s trying to collect the accretion stream.”

“Oh! He’s here to stop our whole world from being destroyed. How nice! Now let’s go and help him, shall we?”
