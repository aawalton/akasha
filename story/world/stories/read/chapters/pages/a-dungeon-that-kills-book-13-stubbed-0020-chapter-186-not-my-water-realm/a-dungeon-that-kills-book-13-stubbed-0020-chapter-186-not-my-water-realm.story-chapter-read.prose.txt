“A water biome?” Ekon gaped. “We’re actually inside a dungeon?”

Ah, yes. Biome.

That was the term used by the adventurers. Viktor hadn’t heard it for a good long while. Ever since he had learned what the Dungeon Cores called it—a domain—the old word had simply faded away from his vocabulary.

“I don’t think so,” he said. “This Folded Chamber has merely been shaped to resemble a dungeon floor by the power of the Abyss.”

A bit unexpected, but not entirely surprising. After all, there had been signs pointing in this direction, like the employment of underwater monsters in the assault on Daelin’s outpost or the masked men’s ability to flood his old castle. So now, when he found out they even had a Domain of Water at their disposal, it felt less like a shock and more like the final piece of a puzzle clicking into place.

And that means... Viktor swept his borrowed eyes across the vast, glassy surface surrounding him. There are almost certainly countless lurking things out there, in the depths below.

This Folded Chamber, unlike his own water realm in the dungeon, had only two islands. The small one under his feet, where the rift had just spit him out and which offered nothing in the way of cover, and the large one in the distance, crowned by that stone structure looming at the far end of the long wooden bridge. That was undoubtedly the base of operations from which the masked men launched their attacks, and the most likely place where their victims were confined.

The only way to get there was walking on that exposed walkway over open water, designed to ensure anyone crossing it would be utterly vulnerable to whatever was hiding under the surface. He could fly, of course, but he doubted his enemies were careless enough to forget that possibility.

He glanced back over his shoulder. The rift through which they had entered was gone. He wondered if the same phrase could be used to open it again from this side. If that were not the case, he would need to rely on Galatea to pull him and Ekon out when the time came.

“What do we do now?” the bald man asked.

“We wait.”

With a layout like this, it was plain that the moment the rift opened, someone in the building across the water would see it. Good thing he didn’t send Galatea through first, or she would have announced the attack with all the subtlety of a herald blowing a trumpet. Now, his arrival must have already been spotted, so there was nothing left to do but wait for the enemy to make the first move.

Soon enough, a figure emerged from the stone building. A single masked man, who stepped onto the bridge and walked toward them at an unhurried pace.

“Should we move too?” Ekon asked.

“No,” Viktor said. “We stay here and let him come to us.”

Moving forward meant they would meet the masked man in the middle of the narrow, exposed bridge. And he had no intention of doing that.

The man, however, came to a sudden halt once he had gone halfway across. He planted himself there and waited in silence.

Oh, well. Viktor chuckled. Looked like the choice had been made for him.

He placed a hand on Ekon’s back and shoved, and the bald man slipped back into his role without missing a beat. His shoulders dropped, his chin lowered, his feet dragged against the wooden planks with the heavy shuffle of someone whose spirit had been thoroughly broken. Together, they walked toward the waiting masked man.

“Why did you come back here? With him?” The voice that came from behind the mask was distinctly feminine. “What happened?”

A woman, huh?

Well, even though he lumped them together as “the masked men,” there had never been any real reason to assume they were all male, and apparently, they were not.

“I managed to capture him,” he replied. “So I brought him back.”

“Your assignment was to observe him. Nothing less, and certainly nothing more.”

“Yes, but I saw an opportunity present itself. Weeks in the dungeon had weakened him considerably. He was in no condition to resist. It seemed wasteful not to act.”

“If you had seen an opportunity,” the masked woman said, “the correct course of action would have been to return here and report that. Not to act independently, and not to make decisions that weren’t yours to make.”

“That would have taken time. Time during which he could have slipped away, or found his way back to his allies. I had weighed the available options and concluded this was the right choice.”

“You do not have choices to make, and you do not have options to weigh. You’re supposed to follow the instructions you’ve been given, precisely as they’ve been given, without deviation and without interpretation.”

Viktor gave a shrug. “My bad, then. But it’s already done. What now?”

Silence stretched between them on the bridge.

“...I don’t know,” the woman said. “We’ve never had a situation in which one of us failed to follow instructions.”

Oh, great. These guys were so thoroughly conditioned to absolute obedience that even the possibility of someone disobeying orders wasn’t considered worth planning for. What a wonderful system.

After another stretch of silence, the woman said, “I’m going to town to seek instructions from our superior on how to proceed.”

“Sensible,” Viktor said. “What do I do in the meantime, though?”

Perhaps they would throw him in the cells with Ekon, which was perfectly fine by him. Because the moment his feet touched that other island, all the pretense would become unnecessary, and this conversation would end in a manner considerably less civil than it had begun.

“You wait here. Do not move from this spot until I come back.”

And just like that, the woman walked past him. Toward the smaller island, at the same unhurried pace.

Viktor arched the dead man’s brow as he watched her go. Did she seriously believe he would stay here just because she told him so?

Well... yes.

She genuinely expected that.

Celeste, he called his Dungeon Core.

[Yes, Master?]

Tell Galatea to inform the other two that a masked woman is about to come out through the rift. Kill her, and—

Wait.

No. Tell them that she might carry poison on her, and if she realizes she’s about to be captured, she will use it to kill herself. So, try to take her down before she has a chance to reach for it. I don’t know, knock her unconscious or something. After that, search her thoroughly and take the poison away.

He had no idea where it was hidden, though, but the Druidess might have better insight into the matter. He didn’t know whether capturing a brainwashed minion of the Abyss would yield anything useful either, but there was only one way to find out.

After they’ve dealt with her, have them come in through the rift. Give them a description of the layout so they aren’t walking in blind. Also, once they’re inside, don’t cross the bridge right away. Instead, wait for the rift to close. I want them to try to reopen it from this side using the phrase. Galatea will remain outside. Just in case we can’t get out on our own.

[Understood.]

Viktor glanced at the masked woman, who had reached the far end of the bridge. A moment later, the rift split open to receive her. Whether she used the same phrase or a different one, though, he had no idea.

“Let’s go,” he told Ekon once the woman was gone.

And so they moved again. Let’s see what will happen, Viktor thought with amusement, when I fail to comply a second time.

He got his answer before they had covered half the remaining distance.

The water erupted as something pale broke the surface. Human-shaped, more or less, but sagging like a half-melted candle. Viktor recognized it immediately, for it was one of the same creatures that had attacked Daelin’s outpost. Looked like they had finally been identified as hostile by the enemy, and the defensive mechanisms of this Folded Chamber had awakened in earnest.

As the thing sloshed onto the bridge, he didn’t waste time admiring its general unpleasantness and instead raised a hand to give it a brisk blast of wind. Back into the water it went, with a satisfying splash.

“Run!” he shouted to Ekon. And the bald man bolted without a fuss.

No sooner had they taken a few more strides than another monster rose from the depths, this one a crone with hook-like talons where its fingers should have been. Once again, a familiar, ugly face. Ekon, whose hands had been free, hurled a fireball at the creature, but its waterlogged skin proved resistant to his flame, and the blazing projectile fizzled upon impact without dealing any significant damage. As the barnacle-covered hag staggered back from the failed attack, however, Viktor seized the opportunity and cast another wind blast, sending it tumbling over the edge.

This was bad.

Fire was obviously ineffective in this environment, while using wind to throw the enemies back into the water was merely delaying the problem rather than solving it. They needed to reach the other side, and fast.

“Just so you know,” Viktor said, without breaking stride, “while I can fly myself, I can’t lift you up like your aeromancer friend.”

Needless to say, upon hearing those words, the bald man’s running speed got an instant boost.

Fortunately, the bridge was not as overwhelmed as it could have been. Perhaps the defensive response hadn’t fully engaged yet, thrown off by the ambiguity of the situation. Whatever the reason, the monsters that jumped onto the bridge were manageable in number, so they just blasted their way through, until they finally reached the larger island.

The moment his foot touched the ground, Ekon reached into his pocket and pulled out a small vial, one that the Druidess had given him before they entered the Folded Chamber together. Without hesitation, he smashed it against the dirt.

Green smoke erupted from the shattered container, hissing and swirling like something alive. It spread outward, and wherever it drifted, the soil rumbled and ruptured. Then came the vines, bursting from the cracks.

Ah, yes. This was what Mandragora had used to keep Khenemhotep’s tomb guards at bay in the battle within the mortuary complex. The same green tendrils now slithered behind them, weaving themselves into a writhing barricade to block the onslaught of the underwater monsters that were surging toward the coast.

“Send them into the building as well,” Viktor said. “Restrain anything that moves.”

Ekon nodded. The vines, as if they had heard the command directly, obeyed without delay, pouring into the structure through doors, through windows, through every dark hole in that stone bastard of a building. Viktor wondered whether they simply took orders from whoever had thrown that potion, or if Ekon had picked up a few tricks from the Druidess.

In any case, the situation seemed to be secure. They had a lovely wall of angry vegetation to shield them from the monsters. Nothing was getting through, at least for the time being. The barrier would hold until Mandragora and Renee arrived.

Or so he thought.

A thunderous roar rolled across the Folded Chamber. A colossal column of water blasted skyward as something erupted from under the waves. For a heartbeat, Viktor could make out nothing but churning foam and cascading water. Then the creature rose higher, revealing its full shape.

Once again, it was something he had seen before. Not alive, but a grotesque mountain of leathery flesh and swollen muscle that had impaled itself into the ramparts of the outpost. The one looming before him right now was not a carcass, of course, but that didn’t mean it was any easier on the eyes. Still, setting the matter of aesthetics aside, the creature’s sudden appearance did introduce many new problems.

True to his expectation, the mutated giant proved far beyond what Mandragora’s vines could handle. A single, casual sweep of that massive arm, and sections of the living wall were reduced to shredded greenery littered across the shoreline.

Viktor activated the wind gems embedded within the borrowed body, launched himself into the air, and circled the giant in a wide arc. At the same time, he unleashed a barrage of fireballs, the projectiles shrieking toward the creature from different angles. He had no illusions about actually hurting it, but at least the attacks would distract it for a few moments and buy a bit more time.

But then, the giant looked at him.

And he fell.

What?

He tried to trigger the wind gems again, but there was no response. None whatsoever from any of them. He tried to use the fire gems instead, and these ones worked without issue, launching a fresh volley of orange globes at the towering creature as he plummeted toward the water below.

I see.

Unless all of his wind gems had suddenly run out of juice at the same time, which was laughably implausible, there was only one explanation for the phenomenon. The giant itself must have disrupted his ability to manipulate air somehow. Made sense. This had to be the masked men’s solution to deal with intruders who tried to cheat the water realm by taking to the sky.

And so, the borrowed body crashed into the waves. As it sank, the monsters came, their claws and limbs gripping it in layers as they dragged it further down into the depths.

Opening his eyes, Viktor found himself back in his room. Yes, it was definitely a good thing that sight and sound were the only sensations he got from his puppet.

Its destruction came earlier than anticipated, but that didn’t really matter in the grand scheme of things. He never expected to achieve victory with that temporary body anyway. He merely wanted to use it to probe the enemy’s defenses.

And now, it was time for him to properly enter the fray.
