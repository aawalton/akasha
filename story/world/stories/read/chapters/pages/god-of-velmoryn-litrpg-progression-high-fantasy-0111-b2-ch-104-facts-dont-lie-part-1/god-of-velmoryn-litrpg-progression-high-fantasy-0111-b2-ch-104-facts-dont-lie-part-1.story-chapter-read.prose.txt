Winter still held the entire forest in its grip.

The snow lay deep, and the temperature beyond Eriavel remained bitterly low. The forest had been painted white, even the evergreens no longer boasting their rich emerald color beneath the frost.

Yet the sun had already begun to regain its strength, but Noah and his armies were nowhere to be seen.

I could not know it with absolute certainty, but I was confident the time limit on the Night God’s penalty had already passed. He should have been able to use his powers freely by now, and the forest was probably no longer protected by whatever spell the Goddess had cast.

Despite that, nothing had changed.

Eriavel continued training while enjoying the warmth provided by the Sunbeam Trees. They were still little more than saplings, their few branches carrying only a limited number of fiery red leaves, but even so, they managed to keep the worst of the biting frost outside the settlement.

I had been surprised by how quickly they had grown.

Part of it was probably thanks to Vivien’s constant care. She visited them every week and nurtured them with her plant magic. But the biggest reason was most likely Orrvyn.

The giant oak’s roots spread not only beneath Eriavel, but far beyond it. The divine tree had connected itself to all ten saplings and constantly supplied them with mana and nutrients.

Normally, I would have forbidden the Crimson Guardian from wasting its strength on something as trivial as keeping the settlement warm. I needed Orrvyn in perfect condition when I expected Noah’s army to arrive quite literally any day.

But after confirming that supporting the Sunbeam Trees placed no noticeable strain on it, I allowed it.

Days passed.

The snow gradually thinned, and the animals and insects that had spent the worst of winter curled inside their nests or buried deep in slumber had begun to reemerge.

My basilisk was one of them. Despite my orders to remain awake, it still spent most of its time either eating or sleeping inside its new den near Eriavel.

In a way, I felt the same. Like I was hibernating. Pretending to be trapped in a deep sleep while doing almost nothing except watching, waiting, and thinking about the war that was supposed to come.

I will not lie. There were days when I started wondering whether Noah had simply changed his mind and the war would never happen.

Those thoughts only fed my paranoia.

I was getting exhausted from doing absolutely nothing but observing and thinking.

Until, one day, my dominion shifted and Amon appeared in a burst of crimson energy.

“I got restless,” he said with a frown, looking around my realm. “What is that?”

He was looking at the small separate space I had created for Aurelion. My angel’s constant training had become exhausting enough that I finally gave him a room of his own, mostly for the sake of my sanity.

“Did you make sure no one would notice your absence?”

“No one even knows I was in that damp cellar. Amanda and the others left on a guard contract a week ago. They are escorting some merchant caravan from…” Amon waved a hand dismissively. “Doesn’t matter. It’s all boring. Too boring.”

He kicked through a crimson cloud as he approached me.

“So, what do you think? Why isn’t he attacking?”

“I have no idea. I sent Dick…”

At the name, Amon smirked. He clearly still found his little joke far more amusing than it deserved.

“I sent him to spy on the mercenary armies Noah has been gathering. The one he is watching now received orders to wait for someone. No one knows who. At least, none of the people Dick has managed to question do.”

“Might be an Inquisitor,” Amon mused.

He walked through the barrier of crimson energy I had created around Aurelion’s space and watched my angel train for a few moments before returning.

“Knowing you, you are probably going mad now that too many things have become uncertain.”

“No. I do not have time for that. I am going mad trying to predict what Noah will do. I thought he would have attacked by now, so I am starting to question everything. What if he already knows I am not asleep? What if the God of Beggars was a calculated move meant to pull my attention somewhere else?”

“What if you are overthinking this, Brian?”

The name irritated me. Far more than it should have. For reasons even I could not fully explain, hearing my old name now felt wrong. Maybe because it reminded me of a simpler life, and how thoroughly I had managed to fail even at that. Or perhaps it fed the suspicion that even in this life, I still had not managed to become entirely myself.

Either way, anger surged through me.

“Do not call me that,” I hissed.

Amon’s expression shifted slightly.

“I may allow you more freedom than anyone else, but do not let memories of that life take shape in this one. I am no longer that man. And you are no longer connected to that existence either.”

“Fine, fine.” Amon chuckled. “But you still did not answer my question. What if you are overthinking this? What if Noah is simply not as clever as you think?”

“He deceived the Goddess. Lilith too, and…” I frowned. “He played me as well, remember? If I had not used my power against that spider mutant mother, the forest would still be protected by the Goddess’s spell.”

“He did not play you, Verde. You had no idea how this world worked. He had information and you did not.” Amon shrugged. “It is easy to manipulate someone who does not even know the rules of the game.”

I was about to argue, but he continued before I could.

“All I am saying is that you should not give him more credit than he deserves. Otherwise, all this caution of yours might end up helping him more than it helps you.”

My first impulse was to counter him.

Quite a few arguments were already lined up in my mind. Amon was not saying anything I had not considered before. He was my clone after all. The problem was that every time I reached the same conclusion, I also came up with twice as many reasons why underestimating Noah could be far worse.

Still, this time I forced myself to seriously consider Amon’s perspective.

“Then tell me. What do you think?”

“I think he underestimates you,” Amon answered immediately. “Think about it. Noah has lived for centuries. If he knew the Goddess, then he is at least a few centuries old himself. Would a deity that ancient truly see some weak god born only a couple of years ago as a serious threat?”

He tilted his head.

“Even you do not really consider Athena a threat just because your Radiance is higher than hers. Now put yourself in Noah’s place.”

“You have a point, but…”

“Wait. Let me finish.”

I sighed, but allowed him to continue.

“The first time you fought him, you won because of your Radiance. Or perhaps he never even intended to win that clash. Then came the message warning you that his Inquisitor was coming for Avenor.” Amon lifted one finger. “Since then, he has been winning.”

“I won several fights.”

“You won fights. He got the outcomes he wanted.”

I remained silent.

“That is different,” Amon continued. “You killed his people, stopped some of his plans, probably annoyed him quite a bit. But in the end, things still moved in the direction he wanted, didn’t they?”

“Well… yes.”

“So why would he suddenly start treating you like some terrifying mastermind?”

Amon smiled.

“As far as he knows, you are an inexperienced god who keeps falling into his traps. His last message alone showed how little respect he has for you. He genuinely seems to believe defeating you will be easy.”

His smile faded slightly.

“And sacrificing the God of Beggars…That plan worked too. He forced enough divinity onto you to put you into slumber.”

“Except I had a second Authority.”

“Exactly. Something he did not know. Stop treating every move he makes as if there is another layer beneath it. Sometimes things are exactly as they appear on the surface.”

“I know, Amon. I’m the one who told you all of this, and I’ve already considered every one of those possibilities myself.”

“You know the facts. You are just refusing to accept what they might mean.”

Amon grinned.

“For once, believe that you might actually be ready. Trust yourself a little.”

He tapped his chest.

“How do you think I became this good at magic? I trust myself. I trust my talents. You should try it sometime. Feels great.”

He chuckled and pulled a pipe from inside his coat.

While he began adjusting it and stuffing some dried plant into the bowl, I found myself thinking about his words.

Maybe he was right.

Maybe I had spent so much time preparing for everything Noah might do that I had convinced myself there was always something more.

But perhaps… Just perhaps… I really was ready.

As if the world itself had decided to answer that thought, something changed in the section of the Window I had dedicated to monitoring the forest.

A flare of divine energy. Dark as night.

Against the white blanket of snow, it was impossible to miss. Black, mistlike energy spilled into the mortal world as shadows stretched unnaturally across the frozen ground, thick and oily, swallowing the light around them.

Then came violet.

Thin veins of color tore through the darkness, spreading with every pulse. The glow became brighter and wider until violet completely consumed the black.

I stared at it.

He’s here.
