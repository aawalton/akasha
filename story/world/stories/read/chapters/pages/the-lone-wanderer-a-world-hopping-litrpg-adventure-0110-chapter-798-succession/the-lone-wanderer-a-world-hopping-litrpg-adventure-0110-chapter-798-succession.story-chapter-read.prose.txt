In what had essentially become tradition by now, the good news of Percy’s recent gains from his mind affinity was soon followed by some unpleasant developments, this time coming from the depths of his underground kingdom.

The Ruby Empress wasn’t doing very well.

Worse still, everyone understood that this was no common illness, as magical beasts didn’t randomly fall sick. Certainly not one as powerful as the White-cored ruler of the hive. Her sudden illness could only mean one thing – something that Percy had both expected and feared for years.

‘She looks just as weak as she did after forcing herself to produce a second heir,’ he diagnosed with a grimace. ‘It’s almost like she never recovered. Even the improvements from her promotions are all gone…’

Percy was standing next to his patient’s right foreleg – a limb that towered over him like an enormous ruby pillar – gently pressing his palm against her reinforced carapace so that they could communicate.

The wasp’s thorax hovered dozens of metres above his head, casting a shadow vast enough to envelop a small village upon the colourful minerals. Brilliant golden markings rippled across her crimson exoskeleton like waves, their glow largely obscuring that of the original star-like pattern that dotted her exterior.

Her soul didn’t look nearly as imposing as her body, however. The silver flame hiding beneath her chitinous shell had shrunk to half its previous size, flickering lethargically like a waning candle.

Percy considered granting her an artificial advancement to stretch her remaining time a little longer, though he knew that he would only be buying her a few months at most. Three times nothing was still nothing, and they had no idea whether the hive could produce enough nectar to sustain a quasi-divine beast.

Nephthys would have it better when she reached that point, because she’d have more time to elevate her second core, acquire new mutations or evolve those she already had, and reduce her stamina consumption. The hive’s population and nectar output would also hopefully be much greater by then. Either way, there wasn’t much that Percy could do for her mother.

He tossed her an apologetic look.

‘No need to worry about me, child,’ the Empress consoled him through the connection, without the slightest hint of fear in her voice. ‘We’ve known this day was coming since our very first meeting. If anything, I’ve only lasted this long thanks to your many gifts.’

‘I’m just sorry that I failed to fulfil my promise,’ Percy replied. The first Starry Princess was still held captive, and the culprit behind her abduction remained unpunished.

‘You will,’ the wasp replied with certainty. ‘You’ve already done things for my kind that none had thought possible. You’ve helped me produce a second heir – a reincarnated deity as well as the most dependable daughter I could have asked for – allowed me to become the first Ruby Empress in history without destroying my hive in the process, and redefined the relationship between our species. I do not doubt your ability to win this war and preserve my legacy, even if I’m no longer here to witness it.’

Percy sighed, patting her with his free hand. ‘I won’t let you down.’

The White-cored creature’s condition worsened over the next few months, and Percy could tell that she was only stubbornly holding on to the final embers of her depleted lifespan for his and Nephthys’ sake.

The loss of one of the alliance’s strongest combatants didn’t sting as much as it would have a year ago, because the war was rapidly transitioning into a phase where an individual’s power mattered less than the size of their army. However, the giant wasp’s metaphorical shoes as the ruler of the hive were a few sizes too large for the Ruby Princess to fill.

Percy’s familiar could match about a quarter of her mother’s jelly output, which was rather impressive with her first core being three grades lower. By the time she caught up, she would be able to generate several times more royal jelly than the Empress.

Unfortunately, the reduced production was somewhat problematic in the short term.

While Nephthys generated enough jelly to support Percy’s, Micky’s, and her own needs, Percy wouldn’t be able to brew enough Gloomy Dawn for Archibald and the leaders of the allied families without eating into his reserves – which would only last a few years.

Another issue was that Percy’s familiar wasn’t capable of laying eggs to repopulate the hive if necessary, nor could she control her subordinates properly. Her Charged Antennae and superior intellect helped her exert her authority upon the lesser wasps much better than what could be expected of a typical Green princess, but it wasn’t nearly enough to cover for her mother’s absence.

Thankfully, managing the other bugs was a lot easier within their natural habitat, as most of their actions were driven by instinct. Nephthys only had to interfere if she spotted an anomaly within the hive’s operations, or required her subjects to deviate from their usual behaviour.

To avoid any accidents, Percy asked Nesha and the enchanters of House Etna to temporarily pause the construction of the mana-gathering array, since his familiar might not be able to guarantee their safety once her mother passed away.

Unleashing the wasps upon their enemies would also be difficult until she tightened her control, as the dumber creatures were just as likely to attack Percy’s allies or devolve into complete chaos, even if Machaon’s side was kind enough to bring the fight to the Fungal Spire.

Percy’s grandfather advised him to keep the death of the Ruby Empress under wraps for the foreseeable future, to avoid giving the Great Houses the perfect opportunity to invade. Nesha and Paraphetes were already working on an excuse to explain to their subordinates why they had to take a break from the underground project, though everyone knew that this would only buy them a couple of years before the news leaked.

They’d have to make up for the princess’ shortcomings by then.

Outside of the practical considerations, Percy and Nephthys reserved a couple of hours every day to keep the dying bug company. Ironically, despite her enormous family and the hyper-organized eusocial structure of her species, the Ruby Empress had lived a somewhat solitary life.

She clearly hadn’t connected with her descendants at an emotional level beyond her capacity as their matriarch, and there were inevitable intellectual barriers between beasts of different grades due to the cognitive jump that followed each promotion.

Percy and Nephthys had been the first people to communicate with her as equals, though they didn’t know whether she valued or craved their interactions. Either way, they supposed that she would have asked them to leave her alone if she found their company unpleasant – which hadn’t happened.

Four months after falling sick, Remior’s first Ruby Empress finally passed away.

Her cores and body were fed to her daughter according to her wishes, which probably pushed Nephthys closer to her next promotion, though wasn’t quite enough to trigger it.

‘Maybe I should give you an artificial advancement, after all…’ Percy offered.

He’d been worried that her body would collapse under its own power if it evolved into a more demanding size and her seals stopped working, though his concerns might have been unfounded.

Having created and studied several Cradles by now, he was reasonably confident in their reliability. The only time his artificial advancements had nearly failed him had been during his battle against Machaon.

Nephthys should be safe so long as she kept her mana above the minimum threshold, and she wouldn’t need to risk her life or safety to participate in any battles directly.

‘I agree,’ the reincarnated goddess said. ‘It’ll solve the issue with my jelly output entirely and greatly improve my control over the other wasps.’

Percy nodded. Preparing the seals wouldn’t take more than a month, so they could do it whenever they wanted. In fact, they could just create and activate the Cradle without compressing her mana, leaving only the final step that could be completed in under an hour if necessary.

The only other concern was what would happen to her mutations.

Evolutions were excellent opportunities for beasts to develop new mutations or evolve the ones they already had. Nephthys had already awakened a mutation for each element within her second core, so Percy wasn’t sure her advancement would be enough to trigger a change.

She should be quite close, since she already had eight of them – excluding the Saint’s Eye. Percy had never met a creature with so many mutations, but he would rather give them an additional nudge before her evolution to increase their odds of merging into something better.

He didn’t want to miss this chance, because he’d already decided that the hive was the key to winning this war, and his opportunities to fundamentally enhance Nephthys’ abilities were limited.

Percy’s upgraded physique had also helped him whittle the minimum threshold required by his Cradles down to almost nothing. Reaching Green with his mind affinity would probably be enough to relieve him of that burden entirely. Nephthys might also have an easier time maintaining her higher grade with a stronger body.

‘I’ll need to figure this out sooner rather than later...’
