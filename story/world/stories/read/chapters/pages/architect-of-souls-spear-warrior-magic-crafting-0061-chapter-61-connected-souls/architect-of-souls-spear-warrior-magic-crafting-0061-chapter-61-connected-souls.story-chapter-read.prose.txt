Ferris was over the moon. Jade was remembering everything.

Mostly, the slime was remembering all the meals they had missed. At least, that was how Jade saw it.

He kept checking Jade’s memory, careful not to test the same information twice. Each time, the slime was able to easily recall everything.

Ferris worked all three days at refining spirit gems, although he didn’t let Jade help him. If the slime ate any of the black rocks or fought monsters, the ants would die in the Consume Pool, so Jade had to stay up on the wall while Ferris refined the gems.

Although work was harder, he did have an easier time each night, as he didn’t have to harvest qi from the Digest River. The qi from his Air Passage was paltry in comparison to what he had been collecting, but it was still something.

Practicing with the legacy imprint spear hadn’t shown any noticeable improvements, but he stubbornly kept at it.

Each night, Ferris watered the ability seeds in their pots, as well as dumping twice his normal amount of qi on the Core Tree’s stump. Jade still became sad every time they saw it, although it made Ferris happy to see that the slime always remembered it was damaged.

Growing a new Core Tree was both simple and hard at the same time: it just required patience. Qi could speed it up dramatically. Even without it, a tree would always grow out of the core crystals in the bonded soul.

When a typical new Adept bonded a weapon or tool forged with a core, they would usually have to wait for a new Core Tree to sprout. Ferris used to have a head start due to familiar bonds, inheriting the already living tree from the living monster’s soul. He wasn’t sure why the Core Tree couldn’t start growing in forged weapons before being bonded.

A new Core Tree usually took about a month from the time it sprouted to producing its first fruit. A tree capable of supporting three fruits at once, like Jade’s had must have been several years old. That felt slow to most new cultivators, although it was fast in comparison to mundane trees. Ferris hoped the regrowth would happen even faster—his stump had a root structure, and he was providing more qi each day than a typical newly bonded could afford.

They checked on the oath statue a few times, but Jade couldn’t help Ferris fight the demons or cut back the white chains. The oath statue, still hanging from a wooden frame in the middle of the chain fort, still hadn’t healed the cracks in its stone. At this point, he doubted it would. There must be something he could do. He was considering giving it some qi to see if that would help repair it, but he wasn’t sure how. He wanted to get it back to his base first, then experiment.

It stubbornly grew the chains lower each day—until Ferris cut them back with his axe.

On the last night before meeting with Serena, Ferris decided to finally move the statue. He was trying to be careful not to experiment with anything that could kill his mental body, but he still wanted to do something.

The plan was to test Uncle Winton’s technique to move large things easily. It had been almost two weeks since Ferris had seen his mentor, and he hoped the man would be with the group of delvers arriving from home tomorrow. Uncle Winton had said he would volunteer, but there were no guarantees. If he did show up, Ferris hoped to have at least tested the technique. Eventually, he wanted to move the entire fort, but had to start with just getting the statue back to his Heartfire.

First, Ferris planned to use qi forming to create a rope long enough to wrap around the entire frame holding the statue. He could lower the statue down from the frame first, but he kind of liked that there was something holding it up off the ground. It made it harder for the oath to dig its chains into him. If possible, he wanted the frame moved over with it.

He considered using demon corpses to strengthen the qi rope, but it didn’t need to be physically strong. He only needed it to have a strong mental connection. It needed to let him consider a large, complex collection of objects as one. For that, he decided to stick with pure qi.

Reaching his hands into the Qi Pool, Ferris imagined what he wanted: a long rope that could enhance his connection to everything it contained. When he pulled it out, a lot more qi disappeared than he’d expected. It was hard to know precisely, but about an entire day’s worth of qi collection from the Digest River had just vanished. If it worked, it would be worth it, but the cost made Ferris pale. How much qi would it take to make a rope large enough to wrap around the entire fort?

Rope tied around his waist, crossbow and axe strapped to his back, spear in one hand and the dark green core taken from the pedestal in the other, Ferris set out to the chain fort. He picked up Jade and ran away from any demons he saw along the way, except for one group that caught up with him.

When running didn’t work, Ferris had to drop Jade and the core to turn and fight, barely getting his spear up in time. The group had three jumping frog demons, which were a challenge to fight alone.

Jade’s memory was doing well, so the slime knew to stay back—although they were unhappy about it.

Sometimes Ferris thought Jade had a suicidal disregard for danger, perhaps from having a body that heals from all injuries—and no way to remember the experience. The slime was like a child that had never encountered anything that could hurt it.

Ferris’ skill with the spear had evolved through practicing with the bone spear, somehow solidifying years of training. It flowed in his hand better than anything before. He hadn’t seen any results from the legacy imprint spear, but using his own had definitely improved his forms.

Swinging the spear, Ferris was able to knock a jumping frog off to the side. A surreal grace moved through him as he positioned the spear to meet the next one—skewering it—while also stepping into just the right position to lean back and dodge the last frog. Before, a coordinated attack from two at once like this would have ended with Ferris bleeding, but now he was barely able to handle it, killing one while avoiding the other. Thankfully, the first frog had gotten slightly ahead while chasing him, letting him knock it aside before the other two arrived.

It was times like this that he could almost forget the lingering questions he had about continuing to practice Bluereef’s spear style. Even without Water Jet, his years of training and hard work mattered.

From there, Ferris finished off the two remaining demons and collected the bodies for later. It gave him even more to carry, but he was able to tie them onto his belt. Jade barely even complained anymore about not getting to eat them, having gone through three days of this. ‘Barely complained’ does not, however, mean ‘didn’t complain.’

Once they reached the chain fort, Ferris quickly set up to transport the frame holding the oath statue. Ferris chopped the white chains back first, then laid the rope in a circle around the frame. He threw the axe and the three demon frog corpses into the middle, hoping not to have to carry them back himself.

Ferris knelt down next to the rope, then called Jade over to climb up on his shoulder. He laid the dark green core on the ground, touching it with one hand, and then grabbed the rope with his other.

Closing his eyes, he pictured the area inside the rope circle as a single item that he was holding. The wooden frame, the oath statue, and even the axe and corpses sitting next to them. The frame had a couple of lengths of wood sticking into the ground. After a moment of consideration, Ferris tried to include them as well—but not the ground itself. He imagined appearing in the slimespace along with everything he was ‘holding.’

Ferris felt the transition happen with a pulse. All it usually took was him spending a few moments imagining moving to the other side, but this time had more weight to it.

Opening his eyes, Ferris was happy to see he was in the slimespace, along with everything he’d tried to bring with him. The oath statue, frame, axe, and demon frogs were all present, sitting inside the qi rope he still held. Jade hopped off his shoulder and started rolling around, excitement mirroring his own through the bond.

Ferris looked at where the wooden frame was dug into the ground. It looked stable, as if it had always been here rather than just appearing. He shook it a little, but the frame didn’t budge.

Stepping back from the rope, Ferris called Jade back into his arms and touched the core pedestal. With a thought, they left the slimespace and appeared back inside the chain fort, dark green core at his feet.

There were holes in the ground where the frame had been, matching where it had extended down. Other than that, there was no sign that the frame had even been here.

After picking up the core, Ferris and Jade left the fort. Traveling was much nicer this time, without having to carry the rope and the axe—much less the three frog demon corpses. They were able to avoid any more fights and soon were back at the Heartfire.

Ferris looked around his base as he considered where he wanted the oath statue. It would be a temporary home, but he wanted it as protected as possible—while not getting in the way or close enough to sink white chains into anything he cared about.

In the end, he placed it back where the statue had appeared. Ferris carried the core over next to where he wanted the frame, then moved over to the slimespace.

Thankfully, nothing on the other side had changed. Ferris hadn’t been sure if the grey ants—or something else—would steal the axe or corpses. He felt the statue was big and heavy enough to be safe, while the other items could be replaced. If something was stolen, then he would at least have learned his things weren’t safe over here.

Ferris knelt, touching both the core and the qi rope. After closing his eyes and focusing on the same collective intent as before, Ferris pictured everything appearing in his soulspace exactly positioned as he wanted. He even tried changing the facing so that the statue’s front faced a different direction relative to him than it had in the slimespace.

After feeling the transition, he opened his eyes again. Everything was exactly how he pictured it. The frame, statue, axe, and corpses were all magically back at his base. The statue was positioned just how he wanted it, and the frame was fully anchored in the dirt.

Ferris returned the core to its pedestal, then briefly checked the slimespace. The dirt on the other side had matching holes from the frame’s wooden beams dug into the ground.

He wondered what exactly had happened when he’d transported the beams into the ground. Had the dirt been compacted and pushed to the side, or did it stop existing entirely? He knew it didn’t trade places, as the area inside the chain fort had matching holes in the ground. Ferris would need to experiment with this; maybe there was a technique he could develop to abuse it.

After he returned to his soulspace, Ferris took a closer look at the oath statue’s cracks, then sighed. I’ll try using qi first, he thought. It’s too important to risk leaving damaged.

Ferris grabbed the bucket he used to water the Core Tree and filled it with liquid qi from his pool. He carefully climbed up the side of the frame until he was on top, above the statue. As gently as possible, he poured the liquid qi over the top of the statue. He hoped it would repair the damage, but giving it a try was the only way to know.

The statue appeared to soak the qi in as it flowed down, and nothing dripped out the bottom. Ferris couldn’t see any immediate change, and the damage didn’t suddenly repair itself, but he figured he would give it more time.

With the oath statue now finally back at his base around the Heartfire, Ferris felt like a knot unclenched inside his stomach. He had been fairly sure it was safe at the chain fort, but seeing it returned gave a sense of cathartic closure.

He finished his usual nightly chores, then went to bed feeling a bit lighter. There were many problems still to face, but at least starting to get things under control.

He was figuring out Jade’s memory. He’d recovered the oath statue.

And tomorrow, if all things went well, he would get to see his uncle again.
