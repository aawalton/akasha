After having signed the contract and officially become a retainer of the clan, the rest of the evening was a bit of a blur. Elder Harper had left Hemon in charge of getting him settled in, scheduling time to meet the next day for the research project.

He had walked with Uncle Winton to the edge of the Hollowfield compound to say goodbye. His uncle had removed Ferris’ travel bag from the man’s soul storage, then left with a promise to check on him the next time he was in Scalefort, probably two weeks from now.

Hemon gave Ferris a brief tour of the fourth branch’s territory within the larger Hollowfield compound. The first branch’s district was located at the center, while the other six each claimed an area surrounding it.

Most prominent in the fourth branch’s section was the library. It was a massive, several story building. It was surrounded by various research buildings, training halls, administrative offices, shops, and housing. A large bath house resided near the housing, along with small food stands and shops. Everything except the library catered to the mundane; apparently cultivator business was done through the clan store in the first district.

Hemon led him to a guard station, where they issued Ferris an enchanted token keyed to his identity. He could use it to gain access to the compound and enter Scalefort without paying the fee. The token would also be required to verify completion of jobs and redeem anything with merit points.

The guards subjected Ferris to a lecture on safety. Practicing dangerous abilities was strictly limited to designated training facilities, located in every district. They were open all day and night to accommodate cultivators' strange schedules. Ferris didn’t know any dangerous abilities yet, but he was excited to learn them.

Ferris learned a little more about Hemon—the man opened up a bit after Ferris made his oath. He was born and raised in the fourth district. His grandfather was a second tiered delver that had died in a rift, and Hemon was very proud of the man. Although he’d long ago given up becoming a cultivator, Hemon boasted about his role assisting Elder Harper.

As it got later, Hemon took him to his boarding. It was a quaint two-story building nestled between identical houses on each side. ‘4B’ was written in large letters above the door, and the windows each hosted a box with flowers in full bloom. Hemon knocked on the door, straightening his back.

A large, boisterous lady with strikingly red hair and an apron opened the door with a smile.

“Hemon, lovely to see you,” she said, pulling the man into a large hug. Ferris could almost hear Hemon’s bones crack as the hug took the man off his feet. After releasing him, her gaze locked onto Ferris. “And who is this? Another child for my home?”

Ferris tried not to frown. He might not be a man yet, but he was an Adept. She really shouldn’t be calling him a child, no matter his age. Maybe she just doesn’t realize? He thought, without much hope. At least once he started delving, he’d be considered a full adult.

“Yes,” Hemon answered, attempting to regain his posture. “I am pleased to introduce Adept Ferris Bluereef. He has signed a retainer contract with Elder Harper, and will be in your care.”

Her large smile somehow grew, and she wasted no time capturing Ferris into his own hug. He doubted he could have gotten away from her even if he had two legs. “Welcome to the Lily House, call me Mother Lily. I take care of everything so that you cultivators can focus.”

Ferris was barely able to hold on to his crutch while trying to survive the hug. “Thank you, I’ll be in your care,” he weakly responded. Looking back at the flowers in the window boxes, he noticed that they were all lilies. I sense a theme, he thought, seeing that the other houses on the street all seemed to have their own type of flower. ‘4B’ may be written above the door, but he doubted many people used it as a name. ‘Mother Lily’ probably had a different name before getting this job at ‘Lily House.’

Setting him down, but not letting him go, she seemed to really look at Ferris for the first time.

“Oh you poor dear, what has happened to your leg?” She exclaimed, face finally losing the smile for a look of concern, gripping him even tighter.

Seeing him at a loss for words, Hemon came to his rescue. “Ferris will be assisting Elder Harper in researching tier one healing abilities that may regrow limbs.”

“That is all well and good, but it doesn’t help him now,” turning her head to Hemon, giving him a disapproving look. “Just imagining the pain he must have gone through brings me to tears.” The tears, however, were nowhere to be seen as she glared at the man.

Hemon had come to his rescue, it was only fair Ferris return the favor. “Hemon has been very helpful, showing me around,” Ferris interjected.

Unfortunately, his good intentions backfired. “Forcing the poor child to carry his own bag all over town,” she scolded, finally releasing Ferris only to steal the travel bag from his back. He regretted losing it, but the look she directed at Hemon kept him from trying to take it back. Sorry Hemon, I tried, but you’re on your own now. It will probably be fine…

“Now let’s get you settled,” she ushered him inside, practically lifting him with a supporting arm, and closed the door on a flustered Hemon. Ferris was sad to see him go, leaving him alone with the woman. He hadn’t even had a chance to properly say goodbye. At least he would see Hemon again while working with Elder Harper.

“Mrs. Lily,” Ferris began, only to be cut off with a sharp look.

“It is Mother Lily, dear.” Her tone stated she would accept nothing else. It felt odd to call someone other than his mother ‘mother’, but she would be taking care of him for the foreseeable future. He didn’t want to start off by insulting her title, strange as it was.

“Mother Lily,” he conceded, “please, I prefer to walk on my own.” Gently, he removed his arm from her supportive grip. Ferris wasn’t sure what to make of the look she gave him, but she allowed him to extract himself.

“Yes dear,” was all she said, hefting his bag like it was nothing. “You can have room three up here on the right. Of course, it is on the ground floor. I couldn’t bear the thought of you having to climb the stairs.”

Ferris bristled a bit at that. He could handle stairs just fine, thank you, but she had walked on ahead without looking back. He decided to not press his luck, content with at least being allowed to walk himself again.

Entering the room behind her, Ferris looked around. Although it was sparse and humble in size, it was private. There was a small bed, table, dresser, and wash bin. He was impressed how clean it was, for a vacant room. Oddly—at least to Ferris—the room had a latch to lock the door from the inside.

“Breakfast and supper are served every day in the main room, just come when I ring the bell. If you miss a meal, I will leave a plate for you in the box outside your door,” Mother Lily said, placing his bag next to the dresser. “If you are eating out, please flip the sign on the box to let me know. For lunch, just swing by the common room. There is always something available, or I can provide a sack meal in the morning. Place your laundry in the hamper at the end of the hall, and I will leave the clean clothes in the bin with your room number on it.”

Ferris gave an appreciative nod. Adepts really did get better treatment. Despite being a retainer, he was expected to spend his time on cultivation rather than mundane tasks of everyday life. Even back home his daily chores had disappeared the moment he'd become a cultivator.

“The evening meal will be ready in a couple hours; I’ll leave you to get settled.” Mother Lily left.

It didn’t take long to unpack his meager belongings: a couple changes of clothes, some personal items, and a portrait of his family. The portrait was especially dear to him, as it had been drawn with impossibly fine details, capturing a moment in time. It had been created with a magical ability of one of the Adepts back home; a present from his uncle before he left.

Finally alone, Ferris was excited to meditate and process all the changes that had happened that day. This was the real reason no cultivator would be expected to share a room—and likely for the lock on the door. Cultivation required many hours of quiet meditation, and any disruption would be dangerous. Even his real mother never casually entered his room uninvited, at least after he became a cultivator.

Ferris laid down on the bed and tried to get comfortable. More than how his mother treated him, everything had changed that day. The day he unlocked his soul.

He closed his eyes, doing his best to focus on meditation. Instead, memories intruded like unwelcome guests.

Four months earlier…

“FINE,” Ferris yelled, “I’ll just sit here and rot.” He slammed the door in his mother’s face, blinking back tears.

A stranger might have called her expression composed, but he knew the difference. She was hurt.

Of course, it wasn’t because he’d lost his temper again. Or, from the words he was already starting to regret. She was hurting for him, and somehow that made it all worse.

How long had it been since he’d lost his leg. A month? He wasn’t sure. It felt like an eternity. Like he was living a completely different life.

The healer had taken away the pain before he’d even woken up. All that was left was the gaping emptiness where it should be.

How could something this big happen, and it hadn’t even hurt?

Well, he blinked back tears, it hurts now. He stared down at where his leg should be. And itches.

He reached out to try to scratch, but nothing really helped. As usual.

Ferris looked around the room, trying to find something to kick. Not that I can, he thought, then shoved his chair over with his crutch for good measure.

A few frustrated minutes of awkwardly pacing around the room later, he gave up. Flopping down on the bed, and glowered at the ceiling. There wasn’t anything to do in here, but it wasn’t like he could go back out now.

No, he thought, remembering the look on his mother’s face. I’m here for the night.

His stomach rumbled, protesting. Why couldn’t this have happened after dinner?

Sighing, Ferris closed his eyes and pretended to meditate. Maybe it would help distract him from the hunger. He hadn’t bothered much with meditation since losing his leg. Mostly a token effort to support his claim to still be trying. Everyone said you needed three things to unlock the soul and become a cultivator: intense physical training, breathing exercises, and meditation to achieve ‘mental harmony.’

With only one leg there was no way he could perform the complex spear routines he’d practiced for half his life. He could do simpler exercises, but everyone claimed that wasn’t as good. The only thing that stopped him from doing the breathing routine was motivation. As for mental harmony… yeah, no.

It used to be, he’d meditate for hours every night. No matter what anyone said about the odds of actually unlocking the soul, he’d always believed he would make it. It was like he assumed they were talking about someone else. Not him.

Well, now there wasn’t any question. Before, the advice had been ‘have a backup plan.’ Now it was ‘let’s talk about your future.’ As if it was already decided.

But isn’t it? His own mind betrayed him. Isn’t it decided? You’ll never be a delver now.

Tears came. Slow at first, then growing into an endless stream of racking sobs that shook his body. An unpleasant amount of snot clogged his nose and refused to be wiped away.

Without anything else, where there was nothing that could be done, Ferris fell back into meditation. He’d been instructed in the mental exercises since before he had memories, drilled on them far passed memorization.

Focus on the breathing, he thought, trying not to think. Ignore the way your body quivers. Ignore the tears. Slowly in, slowly out. Repeat. Let each thought happen. Let each thought drift away.

Slowly, it worked. The familiarity helped. Where before his emotions were a raging river, eventually he felt submerged at the bottom of a calm pond.

He didn’t know how much time passed, nor did he care. There was a stillness in the world that captivated his mind.

Yet, within that emptiness, when all else had faded away, Ferris found he wasn’t alone. There was a… presence. It was familiar, like feeling his own heartbeat. Something that had been with him all his life, but beneath his perception.

Focusing on the presence, it grew to encompass his entire world. It swallowed him up, and when he next awoke…

He had unlocked the path to his soul.
