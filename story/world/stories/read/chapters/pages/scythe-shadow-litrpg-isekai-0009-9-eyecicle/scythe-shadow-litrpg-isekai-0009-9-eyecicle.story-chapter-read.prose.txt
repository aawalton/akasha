A god.

A god spoke to me.

I couldn't believe it, yet, at the same time, it made complete sense. I wasn't exactly a pious individual, but I could tell this being exuded an air of regal authority. He seemed to have some sort of plan—a divine plan, it turned out. No. Him being a god wasn't shocking. It was that he wanted me to come find him. That he had some specific purpose… for me.

Why? Why me?

Jericho had turned back to his Bloody Mary, seeming to interpret this revelation as nothing more than an interesting tidbit. Then again, he didn't seem to take much seriously anyway. But maybe he could still help me find him.

"Jeri—" I started, but was immediately interrupted.

“Jericho, you’re back!” someone called from nearby. A girl about my age stood from a table behind us and waved.

Though there appeared to be a wide range in ages of Reapers here, I wasn’t sure I could trust any of their appearances to be accurate. I looked nothing like my true self, after all. For all I knew, this girl could be a fifty-seven year-old man living in his mother’s basement spending his days reading nothing but ecchi manga.

Though my own age and sex did appear accurate, so that was reassuring. I'd checked downstairs to be certain

The girl said farewell to the others she was with and sat on my opposite side, a beer mug in her hand. Nuzzle sniffed at her warily, but she slid a finger under his chin and rubbed his neck. The poor lizard practically swooned.

I considered continuing the conversation about the god despite this new visitor, but if my suspicions from Jericho's reaction were right, then not many Reapers were graced with the presence of a god during their arrival. I wasn't sure yet if this was something I wanted getting out. Jericho knowing was probably already a mistake. He'd be just as likely to blurt it out for all to hear as anyone.

So I pushed down the temptation and forced myself to be patient for more answers, instead turning to the newcomer.

Her blonde hair faded to pink tips and flared out at chin length just above the Amber cloak that rested over her shoulders. Beneath her cloak she wore a slate gray sports bra and ripped black jeans patched with a swatch of pink fabric that appeared to have rubber duckies on it. I looked to her face and met her eyes boring into me, one dark and Amber, the other—normal one—green with flecks of gold. The eyebrow above her dark eye rose high.

“Like what you see?” she asked.

I blushed even though I hadn't been checking her out like that. “As if you weren’t examining me at the same time.”

She leaned over the bar to look around me at Jericho. “Was I examining him?”

“Not in the slightest.” Jericho said, sipping the last of his Bloody Mary. “This is superb. Why have I never ordered this before?”

“Whose side are you on?” I asked.

“Mine,” the girl responded for him. “Jericho was my welcome committee long before you. I get preferential treatment.” She winked. “Isn’t he the best?”

The best? She had to be joking.

“Something like that…” I finally tasted my own drink. Alcohol to the rescue. It really was quite good.

“So, you got a name?”

“Not yet,” I grumbled, already regretting enabling this conversation.

The girl clapped her hands. “Ohhh, can I name him?” she asked Jericho, practically jumping up and down.

Jericho shrugged as he waved down Judith for a second drink.

“Umm, how about not? I'm perfectly capable of naming myself, thank you.”

“I’ve always liked flowery names.” She tapped a finger on her chin, ignoring me. “Tulip, perhaps?”

“Absolutely not.”

“Azalea?”

“He does look like an Azalea,” Jericho chimed in.

“I agree,” the girl said, folding her arms over her chest.

Nuzzle hopped up and down, licking my cheek repeatedly.

“Even your friend likes it.”

I squished the salamander down onto my shoulder. “I don’t. And neither does Nuzzle. He just gets overexcited.”

“Okay, okay.” She poked one of the large red flowers on the tree trunk beside her. “I’ve been saving this one, but you can have it. Petunia.”

“You’re really starting to annoy me. Who even are you?”

She took a sip of her beer and ignored the question. “Alright, but if you can’t think of anything, just remember I’ve got about twenty-three more we haven’t even discussed yet.”

“I think I’ll pass.”

“Your loss.” She shrugged, then peered around me again. “So, Jer, what’s the assessment so far? Is he metal or silk?”

"Silk!" Judith shouted from the other end of the bar.

How the hell does she hear everything?

Jericho chewed on his celery stalk thoughtfully. “Honestly could go either way,” he said.

“I know what you mean. The way his eye twitches when he’s annoyed… Sooo metal. But then his hands—” She grabbed my left hand and rubbed it against her cheek. “Soft as a baby’s bottom, as they say. Very silk.”

I pulled my hand away. “My eye doesn’t twitch.”

“It literally just did as you said that.”

“I highly doubt that.”

“Jericho saw it.”

"I did," Jericho chimed in.

“He was facing the complete opposite direction!”

“Your lizard saw it.”

“No he—” Nuzzle sat on my shoulder wagging his tail, his tongue hanging out like a dog, left eye twitching sporadically. I couldn’t tell if he was actually mimicking me, or if he was somehow intelligent enough to involve himself in the joke. “God, I hate you all.”

The girl took my arm in hers. “You’ll learn to love us.”

I looked up. “What the hell did I do to deserve this torture in the afterlife?”

I hadn’t noticed before, but the ceiling far above had a mural painted on it depicting a vast island, maybe even continent-sized, though it was hard to interpret any sort of scale. Six concentric rings of different terrain circled a single tall, skinny mountain. The Central Spire? Is that what Zeus had been talking about? From the mountain outward to the surrounding ocean, the rings appeared to be volcanic, arctic, desert, canyon, jungle, and meadow. A few of those same terrain types were themes in this very tavern.

“Wait. Did you say afterlife?” the girl asked. I still had no clue what her name was. She laughed. “Jericho, didn’t you tell him?”

Jericho slurped the last of his second drink through the straw. “Something about dreams and death… I’m sure I mentioned it at some point.”

“Tell me what? All you said was I was chosen to become aware of the Umbral Plane.”

They both ignored me.

“Can I show him?” the girl asked.

“I’m not sure that’s a great idea. He hasn’t even—”

“It’ll be fine!” She raised her left arm to reveal a pointed icicle forming over her hand, growing ever longer by the second.

Jericho sighed.

“What the fuck is going on here?” I asked, leaning away from the girl, who now had her glacial spike pointed directly at my face.

“By the way, there's a hole in your shirt,” the girl said with a wicked grin.

"I kno—"

She thrust her spike through my eye. Oddly, I felt the coldness of it more than the pain, seeping into my skin and blood vessels. Then pressure exploded in my head as it went into my brain and out the back of my skull.

The dull realization that I had literally just been murdered flickered through my mind. The red Health bar atop my vision tanked to zero.

The room spun around me, a vortex being sucked into oblivion. Or maybe I was spinning? My cloak expanded in a hug and formed a tight ball around me, compressing me into some sort of unnatural event horizon.

Then I was gone.
