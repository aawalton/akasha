Chapter 194 / B3-39: New Duds

Joe had always been the stereotypical guy when it came to clothes and his buying practices for them. His wardrobe on Earth consisted of a couple of pairs of jeans, some shorts, a handful of interesting t-shirts, plus one nice outfit for special occasions. When he needed something new, he’d hop online or wander into a store, grab something, and he was done. Very little, if any, thought went into clothing choices.

So it was understandable that inside the Heighted Haberdashery, Joe was feeling a bit of trepidation. He was certain he was going to be self-conscious about modeling the outfits. Yet he had to admit, Kenda and the outfitter, a bucca named Toma Bilberry, were making this fun. With so much variation in the accepted styles, he found he was intrigued by what each final look would end up being. He’d stand in front of the full-length mirrors and found himself looking at a whole new person, both due to the clothes and his new physique.

Joe was now far more built out than he had ever been in his whole life. Even before the cancer set in, he was at best wiry; skinny was the more accurate adjective. When Hawking had brought him over to Illuminaria, Joe had been given a bit more muscle, though not too much more than he had known on Earth.

Now, he’d fit right in with the rugby players he had been friends with in college. His chest, arms, and legs were noticeably muscular. He had actual abs, which were highlighted by some of the outfits that left his chest partially bare.

Since Fort Coral was in a semi-tropical locale, as well as being a city that loved to celebrate, the clothing, even the formal stuff, was made from lightweight materials and focused more on aesthetically pleasing lines than on covering skin. Much of what Kenda and Toma had him try on consisted of long vests, with or without lightweight shirts, and baggy or slitted pants. Gauze, mesh, and silk were common materials.

Fort Coral's formal wear was heavily influenced by its neighbors. To the west was the great forest of the Kingswald, containing the elven court of Hart’s Hold. South of the city lay the vast Voiceless Dunes. To the north lurked the pirate city of Defiance. Each had its impact on the designs Toma brought out. Elven apparel was elegant, tight-fitting, with graceful lines and rich colors, mostly greens, gold, browns, and purples. Dunian formal wear was loose and flowing, hued in more muted shades. Rakish accents were added to both styles, influenced by the northern searaiders.

For his meeting with Dorian, they decided to lean into more of the elven look, though he stuck with his customary blue. Over a white shirt, he wore a sapphire, tight-sleeveless coat that flared at the waist and flowed down to reach his ankles. The pants were tight-fitting yet surprisingly comfortable. Braided wraps covered his shaggy forearms and the back of his hands. The coolest part about these garments was that they were enchanted to wrap themselves. Joe just had to hold the ends and utter the command word, and the light blue, dark blue, and white strips would weave themselves down to his elbow. He had another set of dark blue, gray, and black wraps for his feet. These were connected to a leather sole and performed the same function up to his knee. Joe still had talons tipping his fingers and toes, but so too did other races, such as the saurians.

For the ball, a more dunian outfit was selected; Kenda wanted to make sure he was in something he could dance in. A short, near-black vest with exquisite gold needlework covered a loose shirt with baggy sleeves. Another magically braided item, a woven sash, covered his waist, leading to flowing trousers and a pair of moccasin-like shoes. This outfit also featured blues, but lighter and softer ones than the meeting suit.

They were not cheap, but Joe had seven thousand gold headed his way when he turned in the quest for bringing Margen home, which he planned to do as soon as he was done shopping with Kenda. He wanted to turn in [Bring Him Home] with Myllo so he could discuss the other aspect of the quest, the epic item, which he had been thinking about through most of last night.

Since he and the korrigan guild leader were meeting up at the guildhouse to head over to Dorain’s office together, he would soon be loaded with gold. Joe emptied out most of his current purse to pay for the two outfits, paying extra for enchantments to keep them clean, repaired, and hanging correctly. The last thing Joe wanted was to meet the richest man in the city or the upper crust of Fort Coral with a soup stain on his shirt or a tear in his trousers.

Placing the evening outfit into his dimbag, Joe and Kenda wandered back towards the middle of the city, having fun chatting about nothing and window shopping. Kenda also wanted to get a new outfit, but had waited to see what Joe had picked so she could find something to match it. Her pick was stunning; the deep green dress complemented both Joe’s blues and her auburn hair while accentuating her athletic build. Kenda was no soft flower and made no effort to try to become one. She had worked hard to be a physically fit adventurer. So while the dress made her look absolutely lovely, it was also cut in lines that would allow her to move freely.

Joe’s look of reverent awe won him a kiss.

After leaving the dress shop, they wandered past a bag store. Kenda stopped and turned them into the shop. Joe had no objections; one of the pieces of his gear he most wanted to upgrade was his dimbag. Even though he didn't have his reward yet in hand, it would be fun to see what his options were. He could come back and pick it up after he turned in the quest.

His dimensional bag was one of his most essential items. He could not imagine going back to life where everything he wanted was not right there on his hip. Even so, his dimbag was the most rudimentary version of a storage item. It had a relatively small dimensional capacity. It had no sorting ability to keep items separate in the bag. No security against thieving skills. No preservation magic to keep the items inside from spoiling or breaking. The opening of the bag determined the size of what could be placed inside it. If Joe wanted something, he had to reach into the pouch and push his focus onto the item to get it to come to his hand. Good storage gear summoned the desired target straight into your grasp with just a thought.

Spatial jewelry was more common than actual bags. Kenda had a bracelet she stored her gear in. Wen had a locket. Tezeno’s was a belt. Hah’roo’s a ring. So Joe moved away from the classic pouches to the walls, where smaller items were displayed.

As Joe looked around at the various pieces, his eyes fell on a weird leather cuff. It was designed to be buckled around a belt, but Joe imagined fastening it around the strap to his satchel. The medical bag still went everywhere with Joe. His magic healing alleviated much of the need for the simples in the pouch, yet because his healing was primal in nature, Joe often found the extra materials useful. Wrapping bandages around a recent healing cushioned the tender skin. Ointments helped take the sting out of his accelerated heals. He had cleansing liquids that worked more effectively than [Purge] for dirty injuries.

The idea of combining the two items hanging off his shoulders into one was very appealing. It would also play into Joe’s habit of looking for his items in the same place. The cuff had all the extra features that came with most mid-tier spatial items: durability, security, mending, item summoning, item preservation, large capacity, and non-material opening.

Even though it was one of the less expensive items of its quality level, it was still priced at fifty-three hundred gold, about three-quarters of the whole seven thousand reward. He had hoped the quest money would be able to cover more than just one purchase. Even so, Joe was still considering it. Everything important he owned resided inside his dimbag; anything bad happening to it would be devastating.

“What’s wrong?” Kenda asked, noting Joe’s scowl.

“I was thinking of upgrading my spatial storage, but the price …” he began, only to be cut off by the girl beside him.

“Oh, thank Prewdens. Why do you think I steered us in here? That old bag of yours is a disaster waiting to happen. We all start with one. My first spatial satchel looked just like yours, but it was this gods-awful red color. I was just a kid when I got it, and red was my favorite color back then. But as soon as we can afford it, everyone upgrades,” she stated.

“I wouldn’t worry too much about the price, Joe. You are about to have a stellar reputation with the guild. You and Yuk will be higher than many of the current guilders. That comes with perks. One, you can get advanced money for big purchases like this one. If you want, on my authority, we can buy your item now and have the money sent over from the guild. Another perk is guild discounts. Show me which one you like, and we’ll see what the actual price is.”

“I like that leather cuff there, “ Joe pointed.

“Huh? Not what I expected. Most folks go for an item they can wear directly, not one they put onto another piece of gear, but you can’t argue with that price. This,” she said, indicating her bracelet, “cost almost twice that. Let’s see how much we can get off?”

The guild lieutenant scooped up the leather item and brought it to the counter. Joe expected a round of haggling to ensue, but instead Kendell merely informed the storekeep that this was a purchase for the Adventurers Guild and Joe’s soon-to-be reputation of twenty-six hundred. This immediately shaved ten percent off the price, and Kendell promised that payment would be delivered this afternoon. Forty-seven-seventy was still the majority of his coming windfall. About two-thirds of it, yet knowing that this sort of purchase was pretty much inevitable made it far more palatable.

Joe clipped the cuff to his [Healer’s Satchel] and emptied everything straight out of his old dimbag into his new one. As he witnessed the weird assortment of leftover items that came out of his old bag, kind of like cleaning out a backpack at the end of the school year, he promised to do an item purging when he had a minute. His old pack sold for seven hundred and fifty gold, about a third of what he paid for it. Joe was a bit disappointed; he had hoped for half his original gold back. Since it was a very bottom-end spatial item, Joe was pretty sure he wouldn’t have gotten even that much without his Guild rep.

On the plus side, that seven-fifty was enough for Joe to be able to get the other items he wanted to pick up. He bought a new [Ring of Refurbishing] that had a hundred charges of cleansing or mending in it. He really wanted an item that would be rechargeable, but the price jump between charged and recharging was still more than he could easily afford after buying his new dimbag. The one-hundred charge ring wasn't cheap, but it was still far less expensive than a rechargeable item, and it worked out to be cheaper than buying individual charms of the spells.

He bought four charms of Flutterfall. Being able to dismiss falling damage was too important not to have. Joe planned to learn the ward for Flutterfall, but until then, he wanted to make sure he had a couple on hand in case he needed them.

The same logic held true for Aqualung. He also bought four of the water-breathing charms, knowing that it was another ward he planned to add to his repertoire soon.

He also grabbed a simple [Glowstone]. There was a ward call Brightmark, but that one was way down on Joe’s ‘To Learn’ list. With [Night Eyes], he didn't need light often enough to justify using up one of his known ward slots for it.  As for the [Glowstone], it was inexpensive and fit the 'better to have and not need than need and not have' category.

His last buy was a bit of an impulse one. He picked up two charms of [Obsidianskin], one for her and one he kept. These were far lesser versions of the potion Kenda had mentioned, but it felt nice to be able to give her something he knew she’d like.

Leaving the charm shop with just enough coins left to buy a snack for Mojo, they headed for the guildhouse to meet Myllo.
