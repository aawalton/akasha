“The second round begins now,” the voice called out. The remnants of their swords, the illusionary water, and the steam all disappeared at once at its declaration. Though they appeared to still be under the roof of a wooden hut in the sand. It was hard to believe how high the stakes were, not to mention the hex storm raging outside and the knights fighting back hordes of hex beasts to protect the fort. But he couldn’t focus on those things for now. He needed to focus on the game.

Things might not be going exactly how Merlin had hoped, but he’d still won the first round. He only needed to win three to win, according to the rules. The rules he’d made up on the spot. So far, at least, the voice seemed to be going along with them. Though things might be out of his hands from here, this was his game. There was no way he’d lose and let the others down on the first step of the tower’s trial.

“I’ll start again!” Obie declared before either Merlin or the referee could say otherwise. Obie held a hand toward Merlin. “Fire Shooty Sword!” A sword started to form in front of Obie, but it promptly came to a halt when Merlin interrupted.

Merlin raised a brow. “Do you mean shooting?”

Obie tilted his head. “What’s the difference?”

“Well, shooting would be the proper way to say it. I don’t think shooty is a word. Though it does sound funny.”

“I like shooty! I’m sticking with it!” Obie stuck his tongue out toward Merlin. It looked ridiculous coming from the face of an old man. As soon as Obie had decided, the sword continued to form, as if the trial’s voice had been waiting for clarification as well. The blade floated in front of Obie. Flames licked its sides and dripped onto the ground below. All around them, the landscape shifted. One moment they’d been on a vacation-worthy beach. The next Merlin found himself standing in an apocalyptic hellscape. The ground was covered in scars of magma, and the ceiling had transformed into eerie storm-cloud covered skies devoid of light or hope.

Obie cackled in delight at his floating sword of fire. That, plus the horrid scene around him, made him look like an evil villain instead of a hex knight.

Merlin swallowed. He’d feel better if he could secure a second win quickly to cement his advantage. He had a feeling Obie had chosen a sword of fire to mimic Merlin’s winning decision in round one. If Obie merely copied him, it would give him an advantage in future rounds. Fire was cool, but Merlin took inspiration from the storm raging outside. There was one weather phenomenon that could surely cinch his victory in this round.

“Blade of the unrelenting tornado!” Merlin shouted. His sword manifested as a storm cloud claymore, and like Obie’s, it floated rather than formed in Merlin’s hands. It seemed there was a theme this round. He certainly wouldn’t complain about flying swords. Especially after he’d let the last one slip completely out of his hands. He doubted he’d even been able to properly use such an enormous sword. It seemed more appropriate for a knight like Sir Redrick or Sir Gregory, large men that they were.

Obie tilted his head. “What’s that word mean? Unrel—unrelenty?”

“Unrelenting. It means…it doesn’t stop, no matter what.”

Obie’s eyes lit up. “I’m changing mine. I want a unrelenty tornado too!”

“You can’t change yours after hearing mine! You already chose! Besides, we can’t both use the same sword.”

“Ugh! Fine, but I’m using unrelenty next time!” With a frown on his face, Obie flicked his hand, and the flaming sword shot forward.

Well, I guess we’re starting.

Obie’s sword flew through the air like a flaming arrow. It continued to drip fire, coating the ground of the wasteland in dangerous, quick-spreading flames. But that wasn’t all. The air itself seemed to catch fire as well. What started as a fiery projectile very quickly turned into a roaring fireball of massive proportions.

Luckily, Merlin had a magic sword of his own. The claymore responded in kind. Powerful gusts of wind whipped through the air so fast Merlin could almost see the distortions they caused. Wind wrapped around his claymore. The blade cleaved downward, and a myriad of gusts all coalesced to take aim in the direction it pointed. Right towards the raging inferno rapidly approaching.

Even Merlin’s clothes sounded as if they were whipping against his skin at the rush of wind, and he found himself unable to resist. He looked down and saw an illusory copy of his outfit superimposed over his actual clothes. The playroom couldn’t physically affect him, apparently, but it did its best to convince him otherwise. That was quite interesting. The details of this place were…

Oh right, I should pay attention.

The wind had formed into a massive tornado that stretched so high into the air that he was certain the whole tower would rip apart if this were real.

Obie’s sword cut right through the tornado, aimed directly at Merlin. His claymore moved to intercept it, a second, sword-sized tornado forming around it. Obie’s blade met the claymore, and the sound of their struggle caused a metallic clang to ring out. With the threat temporarily halted, Merlin looked up at the larger tornado. It was now covered in flames, and for a moment he worried Obie’s fire might overpower it. Then again, this wasn’t just any tornado. It was unrelenting.

The violent tornado paused only momentarily as it transformed into a flaming whirlwind of immense power. Then it continued its path toward Obie.

Obie realized quite quickly that his flames weren’t having much of an effect. Now that monstrosity of dual elements was heading straight toward him. “Uh…that’s not good.” He took a step back with wide eyes.

When he realized all hope of winning this round was lost, and the tornado would inevitably reach him, Obie threw his hands up. “Okay okay! I surrender!” The tornado dissipated into nothing. As did the flames and their clashing swords.

It was imaginary, an illusion that wouldn’t actually harm him, but Obie was fully invested. Besides, Merlin had been on the other side of things in the last round. Illusion or not, it was hard not to flinch when a devastating act of nature was barreling straight toward you.

“The second round goes to the challenger.”

“That was a good attempt, Obie! You’re doing well!” Merlin encouraged him.

Obie crossed his arms. “I don’t know. I don’t like this game.” He pouted.

And that kind of reaction was exactly what Merlin was afraid of. If Obie refused to continue playing and insisted on a normal duel, it was all over. Merlin needed to hook him back in before that happened.

He’d originally planned to let Obie win a round or two, but that was when he thought he would be fully in control of the game. But at this point, he might still have to throw a round to ensure they could continue playing. He needed to boost Obie’s confidence. Quickly.

“Obie, I’ve never played this game with someone who’s adapted to the rules so quickly.” He’d never played this game at all. Merlin looked around, as if to make sure no one else was listening. “I’ll let you in on a secret.”

Obie perked up. “A secret?”

Merlin nodded. “Yup. Did you know…the thing about Seven Swords is that a large percentage of new players usually win the third round. That’s when it starts to click for most people. If you give up now, you might miss out on your true calling to be a Seven Swords Grandmaster!”

“Really? So you think…you think I could still win?”

“Of course you could. Seven Swords is anyone’s game! Come on, I’ll start this time. You focus on coming up with a really powerful sword for this round. Alright?”

Obie nodded. He squatted, and his eccentrically long beard looked even more ridiculous wrapping around him in that pose. The boy’s head bobbed as he sank deep into his thoughts, searching for a plan of action.

Merlin chuckled. Let’s see…I need something that’s sure to lose this round. He snapped as an idea came to mind. What was weaker and cuter than a kitten? “Blade of the pestering kitten. That’s what I’ll go with this round.” He threw in another descriptive word just to add some spice to it.

They both turned to watch as the sword formed in Merlin’s hand.

Merlin’s smile faded. Obie burst into laughter. He fell backward onto his back and rolled around on the ground, coiled up in his beard.

The blade of Merlin’s sword was hot pink. The guards above the handle extended out a few inches in either direction, as most swords did. Except this sword’s guard had a decorative cat ear sticking up on either side. To top it all off, the symbol of a cat’s pink paw print was inlaid on the blade’s pommel.

Merlin’s shoulder sank. “Oh, no…Orb warned me about this kind of thing. This…this sword was made for one of them.”

Obie collected himself. “One of them? Who?”

“A furry.” Merlin shuddered at the horrid thought. He was really glad the others weren’t here to see him wielding this. “Obie, why don’t you pick your sword so we can get this round over with? I’d really like to move on and forget this ever happened.”

“Okay!” Obie shot to his feet. He wore a devilish grin on his face. It looked as if he’d transformed from a little kid in an old man’s body to a crazed killer. He clapped his hands together and then slowly spread them apart. “Blade of the unrelenty Dragon!”

Merlin had to admit that was a cool choice. “Great, he get’s a dragon and I’m stuck with…this.” The pink sword glimmered for a moment, and then he heard an audible, very sassy, meow.

“Meowwwww.”

Merlin looked up at the ceiling. “Let the dragon kill me. It doesn’t need to be an illusion. Just end it. I’m ready to go.”

“The third round begins now.”

With a sigh, Merlin lazily moved his wrist and flicked his sword slightly. It was a kitten sword; it wasn’t like it would do anything cool.

A kitten appeared on Merlin’s shoulder. Merlin turned to look at in surprise. “Uh…”

“Meow.” This time the meow was grouchy. It sounded like a forty-year-old man who had lived a hard life was imitating a kitten. “Meow,” it said again. It was hot pink, the same color as the blade of his sword. The kitten eyed its paw, then gracefully leapt off Merlin’s shoulder and into the air. It dissipated into a pink cloud that slowly floated toward Obie. The cloud expanded as it moved forward. All around it, physical manifestations of claw marks cut through the cloud. It sounded as if claws were cutting through the air. The marks formed and disappeared, over and over, and Merlin got the distinct feeling that this pestering kitten sword might have a bit more in store than he’d initially expected.

Obie looked unfazed. He put the blade of his sword over his shoulder, took aim, and then brought it crashing downward in an arc before him. Where it cut, a portal opened in the air. A moment later, a wingless, serpentine dragon came surging out of it. Its green, scaled body moved through the air as if it were swimming. It noticed the pink cloud of kitten claw marks approaching it, but it didn’t alter course. It swam right through as if going around didn’t even cross the creature’s mind.

Merlin smirked. Little does that dragon know…that cloud is a cloud of kitten doom and despair. He shook his head. Okay, who am I kidding? This rounds over. He was only further hurting his own pride at this point.

The dragon’s eyes were firmly fixated on Merlin. At least, it was until the pink kitten manifested directly on the dragon’s head. The serpent-like dragon craned its neck and twisted itself, trying to see what was going on.

The kitten nonchalantly raised a paw to inspect it. Then it looked down at the dragon whose head it stood on and bared its fangs. Its claws shot out. “Reow!”

The dragon roared as the pink cloud converged further on it. Scales flew from its body as claw marks lacerated it all over. Each fresh cut and lost scale only served to further infuriate the dragon. All of that anger was focused squarely on Merlin. The dragon, much like his tornado last round, was unrelenting after all.

The closer the dragon got to Merlin, the more the kitten cloud redoubled its efforts. But finally, Merlin had enough. This round was over, and the dragon wasn’t stopping. “Alright, let’s call it here. I surren—”

“Reow!!!”

The cat reformed on the dragon’s head and looked up at Merlin as if he’d absolutely betrayed it. Then, it leapt directly at him with its claws bared. Suddenly Merlin was more afraid of the cat than the dragon.

“Reow!”

The cat’s battle-cry filled the room alongside the dragon’s roar. Both creatures now aimed at Merlin.

“I surrender!” He said a moment before they reached him. They disappeared. He placed a hand on his heart. “I’m too old for this. That was actually scary.”

“The third round goes to the Defender.”

Obie jumped over and over, pumping his fists into the air in delight. “I won! I won a round! This game is awesome!” He danced happily, and his beard swayed alongside his body.

Merlin allowed himself to laugh. Obie’s mood had certainly improved, and he had to admit his own had as well. In no small part due to the fact that the embarrassing sword was gone now. He would have to further consider Orb’s anti-furry alliance proposal, but that was a matter for another day. For now, he needed to wrap up this game.

“You did great, Obie! I told you, the third round usually goes well. Shall we jump straight into the next one? You don’t want to lose momentum after all,” Merlin said.

Obie nodded. “Okay! Sounds good! You go first!”

“Alright, let’s see…” He needed something strong, something that would unquestionably pull out the win. The playroom seemed to be receptive to words that modified the sword. Unrelenting had created especially persistent attacks. Pestering had seemingly made the kitten sword way stronger than Merlin imagined it would be. So, why not go for something super over the top. He smirked. “I’ll go with…the Shadeflame Blade of the dark abyss! Ultimate sword of darkness and power!”

Obie’s eyes lit up.

A large portal of shadows appeared below Merlin, and from that portal a sword of darkness covered in black flames emerged. As Merlin took hold of the sword, the entire room became pitch black except for two circles of light. One around him, and the other around Obie. Just enough so that they could see. Obie still hadn’t picked a sword, and if Merlin attacked now, he might be able to put some added pressure on him. “Get ready, Obie!” he called out. It was time to end this and send his friends to the next round. He gripped the sword in his hands and swung with all his might. A slash of pure darkness shot toward Obie.

Merlin expected to unnerve the boy.

Instead, Obie giggled. “Okay! I choose…the unbeatable blade!”

“What?” Merlin asked, taken aback by the choice.

One moment the room had been clad in darkness, the next, the sound of glass shattering reached his ears. The darkness crumpled before a force greater than itself.

“The fourth round goes to the Defender.”

Merlin shook his head. The playroom had accepted that? It let Obie pick an unbeatable sword? How the heck did that make any sense?

“The Challenger and Defender are now tied. The next round will decide the results.”

Merlin’s heart pounded in his chest. His mouth suddenly felt dry. He hadn’t expected things to go this way. He’d come up with a solution to avoid a duel he couldn’t win. Merlin had picked something that should’ve been easy for him. Now...now they were tied? He turned to look at the door through which Charlie and Vetica had walked. If he lost, they’d be trapped in there. The knights would capture all of them, and when Brandt arrived in a few days, it was over for them.

He shook his head. This wasn’t happening. No, it’s fine. He just needed to win the next round. He could do this.

“I’ll go first this time!” Obie said excitedly. He was loving the game now that he had a real shot at winning. “I choose…the unbeatable blade!” he declared.

Merlin’s jaw dropped. “What? You can’t choose the same sword twice!” he protested.

Obie tilted his head. “Why not? You didn’t say that before? I want to use this one again!”

“Obie, you can’t. That would be cheating,” Merlin insisted. The playroom had already allowed this tactic to win one round. He couldn’t afford to let Obie use a sword that was impossible to beat twice in a row. Not when this round decided the winner. He had a thought. “Wait, I’ll use it too then!”

“The established rules dictate that the same sword cannot be wielded by both players.”

Merlin hadn’t allowed Obie to copy his sword earlier, and now that rule was being used against him.

Obie frowned. “This is my favorite sword. If I can’t use it, this game is dumb and I don’t want to play.”

The boy in front of him might look like an old man, but deep down, he was still a child. That was evident more now than ever. If Merlin didn’t push back, Obie would win the round no matter what he chose. But if he insisted Obie choose something else, he might back out of the game entirely. What could he possibly do here?

Nothing. The answer was nothing. He’d lost. His friends had counted on him, and he’d messed it all up.

Maybe if Obie truly understood what was at stake, then he’d surrender. Charlie and Obie were friends after all. “Obie, if I lose, then—”

“You’ve got this. Don’t worry about it.” The words Charlie said to Merlin before he left the room with Vetica suddenly popped into his mind. They were counting on him. Sure, the situation felt impossible. But how many other impossible situations had they faced and come out on top of? A resistance in Sange, escaping the Hex Knights in Arcadeya, dealing with Vetica’s crazy family at the Rawlin house. If you’d asked him back then, he’d have considered any of those situations impossible. He wouldn’t lose now. Especially not in a game of his own creation.

He’d been backed into a corner before, and every time he’d done whatever he needed to do to survive. When someone like him was in a life or death situation, he did the only thing he could do to make it out alive.

He adapted.

When Merlin looked back up at Obie, it wasn’t with despair. It was with a grin.

“I choose…the sword of Adaptation.”

Obie frowned. “Adapa…what does that mean?”

Merlin smiled. A sword began to form in his hands. “It means…this sword learns from those around it. When it faces impossible odds, it finds a way forward. The entire time we’ve played this game, this sword has lurked in the distance. It studied all our techniques. It found ways to replicate and overcome them. If I’d used it at the start of the game, it wouldn’t have been very useful. But here, going last in the final round? That’s when a blade like this shines, Obie. For your sword to be unbeatable, there has to be a trick to it. Something that makes it so other blades always lose. Well, my adapting sword has already figured out. It’s the only blade in the world that could beat the unbeatable.”

“Nu huh!” Obie insisted.

“Begin!”

Obie swung his sword, and a massive beam of light clawed its way from the blade and toward Merlin.

Merlin returned his own blow, and this time, it felt as if the sword was pulling him. At first, he almost let go of it, but his hands seemed to grip tightly onto the handle in response. As if he were glued to his blade. It spun, forcing him to do the same. Merlin spun in several circles, and a tornado formed around him. Flames, and waves, and darkness surged from his blade and mixed into the powerful, violent wind around him. A dragon’s roar and a kitten’s meow filled the air. Merlin’s foot planted into the ground, and he spun one final time, swinging his blade downward and towards the beam of light that seemed to slow as it approached in response to Merlin’s display. “Sword of adaptation! Strike of the elemental tornado, Dragon’s breath and kitten’s claw!” Merlin added for good effect. After all, the playroom was just that, he realized. In a room of make-believe and imagination, what really mattered was a convincing performance and the belief to back it up.

And in that moment, Merlin truly believed he could win, while Obie doubted himself because of the confidence with which Merlin spoke and the confusion his words created.

The beam of light exploded, sent scattering by the whirlwind of elements that went shooting towards Obie.

Obie’s eyes lit up as the attack surged, and in the last moment, his expression turned from one of surprise to one of absolute delight. When the attack reached him, Obie threw himself backward and landed hard on the ground. He turned his head to the side and stuck out his tongue to play dead.

“The Challenger Wins!”

He’d done. He’d paved the way for Vetica and Charlie to make it to the second round.

They were one step closer to saving Mary.
