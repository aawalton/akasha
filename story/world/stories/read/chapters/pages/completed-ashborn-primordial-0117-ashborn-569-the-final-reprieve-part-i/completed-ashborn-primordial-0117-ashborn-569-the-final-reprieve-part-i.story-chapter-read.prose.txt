“Absurd, isn’t it? Vason said, echoing Tia’s own thoughts. “A single death. A single appearance of their god, and they all sing to a different tune.”

Tia watched the Saian and Ranian delegations leave the audience chamber she’d appropriated within Alt Janak’s upper city, unsure whether to overjoyed or furious at how easily and abruptly the political situation had flipped on its head.

“Less than a minute, from what I saw,” she scoffed.

“You don’t sound happy about any of this,” Haymi said. “Why? You have everything we could ever have wanted. The lords and kings of the realm dance at your fingertips. There is no higher authority among humankind than you now.”

“How could I be happy?” Tia asked. “For more than a decade, I’ve chased after that woman. Trying to measure up to her. To maybe even exceed her. Yet every time I thought I’d achieved just that, she goes and advances into another realm entirely.”

“Well, you needn’t worry about that any longer,” Vason replied. “You’re humanity’s Saint now. Their guiding star in the sky.”

“If only,” Tia said, staring darkly at the mahogany double doors of her office. Doors that had, until just weeks ago, belonged to Maiya.

“To have chased her for so long, only for the Akh Nara to do in less than a minute what I could not do for most of my adult life… has been one of the most galling experiences of my life. Now, I can never catch up. I’ll forever remain in her shadow.”

All the effort Tia had spent slogging, addressing and accommodating the Sawai lords’ every objection, losing sleep to broker compromises day after day and night after night, chipping away at the impossible mountain before her… had been blown away by the passing meteor that was called the Akh Nara.

Every Sawai lord that had only weeks ago bickered with each other, pushing their agendas relentlessly—who’d stymied Tia’s vision for a final battle that leveraged humanity’s every advantage—had suddenly turned into obedient bandies.

All cowed by the briefest appearance of the demons’ leader.

That, and the death of humanity’s Saint.

Where Tia would have sacrificed anything to have this sort of power only days ago, all she felt now was a roiling emptiness that left her sleepless, exhausted, and stressed beyond belief.

Beneath it all was the question that would go forever unanswered.

Why?

For Maiya, the shining star she’d looked up to despite their vast ideological differences—to simply perish like that…

How could she do that to us? To me…

Tia found she was gripping the exotic wooden arms of her armchair a bit too tightly and forced herself to clear her thoughts as she’d done countless times these past days.

“Ninety thousand strong,” Vason said from beside her, wisely choosing to shift the topic. “With these, we’re at ninety. Add in the civilians Imperatrix Ira Kin’jal has provided, and we’ll be close to the two hundred thousand you’d wished for.”

Tia grunted. “Civilians who can’t fire a combat orb and wield a single talent does not an army make. Nor even much of a militia, for that matter.”

“They have forty days of training in them,” Haymi said gently. “From the best instructors and commanders in the realm. To say nothing of how most of their number are Kin’jal citizens. As we know all too well, battle is in their very blood. Even if they’ve never held a weapon in their life, they take to combat like a fish to water. Our commanders have been astonished with their progress across the board.”

Tia pursed her lips. “Ironic, isn’t it? How the very trait that made them the bane of our country’s existence for centuries is the trait we’ll be most reliant on in the battle ahead?”

“Fate works in mysterious ways,” Haymi said, gazing off into the distance.

“Indeed it does,” Tia admitted with a loud sigh. “All of this is more than I could ever have dreamed of. And yet, whether they become an effective fighting force or not all depends on my choices of where to assign those instructors and generals.”

It was just one of the many duties that had her burning the midnight oil. A newly pressed militia could not be counted on for anything but the most basic of actions, and even then, only when morale was high and the risk of routing low.

Constraints a capable commander could work around, to be sure, but it was a delicate dance. While there was ample need for such footsoldiers, she only had so many commanders capable of directing them, and such talent was far better served in charge of her more elite corps.

Choose unwisely, and Tia would singlehandedly lose them the war. She’d no idea Maiya had been handling this many tasks in the background all this time.

She sat back and stared up at the ceiling, clasping her hands behind her back.

It wasn’t all bad, however. Numbers had always been humankind’s greatest strength against the demons. In an open field starved of prana that denied any large working of magic?

With such advantages, Tia could actually see them winning. A thought that scared as much as it excited her.

“Now all we need is a way to deal with the Akh Nara,” Vason muttered. “As terrifying as those Samsara are, he’s on another level altogether from what the reports say. If he’s truly back…”

“Leave him to me,” Tia said darkly.

“Come now, not this again,” Haymi said. “Thank Janak he refused your idiotic challenge. Our own Saint was no match for him, what makes you think you can do any better? You’ll be throwing away your life for nothing.”

“It is reassuring to know just how much faith you place in my abilities,” Tia muttered.

“Come now, you know that’s not what I mean,” Haymi replied.

“Maiya fell not because she was weak,” Tia said before the mejai could say anything else. “She fell because she never expected a lethal blow from her star-crossed lover. A mistake I, obviously, will not be making.”

“She also had an Artifact capable of focusing S rank magic,” Vason grumbled. “And the keys to that Vimana. And thousands of Asuras. Could’ve at least left those to us. Now they’re all back in the Akh Nara’s hands. As if he needed any more power.”

“He won’t use them,” Tia replied, shaking her head. “At least, I would’ve been confident in that assessment until he murdered his own wife in cold blood. I would’ve told you he’s not some bloodthirsty killer. Now… I still don’t feel he’ll use that Vimana on us, but I have to admit it’s possible.””

“I think I agree,” Vason said, deflating. “I can’t imagine him attacking us with that either. Not out of any moral highground, mind you, but mostly because he doesn’t need it. How is it, exactly, that you plan to survive against a demon who has apparently gained an infinite well of prana, cannot die, and was already the strongest being in the Ash even before his latest upgrades?”

That was the most shocking thing about the recent news that had been going around. That Vir had somehow gained ahold of an infinite source of prana. It was what gave life to the Samsara, and all accounts claimed it was truly infinite.

Tia had her doubts, but any hope she might’ve had of besting the demon went right out the window with that news.

“I’ll handle it,” Tia said at last. “I have a lead on an Artifact. Something that might balance the odds.”

“Ohh, yes,” Vason replied sarcastically. “The Artifact longsword your brother unearthed. That’ll certainly change everything! Not like the Akh Nara’s got any of those. I can just picture him quaking in his boots at your glorious blade.”

Tia shot her friend a scathing glare, who took the cue to shut his mouth. They all knew just how much of a chance she had against that monster, but as a leader, Tia had a role to play. Even here, among her closest friends.

“What news from our scouts?” she asked, turning to Haymi and sounding a hundred years older than she was.

“Much, and none of it good,” the mejai replied, frowning. “The demons seem to be as addled as we are. So far as we can tell, not one of them knew of the Akh Nara’s plans. Not the Samsara, not even the Rajas. And…”

“Yes?” Tia asked, cocking an eyebrow.

Haymi bit her lip. “And they truly do seem to be mourning Maiya. Hundreds of processions are occurring all throughout the demons lands, in every clan. It seems she was… much loved by demonkind, despite being on our side.”

Tia felt like she’d just swallowed Ash’va dung.

“Haymi? Next time you have news like this… keep it to yourself?”

“I…”

“It’s a lot easier to justify killing an enemy you see as evil monsters who want you dead. Who won’t stop until humanity’s cities lay burned or enslaved.”

“Which is exactly what they’re doing, yeah?” Vason asked.

“Yeah,” Tia replied. “And now we know they’re mourning one of us. What do you think that will do for morale if that knowledge gets out, hmm?”

Her retainers fell silent, and they weren’t the only ones.

What do you think it does for me?

Because as much as this was a fight for survival, Tia was under no doubt that, should humanity prevail, every nation would demand retribution. To take the fight back to demonkind and do to them the sorts of atrocities committed upon their friends and family.

How was Tia to condone, let alone support, such an action knowing as she did? That demons were universally evil as much as humans were universally good?

Tia put her face in her hands and groaned. She wondered why life was so much simpler when one was ignorant. Why the more one saw only left them confused and conflicted.

I suppose it’s a good thing, then, that I have no chance against that living god…
