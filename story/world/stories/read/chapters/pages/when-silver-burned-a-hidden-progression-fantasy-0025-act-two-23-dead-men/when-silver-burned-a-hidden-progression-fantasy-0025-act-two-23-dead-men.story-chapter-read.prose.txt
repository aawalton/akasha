SOLDIER

"A good man, you ask? It's hard to say. Hard to fill a glass of water with sugar on one side, salt on the other, and cast judgment when you knock back your drink.

"A man is a man, and he was the most peculiar of all I've fought beside. Maybe that's the salient bit. Not to be remembered as good, or to be remembered as bad; just being remembered is a cliff rocky enough to scale.

"What matters… is that he was one of us."

- The late recountings of Noémi Slipfall

The very first thing Kalsin found out as an aspiring soldier of the Ember Republic is that barracks, in fact, do smell considerably worse than prisons.

Drab both from the outside and the inside, the building was no more than a couple dozen bunks shuffled together in orderly lines with little space left between them. Only a few people loitered around the crammed space, but nearly half of the bunks had already been claimed, made obvious by the ruffled sheets and trinkets left on bedside tables. Kalsin quickly settled in under the watchful gaze of the man who'd escorted him, and in minutes the same man was leading him further into the military complex.

"Training had already begun," the man explained. "Observe your mates, follow orders and you should be just fine. This here is a new batch of recruits—that includes you—and you'll have two weeks of rigorous drills before you're sent east."

"Is that nearly enough time to learn much of anything?"

The man didn’t pause in his marching, just shrugged and continued. "Reckon not, but learning isn't the expectation for now. Least, not under Brownweather, and that's a fact. The norm would be a whole month of whipping you in shape, but you're a late entry and the supply of men must arrive on time. Tough luck on your part."

As they turned a corner, the building ended in a large stone arch leading to a wide yard with trampled grass that might've been green once. It was occupied by roughly twenty men and women facing each other in pairs, going through a basic sword form of one-two-three-four pattern, filling the air with clanks of wood. The movements were at ease, though somewhat clumsy for those obviously less familiar with the weapon. It was early in the day, and Kalsin figured this was as good a warmup as any.

The man overlooking the yard was sitting on a knee-high stone fence, face bored as he observed the pale sky more than the recruits below it. His gray uniform was just as ruffled as his unkempt hair, but the marks at his shoulders identified him as lieutenant. Shirt untucked, there was a hole just above his waist on the side, baring his scar. Though such a display wasn't strictly required in Újtavak, the gesture came as second nature for many people born elsewhere in the Republic.

When the officer noticed Kalsin standing—now alone—in the archway, he gestured for him to approach. A face worn with elapsed years, there was little sympathy and even less intrigue as he looked Kalsin up and down, unimpressed. Just as well. Kalsin hardly thought himself an inspiring sight with not one, but two scars now decorating his face.

"Grab yourself a stick and get in line," the officer said.

Kalsin opened his mouth, but the man interrupted him. "Shut. Don't rightly care what you call yourself. In line, now."

In short order Kalsin found himself standing with the other recruits who, following the lieutenant's barked command, formed a row stretching across the length of the yard. There were some muttered words as everyone shuffled in a line resembling a drunk sailor's artistic capabilities on a swaying barge.

Lieutenant Brownweather's worn face sharpened as he scrutinized the lot of them. He paced, pushing and pulling at the recruits with the gentleness of dried fossil until the line was more or less even.

"Thirteen days, or just about. That's how long you're gonna be enjoying this idyllic resort you ought to recognize as the better part of civilization. What you decide to do with it, I couldn't give a rotfin about. I'll say the words I have to say, show what I have to show, but I have no further duty for this queue of the deceased-in-waiting. Understood." Brownweather said the last word as a statement rather than a question. "Now, if you feel like getting some words off your chest, go ahead."

"Dead men don't speak,” Brownweather said when the yard remained silent. “Positively right, they don't. And don't mistake it either. You're all dead until proven otherwise. I don't care to get to know you, I don't care where you come from, and I sure as fucking don't care about the vowels and consonant your parents picked to represent their ill-fated offsprings." Kalsin felt the last statement was something of a theme here.

Raking his gaze across the recruit one final time, Brownweather muttered something under his breath and retreated to his position at the wall. "Duels today," he said, crossing his legs. "You ever held a sword before, Ghosty?"

It took a moment for Kalsin to realize the lieutenant was talking to him. The obvious mockery at the unusual color of his eyes made him take an instant dislike to the officer. But Kalsin only nodded, deciding after all the theatrics, it wasn't worth the risk badmouthing the fella.

"We'll see soon enough. Crawl, why don't you give it a twirl? Come on up. And the rest, give'em space."

Kalsin tested the weight of the wooden sword in his grip as the recruits cleared out a large circle around him, except for the one who reluctantly moved to stand opposite. He was a man not much older than himself, more lean than muscled and sporting a mop of reddish hair. Kalsin couldn't see his scar at first, but then he spotted the black mark at the base of his left ear.

"Remember, no surge," Brownweather said. "Nature-given gift is one thing, while learned skill another entirely. If you live long enough to master the latter—which, you won’t—only then will I allow power usage during training. That clear?"

Kalsin nodded, watching Crawl do the same. He assessed the recruit's posture, the way he held his weapon, the arrangement of his feet, and came to the conclusion that he wouldn't have much trouble beating him. Crawl must've suspected the same thing based on the trepidation in his eyes. But when the command to begin sounded, he was the one to launch the attack.

Kalsin easily parried the first few swings that came in a predictable pattern nearly identical to the ones practiced before, two hits aimed at the shoulder and two below the waist. He saw multiple opportunities to counter but didn't take them, only sneaking in a couple of prodding attacks of his own. Humiliating the man would've been pointless, Kalsin knew.

So as the swings kept coming, Kalsin steadily took control of the duel and increased the pace, forcing Crawl to adjust on instinct, to strain his limits. It wasn't long until sweat poured down the recruit's face, his footwork becoming a jumbled mess and his swings losing their strength. Kalsin took no pleasure when he finally knocked the unsteady weapon from his opponent's grasp with a twirling motion. Good training for the lad.

Crawl only let his defeated expression linger for a moment before bending to retrieve his sword. Before the man could prove his determination and ask for a rematch—which Kalsin suspected he would do—Brownweather's drawl interrupted them.

"That's enough. Unless your intention is to amuse the Veiled to death with your incompetence, Crawl, I'd take these sessions a bit more seriously. Piss off, will you."

The next challenger was a young woman called Ringtail, though Kalsin had no clue where the lieutenant's inspiration for the nickname had come from. She was tiny enough to barely reach his chest, pale, her chin-length hair dark and windswept. More importantly, she carried herself with a seemingly reckless bearing as she stepped forward. Cuteish, but full of attitude. The way she moved reminded Kalsin more of an acrobat than of a fighter, and he knew right away that this time around he'd have to work for his victory.

Their first clash was quick and chaotic, both combatants exhibiting wildly different techniques that resulted in a jumble of glancing hits, misses, and stifled curses. When they separated to catch their breaths, a thin line of blood trickled down Kalsin's upper arm that might or might not have come from a sharp fingernail, while Ringtail had the faint line of a bruise forming at the base of her neck.

Kalsin knew his own limits. He and Jack had matched themselves enough both against humans and mythos for him to know exactly where he stood with the sword. He was good. Decent physique, impressive sword-form and an impeccable perception to read the opponent. Had he the ability to surge, Kalsin could've been great, even. But that handicap wasn't in play, and for now, that was all that mattered.

Ringtail’s speed was disconcerting, but her unwillingness to give up a losing trade was something he could exploit. They slowly tested each other's limits for the next minute; with her initiating, and him deflecting while pushing to gain ground only to realize a second strike was already coming from a different direction, forcing him to step out of range to dodge. Until he didn't.

Bracing himself, Kalsin stepped toward the coming strike instead of avoiding it. Pain laced through him as the wood smacked into his shoulder, but his left hand found the hilt of her opponent's sword, securing it in a tugging match while his own weapon came rushing down on her dominant arm locked in place. To give her credit, she didn't pull back or relent until her nerveless fingers forced her to drop the sword, even though she had ample time to pull back and save herself a bruised limb.

Kalsin pleasantly noted by the end of their duel that he was somewhat out of breath. He flipped his newly acquired weapon and extended it to Ringtail, hilt first. She swept the training sword out of his hand with a glare and marched back in the circle without waiting for the order.

"You're an embarrassment, Ringtail," Brownweather said with a bored tone. "Your shield form is like a flopping fish, your halberd handling rattled at the smallest farts of the wind, and now the only skill going for you is kicked up by a double-cracked fuck."

Kalsin smiled at the lieutenant with enough venom to fill at least one bottomless fracture. Scarrd once, twice, or three times, it only impacted one's ability to surge, nothing else. To assume he'd perform worse because of his scars was, quite frankly, twelve tons of ass-backwards prejudice.

"And you, Ghosty, don't look so smug. If you'd taken a hit like that in real combat, you'd have a single appendage remaining for three tasks that are best left separated; wiping your ass, shaking hands, and stuffing your gut."

Unpleasant imagery aside, Kalsin was aware the maneuver would've been beyond idiotic against a steel sword, so he accepted the lieutenant's admonishment as his due. He rested his hands on the crossguard of his propped up wooden sword. Still. He managed to come out on top within the context of the current predetermined rules.

"Alright, we've wasted enough time. Littleguy, will you?"

Of course, Littleguy turned out to be the largest damn figure Kalsin had ever laid eyes upon. The hulking man would've been hard-pressed finding a shirt to fit his frame, so Kalsin regarded his bare—and comically muscled—chest with both admiration and sympathy. In turn, Littleguy watched Kalsin, his clean-shaved face unreadable and clear of emotion. They didn't have to wait long until Brownweather's signal to begin came in the form of an unenthusiastic grunt.

Two things happened in that instance. What those things were didn't really matter. Why? Because Kalsin didn't have time to do much of anything while they happened.

Littleguy moved with speed that Kalsin would've never found possible without surge. In the time it took for Kalsin to bring his sword to block—which rattled his arms like a hammer—his opponent's next swing was already falling toward his lower body. Kalsin tried to salvage the fight, shifting his stance sideways, turning what would've been a painful hit into a marginally less agonizing graze.

The details got somewhat lost in his mind, but roughly two and a half heartbeats later Kalsin was sprawled out on the ground with an angry red wrist, bloody lips, and a healthy dose of force-fed confusion.

He could hear Brownweather talking but he wasn't paying it much attention, instead focusing on the meaty hand extended toward him. Kalsin took a second to gather himself before letting Littleguy pull him to his feet. Kalsin wondered how to address the man still displaying a stony expression, but before he could utter a word, Littleguy simply walked back to rejoin the recruits. As the lieutenant's irritated gaze fell on him, Kalsin followed Littleguy's example and scurried back in line, giving a mental thank-you to the large man for retreating before Kalsin could break the no-speaking rule. Which was, apparently, a major crime in the eyes of their superior.

"Now that we've established our next corpse in line isn't completely hopeless with a training stick, we'll be running some speed and accuracy drills." Brownweather said. "Get in pairs, coin toss and switch. You know what to do."

Kalsin ended up with the first man he dueled, Crawl. It wasn't easy to figure out the training process without allowance to talk, but with Crawl’s helpful gestures and a lifetime spent together with Jack, Kalsin eventually figured out the coins lobbed at him weren't just for entertainment. He was supposed to hit the coins with his sword, but only using thrusts, and aiming the swordpoint to perfectly intercept the coin trajectory as they fell. One man did the tossing, the other the hitting, and after irregular periods when Brownweather finally decided to pay attention to them and bark the order, they swapped roles.

Disregarding the lieutenant's apathy, Kalsin found he enjoyed getting lost in the exercise. In the beginning he missed more often than not, but as the minutes stretched into hours, he saw definite improvement in both his and Crawl's performance.

Some time later, as Warmlight crested and started to fall away beyond the clouds, Brownweather's 'dismissed' rang across the yard. Then he walked off without another word.

The moment the officer disappeared through the barracks, the murmur of chatting recruits started up like a forever held sigh finally being released. What once felt like a grim, deathly atmosphere quickly discharged and something almost jovial took its place. Men and women addressed those next to them as they let loose repressed comments, or shouted across the yard to get in a jab or two. As expected, most of them were on the younger side, but Kalsin could see a handful of soldiers pushing their middle years.

"Er, Ghosty?" said Crawl with a cautiously hopeful tone. He waved toward the yard's rear edge. "Our training swords go there, in the baskets below the wall racks."

Kalsin followed suit as the recruits deposited their equipment, walking with the red-haired kid. "So how much agency does the average recruit have when it comes to their own names?"

"I don't know about other officers, but Brownweather leaves little wiggle room. Unfortunately he’s the one leading our batch, so you're stuck with what you're given."

"Really. This glorified mudbooter won't let his trainees use their own names? What happens if someone does it anyway?"

"No, just go with it. Trust me. Protesting won't do good for your health."

A long line of chipped wooden shields hung on the wall above a row of halberds that had their blades covered by protective fabric. Kalsin placed his wooden sword in a half-full basket. "I'll drop the 'y' then," he said.

Crawl shrugged. "Works for me, Ghost."

"How'd you get yours?"

Even in the aftermath of their exercise, Kalsin could see a faint flush come to Crawl's face. "It's, well. I just kind of slipped during my first introductory duel."

"Slipped?"

"And, ah, kept slipping. It was pouring rain and the ground was muddy, and my legs couldn't find purchase, and I just kept sliding on all fours trying to stand."

"Ah," Kalsin said sagely. "The good old horse cock peeping maneuver."

Kalsin spent the early afternoon accompanied by Crawl giving a rundown of the army grounds, and then he spent the late afternoon wandering on his own as he observed the people loitering about the place. Aside from their batch of almost twenty recruits, he could see nearly double that amount of trained soldiers coming and going with purposeful steps. The dining hall was a busy space that might’ve been able to accommodate their numbers on a good day, attached to a steamy kitchen manned by a handful of cooks. Not the kindly, grandmother type of cooks that sneaked a cookie into your pocket when you happened by, but stern faced crones who would probably use an unfortunate trainee's amputated limb to spice their soups.

The camp had no short supply of offices and private bedrooms used by those higher on the rank ladder, but since those were off limits for them, Kalsin didn't see much. Weapons storage was near the training yard with actual steel swords and shields, plus all the equipment required to maintain them. He figured once on the march, they'll be expected to keep their given weapons and armor in top shape; it was something that Kalsin had learned through his travels well enough.

Thinking of equipment brought his mind back to the present. Kalsin walked up to the man standing beside the storage room with a ledger under his arm. "Apologies. I was wondering where I could find my mount. Assuming you have him."

The man gave him a glance. "You a fledgling older boy?"

Kalsin arched his brows.

"New recruit, 's what I meant." The man frowned, looked through his ledger as if by habit, then stared in the distance with a suddenly clouded look. He was digging up memories with a rot's surge. A few long seconds trickled by. "That feller at the gate, 's that you?” He asked. “Right then. Your physical belongings had already been returned to you. You got a sword, that's good, won't be needing to strain army supplies on that front. One shield, one halberd, fish oil and beeswax, chainmail..." The man muttered off a string of words until he arrived at Kalsin's question, "...will be given for the duration of your service. For those fortunate enough to own a mount, whether it be horse, bahten, or kézmoon, they'll be kept with the stable master, and will remain in his care to be brought alongside the army on march."

After asking for directions to the stables, he thanked the man. But before turning to go, Kalsin said, "The medical wards. If I could visit—"

"Could not. The injured are housed off the perimeter, sorry. No allowance for recruits."

Kalsin gave a parting nod. It was worth a try. Though they hadn't spent much time together, part of him hoped Áron and Erazza would make it.

The other part, the part that was still reeling with the revelation about being a scar-magnet… It was hoping he would never cross paths with them again. With him wearing the exact copy of Jack's scar after her miraculous healing—he couldn't even imagine how the heretics would react.

Start up a fucking revolution? Publicly declare him some selfless savior right out of the pages of a fairy tale? Jump him in his sleep, hoping to cleanse themselves of their scars and soil him even more? No. Keeping a distance was the smart move.

Keeping a distance until he figured out what the hell he was supposed to do with this… this thing that he was now.

Jack would likely join the army’s Clenn division now that she was one of them, and march toward the eastern border when the time came, same as Cinder. Though he hadn’t seen his sister since the confrontation at the gate, he wasn't worried. Kalsin knew she'd show up eventually.

She always did.
