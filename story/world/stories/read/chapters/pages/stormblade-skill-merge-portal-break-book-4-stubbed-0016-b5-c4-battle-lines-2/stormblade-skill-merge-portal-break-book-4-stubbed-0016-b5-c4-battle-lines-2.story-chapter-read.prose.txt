Guild leader of the Desert Wind. Increasingly powerful player in Phoenix’s political sphere. Semi-expert hacker, with a network of anonymous online allies. Sister to one of the city’s S-Rank delvers.

And, unfortunately for Jessie Gerald, a high school sophomore.

It was outrageous to even think about having school tomorrow, much less for two more years after that. And then what? College? Absolutely outrageous. Kade wasn’t looking at colleges, and he’d been out of high school for a while now. Why should she?

She’d already decided she wasn’t sleeping, anyway. That meant tomorrow would probably be a bad pain day, and bad pain days were the worst. She wouldn’t learn anything, not even in Civics. It’d be a miracle if she got through to therapy tomorrow night.

So, instead of trying and failing to sleep, Jessie had pancakes cooking when Ellen pushed the door open. “Kade needs your help!”

Jessie rolled her eyes. “What did he do now? Actually, sit down and have breakfast with me. You can explain while I cook.”

Ellen blinked, looking confused. “It’s one o’clock.”

“It’s pancake o’clock.”

Ellen blinked from the door. Jessie saw the moment her brother’s partner saw the disaster zone the kitchen had become in the last fifteen minutes. Jessie shrugged, winced, and cleared her throat. “How many do you want?”

“…three.”

“Great. I’ll cook, you talk.”

Jessie was dying to know exactly what Kade needed help with, and why the urgency had disappeared from Ellen’s voice under the threat of pancakes. It was suspicious. But even more than that, whatever Kade needed help with would almost certainly keep her out of school tomorrow. That meant no jostling for wheel space in the halls, no navigating the elevators and ramps, and a chance to rest her joints while she worked on solving whatever problem Kade had realized S-Rank strength couldn’t handle. It was a lifeline in a storm, and she’d take it.

Plus, there was a good chance that whatever problem Kade needed solved could also help with solving her no doubt much less important problem with the Guardians. User295 and Yreanne had been busy over the last few hours, digging into the guild’s deployment choices during the Siege of Phoenix and the assault on the Carlsbad Portal, and Jessie was sure that the data pointed to one thing.

The Guardians were about to make a play for power in Phoenix, and there were no guilds capable of stopping them.

“Okay. Kade needs information, and you’re the best person to get it,” Ellen said. “Your pancake’s burning.”

“Crap!” Jessie lunged for the spatula, squeaked in pain, and flipped it. Sure enough, the bottom was black and smoking. “Alright, that one’s mine, I guess. What does he need, exactly? Most of my online friends are busy, but I can probably find someone who’s free and good enough. Or, if not, I can try to do it myself.”

Ellen smiled, and Jessie winced. This time, it wasn’t from pain. Her eyes flicked to the crumpled wooden puppet slumped in the corner of the room. Enolda only activated so often—the Crone had better things to do than keep her servant active. Ellen followed her gaze and nodded. “Yeah. He needs to talk to the Crone. It’s information about a place in another world—specifically, a portal world. So it’s going to have to be you. She likes you. We’ll work on getting whatever she wants in exchange for that information. You get him that info.”

Jessie nodded. Visiting the Crone always felt like she was in just a little over her head and didn’t know how to swim. But Ellen was right; of all the people Jessie knew who were better at finding information on Earth, not one of them could get what Kade needed right now. It was up to her.

“Alright. Tell me what you need to know, and I’ll do what I can.”

By the time I found Jessie and Ellen in the kitchen, the pancake mess was sort of cleaned up, and Jessie knew as much as Ellen did. Granted, that wasn’t much, but it wasn’t like I knew anything more than that. The name of Tanat’s sect, a rough description of her features and clothes, and a quick demonstration of her swordplay style. That was it. I didn’t know anything else, except that they knew more about cores than anyone in Phoenix.

It’d have to be enough. The gate to the Black Tower was closed, but Tanat could open it from her side, too. If I’d had time, I could have waited and hoped she returned to my portal world, then made contact with her sect that way. But I didn’t have time.

I did have the rest of a mess to clean up, along with three pancakes—one burnt almost beyond recognition—to eat. Jessie insisted that the clean-up was part of her payment for information hunting.

She also insisted the pancakes were free.

I drowned the stack in syrup and got to work on eating it. The sooner it was done, the sooner Jessie could get me what I needed, and the sooner I could get back to reshaping the Black Tower.

“Hey, how strong are you, Kade?” Jessie asked suddenly.

I shrugged. “That’s a tough question to answer. I’m pretty strong now. My core’s S-Rank, and it’s a more pure S-Rank than anyone else in Phoenix, or maybe on Earth. So I’m pretty strong.”

“Could you beat the Portal Tyrant in a fight?” she asked.

“Probably?”

Ellen joined in. “The Phoenix Reborn?”

I shook my head. “I don’t think so. Not yet. But soon.”

Jessie waited a beat, pancake halfway to her mouth. “Could you have beaten Angelo Lawrence? If he was at full strength, I mean?”

Silence. I let it hang in the air, both because I didn’t want to think about Angelo Lawrence, the Light of Dawn and Depth of Midnight. He’d been the most powerful delver in Phoenix. His guild had been one of the big five. And now, he was gone. As the silence stretched on, Ellen and Jessie watched me.

Something fell with a loud splat, and syrup splashed off Jessie’s plate as the pancake slid off her fork.

“Crap!” she said, reaching for a napkin.

I shrugged. “I don’t know. It’d depend on a lot of variables. Prep time. Battlefield. All of that. I could probably beat him one or two times out of five?” I didn’t want to give myself too much credit, but at the same time…I could definitely steal a win or two, even from him. I was fast, and I hit hard.

Then my eyes narrowed. Jessie had a serious expression on her face, and she wasn’t chewing or eating. “Why?”

She shrugged dramatically and winced. “Well, you know the Guardians, right? They’re the guild in charge of the Wickenberg portal break and the farms it feeds. While you were gone, my friends—“

“Internet friends?” I asked.

Jessie nodded. Ellen raised an eyebrow. My sister ignored her. “Yeah, internet friends. They did some digging into the Guardians after I noticed that Vern Kellen pulled out of the Carlsbad Assault. They didn’t just pull their S-Ranker—he’d have been on the front line with Angelo Lawrence if he’d deployed. They pulled all of their A-Rankers, too. A few low-rankers went along as support. Actually, they committed more support than anyone else, but no front-line delvers. There are a few possible explanations.”

“Like?”

Jessie rolled her eyes at me. “Like the one they’re giving. The Wickenberg portal break has been acting up, and since it’s a threat to the city, the Guardians’ mission to keep control of it supercedes anything else. But that’s bullshit.”

I glanced at her. She stared back at me, and after a moment, I nodded. “And this is important because?”

“My friends saw their internal communications. They got me copies of the top-secret confidential do-not-copy stuff, and it’s all true. Their portal has been acting up, but it’s because they haven’t culled it since the Siege started. And they have culled it because…”

She waited. So did I.

Eventually, Ellen cleared her throat. “It’s like Bob, Kade. They’re making a play for power in Phoenix. They saw a possible scenario where they could gain power relative to the other guilds by not committing their forces, and they chose to follow that path. Now, Vern Kellen’s in charge of the second-strongest guild in Phoenix.”

“First,” Jessie said.

“Explain.”

“I’m trying to, but you’re not listening.”

“You’re trying to make yourself look smart by holding off,” I shot back.

She groaned. “Fine. Okay, the Light of Dawn and Bernard the Wall ran the Roadrunners and Coyotes, respectively. They’re both gone. That weakens those two guilds. The Portal Tyrants also lost a few A-Rankers, but they’re in relatively good shape compared to the other two guilds. They’re still relevant, but barely.

“Then there’s the Iron Falcons. They’re technically part of the big five, but even though the Iron Falcon holds a seat on the Inner Council, he tends to avoid Phoenix’s politics. He’s already got Tucson to deal with, and adding more territory’s asking for trouble. He’s a smart dictator.”

It was funny, in a head-scratching kind of way. I’d grown up with the Portal Tyrant, Bernard the Wall, and the Falcon’s Eye as heroes. Impossible-to-attain levels of power, bravely delving into the most dangerous portals to keep Phoenix safe from danger. Then, over the last few weeks, I’d seen them as people. Lived with them. Fought with them. Watched them bleed and die. Now, Jessie was asking me to rethink how I saw them again. Instead of heroes or warriors, they were political figures. Pieces on a chessboard. Every one of them was a rook or queen, being piloted around the board—and at the same time, they were also the chessmasters, or at least their guilds were.

Except some of those guilds were fighting with a handful of pawns and a single knight and bishop now. The rooks and queens were dead. And one side still had a full army.

“This matters, why?” I asked. I had a guess already, but I needed to be sure, and I needed to hear it from Jessie’s mouth.

Instead, Ellen jumped in. “Vern Kellen’s the S-Ranker in charge of the Guardians. He’s like Bob. This is a chance for him to grow his monopoly on power in Phoenix. He’s going to take it.”

“Exactly,” Jessie said. “Concentration of power is bad for Phoenix. The GC already controls too much of it, but at least they’re nominally neutral. The Guardians, though—or even the other guilds? They’re not.”

“Are you going to eat anything?” I asked.

“I’m getting there. You’re a powerful, fresh S-Ranker. That gives us a lot of leverage, politically speaking. We could step into the void the Roadrunners and the Coyotes left behind—especially if your meeting with the Crone helps fix Ellen’s problem.”

“So, it all ties together,“ I said. It wasn’t a question, and I didn’t wait for her to answer it. “Fine. I’ll help you with keeping Phoenix stable and moving the guild into a position of power, and you’ll arrange a meeting between the Crone, Ellen, and me.”

“Deal,” Jessie said, smiling. She took a bite of her pancake and winced again. “It’s cold.”

Then, before I could say anything, she cleared her throat. “Mind Maiden Enolda, I need a meeting with the Crone.”

“When dost thou need this meeting?” the wooden, loosely-dressed puppet asked. I jumped; I hadn’t even noticed her in the corner, and she’d stood up so suddenly it was like all her strings had been pulled at once.

“Now would be ideal. Sooner would be even better,” Ellen said. Her eyes blazed, and I couldn’t help but smile.

We were on our way to getting her back in the game.

No matter what I had to do back here, that alone would be worth it.

The Crone was waiting for us when we stepped through the portal to the Ghostmarket.

She stood, hunched over her staff, a hag-like monster with hair covering her emaciated, ancient body. I wasn’t fooled; I’d seen exactly what she could do when she’d fought an SS-Ranked monster in the middle of her own portal world. Every second we spent here, Jessie, Ellen, and I were in indescribable danger.

But at the same time, this might be the safest place in the universe for me. The Crone was, by the God of Thunder’s own admission, more powerful than either him or the Placid King. We were untouchable as long as we stayed here.

The Crone knew that.

“Don’t think I’m offering you asylum. None of what you could offer’s worth a three-way war between monsters like us, so if that’s why you’re here, you can forget it,” she said the moment she saw us. “A visit of a few hours? Fine. I can swing that. But residency is out of the question.”

“We’re not here for residency,” Jessie said. “We’re here to make a deal.”

“Well, why didn’t you start with that? That puppet only said you wanted to talk. Spit it out. What do you want?”

Behind the Crone, the Ghostmarket stretched out under her massive Mana crystal. Monsters from every portal archetype I could imagine moved along its dirt paths, stopping at vendors and haggling with each other. I revised my earlier opinion as a familiar-looking, tentacle-covered fish locked eyes with me. There were plenty of threats here, and the sooner we left, the better. “Can we speak privately? I’ve got enemies, and some of them are buyers here,” I said.

The Crone nodded and led us into her tent. Three chairs sat on one side of the table, and one on the other. They were the only decorations in the cloth structure, even though it could have fit three or four circuses inside. She sat on her side and, after some hesitation, the three of us sat on ours.

“We need to know how to find someone Kade met in a portal world,” Jessie said.

“Met but didn’t kill? Interesting. Most portal monsters are in it to kill. Even your friend Eugene and I are not immune to the whispers, you know?”  the Crone said. “If you found something in a portal world you could negotiate with, that’s a rare moment.”

“But it can’t be that rare, can it?” I asked. “Portal worlds are—“

“Carved from the worlds they mimic, with those worlds’ inhabitants carved as well. Or so it appears. I cannot say if that’s true or not, though—and the whispers drive us. Now, listen. I can give you the directions you seek. I’ll even do it while trusting that you can deliver me the payment I desire.”

“Great. We’re after the Soaring Saber sect. Tanat. She’s an A-Ranker—probably S-Rank by now—who I met in a solo portal. We need to get in touch with her because—“

The Crone cut me off with a raised hand. It was the same gesture she’d used to silence the God of Thunder. “I don’t care why you need to find them. You’ll need a portal key, sort of like that solo portal of yours. I happen to have one. You can have it.”

“Thank you,” I said, but Ellen stopped me.

“What do you want in return?” she asked. I readied my hand around the S-Rank core in my pocket. It’d be enough to pay for the information. It might even be more than enough. And the Crone was constantly hoarding power in that massive glowing crystal. She’d take it.

“Simple. Your world has discovered the secret to forging portal metal. I want it. Bring me the weapons your father has been forging.”
