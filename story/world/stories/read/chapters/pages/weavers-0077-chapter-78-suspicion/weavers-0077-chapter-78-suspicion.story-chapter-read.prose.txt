Chapter 78 - Suspicion

The arena simmered with anticipation.

Outside its stone ring, students found themselves packed into the stands. As the start of the competition came close, the cheers kept rising like a large wave forming far out at sea. Just atop the closest spire of the academy, a silver sigil displaying it's crest gleamed beneath the midday sun. An impressive phoenix, facing a griffin, the words "Lena Manibus Ezrae Morietur" inscribed below it. These words had found themselves at the bottom of the crest since the founding of the academy just over a thousand years ago, when magical was in its infancy, and this school was a public teaching house.

The language was ancient, one which had almost no other known matches to speak of. The meaning of the words, despite attempts to pass them down through the generations, are unknown. But the most widely accepted translation is something along the lines of: "Our hands move forth even in death".

Somewhere down in the ring, Kaelin stood among the rest, head bowed, fists clenched. She seemed nervous, but that didn't matter.

Kana wasn’t there to watch.

She slipped around the edge of the outer path, boots muffled by damp grass, until she reached the squat stone structure the students all called the Toolshed. It was more warehouse than shed; an old, square, windowless building. Thick beams hung from the ceiling, warped with age, a testament to the lack of care given to such rarely visited areas. A rusted lock hung permanently open on the door, too stiff to even be turned.

Kana stepped inside hurriedly, careful to make sure she hadn't been seen.

It was cool and dim within. A single candle sat on a shelf to her right. She picked it up, lighting it with the old, one-handed flint and steel which had been placed beside it.

The candle roared to life, the flickering flame casting weak light across the room. Shelves of training armour, outdated spells, and bent blades from past years. It smelled like damp leather and wet dog.

Aria was already waiting, in the dark... for some reason.

She stood by a storage chest near the back wall, one gloved hand resting on the lid as if she hadn’t moved in hours. Her long coat had been pushed back from her shoulders, and the stiff collar of her robe was undone, not a rare thing for the ever rebellious Aria.

“You don't think she's lying is she?” Aria said softly, before Kana could speak. “I want to believe her, but—”

Kana shifted her gaze to stare down at her feet as she leant against a shelving unit to her side. "You think she sounds like a madwoman... don't you."

“Well n- I mean, not exact-... yea, something like that."

Aria turned, her sallow eyes shadowed by doubt. “I've talked to everyone i could find who had any sort of connection to Kaelin. Her parents, family, Lena. But nothing. She's always been an ordinary girl, albeit maybe a little too engrossed with hitting the gym. And yet now she talks about inventions which no one has ever heard of, impossible ones. A chariot without a horse? An interactive painting? I think she called it a touch scream or something. Anyway, at the end of the day, she's just spouting bullshit. There's no way any of that is even remotely true.”

Kana leaned against the nearest crate. “You really think she’s making it up?”

“I-I don't know what I think. It just seems so unrealistic. If it isn't true, then that's quite some imagination she's got there. But she's clearly been through some trauma of a sort, and trauma makes people do weird things."

Kana raised a brow. "That what you think it is then?"

“Look, I’m not dismissing her,” Aria said. “But if we’re going to act on what she remembers. On what she claims o have seen, we need to be certain it’s more than just some insane dream.”

Kana was quiet a moment. Then, with a shrug, she said, “I don’t think she’s lying.”

Aria looked at her, frowning. “You’ve never used to get along with Kaelin. Why the  change?”

“It's not that I disliked her,” Kana replied. “She was just... I hated seeing her always find a way to succeed even without any power of her own. In the real world, you can't overcome a top-end magician with wit and strength alone. She was naïve. But as soon as she got magic, she became one of my biggest contenders. And I liked that. I liked finally being able to see her effort paying off. So I changed my attitude. Simple as."

“Do you feel it too.” Aria asked.

“Feel what, her strength? I've never truly had the 'honour' of facing her in battle, but from what I've seen she's one of the strongest in the whole academy. Probably would be top of the year if Zephyr weren't around."

"I guess not then." Aria muttered under her breath, seemingly oblivious to having said that outloud.

"You say something, Aria?" Kana said, looking up as she did.

"What? No! It was nothing."

Aria stepped away from the crate and paced slowly through the narrow aisle. “I went and spoke to Aric last night."

Kana stared at her in shock. "What?! Kaelin said we shouldn't. Didn't you listen at all? I know you might not believe her, but there's no harm in being safe."

Aria looked to the side sheepishly, "I know, I know. I just... I had to see for myself, yknow? How do you expect me to just follow along with this crazy ass plan without finding anything out for myself."

"I get that, but you still should've told at least one of us. Just in case. What if it was true?"

"Well... That's the thing. Something is definitely wrong with Aric, He was lucid, kind of, but he talked about her directly. He said: 'It's Kaelin. It was her all along. I can fix it, I told you I could!' Then laughed, Like a lunatic.”

Kana’s face darkened. “What does he mean? What makes Kaelin this magical solution he needed?”

"I don't know. After he had that little crazy spell, he just collapsed on his desk and stopped moving. He was still breathing, I'm pretty sure he just fell asleep."

“Well I've been watching him too,” Kana said. “He seems to be switching  more often. Like suddenly some switch has been flipped in his head causing him to slip slowly into insanity. Every time that it gets worse, he had been around mine and Kaelin's class. Maybe this is what he meant, do you think Kaelin's done something to him?"

“No,” Aria murmured. “I don't think she's done anything. Kaelin wouldn't have the heart to hurt her mentor. Why didn't you tell Kaelin about what you noticed before?"

"I wasn't sure yet, so I didn't want to make her worry something was wrong. But I guess Aric got to her before I found the proof. Do you really still not believe her yet?" Kana questioned.

"I just don't want to. If he really is going crazy, there's no telling the damage he might cause. Someone has to put a stop to it. And if I don't help, Kaelin will just end up doing something dangerous alone."

There was silence for a moment. The walls of the Toolshed seemed to close in around them. Their rotting scent enveloping the two like a constrictor. The weight of what they might have to do, finally started to set in.

Let me show you what I've done Aria moved to the back, pulling aside a canvas drape to reveal a heavy satchel, marked with some dyes to make a bunch of cute and whimsical drawings. She didn’t open it. Just rested her hand against the runes.

"If he tries to hurt anyone-" Aria started.

"Is that what that's for?" Kana asked.

"Yea. It's got copies of a few of the forbidden spells Kaelin found."

"You mean she let you see them!? She wouldn't even let me near!"

"Well... Not exactly. I had to sneak into her room and search around a bit. Look, I'm just worried that when it comes down to it, she won't have the heart to do it. not to Aric."

"That's- I can understand that."

“She’s more dangerous than you might think. She’s starting to question everything. Soon enough, she might even question our loyalty to her as friends, we just need to be here supporting her should things go south."

Kana raised an eyebrow. “And even so, you trust her?”

“That's not important.,” Aria said. “What is, is that I am here by her side to help. Because what am I then if not a friend. Either way, if she’s right, if this isn't just some strange group hallucination, then we’re standing on the edge of something which I can't just turn my back on. You have to understand, right?”

“Hey, I don't give a shit what happens, being with Kaelin always makes it interesting. But what are you gonna do if we end up finding out that she had been wrong this whole time?"

Aria looked her straight in the eye. “Then we’ll deal with that together, too.”

Kana flipped her coin. Let it flash once in the weak glow, then caught it.

“Welp, you already knew I’m in,” she said. “But I’m still not jumping off a cliff for a girl who can't differentiate between her own memories, and some weird dream of hers."

“No one’s asking you to,” Aria said. “Just be ready at the bottom to catch us.”

The Toolshed’s old door creaked open behind them as the muffled sound of the arena grew louder—roars of excitement, the bell signalling the first match ringing out across the sky.

Kana turned to leave. “So what’s the plan, We try to trap him? Ambush?”

“We don’t need to trap him,” Aria said. “We need to get him alone. Try to make him act rashly, maybe bait him with something."

“Or someone.” Kana interrupted.

"Or someone," Aria agreed, " but if we can get him riled up, maybe he'll just stop thinking and go off instinct, that's when we could get him."

Kana kicked her heel idly against the tree beside her, the dull thunk echoing faintly in the stillness. “So what, then? We just wait for him to go full mad god and hope Kaelin's got the balls to off him?

Aria gave a sharp exhale, half a laugh, half exasperation. “No, of course not. We need to make a solid plan. I'll try come up with something before the end of the Colosseum match, I'd be willing to bet anything that straight after would make the perfect opportunity."

Kana walked over, her boots scuffing the floor. She leaned on the tree, looking around her. “This whole place is a net of people. There are way too many eyes around to get him to act around here.”

"Just trust me. I'll work something out." Aria said confidently.

"If you say so."
