Faerd noticed it.

Sakura saw it on his face — or rather, in that tiny, almost imperceptible shift that could barely even be called emotion. Faerd saw that she was no longer just hiding behind the stone and trying to wait out the obsidian barrage. He saw that she was preparing an attack. He reacted instantly.

He did not finish the sphere or try to shape it into its perfect form. Instead, with a sharp, jerking motion, he hurled the half-formed black mass forward.

And then he vanished.

Not literally. He simply moved so fast that to Sakura it really did look like vanishing. A heartbeat ago he had been standing in the breach, and then he was no longer where the target for [Devourer] should have been.

Sakura felt the skill fail before it could lock onto him.

“Damn it!” she breathed.

Faerd’s half-formed sphere slammed into the surviving section of wall. The blast was nothing like the clean strike its caster had intended. It was worse. Wilder. Less controlled. The obsidian mass burst in every direction at once, grinding stone, wood, and plaster into a black and gray storm. The wall buckled, the ceiling cracked, and heavy beams and chunks of masonry came crashing down.

“Down!” Gilbert barked.

Sakura had already thrown herself back behind the stone, covering her head with her arms. Something cracked above her, a piece of wood tore loose with a shriek, and the next impact shook the floor so hard it dragged her across dust and rubble. Grit flew into her face. The blast hammered her ears so violently that for a moment the only sound left in the world was a sharp, painful ringing.

Gilbert also dove for cover, barely managing to shield himself with magic. For one brief instant Sakura saw a flash of white-blue light trembling in front of him like a shield, and then everything vanished again into dust, cracking wood, and falling stone.

The tavern was collapsing. Not completely, but enough to make every new sound feel like the last one.

Sakura pressed herself lower, breathing hard, and felt something sharp scrape across her sleeve. A chunk of beam slammed into the ground right beside her cover. Another piece of debris struck the floor and shattered.

Then, very slowly, things began to quiet down. First the heaviest pieces stopped falling. Then only the creak of warped beams, the rattle of pebbles, and the harsh breathing of the living remained.

Sakura slowly raised her head. Her eyes stung. Her mouth tasted like lime and blood.

“Gilbert?” she called hoarsely.

“Here,” he answered at once from somewhere to the right.

She turned. Gilbert was climbing carefully out from behind a broken section of wall, covered in dust, with light still trembling faintly on his fingers. Alive. That was what mattered.

Sakura immediately looked toward the breach, where Faerd had been standing only seconds ago.

It was empty.

Sakura blinked, still not quite believing it.

“Where is he?”

Gilbert was already staring in the same direction, dark and wary.

“Gone,” he said shortly. “We leave while he’s not here.”

Sakura nodded right away. There was nothing to argue about. Faerd disappearing only made everything worse. As long as he was out of sight, he could be anywhere.

They quickly climbed out of the ruined room and dropped down through the broken section of wall. Every movement made their bodies ache harder, and the ringing in Sakura’s ears still had not fully faded after the last explosion. But neither of them seemed critically injured.

Outside was no better. The blast had already drawn half the district. People were running out of houses, pointing at the collapsing tavern, dragging buckets over even though there was almost nothing left to put out. From the far end of the street, the city guard was already charging in. There were too many of them.

“That way,” Gilbert snapped.

He turned hard, pulling Sakura into a narrow alley between the houses. Mud sucked at their feet. The walls were so close their shoulders kept brushing wet boards and stone. Sakura ran after him, barely able to keep her breathing steady. Everything inside her was still shaking from the fight, from the ruined room, from the surge of [Devourer] that had never reached its target.

She glanced back as she ran.

“Soldiers!”

“I see them.”

Shouts were already echoing behind them:

“Block the street!” “Don’t let anyone out!” “Find the mages!”

Sakura and Gilbert tore past a heap of crates, splashed through a street puddle far too deep, and turned again. Somewhere off to the right, a door slammed and some woman shrieked when she saw them, but thankfully she did not start calling for the guard.

Sakura looked back again.

Faerd was still nowhere in sight. That scared her more than anything, because one question kept circling inside her head: Where is he now? Could he already be somewhere in the shadows behind us?

“He’ll find us,” she exhaled.

“Yes,” Gilbert said calmly. “So run faster.”

They burst into another alley, even narrower and darker. Filthy rags hung between the buildings. Water dripped from the roofs. The place stank of mold, piss, and rotten food. Sakura slipped, but Gilbert caught her by the elbow and dragged her onward.

Behind them, boots thundered against stone. The guard had entered the district.

“Left,” Gilbert snapped.

They darted into a passage so narrow it was more like a crack between two houses. They had to turn almost sideways to squeeze past old barrels and sacks filled with who-knew-what. At last, Gilbert stopped behind a canopy of rotten boards and shoved Sakura flat against the wall beside him.

“Quiet.”

They froze. Sakura could hear nothing but her own ragged breathing and the frantic pounding of her heart. Then she heard it again: footsteps, hard and angry. The guard ran past just around the corner, and one of the men cursed. Then the footsteps started to fade.

But Sakura did not feel any better. She drew in a slow breath.

“We’re trapped, aren’t we?”

Gilbert did not answer immediately. He was looking toward the place where Faerd had disappeared, then toward the street full of guards, then up toward the roofs.

“Yes,” he said at last.

Sakura had not even really managed to catch her breath when something inside her jerked again with alarm. It was that vile, instinctive feeling that comes a split second before impact, when the body understands danger before the mind does.

Sakura snapped her head up and saw Faerd.

He stood at the far end of the alley, where the shadow of a crooked house fell across a filthy wall. Tall. Motionless. Like a black pillar in the middle of the chaos. In the half-light, his scars looked even paler, and obsidian was already gathering again in his raised hand.

“Gilbert!” Sakura breathed.

He turned at once and understood instantly.

A sphere was already swelling in front of Faerd’s hand, smaller than the last one. He was not hurrying. He was shaping the attack with that same horrible, steady certainty that made Sakura want, very suddenly and very sincerely, to melt into the wall and disappear.

“Here!” Gilbert snapped.

Sakura did not understand at first what he meant, but in the very next second he grabbed her wrist and yanked her sideways. Right at their feet, half-buried under mud and trash, was a low iron hatch set into the ground. Old, sunk into the stone, with a heavy grated handle and a rusted lock.

Faerd thrust his arm forward.

The sphere tore free from his hand. It was not lightning-fast, but it came at them with crushing, unstoppable momentum, compressing the air around it as it closed the distance.

Gilbert struck with magic before Sakura even had time to inhale. A white-blue bolt snapped from his fingers and slammed straight into the hatch lock. Metal flared blindingly, cracked, and burst apart into rusted fragments.

“Open it!” he shouted.

Sakura grabbed the handle at once. The hatch was far heavier than she had expected, and for one horrible second it would not move. Her heart slammed into her throat. The enemy sphere was already too close.

“Hurry!” Gilbert yelled, already turning back toward Faerd, clearly preparing to cover them for at least a little longer.

Sakura threw her whole body into the pull. The hatch jerked, then flew open, breathing a wave of stale, damp underground air into her face.

“Jump!” Gilbert ordered.

Sakura did not argue. She dropped straight down into the dark onto wet stone steps. She nearly slipped, slammed her shoulder against the wall, but stayed on her feet. Above her, beyond the hatch, white-blue light flared again. Gilbert was striking at the incoming sphere, trying to throw off its path even a little.

A heartbeat later, he jumped down after her.

And almost immediately, everything above them exploded.

The blast hammered the open hatch with a roar, as if the whole alley had collapsed onto their heads at once. Dirt, stones, and splintered wood rained down from above. A wave of heat and dust rushed into the underground passage. Sakura instinctively pressed herself against the wall, covering her head with her arms.

For several long seconds, they simply stood there, almost shoulder to shoulder in the black, cramped dark, listening as Faerd turned the world above them into ruins all over again.

Then it quieted a little, and only dull echoes, falling dust, and their own heavy breathing remained.

Sakura slowly lifted her head into the darkness. They were cut off now. There was no way back up.

Then Gilbert pulled them out of the black. He opened his fingers, and a white-blue flame flared in his palm. It looked cold rather than hot. Its light was sharp and clean, instantly cutting narrow stone walls, a wet floor, and a low ceiling out of the dark.

Sakura let out a slow breath. They were standing in the middle of a tunnel. It was narrow enough to set off claustrophobia. Old, damp, propped up in places by wooden beams darkened with age and moisture. Thin streams of water ran down the walls. Mud squelched underfoot.

Sakura lifted her head, peering into the darkness ahead.

“Where are we?”

Gilbert looked around too, squinting in the trembling light of his own magical fire.

“Sewers, old passages, smuggler tunnels... or all of it at once,” he whispered.

He raised his hand higher. The light sharpened and slid farther along the passage. It revealed that the tunnel stretched ahead and then split after a few dozen steps. Then split again. And again. Side openings, arches, narrow offshoots, stairways leading down, and passages so tight they were almost holes showed themselves in the dark.

Sakura went cold.

“This isn’t just a tunnel.”

“No,” Gilbert said. “It’s a labyrinth.”
