Even though Lora had offered to take them if they preferred to sleep in, both Trevor and Adabelle knew they would be up early for the last day of Fustavele. The streets weren’t nearly as packed as they were the day before, but that was because everyone was already crowding around the beach. It seemed like every inch of sand had been covered by the time they arrived.

Not only that, but people had taken residence on the rooftops, too. The piers seemed mostly empty, but that was because they had been cordoned off. As they rode Clay through the streets, hat firmly on the familiar’s head, it became clear that everyone had appeared early to make sure they got the best seats they could.

“Wow, it’s a good thing we won tickets, huh?” Trevor remarked as he looked over the crowd. There was enough space on the road to come and go, but just barely.

“Very good. It looks like we would have had to come before sunrise if we wanted a place even close to the water,” Adabelle said as they passed an intersection. It was almost completely blocked by wooden bleachers that hadn’t been there the day prior. They were, of course, full.

“This definitely looks like the kind of event that has people camping out in the best spots overnight,” he said. “It was a common practice back on Earth, too. For big shows, or when new products come out.”

The noblewoman tilted her head back in thought. “There were some who did that in Halvsar in order to get the best seats at tournaments, but they were those who cared the most about the sports. Usually it was only in the morning, though, instead of overnight.”

“Differences in population, I think,” Trevor replied with a shrug. His attention was grabbed when they passed a pier, and he pointed. “That’s the Hello Sea, isn’t it?”

Adabelle turned to see where he was pointing at, and nodded. “That’s the name on the hull, yes.”

The Hello Sea was a large yacht with a wide deck. Several people had already boarded and were hanging out by the railing, watching the sea or the beach depending on where they chose to stand. There were several other vessels tied up on the pier, though none seemed like they were going out today. In fact, Trevor noticed that there were fewer ships in the water now than yesterday.

He put that out of his mind as they approached the next dock. There was only one ship here. It was smaller than the Hello Sea, but far more luxurious. Artwork of Cruon flying through the sky was painted on its hull going from aft to fore. It also had a glass bubble that covered the deck almost completely. The name marked it as the Serendipity.

They dismounted from the earth elemental before they got onto the pier, and Clay returned to their stoat form so their master could send their husk to his inventory. That earned them a lot of attention, but he made sure to smile politely and wave in a way that showed off his Summoned tattoo. A lot of the confusion disappeared when he did that, even if they didn’t know what his Power was.

“Welcome to the Serendipity. May I have your tickets, please?” a kobold woman in a yellow dress requested as they approached the cordoned area.

Trevor produced the tickets from his inventory and handed them over. The worker studied them closely, looking for anything that may have given her cause to believe they were forgeries. It only took her a few seconds before she nodded to herself, smiled, and handed them back.

“Congratulations on your victory, sir, ma’am,” she said happily.

“Thanks. It was a big surprise, but we’re really excited about what we’re going to see here,” he replied with a smile. He pointed at Clay on his shoulder. “Are there any problems with my familiar coming aboard?”

The kobold’s eyes locked onto the earth elemental. “Are they prone to act in ways that will disturb the peace?”

“Nah, Clay’s a really calm fella.”

“Then there’s no problem at all,” the woman answered. She moved to the side, unlatching the rope that kept people from entering, and gestured them forward. “You’re all set. The boat will be leaving shortly, and there are seats on the upper deck. You may wander within the bubble or step onto the sun deck outside of it. Just know that if you choose the latter, there is a one hundred percent chance that you will get wet.”

“Thank you for the warning,” Adabelle said as she wrapped her arm around Trevor’s. “Shall we?”

“We shall.”

They walked down the pier as the kobold closed the rope behind them. He looked down at the beaches on either side, seeing a lot of jealous faces as they approached the gangplank and onto the ship. Once they went under the bubble, it was suddenly much cooler than he expected. Air flowed throughout the glass room, countering the sun’s heat.

People mingled all over the deck, mostly humans and blue-scaled kobolds, and ate off of small plates. Trevor’s gaze panned the area until he found where they had gotten it from. There was a buffet table set up near the Serendipity’s enclosed bridge with a large selection of seafood. Adabelle led him to it to check out the selection, though he didn’t partake. He was still full from breakfast and hadn’t expected a meal.

“That’s the guy!” a young voice exclaimed, and the courier turned around.

Siral, the boy who they had met at the fountain the day prior, was standing with his sister and what appeared to be his father. All three of them were wearing fine clothing, with the oldest among them wearing a blue sash across his chest. They were joined by an old kobold wearing familiar robes and a large hat that marked him as someone high up in the church of Cruon.

“Hello, Siral,” Trevor greeted, raising his hand to wave. “Alama. It’s good to see you again.”

Adabelle was quick to take a step forward. “Greetings, Lord Mital Dorrenhorn, High Priest Worth,” she said, curtsying towards them as she slipped from being a mere tourist into a member of the nobility. “I am Lady Adabelle Ramansa Anderson-Katine gen Tosa. This is my husband, Trevor, and his familiar, Clay. It is a pleasure to meet you both.”

The courier looked at his wife before joining her and bowing. “Hello, sirs. We, uh, won a contest to be here.”

Mital looked over the two, his eyes lingering on Trevor’s tattoo, before smiling. “Well met, Lady Adabelle, Trevor, Clay,” he said, addressing them based on how she offered their names. He put his hand on his son’s head. “Siral and Alama told me about the man with the shiny coins who had won the attention of one of our dear alumraes yesterday. It seems as though your good luck was kind to you.”

“It landed on his shoulder and everything, papa, and he still let me throw the coin,” Siral announced excitedly.

“Good luck is something to be shared around, I think,” Trevor said with a shrug. He glanced at Adabelle. While he had an inkling of an idea who this man was, he wasn’t completely sure.

“It was certainly an experience,” she added with a smile. “From one mayor to another, your city has been a delight to visit. I regret not being able to see the beginning of Fustavele de la Cruon on account of our traveling arrangements, but what we’ve experienced so far will remain with us long past our honeymoon. Especially the Basilica Draconian and its wonderful murals, High Priest. That was where my husband won the tickets, and we have been ecstatic about this chance ever since.”

The kobold beamed at the praise. “I am glad you enjoyed our cathedral. And on your honeymoon, to boot. You have very good tastes.”

“And have traveled far,” Mital said. “You mentioned Tosa, but I’m afraid I’m not familiar. Where might that be?”

“Far to the north, in Lucent,” Adabelle answered.

“Ah, I know of Lucent,” he replied, nodding. “That’s the place with the Summoned kings, right? Every few years he gets replaced?”

“That’s right.”

“Then you have come a very long way for your honeymoon. I’m impressed, and congratulations.”

“Congratulations!” Siral repeated, beaming.

“Yes, congratulations are in order. Cruon has blessed you both to have put you on the Serendipity for our most holy of days,” High Priest Worth said. “You must be on a grand journey to have come so far, though.”

“We’re in the care of a friend of ours, and his reach is quite far,” Adabelle replied, casting her gaze towards the city. “This is our second destination and his taste so far has been excellent. Even if Trevor hadn’t won the tickets at the Basilica Draconian, he still provided us with passage aboard the Hello Sea.”

“A wonderful vessel, though it goes without saying that it is inferior to the Serendipity,” the priest bragged, tapping his clawed foot against the wooden deck. “It is not often that outsiders win the tickets, though I am glad you are here. If you don’t mind, I would look forward to speaking with you about the ceremony after my duties on the island are complete. So long as you have the time, of course. They will keep me until sunrise tomorrow morning.”

“I think Lora has us leaving in the morning, doesn’t she?” Trevor asked his wife.

“That is, unfortunately, the case,” she confirmed. “But I am certain there are other priests aboard, and I’m sure we can leave our thoughts with them.”

“A lovely idea, that,” the kobold said, nodding. A bell rang thrice, causing him to perk up. “Ah, it’s almost time! We’re about to cast off. I must go to my post, but it’s a pleasure to meet you Lady Adabelle, and you, Trevor and Clay. Enjoy the ceremony!”

“We will, thank you.”

Mital frowned in thought as High Priest Worth headed to the bridge. “Lora, you say?” he asked.

“Yes. Asalorana, if you’ve heard of her. I imagine she has quite a few dealings around Paladona,” Adabelle answered.

“I’ve met her a few times, yes. A very chaotic woman, but there’s never a dull moment when she’s around,” the mayor chuckled. “That would make your friend the merchant prince Klarkartar, would it not?”

“Yup,” Trevor said, grinning. “He was the best man at my wedding, too. We go all the way back to when I arrived in this world. Klar was one of the first people I met, actually, aside from Jackson. He was my summoner.”

“It seems as though there is more to your good luck than just what the alumrae grants,” Mital noted.

“I have certainly had more than my fair share these last couple of years,” he said, smiling as he looked at Adabelle. Her tail wrapped around his wrist and he turned back to the mayor of Paladona. “This world has been very good to me, and I plan on giving back however I can. Mostly to the people of Lucent, but if you ever need a courier for whatever reason, feel free to reach out to Klar and we’ll see if he can get me out here.”

Mital arched an eyebrow. “A courier?”

“My Power is Inventory, so I’m basically an extradimensional bag without the bag part,” Trevor explained.

“He’s very capable,” Adabelle praised as she patted his arm. “In fact, just the other day he delivered over sixty perfect armors from the dungeon over in Locker. Surtla had to sit down from shock.”

“Well…” he started, but she squeezed his wrist tightly to cut him off.

“He’s also very humble,” she said, glancing at him.

“I can see that,” Mital laughed. “A shame Surtla could not make it today. He had sent one of his apprentices, Jorn, in his place. Something about an opportunity popping up at the last moment.”

“A shame, indeed,” Adabelle replied. The ship started moving, and everyone except for the Summoned braced themselves. Both of them had skills related to their balance, which kept them upright despite the sudden movement. The demonkin received an odd look from the mayor, but didn’t say anything about it. “We won’t take up any more of your time, Lord Mital. I hope you and your children enjoy the ceremony.”

“To you as well, Lady Adabelle,” he said amicably before looking down at Siral. “Say goodbye to the nice Summoned, and then we’ll head to our seats.”

The boy beamed at them. “Bye Trevor and Lady Adabelle and Clay!”

“It was a pleasure to meet you, Lady Adabelle, Trevor, Clay,” Alama said, curtsying before following after her father.

Adabelle’s ears twitched as she watched them walk away, and then she gave her husband a small smile. “It seems as though their dislike of Summoned is generational. A shame, but it happens.”

“Huh?” the courier asked, confused. He lifted the hand that still had her tail wrapped around it. “I didn’t get that feeling at all. Is that why you squeezed me?”

“That’s because you see the best in people, Trevor, which isn’t a bad thing,” she said as she turned to return to the buffet. “One thing I’ve learned is that nobility is nobility, no matter where they are. I can see between the lines better than you can.”

“But we didn’t do anything,” he replied with a frown. “Other than possibly getting Lora to invite Surtla to a worse ship. That’s what happened there, right?”

“Yes, I do believe she got him out of our hair for us,” she answered with a smile. “We’ll have to heap praise upon her when we tell Klar about our journey.”

“I was already planning on it, so that sounds good to me.”

Adabelle politely waited for a couple of guests to finish getting food before starting her own plate. “As for us not doing anything, that is true. But even though we’re nice, that doesn’t mean every other Summoned is. Sometimes, people’s view of us is muddled simply because of past experiences.”

“Well, that’s not fair,” Trevor complained, though he kept his voice down. “I think I should be judged by my own merits, not other people’s.”

“And I’m sure Lord Mital would do that for you if given a chance,” Adabelle replied as she took one last look at the buffet before leading her husband away. She had only gathered a few things, but it would be enough for her at the moment. “He seems like he would give you a fair shot, especially since his children already knew you and he knows you have an innocuous Power. Me, on the other hand? I’m much more dangerous.”

The courier frowned for a moment. “You were worried he’d guess you were Summoned, too? And become stressed out?”

“A little bit,” she admitted as she stopped by the railing near the front of the Serendipity. Mount Cruon was starting to get bigger as they approached. “It’s just common courtesy. He wouldn’t throw us off the ship or anything, though I’m sure he has the power to do so, but there was no reason to let him fret any more than he already was.”

Nodding, Trevor leaned against the railing. “I wonder what happened,” he wondered aloud.

“Something personal, probably, but it’s not worth speculating on,” Adabelle replied as her tail wrapped around his waist and pulled him close. “Let’s enjoy the cruise and the ceremony, shall we?”

Trevor was about to respond when the bell rang again. The other people on the ship began to clap, though when they looked around they didn’t see anything that caused it. Adabelle sensed the shift first, turning back to look towards the mountain, making sure her husband did as well.

There were no waves in the water, not even a ripple. Despite that, a huge, blue dragon ascended from the depths of the sea to start flying in the sky. Croal’s ceremony was starting.
