Teela answered her door wearing only a long, patched shift. She listened to my explanation groggily, rubbing at bleary eyes that grew wider with every word.

“So what do you think it is?” she whispered as we scooped up her boots and clothes, which she had haphazardly strewn across the floorboards.

“Honestly? I have no idea. I mean, the shadows moving by themselves… that can’t be anything from this realm, right?”

She shuddered. “Do you think they’re pacted?”

“I don’t know. Or maybe there’s a monster… I don’t know, doing something to them? Nearby? Watching them?” I sighed as we opened the door to our new room, depositing her things in a tidy pile beside one of two narrow beds. Next we headed to my little blue bedroom.

“Maybe they’re cursed,” she whispered ominously, hefting my knapsack. “Or… salt, Brin, you don’t think… I mean, there was an inn destroyed just a few days from here. And Agnes was at an inn… what if this place is going to be attacked? What if they’re… I don’t know, watching the town, waiting for the right moment to-”

“Don’t say it,” I pleaded, shivering. I didn’t want to imagine this charming little town and its happy residents- most of them, at least- being attacked in the dead of night. “I… I think we should tell the Order about it, at the very least. If something is here… I mean, I don’t think there’s anything else we can do about it, is there?”

“We could stop by their Watch in the morning,” she suggested, though she didn’t sound optimistic. “Hopefully they won’t start stringing people up just because you saw something strange. But maybe they’ll be extra cautious, y’know? Keep an eye out for anything odd.”

“Good idea.” I hefted my boots and cloak, following her back into the dim hallway.

We emerged just as a familiar, dark-haired figure disappeared around the stairwell corner. A moment later Renner stepped out of his own room, gear in hand, looking positively dismal.

“Sorry, Renner,” I offered, biting my lip against another swell of embarrassed heat. “I’m sure there will be plenty of other beautiful women for you to… er…”

“Take hostage?” Teela supplied helpfully. “Intimidate? Threaten?”

“Funny.” He rolled his eyes. Once we were in the room he locked the door. “Right. We sleep in shifts, and the moment dawn breaks we’re getting out of this town.”

Teela moaned. “Shifts? As in, you two woke me up and want me to stay up?”

“I’ll stay up.” I gestured for her to take one of the beds. “Really, I don’t mind.”

Renner frowned. “For a few hours. Here…” he moved to the room’s sole window, peering up through smudged glass. I followed suit and he tapped the pane, pointing towards a shadowy, leafless birch tree looming just outside. “When the moon crests that top branch, wake me up.” He turned towards Teela and added firmly. “You’ll go last.”

She groaned, flopping facedown onto the bed.

I sighed and, since the room had no furniture beside the two beds and the nightstand between them, settled on the floor near the window.

Renner retrieved a small clay jar from his pack and carried it over. He opened the sill, leaned out, and I turned away as the pungent smell of rot stung my nostrils. “Stuff’s too salted hard to get,” he grumbled a moment later, after closing the window and the pot. “And that was the last of it.”

I swallowed, tilting my head back. “Right. And if… I mean, if something happens…”

He snorted. “Start screaming? That oughta wake me up.”

“Right.” On sudden impulse I stood, moved briskly to the door, and double-checked that the bolt was well-drawn. Then I did the same to the window.

Renner watched me thoughtfully, then reached into his large pack. After a moment of rummaging he withdrew a triangular wooden contraption, not longer than my forearm, with wires and gears affixed along its sides. “You know how to use a crossbow?”

I blinked. “I… I know how to fire a regular bow. Durst taught me. He said it was more practical for hunting.”

“You hunt? Alright, so you can aim, at least.”

“Well… in theory.” I fidgeted, heat creeping into my face as one brown eyebrow rose expectantly. “He took me once, years ago. I shot a rabbit in the leg and the poor thing was so hurt. It was awful. We ended up treating the wound for weeks with some salves Clem and I put together. That’s actually how I got interested in apothecary work. Durst helped me nurse her back to health. We built a little cage and named her…”

Renner’s lips were pressed together. He was slowly shaking his head back and forth, as if he couldn’t believe what he was hearing.

I cleared my throat. “Um… he didn’t take me hunting again.”

“Sounds about right.” Teela mumbled into her pillow, voice and shoulders trembling with laughter.

“Yeah. Yeah, ladyship, that sounds about right.” He shook his head again and stepped forwards, fingers tapping at the wood and gears. “Look, it’s simple enough; bolt goes here, you crank it back with this, trigger is here, bolt shoots out here. Point at what you want to hit.”

I rolled my eyes. “I know that. But wouldn’t one of your knives suffice? Or your sword? Durst did teach me how to swing a blade, you know.”

“Uh-huh, until you hit a squirrel with one?” Teela snickered.

Renner’s lips twitched. “My blades won’t hurt a Fae, if that’s what shows up. This will.” He reached back into the pack, pulling out several razor-tipped bolts. They were shorter and a bit thicker than Durst’s arrows, but I would have recognized the silver sheen anywhere.

“Oh,” I said softly, shivering at the very real thought that there might be something truly dangerous lurking nearby.

“Yeah.” He handed me a bolt and the crossbow. “Alright, load and crank, show me where the trigger is, but don’t shoot.

I did as he asked. I held the contraption gingerly, pointed straight at the floorboards, as soon as it was ready to fire. The last thing I needed was to accidentally maim one of my companions.

Renner nodded. “Not bad. It takes muscle to fire, you’re not gonna do it by accident.” He reached out, unwound the crank, and returned the inert weapon to me. “If a monster shows up, shoot it and start screaming.”

“Got it.” I returned to my seat beneath the window, setting the crossbow down. I wrung my hands for a long moment, then murmured, “You… you don’t really think anything’s going to happen, right?”

He pulled his boots off. “I have no idea. If the guy downstairs was planning something, then seeing us together might’ve changed his mind.”

“You don’t think this has to be something to do with Fae?” I frowned, genuinely curious. “But, the shadows… that has to have been some kind of wicked magic. Right?”

Renner shrugged. “Probably. But, hey, I’m an optimist; maybe there’s nothing going on here beyond normal human crime.” He snorted, draping his weapons belt across the little nightstand.

“Crime? What crime could the stablemaster possibly-”

“Ladyship. You’re pretty, you’re dressed like a wealthy city-dweller, and you’re walking around with doe-eyes. I can think of a few reasons some lowlife might choose you as a target.”

I mulled that over as he climbed into bed. Then, “You think I’m pretty?” Heat bolted up my throat as soon as the words were out. Salt, why did I… what does it matter if he thinks… I am, of course, but Renner’s opinion certainly doesn’t-

He chuckled and shot me a smirk. “Not that pretty. You’re evil, remember?”

The comment was made so wryly, so lightheartedly, that a smile blossomed across my face. I let out a peal of laughter as something warm fluttered in my belly.

Teela sat up and threw her pillow, hitting me directly in the chest. “Hey. Sleep. Now.” She flopped back down, grumbling unintelligibly.

Renner snickered as he lay down.

I retrieved the pillow for her, biting down on my grin, then reclaimed my seat.

I blew out the candle and adjusted to the silent near-darkness. The trickle of moonlight coming in was barely enough light to see by. And upon further thought, I wasn’t so sure that I wanted to be beneath the window.

Just in case there is something outside. Which there’s not, of course, but… still.

I gathered my things and slid quietly across the floor. I pressed my back against the wall where I had a clear view of both the window and the door.

This makes sense, right? Now I can see every… I mean, not that anything’s going to happen. Surely nothing’s going to happen. Renner’s probably right; perhaps the stablemaster was planning to rob me. He probably panicked because we were watching him. And the shadows… salt, I don’t know. Renner didn’t see anything, and there were other people down in the common room. No one saw anything.

Is it just me? Is this part of… I don’t know, part of my bargain? I don’t know why it would be. Or… is there something wrong with me? Am I going mad?

The idea was dreadful, but not entirely far-fetched. Clem once said that sometimes people saw awful things and were never the same afterwards; as if something in their mind snapped, and couldn’t be mended. A cold, hollow pit opened beneath my bosom.

My thoughts remained maudlin as my vigil stretched on. But, beyond Teela’s faint snores and Renner’s heavy breathing, our room was quiet. The night was calm. Every nervous glance at the window showed only dark branches and the distant, clouded halo of light from a somber crescent moon.

Almost time to wake Renner up. And then… and then. I wrapped my arms around my knees. And then… what?

Would I be hurt? Threatened? Tortured? How long would my dreams remain bloodless?

My eyes drifted down to the gleaming bundle of sharpened silver.

I am not prey. I am not defenseless.

I can be brave.

A short time later, I gently called Renner’s name.

He was awake in a moment, reaching for his belt and rising to double check the locks. Then he yawned, taking my place against the wall.

And I crawled into the warm bed with a razor-tipped bolt clenched in one hand.
