“But Janak’s work was unfinished. You can’t mean to say the two of you completed it in such a short time?”

“Well, as you know, time is a strange thing,” Vir replied, smiling wryly. “Needless to say, the Crown Chakra mindscape was critical to the process. I wasn’t just learning to suppress my core in there, you know? And it wasn’t so much completing Janak’s research as Siya pioneering something entirely new.”

“If only it were so,” Siya said, shaking her head. “This is untested. It will likely fail. And yet, what choice do we have, now that our time has run down? The only reason I have any faith at all in this is your experience in daddy’s machine.”

“That was pure luck, and I only managed a sliver of a glimpse into Janak’s solution,” Vir replied. “That too, only because I trawled through every possible universe. In them all, there was only one in which he made any headway at all.”

“Even still… for there to be a chance against them… it seems like a miracle.”

Both Vir and Siya exchanged a glance.

“Well,” Vir said. “If anyone deserves the gratitude, it is my predecessors. Without their help, I would never have succeeded. In suppressing the core or in this.”

“The true miracle,” Siya said, “is that enough of daddy’s soul has persisted through the incarnations to enable your predecessors at all. And yet, these miraculous advantages mean nothing if we fail to complete the work in time.”

Vir nodded. “Indeed. The realm stands at the brink of collapse. If this doesn’t work…”

“It will. I’m sure of it.”

Vir let out a long breath. “All we can do now is pray that you’re right…”

Tia remained there in the mud and the thunder and the pouring rain, simply staring.

Watching with disbelief as the Akh Nara heard her words, not giving her a single word in response. Not even a twitch of his face or a nod of acknowledgement.

But he did do something. Something Tia would never in a thousand ages have guessed.

Would never have dared to hope for.

The Akh Nara’s reply was slow. It started with an outstretched hand that grasped at the empty air.

Air that reshaped itself before her eyes, materializing into a triangular form that ended in a brace and a grip.

A form everyone acquainted with the stories of the Akh Nara knew well.

The god of the demons wields a katar crafted for him by the Gods in another realm…

Tia stared at the weapon for a long moment, her mind simply refusing to accept this new development.

That was until the Akh Nara pointed the blade at her chest and her heart lurched in her throat.

The lord of the demons wishes to fight.

Despite knowing she was no threat to him, he wanted to fight? Now, after everything?

Tia could scarcely believe it. Just for an instant, she dared to hope that her words had gotten through to him. That he understood her plan and was honoring her desire.

But she killed that hope. No one, least of all her, could begin to guess what went through his mind these days. Not after Maiya…

It was a good thing, then, that Tia didn’t need to know. She simply needed to stand and fight.

Fight… and lose, for a duel between a mortal and a god wasn’t combat at all, but an execution.

One that would end the war. That would allow humanity to survive as an equal to demonkind, not under their thumb.

And so Tia stood up and she fought.

Bellowing a battlecry that replenished her waning Bulwark Talent, Tia rushed at Vir, deploying every talent her waning reserves could support.

For the first time since the duel began, Artifacts clashed. Imperium metal met Imperium metal, and deafening screeches filled the battlefield as magics beyond her understanding warred with one another whenever their blades met.

They fell into a rhythm. Each strike displaying their mastery over the art of swordplay, each blow filled with the fullest extent of their resolve.

It was why Tia knew Vir was taking her seriously. If not in magic, in the duel itself. He was fighting her with every ounce of skill he possessed, and it was with no small amount of pride that she realized she was matching him blow for blow.

Two Warriors who’d mastered their craft in the crucible of the Ash. Who’d dedicated their lives to the pursuit of strength, standing at the very summit of possibility, engaging in one final battle.

Tia gave herself entirely to that lethal dance. Her body began to move of its own volition, guided by razor-sharp intuition and her vast years of experience. All her training, all her strife and sweat culminating in this singular moment to move faster and with more efficiency than ever before.

Somewhere in the middle of it all, she began to forget the battle.

Her thoughts drifted. To simpler days. To her first meeting with Vir, running a Brotherhood contract for an employer who turned out to be smuggling drugs under their very noses.

How they’d both chosen to abandon that contract, bonding in the process.

She could not have known then how swiftly Vir would enter her inner circle. How they’d adventure across Rani as Spears Edge.

So much had transpired since those tender days. Days she found herself longing for.

You understand, don’t you? You feel as I do.

Vir’s katar suddenly extended, piercing into Tia’s guard, but she didn’t panic. As if moving to a choreographed dance, she had already surged prana into her feet, dancing away as the blade swept in front of her, cutting a lock off her hair.

The battle continued to fade from her mind. Less real and more dream, made all the more surreal by the thunder and the lightning. A stage set for just the two of them.

They were simpler days, then, when her thoughts were filled with her brother’s acceptance. With her next contract and how she might improve her standing with the Brotherhood, her neglected duty weighing on her mind through it all.

It was only when Tia parried Vir’s next blow, using the momentum to launch a thrust of her own that she noticed the tears flying off her face.

It was only then that she saw the cracks forming on her priceless blade. An irreplaceable treasure constructed by a race long extinct. The likes of which would never again be constructed.

The thought came through a thick haze. As though it were not her blade being damaged. Not her body fighting the duel of her life.

This is enough, isn’t it?

She’d fought to her fullest. Had enjoyed the best duel of her life.

What more could a Warrior ask for?

She made the decision then. The next strike… she wouldn’t block or parry or dodge.

In that moment, Princess Tiyana Matali accepted her death. Fully and truly, and when Imperium metal pierced flesh, she embraced it with open arms.

It was surprising, really, how painless the sensation was. The mark of a true master swordsman, she supposed.

Vir had ended her life with such skill that she’d hardly felt it at all. One final mercy.

The blood, however, was undeniable.

The blood… that was a shade too different. A shade too off.

Blood, she finally realized, that was not hers.

When she pulled out her blade, it was stained such a deep red, it was almost black.

Tia stumbled away in disbelief. Paralyzed with shock, unable to even form coherent words.

With the blade gone, the blood gushed from Vir’s wound with redoubled fury.

The Akh Nara faltered, sinking to his knees.

Tia dropped her blade in panic, rushing up to the dying god, grasping his shoulders.

“Why…” she gasped. “Why won’t you heal yourself!?”

“C-can’t,” he muttered, blood dribbling down his lips when he spoke. “Core… rupture.”

“No!” she muttered. “I… I…”

For a long moment, Tia simply held the Akh Nara, staring at her dying foe, her mind still refusing to come to terms with reality.

She was supposed to die here. The thought of her landing a mortal blow on a living deity was unthinkable! It was absurd! It was why she’d put every ounce of her strength into her every strike. The very thought that she might hurt him hadn’t once crossed her mind.

And yet, it was not her bleeding to death, but Vir. It wasn’t humankind rushing to her aid, but demons.

They’re too late, she knew. That as she eased him down to the ground, Vir was dying faster than should be possible. Could almost see his ruptured core containing all the prana of an entire realm eating him alive.

Tia didn’t know what to do. She couldn’t save him, and despite every bit of her heart yearning to do just that, she knew she couldn’t even if she wanted to.

No, there was only one thing she could do. Only one thing that would honor his sacrifice.

“W-well fought, Akh Nara,” she said in a voice that was not her own. That she forced herself to say in as commanding a voice as she could muster for the benefit of the two armies who watched on, even now. “As… as per our agreement, the war is concluded. I swear upon all the gods, as I swear to you and to Maiya’s memory… I will see your dream fulfilled. I… I will be the guiding light. I…”

Her body betrayed her, breaking down into a fit of wracking sobs, and she could say no more.

“I… know,” Vir rasped between hacking coughs, his voice barely more than a whisper. “You will…”

“But the core!” Tia pleaded, not trying to staunch the stream of tears that flowed freely down her face. “All is lost now!”

To this, Vir replied with the smallest of smiles. “The core… it would seem… I simply needed to die.”

“What? What are you… I don’t understand!”

Tia shook Vir, hoping beyond hope to get another response. Even a single word.

Her hopes went unanswered.

For the Akh Nara had breathed his last, and his lips would never utter another word again.

Tia watched with hopeless dread as whatever hold he had on the core failed and a maelstrom of pure blackness enveloped her, taking her before she even registered the pulse of absolute prana.

She couldn’t have known that her more-than-decade of training in the Ash allowed her to survive that lethal wave. Or that the prana core, working outward from Vir’s chest, consumed every last morsel of his flesh.

Nor did she see that, upon finishing with his body, the prana of the Ash did not explode but rather folded in upon itself. Bending, then breaking the very tapestry that held all of reality together until it changed in form entirely.

Not back to flesh, or even to affinity prana… but to something that ought to have been impossible by every rule of the world.

For the prana of a realm that no longer existed turned entirely into Chakra, and of a form the realm had never seen before.

When it was done, no trace of Vir’s body remained, and two hundred thousand souls stood in stunned silence, their resolves broken.

Humanity had won. Demonkind’s war was over.

The realm had survived.

And the Akh Nara, like Maiya before him, was gone.
