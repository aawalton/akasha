Risha stared up at the lights, high in the ceiling of the underground. Siyah got off the carriage behind her, looking both directions before turning to Risha who still stared at the distant lights.

“Lady Risha?”

Risha looked down to see the same dwarf who’d delivered her invitation.

“If you would follow me?”

Risha looked past the dwarf, to the large castle she’d only seen from a distance. Up close, the architecture was even more impressive, reminding Risha of Shyanne’s home in the council of mothers.

“Lady Risha?”

Risha focused back on the dwarf, and smiled wide, “That’s me. I like your lord’s home.”

“Ah, thank you. Please, follow me.” The Dwarven woman turned towards the entrance, and the two of them followed her in.

The entrance was made up of several curved arches, leading through large blackstone walls

Risha passed by several important looking people, but didn’t look at them too long as the lights of several crystals lit up a beautiful entryway, a central water fountain feeding several small streams with small bridges over them. Clear crystal windows covered the front of a home, even more people in all kinds of colors walked behind the windows.

Their guide led them over the bridges and up to an open door, where servants, all of them demons, walked through the growing crowd holding food and drinks.

Risha thanked a demon as she grabbed some kind of pastry from their tray. She glanced at her companion.

Siyah had a sad look on her face as she looked over the crowd, an emotion Risha could understand. She was the only Demon guest. Risha was also the only Goblin guest, but the difference was clear.

The dwarven woman stopped at the door leading into a large hall, then announced to the gathered, “Announcing Lady Risha from the Monster Empire, and her guest Siyah.”

More gazes found Risha, watching her, judging, calculating. Risha ignored them, instead meeting the eyes of the most important dwarf in the room.

He sat on a throne, his clothes sparkled in the light, many tiny crystals spiraling up his arms, replacing sleeves. His skin, visible under the spiraling crystal, had the metallic sheen that Risha was beginning to recognize as common among dwarves here. His black eyes, shining like onyx, met hers. Risha immediately felt on guard, seeing something in those eyes that bothered her. His spirit was expertly hidden from her gaze with technique she was surprised to see. Finally, her gaze fell to the sword at his waist, sensing the power in it.

The dwarf smiled, then stood, those gathered around him quieting as he made his way over to Risha.

He stopped several feet from her and inclined his head ever so slightly, “Lady Risha, it is my honor to have you sharing my hearth.” His voice rumbled, deep and dark.

Risha smiled, doing her best to hide her wariness of the dwarf. “Risha greets the Dwarven Lord. I was surprised by your invitation.”

The dwarf offered her a hand, and Risha stared at it for a long second before realizing he wanted her to take it.

She placed her hand in his, her clawed and callused hands in strong contrast to the metallic and smooth skin under them.

The dwarf pulled her in the direction of his throne, “When a mysterious visitor from the Nation Of Monsters visits, it would be… remiss of me to not invite them. I’ve heard many things about your Empress.”

“She is a strong and wise goblin.” Risha agreed. Risha wondered if he truly didn’t know who she was, or if this was some political game she didn’t understand.

“You know her well, then?”

Risha glanced at the dwarf, then smiled, “We grew up in the same village.” Risha felt very proud of the trickery in her words, until she glanced back to see Siyah standing where she’d left her awkwardly.

Risha motioned for the demoness to join them, and the dwarf beside her didn’t miss the motion.

“You are very close to the demons, I see.”

“They have served the Mother well.”

“I heard differently.”

Risha narrowed her eyes at the dwarf as they stopped at his throne, and he sat down, letting go of her hand. “What did you hear?”

“That they killed and tortured your people, kept them as slaves, like the brutes they are.”

“And you think yourself better?” Risha felt the silence that came after her words. There were more ears listening to their conversation than she realized.

The dwarf leaned forward, his polite smile disappearing, “What do you mean by that?”

“You think yourself better than the demons?”

He watched her for a long moment before answering, “Than those demons? Yes.”

“What about your servants? Do you think you’re better than them?”

The dwarf leaned back, the crystals on his arm clinking against his throne’s armrests. His expression was now a frosty glare, “I am a dwarf lord.”

“Ah, sorry. Risha misspoke. Risha is bad at politics.” Risha stood tall, as she placed her hands behind her back. “What Risha really wonders is why the Demons are kept as servants? Why are there more starving and homeless Demons in the streets than any other people? Why is my friend the only Demon guest?”

A drop of water could be heard from the front as not a soul moved. Siyah stood frozen midstep, halfway across the floor to them.

The Dwarf stood, and for the first time, Risha saw the tiniest flare of his spirit. It was brief, but it confirmed a fear of hers ever since she’d met his eyes.

“You dare insult me, when I invite you into my home? To share the food of my hearth?”

Risha didn’t move as the dwarf’s presence expanded, pressing against the room. He was powerful, but [Pack Leader: Black] kept her standing as she met his eyes.

She tilted her head, then showed her sharp teeth, “Risha didn’t know asking questions was an insult.”

“Listen well, goblin. Demons are murderous and treacherous brutes, I have seen them murder, torture, and enslave. Even their Mother was a blood-crazed, murderess grave-digger. When they have power, they abuse it. It. Is. Their. Nature.”

Risha stared into the burning eyes of the zealous Dwarf.

“You’re wrong.”

The words didn’t come from Risha, but instead from the demoness with broken horns. Risha turned to the young demoness, her smile widening as Siyah glared at the dwarf.

“I have seen the worst of my people, Lord Einar.” Siyah’s hand rose to her broken horns, drawing the gaze of every guest in the room. “I have also seen their kindness. I have spoken to the beggar on the street, who remembers to call me Sister, and offer me a gift, when he has nothing. I have seen a Demon take my whippings for me, die for me. When all he knew was the terror of our leaders, he still had more kindness than I have ever known. My kind are not evil, evil was thrust upon us when we were given nothing. When you turned us from your homes at our weakest. Brutality found us, when it was our only way to survive, when we were hunted by the races of the surface world, when our mother died alone, her supposed allies having abandoned her.”

“You stupid girl-” The Dwarf stepped forward.

“Do not speak to my guest like that.” Risha’s soft voice stopped the dwarf in his tracks as she slowly unleashed her spirit, pressing back against his own oppressive presence. “If it is even you that speaks, Lord Einar.”

The Dwarf turned towards her, his hand going to the sword at his waist, “What-”

“Do not think you can hide from my gaze, madness.”

The spirit of the Dead God flared, filling the dwarf as it turned her gaze to her. It didn’t speak as she stared at it, and it stared at her.

“I don’t know how you escaped, but your corruption will fade.” Risha told the Echo of the Dead God.

The room froze yet again, but this time for a different reason, as the presence of the god descended upon them.

Risha fell to her knees, caught off guard by the pressure of its power. Then it disappeared.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about, Goblin Shaman.”

Risha stared up at the possessed dwarf, surprised at the sudden calm of the creature.

A demon servant carrying a tray walked up beside Risha, and set his hand on her shoulder. “You are one tricky creature, aren’t you.”

The dwarf stepped back, recognizing the Demon who stood over Risha.

Risha looked up as Damien’s protective presence covered her. She met his gaze as his veins glowed with white power. “Risha, grab your people and leave. If you’re saying what I think you are, then you need to warn the other Mothers. I’ll meet you on the path to your city.”

Risha slowly stood, and looked at the wary dwarf, who watched them both with a gaze that wasn’t his.

“What do you mean?” Risha asked.

“The Dwarven Mother is approaching at a speed unusual for her, and this dwarf isn’t the Echo’s main host. You realize what that means, don’t you?”

Risha stepped back in realization, then looked at Damien. “Will you be alright? I can fight with you.”

Damien shook his head, “Not here, not now. I’ll be alright, just go.”

Risha nodded, then turned from the Demon. “Mist-Prowler!”

A Dark Elf stood, then shimmered, turning into a familiar coyote.

“Gather the others, we’re leaving.”

The coyote bowed, then ran from the room. Risha made her way through the stunned and confused guests, all of whom parted in front of her as Damien’s gaze held the Dwarven Lord in place. Risha’s hand grabbed Siyah, and pulled her towards the exit.

It was time to leave Freerala.
