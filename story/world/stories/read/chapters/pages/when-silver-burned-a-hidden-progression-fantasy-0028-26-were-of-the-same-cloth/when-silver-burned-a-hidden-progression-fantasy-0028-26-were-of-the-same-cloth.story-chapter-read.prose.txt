He joined the crowd of recruits dispersing after another exhausting day. Some faces were still unfamiliar to him, but he could spot a few of his acquaintances well enough. Ringtail idly twirled a long piece of dried grass as she listened to Dweller, the stoic woman easy to notice due to her height. Fals was animatedly gesturing in the company of two recruits who'd arrived the previous morning—Crux and Shoreline, he thought the lieutenant named them. Joe, an average looking middle-aged man Kalsin had only swapped a few words with, stepped sharply around Kalsin when their paths crossed. Kalsin gave him a cheerful smile. He didn't fault the man for avoiding him. He'd do the same thing if he ever happened to cross himself.

Kalsin left the yard through the stone arch and made his way to their bunks. It's been a while since he had drawn, and the peaceful company of his pen sounded downright divine just about then. With recruits about to swarm the dining hall to catch up with their neglected stomachs demands, their sleeping quarters would be calm for the next hour or half. Or so he figured.

The first thing he heard was the rhythmic thumps growing louder as he drew near their bunks. There was a regularity to it, a wooden drumbeat accented by an idle second, followed by another beat, looping indefinitely. Though he'd been a hunter for most of his years, Kalsin had never come across any hauntings…well, except for that strange occurrence in Atlas Nevor’s office.

He instinctively glanced behind him, then faced back toward the sound. If it ended up being the same unsettling woman without the face, then today he would see if ghosts could bleed.

When Kalsin peeked around the corner and into the barracks, the reason for the racket became evident in the form of a very flesh-and-blood man. All of a sudden, the name for one of his fellow recruits made a whole lot more sense.

Shutter sat on his bed, his hand wrapped around the handle of his nightstand's drawer as he pulled it out, waited a second in intense reverence, then slid the piece of furniture back in place. He pulled it out again, starting the process all over as if the activity was the very height of entertainment. His motions were smooth and practiced, and strangely familiar. Almost verging on intimate.

Under normal circumstances Kalsin would've cut a quick retreat. Nope, away and out of there. Strange habits and odd behavioral patterns were complications he didn't need in his life. He almost turned on his heels to let Shutter have his peace—but then thought better of it.

Eh, why the hell not. Him and these people, they would be stuck together for the better part of an unforeseeable amount of time. Better to rip off the bandage sooner rather than later.

It spoke volumes to the strength of his trance that Shutter failed to notice the double-scarred man until Kalsin plopped down on the opposite bed, just an arm's reach away. When he did, Shutter jumped, nearly pulling the contents of his entire nightstand on the floor.

"Whaagaahsorhmph—"

The garbled words pouring out of the recruit might've formed something resembling speech had Kalsin not interrupted.

"Well damn. If everyone managed to find joy in things so simple, theaters would be bankrupt right now. I'm jealous."

Shutter's face was red, but at least he stopped making unintelligible sounds. Taking a long look at Kalsin—probably wondering if he was being mocked—the reclusive man slowly pushed the drawer closed. He wiped his hand in his bedsheets, then glanced at Kalsin again. "I'm not crazy," he said quietly. His tone was subdued, as if trying to make up for the noise from moments ago.

"No, you definitely are," Kalsin said.

Shutter tensed up like a cocoon, but Kalsin pressed forward before the shell could manifest. "You're crazy. A nutjob. A right and proper lunatic for sure. And like I said, I'm still jealous of you."

"That makes no sense."

"It doesn't?"

"No."

Kalsin reached out toward Shutter. The man flinched away, but the fear on his face turned to bewilderment as he watched Kalsin pull out the same drawer. Kalsin took his time, trying to dedicate all his focus on the motion, the way wood scraped against wood, the smooth contact of the polished handle pressed against his palm. He slid the drawer back in place, then pursed his lips. "Shit, just as I thought. It doesn't work for me."

At this point Shutter was past confusion. His expression was stuck between relieved and offended, as if his brain had failed the laborious job of figuring out which one was appropriate for the situation.

"Hold that thought," Kalsin said. He turned away, rummaging around his own storage space for a minute to finally emerge with his notebook in hand. He pulled out the pen attached to the back of the leather casing, opened at a seemingly random page, and started sketching. He let out a relieved sigh. "Aaah. Much better."

Long seconds passed.

"I've never seen a pen like that," Shutter said eventually. A somewhat awkward attempt to gain back some vestige of comfort after a distressing experience. A rookie diversion, if you will. But a valiant effort for someone clearly not versed in the annoying art of small-talk, regardless.

"Hm, this pen? Crafty little design. The coal tip is coated in an, eh, unknown substance. I bought it a few years back when visiting Neardawn. To be fully honest, I couldn't tell you the technicalities. I just pop the lid off, replace the coal-rod and voila, the thing works like a whipped mule." Kalsin tilted his head to observe his drawing from a different angle, then continued. "Cost me two months' haul in coins. That's another point in your favor; your method of unwinding seems a lot cheaper on the wallet."

"It's not, I mean, I don't..." Shutter started. He took a deep breath, swiping his hands again. "I'm having some trouble here. Are you flagging me as insane or not?"

"Why does it matter if I think you're insane or not?"

"Matters because people who draw the same conclusions you just did, they tend to attach a good amount of hate with the package," Shutter said, his flat tone almost concealing his dread. Dread that could be only born from first-hand experience.

Kalsin nodded, still not looking up from his work. It was a messed up truth, but the young chap was right. Normal people—normal being a relative term—would either distance themselves from people with irregular patterns of behavior, or they would target them with harassment, verbal, physical, or both. Sometimes it might even be warranted. In Shutter's case though, well, opening and closing objects was hardly harmful for anyone, save for some wooden furnishing. He was a harmless category of crazy that Kalsin was all too familiar with.

"Alright, enough of that. As much as I enjoy teasing the clueless; no, I don't think less of you now than I did an hour ago."

"That's a nice way to say you thought little of me beforehand."

"Aaaahnd that's not what I said, but anyway, shit, let me get to the point. I'm trying to be sincere here." Kalsin paused, made a final stroke, then directed his full attention toward the man sitting in front of him. "Okay. You shut things. You get a rush out of it. For horsenut’s sake, so what? I mean really, who the hell should give a fuck? Emphasis on 'should'. Sure, had it been someone other than me finding you today, it might've been the start of an ugly string of unjustified hatred. But I'm letting you know that's not the case. I try not to hold grudges against people who are different from me. If I did, I couldn't take a step outside without risking a brawl."

"Oh," Shutter said. Though his feathers still seemed ruffled, he was starting to calm down. "Well. Right then." Another awkward silence followed. "So uh, you're any good with that pen?"

Kalsin didn't reply. Instead he turned the notebook toward Shutter, showing him the page he'd been working on.

"It's... just random lines?" Shutter observed with a frown of surprise.

"See? My motions aren't all that different from your own."

His 'drawing' was, indeed, a jumbled mess of criss-crossing lines. Sure, he could've drawn a lifelike sketch of Shutter's priceless expression, but that wasn't the point he was trying to make right now. He didn't want to flaunt his skill. He only wanted to show that useless habits could always be useful to the right person. If for nothing else, then for peace of mind.

It spoke to Shutter's intelligence that he understood without having to spell it out. Kalsin wouldn't give him a hard time for his antics, having gone as far as to show a slice of kinship.

"Good," Shutter said. "We're okay then?"

"Don't get cozy now. Just because I'm not one to discriminate doesn't mean I don't think you're weird as shit," Kalsin smiled.

From this day onward, though Shutter still retained his quiet demeanor, he was visibly more at ease in this new environment. Maybe Kalsin's act of solidarity was only for show, a fabrication. Maybe it was a genuine attempt to reach out to a kindred soul.

His memory of one small bedside conversation would eventually fade as time pressed on, unable to ever recall the words he'd extended.

But perhaps it doesn't matter. In that moment, he had given hope.
