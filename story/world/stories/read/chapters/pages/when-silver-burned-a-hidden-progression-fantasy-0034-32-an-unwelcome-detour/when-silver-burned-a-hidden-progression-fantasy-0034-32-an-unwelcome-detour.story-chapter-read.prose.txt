The late-morning sun, as it crept across the sky somewhere south-east of the city of aVonal, found the newest batch of recruits dictating a heavy pace. Arranged in a string of gray, the congregation strutted along the cleared path, bags of stuffed together armor plates clinking and their horses beating up a cloud of dust at their backs. Having passed the last hills several days ago, the distant mountains only served as a margin between the sky and the frigid expanse to remind them, when they looked back, the place they'd left behind.

Nearing the outer edge of the Republic, it was colder around these parts—though not by much. The earth was dark, nearly black with patches of red and vivid orange grass sprouting here and there. Water gathered in small puddles, like haphazard holes in cheese. Wildlife was sparse, and it was only by chance that Kalsin had spotted a pika—a hamster-like little critter—nesting in a hole. But the most notable difference from the rest of the Republic were the fractures.

In Kalsin's experience, fractures had always been two, maybe three shoulders’ wide rivers used for a large array of purposes; transportation with boats or bahten, fishing, resource gathering, et cetera. Useful things. Vital things, even. But here, fractures had narrowed down to palm-sized little streams that could easily break your ankle if you accidentally stepped inside one. The damned things gave Kalsin an odd sense of claustrophobia, as if they would snap closed the moment you decided to wash your hands in them. According to one of the soldiers escorting the recruits, these streams were too bottomless, like any other fracture. Kalsin took his word for it. He wasn't about to run personal experiments.

Thankfully, the road they followed was free of such pitfalls, allowing them to fully concentrate on whatever their latest pastime happened to be. Marching was boring as seven shits, meaning they had to make their own fun. The cycle went like this; Fals started a song or two in the morning before others joined in, shouting until vocal chords ran dry, so they moved on to telling stories from home by noon. Riddles and guessing games followed soon after, which were enjoyable enough until they fast deteriorated into incoherent bickering. Then finally, much needed silence.

They were at stage three right about then.

"Leaves. Has it any leaves?" Crawl asked.

"No and no. Already told you it's not a plant," said Shutter.

"You say that, but you haven't seen my neighbors' sister. Gal had ears so long, me and my buddies struck up a whole plan to paint them green when the traveling herbalist came to town, and bargain her away as a living-breathing aloe plantation."

Shutter, walking between Crawl and Kalsin, sent the latter a look that was part melancholy and part cry for help.

"How did it go?" Kalsin asked, curious.

Crawl grimaced. Red grass hanging from the corner of his mouth, his expression would've looked abstractly gruesome as a painting. "Like I said, it was a plan. Never came to life. We were still ironing out the details when she ran off with some rich merchant's son. Love at first jingle of the coin pouch."

"Uck, my condolences."

"I believe we call that 'fucked lucked' around here," Pike said from one row behind, a chortle of repressed mirth by Batshit and Youthere next to him.

"Not funny," Crawl muttered. "Swear, one of these days, the good lieutenant's parrot is gonna wake up with rope over his beaks."

Empty threats, but Kalsin diffused on reflex anyway. "Not something I recommend. That parrot has one scary halbird."

For his efforts, Kalsin received a couple of groans, as well as something awfully reminiscent of a facepalm from Dweller up ahead.

The staccato beat of horses drew the attention of the recruits, but the sound was coming from the side rather than the back. Kalsin saw four riders approach their group, going straight to the front of the line to intercept the lieutenant. The line of recruits came to a halt as Brownweather spoke with one of the riders. Kalsin was too far away to hear what was being said, but had a perfect view of the grimace on the lieutenant's face.

Crawl looked at Kalsin. "Say, if a bad person thinks the news are bad, does it mean they're actually good?"

"Could be. Either that, or said bad news are so monstrously bad, in fact, that they manage to further bruise even the most rotten of hearts."

What the spectacle was all about remained a secret. Without a single word barked at his men, Brownweather changed direction at the head of the column, and led the entire group off the dirt road. They were still heading east, but slightly diverging to the north.

"A detour?" Kalsin asked. The walk resumed now towards an unknown destination.

Shutter was squinting back and toward the east, down the long stretch of road they had been following. "The Clenn are still on the original route. They're not changing directions."

Similar comments rose from the crowd in the coming hour. Sour comments, questioning. The long weeks of constant marching had worn out the recruits to a point where the looming trials had become a hopeful prospect in a get-it-over-with sort of way. Endless stories told about the Veiled had filled their days and nights, the invaders painted as ruthless killers in red that just kept coming no matter how many you killed. According to the soldiers, that's what waited for them in the Outer Line of Defense, and the recruits had developed a collective sickness of being told how miserable they were going to be. Just give them a sword and let them face the real thing already. At least defending, the enemy would come to you and not the other way around. Fucking blistered feet.

But first they had a celebration to look forward to in Fort Ambition. At least, that's where they've been heading before the riders intercepted them. Now? Who the hell knew now. The Inner Line was a long stretch of land with dozens of forts and settlements that housed the bulk of the army—all the Clenn soldiers, as well as veteran Scarrd too valuable to throw to the wolves—so there was no real way of knowing where Brownweather was leading them.

They found out soon enough.

It was midafternoon when they spotted the buildings in the distance, a blob of stark gray dropped against the reddish landscape. With low walls and a much wider radius, the place resembled more of a fortified encampment than a fortress or garrison with longevity in mind.

"Righto," Crawl said. "That's a far scream from the festive fort we were promised. See that over there?" he pointed, an unnecessary gesture. Even from this far out, they could all clearly make out the gaping hole that had reduced a section of the wall to nothing but rubble and black dirt.

Before the recruits could boil up their own conclusions, the lieutenant halted the line and shouted until he had their attention. It took a second or two until the last person—Fals, probably—quieted down and set their eyes on the officer.

"Listen here," he said. "You're looking at a two-day fresh deed of an unknown mythos. The thing tore through the walls and rampaged across half the encampment before, by its own devils-given volition rather than by the hands of the locals, it left as quick as it arrived." Judging by the ruined wall, the mythos must've been at least five foot wide. Without conscious effort, Kalsin mentally ran through a list of his known creatures, ticking off a few as possibilities while dismissing others. He then realized what he was doing and scolded himself; he wasn't a hunter anymore.

Brownweather continued, "Good news is, you worthless lot are gonna have the chance to make yourself worth something before you're all slaughtered. Now, instead of drinking away your woes in Fort Ambition, you'll be lending a helpful hand around this here settlement. Things needs doing; hoarding rubble, cleaning rags of the injured, hammering nails to rebuild foundations and whatever-else-fuck. Physical labor. Got it? Got it, sir, we understand, sir. Good. Get to it, then."

A dark murmur of protests started to rise from the recruits, but the lieutenant ignored it. "Oh, and welcome the newest member in your sorry ranks," he said over his shoulder, nodding his head toward one of the riders, a blonde woman with an unslung bow across her saddle. "She's trained in archery, meaning, she's about ten times more valuable than the sum of you. Also meaning, her name is her own business. Else if she's keen on solidarity, just call her Wardoll."

Kalsin watched the newest recruit dismount, though he couldn't make out much from afar. Another late arrival. He didn't like that, wasn't keen on newfound questionmarks scrabbling themselves into his planbooks with the possibility to muck everything up.

His mates, however, had other things to be displeased about.

"Hope that ancient fossil-bitch chokes on his food," Crawl said. Harsh words from the redhead, spoken with, maybe for the first time Kalsin had known him, genuine anger. "This has to be a joke."

Though Shutter didn't curse, his frown spoke enough. "Does he have the authority? I thought the celebrations in Fort Ambition were a tradition etched in stone."

"Clearly not," Kalsin said, but his voice was pretty much lost in the shitshow of cursing all around him. The orderly line had broken up and reformed into volatile circles of righteous frustration. Brownweather, the sole catalyst to their anger, had done the smart thing and quickly disappeared inside the intact part of the encampment. He'd given the orders, and with or without protests, they'd have to do it.

The loudest curses were coming from Fals, who, amazingly enough, had both Ringtail and Cyanfish struggling to calm her. "...Kill him! Let me the fuck go, you, I will! I swear I will..."

Kalsin wanted to smile as he went to her side, but he held his expression clear. "Squeeze out his eyeballs, or kick a hole in his stomach and stomp his guts out his back?"

Cyanfish shot a perplexed look at Kalsin, one that indicated his antics weren't helping. But just as Kalsin hoped, the question knocked Fals from her rage for a moment long enough to actually think about the answer. "Both," she said.

"Or," Kalsin nodded toward the ruined wall, "We could get done what needs to be done. Get it done quickly enough so that, when the day is over, we can give a collective central finger to our dreaded lieutenant and his bowels-gotten orders."

"And then what?" Ringtail asked with a frown.

"Then, hopefully, we get to make our own celebration."
