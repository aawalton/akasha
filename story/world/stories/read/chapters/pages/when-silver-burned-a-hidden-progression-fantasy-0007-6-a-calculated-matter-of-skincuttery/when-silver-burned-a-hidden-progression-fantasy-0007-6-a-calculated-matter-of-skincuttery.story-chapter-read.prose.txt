That night, Kalsin hadn't slept well. Time. He figured they had a few odd pieces going in their favor, but time wasn't a resource they had in abundance. It had taken Sinew nearly a week to get back to them, but he had, and told them the meeting was to take place on the morrow around a sparsely populated part of town, inside an out-of-use limner's workshop. The informant having served his purpose, Kalsin had burned Sinew’s drawn portrait in candle flame and they parted in consensual hopes of never crossing lanes again.

Kalsin stepped outside, somber, watching the streets. It was always the same with the month ending and about to roll into Blindlight; as if a relative's half-forgotten day of birth had suddenly come, citizens crowded the roads to make last-minute preparations and shake their businesses in order before the cold sun had a chance to surface and wash the world in uniform white. Kicked up dust welcomed and accompanied Kalsin as he weaved through the throng. Above him, the faithful chanted their chosen god's revelations, standing atop stacks of hoarded deep-fisher's crates and urging the crowds to donate and repent—in that exact order.

Kalsin followed along the base of Midlane until the fractures that framed the wall bent and disappeared westward. From there, he joined a maintenance road running next to smartly arrayed warehouses and leading farther away from residential areas. The crowds fell away.

After what felt like a stretched hour, he spotted the abandoned workshop. He scanned his surroundings and what he could see from the outside, confirming he was yet alone. Uncorking the vial he had bought in the pitfall, he poured a lick of the colorless liquid into his palm, then started rubbing it all over his skin. His face, arms, chest under his shirt all received a generous amount of the cold substance, but still less than he'd have preferred. With a hint of nervousness he poured a few more doses, eventually emptying the vial to the last drop. He smelled his balmed hands. Nothing. Had to be good enough.

The inside of the limner's workshop was a far leap from the unremarkable exterior, but he had no time to gawk at the half-covered paintings, the extravagant curls of unfinished codex pages.

The informant hadn't played nice. Armed men were waiting for him.

Not a moment after the door fell shut, a blade tickled the back of Kalsin's neck. Three crossbows leveled on him, held by three neatly dressed men in civilian clothing. And sitting on a foldable chair was none other than the collector of Remallon, the moss-green frock coat of his office spread about his sides as he shuffled through a clipped bundle of papers. He glanced up, his clean-shaven face blank as he assessed Kalsin.

He was in the fingers of the region-capital’s Clenn law enforcement.

Kalsin didn't move a muscle, becoming acutely aware of his drumming heart and the cold steel resting against his skin.

The collector waved a hand, one of his men replying with a nod and quickly moving toward the back entrance only to disappear outside. A scout, most likely, making to sweep the perimeter to watch and see if he'd come alone. Good. No more than a single man should be fine.

The collector addressed Kalsin. "Please hold out your hands and turn around, if I may be so brazen. Slowly, slowly. Yes, just so. Good man. Now, my associate here is going to restrain you. Relax, you're in good hands, nothing overt."

The slender woman who'd held the blade at Kalsin's neck secured a firm rope around his wrists in quick order, confiscated his sword, and laid out another chair for him facing the collector, all done in a brisk and systematic manner. Kalsin moved carefully, and he didn't have to fake the fear that coursed through his veins. He sat.

Sweat formed on his forehead. They didn't expect him to struggle, and he didn't struggle because he didn't expect them to kill him. He went into this whole godsforsaken affair based on that expectation. It would be an awful shame if he was wrong.

"Let us share a drink. I despise talking on a dry throat," the collector said, pulling out a bottle from the depths of his frock coat. Not waiting for an answer, he poured its contents into a pair of small glasses resting on the floor at his feet, spilling only the tiniest amounts on the wooden floor boards. "Aqua vitae. You ever had a chance to sample it? No, I imagine vitae doesn't make rounds in Scarrd circles. Strong stuff, though this one has a prime hint of caramel to it."

Kalsin accepted his glass using both his restrained hands and took a sip. Strong didn't begin to cover it, promptly setting his throat aflame. The collector jugged his whole drink down in a single pull.

"Is there a reason why I'm being restrained, Collector..."

"Quin. You're at my service, Quin Bolewár. You may cut the collector honorific. As for the here and now, you're looking at... Well. A frank, lighthearted chat while you've still got your wits strung tight. Then we'll move onto interrogation, and then torture, and then disposition of your body. That's how it usually works with heretics."

Kalsin couldn't stop himself from straining against the rope a bit, testing its limits. They thought he was a heretic, an elusive group of Scarrd who, allegedly, knew about the existence of a cure. Elusive enough that Kalsin had been trying to get a hold of them for years. He was hoping it'd be the heretics Sinew contacted, but seems the rot found it easier to simply report him at the leechouse, the collector's office.

Ironically enough, Kalsin's one advantage was the fact that his jailors didn't know that he didn't know anything.

Therefore, he had everything to learn.

Kalsin drew himself up. "Heretic? I would never stray from the teachings of Tűzmother, warmth-giver, the eye who keeps its target in sight and the necessary known—"

"Religion has nothing to do with heresy, as I'm sure you're well aware. Worship away at your leisure." Quin took Kalsin's near-full glass and downed it with a flick of his wrist. He let out a content sigh. "See, I was a little rascal of a kid. I remember when we played tower—you know, a tower made of rectangular blocks is given. When it's your turn, you carefully extract a single block and place it on top, and the condition of defeat is the tower collapsing."

Quin raised the glass again, only to realize it was empty. "When we played, my mother always tried to remove the most crucial pieces, the lower the better. We sat there waiting for a minute, two, five minutes as she kept prodding, poking, making the tower swing and collapse more often than not. Then one day, I had enough of her stalling, and just before she could pull the block, I smashed the whole thing apart. She asked me why I did it. I said she would have ruined it anyway. You know what her next words were?"

Kalsin shrugged as much as his restraints allowed him.

"She said, 'Quin, don't be silly. The only way to stop the tower from collapsing is if you refuse to play.' From that moment onward, I never played tower again." He rotated the empty glass, as if holding the container of a long-lost secret. "You see, the tower's balance is perfect to begin with. It's a functioning hierarchy where every element, from top to bottom, must remain in place. And the lower the piece, the more it can disrupt the balance when it shifts from its allocated spot."

Kalsin didn't need a scholar's degree to see from a league away where the monologue was going.

"Humanity itself is the tower, and you, the Scarrd, are its very foundation. Unappreciated, but certainly essential. You're the mud under our boots, swiped into the rug like a forgotten nuance left on our doorstep. We wash our clothes to be rid of you, sometimes we confuse you with dogshit, but ey, we can hardly walk around with nothing to step on."

"You know how to make a man feel appreciated," Kalsin said.

"What do you imagine would happen if they found a way to cure scars? To grant the opportunity for the pieces at the bottom to rise? None would be content with their position; the lowly would squirm, shift, strain to climb the ladder while the top would use every tool necessary to keep them down, digging the hole under themselves. The Ember Republic would crumble to pieces—while mythos ravage the populace and the Veiled keep invading the eastern border. Can't let that happen."

Kalsin had more personal concerns than the fate of the Republic. His face must've said as much. Quin stared at him, shrugged and tossed the glass over his shoulder. "Let's get started. Xil, dear, shall we?"

The woman who'd disarmed Kalsin had been waiting patiently at Quin's side. She unclasped a leather folder, presenting a collection of scalpels all with differing blades and sizes. The collector brushed a finger over the instruments. He considered one before moving onto the next. "Your heretic friends, the ones you're looking for. Tell me about them."

"Hmh, let's see," Kalsin said. "Friends. Last I checked I counted just about seven of them. Direct lads, if a little crude. Mostly keep to themselves, a group of four, a group of three and some distance in between. You might've heard of them. They go by 'fuck' and 'off'."

Quin smiled thinly, and though Kalsin put on a brave face, the dangerously gleaming razor in the collector's hand sent an arc of fear jolting through him. As did his next words.

"Jacqueline Thguon. Bodyguard for hire, dirt, scar on her jaw and a hopeless haircut. A childhood friend?" Quin listed. "People bring the most curious tidings to the leechouse. People talk, don't they? Ha, your Jackie might not, but when she's under the scalpel, I'm hoping you'll find yourself more willing to cooperate. It's just a matter of time before the underworld spits something out. It always is." His tongue sneaked out and back. "How many of you are in the city?"

Kalsin didn't answer. His eyes were set behind the collector and lodged onto an empty ivory lectern next to one of the crossbowmen.

The collector's backhand came from nowhere, rocking Kalsin on the chair and nearly making him topple. He flexed his aching jaw as he tasted blood from a bit tongue. It made him clench his teeth that much more desperately. Too early.

"Ugly little thing, is it not, Xil?" Quin asked, standing above Kalsin and leaning in.

"It is, collector," the woman answered from somewhere behind.

Kalsin shivered as Quin pressed a finger to his scar, pushing at it none too gently. "It is, ey? Rotten through and through, though I fail to see why the overworld acts like it's the plague. Squeamish bunch. I mean, come now. Peeling a dried fruit had never killed anyone." With those words, the collector furrowed his nails into the blackened cavity between Kalsin’s eyes, and scratched.

This time Kalsin couldn't stop a cry from escaping him as deep-rooted pain raked him. He violently pulled his head away and would've fallen, but he felt rough hands keeping him in place.

"Where did you gather? Who is the seb-hordozó?"

There you go. You can sing if you want to. Kalsin actually laughed out, intercepting a grunt on its passage out and mixing into an odd choking sound as a result. The collector might've taken that as encouragement, because he motioned for Xil to push Kalsin's shirt sleeve all the way up to his shoulder. "What did you mean to accomplish in Remallon? What do you know about the seb-hordozó? Who is it?"

Though he had been determined from the start, it wasn’t easy to watch as Quin, almost casually, swept the scalpel across his upper forearm. Skin parted in artful slowness and blood gushed from the surgically proportioned opening, sending a wave of agony through him. Kalsin glared at Quin, spitting the red gob that had pooled in his mouth and hitting the collector's frock coat with it.

The questions kept coming. "What's the destination of the azTél siblings? The hordozó, where is it?" As if following a secluded treasure map, the collector's blade always sliced with purpose, always making sure to stray from cutting too deep, and the twin-questions always mentioning this strange title.

Kalsin mumbled something, trying to stay lucid. A fog of merciful drowsiness descended on his mind and dulled the pain, steadily taking hold. He smiled as he felt the effects of the sedative starting to work.

You hear the strangest things among like minded professionals who spend their days hunting the non-human. Like, for example, the curious effects of nocnitsa tears when mixed with blood. Tears, courtesy of the vials he'd rubbed all over himself before entering this trap, and blood supplied by his own surely-to-be-tortured self.

As if on command, the back door burst open and flew through the building like a boulder from a catapult, hitting a crossbowman and sending him under with a crunch. Her large kite shield blocked most of Jack as she marched into the workshop, face covered by cloth from the nose down. She blocked the hastily fired bolt from the remaining crossbowman and she surged, barreling into him with enhanced speed and power. On impact with the shield's hard steel-plate, the man's head banged like a bell and he collapsed in a heap.

Kalsin wrenched his body sideways in the chair and kicked at the collector’s groin, scoring a solid hit on his kneecap to make him buckle. But he couldn’t stop Xil from rushing against Jack.

Or tried to rush. Because the airborne, odorless sedative had done its job; his torturers had been close to him, inhaling the weakening gas for minutes without knowing, without having an inkling about the cause of their reluctant wits and limbs. They could've had the field-leveling powers of the Arches and it wouldn't have mattered when keeping their eyes open was a battle itself.

Preparation and a good plan always beats outright strength and numbers.

Xil was on Jack in a heartbeat, moving with surge too quick to follow as the pangs of sword against shield rang out. In only a second they must've traded a dozen blows while depleting their surges in the short term, going back to regular melee. Jack's shorter sword lashed out from behind her shield, which Xil parried and countered, but could not find a gap in her defense.

Meanwhile, Quin just kept groaning on the floor as he stared at his hand, a glove of shifting earth coating his fist. That confirmed him being at least spark, overworld. Except his manifestation was winking in and out, pebbles raining and disappearing halfway to the floor. He made a faltering lurch toward the duel—and then promptly passed out. The combination of the airborne sedative and drunkenness must've resulted in an ugly relationship.

Unfortunately, Kalsin wasn’t faring much better. He was off the chair, but he could do little more than act out a sack of peeled potato’s live impression with his wrists tied together and arm bleeding all over the floor. He made sure to grab his sheathed sword, though.

Clenn or no, Xil alone wasn't a match for Jack. After another bout of surge, Jack used her superior mass to barrel past Xil's guard, then bashed her forehead into the woman's nose. There was a crunch, a thud of sword falling on wood, followed by that of a limp body.

Sounds went soft and distant as the edges of Kalsin's vision dimmed, but still he could make out murder on Jack's face as she stared at the unconscious city officials. Gasping, he strained out the words. "No, Jack. Please. No killing."

And with that, his mind finally winked out.
