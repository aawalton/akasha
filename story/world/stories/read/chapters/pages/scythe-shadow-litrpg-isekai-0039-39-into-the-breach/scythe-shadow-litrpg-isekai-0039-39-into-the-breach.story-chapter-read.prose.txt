I left Silas to go inform the city guard about the fate of his village and travelled toward the colosseum alone. Eugene greeted me cordially within the main lobby area. He appeared to actually be doing his job since Sibyl wasn't behind the registrar's counter working at the moment.

I had a few of the new items I'd collected appraised by the rather stern-looking man behind the counter. He spoke with a nasally air of superiority. I could see why Eugene had decided not to loiter around and converse with this appraiser. His personality. Yes, that was it…

Most of the items I received were duplicates of ones I'd used in the past, at least in terms of function. Every item appeared to be entirely unique, but the effects overlapped. A shield necklace, a stat boosting pill, among others.

The most useful turned out to be a stethoscope.

[Item: Stethoscope

Effect: Heals three major injuries]

I put them all in my inventory and left the line, only to find a small crowd had formed off to the side, staring at me like they thought I could revive their loved ones from the grave. Eugene held them back from costing me with an apologetic look.

Apparently, word was spreading about the Death Reaper…

I greeted the people, but wasted no time on pleasantries and traversed the stairs straight up to Valciferous' anteroom. I knocked twice on the elaborate door, then waited.

No answer.

On one hand, it was comforting to know that Trix didn't just lounge up here all day drinking wine. On the other hand, I had no idea how to summon Valciferous for a chat myself.

"Rather inconvenient, don't you think?" I asked Nuzzle.

He nodded, hopped down, and peed at the foot of the door.

"Good boy."

Back in the lobby, Eugene was waiting for me, having shooed off the crowd.

"Hey, Zephyr," he said, unable to hold back a wide smile. "I took your advice. I'm having dinner with Sibyl tomorrow!"

I clapped him on the shoulder. "Good on you! Told you she'd say yes."

"Yeah. Yeah… Now I just have to not screw it up. Tall order."

"Just be you. Have a little faith in yourself. And her."

"I'll try. Thanks again"

I started walking away, not entirely sure what to do next.

"Wait," Eugene called. "I do have an… uh… actual message for you as well."

"Perfect, let's hear it."

"Trix said she's investigating a recent Unchained attack in Talbot Village. Said if you showed up to wait here for her return."

"Talbot Village, huh? Alright. Thanks for the info. Enjoy your date tonight!" I winked at him and started to leave.

"Wait, where are you going?"

"Yeah… I'm not just gonna sit around here and wait. I'm going to Talbot Village."

"Do you think that's a good idea?"

"Absolutely. I'll see you later."

I left Eugene standing there, slightly dumbfounded.

Silas was already waiting for me at our agreed upon meeting place in the bazaar.

"I don't suppose you have a map of the Meadow?" I asked.

"What do you take me for? A fool?"

"Well… I don't have one."

"Exactly."

Silas procured the map from his inventory and I snatched it from his hand with a faux scowl. Talbot Village was to the east, almost up to the border of the next zone—a jungle biome if I recalled correctly.

"How long would it take to get here?" I asked Silas, pointing to the village.

"On horseback, almost a full day. And that's without stopping."

I hadn't realized the Meadow was so large. "I better get going then."

"Now?" Silas asked.

"There's someone I need to meet there. It's related to what we saw."

"Can it wait? I'll have to phase out soon. I can already feel my body wanting to rouse."

"Unfortunately, no. Stay in Meadowvale. I'll find you again after this and we can take the next steps together. We'll avenge your village. I promise."

Silas hesitated, clearly not keen on the idea of being left behind. "Alright… Good luck then." He shook my hand. "And take this to find me when you return."

I took the item he held out and added it to my own inventory.

[Monocle of Tracking, 1/2

Effect: Reveals a path to the companion Monocle of Trailing for 1 hour.]

Damn, this kid has gadgets for days. I silently wondered if all Reapers who'd been here awhile had a similar stash of goods. How many does Jethro have after thirty years?

"I'll see you soon," I said.

We both nodded and I was off.

It took more than a day to reach Talbot Village. I defeated half a dozen marked Amber Sins on the way and relinquished their Wills to continue bolstering my stats. Every little bit counted, even if Amber Wills didn't provide the boost they had before my advancement.

My mind wandered to Avery as I rode. I had no idea how long had passed in Vitria since Valciferous stole my shadow and my one ticket home. A day? Two? More? And where's my body? Probably moved back to a hospitably now…

It wasn't enjoyable to think about, but I couldn't prevent my mind from constantly wandering back to it, especially with little else to do alone on the back of a horse.

As I rode up to the village and dismounted, it was clear this place had resisted the Unchained attack it endured far better than Silas' village. A segment of the outer wall lay shattered and breached, but only a single building within was damaged. Whatever Reapers defended this place had apparently been able to handle the threat well enough.

In the distance beyond the village, the horizon was blocked by a wall that appeared massive, even from this distance. This was the first time I'd seen the barrier that separated the Meadow from the next zone with its high ranked Sins and new biome.

I tied my horse near a lantern at the edge of town within the wall, but didn't tap the glass. Even though I couldn't phase in or out, I'd leave Meadowvale as my 'home base,' so to speak.

Trix was easy to find—and not just because she drew the eye in that ridiculous leather attire and Sapphire cloak. She stood outside the tallest building in the village, speaking to another Reaper, who appeared to be in charge.

I strode up beside her, Nuzzle curled up on my shoulder.

She glanced, resumed her conversation, then did a double-take and stared back at me for a moment. "I… thought I left instructions for you to stay in Meadowvale…"

"If you wanted me to stay you shouldn't have told Eugene where you were going," I mused. Nuzzle stuck his tongue out, emphasizing the point.

Trix sighed and introduced the Amber Reaper she was with as Cornelius, fourth mayor of Talbot Village. She allowed the man to resume his work to briefly explain the situation to me.

"They were attacked a few days ago here. Two Amber Unchained Sins broke through the wall during the night but were easily taken care of by five Reapers living here. May be related to your task. May be a coincidence."

I nodded and lowered my voice. "I think there may be a way to find out…"

And so I told her everything Silas and I had seen in the cave, about the three Unchained I'd dealt with on the beach, and about the potential connection to the Shepherds.

"You think there's a cave here too?" Trix asked, understanding my thinking.

"If this is related to the people pulling the strings behind the Unchained activity, then yeah. I expect we'd find one not too far away."

"Guess we should start searching then," she said.

I shook my head. "On the contrary. In all likelihood the searching has already been done for us."

Trix gave me a confused look. I, in turn, cast my gaze to the children running in circles around a nearby flagpole, chasing one another and giggling.

"Kids always have a way of finding these kinds of things."

That statement turned out to be true. After only an hour of asking around, a pair of girls—and their Reaper father—led us out into the Meadow.

It continued to surprise me how many Reapers had settled down into normal everyday lives here. I stared at the father's back as we followed. Did he have an entirely separate family back in Vitria? Were these kids biologically his or adopted? The ramifications of the potential answers were staggering. But of course, I couldn't ask any of that. Too personal. Too invasive. Maybe I can pull some insight from Trix later.

The spot where the cave used to be, was now a misshapen patch of dirt in the side of a grassy hill.

"It was here, I promise!" one of the little girls whined to her father.

He patted his daughter on the head. "I believe you, little one." Turning to us he said, "I hope you find whatever you're looking for, but if it's all the same to you, I'm going to return the girls to the safety of the village."

"Of course," I said, before Trix could suggest otherwise. No sense putting others in danger. Her glare suggested she had more questions, but I was satisfied.

After our companions departed, I said, "It's been filled in. The other cave I found had clear markings that it wasn't natural. They're covering their tracks. They'll probably do the same with the other cave before too long."

Trix shrugged, hiding her satisfaction. "It's what I'd do."

"Got a shovel?" I asked. Silas probably would've had one. Or a stick of dynamite.

"I can do one better." She smirked, cracking her knuckles.

I watched as she thrust a hand to the ground. A large tree root swam through the grass toward the patch of dirt where the cave entrance had supposedly been. It split into dozens of smaller tendrils, forming an intricate web over the dirt. She clenched her fist, causing the roots to contract around a large chunk of dirt. She then lifted her hand, the root cage following suit, and dropped the contents off to the side leaving a spherical hole.

I nodded, impressed, as she repeated the process three more times. On the fourth scoop, she broke through into the cave system.

We entered, hesitantly, as before, though there was no need. This cave had been abandoned. Perhaps that was the reason they'd sealed it shut. A similar, albeit much smaller chamber than the other one I'd seen resided within, containing only four empty cages. There were no signs of Reapers or any recent activity.

This time though, instead of returning the way we'd come, we followed the path that continued on beyond the chamber, our way lit by a torch from Trix's inventory. The only sounds in the dark were our feet crunching on gravel, the faint dripping of water from damp soil above, and Nuzzle chirping and whistling. He danced around happily as the echoes bounced off the walls and returned to him.

A few minutes of travel found us standing before a dead end. A dead end, which had clearly been filled like the entrance we'd discovered.

Without a word Trix resumed her root cage digging. It took longer this time, the plug deeper and more packed, as if it'd been constructed with more purpose and in less haste.

When Trix finally broke through, streams of light burst into the cave along with the scent of freshly fallen rain. Outside, birds squawked and leaves rustled with movement of animals and breeze. The sound of running water trickled nearby. Trix stepped through the opening, silhouetted in the brilliant golden light of the non-existent sun.

I followed through the breach, stepping on a large teal fern. Flora of infinite varieties and nearly as many hues surrounded me. Long vines hung from branches. A group of monkey-like spirit creatures stared at us. Maybe a score of so of them.

We weren't in the Meadow anymore.

I turned around, looking the direction the Meadow should have been. Instead, an impossibly tall stone wall loomed over me.

Nuzzle scurried up to my head and whistled.

We were in the Jungle.
