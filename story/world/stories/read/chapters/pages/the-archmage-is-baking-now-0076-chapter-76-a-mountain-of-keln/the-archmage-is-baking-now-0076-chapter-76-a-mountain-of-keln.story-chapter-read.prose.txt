Jane cleared a space in the middle of the shop and had Brand run through the same exercises as the day before. She watched him closely, looking for signs of improvement.

She found them almost immediately.

"You're doing better," she said as Brand compressed a flame down to the size of a large marble. It was still bigger than her fingernail-sized demonstration, but it was more progress than she had hoped for.

Brand looked surprised. "I can feel it. It's easier than yesterday."

"You're talented. That helps.” Jane called up her own flame and began demonstrating a new exercise, one she remembered from her own training. She didn't mention to Brand just how young she had been when she first learned it. "Watch. You're going to split the flame into two flames, then control them independently."

They worked through more exercises after that, each one building on the last. Brand asked questions until he understood what each exercise was for and why it mattered. He struggled with some more than others, but he approached them all with determined effort.

He was a good student, Jane decided. It was his teachers who had failed him, though she decided not to dwell on that point.

By the time the light outside had shifted to the golden tones of early evening, Jane had run through most of what she wanted to teach him.

"That's it, then," she said. "If you master all of those exercises, you'll be in a great place. Better than most mages, honestly. It's just a matter of time and practice."

"And if I need more?"

"Ask your instructors directly. Now that you know what you're missing, they can help you fill in the gaps. And if all else fails, there's always the academy. They have people who specialize in exactly this kind of thing."

Brand nodded. "Thank you. Again. I know I keep saying it, but I mean it."

"It's nothing. Really." Jane led him towards the door. Then, through the window, she spotted a familiar figure coming down the street. "Actually… do you have anything to do right now?"

Brand considered. "Not particularly. My parents are attending some dinner with the town council. My attendance isn't required."

Jane opened the front door and waved. "Dave! Come here a moment."

Dave changed course and jogged over, his expression curious. "What's up? Need something moved?"

"Not exactly." She gestured toward Brand, who had followed her to the door. "Dave, this is Brand. I was hoping you might show him around the town a bit. He hasn't seen much of Glenfall outside of official events."

Dave's eyebrows rose as he processed who he was being asked to escort. He glanced at Brand, then back at Jane.

"Is that... all right?"

"I don't see any problem with it." Brand’s voice was easy, almost eager. "I'd like to see the town properly. If you don't mind."

Dave seemed to relax. "I don't mind at all. I’m new here myself, but I know some good spots."

"Lead the way."

Jane watched them walk off together. They were already talking as they went, apparently unbothered by the prince’s guards trailing at a discreet distance. Dave was gesturing toward something in the distance, and Brand was nodding along, asking questions. They looked, she thought, like two young men who might actually become friends.

Good, she thought, closing the door. That's good for both of them.

—

The next morning was easy in a way that went beyond easy. Jane woke at her usual time, dressed in her work clothes, and headed downstairs to fire up the ovens. She mixed dough for her loaves, made batter for cakes, and prepped cookies. Each turned out flawlessly, and Jane had them loaded onto her tables with little fuss well before her first customers came.

No messenger knocked on her door with a summons from the town council. No royal personage showed up in need of food or magic training. Not a single dragon reared its head to stop her.

It was a beautifully simple morning. The early wave of customers came in, buying what they needed for breakfast or placing dinner holds.

"Lovely showcase the other day," an older woman said as Jane wrapped her purchase. "Everyone on the street’s been talking about it."

"Thank you. I'm glad people enjoyed it."

"Oh, we did. I have a few folks who would love a class, if you’d ever teach one. We’d pay, of course."

Jane considered this. It did sound fun, if a bit involved for her life at the moment.

"Maybe. Let me think about it. I'd have to figure out when I'd have time."

The afternoon brought more of the same pleasant routine. A few customers asked about the showcase, and Jane answered their questions as best she could while trying to avoid the awkwardness of self-promotion. Most people seemed satisfied just knowing the king had eaten her food and found it acceptable, which was factual enough that she felt comfortable talking about it.

By late afternoon, her shelves were nearly bare. She had judged her quantities well, it seemed, something that was always hit-and-miss for her. A last few stragglers bought up the remaining rolls and loaves just before she closed, and then she was done for the day. Her delivery box took in all her remaining work and would hold it until the customers were ready to swing by for their food.

Jane spent the next hour cleaning her workspace, scrubbing down the counters and sweeping flour-dust from the floor. This was a chance to go for the most intense level of cleaning. Using a combination of normal tools and magical whirlwinds, she removed every speck of untidiness from her kitchen.

Allen didn't stop by. This was unusual, but not concerning. He was probably caught up in his own projects, especially with all the showcase preparation going on. Bella was likely busy as well, getting ready for her own turn with the royal family.

Jane decided that her best move was to leave them to it. If she was mistaken, they would find her. She was fairly confident she wasn’t, though, which meant she now had plenty of time to herself.

A night spent alone, then.

The thought was oddly appealing. She loved her friends and loved spending time with them, but there was something to be said for an evening of complete solitude. It was fun in a different kind of way.

She locked the door and headed upstairs, bringing some bread and cheese with her for an easy dinner in her room. She was the good kind of tired, the kind that came from productive, expected labor. It didn’t take long for her to be comfortably full and relaxed.

As the evening deepened into full night, Jane decided it was time for a proper bath. She let the water warm under the power of her magic as she looked over her growing collection of bath-things and decided which ones she would use that day. The water was perfect when she climbed in. She sank down until only her head was above the surface and let out a long, satisfied sigh.

This is what I needed.

By the time she was finished with the detangling shampoo, the conditioning oil, and a good long soak, she felt like a completely new person. She dried off, put on her most comfortable sleeping clothes, and climbed into bed, warm and already sleepy.

—

Morning came too soon, but Jane awoke feeling more refreshed than she had in days. She stretched in bed, taking a moment to appreciate how good she felt, then got up and started getting ready.

She had just finished dressing when she heard a knock at her door. Heading downstairs, she found Bella standing on her step.

My, but she looks frazzled.

"I need your help," Bella said without preamble. "My showcase is this morning, and I need someone who can help me keep up. First, can you make keln? A lot of it?"

Jane blinked. "How much is a lot?"

"As much as you can make in one cycle of your ovens." Bella pushed past her into the shop. "If that’s okay. And your help cooking, like I mentioned.”

"Of course I'll help." Jane immediately started gathering ingredients. "Why would I be helpful, though? You're a much better cook than I am."

"I am a better cook. I just need another pair of hands." Bella was already pulling out mixing bowls. "You know how it is. Sometimes you just need someone to handle the simple tasks so you can concentrate on what matters."

"That makes sense.” Jane started measuring flour. "Are you nervous?"

Bella shrugged. "A little. But there's nothing I can do about it now, except my best.”

"That's a good attitude."

"I hope so, because it’s all I have. Now come on. We need to make a mountain of keln, and we don't have much time."

"Tell me about the setup,” Jane said, rushing through prep for a ‘mountain’ of keln. “Is it different from the baker's showcase?"

"Very different. There are fifteen of us total, and we're going to cook breakfast for as much of the town as we can before we run out of ingredients,” Bella explained. “The royal family will eat first, of course, but it's a bigger affair than what you lot did."

"Why do it, then? It seems like a lot more worry.”

"Because execution and speed and volume are big parts of what a breakfast cook does. We wanted to represent that."

Jane nodded. "So I'm basically going to be your production line. To help maintain that speed.”

"Exactly. Just handle what I need you to handle. It won’t be hard, I promise. I’ll direct everything."

They drank tea while the large batch of keln baked, standing at Jane's counter and watching the ovens. The keln came out perfectly. Jane helped load the flatbread into two crates that Bella had brought, carefully covering them for their cross-town trip.

"All right," Bella said, hefting one of the crates. "Let's go show them what real breakfast looks like."

With only two crates and Bella’s strength, there was no need for Dave’s handcart. Jane put a quick note on her delivery cabinet to explain her absence to any potential customers unaware of the showcase. Then the two girls carried the crates through the streets towards the town center, drawing curious looks from early risers.

The square was buzzing with activity when they arrived. Tables had been arranged in a large arc, similar to the baker's showcase but on a much grander scale. Some of the other breakfast cooks were already there, most of them setting up portable cooking stations with grills and pans. The air was filled with the smell of heating oil and the sound of metal clanking against metal as everyone got their workspaces ready.

Bella's station was already set up. Jane stopped short at the sight of it. The griddle sitting on the table was, frankly, absurd. It had to be four feet long and at least two feet wide, a massive flat surface of black iron that looked like it could feed a small town all on its own.

"What is that?" Jane asked.

"Army griddle." Bella set down her crate and started unpacking. "I browbeat Brit into making it for me. Took him three days, and he complained the whole time, but look at it. It’s beautiful."

"It's enormous."

"It has to be. I'm making breakfast for hundreds of people, Jane. I need something that can handle the volume." Bella moved to the base of the griddle and began pulling out bags of charcoal. "Help me get this going."

They loaded pounds and pounds of charcoal underneath the griddle, arranging it in a way that would provide even heat across the entire surface. Bella lit it with practiced efficiency. Soon, the coals were glowing red.

While they waited for the griddle to reach the correct temperature, they began prepping ingredients. Bella pulled out crate after crate of supplies, more food than Jane had ever seen assembled in one place outside of a market.

"Start whisking eggs," Bella said, pushing a stack of bowls toward Jane. "And keep them coming. I won’t run out of need.”

Jane stared at the eggs. There had to be hundreds of them. "You're serious."

"Dead serious. Get whisking."

Jane got whisking. She cracked eggs into bowl after bowl, whisking each batch until it was smooth and uniform. Her arm started to ache after the first dozen bowls, but she kept going. This was what Bella needed, and Jane had promised to help.

Meanwhile, Bella was attacking vegetables with frightening speed. She moved through them like a machine, her knife never stopping except when she reached up to mop the sweat out of her eyes.

Once the eggs were done, Bella pushed a massive block of cheese towards Jane. "Cheese next. Grate all of it."

"All of it?"

"All of it."

Jane grabbed a grater and got to work. Her arms were definitely going to be sore tomorrow, but it would be worth it.

By the time they finished prepping, the griddle was properly hot, and the other cooks were all in place at their own stations. Jane looked around and saw a variety of setups, from simple pans to elaborate multi-burner arrangements. Everyone looked tense and focused.

A hush fell over the square. Jane turned to see the royal family emerging from the meeting hall. Xand was with them, holding an amplification device.

"Citizens of Glenfall." Xand's voice rolled out over the crowd. "His Majesty the King, Her Majesty the Queen, and His Highness Prince Brand are here to witness the breakfast cook showcase. Let the cooking begin."
