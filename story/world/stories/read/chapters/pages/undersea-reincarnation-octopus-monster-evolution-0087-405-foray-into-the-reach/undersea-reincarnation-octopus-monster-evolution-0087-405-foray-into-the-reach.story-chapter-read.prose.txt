Switching places with another clone was as easy as taking a step in its direction. The difference was immediately apparent, Henry noted as he dodged out of the explosive blue tongue, dragging Maurice along with him. It cost a minimal amount of mana. It didn’t require any noticeable channeling, and it allowed him to get out of the way of a bone-breaking, painful, and uncomfortably sticky situation.

Not that his avatar had any bones to break, of course. But still, the tongue packed a punch. And it wasn’t just physical, either. Eyes narrowed, Henry watched his poor, kraken-shaped Lifeblood avatar get wrenched down toward the toothy jaw of the creature below. The impact had made his connection with the avatar shudder for a moment. As if it had been spiritually shocked.

But he didn’t want the over-sized chameleon to have a meal for free, so with a tug, Henry dismissed the avatar, and the tongue retreated in the triangular-shaped head before the lizard balefully glared at him while it slowly rose on its hind legs.

“Henry, what’s that? It kinda looks like those drakes we fought a while back… but weirder. With big eyes. And sharp spines. And a weird tongue,” said Maurice, pausing for a second before he added, barely audibly. “I bet its tongue tastes good.”

Henry snorted, looking down at the monsters as one of its yellow eyes tracked him and Maurice, while the second flicked from one avatar to the next. “It’s definitely weird,” Henry mused as he took the creature in. It looked like a chameleon, especially its head. But the body was a little different. Unlike regular chameleons, this thing was oddly proportioned, now that it had shed its camouflage. Basically, it looked like a squat, fat T-Rex. Its front arms were not as small as the dinosaurs, and its claws seemed blunted, but it was clear this thing relied on its hind legs and curly tail to move around. Considering the size of its thighs, Henry would bet it could jump pretty high, too.

It was like a three-story building was glaring up at them and at any moment, it could throw itself up and swallow Henry and Maurice’s humanoid forms in one gulp. If it deigned to, that was. Because once more, its scaly mouth was parting, while a blue mass rippled as it readied another shot.

This time, Henry was a little more ready, so he just tugged them both out of the way, right as the tongue shot out fast enough that a cone of white formed in its wake as it broke the sound barrier.

“You can fight however you like, Maurice, but I’m—whoah—”

Without retracting, the pad—the bulbous, end bit of the tongue—suddenly jerked and shot toward them, which Henry dodged once more by taking the spot of another avatar, and through the other projection’s eyes, he noted how it had gripped its own tongue with its front legs and used it like a damn rope dart.

Well, that’s a first, Henry thought, mentally moving himself out of the way of the tongue. Now that he knew it could move it like that, it was easier to predict, especially when the ghostly Octominds—in their Arcane form—kept an eye on the creature. Though before he committed to the fight?

“You’re not friendly, right? We could stop the fight, if you’d rather talk!” he shouted down to it. In response, it spat its tongue back at him.

Maurice snorted. “Good enough?” he asked, his eyes set on the creature, and Henry nodded with a shrug, repeating the same words. “Good enough indeed.”

With the possibility of peaceful resolution out of the way, Henry flew higher and pointed a hand down. First test, let’s see how effective Unfettered Echoes is at boosting Toxic Ray, Henry thought as he held the nexus at the tip of his finger. For this one, he did activate Rupture Casting, and while he wasn’t sure his efforts would lead to much, he compressed the nexus a little further. He was sure the diameter of the ray wouldn’t just be affected that way, but from Henry’s experience, skills could adapt to their caster’s will. With all of those parameters in mind, Henry channeled a whiff of his new essence to the already glowing hand, then he let it fly.

The flash of radioactive green almost made him miss the tongue coming at him. But that was no matter, because the appendage took the brunt of it.

The thin, bright ray lit up the cliff, letting out a sharp whistle that lasted for only half a second as it lanced through the tongue then through the chameleon’s lower jaw. The creature shrieked, retracting its tongue, and Henry didn’t miss the pale, chalky and bleeding spot where Toxic Ray had burned through the tongue.

Henry’s jaw hung open. The laser—and it really looked like a laser—had punched straight through the tongue, the lower jaw, then through its chest, which was now oozing dark blood and smoking. Henry had gone for a simple paralytic effect, but it seemed the ray had been so concentrated its toxicity had grown much more potent, and it had done damage just by sheer power, before the toxins had gotten a chance to do much.

“Oh. I guess we won’t be needing this, then” Maurice muttered as he dismissed the black orb he’d been channeling. He was clearly disappointed, while below them, the sentinel stumbled as it tried to take a step back, but found its limbs not as responsive as it had expected. It tried to take a shot with its tongue again, but its jaw only half opened, which fouled its shot.

He had so many things to test. But this poor chameleon didn’t look like it’d be able to handle any of them.

Henry stared at the stumbling A-rank. He had done well against low A-ranks before, but it had taken him work. It had taken a slow and methodical approach, and last time, doing damage had taken everything he had. So he hadn’t been expecting his first, haphazard attempt at ranged warfare to practically incapacitate the creature.

“My bad. I expected it to tank it a little better,” Henry said as he sent his Octominds to finish it up. He didn’t mind using and training Toxic Ray, but he didn’t need to let the monsters suffer overly long under its effect.

“It’s fair,” Maurice said. “I mean, it’s not that far from us when it comes to levels. I guess you should aim for something in the 20s? That puppeteer was in its 30s, but I don’t know if we can handle something like just yet… Also! I still need to take down my own weak A-rank,” the crab added, giving him a mock-glare, to which Henry raised his hand.

“Yup. I hear you. The next one’s yours,” Henry promised, and with that, they quickly finished up the poor chameleon and moved on, but not before they harvested a few samples. Naturally, Maurice packed up the tongue for later, which he wanted to grill up with some spices and some salt. Henry lifted them both up and they flew toward the orb, opting to keep low, just above the canopy. Occasionally, Henry would glance up at the suspiciously thick clouds and would see movement, but he didn’t feel like climbing up there himself. Once they stopped for a rest, he’d send an avatar up to check if there was anything up there but for now, they kept going.

Expectedly, ambushers detected them, and they tried to make an easy meal out of them. So Henry and Maurice used them as target practice. Henry worked on improving his Toxic Rays without the use of Unfettered Echoes, while Maurice kept looking for a weak A-rank to duel. Oddly enough, the couple of times Henry had used Find the Path to locate a nearby A-rank to scrap with, they turned out to be pacifists. Or, as soon as Henry and Maurice spoke to them, the A-ranks would either back away or grunt at them to leave them alone. It happened with a glorious antler with metallic horns. It happened with a large raven perched in a high lonesome tree, while its progeny cawed and flew around it. Henry was pretty sure that bird had laughed at them, but it was peaceful enough, so they left it be. And then it happened again with a moss-covered sloth who’d just blinked at them twice before dozing back to sleep.

Thankfully, it only took them half a day to find a decent target for them both. Or, to be more accurate, for a decent prey to find them.

The two ‘predators’ had come swooping down from the clouds. Henry had noticed them already, having had some of his Octominds—which Henry didn’t feel like calling them apostles just yet—watch the sky. Two massive white and blue eagles had tracked them for only minutes before they went on the offense, and as Henry pulled them both out of the way, he had a second to identify them, which he boosted a little with Unfettered Echoes to get an accurate reading, considering what he was sensing out of the larger bird.
