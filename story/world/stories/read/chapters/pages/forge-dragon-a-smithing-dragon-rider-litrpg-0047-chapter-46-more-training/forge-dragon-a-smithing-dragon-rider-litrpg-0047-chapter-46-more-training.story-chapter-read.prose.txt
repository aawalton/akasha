They spent two days training while they waited for the scouts to return. The scouts sent out in the immediate surroundings reported nothing out of the ordinary save for some more corrupted creatures. These however were squirrels and birds, things well within the abilities of a scout group to handle.

Training consisted of Kelia practicing her mana control while simultaneously working on her Imperium Frigoris. She sat on the ground, eyes closed as snow appeared around here before falling to the ground. The snow directly around her didn’t melt. As her mana pool became purer, the air around her grew colder until it was well below freezing. Despite the cold, Kelia seemed unaffected by it. Her bloodline also had an effect that tripled her constitution when resisting cold. With an effective constitution of 33 against cold, no natural weather could harm her.

As such, she’d stopped wearing winter gear, garnering lots of looks from all the men in the camp. The bulky fur lined leathers made shooting her bow difficult, and in her training that day with her mana pool purification, she found that the more things inside her mana pool that weren’t cold-aspected, the harder it was to purify the mana.

“This is probably why the Frigidi all wear what looks like fabric made of snow,” she said.

“If you can figure out how to make it, I think a few flecks of quench steel will help it stay frozen,” Caleb suggested.

He wasn’t the biggest fan of his wife becoming the living embodiment of cold—the previous night’s sleep had definitely shown the downsides to the power—but he wanted her alive more than anything and would deal with using extra blankets at night if it increased her odds of surviving.

Then he pictured himself sleeping with Kelia on one side and Bog, glowing red hot on the other.

I need to make some constitution enhancing bracelets to sleep with, he decided.

Through her practice, Kelia learned how to push wind mana into her ring, and while probing the magical object with her Magical Perception, she noticed that the quench steel within the ring could also store mana. Not as much, but more than Kelia could fill, draining her entire mana pool. Mettius told them that her mage steel ring would likely store mana in the hundred if Caleb had purified it half decently. The upper limit would be in the thousands, but for that Caleb would have to reforge it and push even more of the mana out.

The relationship between natural mana in a metal and its mana capacity was not linear. The purer a metal was, the more it would store, but as the crafter’s ability to detect and purge natural mana increased, so did the amount of mana that could be stored. As far as Caleb could tell there was no left in that ring when he gave it to Kelia, but he was certain he’d find more within once he leveled up his Magical Perception.

So after two days, Kelai had improved her ability to purify her magic. She found that her natural mana pool was a third cold, a third wind, and the rest a mix of the ambients. Her body naturally converted mana to those of her affinities, and the passive nature of mana absorption saw that there was always more being pulled in. By the end of two days training, she’d been able to get the natural resting state up to about two fifths cold.

She also found she could very quickly push all the wind and ambient mana into her ring to have a brief moment of hyper cold efficiency, but the she could only maintain that high ration of cold mana for a few seconds before the ambient crashed in, and then she was left with a pool worse off than it was before and a ring full of mixed mana. She preferred to push all her wind and cold mana into her ring and push the ambient out of her pool. In a pinch then she could pull in more cold, drastically increasing her cold ratio. This was also hard to maintain, but not as difficult as the purging method and left her with more mana, not less. The downside of this was that she had to spend the day filling the ring to prepare it.

If stored her full capacity of 104 mana into the ring and withdrew it later while at full, she’d only get up to 115 mana with a four fifths cold ratio. She could hold this for a minute, but her efficiency would rapidly decay afterwards.

The last thing she improved on was her Imperium Frigoris. She started out freezing the water in the air to make snow, and eventually worked her way up to freezing the air itself. With this technique learned, she found he was then able to push the boundaries of the Frost Bolt skill—or spell as Mettius referred to it. A spell was just a skill that used mana and apparently anyone with mana could learn any spell given time and enough skill.

With this, Kelia practiced empowering her Frost Bolts and managed to level the skill once. Her greeted improvements came to here Mana Manipulation and Magical Perception however which each rose to level 5.

Caleb saw no skill advances for himself. He needed to find more time in the forge to experiment. He tried to do so in his training with Mettius, but practicing magic was a creative exercise—an art. And working out in a training yard wasn’t the environment for him to be inspired.

He trained with Mettius, and they worked to press their limits against each other. Caleb fought to break Mettius’ shields while the prince worked to hold them up. When they traded, Mettius’ shot Force Bolts at Caleb’s chest and the dragon rider practiced his Immutable Metal skill to preserve himself.

Under Caleb’s direction, Bog began to work to incorporate the steel he’d eaten into his body. He’d grown a few centimeters in length and a bit wider in the past week. As he grew his old rough hide split, with new fresh scales coming in beneath. But the old hide still remained unless he worked to replace it.

As far as Caleb could tell, Bog could replicate the properties of anything he’d eaten. So, if he ate a dagger with a mirror sheen and razor edge and had a belly full of the same steel, he could move that to parts of his body and replicate the look and strength of what he’d eaten. In this way many of the crude scales on his body had been replaced with what appeared to be small and wide daggers. The scales had sharp edges, but instead of being the same polished steel color of the blade Bog had eaten, the dragon had chosen to keep them the dull black of forge scale.

Caleb had tested the scales with the pick on the back of his hammer and found the surface finish to be as strong and durable as the steel if covered. It didn’t flake off or crumble as scale would.

Caleb really wanted to see what Bog could do if fed enchanted steel, but they lacked the materials to do as much. He gave him one of his strength ingots as an experiment, but nothing noticeable happened—aside from Bog begging and whining for more.

The green claws were also tested and found to be quite impressive. Even Mettius admitted that the mage steel claws on a dragon could prove useful.

“I’m not sure if he can utilize it for it’s more common uses, but the steel should serve him well. Few unenchanted metals are its equal.”

All the talk of metals had made Caleb itch to get in front of a forge. After the first rapid raid of the quartermasters tent, Caleb had made sure to be properly outfitted for their next adventure. For once, he had access to all the gear he could need and the authority to requisition it. A full set of armor had been given too him, but that left his legs and arms exposed. Other nations armored their soldiers far more heavily than the empire, encasing their men in metal from head to toe. Caleb dreamed of one day having a full set of plate. This wasn’t a dream he’d ever had before this week as he’d never really expected to need it. But, recent events had caused Caleb to refocus on his priorities.

While Caleb was dreaming of walking around covered in steel, Kelia was struggling to wear anything. No matter what she wore, it interfered with her mana purity. After trying on every type of cloth in the camp and finding that there was none in the camp—if cold-aspected cloth existed at all. In the end, they’d sent a messenger back to the village to requisition any cloth that might have been scavenged from the dead invaders.
