Sakura no longer knew whether to laugh or curse.

“Another labyrinth,” she muttered, staring into the darkness ahead. “At this rate, I’m going to get used to ominous winding paths.”

Gilbert lifted the white-blue flame in his palm a little higher, lighting the next turn.

“At least Faerd stayed up there. For now.”

Sakura grimaced.

“I just hope he doesn’t decide to break through the ground and come down after us.”

“That sounds entirely possible. I don’t think Faerd would be above doing exactly that.”

Sakura said nothing. A chill ran through her whole body. Not just from the cold of the underground, but from how deeply tangled they were in all of this now.

They kept going. Their footsteps sounded far too loud here, even when they tried to tread carefully. Somewhere in the distance, water dripped. Sometimes an icy draft came from the side passages, and sometimes it was stale dampness and mold, so thick Sakura felt like she could taste it.

“I hate dungeons,” she said quietly.

“I agree. This one isn’t especially pleasant. One day I’ll show you a better dungeon.”

“Seriously? I’d be happier if you showed me some cozy park with a lake where nothing wants to kill you.”

“Well, now you’re asking for too much,” Gilbert said with a quiet laugh.

Sakura was about to throw something sarcastic back at him when a light flickered ahead.

They both froze at once. Gilbert instantly lowered his hand, dimming the white-blue flame until it was barely more than a thin, trembling spark, and grabbed Sakura by the shoulder, pulling her behind the nearest bend in the wall. They pressed themselves against the damp stone, barely breathing.

Footsteps. Several pairs of them, as if two people were walking there, maybe more.

Sakura pressed herself harder into the wall and carefully turned her head, trying to see something past the corner. A dim yellow light was moving through the corridor ahead, and it was definitely real, not magical like Gilbert’s.

Then she heard men’s voices.

“I told you, it’s complete chaos up there today,” someone said irritably. “The guard’s making a scene. They’re chasing somebody.”

“What’s that to us?” another answered, hoarse and annoyed. “We collect the goods and leave. The faster, the better.”

“Exactly. I don’t want to get stuck down here if those idiots upstairs decide to sweep the lower tunnels too.”

Sakura slowly turned to Gilbert, thinking about what they had just said. Goods in a dungeon? In the middle of mold, filth, and suspicious hidden tunnels? Sure. Goods. Probably fresh pies.

Gilbert was listening too without moving. The white-blue spark in his hand went completely out, vanishing between his fingers so it would not give them away. Whoever those men were, it was dangerous for Sakura and Gilbert to be seen by anyone right now.

Luckily, the suspicious men passed right by their hiding place. There were two of them, dressed in dark cloaks, with cloth masks covering the lower halves of their faces. One had a huge leather flask strapped to his belt. The other had a stuffed bag slung over one shoulder, and an ordinary oil lamp swinging in his hand.

“I don’t like this,” the first muttered again. “It’s too quiet.”

“If you hear squelching, then start worrying,” the one with the lamp threw back. “Until then, keep moving.”

“Maybe we’ll get lucky and it won’t wake up?”

“It always wakes up. The only question is how hungry it is. Just don’t run into it.”

Sakura felt a cold unpleasant weight settle inside her from those words, because there was nothing good in them.

Gilbert waited until the light had gone farther away, and only then leaned toward her.

“They look like gatherers.”

“Gatherers of what?”

“I don’t know yet. But if they come down here for ‘goods,’ then they know the way. And the way out.”

“You want to follow those deeply suspicious creeps?” Sakura whispered.

“Do you have a better idea?” Gilbert arched a brow. “Or should we wander around these tunnels until we run into Faerd again?”

Sakura let out a heavy breath, knowing Gilbert was unpleasantly right. She did not have a better idea. So they waited a little longer and moved after them.

They had to go slowly so they would not give themselves away. Gilbert kept his magical flame nearly extinguished, just enough so they would not step into a hole or smash their faces into one of the pillars holding up the ceiling. Sakura tried to place her feet carefully, but the stone underfoot was wet, swampy, and sometimes covered in slick moss that could easily send her sprawling. Once her foot still slipped, and she started to lose balance. Luckily, Gilbert caught her by the elbow in time and kept her from crashing to the ground, injuring herself, and giving them away with the noise.

The voices carried ahead of them for a little while longer.

“I wish we could just fill up and get out already,” one of the gatherers said. “I really don’t want to be down here tonight.”

“You never want to be down here.”

“Because I’m not an idiot.”

“Then you picked a strange job.”

The second one laughed. After that, the voices grew quieter because the two of them were clearly getting farther away. Sakura and Gilbert paused at the turn so they would not get too close. It would have been too easy to give themselves away with a sound or a light. They were dealing with people who clearly knew these tunnels well, which meant they could very well catch an uncareful tail.

After waiting a little, Sakura and Gilbert moved on, no longer hearing the voices. The corridor turned left, then sharply sloped downward, but the gatherers were nowhere in sight.

Sakura stopped, feeling that something was wrong.

“Did they just vanish?”

“No,” Gilbert said.

Using his flame, he lit the stone chamber more clearly and slowly examined the walls, the floor, and the ceiling. It was empty. Ahead was a dead end with collapsed masonry, and there were no side passages. Only a damp wall, old stone, a layer of mold, and a few rusted iron rings driven into the masonry at different heights.

“They definitely came this way,” Sakura said.

She looked down at the stone floor and spotted a fresh boot print in a thick swampy puddle. So she had not imagined it.

“Could this be...” she murmured, but Gilbert cut her off.

“A hidden passage. Most likely.”

Sakura snorted.

“Of course. Because clearly this place didn’t have enough normal passages already.”

Gilbert walked over to the part of the wall where the rings had been set and ran his fingers over the stone. Then he crouched, looked at the floor, and touched a dark streak along the base of the masonry.

“Tracks.”

Sakura bent down. At first she did not understand what he meant, but then she saw it: a thin smeared line of mud and something shiny stretched across the wet floor. It led straight to the wall and vanished under it, as if a great number of slugs had once crawled there. So this part of the wall had to be the hidden passage. Interesting.

Gilbert straightened and looked at the metal rings. Sakura joined him in examining them. There were three of them: two coated in the same heavy rust, and the third, the lowest one, looked a little cleaner, as if it had been touched more often.

Sakura noticed it almost at the same moment he did.

“The bottom one?”

“The bottom one.”

Sakura grabbed the ring. Gilbert shouted, “Wait!” at the exact same moment she pulled on it.

He managed to shove her in the shoulder. Sakura slammed sideways into the wet wall, hit her elbow painfully, and only then heard a short dry whistle. Something black flashed past her face and struck the masonry right in front of her eyes with a metallic click.

It was a small thin dart. It had lodged itself in a crack between the stones.

Sakura froze, feeling her heart drop somewhere deep inside her. She understood that if Gilbert had not shoved her, that dart would be sticking out of her head right now.

“You really have no survival instinct at all, do you?” Gilbert asked quietly, but with perfect clarity. There was definite reproach in his voice.

Sakura slowly turned her head toward him.

“I thought if they come through here all the time, then the passage would be safe.”

“For people who know how to open it.”

“Yeah. You’re right. Sorry.”

Sakura knew she had acted like an idiot just now. Pulling suspicious rings in equally suspicious underground tunnels was not a great strategy.

Gilbert cast a brief glance at the dart, then back at the ring. His face turned focused and sharp, angry now not at her, but at the mechanism itself.

“Don’t touch anything else.”

“You know, at this point I’m really not eager to.”

He carefully stepped beside that treacherous ring and lit it more clearly. Around it were thin, almost invisible scratches at an angle, and they clearly meant something, though Sakura did not yet know what.

Gilbert suddenly reached for the ring, and Sakura flinched hard.

“What are you doing? There could be more traps!” She realized she was worried about the mage now.

Gilbert did not answer. He looked focused and attentive, as if he knew exactly what he was doing. His fingers closed around the ring, and then he sharply pushed it inward and twisted it.

Sakura froze in horror, waiting for another dart or some other awful underground trap.

But this time nothing like that happened. Something behind the wall gave a low grinding scrape. Gilbert let go of the ring and took a step back. The old masonry shuddered, then slowly and heavily slid to the side, opening a narrow dark passage. A wave of cold dampness and a sharp sweetish smell rolled out from inside.

Sakura grimaced at the smell. Then she looked at Gilbert again in amazement.

“How did you figure it out?”

“If you see a solution that looks too obvious, it’s probably the wrong one,” Gilbert said dryly.

He pointed at the fine scratches around the ring.

“And you see that? It wasn’t pulled. It was twisted. The metal scraped against the stone every time the ring turned.”

“So I almost died because I misread scratches?”

“Yes.”

Gilbert lifted the flame a little higher and stepped inside first. Sakura hesitated for a second, glancing at the dart still stuck in the wall, and made a very clear decision to be much more careful in these tunnels from now on. Then she followed Gilbert and soon heard the hidden stone door close behind them.

The new passage was lower than the main corridor. They had to go down a short staircase with uneven steps, wet enough to look as though water, or something thicker, had recently flowed over them. Sakura kept seeing the slime-like traces everywhere.

After a few dozen steps, it became clear that the gatherers had gone too far ahead. There was no sign of their lamp, no sound of voices, and the water covering the floor had erased any trace of boots.

“We lost them,” Sakura said.

“Looks like it.”

“And now we’re walking through a hidden passage with no idea where the exit is. Heading toward whatever those men came down here to collect. And there could still be traps.”

“What can you do,” Gilbert answered.

“What can you do? It couldn’t get much worse...” Sakura sighed quietly.

But they had no choice except to keep moving forward, hoping they would not get lost in this new maze of narrow stone passages. Gilbert continued lighting the way.

There was more of the suspicious slime ahead. It had gotten thicker, and it gleamed when Gilbert turned his flame toward it. It also stank of something rotten and sweet. The slime pooled in corners, stretched over the stones, and hung from the ceiling in long strands. Sakura stopped in front of one such strand and refused to step any closer.

“What do you think? Maybe this is the goods?”

“Very possible.”

Gilbert examined the slime with visible interest, but did not touch it.

“Alchemical smell. Not natural.”

Sakura looked more closely at the glistening mass too and almost automatically focused on it. A short system line flickered before her eyes:

UNKNOWN ALCHEMICAL MUCUSType: Organic SubstanceThreat Level: Variable

Sakura was silent for a couple of seconds, then exchanged a glance with Gilbert. Obviously, he had checked it too.

“Variable threat level,” he said. “Whatever this is, we’re better off staying away from it.”

“Yeah. I’d already decided that too,” Sakura said.

They kept going, and the slime became even more common. Then Sakura saw something she immediately wanted to forget.

In a side niche lay a corpse, almost completely covered in the whitish mass. Sakura did not notice it at first. At first she thought it was just a heap of old rags, but then Gilbert’s light fell on a decomposing human hand. The dead person’s face was hidden under slime that had hardened into a thick film.

Sakura stopped so sharply her stomach turned. Gilbert froze too for a second, then said quietly,

“Keep moving.”

The path was straight. There were not even any side passages they could turn into to avoid this spreading slime. So after stepping around the body, they had no choice but to keep going.

Then they found another corpse.

And another.

All of them were coated in slime, partly dissolved by it. Some looked as if they had died only a few days ago. Sakura walked in silence and felt everything inside her turning colder and heavier.

“If it’s this dangerous, why do those gatherers still come down here?” she asked at last.

“Because whatever they collect is clearly worth risking their lives for.”

Sakura said nothing. She only clenched her fingers tighter.

The passage finally led them to wide metal doors. One of the leaves had been torn off its hinges and lay on the floor, half sunk into slime. A plaque still clung to the other one. The writing was almost worn away, but Gilbert brought the light closer, and Sakura managed to make out a few words. Not in letters she knew from her old life, but the meaning still formed in her mind the way it had with Crowlis’s notes.

BIO-ALCHEMICAL OBSERVATION ZONE.ACCESS BY AUTHORIZATION ONLY.

Sakura slowly turned to Gilbert.

“A lab?”

“Looks like it.”
