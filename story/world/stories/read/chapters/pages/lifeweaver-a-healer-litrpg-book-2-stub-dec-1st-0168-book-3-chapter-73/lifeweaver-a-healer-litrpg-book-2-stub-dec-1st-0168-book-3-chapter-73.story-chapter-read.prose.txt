After killing the two nurses slicing into Leom and hurting Luke in her stead, he stood over the Singer Lifeweaver, panting and sweating from equal parts pain and concentration.

"Get up."

She was curled into a ball on the floor, sobbing, and hadn't even healed the few wounds she sustained before Luke intercepted.

"I said, get up."

Frustrated at her uselessness, he stitched up the still-bleeding wounds after disabling the nerve clusters responsible for the pain in those areas. That made her stop her bawling, and she sat up, staring at the place on her arm where he'd dealt with one such wound.

Swallowing hard, she looked up at him, tears still in her eyes. "It didn't hurt. How did you do that?"

Wrestling with the cold disdain he'd just felt at witnessing Leom's uselessness, unsure what to make of the emotion, he offered her a hand and pulled her to her feet.

"Nerves," he said, pulling a little mana from her before severing the thread still lingering in the air, connecting them. Singer physiology was similar to a human's, very similar, so it hadn't been too difficult figuring out which nerves to target.

The answer only made her look more shocked. "You can affect nerves?"

Not wanting to give away any more information about the various awesome ways of a Lifeweaver, Luke waved for her to follow and headed out of the room.

"Thank you for saving me. Again," she said to his back, then hurried after him.

Back out in the corridor, none of the non-nurses seemed to care that he'd just killed a bunch of them. Instead, they kept doing their thing, running back and forth to patient rooms and along the corridors, pushing dead or dying people this way and that. It looked busy and intentional, but Luke didn't see a single thing being done to actually help the patients recover, not one. This was all theatre.

While none of the treatment was real, some of the patients were actual Integrated. Perhaps one in five were human. Those, he healed as they searched for a set of stairs or an elevator that would take them up to Dot. Most were in a bad state, but few people were beyond Luke's capability to save at that point.

"A Singer," Leom said at one point, when Luke had just killed a small group of non-human nurses to reach an Integrated who had a Meta-Heart surpassing even his own capacity for storing mana.

Not looking up from his patient as he used the Integrated patient's own mana to heal her crushed spine, while siphoning some off for himself, he replied, "So heal them, if you want."

"Can't you help?" Leom asked, her voice trembling.

Finishing up, he turned from the rising motes of light and faced the Singer Lifeweaver. "What? Why would I do that?"

Her threads of mana trembled in the air, but at least they didn't disintegrate. Whatever she was doing, it was slow going. "Because you're better! You're a healer, aren't you?!"

"Your people are trying to subjugate mine," Luke said, feeling the sneer on his face. Pushing his emotions away again and embracing the cold, he continued. "Helping you is one thing, but I'm not wasting mana to help any other Singers just so they can return and hurt my friends."

"The Singers are working towards a peaceful transition for Earth's Integrated."

Luke couldn't help but chuckle at the naivete of that statement. What sort of sheltered existence did this Leom grow up in? "Sure," he said. "I'm sure that's what's happening here."

When she ignored him and kept working on the half-dead Singer, Luke sighed and was about to join in just to speed things along when another Singer came running down the corridor.

Integrated Trypten. Lifeweaver. Level 27.

Trypten was about average height for a human, which made him short compared to most Singers, and his squat build and round face made the difference to Leom all the more prominent. With long black hair in a braid at the back of his head and some sort of leather armor covering only his chest, it was like looking at the caricature of a indian brave in some spaghetti western, except he wielded a staff rather than a bow and arrow.

"Die, human scum!" he shouted as he charged without hesitation, hefting a staff in both hands.

"Wait," Leom shouted, raising her hands toward Trypten, but it was useless. Either the new arrival didn't hear her or didn't care, because he swung as soon as he reached Luke, aiming for his head.

The attack was far quicker than Luke anticipated, but he still made it under and past the swinging Trypten, though it was a near thing. Rather than cave in Luke's skull, the staff struck the corridor wall with a crack and left a fist-sized hole leading into a room on the other side, cracks forming around it. A kick took Luke in the shoulder, and he spun out on the floor, tumbling.

Broken bones from a simple kick. This new guy brought some serious power. Fractures healing, Luke stood and considered the scene before him. Trypten now stood between himself and Leom, a savage grin on his face, fuming with anger. If those two wanted to continue on their own, who was he to stop them?

Luke waved at Leom, who was still trying, and failing, to heal the Singer while doing her best to catch Trypten's attention.

"See ya!" Luke shouted, then turned and raced away.

When he turned the corner and stopped to peer back around it, they were gone. Not gone as in having gone into another room, but gone as in the whole corridor had shifted into some other place. Now, it was filled with more nurses hauling patients between rooms.

"Weird," Luke said, then spotted a stairwell through an open door. Before that disappeared, like the Singers, he hurried over and ascended one flight. Finding himself on Dot's floor, he exited and almost ran straight into Trypten's staff. Luke ducked and rolled, hearing the crashing of glass from the small window in the door he'd just gone through. Coming to his feet, Luke couldn't help but snarl. "Enough with the staff!"

"Die!" Trypten cried, charging again. Always with the charging with that guy. Or, well, Singer.

Like the two previous altercations, Luke attempted to duck under the blow. This time, it wasn't successful. Anticipating the dodge, Trypten swung low, and the end of his staff connected with the side of Luke's head. The force of the blow drove him to the side and into the wall with a crash, and he slid down to the floor, struggling to keep conscious as the shattered bones in his face and the side of his head knitted themselves back together at great expense to the healing mana in his Meta-Heart.

"Ow," Luke grunted, but blood came out of his mouth rather than the intended word.

Trypten didn't gloat or stand back. Instead, he rushed in again with an overhead swing, not allowing Luke the time to recover. Rolling to the side, Luke avoided the blow, but not the kick that followed. The force of it was enough to push Luke back, and death would have reached out of the depths to claim him if not for the great increase in sturdiness the many points he'd allocated into Vitality had awarded him. As it was, though, things looked bleak. But this was far from over.

Spitting blood as he got to one knee, and then up to his feet, Luke readied himself for the charge that was sure to come. He wouldn't let that staff hit him again. This time, when the charge did come, Luke went high, rather than low, and jumped into the air, reaching forward with a thread of mana. Weavestep brought him over and past the staff and well within reach of his opponent.

Several threads of mana sprang from Luke, who grabbed Trypten's arm to force him to remain close. They entered the asshole's mana channels even as Luke took a swing at the sneering Singer's mouth. The sound of bones snapping wasn't as satisfying as he had hoped, since the bones were his own. Rather than his fist breaking Trypten's jaw, the Singer's jaw broke Luke's fist. Double asshole.

That did not stop Luke. As soon as he reached Trypten's Meta-Heart, he affixed Needle of Life to the threads, changed the shape into that of a blade, and began chopping away. An elbow chopped down at Luke's arm, but he somehow held on. The knee to his gut that followed brought some serious discomfort with it, though. Perhaps it was the ruptured spleen. It didn't matter. With his stores of healing mana still at healthy levels, he could still take a serious beating.

What Luke hadn't expected, even if he perhaps should've, was that his opponent would use The Healer's Moment. He'd experienced it once before. Then, it had been instant. This time, not so. The world seemed to expand around him, and the many not-nurses running around slowed to a crawl as time rippled around him.

By the time he recognized the skill being used and realized he might be able to do something about stopping it, it was far too late to do so. Too slow, he'd been too slow yet again. This would hurt.
