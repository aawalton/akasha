“Are you going to take it from me now?” I asked. I had kept running, but my footwork had shifted slightly, readying myself, if I needed to change direction suddenly.

“I am sworn to protect you, I would not take something from you. However, we are going to need to upgrade the security on and in the ship to better defend it.” she replied.

I nodded my head, glancing up to see the bridge doors approaching, and quickly moved through them before dropping into the pilots’ seat. The moment I pulled the helmet down and dived into the ship, I saw it surrounded by ground personnel and a few small ships had started to fly into the hangar to aim their weapons at our ship.

A grin slowly spread across my face as I watched them, my mind already trying to work out the best way to escape this. A moment later, I heard the communications alert of an incoming call. I accepted, deciding every moment they were talking was a moment I could use to plan my escape.

“Blossoming Terranova to Young Blood, power down your engines and prepare for boarding, a thief has stolen something of immense importance to our clan and people. We demand its immediate return and the arrest of the criminal.” A pissed off sounding voice all but shouted at me.

“What are you talking about,” I replied, shifting power just below activation thresholds for the systems I’d need for my quickly forming plan. “It’s not theft if the owner is simply taking back something that already belongs to them.” I winced a moment later, hearing my flimsy excuse for breaking the glass and taking something.

“It is property of the Museum generously donated by the family, it does not belong to anyone on your ship, and you will return it now! You have two minutes to power down your engines, or we will open fire!” He screamed.

My eyes instantly shifted to the gap between the two interceptors, deciding it was time to leave now. I shoved the power hard as I pulled the nose up and felt the rear thrusters ignite. The heavy ship shifted forwards quickly, while the lower thrusters pushed off the deck, getting us some air. The nose of the ship turned, angling between the two interceptors as I used weapons controls to fire lasers at their exposed engines.

Moments later, I was watching both ships performing emergency landing procedures as they tried not to panic. I added more power to my engines and pushed out of the hangar and into open space.

I winced when I saw the four military vessels undocking from the station. I knew I had precious moments and decided to go all in by pushing the engines to the max power as the ship angled to withdraw a safe distance from the station and planet.

“All hands brace for an emergency jump!” I announced shipwide.

I kept glancing between the military vessels that had begun turning to chase me down, smaller ships undocking from them, I recognised them as interceptors built for speed and boarding tactics.

“Alright” I muttered as I glanced at the nearest exit point I could us. “Dimensional jump would get us to a brand-new place, great for escapes, shit for my current plan. So where do I go if not a new dimension…”

I glanced at the interceptors before I heard the incoming communications, my gaze shifted to it briefly, the moment I saw it was Clara, I let out a sigh and accepted the call.

“What’s up, niece o’ mine” I said as the channel opened.

“Oh, don't ‘niece o’ mine’ me!” Why am I getting reports that state you smashed a museum exhibit, and destroyed two hangar security ships on your way out.”

”hmm, oh because if I had left it, then the entire station and planet would have been destroyed the moment a powerful cultivator found out about it, basically they couldn't protect it, and it's way more useful to me as It's what I need to continue my cultivation. As for the ships… they started it.” I winced, suddenly feeling childish, even though I was unable to think of anything better.

“What? The hell kind of scroll is so dangerous?” She asked, sounding a little confused and hesitant.

“An immortal tier one would start a battle but this one… this one would draw everyone from everywhere, we are talking mass extinction level ‘I want that!’ Arrogance.” I replied, deciding not to say exactly what it was on an open channel.

“Okay, that certainly sounds terrifying but auntie you can’t just brake stuff and take what you want, there are laws, you could have contacted me and asked for it.”

“In hindsight, that does certainly sound like a better idea… Hey, niece o’ mine, can I have my car back?” I asked, feeling a bit cheeky.

I was trying to keep the smile off my face as I dropped the front of the ship angling for a new vector to confuse the incoming interceptors, I had almost reached the jump point and was diverting power, ensuring they remained just below activation thresholds.

“What—now you’re asking for more?” Her voice snapped. “I haven’t even finished yelling at you for the last thing you took.”

I giggled softly, trying to hide it from Clara, as my sensors picked up an attempted fire-control lock from one of the interceptors. I quickly pulled the front of the ship up, shifting it onto a new vector as I inched closer to the jump point.

“Lovely to talk to you Clara, really it has been wonderful. But your ships are trying to put a hole in my hull, so imma need to jump away now, and haven’t even picked a new destination yet.” I said as I rolled the ship trying to avoid another lock.

“Oh… I guess this is the part of the conversation where I need to be the bad guy, you see auntie you did steal from the museum, so we are going to have to arrest you for that, just because you're the ancestor now doesn't mean you get a free pass. You understand? Right, auntie?”

“Of course, dear, but you have overlooked an essential detail.”

“No, I haven't, activate the anti-warp field!” She screamed, though to me, it sounded a little muffled.

“Too late” I whispered back, I terminated the communication and grabbed a hold of the warp ring. A moment later, I was dropping into it, as I felt the ship sink with me.

I waited a couple of minutes before slowly pulling myself back out, before looking around. I found myself in dead space, my sensors and navigation quickly figured out I was seven-star systems away, and currently sitting outside any star systems.

A moment later I turned the ship and sank back down, having decided if they had figured out how to track a warp jump then I didn’t want to be here when they figured out where I surfaced and one more random jump should hide my tail fairly well. I had no idea if I was being paranoid, and I did feel like I was, but I wanted to be safe instead of sorry.

I was slightly surprised when I surfaced in the middle of a star system surrounded by military vessels that were in the middle of shooting each other. I relaxed slightly when one of the missiles exploded against my shields and didn’t even scratch the power levels to maintain the shield. Of course, it wasn't the only thing to hit my shields, a few smaller ships crashed into them, unable to turn away in time due to my surfacing in their path.

“Wow, strong shields.” I whispered to myself. A second later, I felt a little silly for running from my niece.

I silently watched all the smaller ships continue their battle, but the two bigger ships on each side stopped firing and began to hail me. I slowly shrugged, deciding I was safe enough to have a chat.

I accepted the channels, seeing two translucent screens appear in front of me, the one on the left belonged to the ship that happened to be on my left and was of a young woman in a uniform I recognised as the one belonging to the galactic military I once served in.

On my right was a man wearing robes that looked expensive, but in the way that it had been on a budget. I instantly felt like I should side with him based on the way the military had treated me during my service with them.

However, I wasn't willing to play the rebel card just yet, like this middle-aged man had. I decided I should find out what the story is before I start shooting or running.

“Mediator!” The woman said as she gave a half bow to the camera, causing me to freeze as my gaze landed on her. However, I tried to keep my face neutral and not show any confusion that I was currently feeling. “Thank you for your presence,” she finished before staring intently at me.

“Ha! I knew we had the right to defend our star system, only made more evident by the appearance of a mediator” he exclaimed excitedly a moment after her.

“Cease all fighting at once” I stated simply, deciding to see how this played out.

They instantly turned to talk to their bridge crews and I watched as the fighting came to a stop, most ships simply remaining where they were. The smaller fighters, however, returned to the ships they had come from. I didn’t pay much attention to where they were going or why.

“Explain yourselves” I said after the battlefield had come to a calm standstill.

“We are defending our homes from this cur,” he snapped, pointing accusingly. “They flew in here and declared our territory an active combat zone, claiming we had offended a Celestial Marquess. It’s nonsense. We've done nothing wrong, we belong to a Celestial Clan as well. They have no right to invade our territory.

The woman’s jaw tightened, but her posture remained rigidly formal. “That is a selective interpretation of events,” she replied coolly. “This system was placed under provisional interdiction after multiple violations of Celestial transit law. Your clan’s mining platforms were operating inside a restricted astral corridor.”

”that corridor was never ratified!” The man shot back. “It was proposed—proposed—and then abandoned. You don't get to resurrect old decrees just because it suits your politics.”

I slowly raised a finger, both of them stopped immediately, he swallowed like he was biting off more that he had wanted to throw at her. I let the silence linger for a heartbeat longer than necessary, just to make a point.

“Neither of you is here to convince me you're innocent,” I said evenly. “I am here to judge the situation and decide if it should continue.” I turned my attention to the woman first. “State your authority.”

“Commander Arelis Vane,” she said without hesitation. “Third expeditionary fleet, acting under writ granted by the Celestial Accord and Countersigned by Celestial Marquess Halvrenn’s office.”

‘That explained the confidence. And the aggression,’ I thought as my gaze shifted to the man. “And you?” I asked calmly.

He hesitated just long enough to be noticeable. “I am Commander Tollen Kreth,” he said finally. “Steward of the Rhyssan Reach, Sworn vassal of Celestial Count Walker.”

That got my attention instantly, and I narrowed my eyes on him. For a moment, I watched him shift nervously before I finally asked, “this star system, and yourself, is a vassal of the Celestial Count Walker clan?”

“Yes…” he replied, nodding, his voice tight with unease.

I turned back to the woman as I let out a small sigh, “well,” I said, “this just took an interesting turn, and is not looking good for you.”

The colour drained from Commander Vane’s face, just slightly, “Mediator,” she said carefully, “Clan Walker was removed from those listed among protected signatories in the writ centuries ago.”

“And yet,” I replied, “they remain one of the oldest Celestial Clans still in good standing. Which means your provisional interdiction just crossed into unsanctioned aggression.” I decided not to mention that good standing was with me. “Commander Vane,” I continued, “you are one sentence away from explaining to a Celestial Count why his vassal star system is full of military wreckage.” I paused, letting that sink in before calmly adding, "I suggest you choose that sentence very carefully.”
