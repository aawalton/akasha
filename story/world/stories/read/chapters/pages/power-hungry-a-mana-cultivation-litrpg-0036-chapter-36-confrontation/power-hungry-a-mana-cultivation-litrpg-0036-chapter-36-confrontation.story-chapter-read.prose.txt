That’s it Cale. Slow and steady.

But the obvious question started hammering his mind. It was an insistent little mosquito—wielding a sledgehammer with which it banged at the walls of his brain with wild abandon.

Why? Why? Why?

Why did the Nevani create the Omnic Manifest?What was so damn important that they would curse this planet with this calamity. And how long had it been online? When would it initiate the last phase?

And why was my creation linked to it?

Cale realized, with an unsettling clarity, that he had accepted it. Not willingly. Not happily. But the revelation hadn’t shattered him. Hadn’t consumed him. It had simply…

slotted into place, beneath larger questions.

It was still worth examining. Cale pushed aside the bombshell about the Omnic Manifest and examined what he had seen first.

That guy… Shalac-Tron-Faluvan, had been in the chamber Cale had been put into cryosleep. Had Cale known him? How? The Nevani had not been humans. Yet Cale was. He was from a place called Earth, far from here. And Darius, Fiara, and the rest. All humans.

Just what had happened?

“My head hurts…”

“Mine too… This was… Unexpected.”

“We just have more questions…”

“Not a single one I liked,” Cale muttered.

“Be that as it may,” Aura said. “We know more now.”

“Not valuable in of itself.”

“I disagree. Knowing might not be fun, but it is useful. It seems we were also designed to not have fun, but to be useful.”

“How do you feel?” Cale asked.

Aura didn’t answer immediately. When she did her voice was gentle, careful. “I do not understand why my creators would create something so destructive…”

“I was also created… and put in a cryochamber, like some labrat…”

Cale grumbled. It helped to ground him. He resented the notion that someone had created him. Had he ever been just… Cale Bishop, a guy who likes milkshakes and wasting time?

I… I’d like to think I was.

That thought was comforting. But it was also a part of a self that had died long ago. Even if Cale wanted to keep it, he couldn’t. Not in this world.

“Do you still want to know more?” Aura asked carefully.

“I do,” Cale said immediately. He got up. “Leaving things open like this would absolutely kill me.”

“Good! I would not let you rest on your laurels. I for one need to know!”

“What do you think we are?”

“I think they created us and the Omnic Manifest for the same reasons.”

“Yeah,” Cale said and nodded slowly. It wasn’t a particularly happy thought. “I think so too.”

“I want to know why.”

Cale suddenly realized something. All his lamentation, all his rumination and all his internal bullshit would have to wait. Darius’s ascension ritual was still going on. His comrades were in the power room and the Sullied were trying to get in.

“Aura we need to have the deep talks later. How do we get out of this room?”

“Oh about that…” Aura said and Cale swore he could hear a grin in her voice. “I received some upgrades.”

“Well don’t keep me in suspense.”

There was a faint shimmer of purple dancing lights on the back of Cale’s hand. It intensified and a form materialized. A purple, translucent woman, eight inches tall. Her hair was long, curly and flowed unnaturally around her and she was wearing a short summer dress. Her face was strange, a smooth mask with only keen eyes and an excited grin for a mouth.

“Ta-daa!” she said in a real voice that echoed faintly around the room. “My first step in world domination! Material presence!”

“That’s cute and all, but did we get anything useful?”

“Hmph. No appreciation for true art,” Aura said back in his mind and huffed. “Close your right hand into a loose fist. Now watch this.”

Cale did as instructed. The holographic woman on his hand melted into a cloud of glowing particles—then stretched, thickened, solidified.

A shape took form in his grip, humming with power, clearly receptive to Mana. Cale had an Integra. He was only at Body Tempering, but he had an Integra. Mana technology that he could wield and augment.

A two-foot blade of pure energy. Sleek. Vibrant. Lethal.

It felt alive. Cale felt powerful.

He looked at the blade. It hummed and vibrated softly. Cale gave it a swing. There was a heft to the blade. It felt nice. The blade felt extremely dangerous, simply emanating a cutting energy. Cale pushed the sword against the floor, certain that the blade would sink.

It clonked awkwardly, the vibration running up Cale’s arm, nearly twisting the sword from his grip.

He stared at it. Then at the floor. Then back at the sword.

“…Well, that’s a little embarrassing.”

“What are you doing?” Aura sighed. “It is Nevani make. You might as well try to swallow the sun than puncture it with your puny sword.”

“You are the sword,” Cale reminded her.

“I— what I meant was— I HATE it when you do this, Cale!” Aura stammered. “Your lack of skill with the blade is the problem. Not my amazing ability.”

“Okay, you can turn into a sword. And a really dangerous sword,” Cale said and turned the blade in his hand. “I think I like it.”

“Watch this,” Aura said like a giddy child.

Slowly, the blade morphed into something resembling a very short handled umbrella. The blade melted into a shield the size of a car tire. It was impressive, and Cale had the distinct feel that this energy shield could pack a hit, but the morphing has been slow.

“I am still working on taking these forms… It is quite a challenge.”

“Of course, of course,” Cale said. “I think we’ll stick to the sword now. Now where’s the exit?

The stout umbrella morphed back into a blade. At the head of the blade was the distinct face of an elfin girl who raspberried at Cale before turning into the point of the weapon.

“There is a door behind the platform with the screens. Let us decimate these fools!”

Cale laughed and left the secret room.

Cale spent no time reveling in his victory, for he did not know how much he had. All he knew was that the Aura-blade kicked ass.

Lucky me. Let’s see if we can keep it up.
