Both Solana and Ember continued to explain that while both sides had suffered casualties, they weren’t significant, and their fortresses remained intact. This meant that they were preparing for the same thing as we were.

Soon, the sixth wave was coming to an end, and the battlefield was gradually settling down. Fires still burned across the ruined streets, casting flickering light over the aftermath of the clash, while divine warriors moved to clear bodies and reinforce damaged sections of the barricade. Night was approaching, and yet… everything felt too calm.

“It’s too quiet…” I muttered.

For a brief moment, I wondered if I had overestimated them. Perhaps neither side would act. After all, committing to an attack now carried enormous risk, especially with the seventh wave looming. It wouldn’t be strange if both factions chose to endure until the end instead of gambling everything on a decisive strike.

But that thought didn’t sit right with me. A faint unease lingered at the back of my mind as I continued observing the surroundings. That was when I saw Michelle walking inside toward the central area where the statue was located. At first glance, nothing seemed off. Her posture, her pace, even her presence—all of it appeared normal.

Yet something was wrong.

When her gaze brushed past me, she didn’t greet me like she usually would. That had never happened before… Michelle was the type who would greet me whenever she had the chance. For her to ignore me like that… something was clearly amiss.

More importantly, she had said earlier that she would scout ahead right before I met with Ember and Solana. If she had returned, she would have reported immediately—or at the very least acknowledged me. Instead, she walked past without a word, her attention fixed straight ahead toward the statue. Don’t tell me.

Without hesitation, I activated Mana Sense. The moment my perception brushed against her, I felt a subtle inconsistency, like a thin layer of false information draped over something else. It was extremely faint, but I was sure that it was a disguise—a kind of transformation or illusion skill.

As someone who frequently relied on disguises to deceive others, I was intimately familiar with this kind of ability. In fact, among Istellia’s divine warriors, there was likely no one more experienced in detecting such deception than me. It seemed this was the enemy’s move—to approach Istellia’s statue unnoticed and destroy it from within.

True to my suspicion, the moment she stepped within the statue’s perimeter, her hand began to rise, as though preparing to activate a skill. The surrounding divine warriors failed to notice anything unusual, clearly thinking that she was still Michelle.

Without wasting another second, I made my move. My figure blurred as Moonveil Step activated, closing the distance in an instant, while Wind Rush propelled me forward even faster. The Abyss Edge materialized in my hand as I struck straight toward her without hesitation.

Clang!

The attack was unexpectedly blocked. Her arm snapped up at the last moment, intercepting my blade with a concealed weapon. The motion was too fast—way too precise—and nothing like Michelle’s usual combat style. Michelle was also similarly dexterous, but she wasn’t an assassin, which confirmed my suspicion that this was not her. That realization immediately raised another question: Had something happened to Michelle?

No, Michelle has a comm-link. If anything had gone wrong, she would have reported it. Which meant the enemy must have encountered her, copied her appearance, and slipped in immediately after to avoid raising suspicion.

At the same time, her appearance flickered slightly, the illusion destabilizing for just a fraction of a second.

“Tch.”

“You picked the wrong target,” I said, pressing more force into the blade.

Without giving her a chance to respond, I shifted my footing and activated Moonlight Blade. A pale silver radiance surged along the edge of the Abyss Edge, sharpening the strike far beyond its normal limits. I twisted my wrist and drove the blade forward, forcing her to give ground.

She reacted instantly, retreating half a step while deflecting the follow-up strike with a clean, efficient motion. Sparks burst from the clash as our weapons met again, but this time, the difference was clear. She was undoubtedly a Daleth-rank divine warrior, yet she wasn’t one who focused on raw strength. Despite her calculated precision, I was able to keep up with her in the open, especially since I had trained in the way of an assassin for a bit.

“The enemy is here! She’s trying to destroy the statue!” I shouted.

The surrounding divine warriors immediately noticed the disturbance. They turned toward us, weapons rising as confusion spread across their expressions.

“What’s going on—?”

Before anyone could fully react, I attacked again. Moonlight Blade flared brighter as I shifted my aim right for the disguise itself. My blade cut across her shoulder—not deep enough to deal lethal damage, but enough to disrupt the flow of energy maintaining the illusion.

A sharp, almost inaudible sound echoed, and immediately, the disguise shattered. Her form flickered violently as the false image peeled away like a broken layer of glass. Michelle’s familiar appearance dissolved, revealing a completely different figure beneath—slender, clad in dark attire that blended into the shadows, with sharp eyes.

“An infiltrator!”

The surrounding divine warriors shouted and immediately closed in, forming a tight encirclement around her. Bows were drawn, blades angled forward, and mana began to gather in the hands of mages positioned behind the front line.

She clicked her tongue and immediately attempted to break out.

“You’re not going anywhere! Catch her!” I yelled at the others. However, I stopped myself from chasing her as a faint sense of unease stopped me in my tracks.

This feels too simple… There was no way they would send a single divine warrior to destroy another faction’s statue. Even if I hadn’t noticed her until the moment she struck, one attack wouldn’t have been enough to break through the barrier protecting it. After all, the goddess’s statue could withstand the combined assault of several powerful Daleth-rank divine warriors.

This meant there was another move. My eyes snapped toward the statue, and I noticed something right at its base. Almost instantly, a figure stepped forward from within the inner perimeter itself. No—there were multiple of them.

Concealed presences revealed themselves one after another, phasing into view as if layers of invisibility were being stripped away. At the center stood a familiar figure draped in jet-black robes, his face hidden beneath a hood etched with faint red runes. A heavy, oppressive aura spread outward the moment he appeared. It was the leader of the Obsidian Order—the Great Warlock Boris had faced in the tournament. Around him, several of his guild members emerged as well, each already positioned to strike the statue.

Traitors?

I frowned, as I hadn’t expected betrayal to surface this early in the Battle of Divine Will. For something like this to happen, they must have made contact with the enemy within the past few days, likely during moments when no one was paying attention. After all, through a god’s authority, it was entirely possible to switch factions—so long as one was willing to pay the necessary price.

“To think you’d notice so quickly,” the Great Warlock said in a low, distorted voice. “As expected of the leader of the strongest party in Fantasia. Impressive.”

Dark sigils began forming around him one after another, stacking in layers. Ominous mana gathered in the air.

Many of the other divine warriors guarding the statue had already rushed off to pursue the female assassin, leaving me effectively alone to deal with this. She must have been a distraction to draw attention away from the real attack. Even so, I didn’t wait for reinforcements.

I immediately cast {Gravity Field}, causing a crushing force to spread outward as the gravity within the area intensified to three times its normal level, pressing down on everything within its range. At the same time, I cast Cryo Magic in conjunction with Weapon Manifestation, conjuring dozens of icy blades that formed in a silent orbit around me.

Subsequently, I invoked Wind Rush and layered it with Predator’s Veil. My presence thinned to near nothing as my speed rocketed. In the next instant, I crossed the distance between us, slipping through the warped air like a phantom. The Abyss Edge carved through the air, aiming straight for his core before he could complete whatever spell he was preparing.

But just as it was about to connect, a barrier snapped into place at the last moment. My blade crashed against it, sending ripples of dark energy outward as the impact reverberated through my arm. At the same time, his subordinates moved, attempting to intercept me even as the icy blades rained down on them.

Moonveil Step has leveled up.

“Ugh.”

So they were prepared. Of course they were.

“Velkhar…” I muttered under my breath. To weave deception like this—layering one infiltration over another—there was only one faction capable of executing it so flawlessly. This had to be the work of the God of Deception’s side.

Black tendrils of energy began to surge toward the statue as the Great Warlock raised his staff, his spell reaching its final phase. Not good. I was about to use the sword’s special skill—Triarch Tempest—for the first time to interrupt his casting. But then—

“Gahaha! You dare show your face here?!” A thunderous voice crashed through the air—Boris.

He landed between us like a falling boulder, the ground cracking beneath his feet as his presence alone disrupted the gathering mana. Behind him, Tuilë’s laughter rang out as mechanical constructs deployed, Lucian descended with a storm of magic already forming at his fingertips, and Michelle—this time the real one—took position with her bow drawn.

Along with them was Erika, who stepped forward in silence, her blades already unsheathed. And beyond them, several other leaders arrived in quick succession, including Berthold of Silver Herald and Marcel of New Hope.

Right before striking the fake Michelle, I had used the comm-link to contact Boris and the others. Luckily for me, they had arrived in time.

The tide changed instantly, and the Great Warlock’s spell faltered slightly as his gaze swept across the newly arrived forces.

“How troublesome,” he murmured.

“Your plan ends here! Adamant Flesh: Diamond Body!” Boris shouted. A crystalline sheen spread across his body, refracting light like polished gemstones. Without hesitation, he charged forward, his massive frame tearing across the ground as he closed the distance in an instant. He swung his fist and slammed it into the Great Warlock’s barrier.

Boom!

It shattered in a single blow. The dark sigils surrounding the Great Warlock destabilized immediately, their structure collapsing under the overwhelming impact.

At the same time, Lucian’s magic descended. A barrage of spells rained down from above, forcing the Warlock’s subordinates onto the defensive. Tuilë’s constructs advanced forward, unleashing suppressive fire that pinned them in place, while Michelle’s arrows streaked through the air, targeting key points to disrupt their coordination.

In the meantime, Erika slipped in, cutting through the battlefield with silent precision as her katanas flashed. Two members of the Obsidian Order barely had time to react before being cut down, their defenses severed in a single, clean strike.

“Fall back!” one of them shouted. But it was already too late.

I stepped in again, Moonlight Blade flaring as I took full advantage of the opening they had created. My blade cut through the air and slashed across their leader’s side, drawing a streak of blood that scattered into the air.
