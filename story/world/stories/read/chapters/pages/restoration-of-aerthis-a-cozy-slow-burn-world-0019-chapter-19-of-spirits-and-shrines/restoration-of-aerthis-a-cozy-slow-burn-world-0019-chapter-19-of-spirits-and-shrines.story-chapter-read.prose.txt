The sun hadn’t yet touched the church spires, but the first light had started to warm the edges of the shutters.

Elynara stretched slowly beneath the blanket, letting her limbs extend one by one with a satisfying pull that ran down her back. The ache was still there, but it was quiet now. Dull but manageable.

Across the room, a faint trill sounded.

She cracked one eye open to see Starpuff hovering above the dresser, pulsing with gentle anticipation.

“You’re being patient this morning,” she murmured.

It pulsed again, clearly hinting.

“Alright, alright–mana first then.”

She shaped a thread in her palm, slow and even, and let it drift toward the puff.

Almost the moment it touched, Starpuff brightened like a lantern and zipped off into the air with renewed energy, trailing faint glimmers in happy loops. The second loop turned into a third. Then a spiral. Then something vaguely resembling a victory lap.

“Regret,” she muttered, rubbing her face.

Still, it brought a smile.

She sat up properly and leaned against the headboard, glancing toward the shuttered window. The sounds outside were faint: a few steps on stone, the rustle of cloth, one cart wheel squeaking out of rhythm.

She had woken up on farm time. Too early for town.

No wonder it felt quiet.

But that suited her just fine. She wasn’t in any hurry. Alicia had all but shoved her out of the guild yesterday with strict orders to rest, and Father Alaric hadn’t said a word about chores. She still had half a day to laze, stretch, and let her mana come back in peace.

Her fingers moved to her lap almost on their own, drawing the Status panel into view.

And froze.

Name: ElynaraLevel: 3(+1)Mana: 59 / 67(+1.5/h)(+0.2/h)

Stats:Fitness: 8Agility: 9Willpower: 14(+1)Intuition: 12(+1)

Skills:Mana Manipulation – Lvl 2Mana Sense – Lvl 1Fire Magic - Lvl 2(+1)

Traits:Mana Attuned – A body attuned to mana generates and absorbs it more efficiently. Gains an intuitive sense for mana flow and structure.Spirit Sight – Ability to perceive spirits and lingering magical traces. Its strength grows with use and focus.

“Oh,” she said aloud.

That was... new.

Fire Magic had ticked up to Level 2. Her stats had shifted. Her level too. Passive mana regen had nudged higher. And her max mana still sat at sixty-seven.

The system had definitely notified her. She just hadn’t looked. Wait, she had seen something, hadn’t she? Soon after firing that second big spell... she had assumed it was Starpuff doing one of its tricks again.

And just dismissed it in the moment.

“Oops.”

Starpuff zoomed past her head as if in perfect agreement.

She scowled lightly and opened her notebook instead. The Status surprises were nice, but they didn’t change her plans:

Mana Stuff

Mana capacity training is currently most importantStill need to crack the secret behind why it works, rather than it just does. So far, the simplest training method: gather, hold, push, maintain. Strain, but not too much. Repeat. This seems right, even if progress is slow.

Mana Sense

Progress has been more abstract, like tuning a string to the right note.Sometimes I can feel things behind obstacles, other times I sense nothing.Seems to vary by distance, density, focus, possibly more factors in play. Range has been increasing with practice, more can be seen.Growth is slow, but helpful that it's possible to train anywhere: during walks, chores, food preparation.It isn't a flashy skill, but a necessary one, and it's nice to notice things I had been oblivious to before. Like learning to hear better.

Fire Magic

This name doesn't sit right with me, since it's not actual fire. It's more like an explosion using pressure and heat.Sparks sometimes appear from it, but there is no trail of flame, just a sharp, shaped blast compressed to burst apart.Rather effective, but maybe it can do more... and possibly with more efficiency than I have so far. Possible tests: - Change shapes   - Redirection   - Searing burst vs explody burst   - A flash, a wall   - Exclude pressure and use only heat...

Which would just be fire, should probably start with that instead of saving it for last.

She bit the end of her pen thoughtfully, already picturing Mina’s reaction if she could pull off something new.

The other elements though... that would be trickier.

Fire had come naturally. Intuitively. The logic of it made sense. But water? Air? Earth? Were there more?

“You don’t just... rub mana together and get a puddle,” she said flatly.

Starpuff twirled once like it disagreed.

“Well if you figure out how, you show me.”

The puff spun faster.

Next line of thought:

Spirits

Starpuff gets fed plenty. Even if I forget to, it makes sure to remind me…Doesn't channel mana given back like the shrine sprites, not even a glimmer of shared mana with the land nor a reactive bloom.Seems to store mana like it possesses a snack drawer, and is quite smug about it.

Still unsure what purpose the mana it eats is for.

“Probably nothing useful,” she muttered.

Starpuff puffed into a circle and stuck its butt out with a defiant swirl.

“Alright, alright. Maybe mildly useful.”

It glowed triumphantly and flopped against the wall shelf like it had won a contest.

She rolled her eyes and stretched again, this time holding the pose a bit longer. The stiffness was fading. The dull aches had subsided, just enough to ignore, but not forget.

It was still early, and pleasantly quiet thus far.

She sat cross-legged in the center of her bed, resting the staff across her knees. Her eyes drifted half-closed as her breath slowed.

Time for some capacity training.

No hurry.

She focused on the slow draw into and from her core. Pushing and pulling in slow cycles. The repetitive action quickly allowing her to enter her meditative state, making the process just that little much easier.

The quiet hum of training still clung to her breath when Elynara finally rose and made her way downstairs. Her limbs felt looser now, less weighed down, though she still moved a little too stiffly for her liking.

The dining hall already smelled like bread and nut spread.

Father Alaric stood near the table, sleeves rolled and wiping his hands on a cloth. He looked up at her approach, smile soft and immediate.

“Ah, you’re walking straighter today. Good. No rush on chores, of course. Just let the morning be kind to you.”

She dipped her head slightly, managing half a grin.

“I’ll try. But I’ve got a few tests I want to run.”

That earned a small chuckle.

“Just don’t overdo it, you could still use some more rest.”

Nodding along, Elynara went ahead to help the nearest nun set out the rest of the plates. Wooden bowls, tea jugs, jam pots.

Familiar motions, all. Her hands moved by habit, but her thoughts were already darting ahead to magic structures and elemental shaping.

She had just set down the third set of mugs when a hand latched onto her sleeve.

“Breakfast,” Mina said, pulling her sideways with a determined sort of gentleness.

Elynara blinked, then let herself be towed to the bench.

“You could’ve just asked.”

Mina gave her a flat look at that.

Still, she didn’t argue. Warm bread and soft cheese had a way of winning over even the most research-minded mage. She let herself sit and relax for the first time that morning, shoulder brushing Mina’s lightly as they dug in.

“You’re not eating much,” Elynara said after a few minutes.

Mina didn’t meet her eyes. “Not hungry.”

“That wouldn’t have anything to do with the snack bundle from yesterday, would it?”

A pause. Then a tiny embarrassed nod.

Elynara snorted.

“Knew it. I’ll have to tell the matron her gift’s been thoroughly dismantled already.”

“I didn’t eat everything,” Mina said, ears turning faintly pink. “Just... most of the cookies.”

“I’m glad you liked them.”

Mina glanced over, smile curling in the corner of her mouth.

“And you’re not doing nothing today, are you?”

“Definitely not.”

She said it with too much enthusiasm, and instantly winced. Her ribs twinged in protest.

“I was going to take it slow,” she amended.

Mina narrowed her eyes at her, then poked her elbow.

“You better. I’m not above hiding your staff from you if I have to.”

Elynara grinned and offered her a one-armed hug.

“Fine. I’ll pace myself. Short errands only.”

The hug lingered a moment longer than necessary.

Eventually, breakfast ended. Dishes clinked softly as they were gathered. Elynara stood and slung her satchel over her shoulder with practiced care. Mina rose with her.

“You’ll be back before noon?”

“Should be.”

“Good.” Mina hugged her again, softer this time. “Stay safe.”

Elynara nodded and stepped out into the street.

The morning outside was quiet, wind nudging gently against her shoulders as she turned down the familiar road toward the guild. The sun played peekaboo through the shifting clouds, warmth coming and going with each stretch of sky.

Her scarf tugged faintly at her neck, and she adjusted it absently.

The cool breeze felt good. Clean. A little brisk, but refreshing after the closeness of the hall. Her boots thudded lightly against the stone road, and Starpuff drifted just ahead of her, looping lazily like it was watching for puddles to splash.

The town hadn’t changed, but it felt a little different now.

The guild hall door creaked just as it always had. Warm light filtered in through the high-set windows, catching the edges of parchment rolls on the old quest board. Someone was practicing sword swings out back again; Elynara could hear the thuds in the floorboards.

She barely made it three steps past the threshold before Alicia looked up from behind the desk.

“You’re not here to work.”

Elynara blinked.

“Good morning to you too.”

Alicia arched an eyebrow.

“Morning. You’re not here to work.”

“I didn’t take any requests,” Elynara said, holding her hands up in mock surrender. “I’m just following up.”

“Mhm.” Alicia reached under the desk and pulled out a familiar leather pouch. The thunk it made when it landed on the counter was... not small.

“Your payment. And your share from the core sale, and guild bonus for dealing with a major threat. Which you weren’t supposed to handle alone, by the way.”

Elynara stared at the pouch, then back at her.

“I didn’t exactly schedule the monster encounter.”

“You could’ve screamed louder.”

“Next time I’ll schedule fireworks,” she muttered.

Alicia gave her a thin smile.

“Just don’t try to grab any requests today. I mean it. You so much as glance at the board, I’ll have Marcus carry you back to the church over his shoulder.”

That mental image alone nearly made her laugh.

“Understood.”

As if summoned, Marcus appeared not long after. His hands were dusted with something vaguely crumb-colored and he was still chewing the corner of a roll.

“You’re not here to work, are you?” he asked mid-chew.

Elynara held up the coin pouch in answer.

“Oh, good,” he said. “Alicia already threatened you.”

“She did.”

“Good.”

She pocketed the pouch and leaned a little against the counter.

“So... the monster. What was it exactly?”

Marcus sobered at that, scratching the side of his jaw.

“Nothing exact. No records matched. We’ve seen corrupted growths like that before, but not on anything that size.”

Alicia nodded, her fingers moving through papers without looking. “Our best guess? It wandered in and out from the cursed parts of the deep forest. You remember the cursed mountain and bogs I warned you about before?”

“The ones you said to never even go near to?”

“Those.”

Marcus folded his arms.

“Things from there don’t tend to stay stable. Whatever that creature was, it probably wasn’t born like that. More likely it got twisted. Changed.”

“Survived where it shouldn’t have, got hungry, and started hunting anything it could reach,” Alicia added.

Elynara winced.

“That’s... grim.”

“Welcome to the guild,” Marcus said dryly.

She thought for a moment, then said:

“So… Since it has no name… Can I just call it a ‘Horned Bear’?”

Alicia actually snorted.

Elynara blinked.

“Was that a laugh?”

“No,” Alicia said firmly, smoothing a page. “Just a surprise hiccup.”

“I’m serious. Horned Bear. Gets the point across.”

“Not bad,” Marcus said, clearly trying not to smile. “I’ve heard worse.”

“I bet you’ve named worse,” Alicia murmured.

“Excuse me?”

“Oh nothing, Bear.”

Elynara turned just in time to catch the very slow turn of Marcus’s head toward Alicia. His eyebrows rose. Alicia didn’t even glance up, her expression the picture of innocence.

Elynara stifled a snort and slipped toward the door.

“Thanks again,” she called. “For everything.”

“Go rest,” Alicia called back.

“I’m walking.”

“That counts!”

The door closed behind her with a gentle thunk, and the quiet of the street welcomed her back like a familiar rhythm.

Elynara didn’t turn toward the church right away.

The town air carried a different weight than the woods, one of more dust, more cooking smoke, but less urgency. Her satchel tugged faintly against her side as she walked, but there was no real pressure behind her steps.

So she followed the road past the square.

Just a simple loop, she told herself. One pass by the market, maybe the tailor. If she didn’t do it now, it would stack up later. Better to get it out of the way while her limbs still remembered how to move.

Starpuff had other ideas.

It buzzed ahead without warning, then doubled back, booping her head like a particularly indecisive moth. Its glow flickered soft pink for a moment, then turned teasing green as it zipped between a pair of merchant banners.

“Try not to knock anything over,” she muttered.

It didn’t respond. Just spun twice, shot through a curtain, and reappeared near the bakery sign like it had proven something important to the breeze.

Rascal.

The morning crowd had thinned by the time she reached the tarp stall. A few carts rolled by in the distance, one loaded with cabbages, another with bags of straw. She stepped around them with ease, nodding once to the familiar seller hunched behind the display boards.

He looked up from his tally scroll, squinting at her over the rim of a half-spectacle.

“Back already?”

“Yep. Left one behind. And I want a spare.”

“Well, who am I to say no to good business? Here you go, lass.”

Two tarps were produced from beneath the counter. One bound tight with leather cord, the other loosely wrapped and marked with a stitched corner tag. She checked the threading just in case. Not that she particularly knew what to look for.

“Appreciate it.”

The merchant just shooed her away with a smile.

She tucked the bundles into her satchel, shifting the weight evenly. The bag took it without issue, just with a quiet tug that always felt a little like cheating the physical laws of this world.

Starpuff floated back into view upside-down, nudging her ear once before darting toward the produce stalls.

She followed at a quieter pace. Her legs weren’t stiff exactly, but they still ached a little. Even her fingers seemed to have a kind of slow protest in them, as if every joint remembered where she had hit the ground.

The ration booths hadn’t changed.

Dried root bark, oat squares, mixed nuts and dried fruits pressed into bundles in wax paper. She passed over the more expensive goods without pause. Her emergency pouch was still mostly full, and the rest of her stock had held through the trip. Still, she made a note of what was available, eyes scanning price tags just in case.

Something smelled like dried apple and sunroot mash. Another cart had thin strips of salted fish braided into dry loops. She took neither.

She lingered at the herb shelf longer than she meant to.

Some part of her had expected the wild ones to be rarer. More potent. But no, the same stem clusters she had picked by the ridge were there, just bundled up, tagged, and ten times the price. Her head tilted as she recognized the same curled edges. She could have sold these.

Then again, that’s probably where they ended up anyhow.

“Hmm,” she said aloud.

Then moved on.

The tailor’s workshop had both shutters open and a roll of dyed fabric draped halfway out the side window, pinned at the corners by old iron shears. She ducked inside just as the bell above the door gave out a double-chime.

“Oh, hello miss!” The tailor peeked out from behind a divider screen. “How can I help you?”

“I need to repair and replace some things. And searching for something.”

“Well, you have come to the right place. What exactly do you have?”

“One tunic with tears, two undershirts I’d like replaced if possible. And do you have anything in stock that works for rain?”

“Oof,” the tailor said, already pulling cloth swatches. “Going somewhere far?”

“Eventually. Just planning ahead.”

“I’ve got a fold-sleeve travel robe, wax-treated, and a thinner one for layering. Won’t keep you dry if you roll down a hill, but it’ll shrug off weather for a good while.”

“That works.”

They haggled gently, but Elynara didn’t push too hard. The prices seemed fair, and the stitching on the rain robe looked solid. She accepted the bundled package and stashed it alongside the tarp, the satchel once again swallowing weight with smug ease.

She stepped outside to find Starpuff nose-deep in a crate of thistleroot.

The vendor was nowhere in sight.

Elynara crossed her arms.

“You’re going to get banned from every stall in this district.”

The invisible puff twitched with faux innocence.

She crouched beside it, eyeing the way its glow shifted to a hopeful green-blue.

“You can’t eat these. Actually, can you even eat these?”

It made a sound halfway between a squeak and a chirp.

“No, not even if you think it’s funny.”

She gently pulled it back, letting it settle against her collar where it nestled in with only a few huffs of faint offense. Its glow pulsed once; hungry, maybe.

She fed it a short thread of mana and felt the weight of it flicker through her chest.

“You’re lucky I like you,” she muttered.

She kept walking. The square had quieted more now, the morning crowd giving way to midday lull. Cloth awnings fluttered in the breeze. A child laughed somewhere near the baker’s.

As she turned back toward the road, a thought surfaced unbidden.

Could Starpuff wear clothes?

A tiny scarf? A little hood?

The image nearly made her snort out loud. What would it wear? It had no limbs. No spine. Just puff. Anything placed on it would probably fall off immediately, or worse, offend it.

She imagined a tiny cloak floating behind it dramatically and then immediately being discarded.

Yeah, no. Shelf that one.

Still, the idea lingered longer than she expected. Oddly comforting, in a way.

By the time she turned the corner back toward the church road, her satchel felt heavy in shape but not in weight. The ache in her legs hadn’t faded, but her steps felt steadier. Each one a little more rooted.

The farm was behind her. The shrine, the monster, the fight.

But the road ahead was still long. Plenty of things to see and do.

And she had everything she needed for it, packed and ready.

The church courtyard came into view just as the clouds parted for a sliver of sun. Elynara stepped lightly over the path stones, skewers wrapped in wax paper tucked under one arm, a small pouch of sweets swinging at her side. The warm smell clung to her fingers. So did the anticipation.

Almost the moment the gate creaked open, she heard them.

Gasps. Running steps. Then the first shout.

“She’s back! The Fire Queen’s back!”

Elynara winced. Not because she minded; at least, not entirely. But that title had a way of catching too many ears too quickly.

A blur of fabric and bare feet slammed into her side.

“Did you bring snacks?!” one of the kids asked, already digging into the air like it might help.

“Or magic! Show us fire magic again!”

“You said you’d show the BIG fire!”

“I said I’d try,” she corrected, holding the paper-wrapped bundle above her head like a treasure needing guarding. “And tomorrow. I need my hands free to eat, you know.”

There were groans, exaggerated flops to the ground, theatrical betrayal.

She lowered the bundle anyway.

“But snacks now.”

The betrayal vanished.

Small hands darted in. She let them take the skewers first, which were carefully portioned smoked sausage and roasted root, and made sure the sweet puffs were evenly divided. Even the quietest of the group, usually too shy to ask for seconds, got theirs with a wink.

“You’ll still do fire tomorrow, right?” one of them whispered, cheeks stuffed full.

“Yes,” she said. “But only if you don’t call me Fire Queen again.”

They didn’t answer that one.

Snacks in hand, the kids scattered toward the side wall where chalk and coal drawings had taken over the last of the summer stone. Somewhere near the corner, one of the older girls was showing off how many times she could twirl with her arms out. Another had started organizing rock tiles into shapes that probably made sense to her alone.

Elynara took a breath. Smiled.

The courtyard felt lighter with them there.

She found Mina in the far corner behind the church, near the place she practiced her magic before. The younger girl was crouched low beside Pufflet, one hand idly brushing the little one’s fur as it blinked slow and content under her palm.

Elynara stepped close, casting a small shadow.

“Didn’t take any snacks yet?”

Mina looked up with a half-smile.

“Was waiting.”

Of course she was.

Elynara dropped beside her, setting the paper bundle between them. Root skewers, sweet buns, and wrapped dumplings, one for each of them. Pufflet perked up immediately. So did Starpuff, who had spent the walk back pretending to be too busy scouting rooftops to be hungry.

Now it landed squarely on her shoulder, gave her ear a dramatic nudge, and stared pointedly at the food.

“You don’t even eat this,” she muttered, but unwrapped the dumpling anyway.

Starpuff poked it, sulked, then pulsed a color that translated perfectly to “fine, be that way.”

She snorted and fed it a small thread of mana. That earned her a smug spin and a gentle pat on the head before it nestled into the crook of her collar again, victorious.

It was getting quite greedy for mana.

Mina popped a piece of skewer into her mouth and sighed softly.

“I really missed you.”

Elynara’s smile softened.

“I missed you too.”

The courtyard hummed with low voices. Children played, sun peeked between clouds, and the two girls sat side by side on a warm stone, sharing snacks in peace. Just enjoying the moment.

It wasn’t long after that Elynara decided to start on her magic tests. She really didn’t like sitting still for long.

Also, she was curious how her second level of Fire Magic changed things. Though it ended up being a little disappointing? In an ‘obvious’ kind of way.

Her fire magic behaved better now.

…And that was it.

Elynara crouched at the edge of the courtyard, sleeves rolled, fingers lightly stained from soot. A small flame danced above her palm. Shaped with heat and subtle balance. No explosions now.

“Okay,” she whispered, “this time, curl.”

The flame leaned left, wobbled, then snapped back into a static hover.

“Rude.”

Starpuff drifted by with all the gravity of a passing cloud, flicked its glow at the spell, and trilled like it had a better idea.

“Try it yourself, then.”

No response, of course. But the puffball did choose to settle for a front row seat on the low stone wall nearby. Clearly it was entertained by her continued failures, or progress, depending on interpretation.

She tried again. One hand forward, one low. This time, she fed a thinner strand. Not quite enough to form the fireblast spell, but focused enough to hold flame.

A spark caught. Then a steady curl of orange. Not too wide, but stable.

She shaped a second. Then a third.

All three flickered at slightly different tempos, but held.

She grinned.

The third flame always leaned too far to the right, so her control was not perfect, but it was progress. She could make them bigger now. Or merge them. Or spin them along a simple arc, if she didn’t think too hard about direction.

And the explosions? Still needed heat and compression to function, but she could separate them now. Use fire for light.

Cooling or heating it further, though? Still nothing. Mana flared too fast, or fizzled. Maybe a matter of shaping? She’d figure it out eventually.

With soot on her wrists and mild pride in her chest, she let the flames vanish.

“Not bad,” she murmured. “Definitely not bad.”

The rest of the day passed in smaller steps. She helped fold linens. Slowly, carefully, hauled buckets of water. Readjusted the pantry shelves. Cleaned the corner where a broom broke earlier that week and no one had gotten around to sweeping behind it.

Mina tagged along for some of it, content to chime in with small comments and help with the lighter bins. Pufflet waddled underfoot occasionally, while Starpuff insisted on sitting in the sock bin for a full fifteen minutes before she noticed.

Even chores had their rhythm.

She didn’t push herself. Alicia was right; her limbs still ached if she moved too quickly, and her mana pool was cooperative, but sluggish. By the time the sun dipped low, her legs dragged just enough to make her blink harder at corners.

Dinner came with warmth and light conversation. A few younger ones asked about her fire tricks. She promised to show more tomorrow, if she wasn’t too sore to lift her arms. That earned a small round of giggles and an overly dramatic gasp from the peanut gallery.

And when the meal ended, and her steps took her slowly toward her room, the ache in her calves were a reminder that she had earned her rest.

Tomorrow, she’d go further. Refine more. Push a bit harder.

But tonight?

Tonight was for warm sheets, quiet rest, and dreams that flickered in soft orange light.
