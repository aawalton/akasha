The practice hall looked pristine at a glance, though the faint grooves etched into the stone floor were never truly erased. Adrian rolled his shoulders, his gaze sweeping over the chamber.

Zuri moved toward a control panel embedded in the side wall. After pressing a few buttons, a pulse of mana surged through the stone, and in an instant, a translucent dome of magic shimmered into existence around the dueling area. Light rippled through the barrier like the surface of a still pond disturbed by a single drop, its enchantments locking into place with a faint hum that reverberated in the air.

She stepped back, arms crossing as she surveyed her handiwork. “This academy really doesn’t hold back, I’ve seen royal training grounds with weaker protections.”

Adrian exhaled, stretching his fingers as his gaze lingered on the dome. How much gold does it take to maintain something like this? “To be honest with you? I have no idea how much tuition here actually costs."

Zuri tilted her head, a smirk playing at her lips. “Want to find out?”

“Not really.”

The dark elf princess chuckled and reached into her coat, retrieving two small amulets. With a casual flick of her wrist, she tossed one to him. Adrian caught it easily, turning it over in his palm.

“You just carry ward amulets around?” he asked, raising an eyebrow.

Zuri fastened her own around her neck, the thin chain vanishing beneath the folds of her uniform. “They keep them in stock for situations like this. Dueling must be common enough for them to make sure no one dies in the process.”

Adrian slipped the amulet over his head, feeling the magic settle around him like a barely perceptible second skin. The ward wasn’t absolute protection, but it would absorb a limited number of direct hits before shattering. At least this means we won’t have to hold back.

With the barrier in place and their wards activated, Zuri strode to the center of the hall. Adrian followed, stopping a short distance away. He rolled his wrists, loosening his muscles, but his pulse had already quickened.

Zuri tilted her head slightly, studying him as though measuring his worth before the first spell had even been cast. “Rules are simple. Three clean hits to win. Alternatively, if one of us breaks the other’s ward amulet first, that’s an automatic victory.”

“Got it.” Adrian said.

“Excellent, then let’s see what you’ve got.” Zuri said.

Adrian took a slow breath, his fingers curling as a flicker of blue flame danced to life in his palm. The fire cast a soft glow over his skin, illuminating the faint tension in his muscles as he prepared for Zuri’s first move. Across from him, she stood poised, one hand resting lightly on the pouch at her hip, her other hovering just above the ground.

Then, without a word, she struck. She's fast.

Zuri’s fingers dipped into her pouch, pulling out a handful of tiny seeds. The moment they touched her palm, green light pulsed through them, and she flung them toward the ground. The stone floor cracked as thick vines exploded upward, surging toward Adrian with terrifying speed. The first tendril lashed out like a whip, its bark-covered surface splitting the air with a sharp crack.

Adrian threw himself sideways, his feet barely skimming the floor before he twisted mid-air, extending his palm. A stream of blue fire roared outward, colliding with the oncoming vines. The heat licked at the enchanted wood, forcing it to recoil, but instead of turning to ash, the vines hardened into blackened bark that resisted the worst of the flames. That’s new.

His boots scraped against the floor as he landed, already shifting to the side as another thick root tore through the space where he had been standing a heartbeat ago. More vines shot toward him, writhing and twisting like serpents, forcing him to keep moving. Seems like Zuri is using a form of unique transmutation magic, it's incredibly fascinating to see in person.

“You’re quick,” Zuri called out, watching him as she stepped lightly over the shifting roots beneath her feet. “But how long can you keep dodging?”

Adrian didn’t answer. Instead, he thrust his palm forward, sending another jet of blue fire racing toward her. The flames cut through the air in a wide arc, but just before reaching their target, a wall of thick, interwoven branches erupted between them, absorbing the attack with an audible sizzle.

Zuri smirked from behind her shield of reinforced wood. “Not bad. But if all you’re going to do is throw fire at me, this’ll be over fast.”

"I wouldn't be so sure." Adrian replied, smiling.

Adrian’s gaze swept across the battlefield, chest rising and falling in measured breaths. Thick vines coiled and twisted along the ground, weaving a snare that shrank his space with every passing second. At the same time, roots slithered between cracks in the stone, tightening their grip over the terrain. She’s dictating the pace. If I don’t break her momentum now, I won’t get the chance later.

Lowering his stance, Adrian forced the heat in his palm to compress. A sphere of blue flame pulsed in his grip, no larger than his fist but dense enough to warp the air around it. A shimmering haze rose from his fingertips, the sheer intensity distorting the space between them.

With a sharp exhale, he drove the sphere into the ground. Here it goes!

The explosion erupted outward in a scorching ring, devouring the vines in an instant. Fire rushed across the battlefield, licking at the stone floor and casting jagged shadows along the walls. The force of the blast sent embers spiraling skyward, their glow flickering like dying stars. Charred remnants of Zuri’s transmuted plants crumbled into blackened husks.

Zuri vaulted backward, her form cutting a sleek arc through the embers before landing with effortless grace. Amusement curled at the corners of her lips. “Finally thinking beyond just throwing fire at me? I was starting to get bored.”

Adrian let out a slow breath, rolling his shoulders to shake off the residual heat still clinging to his skin. “Your magic’s impressive. Didn’t expect you to be this good with transmutation.”

She chuckled, fingers grazing the pouch at her hip. “I do like to keep people on their toes. Speaking of which—”

Her wrist flicked forward, and another handful of seeds scattered into the still-smoldering ground. The moment they touched the blackened surface, green light pulsed through them, and they grew not as creeping vines this time, but jagged wooden spears that spread throughout the battlefield.

Then, with a twitch of her fingers, they fired.

Adrian barely had time to react. A dozen wooden projectiles streaked toward him in a deadly arc, their speed leaving only a whisper of air in their wake. He thrust a hand forward, summoning a wall of fire just as the first volley reached him. The flames roared to life, swallowing the attack in a sudden inferno. The spears blackened and splintered upon impact, reduced to smoldering embers in the span of a breath.

But even as his fire consumed the first wave, Adrian caught the movement of vines slithering along the ground, creeping toward him like grasping fingers, waiting for him to be distracted.

Zuri tilted her head, watching him with a knowing smirk. “Still relying on evocation, huh?”

Adrian gritted his teeth. If he didn’t change his approach now, he’d be on the losing end of this duel before he even had the chance to strike back. Time to mix things up, I think I've seen enough of what she can do.

He shifted his stance, inhaling sharply as the heat in his palms twisted, reforming into something solid. The flames bent to his will, condensing into the shape of a throwing dagger, its blade a brilliant blue that pulsed with the same energy as his own flames. Then, he hurled it toward Zuri, watching as it sliced through the air, trailing embers in its wake.

Zuri reacted instantly, her fingers brushing the nearest vine. The moment her magic flowed into it, the vine hardened into dense, bark-like armor. The conjured dagger struck true but shattered against the reinforced surface, dispersing in a shower of fading embers. “Better. But not enough.”

Adrian clicked his tongue, already forming another weapon. This time, a spear crackled into existence, the shaft composed of smoldering blue fire, the tip glowing white-hot. He thrust it forward, sending it hurtling toward her with enough force to leave a burning afterimage.

Again, Zuri countered effortlessly. She planted her foot, and the stone beneath her cracked as roots erupted from the floor, forming a dense wooden shield. The spear struck, piercing the thick barrier but stopping short of hitting her. With a flick of her wrist, she redirected the same roots toward Adrian, forcing him to jump back as jagged tendrils lashed at his legs. Damn it. She’s countering everything.

His fingers twitched as he focused, shifting his conjuration magic once more. The spear dispersed in a flare of heat, and instead, he called forth a bow. The weapon solidified in his grasp, its frame sleek and shimmering, appearing more like molten metal shaped into form than anything truly tangible. As soon as his fingers curled around it, an arrow ignited at the bowstring, humming with power.

He loosed the shot in an instant. The arrow streaked forward, splitting the air with a sharp crack.

Zuri’s hand shot up, causing the vines beneath her to shudder and surge upward, weaving into a spiraling barricade. The flaming arrow struck the surface, sending sparks cascading in all directions before fizzling out.

Adrian scowled, already notching another shot. She’s not giving me any openings. I need a more… creative approach.

His thoughts barely had time to settle before Zuri retaliated. A single flick of her wrist sent a thick root surging toward him, twisting through the air like a coiled serpent. He leaped to the side, narrowly avoiding the strike, only for another to shoot from his blind spot.

The root struck him cleanly in the ribs, forcing the air from his lungs as his ward flared. The impact wasn’t enough to send him sprawling, but the shimmering glow around his body flickered: his amulet had absorbed the hit, counting as one of his three losses.

But instead of frustration, Adrian was elated. This is my chance!

His thoughts shot back to the cavern, to the moment he had altered the properties of stone with nothing but a desperate impulse. His magic had responded then, bending to his will. He had forced something solid to change at a fundamental level. Time to do it again.

Adrian’s fingers grazed the gnarled root that had struck him, its rough bark still vibrating from the impact. He ignored the lingering ache in his ribs, focusing instead on the pulse of magic that stirred beneath his skin.

The vine resisted at first, its fibers holding firm, but he pushed harder, threading his will through its structure. He could feel the dense weave of bark, the pulsing flow of energy that kept it alive. With a surge of intent, he altered it, twisting its nature at the most fundamental level. Heat bled into the root’s core, turning its strength against itself.

The wood shuddered beneath his touch, its surface warping as fine cracks split along its length. Then, with a sharp, almost agonized snap, blue fire erupted from within.

Flames raced through the vine’s body, devouring it from the inside out. The once-solid tendril twisted violently, its form buckling as the fire hollowed it out, turning what had been an unyielding weapon into a streak of searing destruction. The inferno surged forward, riding the length of the transmuted root, its heat swallowing the air in a rush of crackling embers.

Across the battlefield, Zuri's stance shifted ever so slightly, her weight drawing back as she took a step away from the encroaching blaze. The flickering light cast sharp shadows across her face, her eyes narrowing as they flicked from the burning tendril to Adrian.

Let’s see how she counters this.
