The men disassembled the huts left behind by the yaddiks whilst Carmack formed new homes and guard stations from stone, overlooking the mine below.

He was a little envious of the insectoids. The vivek beetles obviously gave them a great deal of mobility, as their huts had been made from wood and leather. It was clear that the yaddiks regularly made trips to the surface. In fact, he wagered that they probably even sold their gemstones to surface dwellers.

Laborers got to work almost immediately, digging out gemstones. It was tiring work, and they had run low on people interested in doing it. To incentivise people, double rations were offered to miners.

There was still an issue of the distance between the beryl mine and the spire. With the connecting passages having been closed, they didn’t need to worry about errant monsters wandering in, but that hardly meant nobody could attack.

Carmack used the monster core from the mantis spider and its skull to create the foundation for another phylactery. It was his second strongest core, after what he had given to Aphexia, and technically middle grade. However, being able to watch over and provide aid to the mining camp was absolutely necessary. And not just because he didn’t want to lose the camp, but for the morale of the people staying there.

It wasn’t long before a few smaller gemstones were dug out, including emeralds, and pink and yellow beryls. They were only small gems, but would be fine for the lesser monster cores he possessed.

A smile crept across Carmack’s face as he watched a hundred-odd miners busy at work. It was only the early days, but he felt confident enough to consider this project a success.

Returning to the spire with the gemstones in hand, Carmack makes a quick trip home before escorting a miner rotation down the minithrel mine. The chunks of metal they had dug out over the last week or so were not particularly large and were mixed with a lot of iron. Still, he could craft at least three magical items with what he had.

“Thank you,” Carmack bowed to the miners before leaving them in the dining hall. They shot him strange glances, no doubt taken aback by his gesture. But Carmack was beyond grateful. These miners had done their people a great duty, and because of it, everyone would be better off.

Next, Carmack visited the tailors. Stepping into the stone studio he had built for the tailors, his gaze was immediately drawn to the partially completed suits of armor on mannequins at the other side of the room. None of them were complete, with at least one or two pieces missing from each set. But there were dozens of suits on display, and it gave him hope of what was to come.

“Like what you see?” A tailor said, rubbing his hands on a cloth and stepping up to Carmack’s side. “We got plenty more coming, too. Finest scale leather I’ve ever had the pleasure of working with.”

“They’re amazing,” Carmack said. “You and your people have outdone yourselves. Truly.”

“Ha, we’re just tailors. Without those hides, these suits would be plain old leather.”

“No, you’re too modest,” Carmack shook his head.

The scaled armor was similar to crocodile leather, except that the scales were hard like metal. Carmack grabbed a sleeve between his fingers and was amazed at how naturally the armor bent and moved. It was as hardy as plate metal armor, and yet it moved more naturally than chainmail. Not only that, but the only weak points he spotted were between the scales themselves, which were tiny. Plate metal armor had joints that were weak spots. A dagger could easily slip through and stab them, but that would be much harder with these suits.

“This armor would sell for a small fortune on the surface,” the tailor said. “Sure, it might not compare to wyrm or dragon scales, but who has coin to afford armor like that? I doubt you’d find anybody below Ruby rank with armor of this quality on the surface. Maybe a noble’s son or scion of a powerful clan, but normal people? Yeah, that’s not gonna happen.”

“Really? I must thank you then. You’ve done well.”

If this armor is really that valuable, we might have just secured ourselves another valuable resource.

Unfortunately, for as strong as the beast hides were, they weren’t magical. Which meant they couldn’t conduct mana unless they were woven with minithrel, which wouldn’t be easy and might defeat the purpose of making them. If the suits lost their flexibility and lightness, then they might as well have just made regular plate metal.

“Let me know when they are comple–” Carmack turned to leave, but the tailor cut him off.

“Wait, wait, wait. Hero, please.”

“Huh?”

“That other leather you had brought down to us.”

“Oh, right!” Carmack raised an index finger. He had been so busy he forgot about the giant chimera and the hides he had ordered collected.

“Come, follow me. You need to see this. We’ve fashioned it into a suit. We decided to keep it in the back of the store for safety. Something like this would be bound to attract unwanted attention.”

“Please,” Carmack gestured for the tailor to lead.

Following the man through a corridor and into a back storage room, he spotted the suit hanging up on a rack. The impressive suit was a deep black that seemed to steal the light that landed upon it. Despite not being hard like metal—as the scaled armor was—Carmack could tell that this suit was formidable. Furthermore, the black leather armor hummed with mana.

Impressive. With the right core and gemstone, this could be turned into a powerful magical suit of armor.

Carmack paused that thought. Once it was bound with a lesser monster core, that was it. Binding mana into a magical item was a permanent change, and he had to ask himself if he would come across another hide like this anytime soon. Or would putting a lesser core into such a fine suit of armor be a waste?

No need to be hasty. It’s not going anywhere.

“So, do you like it?”

“Of course I do. It’s a marvelous piece. You have outdone yourself. Please, would you mind having this suit of armor taken to my residence?”

“Yes, of course we can, hero.”

“Great. I’ll be off then. Thanks again for all your hard work. You and the other tailors have not just exceeded my expectations, but provided an invaluable service to your nation. This kind of devotion to work is exactly what we need to survive down here.”

“Really? Wow, thank you hero! Your words are too kind. They truly mean a lot.”

“I’m just being honest. Now, until next time.”

Carmack returned to the smith. There were still many things he needed—including a new staff for himself—but most would have to wait until he got his hands on stronger cores.

Instead, he requested a sword, shield, and spear be crafted with minithrel. By the time he was done, the foundations of their vanguard would be laid down.

Exiting the smithy, he walked into the adjoining stone corridor and was immediately hailed down by a runner.

“It’s the greenhouse. They request your urgent presence, hero.”

“Thank you,” Carmack said and hurried off toward the greenhouse floor.

Stepping through an archway, Carmack was delighted to see people harvesting wheat and rice. There were dozens of them, and piles of harvested crops were already starting to line up.

“There you are, hero,” Said Master Spell Scribe Tibus with open arms as he marched toward Carmack.

“It appears we have food,” Carmack said as they embraced momentarily.

“Yes, plenty of it. We should have no worries feeding everyone, as long as we keep this up. And with the crystals and constant warm airflow from below, we should be able to keep harvests going all year round. Who’d have thought? At this rate, we’ll be producing more food than Lydrus. Amazing,” Tibus shook his head as if he didn’t believe the words coming from his own mouth.

“Brilliant. It sounds like we can remove the ration program and set prices for our harvests. Make sure we have a supply kept for support purposes. I expect everyone to work, but I don’t plan on making anyone go hungry.”

“Will do. My apprentice has gone to get the High Priest as well. He should be here soon.”

“Good. I’m sure he’ll want to see this. I must ask, is there anything you or your people are in need of, Tibus?”

“Us? Well, not really. We have a few people working on maintaining the formations we use on the farms, but it's pretty easy work. Just a few alterations here and there.”

“I see. So, you have spare manpower? Tibus, may I ask what your knowledge of defensive formations is like?”

“We know a decent amount. Lydrus was covered in them, not that it stopped the demons. There’s only so much defensive formations can do, after all.”

“Right. I must admit, I had already heard a little about the defense of the city. I was informed that the walls held up quite well for a while. The real slaughter happened when they fell.”

“That’s correct,” Tibus nodded. “We fortified them with several layers of spell formations. The demons figured this out and concentrated their firepower on a single section of the wall. It was too much, and ultimately our formations failed.”

“As to be expected. Please, divert as much manpower to defensive formations as you can. We have light and food now. That is great, but it’ll mean little if we can’t defend it.”

“Yes, hero. I’ll get to it immediately. Though if I could get one request,” Tibus raised a pointed finger.

“Yes?”

“Paper. To put it bluntly, we need it. We’re running out, and while we can carve formations straight into the rock, it is far more painstaking. A lot of time is wasted if we make a mistake and have to revise the information. But we just don’t have enough paper to spare anymore. We take great care of our dwindling stock, and use it only when we absolutely have to.”

“I must admit that is a difficult request,” Carmack sighed. “There’s no realistic means by which we can produce enough paper down here. That being said, I’m already in the process of organizing a caravan to the surface. I cannot guarantee that I can provide a large stock of it, but I’ll have paper added to our list of needed items.”

“Thank you, hero. This will greatly help us and our ability to support the settlement.”

Carmack had already realized there was an obstacle to removing the ration program. It had become the linchpin of their tiny economy. The people could, of course, revert to a barter system, but he wanted them to move forward, not back.

Currency would be needed. There was some copper and even a little silver and gold flowing through the veins of rock around them. But the main reserves of precious metals available to them were still what remained of Lydrus’ coffers.

Controlling the economy on a micro scale would be too invasive and time-consuming, and Carmack certainly didn’t want any part in that. This was a task that would require consultation with the elites, but he wanted to pen down his ideas before involving anybody. It was clear that any proposals would have opposition, and it was wise to give his rivals as little to work with as possible, which required a thorough plan.

With the food supply entirely controlled by the state, he could set a price and have the economy build itself around said price. The earnings would fund the state coffers until trade could be established, and trades could set appropriate wages based on what people needed to survive.

This would be easy to stimulate since so much of what the trades were currently producing were items requested by the state itself. They would simply continue as they had been, but the state would pay for what they requested, and the trade masters could figure out their own wages.

A knock at Carmack’s door came, and he turned to it with a raised brow. “Come in.”

“They’ve returned, Hero,” his assistant bowed. “The skeleton was still in the upper chamber, and the scouts have advised that they were able to recover it.”

Ah, right, that is welcome news.

“Good to know you were able to secure it. Thank you for that. I have important plans for it.”

“It’s my honor to serve. I have had them store it where you requested.”

“Perfect.”
