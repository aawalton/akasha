Chapter LX (60)

Zoe had decided that the newest misfit child to join them needed to meet more of the children, so she’d cobbled together a group of about ten children, a combination of goblins and humans, to introduce themselves to and chat with Mitsuko. Or, ‘Mia’ as she was currently known. Thankfully, Hallie, the acolyte the most in tune with Verdant Island’s guardian, was not included in the assembled group.

“You’re from Hon?”

“That’s amazing! I’ve heard the sailors talk about Hon before. It sounds so mystical and cool!”

“My ma said your trees are colored like Mauve Island, but only in the spring!”

“Have you ever seen an artifact from the World Dungeon?”

Mitsuko’s ring felt heavy at the mention of a World Dungeon artifact. While she hadn’t been the one to recover it from the depths of the dangerous labyrinth, like all the most powerful objects, it had originated there.

“I’m…too young to delve into the World Dungeon,” she said evasively.

This lie was accompanied by a chorus of sad groans from the children all around her.

“But Mia, you must know something about it, right?” one of the goblin children pressed. “You must know so many legends and met delvers!”

“Delvers aren’t common,” Mitsuko said. “They die a lot while delving. Most don’t live past their twenties.”

“And those who do?” Zoe asked. “They must be powerful, right?”

Mitsuko nodded slowly. They were both powerful and rich. And not the ‘comfortably retire’ kind of rich. A truly lucky delve could set up a person with generational wealth.

“My teacher delved,” Mitsuko finally admitted. “He told me a few stories.”

There was a cheer and all the children huddled in closer to her, ready to listen. Mitsuko couldn’t resist letting a small smile slip at the unbridled excitement and curiosity. After quickly wracking her memory for a tale, she settled on one of her favorites.

“This is a story from his father, who also was a delver. With the World Dungeon constantly shifting and changing, he was on call and alert, waiting for any sorts of interesting rumors. Then one such turned up. A woman emerged from the World Dungeon, alone and brutally injured. But clutching a single totem made of solid gold. When questioned, she repeated the same words over and over, that there was an ancient city of gold currently below them. The totem was proof of her words’ authenticity. But otherwise whatever she’d seen down there had driven her utterly mad. Her mind was shattered beyond repair.

“My teacher’s father didn’t have much time. If they waited, they risked the World Dungeon shifting too much and them losing the city below.”

“How does the World Dungeon move?” the goblin child cut in. “Is it magically controlled like what the wizards on Amber Island do?”

“Those wizards can only dream of controlling one percent of the quantity of power Sekai retains,” Sterling said from where he rested in Zoe’s lap. The girl was petting his back slowly while waiting intently for Mitsuko’s response to the question. “They’re a bunch of upstarts with their noses stuck in the air. Their power equates to bits of dust compared to a Primordial Titan like Sekai.”

Mitsuko ignored the cat. “It’s alive,” she told the children. “It’s like the sea, but more sinister and it makes far less sense. It cannot be predicted like the tides or winds. The World Dungeon creates monstrosities and wonders in equal measures.”

The children tittered at each other.

“My teacher’s father gathered nine other experienced delvers, each of them powerful mages ready to face whatever threats lay beneath their feet. Ten people in total went down into the winding tunnels, guided by a powerful diviner who could cut through the dungeon’s anti-divination fog. At least to some degree.” Mitsuko paused, waiting for the children to be properly impressed by that fact. But of course they just stared at her blankly, waiting for her to continue on. Mitsuko resisted pressing how remarkable that achievement was, and continued on. “It took them over a day of travel, and they fought all manner of monsters on the journey, but they—”

“What sort of monsters?” one of the girls cut her off. “Were they scary?”

“Yes, very scary. I don’t know what exactly they faced but I’ve see—” She coughed, attempting to cover her blunder. “I’ve heard of all sorts of dangerous beasts. Like a monster made up of the eyes of its victims and another that is invisible and waits until you sleep before sucking out all your body’s fluids. There’s a lot of scary monsters down there.”

“Continue with the story,” the goblin boy said, annoyed by his friend’s interruption.

“All ten of them survived the journey down and they found the city. Every building was made of glittering gold and gemstones. Not a single bit of mundane stone existed in those streets. But, of course, to mages, gold is far from the most valuable object found within the World Dungeon.”

“They wanted magical artifacts?” one child guessed.

“Yes. So they began scouring the city, seeking out the mystical items. Everything within the city was silent, so they made the foolish decision to split up during their search.”

“Someone died,” the goblin boy said solemnly. He’d likely heard similar stories before about children who snuck off alone. Some parables had a tendency to transcend cultures and be found universally. But this wasn’t that sort of tale.

“The opposite,” Mitsuko replied. “They regrouped in the city’s entrance to document and research everyone’s findings. But when they did, they found not ten delvers, but eleven.”

A hushed silence fell on the children. Mitsuko reveled in it for half a minute before continuing.

“Frightened by the prospect, the group of delvers spent several hours dissecting and attempting to prove who their eleventh member was. The problem wasn’t that they’d forgotten, but rather that they remembered. Each of them had a deep well of memories for each person present. They were utterly clueless about who the interloper was.”

“So how did they figure it out?” Zoe asked. “What did they do to reveal the fake?”

“They took a vote. And they deemed it more dangerous to linger in the city than to continue out of the World Dungeon. So they began their ascent. As they traveled up, two of their members died to monster attacks. When they finally reached the surface, their number was nine, not eleven. They decided that the eleventh member must have been among the two that died. And they left the World Dungeon behind.”

“Wait! They didn’t uncover the imposter?” the goblin boy exclaimed. “Then he could be out in the world!”

“Or she,” Mitsuko reminded him. “Nobody knew who the eleventh delver was. Perhaps the entity was among those who died. Or perhaps, it lived on and still watches humanity. In our homes, infiltrating our lives with ease. The eleventh delver will likely forever remain a mystery.”

A silence fell on the group of children.

“Sekai truly does create some fascinating monsters,” Sterling said, unaffected by the tale. “I’ll bet that the monster bypassed the powerful wards keeping it contained within Sekai by latching onto the memories of the other delvers and manipulating them into allowing it passage through the power of perception. I’m once again glad Sekai is not allowed on our archipelago. His spawn can be devious and crafty, or powerful beyond words. Granted, our trade here isn’t phenomenal when…” Sterling’s words drifted off, mid sentence.

Mitsuko glanced at the sage but said nothing. She longed to ask him where that incomplete statement ended. But the last time she’d pressed him for information, Sterling had combusted into dust.

“What if the person hunting Mother tries something like that?” Zoe asked.

“Mother is too powerful,” Gavin spoke up for the first time in the conversation. He was another one of the suspected acolytes of the island’s guardian. A goblin with fiery red hair to match his defiant tone. “Mother would whip a monster into pieces. There’s no way something like that thing could trick her. Her music is invincible.”

“Yeah!” the first goblin boy agreed. “It’s too defensive. She’ll protect us no matter what. We’re going to have the entire world to explore after next week.”

The rest of the children muttered agreement, the mood lightening. Still, while several of the children wanted ‘Mia’ to tell them another story from Hon, Gavin put a stop to that. Instead, the older boy took control of the group and dispersed them all.

“Did you intend for your story to align so closely with your current predicament?” Sterling asked as they wandered away.

“No,” Mitsuko replied. She hadn’t even been thinking about how it might connect with her own infiltration into their ranks. “But maybe it’s best if we depart before anyone else starts wondering that same question.”
