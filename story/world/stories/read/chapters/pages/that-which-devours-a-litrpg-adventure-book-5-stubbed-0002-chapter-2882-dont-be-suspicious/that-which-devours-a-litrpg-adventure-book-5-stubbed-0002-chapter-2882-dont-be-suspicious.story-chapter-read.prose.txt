Alex, in the cavern…

I silently inches closer to the tunnel opening behind the waterfall, yet within a foot of the exit the heat increased exponentially. My fingers literally started sweating.

“We should trap the entrance, just in case,” came a voice from behind me.

I clenched my teeth and closed my eyes, hoping I wouldn’t be seen.

“Just an alarm,” said Zepyr, still staring at the water entrance.

Strange padded closer to the wall in the other direction. He was basically as far away from the water as he could get.

“Be quick about it,” Zepyr added, as the unseen person moved on my map.

I lost the connection with Strange as I focused on not being seen or felt.

The guy passed me by, still invisible, but without the buzzing.

I closed my eyes and focused on being unseen.

“It’s gotten much hotter,” he said. His voice echoed past me.

He wasn’t wrong. Even standing in the last switch-back closest to the cavern, the heat increased moment by moment.

I couldn’t stay here. The cavern was a no-go, since as soon as they created some light, I’d be found. That left the water. I sent the plan to Strange. I needed to get to the pool.

He confirmed.

The unseen person hurried back in my direction, this time moving faster than before. As soon as he passed me, I crept after him.

He walked faster than me, and made it back to the cavern much faster.

I hesitated just within the tunnel opening to the cavern.

“The sun has hit the valley, and fires are spreading along the grasslands.” His voice just hovered in the air. “We need to block off the entrance, to reduce the heat and smoke coming in. Give me your shield.”

“I don’t want to use it now,” grumbled Vyrn.

“It’s necessary,” came from Zepyr. “We’d rather not cook to death in these rocks.”

A snort came from the darkness. “If we cooked to death, that’d be a new one.”

I carefully crept into the room and to the left. It’d bring me closer to the pool.

Zepyr stared at Vyrn. “Let’s use the shield. I’d rather be safe than cooked.”

“We can’t be cooked,” argued Vyrn, shaking his head and stretching out each of his fingers one-by-one.

I said nothing as I moved inch-by-inch along the dark wall. The light from the dungeon entrance highlighted the area, but somehow they couldn’t see it.

“We can’t!” This time, Vyrn’s voice came out more like a whine. “These things are expensive!”

“I’ll replace it,” said Zepyr, almost rolling his eyes.

Vyrn froze, mid stretch. “For real?”

“Yes. I’d rather be safe.” He nodded at Vyrn, giving him a look I didn’t catch.

Vyrn pulled something out of his bag and headed to the tunnel. “Alright, I’ll get it set up.”

He passed me by without even looking in my direction, then the unseen person followed him, leaving Zepyr in the cavern alone.

Grumbles echoed down the tunnel. “It’s hotter than before.”

I continued on. Bit-by-bit, inching closer to the pool of water.

Strange rested across the cavern from me. Not doing a dang thing. We needed to get to the water.

Zepyr sighed loudly as he closed his eyes. He shook his head and ran his hands over his face. Something rested along each of his wrists.

“Like, a lot hotter…” Again the words echoed through the tunnel.

Zepyr just shook his head and turned away from the pool. “Why must I always be right about these sort of things?”

I barely heard the words as I inched closer to the pool.

A bag appeared near his feet, and he crouched down and opened the flap.

If he had a light, I was screwed. We both were.

A reassuring sigh rippled down the bond between Strange and I, along with the urge to keep going. Get to the pool.

That was the plan.

Slowly, carefully, I moved closer to the pool. I was careful to not hit any small rocks or make any sounds, looking carefully before taking each footstep.

More arguing echoed down the tunnel from the opening, causing Zepyr to stand up from his pack. He took several steps in that direction before he picked up his pace and turned into the tunnel opening.

I sped up. Still staying quiet, but less careful.

Strange also inched closer to the bag that Zepyr left behind. Which didn’t make sense. We needed to go.

All three dots hurried down the tunnel, heading back in this direction, along with light. It made the tunnel entrance glow.

“The shield is holding against the heat.” Vyrn's voice echoed down the tunnel as the light bounced closer. “I didn’t think the grass would burn like that.”

I sat on the edge of the pool and dipped my boots in. It wasn’t cold, but it wasn’t warm.

“Strange, come on,” I sent down the bond.

He didn’t move. Just sniffed the backpack, like it was the most important thing in the world. Then he literally stuffed himself under the flap in the shadows.

“Strange?”

The only answer he gave me was to hurry.

Then, light washed over everything as I slipped into the liquid, taking a deep breath at the last moment.

The water washed over my head as I swam down and over, trying to get under the rock before anyone noticed the ripples on the surface. Surprisingly, I glided through the water faster than I had ever swam in my life.

Light barely penetrated the water from the entrance to the cavern. My radar highlighted the dark shapes of the rocky tunnel, but not much more. The farther away from the pool entrance I got, the less the light touched anything.

Grizzle’s dot in the distance mocked me. Somehow he was deeper than I was, and much farther away. The water grew colder, darker, but nothing really changed around me as I traveled down the tunnel.

Still, I didn’t dare stop.

Even as my lungs started to burn, I put one hand in front of the other, kicking my legs behind me. Each stroke of my arms pushed as much water as I could. My hand touched rock, knocking me out of my rhythm.

A sharp point of rock jutted down from the ceiling of the tunnel, but I hadn’t seen it in the darkness. I pushed around it and kept going.

My lungs burned, and there was a deep ache inside my chest even as I pushed to keep swimming.

The dot loomed closer.

I could make it.

I wasn’t going to drown on this planet.

Something moved in the water and fear trickled down my spine, but I didn’t stop moving.

Grizzle had a safe area.

My radar wobbled as the edges of my vision darkened. Everything screamed inside me to breathe, but I didn’t dare. My radar vanished, which was a first for me since I’d gotten my class.

I blinked twice, switching to my second sight. Lines curved in the water all around me, and something swam deep under me.

Massive, thin, and twisting among rocks that glowed with mana. The water thickened as it grew icy.

[You have entered an area of concentrated mana.]

Fuck! Not again!

Bubbles escaped my lips as my body struggled against me for a moment, trying to take a breath in water. I triggered my mana shield automatically, and it pushed the water away from me.

I gasped automatically as something like air entered my lungs. My foot landed on the edge of my shield, but it didn’t go through.

My hand touching the top of the cavern kept me from crashing as I focused on breathing.

The draw of mana brought me back to myself as my shield flickered.

I couldn’t stay here.

Like this.

After taking a deep breath I let the shield fall. Water slammed into me from all sides, along with raw mana. Yet, I couldn’t eat it like I did the last time this happened. I didn’t dare open my mouth.

So I swam.

Leaving the eel-like thing alone, and the trails of mana twisting around it.

I stuck near the top of the water filled cavern.

Eventually my radar flickered back on, and Grizzle’s dot loomed nearby.

It suddenly jerked, then moved in my direction. Nothing else appeared on my radar.

His massive form loomed in the distance, cutting through the water and reminding me of the prehistoric creature he was. Fear inched up my spine as his mouth opened.

Yet, I didn’t resist as he chomped down around me. Teeth missed everything important, and he swam through the water like he was born to it. Which, of course, he was.

Water rushed all around me as he picked up his speed, and everything blurred.

Moments later he let me go and pushed me up.

My head broke through the surface, and cold air hit me, making me snap my eyes open. I climbed up on the rocky shore.

Less a shore, and more of a ledge. My radar went wild as creatures appeared all around me.

“Alpha?” Grizzle climbed up behind me.

After a second of breathing the cold air in, I sputtered, “Still here.”

“Why did you go near the Alpha Eel?” he asked in a low voice. “If it had noticed you…”

A shudder moved through the air.

“I might have gotten a little lost.” The water on my skin grew colder. I sat up glancing around the room. Within the darkness hid several creatures, all lower level then Grizzle. Mostlying the crocs from earlier.

As soon as they saw me looking, they froze.

“Friends of yours?” I asked, quietly.

Grizzle snorted. “There aren’t enough of them to try me.”

The closet two flinched back, pressing against the wall behind them.

“You found a chilly spot,” I said, as I triggered my armor. The cold from the water lessened.

“Cold from Eel,” whispered a tiny voice. “Doesn't bother us small ones.”

My head whipped around to find a small spinosaurous like Grizzle.

[Shy Spinosaurous, Cold Claws, Level 62, Tasty Orange.]

My mouth watered at the reminder of the orange flavor from way back when.

Grizzle eyed the dinosaur, but said nothing.

“Good to know,” I replied. I wasn’t exactly a small one, and I didn’t want to get into a water fight. While my shield had unexpectedly held back the water and offered me literal breathing room, a smart creature wouldn’t give me the option to regroup like that.

Plus, my radar had stopped working around it.

Which was the most strange part of all of this.

“Where is the little one?” asked Grizzle.

“Strange, stayed behind. He’s…” I paused and touched the bond between us. “Happy, for some reason.”

I let out a deep breath.

The Shy Spinosaurous sniffed in my direction and took one small step.

“I’m not gonna hurt any of you if you don’t start trouble,” I said very clearly. My voice hung in the air in the cold cavern. “But if you start trouble, I will kill and eat you.”

“I will take that deal,” the Shy Spinosaurous said. She stepped closer to me and farther away from all of the little crocs.

Grizzle remained in his massive form as he flopped down next to the pool.

A shiver went around the room from the crocs. He must have taken care of several problems. The mass of smaller crocs said nothing, but each eyed me like I might be breakfast. Or maybe he didn’t show his strength.

My soaked hair dripped water down my back, and my clothes were still wet. For once, I really wanted to start a fire. Then again, drawing additional attention to this cavern wasn’t a good idea. Plus, the entire reason we were here was the heat outside, along with the three Rank three Clan Vielether sheltering in the dungeon cavern.

And freaking Strange had stayed behind.

I wrung the water out of my hair, careful to not twist it too hard. The water puddled under me. Whispers of cold floated through the air near the water, but faded farther away from it.

I stood and stretched up, letting my spine crack and causing more water to drip down from my clothing. Then, I took a step closer to the small spinosaurous.

“Want to show me this cavern?” I asked, carefully.

She blinked a few times, then glanced at the crocs, but slowly nodded. “I show.”

I stepped closer and she only reached up to my shoulders. Each step left a footprint on the smooth ground.

Several crocs hurried away from my approach, but a few others didn’t. They eyed me hungrily. One growled directly at me. It reminded me of Marb, but without the levels to back it up. The small spinosaurs next to me froze.

[Nimble Purussaurus, Fast Attack, Level 97, Cold Spice.]
