Although I'm keen to check on Artemis, Orion, and Artio, we take our time to carefully clear away the remains of the flower monster in order to access the dome. Not only do we need to be certain the monster won't suddenly regenerate from a fragment of root or centre and attack us again when our backs are turned, but the remains of a Tier three will probably be extremely valuable to our alchemists.

To that end, I contact Loren, still back at the settlement, and ask him to send some available alchemists – preferably River unless she’s too busy – along with some farmers and a Plant Mage to ensure we get as much use out of the remains here as possible. We're not going to wait for them to arrive before accessing the dome, but we carefully move the pieces to one side so they aren't damaged in the process.

I lay my hand on the dome itself and spread my awareness into the stone. I'm more than a little worried by the cracks that have formed in the surface, some of which are deep enough to have breached the dome entirely. Steeling myself for what we might find inside, I carefully crack it open.

A waft of stale air emerges. I sniff carefully. It doesn't seem to have turned poisonous, just lower in oxygen than is ideal. Since the low light inside isn’t enough for normal vision, I activate my darkvision and peer in. Three unmoving shapes lie within and there’s no glint of water in the large basin I left for those here.

Artio, Artemis, Orion, I call gently over our mental network. No reply. I speak aloud, my words disturbing a stillness that feels worryingly like that of a sepulchre.

Nothing moves. No one responds.

I exchange glances with my companions. They don’t say anything, but I can sense that they’re worried too.

Keep an eye on everything outside, please, I ask them, then step in.

Orion is the closest to me. I touch his shoulder gently, keeping myself ready to jump back in case he comes out of hibernation with his instincts more awake than his mind. As it is, he doesn't immediately react, though I feel his flesh twitch beneath my fingers. I'm relieved by that, as well as by the warmth emanating through his fur. Cooler than normal, but given his comatose state, that's to be expected.

Extending my Flesh-Shaping through my fingertips, I sink my mind into his body to check for anything wrong. There's a higher Energy concentration than I would normally expect, which is worrying in some ways, but there's no indication of the same kind of mutations I saw in the beasts that attacked us. In fact, his body looks surprisingly healthy considering he's gone several days with no water or food.

A theory forming, I sink deeper and enter his Core space.

Is it time? He is aware of my presence almost immediately and shows a complete lack of surprise that I’m here.

It is, I tell him. Is this where you've been for the last few days?

I notice several changes to his Core space – areas with far more detail than before, and even a couple of regions that are almost entirely new.

You've clearly been making good use of your time, I remark.

He sends me a sense of nonchalance.

When there is nothing to do but meditate, and the storm provides a great deal of energy that desires to transform and change, this seemed the best use of it, he replies dispassionately.

As a small ball of light I can't nod, but I send him a wave of approval.

Well, it certainly seems like you've done a good job. Has Artemis been doing the same?

As far as I know, yes, he confirms, emanating warm pleasure as always at the thought of her – it stems from having found what he considers an excellent mate. It was her idea.

The manastorm has finished now. Unless you still have progress you'd like to consolidate, it might be a good idea to emerge.

He sends me a pulse of acknowledgement, and I withdraw from his Core space.

Heading over to Artemis, I follow the same process – first checking her body, then entering her Core space to find her consciousness. Like Orion, she's in surprisingly good health given the conditions, and has made considerable progress of her own. I'm not entirely sure which of my companions will be the next to reach Tier Three, but given how much these two have accomplished, they may be some of the frontrunners.

With Artemis slowly stretching as she begins to rouse, I move over to Artio.

Little has changed about his condition which is disappointing in some ways, but a relief in others. His body has clearly absorbed far more energy than it would normally be able to, but unlike with Artemis and Orion, he isn't present to work on his Core space. Whatever the Energy that saturated his flesh was doing, only traces remain. Perhaps it travelled along the link to whatever is currently holding him.

Once more I touch that link and send a gentle summons down it, not wanting to drag him away from wherever he is in case that would harm him, but wanting to remind him that we're waiting. I listen for some time, just in case he's trying to communicate back.

As before, there is no reply.

Interestingly, the plants around him have changed in structure. Before they seemed to be holding him in place; now they appear more defensive in arrangement. If I didn't know he was there, I probably wouldn't have noticed him – not even his ear tufts emerge from the bower of green vines and brown roots. To reach him at all I have to wiggle my fingers through the few gaps to brush his fur.

I sigh and let my hand fall, guilt and worry nagging at my gut. Part of me wants to just stay here and focus on Artio until I’ve managed to get him back – he might be a bit of a grumpy bastard and we may not have known each other as long as I’ve known the rest of my non-human companions, but he’s still one of us.

But there are so many other things going on right now that I can’t justify focusing entirely on Artio when, apart from his consciousness being absent, he seems to be in decent health. He doesn’t even seem to be suffering from lack of water or food like Orion and Artemis were, if only to a certain degree. It hurts, but there are other matters that are higher on the priority list. If he starts deteriorating, I’ll have more justification – if only to myself.

Returning to Artemis and Orion, I stroke my hands through their fur, letting the relief that they, at least, are fine settle deep into my being and release some of the tension of my muscles.

Now, without the distraction of not knowing their condition, I can more easily turn my attention to the food issue and the illness. The illness isn't quite as pressing as the food situation, but it's rapidly growing in importance as more and more people become infected.

Emerging from the dome, I find that the group I asked Loren to send has arrived and is already beginning to examine and process the plant monster – I’m a little disappointed when I spot River’s teacher rather than River herself. The Plant Mage comes over to me with a chunk of stalk in his hand and an impressed look on his face.

"This is a powerful Tier three plant monster,” he tells me with awe in his face, waving the stalk at me. “It's only the second time I've seen one at this level. I’m surprised so few managed to kill it – and with no casualties either!" He pauses, then bows and adds quickly, "I'm sorry, my lord – I meant no offence."

I wave his apology away.

"It certainly wasn't easy," I admit, "but fortunately it had only a single main centre, and once we managed to reach that, we were able to take it down."

Mage Githris nods knowingly.

"That makes sense. We'll search for any secondary centres that may have survived. If we find any, would you like us to destroy them, harvest them, or attempt to transplant them?"

I look at him with curiosity.

"Transplant…why would we want to do that?” Having a violent plant monster near our settlement sounds like the last thing we’d want.

Mage Githris clearly thinks differently.

“Even if it’s no use otherwise – which I doubt – it could act as an excellent guard if what I see here is any judge. Of course, you’d need an experienced Plant Mage tending it and keeping it under control – a Master Plant Mage if one of its offshoots gets to Tier three too.”

“That sounds rather like Taming,” I comment with interest.

Mage Githris makes a distracted shrug, examining the chunk of stalk closely.

“Perhaps we could even grow one of them from cuttings – if your battle hasn’t destroyed its internal structure too much. All the potential of the adult and none of the resistance….” He trails off, then, with a jerk, seems to remember that he’s in the middle of a conversation. “My apologies, my lord.”

“Don’t worry about it,” I dismiss. “In answer to your question, by all means attempt to transplant or grow from cuttings – as long as you’re confident it won’t start terrorising anyone.” I'm already thinking about ways we can use it to defend the farmers, or to guard supplies that would be more convenient if kept out in the forest. It certainly did an effective job of defending the dome, though that wasn't exactly a job I'd intended for it.

Then I consider how similar plant-magery sometimes seems in comparison to my own Class. “If you do succeed in growing and taming one of these plant creatures, I’d be interested to compare techniques.”

"Of course, my lord," Githris agrees, after a brief pause. There’s a hint of reluctance in his voice that doesn't particularly surprise me – mages are often secretive about their techniques – as are most Classers, really. But I'm not above using my position as leverage when it comes to learning more about magic, and particularly about my own Class.

Markus. A mental voice reaches me, tight with tension. I hold up a closed fist to stop Githris from continuing.

What is it, Josiah? I ask, recognising the voice.

I hate to say this, but....we have a problem, he says, with just a touch of gallows humour.

What kind of problem?

A long, watery, serpentine kind of problem. The sense of frustrated exasperation that travels along the Bond would make me laugh if the situation weren’t as serious as it is. It seems the serpent is disinclined to allow us to have the final word.

I sigh aloud. Of course. It seems like whenever I'm not trapped inside during a manastorm, I can't take two steps without being pulled in different directions.

"I apologise, Mage Githris," I say, catching the sideways look he's giving me. "It appears I'm being called away. If you do find an offshoot and it's at all possible to delay the taming, I'd be grateful – as I said, I'd very much like to observe the process. But I understand if it can't wait."

"As my lord wishes," he acquiesces, with a resigned air.

I'm on my way, I tell Josiah, preventing another sigh from escaping. If I keep on like that, I’ll give myself issues with hyperventilation and I have much better things to do.

While regrowing my air wings, I check in with my Bonded. Artemis and Orion decide to stay behind, as do Fenrir and the two Pathwalkers, who have no desire to be involved in another sea battle. Lathani and Ninja tuck themselves into my spatial tattoo in hopes that they might be able to help. Bastet, of course, takes to the skies ahead of me, soaring like she was born to it. I send a long glance at Artio, then turn away again. He’s well enough for now, I think to myself with determination to believe it.

After leaving quick instructions with the samurans and asking Githris to speak to them if he has any questions about the harvest, I also take to the skies. With Bastet flying at my side, we head out to sea.

As I fix my eyes on the smudge that’s probably Josiah’s ship, I have a nasty thought: What if this sea serpent was mutated by the manastorm too?
