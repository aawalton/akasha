Veric was lounging right down the corridor from Joe’s heavy oak door—exactly where Jin expected him to be. A lit cigarette dangled lazily from his lips. His posture carried the highly specific, deeply patient quality of a man who had been waiting for an unreasonable amount of time and had generously decided to only make you feel a little bit guilty about it.

"How are you holding up?" Jin asked, coming to a halt beside him.

"Good," Veric said, offering a casual shrug. He took a drag from his cigarette, the ember glowing bright. "Well, assuming you're not planning on going all possessed and berserk again anytime soon. It's terrible for my old bones."

"Yeah," Jin winced.

It wasn't the dig that stung, but the heavy, uncomfortable memory lurking just underneath it. A part of him hated how many people he'd put in mortal peril over the past few days just by existing. Another part of him, quieter and far more honest, was profoundly grateful he still had people left who cared enough to make the joke at all.

Right on cue, a wall-shaking thud reverberated from inside Joe’s room.

It was immediately followed by the sound of splintering wood and Rudy’s muffled, frantic voice forming the unmistakable auditory shape of 'Please stop!'

Veric slowly raised a single, questioning eyebrow.

"Don't ask," Jin sighed, already turning to walk away. "Rudy was being stupid."

Another loud crash echoed into the hallway, followed by the distinctive sound of heavy furniture breaking.

"Wasn't going to ask," Veric chuckled, blowing out a stream of smoke as he fell into step beside Jin. "Just glad it's not me."

They made their way down the brightly lit corridor toward the elevator. Veric pressed his thumb against the runic panel, which flared a welcoming green before the heavy metal doors slid open. They stepped inside, the descent to Floor 21 smooth and unnervingly quiet.

"Any particular reason we're heading to twenty-one?" Jin asked, leaning back against the cool metal wall and carefully favouring his bruised ribs.

"It's the biggest open floor we've got in the entire Bastion," Veric explained. He took another drag of his cigarette, exhaling a thin stream of smoke toward the enchanted lighting fixture on the ceiling. "Used to be hydroponics and housing. Which means good airflow, reinforced structural supports, and residential annexes already attached."

"And the food supply?" Jin asked.

"Moved the actual farming up to floors twenty-three and twenty-four. Full-time crop cultivation now," Veric said as the elevator doors chimed and slid open. He gestured outward with his cigarette. "Down here, we have five main halls, completely repurposed for our training, logistics, and staging. We haven't fully locked down the divisional layout yet, but the space works."

Jin stepped out of the elevator and took the floor in. Veric hadn't been exaggerating.

The cavernous space was a hive of controlled, purposeful chaos. Sweating recruits in mismatched armour were hauling heavy crates of alchemical supplies between staging zones. The air smelled faintly of ozone, sweat, and oiled steel.

« Finally, it looks like we are preparing for war. »

Yeah, and for once, we actually have the space to do it right.

Jin thought back, a weary but genuine smile touching his face.

"How many processed?" Jin asked, not breaking his stride as they navigated the throng.

"Close to eight hundred," Veric said, exhaling a plume of smoke toward the ceiling. "Two-thirty-something actually fit your absurdly specific criteria. More than I expected, honestly."

"People are terrified, Veric," Jin said, his tone turning pragmatic. "And terrified people desperately want something to do so they don't have to sit around thinking about how terrified they are."

Veric made a noncommittal grunting noise. "Before your big show kicks off, I want you to meet some people."

“Show? You make me sound like a scam artist.” Jin chuckled.

« Isn’t that what your profession is? It says that in your—»

Shut up!

Veric led the way down a narrower, thankfully less crowded side corridor and pushed open the door to a conference room. Inside, Elenor glanced up from her glowing console, offering Jin a brief, professional nod before returning to her work. Beside her sat Maya, arms firmly crossed and projecting a menacing glare his way.

Across the metal table sat two strangers. A man and a woman, both clad in the Bastion-issue fatigues. Jin’s passive aura automatically swept over them, pinging their essence pressure and definitively marking them both at Order II.

Jin carefully lowered himself into a vacant chair.

"Boss," Veric said, carelessly dropping into the chair beside him. "Meet Lisbon and Wayne. Mage Guild, both of them. Order II on rank, but—"

"But they can cast third-circle magic," Jin interrupted. He could see the magic circle around their essence heart thanks to his mystic eyes.

"Got it in one," Veric smirked.

"Well, that’s..." Jin shook his head. “Amazing find. Thank you, guys, for coming on board.”

Wayne shifted in his seat, looking distinctly awkward under the sudden, intense scrutiny. "Thank you, sir. I can only cast one 3rd circle spell; it's all thanks to my grimoire, sir," he mumbled, rubbing the back of his neck. "Family heirloom. Lisbon here... she’s just got the head for it, a pure arcanist."

Lisbon offered a shy but confident smile.

"We've identified a few more personnel from the Guild, thanks to these," Veric added, tapping his cigarette ash into an empty ration tin. "Still vetting them; most are just bureaucratic and researchers. The testing is still going on."

"Right then," Jin leaned forward, resting his elbows on the table. "Let's talk about what exactly it is you two can do. And more importantly, how you fit into the absolute nightmare of what happens next."

◈◈◈

Two hours later, Jin stood on a podium cobbled together from two stacked supply crates and a precariously wobbling sheet of salvaged alloys plating. He looked out at the sea of a hundred faces.

They were civilians who had never held a weapon with lethal intent, garrison guards whose survival during the assault could be chalked up to blind luck rather than skill, and a handful of Mage Guild stragglers scattered through the crowd like poorly stirred seasoning. To Jin’s aura senses, they felt like a chaotic storm of terror, exhaustion, and fragile hope.

Angel. Sort them.

« Scanning. Filtering by potential, essence capacity, channel durability, and cognitive processing speed. Estimated completion: four seconds. »

Four seconds for a hundred people. Jin almost laughed despite the throbbing in his chest. Show-off.

« You told me to be fast. »

I did. Carry on.

He closed his eyes and let the ability [Sovereign's Indifference] settle over him. It stripped away the sharp, biting ache of his battered ribs and the bone-deep exhaustion dragging at his limbs, leaving behind nothing but a grey, frictionless calm. When he opened his eyes again, his mystic eyes flared with quiet silver light. The crowd resolved into something far more intricate than just faces—essence pathways, channel capacities, and mental resilience metrics layered themselves over his vision as soft data that only he could perceive.

None of them was going to be heroes. He wasn't looking for heroes. He was looking for reliable. He had learned the hard way that when fate came knocking, you didn't need a hero; you just needed someone willing to stand in the way.

"Everyone." Jin didn't yell. He didn't need to.

He let his aura—[Dominion of Yield]—press outward. It wasn't the aggressive, crushing weight of a high-ranker enforcing obedience, but a slow, even wave, filling the quiet room like a held breath. The nervous murmur of the crowd died out immediately, extinguished without a single person consciously deciding to stop talking.

"I'm Jin Winters. Acting commander, for whatever that's worth right now," Jin said, his voice carrying easily through the cavernous space.

A ripple went through the crowd. He could feel it in their emotions—spikes of recognition, jagged bursts of fear—and he let his aura settle over them gently, exerting a comforting pressure, the exact way you’d place a hand flat on a shaking shoulder to steady it.

"I'm not going to waste your time with grandiose introductions. By the time I'm done with you, you're going to be the backbone of this city."

He paused, letting the weight of the statement land. In the front row, a young garrison guard nervously clenched his hands.

"I'm not going to stand up here and tell you that you shouldn't be scared," Jin continued, his tone turning pragmatic. "You'd be an absolute idiot not to be. I've seen exactly what's coming for us, and if I wasn't scared, I'd be the idiot."

A few heads lifted at that. The tension in the room didn't vanish, but it shifted. It wasn't relief, exactly. It was permission.

"Fear doesn't magically go away just because you fight it," Jin said, letting his silver-lit gaze sweep across the room, slow and deliberate. "It goes away because you give it something to point at. You give it a job to do. Every single person standing in this room has already lost something. A home. A neighbour. Someone you loved who isn't walking around anymore because some fanatical cult decided your lives were nothing more than a resource to be spent."

He let the silence stretch, the memory of the recent bloodshed hanging heavy in the air.

"They're not finished," Jin said softly, though the words cut like glass. "They want the rest of what's left."

He took a slow breath.

"I'm not going to promise you that the sorcery you learn today will bring any of that back. It won't. Nothing brings that back. What it does do is make absolutely sure they don't get to take anything else from anyone else's family."

He brought a fist up, striking his own chest right over his bruised ribs. He hit it hard enough for the dull thud to echo, ignoring the spike of pain that [Sovereign's Indifference] dutifully suppressed.

"You're not soldiers. I'm not going to lie to you and say you'll wake up tomorrow magically transformed into hardened warriors," Jin said, his voice taking on a magnetic, undeniable gravity. "What you are is the difference between us winning this and us losing it. Every sorcery cast in perfect unison with the person standing next to you becomes a piece of the weapon that ends this."

He let the words settle into the room.

"So, from today, you're not a hundred separate names and a hundred separate fears. You're the Choir."

"Every voice raised together becomes one sound, loud enough to be heard over the screaming. That's what you're building. That's what walks out of this room."

"Right. Now," Jin said, his tone shifting. "Based on the aptitude scans and your performance today, you've been sorted into three divisions."

He paced a slow, deliberate line across the makeshift podium, his silver-lit eyes sweeping the crowd.

"Division One. Backbones. Three groups of twenty-five. One designated lead per group." Jin found the cluster of recruits whose overlay data read as mediocre-but-stable and gave a brief, satisfied nod. "You don't need to be magical geniuses. You don't need raw, world-shaking talent. You need to be reliable. Each of you is going to learn exactly one sorcery. You will cast it perfectly, every single time, without thinking about it—the exact same way you don't think about breathing. You're the engine. This entire machine runs on you doing the exact same thing right, over and over, until it stops being a choice and becomes an instinct."

A few of the newly dubbed Backbones shifted uncomfortably at that. Jin didn't offer reassurances. He let them sit with the uncomfortable weight of it.

"Division Two. Sustainers. Two groups of ten." He gestured toward a knot of recruits whose aura data showed wider, more flexible channels—not devastatingly powerful, but highly adaptable. "Your essence channels run broader than the Backbones'. Your job is to keep the engine from overheating and exploding. Recovery sorceries, stamina potions, and physically dragging people out of the fire when they inevitably burn past their limits."

Finally, he turned his attention to the last fifteen.

"Division Three. Conductors. Five slots, chosen from you fifteen by the end of today." He let that hang in the stale, recycled air. "You're the steering wheel. You take the raw sorcery the Backbones are casting, and you weave it together into one cohesive combination sorcery. For that, you need above-average talent."

A long pause stretched out. Someone near the back shifted their weight, the scuff of a boot agonizingly loud in the quiet room.

"I'll pick the five tonight," Jin added, his voice carrying the absolute finality of a judge.

Behind him, the heavy drag of canvas scraping across a metal framework echoed through the hall. Veric, Vera, and Elenor were efficiently assembling the row of private cabins that would serve as imprinting booths. Veric looked thoroughly bored as he wrestled a heavy tarp, while Vera's pale blue hair bobbed as she secured the frames with practiced magical efficiency. The heavy drapes gave the booths the distinctly unsettling look of something caught halfway between a religious confessional and a plague quarantine tent.

An academic magical lecture on one side of the room. Grim, industrial triage on the other.

That was the job, really, Jin thought wearily.

Jin turned his attention back to the crowd. "You were all issued a Tempad from the Bastion's reserves. I assume you watched the assigned videos?"

A hundred heads nodded in unison. Quite a few of them looked physically sick, their complexions pale and sweaty.

Good, Jin thought, his expression unyielding. They absolutely should be.

"Then you've seen exactly what we're up against," Jin said. He leaned forward, giving them a grim, utterly humorless smile.

"Good, any questions before we start the procedure?"

Hands went up and Jin sighed.
