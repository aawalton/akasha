The air in Gloomvale Woods hung thick with the aftermath of slaughter.

Acidic mist coiled between tree trunks, carrying the stench of dissolved slime and chemical decay. Afternoon light cast spotlights on the ground through the dense canopy, illuminating swirling particles of ash and slime in each sunbeam. Dozens of gelatinous slug corpses lay scattered across the forest floor in various states of dissolution.

Ace sat propped against a moss-covered boulder, his Thornhide armor regenerating itself in an unsettling display of sentient leather. Every breath brought new information—the subtle tang of iron in Tara's blood as she knelt beside him, the acrid fear-sweat still clinging to Marcus's skin, the disturbing smears of blood on Olivia’s cheek as she moved with predatory grace between the corpses.

"Look what I made!" The System's voice rang out behind him.

Ace turned slowly, careful to conserve his energy in case this was another of her twisted games.

Sure enough, the System floated cross-legged in the air, her childlike form backlit by a sunbeam. In her small hands, she held a crude doll made of twigs and what looked disturbingly like actual human hair. Two button eyes had been sewn into the doll’s head with thread, and a ragged strip of familiar leather—from Victor's jacket, it seemed—had been wrapped around its middle.

“What the hell is that?” Marcus asked, pointing at the doll.

The System’s crimson eyes gleamed as she lifted the ragged toy, and she wiggled it in front of Ace’s nose. When she spoke again, her voice changed to a high-pitched, wavering tone.

"Aaaace,” she said in a high-pitched, quivering tone that reminded Ace of someone trying to mimic a ghost. “You killed me... how could you..." She manipulated the doll's stick arms in jerky movements. "Ooooooooooo!"

“Are you serious right now?” Tara asked dryly.

The System giggled, still shaking the doll in front of Ace.

Ace crossed his arms and raised one eyebrow. "Are you done?"

“Aw, man,” She pouted and ran her fingers through the little doll’s hair. "You’re so boring. Most people would at least flinch when confronted with a teammate that they murdered. You're no fun at all."

"If you're trying to make me feel guilty, don't bother," Ace said dismissively. "He was a liability."

It was the truth.

He felt nothing at the memory of chopping off the man’s head.

The System zoomed around to float in front of Ace once again, her eyes narrow with curiosity. "And that's all it takes for you to kill someone now? How marvelously efficient you've become."

Ace met her gaze, but he didn’t reply.

The floating girl poked him in the side of his head. “Don’t you want to see all your stats, Sergeant Grumpy Pants?”

He sighed in annoyance.

Without answering, Ace summoned his stats screen to check. He had quite a few AP to allocate, and given his new Level 25 Skill, he needed to do so wisely.

He leaned against the rough boulder beside him, feeling the solid stone against his back as he examined the floating stat panel only he could see.

The choice wasn't simple, especially with Royal Tribute's mechanics echoing in his mind. The skill would allow him to access combat techniques from those whose blood he'd consumed, but both duration and clarity scaled with Intelligence.

"Intelligence," he muttered, tracing the stat with his finger. Royal Tribute's effectiveness hinged on how well he could recall and execute the techniques he absorbed. More Intelligence meant clearer memories and longer durations.

But Intelligence also determined his Soul Meter capacity—his magical fuel tank. The slug battle had drained him completely. A larger reservoir might have prevented that near-disaster.

Wisdom, on the other hand, governed his Soul Meter regeneration. A massive reservoir of SM wasn’t as useful if it filled at a trickle.

His Strength stat was lower than the others, and it was also the foundation of vampire combat. Vitality meant survival and more HP. Dexterity would give him better reaction time and reflexes.

Tough choices to make, but he was getting the hang of this.

A balanced approach felt right. The physical bump would keep him competitive in direct combat, while the mental enhancements would transform Royal Tribute from merely useful to potentially game-changing.

As he confirmed his allocation, he felt the power settle into his body. His mind expanded, connections forming between memories and instincts he hadn't consciously recognized before. He hadn’t fed on the alpha slug, so he couldn’t glean anything from it, and the Skill was limited to just one hour of consumption.

"You're turning into something interesting," the System's voice echoed in his head. "Not quite the blunt instrument I expected."

Ace ignored her and summoned his updated stats screen.

Huh.

Not bad.

Nearby, Marcus poked at a slug corpse with his disappointing stick-loot, watching as the creature's acid-filled sacs popped and released fresh rivulets of corrosive fluid that ate into the soil.

“Goopy bastard,” the telepath muttered.

A childlike giggle pierced the air. The System twirled above them, suspended upside-down as the skirt of her dress defied gravity. She observed them with the eager anticipation of a child watching ants beneath a magnifying glass.

"My favorite little squad of blood-drinkers.” She twirled a finger through blonde curls, and her crimson eyes fixed on Ace. "Especially you, Sergeant. Is that nasty little voice in your head getting louder yet? The one telling you how good it would feel to surrender completely?"

Ace ignored her.

The childlike entity turned a lazy somersault in midair, her dress billowing around her like liquid shadow. "Now you’re all ready for the Crucible.”

“Oh, goodie,” Olivia muttered under her breath.

“Precisely, my dear little killer,” the System said with a nod. She clasped her hands behind her back and paced through the air like a general giving a pep talk to his troops. “The Crucible studies those who enter. It tests you according to their deepest evolutionary potential."

"Our potential?" Marcus's voice cracked slightly. "Not what we are, but what we might become?"

"Precisely!" The System clapped her hands in approval. "Oh, so clever for a streamer! The Crucible doesn't care about your current, pathetic state. It peers into your essence—" she pressed a finger to her temple, "—and crafts challenges based on what lurks beneath the surface."

Marcus paled. "So it reads our minds?"

"Minds, souls, evolutionary trajectories..." She waved dismissively. "Semantics."

Olivia set one hand on her hip. "You're saying it creates personalized nightmares."

The System's smile widened. "Yes, your deepest fears manifest in there—but more importantly, you get to see your darkest potential. It shows you what you might become if you surrender completely to my devilish little world."

A heavy silence fell over the group as the implications sank in. Each of them had killed humans and monsters. Only Ace had killed a vampire, thus far. Each death had changed them, altered their essence on a fundamental level.

It seemed surreal to think the Crucible could look even deeper into them and uncover the monster they could be.

“What exactly are we going to find in there?” Ace asked, glaring at her.

The System's smile widened, revealing her dagger-like teeth. "Questions, questions. Always so many questions. You'll understand soon enough. Sergeant. The Crucible reveals all truths eventually—especially the ones we hide from ourselves."

Without warning, the System's demeanor shifted. The playful, childlike affect vanished, replaced by something ancient and calculating. She drifted directly toward Ace, her eyes never wavering, and a devilish little smile tugged at the corner of her mouth. Though her appearance hadn't changed, the malice emanating from her crimson eyes belonged to something that had witnessed millennia of suffering—and orchestrated most of it.

"The final chamber of the Crucible contains a choice," she reminded them all, her voice suddenly lacking its usual sing-song quality. "Between immediate treasure and loot, or the power earned through obeying me."

Ace kept his expression neutral, though the abrupt shift in her manner sent warning signals flaring through his system. "You’re going to have to be more specific."

“Am I, now?” She feigned a little shrug, as though she weren’t interested in doing so.

“Please,” Tara interjected before anyone else could say anything.

“Well, since you asked nicely,” the System said with a nod toward their healer. "The Evolutionary Keystone isn't the only artifact hidden within those walls. Most choose immediate power—human instinct for instant gratification never changes, no matter how long the fangs grow."

"And what exactly does 'obeying you' entail?" Tara asked, her voice tight with suspicion.

The System's gaze remained fixed on Ace as she answered. "Bring me the Keystone, and I’ll give you all sorts of prizes.”

“Such as?” Olivia asked.

“For starters, a rare title,” the floating girl shifted her attention toward their Blood Dancer. “It’s called the 'System's Chosen'. A ten percent boost to all your stats, faster Soul Meter regeneration, increased resistance to silver damage, and even some knowledge of safe zones throughout the Floors. I’ll even give you a unique Skill that no one else can ever get.”

“And that is?” Ace pressed.

“System's Mark,” she answered with a teasing lilt of her head. “It grants the ability to sense nearby powerful artifacts and even scare off lesser creatures.”

“Any loot?” Marcus asked eagerly.

The rest of the team turned to look at him, each glaring in annoyance.

“What?” The former streamer shrugged. “I’m just asking.”

“Oh, my, yes,” the System answered. “The Veil Key, which creates a temporary sanctuary for twenty-four hours, and the Twilight Circlet, which gives enhanced perception and limited mental protection from telepaths like this one over here.”

With a theatrical pop, like a bubble bursting, the System disappeared and reappeared behind Marcus. She pinched his cheeks.

“Stop that,” he said, batting her away.

She giggled. “So, you see, giving me the Keystone far exceeds what you’d get out of keeping it. Who cares about controlling your EXP Solutes when you get all those bonuses for turning that little old trinket over to me?”

Hmm.

Ace wasn’t so sure.

"Immediate, guaranteed power," the System continued, apparently oblivious to Ace’s reaction. "Without the messy experimentation and risk of attempting to use the Keystone without proper knowledge."

"Risk?" Marcus asked, edging closer to examine the display.

"Oh yes," the System said with exaggerated concern. "The Keystone responds to intention and blood sacrifice. Use it wrong, and it could corrupt or destroy the wielder entirely. Such powerful artifacts rarely come without... complications."

"And if we choose the Keystone instead of loyalty to you?" Olivia asked, her voice carrying a predatory edge.

The System shrugged, her childlike appearance at odds with the cold indifference of the gesture. "Then you take your chances with forces beyond your comprehension. I would be... disappointed, but the Crucible offers choices to all who enter. That's the rule."

A rule she apparently couldn’t break, Ace noted.

"Tick tock!" she sang, spinning in midair. "The Crucible awaits, my darling little monsters!"

With that, she dissolved into motes of light that scattered like fireflies in the air. The beads of light skittered through a sunbeam before winking out entirely.

The silence that followed hung heavy between the four surviving heroes. Ace stared at the space where she had been, his mind racing through possibilities and threats.

Olivia's face betrayed nothing, but her eyes told a different story. They gleamed with unconcealed interest as she stared out at the forest. Her tongue passed briefly over her lower lip, a predatory gesture that reminded Ace of how close to the surface her vampiric nature lurked these days. The System's offer clearly appealed to her.

Marcus, meanwhile, tried to appear disinterested, but his eyes kept darting back to where the System had been moments before. The tiny muscle twitching at the corner of his mouth revealed more than he likely realized—he wanted those rewards.

Only Tara's attention remained fixed on Ace. Unlike the others, her expression was almost blank. Focused. Intent. She studied him, and it took a moment longer to understand why her features sent a shiver down his spine.

She wasn't weighing whether she wanted the rewards.

She was trying to figure out whether or not he did.

Whether he would sacrifice them all to claim the prize for himself. Whether his talk of protecting the team was genuine or merely a convenient fabrication to keep their loyalty until he no longer needed it.

Their eyes met, and a silent exchange passed between them. The flicker of doubt in her gaze wounded him more than any physical attack. After everything they'd survived together, a part of her still questioned his integrity.

But that was exactly what the System wanted—to plant seeds of doubt, to turn them against each other with the unspoken question hanging in the air.

Who will betray the others first?
