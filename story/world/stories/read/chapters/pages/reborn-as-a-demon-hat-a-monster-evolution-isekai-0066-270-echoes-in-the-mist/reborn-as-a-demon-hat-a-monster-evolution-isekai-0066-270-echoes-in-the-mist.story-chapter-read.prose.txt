The three of them forged the mountain path they’d come down earlier that afternoon, each of them keeping their wits about them lest the stone-men attack once more.

They could take them – easily – even with their penchant for regeneration and mass coalescence. The guardians of the Southern Mountains were no match for the three most seasoned Hybrids in all of Argwyll. The veterans of the Archon Wars, and Guardians of the Last Archon.

But that wasn’t what the issue was, here.

The issue was that they’d been scouring these ranges for the last week, and had found precisely no trace of who they were looking for.

And though none of them wanted to say it out loud, there was a growing feeling among all of them that their quest might be in vain.

“Took down twenty-five today,” Tara said as they walked. “Five of ‘em mages. How bout you, Klaxy?”

“Lost count,” the dogman said, avoiding eye contact with his mate.

“Oh, dogbrain. You’re an even worse liar these days than you were before…”

Fauna kept quiet as they cleared the mists and approached their base camp huddled in the ancient ruins above the Southernmost mountain-top. It was the farthest point of Argwyll – right at the edge of the world. Looking off into the distance showed one only long stretches of mist and wisps of low clouds broken by the mountain peaks. It seemed almost infinite.

Fauna tossed some of their rations to her friends and then stoked their fire again. Then, in silence, she started preparing her new spells for the day ahead.

Her companions shuffled around slightly, looking at their hammocks with longing, sensing the determination that still burned in the Hopla magi’s eyes.

“Any word from the little lady?” Klax asked.

“She’s meditating today,” Fauna replied without looking up. “She says she’s close – that he’s out there somewhere - but he keeps slipping away. She needs more time.”

“Slippery fucker, for a dragon,” Tara muttered as she flopped down into her hammock by the fire.

“She’s alone out there, then?” Klax asked. “I can head down to make sure she’s ok.”

“Pfft,” Tara snorted. “That girl’s got nothing to worry about. The only one that’s got to worry is the poor bastard that gets in her way.”

That got a smile from Fauna, Tara noticed. Even if it was just a small one.

“…but, eh, maybe you’ve got a point, Klaxy,” the Minxit said. “How about we keep watch before night falls?”

Klax’s eyes flashed between Tara and Fauna, noting the winks that the former was giving him, while the latter had begun pouring over their hastily etched map of the area.

“I’ll go on ahead,” Klax finally said, giving Tara’s belly a comforting rub just before he left both female Hybrids alone. “Don’t take too long, now.”

“I’ll be right with you, dogbrain,” Tara replied warmly. Despite all her complaints, she had to admit that she really didn’t mind his new attitude, at times.

When he left them both, Tara rolled to look at her sister. Fauna was so absorbed in her tracking their movements that it was possible she hadn’t even noticed Klax leave the camp.

“…we’ve gone through the Northern reaches twice, now,” she was whispering. “But maybe he knows that. Maybe he’s constantly been relocating this whole time, playing with us. It would make sense, since this fog is clearly of magical origin –“

“Faun.”

“-and none of our spells have been able to negate it. Maybe he’s watching us, testing us just like he did Ethan. But if that’s the case, then what’s his game, here? He must know that we aren’t here to harm him. He’s heard the Mandate just like everyone else. Maybe –“

“FAUN!”

The Hopla Mage looked up as though she was only now just noticing that her companion was here.

But she instantly averted her gaze again. She saw the tired, knowing smile that was painted on her sister’s face.

“Don’t say it,” she said. “I know.”

She did. Tara knew it, too. They’d set out from Arkona eight months ago, acting on intelligence their networks in the East had been gathering for years. Rumors and whispers that spoke of an ancient beast, a fire-breather, who had been touring the world for the past five years since the Archon’s passing. A creature whose name was only known to them – they who had been with Ethan when he had come before that very beast and made a deal that promised him freedom in exchange for a trick that not even Sys could have known about.

Fauna had spent the best part of these long years recovering as many scraps of intel as she could – whenever she wasn’t teaching classes, that was. And finally, they’d received concrete info that he had been spotted flying above these misty skies that had served as his home for eons. Apparently, he’d just been circling the area, absent-mindedly patrolling – perhaps – if dragons truly did anything absent-mindedly.

So of course, Fauna had volunteered to find him. And, of course, Tara and Klax had insisted on coming with her – no matter what complaints Borlor and the rest of the council threw their way.

The journey had been long, arduous, but frankly nothing they couldn’t handle. The worst part had been these quiet moments of waiting. Of inaction. Fauna couldn’t stomach them these days. Once, in what seemed like an age ago, she’d loved little moments of peace between battle or adventure. But she’d been an altogether different Hopla back then. Now, the inaction unsettled her to no end. Because she’d had one thing on her mind for these past five years that wouldn’t ever leave her.

“Look,” Tara whispered. “I’m not gonna force you to forget. And I’m not gonna be a hypocrite. Because if it was me, yeah, I couldn’t move on. I get that. But – it’s a big world out there now. And it’s open for you – for us – in a way it never has been before. It’s what we always wanted, sis.”

She caught her friend’s beady eyes for just a moment across the crackling fire of their camp. And this time, she managed to hold them in her gaze.

“It’s what he always wanted for us,” she said. “You know that.”

Fauna’s ears drooped as she heard those words. She knew they were true. Without exception, she did.

“And you know that knowing that doesn’t make it any easier.”

Tara leaned forward, shaking the cold from her fur.

“I’m not telling you to give up on looking for a way to bring him back,” she said gently. “But we might have to face the fact that the choice he made was what was best for all of us. He could peer into the future, remember. Maybe he saw that this was the best of all possible outcomes.”

Fauna looked from her friend’s sharp eyes to her stomach, and the little bulge that was under her leather jerkin. The little life that was growing inside her had come as a shock to all of them when they’d first found out. The notion of a Lycae and Minxit successfully mating hadn’t even been considered a possibility.

But after Ethan, everything had changed. Fauna liked to think that he’d used Kaedmon’s power to give that to them, maybe as a thank you, or maybe because he knew it would bring out a change in both of them. A desire for peace, and a reason to keep going.

“Maybe he did,” Fauna said, a sad smile draped across her face. “No – in fact, I’m sure he did. I’m sure he thought that we’d all be better off without him. What he did was right, I know it.”

Her mind flashed back to the conversation they’d both had in the Mistborne Sanctuary, nestled within her private quarters there. It was a conversation she’d replayed in her mind countless times since he’d left them. And she had kept it a secret from everyone – especially the part when he’d told her that it was up to her. That if she didn’t want him to go, he wouldn’t…

In that moment, she’d chosen the world – and all the new life that would come into it – over him. She’d made her choice just as he had. She had no right now to drag others down because she wanted – desperately wanted – a do-over. Even though she knew her friends had volunteered of their own accord to help her in her search for the only being who – if the legends were true - might be able to bring him back.

“We bond for life,” she said – the suddenness of her own thought taking her aback. “He might have changed a lot about this world, but he never changed that…”

Tara moved to reach an arm towards her friend, but Fauna’s weary yet contented eyes stopped her.

“You’re right, sis,” she said. “We can make this the final night. If we don’t find him by nightfall, I’ll call it.”

Tara blinked, clearly taken aback.

“You sure?”

“Mhm,” Fauna muttered, a tentative smile pulling at her furry lips. “Find Klax and let him know. I’ll plan our last route for tonight by myself.”

Tara hesitated a little, following Fauna’s eyes to where the rabbit-girl was looking: her belly.

“Faun, if you start going all sappy on me just like the big guy…”

“I know you can take way more than what we went through today,” Fauna winked. “But I’m not about to push anyone to their limit. This was my dumb quest, after all.”

Tara leaned back as Fauna’s eyes returned to their fire. Ironically, she’d started to sound more and more like Lamphrey, lately. Tara knew what she was getting at, however. In a way, this was an apology for involving her friends in what she knew was a desperate rumor-chasing mission when they’d set out from Arcona.

Tara would have said more – wanted to say more – but she also knew her friend well enough to know when she wanted to be alone these days.

“Hey,” she said over her shoulder before she left to find Klax. “We’re a team – yeah? Through everything, we promised we’d stand by each other. And even if this was our last adventure together, I’m honored to have stood with you all these years.”

Fauna glanced up after a few seconds, seeing that Tara had already disappeared into the fog that shrouded their camp perimeter.

The years of new Argwyll had changed her, just as it had changed them all. She was no longer that spunky catgirl who doubted their goals. Now, she was a woman who was seeing that she had a reason to live beyond simply her own self-preservation. And that meant something. It meant everything.

And it was wrong of her to expect that her friends would scour the ends of the earth with her, even with the pact they’d made before they ever found Ethan in that little glade just outside Triant forest. Since the days of Jun’Ei, of Sanctum’s youth, they’d always had one clear goal: the destruction of the Lightborn, Greycloaks, and of the humans who despised and feared them. Now, with everything accomplished, it made sense that their relationship would alter, too. They had their own paths to walk, after all. And in this new world, sometimes such paths diverged.

But there was one thing that had never changed in these five long years.

Fauna glanced around her and then set down her backpack. She opened the strap and looked inside, steadying her breathing for a few moments before reaching inside and pulling out what she’d kept nestled in there.

He still looked exactly as he’d always looked. Blue fabric, little brown patch on the stem, and faint runic lettering that followed the curvature of the hem. The red eye that had once beamed with such life had been dim and darkened ever since that day when she plucked him from the dark angel’s head and wished that, perhaps, he’d found a way to win without leaving this world behind.

She kept him with her and she knew the others knew. She knew the council knew. In fact, she suspected that even Mara realized that was where Ethan’s ‘body’ had been all this time. But none of them had dared confront her about it in all these years. How could they, really? She was the one he’d trusted most. And if he’d had the choice, he’d probably have been fine with her carrying around with her like she did.

But lately, the thought had occurred to her that he might have rejected that notion entirely. Tara was right. And Klax – though he wouldn’t broach the subject – was also right. Here she was literally carrying him around with her everywhere she went. Never mind that his voice, his face, and all his little jokes rebounded around her brain every day since she’d lost him.

She was suddenly seized by the notion that she should leave him here, behind. She could let him go. It would be a simple first step – an important one. But try as she might, she couldn’t force her hands to drop him.

Instead, she allowed herself one brief moment of weakness, clutching him to her cheeks and letting him absorb the small tears forming at the corners of her eyes before they fell. She would rarely do this, even on bad days. And she always feared what would happen if someone saw her in a moment of frailty like this.

She didn’t know that, in actual fact, she did indeed have a little spectator this time.

She heard the shuffling of tiny feet nearby, and saw the shadow of a slender figure appear through the mists at her side. Even though, clearly, this person had been watching her for some time.

And there was only one person in the entire world who could truly sneak up on Fauna, the Archmage of Westerweald.

She lifted her eyes to see who it was, though she already knew, and already didn’t even try to hide her embarrassment.

Mara was staring right at her.
