“Memory is the only paradise from which we cannot be expelled.”

May 1st

The girl with the long black hair awoke drowning.

Her head broke through the rushing, turbulent water, and she spat out a mouthful of icy foam. She thrashed, trying to find which way was up.

She was naked, dragged by the freezing current, and remembered nothing—no name, no past, no family. Nothing at all.

Where am I? she though.

It was night. The sky threatened a storm, distant lightning sketching ghostly silhouettes of mountains and forest.

After struggling against the water—and against something that had tried to pull her down—she fought her way to the shore, her legs striking against hidden stones until, at last, she felt ground beneath her feet.

She turned and saw the river she had just escaped.

The world was a blur of shadows and outlines. Heavy clouds smothered the moon, and only the occasional lightning tore through the dark, offering fleeting glimpses of spectral clarity.The bank was slick with mud and pebbles. Her skin shivered under the cutting cold. She didn’t know how long she stayed there, lungs burning, body bruised, until she tried to stand—and pain shot through her leg. Something had scratched her. Or bitten her. Down in the depths.

Dark blood dripped down her calf, mixing with the muddy water. She glanced toward the river and saw… something.

She wasn’t sure what—only that it moved beneath the surface, watching.

Pale eyes, like pearls, stared back before sinking into the black.

But there was more.

Between the flashes of lightning, she thought she saw elongated shapes drifting along the current.

A shiver crawled up her spine. Were those… bodies? And what was that thing beneath the water?

Thunder rolled through the sky, loud enough to make her teeth ache.

She couldn’t stay there.

The feeling of being watched made her tremble—not just from the cold this time.She stumbled to her feet, unsteady. The dark undergrowth at the river’s edge seemed to offer refuge, so she took a step, then another, her bare feet sinking into the soft ground.

Something cracked in the distance.

A sound between a growl and a lament drifted through the trees.

She froze. She was not alone.

Not here. Not tonight.

She forced herself to breathe, to move forward even as her instincts screamed run. Every shadow seemed alive, every leaf seemed to whisper her name—though she didn’t even know it herself.

Then she heard it: a whisper, wet and bubbling, like words forced through a throat that had never learned to speak.

She turned sharply—and saw it among the trees: a long, thin figure, skin taut and gray, eyes milky white reflecting the lightning.

The river wasn’t the only place something hunted her.

Another flash lit the water, and she looked back at the river once more.

The murky surface twisted under the night wind, and shapes floated lazily, as if they were nothing but branches caught in the current.

But they weren’t branches.

Her stomach tightened with cold certainty. Arms. Legs. Heads turning with the tide, drenched skin, blank eyes catching the lightning’s ghostly glow.

The river was a road of rotting corpses, carried downstream toward some unknown place, slipping between rocks and reeds as if the earth itself tried to swallow them.

And she—she had come from there.

She felt the taste of something sour rising in her throat and vomited liquid. She wiped herself with the back of her hand while looking the scene in terror.

Corpses and body parts floating. Now out of the water, her sense of smell was returning, and she could smell not only the rot on the shore but also the smell of decomposing bodies.

Had she been among them?

Was she one of them?

Dead—or alive?

Why couldn’t she remember?

Another shiver through her spine, and she ran.

The ground was uneven—soft in some places, sharp with stones in others—and every twig that snapped under her bare feet felt like a scream tearing through the night.

She didn’t look back. She couldn’t.

She just ran, heart pounding like a war drum.

The trees fell away, giving rise to a vast plain of rolling hills, tall grass swaying in the wind.Here there were no trees to hide in, no river to drown in—only an endless expanse stretching toward distant mountains, their silhouettes etched in lightning.

She walked, then ran again, without knowing why, without knowing where.

Her feet decided for her what her mind could not.

She tried to remember.

Anything. A face, a name, a home. But all she found was a cold, merciless void stretching as far as the plain she crossed.

Past lost its meaning in her mind.

The moon had finally emerged between the torn clouds, bathing the grass in silver light.

Moon. The moon. How did she know that word? Corpses, river, mountains, sky, clouds, storm.How did she know the names of so many things, when she remembered nothing of herself?

Her skin burned from the wind, the cold an ever-present enemy.

Why was her hair so damn long? If only it were dry—it clung to her like seaweed. Her legs trembled, exhaustion battering her with every step.

She couldn’t stop.

She mustn’t stop.

But then, the wind changed.

It blew heavier, thicker.

And with it came a sound—low, distant, a vibration that crawled through the ground, humming against her skin.

She froze, breath ragged, ears straining. At first she thought it was just the wind howling through the grass and over the far hills—

But it wasn’t.

It was something else.

A murmur—almost a chant—but guttural, formless. The sound carried no words, only the echo of something ancient and inhuman. It seemed to come from everywhere and nowhere at once, spreading across the plain like an echo without origin.

The girl swallowed hard, her body tensing. There was something in that sound, something that didn’t belong to this world.

Or perhaps it did—and she was the intruder.

Who was she? Why was she here?

She looked around, searching for shelter—something, anything that could make her feel less exposed in that naked immensity. But the plain offered only her own fear reflected back at her, and the murmur grew nearer.

Then she saw them.

At first, she thought they were shadows sliding across the plain—but the darkness and distance made it impossible to be sure.

The shapes moved in erratic, liquid motions, as if they had no fixed form. They were enormous—taller than any man—and though she couldn’t see their feet move, she saw thin, trembling arms stretching outward, their bodies gliding over the grass without touching it, writhing through the air as though their very existence were cursed.

A knot formed in her throat. Every primitive instinct screamed one word: run.

And she obeyed that instinct.

Her bare feet struck the ground with desperate urgency, her lungs burning with every breath. But the sound of those things followed her—a deep, droning hum, like the friction of something chasing what did not belong in this world.

The land rose and fell in gentle slopes, but amid the rolling terrain, she saw it: a distant shape, vast and solitary. It stood apart from the hills like a wound in the earth.

A pyramid.

The fact that she knew its name didn’t trouble her. She simply knew it—and that was enough. She to accepted for now.

Its color was dark, almost the same shade as the wet soil, rendering it invisible at first glance. Its slanted sides devoured the light, as though made of a material foreign to reality itself.Her chest tightened the moment she saw it. Something within her whispered that she had to go there. But it was still far. Too far.

Still—perhaps someone, something, waited there.

Then the storm-torn sky split open with light.

A falling star—but not a normal one.

It plunged from the heavens, leaving behind an electric-blue scar across the night, as if it had sliced through the fabric of the world. Its glow reflected off the pyramid, bathing it in eerie luminescence before vanishing in a burst of impact.

A vibrant flash ignited at the pyramid’s base.

Seconds later, the ground trembled beneath her feet, a vibration that rattled her bones.

And somewhere deep in her fractured mind, something stirred.

That light—she knew it. She didn’t know how, or from where, but the sight of it had awakened something raw and visceral in her memory. She had seen it before.

When? Where?

Behind her, the shadows writhed. They had felt it too.

For a heartbeat they froze—shifting like smoke trapped in a windstorm—then surged forward, faster, more violent than before.

The girl had no choice.

She couldn’t recall her past, couldn’t comprehend her present, but one truth burned clear: she had to reach that pyramid before those things reached her.

Her heart pounded in her chest, her throat seared with each gasp. She ran toward the colossal structure, praying in silence that whatever waited inside would be less terrifying than what hunted her.

The pyramid shimmered in the distance like a mirage, a dark silhouette that seemed to retreat with every step—as if mocking her desperation.

But there was no other choice. The plain was an ocean of restless grass with no hiding place, no rock, no tree to protect her.

Her legs trembled from exhaustion, sweat and cold clung to her skin. Still, she pressed on, feeling the breath of the things behind her—thick, sticky, and close.

They were faster than she was.

She didn’t need to turn to know. She could feel them—hear the low, distorted hiss rising from their impossible forms.

She didn’t know how long she ran before she reached the remnants of an old road—broken marble tiles half-buried in the earth, surrounded by fallen columns hidden among the grass. She didn’t stop to look.

She couldn’t.

When she crossed the threshold of the structure, a chill crawled over her skin. The immense pyramid was more than a ruin—it was a corpse from another age, a relic of something that should never have existed.

The stone walls rose around a colossal plaza, a vast space where the ruins of temples and crumbled buildings stood like forgotten skeletons.

Shattered sculptures littered the ground—broken forms that might once have represented gods or beasts, now little more than fragments eroded by rage or time.

Twisted statues of animals lay scattered—bronze horses shattered to pieces, gigantic elephants with slender legs and toppled obelisks strapped to their backs.

And at the very center, a blackened crater still smoked—the mark where the blue light had struck.

But there was no time to understand.

The shadows had followed her in. They slithered into the dead geometry of the place, reshaping themselves into forms more distorted, more grotesque with each heartbeat.

The girl ran, weaving through ruins of shapes, concepts, and memories her mind almost recognized, though her memory refused to say how, when, or why.

Her feet stumbled on rubble, her breath broke into sobs.

Then—just as they were about to reach her, just as death’s hand closed around her neck—something happened.

Something she did not expect.

Another shadow emerged from the darkness—but this one was different. It didn’t twist and writhe like the others, nor whisper with that hollow, broken voice.

It was solid. Tall.

And its very presence froze her where she stood.

Something inside her screamed that this one was worse—far more dangerous than the things that had been chasing her.

She couldn’t scream. She couldn’t move.

And then—blackness.

The cold vanished.

The night dissolved, torn away from her skin like a discarded veil, replaced by an absolute, suffocating black.

Yet the echo of what she had seen lingered in her mind, etched with the certainty that it hadn’t been a dream.

***

Her lips stirred first—and she tasted metal.

Then came the smell: wet asphalt, filthy and sharp, pulling her fully back to consciousness.

It was still dark, but the first thing she saw when she opened her eyes was a cat.

A small, gray-furred creature staring at her with wide amber eyes, as if judging whether she belonged.

She didn’t know how, but she knew: it was a cat. A well-fed, furry little thing.

Something deep inside her recognized it.

The cat, startled by her sudden awareness, leapt away, vanishing between a stack of trash bins at the edge of a narrow alleyway.

There was no time to think.

As soon as the creature disappeared, the girl slowly pushed herself up, feeling the rough pavement beneath her naked skin. She looked at her skin. It was slightly dark in color, and she thought she saw some kind of pattern on it, like a fluorescent tattoo. But it disappeared so quickly that she couldn't see it anymore. Maybe it was just her imagination.

The air was cold and heavy—but not nearly as cold as the storm inside her head.

She saw strands of hair fall before her eyes—long, silver strands. Very long hair.

Was that her color?

The world around her ears was chaos.

Distant sounds of engines revving, voices overlapping in rapid bursts, laughter and chatter that blended into one another.

Vehicles.

She didn’t know how she knew the word—but she did.

She looked around. It was nighttime, but not cold.

She sat up abruptly, dazed, her mind struggling to keep up with what her eyes were showing her. The distant hum of city life wrapped around her like an alien fabric.

She looked at her hands, her arms, then ahead. Dirty walls, covered with graffiti and torn advertisements. Her mind couldn’t process everything at once—but something about the writing was unmistakable.

Some of the words on the walls were written in characters that needed no translation.

The language… Japanese.

She could feel small bursts of static inside her head—concepts sparking into being.

Cat. Alleyway. Walls. Language. Voices. Vehicles. People talking. City.

Japan. Tokyo. The name appeared out of nowhere.

A city. Kantō Prefecture.

Japan.

The concept, the name, the country—it was all there, floating within her consciousness like a half-remembered truth wrapped in fog, yet utterly undeniable.

She didn’t know who she was, nor how she had come to be here—but she knew she was in Japan. She understood the kanji and text on one of the posters. Night jazz club, Shibuya Nights.

She could read the signs. She understood what they said. And yet… none of it felt anchored to a memory.

A bright-colored drink ad hung nearby, its images warped and fuzzy, as if reality itself were slightly misaligned. Something about the scene felt off—but also right, in a strange, instinctive way.

Gradually, she began to focus, and the words seemed to align inside her head. The kanji didn’t move, but her mind did—like her brain was slowly rebooting from a dream she couldn’t recall.

Without thinking, she got to her feet and started walking. The alley was dark, poorly lit, but her body moved quickly, guided by an instinct she didn’t yet understand.

Then—suddenly—a flash of light blinded her.

Voices followed. Distant at first, then clear.

“—I found her!” someone shouted, the tone sharp with urgency. Footsteps echoed against the pavement, getting closer.

Her heart jolted. Fear surged through her veins like electricity.

Without a second thought, she ran—away from the voices, deeper into the maze of narrow alleyways.

Each corner, each turn swallowed her in more darkness, but it also pulled her away from whoever was chasing her.

The footsteps grew louder, pounding in rhythm with her heartbeat. The echo of her own breath sounded like a stranger’s voice in her ears.

Flashlights cut through the shadows—thin beams sweeping the walls, drawing closer and closer.

She didn’t need to see their faces to know they were after her.

She felt it.

Her chest tightened. Her breath came out ragged, but she kept moving—dodging debris, leaping over puddles that glimmered under the light of passing beams.

Then her foot slipped.

Her right leg scraped hard against the rough concrete, sending a sharp pain shooting through her body. She staggered, nearly falling—but didn’t stop. Couldn’t stop.

Warm blood trickled down her shin.

Another light struck her full in the face—so bright, so close it seared her vision white.

For a moment she froze, caught in the glare.

And then a voice cut through the chaos—calm, clear, feminine.

“Wait—don’t run! Can you understand me?”

The girl blinked, her whole body trembling in the brightness. The voice didn’t sound hostile.There was urgency, yes—but also something… gentle? urgent maybe? in it.

For the first time since waking, she hesitated.

Maybe… this one wasn’t here to harm her.

The darkness around her was still thick, but the flashes of light were multiplying.

When she looked up, she realized she was surrounded. Three men, two women. Four of them wore blue uniforms — police officers.

She didn’t fully grasp the meaning of that word, yet her mind processed it with unnerving clarity.

Police.

The word floated in her head with a certainty that sent a chill down her spine. She knew it— without knowing how.

The officers stood there with their flashlights trained on her, eyes filled with questions… and something else.

Concern, perhaps?

The woman who had spoken, however, wasn’t in uniform. She stood apart—long black hair, dark jacket, no insignia. Her eyes were hidden behind dark glasses, and it felt as if she was searching for something in her, something she herself couldn’t understand.

Dark glasses… in the that dark alley?

A knot tightened in the girl’s stomach.

She didn’t know who they were, or why they were chasing her — only that her being there made no sense at all. But neither was the girl, who was completely naked running from the police.

The woman who had spoken, the one who seemed to want to help, watched her with a mix of caution and curiosity.

But the others—the uniformed ones—didn’t share that same glint in their eyes. There was something in their posture, their silence, that made her feel like she was about to be trapped in an invisible net.

Fear rose again, a distant scream threading its way through her mind.

The woman spoke once more, her voice calm, a balm against the confusion.

“We won’t hurt you. I promise.”

But the girl wasn’t listening.

Something primal pushed her toward the only option that made sense: escape.

And yet, she had no intention of running without a fight.

Something deep inside told her that if they were going to catch her, she wouldn’t make it easy. Her fingers curled into fists—and in one fierce, desperate motion, she lunged at the woman.It was instinct, fear, and fury all fused into one.

But the woman didn’t stay still either.

In a sudden, fluid motion, she dropped her flashlight—and before the girl could understand what was happening, the stranger’s arm locked around her like a trap.

And then, without warning, the world flipped upside down.

The girl didn’t even have time to scream.

It was as if a hurricane had picked her up and hurled her through the air.

The sensation was so violent, so sudden, she didn’t even register when her body hit the ground.

Then the impact came —a brutal embrace from the concrete that made her bones rattle on contact.

Pain came later, blooming across her back like a dark tide. But stranger than the pain was the confusion. She didn’t understand how it happened—or why—or where such force had come from.

Everything had unfolded so quickly she almost wondered if it had been some kind of dream,a clumsy fall so absurd that even the police seemed to wince.

The silence that followed was thick, suspended.

Then, through the fading echo of her own fall, she felt pressure on her arm.

The woman was kneeling beside her, her expression not one of anger or fear—but of sheer disbelief.

“Sorry,” the woman said quietly. “I think I overdid it…”

The words sounded almost absurd in the stillness—so sincere that, for a fleeting moment, the girl didn’t know whether to laugh or cry.

It was an apology so out of place it almost felt like a joke shared between strangers at the end of the world.

Maybe, she thought, if she hadn’t been so dazed, she would have laughed — because of how utterly ridiculous it all was.

But she didn’t have the chance to process it. The blow had been too much. The lights, the blurred faces, the sounds of the city — all of it began to fade, slowly unraveling. The world spun and broke apart around her, like a rubber ball bouncing into nothingness.

The last thing she heard, distant and fading, was that same voice, soft and regretful:

“Sorry…”

And then everything went dark.
