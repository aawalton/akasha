I bolted upright with a gasp. Warm air swept over my face, filling my mouth and throat.

Everything spun for a moment before settling into a familiar scene. I was in my bedroll, stretched lengthwise. My bare feet poked out from the blankets, warmed by a low, smoldering fire. The embers flickered lazily, enough to heat the area nearby but certainly not the rest of the common room, which was quite bare after the evening’s fortifications. Every door was shrouded in shadows, but I could just make out the tilted tables and wooden chairs propped against them.

Royce snored just beside me. His massive frame seemed enviably carefree; limbs askew, mouth ajar, face free of worry. Firelight cast the smooth planes of his jaw and cheeks in a peaceful glow. His messy blonde hair looked as thick and warm as sun-baked honey in the light.

Renner was nearby, somehow managing to look angry even while unconscious. Or perhaps angry wasn’t quite the right word. Tense. A line of weapons and gear lay beside him. His thin lips were pressed together, jaw clenching and unclenching beneath a layer of dark scruff. One hand was behind his head and the other lay near a sheathed knife.

Teela was upright, propped with her back beside the stone fireplace. Her hair, horns, and eyes glowed an ominous shade of red, nearly matching the embers nearby. Renner’s crossbow and several gleaming witchwood bolts lay on the floorboards beside her.

“You okay?” she hissed, crawling towards me.

I stared at her. Nauseas. Breathless. Stunned into a baffled, limp stupor, half from Horace’s wicked declaration and half from being abruptly ripped out of my dream.

Ripped out of my dream. Thrown back here. The thoughts spun, and I clung to them. Steadied myself with their urgency. That’s happened before, twice, when… when there was…

The shadows around me suddenly seemed much darker. I looked around warily, shoulders hunched, clutching my blanket. Frost crawled across my skin at the realization that most of the room was too dark for me to make out… well, much of anything.

Which means there could be anything.

“Brin?” Her whisper was hoarse.

“I’m… y-yes. Yes.” I scanned the room. Peered into each corner closely, though I was only met with blackness. The fire’s dwindling light didn’t reach far past my bedroll, and the few moonbeams from the windows were meager illumination.

Should I wake Renner and Royce? The other times I woke up like that, it’s because I was in danger. There were Fae nearby.

But all I saw was Teela’s angular face, glowing coals, and shadows. My fingers knotted together.

Perhaps I’m overreacting. Horace frightened me, put me on edge, so I’m jumping at shadows and I’ll wake everyone up for absolutely no reason and we’ll all be even more exhausted-

A twig snapped outside. I froze, breath catching in my throat, as Teela twisted to face the sound. It came again, followed by a slow, shuffling noise from beyond the back door.

Caution be damned, I thought, practically lunging towards Renner’s bedroll and hissing his name.

He woke immediately, tearing upright amidst a flurry of cloth and hissing metal. A dagger gleamed bare in one hand before his eyes were even fully open. He gave a bleary grumble, head swinging towards me, and shook himself into alertness upon seeing my face.

“Probably an animal,” Teela was mumbling, staring at the door. Her hands inched towards the crossbow. “A deer. Right? Definitely a-”

The odd sound came again; a slow, scuffing shhh-thump. As if something heavy was being dragged across grass, then settling with a thud.

It repeated, quite rhythmically, as Teela’s shaking hands closed over the weapon. Renner grabbed his sword belt and boots, pulling each on with practiced speed.

I crawled a pace over, shaking Royce’s shoulders. “Royce!” My fierce squeak earned nothing but a snore. “Hey! Wake up! Royce!” Nothing.

A boot slammed into his side. I made an indignant sound at Renner as my friend groaned, lurching onto one side.

“What the-”

“Shut up. Grab a weapon.”

Royce clambered up at that, unkempt sandy hair falling across his forehead. He looked bleary-eyed and quite unhappy at having been kicked, but stayed mute while he drew his own sword.

Shhh-thump. Shhh-thump. Closer, now. It sounded like whatever it was had reached the back porch. I could just make out an odd squelch at the end of each thud, alongside the patter of fat raindrops against wood.

“Take the crossbow and duck behind the counter.” Renner’s command was low and harsh, directed towards Teela and snapping her into motion. He turned to me as the young woman scurried, white-faced and wide-eyed, to crouch near the bar. “Other side of the room. Be ready to set something on fire.”

I obeyed without a thought, snatching up my pack and belt as I went. The nearest bedroom door had a table wedged lengthwise against it, and I huddled behind the wood. One shaking hand delved into my pack, searching for familiar heat. The shard felt sharp and warm. A little fragment of sunlight cradled in the palm of my hand.

It’s too small to be much of a weapon. Not a practical one. But, ashes, I do feel safer when it’s nearby.

“Stand here and look big,” Renner muttered to Royce. Then he slipped to one side, boots silent on the floorboards, and prowled along until he was at the inn’s back wall.

My friend nodded, blue eyes wide and broadsword gleaming in one white-knuckled hand. Then he spun, hissing, “Wait, am I bait?” in a frantic pitch.

“Shut up,” the smaller man snapped, sidling along the far wall. He paused beside a window near the door, grim expression illuminated by slivers of moonlight. His weapons were bare.

Shhh-thump. Right at the back entrance. Shhh-thump.

I jumped, stifling a scream, as something thudded against the back door. Wood groaned and shuddered, but the door and the table in front of it held fast. Then…

Drip. Drip. Drip.

I pressed a hand over my mouth. Couldn’t seem to tear my gaze away. I was vaguely aware of Teela whimpering in fright, of Royce shifting forwards with a little growl, of Renner peering sidelong through the window.

Whatever it was stopped moving. There was no thumping or creaking wood, just uneasy quiet, and every now and then…

Drip. Drip. Water, or what I hoped was water and not something far more terrible, spattered against the porch. Drip. Drip.

“What is it?” Teela breathed from her hiding spot. “Can you see?”

Renner shook his head. He inched closer to the glass pane, craning to catch a glimpse of whatever lurked outside.

Drip. Shh-thump. Drip. Drip.

The sound dragged sidelong, towards Renner’s window. He flattened himself against the wall, shifting out of view as a face appeared behind the glass.

Its visage blocked the moonlight, but our dying fire was enough to reveal the woman’s features. Swollen, black lips. Pallid white skin, slick and gleaming with moisture, peeling away from rotting flesh about the forehead and cheeks. Bulging, glazed eyes. Her head was tilted almost sideways, as though she’d suffered a violent fall. Pearly bone jutted out from long-dead skin.

And atop it all was a mass of sopping blonde hair. It clung to her cheeks and throat. Several clumps were torn and dangling, with strips of white skin tangled within.

Renner went still as death, Teela let out a muffled cry, Royce stepped back, and I swallowed a scream as the hideous visage pressed flush to the glass. Her lips and tongue spread against it, leaving trails of thick saliva behind.

Drip. Drip. Moisture slid down her waterlogged skin and clothes. Then, after peering in vacantly, she dragged backwards with a shhh-thump before lurching forwards. I didn’t quite manage to swallow the scream this time. It rang out alongside the sound of cracking glass.

The woman slammed her head forwards again and again, splintering the thin barrier until it shattered with a crash. The skin along her face was torn in places, oozing slick rivulets of water and blood, but she didn’t seem to notice.

Renner shifted, blades glinting dull red in the ember’s glow. Teela whimpered again and Royce, voice quavering, stepped forward with a brave, “Hey!”

The woman’s dripping face tilted, glazed gaze steering towards my friend.

He gulped audibly. “Uh… c-come and… get… salt.”

It worked. She made no noise but lifted both swollen, pallid hands and clawed at the sill. Wood groaned and the remaining shards of glass fell with a clatter. Her head slid through the opening. Ropes of drool dangled from parted black lips. Sopping blonde hair slung down, drip-dripping onto the glass and wood, and she pulled-

Renner’s sword flashed silver. One clean motion, and with a horrifying squelch the head thumped down. Her body went limp in the window frame, arms dangling half-in the room, then dropped out of sight.

The kill- if that’s even what it was, saints, she was already dead!- happened so quickly that I could only stare in stunned horror.

For a moment. Glass tinkled at Renner’s feet as the thing’s jaw stretched, then snapped shut. Teeth clicked together, blank eyes lolled, and he kicked it away with an oath. Wet lips flapped, a bulging tongue lapped at the air, and I felt another scream bubble up-

Thunk. A narrow shaft of silver sank into the woman’s matted skull. Teela squealed, as though her own shot had startled her, then scrambled to reload.

Firelight flickered on the woman’s face as every feature went limp. The bolt shuddered and, just as I’d seen a living witchwood kill a living Fae, the inert wood seemed to flex and shiver against the monster’s flesh. Then both went still.

“Nice shot.” Renner knelt, prodding the inert head with the tip of his sword.

“Thanks,” she squeaked, ducking under the counter again.

Wood creaked from outside the window. I turned in fresh dread as slick white hands grappled against the sill. More glass fell loose as the headless body dragged itself upright.

Renner lunged to Teela, grabbed the crossbow, crossed to the window, and aimed down. The bolt thunked, the body dropped, and silence fell once more.

I went to the window on legs that felt like jelly. Stood trembling beside Renner as he scanned the hazy dark, doubtlessly searching for more danger.

My own gaze fell on the motionless, drenched body. Took in the bloated flesh, waterlogged shift, and tattered apron with a rise of bile. And there, visible in the moonlight… My eyes traced the trail of damp earth and tamped-down grass. It led straight to the well.
