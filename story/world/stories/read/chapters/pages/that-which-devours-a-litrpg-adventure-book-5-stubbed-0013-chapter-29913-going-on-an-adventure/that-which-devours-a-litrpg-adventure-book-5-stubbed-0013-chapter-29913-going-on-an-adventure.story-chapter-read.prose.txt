The wind raced through my hair as John sped ahead of me on his hoverbike. Drones circled around us and flew through the trees without a problem, dodging in and out just like we were, several feet above the ground.

We rode high enough that none of the local fauna bothered us. Honestly, we should’ve been flying above the treetops, but this was too fun at the moment.

John’s warning about needing to go higher once we passed a bright red tree echoed in my ears. He’d painted it to remind those who didn’t have all the weird perception skills he’d trained, and the data they gave him.

Laughter bounced around from both of us, and for a little bit I could pretend that life could be this carefree forever.

Then, John started rising in the air, aiming up.

The hover motorcycle under me followed suit without any input from me.

My radar flashed almost red from the creatures in the far reaches of the trees. None of them were a problem for me, but they were there.

A light flashed on the handlebars, before John's voice came out. “Up we go. It’s better to not rile up our neighbors."

“So you think…” I muttered.

“You’ll still get to see plenty of them, though,” he said, unable to hear me.

Once we broke through the treetops using a gap over a cluster of boulders, the wind increased.

Small dots flying in the distance turned in our direction.

This time, I pressed the button to speak. “What about the fliers?”

“They haven’t been able to catch up to me,” he said, before his voice cut out.

While we were going fast, we weren’t going so fast that we couldn’t see the canopy end and grasslands stretch out, before the trees that touched the cliffs of the Sanctuary rose in the distance.

Paras flocked together, all headed to the glimmer of water in the distance. Something else moved just beyond them, and I stared for a moment until the dust let me take a peek.

Giant spikes jutted upward from its spine, then down a long tail with a spike on the end. It was some sort of Stegosaurus combo, maybe, with a bit of Ankylosaurus added to it.

I wasn’t close enough to trigger my perception, and just enjoyed the wonder.

Sunlight washed over the land, heating things up even at the height we flew. John pulled back a little, flying right next to me. He motioned to all of the animals below us, then gave me a thumbs up.

I nodded.

Yet, my attention went back to the specs in the distance that had gotten just a bit bigger.

Those fliers.

John tapped my arm to get my attention. He pointed into the far distance, then made the motion of going faster.

This time, I gave him a thumbs up.

He clicked something on his bike, and the seat slowly moved so he leaned even farther forward, almost laying on the seat.

Then my bike did the same thing, and I went with it. A thin shield slid up, covering my head and my back.

“Alright, here we go,” said John. His voice came up with that blinking dot again. “Time to pick up the pace, otherwise this would take us days.”

It had taken us days in the shuttle to fly all the way around the Sanctuary, but Lenna had said to meet her today.

I really wished I could talk back to him, but his comms were one way only.

A light blinked faster, then turned a solid green color. Everything shifted as we jerked forward, going faster than even I expected.

“I have control of your bike, given the speed we’re flying,” he said calmly.

This was a lot less fun then I’d thought it’d be. Still, we’d get there in record time.

The connection between Strange and I suddenly snapped from leading me back the way we’d come, to in front of us. I didn’t know how that was possible, given where I thought the portal was, in their main city.

Still, everything screamed at me that this was the faster path to get to him. Not that anything said he needed help. Calmness arose from him, along with sleepiness.

I was pretty sure he was napping.

I didn’t touch his eyesight, and just relaxed, trying to enjoy the ride.

It took longer than I thought it would, but eventually we slowed down, and the bikes shifted back into a more upright position.

“We are almost to Hammy’s Hangout,” said John. “We named it after him. It’s a small safe area where our people can rest.”

Then, we were landing in a small clearing in front of the crashed drop-ship. Surprisingly, it looked rather well taken care of.

The drones flew off in different directions, without a sound.

John climbed off his bike, with the biggest smile on his face.

I slid off the bike and stretched up. “So, here we are…”

Somehow, it was only a little past midday. Though, I reflected, we had gotten started very early in the morning, after letting Abby know our plans.

“Yeah, feel free to walk around for a couple of minutes. The next jump is short and slow.” John didn’t look at me, though he really wasn’t looking at anything in front of him. His eyes were focused on the middle distance, clearly looking at things I couldn’t see.

Nothing appeared on my radar, so I didn’t worry.

“You good?” I asked, after a few seconds.

“Yeah, just checking near the exit of Sanctuary and making sure that my cameras are still working…”

I paused, then turned to look at him. “You have cameras?”

“Kinda. The drones download the readings from them. More like sensors, I guess. I use them to see how many beings leave the Sanctuary.” He shrugged. “I usually provide the data to Lenna’s people. Figured I’d grab it, since we were heading their way.”

“So, any graduates?” I asked, slowly walking around the clearing. The entire area made me feel like I’d walked back in time. Even more than Lakeside Landing.

“Just three since last time I came by, which feels like a lot.” A drone reappeared, then shot off in a different direction. One that I assumed led to the village. “Alright, we can get back on the bikes.”

I climbed on with a smile, and waited for him to follow.

John took the lead again, and we took off, staying under the canopy.

This time we moved much slower, faster than we could walk, but not as fast as I could now run. Then again, John wouldn’t be able to keep up with me. Not many beings on the planet would be able to, I’d have bet.

A single dot appeared on my radar, and John waved in that direction way before we were close enough. Another didn’t appear for at least 20 minutes. Again, he waved before we really could get close to them.

Then I saw them.

Massive trees.

So freaking massive.

They were maybe as large as the ones from Nessetra’s world. The trees were definitely big enough to dwarf anything I’d seen on Earth. They formed almost a wall, but there were small gaps in between them that looked intentional.

John steered us down a well-trod trail between two of the forrest giants. Then, he stopped as we approached the opening. Two red dots hid in the shadows, though I could see them clearly.

Both had bows, and both were jungle folk.

“Hey guys.” John smiled and waved. “This is my sister…”

“Alex, you made it,” said a very familiar voice. One that made me smile. Her red dot had approached pretty fast.

“Lenna, did everything work out?” I asked, stepping closer.

Her brown eyes widened, as she really looked at me.

“You did it,” she said, with just a little awe in her voice.

I scratched the back of my head, and the fresh braid from last night. Though, it was probably frizzy already from all the wind. “Didn’t happen how I meant it, but yeah, I ranked up.”

John spoke in a low voice to the two others, who couldn’t help but keep glancing in our direction.

“Should I be worried?” I asked quietly.

“No,” she said, shaking her head. “My people know you don’t want to take over the settlement, and that you spend barely any time on the planet as is.”

“Good, cause this world is just a little hard to be on anymore,” I added carefully.

Lenna nodded. “My grandmother warned me about that. It’s one of the reasons my parents haven’t pushed harder for the stars… They love our village and don’t want to have to leave.”

“Yeah, no one warned me about that.”

Lenna raised an eyebrow at me.

“… not that it would have changed my mind, I suppose.” I shivered, thinking of all of the energy that had flowed through my body. “Anyway, you're joining me to go get Strange, right?”

“Is that the path?” she asked, glancing up at the stars.

“Yeah, I think he’s in one of those megacities… I don’t really know at the moment. I only know that he had a delivery quest for me that didn’t go how we wanted it too.” I shrugged, and glanced around. “Where’s Dengu?”

“He won’t be joining us.” She smiled softly. “He found a lady friend, and needs a bit of a break from the travel.”

I stepped closer. “Are you okay?”

She nodded, but a bit too quickly. “I am.”

I didn’t believe her at all. Especially not since she had spoken about how their dream was to travel the stars together.

I patted her shoulder, as John laughed with the two others. “I’m ready, are you?” he said to the two of us.

“Yeah, we need to head to our main portal.” Lenna responded. She shifted her weight to one side, eyeing the motorcycle. “What are those?”

“It’s fun, I swear,” I said.

John joined us, standing a little taller. “It is. I created them.”

She flashed him a smile. “Are you joining us?”

“Not at all, just loaning Alex the bike to get you two where you’re going.” John chuckled to himself. “I have a massive build I’m in the middle of. I’m working on making a mobile workshop, so when we leave this world, I won’t lose anything.”

Lenna didn’t say anything, but she motioned to the bikes. “How do we do this?”

“You can sit in front of me,” I said, before John could. I just knew she’d love the bikes.

My assumption turned out to be true, as Lenna steered us in the correct direction, somehow following the connection between Strange and I. Though she said it was the stars that led her, they weren’t even out.

It wasn’t as far to the northeast as I feared but there wasn’t any way I would have found it on my own. The small settlement surrounding it, along with more of those massive trees, hid it well. This time, they did form a wall, with only a few openings.

The ones who guarded this entrance were higher level than those in Lenna’s village, but nowhere near me.

We landed outside the walls, and we all climbed off the bikes. The drones stayed close to John, who was underleveled for the area.

“Don’t get into too much trouble,” said John, after he pulled me into a hug.

“I’ll be fine, and I’ll be back before you know it,” I replied. I gave him a smile. “Make sure you get to a higher level, and visit Kabi’s village more.”

“I will, I will.” He blushed when Lenna gave him a hug, then she turned to me.

We both walked into the settlement side by side. No one stopped us. Not that any of them could have.

Still, Lenna didn’t waste any time, and took me directly to the portal. Cobblestone surrounded it, and several jungle folk remained hidden high up in the tree, all staring down at it.

Like the enemy would be coming through it, not leaving.
