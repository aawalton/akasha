Carcan

The moment Josh's steel-clad fingers slipped from her grasp, Carcan's entire world narrowed to a single, terrifying point of focus.

She watched the towering human Guardian turn his broad back to the safety of the open iron gates. She watched the blinding, crackling aura of blue lightning erupt from his armour, illuminating the freezing, blood-slicked mud. And she watched him charge headlong into the shrieking, rotting teeth of an apocalypse.

"Josh!" Carcan screamed, the sound tearing violently from her throat, completely stripped of its usual melodic grace.

She did not think about her own safety, nor did she consider her lack of physical armour. She gripped her amber-topped staff, fully intending to surge forward into the freezing sludge to weave her golden magic around him, to keep him breathing no matter how many monsters tried to drag him down.

She took a single, desperate step forward.

A heavy, leather-clad arm slammed into her chest like an iron bar, forcefully halting her momentum.

"Let me go!" Carcan shrieked, twisting wildly.

Garrik had stepped into her path. The tavern landlord had his heavy iron broadsword lowered, using his thick, muscular left arm to physically bar the elven healer from following the Guardian. He was a broad-shouldered veteran who had spent decades brawling with drunken mercenaries, and his footing in the slippery mud was absolute stone.

Carcan completely lost her serene, aristocratic composure. The terrifying prospect of watching Josh die in the mud shattered her control entirely.

She shoved against Garrik's arm with both hands, her silver eyes blazing with a fierce, panicked fury. "Move out of my way, Garrik! He is going to die out there alone! Let me pass!"

"I cannot do that, lass," Garrik grunted, his voice a low, strained rumble from beneath his dented steel greathelm. He did not yield a single inch. He kept his arm locked rigidly across her chest, pushing her steadily backward towards the surging, panicked crush of civilians attempting to squeeze through the portcullis.

"I will curse you!" Carcan threatened wildly, her voice cracking as she struck her pale fists against his heavy leather tunic. "I swear by the Mother Tree, I will never cast a single healing spell upon you or your kin if you let him fall! Unhand me!"

Garrik absorbed the blows and the desperate threats with stoic, unflinching endurance. He knew exactly what she was feeling, having nearly lost his own wife in the dark tunnels years ago. He knew the panic was speaking, not the healer.

"I made a promise to the lad," Garrik replied, his voice completely devoid of anger, carrying only a heavy, fatherly sorrow. "He asked me to get you through those gates. And beyond that, Barb would absolutely skin me alive if I came home and told her I let harm come to you. She still might for not stopping him," he added, sorrow filling his voice.

The crush of the terrified militia members pressing against them from behind suddenly intensified. The crowd was a blind, frantic animal, surging towards the thirty-foot-wide archway of the city. Garrik was forced to take a step backward, his boots slipping in the churned earth. He realised that he could not maintain a defensive perimeter and physically guide a struggling, frantic elf through the bottleneck.

Garrik let out a harsh, frustrated breath. He opened his right hand, letting his heavy, reliable iron broadsword fall uselessly into the freezing mud. He needed both arms for this.

"Forgive me for the indignity, my lady," Garrik muttered.

Before Carcan could register what he was doing, the tavern landlord ducked low. He wrapped his thick, muscular left arm firmly around the back of her waist, clamping his hand behind her knees. With a massive surge of strength, he hoisted the slender elven healer off the freezing ground, throwing her unceremoniously over his broad right shoulder in a classic fireman's carry.

Carcan gasped in profound shock, the breath knocked from her lungs as her stomach hit his shoulder guard. Her amber staff clattered against his backplate.

"Put me down!" she demanded, struggling wildly against his grip, but the tavern keeper held her legs in a vice-like lock, using his free right hand to begin violently shoving his way through the panicked crowd.

"Make way!" Garrik roared, driving his heavy, steel-clad head into the backs of the terrified militiamen blocking the gates. "Move, you cowards!"

Suspended upside down, bouncing painfully with every heavy step Garrik took, Carcan's field of vision was suddenly elevated above the surging, screaming crowd of refugees.

Through the swirling, foul-smelling smoke and the falling snow, she could clearly see the exact epicentre of the battlefield.

A brilliant, pulsing sphere of crackling blue lightning dominated the gloom. Josh had planted his boots deep into the mud, his dark heater shield raised high. His Taunt was working with absolute efficiency. The mindless, shrieking horde of zombies and Crypt Ghouls had completely abandoned the fleeing civilians. They were swarming the human Guardian like a tide of rotting insects, throwing their bodies pointlessly against his electrified steel.

Carcan watched, her heart hammering a frantic rhythm against her ribs, as Josh swung his longsword in wide, brutal arcs, turning the rotting meat into glowing ash.

Then, she noticed the ripple effect.

Because the horde was entirely focused on the bright blue beacon of the Guardian, the horrific, crushing pressure on the rest of the retreating militia had started to recede, and then vanished as the closest enemies died. The civilians who had been seconds away from being torn apart were suddenly ignored. They stumbled backward, weeping in relief, completely free to sprint for the city walls. Josh had successfully created a breakwater by putting his own life at risk.

Would everyone make it to safety before he was overwhelmed? Would he make it out safe, or would this be the last time she saw him?

Then she saw movement out of the corner of her eye.

Through the smoke, she saw the heavily scarred figure of Torin charging wildly across the mud, swinging his heavy iron mace. He was followed closely by Korg the beastkin and a small squad of the city's finest veteran adventurers. They did not run for the gates. They slammed violently into the rear of the zombie swarm, carving a bloody, efficient path straight towards the isolated Guardian.

A sob of sheer, overwhelming relief caught in Carcan's throat.

Carcan watched as Torin and the veterans reached Josh. They locked their shields and shoulders together, forming a tight, impenetrable ring of iron and experienced muscle around him. The single, desperate breakwater instantly transformed into a jagged, lethal reef.

"They have him," Carcan whispered, her voice trembling violently as the tears finally spilled over her pale cheeks. "They are backing him up."

Garrik finally battered his way through the absolute thickest part of the bottleneck, his heavy boots crossing the threshold of the massive iron portcullis. They were inside the city.

The tavern landlord carefully lowered the elven healer to the cobblestones of the staging area, breathing heavily from the immense physical exertion of the push.

Carcan found her footing, instantly turning back towards the open gateway.

Through the massive iron archway, she caught one final, lingering glimpse of the battlefield. The veteran squad was no longer completely stationary. Having established a solid perimeter, they were now executing a fighting retreat.

Her last sight of Josh was his towering, steel-clad silhouette, bathed in the brilliant blue light of his own magic, bringing his longsword down in a devastating, executing strike against a towering forest troll. He stepped backward in perfect unison with Torin, the group slowly, steadily making their way towards the safety of Ashenfall.

Carcan gripped her amber staff tightly, pressing her hand against her chest, offering a silent, desperate prayer of thanks to the Mother Tree that the stubborn fool was still breathing.

Perberos

Two miles away, deep within the suffocating darkness of the corrupted northern forest, prayers were entirely useless.

Perberos sprinted through the dead, thorny undergrowth, his lungs burning with a fierce, metallic agony. The freezing winter wind whipped at his face, but he did not feel the cold. He was entirely consumed by the pure, unfiltered adrenaline of the hunt.

But they were no longer the hunters.

The deafening, unified shriek from thousands of monsters abandoning the siege of Ashenfall to pursue them was a sound that would haunt Perberos until his dying day. The sheer, physical volume of the horde crashing into the treeline behind them sounded like a massive, rolling avalanche of snapping timber and clicking bone.

"Do not slow down!" Ronald roared from the front of the staggered formation.

The feline beastkin was a blur of muted green motion, slicing his curved hunting daggers through the thick, grey necrotic webbing that hung between the dead pine trees. He was carving a frantic path for the rest of the squad, relying entirely on his primal instincts to navigate the treacherous, uneven ground of the forest floor.

Perberos ran at the absolute rear of the group, acting as the fragile rearguard.

The noise of the pursuit was growing steadily louder, closing the distance with terrifying, unnatural speed. The dark magic of the Bone General was actively enhancing the physical capabilities of the undead, pushing the rotting muscles of the hounds and ghouls far beyond their natural limits.

Perberos did not stop running, but he smoothly unslung his elven longbow. He twisted his torso, running awkwardly sideways, and drew a standard iron broadhead from his quiver. He fired blindly into the pitch-black brush behind them.

A sharp, hollow shriek confirmed a hit, followed instantly by the wet, heavy thud of a monster tumbling into the freezing mud.

Garth, running just ahead of the elf, mirrored the tactic. The grizzled tracker hauled his massive heavy crossbow over his shoulder, firing a thick, oil-soaked bolt directly into the darkness. The heavy thwack of the weapon was followed by the sound of shattering bone.

"It is a drop in the ocean!" Garth grunted, his heavily scarred face pale and slick with sweat as he frantically cranked the heavy winch of his crossbow to reload on the run. "They are too fast! The hounds are going to catch our scent!"

"Keep moving!" Ronald yelled back, leaping gracefully over a massive, rotting log.

Silas was running between Garth and Ronald. The young human ranger was remarkably fast, but he lacked the seasoned, effortless stamina of the older veterans. His breath was whistling harshly through his clenched teeth, his boots slipping dangerously on the patches of slick, black ice hidden beneath the dead pine needles.

They burst through a dense thicket of briars, entering a slightly more open clearing in the forest.

That mistake was fatal.

A massive, corrupted skeletal hound, its bones glowing with a sickly, malevolent green light, burst violently through the treeline to their immediate right. It had flanked them, using the thick undergrowth to mask its approach.

The beast did not leap at the heavily armed beastkin or the towering tracker. It targeted the weakest, slowest member of the running herd.

Silas did not even have time to scream.

The massive skeletal hound crashed into the young ranger's side with the force of a runaway cart. The heavy, fleshless jaws clamped brutally around Silas's right thigh. The horrific sound of snapping bone and tearing leather echoed sharply in the clearing. Silas was thrown violently to the freezing earth, his shortbow spinning uselessly away into the mud.

"Silas!" Perberos shouted, his stoic mask shattering entirely.

The elven scout dug his boots into the frost, halting his sprint instantly. He dropped his longbow, his hands flying to the hilts of his twin curved blades. He prepared to dive into the mud, fully intending to sever the hound's spine and drag the young ranger back to his feet.

A heavy, furred hand clamped viciously onto the collar of his leather armour, violently jerking him backward.

"Leave him!" Ronald snarled, forcefully shoving the elven scout forward. The beastkin’s jade-green eyes were wide with absolute, primal terror.

"I can reach him!" Perberos argued frantically, struggling against the instructor's grip.

"Look at the trees!" Ronald roared, pointing a sharp claw past the struggling ranger.

Perberos looked up, his silver eyes piercing the gloom.

Bursting through the dead treeline, completely ignoring the thick underbrush that snagged the lesser ghouls, came the elite guard.

A dozen Wights, their pristine, blackened full plate armour reflecting a chilling, spectral blue light, marched silently into the clearing. The piercing pinpricks of soul-fire burning within their dark visors were locked entirely onto the downed human. They carried their massive greatswords lowered, moving with a terrifying, inescapable lethality.

They were less than thirty yards away. If Perberos stopped to fight the hound, the Wights would be upon them in seconds, and they would all be slaughtered.

Silas looked up from the freezing mud. The massive skeletal hound was thrashing wildly, tearing at his leg, but the young ranger did not reach for his hunting knife. He looked past the beast, seeing the advancing phalanx of dark steel. He looked at Perberos and Ronald, his face completely drained of colour, twisted in a mask of pure, absolute agony.

"Run!" Silas screamed, his voice cracking violently, a bright stream of blood pouring from his lips. "Get out of here!"

Perberos felt his heart physically fracture in his chest. The cold, logical calculus of the battlefield demanded that he leave the boy to die, but the sheer, horrifying reality of abandoning a comrade tore at his very soul.

Ronald did not give him a choice. The beastkin grabbed Perberos by the shoulder, physically dragging the elven scout away from the clearing and plunging them back into the dense, suffocating darkness of the corrupted forest.

Garth was already running, the grizzled tracker’s face completely devoid of emotion as he sprinted blindly through the trees, tears streaming silently down his scarred cheeks.

They ran.

The fear that took hold of them was no longer the sharp, tactical anxiety of a stealth mission. It was a cold, primal, animalistic terror. They were prey, fleeing from a predator that did not tire, did not breathe, and would never stop hunting them.

Behind them, echoing through the freezing, dead trees, the sound of the skeletal hound tearing into flesh was suddenly joined by the heavy, metallic ringing of blackened steel greatswords.

Silas's screams were mercifully, brutally brief.

Perberos squeezed his silver eyes tightly shut as he ran, desperate to block out the horrific, wet sounds of the execution. He focused entirely on the rhythmic thudding of Ronald's boots ahead of him. He forced his legs to keep moving, his lungs burning, carrying the crushing, unbearable weight of the young ranger's sacrifice with every single step.

The assassination had failed. The General was alive, and the nightmare in the northern woods had only just begun.
