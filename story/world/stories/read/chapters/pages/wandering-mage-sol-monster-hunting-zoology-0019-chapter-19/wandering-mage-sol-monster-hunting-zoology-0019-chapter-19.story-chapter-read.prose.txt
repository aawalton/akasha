After the battle of rabbits, as Tressa would go on to call it, they made their way northeast back toward Ligis.

“So, um,” Tressa began.

“Hmm?” Lisha asked, looking at her.

“Um.” She scrunched her face, trying to remember the word in the common tongue.

“Yeah?”

She bit her lip.

“Can't find the word?”

She nodded. “I can't. It's not coming to me.”

“What are you trying to describe?”

“Ah. The eggs. What do they make with them?”

“Make?”

“Yes!”

“Don't know. They are silky, so they might unravel them or turn them into delicacies to cook. Who knows? Humans have a weird taste in cuisine. They will eat almost anything if it tastes good.”

“I see,” she said. It was true. She had seen it, and the thought of them eating the eggs made her gag. How could humans eat the sticky, foul-smelling eggs? Then she stopped as she realized that if she had to eat them, and if that was her only option for survival, she’d consider it.

Looking at an egg, suddenly she was wondering how to prepare it. She knew she couldn’t experiment with these eggs as they were for a bounty, but now curiosity had gotten the better of her and she hoped for a chance to get more for research purposes.

“Stop!” a guard said as they came to the mouth of the northeast gate.

“Holand, how are you?” Briadon greeted the familiar guard, glad to see he was the one on duty.

“Just fine, Briadon. Those eggs for a bounty?” he asked, looking them over.

“You betcha. Got chased by the damn things.”

“Rock Scorpions? Lucky you got out alive.”

“Hahaha, was no trouble for us.”

“Why are you all bloody?” he asked, looking them over to see the dried blood and bits of fur.

“Horned rabbits attacked. Scout caught a wiff of our eggs and alerted its pack.”

“Damn. Well, since you have more than just the eggs, I'll have to see the rabbits along with your contract so we can process them in and make sure that's all you're bringing in.”

“Figured as much,” Briadon said as he slugged off the bag of rabbits that they had decided to leave out. The guild contract would make the eggs at least exempt from any taxes.

Briadon set down the bundle of four rabbits onto the scale.

“Hmmm. That will be six copper,” he said.

“Thank you,” Briadon said as he handed over the six copper coins. Then he picked up the rabbits and slung them back over his shoulder.

With that, they bid farewell and entered the city, Tressa putting up her hood to try to hide her ears and the fact she was a High Elf a little better, though it would still be clear to anyone who looked close enough. As they walked, several people raised their noses at the group in disgust and at their blood-covered clothes.

Tressa had gotten used to the looks. Every so often she would hear whispers about their group, and some would point at Tressa, whispering about an elf in town.

They walked down the street and headed toward a large building to the far east of town that belonged to their client.

One of the guards outside the building recognized them.

“You three are back already? Did you get the eggs?”

“Yeah, we got lucky,” Lisha responded, adjusting one of the massive eggs under her arm.

“Hm impressive. Well, come on in,” he said, his nose wrinkling a bit at the blood on their clothes, but he didn't respond and opened the door for them, only to be met by a servant on the other side who bowed without saying a word, and turned around to lead them down the hall to the contractor's office.

He sat at his desk, quill scribbling across the parchment he was currently writing on. The contractor was very large, with fat rolls under his neck where it met his collar, and the top button of his esteemed coat seemed a little too tight. Sweat beaded on the side of his face, rolling down into the folds of his neck, and his hair was cut short and slicked back with some sort of cream or grease, though his hairline had a large widow's peak showing that he would soon be going bald.

“I asked for a bottle of wine. Where is it?” he groaned, not looking up.

“My lord,” the servant said. “The hunters have returned.”

His quill stopped, and he looked up, his eyes seeming to be like little beads in his head. He looked them up and down and placed the quill back into the bottle of ink.

“Well then,” he said and stood. “Please set them down over there. I'd like to inspect what you brought me. Go get my quality negotiator.”

The servant bowed and left the room.

Tressa stared at him, getting a bad feeling by the way Lisha and Briadon shared a look.

They did as he asked, placing the massive, silky, slimy eggs on the ground.

He walked around them, touching the surface and inspecting it for damage. His eyes squinted, making them disappear into his head more. To Tressa, he reminded her of a gobfrog. She let out a soft snicker, and his head shot up.

“Something funny, elf?” he spat, his tiny, beady black eyes staring at her, annoyed.

“No, sir,” she said, straightening up and letting out a soft cough. She had thought that since he was so fat, it boded well for her theory that he planned to eat the eggs.

Once again, his eyes squinted at her, and she stood straight, trying her hardest not to laugh at his bulging, bubbly form that moved around as he moved, really resembling that of a gobfrog. There were no elves with such physiques. Humans truly were strange, taking so many body shapes, some fat, some muscular, and others frail or weirdly tall and lanky.

Lisha gave Tressa a look, and she responded by mouthing the word, “Later.”

After a few minutes of him inspecting them, he went back to his desk, pulled another sheet of parchment from a shelf, pulled the quill from the bottle of ink, and scribbled a few things on the paper.

The door opened after a soft knock, and a thin man with a hooked nose that a mustache seemed to grow directly out of, hunched bony shoulders, and a half-bald head with liver spots entered the room. He also wore massive glasses that seemed far too big for his head and made his eyes look almost magnified. His knees were permanently bent, and his butt jutted out. He adjusted his glasses and smacked his lips as he spoke.

“You called for me, Master Merlle?”

“I did,” he said back with a heavy breath. “Please inspect the eggs. I haven't found anything wrong, though I might have missed something.”

The man adjusted his glasses, smacked his tongue and lips once more, and walked over to the eggs with body tremors that made Tressa and the others think he would fall apart at any moment.

He smacked again as he adjusted his glasses, and with an extremely bony finger, he traced it over the eggs and bent down in a way that surprised the group, as he looked as if it would have been impossible with his already-bent knees.

“Hmmm,” he said as he hobbled around the room, softly touching the eggs, adjusting his glasses over and over as he smacked his lips.

“So?” asked Merlle from his chair, as remaining standing seemed far too much of an effort, while he dabbed at the sweat on his forehead with a cloth.

“They are, well… in excellent condition.” He smacked his lips.

“Really?”

“Yes. They did well to care for these. Worth the payment you offered. Well, good. Very well,” he said, staring off into space, no longer making sense.

“Lottry?”

“Ah, yes. High quality,” he said. “I leave.”

“Very well,” Merlle said.

With that, Lottry hobbled out and closed the door behind him.

“Please don't mind him. He may be a bit off with age, but he is wise and well-versed in his craft.”

“Of course. He seemed to know his stuff,” Briadon said as he let out a snort and a slight tip of his head, his ears twitching. Tressa knew Briadon would always agree that the man was an absolute expert, as long as he gave a passing grade to their bounty.

“Thank you. Well, since they are in the condition I wanted, the payment is sixty-five silver and forty copper.”

“Yes, sir,” Briadon responded.

“Sign here,” he said so they could complete the quest.

Lisha walked up, read over the contract, nodded, then signed. Then Briadon, and finally Tressa.

“All right, then. The quest is complete, and here we go,” he said. He dripped some wax onto the line under where they signed and stamped it with his seal to signify he accepted the delivery, then pulled out a chest with coins and counted them out.

Briadon looked at Lisha, and she took the coins, putting them into her pouch, and they bowed.

“Thank you for the contract, Lord Merlle.”

“Happy to oblige. How long will you be in town?”

Briadon paused, thinking. “Probably a few more days.”

“If that's the case, Terik the smith might need assistance. You should go check him out.”

“A smith? Needing hunters?” Lisha asked.

“Yes. He asked if I could send you his way. And now that I have seen the quality of your work, I do prefer it over the human hunters that tend to be, well, less on the… well, how to put it… greedy, loud, annoying, and full of self-righteous pride, and who would argue far too much.”

Tressa laughed.

He shot her a look and she stopped. She wasn’t sure what to make of this person. He seemed kind of nice but not nice at the same time. Maybe it was just because she was a High Elf that he seemed to tolerate her less than Briadon and Lisha?

“Thank you for the tip. We'll head that way shortly.” Briadon bowed once more.

“Much obliged. Though I would pay for a story or two next time you are in town, as I do so much enjoy the company you three bring.”

“That sounds wonderful. Thank you so much for the compliment,” Lisha said with a sharp-toothed grin. “What would you like to hear the next time we are here?”

“Well, if I don't have a quest, I'd like to know how a Stitiki and a minotaur met and how you convinced a high elf to leave the shelter of their deep forest.”

“Hahaha, yes, 'tis quite a story indeed,” Briadon joked as he pretended to act dignified, which in turn made the master laugh.

“Yes, indeed. Anyway, you must all be busy and need to get back to the guild. And remember to check with the smith.”

“Of course, sir. We thank you again,” Lisha said, and they turned, leaving the master behind.

Once outside, Lisha looked to Tressa. “So, mind explaining why you started laughing in there?”

A smile spread across her face, and she burst out laughing once more and she reached into her satchel. She looked through the notebooks, then pulled one out and flipped to a certain page.

“He looked like this,” she said, pointing at the drawing of a massive frog that had so much flesh it looked like a muddy, layered mass of bubbles.

“What the hell is that?” Briadon asked, studying the picture and realizing she was right. It did look like Merlle.

After she was able to stop laughing, she thought of the words and spoke in elvish.

“Uh, what?” Lisha asked.

“Well, what is the word for a creature that looks like this?” she said, pointing at another picture of a toad.

“We call that a frog, or its bigger form, a toad.”

Tressa nodded. “Froog.”

“No frog,” Lisha said.

“Fluge?”

“Say it with me: f-r-o-g.”

“F-r-o-a-g,” Tressa said, trying the word on her tongue but still having no luck. “Okay, anyway, what is the word for eating fast? It also goes around neck. Like this.” Tressa moved her hands like she was tying a scarf around her neck.

“Shawl?” Lisha offered.

“It is?” Tressa said. She tapped her chin trying to think how to explain what she wanted. “No it’s scarf!”

“For eating fast?” her tongue flicked. “You’re mistaken, Tressa. And the clothing around a neck is a shawl.”

“No, she's right Lisha,” Briadon interjected. “Scarfing it down means eating fast.”

“Really?” Lisha asked. “Where did you hear it?”

“I overhear other adventurers at the bars we ate at.” Tressa snickered. “They smelled, were dirty, and ate fast. I bet they'd been out for a long time. They said, ‘scarf it down!’”

Lisha nodded. “You are very perceptive.”

“Anyway,” Tressa continued. “I think it is similar to the sound a turkey makes?”

“Gobble?”

“Yes! Goobfraog,” Tressa said, though the word was completely off. “Because also you can gobble something up!”

Lisha and Briadon gave each other a look, Briadon scratching the fur on his chin and pulling at his braided beard. “I did not know that one. But I like it!”

As they entered the market district, it was louder than Tressa expected. There were people walking all around, stopping at stalls here and there, while vendors called people over to check out their wares.

“So it's a Goobfroag?” Lisha asked, still thinking about it.

“No no um g-o-b-f-r-o-a-g,” she said, sounding out the word.

“Gobfrog!” Briadon said, snapping his fingers.

Her face lit up. “Yes! Large froags with tiny needy eyes—”

“Beedy,” Lisha corrected.

“Yes deedy.”

“Beedy.”

Tressa shook her head, not wanting to try and break down another word. “Anyway, they have deedy eyes that go pop and decome large,” she said, making a motion with her hands by creating a fist, then making a pop sound with her mouth and splaying out her fingers.

“Become,” Lisha corrected again.

Tressa made a face. “I learn it later! But they are massive fleeshy froags that giggle when hopping and groan when trying to rinbt.”

“Ribbit, and fleshy,” Lisha said, giving her the words as she guessed what Tressa was saying.

Tressa threw back her head and groaned.

In truth, though it was frustrating sometimes, she appreciated Lisha helping her learn to speak better. Compared to even a couple of months ago, she felt her Common tongue was coming along quite well, as she could at least properly communicate with her companions now.

“So the shop should be around here,” Briadon said as they took a turn from the market street to the quieter part of town for crafters and makers. They passed a shoemaker's shop, and then what looked like a tavern. From inside, Tressa heard a scream and something explode, and then finally the shutters opened to let smoke out as a woman coughed and left the building.

“Not again!” she yelled and started cursing.

“So what happens if you meet a Gobfrog?” Lisha asked as she looked away from the woman and back at Tressa.

Tressa thought. “They spit mud. And have small nerve toxins. Edible though.”

Lisha stopped and looked at her in disgust. “You ate one?”

“Yes. Legs are tasty. Though the rest is fat and fleeshy. Not the best for eating.”

“I can't believe you actually ate one of these,” Lisha said as she looked in the book at the detailed drawings of how to eat and cook the frog. Her stomach churned and her scaly lip raised in disgust. She hated the smell of frogs, even the quakers to the south. They stank and lived in nasty ponds and bogs.

Briadon leaned over as he stopped walking to see why Lisha looked so grossed out, as he hadn't been paying attention. “Gross. Do all elves eat things like you? Or are you just special?”

“No. I, well… I was curious,” she said. A thought crossed her mind. She was wrong to judge humans. She was actually much worse for eating strange things. Though in her case it was often for research. When she got really interested in studying a creature, she wanted to know everything about it. Its habits, its likes and dislikes, how it hunted, what it ate, and in turn, what it tasted like!

She decided she would keep the truth that she was worse than humans to herself. Though, it never occurred to her that her companions had already figured that out a long time ago.

“You're a very odd elf. I'm surprised they haven't burned you at the stake for being insane,” Briadon said.

“We don't kill each other like that, let alone judge each other for being different,” she said, though that wasn't entirely true.

Tressa didn't have any friends her own age growing up. She had an easier time getting along with the servents than other nobel girls. They would all rather study etiquette and proper tea parties, learning forest magic and healing, and the boys were more interested in swords and bows, scholars, watchers, and soldiers.

They tended to stay away from Tressa as they saw she was odd. While they spent time doing their own court-approved studies, she would have her nose buried in books about runes and monsters, playing in the forest, catching bugs, eating them. She was the odd one of her people. And it also didn't help that when she was younger she had been stuck in the castle under the mad king.

Tressa's face darkened as the memory of the castle resurfaced a little.

“Tressa,” Lisha asked, looking up to see a change in her demeanor.

“Nothing! But yes, I guess I am odd.” She laughed as her more bubbly personality came back.

Lisha had seen that look once or twice on Tressa, as if she were remembering something dark. She hoped to one day figure out what Tressa wasn't saying. To her, Tressa really was a true mystery. She never spoke of her parents or the fact that she was royalty. Nothing. All she really shared were her times in the forest and the things she researched. Anything other than that about her past she would quickly deflect and change the subject.

Briadon didn't catch what Lisha had and grabbed the book from her. He flipped through it and saw the ingredients and how to skin and eat the gobfrog. Though he couldn't read the Elvish, the illustrations were enough to give him a basic idea.

“So do they taste good?” he asked, as he hadn't been listening to the earlier conversation.

“Weren't you listening?” Lisha snapped.

“No. I was looking for the smith!”

“Well, pay attention next time, bull head!”

He huffed and looked at Tressa, handing back her book.

“Well, only the legs. The rest is not so good for eating,” Tressa explained.

“I'd like to try,” he said.

Lisha rolled her eyes, and Tressa smiled. “Okay, if we find one I will make it!”

“Cook it,” Lisha corrected.

Tressa nodded. “Maybe the next time we are near Falidorn. As far as I know they live in the rivers, ponds and streams around the forest.”

“Hmmm. So none out here?”

“As I said, as far as I know that's where they live. There might be some all the way down here, but I'd have to search for them or get some of its skin to use a tracking spell.”

“Hey, you there, minotaur,” a nearby merchant called, drawing the group's attention.

The group turned to look at him, and Briadon walked up, his hooves clicking on the ground as he approached. He towered over the man, who chuckled nervously.

“Yes?” Briadon asked, folding his arms.

“Ahem. Tell me, sir, do you have trouble with dirt and mud and all manner of things getting stuck between your hooves?”

Briadon turned his chin up and huffed through his nose. “Sometimes.”

“Well then, I have a new array of wares to help with that,” he said as he pulled out a box and opened it. “Take a look! I’ve just gotten in this new iron hoof pick that is guaranteed to last longer than standard steel. It's longer and more durable. I also have a file to help if your hooves grow too long, and it is excellent and won't wear down from use for a very long time! If that doesn't take your fancy, I also have a stiff brush to help wipe off the stubborn dirt that refuses to loosen! And finally, a nice polish to make your hooves and horns shine!”

With each item he talked about, he pulled it from the box and laid it on a black cloth. The items looked new and well taken care of.

Briadon picked up the pick and moved it around his hands, inspecting the semi-polished metal. “No. It's too shiny. Might break on the first use. I like the steel.”

“Ah, but that's the appeal! It's newer, shinier, and more durable than steel,” he said as he took it back and pounded it against the cobblestone ground. “See how it doesn’t bend? Far more durable!”

A small crowd began to gather around them, the spectacle of their odd group and the merchant’s salesmanship drawing them in.

Briadon huffed. “What of the polish?”

“Good choice. Here, let me demonstrate.”

He reached into his pack and pulled out a horn, looking up to make sure Briadon wouldn't get angry over him having a horn.

Briadon nodded for him to continue.

The vendor let out a relieved breath, then pulled out the polish and a soft cloth and dipped it in. The horn was dirty and dulled. He then rubbed a bit of the polish onto it, showing a sleek streak that brought out the natural hues. There was gray, brown, red, white, and black in the horn, showing the polish had done its work.

“Hmmm. I like it.”

The vendor beamed. “Thank you. It also works the same on your hooves and creates a protective layer that boosts defense from attacks. You see, this is enchanted with druid magic.”

Tressa stepped forward. “Hold on, what?”

The man paused when he saw Tressa.

“Ah… is everything alright, ma'am?”

“I find it hard to believe Druids have interacted with humans, let alone blessed them with magical items, in centuries. They stay within their forest to the north, near the blood elves' land.”

The man laughed cautiously as the crowd started to murmur. He coughed, regaining his composure after being called out.

“And how would you know that, miss?” the merchant asked.

“Because I grew up around them.”

“I don't—” He then took a closer look, peering under her hood. Then his eyes widened as he saw her pointed ears and silver-blonde hair. “You're a… a High Elf!?”

Tressa ignored the gasps of the crowd, shrugging as she lowered her hood, figuring there was no point in hiding it now.

“Yes. And this is my teammate. So tell me, who did enchant it? Because even though it wasn't blessed by a druid as you say, it still has mana coming from it. And a decent amount. You are not lying about the protection spell that boosts defense and strength,” she said, looking at the gray light that surrounded it. At times like this, she was grateful for the fact that she could see mana. She picked it up and turned it over and over in her hands as she inspected the mana flowing from it.

The murmurs continued and many backed up due to realizing what she was.

Tressa picked up the container, and her eyebrows scrunched up as she focused on it.

“Tressa…” Lisha said, looking around at the crowd.

“One second. He lies about the druid blessing. But the mana is here.” She tilted her head, studying it. “I don't recognize this magic. But I do agree, Briadon, you should get it.”

Briadon took it from her but set it back down. “Not if he lies about who enchanted it. After all, it could cause harm to me if I'm not told the full truth.”

The man looked around, a little nervous now that he had been called out as a liar. His wares wouldn't sell, and many might come back for refunds. He couldn't have that.

“So who enchanted?” Tressa asked.

“An alchemist. I have a friend who uses alchemy.”

Tressa blinked as her eyes returned to the mana and she muttered in Elvish. “Alchemists can do this?” She had always thought only druids would be capable of such enchantment. She wanted to meet an alchemist even more now to learn just how this was possible.

“It is true it will enhance your strength and defense,” the merchant explained nervously. “But it does have a limited effect of ten minutes. Your horns and hooves will stay nice and shiny for many weeks, however!”

Briadon thought it over and looked down at Tressa.

She gave him a nod. “As I said, it is still enchanted, and that is a good thing. Even if not by a druid. Protection is protection.”

“Fine, I'll take it.”

The vendor blinked.

“So long as you stop lying about your wares. And I'll take the brush. Mine is worn down.”

“Of course. That will be seventeen coppers for the brush and a silver for the polish. I will even throw in the pick as gratitude.”

“Hahaha, you make a great deal!” Briadon boomed, laughing. And he handed over the coins and took the items.

“Ah, vendor?” Lisha asked.

“Yes?” the man said, his face sweaty from nearly losing the crowd of customers after being called out as a liar.

“Do you happen to sell anything for Stitiki?”

He paused. “Like what?”

“A scale pick? Sometimes dirt gets stuck between the scales, and a standard brush doesn't get between them to get it out. Or something for the teeth?”

“Unfortunately, I don't. If you keep heading south there should be a small farming village called Tipier.”

“Hmm, very well.”

They said goodbye, and then went back to looking for the smith.

“That means that the caravan has already moved on, and that means our next job is definitely going to be the smith.”

“Aye,” Briadon agreed.
