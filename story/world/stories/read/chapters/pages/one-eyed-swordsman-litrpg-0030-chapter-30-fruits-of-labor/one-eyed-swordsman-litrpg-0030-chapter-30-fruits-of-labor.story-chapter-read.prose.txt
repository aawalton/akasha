A faint tremble kissed Oswald’s fingertips as he let his eyes fall shut. The air felt as if it pressed in close, heavy and wet. Nothing stirred. Not at first. Then the chill crept in.

When he did open his eyes, a ripple fanned out beneath his feet. Not soil. Not stone. Just that same glistening sea of crimson, spreading in all directions

He was now back inside his Animus. The place didn't look too different.

Narrow veins of darker blood cut through the surface, writhing slowly, dragging with them pieces of what once were people: an arm, a half-sunken rib cage, even a jawbone still locked in a scream. One skull drifted close, its sockets meeting his gaze, then sank without a sound. Still awful as expected.

Oswald had gotten used to how gruesome the environment here was. Perhaps he'd simply been desensitized to the sights within his Animus. Guess I’m gettin’ used to it. Kinda sad, really.

The boy inhaled through his nose. The air burned sharp and metallic, thick enough to taste. He winced and rubbed at his face with one hand. Time to get this over with.

“I’m here, Zvarah. We need to talk.”

To his surprise, there was no reply. Oswald narrowed his eyes and turned his head slowly, listening for even the faintest sound. But there was nothing.

“…Zvarah?” he called again, louder this time.

Oswald clenched his jaw, grinding his teeth together as he stared toward the horizon. Just me out here it seems.

He scratched the back of his neck, fingers brushing against dried blood and sweat. Weird. She’s usually here. I’ll look around, maybe she’s nearby.

He took a step forward. The ground responded with a wet, sucking sound as his boot sank into the blood-slick surface. He moved slowly at first, half out of caution, half because he wasn't sure if the place never followed normal rules.

The crimson liquid underfoot shifted with every step. His reflection wavered below, stretched, and bleeding at the edges, until it vanished entirely in the ripples he left behind. Bones jutted from the surface like jagged teeth, clustered in tangled piles. Some were cracked and freshly stained. Others bleached white, picked clean by time or something worse.

Then, a faint splash pulled his eyes to the right. At first, he figured it was just some piece of meat breaking loose from beneath the surface. But then it moved again.

A small blob, bright red and gelatinous, no bigger than a clenched fist, wobbled into view. It bounced several times, its jiggly form trembling with each little hop.

“What the...?” he muttered, slowing his pace.

More of them emerged, slipping out from behind a half-sunken skull. Four... maybe five. Same size, same shape, like little blood-drops made solid. They shimmered under the lightless sky, red like everything else here, but brighter. Glossy. Alive.

They hopped without any rhythm, spreading out in a loose half-circle around him. They didn’t get too close, just hovered at the edge of his space, bobbing like they were watching him. Are these slimes? Since when do slimes show up here? Didn’t think stuff like that could even exist in this place.

Out of curiosity, Oswald lifted a hand and gave the nearest one a lazy wave. The blob froze mid-hop, wobbled in place... then bounced once.

The others joined in, little ripples passing through their squishy forms as they bounced softly in place. One by one, they turned and hopped away, drifting off in no clear direction, leaving faint impressions in the surface where they passed.

Oswald stood still for a moment, brow furrowed. Well, that’s new. Never seen those before. This place never had creatures like that.

A thought crept in, slow and quiet. Did feeding Zvarah all those souls do this? Is this place changin’?

Oswald chewed on the thought in silence, letting it roll around in his head. His feet followed the faint impressions the slimes had left behind, small dents in the blood-slick surface.

Eventually, the land dipped ahead. His eye caught movement in the distance.

A jagged cliff rose from the liquid expanse like a torn scab, black stone jutting upward in sharp angles. Shadows clung to it, thicker than the rest of the world around him.

Trees clung to the cliff's edge, twisted and gnarled. Their roots dug into the rock like talons, desperate and stubborn. Their red leaves rustled softly in an unseen breeze, like the faint whisper of bones brushing together.

And there, sitting beneath one of those trees, was Zvarah. She looked almost carved into the scene, motionless except for the slow swing of her legs. In one hand, she held a black fruit, its skin glossy and uneven like polished coal.

When Oswald approached, her crimson eyes flicked up just long enough to let him know she saw him. But she didn’t speak.

Instead, she took a slow bite. The fruit gave a soft, wet crunch, its inky juice trailing down her fingers in thick, glistening rivulets.

Oswald slowed, eyes fixed on her. "Uh, hello?"

She didn’t answer. Just kept chewing. Every bite looked more like a statement than a meal.

He narrowed his eyes. Why’s she acting off? She was usually smug, sharp-tongued. Being this quiet… it’s not like her. Something’s wrong.

He stopped a few paces from the base of the tree, looking between the fruit in her hand and the faint smile tugging at her lips. “What, are you sulking or just feeling dramatic today?”

Still no answer came from Zvarah. What’s up with her?

Her smile deepened, as if she’d been waiting for him to catch on. Then she patted the space beside her with a lazy flick of her fingers. “Come. Sit with me, Oswald, the view is lovely from here, yes?”

He blinked. The view? What, are we on a picnic now?

His brows knit as he gave her a long, sidelong glance. “You planning on pushing me off once I sit?”

“Even if I did, you would not die. Not here. Not like that.”

“So… you still wanna kill me, then?”

Zvarah let out a long sigh, almost as if he’d said something deeply disappointing. “It is painful speaking to you sometimes. You have a talent for dragging every conversation through the mud, yes?”

Oswald rolled his eyes, but the tension in his shoulders eased a bit. He took a few steps forward, before sitting on the ground beside her.

Feels... weirdly calm. Like she’s not trying to mess with me for once.

He glanced over, studying her face, watching the way her lips curled as she took another bite of the fruit.

“Alright… I gotta ask. Something wrong with you? You’re acting real strange today.”

Zvarah raised a finger in silence, chewing slowly. Only when she swallowed did she speak. “Wrong? No. Quite the opposite. I am fine, Oswald. Better than ever, in fact.”

He narrowed his eye. “Huh. Could’ve fooled me.”

Zvarah let out a soft, amused breath through her nose. “I understand your confusion. After all, the last time we saw each other…” She trailed off, her grin deepening.

“Yeah,” Oswald muttered, scratching his head. “Last time, we were trying to tear each other to pieces.”

“That’s all in the past, I've felt much better ever since you started collecting more and more souls."

“So that’s all it takes to please you, huh?”

The demoness chuckled. “I never said I was complicated. Demons are predictable creatures, Oswald. We want to grow stronger. That is the truth of our existence. As long as you continue to serve that purpose, we will both be happy, yes?”

Oswald stared out at the bloody horizon, watching the clouds shift above the cliff edge. Guess that’s her version of happy.

He rubbed at his jaw, thinking for a moment. “Since you’re all relaxed now, I wanted to ask, do you know anything about vampires?”

Zvarah burst out laughing. A sharp, sudden sound that echoed out over the dead trees and crimson sea.

Oswald blinked. “What? What’s so funny?”

She waved a hand airily, the last bite of her fruit still held in her fingers. “Oh, nothing, it’s just… vampires. You’re asking about vampires, of all things.”

“Well, yeah. You gonna answer or not?”

Zvarah smirked and finally finished her fruit, tossing the blackened core away. “I know quite a lot about them, yes, but it’s nothing you can’t learn for yourself eventually.”

Oswald rolled his eyes and huffed. “How hard can it be to just tell me what I wanna know?”

Zvarah rose without warning, fluid as smoke. Her limbs unfurled in a slow, deliberate stretch, arms reaching high into the still air before drifting down.

“I simply do not feel like it, besides, we both know that isn’t why you came, yes?”

Course she knows, Oswald thought, exhaling sharply through his nose.

He pushed himself up, coat rustling as he brushed at the side, more out of habit than anything.

“Let’s skip to it, then. What’s the cost for the next skill?”

Zvarah’s crimson eyes brightened with amusement, hunger, maybe both. She lifted a single finger and flicked it through the air.

The space between them quivered, then split apart with a sound like tearing cloth soaked in oil. A flash of red lit the space, and glowing letters scrawled themselves into the air.

[Displaying Skill Shop]

1 New Pactbound Skill [Cost: 500 Souls]

Note: Pactbound Skills always start at a minimum of Tier 3!

“I’ve increased the cost, but it should still be within reach, yes?” Zvarah asked.

Oswald squinted at the glowing words, then scoffed. “We’ll see about that.”

He opened his system with a thought. Words unfolded before his vision.

Souls: 1000

Resonance: 1

Invocations: ???

Days until Tribute: 5

“Well, looks like I do have enough” he muttered, turning toward her with a dry look.

Zvarah smiled. “So then… do you wish to make a purchase?”

“Yeah. Let’s do it.”

[Deducting 500 Souls]

[Payment Successful!]

A sharp rush hit him. Crimson wisps erupted from his right eye, tearing into the open air like threads unspooling from a wound. Thick streams of soulstuff twisted toward Zvarah, spiraling through the air before sinking into her chest. She shuddered as it entered her, lips parting in a soft, almost delighted gasp. Her wings flared slightly as her body arched ever so subtly.

The energy coiled around her like silk spun from blood, wrapping over her arms, her neck, vanishing beneath her skin. “Ohhh… lovely. You will keep feeding me in the future, yes?”

That's... a little creepy, he thought, stepping back warily.

Meanwhile, the air buzzed around Zvarah like heat rising off scorched metal. “I have something special in mind for you this time.”

Oswald’s back stiffened. “Special how?”

In the blink of an eye, Zvarah was standing directly in front of him. Before he could move, her hand shot out and tapped him lightly on the forehead.

Pain exploded through him instantly. Oswald dropped hard, knees slamming into the blood-slick ground with a wet crunch.

The ground beneath him cracked open. Fiery chains burst forth in a vicious, spiraling dance, their links glowing a molten gold-red. They struck like spears.

The first one tore through his shoulder. He screamed. Another slammed into his thigh, drilling straight through with a crack of bone. Then a third, right through his side. Each chain burrowed into him, biting deep, writhing inside his flesh.

“Fucking shit!” he shouted, ragged with pain.

Zvarah stood a few steps away, arms crossed. “Honestly, must you scream? It’ll be over soon. Do try to keep your dignity intact, yes?”

Dignity my ass! Oswald’s fingers clawed at the ground as another chain wrapped around his chest, tightening like a vice. He felt it slither across his ribs, then pressing into his heart .

However, as quickly as it struck, the pain began to fade. The chains lost their glow, then crumbled into ash and vanished without a sound. Oswald’s breath came in short, shuddering bursts. He blinked, heart thudding against his ribs like a caged animal.

He looked down. There was no blood or wounds. Just faint red lines where the chains had been, fading already.

[New Pactbound Skill Acquired: Soul Binding Chains (Tier 3)]

Oswald pushed himself up, unsteady at first but regaining his footing. "This skill better be worth it."

Zvarah watched him with a smirk. “The skill I granted you just now is one of my personal favorites, actually."

Oswald spat to the side, still catching his breath. “Yeah? Why’s that?”

She stepped closer, her smile widening. “Because it can do this.”

Zvarah raised her hand toward the sky. For a breath, the world held still. Not a flicker in the blood-soaked air. Not a whisper from the sky above. Then, her head tilted just slightly, as if listening for something distant.

Heat shimmered at her wrist, warping the space around it. A coil of flame twisted into chains, winding up her arm in tight, searing spirals. Each link glowed with a molten pulse, alive, angry.

The chains hissed as they climbed her flesh. Sparks danced from her fingertips, scattering into the air like fleeing embers, as though the very world recoiled from the heat she wielded.

Then, the ground split apart. With a roar that split the stillness, the ground erupted. Chains burst from the crimson earth in jagged, radiant arcs, tearing upward like spears.

Above, a winged creature let out a shriek of alarm. It was already too late.

The first chain drove clean through its chest, a sickening crack echoing across the landscape. Another lashed around one wing, yanking it backward with enough force to snap bone. Then more followed as dozens of fiery tendrils piercing the air, slamming into the beast, binding it mid-flight and dragging it down.

It struck the ground with an impact that sent a quake through the blood-slick terrain. Flesh hit stone, wings twisted beneath its weight, and chains pinned it where it fell, twitching and broken.

Oswald’s eye snapped to Zvarah. Whoa.

The flames on her hand hadn’t faded. They flared hotter, burning brighter, tighter. The chains that obeyed her will were burrowing deeper into her skin. The scent of scorched flesh drifted on the air, faint but unmistakable. That’s burnin’ her... and she ain’t even flinchin’.

Oswald’s gaze shifted from her charred hand to the beast still writhing on the ground, held fast by the very chains that were hurting her. It’s hurting her, too. Even now, it’s taking something out of her. There’s always a price.
