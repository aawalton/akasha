Wind rushed against Adam’s face as he fell like a missile.

His eyes sprang open, seeing the black sky above and the trailing bright flame of his flare, before he quickly reoriented himself and pulled hard on his raiment to slow down before he’d splatter against the green hill flying up to meet him.

Adam sensed a great many bodies filled with his mana below him and, as the many G’s of his braking manoeuvre tore at him, he swept above the swathe of the forlorn army that he’d devastated with his afflictions. Before the other soldiers could react or the Forlorn Shadow could reanimate the corpses he’d made, Adam pulled them to him, absorbing as many as he could into his armour.

Successive announcements from his cube rolled in, one after the next, before it simply switched to using numbers.

[11 Forlorn Elites defeated.]

Adam didn’t push his luck, and immediately shot himself back to the wall of Gothershall.

He soon flew past a white fog that stretched outward from the city like a welcome mat for the invading force.

When he landed back atop the towering wall, Seo-Yoon was still walking in a small circle while moving her staff through fluid motions.

“You look unwell,” she said. “Did it work?”

Now that Adam was back near the lights of the city, he realised just how badly he’d damaged his mind with the mantle, because his vision was blurry and fragmented, like a cracked camera lens.

He sat down with his back to the edge of the wall and saw how his hands shook violently.

Adam tried to speak but his speech was slurred and nonsensical.

Seo-Yoon stopped walking in circles and looked down at him.

Her mouth moved but he couldn’t hear what she was saying.

A tinnitus whine steadily grew inside his head.

Then his back slid sideways along the wall and he banged his head against the stone floor.

Adam came to as an objective announcement rolled in. Next to him lay one of his healing potions, though it was empty.

Seo-Yoon was watching him while she worked through the motions of her mist-casting spell.

The sky was still dark, but there was a light slowly blooming into life from the eastern horizon.

“Are you okay?” Seo-Yoon asked.

“I think I passed out,” Adam croaked, his throat feeling like sandpaper when he spoke.

“You woke up in fits and said things I couldn’t understand,” she told him. “You were like that for a few hours. I thought you were dying, so I fed you one of your potions. What happened?”

“The backlash from the mantle is too dangerous,” he replied. “I managed to afflict 116 soldiers though. My affliction damage is now 724%, although I will die from even the weakest affliction I receive.”

A horn shook the air, pulling Adam back to stage four briefly. Then a tremor rolled through the wall they were standing on.

He got up and looked down over the edge, where a carpet of shadow-clad soldiers crowded against the white stone and a forlorn knight wielding a sword and shield slammed into the sealed gate like a battering ram. He was covered in rusted plate armour and wore a helmet with horns, with a faded blue scarf around his neck that fluttered every time he slammed forward and sent quakes through the wall.

Further away from the human ram, standing like a tower amongst the soldiers, was the three-metre-tall spindly herald, whose clothes were very similar to what the aristocrat in stage four had worn, except for the blue cloak he carried over his left arm and shoulder. He held a long horn with a faded blue banner hanging from it in his right hand. In his left was a long sword with a simple crossguard.

“I spread the mist as far as I could,” Seo-Yoon said, and Adam at first didn’t even register it was there, because it was everywhere and encompassed the entire army, blanketing something like two-hundred square-metres in front of the wall.

“I didn’t know you could make the mist this big,” he replied, impressed.

Ballista bolts, crossbow projectiles, and arrows flew out from various gaps in the wall, barrelling through the horde below. But even when they killed the soldiers by the dozen, those same soldiers got back up within a minute, returned to their feet by the Shadow’s power.

Adam sensed about a hundred melodies waiting behind the wall inside the city. They belonged to the defenders who were prepared to throw their bodies into the breach when it formed, even if it would only buy their friends and family a few seconds longer.

While Gothershall had a great defensive advantage given its location, it was obviously susceptible to sieges starving out its people, and the fact that they’d put all their faith in the towering wall to protect them, meant that once it did fall, they had no way to escape the invaders.

“We have to defeat them before it’s too late,” Adam said.

Natalia and Chien were amongst the defenders, and Adam flew down to pick up both of them before they even knew what was happening.

He returned to the top of the wall and quickly shaped a Shade Barrier construct for each of them.

“What’s the plan?” Natalia asked. Based on her melody, Adam knew she hadn’t slept at all. She was filled with anxiety and guilt, no doubt because she’d been the one to kill Diwa. She clearly appreciated the distraction posed by the forlorn army.

“We need to get rid of the herald and the bulwark,” Adam reminded them. “Seo-Yoon has primed the battlefield with her mist spell, and as soon as we’re ready, she will turn it all to ice, incapacitating the army for a moment.”

Chien looked at Adam. “Why are you covered in blood?”

Adam paused. “I am?”

“Yeah,” Natalia said.

“It’s a lot,” Seo-Yoon added.

She stepped out of the metal tub of water and Adam went over to look at the reflection that stared back at him from within.

Dark dried blood ran from his tear canals, nostrils, ears, and mouth, creating sinister-looking lines down his face, as though he had intentionally dressed himself in warpaint.

He scooped up a bit of water and cleaned himself, then addressed the others again. “I managed to eliminate about a hundred soldiers last night, but the mantle’s effect was more punishing than I expected.”

He cleared his throat.

“Anyway. The plan is as follows,” Adam said. “Once the army is enveloped in ice, I will drop you two down there so you can eliminate the herald. If Chien is unable to deliver the killing blow, then you must use your ring to finish him off, Natalia.”

She nodded.

“I will fly to Old Town and retrieve another true flame if we need it, but I am hoping that defeating the herald will disable the army for good, leaving us with just the forlorn bulwark remaining. He is the one currently smashing his body against the city gate, but we should have a few more minutes before he can break through.”

“I thought it was a battering ram,” Natalia muttered. “I didn’t realise a knight was doing that much damage…”

“He has a shield with a ramming power,” Adam explained. “He’s quite dangerous to fight up close because of this unpredictable attack, so Seo-Yoon and I will attempt to give you two an opening to deal with him.”

“Couldn’t I absorb the true flame from the city wall and use that to take them out?” Seo-Yoon asked.

“No, we need to leave it in place,” Adam replied. “Even if we defeat the army, the Forlorn Shadow isn’t going anywhere. The true flame acts like a barrier along with the mountain range, preventing it from getting inside the city and reanimating the dead within. Without it, it would just be a matter of time before the city fell, and we need this place to remain as a safe zone, since otherwise our attack on the castle will be slowed down by the need to go all the way back to Old Town to rest and resupply.”

“I don’t know,” Adam replied. “It’s a possibility.”

“I see. I will utilise Diwa’s cape to make them focus on me,” he said. “No one else will die.”

“I don’t think it’s that effective on bosses,” Natalia pointed out. “After all, Diwa struggled to keep the horsemen’s attention, remember?”

“The Forlorn Shadow wants to kill him, so it may be more effective,” Seo-Yoon pointed out. She was back inside the tub, walking in circles and waving her staff.

Natalia rolled her shoulders and held tight to her katana’s handle and sheath. “I’m ready.”

“Me too,” Seo-Yoon said.

“We will defend this holy city from the vile invaders,” Chien remarked piously.

Adam had to steady himself against the wall as he struggled to retain his balance on his own. Despite feeling like there was a drill working its way through the inside of his skull, he managed to cast them all a grin. “Let’s go fuck up the Forlorn Shadow’s plans.”

Seo-Yoon suddenly gripped her staff and ran her right hand down its length.

Down below, at the foot of the Gothershall wall, the blanket of mysterious fog condensed, tiny aerosolised droplets coming together like static rain and clinging to every soldier and blade of grass.

“Go,” Seo-Yoon said.

Adam lifted himself into the air, using four floating hands of bone to pick up Natalia and Chien.

The static cloud of rain hovering around the forlorn army suddenly crystallised, producing a pale-white frigid wind that flowed through their ranks and spread across their armour, pushing past the black veil that protected them from most forms of attack.

The army turned to statues of ice, and Adam rushed himself and his two passengers down to the ground.
