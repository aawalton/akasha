The emerald lance burned through Cade’s chest like liquid fire, searing flesh and magical pathways alike. Bernard’s strike had been perfect—a killing blow from which there could be no recovery. Blood pooled beneath him on the ancient stone, spreading in a crimson halo that seemed oddly beautiful through the haze of shock.

Cade tried to move, but his body refused to respond. The cathedral’s broken ceiling spun above him, dust motes and debris dancing in shafts of emerald light. Everything felt distant, as though he were watching the world through water.

His gold-rank senses, usually so sharp they bordered on overwhelming, were fading. Sound became muffled. Color leached away. Even the pain—that white-hot poker through his chest—began to dull.

So this is dying, he thought with surprising clarity. Less dramatic than I expected.

Bernard’s victorious laughter reached him as if from miles away. Chubbs’ desperate scream—“CADE!”—tore at what remained of his heart.

A coldness washed over him that had nothing to do with blood loss. The air around him seemed to thicken, condense, as though reality itself were folding inward. With his fading magical perception, Cade sensed a presence materializing beside him—ancient, patient, inevitable.

“Finally... come to collect?” Cade whispered, blood bubbling between his lips. “Didn’t think... I’d run up such a tab... this soon.”

Death’s form partially solidified beside him, translucent and rippling like smoke in a hurricane—which, considering the literal hurricane of magical energy tearing through the cathedral, seemed oddly appropriate. His expression was uncharacteristically grave, lacking the usual smug superiority that Cade had come to expect.

“That’s not why I’m here, Cade Stormhollow,” Death replied, his voice reaching places beyond physical hearing.

“Shame.” Cade managed a bloody smile. “Was hoping... for a loyalty discount.”

Death’s frown deepened. “Your humor, as always, is misplaced. I come with a warning, not for collection.”

Cade was pretty sure no one else could see the deity, what with the gaping hole in his chest and the fact that everyone else was preoccupied with not dying. Somehow, that made it worse.

“Cade. You must destroy that foul creation,” Death commanded, gesturing toward the glowing tendrils pushing through the fissure. “The Oakthorn must not be allowed to fully manifest.”

Blood bubbled between Cade’s lips as he forced words through the agony. “Little... busy... dying...”

“That emerald abomination is a nexus point of seven dominion ley lines,” Death continued, his voice tightening with urgency. “Once it fully emerges, Bernard will ascend. He will become a god with power rivaling our own.”

Cade wheezed out a laugh that sent fresh pain lancing through his shredded chest. “So... this is about... job security?”

Death’s eyes narrowed, his normally impassive face tight with what might have been genuine fear. “Joke if you must, but this is far beyond your comprehension. Kill it, Cade. Kill it now, while you still can. If you fail, all your friends will die here.”

Cade tried to lift his hand toward the emerging tree—a futile gesture. He couldn’t even feel his fingertips anymore, let alone summon enough magic to destroy a divine nexus. Just as he opened his mouth to tell Death exactly how useless his warning was, the deity’s form dissipated like mist before a breeze.

Bernard’s shadow fell across him, the corrupted man’s eyes gleaming with triumph as he studied Cade’s broken body.

“Talking to yourself at the end?” Bernard mocked. “How very... human.”

Cade tried to focus on the emerging tree—if that was truly what it was. Through the haze of pain and blood loss, he could sense seven distinct magical signatures pulsing within it, each representing a different dominion. The power radiating from it felt ancient and neutral, neither good nor evil, merely... potential.

Something in his chest rebelled at Death’s command. Destroying the tree felt fundamentally wrong, like being asked to burn the world’s oldest library without first checking what knowledge it held.

His thoughts scattered as a scream tore through the cathedral—high, desperate, and heart-wrenchingly familiar. Summoning what little strength remained, Cade turned his head toward the sound.

What he saw made his failing chest lurch with renewed agony.

Golden light erupted from Chubbs in violent waves as her small body strained against new shackles that had materialized around her wrists. These weren’t there before—weren’t part of his altered ritual. They glowed with virulent green runes that pulsed in time with Bernard’s crown, and where they touched her skin, divine essence streamed directly into the ritual.

This was something else entirely. A trap Cade hadn’t accounted for.

“Ah, now you see it,” Bernard said, following Cade’s horrified gaze with a satisfied smile. “Contingency planning is such an underappreciated art.”

The words struck Cade like physical blows. He’d been outplayed completely—his careful alterations to the ritual rendered meaningless by Bernard’s foresight.

“You... bastard...” Blood flecked his lips with each syllable.

“Engineering,” Bernard corrected, kneeling beside him with the casual air of a professor explaining a particularly clever theorem. “Divine engineering, to be precise. The more she struggles against those shackles, the faster her essence burns. The more power she expends trying to save you...” He smiled coldly. “Well, I won’t bore you with the concept of escalating costs.”

Rage surged through Cade, momentarily eclipsing even the pain of dying. He tried to summon his magic, to lash out with wind or flame or chaos—anything—but the emerald lance had devoured those connections, leaving him as helpless as a newborn.

Bernard leaned closer, his voice dropping to an intimate whisper. “I never imagined she would awaken solely to save someone who betrayed her so thoroughly. Perhaps the vessel has changed her after all.”

The Progenitor straightened, contemplative. “Not for the better, though.”

A thunderous crash diverted both their attentions. Across the cathedral, another battle reached its zenith.

“Ah, the old bastard, Dread, is trying to sneak in through my gateway? A coward, to the end,” the one Chubbs had called Mordred said.

Nora faced off against the towering entity wrapped in shadows and fear. The god’s form wavered between solid and smoke as the ritual’s pull affected even his divine essence.

The paladin’s massive blade left trails of blue light as she forced the deity back toward the fissure step by agonizing step. Blood streamed from a dozen wounds across her body, but her eyes burned with enough ferocity to rival the stars. Dread raked his claws at her, but she ducked beneath the wild swing to headbutted the god. He reeled back, cursing in a tongue that sounded like bones scraped across stone.

“An unexpected bonus,” Bernard commented, gesturing toward the shadow deity. “Dread’s essence will accelerate the Throne’s ascension considerably.”

For a moment, Cade felt a flicker of hope. If Nora could banish Dread back to the spirit realm, perhaps they could still—

The shadow god let out an unholy scream as Nora drove her blade into what passed for its chest. Dark ichor spilled across the floor, but instead of retreating, Dread surged forward with renewed fury. Tendrils of living darkness lashed out, opening fresh wounds across Nora’s arms and face.

“I name you FEAR!” she roared defiantly, blood streaming down her face as she held her ground. “And I shall not let you touch them, demon!”

Something extraordinary happened. At her declaration, the shadow god recoiled as though struck. His form solidified further—becoming more substantial and, paradoxically, more vulnerable.

“And I am NOT afraid!” Nora drove her blade forward with a final, devastating thrust.

The shadow god screamed as he was forced back into the fissure, which momentarily contracted around his retreating form. Nora stumbled backward, exhausted but triumphant.

Finally, something went their way in this gods-forsaken swamp.

But Cade’s relief evaporated as Bernard merely smiled.

“Your friends fight admirably,” he conceded. “But look around you, thief.”

Even as Dread retreated, new horrors crawled from the edges of the ritual circle—corrupted remnants of the cathedral’s former guardians, their bodies twisted by the Oakthorn’s emerging power. They surrounded Nora, cutting her off from reaching either Cade or Chubbs.

Cade wanted to scream.

“The ritual nears completion,” Bernard continued. “Creation dies. The Throne rises. And your efforts amount to nothing more than a footnote in the new history I shall write.”

Golden light flared at the edge of Cade’s vision. Chubbs’ small body arched in agony as more divine essence poured from her into the shackles. Her eyes found his across the chaos, and something passed between them—recognition, resolve, and heartbreaking regret.

“C-Cade,” she called, her voice deeper, older than it should have been. “I’m s-sorry.”

The apology struck deeper than Bernard’s lance ever could. What did she have to be sorry for? He was the one who had lied to her, hidden her true nature, and brought her into this nightmare.

Bernard laughed, the sound echoing unnaturally through the dead cathedral. “Touching. The divine apologizes to the mortal before they both cease to exist.”

Something changed in Chubbs’ face then—a flash of determination replacing despair. Her small hands, still bound by the shackles, opened and closed as if grasping for something. Golden energy sparked from her fingertips, forming tendrils that stretched across the distance between them.

Bernard’s amusement vanished. “What is she—”

The golden tendrils reached Cade before Bernard could interfere, sinking deep into his wound. For a heartbeat, blessed warmth spread through his dying body—a promise of healing, of life renewed.

Then came the rejection.

His Destruction core reacted violently to Creation’s essence, like opposing magnets forced together. Cade’s back arched off the ground as the two cosmic forces gouged deep trenches through what remained of his body. The emerald lance pulsed brighter, and the diameter of the wretched weapon expanded from where it was lodged in his chest.

It was feeding off of his arcane discord, Cade faintly realized. But there was nothing he could do about it. It felt like a sick joke. He’d been given some of Chubbs’ final vestiges of power to heal, but his own body rejected the help. Then, in that rejection, the very thing killing him was thriving.

Bernard watched with clinical fascination as Cade convulsed. “Fascinating,” he murmured. “Even in death, polar opposites cannot reconcile.”

The pain was indescribable—worse than dying, worse than anything Cade had experienced. Every magical pathway in his body burned as Creation’s essence battled his Destruction core. He was being torn apart from the inside, unmade at the most fundamental level.

This is it, he thought distantly. This is how I die. Not by Bernard’s hand, but by trying to be saved.

In an act of self-preservation, he retreated into his mind, using a facsimile of his meditative technique to numb the all-encompassing pain. It worked. His senses grew dull and distant. His consciousness dove deeper and deeper, past the writhing core and into that place between his oldest memories and most profound imaginings. And it was like a storm brewed far above where he hid.

But down here, he was alone from the pain.

He was safe.

There, in the darkness, something stirred. He whirled to face it, indignation on his lips as he was ready to shout at whatever or whoever attempted to interrupt this moment of blissful relief.

Red energy danced on an unfelt breeze. It lit up his darkness, warm and inviting in its wild dance. It shot past him, racing to meet the twin storms of energy that permeated his core.

Curious, and feeling a bit fatalistic, Cade followed it up, though he remained as close to the numbing darkness as he could manage while still observing what his Chaos magic was about to attempt.

It flooded through the colliding forces. But, to Cade’s shock, it didn’t resist Creation’s healing attempt or support Destruction’s rejection—it moved between them, a mediator amid ancient enemies.

What are you doing? Cade thought as he observed the Chaos magic continue its dance.

Where golden light and crimson fire collided, threads of something new emerged—neither Creation nor Destruction, but a synthesis. A bridge.

Cade seized this moment of clarity, forcing what remained of his consciousness into the maelstrom. Creation’s essence couldn’t heal him directly—that much was clear—but with Chaos as intermediary, perhaps he could redirect it.

Shape it. Transform it.

With agonizing focus, he used his Chaos energy to guide Creation’s power. It resisted, thrashing against the restraint for several excruciating moments before he released his pain-induced vise grip. Creation eased, settling once again into a loose semblance ofto a kaleidoscopic river. He switched tactics, using his crimson threads to weave a funnel for the magic. It flowed across the magical canal with what he could only describe as giddiness.

Grunting at the effort, he wove the river toward his wound. He could sense the potential of this gifted magic. It wasn’t Life. It would not heal him, so he didn’t pretend it could. Instead, he gave it one command:

“Repair.”

Then, before it could respond, he used his Destruction core to make a gauntlet around his right hand. It was a messy construction, but it had to do. Then, cursing at the pain and the one who dealt it, he roared in his mind as he clenched down on the lance’s expanded haft. It jolted and shifted in his chest. A cry threatened to escape from his blistered lips.

He took in a sharp breath and yanked down, shattering the tip of the lance.

Darkness circled his vision as his blood seeped across his fingers. He wasn’t done. There was one final step, and it would take everything in him to accomplish it.

Please. Let this work.

He used both of his shaking hands to shove off over the stone pillar he was skewered to. The spear’s shaft ripped against his flesh, and he briefly blacked out from the pain. But when he came to, his hair was rippling through the air as his face collided with the rubble-strewn floor.

“Do it,” he commanded Creation’s power with his final ounce of willpower.

And there, on the bloodstained floor of a forsaken cathedral, Cade refused to die.

Metallic filaments spread through his gaping wound, weaving through tissue and bone in an intricate latticework. Where emerald energy had burned away his chest, golden-red metal formed in its place, fusing with his remaining flesh in a synthesis of organic and inorganic materials.

The broken emerald lance dissolved.

Bernard stepped back, his confidence faltering for the first time. “What is this?”

Blinding pain gave way to strange warmth as the transformation completed. Cade gasped, drawing a full breath for the first time since Bernard’s attack.

“Impossible,” Bernard whispered, genuine fear flickering across his corrupted features.

Cade pushed himself up onto his elbows, marveling at the metal-organic fusion where his wound had been. The skin around the metallic structure was raw and red, but he was alive. Creation’s power, guided by Chaos, had rebuilt him.

“Turns out,” he coughed, fresh strength flowing through his transformed body, “I’m full of impossible things.”

Bernard’s surprise hardened to fury. With a snarl, he raised his hand, emerald energy coalescing for another attack. “This changes nothing. You merely prolong your suffering.”

The ritual circle beneath them pulsed, and Cade could sense that it was nearing its completion. The Oakthorn pushed further through the fissure, branches unfurling in the cathedral’s dusty air. They were running out of time.

Cade glanced toward Chubbs. The effort of saving him had nearly extinguished her. Her divine light flickered dangerously. He studied her young face—now oddly aged beyond its years—ashen with exhaustion. The shackles continued to drain what little essence she had left.

“Please,” she whispered, her eyes locked on his. “Help me.”

Cade struggled to his feet, legs trembling with the effort. Sure, he was healed, but that didn’t restore his overworked core back to full. It was refilling with energy, but he was nowhere near his full capacity.

His newly forged chest ached with each movement, but the pain was distant now, secondary. All that mattered was reaching Chubbs before it was too late.

Bernard moved to intercept him, his corrupted face twisted with malice. “I’ve toyed with you long enough, thief.”

“If that was you toying with me, I’d really hate for you to take me seriously. Please. Keep toying,” Cade muttered with a tired half-grin.

Emerald energy gathered between Bernard’s palms, forming a sphere of corrupted power large enough to obliterate Cade completely this time—no chance of survival, modified chest or not.

Cade braced himself, knowing he had no strength left to dodge. He called upon his own magic, but only a trickle responded. Tiny red sparks flickered across his palm.

“Sorry, just some performance issues,” Cade informed the corrupted Progenitor.

“It’s time to end this farce,” Bernard declared, raising the sphere for a killing blow.

A thunderous crack split the air as a portion of the cathedral ceiling collapsed. Bernard hesitated, his attention momentarily diverted by the new threat.

That moment was all it took.

A blur of motion streaked through the cathedral, so fast even Cade’s gold-rank vision struggled to track it. One moment Bernard stood before him, sphere raised. The next, a drenched figure collided with the corrupted man with enough force to send him staggering.

His adhesive-gloved hands connected with the crown, sticking instantly to the ancient metal. Bernard roared in outrage, emerald energy exploding outward in a desperate attempt to protect his power source.

The corrupted man shot his sphere of emerald energy at the redhead, but Jer was too quick. With a triumphant yell, he ripped the crown free from Bernard’s head in a single, fluid motion. The corrupted man crashed to his knees with a howl of rage and pain, his connection to the Progenitor severed in an instant.

“Sorry, buddy,” Jer quipped, flipping over Bernard’s head with acrobatic grace that defied the cathedral’s oppressive magical atmosphere. “But that hat? Definitely not your style.”

Jer materialized from the blur, his enchanted boots still trailing afterimages as he skidded to a halt beside Cade. His normally cheerful face was set in grim determination, but his eyes widened at the sight of Cade’s transformed chest.

“What in the four hells happened to you?” he gasped.

“Jer!” Cade managed. “You—You’re not dead!”

“I knew I could count on you for stating the obvious,” Jer said with a wink.

A shadow dropped from the newly created opening in the ceiling. Orro landed in a crouch beside Chubbs’ altar, his orange blade glowing with intensified brilliance. Without hesitation, he brought the weapon down on her shackles.

The blade sliced through the magical restraints like they were paper, but the contact triggered an immediate reaction. The ritual’s drain seized on the blade’s power, pulling at whatever energy source fueled it. The orange glow flickered and dimmed as Orro staggered, visibly weakened by the sudden drain.

Despite this, he caught Chubbs as she collapsed forward, cradling her small form with unexpected gentleness. “I’ve got you, little one,” he murmured.

Hope surged through Cade.

Against all odds, they’d done it. Chubbs was free. Bernard was separated from the crown. Perhaps they could still—

The cathedral floor buckled violently beneath them, the stone cracking in elaborate patterns that mirrored the ritual circle. Bernard convulsed on the floor, his blighted features shifting rapidly between human and something ancient, terrible. Blood streamed from his eyes and mouth, but despite his obvious agony, he laughed.

“Your attempts to stop me are once again meaningless, you despicable mortals,,” he gasped, pointing toward the fissure. “The Throne... rises...”

The fissure widened with a thunderous roar, revealing not darkness but blinding light. The Oakthorn pushed through completely, its massive roots spreading beneath the ancient stones in a network that encompassed the entire cathedral floor.

As the tree emerged fully, the fissure sealed behind it with a final, catastrophic crack. The portal to the spirit realm closed, cutting off any chance of banishing the tree back to whatever dimension had contained it.

The Oakthorn Throne stood manifested in all its terrible glory, its bark gleaming with all seven dominion energies woven together in impossible harmony. Power radiated from it in waves that made the air shimmer and distort, bending reality around its presence.

But something about it felt off to Cade. He couldn’t quite put his finger on why exactly. Perhaps it was that it was attuned to Bernard and not a proper god, like Life’s Oakthorn Throne had been.

No, he decided. Something is different from that Throne.

Bernard’s laughter grew stronger as he pushed himself to his knees, his human form stabilizing despite the crown’s removal. The ritual’s completion seemed to have granted him some measure of the power he sought, even without the Progenitor’s direct influence.

“I’ve won,” he declared, blood-flecked spittle flying from his lips as his gaze locked on Cade. “For all your clever alterations, for all your friends’ heroic efforts—I’ve won.”

The cathedral trembled as the Oakthorn’s power continued to grow, feeding on the ambient energy of the ritual circle. Cade looked from Bernard to the tree, then to Chubbs in Orro’s arms. They’d saved her, yes—but at what cost? The Throne had risen anyway. Bernard, or Mordred, had achieved his goal.

What had Death said? “Once it fully emerges, Bernard will ascend.”

As if in answer to his thoughts, Bernard rose slowly to his feet, emerald light dancing across his skin like lightning. His wounds closed before their eyes, his form strengthening with each passing second as he drew power from the manifested Throne.

“Now,” he said, voice resonating with new authority, “shall we begin again?”
