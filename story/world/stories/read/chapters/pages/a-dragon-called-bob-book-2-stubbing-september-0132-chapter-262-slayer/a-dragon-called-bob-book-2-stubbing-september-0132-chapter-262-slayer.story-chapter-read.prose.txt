Finding an elf in the forest was like trying to find something worth watching on TV.  They were few and far between.  The local fauna wasn’t making things any easier, either.  The big cat with six legs was watching me from a branch four metres up a tree, amber eyes staring at me unblinkingly.

“Shoo!  Psst!”

It yawned in response, showing me an impressive array of dental work, then resumed idly eyeing me up.  I let the bag on my back fall to the grass and stepped closer to the big, hairy bastard.

Said pack had contained one change of clothes, a small paring knife, a lump of rock, a loop of grey metal, and a large pot.  No weapons, no tent, no blankets, basically, it was bugger all use, and I was starting to regret the whole no magic thing.  There were beds, duvets, blankets and pillows tucked away in my pocket dimension, and if I had to rough it as a human, I ought to at least have the option to be comfortable.

“Either go away or come on, this isn’t mandatory suicide,” I snapped, opening my arms wide.  Sending mixed signals was no way to communicate, but I neither wanted to kill it nor cared if I had to.

It rose languorously and stretched.  Two of its pairs of legs were located close together at its rear, and the more central of the legs had six-inch-long claws that dug furrows into the treebark as it moved along the thick branch towards me.

“Bob the Bumpkin has got the attention of a gutslasher, one of the less deadly species on the island!  Can he survive with his intestines on the inside, or will they be spilled out across the dirt, leaving him to die slowly as the feline eats him alive?”

“You really are a prick,” I muttered, keeping my attention on the gutslasher.  On the plus side, the soon-to-be-dead announcer had just told me how the thing liked to attack, which was useful to know.  It looked strong, fully grown and worryingly experienced.  Not being able to get its level and stats was rather frustrating, but I assumed I’d outclass it.  Puny mammals.  With a mental shrug, I leapt up at it, grabbing the branch it was walking on and heaving myself around to kick it from its perch, hoping to scare it off.

I missed.  Like a regular cat suddenly noticing a cucumber next to it, the monster had hurled itself upward to evade my attack, and it was now rapidly returning with outstretched claws thanks to my old friend gravity, looking to do unto me as Wolverine does unto his enemies.

A judo chop with my left hand snapped the branch on the treeside, and I began to fall as well, maintaining the distance between us.  A brief look of confusion crossed its snarling face, but as the ground caught me, the moment of worry was replaced with a furious intent to eat me.

That really wasn’t on at all.  I ate, I didn’t get eaten, and anything foolish enough not to understand that on first glance was welcome to commit suicide by dragon.  Besides, as it zoomed closer to my face, I couldn’t help but notice how thick and luxuriant the thing's fur was.

Forward-down-down/forward-forward-press-A.  I rose like an angel of death.  Resisting the urge to bellow the no doubt trademarked battle cry from Street Fighter took all my self-control.  But I managed it.  Those amber eyes crossed briefly as it frantically attempted to discover the power of flight and avoid the punch.

My fist intersected the gutslasher's snout, and then it was raining blood.  I landed in a world painted bloody and stepped smartly to one side as the body hit the floor.  The creature spasmed briefly, but there was no way it was getting up with its head splattered over a nine-foot wide area.  A rumble came from its guts, and faeces spewed out with a loud farting noise.

Gutslasher level 64 slain!

Gold earned… Nothing!

400.2kg of biomass added to storage.

“There was no call for chemical warfare.”  Putting some distance between myself and the monster.  I was starting to feel ever so slightly full.  After the incredibly spicy meal that the Dreadchef had force-fed me, coupled with what I’d just received, I was back up to…

Biomass Stored:  1704.3 KG

Hamster Cheeks was putting in the work.  I’d wanted to explode at around the one K mark before.  It felt as though I could squeeze in another five hundred kilos or so before I’d need to get some evolutions to free up space.  Setting aside the thought that I probably should have cleared out the reserves before setting off on this jaunt, I scaled the tree like a monkey.

Fond memories of climbing that trunk against Geeku in the Tree Tests to win the tribes allegiance passed through my mind for a moment, then the trunk started getting dangerously slim.  It flexed and wobbled under my weight.  This tree wasn’t bloody tall enough.  I jumped down, falling forty feet to land next to the body.

“I’m fairly certain that was an illegal move…” muttered the announcer.

“You’ve left your mic on again, dittohead.”  I knelt and rolled the fallen monster over, carefully pulling it away from its terminal bowel movement.

“My what?  Ah crap.”  The voice cut off, and I was left in peace, for now.

“Time for a post mortem.  I kind of hope you weren’t the end of your bloodline.”  Enough jokes, Bob, no one on this world will have ever heard those songs.  Fetching my paring knife, I began to carefully skin the beast, silently complaining about not being able to just shapeshift a claw and do the job in half the time, or being able to fly up over the trees and use my thermal vision like the cops tracking an escaped prisoner to find the rest of my prey.

A normal man would have needed to string the three-hundred-pound carcass up on ropes to do a clean job, but my strength stat made it easy to manhandle the body.  Surprise, surprise, skinning an animal was a skill, one that I did not possess, and those quadruple back legs were a bastard to work around.  I got the front end done, then took a break.

There had been a stream a few minutes back up the animal trail I’d been following.  After washing my face and hands, I took my pan and filled it with water.  It looked clear and clean… I doubted any kind of bacteria or amoeba existed that could survive the Scoville furnace that was still radiating warmth from my stomach.  Besides, I ate people whole, so I could handle a little dodgy water.

“Oi!  Get off that, you little shits!”  A swarm of monkeys looked up from pulling at the meat on the gutslasher's shoulders and charged at me as I got back to my kill.  With one measly breath attack, I could flambe the lot of them, but being a bloody undercover dragon took that choice off the table.  Right then, I was a dragon of peace.

The first one to leap at me opened its strangely insectile mandibles and reached out to give me what I doubted would be a loving and tender embrace, so I slapped it aside with Bad-Dog-No.  The body slammed into a tree and fell broken onto the exposed roots.  Oops, I hadn’t meant to kill the thing.

Forest Child level 31 slain!

Gold earned… Nothing!

75.9kg of biomass added to storage.

The rest scattered, a hideous, clicking screech echoing out from them as they seemed to mourn the loss of their comrade.  Ah, well, truth be told, that was my food, and I didn’t feel like having dinner guests.

Naturally, having recently learned a spell to clean my clothes with magic, I wasn’t able to use it.  I stripped off my tunic and tossed it on the grass, away from the crimson pizza that remained of the gutslasher’s cranium.

In the end, I just gave up on trying to get the last pair of legs skinned properly.  I cut the skin off and turned it over in my hands.  Gross.  Bits of muscle and strands of something tough and sinuous clung to it.  With a sigh, I picked up the knife once more and began scraping all the gore off while resolving to wear the thing skin-side-out.

The sun was starting to set when I was finished.  Breaking the branch up into foot-long chunks was pretty straightforward.  I propped it up over a nearby rock and punched the upraised section to break it off.

Fire had been mankind’s friend since the dawn of time, and it came naturally to dragons as well.  Unfortunately, my usual method of starting a fire, a one-nostril snot rocket of orange-green flame, was out of the question.  It was time to rub some sticks together.

“Bonkers Bob the Bumbkin is too thick to use the flint and steel!  What an absolute dunce!”

“Thanks for the help,” I growled, fetching the previously confusing lump of rock and loop of metal.  I stared at them for a moment.  Old YouTube videos ran through my mind, watched while taking one of my many lengthy dumps at work, and I struck the rock with the steel.

It took four tries before I was throwing fat sparks, but the logs wouldn’t catch, no matter how gently I blew on them once they landed.  A pile of woodchips from my log chopping, mixed with some dry leaves I found under a bush, were brought together, and finally the spark caught on something and a tiny flame flickered into life.

I placed a series of small twigs over it, trying to nurse it from something that—shit.  A gust of wind had blown it out.

Darkness had fully fallen before I got the fire going properly.  The wood burned badly, thick smoke billowing out, but it burned.  Score one for Bob, woodsdragon extraordinaire.

Slices of meat went onto sticks and were propped up over the flames to cook.  As the first started to blacken, I pulled it out and nibbled at it.   I needed to survive until tomorrow night when Gorboon would join me.  That would be a blessing and a curse.

Even with his extensive and brutal training under Salnia, he wouldn’t last five minutes on his own.  He’d be gutslasher shit before he’d walked fifty feet in these woods, so I’d have to babysit the chef.  Pulling open the pouch Esme had given me, I added salt and pepper to the meat and took another bite.

Still tasted like shit.  Chewy and tough.  Slow cooking might make it palatable, maybe give it a suitable mouth feel.  I shuddered at the thought of the words, but the leather-like snack did make me reassess the pretentiousness of chefs, at least a little.  I put the pan on the embers and waited for it to start to steam.

Maintaining the facade of efldom was going to be a chore.  I might not care about boiling water before I drank it, but a normal elf probably would.  I took a long drink once it had cooled a little, the warm liquid merging with the burning sensation in my stomach and making me belch.  I scrubbed my tunic off as best I could.  I’d opted for dark colours to blend in with the woodland, but I should have just picked a nice crimson top.  It wouldn’t show the blood.

Settling down with my back against the trunk of the tree the gutslasher had called home, with my new furcoat wrapped around my shoulders, I let my eyelids droop.  I’d need to be up early and on the hunt.  If I could hunt down the rest of them before lunchtime, I could wrap this whole thing up, do the whole ta-da-I’m-a-dragon join my empire bit, and be sleeping on my shines tomorrow night.

I raised one eyelid.  That chittering noise was getting closer.  And louder.  I sat upright and realised it was coming from every direction.
