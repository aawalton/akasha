The heavy, steel-tipped bodkin tore through the freezing winter air, a silent streak of absolute, lethal intent.

Time seemed to entirely dilate for Perberos. The elven scout remained perfectly still high in the dead branches of the ancient oak tree, his left arm locked and his silver eyes tracking the impossible trajectory of his shot. He watched the arrow cross the vast, corrupted expanse of the valley. It bypassed the swirling snow, cut cleanly through the thick plumes of foul-smelling smoke rising from the burning pitch trenches, and threaded the needle between the massive, scuttling Crypt Weavers guarding the rear lines.

The spinning vortex of dark shadow magic wrapped tightly around the steel shaft acted like a highly concentrated, magical drill. It did not bleed excess energy into the air. It remained perfectly compressed, focused entirely on the singular point of impact.

Down in the valley, the towering Bone General had just turned its massive, horned skull to look towards the eastern ridge. The creature was entirely unaware of the lethal projectile racing towards it. Its runic armour, capable of deflecting ballista bolts and absorbing high-tier elemental strikes, remained completely dormant and relaxed.

The arrow struck the boss monster directly in the centre of its right eye socket.

The impact was flawless. The heavy steel bodkin met the dormant, invisible magical barrier protecting the creature’s skull. The sickly green runes etched into the ancient metal flared violently, desperately attempting to snap shut and harden against the sudden kinetic force.

But Perberos’s Shadow-Strike enchantment was designed specifically for this exact microscopic fraction of a second. The spinning dark mana violently chewed through the rising barrier, shattering the magical ward like brittle glass. The heavy steel shaft punched cleanly through the shattered shield and buried itself deep into the glowing green soul-fire burning within the General's skull.

A split second later, a massive, concussive impact echoed across the rear lines.

A heavy, oil-soaked crossbow bolt, as thick as a man's wrist and fired from Garth's massive heavy crossbow on the adjacent ridge, slammed with terrifying velocity into the centre of the General’s breastplate. The incredible kinetic force cratered the ancient, blackened metal inward, severely cracking the fused ribs hidden beneath the armour.

Almost simultaneously, a third projectile arrived. A standard steel-tipped arrow, loosed by Silas, struck the monster directly in the exposed, unarmoured joint of its heavy right shoulder guard, burying itself deep into the necrotic connective tissue.

The towering monstrosity did not instantly disintegrate like the lesser dungeon beasts. It was a high-tier boss, possessing a vast, humming reservoir of dark magic. But the perfectly coordinated, compounding kinetic shock of the crossfire was utterly devastating.

The sickly green light bleeding from the General's armour flickered wildly, violently strobing between blinding brilliance and total darkness. The massive creature staggered backward, the heavy iron broadsword slipping from its grasp to crash heavily against the frozen mud. The skeletal warhorse beneath it shrieked and buckled.

The Bone General dropped heavily onto one iron-plated knee, its massive hands coming up to claw frantically at the steel bodkin buried deep within its skull.

Then, the monster screamed.

It was not a vocal sound. The General possessed no lungs or vocal cords. It was a massive, psychic shockwave of tearing, unravelling necrotic magic. The scream radiated outward from the creature’s fracturing core in a violent, invisible dome of pure sonic agony. It ripped through the corrupted valley, flattening the dead grass, shattering the freezing fog, and carrying a terrifying chill.

Standing in the churned, blood-slicked mud directly in front of the sealed iron portcullis, Josh was entirely unaware of the miracle unfolding in the distant rear lines.

The human Guardian was fighting a desperate, doomed, and suffocating battle for his life.

The phalanx of Wight knights had closed the distance. There were no more polite, honourable duels. Dozens of the elite, blackened steel warriors pressed inward simultaneously, forming a tight, claustrophobic ring of raised greatswords and heavy tower shields around the human and the dwarf.

They did not fight with the mindless, shrieking frenzy of the lesser ghouls. The Wights moved with the terrifying, silent precision of a highly disciplined military unit. They stepped in perfect unison, their heavy boots floating just above the mud, their strikes coordinated to overwhelm their targets through sheer, calculated volume.

Josh swung his electrified longsword in a brutal, sweeping horizontal arc. The crackling blue blade struck the heavy tower shield of an advancing Wight, sending a massive shower of blinding sparks and raw voltage into the freezing air. The impact forced the creature to take a half-step backward, but three more instantly stepped into the gap, thrusting their heavy greatswords forward in a synchronised, lethal lunge.

Josh brought his dark heater shield up, angling the metal to deflect the punishing blows. The kinetic force was absolutely staggering. The ringing of steel against steel was deafening.

But it was the cold that was truly killing them.

The Wights radiated a severe, spectral frostbite. Every single time their weapons clashed with Josh’s shield, a violent wave of necrotic cold travelled straight up his arm. It bypassed the thick padding of his gambeson entirely, sinking directly into his bones. His joints were stiffening, his muscles locking up in protest. The freezing sweat on his face was literally crystallising into thin sheets of ice against his skin.

"Left flank, Bhel!" Josh roared, his voice cracking from the freezing air tearing at his throat.

Bhel was fighting like a cornered, rabid badger. The dwarven fighter was a spinning blur of iron and fury. He ducked beneath a sweeping decapitation strike, rolling tightly across the freezing cobblestones. He surged upward, driving his heavy left axe violently into the exposed knee joint of a Wight, cleanly severing the leg. As the knight collapsed, Bhel brought his right axe around in a crushing overhead blow, caving in the dark helmet and extinguishing the blue soul-fire within.

"I have them!" Bhel bellowed back, his breathing reduced to a harsh, wet wheeze. The pristine white bandages wrapping his broad chest were now completely soaked in a terrifying mixture of his own bright red blood and the black, foul-smelling sludge of the battlefield.

At their feet lay a crude, mounting pile of destroyed blackened armour and golden dust. They had managed to permanently end eight of the elite knights, fighting with a level of perfect, unyielding synchronicity born of countless dungeon runs.

But they were fundamentally breaking.

Josh’s stamina core was practically empty. The blinding blue lightning wrapping his sword flickered erratically, the high-voltage static failing to maintain its lethal intensity. The silver runes inlaid on his heavy shield glowed faintly, overwhelmed by the continuous, crushing impacts.

A massive Wight, wielding a rusted heavy mace instead of a greatsword, stepped smoothly past the ruined body of its fallen comrade. It did not swing at Josh’s shield. It feinted low, forcing Josh to drop his guard, and then brutally bashed the heavy iron rim of its own tower shield directly into Josh’s faceplate.

The concussive shock rattled Josh’s brain against his skull. His vision swam with dark, pulsating spots. He stumbled backward, his heavy steel boots slipping on the frozen, glassed mud of the crater.

Instantly, a second Wight exploited the momentary opening.

The knight stepped forward with terrifying speed, thrusting its heavy, dark steel greatsword in a punishing, upward lunge. Josh desperately twisted his torso, attempting to parry the blade with his failing longsword, but he was a fraction of a second too slow.

The jagged, freezing steel of the greatsword bit savagely into Josh’s left side. It slid cleanly into the narrow, vulnerable gap where his heavy breastplate met the overlapping metal faulds protecting his waist.

The pain was absolute, blinding agony. It felt as though a jagged shard of solid ice had been driven directly into his internal organs. The necrotic magic coating the blade instantly flooded his veins, attempting to freeze his blood solid.

Josh let out a deep, ragged roar of pure pain. He did not pull away from the blade. Driven by pure, uncompromising survival instinct, he stepped forcefully forward, driving himself deeper onto the greatsword to trap the Wight's weapon. He raised his heavy right arm and drove the heavy steel pommel of his longsword brutally down into the top of the Wight's helmet, violently crushing the skull beneath and dropping the creature into the mud.

Josh ripped himself free of the falling blade, hot, bright crimson blood pouring freely down the side of his dark plate armour, steaming instantly in the freezing air.

He dropped heavily onto his right knee, his dark shield clattering against the cobblestones. The strength completely left his legs. His breathing was shallow and rapid. He tasted copper and ash in the back of his throat. He looked up, his storm-grey eyes dimming, and saw the impenetrable wall of remaining Wights closing the final few feet.

There were still over two dozen of them. They stepped over the ruined bodies of their comrades, their icy blue eyes locking onto the kneeling human.

Bhel saw Josh fall. The dwarven fighter let out a deafening, heartbroken roar. He completely abandoned his own defensive positioning, throwing himself wildly in front of the human Guardian.

Bhel stood over Josh, crossing his heavy iron axes in front of his bloody chest. He was panting heavily, his face pale and drawn, his arms trembling violently from the sheer, crushing weight of the prolonged melee. He looked at the advancing wall of dark steel, a fierce, unyielding defiance burning in his dark eyes. He was fully prepared to die swinging, to buy his friend exactly one more second of life.

The largest of the remaining Wight champions stepped forward. It was a massive, towering entity wearing a rusted silver crown upon its helm. It raised its heavy greatsword high into the freezing air, the dark metal humming with a lethal, freezing energy. It prepared to bring the blade down in a devastating, executing strike that would effortlessly cleave through the exhausted dwarf and the kneeling human behind him.

Josh tried desperately to raise his shield, to trigger his Guardian aura one final time, but his magical core was entirely dry, and his arm refused to obey his commands. He looked at Bhel’s broad back, a profound, heavy sorrow settling into his chest.

They had fought well. They had held the gates. But this was the end of the line.

The Wight champion began the downward arc of its executing swing.

Then, the psychic scream hit them.

The shockwave of unravelling necrotic magic tore across the cratered clearing, entirely silent but physically devastating. It struck Josh like a violent blow to the temple, forcing him to drop his sword and clutch his heavy steel helmet with both gauntlets, his vision completely blurring. Bhel staggered backward, dropping one of his axes to press a thick hand against his ears, groaning in pain.

But the effect on the undead was apocalyptic.

The massive greatsword descending towards Bhel’s neck froze a mere inch away from the dwarf’s helmet.

The entire phalanx of Wights shuddered violently. The icy blue soul-fire burning within their dark visors flickered erratically, strobing as the dark magical tether commanding them fractured. Their perfect, terrifying mechanical discipline started to dissolve.

The towering champion slowly lowered its greatsword. It did not look at the bleeding prey lying defenceless at its feet. It turned its massive, horned helmet away from the city gates, looking northwards towards the distant valley.

In perfect, sudden unison, the remaining two dozen Wight knights turned their backs on Josh and Bhel. They lowered their heavy tower shields. They did not run, but they broke into a rapid, highly urgent forced march, moving with desperate speed back towards the northern horizon.

Beyond the Wights, the massive, sprawling ocean of lesser infantry was thrown into absolute chaos.

The writhing, shrieking tide of zombies and ghouls that had been relentlessly pounding against the timber walls suddenly lost their singular, focused drive. Without the direct, commanding intelligence of the Bone General forcing them forward, the ambient dark magic animating them reverted to basic, primal instincts.

Thousands of the rotting monsters stumbled to a halt, screeching confusedly at the pale morning sky. They began to pull back from the intense heat of the smouldering pitch trenches and the continuous, deadly rain of arrows falling from the ramparts. They did not retreat entirely, but they milled about aimlessly, pulling a few hundred yards back into the open plains, forming a massive, disorganised, and passive swarm.

The grinding, deafening noise of the meat grinder simply ceased.

The sudden, ringing silence that descended upon the battlefield was profound and entirely disorienting. The only sounds remaining were the mournful howling of the winter wind and the crackling of the burning alchemical fires.

Josh remained on one knee in the freezing mud, his left hand pressed tightly against the deep, bleeding wound in his side. He was breathing heavily, his chest heaving as he stared blankly at the retreating backs of the Wight elite guard.

Bhel slowly lowered his axe. The dwarven fighter stood perfectly still, his thick chest rising and falling with ragged gasps. He looked at the retreating knights, then at the massive, milling sea of confused zombies standing passively in the distance.

Bhel wiped a thick smear of dark sludge and his own blood from his face. He turned to look down at the kneeling human, his dark eyes wide with absolute, profound bewilderment.

"What in the deep mountain do you suppose that means, lad?" Bhel asked, his gravelly voice a harsh, breathless whisper in the sudden quiet.

Josh slowly pulled his gauntlet away from his side, inspecting the bright crimson blood staining the dark steel. He felt entirely hollowed out, entirely spent, but miraculously, incredibly alive.

He slowly shook his heavy steel greathelm, a ragged, exhausted laugh escaping his lips.

"I have absolutely no idea, Bhel," Josh replied, his voice rough and incredibly weak. "But I am not going to complain about the timing."

Josh grabbed the rim of his heavy dark shield, using it as a crude crutch to push himself painfully up from the freezing mud. His legs trembled violently, threatening to give out beneath his immense weight, but he forced his boots to lock, standing upright amidst the crude, smoking pile of shattered bones and blackened armour they had created.

He slowly turned around, looking back at the towering, solid timber walls of Ashenfall rising thirty feet above them.

The wooden ramparts were lined with hundreds of terrified, exhausted defenders. The militiamen, the veteran adventurers, and the city guards were all leaning over the parapets, staring down in absolute, stunned disbelief at the two heavily armoured fighters standing alone in the clearing, and the retreating army beyond them.

Josh raised his head, searching the crowded walkway until he found a familiar flash of brilliant, pale green and white.

Carcan was leaning dangerously far over the wooden railing, her amber staff clutched tightly in her hands. Her silver eyes were wide and shining with overwhelming, profound relief, her face pale as a ghost as she looked down at the blood soaking Josh's armour. Beside her, Brett was staring at the retreating Wights, his jaw literally hanging open in sheer shock.

Josh let out a long, shuddering breath, offering them a small, weary salute with his blood-stained gauntlet. He looked up at the defenders, his voice carrying the final, absolute exhaustion of a man who had completely emptied his soul upon the battlefield.

"Can someone please," Josh shouted hoarsely up at the wall, "send down a bloody rope?"
