Sakura did not open her eyes right away. It was slow and heavy, as if she were dragging herself out of some thick, alien darkness, and for a few seconds she simply lay on the cold ground without moving, because her body still did not fully understand whether it was alive or not. Above her hung a low, dense sky, wrong gray, nothing like a city sky or an ordinary sky before a storm. It pressed down on everything, as if the light here had been born sick and weak. Wind blew against her face.

“What...”

Sakura pushed herself up on her elbows, and she could have sworn she heard whispering in those gusts. Not just the rustle of leaves, not the usual whistling through cracks, but actual whispering, distant and many-voiced, as if someone were trying to speak to her from the forest with a hundred mouths at once, but the words still would not come together into anything she could understand. Sakura drew a slow breath and shivered at once. The damp, cold air smelled of wet earth, ash, and old stone, with something metallic underneath. It must have rained here not long ago.

She sat up sharply, and only then did she see where she was: all around her stretched ruins of black stone, smooth and gleaming, broken into sharp edges in places, as if a giant fortress had once been carved out of solid obsidian and then shattered and left to die in the middle of the forest. It was an impressive place, one that stirred an uneasy sense of grandeur.

Sakura did not rise at once. First she got onto her knees, then to her feet, and the world swayed immediately, but did not disappear. Her heart pounded loudly in her ears, and her fingers trembled. Her mind had not fully returned to her body yet, but fear was already beginning to seep into it.

“Oh gods.”

A heavy pain throbbed in her head, and through it, slowly and unwillingly, the only memory she had came back, so vivid that everything else crumbled to dust against it. The subway. The crush of bodies. The red emergency lights. Her throat torn raw by smoke. The heat with nowhere to escape it.

She had died there.

Sakura knew it as surely as she felt the cold beneath her feet through the thin soles of her shoes, and that certainty made her feel sicker than the weakness did. She remembered death, remembered the air vanishing, remembered darkness entering her body, and after that there was nothing. Inside her there was only her name, as if it had been nailed somewhere deep inside and could never be torn loose, and the knowledge of exactly how she had died. Sakura. Everything else was emptiness.

She took one uncertain step, then another, and stopped, staring at the remains of a black wall ahead of her, behind which even larger broken shapes rose like the teeth of a collapsed castle. The forest pressed almost right up against the ruins. The trunks of the trees were dark and tall, and the leaves, if they were leaves at all, carried the dull sheen of gray-green metal. The wind moved through them again, and the whispering grew louder. Sakura even thought she heard her own name, but then a pale glow flashed before her eyes, and she recoiled sharply, throwing up a hand. A translucent panel unfolded in the air directly in front of her.

SYSTEM INTEGRATION COMPLETE.Welcome to the world of Veyr.Region: Ash Border Zone.Threat Level: Medium.HOST IDENTIFICATION:Name: Sakura AraiRace: HumanLevel: 1Class: NoneTalent: UndeterminedChance of survival in the next 24 hours: 3.8%

Sakura opened her mouth, then shut it at once. What even was this? How was she supposed to understand it? She took a step back, but the System messages followed her, refusing to disappear.

“No, no. I can’t die again,” she blurted out. And then the realization hit: again... She had died. And now she was supposed to die a second time? In this strange, incomprehensible world.

Sakura blinked, and the notification changed.

PHYSICAL ATTRIBUTES:Strength: 3Endurance: 3Agility: 4Constitution: 3Perception: 5Overall Condition: WeakenedSystem Recommendation: Survive

The panel vanished as suddenly as it had appeared, and for several more seconds Sakura stared at the empty air, as if waiting for the text to return and explain why the world was called Veyr, and who or what had brought her here. Her name was Sakura Arai. So that name had not been some random flicker in her mind. She had no class. No talent. Almost no chance of lasting even a single day. She slowly lowered her hand and only then realized how hard she had been clenching her fingers, hard enough for her nails to dig into her palm. All of it sounded like the kind of delusion a fevered mind would produce, but the cold wind touching her skin was real, the black stones were real, the weakness in her legs was real. Everything around her was real.

A quiet, low growl came from somewhere ahead, beyond the collapsed wall, and her attempt to accept this new reality ended at once. Sakura froze. The sound was heavy, with a vibration that sent goosebumps racing across her skin. Then a second growl came, closer this time, and a black, glossy shape moved between the broken stones. Sakura did not think, she just darted sideways and crouched behind an overturned slab of the same obsidian stone, instinctively pressing herself against it as if a thin piece of ruin could save her from anything. From there she had a narrow view through the rubble. Beyond it lay a wide ruined courtyard, once probably serving as the outer grounds of the castle, now buried under cracked columns, black grit, and dead flowers that had grown right through the stone. The first creature stepped out of the forest, and for a moment Sakura forgot how to breathe.

It moved on four limbs, but calling it a beast was almost impossible. Its body looked as if it had been made from uneven plates of obsidian fused into a rough, ugly shape, each muscle seeming carved from black glass, while its joints glowed from within with a deep red light, as if heat were smoldering inside the stone. Its head was low and stretched out, with a torn-looking maw full of long teeth, and when the creature turned its neck, Sakura saw sharp growths jutting along its spine like spikes.

Behind the first monster, a second one emerged from behind a ruined arch, even worse than the first. This one was taller, with long forelimbs and a sunken chest where a red light pulsed between ribs of black mineral. Its face was not wolf-like, not cat-like, not really animal at all, but something assembled from mismatched parts that should never have fit together, and when it opened its mouth, glowing mineral veins flashed inside.

The two creatures saw each other at once, paused for one tight, tense second, then lunged forward so fast Sakura barely had time to understand that the fight had already started. In her head she was praying to every god she knew that the monsters would not notice her, would not smell her. The System had made her chances very clear: 3.8%. A catastrophically small chance.

The monsters did not circle each other or try to intimidate each other the way normal predators would. They rushed in to kill. The first one slammed sideways into the second with a crack, sending black shards spraying across the ground. The second instantly clamped its jaws around its enemy’s neck and jerked so hard that half the obsidian spikes snapped off and flew aside. The first let out a howl and smashed a forelimb into the other’s head, cracking part of its face open. The red light beneath the black armor flared brighter and brighter. They tore at each other without pause. They rolled across the stones, crashed into columns, smashed rubble, and bit into joints and bellies. Pressed against the slab, Sakura realized with horror that this was not a fight over territory and not some random clash. Here, existence itself demanded that one monster destroy the other and take everything inside it.

At last, the taller creature threw its opponent onto its back and sank its teeth into its chest. The red light inside the broken stone body flared and poured out in a thick crimson glow. The victor drew it in greedily. It lasted only a few seconds, but right in front of Sakura’s eyes, the monster began to grow. Its torso swelled, its shoulders rose, the spikes along its back stretched longer, its limbs thickened, and its maw opened wider. It truly became almost twice as large, and far more horrifying.

Sakura clenched her fists even harder, not even feeling how badly she was scratching her own skin.

The obsidian monster threw back its head and let out a roar that made the air between the walls shudder. Now it towered over the ruins like a living siege engine, its black body full of crimson heat, cracks running through its armor with red light leaking from them, and Sakura did not even try to think about escape in that moment. She only stared, unable to believe something like that could exist so close to her. Then the creature cut its roar off sharply, went still, and slowly turned its head in her direction. Sakura felt it before she fully understood it. The monster’s gaze found her through a narrow gap in the rubble, and everything inside her went cold and hollow. Only a moment later did she see the two dark red eyes fixed directly on the place where she was hiding.

The monster had noticed her.
