Ysara stared at him, her expression a mixture of hope and uncertainty. His final words from their last conversation, "I’m going to expect the contrary from you", had echoed in her mind. For the first time, someone of immense power had not only acknowledged her compassion for the slaves but seemed to encourage it. It was a dizzying thought, a spark of hope in the suffocating darkness of her life. But Arthur saw the flicker of vulnerability in her eyes and immediately suppressed the part of him that wanted to trust it. That part, the one that remembered the kindness of two slaves in a mud hut, was a liability, a ghost from a life that had ended in betrayal. He couldn't afford to trust anyone, not yet. His smile vanished, replaced by the cool, unreadable mask of a strategist.

"I have plans," Arthur said, his voice pulling back from the brink of warmth, becoming once again the flat, pragmatic tone of a commander. "Plans that involve the people you care so much about. I will need your help with some of them."

The shift was jarring. Ysara's expression tightened. His tone was dismissive, clinical, speaking of the slaves as if they were pieces on a game board, not people. She hated it, but she had learned long ago to mask her dissent. She inclined her head, a gesture of regal acceptance. "As you command."

"And Yudursh?" Arthur asked, his focus shifting. "What is his opinion on the matter?"

"He is loyal to me, but... he does not share my feelings on certain matters," she said carefully. "He is a half-born. He sees the world through a different lens."

"A half-born?" Arthur asked, seizing the thread of new information.

"A child born from the union of a noble and a slave," Ysara explained, a hint of bitterness in her voice. "They exist in a world between worlds. Some hold minor positions in the kingdom, but they are never truly accepted. They are treated as bastards, a constant reminder of a noble's indiscretion."

Arthur filed the new information away, another piece in the complex puzzle that was Yudursh.

Eager to move on from the sensitive topic, Ysara changed the subject. "Where are we?" she asked, her gaze sweeping the filthy room. "And the Great Hunt?"

"The Hunt ended a couple of days ago," Arthur said, intentionally omitting the most important detail. "We're in a boarding house in the city of Copperpine."

A flicker of alarm crossed her face. "The Hunt is over? Then my father... he will be looking for me." The thought of the king's resources being mobilized sent a chill down her spine. Her disappearance would no longer be a minor issue for a search party; it would be a matter of state.

Arthur asked, "Is the palace here? In Copperpine?"

"No," she said, shaking her head. "The palace is in the capital city, Valoria. It's... far from here."

"And how were you planning to get back?" Arthur pressed. "Before I... interfered."

"We would have gone to the Mayor of Copperpine," she explained. "He is a vassal of my father. He would have arranged for a portal mage to transport us directly to the palace."

"Is that route still an option for you?"

"Yes," she affirmed. "The Mayor is loyal. He would help me."

Arthur fell silent, his mind racing. He could let her go, but he would lose his most valuable asset. The alternative was to go with her, a staggering risk that placed him directly in the heart of the kingdom's power. It was a gamble of the highest order, but the potential rewards of access, intelligence, and proximity to power were too great to ignore. He had spent his life in the shadows, a spy who thrived on calculated risks. This was no different.

He met her worried gaze, his decision made. But before he revealed his plan, he needed to control the variables. He turned his attention to the ring. With a silent command, Yudursh materialized beside her.

The stoic warrior was on his feet instantly, his hand flying to the hilt of a sword that wasn't there. His eyes darted around the room, taking in the filthy conditions before landing on the princess. Seeing she was unharmed, he visibly relaxed, his posture shifting into a protective stance behind her.

"Yudursh," Arthur said calmly, his voice even. "The Great Hunt is over. We are in the city of Copperpine. You are safe." He gestured to the remaining bowl of stew on the mattress. "Eat. You'll need your strength."

Yudursh looked from Arthur to the princess, who gave him a slight, reassuring nod. He hesitated for a moment before taking the bowl and eating with the efficient, no-nonsense movements of a soldier. Arthur waited patiently, letting the silence stretch, giving the warrior time to process the situation.

Only when Yudursh had finished the last of the stew did Arthur speak again. His tone shifted, losing its casualness and taking on the hard edge of a command. "Now that you're both here, this is the plan. I will be accompanying you to the palace."

Yudursh’s head snapped up. "Sir, I must object," he said, his voice a low rumble of disapproval. "That is not a sound tactical decision."

"And why is that?" Arthur asked, his tone neutral.

"The Great Hunt has just concluded," Yudursh explained, his logic sharp and clear. "The only new name of any note on the rosters was 'Arthur.' The princess returning from the forest with an unknown man of your skill... conclusions will be drawn. Dangerous conclusions. My oath is to protect the princess, and by extension, you. I cannot fulfill that oath if your identity is compromised the moment we step foot in the capital. I must respectfully request that you do not accompany us."

"He's right," Ysara added, her voice laced with worry. "My father's court is a viper's nest. They will see you as a threat, or a pawn. It's too dangerous."

Their concerns were valid, but he already had a solution. A faint smile touched his lips. "You need not worry about my identity," he said calmly.

He closed his eyes and pushed a sliver of mana into the Mask of Infinite Faces. The change was subtle at first, a ripple across his skin. His cheekbones sharpened, his jawline softened, his hair seemed to darken and lengthen, falling across his brow in a way that was not his own. When he opened his eyes, the face that stared back at them was no longer Arthur's. It was the lean, roguish face of a man they both knew.

Ysara gasped, her hand flying to her mouth. Yudursh's eyes went wide, his stoic composure shattering for a brief, unguarded moment.

"Kaelin?" Ysara whispered, the name a ghost on her lips.

"How?" Yudursh breathed, staring at the perfect replica of their fallen teammate.

Arthur, now wearing the face of Kaelin, gave them a wry, unfamiliar smile. "How is not important," he said, his voice still his own. "What's important is that you tell me everything there is to know about Kaelin. And about the city we're about to walk into."
