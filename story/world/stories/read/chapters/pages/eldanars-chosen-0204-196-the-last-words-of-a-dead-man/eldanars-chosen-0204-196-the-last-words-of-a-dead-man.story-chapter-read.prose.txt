The five minutes of allotted rest felt like five agonizingly short seconds.

Josh leaned heavily against the damp, rotting trunk of a dead oak tree, taking slow, deliberate breaths. The freezing air stung his lungs, but it helped clear the lingering haze of battle from his mind. He carefully adjusted the leather straps holding his breastplate together, wincing as the rough material rubbed against his skin.

A few feet away, Bhel was aggressively dragging a whetstone down the edge of his right axe. The dwarf had completely ignored the mud and the foul-smelling gore coating his armour, focusing on restoring the lethal bite to his iron weapons. The rhythmic, metallic scraping sound was oddly comforting in the dead silence of the clearing.

Brett was sitting on a relatively dry rock, looking thoroughly miserable. The mage had pulled his wax-sealed jar of peppermint balm from his satchel and was applying a thick layer directly under his nose. He offered the small jar to Carcan, who accepted it with a tired, grateful smile.

"The dark mana required to sustain a creature of that size is terrifying," Brett noted quietly, looking at the massive, settling pile of golden dust that had once been the Crypt Weaver. "If the dungeon is actively projecting that much power outside of its own portal boundaries, the local environment is going to completely collapse."

"We just have to collapse the horde first," Josh stated, his voice firm, pushing himself away from the tree. "Perberos, mark the clearing. We need to keep pushing our grid sector northward. The sun will be setting in a few hours."

The elven ranger nodded silently. He retrieved a spool of bright crimson fabric from his belt, tied a long strip around a prominent branch, and drifted back into the shadows of the treeline to take the lead position.

The party reformed their tight defensive structure and marched out of the clearing, leaving the golden dust and the rotting wolf corpses behind them.

The atmosphere in the forest had changed. The death of the Crypt Weaver seemed to have triggered a ripple effect through the immediate area. The oppressive, heavy silence was broken by the distant, restless sounds of movement. Twigs snapped in the gloom, and the unnatural, shuffling footsteps of the dead echoed faintly from the surrounding ridges.

They had not walked for more than twenty minutes before they encountered their next obstacle.

The muddy path narrowed significantly as it passed through a dense thicket of dead, thorny briars. Standing shoulder-to-shoulder in the choke point, perfectly still and completely silent, were four skeletal archers. They wore rusted chainmail and carried ancient, decaying yew bows. Their empty eye sockets glowed with a faint, malevolent green light.

They did not roar or charge. The moment the party stepped into view, the four skeletons raised their bows with terrifying, mechanical synchronicity and loosed a volley of rusted, iron-tipped arrows.

"Shields!" Josh commanded, stepping instantly in front of Brett and Carcan.

He raised his heavy steel shield, tucking his chin tightly behind the rim. Three of the rusted arrows slammed into the metal with a sharp, ringing clatter, shattering upon impact. The fourth arrow buried itself deep into the thick, boiled leather of Bhel’s shoulder pad, failing to penetrate the dwarf's dense muscle beneath.

Bhel simply grunted, reached up, and snapped the wooden shaft off with his thick fingers.

"My turn," Perberos whispered from the shadows of the flank.

The elven ranger did not step into the open. He fired two arrows in incredibly rapid succession through a narrow gap in the dead branches. The heavy steel broadheads struck the two central skeletal archers directly in their glowing green eye sockets. The arrows shattered their fragile, ancient skulls, instantly breaking the necrotic magic holding them together. They collapsed into piles of loose bone and immediately dissolved into shimmering golden dust.

Josh did not wait for the remaining two skeletons to nock another volley. He triggered his Juggernaut's Tread skill. Blue lightning sparked from his heavy boots as he ignored the thick, clinging mud entirely, closing the distance with unstoppable momentum. He slammed his shield into the third archer, crushing its ribcage into powder, while his electrified longsword sheared the final skeleton cleanly in half.

The brief, brutal skirmish was over in seconds, leaving two fresh piles of golden dust in the mud.

"Dungeon spawn," Brett observed, stepping carefully through the thorny thicket. "They are deploying ranged units to hold narrow corridors. That indicates a level of tactical coordination far beyond mindless hunger."

"It means they are getting ready for a war," Bhel grunted, kicking a rusted iron arrowhead out of his path.

They pushed deeper into their designated grid sector, tying crimson ribbons every fifty yards. The encounters became more frequent, but the party operated with a flawless, unspoken synergy that made the combat feel almost routine.

They cleared a pack of six corrupted, rotting boars that charged blindly from a dry riverbed. Josh acted as the immovable anchor, absorbing their frantic charges with Titan's Anchor, while Bhel and Brett systematically butchered and burned the native beasts, leaving behind a horrific mess of charred meat.

Half a mile later, they intercepted a roving group of Crypt Ghouls attempting to herd a pair of massive, zombified forest bears towards the main road. Brett utilised his Focus Mind skill, sacrificing his peripheral vision to unleash a devastating, highly compressed torrent of white-hot plasma that vaporised the dungeon ghouls into golden dust, completely severing the magical control over the bears. Without the guiding intelligence of the ghouls, the native bears simply stood roaring in confusion until Perberos and Bhel methodically put them down.

It was exhausting, repetitive work. They were covered in mud, ash, and foul-smelling blood. Every step required physical effort, and the constant ambient necrotic energy was slowly draining their stamina.

As they crested a steep, heavily wooded ridge near the middle of the afternoon, the cloying stench of decay suddenly changed.

Perberos stopped dead in his tracks, his delicate nostrils flaring slightly. He raised a hand to halt the party, his silver eyes scanning the dark, tangled roots of a massive, dead oak tree situated at the bottom of the slope.

"Blood," Perberos murmured, his voice incredibly low. "Fresh, human blood. Mixed heavily with the rot."

Josh instantly tightened his grip on his sword, stepping forward to peer down the ridge. "An ambush?"

"No," Perberos replied, slowly lowering his bow. He pointed towards the massive oak tree. "A survivor."

Slumped against the thick, grey roots of the dead tree, half-hidden by a patch of withered ferns, was a human figure.

Josh did not hesitate. He scrambled down the muddy slope, his heavy armour clanking loudly, with Carcan rushing closely behind him.

As they reached the base of the tree, the true, horrific reality of the situation became clear. The figure was a man, likely in his late twenties, wearing the shredded, blood-soaked remnants of high-quality leather scout armour. A tarnished silver badge depicting a soaring griffon was pinned to his chest.

He was in terrible shape. His right leg was twisted at a sickening, unnatural angle, the bone visibly protruding through his dark trousers. His left arm ended in a ragged, hastily bandaged stump just below the elbow. The makeshift tourniquet was soaked through with a mixture of dark red blood and a thick, foul-smelling black sludge.

But it was his skin that made Josh's stomach twist into a cold knot. The man's face was deathly pale, covered in a sheen of freezing sweat, and a chaotic network of pitch-black veins spider-webbed violently up his neck and across his jawline.

He was breathing in shallow, ragged gasps, his eyes closed.

"Light preserve us," Carcan gasped, immediately dropping to her knees in the freezing mud beside the man.

She did not bother with diagnostic questions. She slammed the base of her staff into the dirt, her hands glowing with a brilliant, intense golden light. She pressed her palms directly over the horrific wound on his arm, humming her resonant healing melody with desperate urgency.

The man jolted awake with a weak, agonised cry. His eyes snapped open. They were entirely bloodshot, the whites tinged with a sickly, creeping yellow. He looked wildly at Carcan, then up at Josh's towering, armoured form.

"No... stop," the scout coughed, his voice a wet, rattling wheeze. He weakly raised his remaining hand, trying to push Carcan away. "You are... wasting your mana, Sister."

"Be still," Carcan commanded softly, her brow furrowed in intense concentration. "I can stabilise the bleeding. I can knit the bone."

"It is not... the bleeding," the man gasped, his head rolling back against the roots.

Josh watched in quiet horror as Carcan’s holy magic met the wound. The brilliant golden light washed over the torn flesh, attempting to seal the vessels and promote cellular growth. But the black sludge coating the stump reacted violently. It hissed and smoked, aggressively pushing back against the healing magic. The dark, necrotic veins on the man's neck pulsed angrily, spreading an inch higher towards his eyes.

Carcan gasped, recoiling slightly as a sudden, sharp shock of dark mana travelled up her arms. The golden light faded from her hands.

"The necrotic saturation is absolute," Brett stated grimly, stepping down the ridge to stand beside Josh. The mage looked down at the dying scout with profound, clinical sorrow. "The corruption has seeped entirely into his bloodstream. It is causing a systemic failure of his biological processes. Healing magic will only accelerate the conflict within his body."

"He speaks... the truth," the scout whispered, his breathing growing incredibly shallow. He looked at Josh, his bloodshot eyes struggling to focus on the heavy steel breastplate. "You are... high tier. I can feel the mana density on you. You are clearing the woods."

"We are," Josh confirmed softly, kneeling in the mud beside Carcan. He took the scout's trembling, freezing hand in his own heavy leather gauntlet. "My name is Josh. We are from Ashenfall. What is your name, friend?"

"Fenn," the scout replied, a weak, bloody smile touching his cracked lips. "Silver Griffons party. We were... we were monitoring the Crypt perimeter when it broke."

"Torin told us several parties were lost," Josh said, his voice thick with empathy. "You fought well, Fenn. You survived."

"I ran," Fenn corrected him bitterly, a tear cutting a clean track through the grime on his cheek. "My party held a choke point. They bought me time to run and deliver a message to the Guild. But the beasts... they hunt in packs. They chased me for days through this rot. They finally caught me yesterday morning. Took my arm. I managed to crawl under these roots and hide."

Fenn gripped Josh’s gauntlet with sudden, surprising strength. The desperation in his eyes burned fiercely, overriding the immense pain of his dying body.

"You have to listen to me, Josh," Fenn demanded, his voice dropping to a frantic, rattling hiss. "The Guild... they do not understand the scale of it. They think the dungeon simply overflowed. They think the doors broke and the monsters just spilled out randomly into the woods."

"Is that not what happened?" Bhel asked, stepping closer, his thick brow furrowing.

"No," Fenn coughed, a thick splatter of black blood coating his chin. Carcan gently wiped it away with a clean cloth, her hands trembling. "I saw it. I saw deep into the mountain pass before I ran. It is not an overflow. It is like an assembly line."

Josh felt a cold chill run down his spine. "What do you mean, an assembly line?"

"The dungeon core," Fenn wheezed, his eyes wide with remembered terror. "It is pumping them out with terrifying purpose. But they are not just wandering aimlessly out of the caves. They are stopping. They are forming ranks in the mountain basin."

"Forming ranks?" Brett repeated, his sharp mind immediately calculating the horrific tactical implications. "Mindless undead do not form organised military units unless they are being actively directed by a higher intelligence."

"Exactly," Fenn nodded weakly, his breath coming in shorter, more desperate gasps. The black veins on his neck were visibly creeping higher, reaching his cheekbones. "There is a commander. I saw him from the ridge. A massive figure. He wore black iron armour that absorbed the light, and he carried a greatsword the size of a man. He is a General of Bone. He is actively organising the dungeon spawn and the corrupted native beasts into a unified, massive army."

The revelation hung heavily in the freezing air of the clearing. They were not just fighting a chaotic swarm of hungry monsters. They were facing an organised, directed military force with a singular, horrific purpose.

"Why haven't they marched on the city yet?" Perberos asked quietly, his analytical mind searching for the tactical flaw. "If they have the numbers, why wait in the mountains?"

"They are waiting for the critical mass," Fenn whispered, his grip on Josh’s hand beginning to weaken. "The General is waiting for the corruption to fully saturate these woods. Once the trees are dead and the ambient dark mana is thick enough to shield them... they will march. And they will not stop until Ashenfall is a graveyard."

Fenn let out a long, shuddering groan, his back arching slightly against the tree roots. The yellow tinge in his eyes began to shift, clouding over with a milky, vacant white. A faint, sickly green light flickered deep within his pupils.

"Carcan," Josh said urgently, looking at the healer.

Carcan shook her head, tears spilling openly down her pale cheeks. "I cannot stop it, Josh. The system is failing him. The necrotic mana is completely overriding his nervous system. He is turning."

Fenn heard her. A look of absolute, profound terror broke through his pain. He looked at Josh, his fingers digging desperately into the leather of Josh’s gauntlet.

"Please," Fenn begged, his voice dropping to a terrified, weeping whisper. His body gave a violent, unnatural jerk, his broken leg scraping loudly against the mud. "Please, Josh. I have seen what happens to them. The native corpses. They trap the soul inside the rotting meat. They make you a puppet."

Fenn squeezed Josh’s hand one last time. "Do not let me wake up in the dark. Do not let me hurt anyone. Please."

The heavy, crushing burden of leadership settled squarely onto Josh’s broad shoulders. There was no glory in this moment. There was no epic battle, no cheering crowds, and no system notifications rewarding his bravery. There was only the grim, horrific reality of the frontier, and the final, desperate request of a dying man.

Josh looked at Carcan. The elven healer was weeping silently, her hands covering her mouth.

"Turn away, Carcan," Josh ordered softly, his voice thick with unshed emotion.

Carcan did not argue. She stood up, her robes dragging in the mud, and turned her back to the dead oak tree, burying her face against her brother's shoulder. Perberos held her tightly, his silver eyes completely stoic, understanding the grim necessity of the act.

Bhel and Brett remained perfectly still, offering silent, respectful witness to the tragedy unfolding before them.

Josh looked back down at Fenn. The scout's body was convulsing violently now. The milky white cloud had almost completely consumed his eyes, and a low, unnatural growl was beginning to build deep within his ruined throat.

The man named Fenn was gone. The monster was waking up.

Josh did not draw his longsword. It was too long, too unwieldy for this intimate, horrific task.

He reached down to his hip and smoothly drew his heavy steel short sword. The blade caught the pale afternoon light, gleaming clean and sharp.

"You fought well, Fenn," Josh whispered to the empty, twisting shell of the man. "May the Light grant you peace."

Josh did not hesitate. He did not close his eyes. He owed the man the dignity of seeing it through. He placed the tip of the short sword directly over Fenn’s heart, angled slightly upward beneath the ruined ribcage.

With a single, powerful, and entirely merciful thrust, Josh drove the blade deep into the chest cavity, severing the heart and destroying the central nervous system instantly.

His body went completely rigid for a fraction of a second, his eyes flashing brightly one last time before extinguishing entirely. The unnatural tension left the muscles, and the corpse slumped heavily into the mud, a permanent, physical shell. Because Fenn was a native of this world, and not a product of the dungeon system, he did not dissolve into golden dust. He simply remained a dead man in the dirt.

Josh pulled the blade free with a wet sound. He knelt in the freezing mud for a long, silent moment, the heavy steel sword resting across his thigh. His hands were shaking, not from adrenaline, but from the profound, sickening weight of taking a human life, even one given in absolute mercy.

Bhel stepped forward, reaching down to place a massive, comforting hand on Josh’s armoured shoulder. The dwarf gave a firm, reassuring squeeze.

"It was a good death, lad," Bhel rumbled quietly, his voice carrying the weight of centuries of underground warfare. "A clean end. You saved his soul from a terrible slavery. Do not let the guilt rot in your own heart."

Josh took a slow, deep breath, pulling the freezing air into his lungs to steady his nerves. He stood up, retrieving a clean rag from his pouch to methodically wipe the dark blood from his short sword before sheathing it with a heavy click.

"We need to bury him," Josh stated, his voice completely devoid of emotion, a solid, unbreakable wall of determination taking its place. "We cannot leave him to be scavenged."

They used their weapons and hands to carve a shallow grave beneath the sprawling roots of the dead oak tree. It was difficult, exhausting work in the frozen mud, but they did not stop until Fenn’s body was respectfully covered and heavy stones were piled on top to deter the wolves. Carcan stood over the makeshift grave, humming a quiet, beautiful prayer for the safe passage of his soul.

When the brief ceremony was finished, Josh turned to face his party. The sun was beginning to dip significantly lower, casting long, dark shadows across the corrupted forest floor.

The busy work was truly over. The reality of their situation had been laid bare by the dying scout. They were not just culling a chaotic overflow of beasts. They were walking directly into the teeth of an organised, assembling army of the dead, commanded by a powerful entity intent on wiping Ashenfall from the map.

"We have our warning," Josh said, his storm-grey eyes hardening with absolute, uncompromising resolve. He looked at Brett, Bhel, Perberos, and Carcan, drawing strength from their presence. "They are massing forces in the basin. If we wait for them to march, the city will fall."

"Then we do not wait," Bhel growled, hoisting his axes.

"We break the grid," Josh ordered, his voice ringing with absolute certainty. "We stop sweeping the woods for stragglers. We march directly for the Crypt, and we cut the head off the snake before it can strike."

The party offered no arguments. They simply tightened their gear, their faces set in grim determination. They turned their backs on the shallow grave and marched deeper into the rotting darkness.
