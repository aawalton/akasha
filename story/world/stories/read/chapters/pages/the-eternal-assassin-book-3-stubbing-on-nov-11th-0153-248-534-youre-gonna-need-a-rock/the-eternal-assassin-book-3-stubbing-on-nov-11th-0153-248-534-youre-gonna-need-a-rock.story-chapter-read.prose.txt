Having picked out their next target, Asher was feeling pretty good. Getting the chance to take out two of the remaining twelve liches at once was merely icing on the cake, and he was excited to head on over to the Deadlands and get started. Though the very next day, when he was invited to chat with Donvath over breakfast, the High Prince revealed two glaring flaws in their plan.

“Do you have any idea how long of a journey it is from here to the Deadlands?” Donvath asked, shaking his head after Asher told him what they were intending to do. “If you travel via caravan, the trip would take you about two months! And that’s if you took a dedicated one that didn’t make many stops along the way!”

“Is it really that far?” Asher asked, frowning as he realized he’d never really stopped to consider how large their continent was. “What about via flight? Like if we hired a pegasus carriage?”

“Faster, no question there. But it would still take some time. Your two to three-month journey would be reduced to about a week and a half, maybe two. Pegasi are fast, but they’re not known for their endurance. They need to rest every couple of hours. Because of that, it would actually be faster to take a griffin. A little slower than a pegasus, but capable of staying in the air for far longer, so it more than balances out.”

“Good to know,” Asher said, opening his mouth for his next question. Donvath clearly knew what he was about to say, however, because he frowned and cut him off.

“No, you can’t take a griffin to fly you to the Deadlands! Those are bred specifically for my truthseekers, and unless you’ve forgotten, they answer directly to me. If I offer you any direct help against the Council of Death…”

“They launch a full-scale attack on the Noala Kingdom, I remember,” Asher finished. “Out of curiosity, do you know how many of the liches I need to kill before the threat of them attacking the kingdom isn’t actually much of a threat? I was sort of hoping once I whittled them down to only say, five or six of their original number, you’d finally be willing to step in and help finish off the rest.”

“It depends on precisely which of the liches you choose to go after,” Donvath said, closing his eyes as he rubbed his temples. “But yes, I can assure you that once you weaken the Council of Death as a whole, you’ll have my aid and the full might of the truthseekers working behind you for the remainder. Presumably along with a significant amount of aid from some of the other countries on our continent as well.”

“Thank you,” Asher said, giving the High Prince a concerned look. “Are you still struggling to see the future critical points? You seemed a bit alarmed the other day when I came back and let you know we’d gone after Zygrul and killed Hylm.”

“That’s the scary thing,” Donvath sighed, opening his eyes once more and giving him a weary grin. “I don’t understand what Zygrul is doing, but I’m pretty certain it’s using its Void element to hide from even my skills somehow. I’ve never experienced anything like this before. There are certainly plenty of skills and artifacts capable of hiding people from the Timeline, but those take the place of large gaps. Missing chunks that, while I can’t see what they’ll do, are readily apparent. You and your team’s whole mission against Zygrul… It just wasn’t a possibility at all. I didn’t have the faintest idea that was going to happen, and that scares me beyond anything you could possibly imagine.”

“So the Void element is strong enough to hide from even your Time skills,” Asher muttered, realizing something. “Out of curiosity, you said I was also tricky to track via the timeline, right?”

“Yes. Greater demons, wizards, and you are the three banes of my existence,” Donvath confirmed, scowling at him. “I’m pretty certain it’s either because all three of you have access to the realm of ages, or some crazy tritessence shenanigans I don’t fully understand.”

“But I can’t control my tritessence like wizards can, and I can’t access the realm of ages,” Asher pointed out. Though I will soon have a fancy Void dagger in my back pocket that might work to help conceal me from such abilities.

“You think my Time element came with a manual?” Donvath asked, throwing up his hands. “Regardless of the why, that’s how it works. Getting back to the matter at hand, you need a method of actually getting to the Deadlands, right?”

“Something faster than two weeks, if possible,” Asher confirmed.

“Then you really only have two options. Both of them only available because of your unique skill set. Either have a wizard teleport you there, or hire a pegasus courier.”

“Wait, teleportation is an option?” Asher asked, thinking back to his first time ever meeting Richard. “Richard complained about the tritessence cost of teleporting a few of us just from the outskirts of the capital to the palace. I figured moving someone across the entire continent would be far too draining.”

“Moving someone, yes. But not something,” Donvath corrected as he held up a finger. “Just slap an Eternal Mark on a rock and have Richard teleport that instead. It will probably still take every drop of tritessence he has, but it would at least get you close.”

“That’s a good idea,” Asher admitted, wondering if it was worth burning his blanket favor Richard owed him quite so soon. “What about the pegasus courier? I thought you said pegasi wouldn’t be all that fast.”

“That’s a pegasus dragging a carriage behind it,” Donvath explained. “Pegasus couriers are nobles who have skillsets devoted to shooting around the kingdom and lands beyond at top speeds, delivering small packages or important missives. They are designed for this sort of thing, and one of them could probably make it from here to the Deadlands in only four or five days. Same deal, slap an Eternal Mark on a rock and have them deliver it for you. It won’t be cheap, but last I heard, you were sort of rich. Realms, you even already know Theorn, and he’s one of our best. Seeing as she’s alright again, I bet he’d even be willing to look past how you tried to kill his aunt.”

“Let’s maybe not bother Theorn just yet,” Asher said, his face heating up slightly as he remembered how he’d used Theorn to infiltrate his family’s estate in his hunt for Eight’s head. “But thank you, I’ll figure out which of the two methods works better and go from there.”

“Great! Which means now would probably be the right time to tell you going out into the Deadlands in the first place is a waste of time,” Donvath said, grinning at him as he sipped from a cup of morning tea.

“What do you mean?” Asher sighed. He’d forgotten how annoying the High Prince of the kingdom could be at times.

“Do you really think that lich would have managed to survive all these centuries if getting into the Deadlands was really that simple?” Donvath asked, raising an eyebrow. “An evolved death resistance will only get you so far, and the Deadlands covers dozens of miles. I had no idea a lich was responsible for the phenomena, but I remember reading up about that particular oddity in the environment when I was younger. Plenty of missions have been sent into the Deadlands with the intention of clearing them once and for all, and not a single one ever made it even close to the center. Even if you can get past all the random undead monstrosities wandering about, the Death Aura is simply too powerful.”

“You don’t think my Complete Resilience would be enough?”

“Not after the first couple of miles, no,” Donvath admitted. “It’s not like we’ve run extensive tests or anything, but I’m pretty certain your Complete Resilience functions similar to an evolved resistance, or even a bit stronger. Just for basically everything. Then again, speaking of your weird skill-set…”

Donvath put down his tea, staring at Asher as if deep in thought. “…Actually, I think there is a way you can get to the center. And a pretty simple one at that. Even if you can only withstand the Death Aura for a bit at a time, you have the unique ability of being able to Recall back to safety whenever it starts getting too much for you, and the ability to repair the damage it caused easily enough. Then, once you’ve recovered, you can Recall right back to the spot you left. Man, instant, long-range teleportation opens up so many options to you.”

“Actually, if we’re going that route, what if we just had Samantha fly over the Deadlands, and drop a rock I’ve marked straight down into the center?” Asher asked. “Wouldn’t that save us the whole hassle of making our way through it on the ground?”

“Would you believe you’re not the first person to have that idea?” Donvath laughed. “Not about the whole rock bit, of course, but air-dropping into the middle. The biggest issue is that the Death Aura extends in all directions, and that includes straight up. You have to be able to fly dozens of miles up, which is no easy feat to begin with. Not to mention the air isn’t breathable up there. Of course, it was still attempted a few centuries back by a single brave soul with both the Wing and Air elements. Flew up and over the Deadlands, with the plan to then dive straight down, aiming for the middle.”

“So what happened?”

“Presumably? He died before even getting close,” Donvath shrugged. “Seeing as he was never heard from again and the Deadlands didn’t change in the slightest, that was what most people concluded. Though it was probably the Death Aura that did him in on the way down, so your rock plan would probably work in that case.”

“Well, it’s nice to know we have options,” Asher said, getting to his feet. “I guess I’ll check in with Samantha and see how comfortable she feels flying up and over the Deadlands. Though if the Death Aura really grows that much more powerful the closer one gets to the center, I’m starting to think I might have to go this one alone. Either way, I appreciate all the advice.”

“Where do you think you’re going?” Donvath asked as Asher went to head out of the room. “Honestly, did you forget I was the one who asked you to come eat with me in the first place? You walked right in here and started asking questions. We haven’t even gotten to what I wanted to talk about!”

“Ah, right. Sorry,” Asher said, shooting Donvath a sheepish smile. “What’s up?”

“‘What’s up,’ he says,” Donvath muttered, shaking his head with a bark of laughter. “You’re the only person in the entire kingdom who doesn’t seem to have any respect for me as the High Prince, you know that?”

“To be fair, other than lending us a room in the palace, you really haven’t been all that helpful in taking down the Council of Death,” Asher pointed out.

“Hey now, I’m doing everything I can without drawing their ire!” Donvath declared, looking genuinely upset. “Do you have any idea the kind of fine line I’m balancing on here? It’s not easy being responsible for hundreds of thousands of souls! Most people can only guess as to what the future holds, but I can see what it does! What many of them do! And they are not pretty, to say the least.”

“Sorry, that wasn’t fair of me,” Asher said, immediately back peddling as he realized he’d struck a nerve. “What did you want to talk to me about?”

“The Eight Crests’ team of realm wanderers arrived yesterday, and they’re interested in offering you a position within them,” Donvath grumbled, still looking a bit annoyed. “The Noala Kingdom isn’t large enough to have our own team of dedicated realm wanderers, so I thought it was only fair to let you meet them and decide for yourself if you were interested in potentially working with them. I’m not so foolish as to think you’re going to stick around here forever, especially not after Samantha’s village burned down. They’re expecting you in an hour, out in the eastern training grounds of the palace.”

“Wait, can’t you see if and when I’m going to eventually leave the kingdom anyway?” Asher asked. “I thought that was the whole point of your Time element?”

“Zygrul going completely missing from the Timeline has shaken me a bit more than I’m letting on,” Donvath admitted, shivering at the thought. “Not to mention your own future has always been difficult to make out. I’m starting to realize predicting what you’ll do might just be more hassle than it’s really worth. Hopefully you’ll stick around long enough to finish off the Council of Death, and even the Assassin’s Guild, but after that…”

Donvath shrugged, raising his tea cup and nodding toward him. “Well, after that, I figure you’ll go find some other impossible enemy to take on and have fun killing yourself repeatedly against next.”
