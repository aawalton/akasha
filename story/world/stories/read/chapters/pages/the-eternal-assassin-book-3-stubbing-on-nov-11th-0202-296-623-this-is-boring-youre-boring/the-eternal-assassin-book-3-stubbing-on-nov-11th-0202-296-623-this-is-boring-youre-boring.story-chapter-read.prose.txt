Admittedly, Asher hadn’t been sure of what to expect from within the cave system that a millenia-old lich was hiding out in. Undead minions hiding behind every corner, waiting to jump out at them and eat their brains. Incredibly advanced magical traps scattered all over the place, detonating from simple proximity or creating thousands of other magical effects too varied to counter. Or maybe even a collapse of the cave entrance the moment they were in deep enough, preventing them from fleeing and forcing them to continue onward. Instead…

It was dark and damp.

“This is a lot more boring than I thought it would be,” Moxy grumbled, saying Asher’s own thoughts out loud as the five of them continued walking. They’d been walking for nearly twenty minutes now, and they’d yet to encounter anything more than the occasional dripping stalactite. Rulfar had thrown up a hand to halt them at one point, and Asher, Samantha, and Moxy had all tensed, prepared to strike at whatever the bloodhound had spotted. Right up until Rulfar pointed and they realized he had spotted a bat sleeping up against the roof of the cave and wanted the rest of them to move farther away so as not to wake it.

It turned out the giant northman had a bit of a soft spot for animals.

“Why waste the time and energy needed to set up traps when the odds of anyone ever finding you are pretty much zero?” Rulfar pointed out, pausing at another split in the cave as he looked between their options. Sniffing a few times, he shrugged before picking one seemingly at random and heading in. As they passed, Asher made a mark against the cave wall with chalk, indicating which direction they’d gone. “Still no scent for us to work with yet, but I’m hopeful. Even a single strand of hair or a drop of blood and we’ll be in business.”

“She’s an undead with a Flesh element, so no blood. And she has a Hair element, so presumably no hair either,” Asher pointed out. “What’s your back-up plan?”

“Wander the caves for the next few days until we get lucky or she attacks us,” Rulfar shrugged, taking another turn seemingly at random as Asher marked that one as well. “Remember, once we hit one of her minions, we might be able to use it to find her.”

There really wasn’t much more to say on the matter, so Asher let it drop and they continued wandering around. Every so often they’d hit an end to one of the tunnels and be forced to backtrack to the bend they’d taken where Asher would update their chalk marking and they’d take a different passage instead. One hour passed after another, until eventually, the Darkvision potions Samantha, Moxy, and Rulfar were using wore out and they needed to pause to drink new ones.

“Okay. I’m sort of pissed off that this lich seems to have figured out my one weakness,” Moxy admitted, frowning as she tossed Asher back the empty bottle to shove in his rift. “We’ve spent the last half a day walking up and down these tunnels, and we don’t have a damn thing to show for it! If I don’t get to punch something before long, I’m going to lose it!”

“If it helps, I highly doubt she’s targeting you specifically,” Rulfar chuckled. “This type of monotonous work isn’t at all unusual for a bloodhound. A lot of the job is meticulously searching for something to work with in the first place. Walking up and down the road hunting for a splotch of fallen blood to track down someone abducted by highwaymen, or combing through a meadow for a fallen object. You need to be patient.”

“I need to punch something,” Moxy grumbled as she grabbed the chalk from Asher’s hand and scribbled it along the wall before tossing it back. “You get until this second potion wears off. If we still haven’t found anything by then, just send me back to the royal palace and come get me when something exciting happens.”

Asher thought about arguing, before deciding against it and simply nodding. It was a good reminder that the five of them were all a part of this team for different reasons. Something he admittedly forgot about from time to time.

He and Samantha had the clearest motivations. The Council wanted to tear Asher’s soul apart, so regardless of what he wanted, he needed to take them down. Samantha’s home village had been razed to the ground around her and the two of them were sort of a package deal now, which meant she wanted to protect him and to get vengeance on the Council. And of course, Kroult’s reason for being there was just as simple. The lich wanted to die, and only Asher could help him with that. Not to mention the Council had trapped him underground for six hundred years, so he was quite willing to make that deal with Asher for his assistance.

Rulfar was a bit less clear. He’d clearly been horrified from witnessing Asher’s memory of Horntho Village burning down around him, but Asher couldn’t shake the feeling that the bloodhound had an ulterior motive for joining them. It wasn’t until after he’d explained how the liches wanted to hunt him down and rip the element of Eternity out of his soul that Rulfar finally agreed to join them. Asher didn’t think his unspoken motives were bad by any means, but something that was most likely connected with his past that he wasn’t willing to talk about. Regardless of how much they tried, the man refused to go into any detail about his past other than admitting he wasn’t from around here and had wandered all over the place.

And Moxy, unfortunately, was obviously just in it for the fighting. The woman lived and breathed combat. She didn’t care about the lives they were saving or the good taking down the Council would do everyone. She wanted to throw herself against powerful warriors and come out on top. She was similar to Asher in that regard, only she took his thirst for combat to what Asher considered was an unhealthy extreme. Their team ended up fighting stuff so frequently Moxy’s motivation was easy to forget, but now Asher found himself wishing they would stumble upon even just some random monster wandering the cave system in order to keep her pacified. The Lost Lady was an opponent who looked like she focused more so on speed and mobility as opposed to armor, which meant Moxy would be an excellent help in taking her down. The last thing he wanted was to send her home before the fighting even got started.

Yet as hour after hour passed and all they had to show for their hunt was a gradual depletion of their chalk as they kept marking the tunnels, Moxy continued to grow increasingly restless. It wasn’t until Asher thought she was about to snap and start trying to fight one of the nearby stalagmites that their quietest member stopped them with a raised hand.

“Something is not right,” Kroult said, causing everyone to blink and turn toward him as he stared at the wall of the cave. Kroult almost never spoke up on his own, so the fact that he was volunteering something meant it was probably important.

“How so not right?” Rulfar asked, his hands already gripping his large battle axe as he looked around.

“We have been here already,” Kroult said, frowning as he continued staring at the wall. “Someone… or something… is altering our chalk markings. I have been mentally keeping track of our number of turns to pass the time, and this does not match up with my own count.”

“Seriously? I’m almost more impressed you’ve managed to keep track of all the twists and turns we’ve taken than I am worried something’s been following us all this time,” Asher admitted, letting out a sigh of relief as he spotted Moxy’s agitated fidgeting quickly morph into her usual bouncing on the balls of her feet in anticipation. “Any idea how long they’ve been on our tail?”

“This is the first removed mark,” Kroult admitted. “The cave system is complex enough that I can only assume they wanted to allow it to do most of the confusing for them. They probably did not think we would notice a single removed mark, or that we would think we messed up and have to decide whether or not to restart our entire search efforts.”

“Sounds like The Lost Lady is a bit more hands on than we first thought,” Samantha said. “So, what’s the plan? Lay some sort of trap for them and jump them when they go to erase another mark?”

“It’ll be tricky. We’ve left hundreds of marks at this point, and they’ve only erased the one as far as we know,” Rulfar pointed out. “Kroult, is there any way you can make a guess as to the next mark they might go for?”

“This one was one of the ones leading down a particularly long stretch of tunnels,” Kroult said. “There are only two other stretches of equal length we’ve gone down thus far. If they have not already erased those two marks, I would assume that would be their next target.”

“Works for me,” Asher said, hesitating as he looked toward his team. “Though I’m stretched thin on Eternal Marks at the moment.”

“Remove the one on me and place it at one of the chalk marks,” Rulfar suggested, a split second before Moxy could by the annoyed look on her face. “Let’s face it. I can turn into a ten-foot-tall, half-ton, bear-human hybrid. I’m the least likely one of us to get snatched up.”

“Can’t argue with that,” Asher chuckled, motioning for Kroult to lead the way. “You guys wait here for a second. Maybe if we split up, The Lost Lady will make the mistake of trying to launch an attack on one of our groups.”

Thanks to Kroult’s impressive memory, the two of them managed to backtrack to the first mark before long. And just as Kroult had predicted, that one had been removed as well.

“I guess the best way to counter a lich’s way of thinking is with another lich,” Asher muttered as they started jogging over to the second one.

“I do not believe being a lich has anything to do with it,” Kroult admitted. “This is nothing more than simple logic. I would bet once they figure out we are on to their plan, they will step it up and try removing all of the marks in the hopes that we become lost and simply starve to death within the cave system."

“Unfortunately for them, we’re a little bit tougher to deal with than that,” Asher grinned. After his recent encounter with the King of Death, where he’d nearly fallen to the lich’s Death Aura after discovering he wasn’t able to Recall to safety, Asher had been keeping a more careful eye on his skills, ensuring they were still functional regardless of how deep they got within the cave. He hadn’t spotted anything like glowing gemstones or enchanted symbols to mark any sort of trap, but he wouldn’t put it past a lich to do something wild like hide such things inside the stalagmites or the like.

“The next mark is just up ahead,” Kroult said as they turned the corner, only to come to a dead stop at the sight of something other than dripping stone and sleeping bats at the end of the tunnel.

A naked man stood there, calmly wiping a bare hand against their chalk mark and wiping it clean. He stood nearly seven and a half feet tall and had a body that spoke of not just muscle from working in fields, but someone who clearly was taking either steroids or something even more powerful by the looks of things. The best way to describe him, in Asher’s opinion, was disgustingly ripped, with muscles that were practically bulging out of him like tumors all along his body. Though that wasn’t even the most surprising thing about the man.

Asher actually recognized him.

“Grant?” he called out, blinking in shock at the sight of the man from his recent vision. Despite the fact that he was much larger than he had been a few years ago when The Lost Lady had shrugged off the stabs from his trowel and kidnapped him, Grant’s face was still largely the same. It wasn’t until the man paused in what he was doing and turned to face them that Asher’s blood ran cold.

There were a series of glowing, pulsing runestones embedded all up and down his torso, their power clearly flowing into the man’s body in countless ways. The largest one was on a thick runestone located where his heart should have been. That on its own was freaky, but that wasn’t what gave Asher pause.

Grant’s aura was plain as day, taking the form of wandering tentacles of green grass that stretched out from his body, almost like they were both hunting for something to grab and reaching out in a desperate cry for help. Grant had definitely been turned into a monster… but he wasn’t an undead one.

He was still alive.
