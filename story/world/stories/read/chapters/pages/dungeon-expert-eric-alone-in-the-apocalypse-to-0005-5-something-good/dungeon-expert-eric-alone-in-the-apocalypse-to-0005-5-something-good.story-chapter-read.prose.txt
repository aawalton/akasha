The chest creaked open. Once again, nothing leaped out and no traps activated. Eric leaned over, tentatively peering inside. Armor! Another spell book! Armor! A spell!

What laid inside was neither. Instead, it looked for all the world like the kind of light-up children’s toy wand that his sister had beat him up with as a kid. A plastic stick, molded with twisting vines and unnecessary decoration, twined its way up to a blue, star-shaped translucent top piece, filled with glitter and LEDs. He picked it up delicately, a little uncertain, and pressed the button at the top. It lit up with blue, white and yellow lights and played a cheerful tune. A little rod inside the star stirred the glitter into a flurry.

Immediately, Eric shut it off again. He stared at it, at a complete loss for words. What the hell. What is this supposed to be?

As helpful as ever, the System offered him a tooltip. Magical Wand.

“Okay, but what does it do?” Eric emphasized. At the same time, a part of him cringed. Please tell me I don’t have to use this to cast spells. Please.

He had cast a spell moments ago without the wand, but in his panic with being confronted with the childish Magical Wand, he’d totally forgotten that. He stared at it for another second, then narrowed his eyes, really focusing on it. The System usually gave him more than just the name of items. If he calmed down for a moment, it should—

Magical Wand

What’s the difference between this and a Magic Wand? Only one of them can satisfy your girlfriend, and it’s not the one you’ve got—don’t worry, she’s used to that. Takes AA batteries. Fires mana bolts (cost: mana, battery charge). If the song ain’t playing, you ain’t firing. Get out there like the pretty pretty princess you are and let’s see some glitter! ✨✨✨

He blinked, looking at the description, then back at the Magical Wand in his hand. The insult flowed right off his back; he couldn’t get upset about the girlfriend he didn’t have. The System seemed to like tossing random insults into the aether to see if they stuck. It felt a little like playing one of those slightly-cringey games that thought it was being edgy and doing dark comedy, when really it was trying so hard it was tough to laugh.

Carrying around a child’s toy was embarrassing, but firing mana bolts sounded insanely powerful. Certainly compared to hammering things with a bat. It also let him immediately realize his dream of firing from afar rather than fighting in melee range, which was another point for his continued survival. Regeneration and now a mana-based ranged weapon; he was eating good…as long as he was willing to bear a little humiliation.

He weighed it in his hand, putting his pride up against the most powerful weapon he’d found so far, and sighed. I guess I’m a pretty pretty princess. It was too valuable not to use it. He could wrap it in black fabric if it was too embarrassing. There were plenty of questionable t-shirts just waiting to be cut up back at the gas station.

Besides… who am I embarrassed of? Who’s going to see me use this? There was a chance that he’d end up on this Great Game show, but given how many humans there were in the Tutorial, he doubted he’d appear at all, much less get any screentime. He wasn’t fighting flashily or posing for the cameras. He was cautiously clearing the dungeon one room at a time, as boring as could be. Doing what he needed to do to gather enough power to survive so he could be flashy later. It was essential for him, but it didn’t make for great television.

But before he used it on an enemy, he had to figure out how it worked. He’d be caught with his pants down if he rocked up to a bunch of snakemen only to find out firing it was a twelve-step process. So thinking, he lined the tip of the star up with a tree. A little nervous, he flicked the switch on. Music began to play, and the little lights show resumed in the star. Remembering how the spellbook had made him feel, he called forth his mana, forcing it into those vein-like structures again. As soon as he did, the weapon drew on his mana, greedily drinking it up. Mana poured out of him in an instant, so much it made him gasp. Tiny sparks of light that had nothing to do with the spell flashed in his eyes as the edges of his vision darkened. At the same time, blue light gathered around the star. It grew stronger and stronger, then blasted off. A star-shaped projectile flew from the tip of the weapon toward a nearby tree.

Crack! It tore right through the tree and out the other side. The tree shuddered, then began to fall, toppling toward him. Eric threw himself to the side. The trunk smashed into the ground right behind him, while the thinner branches whipped him on their way down. He stumbled out from the depths of the tree and brushed himself off, knocking leaves, acorns, bits of bark, and bugs out of his clothes and hair.

He looked at the star wand, then at the tree, then back at the wand. Twinkle Twinkle Little Star still played from its tinny speakers, and the lights continued to strobe with the glitter. He shut it down, still staring. “The hell!” It was way stronger than he’d expected. It took more fuel, in return. As the adrenaline of the falling tree almost hitting him faded, exhaustion rolled in. It felt as though he’d just run five miles, sans the aching limbs; his whole body was devoid of energy, totally washed out. He had to sit down and eat another snack, taking a little break to replenish his energy. No, not energy… mana. My mana.

It was still a foreign idea: him, having mana. Having spells. It made him giddy every time he thought about it, but it also gave him pause. There was a lot to learn, and he had barely scratched the surface.

Eric turned the star wand over in his hands, marveling at it. It was a powerful weapon, but it wasn’t the catch-all solution he’d hoped it was. At a guess, he had two shots of the wand before he passed out. It wasn’t a weapon for normal enemies, but a shotgun to use on bosses at close range where he could be sure it wouldn’t miss. Assuming there were bosses. Though, given the structure of the scenario so far? He’d be more surprised if there weren’t.

He braced himself for something to come investigate the loud noise, but nothing did. He waited a little longer to be sure, but not even the little snakes came to attack him. Are the monsters locked to their paths, or are they just not naturally curious? He didn’t know. All he could do now was be grateful no one came to look while he was exhausted.

His energy—mana—returned, and he stood, tucking the wand in his back pocket and retrieving his bat once more. There were two more routes to examine before he headed back to the station for the night. He didn’t know that he was going to take on whatever big monster laid at the end of this, but now that he had the wand, it was an option. It hadn’t been an option before.

First, let’s scout the other two routes. Once I know what’s going on, then I can decide whether I want to take on the boss or not. If there even is a boss.

This path dead-ended at the storage cave, so he turned his feet back the way he’d came and rewalked the path until he reached the intersection once more. This time, he took the right path, leaving the central path for last.

Practically speaking, the central path was most likely the most important path, given that they were in a narrow ravine with limited space to the left or right. On top of that, his video-game knowledge screamed that the central, obvious route was the most important. No matter how he looked at it, right was almost certainly not the main route. It was always better to fully delve the side paths to get all the loot before he proceeded to the boss. Or, at least, that was his philosophy in video games. He didn’t see any reason to change it now, now that his literal life was on the line. More loot meant a better chance to survive. A better chance to survive meant he lived to see tomorrow. And that, in turn, meant he got strong enough to start thinking about things other than survival.

The right path wound around through the woods for a while, drifting further than the left path had, far enough that he began to suspect he’d misjudged. He slowly climbed as he walked, sloping up around the wall of the ravine, the earth falling off sharply to his left and climbing sharply to his right. Just as the doubt began sinking in and he slowed to turn back, he heard it: the scrape of scaly feet on hardpacked earth. Ears pricking, Eric adjusted his grip on the bat and snuck closer. A sharp turn in the ravine wall blocked him from seeing ahead, and the sounds came from just around the corner. He glanced down. One misstep, and he’d go tumbling into the forest… but the same was true of his foes.

He put his back to the rock wall and peeked around the corner. Two snakemen. One sat on a barrel, picking at its teeth in boredom, while the other stood on alert, gazing down over the valley. Neither actively looked at the path, but it was easily in both their fields of vision; especially the one on the barrel. That one wasn’t paying as much attention, but it was better oriented to see him. It was also further from Eric, sitting down at the very end of the ledge the snakemen were using as a sort of lookout. He’d have to pass the alert snakeman to reach it.

Eric ducked back around the wall. He took a deep breath. Smack the alert snakeman. Run by. Kick the barrel-sitter off the ledge. Spin. Push the alert one off.

Alright. Here goes nothing.

He charged out from behind the wall, bum-rushing the alert snakeman. The snakeman on the barrel looked up, then startled and scrambled to its feet, letting out a hissing cry of alarm. The alert snakeman turned in time to take Eric’s shoulder to the face. He smashed into it and bowled it off the ledge, barely stopping himself before momentum carried him over, too.

Right. I forgot how small they are.

He whipped around, closing in on the barrel snakeman. It tossed its spear aside and drew a short dagger, hissing viciously as it darted in. Before it could close the distance, Eric unleashed a strike. It hit air, whooshing by as the snakeman ducked his blow and slipped inside his reach. He had enough time to feel horror before he felt a cool blade pierce his chest.

Eric screamed and kicked as hard as he could. The snakeman staggered back before its blade sunk home, but blood still ran down his chest, wet and hotter than he expected. Adrenaline shot through him like lightning. He kicked it again and again, swinging wildly with the bat to keep it at range. It lashed out, slashing his leg, but with the adrenaline pumping, he felt nothing. Every kick forced it back. Its heel hit the barrel, and it staggered. Eric didn’t hesitate. He kicked it one more time, landing a devastating blow on its chest. It flipped backward over the barrel and tumbled head over heels into the ravine below.

Eric stood alone on the cleft. He breathed raggedly, searching for air. Wiping his face, he looked at his shaking hands, at the blood soaking his polo. I’m injured.

The wound stung. He put a hand to it. Poisoned? He didn’t know. He didn’t have the presence of mind to check his status, either, so he just pulled a grandma candy out of his pocket and sucked it down. The sting lessened, but the blood kept flowing.

Regeneration. He tried to focus, but panic and his racing heart made it hard. Eric breathed slowly, forcing himself to calm. From out of the panic welled peace, and with it, he forced his mana back into shape, forced it to once again move the way the tome had taught him. His wounds closed, all of them closing equally at the same pace, the slashes on his thigh healing just as quickly as the hole in his chest.

At last, blood stopped flowing. The wound scabbed over. It still hurt when he breathed or moved his chest too quickly, but the wound was shut. He backed away, putting his back to the wall, and just breathed for a minute, taking a moment to calm down and think about his next move. Press on, or turn back?

Down below, laid out plainly before him, was what the snakemen had been guarding: an encampment. They wandered around, paying no attention to their sentrypost. He counted five, ten, fifteen before he stopped counting. There were tons of them. The ravine was infested with them.

The camp was all business, almost an army outpost. It wasn’t a village; there were no children or elderly, only adults of fighting age. At the back of the camp stood an enormous tent, large as a big top, and a handful of the twenty-or-so snakemen patrolled the tent. They were protecting—or guarding—whatever was in there. That had to be the end of the dungeon. The boss or the treasure room, whichever.

Eric glanced at the sun. I have time. But am I ready? He was struggling to deal with two snakemen at a time. One single competent snakeman had almost killed him. He had his boss-hammer, his Magical Wand, but it would only do so much to get him past twenty snakemen. If he used it early, he couldn’t use it on the boss. He had Regeneration, but it was slow and only worked out of battle. He couldn’t focus enough to use it immediately after a fight, let alone in a fight.

He shook his head. I’m not ready. He needed more. More practice. More muscle. He was still the same pasty office worker he’d been yesterday, in no shape to fight organized, armed monsters. Making it this far was a small miracle. Even if he was unlikely to hit level 3 (according to his own calculation based on a lot of guesswork), he could still practice fighting and just straight up lift weights, do stamina training, and build up his body into something that looked a little less flabby. He didn’t know how long that would take, but even a few days or weeks of training would leave him in better shape than he was right now.

Going down there right now was nothing but suicide. But he’d still gained something. He had his loot, his spell and his wand and even the grandma candies. Even more importantly than that, he had knowledge. He knew the shape of the dungeon and where he’d face resistance, and roughly how many enemies he’d face at once.

Mind made up, Eric turned back, leaving the dungeon behind. Another time. Later. After he’d trained, he’d come back fully prepared to take it out and crush the dungeon.
