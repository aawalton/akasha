I hurried up the stairs. Cresting the top step revealed a long, carpeted hallway set with sconces of lit candles. Royce’s broad frame was several paces ahead. His shoulders were stiff and his fists were clenched.

“Royce…”

He glanced back at me, then grumbled something unintelligible and stormed into one of the empty bedrooms.

I followed after, heart racing, just in time to see my friend practically throw his pack and bedroll. Both crashed down beside a small wooden table set with a candle and a small vase of pink flowers. The room’s far side held a wide window and a broad, quilted navy bedspread.

Saints, I don’t think I’ve ever seen him so upset. Annoyed, perhaps. Sullen, certainly. But… not like this. “Please say something.”

Royce aimed a scowl at me. Then he took in my expression, looked briefly chagrined, and directed his ire towards the ceiling instead. “Just… I can’t even… Just… Him?!”

That… wasn’t what I expected.

“Salt, Brin! He wants to kill you?!”

“Not anymore!” The ridiculousness of that statement hit me and I winced, slumping back against the door. “He just… that was what he asked for. In exchange for getting me to Pinnacle, I mean. He wanted to bring me to the Order and tell them… well, he thought I was dangerous. Look, I know it sounds awful-”

“It doesn’t sound awful, it is awful!” He started pacing. Every step was a heavy thud, rattling the vase and making the floorboards groan. “Ashes, when were you going to tell me all this?”

“I… I didn’t think it mattered.”

“How can it not?!” His voice grew thunderous. “We’re out in the middle of nowhere with this… this thug! This prick who won’t stop leering at you and apparently wanted you dead a few days ago!”

“Royce, I’m alive because of him. Teela, too.” Heat bolted up my throat. “And he’s not leering at me!”

“Oh, no, he definitely is! He hardly looks away from you! Don’t act like you don’t see it!” His shoulders sagged. “You’re certainly not stopping him.”

An old, familiar knot formed in my chest. The sensation that swelled when Royce looked at me in that way. Somehow managing to make me feel very wanted and very uncomfortable all at the same time.

I drew a deep breath. Then, meeting his gaze firmly, “Are you so upset because he’s… er, leering at me, or because he wanted me dead?”

“Both!” He slammed a hand onto the table. Wood and porcelain trembled as the loud sound rang through the room, making it seem much too cramped. Then, after a pregnant pause and crimson flush to his cheeks, he looked away and muttered, “Mostly the dead thing.”

A moment later one hand dragged through his hair, tousling it even further. “Right. It is mostly the dead thing, Brin. The other thing’s… I don’t even know. Not important, when you look at everything else.” He swept an arm out, gesturing towards nothing in particular.

Or perhaps he was gesturing towards my pact, the shard, my Fae tormenter, the brutal world outside, and the man downstairs who’d recently wanted to murder me.

It was hard to tell.

“Royce, I know you better than anyone.” I offered a tiny, sad smile. “And it… it seems like it might be. Important.”

He sank onto the bed. Corded arms stretched back, bracing behind sandy hair as he leveled another scowl upwards. “Maybe,” he grumbled, face red. Then his chest swelled with a heavy sigh and his voice hardened. “So. He wanted to turn you over to the Thalessians a few days ago. To be killed. What changed?”

What, indeed? That’s the very thing I’ve been wondering.

The silence stretched between us, expectant and uncomfortable. I drew towards the bed and settled beside Royce, knees curled to my chest.

“I… I don’t know, exactly. We haven’t exactly sat down and talked things through.” I plucked at a loose thread. “Honestly, until earlier I wasn’t even sure he… I don’t know. Half of me wondered if he still wanted to turn me in.”

“This isn’t helping,” he growled.

I snorted. “Sorry. I just… look, I’m not convinced the Order will execute me, for what it’s worth. Why would they? They hunt Fae. They protect people. I think if I can sit down and explain everything, tell them what’s happened, everything I know… I haven’t hurt anyone. I’m not dangerous. Salt, if I can change Renner’s mind, surely I can convince the paladins I’m not a threat!”

“I actually agree. I don’t think holy warriors would just… kill someone who’s surrendered. But,” his tone grew stern again, “that’s not what I asked. How do you know that prick’s not gonna change his mind? Again?”

I looked down, fingers twisting at threads as a dozen chilling images swam behind my eyes. “Royce… we… we told you what h-happened. With Baltha, I mean, and the pacted in Snowmelt, and… and all of it.”

“Sure.”

“But… well, we didn’t tell you, though.” My vision blurred. Warmth dripped down my cheeks and I swiped it away quickly, sniffling. “Sorry. I mean that we didn’t… n-no one went into detail. About everything that… everything.”

The bed squeaked as my friend’s massive frame shifted towards me. Hard muscle and familiar heat curled around my shoulders. “Do you want to talk about it?”

Memories hovered just behind my eyes. Too vivid. Too close. The words tumbled out too fast, tears spilling with them, but I couldn’t seem to stop the deluge.

“Not all of it, but… There was a f-fight. At an inn. Renner had left, but he came back, and… all the pacted decided to… one of them… Thom… h-hit me. A… a lot. He h-held me down and he didn’t stop and I… I thought… I thought I was going to die, Royce. I th-think… I think he w-would have killed me.” It all tore out, ragged and hoarse between sobs, as my friend’s embrace grew painfully tight. “Renner… killed h-him. I couldn’t get up. I could barely breathe. I could barely think. He… he kept fighting. Ashes, Royce, I think he would’ve killed all of them. But then Agnes… she… she held a knife to my throat. He could’ve run, I wanted him to run, but she said she’d k-kill me if he did and he… saints, he just stood there. He just let them tie him up.”

All the warmth and strength wrapped around me vibrated as, voice thick, Royce grumbled, “Well, fine, but he’s still a prick.”

My tears were swallowed by a choked, startled laugh. He gave a hollow chuckle, holding me close. We sat like that for some time. My breathing evened out alongside the steady swell of my best friend’s chest.

“I’m really sorry.”

He groaned. “Brin, you have to stop apologizing. What are you even sorry for?”

“Just… I came up here to try and help you feel better, and I’m…” I drew another deep, steadying breath. “Look, I won’t say Renner’s not awful sometimes. He hates pacted people, and I’ve seen him do some terrible things. But… I don’t know. After everything that happened, everything he did… I trust him.”

An unhappy sound rose in the back of his throat.

“You don’t have to like him.”

“Good.”

“Sometimes I don’t really like him.”

“Even better.”

“And… and about the… er, other part of it.” I hesitated, choosing my words carefully as his arms went rigid. My heart hammered so loudly I was sure he could hear it. “Royce, right now I’m focused on staying alive. On getting through each night. I’m not… it wouldn’t be right to…”

“Look,” he rumbled, heaving a sigh, “The other thing is my problem. Not yours.” His voice was firm, despite its bitter edge.

“I’m sor… I don’t want to hurt you.”

“I know.”

I didn’t know what else to say. What other consolation or comfort to offer. We sat looking out the window for a while, watching orange and pink creep along the horizon.

Finally I turned, meeting his gaze gently. “I should get settled. Saints know none of us got enough sleep last night. Do you… want to come back downstairs?”

He offered a smile that just barely reached his eyes. “Maybe in a bit. Right now I need to… I don’t know. Brood, I guess. Be stoic and manly and… stuff.”

“Okay.” My lips twitched. “Well… when you’re done being manly, if you need anything…”

“I know.”

Rather than immediately return downstairs, I gathered my things and went to another of our designated rooms. Opening the door revealed a space that was, in a word, charming.

A plush, faded red armchair sat beside the door. It was worn in places, with discolored patches of fabric sown over dripping fluff. There was a little wooden table beside the chair. It was decorated with an unlit candle and a colorful, chipped vase of freshly watered snowbells.

The bed itself was narrow and set against the side wall. A thick red quilt lay flat atop a mattress that was, upon sitting, dense and well-cushioned. My thighs sank into the fabric and I caught the faint, musky scent of wool. Fragrant pine wafted in from the room’s sole window. It was draped in thick burgundy cloth, opening to a sea of leafless maples and tall, lush firs swaying beneath a darkening horizon. I drew the curtains wide and leaned out, admiring the scenery.

I bet the sunrise will look lovely from here. If I’m awake in time to see it, that is. I left the drapes spread wide and set my pack down on the floor.

The next several minutes were spent retrieving soap, salt, and sage, and filling the room’s small washbasin with a quick murmur of, “Alar.” I attended to my blotchy cheeks, my teeth, and the various bits of grime from our day’s travel.

Finally feeling refreshed and ready to show my face again, I headed downstairs.
