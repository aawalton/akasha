I woke up all alone, for once. No Chester on his designated half of the mattress, no Olethros pretending I was the one initiating sleep-centric cuddles. My fake alarm clock, fuzzy at first, eventually guestimated the time as late morning.

I looked up at the ceiling, soaking in those few savory moments of silence.

Today was already busy for everyone else, but I had been given a reprieve, knowing what was coming.

***

Nora’s briefing was both efficient and yet prickly.

“Mother Liora’s announcements from Paradise have been nonstop.”

General Ragnerus, Liora had declared repeatedly, had escaped containment. Her tearful cries had conveniently omitted the part about where he had been left completely unguarded in the middle of an unpopulated mountain range with no obvious plan for what would happen if someone sprung him. Her calls had gone out across the communication orbs to every church: all faithful souls were needed to defend the Holy Land of Chairo.

“The Holy Land of Chairo,” I echoed almost absently. “I feel like it sounds a bit strange. Don’t they say The Holy City of Chairo?”

Nora nodded curtly. “Except it’s not just that. According to older maps, ‘Chairo’ is much more than just a city. Ecclesia just ate it up a bit once it was established, but Chairo was first. And therein lies the problem.”

“Only one?” I asked, only to be given a hard look.

“Everyone is in Chairo,” she stated flatly. “That’s what my phone says—basically. I can’t tell you if they’re in the city, if they’re outside the walls. Heck, twenty miles down the road is still Chairo!”

I frowned. “So, we don’t know battlefield leadership composition.”

“And it’s a bit risky to spy at the moment,” she added. “It might give away more than we’d learn.”

I thought for a long moment. “I’m… Thinking we don’t have much of anything to hide at this point. Well, if Ragnerus is successful, that is—”

Speak of the summoned demon.

The General appeared in a smoky flash, though no smell accompanied it.

“I will address those who recognize their lineage to me,” he boomed. “After sunset.”

Then he stared at me.

It wasn’t a threat; that much I could tell, but he was making some sort of decision that definitely involved me.

Olethros then appeared, arms already crossed.

Ragnerus glowered at him. “She needs horns.”

Olethros turned, presenting his shoulder and snapping tail to his older brother. “She needs no such thing.”

Ragnerus let out a low growl. “The King of Deception reveals his bias. Yet my observation is not personal.”

I tried a neutral-looking shrug.  “It’s not like I could grow any, even if I tried.”

“My demons will not follow the hornless,” he insisted. “It is not merely a preference; it is how things are.”

“What if it’s a hybrid with horns?” I asked, half-curious and half-annoyed at the declaration. “Would they follow them?”

Ragnerus huffed, clearly wanting to say no. “There are exceptions to the tradition—and none of them apply to this situation!”

“Well, I guess it’s a moot point then—”

“Conservatives,” the General interjected. “They will need something they can see and point to.”

What. A spectacle?

I frowned. “Putting on a pair of demon’s horns… I’d be worried it would be more of an insult than anything.”

Ragnerus’s eyes narrowed. “Not if we present them to you in front of the masses.” His gaze shifted upward slightly. “A formal gesture...” He paused, then folded his arms. “With function. Preferable to your forehead jewelry.”

I can ditch the tiara?

Ragnerus then turned to Nora, and I saw him flinch a bit under her direct gaze. “Otherworlder. I would enlist your assistance.”

Nora blinked. “Alright, General. Lead the way.”

Olethros and I did a double-take as Ragnerus bowed to her. Then he disappeared quickly, making sure to be as awkward about it as possible.

Nora ran a hand down the back of her neck. “Olethros… Would you, uh…”

“Come along with you?”

“He’s a lot,” she admitted, nodding. “I’m not sure how to deal with him.”

Olethros shrugged as he summoned a mirror portal. “Let me know if you ever come up with a plan to do so.”

“Before you two go,” I called. “Can you tell me where Chester is?”

“With Vernie,” Nora answered. “Last I saw.”

***

I had been avoiding Vernie.

Not out of malice or anything, just in that way where things are technically resolved, but you didn’t follow up, so things still sit slightly wrong, and now it’s too late to bring the original problem. She apologized for the drunken attack on Olethros. She had been staying sober. She had even given me some solid relationship advice.

But the awkwardness was in my court. I hadn’t done anything to maintain or optimize our relationship.

Vernie’s room was how she wanted it, stripped down, shadowy, but with a large, central table to display little knick-knacks. Except all of them were on the floor now. Instead, Chester sat in the middle of that table, looking for all the world as if he had made a major accomplishment.

“I know it was you!” Vernie said, hovering over him. “There’s no way it wasn’t!”

Chester glanced at me with glittering eyes.

“He is known to knock things down,” I agreed. “But usually, he does it to get your attention—”

“No, I did that,” Vernie said absently. “Rae-Rae. It’s him!”

“Chester?”

“No! Whiskers!”

I leaned in. “What about his whiskers?”

Vernie stamped her foot. “He’s Whiskers! Make him confess!”

Understanding slowly dawned on me. “You’re saying he’s the cat who helped you steal the blueberry pie when you were a child?”

“Teenager, but yes! That’s him!”

I looked hard at Chester, who was licking a patch of fur demurely on his backside.

Vernie pointed her finger. “He can change his appearance all he wants, but his mannerisms are the same! And no two cats have the same personality!”

Chester glanced at her fingertip, then at me, and then looked Vernie right in the eyes. With deliberate slowness, he shook, shifting his fur into a black and white tuxedo pattern. The polka dot bow was the last thing to appear.

After holding the form for approximately three seconds, he was himself again, a gray-and-black tabby with green eyes. With a loud purr and a slow, three-cycle blink, he then hopped into Vernie’s shadow and disappeared.

“I knew I was right!” she said, crowing in delight. “Whiskers!”

I leaned low to get a better look at her shadow, only to find two green eyes staring back.

“What’s this all about, Chester?”

He flashed a set of claws, and Vernie whistled as I dodged back a little.

“I’m going to take that as an intentional miss, Mister!” I snapped. “Sneak strikes are against the rules!”

“Sneak strikes?” Vernie paused, then put a hand on her hip. “Alright. It’s decided. I’m taking him with me to Chairo! Whiskers and Vernie, partners again.”

“Mrrow!” came Chester’s agreement from the shadow.

Vernie shifted on her feet a little. “You’re alright with that?”

I thought about it for a long, long moment before I acquiesced. “Yeah. Though I don’t expect for one minute that either of you will keep the other out of trouble.”

Vernie grinned, and for the first time in a long time, gave me the rough hug I didn’t even know I was missing. “That’s the whole point, Rae-Rae!”

***

True to his word, the General held court late that night. A large audience had been assembled in The Parlour. All possible shapes and configurations of Demon packed the space, with more attending through the mirror portals that Galenus had opened to other pockets of Demonkind all across Speranza. To my astonishment, there were a few, very few, hybrids and humans in the mirrors as well.

General Ragnerus was the first to take the stage, flashing his crimson aura while deliberately using both eyes to sweep across the crowd, leveling a calculated glare at anyone who even smelled like they’d step out of line. Those who cheered him were spared the look, of course.

Olethros appeared beside him next, and the entire parlor went completely silent, like the air itself rebuffed all sound. The crowd itself also froze, unprepared for the sight of these two standing together without tearing each other apart.

Then the elbowing started. Neighbors were nudged, and started nudging back.

The General reigned them in with a single hiss that sharply cut off as he held up his crackling sword.

“Chairo,” he boomed, without any preamble. “Is under unauthorized occupation. The human who calls himself Holy Sage Relias has appointed himself God without sanction from any recognized authority!” Then, he turned to Olethros. “You also bear witness to this, Your Majesty?”

Hooo-lly shit.

How had he gotten him to agree to calling him that?!

Olethros waited for the shrieks to die down. He nodded casually. “I certainly did not agree to it, General. And I see no evidence that even the Goddess Euphridia did, either.”

“The Chosen One!” Tarek, our plant in the crowd, shouted on cue. “Maybe she did?”

I stepped out of a mirror portal directly onto the stage, Will already in hand, and planted it hard against the floor with a loud, resounding clang. “Absolutely not. No one wants a Murderer as God, least of all me.”

The wait to continue was excruciating, but I had to let the rumbles die once more before I added loudly, “His self-certified apotheosis is a complete and utter fiction.”

The General nodded, then stepped forward once again. “Chairo, under his direction, holds several beings against their will. Including my sister. I have conferred with the Demon King. The Captain of the Holy Order of Gold. None here has chosen Relias as God. We will liberate those in Chairo, no matter what it takes.”

Olethros looked out over the crowds and through the portals.

“Join us, and be recognized in united Purpose. Flee, and stay hidden long after the dust clears, for no glory awaits a coward. But if you oppose us, know that you will fall before us, never to rise again.”

“His Majesty has spoken,” General Ragnerus rumbled, his jaw tightening around the words that must have cost him dearly. “Those of my lineage, I call upon you. Do not allow this man to take our kind into eternal servitude!”

He held out a fist, showing a familiar, shattered-but-sealed Ereborite jewel. “Lieutenant Bodhil was once cursed with this. But it was the Chosen One who freed her, instead of letting her succumb to Naught. In anticipation of freeing more from his hold, we honor Captain Lightbringer’s efforts to reform.”

What an interesting turn of phrase...

He held out a cross between a helmet and a crown. It was a thick, reinforced circlet with two spiked, forward-sweeping horns. The blue sapphire from my tiara was in the middle. Two sideburns like guards swept over the temples.

My first instinct was renewed discomfort. I was not a demon. Wearing them would be like taking something that wasn’t mine.

But Ragnerus was holding them out, publicly. In front of every demon watching.

There was a difference, I eventually decided, between taking something from someone and being given it by the person to whom it belonged.

I'm not wearing a costume; they're asking me to show recognition.

I stepped forward and lowered my head.

Ragnerus placed the horned circlet carefully, though they were heavy with meaning.

Not everyone in that crowd would agree with what I did. And I was okay with that. But in that moment, I did what I thought was most right, knowing I would make more enemies than ever.

“I’m honored, General,” I said after bowing slightly. “I will wear them to free Chairo and every soul Relias claims as his own.”

Then I turned to Olethros and offered him my hand once more. He shook it for all the world to see with a grand flourish.

Then I offered it to Ragnerus, who looked at it for a long moment before he shook it back.

“We march on Chairo tomorrow,” the General declared. “Let all of Speranza know of our intent. No longer will we hide in the shadows!”

Wait, tomorrow?!
