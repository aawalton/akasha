“Before you continue,” Rosslyn said, interrupting. “What should I call you, spokesman?”

“Um, me, Your Highness?” said the spy who had been speaking to this point.

“Yes, you!” said King Janus impatiently. “In case your contributions prove highly meritorious, she may wish to reward you in the future—or perhaps she will wish to question you further in future. Give her your name so that we may proceed.”

Rosslyn gave Adon a subtle sidelong glance. What is up with him?

He seems to have eaten something that didn’t agree with him, Adon sent back after a moment. His stomach is doing backflips. He might need a break later.

All right, she thought. Well, I have to tolerate him being rude to our people anyway if he chooses to be. At least there is a sort of reason for it.

“My name is Randall, Your Highnesses,” the spy said. He stepped back from the table and bowed low. “It is my great honor to be in your august presences. I—”

“You may be seated, Randall,” Rosslyn said, smiling slightly. “Please go on with what you were saying.”

“Yes, of course. I speak for both the corps of spies and the scouts, as we have consolidated our collected intelligence to be comprehensive. We have observed the Empire is now sending thousands of its troops away from the capital.”

The room stiffened at his words.

“Sending troops away where?” Rosslyn asked immediately.

“Are we about to be attacked here?” asked General Muller.

“They appear to be going home,” said Randall.

Everyone went silent for a moment.

“That seems an improbable move,” said General Saxon in a diplomatic tone.

“It’s absolutely ridiculous,” said King Janus.

“Have they been reinforcing the city’s defenses as they reduce their troop commitment in the capital?” Rosslyn asked. “Perhaps they determined they could hold the city indefinitely and needed to quell uprisings elsewhere in the occupied territory.”

“They were rebuilding the ruined walls and sending in reinforcements up until two weeks ago, Your Highness,” Randall said. “Then the movement of soldiers reversed direction and accelerated, and the efforts to repair the city defenses slowed to a crawl with the departure of much of the Demon Army’s labor force.”

“You have claimed that the enemy are ‘going home,’” General Saxon said calmly. “What is the basis for that assessment?”

“Yes, if I simply heard that they were leaving the city, I would assume that they were seizing additional high value targets,” Rosslyn said. “Wayn is an important population center, but no city stands alone.”

“The direction of travel has been consistent, Your Highness,” Randall said. “The scouts have followed these troops for miles, in a few instances fairly close to the border with the Empire. They do not, at any point, turn around and change destinations. We also have our spies placed within Wayn itself—”

“Are you certain it was wise to take so many of your number back here?” asked King Janus. “With a significant chunk of the intelligence gathering apparatus in this fort town, will we not be left blind? What if this is merely some complicated feint? The Empire runs on deception at all levels.”

Every general in the room nodded in agreement with that last sentence.

Adon read the shared sentiment that the Empire’s officials could not even be trusted by each other, that it was a society where each man might betray another to advance further up the hierarchy and closer to the Emperor’s inner circle. The Demon Empire was dominated by a culture and worldview based around treachery, dishonesty, and paranoia, and that inevitably seeped into military doctrines.

The spy swallowed nervously. “Your Highnesses, this is not a large commitment of our spy or scout corps. I—we brought the minimum number we could to be able to answer any detailed questions you might have. In times of conflict such as these, we continuously recruit as best we can from the most trustworthy people available on the ground, so our networks are ever expanding. You will never be left blind, as long as a few of us remain alive.

“As for the reality of the movements, we are as certain as we can be that the troop movements themselves are real. There is a great expense to shifting soldiers around, and they are moving so much materiel out of the city at this point that if they are not sincere, they are putting the capital at great risk of being retaken for a gamble. The advantage they could hope to attain from that is unclear. As for the motive behind this, we have strong indicators that the Empire believes their soldiers are needed elsewhere.”

A small commotion broke out at this.

“Needed elsewhere?” questioned General Muller. “What could be more urgent than their ongoing invasion?”

“Are you trying to tell me the Empire has another front somewhere?” asked King Janus incredulously. “When they’re in the middle of trying to conquer Claustria—” He stopped midsentence, his face suddenly paling. “Wait, are they attacking Parmonia?”

Adon felt the panic from the Parmonian King hit him like a slap in the face. Janus’s mind ran through what felt to Adon like extremely realistic scenarios of the Empire invading his country, the only other major power remaining that had a land border with the Empire. He imagined the demons riding over the mountains and sweeping through the country or mounting an amphibious invasion—the Empire also shared a sea border with Parmonia—that would seize the capital and shatter Janus’s control over his own government. King Janus imagined both military and political disaster, millions dead, his legacy shattered—he would be “the King who lost Parmonia.”

King Janus is losing his grip a bit, Adon sent to Rosslyn only.

It wasn’t helped by the stomach issues Adon had mentioned before, although those were the least of the problem. The King had been experiencing some dietary discomfort almost since he arrived in Claustria, from what Adon had noticed in past encounters. He still wasn’t used to the local food, even though he had several chefs catering to him personally and making dishes from his homeland.

But mostly, Janus felt insecure because he knew he had left his homeland lightly defended in his zeal to be seen honoring his agreement with King Alistair and fighting the Empire.

“My apologies, Your Highnesses,” said Randall. “I regret that we actually do not know for certain what the withdrawn troops are meant to be doing. We have not penetrated the innermost circles of the Empire’s command structure. We have only theories and rumors to go on.”

“Well, give us your best theories and rumors, then,” Rosslyn said, frowning.

“The idea that they could be attacking Parmonia does have some merit,” mused General Forbes.

The Queen gave him a look.

Do not scare King Janus more than is necessary, she thought but could not say.

So Adon passed along the message.

King Janus’s state of mind is concerning to the Queen, he sent to General Forbes only. Please avoid speculating that his country is being attacked.

The General’s eyes widened, and then he gave Adon and Rosslyn a subtle nod.

“If the Parmonians are being attacked, the Empire has chosen a very inefficient method by which to do it,” said Randall, almost as if he too was aware of the need to calm King Janus. “There is a route directly through the Kingdom to the Parmonian border, and they have sent no troops in that direction, even though it seems strategically important to do so if they wished to attack King Janus’s territory. They would need to either destroy the Parmonian Army or at least cut off your route home, or they would have to worry you would swing back the way you came and crush them before their invasion could succeed.”

King Janus took a couple of deep breaths and seemed to take some solace in what Randall had said.

“Then what is your working theory?” Rosslyn asked. “You have not answered that.”

“There are only a few possibilities that we have been able to piece together from discussions among the officer corps, Your Highness,” said Randall. “First, the Empire anticipates that this will be a long conflict, and they cannot afford the expense of supporting so many troops all participating in the occupation. Therefore, they have decided to conserve resources in periods of the conflict when they anticipate a low probability of violent exchanges.”

“It seems unlikely,” said General Saxon. “They are awash in treasure from the populations they oppress, and their lust for conquest is legendary. Why would they essentially give up on a power that is already half-conquered? Just lay back and hope no one retakes what they have seized? It is a poor strategy as well as out of character.”

“They are still reinforcing Wayn,” Randall said. “In this line of thinking, they are not just lying back. They are retrenching and conserving resources, planning to continue the invasion later—”

“What is your next theory?” Rosslyn asked flatly, unimpressed.

“The Empire has determined that the fruits of the conquest would be less than the blood and treasure they would expend to succeed in finishing it. In part because the Parmonian Army’s arrival has shattered their calculations. They were relying on the Holy Kingdoms being divided and indecisive, as during some of their previous campaigns. Now that they know King Janus’s army is with Claustria, they are minimizing resources invested and focusing on extracting what they can from the land. This theory is supported by a piece of information I had yet to mention: some of the groups of soldiers moving out are allegedly laden with treasure. There have been reports that much of Wayn’s material wealth has been siphoned away, including the Crown Jewels. Anything gold and silver is supposedly being taken back to the Empire.”

“So, this is the theory that they are just giving up,” said General Saxon, who looked like he wanted to roll his eyes.

“According to this line of thinking, the Empire is trying to take a more conservative approach. They may wait and see if anyone dares try and reclaim the half of Claustria they have taken and simply build up their physical defenses in anticipation of that moment. Or they may actually sue for peace.”

General Muller snorted, apparently joining General Saxon in his assessment of this theory.

“Are there any other ideas being explored by our intelligence community?” Rosslyn asked, even less impressed by this idea than she had been by the previous one.

“The last major theory is, um, stranger,” said Randall.

“Spit it out,” said King Janus a bit rudely. “Stop teasing us.”

The representative of the front line spies frowned and then continued speaking.

In the moment when he was quiet, Adon observed that the other spies and scouts in the room all felt supportive of Randall. He had been doing all the talking for their group, not because he was a dominant personality who had demanded this pride of place or something, but because they all trusted him to summarize their findings and views honestly and well.

There really must be strong empirical proof that the withdrawal is real, Adon thought. He silently conveyed that impression to Rosslyn, and the Queen frowned but nodded.

I suppose there must be, but the theories so far leave much to be desired, she thought.

“The last major idea is that the Empire actually has another front they are fighting on somewhere,” said Randall. “A major front that is significant to them, probably more significant than Claustria. It could be Parmonia, but if that were the case, Your Highnesses would probably know about such an attack from other sources before we could verify it. But most of us—” He swallowed—“most of us are of the opinion that it is not Parmonia.”

“What other front—” began King Janus.

“We do not know,” Randall interrupted. “Your Highness, we cannot know. The Empire is vast, and to our knowledge, it dominates the entirety of the neighboring continent. That space is an enigma to us. We only know that, as far as has been explored by humans willing and able to return and report to our own authorities in the past, all of that territory was under the boot of the Emperor. But that means that for us, it is logical to assume that whatever enemy they are shifting troops to face, it might be a threat to the Imperial heartland. Perhaps even an existential threat.” He swallowed again. “Perhaps… a power on the opposite side of the Empire that could be an ally for us. Frankly, I speak only for myself when I say this, but my suspicion is that this third theory is correct. The Empire is under attack by an as-yet-unknown power.”

The spy spoke in tones of uncertainty that reflected a lack of confidence, but Adon sensed that he was actually understating his true degree of conviction.
