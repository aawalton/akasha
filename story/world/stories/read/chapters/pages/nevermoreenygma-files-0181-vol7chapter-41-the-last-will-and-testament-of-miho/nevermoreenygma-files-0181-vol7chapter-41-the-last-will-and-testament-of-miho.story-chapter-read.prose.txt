The Last Will and Testament of Miho Sagara

Mai returned home a few days after the attack, once her regeneration had finally sealed the wounds. She’d been questioned by at least fifteen different departments, though everything still swirled in her mind like a storm she couldn’t escape.

The fey and the entire occult world in Tokyo was drowning in confusion, headlines hammering through her skull like hydraulic drills. News outlets wouldn’t stop talking about the hotel attack… but she still couldn’t fully process it.

Miho’s friends handled the funeral with quiet discretion, the tragedy hit too close, too many people knew her. But Mai knew there was more beneath the surface.

And that was confirmed days after the cremation, with a phone call.

Miho had left explicit instructions in case anything happened, and Mai was stunned when an attorney arrived after the call.

But no amount of news could quiet her mind.

The loss sat inside her like dead weight.

The lawyer arrived in the late afternoon, when the shadows were stretching long across the floor and the house—now silent—was sinking into darkness. His face was walled-off, the kind that looked untouched by whatever happened in the world outside.

He handed her a sealed folder with a pair of cold, formal words:

“Last will of Miho Sagara.”

Mai didn’t want to think about it.

She didn’t want to face that reality.

But there it was—

Inside the folder she found everything: the house, the studio… even a small property in Saitama. All of it under her name, except for a few items listed for other specialists.

Yet having everything transferred to her felt strange, unreal.

When had Miho done this? Without saying a word to her?

Mai Izumi.

An identity Miho helped built with her—one the government had accepted as if she were any ordinary person, even though her existence as a fey was far more tangled than anyone knew.

Miho hadn’t left anything to chance.

Every detail was meticulously prepared.

And despite everything, Mai couldn’t help wondering if Miho knew.

If she’d seen all this coming—that uncanny foresight she carried like a second heartbeat.

The idea that Miho had left her not just a home, not just her life’s belongings, but a piece of herself… unsettled her.

How was she supposed to move forward with all this?

After seeing the lawyer out, papers still in hand, Mai looked around the house—now emptied of the presence that had once filled it.

No more laughter.

No more late-night conversations.

No weekend plans made over drinks.

None of it.

Just the echo lingering in her memory.

Yet something pushed her toward Miho’s small study.

She knew that room held the deepest part of who Miho had been—the part no one else ever saw.Opening the door, Mai found herself wishing she’d see Miho inside, answering calls, summoning her for an assignment, or simply waving her over to tell her something amusing.

It had only been a bit more than two years… but to Mai it felt like much longer.

Within those walls, Miho had built a life that now rested on Mai’s shoulders.

And despite the grief, she had to decide what to do with it.

The idea of Saitama felt like a distant refuge, somewhere she couldn’t reach yet. For now, she was here, surrounded by choices she couldn’t make and a future stretching out before her like a long, empty road.

One question echoed endlessly in her mind— the same question everyone who knew Miho kept asking: What had truly happened during the attack? How could something so devastating occur when someone like Miho should have sensed it early? And the security?

But of course— if even the government’s personal guard, the vampires’ security, and the Wishper team had been powerless… what could Miho have possibly done? Everything suggested she’d only realized it at the very last moment.

A curse, she’d said. The Shambler. Had that really been magic?

The confusion inside Mai was as thick as the fog seeping into each corner of the empty house. She couldn’t shake the feeling that there was something they were all missing.

Had Miho not been able to foresee her own death?

The echo of Miho’s final final words at her mind, pain blooming in her chest every time she tried to remember.

Mai knew that many psychics and precognitives were unable to see their own fate—their own end.Their future was cloaked behind a dense curtain of fog whenever the vision concerned themselves.

As Miho had once told her, having such abilities meant placing them in service of others—never for the benefit of the one who carried them. At least, that was her working philosophy. To use them otherwise would be no different from the charlatans who cashed in on their so-called gifts.

Maybe that was what had happened with Miho.

Maybe she had foreseen everything that was about to unfold in the moment—everything except her own end, and the disaster that followed. And yet, there was still that lingering sensation that something else had been present in those final days… a restlessness Miho had never been able to shake. She had spoken of a feeling, a premonition the day before when they found that lost boy.

Impossible.

The rumors circulating among those close to the investigation in the weeks that followed claimed the attack hadn’t been a coincidence at all. But neither had it been solely an attempt to sabotage an agreement. It had been the result of an internal power struggle within the most influential families of the vampiric world. The Rozenkreutz and the Rackenhell—both had been the direct targets of the assault. Especially their representatives.

The new matriarch of the Rozenkreutz herself had died in the attack, leaving behind a power vacuum that her mother, the infamous Carmilla, would now have to reclaim again.

The nature of the attack, according to whispers and leaked reports in the Inoue Special Section, pointed toward a carefully orchestrated coup. Someone inside the Rozenkreutz had struck an alliance with external forces with a single goal: eliminate the two heads of the families and seize power for themselves. The death of Ella Rozenkreutz had merely been the opening move in a internal war far from over. The real question was who those external forces were.

Though, of course, all of this came through rumor.

Mai sat quietly, her gaze fixed on the long shadows stretching across the walls. The attack—the chaos that had erupted—had not been accidental. It had been a calculated move, planned with precision.

Ella, the head of the Rozenkreutz, had fallen in that opening blow, and now everything lay in ruin. The question looped in her mind like a dark mantra: Who was behind this? Who had both the power and the will to challenge two of the oldest and most feared families of the hidden world, along with the governments entangled with them? Mai knew the answers lived somewhere in the dark… but she also understood that the dark wasn’t ready to speak.

With the scraps of information reaching her, it was entirely possible the matter would be settled internally, the truth buried forever—never meant for outside ears.

That changed when she was intercepted by someone who had been involved.
