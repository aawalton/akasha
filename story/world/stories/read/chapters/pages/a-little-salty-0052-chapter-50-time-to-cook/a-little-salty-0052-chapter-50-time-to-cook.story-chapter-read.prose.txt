I quickly begin to weave through the sandstone cavern, making my way to the Alchemy Chamber at the other end of the room. I need to grab some more potion supplies to restock after today’s fiasco. I pass a handful of other Guild members as I go, keeping my head down and my gaze to myself.

There are something like a hundred members in this city, when you include the merchants and blackmarket dealers who stock the shelves and bring in interesting weapons and magical artifacts for trade. I’ve heard some of the goods come from the palace itself. Various tunnels lead to side chambers, each featuring different but useful resources, including training rooms, ingredient merchants, or weapons dealers. And of course, the Job room.

Each tunnel has no doorway, but the spell circle above the entrances are designed only to permit entry to those who have access permissions. I can peruse all of the general rooms, and a handful of specialties pertaining to my poison and potion background, but the more dangerous and prestigious chambers are locked to me. Another reason why I need to upgrade to Rank C jobs. The higher I climb, the more opportunities will open to me.

No one spares me a second glance as I make my way to the Alchemy chamber. Good. I want to be in and out of here without talking to anyone, so I can head back to my place and fume in peace.

“Sal!”

I wince, slowing down as Cyros jogs over to join me. He’s exactly who I was hoping not to run into. I try to plaster on a neutral face as I turn to the dryad.

“How’d it go?”

And that’s exactly what I hoped he wouldn't ask.

“Fine,” I say shortly, continuing toward the back of the chamber.

“Uh oh.” Cyros winces as he falls in step beside me. Like all dryads, he has brown bark-like skin and leaves in place of hair. A few of them are starting to turn yellow, which hadn’t been that way when I first met him about six months ago.

“Uh oh, what?” I ask, glancing away.

“If the job went well, you’d be bragging about it,” he says.

“I would not!”

He snorts. “What about the cursed amulet heist last week?”

“What, I can’t be proud of my success?” I say, at the entrance of the Alchemy Chamber. There’s a moment of faint resistance, like I’m stepping into a building with higher pressure than the air outside, then the sensation falls away as the spell recognizes my patch and lets me through. Inside, rows of equipment line shelves. At the back wall is an empty counter, behind which are jars of eyeballs, flaming flowers, and bone dust.

“And admit it; using that Heat potion to trick the amulet into thinking someone was holding it was really clever,” I add.

“See? If the job had gone well, you’d be telling me about it.” Cyros hesitates. “Especially since your job rank depends on it.”

I don’t say anything, deciding to glare at an array of glass bottles instead.

Cyros lets out a quiet breath. “It’s okay if you couldn’t do it.”

“I could,” I snap, snatching up a handful of empty vials. “I was just interrupted. I’ll finish the job tomorrow.”

“Okay,” Cyros says. “But I’m just saying, it’s okay if you can’t. You can always put that job back up on the wall. I mean, this summer was the first time I killed someone.”

“This wouldn’t be the first time for me,” I growl, stalking over to the counter. “And you’re basically my same age, anyway!”

“Yeah, but I just meant, I’ve been training a lot longer than you,” Cyros says. “I’ve been building up to this for years. I knew what I was getting myself into.”

Ugh, first Shirasil, and now Cyros? I deposit the armful of glass vials on the counter and glance over the shelves of  ingredients. “You don’t have to treat me like I’m your kid, you know. I can take care of myself.”

“Ew, what?” Cyros laughs. “I’m way too young and cool to be a parent.” He flashes a teasing grin, but when I don’t reciprocate, the expression fades. “I know you can take care of yourself, Sal. But you’re still my responsibility, whether you like it or not.”

I sigh, turning back to him. He’s right about that, at least. Even though he’s still a student himself, since he was the one to initiate me into the Blackcloaks, he’s sort of like my guardian. If I get in trouble, the fallout will blow back on him.

I just hate having others worry about me.

“I can handle it,” I assure him. “I just had a slug experience.”

Cryos’s eyes widen. “Slugs” is what I’ve been using to refer to the gods—which, generally speaking, is almost always referring to Shirasil.

Cyros never met the god—in fact, he never even met Lisari when she was playing pretend as a human—but he knows my history with Widengra, and I’ve told him all about Shirasil’s meddling, too.

Well. I didn’t tell him that Shirasil made me his champion. But since I’m not planning on that being a permanent state of matter, I don’t see the point.

I’m not sure if Cyros completely believes me, anyway. It’s a wild story, especially because I don’t have any proof, and the god seems to think it’s funny to appear around me and Cyros when I’m the only one who can see or hear him. I swear, it’s like having my own personal haunting. Except instead of trying to scare me, he’s trying to tick me off. And he’s damn good at it.

“Is everything okay?” Cyros asks.

“Yeah,” I grumble, turning back to the counter. Zaquedyil, the alchemy dealer, has abruptly appeared before us. I manage not to jump, despite her appearance.

Zaquedyil is a creepy-looking half-spider, half-woman, with a human torso emerging from an eight-legged spider body. She has two humanoid eyes, with three more arrayed around each like a set of eyebrows. Her carapace is a ghostly white, with black stripes dramatically framing her face and spiraling down her arms. She wears flowing black silks, and moves as silent as a shadow. The first time she jump-scared me in this shop, I nearly had a heart attack.

Arachnoids are more common here than they had been in Fairwood, and it took a little bit to get used to their presence. But once I noticed most other arachnoids don’t dress half so dramatically, and in fact most of the black stripes are makeup, painted on to add to her unnerving look, I realized the whole thing was just a gothy performance. I’m pretty sure the quiet alchemist enjoys startling her patrons as a way to stave off boredom; it soon became a game for me to not be startled by her silent and abrupt appearances. I think she appreciates the challenge. Well, I like to think so, anyway. She doesn’t say much.

“Hi, Zee,” I say, straightening up my pile of bottles and vials. Zaquedyil dips her head in acknowledgement. “Do you have any more powdered griffon talons?”

The arachnoid turns away, straightening her legs and towering over us as she stretches up to reach a jar on the top shelf.

“Do you want to spar after this?” Cyros ventures as Zaquedyil starts gathering all my ingredients.

He’s trying to cheer me up. He knows how much I like to fight—or, more specifically, level up and get stronger—and usually my response would be an automatic yes. But I’m just not in the mood for it today.

“Tomorrow,” I say, trying to keep the weariness out of my tone. “I need to make some more potions and prep for tomorrow. I’ll tag up with you after I finish the job.”

Cyros’s forehead wrinkles in concern, but he just says, “Yeah, that sounds great.”

“Anything else?” Zaquedyil’s voice is quiet and airy, the kind of voice that makes you want to lean in to hear better.

“Yeah,” I say, pointing over to a side cabinet. “You got any fireroot left?”

Wordlessly, Zaquedyil heads over to retrieve the requested herbs.

Cyros snorts. “What potion uses that? You trying to season them for a tasty stew?”

I stick my tongue out at him. “You ever got a faceful of steam from fireroot chili? It makes you cough and cry so hard you’re basically debilitated. Thought I could try it in a new smoke bomb I’m making.”

Cyros laughs. “Leave it to you to turn food into a weapon.”

“That’s my specialty,” I say, counting out the coins as Zaquedyil wraps up my goods.

Cyros walks with me when I leave the Guild and head back to my house, chatting all the while about the missions he’s recently been on with Master Nieve. They do sound fun, but tonight I’m hardly listening, and I nod along and laugh on autopilot.

My place is a little stone room cut into one of the canyon walls, almost all the way down at ground level. All the cheapest houses are clustered toward the bottom, with wealth and social status increasing the further up the cliff faces you live. The area I’m renting a room in is just barely removed from being a slum, but that works just fine for me; no one comes poking around when I’m experimenting with potions or come and go at odd hours. Cyros has a room a bit farther up; he’s staying with his mentor, Nieve, who he’s still studying with.

“See you tomorrow,” I say to Cyros, only realizing I’d interrupted a story he’d been telling when he gives me an odd look. “Sorry. Just want to get some rest. Long day, you know?”

“Yeah, sure,” he says. “Of course. Get some rest, okay? And let me know if you need help with anything for tomorrow’s job.”

“Will do,” I say, wearily giving him a two-fingered salute.

He smiles, waving back, but there’s worry in his eyes.

Cyros heads off as I let myself into my flat, the handle glowing blue as it activates with a bit of locking magic that’s similar to the permission system used in the Guild. The house didn’t come that way, but I bought a security charm from a Blackcloak dealer as soon as I could afford one. I dealt some serious damage to the first person who made the mistake of trying to jump me down here, and while no one has tried me since, I sleep better with the locking spell in place.

Inside, the windowless stone room is quiet and empty. Well, mostly empty. I’ve got a bed mat in one corner and a flameless cooking spell circle in the opposite. The less stuff you keep in your place, the lower the incentive is for someone to break in and try to steal anything. Besides: why leave anything here when I can take it all with me?

[Apothecary removed from Inventory.]

A giant mat of woven leather appears on the floor before me. It takes up almost the entire room. The Apothecary, as Echo has labeled it, is covered in hundreds of straps, each of which tightly bind different supplies to the spread. I’ve got everything from knives, to potion ingredients and equipment, to food, to clothes. Basically, everything I own that isn’t already on my person goes in here. It’s so big and unwieldy, I can’t really use it in a fight, so I have to prepare in advance, strapping any potions or weapons I might need to my belts and bandoliers. But having a personal Inventory space—even if it’s a space that requires me to remove and return everything I own at once—is one of my greatest assets.

I set about adding the new supplies I gathered from Zaquedyil into various pouches, bottles, and vials, strapping each one tightly to the leather mat. If anything isn’t packed securely enough, Echo stops recognizing it as part of the “Apothecary” and leaves it behind when I try to add the entire display back into my Inventory. Inconvenient, but as long as I have time to properly secure everything, easy to work around.

Once everything is packed away, I remove a pot, a chopping board, a knife, and a handful of meat, spices, and vegetables. I stick the Apothecary back in my Inventory as I sit down next to the cooking spell circle to start to prepare some dinner. I dice some veggies and toss them in the pot. The meat is raw, and will be cooked by the stew; nothing seems to go bad while it’s stored in the Inventory, but I still have to be mindful of how much time the Apothecary spends outside of it. I can stick some meat in there and stretch it for several weeks if I only remove the Apothecary for a few minutes at a time, but it becomes hard to keep track of how much the meat has actually aged, and the last thing I want to do is make myself sick. I don’t have time to be sick. I need to get stronger.

[Daily Role Requirement met,] Echo says as I pound out the meat and rub spices into it. I set these aside to sear—Iski would crucify me if she saw me dump them straight into the stew first. I smile a little at the thought, imagining the goblin’s aghast and disgusted face.

I already completed my daily cooking, so it must be past midnight, and I just satisfied my Requirement for the next day. That’s convenient—one less thing to think about tomorrow. That said, I hardly think about meeting my Role Requirement these days. I cook myself breakfast every day, no exceptions.

The smell of fireroot prickles my nose as I uncap the bottle and add some to the pot. My mouth waters as the rich, warm aroma permeates the room. Tension bleeds away from my limbs as I let the comforting, familiar smell wrap around me. It smells just like Iski and Gugora’s inn. This was their favorite meal to serve.

As the food bubbles away, I clean up, return my equipment and ingredients to my Inventory, and lean against the wall with a content sigh. Potion making can wait until tomorrow. Right now, I just need good food and a warm bed. I close my eyes, thinking of the wonderfully fluffy bed of the Starlight Inn. The flat bedroll here just doesn’t compare.

But the food tastes the same. Eventually, I ladle out a portion and blow over the surface of my bowl, its steam stinging my eyes. Once it’s cool enough to eat, I try a spoonful and let out a blissful sigh as my tastebuds clench from the rich, spicy tang. It’s perfect.

My throat knots. I cough as the fireroot stings my lungs, and blink as it waters my eyes. I swallow the soup down in large, burning gulps, trying to force the knot away. I sniff, and tears stream down my face as the broth continues to torment me. I devour the stew, like I haven’t eaten in years, and the more my throat feels tight and my eyes burn, the faster I scarf down the meal. When I’m done, I set the bowl aside, tuck my legs up to my chest, and bury my face in my knees. My pants dry my tears as I clench my teeth, holding down a sob.

It’s just perfect.
