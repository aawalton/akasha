At first, Ferris was hesitant to hear Foreman Carter put restrictions on giving him a slime. However, his fears were soon washed away. What the man really wanted was to ensure Ferris wouldn’t let the monster hurt anyone.

He accomplished this by personally drilling slime safety into Ferris.

The man put him through an entire orientation designed for new ranchers. It took hours, but Ferris got to see every part of the slime ranch in much more detail, and even assisted a little. He had to be extra careful with his crutch, but that wasn’t anything new. Foreman Carter spent half the time lecturing on safety, and the other half trying to change Ferris’ mind—not very subtly. Despite it all, he was enjoying himself. The ranchers’ camaraderie as they worked was infectious.

When it was all over, Foreman Carter took him off to the side. “Now, if you’re still dead set on this craziness…” He looked at Ferris, somewhat hopefully.

Ferris nodded. He didn’t care what anyone thought of slimes, he wanted his leg back.

“Then there is one final lesson. Follow me.”

The man took him over to an area designed to confine slimes. It was a smaller pit used for experiments and training. Earlier, as part of the orientation course, Ferris had helped wrangle a slime out of the pit and tie it up here to a pole. The green blob was still rolling around, probably looking for food or a way out.

Keeping a slime confined was as hard as it sounded. Their bodies could fit through any gap large enough for their core, which made it tricky.

Thankfully, the ranchers had long since developed plans to work around slime physiology. They took a long pole with a small basket, stuck it into the slime’s body, and scooped the core. It reminded Ferris of a mesh basket his parents used to dunk fried food in hot oil. The rancher then slowly lifted the pole, slime along with it. The basket had just enough area for the slime to hold onto, but if they moved too quickly the core would be pulled out of the body. It wouldn’t kill the slime, but it would take it a while to recover.

Once the slime was pulled out of the feeding pit, a small metal cage was carefully placed around its core. The cage was slightly larger than the core, about the size of a clenched fist. While the slime’s body was dangling down, the cage was latched shut around the core, then left to float inside their body. This cage had a chain connected, which extended out about as long as he was tall. They hooked the other end to a pole in the center of the training pit.

Of course, all the tools the slime ranch used needed to be protected against the slime’s acid. A normal piece of iron would simply be dissolved away inside their body. The chains, pole, and small cage were all forged by Adepts using magical materials, which strengthened them beyond mundane limits. The metal tools were fascinating, some appearing fragile despite their magical resilience. The chain’s links were only about as wide as his pinky, but they didn’t show any bending or flex when he pulled on them. None of the basic slimes at the ranch could eat through them.

Ferris had excitedly asked if the tools were enchanted, but Foreman Carter said no. It would have made them more expensive, with no real need.

Foreman Carter climbed down into the training pit, staying outside of the slime’s range. Ferris crawled down after him, curious what this ‘final lesson’ would be.

With the mixture of confidence and caution that comes from working for years at a dangerous job, the man walked up to the slime. The monster was just smaller than the size of the man’s head, which appeared average for ones that weren’t bloated on food.

“Never forget, slimes are dangerous,” he said, carefully grabbed the chain connected to the slime’s cage. The man pulled it up slightly, until half the cage was outside the monster’s body.

The slime noticed immediately and began rolling towards the disturbance. Foreman Carter deftly unlatched the cage with a single quick motion. Ferris knew the man had practiced this motion, because he’d made Ferris do the same at least twenty times.

“The slime’s acid takes a few seconds to start dissolving, which can be deadly if they take you by surprise,” he said.

Pulling the chain free, the man stepped back out of the slime’s path. It didn’t appear to notice and kept rolling in the same direction. Free of the chain, the monster seemed intent on escaping.

“But for all their danger and ability to shrug off bodily harm, they have a fatal flaw,” Foreman Carter said, walking towards the slime. The man lifted a boot, then brought it down with a stomp directly above the slime’s core.

Ferris couldn’t hear the core crack over the squishing sound, but as soon as the man’s boot landed the slime appeared to deflate. Their body, which had held itself together like a gelatinous ball, dropped down into a puddle.

“Destroy the core fast enough,” Foreman Carter said, kicking some of the slime off his boot, “and the acid loses all magical potency. Now, it’s little more than fertilizer.”

The man called for a bucket and shovel, which he used to clean up the remains. It was little more than some broken rocks and soaked dirt.

“You can have your damned slime, we have more than enough to go around. But you will have to keep it here until you can control it,” Foreman Carter said, then left Ferris to his thoughts.

He’d read that slimes needed to protect their core, but now he felt the book had understated the problem.

Gumly had been described as an invulnerable terror. As large as a horse, Janice’s familiar had simply rolled on top of any monsters. The size had helped protect Gumly’s core, but the true protection was an ability Janice had developed to surround it with a protective layer of manifested qi constructs. It was like the crystalized qi spear his uncle had created, but formed inside the slime’s body.

Ferris believed in his plan. Slimes may be vulnerable at the start, but with intelligent development they were better off than other familiars.

This was simply a reminder that he needed to be especially careful in the beginning.

With his resolve restored, Ferris sought out a rancher to help him retrieve another slime from the pit. They let him pick one out, but they all looked rather similar. He ended up choosing the smallest one he could find, hoping it would be easier to work with.

Once the slime was secured in the training pit, with their core bound by the magical cage and chain, Ferris considered how to proceed.

The bond needed a ‘willing’ monster, but to even try required physical contact. This was a problem because slimes instinctually ate anything that touched them. Watching the slime ranchers had given Ferris a plan. I just need to train my slime to only eat certain things. Ferris smiled at the thought. My slime, I like the sound of that.

Sitting down at the edge of the slime’s range, Ferris examined it up close. The slime wasn’t very big—larger than his hand, but slightly smaller than his head—he could see why people wouldn’t see slimes as a threat. The monster’s body was a translucent green with a dark green core floating inside, partially visible inside the metal cage. There were no eyes, mouth, or any other discernible organs. Ferris thought back to the drawings from the slime journal. He’d wondered if they were simplified sketches, but there really wasn’t much to a slime’s anatomy.

Ferris considered the setup of the slime ranch. To train the slime, he would need positive reinforcement, negative reinforcement, or both. For positive, he could use food or the berries the slimes seemed especially fond of.

For negative reinforcement, Ferris couldn’t think of anything he had. Hitting the slime’s body with a stick wouldn’t hurt it; the slime might not even notice. The slime journal claimed magic or special abilities could cause visible pain, but Ferris had neither—yet. Damaging the core would also hurt, but Ferris did not want to risk killing the slime or cracking the core he would bond. Perhaps something like fire could work, but that risked getting out of control and hurting himself. Also, I probably shouldn’t start off a familiar bond with torture.

Instead, Ferris intended to use positive reinforcement to train the slime. This was the vague recommended method from the journal on Janice and Gumly, after all—feed them until they like you. Faced with putting words into action, Ferris found the instructions to be severely lacking.

The slime was currently rolling around in a circle. They would first move as close to the stake holding the chain as possible, then circle around it gradually moving outwards. As soon they completed a full circle at the limit of the chain, they would start the whole process over again.

First, Ferris tested what the slime would react to. The ranchers had told him they only responded to physical touch, ignoring sounds or vision. He believed them, but it was worth verifying.

As expected, the ranchers were correct. The slime ignored anything Ferris did nearby, be it shouting or dancing right behind them. Ferris did make two discoveries though. First, the slime would move towards Ferris if he slammed a stick down on the ground, presumably chasing the vibrations. They didn’t appear to notice normal footsteps, but it was something. Second, any time Ferris left footprints in their path the slime would pause to investigate them.

Next, he wanted to know if he could get them to stay still. The ranchers had helpfully provided plenty of food: hundreds of small pieces of chopped vegetables in a bag, as well as five of the more desirable berries. Ferris was warned that additional berries would cost him. He was both surprised and grateful for how generous the ranchers were with their time, knowledge, resources, and even giving him the slime for free. Clan Hollowfield was larger and less personal than Ferris was used to… but they were still good people, working to help each other.

He waited until the slime rolled by, then drew an x on the ground at the limit of the chain’s range with his stick. When the slime rolled over that spot, they stopped to inspect the ground. As soon as it stopped, Ferris tossed a piece of food. The vegetable hit the green dome and sort of rippled through the slime’s outer layer, sinking a couple inches deep.

More ripples pulsed through the slime as they digested the food, followed by a frantic search for more. After a fruitless yet thorough examination of the area, the slime resolutely returned to a circling pattern.

Ferris drew another x, exactly on top of his previous one, and waited. Once again, the slime stopped on the x, and once again Ferris tossed them some food. The same dramatic search played out, and he waited for the slime to calm down.

On the third time, the slime started to catch on. Once they found the newly drawn x, they quivered with expectation. Ferris delivered the food, but only after waiting a couple seconds. Unfortunately, after receiving the meal the slime continued to search around for more.

The fourth time, Ferris made the slime wait even longer. He could almost feel its excitement as they found the x, but confusion at not receiving the reward. Ten long seconds passed, the slime swirling slightly in place, before he finally tossed the snack.

Finally, the slime didn’t immediately move away after digesting its food. It sat there, almost hesitantly. Ferris carefully counted out another ten seconds and gave another treat.

The slime rippled greater than ever before, immediately going to work consuming the reward. Ferris carefully counted out ten more seconds and continued to feed.

Ferris now had the slime staying still, but he had to constantly feed them. After a couple of cycles, Ferris started to lengthen the time between feedings. Bubbles started to appear within the slime, but they remained in place.

Once the rewards were spaced about a minute apart, he contemplated his next step. Foreman Carter had claimed slimes had ‘rocks for brains’ but Ferris was beginning to disagree on the specifics. Watching the slime respond to the training, Ferris could not help but acknowledge their ability to learn. The slime was smart enough to see patterns.

Even if Ferris disagreed with the rancher, the man’s experience was still valid. According to him, many attempts had been made to train a slime, and it worked… for about a day. You could spend hours teaching a slime to act perfectly, only to come back the next day having to start over. Slimes always reverted to wild behavior.

This meant Ferris would have to train the slime enough to accept the bond in a single day. Afterwards, the familiar should start to grow smarter. He wasn’t sure how, other than the vague statement that the monster’s spiritspace transformed into a soulspace. At least the observation journal on Janice and Gumly had not written about any trouble controlling her slime familiar.

Physical contact was required for the bond, so the next task would be to acclimate the slime to his touch… ideally without losing a hand. Foreman Carter had gone over slime safety regulations extensively. Rule number one was to never touch a slime with bare skin: exactly what Ferris intended to do.

As Foreman Carter had dramatically shown, the acid took a few seconds to start to burn. If Ferris was paying attention, he should be able to remove his hand before getting hurt.

Still, before physically touching he would train wearing gloves, and before gloves he would use a stick. The gloves were borrowed from the ranchers and could survive a typical slime’s acid for a second or two, mostly just the time it took for them to dissolve away. They weren’t magical like the chain, but that made them cheap enough for every rancher to have a pair.

Lifting his stick, Ferris waited until about ten seconds before the next reward was due, then touched the slime on the side. The slime immediately tried to eat the stick, which Ferris pulled back out of reach. The slime bubbled a bit, but then went back to the x. Unfortunately for the slime, Ferris did not give the reward when the time was up, causing the bubbles to flare dramatically.

The slime continued to stew, bubbles simmering, but resolutely stayed on the spot. A minute later Ferris gave a reward without trying the stick. As soon as the food returned, the bubbles vanished as if they had never been.

It took seven more cycles until the slime finally ignored the stick. Ferris wasn’t sure who was happier: him or the slime as they rippled in what he could only interpret as joy. Luckily, after something worked once, Ferris had no trouble getting the slime to repeat a trick.

Gradually Ferris escalated the stick training. He touched other sides, maintained contact for longer, and even started poking the slime. Surprisingly, the slime did not seem bothered at all. Each jab was rewarded with a very satisfying jiggle, and Ferris spent a little longer than strictly necessary playing around. If he pressed too hard, the stick would sink into the slime’s body. It required a light prod to get the best response.

Next, Ferris tried touching with a glove held up on his stick. The slime shifted a bit with the change of texture but left the glove alone. Soon he was rubbing the glove all over without difficulty, going as far as to put two gloves on two sticks to touch twice at once. He even tried lifting the slime up between the gloves. The slime didn’t like that, and quickly flowed back to the spot, but thankfully did not try to eat the gloves.

Eventually, Ferris put one of the gloves on his own hand. The slime was at the limit of the chain’s range, so he only needed to place his hand within the danger zone.

Reaching out, Ferris patted the slime on the side, but they showed no more reaction to the gloved hand than the gloved stick. It was a bit tricky to feed like this, but he managed with his free hand.

Soon, Ferris could think of nothing else left but to touch the slime with his bare hand. They seemed docile now, but they were a monster that wanted to strip the flesh from his bone, then melt the bones.

His missing leg ached.

Tentatively, between pats from the glove, he lightly touched with one finger. The slime immediately shifted at the new texture, but just like with the gloves didn’t try to eat it. Ferris pulled back his finger and waited for the next cycle.

Slowly, he acclimated the slime to his touch until he could lay his hand fully on them. He didn’t escalate the touch with his hand—no poking or rough handling—but he carefully left his palm on the slime for up to half a minute.

The slime felt… odd. Soft, warm, and slightly like a wet sponge. Strangely not slimy or oily, but still a bit damp. Ripples would pass just under the surface, which shifted around strangely. The slime felt solid, but he could see the food pass right through into the acid swirling below. The closest thing he could compare to was holding a bag of water filled with minnows, feeling them swimming around inside.

Ferris shivered a little, wondering if the slime was tasting him. With a thought, they could pull themself up his hand and engulf it. Only about an hour of training and a constant supply of treats held the slime back. The magical chain would stop the monster from reaching his head, but that wouldn’t help his hand.

The first requirement for the bond—physical contact—was met. Now he just needed the other two: time, and for the slime to not fight the bond. Time would allow Ferris to meditate and open his soul while touching the slime. For the other… he planned to bribe them with the berries.

Giving a reward every minute was helpful when teaching new things, but now Ferris started waiting longer and longer between each feeding. The slime would give that same slow bubble that Ferris saw as displeasure, but continued to wait for its treat. All the while, Ferris cautiously maintained physical contact.

Once he had the cycles up to two minutes, Ferris was hesitant to push any further. Two minutes was not long enough for him to meditate, but he thought of a different way. It would be a mistake to leave himself at the mercy of a monster for any period of time, so why risk it?

Ferris called over one of the nearby ranchers and asked for help. Word got around to Foreman Carter, who decided to assist personally.

“You want me to what now?” Asked the bald man, staring unhappily at Ferris hand. The hand that was breaking the rules, resting on the slime.

“Feed the slime a single treat every two minutes, and pull me away if they try to eat my hand.”

“And you will be…” Still not looking away from his hand.

“Meditating, unconscious to the world.”

“It’s a damn mistake,” were the foreman’s words, but he diligently took over the feeding and assigned one of the other ranchers to hold a rope they tied around Ferris’ waist. Apparently, it was against rules to distract a safety line operator with any other responsibility. There was probably an unpleasant story behind that rule.

Now came the tricky part: using the berries to entice the slime to accept the touch of his mind.

As calmly as he could while touching a monster, trusting in the rancher holding the rope, Ferris let his mind drift to meditation. It took longer than usual, not helped by the growing crowd of ranchers. Meditation was usually done behind closed doors, after all.

Despite the distractions, Ferris eventually found the calm emptiness where he could feel his soul. Normally he would embrace that feeling to enter his soulspace, but today he looked beyond. Following the feeling in his hand, he found a connection to a mental version of the slime’s core. Bonding was simple—for familiars, weapons, or anything with a core—just find the core with your mind, open your soul, and take it inside. Of course, if the monster resisted there was no way to move the core anywhere.

Gently, Ferris reached out a mental connection and brushed the core. As quickly as possible, he pulled back out of meditation and removed his hand from the slime.

Opening his eyes, the slime was sporadically swirling. The rancher had pulled him back at the odd behavior, but Ferris was smiling. The slime had felt something new, now it was time to bribe them.

Instead of the normal treat, Ferris gave the slime one of his five berries on the two-minute mark. Predictably, they burst out with happy ripples. He almost felt sorry as the ripples turned to bubbles when they received the normal treat on the next cycle. They were happy as could be with the vegetables, until they had a taste of berry. Ferris sighed at the thought.

Ferris used the glove to test the slime’s temperament, but the training held despite the occasional bubble. Now he just had to do it all again. Entering meditation was easier the second time, and he found the slime’s core faster. Instead of a light touch, Ferris wrapped the core in the mental equivalent of a hug.

After he woke up, the slime swirled oddly but did not attack. The second berry was received as excitedly as the first.

The third time, Ferris did more than touch: he attempted to move the mental core. The slime resisted the core being moved, and Ferris in turn withheld the reward. The bubbles when they did not receive a berry made it look like the slime was boiling.

On the next attempt, the slime allowed the core to be moved… grudgingly and slowly. The ripples on receiving the third berry were somehow the greatest yet.

After that, Ferris was able to move the core easily. He did stop halfway to reward the fourth berry for safety, but the slime was content to let Ferris’ mind drag the mental representation of the core around all he wanted.

Arriving at his soul—slime core in tow—a giddy excitement came over Ferris. He had dreamed of this moment all his life. It wasn’t like he’d imagined, but he’d still beaten the odds.

Ferris felt like a blossoming flower as he pulled the core inside, bonding it to his soul.
