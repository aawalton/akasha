Opening his eyes, Luke felt like a new man. It was hard to pinpoint what had changed, but the amount of control he’d asserted over himself and his own destiny brought a sense of accomplishment. Almost like a middle finger to the ones outside looking, the ones who took his levels. If he pushed what he’d learned a little further, might he not be able to push back if the same thing were to happen again? It was a small chance. He realized that much, but it was larger now than it had been a few moments ago, before he decided to take charge.

Hannah: “Luke?”

Luke: “What’s up?”

Hannah: “Where did you go?”

Luke: “What do you mean?”

Hannah: “I’ve been practicing, kind of, and I was sort of able to get a sense for your location after hours of almost giving up. Then you disappeared.”

Hannah: “You’ve been gone a while.”

Luke: “Gone?”

He looked up at the sun, but the sky was overcast, so it was difficult to tell the time of day. Society had better get its act together soon, because he was fed up with not having access to information about the outside world… or the time! Stupid semi-apocalypse.

Luke: “I’ve been meditating. Sort of.”

Hannah: “That makes you fall out of the system?”

Luke: “Not that I noticed.”

Hannah: “Weird. Oh well, at least you aren’t dead.”

Luke: “How did the training go? Can you reach into the weave?”

Hannah: “No. It feels kind of impossible. Frustrating. I’m going out to deal with that frustration now.”

Luke: “Oh?”

Hannah: “Dungeon with Ray, Lemira, and a couple of others. I’d ask you to join, but you’d mess up the experience gain with your high level.”

Luke: “Right. I reached Level 50 dealing with the void thing.”

Hannah: “Lucky bastard.”

Luke: “Go have fun and catch up!”

Hannah: “Double bastard. Just you wait!”

Luke checked in with Milla to see if they needed help with healing, since a lot of members of The Band were dungeon diving almost non-stop. They did not. Lana, being an Architect, had created a space within the guild where healing was possible. Before the discovery of dungeonization, or whatever they wanted to call it, Luke had been the one Integrated who could heal people outside of dungeons. Now that advantage was gone. Most people didn’t have access to an Architect, and it still only worked with injuries sustained by monsters or other Integrated, but it meant the guild didn’t need him on standby. It was nice, he decided, ignoring the small, unreasonable voice that whispered they didn’t need him as much anymore.

His sister was very interested in hearing more about what Guide had shared and Luke’s new job as one of Earth’s protectors from the void. Since this also meant he was supposed to be some sort of spokesperson for their system once The Greater System opened up to them, she wondered how that would play out with the guild alliance thing she was setting up and whatever governments found their footing again now that the threat of the Singers had passed and they had information to bring modernity back using monster cores. In answer to that, Luke shrugged. That sort of political shenanigans was so far outside his sphere of interest that he couldn’t even work up enough energy to fake curiosity, leaving Milla irritated before their conversation was over.

With Smudge jumping around and smelling every rock, piece of glass, and broken artefact of what was once New York, and his friends busy elsewhere, Luke drew in a deep breath and got to his feet. Yes, the lure of leveling his profession up to rank four was still there, but he needed to move a little after what he’d just been through, or risk getting stiff enough to pull a muscle when he did eventually get up. While the idea of going into a dungeon did little to fill him with enthusiasm, it was a good way of testing what he was capable of against proper monsters, and not demons or Singers. It had been a while, and he’d undergone quite a few changes. Also, a portal had just opened up on the next street over, and someone needed to get on the job of closing it.

It wasn’t likely the whole city would be rebuilt. The resource costs for that would be staggering, and at the moment there wasn’t even enough of a government to begin planning such a massive undertaking. Even so, it wouldn’t do to have monsters roaming the streets. From what Luke could tell, there were no other Integrated making for the portal. Walking up to it, still affected by the ordeal he’d just put himself through, he sighed. Going through yet another dungeon was not what he wanted.

With motes of light drifting across his vision, Luke sighed again. He was about to step through when a thought struck him. Maybe he wouldn’t need to enter after all. Instead, he closed his eyes and focused. Like with all other portals, a thread connected it to somewhere far beyond the borders of Earth’s system. Cutting that thread would be a simple thing. That should take care of the problem, shouldn’t it? Sure, he wouldn’t be getting experience, but no portal meant no monsters coming out to blunder about the place and put whoever might still be around in danger. Perfect.

Luke reached out with a thread, marveling at the ease with which he used the skill, how natural it felt compared to before.

Don’t.

He blinked. “Guide?”

The portal is there for a reason.

“Then you better show yourself and explain, or I’m cutting this thing,” Luke said, hovering near the portal thread as he added a Needle for good measure.

“Blackmail?” Guide asked. This time, his words sounded out loud instead of being beamed into Luke’s head.

Luke turned, opened his eyes, and shrugged as he let the thread dissolve. “I have a flair for drama.”

“You don’t,” Guide said.

“How about you stop looking like my old teacher?”

“How would you like me to look?”

“Like yourself.”

“That is impossible.”

Luke frowned. “Why?”

“I have no memory of a corporeal form.”

The silence stretched.

“Um,” Luke began. “That sucks. But what does that mean exactly? Please tell me you’re not some computer program.” He stepped forward and tapped Guide’s shoulder with a finger. It was solid.

“I am sentient, and at one point, I was born from a mother.”

“Cool. How long ago was that, then?”

Guide frowned ever so slightly. “I do not recall. Records state that my age was unknown when I stepped into my current role.”

A gentle throbbing behind Luke’s right eye when he considered what this being’s past looked like told him it was best to move on from the subject. “Why not close the portal?”

“I am unable to divulge…”

“Cut the shit. You made me one of the guardians, or whatever. That means I get information.”

Guide seemed to consider that for a moment, then came to a decision Luke, in all honesty, didn’t expect. “You’re right.”

“I am?” Luke asked, catching himself. “I am.”

"Would you like to return to my... office to continue this conversation?"

Luke looked around at the surrounding devastation. "I'm good right here."

"Very well," Guide began as a flash of physical strain crossed his face, almost as if he pushed against some hidden barrier. "The dungeons are a product of the void."

That single comment set off a fire in Luke's head, and questions raced in his mind, but all he could think to ask was, "How do you mean?"

"The worlds you enter through these portals are lost systems. Once, they were like Earth. Some were new, like here, but most were more advanced in their integration, their Integrated more powerful. That power did not stop the void from consuming them. What you perceive is a mere shadow of what once was."

Luke frowned. "And the monsters?"

"Creatures of the void wearing the past as a second skin."

"But that doesn't make sense," Luke argued. "What about the people? Like the Lilineese, or, you know, the Hollows? Not everything attacks you. Also, those refugees we took in from the ants? They're not void... things." He hesitated. "Are they?"

"They are not. Past Integrated can exist in a way in dungeons, but it is rare. They mirror those who lived, but no longer do so. Other species can survive as well, as with the Hollows. Not all dungeons are of the void. Some act as prisons or containment. Spaces kept safe by The Greater System despite no Integrated remaining. Or in your refugee scenario, some Integrated remaining."

"Prisons. Like with Relian?"

"That... man is a special case, but at the core of it, yes."

Luke narrowed his eyes. "Special case, how? Did he survive the Singer system destroying itself?" He chuckled. "What am I asking? Of course he did."

"While I am able to share some information with you, there is much I must keep to myself. For now."

Luke ran a hand through his hair and then rubbed at his eyes, looking up as if searching for answers in the overcast sky above. "So, what? We kill void creatures that dress up as monsters, and that closes the gate? That gives us experience. Or you give us experience, I guess? Why?"

"It is a complex system working on a precarious balance," Guide said. "Experience is assigned to monsters and to the quest in dungeons based on difficulty and the dungeon's volatility. The Greater System does not award experience in and of itself."

"But it does take it away. Or 'The Council' does."

"There are no more than a handful of recorded events of that nature ever taking place. In your instance, the experience was not yours to begin with, since you did not, in fact, initiate the end of the system, nor did the Singer who launched the attack."

"I don't get it."

"The blast of energy, far more powerful than what that system should have been allowed to harness, breached the protections. That is dangerous but not lethal to a system."

"The damn weave unraveled."

"The void was waiting and struck with far more precision and control than what it is known for, and the assigned protector was not present in the system at that time."

Luke shook his head. "Seems like bad timing to be away."

"She was pulled away for questioning. But yes, I agree. It was poor timing."

"And the void just jumped in."

"In a sense, yes."

"That sounds... almost engineered. Too chaotic for chance, especially with the void ready for an attack."

"Probability is a fickle mistress," Guide said, the shadow of a smile at the corners of his mouth.

"Relian," Luke whispered. Then he looked up. "He was involved somehow! I'm right, aren't I?"

"I am not at..."

"You can't talk about it, yeah. I get it. Well, I don't get it, but whatever."

Luke considered what he'd learned. A lot, but also not much. "So the dungeons is us fighting the void?"

"On a small scale and under controlled conditions, yes."

"Guess that explains The Deep Dweller, sort of."

Guide remained silent.

"It's a playground for us to level up?"

"Yes and no. Attacks from the void are ceaseless and ever-present. Pressure mounts, and allowing some of that pressure into what you call dungeons is the best method we have found for Integrated to learn combat and grow in various ways. Some graduate from that initial stage, like you, and can take part in more direct action. Others, like some of your friends, might never get there. That does not mean their efforts are without merit. A powerful Integrated working tirelessly to complete dungeons offers a great deal of relief and room to navigate for those operating in 'the weave' as you call it."

"But... hold on... What I'm able to do is not because of my level. I get that. So, what is it? It can't just be willpower."

"It's not," Guide agreed. "And truth be told, we are not sure."

"You're joking, right? You don't know?"

Guide held up a hand. "Most agree it has to do with motivation and curiosity. Willpower plays its part, but there is also a quality of sharing a bond of sorts with The Greater System, one that is difficult to quantify."

"So what you're saying is, I shouldn't close dungeons without completing them because then they burst like an infected wound and spray gunk all over you guys?"

"That is a colorful way of putting it. Your main task is as I explained in our previous meeting, but completing dungeons is still a great help to The Greater System and yourself when time permits."

"Fine," Luke said with a sigh. "I'll do the damn dungeon."
