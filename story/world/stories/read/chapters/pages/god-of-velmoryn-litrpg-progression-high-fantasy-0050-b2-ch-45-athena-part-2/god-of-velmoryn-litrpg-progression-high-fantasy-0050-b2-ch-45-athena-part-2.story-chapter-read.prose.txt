“My apologies. I forgot to mention that the dark elf I found is a vampire. I hope that poor creature still interests you.”

A vampire?!

Finding the location of any velmoryn wasn’t something Athena could outdo me in, but if they were vampires… that changed everything. I wasn’t certain at this point whether vampirism was considered a race or a class, but if becoming a vampire meant they were no longer considered the race they had been before, then it all made sense. Only under that condition could Athena have found a velmoryn I had no connection to.

“You’ve piqued my curiosity,” I said. I hoped she would reveal the details before presenting her proposal. I would likely reject her offer and I didn’t want to ruin my chances of finding this vampire velmoryn.

“That pleases me. But unfortunately, I couldn’t find much about her. And I wouldn’t dare share speculation or unconfirmed tales,” Athena said calmly. Although as she spoke, her fingers kept nervously folding and unfolding the edge of her dress. “However, I know for certain that she is over three hundred years old. She lives among humans now and recently married a high noble. That is how we found her.”

Taking a pause, she extended her hand, and a small vial appeared atop her palm. Inside the transparent vessel, a single drop of red blood shimmered faintly.

“I obtained her blood so that Your Lordship may easily find her, even if she attempts to hide from your sight.”

She made the vial float toward the God of Velmoryn seated on the throne.

Accepting it, I reconsidered Athena once more. This time, I genuinely couldn’t decide how I felt about her. Her believer approaching Aurelion had been nothing but a careless move. Her willingness to enter my dominion only doubled down on that. And offering a tribute before securing what she wanted was simply too much.

Either she was incredibly gullible and thoughtless, to put it mildly, or she was a master manipulator and everything that had happened so far was part of a larger plan.

Guidance.

[A Droplet of Blood]

Blood is a potent medium. It may be used to enforce a divine contract, uncover secrets, or trace the location of its owner. The scope of what can be achieved depends on the wielder’s ability and the strength of the one from whom the blood was taken.

“Thank you. I will put it to good use,” I said, moving the vial outside my dominion. Just in case she changed her mind after I rejected her offer. “Now tell me - what is it that you wish to propose?”

“I see,” Athena chuckled softly. “The rumors about the God of Elves being a deity of few words turn out to be true. And here I was hoping we would get to know each other. I have so many questions for You, after all…”

She feigned regret, and I chose to play along.

“You came with a present. It’s only polite that I also show goodwill. Ask,” I said, making the God of Velmoryn on the throne adopt a more comfortable position.

“Thank You!” she clapped lightly. “None of the humans have encountered elves for centuries. Some even wondered if they went… someplace else.”

“One’s inability to see certain things doesn’t mean what they seek isn’t there,” I replied. “It only means they need to look better, or perhaps they are not fated to succeed.”

“That is true,” Athena muttered. She paused before asking another question. “Even the traders who made their living thanks to elves, selling fruits and plants, have not seen them. Families were ruined. Entire generations, once thought to have bright futures, ended up in the streets after their fathers failed to adapt…”

“Life is unpredictable. Even for deities.” The God of Velmoryn tapped on the armrest with its index finger.

Athena froze. She considered what I might have meant.

“May I ask if the trade relationship can be reestablished?” she asked carefully.

“With whom?” I returned.

“With… with the elves, of course,” she stammered.

“Are other deities involved in such trivial matters of the mortal world?” I asked again, deliberately avoiding a direct answer. I still hadn’t decided whether I wanted to continue disguising myself as the God of Elves.

In truth, I was surprised Athena didn’t know the Goddess had perished. Elven Authority was one of the most coveted, after all. That was the real reason for my hesitation. If Athena told other deities she had met the God of Elves, there was a good chance I would become their target. Unless someone perceptive saw through the lie, in which case I would be exposed, and my image would be forever ruined.

“Well, human deities are,” Athena replied at last. “We have no choice but to help our believers because of humanity’s tendency to switch faiths quickly. They are cynical, but lovely creatures…”

Her voice softened, a gentle smile spreading across her face.

“Luckily,” she continued, her smile brightening, “my powers make me a desirable deity both among mortals and gods.”

Among gods?!

I was intrigued, but I remained silent.

“My power guarantees that a woman will become pregnant after intercourse,” she said, a faint blush warming her cheeks. “For mortals, having a beautiful Goddess who actually helps them is a true blessing. While for deities, I provide a way to increase the number of their believers. No matter who they are, as long as they decide to place their faith in me, they always win. Some more so than others…”

As she spoke, countless possibilities ran through my mind. Not only could her ability be extremely useful to me, but the simple fact that she was already cooperating with other deities opened an entirely new avenue. One I had never even considered before. Gods helping each other, not only in moments of need, but by leveraging their unique powers as well.

And Athena’s ability… for a small race like velmoryns, it would be extraordinarily useful. It would practically solve my greatest problem. However, the very first thing that crossed my mind was my mythical creatures. If her power truly guaranteed progeny, then repopulating their species would be possible. So many possibilities…

Forcing myself to refocus on the goddess standing before my manifestation, I asked the question that concerned me the most.

“What do you ask in exchange for your services?”

“Fifty percent of the initial divinity a god receives from progeny, and full compensation for the divine energy I expend using my skill,” Athena replied, her voice turning serious. “If the deity has the means to continue harvesting divinity from them afterward, I will not claim any share of that. I should also mention that there is a minuscule chance that female offspring will be born bearing my mark. However, I will not oppose them being converted to their patron god’s faith, even if force is used.”

There it is. The true nature of gods.

The way she had spoken about humans earlier had almost made me think she cared for her believers the same way the Goddess had. Not anymore. Perhaps she cared for certain individuals, or only for her human followers, but she was nothing like Sophia. And that wasn’t necessarily a bad thing for me. As long as her motivations were grounded in profit and rationality, her actions would be predictable.

Still, that “minuscule chance” bothered me.

“How much divinity do you spend to use your skill?” I asked. “And more importantly, what exactly do you mean by minuscule?”

“Unfortunately, it’s impossible to know the cost without using the skill first. But I always form divine contracts that guarantee I only take as much as I spend,” she responded. “Your Lordship, I should also mention that using my skill on timeless creatures is impossible. I fear I will not be of help with elves... However, if dark elves are also of interest to you…”

Her green eyes landed on the God of Velmoryn. She truly hadn’t realized it was nothing more than a puppet on the throne.

“And what about my second question? What is the probability of newborns bearing your mark?” I repeated, this time with far less interest.

As she spoke, I realized that I shouldn’t care whether the newborns carried her mark or not. The velmoryns were under my absolute control. I was confident I could erase her mark even while the new generation was still in the embryonic stage. If I had managed to erase the God of Elves’ mark from Karla, removing Athena’s would be no different.

“Less than 1%.”

“I would be interested in such cooperation,” I responded after a brief pause. I waited for her to produce a contract. Instead, I noticed her growing restless.

“Your Lordship,” she said, her voice uncertain. “May I see your divinity?”

What the hell? What kind of request even is that?

I quickly considered why she might ask such a thing. What could she possibly gain by seeing my divinity?

But I couldn’t come up with a single explanation.

In the end, I decided there was no point in refusing. As soon as I made up my mind, a crimson orb materialized in front of her, bright and violent.

Suddenly, she shot her hand toward it and pierced it.

[Warning: The Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility is attempting to assimilate your divinity!][Warning: Your Radiance has overwhelmed the Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility!]

With a low gasp, she yanked her hand back. Her skin sizzled and steamed as it struggled to heal against remnants of my power that continued to burn her arm.

“I apologize, Your Lordship.” She fell on the floor, kneeling. But I didn’t miss a strangely happy smile on her face.

[The Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility has given you 10 Divinity Points!]

“Your Radiance…” she muttered as she raised her head. Her eyes settled on the God of Velmoryn. “Please… please help me against the God of Justice…”
