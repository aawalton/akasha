Chapter 391

As they had all expect, Heath passed his final smithing exam with flying colors, and could now finally call himself a full-fledged smith.

Perhaps he wasn’t exactly one of those once in a hundred years type of geniuses, but the guy obviously had an eye that lent itself to that kind of work, and, despite his ever-expanding size, he had hands and fingers that were surprisingly dexterous and could follow along just fine as well.

“Binny!” he shouted when he saw the two of them push through to the front of the crowd that had been watching him put the final touches on the piece he had been preparing for the last few days. Kikah had passed just a few months before, so he, a number of other smiths, as well as the suppliers and distributor Heath had formed friendships with over the years, had all come to see his crowning moment.

The massive smile plastered on his lips seemed to refuse to shrink for even a moment. It was infectious.

Bin let herself be swept up into his massive arms. “That’s the prettiest sword I’ve ever seen, Uncle Heath!”

The gigantic smith spun her around. “Oh, you know just what to say, don’t you?”

“I know how to get what I want.”

He stopped and looked at her. “And what do you want?”

“A knife.”

For a moment he just stared, but then he exploded with laughter. “A knife! Hah! There are several S-rankers and an X-ranker who would rip my head off if they found out I’d given you a weapon…” he trailed off before leaning in, a hand covering his mouth. “So, you’ll have to make sure they don’t find out.” The glint in his eye was reflected in her own.

“I am a master of subterfuge, Uncle Heath.”

“Wow. Big word.”

“I got you something. As a gift to congratulate you for becoming a real smith.”

“Oh?” If he had had a tail, it would probably have been wagging.

She fished a small package out of the near depthless bag of holding strapped securely to her waist. It was roundish and wrapped neatly in brown paper.

Bin’s hands trembled as she untied a little piece of frayed cord and unwrapped the gift.

“What’s this?” the large man asked as she placed the cream puff gingerly on his upturned palm. Chocolate-infused cream practically oozed out of it, like an ice cream melting in the sun. “A desert?”

Bin struggled to get the words out. “My dad made it.”

Heath froze, eyes fixed on the puff, and swallowed hard. “Oh, little Binny. How many do you have?”

“Three left now.”

He wrapped his thick fingers around her wrist and opened her fingers gently. “This is not for me to take, Bin,” he said and plopped the cream puff into her hand.

“No, I don’t want it!” she said, raising her voice as a sudden wave of emotion seized her.

“You should keep what you have left of him,” the large man said with a gentle smile.

“If this is all I have left of him, then what’s even the point of it?” Tears welled up in her eyes and she turned away. There were way too many people here.

Rather than face Uncle Heath and receive the love she knew he would shower her with, Bin ran.

There wasn’t anything like a goal in her mind. All she knew was that she needed to get away. Away from the embarrassment and away from her emotions. Anywhere else would do.

She ran for a solid half hour, countless faces rushing by, but immediately forgotten. It wasn’t fair. All of these people were living normally, without her grief and sorrow. They didn’t even know how she was feeling. Something akin to resentment welled up in her.

“Hey!” Strong fingers grasped her arm tightly, stopping her dead in her tracks. “You think you can just elbow someone without a word of apology, huh?”

She looked up, fury coursing through her. The guy was enormous. Probably at least eight times her weight. It was a Gohkamorian.

They hadn’t done anything at all to Earth since her father’s disappearance. Many had thought they would enact some sort of revenge after he was gone, but they never did. Eik had managed to eradicate the entire top leadership, leaving only a few middle-rank military people and administrators, and they were still collecting the pieces of their society.

They simply weren’t in a place to do anything to Earth, and much less to Eik’s family, protected as they were by Chop the Blade and multiple high-rank S-rankers and their allies.

Even without Eik, Bin’s family remained the most untouchable people in the Unified Mass. The greatest elites of the greatest Prime Civilizations had weaker allies than Bin. Even right now, she was sure someone was watching her every move. But they would not step in unless absolutely necessary.

“Let go of me, you big, fat bastard!” she shouted and jerked her arm hard. It didn’t budge a single centimeter. His arm might as well have been wrought steel for all the good her struggle did her.

Changing strategies, she clenched her fist and hammered in a frighteningly precise nut shot, but even that did nothing.

By this point, passersby had noticed the confrontation and stepped in to defend Bin. It was obviously an unbalanced match-up.

“I did nothing!” the Gohkamorian protested, loosening his grip on her arm. Only when he did, did she realize that her circulation had been totally cut off by his hand. And while his attention was on the others, she ran.

Even here, where nobody knew who she was, and none of her uncles or grandpas were with her, her father’s protection remained.

When unease and fear of the Lord of the Moon had been at its peak, her father had gotten up in front of the entirety of Gimleh and expressed his will to protect them all from harm. Living Manifestations had concealed themselves throughout the city in order to be able to react at a moment’s notice if something should happen. And at some point, he had updated those orders to include dangerous conflict among people in Gimleh.

Although many had protested, claiming it to be an infringement on personal freedom and an act of excessive surveillance, it had ended up effectively ending the power harassment by less-than-benevolent Awakened that had otherwise been an accepted part of life for a vast majority of the population.

Unlike nearly all other people in power, her father had been so wealthy that no amount of bribe money could sway him, and so infinitely mighty that the promise of powerful allies had done nothing but anger him. He had been the perfect protector. Bin knew that.

And that willingness to protect had rubbed off on the people who had seen their worries eased. It wasn’t universal, of course, and it wasn’t as effective as before, but something had definitely happened. People stepped in in defense of one another to an arguably greater degree than before.

She ran for another ten minutes before stopping, breath coming in ragged gasps. That sprint had been fueled by emotion rather than stamina. Where had she even ended up?

Head on a swivel, she scanned the cityscape for anything she recognized, but there was nothing. Neither storefronts, nor landmarks rang any bells. She started walking back in the general direction she had come.

No more than five minutes later, she recognized something. The facade of the cafe over there was familiar. The offensively vibrant red-green-blue striped awning was hard to forget. That meant the fracture hall where Mikla was in charge was just around the corner.

No matter how she twisted and turned it, she had overreacted with Uncle Heath. It was embarrassing. But if she could catch a ride through a custom fracture straight to the house, without passing the Forest fracture hub, she could avoid Mom’s questions.

As she approached the entrance, she wiped her eyes and forced a beaming smile onto her lips. The last thing she needed was for Mikla to start asking questions as well.

“Uncle Mikla!” she called as she stepped through the automatic doors.

“Binny!” A man who was obviously not Mikla stood waiting.

She froze.

“Uncle… Heath?” she stumbled. “What are you doing here? Philip?” she asked when she noticed Philip looking at her nervously. He had been studying some kind of scanning device off to the side. The guy loved that kind of thing. He might like gadgets and tech more than people.

“Hey, Bin,” Mikla called from the operating room and offered a friendly wave.

Perhaps a direct ride to the house wasn’t in the cards today.

The enormous smith sent her a disapproving eye and crossed his meaty arms over his chest. “What the hell did you think you were doing? You can’t just run off on your own! Do you have any idea what kind of low-lives roam the streets of Gimleh?”

“I took care of it, Uncle Heath,” she said with a groan.

His eyes crackled. “Took care of what, exactly?”

Ah, crap…

“How did you find me anyway?” she asked.

Heath rolled his eyes. “I know what direction you fled in, so it’s pretty obvious that you would come here. You don’t want to go through the fracture hall and have to talk to Ihasu, am I right?”

“N… No.” Nail, meet head.

The smith sighed as Philip wandered over to stand next to her. “How about we go back to the workshop, eh, Binny?” he suggested. “We still haven’t eaten any of the cake Mikey brought, and he’s waiting for you to come back.”

She had to clench her teeth to hold back the tears.

“And extra large pieces for the two kids about to go through their growth spurts,” Michael said as he handed Bin and Philip a plate of cake each.

“Thanks, Uncle Mike.” After all that running, she was starving.

“By the way, did I ever tell you? I’m opening my own clinic!”

“You are?” Bin exclaimed excitedly. “When?”

The healer smiled. “Oh, not for a while yet. It takes time to set up that kind of stuff. Ideally, I would also need a few more people to make it all go around. Say, you two, you wouldn’t happen to be looking for part-time jobs, would you?”

“That’s child labor, Uncle Mike…” she deadpanned.

“O-Oh, right… I suppose it would be,” he said nervously.

Heath put an arm around his friend’s shoulder. “This guy is having trouble filling his roster. I think he’s letting his desperation shine through a little too much.”

Michael could only laugh sheepishly. “I have deadlines.”

“Why don’t you just use some of my dad’s connection?” Bin asked.

“Oh, I-I couldn’t…”

“He could,” Heath broke in. “I’ve told him a million times. But he doesn’t like the thought of it.”

Bin understood that completely.

“I’ve actually decided to expand my recruitment efforts a bit.”

“Oh, yeah?” Heath asked.

“Yes. You know that Japanese settlement? The large one. Apparently, they actually have a bit of an excess of healers now that things have calmed down a bit on Earth.”

“No kidding!”

“I’m actually going there tomorrow.”

Bin beamed at that. “Can I come?”
