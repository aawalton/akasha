The shift in the room wasn’t loud, but Oswald felt it all the same. The scrape of wood legs dragging across the floor followed by chairs being pushed back as men stood. Their eyes held no sympathy for Merrick, just the gleam of restless anger. The kind that didn’t care who the target was, so long as someone bled. A few others, less eager for broken bones, slipped quietly toward the exit.

Behind the bar, the barkeep didn’t so much as twitch. He leaned against the wall with his arms folded, watching like a man who’d seen enough fights to know when not to interrupt.

Nonetheless, Oswald knew he had to get his hands dirty, at least a little bit. Let’s get this over with.

The first man came at him hard. He was broad-shouldered and top-heavy, with a gut that hung over his belt. His fist cut through the air in a messy arc, a wild right hook fueled more by alcohol than precision.

Oswald ducked low, feeling the breeze of the swing whip past his cheek. He drove his elbow into the man’s ribs with a solid thud, knocking the air from the brute’s lungs in a sharp grunt. As the man staggered, Oswald hooked a foot behind his ankle and shoved. The drunk went down hard, crashing into a table leg with a weighty crack that sent a nearby stool clattering across the floor.

Another pair moved in fast, one of them lunging with an empty tankard clenched like a weapon. Oswald side-stepped smoothly, catching sight of a half-full bottle sitting on the edge of a table. He snatched it up mid-step and shattered it across the first man's face. Glass exploded in a spray of shards and ale, the man’s scream muffled by his own hands flying to his eyes.

The second one tried to capitalize on the moment, charging low like he thought he could bowl Oswald over. But Oswald had already pivoted, catching the movement in the corner of his eye.

Too slow.

He slammed a knee into the man’s gut, doubling him over with a strangled gasp. Then, Oswald grabbed the front of his coat and hurled him backward. The man flew into a nearby table, which splintered beneath his weight. Dice scattered like pebbles, tankards overturned, and cards fluttered through the air as the table collapsed in a heap of limbs and broken furniture.

A fourth attacker stepped into the fray, a man with pockmarked skin and teeth the color of old parchment. He raised a chair over his head like a club, eyes wide and reckless.

Oswald surged forward before the chair could swing down, closing the distance in a blink. His palm slammed into the man’s throat, cutting off his breath with a sharp wheeze, then followed with a quick jab to the side of the temple. The man dropped fast, crumpling beside a knocked-over stool.

Behind him, glass shattered. Oswald twisted just in time to catch a fifth man stepping in gripping the jagged neck of a broken bottle like he meant to carve Oswald’s spine out. No, you don’t.

Oswald grabbed a heavy iron tankard off a nearby table and flung it sidearm with practiced force. It spun once and struck the man square across the bridge of his nose with a dull, wet thunk. Blood sprayed as he staggered, yelping, one hand flying to his face.

Immediately after, Oswald dashed and grabbed the man by the front of his tunic before slamming him into a stack of overturned chairs. One snapped beneath the impact, the splinters skittering across the floor as the man tumbled down, groaning and cursing with more confusion than fight left in him.

After that, nobody dared to go against Oswald anymore. The whole bar turned eerily quiet.

What few patrons remained just pretended to ignore everything. They made sure to avoid Oswald's gaze, which worked well enough for him.

Now, it was time for him to check on Merrick. The bastard was still on the floor, just starting to push himself up with one elbow.

A thin stream of blood leaked from the corner of his mouth, trailing down into the stubble on his chin. His other hand rubbed at his jaw, the stunned look on his face said he hadn’t expected any of this.

Oswald walked right in front of the man. "Now, you’re gonna take me to that cave."

Merrick wiped the blood from his lip, looking around the wreckage of the tavern.A lof of men were slumped over tables, groaning on the floor, or out cold where they'd dropped. The only one still standing was Oswald, and the surprisingly indifferent bartender.

"Y’know, now that I think about it, I'd be happy to take you to the cave. No trouble at all."

Ever since the bar fight, Merrick was very quiet. Oswald followed him from behind, making sure the bastard didn't get any funny ideas as the two of them headed for the cave.

Merrick's boots crunching against the damp ground, his coat swaying with each step. Every so often, he glanced over his shoulder, shooting a glance toward Oswald before snapping his head forward again.

The trees thickened as they pressed on, huddled so close together that sunlight barely cut through. The path ahead littered with twisted roots and moss-covered stones slick with moisture.

Soon, the two broke through the last thicket of trees, stepping into a clearing. Before them, a waterfall spilled from the rock face, its waters crashing down into a shadowed pool below.

Merrick stopped just short of the water’s edge and jerked a thumb toward the waterfall. "There, that’s your cave."

Oswald stared at him. Then at the waterfall. Then back at Merrick. "You fuckin’ with me?"

Merrick let out a nervous chuckle, rubbing the back of his neck. "No joke, boy. That’s the place. Right behind the water."

"Then you go in first."

"Fine. Not like I got anythin' to hide."

His boots squelched against the damp earth as he stepped forward. The ground sloped gently toward the falls, every step sinking slightly into the spongy moss and mud. \

The curtain of water soon swallowed Merrick whole. Only then did Oswald follow.

Cold slammed into Oswald like a wall, soaking him from head to toe in a heartbeat. The water struck hard, relentlessly pounding down across his back and head like it wanted to knock him flat. His breath caught from the sudden chill, but he pushed through, one hand pressed against the slick stone for balance.

The roar of the waterfall grew muffled as he emerged into the space beyond. But Oswald couldn't help but stand in disbelief. There was nothing.

All he could see was a hollow space, maybe a few paces deep, carved out behind the falls. However, there was no passage, no tunnel, no cave. Just a wet dead end.

Merrick stood still, water dripping from his tangled hair and soaked coat. His eyes were wide, darting over every inch of the wall as though willing it to open. "No… no, no, no… This ain’t right, I swear , there was a cave here! I took folks in, I know I did!"

Oswald stared at him. He’s either a fool… or he thinks I am.

Patience wearing thin, Oswald stepped forward and grabbed Merrick by the front of his coat. The soaked fabric bunched in his fists as he shoved the man backward, slamming him against the nearest wall of the hollow. Water streamed down around them, dripping from the ceiling and pooling at their feet.

"You lied to me once. I ain’t givin’ you a second try. So you better start bein' honest." Oswald threatened.

Merrick’s chest heaved as he struggled to piece together something that would get him out of this. "I ain’t lying! I don’t know what happened! The entrance was here! Swear it on my own damn life!"

Oswald studied him. Merrick looked genuinely lost, like something had been ripped out from under him. Is he still lying? Or maybe…

Suddenly, Oswald drove his fist into the side of Merrick’s head. The man let out a strangled grunt before his body went slack, unconscious.

Oswald stepped back, taking a good look at the stone blocking his path. As much as he disliked Merrick, he didn't seem like he was lying.

And so, Oswald's mind went back to his Animus. He recalled that he had the [True Sight] ability, which should allow him to see things he normally can't. He hadn’t used it much, but if there was ever a time for it, this was it.

Oswald reached up and slipped the eyepatch free from his face. Cool air kissed the skin around his right eye, still damp from the spray of the waterfall. He blinked once, letting his eye adjust to the dim light within the hollow. A dull ache throbbed behind the socket, but then the darkness shifted.

The change was subtle at first. Then, like ink blooming through water, the illusion unraveled. Solid stone melted away into shadow, revealing a cave mouth that hadn’t been there just moments ago.

Figures. Of course it took the eye.

His gaze drifted toward Merrick’s motionless body. The man’s chest still rose and fell in shallow rhythm, which meant he was still breathing. Eh. He’ll be fine... probably.

Turning away without another thought, Oswald stepped into the cave. The air thickened as he moved, but beneath it lingered an old, rotting stench that crept into his nose and settled in the back of his throat. Smells like somethin’ died in here. Long time ago.

The tunnel ahead narrowed gradually as it pulled him deeper. Stalactites drooped from the ceiling like jagged teeth, their tips slick with constant moisture. Thin threads of water traced lines down the walls, collecting in shallow pools along the uneven floor.

At first, nothing stood out. Just rock, water, and darkness. Normal enough, for a place like this.

Then the feeling hit him. A tingling weight settled along his shoulders, crawling down his arms. Something watched him. I ain’t alone down here.

Just in case, Oswald kept his hand close to the hilt of his sword. He didn’t draw it yet, just let the touch steady him.

He kept moving, each step splashing softly through shallow pools of stagnant water. The tunnel sloped downward in a slow, steady descent, drawing him deeper beneath the earth.

After several minutes, he finally caught sight of something moving. Just ahead, half-swathed in dim light, a figure stood in the passage. It was shaped like a person, but only at a glance. The longer he stared, the more wrong it became.

Its skin hung too tight across a skeletal frame, stretched like parchment over bone. Ribs jutted from beneath the scraps of what had once been clothing, and its arms hung low at its sides. So this is where the ghouls’ve been hidin’.

The creature didn’t move. Just stared, utterly still in the silence.

Oswald’s hand tightened around the hilt. He drew his sword slowly, the steel whispering free with a metallic hiss that echoed off the walls.

The ghoul tensed. Then, to Oswald's surprise, it turned and ran away.

Oswald frowned, blade still raised. I didn’t even touch the damn thing. Did the last one I saw tell its friends about me?

His grip on his sword tightened as he pressed forward, scanning the tunnel for any more of them. If they were lurking in the dark, he’d rather meet them head-on than let them creep up on him.

As he went deeper, the cave floor slanted more towards a downward slope. The passage widened as he pressed deeper, soon unraveling into a vast hollow space that sprawled into the dark.

The stone here had deepened to a darker hue, almost black in places, slick with constant moisture. But suddenly, Oswald stopped as he felt an intense pressure approaching.

It wasn’t like before. Being watched by those ghouls that had been unsettling, sure. But this? This was different.

The air thickened around him, pressing into his chest. It wasn’t just presence, whatever was waiting ahead didn’t just see him. It knew he was here.

Oswald quickly ran behind a jagged outcrop of stone. He lowered himself into the shadows, back pressed to the damp wall. His breath slowed to a careful rhythm as he peered out past the edge.

Footsteps echoed from the dark ahead, but not the dragging shuffles of ghouls. These steps had weight behind them. Control. Every movement purposeful, like whoever it was had all the time in the world and knew exactly where to put their feet.

Oswald’s fingers hovered near the hilt of his sword. Then, he edged forward just enough to risk another look.

A figure emerged from the deeper dark. Heavy footfalls echoed off the cavern walls. Dust trembled loose from the ceiling above, tiny fragments drifting down like falling ash. Sounds like armor.

“I know you’re here, human.”
