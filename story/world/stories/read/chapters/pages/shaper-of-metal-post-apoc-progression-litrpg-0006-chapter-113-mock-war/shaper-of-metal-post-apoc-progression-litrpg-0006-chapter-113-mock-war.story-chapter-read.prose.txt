Chapter 113: Mock War

The holographic projector spawned a view of a fortress and a well-lit, cracked, desert landscape. Four tiny figures were glowing blue in the fortress, four were red outside of it. At first, Jack thought he was looking at a digital image, if not super high quality. And then it zoomed in, adjusted resolution, and he realized the fine details were in fact a digital rendering of physical matter. Extremely well-carved clay. It was so well done, it was like someone had etched a hyper-realistic painting version of it. If it had been cake in a ‘Is It Cake, or Reality’ show contest, he’d have lost.

“Holy shit,” Jack exclaimed. Everyone looked at him quizzically. He gestured at the hologram. “What, this is normal to you? Perfect clay models?”

Everyone once again looked confused.

“Models?” Highfive asked.

Meanwhile, Augur was staring at him with his mouth open. “You could tell already?” He sounded a bit crestfallen.

When Jack realized what was happening, he waved his hands apologetically. “No, no, it’s not obvious! Not to anyone lacking the” — he put on a faux, deep smug tone with an exaggerated expression — “Eyes of the Eagle.”

Highfive and Bo groaned, and Bo threw a throw pillow at him.

Augur gasped slightly. “Ah! That’s it. Yes. I knew that. Your modifications. I underestimated the eyesight improvements! Damn. Anyhow…” He cleared his throat and began working the controller in his hand. A flash of scenes flowed by, showing disturbingly life-like clay replicas of them fighting against the other four, their precise movements more obvious by the glow around them. Nothing was super clear or lengthy, as dozens of snapshot-like renderings flowed by in seconds. Hiccup and Highfive locked in combat… Délight fading away from a backstab attempt as Defiant intercepted Inkblot… Chime striking what looked like wooden rods together and sending a cone blast knocking Jack, even as he was a hair away from snaring his enemy with a cord of metal.

Augur narrated over this, “This took quite a while, as there was an extensive amount of guesswork to do.” The scenes stopped flashing. “So I can’t show a worthwhile single match. I did sims part-way and then made adjustments until I got as close to satisfied as I could get. One thing I can say for certain? Délight and Defiant are mega hiding things. I couldn’t sim them without potentially inaccurate imaginations. At best, Délight is three-fourths accurate, Defiant not even two-thirds. Those are wild variations compared to the other two, who are ninety-three, ninety-four percent, or so. I can say almost absolutely, Bo’s assessment of their levels must be accurate.”

“Well, that sucks,” Highfive said. “Too bad we didn’t know a little more.”

“Come on, Fiver,” Bo exclaimed. “You don’t buy into this snake oil bullshit, do you? Might wanna tell him your star sign while you’re at it.”

“Excuse me?!” Augur said, eyes wide and baring his fangs. “It isn’t snake oil! Take that back!”

“Hey, now,” Jack said, casting a firm gaze Bo’s way. “You’re totally out of line, this time, Bo. This is an ability. If it didn’t provide any decent insight, Augur would tell us as much. Let’s hear him out and get what nuggets we can.”

Bo scoffed, but looked away and tossed her hand. “Fine. I take it back. Go on.”

Augur glared for a moment longer, but took a deep breath and continued. “I had to make multiple guesses and simulate to narrow down the things they might have. I dismissed things that were too weak to affect the outcome, focusing on what narrowed results in their favor. I figured this was best, as you wouldn’t really care if you were gonna wipe the floor with them, anyway. I don’t claim to be able to determine exactly what they can do, but I believe I have down the equivalent magnitude that would see them as the elite team they appear to be. I even ran it against other teams, and it held up. In other words, I made them as their worst case scenario… and I honestly don’t think it’s very far off from reality.”

The hologram shifted to show Highfive, covered in his telekinetic sheath, ramming right into the black-armored and masked Defiant, who just stood there and took it. The sheath warped and bent around him like he was an unrootable tree. Almost. Defiant, just barely, finally moved, taking a step back and sliding across stone, as rocks went flying where he re-rooted. The rebound actually bounced back, cascading through Highfive like a visible ripple, as he gritted his teeth and absorbed it. Even Defiant’s armor wasn’t damaged, as if a part of his invulnerability.

The force didn’t reflect fully. Not enough to truly damage Highfive. “Defiant doesn’t unleash that, does he? As in, he absorbs, then releases later?”

“No way,” Highfive said dismissively. “Everything is consistent that his offense is pretty shit. He goes for grapples sometimes. A chokehold is one of his better maneuvers.”

“No,” Augur said, shaking his head. “Like Fiver said. If he absorbs, it must fuel his defense, not offense. Or maybe his speed? Not a bad theory…”

Another scene showed Defiant warping across space, interjecting between and absorbing several fast-flying metal darts meant to hit Chime. The metal shattered against his body, face, and even his hand, as he reached out almost calmly to prevent the passage.

One of the trailing darts morphed and became a band, wrapping around his neck. He ignored it. It squeezed, twisted, went smaller and tighter. Nothing. He just kept moving. It didn’t restrain him, either.

Augur narrated, “Defiant, in practice, has something suitable to his name, certainly. He defies attackers. Annnny given fool might make the deduction that it is simply, as leaked, ‘Defiance.’ ‘Resistance.’ That he has a mythical ‘high concept.’ If that were true, we’d probably be screwed.” He sighed. “I can’t figure out what it is, but more likely, it’s a two-worder, or effectively, that’s how it works based on whatever his Primary Mutation is. So, maybe ‘Defy This,’ ‘Resist That.’ What mega sucks is he’s been shown to resist whatever was thrown at him so far. So what is that word? Harm? Force? I dunno. Maybe it’s all a huge lie and a misinformation campaign, and he’s an absorber, as Jack suggested. Maybe that’s a silly divergence, and he’s as reported, but we just need more exotic damage than we have.”

“Well, that can’t be true, right?” Jack asked. “They wouldn’t have a match if there wasn’t a way.”

Highfive frowned and shook his head. “I dunno.”

“We don’t know that, Ferro,” Bo said. “They might, just to experiment with us. Frag with our heads.”

Jack frowned. “That would piss me off.”

“Sure, but we might not ever know. I’m sure that guy is gonna keep a lid on his shit as long as he can.”

Jack recalled his battle with the slow-ass stone tank, which Lindsay had simulated in HRVR. He’d forced a draw in the end because he couldn’t manage to damage the bastard. This seemed similar, only the guy had some speed, if conditionally. Jack would do his best to cook up a solution, though. He could see this as a test for something in the field. When something looks impossible, even to the eggheads, what do you do, then? Guys like Augur, talking about percentages?

They might be quite curious about what someone does, then, even with the exhaustion of extra informational tools.

I feel like I’m in a chess match with someone, sometimes. Maybe that’s my paranoia again. Didn’t serve me well when I thought Lindsay was a simulation, did it? And who would it be? Memoria? People keep hinting about others in Central. I’d think it maybe Stitcher herself if she weren’t so aloof and out-of-touch at times. She’s been away, supposedly.

If it were some other mysterious figure, or perhaps Central as a collective whole, and if he ever found that out definitively, he could say his paranoia was well-founded.

Augur continued on, showing his simulations and narrating. The flashing holograms showed their team losing handily. “I did multiple runs. The first three, they won, progressively by smaller margins as I attempted to tweak things favorably for us. In the end, they won four out of six, and definitely have the advantage, namely due to our crummy understanding of Defiant and what Délight’s new capabilities are. I’m guessing either a very wide area perception reducer, a buff, or more likely, something mega stronger single-target, possibly something that spikes instead of her normal wearing people down thing.”

He sure is saying ‘mega’ a lot…

The hologram switched to show both Délight and Highfive, and Délight was smiling and holding out her hands across the distance, glowing golden, and Highfive, in the middle of combat, had turned his head to her and was apparently staring like a deer in headlights, through a shattered mask, his eyes just visible.

“That won’t happen, guys!” Highfive called anxiously. “This isn’t real! I won’t!”

“It will happen,” Bo said. “She could already focus it for distraction. That is literally nothing new. She’s probably been training hard on it.”

Augur grunted. “Let’s hope the modified faceplates help. For a time, anyway. My Mock War shows it is almost certain that Chime will target faceplates.” The hologram switched to show Chime ringing a cowbell, and a barely visible distortion making a super-fast cone through the air, headed right for Jack, who was trying to dodge at the last second, occupied as he was with other shit. It didn’t work, as Chime kept up his ringing and swept the cone over Jack’s head, where it narrowed slightly. His faceplate spiderwebbed with cracks.

That would suck to see out of. Might have to remove it, which is not good.

“There are too many advantages for this,” Augur declared. “It attacks vision, head protection, hearing, all these juicy elements at once. I think he’s gotten better and better at this targeting, and it doesn’t help that most participants have the same gear.” The hologram switched to show what looked like a different combat, and Chime was doing more of a tight AoE cone, hitting both Bo and Jack from a flank, cracking two faceplates at once. The image shifted around slowly 360 degrees, showing a metal wall between them. “I believe he can even do it in an AoE. Jack’s metal won’t stop Chime’s sound, not well, and if he can guess where you’re at, he’s still going to get you. He’s practiced at getting through walls of various kinds, especially thin ones. What is lost in precision is made up for in adjustments to turn it effectively into an amp.”

“My new Absorption technique should help, right?” Jack asked.

“A little. Not enough to stop him. The key is that he’s basically wiping the default assumption of the wall’s physical protection.”

“Can I modify the walls? Soundproof them?”

Augur hesitated briefly. “Probably? You’re somewhat just using bad material against sound. I think soundproofing usually uses insulation and multiple materials. Learn mega quickly, I guess. Figuring out something unique would be great. Using something he doesn’t know about would be best. If nothing else, I suppose a weird shape will throw off his targeting on the other side? I’m just guessing right now. The timeframe to get things done is a mega serious issue…”

Jack took a deep breath and nodded. It did sound daunting without time. Perhaps jagged walls, hoping to throw him off slightly? “Alright, then.”

“You might also be able to simulate something to test yourself. Er-” Augur glanced over at the monitor and interface station. “Actually, I should probably be the one to make sims based on these people, at this point…”

“Yes,” Bo said.

“Agreed,” Highfive added.

Augur rather preened at these acknowledgements. “Okay, but first, let me go through other things of note in the simulated battles…”

He took them through the various scenes of defeat and victory, and the more isolated incidents that made the difference. As far as the sim went, for them to win on even footing, it seemed like they needed to stack a lot of smaller wins or see their opponents make mistakes. Lack of knowledge about Bo’s recent mutation. Keeping Chime occupied so as to not detect an invisible Ink (in a vacuum, he was way too good at it, which sucked for assassinating him first). Avoiding ambushes and resisting Délight long enough to dodge a combo-catastrophe from their interacting teamwork-kills. Bo hitting the right person with a Sandy very early, and interrupting Chime at the right time for Inkblot to take him out.

It depended on Defiant. He had to be occupied, forced to pick one person to cover and protect. If he were in a group reacting to your individual attack, you had to simultaneously attack two people. It was clear they had to be flexible on who to target and have contingencies. But things could go to shit and chaos quickly thanks to Délight’s disruption.

It was a bitch of a choice. Ideally, Délight had the highest chance to both disrupt with Bo and target for a takedown, since she then couldn’t disrupt, leaving Chime as the one who had to counter. Which he definitely could, but not like the team mistress.

“Yeah, man, we gotta go after Délight,” Highfive said, arms crossed and shaking his head. “She can spoil whatever else we’re doing, if she’s unchecked. She’s kinda like Bo, you know?”

“The trouble is,” Jack replied, “that if forced to choose, Defiant will absolutely protect her.”

“Hard feint to Hiccup,” Bo declared, “right after she’s hit with the Sandy. She’ll report almost instantly. If they don’t know about that, there should be at least a bit of panic. Adjustment. If we appear to be targeting her, and someone actually strikes, Defiant will have to act and rush to her defense. Boom. I disrupt Miss Sleepsaround, and Ink strikes. She’s out. What am I missing?”

“Chime, obviously,” Inkblot said. “He won’t just have his thumb up his ass at this point. He’ll already be striking.”

There was a pause.

Jack cleared his throat and said, “Delay him with Highfive. He can’t be ignored when he charges. I’ll be the decoy striker against Hiccup. That way, Chime isn’t braining me through my metal shields or whatever. He can hurt you, but he can’t disable you quickly, right, Blondy?”

“No way,” Highfive replied. “If left alone, I’ll keep him occupied for a while. He’s pretty survivable, but he can’t make mistakes if he has to defend, and he’ll be taking risks if he turns up the offense. We should be a draw for a while. Unless one of us gets lucky.”

“Well, this sounds kinda ideal,” Augur said doubtfully. “Like a first strike, as if you’re on offense. But you guys are on defense.”

“Their stealth can’t be that great,” Jack said. “We just need to see them coming, and we can strike first, defense or not. Anyway, even if something happens where they pull off a defense, we might still have the advantage in this scenario. Maybe we could stay on Délight, or maybe we pivot to Hiccup. By the nature of Bo’s ability, they can’t put them together.”

“Uh-huh. Just remember, Defiant doesn’t care about her debuff. He’ll resist it.”

Jack groaned. “That is a problem… It’s still hard to defend two people at once. If they’re apart, that has advantages; if they’re close, it has others. We can work with it.”

“What if, despite your ambitions, you don’t get this first strike?”

“We’ll train on weathering the storm, and this can be our counterattack.”

Augur’s lips quirked around, but he nodded slowly. “I guess that’s as good as it gets, if you have to clash head-to-head on even footing.”

After a long pause, Inkblot said, “I don’t like this matchup. Délight is devious in the extreme.”

Oof. Ominous, coming from him.

Highfive sighed. “Aww, man, not you saying that shit!”

Jack put out two calming hands. “Let’s just get to training, eh? With confidence locked and loaded. I think we’ve seen enough. Any more is going to be counterproductive for our overstuffed brains at this point. It’s time to get the muscles moving, too.”

“Fine,” Augur said, clicking off the hologram and strolling over to the simulation interface station. “I’ll design the sims. Should be just a few minutes.”

Jack rose too, headed over to grab his mostly personal gear off the table, then to the gear room for the few things not custom. “Do Chime first, if it matters. Need to play with walls, just in case I need to block him. Oh, and make sure the sims look different. Like sims.”

“Why?”

“So we don’t intuitively expect what we shouldn’t when we face the real thing. We’re only going to get so much from this.”

“Mmn. If you say so.”

They ended up getting themselves used to the fortress they’d defend first and foremost, as Jack figured out a good wall design. For the match, they’d have seven minutes of preparation time, which was over double what they had for the Wall map match.

It was a fairly simple, small outpost, but sturdy and defensible from a conventional viewpoint. Mostly in an antiquated sense, as it had a medieval feel, but that was perfectly problematic to attack without artillery, the equivalent of artillery in firepower, or certain special powers. It was a squat keep of a very thick stone structure, kind of like a wall with a roof and about two stories, or three if you counted the area wrapping around the roof. There were fortifications with crenellations and the whole nine yards, all reachable by steps inside the fortress.

In addition, all around the keep were walls that were slightly lower than the top of the keep. There was a tunnel-like, double portcullis killing zone for an entrance, and circular towers built above it, as well as at each corner of the walls.

Immediately outside the walls, it was rocky. Decent cover, not great, especially in the glaring sun, though that also did make for some shadows. Outside, maybe thirty to forty meters from the walls, the terrain had plenty of good cover.

Would be so good for Ink to skulk around in, but we can’t risk it with Chime’s annoying ass. Even Délight has some active measures to sense him.

The structure was some sort of super-reinforced stone, but it was definitely not impenetrable. Jack asked Augur to test Chime’s capabilities in attacking through the walls, while he juggled ideas quickly and practiced wall structures. Everyone else scoped the place out and talked strategy.

The two things that occurred to him to use were jagged, jacked-up structures, and/or layers. The first was quicker and made for an overall weaker structure, the second had a longer time frame and overall better strength. Two or three separated metal walls with air between would mitigate vibrations and change his harmonics. Random, chaotically shaped material would do the same, but probably could be bull-forced for some sort of effect, and more easily straight-up damaged. The layered approach needed some thin connections between for ease of creation, movement, and repair, but they were mostly intended to be completely separate.

Augur had a Chime sim copy ready for testing. As for the keep’s primary outer walls, Chime did not easily penetrate them, as they were too thick and dense. Disturbingly, though, he could crack the walls and damage them with enough time, AoE focus, and fatigue. He also rather easily went through the roof at the top, which was understandable much weaker. Same for the keep’s inner walls of thinner, weaker stone, and obviously, the two portcullis gates were basically irrelevant to him. He could break through them or attack past them.

They tested the metal materials, and though it was slightly underwhelming, it did work to mess with his ability to penetrate, especially if he didn’t detect it immediately. He could adjust progressively after one and two strikes, though, clearly able to ‘sense’ the structure as he passed sound through it.

Annoying. But I could just imagine the tricks someone would learn after years and years of doing almost nothing but training and inventing.

Jack practiced and refined the structures a little more, seeing a modest gain for his efforts. Mostly, he made sure what he built was weird on the inside and looked like an average steel barrier on the outside. The last thing he needed was Chime guessing how to penetrate an obviously unique material structure, robbing Jack of even one or two improved blocks.

It’ll just have to do, I guess.
