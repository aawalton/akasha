Time was running out.

Ace raced across the scorched earth, his Phantom Step Resonance Boots absorbing the sound of each footfall. The blackened grass disintegrated beneath his weight, releasing puffs of ash that rode the breeze like tiny specters. His body still thrummed with Tara’s blood, her sacrifice flowing through him—a warm current that mingled with the colder, sharper edge of Raven’s corrupted essence he’d already consumed.

Twenty yards ahead, Raven spun in erratic circles, his shadow tendrils whipping outward in all directions. The corrupted guardian no longer bothered with duplicates or strategy. Pure chaos erupted from his form as wispy tears in reality revealed glimpses of the Shadow Realm beyond.

“They left me!” Raven screamed, his voice fracturing into multiple tones at once. “They ran when Lysandra separated us! They left me alone with her!”

The outburst sent a fresh pulse of shadow corruption through his body. Veins of darkness crawled across his face, replacing what little humanity remained with inky blackness. His eyes, once merely rimmed with shadow essence, now leaked it like tears.

Marcus and Olivia had taken positions behind separate boulders, spaced thirty yards apart. Through the hivemind connection, Ace sensed them without looking—their determination, their fear, their readiness to strike. Even Tara, though physically spent, remained linked to them all. Her presence in the connection felt like a hand steadying a weapon, guiding without applying direct force.

Raven’s head snapped toward Ace, his neck twisting at an impossible angle. “Will you just die?!”

“Polite pass,” Ace replied.

Raven screamed in fury, the sound rippling through multiple octaves at once—a symphony of agony that shattered the air. The shriek clawed at Ace’s eardrums, vibrating through his skull with such intensity that his vision blurred at the edges. Raven’s shadow tendrils lashed forward, no longer the controlled weapons of moments ago but frenzied, living nightmares. They tore into the earth, ripping chunks of blackened soil skyward in their wake. The ground split open in jagged lines. Where the tendrils struck, they left furrowed lines through the dirt. Steam hissed from the ground, carrying the sickly-sweet stench of rot and the coppery tang of blood.

Ace ducked and rolled sideways on instinct, and one tendril missing his face by inches. The corrupted shadow burrowed into the earth where he’d stood. When he recovered, he came up in a fighting stance with his muscles coiled tight.

Ready to strike.

All he needed was a chance to take the shot.

Another surge of corrupted shadows undulated with a hypnotic, unnatural rhythm as they carved their way toward Ace. Their tips hardened into obsidian spears dripping with what could only have been poison. The very air between Raven and Ace distorted, warping with an icy chill that stole the breath from his lungs. The boundary between worlds strained against the sheer force of Raven’s attack.

The sentinel was distracted, and Olivia took the opening.

Olivia vaulted from atop a boulder, her body already twisting into the first sequence of Crimson Waltz. Sunlight caught her face for an instant, illuminating a smile that held nothing but cold appreciation for the carnage about to unfold. The corrupted guardian whirled toward her, shadow spears materializing and pivoting in midair.

A pulse of irritation flashed through the hivemind. Olivia hadn’t expected to be spotted so quickly. She twisted mid-step, her spine arching at an angle that should have been impossible. The spears sliced through the space she’d occupied milliseconds earlier, and her lithe body cut through the air as she pirouetted away from certain death.

Alright, well, that didn’t work.

Time for take two.

As Ace dodged yet another spear—which burrowed deep into the earth where he had been standing seconds before—Marcus emerged from behind the cracked outcropping of rocks they had previously used as cover. His hands wove intricate patterns that released waves of psychic energy, and his eyes glowed violet as he unleashed Terror Cascade.

It hit Raven hard.

The sentinel staggered, his shadow form wavering as Marcus’s attack penetrated his mental defenses. For a moment, the corrupted guardian’s human face emerged from the writhing darkness—hollow-eyed and gaunt with centuries of suffering.

His momentary vulnerability only lasted a millisecond, so brief that Ace almost missed it entirely. It vanished as the shadow essence flooded back, obscuring his humanity once more. Even more tendrils erupted from his back and spiraled outward like mutated wings. One caught Marcus across the chest, sending him flying backward into the jagged rock formation once again.

Pain spiked through the hivemind connection—sharp, white-hot, and brutal. He still had a pulse of life to him, but the impact had shattered more of his ribs and possibly punctured a lung.

Ace charged toward Marcus, but Raven’s spears lanced the ground wherever Ace tried to run. He leapt out of the way at the last second, dodging each one in the nick of time. It was like a hail of arrows raining down on him—and one even caught him through the shoulder.

“Damn it,” he said through gritted teeth as he rolled away. His hand pressed against the injury to stem the bleeding, and he did his best to ignore the sharp jolts of pain that shot through his body from the wound.

Raven whirled toward Olivia, who had completed the first sequence of her Crimson Waltz. Her eyes narrowed as she calculated angles and distances, her body already flowing into the second sequence.

But Olivia’s rhythm faltered for a fraction of a second as she caught sight of Ace’s injury.

It was enough for Raven to notice. He lunged forward, the shadowy spears condensing into a scythe-like appendage that slashed toward her midsection.

Ace pushed himself to his feet, and as much as he wanted to draw Moonfall, he resisted. The sword didn’t work against Raven—but the Duskblades did.

Instantly, one materialized in his hand. He hurled it with all his might, and the enchanted blade caught the shadow scythe with such force that it sent sparks cascading across the battlefield. The impact vibrated up Raven’s body, and the sentinel took several uneven steps away from Olivia.

Olivia didn’t waste the opening. She completed the second sequence of Crimson Waltz, her body becoming a blur of grace and death. Each step left traces of blood-red energy that solidified into crescents of force. They sliced through the air toward Raven, shearing off tendrils of shadow as they connected.

Raven howled, his form compressing for a few seconds, only to then explode violently outward. The blast knocked Ace backward, and his heels skidded across the scorched earth as he fought to stay upright. Blood trickled from his nose, metallic and bitter on his lips.

Through the hivemind, he sensed Marcus struggling to get to his feet, refusing to stay down despite his injuries. Pride and concern warred in Ace’s mind. They couldn’t afford to lose anyone else—not after Rachel.

Raven’s form had changed again. The shadow corruption now consumed eighty percent of his visible mass, leaving only fragments of his original form. His right arm remained almost human, though black veins pulsed beneath the pale skin. One eye still held a flicker of awareness, darting frantically as if searching for escape, while the other had been fully consumed by shadow.

“She’ll destroy you,” Raven’s voice cracked. “Just like she did us. One by one. Piece by piece.”

His human arm reached toward them in a gesture that might have been warning or plea, but shadow tendrils immediately wrapped around it, crushing him until his bones snapped with hollow pops. The corruption spread faster, flowing up his arm like hungry tar.

Ace took advantage of the internal struggle. Tara? Do you have any energy left?

A gentle pulse returned—she couldn’t fight, but she could channel healing where needed. Her presence in the connection steadied them all, monitoring their vitals and sending small bursts of energy to close minor wounds.

Good.

An idea was starting to form, and they would definitely need their healer to survive this.

Raven roared, the sound no longer remotely human. His remaining eye vanished beneath the tide of shadow essence. What stood before them now was a churning vortex of pure shadow corruption shaped vaguely like a man—a hungry void consuming everything it touched.

Oh, fuck.

He was drawing energy from the Shadow Realm, and that meant he was up to something dire. The corrupted guardian had become a conduit, a doorway for something infinitely larger.

The air temperature plummeted. Frost crystallized on the blackened grass, spreading outward from where Raven stood. The corruption expanded, first engulfing his form entirely, then shooting across the ground in pulses of absolute darkness. Each wave froze the ground it touched, as though reality itself was beginning to crack under the pressure.

Through the hivemind, Ace sensed Marcus’s psychic probes being systematically crushed by whatever lurked behind Raven’s consciousness. This wasn’t just corruption anymore—something ancient had taken notice of their battle.

Olivia abandoned her position and darted to Marcus’s side as his knees buckled. She caught him before he hit the ground, her own breath coming in short gasps.

A surge of panic flowed from her through their connection, followed by a desperate plea to retreat.

Raven—or whatever remained of him—had stopped attacking. The corrupted guardian now stood motionless at the center of the expanding darkness, his arms outstretched. Shadow essence poured from him, creating a whirlpool effect that dragged smaller rocks and debris closer.

A laugh escaped the vortex—not Raven’s voice but something deeper and older. It resonated in Ace’s bones rather than his ears.

Ace closed his eyes, drawing upon the combined blood essence now flowing through his veins. Tara’s sacrifice had given him incredible strength.

Time to test how far he could go.

His skin tingled as he activated Shadow Realm Immersion, but instead of fully phasing, he held himself at the threshold between worlds. Through half-lidded eyes, he saw reality overlaid with the monochromatic Shadow Realm. The vortex appeared different from this perspective—not expanding chaos but a structured pattern, a deliberate summoning.

The boundary between realms was thinnest here, drawn open by Raven’s desperation. But that thinness worked both ways.

Ace stretched out his senses through the hivemind, connecting not just to his teammates but to the blood essence that flowed between them. Tara’s healing energy, Marcus’s psychic patterns, Olivia’s blood magic—all created a network united by his presence at the center.

I need your strength, he transmitted. All of you.

Understanding flowed back to him. Without instruction, Tara channeled her remaining energy into the hivemind. Marcus redirected his psychic power to enhance their connection, and Olivia released a burst of blood magic.

Their combined essence flowed through Ace. He stood at the threshold between worlds, one foot in reality, one in shadow.

Through the hivemind, Ace felt their essence merging once more, creating patterns of energy that flowed between them. He closed his eyes, sensing the thin boundary between realms, the shadow that waited just beyond perception.

“Well,” he said. “Here goes nothing.”
