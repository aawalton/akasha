◈◈◈

The barrier took the brutal onslaught of the monsters the moment they stepped out onto the surface, and Bram felt the impact it in his teeth.

The veil drowned the whole city in a thick, dark sickly red aura, and only now he could the ramifications of what boss meant by the ambient atmosphere killing anyone not at Order III. There was a persistent ringing in his eyes and even though he was wearing the specialized gear and had his aura shield him from certain death, it wasn’t a 100% shield.

He shuddered, thinking what would have happened if they were still low rankers…

Focus, he chided himself, poured more essence into maintaining the combined thread of sorcery and directing it to the various node points.

The pale blue of the barrier pulsed and flexed inward across a forty-metre face. It went white and the essence density thinned where the pressure gathered but it held.

The Second Company held it.

Bram could see their leads inside of the barrier line with their arms up and their feet planted, mouths working through the same six words over and over.

"TWO, REPORT." Veric came through the comms and Bram was grateful for the voice helping it find calm.

"Two holding. Eighty-one percent."

"Two, you're bowing at the left seam."

"Aware. Correcting."

Above the essence wall, an abomination climbed the corpse of another abomination and came down on the barrier with everything it had. A third and fourth imitated that one..

They were stacking. Nothing in the reports had said they would stack, but then again the monsters were throwing themselves at them uncaring of thier lives. He should expect all known norms of battle to change today.

"They're not stopping," Clara Mashburn said at his shoulder. "Sir. They're not even slowing at the wall."

"No."

"They should slow. Anything alive slows."

"Alive should…" Bram wet his lips. "These things aren’t free will, Clara. Focus. If the situation worsen the lords and the boss will move."

Around him, his hundred men stood dormant in the belly of the column with their palms empty. Only Bram was on correcting and maintaining the sorcery weave duty. Rest had to preserve strength for later.

Waiting for later.

That was the hardest thing Bram had ever done in his life.

"SIX. Take up."

The block at the column's centre raised their hands.

Bram had never worked out what to make of the Chorus. A hundred mages who never attacked anything, ever. They caught what the other companies weaved and they braided it, and the air over the Mile had gone thick and bright with threads that came apart if you tried too hard to look at them.

"What are they doing," he said.

"Catching Four's spill." Clara had her eyes shut, reading it off her own senses. "Four's about to take every drop of moisture out of this end of the avenue. If nobody catches the runoff, Seven through Ten lose their ambient and the sustainers go dark."

"FOUR. Charge."

Fourth Company began the water pulse. It was a one-verse sorcery, and the sorcery wasn’t anything powerful. It was a glorified sorcery that doused the target with water. Like a splash of water.

Still when 100 of these combined together into one weave the pulse looked like a flood. Moisture came up out of the stone and the dead and the sea air, gathered along the barrier's outer face, and hung there in a slow curtain twenty metres deep.

The horde ran into it and kept running. It didn’t do any damage and only stopped them for a few moments. Still, it enabled the company to enact true attacking sorcery with much higher damage potential.

"SEVEN THROUGH TEN, watch your ambient. Four's pulling everything out of the air in front of you."

"Copy, Command."

"FIVE. Stand by."

Fifth Company raised their arms. Bram did not look away, and afterwards he was never sure whether that had been discipline or the other thing.

"Command, Two… left seam's going. I need relief."

"Three, panel up on Two's left. Move now."

Third Company moved, and they moved badly. Two of the front rank crossed feet and went down. The panel came up three seconds late, and in those three seconds something with too many elbows got a shoulder through the seam.

It made four steps into the column. Second's second rank put it down with essence-honed blades, and one of them was slow getting clear and lost most of an arm below the elbow.

Bram saw the arm hit the ground before he saw the woman it had come off. She did not scream.

"Casualty, Two, front rank, arterial—"

"Marked. Eleven, extract." Veric did not raise his voice, and somehow that was worse. "Three. Do that again and I'll have your lead's badge, and then I'll have yours. Panels come up before they're needed. Not when."

"Understood, Command."

Three seconds. Bram made himself hold on to the number. Three seconds of two people finding their feet, and a woman would spend the rest of her life learning to write with the other hand.

"FIVE." A pause on the net while Veric watched something Bram couldn't see. "Chain."

Fifth Company fired.

The lightning combined with sorcery erupted in blinding white and struck the horde. It went clean through the frontal wave of the monsters, blowing their bodies clean off.

Hundreds of monsters dead in an instant. But neither the horde nor the sorcery was finished. Just as more monsters rose over the corpses of their kin, the bolt of lightning splintered and found the bodies doused and standing in the water, and every one of those bodies was touching others. The lightning arcs toward each of them.

Bram watched the flashes of white, and the cacophony of dying screams and wails filled the battlefield.

Thousands upon thousands of abominations convulsed together as the mage company repeated the water pulse sorcery and then followed up again with a lightning sorcery, but this time focusing more on chain rather than raw damage.

The number of corpses, the terrible smell of charred corpses and the cacophony only rose with each step the army took forward.

The barrier also had arcs of light sliding off its surface as it pushed forward.

"Keep advancing." Veric again on the comms. "Barrier factor in and allow dead signatures to pass the barrier. Push against the living signatures. Walk. Two, Three, keep the panel on the front, don't chase the flanks. Go."

The column moved. And another fresh wave of monsters collided with the barrier again.

Eighty seconds. That was the cycle. Barrier, water, chain, twenty to fifty metres.

They had drilled it hundreds of times on the surface, and Bram had hated every drill. But he understood now that he had hated them because they were boring and that boring had been the entire point.

Because today was not boring… it was hell.

◈◈◈

The comms were a mess by the second kilometer marker.

"—Nine, we've got ambient collapse in the green, I can't hold sustainer output at this—"

"Nine, this is Command, throttle to sixty and cycle with Twelve—"

"Command, Four, my second's down—"

"Who's stepping up."

"Voss."

"Voss, you have Fourth Company. Confirm."

A pause. Then a young voice, dragged up out of a well. "Voss has Fourth. Confirmed, sir."

"Good lad. Charge on my count."

Bram listened to a nineteen-year-old inherit a hundred people and take it without arguing, and something went hot in his chest and stayed there. He wanted to find the boy afterwards and tell him. He knew he probably wouldn't, and he was ashamed of knowing it.

Just when they were making good progress and getting used to the surface… a renewed and stronger pressure bore down on them.

Bram's knees folded before his brain had finished registering anything, and he went down on one hand and stayed there with his breath punched out of him. All around him people dropped. Fourth Company's water pulse guttered and came apart in the air.

The barrier flickered.

An Order IV. Somewhere ahead, up the Mile, past the horde and Order IV had showed up.

Bram had never been afraid like this. The fear did not feel like his. It felt installed, put into him from outside by something that had decided he should have it, and the part of him that could still form sentences hated that more than the fear itself.

"AURA EVENT." Vera on the net, thin. "Command, aura event, bearing—"

"I have it," Veric said. "Everybody down and hold. X, one out."

Something went over the top of the barrier.

Bram got his head up in time to see a shape cross the shell from the inside, land in the middle of forty thousand abominations, and vanish into them.

Two seconds of nothing.

Then a sound rose from the horde. It was a devastatingly loud slicing sound, like a sword cutting cleanly through bamboo, only played on the world's loudest speakers. It’s sound, and a shockwave swept across the entire battlefield.

And they just like that, the pressure was gone.

Bram got his lungs working as his aura rose. The column came up off its knees around him. Fourth Company's leads started their verse again from the top with their hands shaking, and the barrier steadied, and somebody behind Bram was being sick and apologizing for it between heaves.

A figure shot from the sky and landed in a crouch six ranks ahead. Bram saw the flash of silver hair. Mr. Silvers straightened and rolled one shoulder. There was blood on him to the elbows, and none of it was his.

"Command, Starkiller," Salvatore's voice on the net was bored. "One underlord abomination. Threat taken care of. There'll be more."

"Copy. Thank you, sir."

"Don't."

Bram was staring. He knew he was staring and could not stop. His fingers dug into his palms. A few weeks ago, he was nothing. He was a salty, helpless young man who took out his frustration on others. And now he stood shoulder to shoulder with figures he could only look up to but never reach.

Whatever one might say about Jin Winters. For Bram, he was his boss who made this possible, and he would not stop at being just an ORDER III. He will rise and protect his people.

A cold chill ran up his arms, breaking his line of thoughts and he looked down to find a silverly chain coiled around his right arm.

“Boss chains?” He muttered.

Then all of a sudden he felt a sting on the arm and everything cleared. The miasma covering and bearing down in his senses cleared up and a rush of fresh essence flooded through him.

The chain then uncoiled and disappeared.

He also noticed the same chains around other leads and also coiled twice around Sir Silvers’ arms.

Clara had felt too. "Sir—"

"That was boss, don’t find the chains."

“Sir,” she nodded.

◈◈◈

The cool wash from the sustainers came at the fifth kilometer marker, and Bram was not ready for how badly he needed it and he realized Boss chains were doing the same thing, but on a more personal and deeper level.

Down the column a hundred people in the Eleventh stood with their hands out and their eyes shut, feeding it forward through the formation. They were the only ones on that road not looking at anything.

"Eleven, you're beautiful," someone said on the open channel.

"Get off the net," Veric said.

"Sir."

"You can marry them after. Two, your panel's dropping. Three, relieve."

Bram laughed. He heard himself do it and could not entirely account for it. Two ranks up a girl called Lira laughed too, and then four more of them, and for about six seconds a section of First Company stood in the middle of a slaughter and laughed like idiots.

Then the horde hit the barrier again, and they were stopped by the might of thier powers.

The survivor’s Wrath.

◈◈◈

The second aura event came at the seventh marker and the sudden increase in pressure was even worse than the first time.

This time there were two of them.

"BREACH RISK, TWO, TWO, THE PANEL—"

"HOLD THE PANEL." Veric went through the net like a nail. "HOLD IT. X—"

Starkiller was already in air.

That was the part Bram would remember, and not the kill because Sir Silvers was flying.

This time, along with the sounds of explosion Bram could see multiple buildings collapsing in a wave from the sheer shockwaves of the attack.

And the pressure came off the column like a lid off a pot.

"Two down." Salvatore sounded, for the first time all day, faintly interested. "Command. Something's…"

"Say again, sir?"

A long pause on the net.

"Nothing," Salvatore said. "Keep your count."

◈◈◈

The chains came at the eighth marker, and nobody on that road ever agreed afterwards about where they came from.

Bram was watching Fifth Company charge when the light over the column changed. He looked up.

Chains… Hundreds of them… rising from somewhere back and low in the formation, arcing over the heads of twenty-two hundred people and out past the barrier in a spread that took the whole width of the Mile.

They went into the horde like rain into a field.

Bram saw one hit an abomination in the chest and pass through without slowing. He saw the thing stop mid-lunge. Then it sagged, and the mass went out of it in under a second, and what dropped on the stone was a grey shell shaped like something that used to be alive.

The chain was already three bodies further on.

They worked in silence. That was the part that got into people afterwards. Forty thousand things screaming, five companies chanting, the barrier singing under load and the chains made no sound at all while they emptied the front two hundred metres of the avenue.

Then the power came back down the line as those very chains found him.

His essence heart had been running at half since the fifth marker. It filled in about two seconds and kept filling until he had to dispense the excess back into the weaves of sorcery.

Up and down the column, twenty-two hundred people staggered and then straightened.

"Command—" Somebody was almost shouting. "Command, my reserves just went to full, what was—"

"Command, Fourth, same, we're at—"

"Command, what is that? Sir, what is—"

"Everyone shut up and use it," Veric said. “Boss helping us out. Don’t question.”

◈◈◈

The Mile ended at the eleventh marker.

Second Company walked the barrier the last forty metres. The horde thinned, and thinned again, and then there was open ground in front of them and the inner curtain beyond it.

Bram had read the brief. He had read it four times, the last time by lamplight at three in the morning with his boots still on. Stone curtain wall, nine metres, single gate, garrison strength unknown.

The column halted. The barrier came down to a maintenance panel.

Somewhere behind him a man was crying and not bothering to hide it, and Bram let him. After all this place was a symbol of people’s faith. The primes were the gods of Vienna and now thier very temple of power corrupted into this.

Ahead, the lead counted off casualties. Bram made himself listen to the whole list. He owed them that much, standing there all day with clean hands.

Then he walked forward through the ranks, because he was First Company and First Company went first, and he stopped about thirty metres out.

He got close enough to see that the wall had faces… faces that resembled vaguely human and judging by the sudden shift in mood and rage he was feeling…

These faces belonged to the once people of the city who were likely captured by the cult.

Comms filtered in information that the wall was just for… show… there was no purpose for it other than to…

“Sadistic cocksuckers,” Bram cursed.
