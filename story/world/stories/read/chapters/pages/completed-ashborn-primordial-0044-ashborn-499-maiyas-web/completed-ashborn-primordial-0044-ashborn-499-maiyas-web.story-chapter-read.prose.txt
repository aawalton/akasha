Maiya ordered her fourth drink at the upscale tavern she had specifically chosen. Not that upscale establishments were anything out of the ordinary in Alt Ashani. Quite the opposite, actually. One would be hard pressed to find a place that wasn’t.

It was all artificial, of course. The poor had been either driven from the city or the country entirely, creating a garden of elites, just the way the Altani wanted it.

The city’s appeal had soured for her after she’d learned that little truth during her time working for Ira.

Now, she didn’t have to order four drinks. Didn’t even have to order one, really, though getting drunk at a time like this seemed like exactly the sort of thing she ought to be doing given their dire circumstances. But, well, the alcohol did help her forget just how much she detested this.

Why couldn’t she roam the streets of Alt Ashani, looking for information like Vir was? Why’d she have to come here, of all places?

She knew why. As with many of the detestable things she'd done in her life, this too, had been her choice.

Maiya sighed. What was the use of her good looks if she couldn’t even use them to lure unsuspecting prey?

She nearly giggled at the thought. Hadn’t she always wanted to do this ever since she was a little girl? To have men fighting for the grace of her hand? To be coveted like this?

But then, of course, she’d found Vir. Or rather, she’d always had him.

Which was entirely the problem.

Vir was a better partner than any alive. There wasn’t anything in any universe more surer than that. He was her soulmate, her one and only… and as great as that was, it was all just a little boring. Imagine having someone so perfect, so ideal, that the whole concept of anyone better lost its meaning. It killed the entire thrill of the hunt! Of the butterflies of courtship, of wondering if this person would be ‘the one’.

The sad truth was that reality had a way of never measuring up to the dream of a thing. That every great goal, every significant milestone, once accomplished, felt somehow less impressive after the fact. Like the very act of coveting it was the more meaningful part.

But here, in this universe, none of that mattered. Because she could be whoever she wanted. She could do whatever she wanted, without consequence. And what better way to hunt for information than by seducing someone who’d spill every secret she wished to know?

A couple of men had already approached her, of course. A pretty girl sitting alone in a tavern was practically begging for it. But though she’d said sweet words, she’d sent them both away. While they were well-dressed and acted fine, neither looked connected enough for her purposes. She needed someone with influence. someone who carried useful information.

An upper echelon Sawai.

The Altani didn’t really have Sawai nobility like the other countries, of course, but thanks to refugees pouring in from every corner of the Known World, the city was practically drowning in them.

Little wonder, seeing how only the affluent could afford the price of entry into the last bastion of civilization in this blighted universe.

It was on her third drink that she spotted the right catch. He struck her as truly different. Not just in his clothing, but in the way he carried himself. That calm self-assuredness that came with someone comfortable lording over others.

Considering his tender age, that would’ve been on account of his upbringing, rather than any accomplishments he earned himself… but that didn’t matter for Maiya’s purposes. Even better, actually.

A few furtive glances, a couple of flirtatious smiles had the boy walking over.

Though not an especially difficult task, Maiya was glad to see her acting skills hadn’t eroded over the past years. She’d had precious little time to practice, what with the realms going to shit and all.

He introduced himself as Rishab, of some family he boasted about, that Maiya promptly forgot about after acting suitably impressed.

All Maiya had to do was looked him up and down and give him coy smiles, and it wasn’t long at all before they were laughing, chatting, and trading all sorts of information about the city, and after he’d had a few drinks, he’d become a veritable fountain of information. She merely played the part of the hapless refugee princess—wide-eyed and fearful. A sheep who knew nothing of the world, looking for someone to guide her.

He loved the sound of his own voice, which made him truly insufferable, but in this case, it was useful. Maiya barely had to put in any effort to extract information.

An increasingly frequent occurrence the more drunk he became.

“And did you know,” he said, leaning closer, “the Altani Council has no idea when the Ash Boundary will finally claim Alt Ashani? Unbelievable, isn’t it?”

“Truly pathetic! Is it really true?” Maiya asked coyly.

“Bah! Of course not. It’s what they tell the masses to keep them from panicking. The truth… the real truth is only known to the highest of the Sawai. Which includes my family, of course.”

“Of course. And what do the highest of the Sawai know?”

“Well, that… er,”

He coughed dramatically.

“I… should not divulge such things.”

“Oh, come now,” Maiya said, batting her eyelashes. “Who would I tell?”

“Hm, true. But keep it quiet, yes? It… is not good. Seems we have less than a year before the boundary claims us. If the Academy’s grand plan doesn’t pay off by then, all will be lost.”

“Grand plan?” Maiya asked. “Tell me more.”

And of course, the drunk boy spilled every detail she asked for. Keeping him on track, however, proved challenging. He constantly wandered into tangents, and she had to gently steer him back without raising his suspicion. She made sure to act curious about a range of topics, just in case he got wise.

He wouldn’t now. That was for sure. But later, when he was sober? That was another story. As light as the mood seemed, Maiya could very well be speaking with an unwitting agent of the Fateweavers. She had to exercise extreme caution.

The risk, however, was worth it, as what she learned would prove immensely valuable for their group.

Rishab’s lips only grew looser with each drink he took, though that also meant Maiya’s opportunity to mine information swiftly came to a close. Once she had squeezed every bit of information out of him, she excused herself, rising from the table.

But she didn’t get far before Rishab’s hand closed on her arm.

“Please, allow me to escort you out,” he slurred, knocking back his chair as he rose unsteadily to his feet. If anyone needed escorting, it was him.

Maiya raised a brow but nodded. There were too many eyes on her to create a scene, and she knew exactly where this would go if she resisted.

She allowed the boy to escort her out into the cool night, where a gentle breeze rustled the leaves of nearby trees.

As inconvenient as it was, she’d be lying if she said having the envious eyes of every man in the room hadn’t felt good. There was a time in her life when she lived for this sort of thing. Now, though, whatever she felt was drowned out by the chance of the Fateweavers catching even the slightest hint as to their true identities.

“Well then, Rishab,” Maiya said, once they were outside. “It has been a delightful evening. Perhaps we shall meet again.”

Hopefully never, she didn’t add.

“The pleasure was mine, Tara,” Rishab said, using the alias Maiya was using. “But please, let me walk you home. It would be ungentlemanly not to.”

She shook her head. “Thank you, but that won’t be necessary. I’ll make my way from here.”

“Oh, come now, don’t be that way,” he said, stepping close. Too close. He extended a hand against the wall next to Maiya’s head and leaned in, pinning her.

Maiya sighed. He simply wasn’t going to get the hint. Perhaps if he were sober, he might have noticed the edge in her tone, but he wasn’t.

She supposed it always came to this.

“Then I graciously accept,” she said, leading him down the street and turning onto a small side road. There were no true alleyways in Alt Ashani—the city had purged anything unsightly long ago. As such, even the smallest streets were safe.

Tonight, however, this one belonged to a predator far more dangerous than any thug or hormone-driven teen ever could be.

The moment they were alone, Maiya made her move.

She didn’t announce her plan. She didn’t give any indication that she was anything other than a weak girl.

She simply moved faster than she ought to have been, clubbing the boy across the back of the neck, knocking him unconscious and sending him crumpling to the ground.

The man would never know what hit him.

Maiya breathed out in relief. At least her combat arts hadn’t left her. Silver linings, and all.

She’d been ready to use her Imperium Artifact dagger. She’d been ready to threaten him with a precharged Wind Blade orb.

In the end, she was thankful she’d overestimated him.

Maiya left him there, feeling only a little guilty as she rifled through his coat and pulled out his coinpurse.

While she and Vir weren’t exactly hurting for currency, they carried mostly demon realm coinage, which would probably raise eyebrows in a universe that seemed not to have linked up with the Demon Realm.

She gave his unconscious form one last look. She had tried to warn him. It wasn’t her fault if he was too oblivious to pick up on that… right?

Maiya left the boy with a skip in her step, entirely oblivious to the fact that it had been her who’d gotten him drunk in the first place.
