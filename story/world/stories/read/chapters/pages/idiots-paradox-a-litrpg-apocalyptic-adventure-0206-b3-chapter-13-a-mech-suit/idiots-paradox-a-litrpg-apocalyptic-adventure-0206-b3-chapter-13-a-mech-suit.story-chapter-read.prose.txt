Whenever he’d figured out a new skill, it took concentration and a calm mind. Through trial and error, he knew that getting frustrated was a quick way to zero breakthroughs. Logan had to empty his mind, push away the worry about the upcoming battle, and think of nothing but the here and now.

It helped that he’d left early in the morning and that the land connecting his cabin to Jack’s was empty of people. Logan could hear the faint splashes from Ernie’s massive battle drills down at the lake, but other than that, it was quiet. Quiet meant no distractions.

Logan took a deep breath. Rolling his shoulders and shaking out his limbs, he bounced on the balls of his feet, stretching his hamstrings. With a crack of his neck, he relaxed his arms to the sides and dipped his head as he stared at the open pouch.

And stared.

Huh. Although Logan had no idea how to approach this, why did he need a new way to form his exoskeleton? Why couldn’t he treat the metal shards as if they were sand? They resembled sand, and he knew how to form his sandstone armour like the back of his hand.

There was trial and error, and then there was being smart.

Deploying [Mimicry Armour], Logan willed the metal to rise in the air. If this material was just like the sand, it should lift into the air as if it were cushioned by a cloud.

And yet… nothing.

He frowned.

Once again, he deployed [Mimicry Armour] and willed the metal into the air. Was that…? He felt a resistance, as if the metal wanted to cling to the other pieces like a magnet. As if the damn things had fused to the pouch. Why wouldn’t they move? Well, if there was one thing he knew, sand was easy to hover because it was lightweight, the fine grains like specks of dust. The metal had to be different, weighty, solid. That meant it would take more effort.

Logan narrowed his eyes and then decided to change his focus. Up in the air while flying, he’d attempted to form his exoskeleton while the wind buffeted him from all sides. An impossible task—at least while flying at high speeds. But practice had made him good at latching onto the sand like a vise and not letting it go. It took more Karma, but it was manageable. If he treated the metal the same way, he might make progress.

Planting his feet in a wide stance, his muscles tightening in readiness, Logan deployed [Mimicry Armour] and pushed. There was a feeling of tightness in his skull, as if the mental strain were causing a physical reaction. Logan pushed again and ground his teeth. One metal grain rose up in the air so quickly Logan could hardly track it, followed by another and another like a reverse sand through the hourglass.

Logan felt a burst of satisfaction. That hadn’t been hard at all. If it needed only that much effort, he should form armour in no time—

Logan jerked his head back in confusion.

Unlike sand which would hover how he wanted it to, these things were fighting him. That first grain of metal jerked in the air in a zigzag, then pivoted like a boomerang, the other grains of metal following it like flying ants in a production line.

The hell?

Just his luck that the System would give him metal that had a mind of its own. He already had to deal with the Cursed Rope and whatever the hell was going on with the Sabáktes dagger, he didn’t want armour with its own personality, thank you very much.

Logan clenched his fist, forcing it to still, which allowed the rest of the metal to catch up. It looked like a mini-black cloud that tumbled in front of his eyes. Glinting in the sun, blasting off an aura that shimmered. Whatever this metal was made of had to be powerful. If he could make this work, he could create armour that was way overpowered than his sandstone exoskeleton.

But first he had to make it work.

With half his attention, Logan peered inside of the open pouch. Everything was gone. All the metal swirled in that small cloud the size of a kitchen bowl. Fuck. There was no way that was enough material to form a full armour suit.

And yet the System description had been clear, which meant something else had to be at work.

[It will never run out.]

Well, only one way to find out. Clenching his fist, Logan used [Mimicry Armour] to force the mass of metal to fly over to him. Logan held out his arm and pulled up the sleeve of his shirt, leaving bare skin. To form sandstone, it was effortless, like riding a bicycle, a mindless motion. He didn’t have to break the construction down. But this time, he had to slow down his natural inclinations and think about the process in detail.

Logan furrowed his brow and then forced a handful of the black metal to jump to his hand. The size of a tablespoon. The little black specks landed on top of his skin, so light that Logan could hardly feel them. They clumped together like mud, but he could tell that one second of lost concentration would result in them falling off like dirt in a windstorm. No, this wasn’t the way.

Back when he’d first formed his sandstone, it had taken mental gymnastics, but he’d figured it out by envisioning the sand weaving together like a patchwork quilt. Each grain of sand had jagged edges, edges that he could match up with the other grains to form his cohesive, flexible sandstone material. Could he do the same with the metal?

With a burst of Karma, Logan commanded the remaining metal to continue hovering in a black storm cloud and then examined the grains on his hand. They shimmered, and yet, Logan suspected that they would emit light even if there wasn’t sunlight blasting on top of them.

Leaning down as he held up his hand, he used his new higher perception attribute to examine one of the specks. Unlike sand, it looked polished, rounded, like a pebble worn down by a river. Each grain was the same. No jagged edges, nothing he could use to slot against the others.

Crap.

Okay, okay, so his normal method of building sandstone wouldn’t work.

Metal. How could you get metal to stick together? The obvious answer was a magnet, but Logan wasn’t a walking magnet, and even if he could create one, the goal here was to form armour that was effortless, effortless like his exoskeleton.

So, not magnets.

You could melt metal and form it into new shapes. Logan thought of gold roasted in a kiln, then poured into a mold. And yet, if he followed that idea to its natural conclusion… yeah no. Logan wasn’t going to roast himself alive.

So, he couldn’t melt the metal, but could he heat up each grain, allowing it to weld to the others? Somehow, he didn’t think that was the answer, since although it would turn into liquid lava, it would still heat up and be painful each time he formed armour. Although [Idiot’s Paradox] had leveled up to the point where it reduced a lot of pain, he wasn’t going around welcoming pain when he could avoid it. Logan wasn’t a masochist. He imagined it would be like taking thousands of tiny specks of burning hot coals and resting them against his skin. It would be like going through the perception trial all over again.

Logan went back to the System’s description again.

[Renewable Flexible Crafting Metal. B grade. This metal flexes, warps, and obeys the user’s will. It will never run out.]

Was he making this too complicated? According to the description, it would flex and warp and obey his will. Something like that shouldn’t require heat to work. But how?

Tentatively, Logan touched the metal clinging to his hand with the fingers of his other hand. They felt rough, like loose gravel or dirt. Not warm, but not cold either.

“How do I make you stick together and smooth out?” Logan mused.

In front of his eyes, the metal twitched and shimmered as if he’d polished it with a wet cloth. It smoothed and became solid and slick.

Logan’s eyes widened. Underneath the new metal coating, his skin tingled with shock. His mouth dry, he licked his lips. That hadn’t come from anything he’d done; the rest of the metal was still hovering in its mini-storm cloud; he hadn’t envisioned anything. Was he dealing with something that needed verbal commands?

“Cover the rest of my hand in smooth metal.”

Feeling like he couldn’t believe his eyes, Logan watched as the metal spread like a pool of dark tar over his fingers, coating them and wrapping around his nails and finger pads, while the metal on the back of his hand spread over his palm. The material glimmered as it stopped at his wrist.

Logan tilted his hand. It looked like a black glove. And it felt neither hot nor cold. Room temperature.

Holy shit. Was it really that easy?

Wait.

Logan tried to move his fingers, then scowled.

Yeah, no. It wasn’t that easy.

Although the metal had obeyed, it was just metal. Unlike his exoskeleton, it wasn’t flexible or breathable. He was covered in the stuff, effectively making his hand into a hand statue.

Logan eyed the hovering metal cloud. It hadn’t moved; [Mimicry Armour] was still active. He studied his hand again. In effect, Logan had just used the metal for its original purpose. According to the System, it would do what he wanted and formed whatever he commanded, but that didn’t mean it would turn into armour.

That’s what [Mimicry Armour] was for.

With a clench of his fist, Logan sent the entire cloud hurtling towards his chest, then forced it to latch onto his khaki shirt. It clung to it like black mud, its tiny grains shimmering with unnatural light.

“Merge together into a cohesive hole, then smooth out, covering my whole body except my face and head.”

The metal obeyed. The pile of mud smoothed out, shimmering and gleaming as it spread down his chest, trickling over his clothes. He had wondered if there would be enough material, but he shouldn’t have worried. It kept expanding. Expanding way more than a pouch’s worth. He suspected that if he wanted to cover a building in this material, it would produce enough to do what he wanted.

“Go faster.”

With a surge, the metal spread to the rest of his body so quickly it was like he’d dumped himself in a bucket of black water. Inch after inch, spreading itself until he’d frozen in place.

Logan looked down. His entire body shimmered in black material, a material that looked slick, System sourced.

Wait.

Aww, man. He’d looked down, but that’s all he could do.

Logan held back a snort. He was a statue, a Logan statue. He couldn’t move his arms. His legs were frozen. If he tried, he suspected that he could use his strength to break out of it, or at least free an arm, but Logan didn’t want to ruin this. At least not yet.

Scrunching his eyebrows, Logan deployed [Mimicry Armour] while he looked down at his new metal covering, envisioning it transforming into a flexible, breathable material. A material that was strong as hell, and yet acted like chitin, giving him mobility.

Nothing yet.

Logan looked down at his fist, at his fingers. He envisioned the material as opaque, as metal that obeyed his will. He was the master here. The skill would obey. The material would adapt.

But still, it needed one more push.

“Obey the skill.”

As if he’d released pressure, a pressure of material fighting something it wasn’t designed for, the metal became less restrictive. His arm moved, followed by his fingers. He could move his leg, then step forward. Taking a running leap, Logan broke into a run, exploding with speed, then turned back around. Unlike the sandstone material, the metal felt lightweight, as if he were wearing clothes. Rolling his shoulders, Logan lifted his arm and flexed his wrist.

Huh.

Although it had worked, Logan wasn’t sure if this was a practical long-term solution. If he had to talk to his damn crafting material every time he wanted to deploy his armour, the battle would be over by the time he was ready. It covered his body in a lightweight material, but what if he wanted to thicken his armour in situations where he needed extra protection? That was the reason the sandstone was so versatile. He could do whatever he wanted with it, even make his boots have extra heels or form spikes. Not to mention his talons. He could tell the metal to form talons, but again, that went back to the whole problem of having to communicate with your armour before you put it on.

With a frown, Logan released [Mimicry Armour]. All at once, the metal became solid and unflexible again, turning him back into a Logan statue.

“Turn back into metal grains.”

With another snap of pressure, the metal fizzled, like bubbling water on a hot element, and the solid mass collapsed, turning into fine particles that fell to the ground and then zoomed into the open black pouch.

Logan paced, eyeing the pouch of metal and huffing. He wasn’t giving up. There had to be a way to make this workable. Well, if he couldn’t use the metal in its natural state, could he ask it to alter itself until he could?

Walking back over to the pouch, he redeployed [Mimicry Armour] and rose the metal back into the air, turning it into its mini cloud. It shimmered, as if the thing had specks of precious gems that glinted in the sun.

“Turn every grain of metal into grains that have jagged edges. No more smoothness. I want you rough, like carved and chipped rock.”

The metal flexed in the air like a swarm of black flies until its shape altered. Nothing perceivable with the naked eye, but Logan was sure it had changed.

Excitement surging, Logan forced the metal grains to latch back onto his hand, then just like with sand, he envisioned them slotting together like a key in a lock. One after another, forming a cohesive, flexible whole. Holy shit, it was as effortless as breathing! His pulse speeding up, his legs feeling on fire with energy, Logan spread the metal up his arms, then his chest, covering his legs. Sculpting boots that shot him an inch up in the air as the metal spread under his shoes, he kicked his feet, beaming from ear-to-ear.

Ding!

[Mimicry Armour is level 6!]

A level up notification!

His new armour moved just like the sandstone material, flexible, breathable. With a blink, Logan activated the camouflage effect, turning himself invisible. Hell yes!

With another blink, he removed the diamond dust from his spatial storage collar and reformed his talons, fusing the material to the ends of his new black gloves. Logan sculpted them until they were nine inches long, curved with wicked edges.

Logan had just formed a mech suit! Logan was frigging Iron Man!

Now it was time for war.
