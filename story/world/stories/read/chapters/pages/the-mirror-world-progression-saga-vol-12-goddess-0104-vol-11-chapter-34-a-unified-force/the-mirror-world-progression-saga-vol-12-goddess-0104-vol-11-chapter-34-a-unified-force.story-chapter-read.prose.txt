All the rest of the elites were occupied fighting against the giant tentacle rings, leaving few capable of stopping the eyes falling like meteors. It didn’t look good to Samantha.

An elven druid, name unclear, activated some reserved ability, and spawned an explosively grown mass of wood like twisted-together tree trunks, arcing upward and batting an eye right before impact, throwing it off and causing it to tumble into the dirt.

A cleromage, name unclear, had been casting divine creation magic to make a force-based barrier, likely with the original intent of temporarily blocking off tentacles. Instead, thanks to communication in the gestalt, she pivoted and generated the wall in the path of an eye. The eye struck the wall, cracked it with a resounding impact, and shattered it. But as the eye continued falling, it was off-track and only clipped one edge of a stone pillar. It cracked, but held where it was, supported by the reinforcing stone of the one minotaur’s efforts earlier.

Samantha was amazed and perhaps a bit touched by these unsung heroes saving the day.

One eye, sadly, was not stopped and crashed into a standing stone structure, shattering one pillar and knocking the rest over as the eye hit the ground and kept rolling.

The ritual’s energy sank and sputtered in a heartstopping sensation that pulsed both through the environment and in the very nerves of all those trying to protect it.

Things looked dire.

Glae did something within the cage, and a massive surge of ghostly, antimagic power bloomed from out of his central position, arcing in all directions like a mutant flower. Fibers of this power branched into the earth and connected to the standing stones, fueling and supercharging them. It made a new structure and directive focus for the circle, now stronger than before. All the lack from before was eliminated as the ritual continued with twice the intensity.

Cheers went out, as all of the combatants on the side of the ritual felt a surge of new inspiration and motivation.

Ha, that bastard had a failsafe! Of course he did. Samantha could tell it hadn’t been long since the effort could be made. Glae had been utterly consumed in the beginning. He’d simply arrived at a new stage where he could act.

Now, Glae was layering the final focal touches into the hammer. Like an undertow, the waves of power radiating out into the world had a portion return, falling into the hammer like a well.

“Just a little longer,” Samantha muttered.

The voice of the demon in the sky spoke again. “The tides seem to have turned, but beware an underdemon. Get it? Underdog, underdemon. A little mortal humor.”

Oh, brother.

The demon eyes, seemingly indestructible, rallied themselves from their various spots, marshalling for new attacks. Rather than rolling, they slid over the ground at high speeds, their cat-like pupils darting around at the front.

Samantha and the others had the initiative on them, however. In the gestalt, she commanded, “Coordinate and concentrate your power to keep them back! I’ll continue to handle two of them.”

Suiting her words, Samantha used two instances of telekinesis to knock two demon eyes into each other, which caused an impact like two mountains colliding. It was enough to disorient and stun them temporarily. Meanwhile, she had her [Conjure] instances create layers of walls between the other demon eyes and the center, to help slow down the creatures others had to deal with.

“I’ve got a third!” called Ozra, as a massive Hard Light polo mallet came swinging down and hammered an eye from the blind side, with a terrible crack of impact. It sent the demon eye flying high and well over any convenient targets, spinning end over end into no man’s land. After such terrible force, the polo mallet cracked and promptly fell apart.

Meanwhile, the Naugite war priest did an admirable job occupying one eye by himself, and the other two stood little chance against the concentrated might of clerics and minotaurs. The minotaurs did an interesting tiered charge, one slamming the eye, knocking it, setting it up for another to knock, and so on, five times, sending the eye tumbling into the foothills on the far border, having knocked a visible trail through the trees.

The cleromages did something simpler: battered the eye with volleys of force-type attacks of various stripes. Though it wasn’t as effective as the minotaur heroes, it got the job done, keeping it well away.

So it went for a brief period as they collectively played keep away. Samantha tried more destructive effects feathered in here and there, but the eyes were not even scratched.

It’s just as Deathsting said: this fucking demon shouldn’t be here. Its existence must always be on borrowed time. You can’t kill this thing, can you? Only endure it until its time is up, or until whatever effort it attempts to interrupt succeeds.

Both demon rings were heavily damaged from counterattack onslaughts, pockmarked with extensive damage and many ripped-up eyes, with entire gaps of the ring broken, basically making it into several sections rather than a ring. Most tentacles had been blown or burned away, and there were only a dozen left, all of them simple, unwieldy clubs. Three of them hit the cage that Glae was protected by, making a sound like a gong each time. Though Gabriel’s shield duration had passed, the attacks still succeeded in nothing, as the cage seemed about as indestructible as the demon eyes were.

Seeing this was satisfying and worrisome at the same time. Glae’s power, at least in his own homeland, was definitely not to be trifled with.

On the other hand, I’ve already broken the unbreakable.

“Well done,” the colossal demon called from above, “But desperation breeds innovation, does it not? Though we hardly remember them, we’ve learned from countless battles…”

The demon's body was still sorely inhibited by Servitors grappling its hands and limbs, and attacks ripping into its admittedly endless, tough flesh.

A foot curled upward, taloned toes contorting in a sign, just as its fingers had done before. Runes Samantha hadn’t caught lit up the surface.

Shit…

Dark energy coalesced all around the ritual grounds, focusing into over twenty ten-foot-tall forms that materialized as eyeless demons of prolific spines, spikes, and teeth. They charged the cage immediately, forcing engagement with the defenders, though they had the numbers.

Samantha pivoted to fly over at incredible speeds, enough to get her [Conjure] use in range. She couldn’t make a ‘wall’ around Glae due to the restrictions of the ritual needing gaps, so she did the next best thing: made responsive decoy bodies fit to occupy and stall the onslaught yet again. Each was glaring bright like her, and at least used illusion to block visibility via a light wall. Maybe it wouldn’t help, but it was worth a shot.

This was technically just a quick thought allocation for her; the rest of the operation was automated. Before the demons were even engaged with these defenses, she threw down a spherical AoE utilizing [Light Manipulation: Twisted Light], to transmute the energy into the heroic energy of the hour so far, as proven by her cleromages: force, which could knock, slow, and stall enemies. Fortunately, the ability also had automatic ‘shaping’, allowing her to avoid affecting her allies.

A pulse of tremendous force energy blasted out in all directions from Glae’s cage as the central point, and basically only started from the new perimeter of her decoy light-forms. Some of the demons had leapt through the air to get to the cage, and these got especially fucked up by the force blast, knocking them entirely out of play. Others were charging through gaps in the defenders, and the force blast only stalled them, stopping them temporarily and causing them to slide back as they resisted the full force of the effect with their prodigious strength. A large number of their apparently fragile spines were broken off and carried away with the power of the blast. Each oozed some luminous green ichor, indicating they were likely full of venom or some corrosive substance.

The effort of slowing them down allowed a new rally in the defenders, as more moved to engage and occupy with attacks, shields, and inhibiting barriers. Spell responses from clerics and Servitors mostly continued to be delayed in the sparse amount of time everything was happening, though it was ongoing.

The more stalled-out demons chose at this time to begin unleashing explosions of their spines, both in radial AoEs and in cones designed to cut through the decoys Samantha had put in front of them. Those with better angles redoubled their efforts to charge, engaging the light-form decoys and shredding them apart, bowling through them.

They were fast creatures and worked in a kind of intuitive, chaotic coordination no one much expected. Inevitably, as they collapsed together and concentrated attacks on points in the defenses, they made gaps that opened up angles to the cage. Precisely two got a wide-open path to attack inside, from range. Rather than use cones, they shot multiple spines through the gap, pelting Glae with oozing darts. This was a sign they had learned from their mistakes.

Samantha thought she heard a chant coming from him falter, practically getting coughed out.

“Welp, he’s dead,” Azure commented in the gestalt with little actual regret. “We did our best.”

Samantha sighed internally. In that instant, thinking, maybe, just maybe, it could happen, it could be allowed that her hated rival died, she somehow knew it would be very, very bad for Orchestration.

The voice of the Director inside was suddenly livid. ‘Won’t you fools ever understand? Only unified could reality be whole! Why?! Why must we run amok again and again, forever divided and broken?!’
