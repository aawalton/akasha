Despite Tetora’s order that he be made immediately aware of his sister’s location, I put my foot down, literally.

“This rag-tag trio needs rest, and so do I!” I snapped with a stomp. “None of us is getting paid overtime, so we have no need to burn out anyone’s candle from both ends.”

“All I need is the location,” he reiterated, his ears swiveling backward. “Then you can sleep soundly—”

“While you go off on a solo hunt?” Nora folded her arms. “Nothing doing. And Galenus agrees on this, so no sneaking around our backs.”

A groan from one of the twins cut off any further argument. Or perhaps due to Chester’s subsequent mrowl, carrying with it the unspoken order to scoop up the princes so they could be carried to their new rooms. Tetora bagged the two with a sigh, and Emmy helped Prince Mito to his feet.

“Oh, about Chester—” I started, but Emmy cut me off.

“I wouldn’t expect the Chosen One’s cat to be anything but extraordinary!” she replied as she followed his lead.

I blinked, my mouth still half-open. That wasn’t what I was going to say at all. “I was just going to mention—”

“Come on, Miss Rachel!” Emmy was already moving toward the hallway. “We should let him rest, too!”

I guess she wanted Mito all to herself.

Nora nudged me with a knowing smirk. “Quick debrief in private, then it’s our bedtime,” she advised.

“Alright.”

Nora reported, much like Castle Amantia itself, the surrounding capital had lacked any holy knight champions. There were plenty of amity users wandering in patrols, but their attacks were swiftly countered before any clash took place. Further questioning revealed that, indeed, neither side ended up close enough to actually engage.

“Swampland,” she said cryptically when I asked her how she managed to pull that off.

“Come again?”

“I got pushback from Aina, but I managed to coax a few clouds to divest themselves of their ideal form.”

“Aina spoke to you?!”

Nora shook her head. “Not exactly. She can alter subroutines like weather, remember? She wasn’t so much yelling at me as she was the clouds themselves. I could still hear it, though, probably because it involved issuing commands to Naught. Anyway, after she canceled my spell over the ones nearby, I just went a little farther out and encouraged them to zip down the jet stream. By the time she realized what I was doing, it was too late.” She paused, then cleared her throat. “I wouldn’t suggest doing it again. I might have spun off a bit of a blizzard towards the east.”

“A bit of a blizzard?!”

Nora shrugged. “I’m sure Aina will stop it before it hits Chairo. Oh, and I think I’m locked out of messing with clouds now, too, so there’s that as well.”

I started to swear at nothing in particular.

“Emmy made it look like she summoned the deluge with her glowing sword,” Nora added when I finally reached for a breath. “So all credit goes to you!”

I bit my tongue, closed my eyes for a good five seconds, then asked, “Casualties?”

“None that I know of!” She coughed. “Just a very wet and disgruntled front line.”

“I can tolerate that for the enemy,” I admitted.

“Well, ours too, kinda.”

I stared at her.

“What? You expect me to control the rain in a precise location? Rather unrealistic, if you think about it.”

“Nora!”

She waved her hand. “Galenus already pulled them out of the muck, so they’re well on their way to clean up and find a nice, warm blanket. Which I suggest we do, too.”

Try as I might, I was out of swears.

The map was already on the table when I came into the war room the next morning. I sat down across from Tetora, who was already waiting, and bobbed my head in acknowledgment. I tried some light banter, but he couldn’t commit; the anxiety over his sister’s whereabouts was plastered all over his face.

Well, at least he was here, giving the princes some peace.

Fabian appeared in the doorway a few minutes later, still looking tired but with a better complexion than last night, and leaned over the map without preamble.

“There,” he said, tapping the middle of the eastern ocean. “Nearhaven.”

Tetora stared in disbelief.

“Are you sure?” I asked.

Fabian nodded. “Nearhaven’s far east from the coast of Ecclesia, surrounded by coral reefs.”

Galenus appeared with a soft buzz and offered the twins a quill and inkpot. “Would you be so kind as to approximate its borders? I will need to make quite a few adjustments to our scanning sensors.”

Fabian flinched and stumbled back.

“He can portal us there,” I explained. “But he has to know about it first.”

“The bastion must recognize it,” he clarified. “Any additional descriptors you may provide will reduce the time needed for verification.”

Fabian looked at me as if asking permission. I nodded even as Tetora shouted, “Tell us about Nearhaven!”

“I’m told it’s a small island with a rocky shore—it’s a place certain scoundrels were sent to over the centuries.”

Nora bopped her way in through a portal. “What type of scoundrels?”

Fabian took a moment to stare at her, too.

“You’ll get used to it,” I murmured.

“Politicians and religious figureheads turning their followers on Chairo,” Fabian said, returning to the topic. “It wasn’t called Nearhaven back then, either. It was The Cliffs—the location was a final sentence. I believe it was abandoned over 500 years ago.”

He paused. “However, recently, one of the pirates—er, independent seafaring merchant groups- has been touting it as a place where nobody knows your name.”

That doesn’t sound so bad, to be honest.

Galenus brayed out a loud laugh, his fingers swiping at several floating runes that had surrounded him. “Excelsior! The Mistress has historical knowledge saved on The Cliffs. I can even see that—”

He fell silent.

“Galenus?” I asked carefully.

“I require Ambrose’s Heart for clearance,” he stated. “The knowledge is considered top secret.”

Nora and I nodded at each other, and she rifled through her cloak.

“Why do you not carry it?” Galenus asked.

“I have enough unique items to keep track of,” I replied.

The answer was a little more complicated than that. However, I didn’t think it was necessary to note in public that Nora still felt guilty about Ambrose’s choice to surrender his body, and so she had insisted on keeping what remained of it safe to return to Aziza when the time was right.

Nora temporarily relinquished the heart to Galenus. It glowed in his hand, and he cycled through several expressions as he scrolled through the endless runes appearing before him. At first, his reaction seemed born of disbelief, but as he continued, his eyes widened, his horizontal pupils shimmering.

“What?” Tetora demanded. “What are you seeing?!”

Galenus straightened. “I believe we will find kindred spirits,” he said. “Allies, if you will.”

“Speak plainly!”

“I have done exactly that,” he quipped, turning to the side. He tapped at several glowing runes. “Coordinates locked. Do you wish for me to transport you now?”

“We’ll go with you. Tiger,” Bodil said from the ceiling above us. “Beigaldi and me.”

“No, you won’t!”

Beigaldi had enough courtesy to show himself. “You won’t be able to convince her to join us on your own. Remember, we were her allies long after you.”

“I’m her brother!”

“Yes. Her brother, who abandoned the clan and left her in charge without resources.”

Tetora’s tail went rigid. “That’s not—I had my reasons!”

I cleared my throat. “I’ll go along as—”

Olethros tugged at my arm. “You are spreading yourself too thin. A good leader knows when to take a step back and delegate, so they can prioritize the tasks only they can do.”

He wasn’t wrong, and I remember telling him that I was getting overwhelmed, but sending Tetora alone with those two?

I frowned. “Will you go, then?”

He shook his head. “Amos will represent my interests in my stead. I would request that you meet Tarek with me later today. He said he would like both of us present when he arrives.”

I turned to Nora, who had once again been absorbed in her magic phone. “What about you? How does going to the beach sound—”

“Nope,” she replied, not looking up. “I prefer sandy beaches to rocky ones. Besides, I need to go pick up Captain Armand.”

I tilted my head sideways in incomprehension. “Captain of the Holy Order of Blue?”

“Are there any others?” She tucked her phone away. “Tetora and I talked. You need someone who can teach mounted saber work properly, and Armand was doing it before either of us was born. I’d prefer Captain Sonea or a subordinate, but they’re all inaccessible in Chairo now. So, I need to wake up Vernie and recruit Armand before whatever’s spiked his faith and trust in you comes crashing down.”

“What if it’s a trap?”

Nora waved a hand dismissively as she walked toward the exit mirror. “Then I trip it. No big!”

Tarek contacted us about an hour later, a low pulse of dark frequency erupting from the mark on my sword.

What am I, some mobile answering machine?

Olethros watched the mark pulse for several seconds, then started to fade. “I will fetch the dark prince. Please excuse me.”

A few moments later, a very red-faced and flustered Prince Mito was pushed through one of the mirrors. He was fumbling to put on a shirt, lipstick smeared across one cheek.

“Have you no decency, foul demon?!” he cried, before freezing at the sight of me.

My eyes widened as I started to turn red in sympathetic embarrassment. “He interrupted your reunion with Emmy, didn’t he?”

“Miss Rachel!”

“What?”

He groaned, hiding his face in his hands. “It would be polite to ignore me…”

“But I approve. Of your reunion, I mean. Not… interrupting it.”

Olethros formed next to me. “His immediate presence is necessary, I assure you.”

I gave him a side-eye. “Alright.”

Olethros looked up at the ceiling. “Send His Highness first. As soon as he finishes buttoning his shirt.”

After Prince Mito was translocated, Olethros leaned in.

“I know I said to delegate and prioritize, but you may wish to learn a few things from Emmy when you have a moment. Feel free to practice her maneuvers on me at any time.”

“Will you be serious?!”

“I’m being exactly that. My offer is genuine.”

“Galenus!” I shouted, turning away. “Send Olethros off now!”

He disappeared with a laugh as the dark vines engulfed him, and I took a few moments to compose myself before following in their stead.
