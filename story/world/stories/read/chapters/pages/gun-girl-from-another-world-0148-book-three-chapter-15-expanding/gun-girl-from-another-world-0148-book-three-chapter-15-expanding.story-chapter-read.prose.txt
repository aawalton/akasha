Chapter 15

Expanding

Everyone looks up when I come sprinting into the main hall with Empower active, but we don't have time for their bewilderment.

“Grab your things, time to go,” I'm calling out as I barrel through. “Time and dungeons wait for no woman! In fact, if it's not irreplaceable, just leave it!”

My behavior clearly confuses Ayre, and the archer's face twists to one side. “Why are you in such a hurry? We've still got several–”

My best friend's expression shifts again radically, now halfway between a glare and a frown, when the room begins to shake. “Oh, right, we left you alone.”

“With brain juice,” I chirpily remind the elf with a beaming smile.

“But I haven't finished studying,” Angel protests.

I give her a quick headpat. “We'll be right back as soon as the dungeon finishes settling! Then we can finish your tutoring and check out the new upgrades together!”

That makes Ayre look at me with suspicion. “... Remmi, did you–”

“No time,” I insist. “Unless you want to be keeping Angel eternal company, get those gams going!”

I scoop up Korrigan without waiting any longer. Kyuuga takes that as his signal, too, and darts for the exit. I give Angel one last wave and chase after him, Ayre on my tail.

“Remember,” I call out as the park-like main hall disappears behind the curve of the yellow-illuminated stairwell, “don't stop at the exit! We have to keep running until we're clear of the whole zone of control!”

“That's a long sprint, Remmi,” Ayre shouts up to me.

“It could be all the way back into town, and it wouldn't matter,” I reply. “Either we make it, or we retire here! Good news is, the quaking is slower than we are! We can outrun it if we keep up the gas!”

“Remmi, that doesn't mean anything,” comes Korrigan's complaint.

But Ayre doesn't need a translation. Context clues are enough. “She means keep running!”

And that's what we do. We break back out into the real sunlight and fresh air, and I've never been so grateful for a remodeling job. If we had to run through the tall grass and misdirection of the old field, the drag would drain us like a vampire. Imagine sprinting for all you're worth just to find out you ended up off-course and …

I feel my face go slack as I follow that thought home. If that effect is still up, then chasing the road is actually the wrong direction.

“Everyone,” I shout as I veer sharply for the tree line, “get off the road! Head for the trees!”

“Remmi!” Ayre is slow to follow, but too loyal to leave me. “We have to head straight back! We have to follow the road!”

“Yes,” I immediately agree. “After all, what happens when we get too far off course around the Forest Cavern Dungeon?”

“It sends us back out!” There’s a pregnant moment of silence as he realizes what he just said, then he repeats himself with zeal. “It sends us back out! Oh, Remmi, you genius! I could kiss that Four Hundred Intellect brain of yours!”

“Technically, it's still twelve hundred for another half hour!” The fog starts wrapping around us. “Lost Woods, here we come! Time to boundary break and bail out of here!”

The fog goes to Silent Hill levels, then full-on wall in moments, and it feels like something is clawing at us, gripping our hems, trying to find hold on our limbs and shoulders and waists, struggling to pull us back as the whole world rattles to collapse underneath us.

And then it's all gone. Between one stride and another, the air is clear, the earth is still, and our momentum is free of the pull so suddenly that we all go sprawling across the ground. I manage to tuck and curl Korrigan so I don't land on her, but the impact knocks her out of my arms like I'd forgotten how to keep my grip closed.

The coachman we'd left my carriage with rushes over to stand over us. “Hero Remmi,” he gushes, “are you alright?! You all suddenly appeared from nowhere and threw yourselves to the ground!”

My first thought isn't to answer him, however. I sit up as quickly as I can and cast my eyes around. “Role call! Sound off!” After a heartbeat, I think to clarify. “That means let me know you made it! Ayre?”

The elf is pulling himself up slowly, taking in heavy breaths to recoup the oxygen debt all of that running had built up. “I … I'm here …”

“Korrigan?” I know she's here, but never skip checks when the danger is high.

“You dropped me,” she whines.

“Remmi? Here!” I check myself and answer immediately, more on impulsive levity than with any thought about it.

I'm halfway through a sigh of relief before I choke on it. “Wait, what about Kyuuga? Did he make it?” I get up halfway to standing as I look everywhere my head can turn. “Does anyone see Kyuuga?!”

“KYUUUUU!!!”

Something small and fluffy collides with the side of my head, sending me sprawling out and skidding across the ground again. One hundred thirty-four pips of health this time. He really is doing it on purpose.

“Found him!” It’s Korrigan’s voice, and as I pull myself back out of the dirt again, she’s already got her arms around the large rabbit, who is soaking in the attention, both of them acting like the sneak attack on me didn’t just happen.

I rub my cheek tenderly. “Kyuuga, you’re a menace,” I grumble, and I swear I catch him sticking his tongue out at me.

“What happened?!”

The coachman’s repeated demand reminds us all that he’s still here, but motion from the direction of the dungeon interrupts him again and captures our attention. A fog bank billows out like an ocean tidal wave and crashes against the border of the domain mere feet from us, close enough to make us all recoil. It doesn’t cross it, though, instead roiling upward and revealing the border to be a sphere up into the atmosphere. We all stare until the entire thing is filled to solid with impenetrable fog.

And, even then, a few more moments pass before I remember I was asked a question that I’m still not entirely sure how to answer. “Uh … dungeon things?”

I’m pretty sure I hear Ayre mutter, “Twelve hundred intellect,” under his breath, but what I don’t hear is him giving any better answer, so I ignore it.

Instead, I stare at the fog so packed that there isn't even any visible swirling. “Hey, Ayre, how long does it usually take a dungeon to finish expanding?”

I know he's giving me judgmental stares into my back without having to look at him. I can hear it in his voice even as his response is perfectly passive. “I don't know. I've never witnessed a dungeon expansion. You'd have a difficult time finding anyone who has. It's something we actively try to prevent.”

The coachman's voice jumps several hysterical octaves. “The dungeon's expanding?!” He jumps up and starts moving for the horses. “We need to move! We need to warn people! We need to–”

I jump up and grab hold of his coat. “Easy, there, buddy. Easy. It's a controlled, intentional expansion. We're perfectly safe. It's all part of the plan.”

From my new angle, I can absolutely see Ayre crossing his arms with a cock of his hips. “A plan that probably should have been run by … I don't know, anybody?”

I surprise him when I turn contrite. “You're completely right, Ayre. This was a serious matter. Both the High Temple and the Throne deserved to be consulted. I bear full responsibility for any displeasure they may have. The next time we're faced with an expansion in a matter of hours with no way to cull the dungeon, I'll send out letters to them both, and the regional lord and the mayors of any local settlements before I make any hasty decisions.”

I can tell he sees what I'm doing long before I finish, because his shock turns into sour annoyance. “We can hold a committee where all affected parties get consulted, any proposed ideas can be discussed, and the proper channels make the final call.” I put on an innocently contemplative expression anyway, tapping one toe on the ground as I hold my cheek. “How long do you think that would take? Should I plan a luncheon?”

He gives a deep sigh. “Remmi, I understand that we were working on a hard time limit. Obviously, I'm not saying call the whole empire. But you didn't even run it by us! I gave you the benefit of the doubt because you acted like you had some kind of plan, but if I had known that plan involved triggering an expansion event, I definitely would have asked more questions. Instead, you talked like it was just a precaution if your plan didn't work.”

He’s got a point, and I lower my head, genuinely this time. “Ayre, I’m sorry you feel like I hid important information from you, but the truth is that I didn’t know that was going to be my plan, either. Going in there, I assumed there was some new puzzle, or maybe the apples gave all the monsters regeneration, so if you couldn’t do enough damage outright, they’d just pop right back to full. I didn’t expect …”

Lost for words, I motion back toward the direction we came from with both of my hands. “... Y’know, all that. Obviously, we couldn’t just shoot Angel, which is probably the same problem everyone else ran into, too. Sure, that would have instantly solved the problem, but who’s heartless enough to do that? What if the poor girl remembered it?”

“... So you just decided on a whim to try to rewrite a dungeon core?” Ayre’s face has softened, and it’s almost more of a question born of confusion than an accusation.

“It was in the dungeon alert,” I say, raising a finger. “At some point in the past, the System expected that a dungeon expansion could be a planned, deliberate thing. But why? If a dungeon expansion is just an uncontrollable land grab, why would it be something anyone would want to happen?”

I don’t wait for anyone to come up with the answer on their own. “Because the dungeon has a trait you want, but not enough of it, or it doesn’t have enough energy to sustain it. Our problem was a bit of both. The dungeon spent so much energy on making Angel that it didn’t have an actual dungeon to cull anymore. To have the space and energy for something to actually burn that energy on without deleting her, it had to be allowed to grow. It was the only way to save both our new friend and the region.”

The archer nods slowly. “Okay, I’m following you so far, but what made you think you could dictate an entire expansion? Sure, you had an impact before, but that was just a suggestion when the core was in a vulnerable state. It was still the core that made all of the decisions.”

“Because it had to work!”

“... Remmi … that is not reason enough to risk an entire region.”

But I shake my head. “I’m serious! If we accept that expansions can be planned, then it must be something that doesn’t need to be left completely to chance! Why permanently give up valuable land and increase the potential threat level of a local dungeon if you’re just gambling that the dungeon is going to do what you want? If there’s no way to reason with the core, then it would just do more of whatever it perceived to be successful by its own internal metrics.”

I cross my arms with a firm, serious nod. “Instead of giving us anything to cull, the Forest Dungeon would probably have just spent that new energy making another Angel. And then it would expand again, and make another, and again, and make another, until the whole of the region is overrun with cute, innocent, helpful bird girls! A birbpocalypse!”

As everyone stares at me in the wake of my stunningly brilliant conclusion, I remember that it’s not just my party here, and I slowly turn my head around to stare narrow-eyed at the coachman. When he notices, he jerks back.

“This stuff’s top-secret, okay?” I tell him with deadly seriousness. “The guild will get a report, and then they’ll decide who gets to know. You’ve got to keep your mouth shut about this, or we’ll sic Dina on you!”

“Of course, of course,” he’s quick to promise. Maybe the receptionist’s scarier than I thought? “I understand. This isn’t the first time I’ve heard things above my station. Don’t worry! Tight lips are part of my contract! Besides, I still barely understand what’s going on!”

While I’m still staring at him like I’m trying to figure out just how much worry I should have, Ayre is following my logic through to the same conclusion I laid out.

“So in minutes, you assessed the situation, made the connection with the System alert, and came up with a completely new plan?”

“Well, technically, the plan took a few hours,” I humble-brag. “Or at least making sure it was suitably detailed did.”

“Wow,” Korrigan breathes, her own vacant expression suggesting she’s still quite lost, “Hero Remmi is amazing!”

Ayre’s sigh this time is more one of surrender, his ears dropping nearly to pointing at his shoulders as he turns slightly away and to the side. “Yeah, okay, considering the options we had to work with, I guess you did alright.”

I lean in close to my best friend, tilting the side of my head toward him as if expecting something, but the elf stares back at me in confusion.

“Remmi, what are you doing?”

“You said you wanted to kiss my genius brain! Here’s your chance!”

Ayre stares for a long moment, then does exactly what I expect, the hard impact of the archer’s bow colliding with the side of my skull.
