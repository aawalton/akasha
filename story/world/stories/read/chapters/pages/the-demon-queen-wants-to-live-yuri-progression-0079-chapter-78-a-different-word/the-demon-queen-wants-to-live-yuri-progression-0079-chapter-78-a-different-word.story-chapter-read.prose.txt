"What does that word mean to you?"

It was a strange question. I had prepared myself for one thing: for Ash to request a difficult boon. That would explain how long she had taken to ask, and the hurried pulse of her hand. The pulse of someone about to request a thing she feared herself unworthy to receive. Sara had taken half an afternoon to ask me to make Marin bloom again. Whatever it was, I had already decided the answer before the question came.

If Ash asked for a thing, I would give it. If it required gold, I would acquire gold. If the thing did not exist, I would make it exist. If it existed and belonged to another, that was a matter easily solved. Instead, she had asked me what a word meant.

I looked at her. The stream moved past our feet and carried the stars along with it, and Ash was waiting for my answer. "In this world, a retainer is someone who has my trust," I said. "Someone I keep in confidence."

I felt her receive my answer. Ash did not receive my answer as I had intended it. The faint crease between her brows deepened, and her thumb went still against the side of my hand. "Is that all a retainer is to you?"

"Those are not petty things, as you seem to be thinking," I said. "Trust in my time was difficult. Many have served me for centuries without earning it. In my long life, I have trusted only one other. Do you understand what I am giving you?"

Her frown stayed. When she spoke, her voice was very soft. "I know," she said. "That's the hard part."

I did not understand that. Ash was not ignorant. She seemed to understand what I was offering her, and yet she found it lacking all the same. Why had her voice gone soft? "If you have some complaint, then you should voice it, Ash. No Queen can read the mind of her subjects. Not even I."

Ash looked away from me and up at the stars. "Her subject," she murmured. Her hand left mine. The warmth went with it. I felt the exact moment she let go of my hand. It was no colder now than it had been a moment ago. Therefore, the absence should have been a small thing. It was not a small thing. Something inside me hurt.

My hand closed against the grass. The blades bent between my fingers and remained there, trapped in my fist. They felt made of fire.

"You have many retainers now, you know," she said, looking at the sky. "Though I suppose you haven't realized it yet."

"I have three, at present. You, Sara, and Solara. Three does not many make." My voice was smooth, which surprised me.

I understood some of what the Hero was reaching for now. "I am not rebuilding my kingdom," I said. "If there are presently a few strays who have attached themselves to me, that is a temporary affliction. Before long they will think better of it. I am certain."

"I don't think they will, Lys."

"And how can you say that?"

"Experience." She shrugged with one shoulder. "You can always tell. And I wouldn't be surprised if you get more, as time goes on."

I said nothing. A cool breeze came from the east and blew across my face. Even if the Hero was right in what she said, that did not explain why she sounded so. It made no sense. Not unless I considered it from a new angle. Perhaps Ash wanted followers of her own, though she would never want for admirers. If she wanted a court, she had only to hold out her palm and wait. Perhaps she was blind to it. Heroes so often were.

"Ash," I said, "if you wish, you can simply-"

"I'm your retainer, Lys," Ash said. I stopped. "You say you have three. But you have more than three, and you'll have more still." She was still looking at the sky. "In the arena, you made Edda stand up for herself. Do you know that Corin always calls you Your Grace? He does it when you aren't listening, because you told him not to, but he still does it. Then there's Ren. She skipped all of her classes for three days just to help you practice. That's not even the end of it. Alisa -your rival- gave you two Essence Hearts. And we just found out they're supposed to be valuable." Ash's faint smile dipped until it was only a frown. "That's how it starts."

"That proves nothing except that this Academy is full of fools," I said.

"So how long?" Ash said. "How long before-" The Hero paused.

"How long until what?" I asked.

Ash considered for a moment before answering. "Until you have even more retainers. Dozens of them. And when you do…I'll just be one of the dozens," she said. "How long until the word retainer doesn't mean anything at all?"

The objections came to me at once. There would never be these 'dozens'. The title of retainer was not one I gave out so freely. And as such, the word would always mean what I had told her. I almost said it. But I knew that none of this would soothe whatever afflicted Ash now.

"You know, everyone always wanted me as the Hero," Ash eventually said. "They all called me The Champion of the People. Of the People, Lys. A Title that belongs to everyone, while also belonging to no one." She looked away from me. At the grass. "Even my mother's eyes sparkled for a song about a stranger. You're the first person who's ever-" She stopped.

The Hero had wanted to say more, and yet had not. I still understood something of what she was trying to say now. She was talking about the veil she wore. Not her mark, but the life she had lived to get it. Ash, a woman who had spent one life learning to be less, was asking whether time would make less of her in this life too. It was simple arithmetic. More prestigious to be one in three than to be one in three hundred.

"Heroes are fools," I said.

Ash turned to look at me. "What?"

I raised one hand toward the air, toward the stars. "There is one thing you have gotten wrong, Hero. There are retainers, and there are retainers. Those two may sound the same, but they do not mean the same thing. A Queen may possess a hundred thousand who follow behind her, but those hundred thousand do not serve at her side. Only the lucky few have that right. In my old life, all of my Generals watched from a distance not of our making. You have read that journal, and so you know this as well as I."

Ash opened her mouth, then closed it. She had read the same journal.

I took a deep, steadying breath and looked at her. "The same word can mean different things, Ash. For almost all, there is always a distance." I paused. The words that followed were simple. They did not leave simply. "You are the one who is not at a distance."

Ash went very still. "So you're saying I'm... a special retainer."

"If that is how you wish to understand it. Yes," I said.

Ash was quiet. She was quiet for so long that I thought I might need to repeat myself. "And how can you be sure it will stay that way?" she asked. Her voice was very soft.

The answer was obvious. So obvious I was surprised the Hero had not come to it herself. "Because I have said it. And if I say a thing, it will be so, or I will bend the world until it is."

Ash laughed. I had heard the Hero laugh many times by now. This laugh sounded strangely choked. "Right," she said. "I suppose you would answer like that, wouldn't you." Her voice was warm, but there was something pained beneath it.

"If you find the word retainer ill suits you," I said slowly, "then I will grant you a different one. You may choose it yourself, if you wish. If that is what you require, then so be it."

"A different word," Ash said. And for some reason, color climbed up her face.

"Oh?" I turned toward her. "Do you have one in mind, then?"

"No- yes." Ash looked at her hands. "I don't know. I didn't think this conversation would go the way it has."

"How did you think it would go, Hero?"

Ash opened her mouth to say something and closed it without saying anything at all. She tried again, and failed again. I waited for her. A Queen could be patient when patience served her. Ash stared at the water as though the correct answer might drift past our feet. After some time, I decided to help her.

I straightened. "Very well then, Ash. If you have trouble coming up with a title, then I shall give you one. From this day forth, you will be styled as Queen's Own Blade." Yes, now that was a name fitting of Ash.

"Lys." Ash let out a small groan. Strange. Perhaps she simply did not understand why I had chosen this for her.

"This name is as accurate as it is fitting. You are deft with a blade, you are mine, and I am a Queen."

"It's-" Ash pressed a hand over her eyes. "It's…it's a bit too literal for my taste."

How curious. Ash wanted a name with deeper meaning, did she? That was easy to provide. "Then how about I style you as: First Sword of the Dark Tower."

"That's a lot of words, Lys. And we don't live in a dark tower."

"I have not ruled out reacquiring one," I said. "Surely this new world will have something resembling one."

Ash pressed a hand over her face. Her shoulders shook. The Hero made no sound, but I suspected she was laughing at me.  I chose not to punish the offense. I was nothing if not generous. "If these names do not please you, then I will wait," I said.

"You are foremost among my retainers. That much I grant now, and it is not a small grant." I looked at the stream. "If you need time to find the word you seek, then you may have all the time you wish." How strange. A Queen's court was not supposed to have vacancies, yet mine now had one.

Ash did not answer for a long moment. "It went differently in the book," Ash murmured. "Though I guess there she actually-" Ash stopped and her mouth closed so fast I heard her teeth meet.

I turned to look at her. Ash's face was a shade of red I did not think human faces could reach. "You are referring to that book," I said.

"It's nothing. I didn't say anything about a book," Ash said.

She refused to meet my eyes. Did she think I would mistrust my own ears? "You spoke of the book you would not show me just now, I am certain of it." I leaned toward her. "You consulted this book…for this?"

"Oh god, don't say it like that, Lys."

"Then I would like to see it for myself."

"Lys, I would rather the earth open up and swallow me whole."

I sat back. This was, I recalled, only the second time I had ever seen Ash look genuinely stricken. The first had been at the inn, when that strange man had approached me. I rose to my knees and pointed. "Was this a book on human mating rituals?"

"WHAT?" Ash sat up immediately. "No- no, oh god, not this one, this one was nothing like that, I assure you Ren didn't-"

"This one?" I said, eyebrow raised.

There was a small chirp from somewhere in the grass. We both turned to see Solara raising her head. The tiny phoenix's beak slowly opened and closed in what must have been a yawn. She blinked at us, and her enormous golden eyes opened and closed once. We had disturbed her rest, evidently.

"...Sorry, Sol," Ash said. Sol let out another chirp, tucked her four wings, and went back to sleep.

We looked at her for a moment. Then I looked at Ash again. "Well?"

"It's not that kind of book," Ash said. Her voice was lower now, but I could see it strained her. She was not meeting my eyes. "But it's... I guess it's not too far off."

I nodded slowly. "Very well. For now, I shall believe you. Though it would be no bad thing if it were. Human customs are strange, and I would like to research more."

"Lys. Please don't say it like that," Ash said. Her voice sounded even more strained now than it had before.

"I said nothing improperly."

Ash groaned into both hands. Then she laughed brightly. The way she always did. "Honestly. What am I going to do with you?"

"That should be my question, Hero."

Ash dropped her hands. Her smile was still there, but it was smaller now. It was no less bright for it. "The book made this sound a lot easier," she said, mostly to herself. "Ronan was right after all."

"Right about what?" I asked.

"Nothing." She shook her head. "Something about songs."

I did not understand what the Hero meant, but right now, it did not matter. Something else was far more important. "Ash. This concern of yours. Is it still there?"

Ash looked away from me. She took a very deep breath. "Yeah…I guess it's still there," she said eventually.

"Ash. I do not want it to be this way," I said at once. "If you have a concern, then voice it, so I may address it properly. I do not want you to carry it for longer than you have to."

The Hero's smile returned to being as wide as it had been. It did not feel nearly as bright as it had a moment ago. There was sadness within it, but I did not think it was directed at me. "Well, Lys…" Ash shrugged. "I just don't know if you can," she said. Then her hands raised quickly. "Not that it's your fault! I guess it makes sense that you wouldn't know. And it wouldn't be fair of me to ask anyway, not right now."

"If this is a problem of understanding," I said, "then I wish it corrected. Your vagueness serves only to vex me, Hero."

Ash laughed. "Sorry. I guess I'm used to this too, when it comes to-" She trailed off and took a breath. "I guess I want to be more than just a retainer, Lys."

I turned the request over. It made little sense. It was a boon I thought I had already granted mere minutes ago. She did not have the name for what she wished for, so how could I grant it to her? All I could do was leave a space by my side, and let Ash style herself however she wished when she took it. I chose to reassure her again.

"Very well, I will say it again," I said. "Ash, you are foremost among-"

Ash smiled. "I figured you'd say that."

She leaned in. I froze. My heart skipped. One beat always led to another which led to another still, and yet the order was skipped. Ash's face came very close. Closer than it had been in the chamber, closer than it had been that day when she had shown me her stretches. Her lips parted. Her hair fell forward and brushed my cheek. Then her lips rose, and she kissed my forehead.

Everything around me went still. The entire world seemed to hold its breath, just for that moment. Ash's lips were very warm, and very soft. Even the Ember Core seemed quiet compared to the beating of my own heart. I knew what this gesture was. I am not a fool. I had seen it in Hamel, between mothers and children and the married by a bonfire. I had seen it traded between a few of my own generals and some of their followers, when they believed I could not see. Zera had tested that boundary often.

Regardless, this was not a gesture anyone had ever tried with me. None had been fool enough to. No, that was not quite right. One had almost dared, and she had paid for it. Though perhaps not so much as I had once thought.

Ash pulled back. "What manner of attack is this?" I demanded. My face was very warm. I was aware that my face was very warm, and there was nothing I could do about it.

Ash grinned wide. "Lys. What I'm trying to say is that- I want to be more than just a retainer to you. Well, if you'll have me, of course."

They were almost the same words she had said before, but they did not sound like the same words. "I have already granted you that honor," I said. My voice sounded like a small thing.

Ash sighed. "What will I do with you?" It was unclear whether she was actually asking.

We sat for another moment. The stream moved, and I saw the stars move with it. The place on my forehead still felt warm.

The Hero was resting her shoulder against mine again. I noticed it now like I rarely ever had before. Eventually, Ash drew her legs from the water and reached for her shoes. "Come on. Let's go back. It's getting late." Ash looked around. "Even the city is starting to fall asleep."

I rose. My legs were steady. They had no reason not to be. I slowly knelt down, and gathered Solara into my cupped hands. Her golden eyes opened once and then closed. Carefully, I set the tiny phoenix on my crown.

Ash looked at me, and I looked at her. Then she was looking down at my arm. I was sure she was not looking at my mark. "We should return the way we came, Hero," I said, and extended my hand out toward her.

Ash looked at my hand. Her smile slowly returned, and she took it.

From there, we climbed the bank together. In the distance, I could still hear some of the sounds of this 'festival'. There were people playing songs for a victory over a dragon that had never existed, in the name of a god I had never heard of.

We walked toward them. The spot on my forehead stayed warm, which was impossible. Many things around my Hero were.
