“How is it even possible for you to have so much turbulence?”  Skippy complained as Kyle soared through the air, twin bundles of vines trailing behind him.

“If you don’t stop whining, I’m going to leave you here,” Kyle growled, continuing to push Aura Force as fast as he could.

After providing field triage to Tillienne, he’d received a warning from C.H.A.D.D. that a fungal construct wandered into the central cavern, only to be immediately destroyed by the potent antifungal mist.  As C.H.A.D.D. expanded its search, the drone quickly reported that many of the constructs were still shuffling about, all seeming to mindlessly go about the last tasks they’d been assigned.  That discovery had spurred them to return to the shuttle more quickly, a decision that proved to be a prudent one as they drew closer and saw the shambling horde of constructs all moving in the same direction.  It didn’t take Kyle long to realize what their orders may have been, as they saw more and more all heading toward the landing site.

[DR. MAYHEW HAS AN UNFORTUNATE HABIT OF UNNECESSARY JOSTLING,] C.H.A.D.D. agreed, much to Kyle’s chagrin.

“Hey now, be fair,” Tillienne said from within her own pod.  “It’s got to be exhausting cleaning up after your friends risked life and limb to do all the heavy lifting.”

“Now that you mention it, he really couldn’t have done it without us, could he?”  Skippy agreed.  “I think it’s fair to say that he owes us each a favor.  Like an actual sparring match when I’ve recovered.”

[YOUR CONTRIBUTIONS WERE CERTAINLY CRUCIAL TO THE MISSION’S SUCCESS.]

“And if you’re not careful, I’ll stop and take some notes to learn from how you deal with an army of mushrooms,” Kyle snapped back.

Of course, there was no real heat in his voice.  Despite the lighthearted ribbing, they were right.  Without their help, DeRosa’s amplification field would have severely limited Kyle’s own domain skills.  Even if he could have escaped the cavern, he wasn’t confident that he could have finished DeRosa.  The man had more Vitality than any other C Grade Kyle had seen, and within the heart of his power, it wasn’t a stretch to imagine him using some final, life-saving measures.

So, for now, he kept his eyes forward as he flew toward the waiting shuttle.  They were nearing the edge of the fungal army when Kyle saw smoke rising on the horizon.  With a deep breath Kyle allowed his domains to expand, pressing down with Aura Force as Devourer’s Domain ripped away what little mana infused each creature, further fueling his skills as he propelled himself forward.

As he tore through the encroaching army, he finally saw smoldering waves of fire burning around a raised stone shell.

[DR. MAYHEW…] C.H.A.D.D. began.

“I know,” Kyle replied.

The sheer number of creatures far surpassed what they’d expected.  Worse, Kyle was beginning to feel pressure on Aura Force as his mana wasn’t returning to him the way it normally did.  Devourer’s Domain still helped to fuel his skill by drawing from the creatures, but with Tillienne and Skippy both unable to fight, fighting through these numbers would be a slog.  If the expedition was in a similar condition as Kyle expected, they’d need to retreat, and soon.

All banter ceased while Kyle pushed his speed to the limit, narrowing the focus of Aura Force to limit the mana loss while drawing as much as possible from Devourer’s Domain.  A few of the fungal constructs launched tendrils and spears toward him when he drew closer to the still-smoldering perimeter, which he didn’t even try to dodge.  The blows simply weren’t powerful enough to pose a threat, and he didn’t want to delay even a second more than necessary.  Finally, he crested the lip of the stone barricade, where he saw Chester Drake waiting outside the shuttle.

“Is it done?”  The Corthian Mining foreman asked, exhaustion clear on his face.

“It is,” Kyle confirmed.

“Those are your companions’ bodies?”  Chester pressed.

“I’m not dead!”  Skippy whined.

“Then get inside.  We’re leaving.  Now.”

Kyle didn’t argue as he landed near the ramp to the shuttle and rushed inside, Skippy and Tillienne still in tow.

He felt a small surge of power from Chester behind him, and then the entire stone construct exploded outward, showering the approaching fungal soldiers with stony shrapnel.  The man followed right behind Kyle, and before the dust could even clear, the shuttle’s thrusters engaged and the ship took to the air.

Rushing Skippy and Tillienne to the medical station, Kyle passed by dozens of slumped and unconscious expedition members who otherwise looked relatively uninjured.  Arriving at the med bay, he saw a haggard-looking Randolph applying an antifungal potion to a deep puncture wound in a Fire Mage’s shoulder.  He gave Kyle a nod as he entered, and tossing a look at the bundles of vines behind him, gestured toward two empty tables.

“What happened to everybody else on the ship?” Kyle asked as he set Skippy and Tillienne down, slowly unraveling their transport cocoons.

“Ask your damn friend when he wakes up,” Randolph said with a huff, ignoring the wince of pain on his patient’s face as he began to mend the puncture wound.  “Some skill of his overdrew the mana of virtually every remaining combatant.  They’re all going to have a killer headache when they wake up.  I bet half of them are going to be darkening my door for painkillers when I’m still treating actual patients.”

Kyle blinked at that, surprised to hear that Garth was the cause.  “Sounds like a hell of a skill.”

Randolph snorted.  “It was.  If he hadn’t bought the time he did, we’d all be in orbit already, and you’d be making new friends with some mushrooms.”

“Then it sounds like I owe Garth a beer,” Kyle replied, though his expression was pure focus as he used Identify to examine Skippy and Tillienne’s injuries.

As he’d feared, the transit back to the ship hadn’t been kind to them, particularly Tillienne.  Her lower-left shoulder was still a mess, and though Kyle had excised most of the corruption from the fungal tendrils, there were still shards of bone and necrotized tissue that he needed to address.  Skippy’s condition was better, though he still had torn muscle fibers all across his body that Kyle would need to mend.

He settled in and got to work, his everyday prosthesis appearing on his left shoulder.  In short order, he began to feel his mana reserves recharging the way they should have, and tossed a glance to Randolph who drew in a deep, satisfied breath.

“Feels good to actually be getting mana again,” he said.  “Not fun for anybody when I can’t afford to use anesthetic skills.”

“Wait, that was an option?”  Skippy asked.  “Mayhew never used one on me, and he’s healed me a bunch of times.”

“I’m a field medic, so deal with it,” Kyle replied with an exasperated sigh, though he was glad to hear his friend doing better.

When Skippy and Tillienne were both stabilized to the best of Kyle’s ability, he looked at the triage list, then turned to Randolph.  “Would I be getting in your way if I helped work through the list?”

The bearded man didn’t look up from the stitches he was applying.  “If you’ve got the energy to spare, I’d appreciate it.”

Kyle simply nodded and got to work.  He and Randolph fell into a rhythm, with Kyle priming most of the patients with a targeted use of Healing Impetus, while Randolph would use more precise healing techniques as needed.  They worked in amicable quiet, making quick progress through their patient list.  Finally, as the overdrawn but uninjured fighters began to wake, a call came through the intercom.

Kyle Mayhew, your presence has been requested at the bridge.

“I guess that means my shift is over,” Kyle joked, setting her left leg in place before pushing one more small wave of Healing Impetus into the broken tibia.

“So it does,” Randolph agreed, moving to wash his hands, then walking up to Kyle.  “Thank you for your help, and I’m glad you made it back.  It was an honor working with you, Dr. Mayhew.”

Kyle felt like he’d taken a physical blow hearing somebody other than C.H.A.D.D. use the title.  A warm, genuine smile crossed his face as he shook Randolph’s hand.  “The honor was mine.  And when all this is over, if you’re looking for a place to settle down and set up a practice, let me know.  Your expertise would be welcome as we get established.”

“I might do just that,” the bearded man replied.  “Now get going, you don’t want to keep Chester waiting.”

Kyle made it to the bridge, where Chester and Garth were already waiting.  Chester nodded for him to enter, and as the doors closed behind him a projection of Angela Corthus appeared.

“Good to see you alive, Mr. Mayhew,” she said.  “I’ve heard the reports from Chester, but wanted to hear the details from you.  First and foremost, is he dead?”

“He is,” Kyle confirmed.

“Did he suffer?”

The question took Kyle off guard, but as he thought about the final explosion of antifungal, the answer came to him surprisingly quickly.  And with a chill running down his spine, he found that he didn’t particularly care.  “Yes.”

The projection gave a curt nod.  “Good.  Now, with my burning curiosity sated, please give me your report.”

Kyle nodded, detailing the approach, encounter, and escape.  Occasionally C.H.A.D.D. included some details that Kyle hadn’t been paying attention to, particularly in terms of troop movement and other fungal structures that DeRosa had created.

When they finished, Angela gave them an approving look.  “You did well.  Certainly a performance worthy of your pay.  I’ll see to it that those details are finalized shortly.  I do have one other question for you, Kyle.  What will you do, now that this is all over?”

“Why do you ask?”  Kyle countered, looking first to Angela’s projection, then to Chester.

“As you know, Corthian Mining is dissolved.  That doesn’t mean that I won’t be working and trying to rebuild something better.  When you left Earth, I was clear that I wanted us to have as positive a relationship as possible moving forward.  Seeing how you’ve grown, I still want that.  Would you consider working for me?  Joining with us as we look to rebuild?  I can promise the pay will be good, the opportunities to grow will be plentiful, and the benefits will be unquestionable.  You’d be a tremendous asset.”

Kyle smiled, and shook his head.  “I appreciate the offer, Angela.  But I’m very happy with the Courier’s Guild, and the work that I’m doing.  But if you ever need a contractor, I’m sure we can work out a reasonable rate.”
