Mayle abruptly laughed, almost squeaking. “You? Owe Hayden a favor?”

Ritrix frowned. “Will you set something up or not?”

“Of course I will. Can I ask what the meeting’s about?”

“I’d tell you, but… I don’t know.”

Is he just playing everyone? Mayle wondered, seeing Hayden’s cold eyes in her mind. “Sure. I’ll set up a meeting. Encrypted link or in person?”

“In person, tomorrow afternoon.”

“Okay,” she said. “Name the time, and I’ll figure out the details.” Suddenly, she got a call from Jeela Rian, the owner of the Nebula Auction House. “Is there anything else?” she asked. “I have an equally critical call coming in.”

“No, that’s it. Goodbye.”

“Bye.” Mayle hung up and answered Jeela’s call. “Hello… Ms. Rian. I wasn’t expecting a call.”

“Neither was I,” Jeela said with a slight smile. “I was just wondering if you would… sate a curiosity of mine.”

Mayle swallowed. “You’re free to ask.”

“Then I won’t be humble.” Jeela leaned forward in augmented reality mode. “Would you happen to know why the Sickles are buying thousands of Clear Line spirits?”

Mayle’s heart thrummed like a plucked cord. She cleared her throat. “That’s rather sensitive information. But I’m willing to part with it… if you were to aid me with my current situation.”

Jeela’s thin smile didn’t falter. “I don’t see why not. Assuming, of course, you can help me… talk to my mutual acquaintance. From what I’ve heard, he’s quite famous for turning down opportunities.”

Mayle blinked a few times. “Talk to him, as in…”

“A meeting. In person. I’ll travel to Junolyx.”

Mayle was floored.

“Interesting…” Jeela looked down and tucked a lock of golden hair behind her ear. “Here I was thinking you were his handler…”

Mayle’s bones ached. Hayden was acting behind her back—and he had the power to influence universal leaders. She was clearly grateful—but the thought that Hayden could sink Junolyx at any second was chilling.

It became clear, at that moment, that Hayden was her patron—and after remembering his eyes when he said he’d kill Keaden, her body seized up. Keaden, Carian, Vestra—Hayden was ruthless—and he now had the power to exact his will on his enemies without constraint. That revelation chilled her to the bone.

Two days later, Hayden found himself sitting in an elite conference room, deeply satisfied that he finished his efforts before Keaden’s betrayal. His moves were far from ideal—they would complicate his life—but he and Mayle would survive the onslaught. That’s what he was thinking when Ritrix entered his conference room, slowly sitting before him like an interrogator. Ritrix then took off his glasses, exposing the sky-blue eyes Kayline inherited.

“As a father, I’m both grateful that you helped my daughters, and aggrieved that you used them to get my attention.”

“There were many ways I could have obtained your attention, Mr. Iska,” Hayden replied. “I chose this solution because it was agreeable.”

“Agreeable?”

“I see potential in them.”

“Oh, you see potential in them. I can’t tell if you’re saying that as a teacher, or someone who seeks to use them.”

“I aim to do both. Should you allow me.”

“Should I allow you…” Ritrix’s eyes drifted. “You don’t take me for someone who asks for permission.”

“Not in most things, no. But in my eyes, your daughters are children. I feel honor-bound to consult their parents, should they be responsible adults.”

Ritrix examined Hayden closely. “How old are you?”

“Eighteen. I’d be a fool to say anything else.”

Ritrix smiled faintly, but it quickly faded. “What do you want?”

“I would like to make use of Lina’s core. In exchange, I will teach her how to use it. I assure you, Mr. Iska—this is the greatest opportunity she will ever get.”

Ritrix stared him in the eye. “Will they be joining you in the rift if I say yes?”

“Yes.”

“Then no. You have the Crimson Sickles gunning for you—and I refuse to let my daughters get between you.”

“Mr. Iska, do you take me for a fool?”

“I do.”

Hayden stared at him for about twenty seconds before saying, “Ask your daughters what they want to do. Do that, and I’ll solve your transportation strikes tomorrow. If you don’t, well, I’ll view you as a fool as well.”

“That’s absurd.”

“Then there’s no harm in letting me prove it. Ask your daughters.”

Ritrix drummed his fingers on the table in thought. “When does the rift open?”

The rift would open in eleven days, but Hayden wouldn’t disclose that to anyone for free—even his handlers. That said, the Iska sisters would be an invaluable resource. So he considered the risk.

“Are you sure you want that information?” Hayden asked. “No one else has it—even my handlers. That means if anyone finds out—you’re to blame. And if you’re to blame, I’ll have you ousted from your job in two weeks.”

Ritrix picked up his glasses and put them on. “I don’t take kindly to being threatened.”

“Then you shouldn’t ask for protected information.” Hayden stood. “You also shouldn’t threaten or disregard people of unknown strength. Especially after they tell you they can end galactic transportation strikes tomorrow.” He walked past Ritrix and activated the sliding door.

“Where are you going?” Ritrix asked coldly.

“I’ve made my offers. If you agree to let them into the rift, summon me tonight and I will discuss my plans.”

Ritrix scoffed. “Tonight?”

“Time’s short. Consult your daughters. Do that, and your problems will disappear.”

Ritrix sat in the same spot for a few minutes after Hayden left, trying to process the fact that an apparent teenager just called him fifty thousand light-years to Heline—for a five-minute meeting. Not only that, he threatened Ritrix and demanded that he let his daughters be his meat shields. It was the most unbelievable situation he had ever been in.

That said, Hayden offered him something absurd for so little a price. It begged the question: could Hayden end an intergalactic transportation strike in a day?

There was nothing to lose, so he called his daughters in a group call. Kayline was with Lina, sitting at her bedside, so she answered for the both of them.

“How’d it go?” Kayline asked nervously.

Ritrix closed his eyes, thought through his words, and explained Hayden’s proposal.

Later that night, Hayden sat in the private room of a five-star restaurant, facing Jeela Rian of the Nebula Auction House.

“I’ve chosen to help you, on certain conditions,” she said.

“Which are?”

“First, I want you to tell me why the Crimson Sickles are hoarding Clear Line spirits. Your ability to sway markets makes you both an asset and a calamitous legal threat. Belia has already put me under investigation for market manipulation—and will continue to do so. So, I feel this is fair.”

If Hayden were a time traveler, everything he said would be an undisclosed material fact, which was to say that a business was buying, selling, and trading stocks using insider information—and that was highly illegal. That also applied to auctions, as Jeela could hoard spirits and catalysts before Hayden disclosed their value, allowing her to corner the market when the price skyrocketed. That was known as bid-rigging, and it was equally illegal. Since Hayden was obviously a time traveler, his enemies would exploit these laws to the point that even knowing him would be a legal calamity. Jeela knew that. That’s why she wanted him to disclose information on his actions in advance, so she could prepare. That said, it was an exercise in servility for the sake of nothing, so he wouldn’t entertain it.

“Our business arrangements will be conducted strategically and within legal bounds,” Hayden replied. “Beyond that, my actions are none of your concern.”

Jeela’s eyes narrowed. “Your actions affect me.”

“Yours affect me. So do Mayle’s and Belia’s—and your competitors. Such is the way of the world. I’m not your employee, servant, or tool. I’m an independent entity, and if you’re unwilling to treat me as such, I’ll choose another auction house.”

“Such mindsets poison relationships,” Jeela warned.

Hayden maintained silent eye contact until she looked away. That’s when he spoke. “What are your other conditions?”

“That you disclose information to me so I can prepare, but apparently that’s off the table.”

“As unreasonable demands should be.”

Jeela drank wine and brooded.

“Well?” Hayden asked. “Do you want to be my partner? Because I’m announcing my auction whether you choose to join me or not.”
