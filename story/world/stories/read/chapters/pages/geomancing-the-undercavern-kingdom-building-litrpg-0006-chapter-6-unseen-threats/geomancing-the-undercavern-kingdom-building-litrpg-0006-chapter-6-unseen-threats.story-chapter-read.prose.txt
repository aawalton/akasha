Holding tiny aether shards in their hands, the hunters briefly flicked their thumbs off and on of the crystals to send blinking messages via the light to one another. It was a method the Great Hero had taught them, apparently popular in his homeworld.

Jorgan had been doubtful initially, but after they had completed training and learned a few different commands, he realized just how useful it was.

Due to the worms' size, the hunters worked in groups of five, and the coordination required to pull off the hunts would have been impossible without the light technique.

The light flashed five times, and he knew that meant the others were in position. A moment later, it flashed twice more, indicating their prey was moving toward them.

The scuttling, moist, slithering sound that followed was still one of the most discomforting sounds he had heard, and sent shivers down his spine. He never would have even considered hunting giant worms if the meat had not been delicious and tender.

Moments later, the giant larvae-looking worm appeared, dimly lit by an aether crystal they had tactically placed on the ground. Being native to this land, the creatures were unbothered by the sight of the crystals and continued forward.

The moment it stepped over the crystal, Jorgan’s teammate pulled a rope, releasing large rocks they had placed in a net above.

The rain of rocks thudded hard against the worm, and it squealed in agony, but their attack had only just begun. Simultaneously, four men charged out, one from each corner of the beast. They wielded spears and, at once, stabbed into the creature.

The worm flailed against their attack, but it only caused the spears to penetrate deeper, and within seconds, its life was fading.

As the worm slumped to the ground, Jorgan wiped his brow and sighed. It was all in a day’s work.

Scenes like this had been playing across the entire tunnel network ever since Carmack had led them down here and advised them how to hunt the huge worms.

And it wasn’t just those with experience, either. Hunters and fishermen had taken to training anybody who needed work. Lydrus might have been a rocky, mountainous city that resided beside the water. And while the hike down and up the steep cliff faces to reach that water was treacherous, it was also prosperous, and a decent number of people made a living off the ocean.

There were differences in freshwater fishing from relatively shallow streams to the deep, choppy ocean water beside Lydrus. Still, they knew enough to compensate and fill the gaps. Baskets of fish and crustaceans were being hauled up to the settlement shortly after they arrived in the tunnels scouted by Carmack.

However, hunting was not as straightforward. The giant worms were difficult to kill with bows, and many hunters froze up, terrified when they saw them. Traps became the go-to. Nets and tripwires that triggered falling boulders could disable or kill the worms, and like Jorgan and his team, hunters could finish them off with spears at a distance.

It wasn’t just hunters and fishers, though. Gatherers collected mushrooms and mosses, and scouts explored the caves ahead. There were even a few guardsmen dotted around the caves, and a basecamp was built on the platform between the caves and the bridge connecting them to the spire.

However, even with all of this, it wasn’t enough to feed everyone, and the supplies that had been carried from Lydrus were used as supplements. Still, their food-collecting productivity had risen so much that their rations were expected to last for months. And if they could expand the operations, that would stretch them further, providing more than enough time for crops to grow.

“Alright, men, let’s get to chopping,” Jorgan said, drawing a cleaver from his belt.

The four men each picked a section of the giant worm and began to dissect it. They would each carry a chunk to the basecamp, where they would leave it for runners to take it the rest of the way to the spire.

“Great Hero!” One of the hunters called, and Jorgan turned in their direction.

“Oh, hi there. I left a few corpses in the adjoining chamber. Would you mind seeing that the porters from camp collect them?”

“Yes, of course, Great Hero!”

Jorgan nodded as he watched the man pass. There wasn’t a scratch on him, and yet he had traveled deeper into the tunnels alone and slain several of the beasts.

Does he even use traps?

Jorgan shook his head. That wasn’t important. No, the fact that their hero was down here hunting beasts was far more intriguing.

Why does he come down here to help us hunters?

A thought struck him. These beasts provided a good experience. He had been a hunter back in Lydrus, but had long stopped earning experience from deer and boars. However, in the last couple of days, he had gained another level thanks to these worms.

Could that be it? No, it can’t be. There’s no way the hero needs to kill beasts like these to level up. It must be something else.

The only other option was that, unlike the nobility he was used to, this hero, Carmack, saw himself as no higher than a commoner. He would spare his time to help them hunt and collect food for the settlement.

Jorgan’s thoughts swirled. He wasn’t sure what to make of such a humble man, but he knew that man had gained his respect. This was someone worth fighting for. Maybe even someone worth dying for.
