"Arrrghhh," Levi screamed.

The rogue bolted upright and pressed a hand against his forehead in anguish. Beads of sweat rolled down his forehead, and the bed he'd lain on was also soaked. He struggled to regulate his breathing, his eyes darted around using the soft glow of the two yellow glow stones in the room to search for who or what had hit him on the head, but there was nothing else... There was no one else in his room. And it took a Levi seeing a system notification for him to realize what had happened.

[ Branding for day two completed.

One more branding session remaining.]

Levi groaned as he threw himself back on the bed. The stupid brand on his forehead had updated while he'd been sleeping. The pain from the branding had been enough to wake the rogue; it'd also almost caused him to have a heart attack, as he'd somehow believed that Ghost had found him. A sigh of relief exited his lips at being proven wrong. Ghost hadn't found him; it'd just been the artifact branding him for the second time.

Out of the corner of his eyes, he looked at the small window that was in the room he'd been given. Although the window had been locked for his own safety, he could still see soft morning light streaming in through the cracks of the uneven wooden windows. From what he could see, it was still early in the morning. A lot earlier than he'd have loved to be roused from his sleep.

"Graking brand," Levi cursed.

He stopped looking at the window and instead looked at the ceiling, the plain surface allowed him to drift into the recesses of his mind. Analyzing what he'd done and what he hadn't. It allowed him to find improvements in his actions. And while he so desperately tried to focus on his fighting ability and what he'd seen Ghost do, his mind kept drifting to the mage he'd robbed.

A smile appeared on his face at the thought of her. He still remembered what her perfume smelled like—jasmine mixed with honey. The look of confusion on her face brought a soft laugh to his lips as well. Her attitude and her fireball to his face had left an imprint in his mind, as well as on his eyebrows, baby moustache, and front hair. He couldn't lay a finger on what exactly had him thinking about the girl. Sure, she was pretty, but there'd been a few pretty girls in Gantus; of course, none were on her level.

Or is my mind trying to distract me from the messenger of death on my heels? Levi shook his head at the thought.

It was plausible, but he wasn't one to get distracted by a pretty face; never had he thought twice about any girl he'd seen. Never before had he noted the scent of someone who wasn't a mark. Well, he could say she was a mark, especially since he'd robbed her, but at the same time, he hadn't wanted to, which made the matter all the more confusing.

Exhaling in exasperation, he pushed those thoughts to the side. Thinking about the girl wouldn't save him. Thinking of himself should.

So, Levi spent the next few hours of early daylight analyzing what he'd done. The Inn, the Dungeon, and the way, all these were places where significant things had happened, and among the three, none was more important than the Inn. It was the place where his entire life had been turned upside down. The place that he'd been brought to die, he'd been carried along like livestock. Levi bristled at the thought but ultimately discarded the emotion. It wouldn't help him now; only logical thinking would, and so the rogue focused on the events that had transpired in the Inn.

The entire night had gone to plan right until he'd gone down the stairs and had seen the dead bodies as well as a corpse... A corpse. Levi's eyes lit up at the thought of the corpse in the glass casket. The woman in it had also been a rogue. Just like him, she had half a dagger brand on her forehead. Her brand had been cut in half because of the claw marks that had torn through her face, tearing away strips of flesh, conveniently tearing away the bottom half of the brand, leaving room for the possibility that she had a different tattoo. Maybe the other half would complete a sickle?

It seemed like a reasonable assumption to Levi, but everything in his gut told him that the dead rogue and he, shared the same brand, or at least they would — if he survived the next day. Plus, he had to admit that the pieces of his jagged puzzle had begun to come together. The empty pedestal in the casket had been meant to hold the stone; the corpse in the casket had the same brand that the stone intended to give him. It wasn't much to go on, but it made him feel sensible; it made him feel as though he could understand some part of what exactly was going on.

"Graking hell, this shite is driving me nuts," Levi cursed again. His eyes fixed on the ceiling as his brain worked things out.

The mage had died, forcing Levi to absorb the stone, which meant it was something worth dying for. By his guesstimate, the stone was priceless—an unhelpful guess, but one that did put things into perspective for him. An artifact like that, unabsorbed and in his palm, would've changed he, Brees, and Silas's lives. They'd have gone from living in squalor to living like kings and queens. Unfortunately for him, he couldn't 'unabsorb' the stone, not without losing his life at least. The irony wasn't lost on the rogue; he finally had a way to make him and his family rich, but he'd have to kill himself to do it.

I wonder where Silas and Bree are? I hope they managed to get out in time.

He'd seen his siblings last night, and yet it felt like years had passed since he'd last laid eyes on them. He'd given them his stash to be able to get out of town, a situation they wouldn't have had to be in if they weren't affiliated with him. But they were, and now Levi was stuck, wondering if he'd given them enough coin to survive, stuck wondering if he'd be able to find them someday, stuck wondering if... if they were still alive. These were the ripple effects of his choice to take that job. These were the consequences of his line of work, but that line of work was the only way he knew how to keep food on the table and roofs over their heads, and that wouldn't matter now.

A saying came to mind, one that was synced targeted. One that he'd paid no heed to at the time he'd heard it, but was now relevant to the situation. The saying went: Let the synced in their hunger for power go into the dungeons and hunt monsters. If they succeed in clearing the dungeon, their hunger ends. If they fail and are killed by the monsters, their hunger still ends.

He'd laughed at the old, tired-looking sync that had said that, but he surely wasn't laughing now. His quest for money and power had led him here, right to this crossroads. Would he survive his hunger for power? Or will it be the death of him? With Ghost hot on his heels, Levi needed to find a way to get stronger, fast. It wasn't as if he had any S-ranked family member or friend who could protect him from Ghost.

He was on his own, a lone sync left to face the problems of Rinesta with no one else to call on. It was harsh, but harsh was the only thing he'd ever known, and he'd survived countless times. This might be on a scale higher than the rest, but it was still a hurdle he could overcome if he were clever enough. As for Bree and Silas's, he'd see them again if Cerpital wished it. If not, they'd have to find a way to survive. The world was out to keep those from the streets down, no matter how pure their hearts were.

"Done with all these sad thoughts," Levi said with a weak grin. The rogue finally sat up in the bed and grabbed the mage's pouch from underneath his pillow. Her perfume wafted up from the pouch, but he didn't think too much about it. He had work to do.

"Alright, time to count these coins," Levi said, his grin growing stronger as he undid the strings of the pouch and tossed its contents on the bed.

Jackpot!

Gold, silver, and copper exited the pouch, the coins doing a little bounce on the bed before resting on his bed. The yellow glow of the glow stones in his room reflected on the metals and onto Levi's face, causing the rogue's grin to widen even more. His eyes drifted between coins as he began to count his, heart jumping with joy at the sight of so much wealth.

" ... Six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve. Twelve gold," Levi said in amazement as he counted the last of the coins. The total sum of all coins, including copper, silver, and gold, amounted to roughly twelve gold coins. Four times what he and Killian had agreed he'd get for stealing the stone. The coins were divided into two hundred coppers, eighty silver, and two gold.

That amount of coin lay right before him, all his for the taking, and all he'd had to do was take a fireball to the face—a much better deal than being killed for three gold coins. The rogue couldn't believe his luck; he'd gone on the run without a single coin on his person and had somehow ended up with more coins than he'd made in his entire life. All from an honest mistake. With this amount of luck after so much misfortune, he had to suspect Cerpital for looking out for him.

The different thoughts and emotions came to a halt as he spied something else that had fallen onto the bed. Upon looking closer at the item, Levi had to assume that it had fallen out of the pouch as well. He picked up the item and brought it to eye level. The item that had caught his attention was a ring, made out of a silver-like metal, adorned with tiny orbs of rubies, and a singular big ruby sat at the top of the ring. It looked exquisite, royal, expensive... Feminine.

Is she ... Is she engaged?

He didn't know how he felt about that, and he couldn't let himself go down that rabbit hole, so he did the next best thing. He kept the ring to the side, yawned, and got off the bed, grabbing his cloak and putting it on. From the little light streaming through his windows, he could tell that day had broken properly. Time had flown by while he'd been thinking; his exhaustion from the day had vanished, courtesy of a hot bath and a partially good sleep.

He scooped the coins off the bed, dividing them into three parts and stuffing one into his right pocket, one into his left pocket, and the rest he put in the pouch. On a whim, he added the ring, then tied the string taut before hiding it within his cloak. Done with arranging his coins, he did some light stretching exercises, then exited the room, heading down to the counter. Hunger had replaced his exhaustion.

"Good morning, can I get breakfast here?" he asked as he reached the counter. The middle-aged barkeep was still there, cleaning yet another mug.

"We have some leftover stew and bread, it'll cost you two coppers," The barkeep said.

"Sure, I'll take it," Levi said.

The barkeep nodded, dropped the mug she had been cleaning, and went into a back room that he hadn't noticed the previous day. His exhaustion last night had made him somewhat lax. The barkeep returned later with his meal, and Levi dug into it savagely; he barely tasted the stew as he rushed to eat. He'd gone a quarter of the way through the food when someone barged through the doors of the broken torch.

"You!!" a voice thundered in his direction.
