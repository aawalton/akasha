While Ruben had broken a couple of pickaxes as if he didn't have the proper technique needed to utilize the weapon, the berserker was not the only one at fault for the heap of broken pickaxes that littered the entrance of the mine.

Both Ciara and the ranger, Tasha, had also largely contributed to the heap, as neither of them knew how to mine, but they had learned on the spot.

Their repeated attempts to learn were rewarded, as Ruben and the others now had an idea of how to use pickaxes.

Soon enough, he saw that their understanding of how to use the pickaxe increased the amount of Tora ore they mined in a day. And while the work itself was soul-crushing, monotonous, and highly boring, Ruben somehow managed to keep himself entertained by either humming or just chilling with the  Neran rabbit whenever it graced his presence—this was one of those times that the rabbit had seen fit to do so.

As soon as Nathan had left, Ciara divided the three of them into two groups and explained how things would be run in Nathan’s absence.

The schedule was simple; they alternated days.

Ruben would have to mine alone on his day on, while the two girls—Ciara and Tasha—mined together as it offered them more protection and freedom compared to when all three mined at the same time.

And on his off days, Ruben either slept through the day, tired from mining, or he took on one or two dungeons by himself.

So far, he’d managed to clear three F-ranked dungeons as well as an E-ranked dungeon by himself. Of course, the ranger and the mage had managed to do some good for themselves.

While it hadn't been that long since Nathan had left, Ruben couldn’t help but note that he’d barely run into any monster situation on the island itself.

It seemed either that the monsters on the island didn't find them worthy of their time or that they were afraid of them. Ruben actually wasn't sure which was true, but he chose to believe the latter.

"Guess the monsters won’t come out to play if Nathan isn’t here," Ruben muttered, his eyes locked on the bunny on the floor.

So far, it had almost killed him at one point, and that was when Ruben had realized how much of a threat the bunny was.

The berserker had a hunch that it had tried to do that because he had tried to name it Poncho.

Judging by the way it had slammed into him, paralyzing him with its slime and almost biting off one of his hands, Ruben had to assume that the bunny disagreed with the name. And if not for Ciara, who had stepped in to shoo away the bunny, Ruben was pretty sure he would have lost his right hand. The bunny obviously had no qualms about biting the hand that had fed it.

Ruben could vividly still remember how the bunny's mouth had widened unnaturally as it revealed rows of sharp canines before biting down on his right wrist, nearly severing it.

It had taken some time, but the pair had managed to reach a truce after the almost tragic event that had transpired between them.

Since then, both Ruben and the rest of the group had decided to hold back from naming the bunny. To be honest, a part of Ruben was a bit stunned that the rabbit could even understand what he’d been trying to do. Or maybe he had it all twisted, and the bunny just didn't like the word ‘poncho’. Either way, Ruben wasn't keen to try again.

The berserker picked up the pickaxe resting by his side. It had taken a while, but he’d eventually gotten used to the pressure that the Tora ore lining the walls of the mine emitted.

Or maybe it was just because he had gotten stronger that the pressure seemed to roll off his shoulders. Then again, he supposed both things could be true at the same time.

But pressure or no pressure, the constant swinging at the Tora ore embedded in the walls of the mine was killing his back. And it felt like he was going to get a hunchback from all the swinging that he had been doing.

Sure, he had improved his technique, but an amateur self-learning without any books, videos, or, well, anything in his case, was sure to still be doing it wrong even with his improved technique.

I wonder what type of day Nathan and the others are having.

Ruben swung his pickaxe towards a spot where tora ore was peeking out of the mine walls.

A heavy thud echoed in the mine as the lump of Tora ore dropped to the floor, but Ruben barely paid any attention to it as he swung again at the same spot, knowing from his repeated mining that another lump would be close by, probably just a little bit further behind the last one that had just dropped.

His aim was on point as his second strike dislodged another lump of tora ore. Ruben couldn’t help it as his mind drifted to his thoughts, even as his eyes tracked the lump of ore falling to the floor.

So far, they haven't heard from Nathan or anyone in a while. Besides, it wasn't like they actually had a way to contact the berserker and the others who had sailed on the ship.

Even worse was the fact that none of them who’d stayed back had a time estimate of when Nathan would return to the island, which meant they were on their own for the time being.

Ruben readied his pickaxe to swing again when an odd sound from outside the mine reached his ears. Are those voices?

“Well, I hope they brought some food because I’m hungry,” Ruben muttered as he moved towards the mine's exit.

As he neared the exit, Ruben realized the voices were masculine and alien-like, causing him to pause in his tracks and peek out from the corner of the mine's exit to the unwelcome sight of six strange-looking entities.

Ruben pulled back into the mine, his heart beating against his chest.

“What the hell are those?” The berserker asked himself as he dropped deeper into the mine, hurrying to trade the pickaxe in his hand for his sword that lay discarded on the floor.

With his sword in hand, he felt a little more confident about returning to the mouth of the mine, and that was what he did.

Leaning close to the walls at the mouth of the mine, he listened, managing to make out a few words.

“... spread out and find the native inhabitants on this island. Bring them to me here, dead or alive.
