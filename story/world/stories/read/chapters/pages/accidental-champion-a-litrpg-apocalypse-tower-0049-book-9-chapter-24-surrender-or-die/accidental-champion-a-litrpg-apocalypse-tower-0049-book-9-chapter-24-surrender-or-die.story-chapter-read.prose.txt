The A Grade captain barely put up a fight.

The queen and her people hadn’t been needed. The entire time, they’d watched as Xavier did his bloody work. Though they had killing intent in their eyes, and this was exactly what they wanted, when every Denizen on the first ship lay dead in that hall, small waves of fear radiated from them.

They looked at him differently.

The hunger Xavier experienced after killing those Denizens didn’t dissipate when he made it to the next ship, nor the next after that.

If anything, the hunger became stronger.

This would work.

This would be enough to push him to the next level of advancement.

And he would be able to do it. He would spend a long, long time inside his time dilation field, but he wasn’t afraid of that. And this wasn’t the first time he’d performed what amounted to executions against large numbers of Denizens.

He’d brought justice to the entire Ventorin sector in a similar way.

The difference here was, Xavier didn’t reap the souls of the dead. While he didn’t release his enemies from their contracts and try to see if they could be redeemed—such a thing would go against his needs—he wouldn’t damn them to a true death. Their spirits would live on, and if they were lucky, they would make their journey through the void and find shelter in the Otherworld.

If they were even luckier, they might one day find themselves reincarnated, having another chance at life.

It was the only mercy he allowed himself to grant them, and even that mercy felt unnecessary to the hungry part of him.

When he made it through the first thousand ships, he found he needed time to recover.

He was able to recover most of the Soul Energy required to break through the force fields between ships, but he’d been defeating the enemy crews with increasing speed. Eventually, his Soul Energy became a little too depleted for his liking and he forced himself to rest.

In the one thousandth ship, he sat cross-legged on the edge of the vast hall filled with corpses. Queen Aecia and those who’d accompanied her were still with him, diligently following him onto every ship. Their wariness of him had only grown with each ship crew he’d defeated. Yet through all that time they hadn’t said a word.

His eyes were closed, hands resting on his knees, when Queen Aecia cleared her throat. He popped one eye open and looked at her. “Is there something you want to say?”

The queen glanced at Brarri. The mage inclined his head. Though they’d been silent with him, it was clear enough that other conversations had been going on through their own Communication Stone channel.

“Is it perhaps time to offer the enemy a chance to surrender? To turn back?” Queen Aecia glanced at the hundred-thousand-and-one corpses. “You’ve proven their crusade cannot work. Hiding, and our shielding, has been enough to protect us in the past.”

Xavier opened his other eye. “You really aren’t like most beings under the System, are you?”

Queen Aecia gave a weak smile. “I will take that as a compliment.”

“Tell me, how did you manage to become A Grade? And how are so many of your people B Grade? Every inch of your planet has been, for lack of a better word, civilised. I didn’t see a single wild beast.”

“Beasts were banished from Galadsea many years ago,” Brarri said. “We wish for our people to live in peace.”

“An admirable goal, and the reason I chose to fight for you in the first place. I want the same for my own people.”

“Your own people?” Queen Aecia asked. “You are a ruler?”

“I have two sectors under my control. I intend for that number to grow considerably,” Xavier replied.

“You are a conqueror.”

“I am a uniter…” Xavier sighed. “But yes, I am also a conqueror.” He peered at her. “You must have done similar, to unite Galadsea.”

“I was not the one to unite Galadsea.” She raised her chin. “I am not her first queen. That was my mother. She was a… very determined woman. We did not always agree, but I respect what she did for our people. Bringing them together was the right thing to do, even if the ways she did it were… not.”

“Every ruler has blood on their hands, whether they put it there themselves or inherit it.”

“Indeed. It is a burden I must carry.”

“You failed to answer my question, Queen Aecia. How is it you reached A Grade with such an isolationist, pacifistic ideology?”

“We are not pacifists, Xavier Collins,” Queen Aecia replied. “We may go against the will of the System, but we are not fools. We know that in order to live in peace we must be able to back that peace up with strength. I, and the others who fight for Galadsea’s freedom, found that strength the same place you find it now. In the Tower of Champions.” She cocked her head. “Our version of the tower.”

“Being a Champion of the tower, you understand my task it not as simple as giving the enemy a chance to surrender.”

“You say that without having tried,” Brarri remarked. “You are a powerful A Grade. You cannot gain much from the deaths of so many B Grades. What if you only dealt with their captains?”

Xavier gave the man a thin smile.

Honestly, he couldn’t fault them for talking to him this way and floating these ideas. If he were in their shoes, he would be thinking the exact same thing. Xavier had a remarkable gift for grand-scale violence, but that didn’t mean he always wanted to use it.

The hunger inside him was another matter.

Putting that aside, if he could advance to B Grade another way he would.

“Their surrender isn’t an option,” Xavier said. “If you do not wish to witness their deaths, then you can return to your planet’s surface. I will continue on without you.”

Queen Aecia glanced at Brarri and the others, then sighed.

“I can’t say I expected you to respond differently. People like you, who have embraced the System, violence is a natural response.”

Xavier stiffened. Embraced the System. If only she knew.

He could tell her—everything except his plans to overthrow the System. But what would be the point? He had nothing to prove to this woman.

Xavier closed his eyes and continued to meditate, allowing more Soul Energy to flow into his body. Knowing he would be here a long while he decided to contract his time dilation field, leaving the others outside it, so they wouldn’t have to experience the months pass.

When his soul reserve was roughly halfway back to being full, he heard one of the others shift their weight.

What…

His eyes snapped open. The others were no longer trapped outside his time dilation field.

No…

It wasn’t his time dilation field. He hadn’t moved it, nor had he made any of the time dilation fields his drones kept active move.

Something else had happened.

His Immortal Soul lens zeroed in on the foreign source of time distortion. It had washed over him too. Somehow he hadn’t noticed. The effect had been incredibly subtle. The field was different to his own, but it was clearly a Time Alteration spell.

Alarms blared across the ship. Crystals embedded in the ceiling flashed red then white then red again.

A message sounded in his mind.

INTRUDER ALERT. INTRUDER ALERT.

TEMPORAL THREAT HAS COMPROMISED FLEET.

TIME DILATION FIELD INITIATED IN RESPONSE.

THREAT DETECTED ON AURARA B182.

FLEET ORDER: ELIMANTE THREAT.

Xavier rose to his feet. He tried to teleport, but that was blocked. Time Alteration wasn’t the only spell that had been cast on the area. Notifications appeared in his vision.

A Portal Lock has been established over a light year’s radius.

A Teleport Lock has been established over a light year’s radius.

Xavier’s spiritual sense flung outward, different parts of his mind assessing the situation. The first thing he was glad to find was that the time dilation field, while it covered the entire fleet, hadn’t reached the barren moon.

The other Champions would remain locked out of this fight.

But now the rest of the fleet—and though he’d dealt with the crew of a thousand of their ships, there were still almost three million of them—had become an active threat.

“We have to mo—”

The ship rocked. An energy beam burned through the walls, as thick as a house, obliterating everything in its path. When the beam disappeared, it left a massive hole in the ship. Bodies that had been half-in and half-out of the beam’s radius had the affected area completely vaporised.

Though Xavier had only made a small gap to get through the ship’s shielding, when he’d killed the crew of the ship it had killed the power source for the shields—the second that time dilation field had spread over the ship, the shielding had failed.

The others were already fleeing. Xavier’s heart pounded as panic flooded his entire body.

I was right… this floor was too easy. Too good to be true!

As he flew through the ship, he manifested spells, and cast Untethered on each of the others, anchoring them to him. He plummeted through the hole where the laser beam had just cut through the ship, the others trailing behind.

All the while he tried to pinpoint the location of the enemy time mage. He needed to take out whoever had cast that damned spell. He had no idea how the ships’ beam weapons worked, but even if each ship could only fire a beam weapon once inside the time dilation field, unable to cooldown, there were millions of ships.

Xavier realised this eventuality was the absolute last thing he’d been prepared for.

What the hell am I going to do?

Another beam slammed into the ship, a few feet away from where they’d been a split second ago. It took out a least ten of his drones. That had been on purpose—he’d had several of his drones drop their veils so the enemy could target them.

The loud, telepathic voice that had announced the alarm before sounded once more in his brain. He wasn’t able to block it.

SURRENDER OR DIE.

SURRENDER AND YOU WILL BE ABLE TO SERVE THE GREATEST EMPIRE IN THE UNIVERSE.

DIE AND YOU WILL SERVE NOTHING.

CHOOSE BEFORE YOUR CHOICE IS MADE FOR YOU.

Xavier dragged the others out of the spaceship’s hole—not that being out in the open was any better. The others were screaming at him through the Communication Stone link, but he was ignoring them, manifesting more spells inside himself and his drones, trying to distract the beams.

He cast a series of spells that allowed him to become invisible and should make him undetectable. He extended that same ability to the others, flying them farther and farther away from where he’d left his drones.

His drones didn’t last long. After only two seconds had passed since the foreign time dilation field had crashed over the entire fleet, all his drones had been hit by the ships’ beam weapons.

He noted that every single beam that had fired had come from a different ship. It didn’t confirm that they only had one shot in them while inside the field, but it certainly made that more likely.

Xavier wanted nothing more than to put a force field around himself, but he didn’t have a force field spell strong enough to stop one of those beams. He could feel the energy in them. It was immensely powerful—more powerful than the A Grades in the ships he’d killed should be able to possess.

When he made it to the edge of where the time dilation field stretched to, the others still dragged along by Untethered spells, he stopped. Queen Aecia and her people must have noticed the fact that he’d hidden them, as they’d stopped screaming at him once he’d done that.

Only he was able to see the others, as he’d been the one to cast the spell that hid them in the first place, and so they were all invisible to each other.

The beams stopped firing. With any luck, the enemy would assume they’d been dealt with. The kill notifications they’d receive from taking out his drones would be genuine, not mentioning anything about them being drones at all.

Xavier: [All right. Everyone remain calm. I’ve hidden you from the enemy forces.]

Queen Aecia: [You’ve hidden us from each other!]

Xavier: [I had no other option.] He paused, thought of asking if she thought the Empire of Servitude would consider surrendering and turning back around now, then thought better of it. [I need to pinpoint the enemy time dilation field.]

Queen Aecia: [You need to take us back down to the planet! It would be better to fight the enemy in the waves the System has allowed, rather than out here.]

Xavier bit his lip. The woman had a point, but if he allowed time to pass normally here, those ninety-nine parties of other Champions would enter the mix. They were wildcards he didn’t want to deal with.

Xavier: [I’m not sure that’s the best plan.]

Brarri: [This plan has failed. Your time dilation field has been countered, as I thought it would be—]

The beams started firing again.

This time, they slammed into the other ships he’d already been on, destroying every inch of them. Not one-by-one, either—thousands of ships fired their beams, at least a hundred beams each slamming into the empty ships, destroying them in an instant and not leaving any margin for error.

THE SYSTEM TELLS US YOU STILL LIVE.

SURRENDER NOW.

YOU CANNOT SURVIVE OUR WRATH.

SURRENDER NOW.

The message, surrender now, repeated over and over in his mind until he finally figured out a way to counter the noise. While the block didn’t mute it completely, it basically allowed him to alter the volume so it became no more than a background whisper.

Queen Aecia: [You’ve gotten us this far. What’s your plan then, Xavier Collins? How are you going to get us out of this?]
