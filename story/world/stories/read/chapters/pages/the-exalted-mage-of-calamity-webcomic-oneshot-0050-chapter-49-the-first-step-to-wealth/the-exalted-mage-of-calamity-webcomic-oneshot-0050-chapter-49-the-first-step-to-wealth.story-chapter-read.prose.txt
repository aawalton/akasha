After a night’s sleep, Veronica groaned as she awoke, refreshed, stretching her body inside the covers. She threw the comforter off her body; her legs slid until they swung off the bed, and she sat up. Slowly, she rose to her feet and began her morning stretches, working the stiffness from her limbs.

[Good morning, Veronica.]

“Morning,” she replied lazily, bending forward to touch her toes.

Her body felt far better after proper rest. Traveling for weeks and sleeping in a carriage had not been kind to her muscles. Last night, she had melted completely into the bed the moment she lay down.

[This is a reminder to complete your morning jog.]

Veronica closed her eyes briefly as she stood straight. “Ah. Right. I promised to do those from now on.”

If she wanted to continue improving her body—and Sage’s rewards—she’d need to be diligent. The “pity” reward she had received still left a faint bitterness in her thoughts.

That said, the ascended body spell she’d been granted was undeniably valuable. It enhanced her durability and physical capabilities overall. Even as a mage, mobility in combat mattered—especially while she lacked access to her most destructive spells… or the ability to fly.

Veronica crossed the room to the washbasin. Warm water flowed from the pipe when she turned the knob. She splashed her face several times, then gripped the sides of the basin and looked up into the mirror.

There was a world of difference between now and her last inn in Greystone. Back then, she’d been running tirelessly through the night. Covered in dirt and rags, she looked tired. Almost like her future self. Now?

She looked… real. Human. Healthy. Alive.

“Sage. You said Galland should be working right now at the High Crates merchant house?”

[His records show that he had been working as a lower-tier merchant at one of the larger merchant houses in Ronswick. Based on other data supplied to me, I believe he is most likely working at High Crates.]

“I see,” Veronica said quietly.

Galland… no one recognized your potential. Let’s see if I can change that.

Veronica washed her hair and dried it off with wind magic.

Her clothes rested atop the bedside stand, where she had left them the night before.

After returning from City Hall, she visited the inn’s bathing chambers before turning in for the night. She rinsed her clothes there while she washed herself, wringing them out as thoroughly as she could before dressing again to walk back to her room.

Back inside, she stripped and dried them properly with wind magic—the bath chamber had been too small to risk it. Some of the thicker layers, however, remained faintly damp, so she set them aside to finish drying as she circulated the current in the room.

She meant only to lie down for a moment.

Inevitably, she fell asleep naked.

Sleeping without clothes on… wasn’t as uncomfortable as she’d expected.

She could understand the people who did it now.

After spending the few short minutes to wake up, she dressed quickly, pulling on her tunic, tights, and skirt before fastening the straps at her waist.

If she intended to recruit Galland, she needed to arrive prepared. Fortunately, she now had far more resources at her disposal.

“Alright,” she murmured. “Jog first. Bath. Breakfast. Then the mage tower.”

It was still morning, and the streets of Ronswick were just beginning to wake up.

Jogging along the sidewalks in the city felt odd, like she was a soldier training. After a couple of minutes and a few stares, she left the city to go along the trail. She could always get back in quickly with the viscount’s seal.

After returning for a quick bath, she set out again.

This time, toward the magic tower.

The five-story spiral rose above the surrounding buildings, its pale stone catching the morning light. The entrance stood open, and inside she could already see alchemists and studious mages moving about their routines.

Naturally, the two golems remained behind the counter.

She approached.

“Krrrk. Welcome to the Ronswick Branch Magic Tower,” one creaked.

“What tea would you like served today? We have black, green, earl grey, dirt, seventy-four—” the second golem’s voice ground to a halt, then resumed in an oddly hostile tone. “—your application has been denied. Please leave and never return, you heretic—”

Veronica raised a brow.

“Ugh… come on! You’re still not fixed?” came an exasperated voice from across the lobby.

A girl in her late teens hurried over and slipped behind the counter. Her expression was tight with irritation as she turned toward Veronica.

“Sorry about that,” she said. “This one’s been malfunctioning lately. It used to work at a church. Please ignore anything it says.”

Veronica chuckled softly. “It’s fine.”

The girl gave a strained smile. “Um… is there something you needed? I’m only here as an engineer, but I can probably point you in the right direction.”

“Do you know if Mr. Hugh is available?”

“The head of study?” She blinked. “Uh, yes. I think I saw him arrive earlier. He should be in his office. Do you have an appointment?”

Veronica shook her head. “No. But I do have some business with him, so I was hoping he might be available.”

The girl’s expression turned conflicted. “Well… I know where his office is and could show you, but I don’t really have the authority to let you go up. If you know him, though…”

“It’s alright,” Veronica said. “I know the way. I’ll head up myself. Thank you.”

The girl nodded. “Alright. But if you get in trouble—I never told you to go up,” she said.

Veronica gave her a nod. “Never even saw you.”

“Name’s Chelsea, by the way,” she added, extending her hand.

Veronica shook it. “Veronica.”

Chelsea gave a small nod, then turned back to the faulty golem, lifting it slightly and shuffling it toward another room.

Veronica climbed the stairs, heading straight for Hugh’s office. A few mages moved through the corridors, but none paid her any particular attention.

She reached his door and knocked. “Hey, Mr. Hugh. It’s me—Veronica. Are you available to—”

The door clicked open before she could finish.

Hugh stood there, beaming.

“Veronica! What a pleasant surprise! Please, come in, come in!” he said.

“Ah… okay,” she replied, slightly taken aback.

She stepped inside and moved toward one of the chairs. Hugh shut the door behind her and asked, “I wasn’t expecting you back so soon. Would you like some tea?”

“I’m good,” Veronica said. “I’d prefer not to be called a heretic.”

Hugh froze and turned toward her, confused. “What?”

Veronica shook her head. “Just a joke. It’s nothing.” She settled into the chair. “Did something good happen? You seem more energetic than yesterday.”

He took his seat behind his desk. “Yes, actually. And it relates to you.”

“Me?”

He nodded.

“Your notes.” He opened a drawer and withdrew the documents she had written for him. “As I mentioned last time, the tower head was bound to notice me withdrawing funds without authorization. He confronted me last night and demanded an explanation. That was when I showed him your work.” He glanced up. “I hope that was acceptable.”

Veronica inclined her head. “That’s fine. You plan to submit it to the Mage Association eventually anyway.”

He nodded quickly. “Good. Good.” He tapped the pages against the desk to straighten them before laying them flat. “When I showed Mr. Rarran—the tower head, that is—his jaw nearly hit the ground. He said he had never seen a formula structured like this. The theoretical framework alone left him stunned.”

Veronica remained quiet, letting him continue.

“He instructed me that if you returned, you were to be treated with the utmost respect. Of course, I intended that already.” Hugh’s face brightened. “But the reason I’m so happy—he gave me a raise!”

Veronica chuckled softly. “Congratulations, Mr. Hugh.”

It struck her, faintly, how most people worked for a steady wage. In the past, she’d just been given money. She had no shortage of vix or mana stones. Even now, guild commissions were her way of earning money. They required effort, but it wasn’t really a job. She simply chose the more dangerous requests when she needed more coin. No employer stood over her shoulder.

“It’s all thanks to you, Miss Veronica,” Hugh continued. “Mr. Rarran believes that once we finalize these papers and submit them, the Ronswick branch will receive significantly greater support. I’m speaking of full renovations, access to higher-tier alchemical ingredients, expanded funding—”

“Oh?” she said.

That was more than she had anticipated.

And, perhaps, exactly what she needed.

“Actually,” she said, straightening slightly, “I came today with a business proposition.”

The atmosphere in the room shifted. Hugh’s smile faded into attentive seriousness, his eyes steady on her.

“Is it another formula? We would gladly accept anything further you wish to share. Mr. Rarran has already authorized me to continue supporting you—financially as well,” he added. “He even scolded me again for offering you only a thousand vix.”

“No, it isn’t additional spell notes,” she said. “Though what you’ve told me is quite convenient.”

Now Hugh sat in silence, listening.

Veronica switched to her formal way of speaking. Her meeting with Hugh today was just that important.

“I would like to establish a business arrangement with this mage tower.”

“A business arrangement?” he repeated.

“Yes. There is a person I wish to sponsor. A merchant. I believe he has the potential to become the leader of a new, greater merchant house. One to rival even the Gleaming Gems group.”

Hugh’s eyes widened. “Larger than the Gleaming Gems? They are the largest merchant house in all of Vitian,” he said. “You’re saying that this individual person—not even an established group—can become that large?”

Veronica nodded. “He is unknown. At present, he is only a lower-tier merchant employed by one of the houses here in Ronswick. But I have seen his potential. I intend to recruit him and invest in his future. And to do that, I must first secure his resources.”

Hugh steepled his fingers, sitting straighter. “You wish to align our mage tower with this man?”

“Yes,” Veronica said plainly. “To be blunt, I will need leverage to persuade him. A… bribe, essentially. You mentioned the tower head is willing to cooperate and support me.”

Hugh nodded. “Yes. Within reason.”

She smiled. “Then I wish to offer you a deal.”

He stared at her, waiting for her to continue.

Sage. You’re up.

[Repeat after me.]

“I wish for your tower to dedicate the resources it produces straight to me and the person I am sponsoring.”

“A supply arrangement?” he asked.

“No,” Veronica said, shaking her head. “I wish for a business deal.”

She leaned forward slightly, gesturing deliberately with her hands.

“Magic towers profit by the selling of tools, artifacts, potions, and even combat implements, among other things. I am proposing that I—he—act as this branch’s first-party distributor.”

Hugh’s expression turned complex. “Distributor…”

“I am requesting first claim on the initial batches produced here at discounted rates,” she continued. “Health potions, convenience enchantments, minor artifacts, and any weaponry shaped through your imbuement halls. I would also request first access to newly developed works, with the right to carry them to market before they are offered more broadly.”

“New products?” he asked.

“Those notes I gave you,” she said, gesturing toward the papers on the desk.

“I’ll provide your tower with more notes—perhaps once or twice each month. I have many more theorems on spell-craft. Specifically on efficiency. Once your tower has studied and applied them, you’ll have exclusive rights to release them under your own authority.”

She waited a few moments before continuing.

“With them, your mages will have stronger foundations. They will improve drastically in their craft. Your tower will greatly outpace neighboring towers in both quality and yield. Production will increase, providing more profit to you, combined with the additional support you expect from the Mage Association. This branch may rise from a provincial outpost in Ronswick to a large magic center that could rival Annesheim’s.”

Hugh sat very still, focused entirely on her words.

“In return,” Veronica continued, calm and composed, “my sponsored merchant will ensure these goods travel swiftly into the markets of the nearby region and expand their reach. Instead of selling just to townsfolk or small businesses here, you will have the potential to have clients from all over Vitian.”

Veronica folded her hands neatly on her lap.

“For this privelege, Ronswick will retain full authorship of all works, but our branding will come attached. In exchange for these early rights, we will return a percent of all profits earned from these exact goods back to this tower.”

Hugh blinked. “A return?”

“I will not set the exact figures,” Veronica replied. “That will be the task of the man I intend to sponsor. But I assure you, the share will be both fair and generous.”

She paused before continuing.

“And one last thing. I would ask that he be permitted some voice in research and development. I believe he has a keen instinct for what people truly need—not merely what they admire. If he is given the opportunity to offer ideas and see them realized, this tower may produce works that serve both the common folk and the great houses alike.”

Hugh remained silent for several moments.

Finally, he spoke. “All of this… are you truly that confident in this man? To stake the value of your notes? Notes that could alter the very foundations of magic as we know it?”

Veronica sat a little straighter.

She thought back to The Fall.

When the demons descended, no kingdom was spared. Cities burned one by one. Trade routes collapsed all over Vitian. Refugees fled in endless lines, carrying what little remained of their lives.

But amid that ruin, one merchant house did not close its gates.

Galland had instead opened his vaults.

Where roads were destroyed, he funded caravans to carve new routes. Where cities fell, he paid to transport survivors to distant strongholds. Where famine threatened, he released grain reserves meant to last generations.

He supplied tools, blankets, medicine, weapons, and shelter—not as loans, not as investments, but as life-saving aid.

Like the elves who took care of Yggdrasil, coin flowed from his coffers like water poured into a dying field. While others turned inward, focused on their own survival, Galland did not. He did not hoard. He did not bargain. He did not demand repayment or favors. He gave—until there was nothing left to give.

Because of him, people endured as long as they did. Without his efforts, humanity might have survived only two years… not three.

Veronica’s gaze steadied on Hugh.

“I believe he is worth the effort,” she said quietly. “I do not seek to sponsor him just for his skills at making profit. That is a large portion of it, yes. But that’s not all. I also am investing in his integrity.”
