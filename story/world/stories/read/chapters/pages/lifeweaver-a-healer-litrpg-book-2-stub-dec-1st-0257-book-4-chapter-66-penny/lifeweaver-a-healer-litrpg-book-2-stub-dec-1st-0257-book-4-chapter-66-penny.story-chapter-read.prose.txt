Being one with the sky and the weather, flying through the air, wasn’t easy when you had an arrow stuck in you. Even less so when that arrow infected you in a way that shouldn’t be possible. The void wasn’t an infection. Even so, the town below looked like something out of a zombie thriller, a bad one, and the tendrils clawing at her from inside were real, at least in the sense that they affected her in the dungeon. Something was very wrong with the hole they had thrown themselves into this time. Luke had told them it would be more difficult because of the large amount of added Source, but that wasn’t it. It was more like the dungeon was alive, or that something inside it was. And that wasn’t all. She kept feeling like she was being watched. Creeeeepy.

Seeing Kenny dissolve into a puddle of nothing had been enough to turn her stomach, and getting struck with an arrow herself had hurt. A lot. Like, a lot, a lot. For a moment, she’d forgotten everything she had learned about using her will, but it had come back to her. Since her friends needed help down there, she hadn’t dealt with the arrow yet, but it was starting to be about that time. Dividing her focus was a good way of getting yourself electrocuted when dealing with weather phenomena.

Penny raised her hands into the sky and felt the tempest surge around and inside her, pulling her along for the ride. That was the secret. Taming the weather was not a thing, but if you made it your pal, you might be able to direct it in small ways. At times, you needed a stern voice to command it, like you would a misbehaving puppy, but most of the time, it wanted to help. That immense power filled her to the brim, and she borrowed from it, using it to aid her will. She was still clumsy with it and lacked finesse, but it wasn’t like she was about to perform magical surgery on herself or anything. That would be crazy. She directed her will inward and cut out the infection as a lash of lightning crashed into the arrow, burning it away. Surprised, Penny found the arm still attached when she was done, and the void was gone. It had worked. Hell, yeah, it had worked.

The infection was gone from her own body, but it spread below like a plague. Poor men, women, and even children were being turned into hideous creatures faster than the others were able to put the dead and the dying down to contain the spreading disease. She wasn’t sure what was going on, but it was time to end this.

Come gather ‘round, people,

Wherever you roam,

And admit that the storm will make you groan!

Not her best words of power, and they were mostly stolen, but it was the intent that mattered, and the force with which she spoke them. The clouds gathered for her, eager to unleash their terrible power. Holding the image of the void-infected townspeople clear in her mind, she unleashed something she didn’t often use, due to its incredible mana requirement. Lightning slammed into a growing group of half-formed void creatures, carrying Penny’s will with it, enough to break the Source inside them. They all died in an instant, but that wasn’t enough. That same bolt of lightning carried on, dividing itself into five forks that searched and found new victims in the town. Each time a void creature died, the lightning spread, much like the disease it was burning away. She watched, panting and ragged, as hundreds of void creatures died on the spot. Imbuing her will into her spells had made them much more powerful, and the storm followed her intent and meaning to a much greater degree, allowing her to focus on hitting the monsters where it hurt, right in their Source.

Satisfied with a job well done after having eliminated the void scourge, Penny lowered herself to the ground and released the storm. Bone-deep exhaustion grabbed her, like always, and she stumbled to the side before catching herself. Curtis stood beside her, but in her tired state, she focused on something else, something above them and not. The system wasn’t in the sky, not in Penny’s domain. It was everywhere and in everyone. She knew that much. Even so, she couldn’t help but look up. A knot in her stomach told her something was wrong the moment they stepped through the portal, and it soon became apparent why. The void was in there with them, and it was now pushing in on the system of the dungeon, twisting it into something it wasn’t meant to be. This was far from over. Things were just getting started.

Nick: “How are you guys doing over there?”

Nick: “Can you come?”

A dark, tall figure rose near the center of the town, a fair bit away from where she was standing. It looked humanoid, but dark as night, and towered over the rooftops like a giant.

Penny: "How urgent is it? We're having some... difficulties here too."

Nick: "It's Hannah. She's fighting something, and we can't go near. I tried, but was almost sucked into them. They're like a black hole. And the locals are slaughtering each other and turning into things we can't really handle without Hannah."

Penny: "Is everyone alive?"

Nick: "Of us? Yeah."

Nick: "You didn't lose anyone, did you?"

Penny: "Kenny is gone, and Priyah lost an arm. We'll finish up here and come to you. Don't let attacks hit you."

Nick: "Damn... We'll try to stay out of their way, but it's difficult to keep Mateo back. This guy is a pain in my ass. I'm sorry about your guildmate. Hurry, but be careful."

"I'm heading down there," Curtis said.

Penny had almost forgotten he was standing there. He'd been so quiet. "To the giant? I'll join you."

"I'm sure it'll have a lot of energy."

"Huh?" Penny asked.

Curtis pointed to the side, where Martin was backing away from a group of townsfolk who still hadn't turned. Many others were marching straight toward the giant, and they weren't stopping when they reached it. Instead, they just kept going, walking into its darkness.

Ray was fighting again, too, and doing a good job of it despite having to protect Priyah. That girl had some spunk, because she had gone right back to fighting, as if missing an arm didn't bother her. That showed real character in Penny's book.

"Why don't you help them and I deal with the void giant?" Penny asked.

The corner of Curtis's eye twitched. "I would rather stay away from Ray. His knife..."

It was a strange weapon. Apparently, he had taken it from a demon at some point - Penny had missed a lot staying in Chicago for so long - and it was said to store the souls of those he killed. Penny didn't know about souls, but that thing was bursting with Source.

"You don't like it?" she asked.

Curtis just grunted and started down the hill, running toward the square where the giant was. Also, it was growing larger. What the hell.

Penny (Party): "Nick and the others need our help. Let's finish this and then join them."

Focused red light beams erupted from Martin's weapons, and they sheared through several layers of pressed-in townsfolk, cutting them in two. That cut off their terrible screaming. The sound of their wailing would be stuck in her head for days after this, she figured. Stupid horror town. Ray killed off a good number of people too, aided by Priyah holding them in place. It was enough to make the remaining townsfolk turn and start walking toward the giant, joining their fellow weirdos in their screaming procession. Ray being Ray, he cut them all down from behind as they walked away, not leaving a single screamer alive.

It felt like they had been fighting the entire town, because the ground was covered in bodies and blood, but Penny realized that was far from the case. Rising into the wind again, she surveyed the town from above and saw hundreds of people marching to their deaths to feed the giant, even as Curtis moved in to attack it. He looked like a small animal compared to the massive void creature, like a yappy dog biting at its ankles. Penny couldn't help but burst out in a chuckle despite it all. Imagining Curtis as a growling, glaring little chihuahua was just too much fun.

Her laughter cut off when townsfolk started throwing themselves at Curtis, piling on top of him. It didn't take long for him to fall out of sight, and Penny debated whether it would be a good idea to chuck some lightning bolts at the growing mound.

A scream shot through the air and would have collided with her if she hadn't used a gust to throw herself to the side. The void giant pulled back and threw a damn wagon at her, forcing Penny to dodge again. Moving to change positions, she spotted Martin approaching from an alley behind the giant. A red light appeared at the tip of one of his swords. It intensified for a second, then burst forward, shooting straight into the void giant's back.

The laser, because that's what it looked like, disappeared without damaging the giant in the slightest. Seeing their opponent's swift reaction was a shock, because it shifted around without taking a step and slammed a giant hand down right on top of Martin. His standing in that narrow alley between two stone buildings meant that the fist tore through both structures, razing them pretty much on top of Martin, who disappeared from view.

"Shit," Penny said. This was not going well.

Ray appeared as well, his glowing dagger showing through the gloom. Night was coming. Not a great time to be fighting monsters made out of darkness. Well, not darkness, but kind of. Curtis would probably grunt at her for that description, but that dude grunted more than a pig digging through mud for scraps of food. Ray, to his credit, didn't charge in the second he saw a towering void giant accompanied by a sea of townsfolk doing their best to sacrifice themselves. Instead, he climbed up a building, dragging Priyah behind him, with some difficulty due to her, well, missing an arm. Could Luke fix that? Maybe.

That brief thought of Luke brought with it a sense of familiarity with the dungeon, which was strange. It was as if his presence was in there with them, in some way. Messaging him hadn't worked, because of course it didn't, but there was something here more than just added Source, something dangerous. That was the reason for the void giant and the transformations, and she thought it would only get worse.

Martin was fine, because of course he was. The guy could phase through anything, and he leapt back and scrambled up to a rooftop. Once there, he plopped down next to a chimney.

Martin (Party): "I can't hurt it."

Ray (Party): "I'm not going near that thing."

Penny dodged another thrown screamer and kept her eyes on the ground.

Penny (Party): "It's probably best if you take it easy. Looks like Curtis is making a move."

The now even larger pile of townsfolk on top of Curtis stirred.

Curtis (Party): "Don't call it that."

Ray (Party): "Hey Curtis, make your move. I'm sure the giant nightmare man will fall head over heels for you."

Penny (Party): "You're like ten years older than me but about as mature as someone ten years younger."

Ray (Party): "Thank you."

Even from a distance and above, watching the square in the diminishing light, Penny saw the shift when it happened. Darkness oozed up between limbs and bodies, slithering up and out through the creepy townsfolk until it covered every inch of them. The darkness undulated for a moment, then pulled back and into the shape of a man. For a moment, it looked a lot like a smaller version of the void giant, but then it took the form of Curtis, clothes and all.

"Yeesh," Penny whispered to herself. That guy had been weird from the start, but he was looking more and more like a monster himself at this point. Good thing he was a friend, because the whole storing Source inside him like a void creature thing was kind of weird, to say the least. Eating, and Penny couldn't think of it as anything else, all those people had given Curtis a lot more of the stuff. There still wasn't much, but even a little was too much in her book. Luke had told her taking it inside yourself would mean instant death, but Curtis must've slept through that briefing, or he just didn't care. Either way, he was doing it just fine. Damn weirdo.

Ray (Party): "Yeah, Curtis! Eat those assholes!"

The void giant stopped throwing townsfolk at Penny the moment Curtis appeared. It faced him and tilted its head to the side, as if taking an interest. The void rippled and shuddered, and soon the giant transformed, changing its shape into something different.

"Is that a worm?" Penny asked no one in particular. "Worms are disgusting."
