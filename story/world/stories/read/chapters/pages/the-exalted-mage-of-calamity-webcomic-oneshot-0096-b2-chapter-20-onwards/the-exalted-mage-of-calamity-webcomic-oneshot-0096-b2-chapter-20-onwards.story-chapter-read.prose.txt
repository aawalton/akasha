The trip out of the Valley had been much slower than any of them wanted.

After Hevestra’s defeat, ambient mana had begun returning to the valley. That gave Gwen just enough mana to continue healing them day after day, applying various spells whenever she had enough strength to do so.

Nasha was the most visibly injured, though she could still string together an arrow or two if the situation demanded it. Veronica, meanwhile, looked mostly fine. Her problem was much deeper.

Due to overexerting herself, Sage had given Veronica a perfectly clear warning.

[Your body’s constitution is in an extremely precarious situation due to internal wounds and stress. Your Exalted Form is currently unavailable. Attempting to activate it before you are fully recovered may result in permanent damage. Please do not cast any tier-4 spells and keep tier-3 spells to an absolute minimum. Further use will delay recovery]

Thinking back on her battle against Hevestra, and the demon back in Greystone, it was clear that fighting at such a low tier simply wasn’t sustainable. She wasn’t used to fighting with such weak magic.

She had come from a world where the first time she participated in any combat drills was after she had reached the sixth tier. Her first real fight hadn’t happened until the seventh. This was like giving a spearmaster a wooden sword and expecting them to perform the same. They would still have their skills, but they would naturally fall back on their normal fighting style. And if they tried to use the sword like a spear, they would make countless mistakes.

Weight. Length. Handling. It was all different.

The disparity she felt was the exact same. She had overestimated herself, thinking she was still the strongest in the world.

But no. That wasn’t the case.

Not now.

She needed to start ascending faster if she intended to keep going on dangerous journeys like this. Perhaps she also needed a new staff, or artifacts to help make up the difference. Things would need to change quickly.

“Hey guys!” Gwen shouted from the front of the wagon. Her face was slightly hollow, but there was relief in her voice. “We’re finally here! I see the settlement!”

Veronica opened the curtain at the front of the wagon and peered through. Ahead of them, slowly growing larger, was the settlement. They had finally reached the edge of the Valley of Silence.

“Nasha,” Veronica said, turning back into the cabin.

Nasha was sitting inside, asleep.

“Wake up. We’re at the settlement.”

Nasha stirred, opening her eyes slowly.

Veronica looked ahead again, watching as they approached the settlement. However, something felt different.

The crooked houses and ramshackle huts stood exactly where she remembered them. The thin fencing that outlined the settlement’s perimeter was still rotted and brittle. Everything looked the same as before. Except there was one thing different.

The people.

Gwen, seated in the front coach, drove them farther into the settlement.

As she suspected, there was a severe lack of people. No one came out to greet them. Not even a single prying eye watched from behind a window, trying to decide whether they were welcome or unwelcome guests.

Sage?

[I do not detect any movement that suggests anyone is around. I also do not detect any energy signatures.]

Veronica narrowed her eyes. She was now sitting on the coach beside Gwen. “The settlement is empty. No one is here.”

Gwen glanced at her once before scanning the road ahead. “Gone? Where did they all go?”

Veronica pursed her lips for a moment. One possibility came to mind.

“They were likely here because of Hevestra’s curse magic,” she said. “Maybe he was using them as livestock to feed from. With Hevestra dead, they no longer have any lingering attachment to this place.”

They continued deeper into the settlement until they reached the elder’s hut.

“Stay here,” Veronica said.

She stepped down from the coach and entered the hut. After about a minute, she returned with a piece of paper in one hand and a small pouch in the other.

“What’s that?” Gwen asked.

Veronica sat back down on the coach seat. Then she lifted the note in front of her and read it aloud.

To the travelers who entered the Valley,

If this letter has reached your hands, then it means you returned.

For that, I am glad.

I do not know what you found within the Valley. I do not know what burden you carried or what price you paid. But something changed after you left.

The morning after the sky shook and the earth trembled, the people of this settlement woke with a feeling we did not understand.

For the first time in many years, the Valley did not call to us.

No treasure waited beyond the hills. No whispers urged us to stay. No lost hope pulled at our hearts and told us one more journey inside would be enough.

Some of us wept. Some laughed. Some remembered names we had not spoken in years.

I think, perhaps, we were cursed. Or perhaps we were simply held too tightly by grief and something inside the Valley learned how to use that grief against us.

Either way, the hold is gone.

We have decided to leave. There is nothing left for us here but old wounds, empty homes, and memories we no longer wish to feed. Some of us will head south. Others will search for their families. And the rest only wish to leave this valley and wander.

I believe you are the reason we have woken up.

For that, you have my thanks.

We did not have much to leave behind. Not that we had much to begin with. But there is one thing I thought might belong with you.

The bundle beside this letter contains a seed. It was found many years ago near the ruined village within the Valley. It never grew in this land, but it never withered either. Some believed it was left behind by Selene. Others believed it was simply another strange remnant from a place too old for us to understand.

Perhaps you can find a way to use it. Or perhaps sell it as a rare commodity.

Whatever the case, it is up to you.

May the world be kinder to you than the Valley was to us.

Veronica lowered the letter. “It seems the curse holding them here was broken.”

The people had lived at the edge of a corpse-like land for years, feeding a demon they had never known existed. They had sent their own into the Valley over and over again, almost like a sacrificial rite, all while chasing a treasure that had never truly been there.

Now they could be free.

“Looks like we saved all the people here, then,” Nasha chimed from inside the cabin.

“…Yeah.” Veronica folded the letter carefully and stored it away. As for the pouch beside her, she opened it.

Inside was a small grain.

It was earthy and rough to the touch, oval in shape, with brown and dark purple blotches. It looked almost like an almond.

“Is that a seed?” Gwen asked.

“Looks like one,” Veronica replied. “Though it seems strange. The purple coloring, the fact that it survived in the valley until now, and the elder mentioning that it won’t grow.”

Sage. Any idea what this is?

[It does not seem to match any of my records. I can begin an indepth analysis, but it will take quite some time.]

Alright. Let me know whenever you find out what it is.

Veronica placed the seed back into the pouch and stored it in her pocket. “I’ll have Sage try and find out what it is.”

She looked around at the empty settlement. “For now, I think it’s safe to say this place is abandoned. There’s nothing left for us here.”

She paused for a moment.

“Let’s go.”

Gwen nodded. She slid her hand across the front panel of the wagon, and it began to move.

Their expedition to the Valley of Silence was complete. They had obtained what they came seeking. And maybe, they had gotten far more than they had originally bargained for.

Gwen let out a satisfied sigh and threw her head back, simply reveling in the moment.

“Oh, it feels so good to breathe fresh air.”

Veronica took a deep breath. “It certainly feels better than the valley.”

They were now traveling along a thin path between rolling hills. Small trees gathered around them, part of a wider forest, with little ponds scattered here and there between the grass. It was nature on all sides, warm and alive. A welcome change from the overgrown, ashen ground of the valley.

Veronica reached beneath the wagon’s front compartment and pulled out a small communication crystal. This was the crystal connected directly back to Ronswick.

She tried once, waiting for the link to connect.

Nothing happened.

She tried a few more times, and after about ten minutes, the communication crystal finally flashed.

“Ah, sorry for the delay. I was a tad busy.”

It was Galland.

“It’s fine,” Veronica said. “I just wanted to let you know I’ve completed my journey to the Valley of Silence.”

For a few seconds, the communication crystal remained quiet.

Then Galland let out a long breath. “You have no idea how relieving that is to hear,” he said. “When you said you were going into the Valley of Silence, I prepared myself for the possibility that I would never hear from you again.”

“That would certainly be inconvenient,” Veronica replied.

“Quite,” he said. “Did you find what you were looking for?”

“We did. Though it was much more troublesome than expected.”

“Mm. For some reason, it sounds like you’re downplaying it.”

“We almost died!” Gwen chimed, shouting over into the crystal.

“Likewise,” Nasha said from behind Veronica.

Veronica sighed, her shoulders drooping slightly. “You guys can stop making me feel bad. I know I brought you into danger.”

Nasha reached out from behind her, wrapping her arms around Veronica’s shoulders. “Relax. We’re just kidding. We knew the risks.”

Gwen nodded and tapped a fist against her chest. “She’s right. We’re just pulling your leg. Don’t worry about it. We’re much tougher than we look!”

Nasha glanced over at her. “Says the one who didn’t have a single scratch on them.”

Gwen stuck her tongue out. “Not getting hurt means I’m tough.”

A light smile formed on Veronica’s face.

In the past, if she had done anything to put those around her in danger, she would have been berated. Punished, lectured, and everything in between.

Veronica rested her hand on Nasha’s arm. “Thanks, girls.”

Galland hummed over the crystal. “Certainly sounds eventful. I hope you are all right.”

“We’ve certainly been better, but we’re all right now. We’re currently on our way to Maftem,” Veronica replied. “Has anything happened in Ronswick so far? How goes business with Zenith?”

Zenith was the name of Galland’s new trading company. It also happened to be the name she remembered from the future. Oddly enough, she hadn’t told him the name he would use. Perhaps he had always had it in mind.

“Several things.” Galland’s voice shifted slightly, becoming more composed. “Trade has improved. Considerably. After the scavenger issue was handled, many of the smaller merchants became much easier to negotiate with. They were wary at first, but once they realized the road was safer and the tower was actively involved, requests began coming in.”

“Requests for what?”

“Transport contracts. We have set up a trade route and are delivering shipments ourselves with our own mercenaries. Along with regular trade deals, many are commissioning us to sell their goods for them. A few merchants are also asking to work directly with Ronswick’s tower instead of going through the usual third-party channels.”

“We’re doing escort work now?” Veronica asked. “Weren’t we supposed to be a merchant company? Shouldn’t we be the ones hiring guards or mercenaries?”

“Normally, yes. But after the Scavengers disbanded, many of their members were left looking for work. While most of the upper echelon were quite corrupt, the newer members and those on the lower rungs had clear histories. That being the case, we now conduct trade while also handling our own caravan deliveries.”

“Combining both companies into one? Are the mercenaries going to be that trustworthy?” Veronica asked.

She swore she heard a chuckle come through the crystal.

“Normally, no. Because with a growing company, there will always come executives who become a bit corrupt. That is exactly how the Scavengers fell. For now, this is why we’re only doing caravan trade with local towns and smaller villages near Ronswick. I’ve gotten into contact with the Mage Association thanks to Hugh, and we’ve been able to acquire some 4th-tier revelation mages on contract. They will accompany the caravans, monitor the entire route themselves, and maintain a direct recorded link to the mage tower.”

“What? How’d you manage that?”

“I didn’t manage that. You did, Veronica,” Galland replied. “Apparently, the Mage Association has been in an uproar lately because of you. Hugh has been telling me to let you know that a representative would like to speak with you whenever you called. If you’re heading to Maftem, I can tell them to send someone there, assuming you plan on staying for a while.”

“Yes, I do. The duke’s city should have a lot of resources for me, so I plan to stay there for quite some time unless something comes up.”

“Perfect. I’ll let Hugh know and relay that information.”

“Hey! What about Rocky? Have you heard anything about him yet?” Gwen asked, leaning over.

“That large rock creature, right? Hugh told me Rocky is still on his way to the Berdow Highlands. I suspect he should arrive there in about one more month.”

“How is he being transported?” Veronica asked.

“It caused quite an alarm here, and it was my first time seeing one, but the association sent over an airship to pick him up.”

Veronica blinked.

They had actually sent an airship to pick him up? It was certainly one of the best methods of transportation, aside from long-range teleportation. She just hadn’t expected them to go that far.

“Ah, and before I forget. If you’re going to Maftem, does that mean you’ll be participating in the city auction?”

“Auction?” Gwen asked, immediately leaning in closer.

Nasha’s voice came from behind Veronica. “Did he say auction?”

Veronica glanced back at them, then returned her attention to the crystal. “What auction?”
