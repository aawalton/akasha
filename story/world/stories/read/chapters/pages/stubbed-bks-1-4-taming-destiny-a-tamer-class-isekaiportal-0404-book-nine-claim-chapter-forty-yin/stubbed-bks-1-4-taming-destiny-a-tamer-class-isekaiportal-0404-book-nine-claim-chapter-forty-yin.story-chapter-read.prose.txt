“Finally,” I sigh, entering my cabin and falling face-first on my bed. After the meeting with the lords, I needed to have a long conversation with the four currently managing my ship – Sarran as Expedition Steward, Maxen as leader of the fighters, Loran as my manservant, and the second mate – Tavalis Waveborn – an experienced sailor who is likely to be promoted to captain if he does a decent job in the next week.

After everything that’s happened recently, we had a good deal to discuss, and also needed to set sail – at quarter-speed due to the repairs that several ships still need to undergo. But our new Alliance made it far easier to decide when we would actually set off – and in what order. Due to the repairs my ship needs, Layton’s is taking point behind Sor’az’s scouting vessel while Valence and I are sitting in the protected spots with Josiah and Valence on either side and Garous bringing up the rearguard.

Thank goodness for the ability to delegate, though – although I had to discuss what needed doing on the ship, I didn’t have to do anything about it. And given how tired and strained I felt, I didn’t even feel guilty about giving out orders and then disappearing into my cabin.

I hear the faint brush of claws against wooden boards and a cool nose presses itself into my neck and snuffles ticklishly. I groan and push myself up, glaring at Bastet. The damn feline just looks smugly at me, though I can see the tiredness in her that underlies everything else.

She’s not the only one who’s joined me in the cabin. Ninja has curled up on a rug with the three male raptorcats cuddled up close to her, Fenrir has leaped up onto the end of my bed, and even Catch has sidled into the cabin and has taken up a guard position near the door.

I raise an eyebrow at him.

“Don’t you need to be with the hatchlings?”

Smith is with them, as is Pride, he assures me. Right now, they’re easy enough to manage during that – most are sleeping off the bellyful of meat they took from the roc. Eats-dirt is with Reducer, the Great Predator, and Lathani on another ship, interviewing the humans one by one. When they have completed that ship, they will move onto the next.

I close my eyes briefly, my fatigue weighing even more heavily on me. I’d forgotten about that. It seems crazy that I did – the lords were so outraged at the idea and I had to argue strongly to convince them – but I suppose I’ve had other things on my mind since then.

“Are they OK?” I ask Catch instead of reaching out to them individually – I don’t want to distract them in the middle of something critical.

Well enough, he confirms. Then, perhaps sensing the guilt in me that I’m not actively involved in the investigation, he continues. We had little to do in the battle with the Sovereign. Anyway, they are happy to help now. And while you are resting and we are uncertain whether there are any with ill intentions on this ship, I will guard you.

His tone defies me to argue with him; I don’t bother trying – I’m grateful for his loyalty. I do reach out to Kalanthia, though, brushing against her Bond to politely ask if she’s available.

She gets back to me about half a minute later, sending me a questioning feel.

Listen, I appreciate what you’re doing there, but if you need to rest after the battle, please do, I urge her earnestly. Lathani, River, and Iandee might not have had much part to play in the escape from the Tier five, but Kalanthia pushed her magic almost as much as I did.

I am well, Kalanthia replies, a mixture of affront at my lack of faith and appreciation for my concern intermingling. This is not tiring. If you wish to recognise my efforts, however, I would appreciate a Core to absorb on my return.

You can choose one of the Tier three Cores whenever you ask, assuming I am awake, I assure her. I might not have many Cores of that level, but she’s most certainly earned one.

Kalanthia replies with appreciation and I pull back from the Bond. Just as I do so, I realise I have another question that I should have asked. Instead, I direct it at Catch.

“We’ve set sail – how are they moving from ship to ship?”

Ivor and Noir are aiding, Catch answers easily. I shake my head – I should have realised that.

Well, it seems that issue is dealt with for now. Perhaps by the time I wake up I’ll know if there are any others I’ll need to bring to trial – part of the questioning my investigative team are doing is to find out if any other acts of sabotage have already taken place without us knowing.

For now, I want to check out Bastet’s internal matrix more closely and try to heal the strain in my own, then get some sleep. Actually, maybe I should check out Ninja’s too – I did look her over after the battle with the roc, but we were so busy dealing with the results of the battle and then were sucked into the issue with the water stream that I didn’t inspect it in as much detail as I would like.

It’s a hard decision to put my own healing as a priority – I always prefer to make sure everyone else is healed before myself. In this case, though, I recognise that I will be far more effective as a healer when I’m actually able to use magic without it damaging me further.

Digging into my internal matrix, I’m relieved that the healing is simpler than I was fearing. Unlike when I actually cracked my Core, the faint lines over them don’t worsen as soon as I pull mana out of the crystalline structure. I sense that if I pulled a lot of mana all at once they’d be likely to develop into actual cracks, but healing doesn’t require that much.

Filling in all the lines makes a noticeable difference. My internal matrix is still strained and inflamed, making using magic feel like walking around a day after a heavy session at the gym where I concentrated on loaded squats and lunges. Thankfully, though, it no longer feels like I’m about to do myself an injury.

I try healing the strain to the Energy channels and confirm that, yes, I can heal them directly, but it probably isn’t worth the resources – they will heal themselves with time and rest. Hastening the healing is not very mana-efficient, and might not do quite as good a job as simple recuperation.

Lesson learned: don’t overdo cleansing potions. It’s not worth the consequences. Then again, without pushing ourselves to the absolute limits, we might not have survived at all.

In a far better state now I’m just tired and not also injured, I move over to my companions. Bastet’s internal matrix hasn’t changed much since I saw it last, though some of the strain I spotted from the Evolution is healing. I offer her a Core but she rejects it.

I need to rest and consolidate first, she tells me firmly. Bowing to her superior instincts when it comes to her own Core space, I move onto the younger raptorcat.

As I saw when I checked her over briefly before, her internal matrix is far more strained than Bastet’s own – and it’s very…unique. And lopsided.

Unlike Bastet’s that, like many beasts’ correlates well with her body, Ninja’s, now with the Core in place, seems more like an oddly shaped tear drop with a ball hanging off the tip. The ball is her Shadow Projection ability, and is connected to the rest of her matrix by a single thread.

While it seems to be working fine, experience and instinct indicates that it won’t survive the strain of an Evolution. Therefore, until she’s figured it out, she is likely to be stuck at Tier two.

I twist through the Core space and look at the design in another way. Maybe it’s not a tear drop. From this angle, it looks more like a yin-yang symbol. Her core is like the dot in the middle of the white half, and the section with Shadow Projection is like the dot in the middle of the black half, except that the black half isn’t delineated in the way of the white half.

Then, I open my eyes again and ask Ninja to show me her Shadow. The pieces click into place.

I gaze at the shadow, my eyes tracing the lines that no normal shadow would ever have and have to swallow hard as grief wraps a tight hand around my heart.

Next to me, Bastet is frozen where she’s sitting, and Trouble lets out a heart-rending whine, almost higher pitched than I can hear.

“That’s Storm, isn’t it?” I ask Ninja quietly.

Ninja just stares at the shadow for a long moment and then sighs heavily.

Yes, she answers quietly.

“How…how did it happen?” Is this really Storm’s spirit or just your desire to see her again taking semi-tangible form? Is the question I want to ask but don’t, holding the Bond between us tightly so it doesn’t allow the question through without my permission.

I do not know, Ninja replies, staring longingly at the form of her sister. I only remember feeling that I could not fail again, even if it meant my own death. That I needed help. I needed my sister. And she was not there, because when she needed me, I did not aid her. She looks away, hunching her shoulders, her posture not nearly a match for the bone-deep grief that I feel over the Bond. And then my shadow moved where I could not. It was only afterwards that I realised who had come.

She gazes towards the shadow once more and I feel her desperate longing to touch, to brush heads and shoulders with her sister, to lie together in a pile. And I also sense her knowledge that if she tries, the shadow will disappear as if it had never been there.

I shift so I’m breaking Ninja’s sightline with the shadow. Her face scrunches in a snarl as her eyes are forced to meet mine.

“Ninja…you’re not to blame for Storm’s death,” I tell her firmly. She looks away from me, so I reach out to gently guide her head back to me. “You’re not,” I repeat. I see the disbelief in her eyes; I feel it in the Bond. But I will repeat it again and again until she agrees. “You did not kill her. You did not put her in danger,” as I did.

But if I had moved faster than she did–

“Then you would have died in her place,” I interrupt her snarl. “Or perhaps she would have been faster and would still have died. You are not guilty of Storm’s death.”

A large shape approaches us and Bastet settles on the floorboards next to Ninja, her new size dwarfing the younger female.

Packleader is right, she tells her cub firmly. It is the fault of the one who wielded the sharp stick. And he is long gone. We mourn your sister, our packmate. But you are here with us and we are grateful for it.

Trouble rumbles a fierce agreement, shifting over from where he was on the rug to press against her side again. Blaze and Spot follow soon after, Spot almost lying on Ninja’s tail; Blaze daring to get between me and Ninja, though worming his way in to lie low to the ground near her paws. Fenrir and Catch also come closer to give her their silent support. The others, perhaps feeling her aching grief even if they might not know the cause, reach through the Bond network to hug her as best they can from a distance. She’s surrounded by love if only she will open herself to it.

Yet she still holds herself apart from it, her physical body stiff, her mental Bond tense. I cup her head in two gentle hands, leaning in to rub my cheeks against her own and then press my forehead against hers.

“Ninja, we all miss Storm,” I murmur. “But we don’t want to lose you too. Don’t think that I missed how you put success in your task above your own life there. We want you. We love you. We need you. Don’t leave us, please.”

I have to close my eyes tightly and breathe steadily as my own emotions surge to overwhelm me. I’ve lost too many in my life. I might have come to terms with most of them, but I will never face the possibility of losing those dear to me with equanimity. And Ninja, part of my life since that fateful day when I met Bastet and we all almost got eaten by a pack of lizogs, is most definitely dear.

For a long moment more Ninja holds herself stiff.

And then, suddenly, like every bone in her body has disappeared, she collapses into us. As she does so, her Bond also relaxes entirely, waves of overwhelming grief, guilt, and fear of not being good enough to justify that she lived where her sister died wash through the Bond network.

She trusts us with everything she has been holding back, keeping concealed. She has taken a step off the cliff, moving from the painful known into the intimidating unknown.

And we do not let her fall.
