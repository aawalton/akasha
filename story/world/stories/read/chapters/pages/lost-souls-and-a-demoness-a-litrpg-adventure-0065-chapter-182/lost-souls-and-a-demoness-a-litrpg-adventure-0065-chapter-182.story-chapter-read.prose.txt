The central cathedral was in poor shape. Collapsed pillars lay across cracked stone tiles, piles of rubble dotted the floor below holes in the ceiling high above, and broken windows let in the chill of the false night. Despite this, it was one of the most magnificent structures Tara had ever laid eyes on. Ornate carvings encircled what few columns still stood, beautiful reliefs adorned the walls, and brilliant mosaics dotted the floor, their luster untouched by the passage of time. It was hard to reconcile the melancholic beauty of this ancient structure with the violence and cruelty she'd experienced from demonkind over and over.

Those thoughts were swept away as Tara crossed the threshold, ushered inside by the angel they'd been preparing to battle only a few moments before. The inside of the structure was one enormous room, dimly lit by flickering magical orbs that hung suspended in the air like twinkling stars. And at the far end of the cathedral, seated upon a throne of dark stone, was Vastrozun. The demon lord needed no introduction. He was tall and muscular, clad in armor that appeared to be formed from darkness itself. A great shadow loomed behind the throne, flowing upward in a vortex of magical energy of a magnitude that Tara's suit sensors couldn't begin to measure. The dark essence disappeared through a hole in the ceiling, rising to blanket the sky.

"Welcome to my home, champions of the gods."

Vastrozun didn't raise his voice, yet his words carried easily across the vast, open chamber. His voice was low and powerful, but his tone was not threatening. That just made Tara worry more. She cast a surreptitious glance at Jade, who was following the angel deeper into the room. The succubus carried herself with a calm confidence that the tek'kalla knew she couldn't truly be feeling, yet her collected presence was reassuring anyway. This was all according to plan. It was, right? Well, the plan didn’t involve being granted a formal audience, but maybe this would work out better.

"Lord Vastrozun!" Jade called out. Her voice sounded small in comparison to the demon lord's, and she had to raise it for her words to reach him. "We have come to stop the demon invasion of the Labyrinth! Your people bring pain and death to the peaceful people who reside there and have sown discord and destruction across our own world. We can't allow this to continue."

Geez, that was a little melodramatic, wasn't it? Tara thought she knew what Jade was trying to do, but her acting left something to be desired. They finally halted, having crossed most of the length of the room. The angel moved on ahead, joining the demon lord beside his throne. Perhaps fifty feet separated the two groups from each other. Beside her, Naomi shifted nervously. Tara squeezed her arm.

"Is that so?" Vastrozun didn't sound particularly concerned. No wonder. The level of power she could sense in his aura dwarfed anything she had ever felt before. Yet he didn't scoff at the threat either. Instead, he leaned to one side, propping his head up on one arm. "I see you brought a divine splinter with you. To think that the goddess would so casually part with a sliver of the divine. The power held within your hand could wipe out a city, Jade of Earth."

"Or defeat a demon lord."

The angel's eyes flashed red, and she took a step forward, moving to draw her sword.

"Try it and see how far you get." She said coolly. Tara still had trouble sensing the angel's aura, but the small glimpses she managed were enough to send a chill down her spine.

Jade, I hope you know what you're doing.

"Calm yourself, Ira." Vastrozun rose a hand gesturing for his attendant to relax. A strange darkness clung to his figure, shrouding his features from sight, but Tara somehow got the sense that he bore an expression of immense weariness. "Perhaps violence is inevitable, but I would prefer we solve this confrontation in a more peaceful manner."

"Peaceful?" Luis asked skeptically. The large man crossed his arms, uncowed by their intimidating presence. "That'd be a first. Maybe your people should have tried that from the start, instead of killing their way through our cities."

"A regrettable necessity. But a divinely crafted prison is not easy to escape from. We needed supplies and materials, as well as closely guarded relics. There was no other way to obtain what we needed in the time that we had. But contrary to what the goddess undoubtedly told you, it is not war that I wish for. Merely freedom."

"Freedom to do what?" Tara demanded. "Try to conquer the cosmos again? To create a second cataclysm?"

"Is that what you were told?" The demon lord surprised them by laughing. He shook his head in clear amusement, causing his shadowy aura to ripple and distort. "I shouldn't be surprised. History has always vilified the defeated."

"Excuse us if we're skeptical." Jade said. "We've met enough of your people to have no trouble believing what we were told about you."

"You know nothing." The angel growled. "It is the gods that have erred, and the abomination that is the Labyrinth is a monument to their malevolence."

The Labyrinth? Tara wasn't sure where this was going, but her curiosity was piqued. Despite the danger, she found herself wanting to hear more.

"…What do you mean?

"If we are going to speak, then I must ask that you lower that relic." Vastrozun said.

"Absolutely not." Jade didn't waver. She continued pointing the crystal shard straight at the throne. "The moment I do, nothing stops you from killing us."

"Little is stopping me from doing so already. I very much doubt the amount of Destruction essence you possess would be enough to kill me.""Are you sure about that?"

"No, I'm not." Vastrozun's frankness surprised Tara. "I'm fairly certain, but you don't live as long as I have by betting on 'fairly certain' outcomes. Instead, I shall do this."

He gestured for Ira to return to his side and then put a shadowy hand on her arm.

"I, Vastrozun, pledge before the divine to neither attack nor harm you without due warning, so long as you do the same."

The words thrummed in the air, resonating with unfamiliar power. They solidified into something firm and unyielding, an oath given form.

"A promise before the divine is no easy thing to break." Vastrozun rumbled. "Does that satisfy you?"

Tara caught Jade's eye and gave her a small nod. The old stories were clear on this account. Oaths sworn before a celestial were binding in ways that normal promises were not. Dire consequences awaited for any who dared break one.

"…Very well." Slowly, Jade lowered the relic. She didn't put it away, but neither did she keep it primed for use.

"What did you mean about the Labyrinth?" Tara asked. "It was created to imprison demon kind. To stop their conquest of the cosmos."

"I'm sure that's what you've been told." Vastrozun said, the weariness in his voice more prominent. "I could go on about how demonkind was the victim in the wars that came before, about how the rest of the cosmos envied the divine power our homeland had been blessed with. I could detail the exact events that led to our colonies being ambushed, and our very home invaded, leaving it the scarred wasteland you now see it as. But it wouldn't matter. You wouldn't believe me."

The demon lord paused to take a breath. None of them spoke. Tara didn't know what to say. This wasn't what she'd expected Vastrozun to be like.

But no, she had to stay focused. She couldn't let him distract her. What they needed to do hadn't changed. Whatever had happened in the past, the demons were a danger now. She kept a careful eye on the angel. If they stayed on track, she would be the greatest danger…

"And then there's the Labyrinth." Vastrozun continued. "A prison formed from the trapped souls of the gods' servants. Did you ever wonder where the spirits that manifest its monsters come from? Tell them, Ira."

"They're the souls of my kin." Ira growled. "The gods created that abomination by imbuing it with celestial power. Power taken from their own angels. Their, our, souls are bound to it, compelling us to lose ourselves and meld into the whole, so that we can be remade into one of the countless gears that keeps the system turning."

The angel's expression was nearly as dark as the shadow that covered Vastrozun's form. Her words thundered across the chamber, filled with caustic vitriol and thick with regret. Tara was shaken. There had always been theories that the celestials who had vanished with the gods were involved in the Labyrinth's mechanisms, but to think that they had been unwillingly forced into that role… She thought back to all the monsters she had blasted apart, all the pain she'd inflicted upon them. Now cast in a different light, those conflicts, their desperate aggression, made Tara's stomach twist.

"Why would the gods do such a thing?" she asked, squeezing her wand tightly. "Surely there was no shortage of angels willing to help them."

"Some were." Ira said coolly. "But you do not understand the sheer scale of the Labyrinth. 'Some' wasn't enough. They needed every scrap of power they could muster to create it, and an army of spirits to populate it. The strongest of those were used to guard the floors closest to us, and the weakest thrown at the masses as simple training fodder." Ira's eyes flashed as she continued. "Do you know what it's like? To be torn apart, only for the cosmic entity that binds your spirit to restore you in a new form so that it may happen again? The torment is unfathomable, and I will not allow it to continue."

"Is that why you're helping them?" Luis asked. "So you can break the system?"

"Yes. Lord Vastrozun gave me shelter when the Labyrinth was created, and there was nowhere else to hide. And once we escape, we can work to destroy the system that has tormented the spirits of my kin for so many centuries."

There was a long silence as they processed this deluge of new information. Tara glanced at Jade. The succubus still clutched the Destruction relic tightly in her hands. It was shaking.

Keep it together. She urged her friend. This doesn't change what we have to do.

If Ira's story was true, and Tara found the word of an angel difficult to disbelieve, then the Labyrinth was a cosmic abomination on par with the worst myths her people had. She remembered Seraphine's tormented expression, and the reckless abandon with which she had thrown herself at them in the corespace. The memory made her feel sick. And yet, none of that gave the demons the right to slaughter their way through the Labyrinth. As deplorable as she found what was happening to the celestials of old Tara had a duty to protect her people first, and that was not a responsibility she took lightly.

"So that's your plan?" Luis asked. "Help the demons escape this world, and then recruit them to help you destroy the Labyrinth?"

"Yes."

"And what about all the people who live in the Labyrinth? What about the worlds connected to it?" Jade demanded.

This time, Vastrozun replied.

"We do not seek conflict with anyone, other than those who put themselves in our path."

"What did the people of Earth do? I watched an incubus spy cut his way through a dozen of my people. They stole dangerous technology and killed to get it!"

"As I said, an unfortunate necessity for us to acquire the means of our escape in the small amount of time that we had. But-" Vastrozun raised a clawed hand, quieting them as several members of their group made sounds of derision. "But what if I told you that, rather than fighting, we could come to an agreement? One that would protect all of your people, both in and outside of the Labyrinth."

"I'd find that hard to believe." Tara said. She didn't take her eyes off Ira. It hadn't escaped her notice that only the demon lord had sworn to not initiate conflict.

"Even if we wanted to, how could we believe anything that you say?"

"Ira is an angel. An oath made before her is a sacred thing. For someone with my power, breaking such a vow would likely be fatal."

"…then what is it that you propose?"

"Jade," Tara tensed, but Luis put a hand on her shoulder.

"Trust her." He squeezed her gently. "We don't lose anything by hearing him out."

Tara scowled, but she held her tongue for the moment. She held her breath, waiting for the demon lord to continue.

"You have in your possession a divine relic. One that would give me the power to expedite the exodus of my people. If you give it to me, I can use its destructive power to tear new portals in lands far from any of your homes. Our people will not come into conflict, and you would have my word that we would never seek out your worlds or incite conflict of any kind with them. I do not want war for my people. The harsh world we have been imprisoned in has forced them to live violent and brutal lives. I desire to end this. We can find a different region of the Labyrinth, far from any world known to you, and create a new home in a land of abundance."

"So, you're offering us the chance to make you someone else's problem?"

"You can frame it that way if you wish, but I hope to find more diplomatic solutions to conflict, once we have the luxury of choosing that path."

To Tara's surprise, she found the demon lord's earnest tone compelling. That didn't do much to ease her skepticism, though. This Vastrozun may have good intentions, or at least less malevolent ones than they'd been led to believe, but they'd encountered enough demons to know what most of them were like. She had a hard time imagining the greater demons Elyx had spoken of resolving any conflict "diplomatically". Maybe if most of them were like her… but they weren't.

"Hang on." Luis said, holding up one finger. He looked between the demon lord and his guardian angel. "Wouldn't moving your people into the Labyrinth be a problem, since she wants to destroy it?"

"It is the system that holds the spirits of my brethren enslaved that I seek to unravel, not the land itself. I doubt anyone has the strength to destroy a world the size of the Labyrinth, even the old gods."

With that, they fell silent. The only sound was a soft whoosh emanating from the pillar of flowing darkness, which moved like a liquid being drawn into the sky by some invisible force. Tara felt her heart pounding in her ears. She glanced at Jade, trying to gauge her reaction. This was the moment, wasn't it? They wouldn't get a better chance. She remained silent, not trusting herself to speak.

"I have made my offer," Vastrozun intoned, leaning forward in his throne. His armor creaked. Tara couldn't see it well beneath the flowing shadows that covered him, but it sounded old. "What is your response?"
