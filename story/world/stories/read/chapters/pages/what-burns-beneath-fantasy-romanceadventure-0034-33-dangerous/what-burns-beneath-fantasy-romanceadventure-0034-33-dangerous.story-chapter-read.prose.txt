Anger chased me down the street. It nipped my heels, clawed the nape of my neck. Whispered for me to stop, throw my head back, and scream.

He left. He actually left. He threatened me. He abandoned Teela.

I’m better off without him.

Something in the back of my mind murmured a sharp disagreement. Insisted that I was in danger, and that Renner was dangerous, and dangerous was what I needed to make it out of all this.

I can be dangerous, too. I have magic. Salt, I can summon up fire! And I know how to swing a sword. I… I don’t have a sword. Maybe I can find one.

I hurried along, boots thudding with every step. Cold water sloshed inside the soaked leather, threatening to turn my feet numb. Only a few townsfolk were out, all of them huddled beneath clutched shawls and thick cloaks. Quite a few took in my frantic pace and dripping clothes and stopped, giving me odd looks.

Before long the scent of earthy, sweet flour hit my nostrils. Gil’s house was silent. I rushed to the front door, pushing at it… closed. I shoved, but it didn’t budge. I ran to the back, trying that side, but it was either locked or braced from the inside.

I pressed my lips against the cracked wood. “Gil? Sir? Can you get to the door?”

No answer.

Renner must have locked everything before he left. But if he locked them from the inside… he must have climbed out a window. Did he shove me through a window?

The image was so ludicrous that I shook my head. Surely I would have had bumps and bruises to show, if that had been what happened.

Okay. Okay, I’m Renner. I’m awful and cruel and heartless and… that’s not helping. How did he get out? That would be the way to get back in.

I hurried around the side of the house, trying to open each window as I went. They were so fogged and mud-smeared that I could barely see past the glass. Pushing against the cold surfaces accomplished nothing. One around the side of the house inched inwards just a bit, bringing me a swell of triumph… but then it stuck. I could fit my hands up through the wrist through the crack, but beyond that it didn’t budge. I growled, circling around to the back.

I’m Renner. I’m in a house with an unconscious Brin. I take her out the door. I go back in and lock everything. Then… how do I get out?

I looked up. There was a narrow chimney poking out from the slatted roof, but I didn’t believe for a moment he had shimmied his way up that. My gaze swept around the base of the house before settling on a small pile of rocks.

Dangerous. Right.

A quick glance around showed that no one was nearby. I grabbed a fist-sized stone, took a deep breath, and slammed it against the back window. The glass cracked. A few more hits sent it clattering inwards. I pulled a spare tunic from my pack, wrapped it around my arm, and swept away the sharp shards poking out of the frame.

Can’t say I don’t learn. Although the last time I climbed through a window, I didn’t exactly have time to clear the path.

I heaved myself through, landing in a graceless heap on the moldy floorboards. Sour, rotten odors hit me full in the face as I moved towards the kitchen.

“Sir? Are you… oh.” I froze for a heartbeat, jaw slack, before surging forwards.

Gil lay on his kitchen floor. The floorboards beneath him were dark. His hands and feet were bound with what looked like twine from a hunting snare, and there was a strip of black cloth wrapped across his mouth.

Renner had removed the dagger.

“I’m sorry, I’m sorry, I’ll help you.” I babbled apologies and reassurances, tearing the bindings away, then grabbed another spare tunic and pressed it over the wound.

Gil moaned, head lolling to one side. He didn’t seem to be fully aware of my presence.

It’s not fatal. Given time…. saints, he’s a monster. It would have been fatal without help. But not yet. He’ll need a poultice and something to keep away a fever and I’ll need to clean the wound, but I got here in time. He’ll be alright.

The final thought made me pause. A hollow pit opened in my chest as I looked around. Broken bottles, rotting furniture… a shaking, mutilated hand.

I wasn’t sure if Gil would ever be alright.

“You… came back,” he slurred, blinking up at me.

I sniffled. “Y-yes. Of course I did.”

“Shouldn’t… have…”

A chill swept over my skin. I wasn’t sure if he meant that because he’d wanted me to run, or because he wanted… something much darker. I chased the thought away.

“Do you have any herbs here, sir? Or… or clean water…?” I trailed off, looking around hopelessly. All I saw were bottles of liquor, half-molded fruit, and stale bread.

He groaned, giving his head a little shake.

“Okay. That’s okay. You’ll be alright.” Master Lewen mentioned the temple… in the town square, I think. Surely they’ll have supplies. “Sir, I’m going to leave you for a little while. But I promise I’ll come back.”

Gil shifted one elbow, trying to push himself up. His face was the color of ash. “No. You should… leave.”

“People keep telling me that.” My mouth twisted into the shadow of a grin. “I don’t always listen.”

He coughed, spittle spraying onto the floorboards, then sank back down.

“I’m going to go get help. I’ll get the cleric and come right back.” I lifted his hands, placing them over the bloody brocade. “Keep your hands just like this, okay?. Don’t let go.”

He looked at me with empty, glazed eyes… then gave a jerky little nod.

I breathed a sigh of relief. “I’ll be back. I promise.”

Gil’s front door was set in a corner of the kitchen. There was a wooden chair propped beneath the knob, wedging it shut. The sight of it made my blood boil. I tore the chair away, opened the door, and ran.

We had seen the town square the previous evening, when I’d been chatting with townsfolk. I reached it in a matter of minutes. There were more people out and about, but the streets still seemed too quiet as I hurried along.

Still no birds. Still no animals.

Snowmelt’s temple was easily recognizable. It was a small wooden building that had been painted a sunny shade of yellow. There were cheerful blue shutters and a dull brass sun, the symbol of Anduin, affixed to its roof.

I burst in, panting and flushed, and was greeted by two very surprised faces. Both were creased by laughter lines and mottled with age spots. The man had wispy black hair and was thin, with angular features and sharp brown eyes. The woman was tall and broad, with ivory hair tied back into a tidy knot. They were sitting at a small wooden table and drinking tea.

The woman’s face bloomed into a pleasant, though startled, smile. “Visitors! What a nice-”

“A man’s been stabbed!”

The man’s cup hit the floor, amber liquid pooling out across dusty tiles, and he bolted towards a small side room. He disappeared inside and the clatter of potted jars and glass bottles against wood echoed from within.

The woman strode towards me, arms stretched out. She gently nudged me aside and reached past, grabbing two wool cloaks from a thick coatrack beside the door.

“How bad is it?” Her voice was bright and clear.

“In his side; here.” I pressed one hand against my abdomen. “He’s awake, and there’s a binding over the wound, but that’s all I could manage.”

She flashed a quick smile. “Doesn’t sound dire just yet, then! We’ll see to it. Trouble on the road?”

I winced, glancing away. “Ah, no. It’s… your stablemaster.”

Her eyebrows shot into her hairline. “Old Gil? Stabbed? Merciful stars, what in the realms happened?”

I wracked my brain for an answer. My mouth was suddenly very dry.

Fortunately, the man reappeared and bustled towards us. He was holding several large leather satchels. With practiced efficiency he turned so the woman could affix his cloak, then handed the smallest pack to her.

She hoisted the strap over one shoulder. “Right, tell me when we get there. Where are we going, dear?”

I led them at a run back towards Gil’s house. More townsfolk stopped and stared. The sight of their clerics sprinting down the streets brought wide, worried eyes and hushed voices.

The pair followed me without question into the kitchen. I twisted at my damp tunic as we entered, cold with fear at the thought of what state we might find Gil in… then I sagged against the table in breathless relief.

He was propped against the fallen chair, dazed but awake. His hands were white and knotted around my tunic. The pool of blood had not grown.

The man swept to his side, all business. He began opening packs and setting things out, many of which I had seen or used with Clem; clean cloths, bundles of herbs, glass bottles that held cloudy tonics, and jars that smelled like linseed, honey, and flour.

“Can I help?” I sank to the ground beside them.

“You have helped, dear. We’ll set him right.” A warm hand settled on my shoulder. “What’s your name?”

“Brin.”

“Brin? Well, Miss Brin, I’m Sister Embra, and this is my husband, Brother Pel. And we’re going to tend to Gil, here, but we’re also going to tend to you.”

I blinked up, baffled. “Me? I’m alright.”

She stooped down, pressing the back of one hand against my forehead. “Well, you don’t feel feverish. Were you injured at all?”

I shook my head.

“Good, that’s good. Can you tell me what happened?” She gestured around us, to Gil and to the shattered glass and fallen chair.

Brother Pel removed the cloth and began dabbing Gil’s wound with a cloth soaked in currant-colored wine.

The stablemaster’s face was drawn and pale. He hissed through his teeth.

I swallowed, mind racing. How in the realms would I explain all of this? ‘My friend stabbed him because we’re convinced he’s working with a monster and kidnapped someone’. Somehow, I don’t think that will go over well. At least they can’t see the window I smashed from in here.

Renner’s warning came to mind, too. ‘There are other people in this salted town who are gonna get violent the moment you start asking questions.’

I glanced furtively at the pleasant, crinkled face above me. These were clerics. Healers. It was impossible to think they might be involved in all of this. And yet… prudence held my tongue.

Gil wheezed. “Fell,” he bit out, wincing as the soaked cloth met raw flesh again. “On… on that.” His good hand jerked towards the shattered bottle nearby. “Glass cut me.”

“This was deep,” Brother Pel commented. His voice was very soft, and held a bit of a lisp.

Gil groaned. “Yeah. Uh… I pulled it out.”

Embra tutted. “Master Gil, you’re not supposed to do that. It makes the bleeding worse.”

“…‘Kay.”

She knelt nearby, uncorking one of the little bottles. She lifted it to the stablemaster’s cracked lips, cupping her other hand beneath his chin. “This will help with the pain, dear. Drink it all at once, though. The taste lingers, and it’s quite foul.”

Gil gagged the mixture down.

Embra slid the bottle aside. “Good man. Now, Miss Brin, your skin is very cold and pale. That’s worrying. You’ve a lovely complexion!” she added quickly upon seeing my expression. “But I can also see your clothes are wet. I don’t need the details, but I think it’s very important that we get you warmed up.”

I looked down. My hands were bright red, the veins a cool blue beneath the flesh. The skin of my arms was pallid and cold.

“Blankets in there.” Gil jerked his head towards an adjoining room.

“I’ll change, too.” I climbed to my feet. “That should help.”

Embra stood, hands outstretched. Ready to help if I needed her. She offered a wan smile, green eyes warm with concern.

I gathered my things and made my way into Gil’s bedroom. The cot was straw-stuffed and smelled like it hadn’t been washed in months. There was precious little in the way of furniture or decorations.

The tinkle of more glass bottles and the sound of Sister Embra’s rich voice beginning to hum made me turn. I closed the door quietly, peeling off my wet clothes, then began to scrounge through my knapsack.

I only had one clean outfit left. It was one I had shied away from, previously; something about the blood-hued fabric and the intricate, gold-threaded bodice just seemed too bold. I always favored earth tones, colors that made me feel like a natural feature of my surroundings rather than a point of interest. The vivid scarlet seemed like it would do quite the opposite.

But I had little choice. The fabric was luxurious and warm. Its sleeves were cuffed short, leaving my arms bare to the cool air, but the immediate warmth over my breasts and stomach brought a sigh of relief. The trousers beneath were thick and wood-brown, draping comfortably to my ankles. A dark cloth belt around my hips completed the ensemble.

My wet boots felt wretched, but there was no help for that particular discomfort. I collected my things, opened the door… and paused. Silver gleamed beneath the bedroom’s window, just out of the corner of my eye. I turned to look.

It was the window I’d managed to crack from outside. A little wooden handle was affixed to the bottom of its frame. And, around the handle, a short length of twine had been wound into a loose, knotted loop. The tail of it stretched downwards and was tied to the hilt of a very familiar dagger. It was embedded diagonally, hilt-down, in the molding wood. Crimson drops of blood lay spattered on the wall and floorboards beneath the knife.

I stretched my fingers out, trying to tug the window open. Just as before, it slid up and inwards a bit… then the cord went taut.

I stared.

Part of me was vaguely impressed.

And part of me wanted to scream.

Movement from the kitchen spurred me into motion. I bit down on my fury, untying Renner’s crude lock. I cast the twine onto the floorboards, stomped on it once for good measure, then carefully wound the dagger into my belt.

There. Now I have a weapon.

It wasn’t made of witchwood. It was stained with Gil’s blood.

But it still made me feel a bit better.

I can be dangerous, too.
