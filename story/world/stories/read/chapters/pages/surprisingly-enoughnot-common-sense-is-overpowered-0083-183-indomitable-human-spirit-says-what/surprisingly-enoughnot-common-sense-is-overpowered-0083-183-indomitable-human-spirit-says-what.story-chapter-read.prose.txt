The sun rose slowly over the courtyard as Ning thrust his spear straight forward.

Golden morning light fell across his half-dressed body, outlining the lean muscles along his arms, shoulders, and back. To be fair, Ning was not the kind of absurdly muscular cultivator whose single calf looked large enough to dwarf a human head.

Still, his strength was far from weak.

His intimidation stat might have suffered because he couldn't scare people into submission through sheer size and height alone, but there was some consolation in looking deceptively harmless while still being perfectly capable of beating up those muscular monsters.

In some ways, that was even better.

[Cold River Spear Art: Still Water Pierces Stone]

Ning slowly thrust his spear again.

This time, however, he didn't perform the technique in one smooth motion. He deliberately broke it apart, separating the thrust into its smallest components and examining each one individually.

His feet shifted first, finding the correct angle against the ground. His waist turned next, followed by his spine and shoulder, until the motion finally carried through his arm and into the spear.

He was tracing the pathway of a perfect thrust.

Every movement was slow and carefully controlled. The spear advanced inch by inch as Ning repeated the sequence, paying attention to each shift in balance, each transfer of force, and each tiny adjustment that changed the final result.

Completing a single thrust this way took far longer than performing the technique normally. Breaking it apart, correcting the flaws, and rebuilding the motion piece by piece could easily consume dozens of minutes.

To an outsider, it might have looked like a ridiculous waste of time.

But Ning knew better.

He had the power of anime and xianxia on his side, and if there was one thing those had taught him, it was that shit like this worked.

Honestly, Ning would have loved nothing more than to lock himself away on some remote mountain and practice martial arts from sunrise to sunset for fifty years, only descending after becoming invincible.

That sounded exactly like the kind of ridiculous yet effective training arc a proper cultivator deserved.

Unfortunately, Ning did not currently have fifty spare years lying around.

Maybe when his lifespan reached the thousands, he could seriously consider such a run.

"That's enough for today," Ning muttered.

He slowly lowered his spear and ended his morning training session. Compared to his usual routine, today's practice was light. After all, this was not an ordinary day.

Today was the day he would enter the Heavenly Origin Tower.

The Heavenly Origin Tower, also known as the Hundred Floor Tower, had existed within the Pure Qi Sect since its founding.

At this moment, an elder stood before the enormous pagoda and addressed the gathered disciples. Behind him, the tower rose like a behemoth, its ancient structure piercing into the sky layer by layer. Even without releasing any obvious pressure, its presence alone made the disciples instinctively lower their voices.

"The Heavenly Origin Tower is one of our sect's protective treasures," the elder said, his voice spreading clearly across the crowd. "Each opening of the tower requires five years of accumulated resources and spiritual energy. That is why this opportunity is not easily granted."

The disciples listened with varying degrees of attention.

Some nodded solemnly, absorbing every word. Others were clearly staring past the elder at the tower itself, their eyes bright with excitement. A few looked as though they had already entered the tower in their imagination and obtained some heaven-defying inheritance that would immediately make them the protagonist of the era.

Ning stood among them and nodded along with the crowd.

Naturally, he was also excited.

During the past week, they had watched many Foundation Building cultivators enter the higher floors and display all sorts of wonderful performances. The disciples had enjoyed the spectacle thoroughly. After all, watching powerful seniors fight, struggle, and show their best prowess was quite entertaining.

It also helped that sometimes they could view some embarrassing moments of such powerful elders.

"All of you only have one chance to gain something of true value from the tower," the elder continued. "Only your first entry is fully recognized. After that, the tower will no longer grant the same opportunities. This is a boon left behind by our respected ancestors for the younger generation, so treasure it properly."

He paused briefly.

"Only first-time entrants under fifty years of age may access the first five floors. These floors were left specifically for the younger generation."

The elder raised his hand, and several servants stepped forward carrying trays. On those trays rested small iron tokens, each one carved with fine golden patterns.

"If you encounter a fatal injury inside the tower, you will be teleported out immediately," the elder said. "However, remember this clearly. Do not lose your entry token. The token is the object that connects you to the tower's protection. Without it, you will be placing your life in unnecessary danger."

The atmosphere finally became more serious.

Even the disciples who had been whispering earlier quieted down.

The elder's gaze swept across the crowd.

"Now, all of you do your best. Those who wish to enter the tower, come forward and receive your tokens."

He paused for a moment before adding, "Also, remember this. To reach the top five floors accessible to new entrants, it is not enough to merely pass the tower's threshold challenges. Your performance must also surpass others. The tower does not reward mediocrity. It rewards excellence."

Those words instantly stirred the crowd.

Several disciples' eyes sharpened. Others unconsciously looked around, measuring their fellow participants. The moment comparison was mentioned, the peaceful gathering immediately gained the faint smell of competition.

Ning sighed inwardly.

Of course, it was not enough to fight the tower. They also had to fight the leaderboard.

"Do your best, Junior Brother," Ling Yinran said from the side.

Her voice was gentle, but there was clear encouragement in her eyes. Even though she knew Ning's temperament and strength better than most people here, she still looked slightly worried.

Ling Xin stood beside her with her arms crossed, her expression far more casual.

"Remember, safety first," she said. Then, after a brief pause, she added, "But don't forget to kick some ass."

Ning nodded seriously.

As expected of his master.

First survive, then commit violence.

Where else could he find such wisdom?

He stepped forward with the rest of the disciples. After registering his name, Ning quickly received an iron token from one of the inner sect disciples. The token felt cool in his palm, and faint spiritual energy flowed through the carved golden lines on its surface.

Once everyone had received their tokens, the tower gates opened.

A deep rumble spread through the air as the enormous doors slowly parted. One by one, the disciples entered.

As its name suggested, the Hundred Floor Tower had one hundred floors. However, only disciples who had never entered before could access the first five floors. These lower floors were meant for the younger generation, a place where new talents could be tested and perhaps rewarded.

The moment Ning stepped onto the first floor, the first thing he noticed was its size.

It was enormous.

From the outside, the tower had already looked large, but the space within completely ignored common sense. The hall stretched far beyond what the exterior should have allowed. Massive pillars carved from dark jade rose from the floor and extended toward a ceiling. Ancient golden runes flowed across the stone surfaces, illuminating the hall with a soft and sacred glow.

The surrounding walls were covered in murals.

Some depicted immortals splitting mountains apart with a casual wave of their sleeves. Others showed colossal beasts devouring suns and moons, their bodies coiling through the void. There were scenes of cultivators standing against heavenly tribulations, their weapons pointed defiantly toward the sky as divine lightning descended in endless streams.

Every image carried a faint pressure.

It was not strong enough to injure anyone, but it was enough to make the disciples feel small. The murals seemed similar to the spiritual calligraphy and paintings displayed in important areas of the sect. Whoever had carved them must have imbued the figures with a trace of their will.

Ning looked around with interest.

"From the outside, it doesn't look this large," he muttered. "Size as you wish?"

Artifacts that reached the Nascent Soul level were often called magical treasures or fourth-tier weapons. At that level, they already possessed wondrous qualities beyond ordinary spiritual tools. Expansion and shrinking were among the more basic abilities. Some even possessed properties similar to a cave heaven, forming a separate, independent space carved out and stabilized by cultivators.

In other words, the tower being larger inside than outside was not impossible.

Ning could already see that cultivation only became more ridiculous the higher one went.

As he surveyed his surroundings, the disciples around him quickly began forming groups. From a rough estimate, Ning could see at least forty cultivators gathered on the first floor.

The hall soon became lively.

Friends sought one another out. Peak factions quietly reorganized themselves. Some disciples immediately began discussing strategies.

Ning did not pay much attention to them.

Instead, he quickly searched for the protagonist.

A moment later, he found Xiao Fan leaning against one of the dark jade pillars. The young man stood quietly with his arms crossed, looking far less excited than most of the surrounding disciples.

Compared to those who were eager to show off, Xiao Fan appeared almost too calm, as though the commotion around him had nothing to do with him.

Xiao Fan also noticed Ning's gaze.

Their eyes met across the hall.

Both of them gave a simple nod.

Ning originally considered going over to talk to him, but after thinking about it for half a breath, he restrained himself with great wisdom.

Approaching Xiao Fan in an inheritance tower was basically inviting trouble.

Moreover, Ning could already see Qiu Han walking toward Xiao Fan from the side.

"Brother Ning!"

A familiar voice rang out from somewhere within the crowd.

"It's been a while, guys."

"You beat me to it," he said, sighing with genuine regret. "And today, I really thought I would be the one correcting you."

"That would have been a surprise for sure," Wei Zhusang said dryly.

"I heard you were able to apprentice under Senior Ling Xin. Congratulations. It seems our group will have another alchemist now."

Then he shook his head with mock regret. "It's just a pity you won't be able to help me as much in alchemy anymore."

"That only means we can discuss different things instead," Ning replied with a smile.

As for Lang Rulang, Ning simply gave her a polite greeting.

"It's good to see you too, Sister Lang."

"Same to you."

Ning froze.

The others also paused.

Lang Rulang had actually responded.

Not with a cold grunt or even a faint nod, but proper words.

"T-this…" Ning stared at her in shock.

Lang Rulang narrowed her eyes.

"What is that reaction?"

Ning also smiled.

After entering the inner sect, their chances of meeting had become much rarer. During the first year, everyone had been busy adjusting to their new environment. The inner sect was not like the outer sect, where disciples could roam around with fewer obligations.

Each person had their own peak, their own master, their own cultivation arrangements, and their own responsibilities. Zhang Feng and the others, as heirs or important juniors of their respective cultivation families, had also apprenticed under Foundation Establishment relatives or seniors within the sect.

Because of that, seeing them gathered together like this felt oddly nostalgic.

Information was always useful.

Especially free information.

At the center of that group stood a handsome young man wearing expensive robes embroidered with silver spirit-thread. The fabric shimmered faintly whenever he moved, and even from a distance, one could tell that his outfit was worth more spirit stones than some disciples' entire net worth. Several cultivators surrounded him in a loose protective formation, their positions casual at first glance but clearly arranged to guard him from all sides.

"Because that's exactly what he is."

Seventh-stage Qi Condensation was impressive, but not unusual among the disciples entering the tower.

Being the grandson of such a figure practically meant that Lei Dong had been born standing on a mountain while others were still searching for a hill to climb.

No wonder he had guards.

He already had a guess. And that guess was extremely xianxia.

However, cultivation still has its advantages in certain levels. So these people deliberately waited. They have been in the inner sect for years but never entered the tower. Now, they are entering for the first time only to help their young master."

He was not surprised.

Blatant rule-bending from a young master was practically a local specialty in cultivation worlds. In fact, compared to some of the nonsense he had read in novels, this was almost restrained. At least Lei Dong had not arrived in a dragon carriage pulled by enslaved phoenixes while loudly declaring that everyone beneath the Purple Mansion realm should kneel before him.

It made sense.

Zhang Feng, Wei Zhusang, Lang Rulang, and Su Fan all came from decent backgrounds themselves. Because of that, they understood better than most just how large the gap between privilege and even greater privilege could become.

Someone without that experience might only see Lei Dong’s arrogance. They understood exactly where that confidence came from.

Most of them, however, were not as eye-catching as Lei Dong. Since the Heavenly Origin Tower only opened once every five years, many of the disciples entering had joined the inner sect during that period. Their cultivation levels generally ranged from the seventh stage to the early eighth stage of Qi Condensation. There were no other blatant high-tier Qi Condensation cultivators roaming around.

What Ning found especially interesting was that Wei Zhusang also spoke seriously about Xiao Fan and Qiu Han.

Even though he is only at the sixth stage of Qi Condensation, he must be watched carefully."

Hm. Maybe there is a pill for that, or I could make one. Ning mused. It was not impossible after all.

A very dangerous child, admittedly, and one whose pets could make most beasts from Earth look like puppies.

Coward.

Traitors. The lot of them.

Xiao Fan.

It was wide enough that fifty disciples could climb side by side without crowding. The steps themselves were pale gold, smooth-surfaced, without any visible mechanism or obvious trick.

Just stairs.

Wei Zhusang glanced toward the stairs. “We’ll know soon enough.”

The golden stone beneath his foot felt perfectly ordinary. The surrounding spiritual energy remained calm, his qi flowed through his meridians as smoothly as usual, and for a brief moment, the entire staircase seemed almost peaceful.

They looked like the type of fodder that existed only to show the severity of the situation.

By the time Ning reached the tenth stair, the first trace of pressure descended.

It was faint at first, so subtle that it almost seemed like an illusion. The golden stone beneath his feet had not changed, and nothing visible pressed down upon him, yet Ning could clearly feel the surrounding space become heavier.

The pressure came from everywhere at once, seeping into the air, wrapping around his limbs, and pressing against his body from every direction.

Most disciples did not even notice it precisely because it was subtly ever-present.

Ning continued climbing.

Twelfth stair.

Fifteenth.

Twentieth.

With each step, the pressure increased by a fraction. The disciples who had been laughing grew quieter, focusing on maintaining their breath. One by one, the careless smiles vanished from the staircase, replaced by stiff expressions and narrowed eyes.

By the twenty-fifth stair, the trial finally revealed its teeth.

A disciple several rows ahead suddenly staggered. His face drained of color, and the veins along his neck bulged as he desperately circulated spiritual qi through his body. For a few breaths, he tried to force himself upright through sheer pride, but pride was useless before the tower's judgment. His knees buckled, his palm struck the stair beneath him, and before he could even shout in frustration, a beam of golden light descended from above.

The disciple vanished instantly.

The surrounding crowd fell silent for half a breath before whispers spread through the staircase.

"That was a seventh-stage cultivator."

"He failed before thirty steps?"

"What kind of staircase is this?"

Ning narrowed his eyes slightly.

Interesting.

The pressure was not simply testing cultivation. If it were, that disciple should not have failed so quickly. His realm had not been low among the inner disciples, and his qi had not seemed particularly weak. Yet the tower had removed him before many others who possessed lower cultivation.

That meant the staircase was examining something deeper.

Something hidden beneath the surface of cultivation itself.

Ning continued upward.

Thirty steps.

Thirty-five.

Forty.

The pressure steadily increased, but by now it no longer felt like simple weight. There was something almost inquisitive about it, as though the staircase itself were examining him.

It was a strange sensation. Ning could feel that invisible attention passing through his body, probing deeper than the pressure from before.

Nothing seemed hidden from it.

The stairs know what you are.

The tower’s earlier words resurfaced in his mind, and Ning’s eyes brightened.

“So that’s what it meant,” he murmured. “Foundation.”

Zhang Feng looked over. “What?”

“The stairs are testing our foundations,” Ning explained. “Two cultivators at the same stage aren’t necessarily carrying the same pressure. If someone rushed their breakthroughs with pills and left flaws behind, the tower makes them pay for it.”

Wei Zhusang immediately understood the implication. “Then a higher cultivation stage could actually become a disadvantage if the foundation underneath it is poor.”

“Exactly.”

Su Fan glanced farther up the staircase. “That explains quite a lot.”

Outside the tower, Lang Feilang leaned slightly toward Ling Xin.

“Your disciple is sharp. He figured it out quickly.”

Ling Xin smiled, clearly pleased. “Well, he learned from the best.”

Back on the staircase, Ning’s thoughts naturally turned toward Lei Dong and his guards.

A higher cultivation stage already meant a heavier starting burden, and the staircase would only make things worse if the foundation beneath that cultivation was lacking. That was especially troublesome for Lei Dong's older guards. Their higher cultivation might help in ordinary trials, but here it also meant enduring a much heavier burden from the beginning.

The stairs were probably not going to be kind to them.

Far ahead, several powerful disciples were already approaching the sixtieth stair. Some moved with steady confidence, their robes fluttering faintly in the heavy air, while others were clearly beginning to struggle. Even among those known as talents, differences were beginning to emerge. The higher they climbed, the fewer companions remained, and the staircase gradually turned from a crowded trial into a merciless sieve.

Step forty-five.

Step fifty.

At the fiftieth stair, the pressure transformed completely.

The bones throughout Ning's body creaked faintly. His muscles tightened as though invisible hands had wrapped around every part of him and begun squeezing with force.

It felt as though the world itself was rejecting his ascent.

Several disciples stopped entirely. Some sat down immediately, knowing that another step would only humiliate them. Others stubbornly attempted to move forward, only for their legs to tremble violently before collapsing to their knees. Golden beams descended one after another, removing those who had reached their limits. Each disappearance made the remaining disciples more silent.

The staircase had become merciless.

Yet Ning's expression gradually calmed.

Because this feeling was familiar.

Training hurt. Body refinement hurt. Practicing spear arts until his palms tore open hurt. Having his muscles scream beneath the pressure of repeated failures hurt. The body did not become stronger through comfort, and cultivation had never been some graceful immortal fantasy where one sat beneath the moon, breathed twice, and ascended while flower petals danced around them. Them be stuff of legends.

Reality was often disappointing.

For him, cultivation had always been sweating until his robes clung to his skin, staggering back up after being knocked down, forcing his qi through aching meridians, and convincing himself that one more attempt was possible even when his entire body disagreed.

Compared to those things, this staircase was simply another form of suffering.

And Ning had become surprisingly good at suffering.

The Path of the Grind did not exist because training was enjoyable. No sane person looked at pain, exhaustion, repeated failure, and spiritual drain and thought, Ah yes, my beloved leisure activity.

The Path of the Grind existed because improvement was worth the suffering. It existed because every drop of sweat became accumulation, every bruise became experience, every miserable step forward was still a step forward, and at the end one could see the effect of their effort.

Ning exhaled slowly.

Then he climbed.

Step fifty-three.

A sharp pain suddenly pierced one of his meridians.

His expression changed immediately. He recognized the location.

When he had advanced using the Dragon Qi Grass, a tiny imperfection had formed within his qi circulation. Under normal circumstances, it was so insignificant that even Know Thyself had never registered anything unusual. The overwhelming pressure of the staircase, however, forced his meridians into conditions they normally never experienced, magnifying that tiny imperfection until it finally became observable.

Ning did not panic. Now that Know Thyself could actually perceive the flaw, correcting it was much easier.

He adjusted his circulation at once. His qi slowed, compressed, and shifted along a slightly different route. The pain sharpened for a breath, then gradually faded as the flaw was relieved.

"A mirror." Ning's eyes brightened as he looked at the shining stairs.

A mirror capable of exposing flaws even cultivators themselves could not perceive.

That was the correct attitude.

Ning didn't immediately advance; rather, he took this time to catch his breath.

One hundred and fifteen.

Endure.

Accumulate.

Repeat.

For cultivators walking their own paths toward immortality, perhaps this was the most honest trial of all.

Just the question hidden beneath every trial and tribulation.

When your body trembles, when your qi strains, when your pride is gone, when no one can carry you, and when stopping would be perfectly understandable-

Can you still take one more step?
