As water lapped at the edge of the pool, Henry took a deep breath and pulled up his notifications. There were dozens, if not hundreds of kill notifications concerning the birds. Some he’d greatly participated in. Some his contribution had been minimal, which earned him only smaller portions. But there were plenty of cores to go around and so, Henry started burning them for levels, one by one, until he reached the final milestone of C-rank.

Watchful Guardian (C) - Level 97 achieved!

...

Watchful Guardian (C) - Level 100 achieved!

1x Stat points awarded.

1x Strength Stat points awarded.

1x Spirit Stat points awarded.

1x Constitution Stat points awarded.

Before he could assign his new free point, a new prompt showed up and as he pulled it forward, Henry wondered if this was going to be another class change or if he was finally getting a species' evolution once more. He hadn’t gotten one of those at C-rank. Now would be a great time for one.

Congratulations. All your Aspects have been identified as high-tier fused Aspects.

Special evolutions with a merge of the class <Watchful Guardian (C)> are possible. Evolutionary points can be used to improve and empower the offered options as well as their cornerstone skills.

Do you agree to expend all evolutionary points to be earned? Please note that Evolutionary points cannot be carried over past B-rank.

Henry considered that for a second and smiled. It seemed that having spent so much time working on the fusions might have been worth it. But did this mean the class would be gone soon? It hadn’t done that much for him aside from the stat points and an occasional empowerment for certain defensive skills.

Then again, that’s not nothing, Henry thought, reconsidering while he chucked a piece of juicy tuna in his beak. The fight had made him hungry. Turning his attention back to the class, he hummed. Watchful Guardian had basically played the role of an extra Aspect and it had provided plenty of points. He wouldn’t mind keeping it around. A fusion should allow him to safeguard some of its concepts for the future. Also… hadn’t Arisia told him that his class would stick around until the end of B-rank?

This is interesting, Henry mused. It seemed that fusing all of his Aspect this early meant the class could be done away with. The training wheels could be removed. But did he want to give away all of his Evolutionary points?

Well… do I even need them at this point? Henry asked himself. I’m capable of fusing my own skills and Aspects. What else could they do? Help with grafting skills away from a class? I can do that as well. And I’m about to lose the class anyway.

Henry eyed his list of abilities. He could do everything himself, but better evolutions and a potential system-assisted boost seemed plenty worth it, especially considering the points were about to become useless soon.

Ah. What the hell, he thought, chuckling. A better upgrade would be plenty worth it, and it’d be one less resource he’d have to worry about. It was win-win.

Calculating Evolutionary Points…

Triumphed over multiple predators more than 50 levels above your own.

Triumphed over multiple predators more than 100 levels above your own.

Triumphed over multiple B-Rank beings as a C-Rank being.

Succeeded in creating 3 perfect fusion of two Aspect and their cornerstones with minimal System assistance in C-rank

Successfully merged more than 5 skills without System assistance in C-rank

Successfully scried a creature with heavy anti-scrying capabilities

Successfully immobilized an A-rank Windscythe King for 0.679 seconds

Calculating results… 57 Evolutionary Points granted!

Henry blinked, a little surprised. That was a little more than he had expected and now, he was most excited about the options that were about to be offered to him so as soon as they showed up, he pulled them up and got to reading.

Please select one of the following evolutions:

Eidolon Kraken

A psychic phantom cloaked in illusions and deceit. The Eidolon Kraken weaves false truths, bends the weak-willed to its design, and turns the enemy’s own minds into weapons against them.

Fractalkin Kraken

A kaleidoscope of flesh, shadows, and illusions. The Fractalkin Kraken splits into mirrored selves, each one real enough to wound and vanish, and as long as any one of them lives, the kraken cannot know death. A perfect predator for sowing confusion and surviving all of its enemies.

Veilwatch Kraken

A sovereign of foresight and concurrent thoughts. The Veilwatch Kraken commands sentient mind-projections that orbit its body like unseen wardens—each one watching, shielding, and striking with autonomy and precision. With many minds acting as one, the Veilwatch Kraken outthinks, outmaneuvers, and outlasts.

Bloodlord Kraken

A cunning, parasitic evolution that gorges itself on life and magic alike. The Bloodlord Kraken is not a mindless beast—it is a schemer in the dark, charming, warping, and draining all who wander too close.

Red Vow Kraken

A wrathful sentinel forged from betrayal and resolve. The Red Vow Kraken does not forget, does not relent, and does not rest until every injustice has been answered in kind.

Henry read everything once, then twice, then thrice before he finally let himself sway for a moment in the pool.

“Crap. These are all kinda good.”

“A good problem to have,” mused Zerathstra from the other side of the pool, and Henry could only nod. Taking a deep breath, Henry pulled up the mental whiteboard and started picking the options a part. He had to go through them each, though he could thankfully instantly rule out the Red Vow Kraken option. That one was just too angry, and it was nowhere near as good as everything else. And it almost sounded like it could affect him. While Henry could carry grudges like the next person, he wasn’t that intent on making it his whole personality. With that option removed, he had just four to look through.

First, Eidolon, Henry thought. Cool name, and it seems like it’s a bit of its own thing compared to the others. While the others are upgrades to my existent Aspects, this one’s taking my illusions and telepathy a step forward, though the part where it might influence the minds of others rubs me the wrong way. ‘Bending the weak-willed to its design’ is some villain crap, but it certainly sounds powerful.

Henry moved on to the next option, which, if he was honest, was one of his possible choices. Fractalkin sounded like an upgrade to Dancing Shadows. It would make that area of his powerset a little more pwoerful, and it’d give a big boost to his illusions. It would probably make the clones more powerful, which was a weakness Henry still had to deal with. His clones were nearly a rank weaker than he was, and while he could work around that with their numbers, it’d still be good to patch that issue up. Also, the survivability it’d give him was not to be underestimated either. All in all, it was a solid option. It was a strong contender. But it was not at the top of his list. Because at the end of the day, he could eventually empower his clones one way or another. For the survivability, he could manage as well. He had enough clones and he could always slip away from danger to one of them. But the upgrade to his Octominds?

That might be a little more challenging to do by himself. Which was why, at this moment, Veilwatch was at the top of the list. But for now, he skipped it to rule out Bloodlord before swinging back. Because if Fractalkin and Bloodlord didn’t manage to convince him, he was selecting the Veilwatch option.

Bloodlord sounds like a vampire, Henry decided after a moment. And it very much seems like that’s what it’s about. A parasitic evolution that drinks life and magic. It also has that charming keyword thrown in there, which is… interesting.

Henry stared at the definition for a few more moments. This really was the vampire option. Which was intriguing, but honestly, Henry didn’t much care for it. He didn’t have that big of a fascination with the mythical beings.

With that ruled out, Henry finally turned his attention to what he believed was the best and strongest option, and the one that seemed to have incorporated the most out of his Watchful Guardian class. The Veilwatch kraken was clearly themed around Thaumaturgy and his Octominds, and it would upgrade them even further. To the point where they can manifest in real life, with their own forms and not a borrowed clone. That, along with the mental boost, felt like it’d give him a solid foundation for A-rank, and Henry liked that very much. Plus, the possibilities of even more powerful Octominds sounded exciting. Henry had already made his decision, though he gave himself a few seconds to sit with it before he agreed. After all, this might be one of the last selection of evolutions he was offered.

Unless there’s more past A-rank, he thought. But those were worries for another day. For now, Henry took a moment to breath in the cool water, then he made his selection, and his soul immediately trembled. Energies and magics began swirling around him, visible to the naked eye as they immediately eclipsed those of the Pillar itself. His vision began to vibrate and shake as a cocoon slowly formed around him, and as his vision began to dim, Henry had just one last thought.

He wondered if he was about to have a chat with the System.

***

“How’s it going, Henry?”

Well I guess I got my answer, Henry thought as his eyes snapped open, and once again, he was back in the turtle shell, in the first reef he had opened his eyes in. Stretching his awareness inwardly, he sensed nothing, while only a moment ago, he’d been sensing the accumulating magics that were about to make changes to his soul. But no change has been made. In fact… he didn’t even sense his Aspects.

That was interesting.

Henry wasn’t alarmed. This just meant he was just talking to the System with his mind only. Within his own mind?

“You’re sharp,” said the golden octopus facing him, and Henry finally focused on the apparition.

“Thanks. You kinda have to find your own answers when someone isn’t that forthcoming with them,” he said, giving the projection a knowing look. There was no need to waste time on frivolous diplomacy. They needed answers.

The golden octopus rubbed two arms together, seemingly nervous as it used a third arm to wipe at its brow. “Whew. I thought you’d be happier to see me. Does that have anything to do with some… new people you talked to?”

Henry shrugged. “You know everything. And from where I’m standing, their anger toward you seems perfectly understandable. So let’s keep things short. Any chance I can get some answers this time? Specifically, how I ended up in this world? And why is it that those who cross past A-rank just disappear? It’d go a long way if we could get some clarity.”

Henry was a little surprised at the heat that had entered his voice, but he didn’t take it back. The jocking, friendly System had been fun early on, but now that he knew this being was withholding the answer to plenty of important questions, the lightheartedness was rubbing him the wrong way. Was it toying with them? Was it something else? Because right now, it felt belittling. Patronizing. Maybe the System was innocent. Maybe it was forced to hold such knowledge secret, but until it gave some hints—or better yet, answers—it’d be difficult to fix its crumbling reputation. If it even cared about how it was being perceived, that was.

The golden being ahead of him peered back at him, and it seemed to sigh before its form began growing translucent.

Henry cursed inwardly. Zerathstra had said it might leave if the A-rank subject was brought up.

“I understand your frustration. And the frustration of others,” the projection said. “But unfortunately, there isn’t much I can say. There are… rules,” the being said, and for a moment, Henry felt bad. But he shoved that away and stuck to his plan. After all, he’d considered this possibility.

“I was not in this world when its rules were written,” Henry responded, and the System’s disappearance paused as it tilted its head. It stared at him for a moment, and Henry could almost hear the gears turning in the behind the golden gaze of the octopus. “Huh. Maybe…”

Henry didn’t stop. “I’m not from this world. Isn’t that worth a hint? An answer? Work with me. There’s something going on. What is it you want from me? From the people of this world? What is going on?”

It seemed to think for a moment, then it shuddered. “Maybe not. Or maybe I can? Hmm… just a hint?” it mumbled. Then its eyes snapped toward Henry, and it nodded, eyes intense. “Screw it. Here’s a hint. Keep doing what you’re doing. Find the rest of the set. The metal orbs, as you call them. Find that turtle. Both are… connected.”

And then it just froze, eyes wide, before the projection shattered away into motes, and as suddenly as he had come here, Henry suddenly regained the sensation of his own body right as a monstrous amount of magic slammed into his soul.
