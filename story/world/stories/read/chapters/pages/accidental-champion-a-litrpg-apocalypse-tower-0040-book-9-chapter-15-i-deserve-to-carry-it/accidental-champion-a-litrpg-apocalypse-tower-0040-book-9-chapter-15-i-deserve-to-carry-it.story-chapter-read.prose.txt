Palini’s family—his entire family, hundreds of members down several branching generations—had been taken to the Silver River sector. Not to Earth. The planet was still restricted, and many of Palini’s family members were too powerful to set foot on it.

Instead, Xavier found them on the planet Garstar, in the city of Mareketh. The same city where he’d stumbled upon a certain bookstore owned by Elitsa Flian, the woman who’d given him An Inscriber’s Handbook.

Elitsa Flian wasn’t actually a humble bookstore owner, of course. She was none other than the former Empress Larona.

Larona didn’t merely own the bookstore, she also owned a block of apartments a few streets away, along with many other buildings all throughout the city.

It was in one of those apartments where Xavier sat across from Palini’s wife, Erina Damascus. The woman stared at him with a blank expression. The fact he’d appeared alone told her everything she needed to know.

“You couldn’t save him.” Erina clutched a teacup with both hands. “Do you remember what I told Palini, when you both came to me, before you went off to kill The Collector?”

Xavier swallowed. “You told him it wasn’t worth his life. That the right thing is not fighting other people’s battles but staying to protect those closest to you.”

“The second I saw you I worried you’d get my husband killed.”

Xavier didn’t know what to say. Nothing felt enough. “I’m sorry.”

“You’re sorry?” Erina stared at him. “Hundreds of years I shared with that man. Now he’s gone, and that’s all you have to say?”

Xavier’s mouth fell open. He sank back in the armchair.

They were in a small library. Xavier had slipped into the apartment and encompassed only Erina within the time dilation field. He hadn’t known how to give the news to the whole family, he’d barely known how to give it to her.

The silence stretched.

“How did it happen?” Erina asked.

Xavier raised his chin, looked the woman in the eye. “Have you heard of a man named Jhanku?”

The teacup in Erina’s hand shook. She placed it on the table in front of her. “The Jhanku? The A Grade in charge of who knows how many galaxies in the inner sectors?”

“The very same.”

“Do you care how many lives you’ve put at risk with your crusade?”

Xavier’s emotions almost bubbled over. They’d been closer to the surface ever since he’d tapped into the Immortal Soul lens. He took a steady breath. “When Palini returned, after The Collector’s death—after Sovereign Rewke Fouran’s death—did he tell you what I mean to do?”

“Some nonsense about saving the universe,” Erina muttered. “Sounds utterly insane if you ask me.”

“How long have you been here, on this world?”

“Too long. Many decades under these ridiculous time dilation fields, waiting for you to bring back my husband.”

“Then you must have spoken to others around here, seen what they can do, maybe even learnt how to do it yourself.”

The woman looked away. “Power,” she muttered. “I’ll grant you have that. There’s no denying it. And waving around the name Jhanku and still coming back alive…” She shut her eyes, tears dripping down her cheeks. “But do you have the power to bring him back?”

“No.”

The conversation didn’t last much longer than that. Xavier wanted to talk to her more—at the same time he wanted to get the hell out of there as fast as possible. Guilt already hung on him like a heavy weight.

Speaking to Erina had made that weight ten times heavier.

I deserve to carry it.

He’d left her with the body. Asked if there would be a service. Then he’d hesitated before asking if she wished to see Xavier’s last memories of Palini.

Erina seemed to fight with herself before finally nodding.

And so he left a memory orb with her, one he’d watched her hold but not access as he’d teleported out of the apartment and to the front of the bookstore with the creaking sign. He’d leant heavily against the doorframe, taking slow breaths.

The sign took a long time to creak, but even inside his time dilation field he could see it move. This whole planet, like every planet in the Silver River and Ventorin sectors, was under its own permanent time dilation field, one that encompassed the entire world. It wasn’t a field near as powerful as his own, but it was powerful enough.

“Are you going to come in?” said a familiar voice from within the shop.

The voice was slowed considerably, but not as much as it should have been given the time stream differences between his field and that of the city.

They must have a strong one of their own.

Xavier entered the shop. Elitsa Flian, the owner of the bookstore, stood behind the counter, telekinetically stacking books onto shelves. She looked exactly as she had the first time he’d been here.

“Why are you wearing that guise?”

Larona peered at him. “People still remember who I was. It’s quieter living as Elitsa.” She smiled, looking around at the stacks. “Besides, I always liked this shop.” She ran a hand along the spine of a book. “It’s one of my favourite places.”

Xavier walked deeper into the store, coming to stand at the counter. He didn’t glance at the books. “You seem different.”

“You defeated the World Destroyer.” Larona let out a long breath. “I had a vision of it destroying the Silver River sector a thousand years ago. Ever since, my entire life has been dedicated to finding a way to save this sector, to finding you.” She closed her eyes, tilted her head back. “That moment, when everyone in our galaxy was so connected, when we shared our power with you—I’ve never felt such serenity.” She gestured around. “And now, everyone is safe.”

Xavier blinked. “Safe.” There was a chair in front of one of the stacks. He slumped into it. “I’m not so sure that’s true.”

Larona’s face fell. “What have you done now?”

His mind flashed to the moment following the World Destroyer’s defeat, after his arm had exploded, when he’d taunted the most powerful beings in his universe.

Then to Palini’s corpse on the stone floor of a steel-walled room.

“It’s hard being in charge, isn’t it?” Larona ventured. “All the decisions you make, effecting so many people…”

“Someone I care about is dead because of me.”

Larona cocked her head. “You killed them?”

“No.” Xavier scowled. “But I’m responsible.”

Larona tapped a finger on the wooden counter. “You didn’t come here to look at the books and visit an old friend, did you? That family the other version of you asked me to give refuge to…” She nodded. “That’s what this is about. Palini. You weren’t able to save him.”

Xavier stood. “Coming here was a mistake.”

“Not every death is your fault.”

“I’m leaving now.”

“You can’t save everyone, Xavier.”

“I know.”

Larona came around and grabbed him by the shoulders. Those eyes of hers, the eyes of a seer, delved deep into his. “Are you sure about that?”

Xavier almost pushed her away. Almost teleported out of there.

Why was this harder than talking to Palini’s wife?

He remembered how strong Arrack had been. How, when they’d shared a time dilation field, there hadn’t been anything Xavier could do. And a man like Jhanku—he was far stronger, and he would come prepared.

He would have a thousand—a million—A Grade time mages at his disposal.

He might not have been around as long as the Old Man, but he was still billions of years old.

Who the hell was Xavier to taunt people like that?

Now I’ve killed his great-great-grandson. He’s going to have to take me even more seriously. And who knows who else is out there, ready to sign my death warrant?

“I think I’m in over my head.”

Larona threw back her head and laughed. It seemed to last a long time. Finally, she wiped a tear from her cheek. “Are you just starting to realise that?”

Xavier turned away, took a few sharp steps, almost activated a Teleport spell.

“Why did you come here, Xavier, if not to talk about it?”

“How did you handle it?”

“Handle what?”

“The pressure.”

“I didn’t. Not always. Sometimes it pressed down so much I couldn’t move. For days. For weeks.”

She shrugged. “But that’s the price we pay.”

“For being powerful?”

“No, Xavier. For giving a damn. For caring what happens to those beyond ourselves. When I had that vision of the World Destroyer coming to the Silver River sector, consuming everything, I knew it was a long, long way away. I had ample time to become strong enough to leave this sector, to find a safe haven somewhere else, even take some people with me.”

“But you couldn’t take everyone.”

“No. So it wasn’t enough. I may have made some questionable decisions along the way—”

“May have? That deal with The Collector—”

“Allowed me to keep on living,” Larona snapped. “It allowed me to find you.”

Xavier crossed his arms. “So, what do I do?”

“You could always leave. Find a safe haven. Hell, you’re not even limited to this universe anymore, are you? In a few billion years when this universe is truly under threat, you could be long gone. You could keep hopping, live forever. And you could drag along those who matter to you most.”

Xavier shut his eyes, imagining that for a moment. Living on a quiet world, somewhere far away. A house with books on every wall. A desk where he could sit quietly and write. Always falling asleep beside Adranial and waking up next to her. His found family, his friends, not far away—always close, always safe.

Children, a son, a daughter—someone to teach.

Someone to protect.

He ached for that kind of life. A simple, quiet life, when all the fighting was done, and he had the freedom to do all the things he never felt like he had time for.

At the expense of everyone else.

“I can’t do that. God, I want it. But I can’t.”

“Then there’s nothing you can do but keep moving forward until your work is done.”

“There’s no book you can hand me? Something… something that might help.”

“I think you already know everything you need to know to make your next decision, Xavier.”

Xavier sighed.

She was right, of course. He’d known all along what he needed to do.

Keep expanding. Consolidate my power. Protect my domain.

And, perhaps most importantly, he needed to advance again.

It was long past due that he became B Grade.
