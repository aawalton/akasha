[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 63 — 11:15 AM[Location]: The Palace of the Eternal True Sun · Observation Pavilion

Hathaway sat at a mahogany table overlooking the plains, and twelve pages in, she had reduced the most destructive payload in the recorded history of the house to open-source documentation, idiot-proofed for whatever generation came next.

Anybody who opens this file is already having the worst morning of her life, her inner UX designer reasoned, ruling off a clean bulleted list.

Wilhelmina came in without knocking and took the sheets off the table as they dried, and read a plaintext teardown of a Grand Witch's masterwork with her jaw doing something complicated.

"You have removed all the mystique from it, niece. It reads like a municipal plumbing schematic."

"It runs like one." Hathaway tapped the final node without looking up. "Intake, routing, output. All anyone needs to know is which pipe not to touch."

The Matriarch of the Eternal True Sun set the parchment down and smoothed it flat with one heavy palm, and left the hand where it was, and the indulgence went out of her.

"Which brings us to the chassis."

Hathaway rubbed her sternum, where the bone-knitting hexes had finished their work four hours ago and left behind the specific dull throb of a skeleton reassembled by somebody working from memory.

"You held the door and you executed it flawlessly," Wilhelmina said, walking to the arch and putting her back to the caldera. "In a sealed range, with your family standing on the ridge, that is a triumph. On a live field it makes you an immobile turret with exactly one shell in the chamber. You fire, you fall over, and whatever survives on the far side walks up at its leisure and puts a heel through your spine."

"I know the math." Hathaway leaned back and crossed her arms. "I can just pick up a buff that pushes my constitution into legendary tier for as long as it lasts."

"A patch over a foundation. What you require is a permanent elevation of the baseline, and I intend to have it."

"That is the part I can't fix, Aunt Wilhelmina." She spread her hands. "Whatever I ingest, my body takes it apart and burns every fraction of it into mana. An entire sacred volcano went the same way. Which leaves running laps until my legs give out, and I would like that entered in the record as something I am not going to do."

Wilhelmina turned around and aimed that shut-eyed face at her, and let the silence run one beat past comfortable.

"Do you actually believe that."

"I have the telemetry."

"Then let us review your morning." The Matriarch came back to the table and rested her folded hands on it. "Three hours ago you drank a Tier-8 assimilation draught. It went into your stomach, it came apart, and it rewrote a physical quadrant of your cerebral cortex to house a runic matrix." She tilted her head a fraction. "Did your constitution convert that spell into mana?"

Hathaway opened her mouth. Nothing arrived.

It discriminated. Her developer brain pulled the log and ran it backward at speed. It shredded the residue and left the payload.

"I left it on default," Hathaway said, to the middle distance. She was a designer. She had shipped options menus. She had personally written the tooltip explaining that the defaults were only defaults.

"Evidently. Now." Wilhelmina squared the parchment against the edge of the table. "Having established that your biology takes instruction, we raise the durability. Three routes reach Legendary. A class change into one of the melee disciplines, several years of ascetic grinding toward a close-combat feat of that grade, or you may simply elect to stop being a human witch."

"The first two cost time I do not have." Hathaway shut that door before her aunt could produce a ritual knife to hold it open. "The A2 curriculum is a hostage situation, and the third is the sort of decision my girlfriend would expect to be consulted on."

Wilhelmina absorbed the domestic disclosure without a flicker and turned her head toward the corridor.

"Frederica."

The Elder came in with her sabatons striking marble in a rhythm that had been drilled into her before Hathaway was born, the phoenix riding her pauldron and preening. She set a frost-warded strongbox on the table hard enough to rattle the inkwell, and flicked the latch, and cold rolled out over the mahogany.

Six vials sat in the velvet. The liquid inside them was opaque and pearl-white and thick enough to move like something that had opinions about being poured.

"Bone-Forging Elixir," Frederica announced, at a volume calibrated for open ground. "Traded out of the fox witches of Renavelle. One vial per week. You need not concern yourself with residue accumulation, given your constitution, but the efficacy degrades as the baseline rises, and our apothecaries put the floor at the sixth dose. After that the bottle is water."

"And six leaves me where."

"Bone density and tissue resilience at the ceiling of the sub-Legendary threshold." The Elder shut the lid with a clap. "Enough that the next time you hold that door, your ribs remain on the inside of you. Past that you are on your own."

Yenna had talked about her clan once, in passing, and Renavelle had come up in it.

"I'll start tonight," Hathaway said, sliding the box across the wood toward herself. "And I want range time. The cast time has to come down, and the only way it comes down is repetition."

Wilhelmina's smile arrived slowly and did not stay decorative for long.

"See that you do."

[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 63 — 9:20 PM[Location]: Yggdrasil Academy · Dormitory [Golden Bough] · First Floor

The lumen-globes were down to a civil amber and the arcane hearth was putting out a low, steady heat.

Victoria had the end of the sofa with her legs crossed at the ankle and a brick of Ultra-Modernist theory open on her knee, silver hair down, top button undone, the whole picture of an aristocrat at rest inside her own wards. Hathaway came down the stairs barefoot with her hair still damp, wearing a sweater she had removed from Victoria's wardrobe without filing a request, crossed the rug, and folded onto the cushions with the boneless efficiency of a cat arriving at a decision.

She tipped her chin up and waited.

Victoria finished her paragraph. She marked the page with a silver ribbon, closed the book, set it on the side table, and then took Hathaway's jaw in one gloved hand and kissed her, without hurrying any part of it, until the whole morning came off Hathaway's shoulders and went somewhere else.

When they parted Hathaway slid down and put her head in Victoria's lap and lay there with her eyes bright in the firelight.

"You know," she said. "I learned it."

The fingers moving through her damp hair stopped.

"The original." Her voice dropped into something conspiratorial and delighted. "[Ovelia's Evil Lantern Cat Sunflare Burst]. This morning."

The fire cracked. The fingers did not resume.

Hathaway's confidence developed a hairline fracture, and she tilted her head back to read the face above her, and found nothing in it that she could use.

"Are you angry?"

Victoria sighed. It was almost inaudible, and it moved her shoulders perhaps half a centimeter.

"How should I put this." The fingers started moving again, unhurried. "From the day they put that tome in your hands, I have expected this. I misjudged only the timeline."

Heat came up Hathaway's face fast enough to embarrass her twice over.

"You trust me that much?"

"You always manage to create miracles."

Hathaway put both hands over her face and lay there breathing. Her spectator thread reported from somewhere under the wreckage. No tell, no wind-up, straight through the armor. That is not a fair build.

Victoria waited with the serene patience of a woman who had all evening and had watched this happen before.

"Describe it to me."

So Hathaway told her. "It decided to come," she said, to the ceiling. "And it was the quietest thing I have ever been inside."

Victoria listened to all of it without interrupting once, mapping the load onto a grid nobody else could see, and her fingers never broke rhythm.

Hathaway reached up and traced the line of that jaw with two knuckles.

"Have you ever met her?" The question got out ahead of her filter, and she went after it anyway. "Ovelia, I mean. Not a banquet, not standing in a line with your family. Privately."

The hand in her hair went still a second time.

"No," Victoria said, and the aristocratic lacquer was not on the word at all.

Hathaway caught the gloved fingers and laced her own through them, and lay there running everything she had about Ovelia.

"You know," she said. "I'm actually a bit glad about that."

Victoria laughed, low and genuine, the sound of it arriving through her ribs more than the air. "Why?"

Hathaway went looking for it. It took a while, and what she came back with was not the shape she had gone in for.

"Because her existence is a gravity."

Victoria leaned down, and the silver curtain of her hair came forward and closed off the room, and she kissed her again.

[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 70 — 10:00 AM[Location]: High-Altitude Research District · Sector 6 · Lab 606

The overnight telemetry out of Sector Four came up on the secondary terminal at nine minutes past the hour, and the loss column had closed.

Eight weeks of housing subsidy in the bottom quintile and a family formation incentive priced per unit of retained yield had put the night terrors back under the decadal average, taken the cardiac bloom out of the over-fifties, and dropped the suicide rate below the line it had been sitting on before anybody bolted the first coil to a flagstone.

The ambient baseline had come up another two and a half points.

Hathaway stood behind Victoria's chair with both hands on the backrest and looked at two curves climbing in the same direction.

Then the airlock cycled, and Nino came through it with the morning's courier delivery under one arm.

It came out in heavy wax-sealed parchment, and she sorted it onto the central bench in three piles with the unhurried care of a woman laying out ordnance.

The pile on the left was Nino's, and it had architecture. Drop a paradigm-breaking conversion matrix into a hostile ecosystem and every witch on the continent with an opinion and a quill will mail it to you.

The middle pile was Hathaway's. A third of the height, and thick enough to be an editorial statement.

The pile on the right was Victoria's, and it was one envelope.

"Sophistry," Nino said, seating herself and reaching for the letter opener. "Yours are there. Read them."

She slit the first seal on her own stack, scanned two lines, and made a small sound at the back of her throat that Hathaway had never once heard in that laboratory. Then she uncapped a red quill and started annotating in the margin, fast, and her shoulders came down off her ears, and she did not look up again for a long time.

Beside her, Victoria picked up her solitary envelope with two gloved fingers, broke the seal with a thumbnail, read it in roughly the time it takes to pour tea, folded it once, and put it inside her jacket.

"Well?" Hathaway said.

"Accepted. No revisions requested."

Victoria had filed the only paper in the symposium carrying primary laboratory data, and not one reviewer on the continent had been willing to walk out onto that field.

Hathaway pulled her own stool up to the steel and reached for the top of her stack.

The handwriting was jagged enough to scar the reverse of the sheet. The signature at the bottom read Morana Duskhollow, and Hathaway's threat arrays came up hot before she had processed a word, because she had first met that name in her first month at Yggdrasil, in a rebuttal paper written by a woman who had been shot to death over membrane permeability and had come back from her mandatory spa leave with notes.

Ten seconds in, Hathaway's posture began to deteriorate. At thirty she was leaning on the bench with her forearm taking most of her weight.

Duskhollow had gone looking for the seams, and she had found all of them. The correction in the void-exchange section had not been carried forward, and two later derivations were still standing on the version Hathaway had abandoned. The conclusion was drawn an entire order wider than anything the body of the paper had actually demonstrated. And the reference list, assembled with such conspicuous rigor, cited in four places an edition whose central result its own author had abandoned three years later, under fire.

Then, on page six, evaluating the one derivation Hathaway had actually been proud of, Duskhollow wrote that the mathematics was technically adequate.

Hathaway put the sheet down very carefully.

She used it on me. Her developer brain went and sat down somewhere quiet. I have been formally welcomed.

She reached for the next envelope at speed. Crimson wax, a jagged crest, and handwriting that looped across the margins like it owned them. Seraphina Blackwell.

Hathaway braced for a second demolition, and it never came. Blackwell opened by quoting three scholars out of Hathaway's bibliography and spent half a page establishing that all three were frauds. Then, at the top of page two, she noticed that Morana Duskhollow was seated on the same review panel, and pivoted with the mechanical speed of a turret acquiring a better target.

The remaining three and a half pages had nothing to do with nightmare conversion. They concerned Duskhollow's lineage, Duskhollow's reading comprehension, Duskhollow's back catalogue, and the personal and professional insult of being asked to share a symposium with her.

Hathaway watched a twenty-year artillery exchange sail cleanly over her head. I'm the terrain.

The only line addressed to her arrived as a postscript, jammed into the bottom margin at an angle.

Your formatting is the first competent thing this journal has printed in a decade. Delete the acknowledgments. Advertising that you required assistance is a vulgarity, and I will not defend you from what follows if you leave it in.

She had not asked this woman for protection and had acquired it anyway.

Heavy white wax, stamped with a geometric figure caught in the act of coming apart into its components. The Fourth Seat had reviewed the submission personally, and had written four pages.

It would be premature to characterise the present submission as a contribution to the literature, though the Journal has never held that prematurity in that direction is necessarily to be preferred to prematurity in the other, and one would not wish the author to draw the inference that no inference is available to her.

Hathaway read that sentence three times, extracted nothing, and kept going.

The remaining pages proceeded at the same altitude, impeccably courteous, exhaustively qualified, and committing to no position on the mathematics, the bibliography, or the conclusions. Twice she thought she had located a verdict and both times it dissolved into a subordinate clause.

She's canvassing. Her inner UX designer turned the page with the blank calm of the recently concussed. The only thing she actually wanted to say is a pamphlet for her own religion.

One envelope left at the bottom of the pile. No crest, no wax, a single clean fold, and Hathaway knew the hand before she had it open.

On page nineteen you have quoted me. The year, the volume, and the page are all correct.

I was not describing a closed system.

Nobody else on that panel could have caught it, and Irene had gone down forty-one entries until she reached her own. She did not say what to do about it.

The acknowledgments section should be retained.

Hathaway sat with the sheet flat under both palms. Is that a roof over my head, is that a position being built in me, or is that the first line item on the bill for the tea?
