The beast seemed like a good candidate, but that wasn’t enough to risk the entire settlement. Carmack needed to test his theory. He needed to be sure the dragon would be tricked.

And there was only one way to do that: offer the beast one of the juvenile wyrms.

Carefully, he left the tunnel without stirring the beast and collected his party. Hurrying back to the wyrm lair, Carmack devised a plan.

Warlocks were masters of summoning demonic constructs and beasts. The origin of their power was an interesting tale he had been told by Managra. Lydrus, like the great western empire of Tradmia, had long existed on the borderlands of the demonic wastes. It was there that they learned from their hostile neighbors over many centuries. They were the opposite of the Priestesses of Lydria, who were a beacon of light against the demonic powers. Instead, they embraced those powers and learned to use them against the demons.

Carmack’s plan involved the warlocks summoning a small, one-eyed demonic beast that stood on two legs like an ostrich called an urgeth. The warlocks didn't know if the demonic monsters could be found in the Undercavern, but they hoped they would still be seen as prey. They were at least small and not particularly intimidating.

With the demon summoned and obeying its warlock commander, Carmack selected a wyrm toward the nest’s rear. The carrion wyrm youth was isolated and easily surrounded.

Preparing themselves, they hid behind rocks and sent out the urgeth. While carrion wyrms mostly relied on the dead kills of other beasts, they were more than happy to feed on weak prey when they presented themselves.

Stirring with a twitch of its beak, the wyrm gently raised its head, and its eyes began to follow the two-legged demonoid.

Unlike the angry, croaking warning that the other wyrms had given the party when passing through their nest, this wyrm lowered its head like a beast about to stalk its prey.

With a shake of its tail feathers, the wyrm crept forward, keeping a low profile. Before it, blinking its oversized eye and wandering unperturbed, the urgeth turned back toward the party as the warlocks commanded it. For a second, the wyrm paused, raised its head as if on the lookout—spiking fear in the party that they had been spotted—and then continued to stalk.

With bated breath, the party watched as the urgeth slowly lured the wyrm toward them.

Reaching a central spot surrounded by the hiding party, the urgeth then stopped. A short moment later, the wyrm pounced from the shadows, wrapping its claws and beak around the little demonoid.

A crack sounded, and ropes sprang out, coiling around the wyrm and pinning it to the ground. The beast squawked in rage and pulled against the ropes, which tightened further as the guardsmen tugged harder in response. With the use of special knots, the rope tied in on itself as they pulled, tightening the wyrm’s wings and legs and fastening them to its body.

The moment it was secured, two men rushed forward with a gag they stuffed into the beast’s mouth and tied it tightly.

“Good job, guardsmen. I’m impressed by your teamwork and cohesion. Now, if you wouldn’t mind, could you collect the bait?” Carmack instructed, and the guardsmen collectively heaved and lifted it up onto the shoulders of the twelve men under the direction of Gorn.

It was a big beast, but they were fit men, and together, its weight was spread, and the load wasn’t too bad.

“Alright, men? March!”

Carmack had been paying equally close attention to Lieutenant Gorn as he had the mission. He wanted to get a feel for how the man commanded his troops. As impressed as he was by his story, a story alone wasn’t a reason to promote someone. If he failed to impress him during this mission, he intended to choose another for the honor.

The march was orderly, and despite the circumstances, the men were not afraid. He was not trying to rush his men and take unnecessary risks in the hope that they could return home sooner. Instead, he kept the pace even and his voice calm.

Barely a bead of sweat graced the men’s faces. They were clearly calmed by their lieutenant's unwavering command, and worked at a fair pace; they were not exhausting themselves.

He has great command over his men. Furthermore, the real threat out here is unpredictability. We don’t know these lands or what might be out here. Keeping his men fresh ensures that they will have fight in them if needed.

A lesser man might have feared the return of the brood mother or something else and rushed this vital step. Carmack knew that was foolishness. Yes, there was a threat that it could return. But they had to be smart if they wanted to outplay such a creature, and losing their calm would most likely spell their doom.

You impress me, young lieutenant.

Returning to the cave, Carmack ordered the ensnarled wyrm placed by the entrance, not wanting to risk his own men becoming prey to the beast slumbering within.

Channeling mana into the rock beneath the wyrm, Carmack created a small, moving platform with his Earthshaper spell and sent it further into the tunnel. The guardsmen switched from holding the ropes' tightening ends to their loosening ends, and as the ropes stretched taut, they unraveled.

A moment of confusion passed over the juvenile wyrm, and then it wiggled and jumped to its feet. But it was too late. The platform of rock had reached just below the mutated beast’s giant nostrils, and it inhaled the wyrm’s scent.

In an instant, huge, yellow eyes flung open, and the wyrm squawked in terror as it fluttered its flightless wings and turned to run.

But the beast wasn’t about to let an intruder and free meal escape its grasp, and in a single, behemoth act, it leaped atop the wyrm, biting its head off in a single snap.

So, it does have a taste for carrion wyrm. Good to know.

Silently, he waved his men back as he watched the beast devour the wyrm. They were some distance from the beast within, but no unnecessary risks needed to be made.

Adrenaline calmed once the men had created some distance from the tunnel, and panting followed.

“You all did good. This was no easy mission, but this is what it takes to survive down here. Remember that, and remember that the hero knows what he’s talking about,” Lieutenant Gorn reassured his men.

“Alright, let’s move,” Carmack waved a hand in the air and began walking back to the nest.

It seems he has a grasp on morale as well, at least to some extent.

“So now what? We capture them all?” One of the warlocks said.

“No, don’t be foolish. That would take far too long and be a waste of good meat when we’re short on supplies.”

“What? You’re not suggesting we eat those things, are you?”

“Precisely,” Carmack smiled.

“Not a poultry fan, warlock?” Gorn chuckled.

While the wyrms might have picked on weak prey if the opportunity arose, it was all too obvious why they were called carrion wyrms when they returned to the nest. Perhaps juveniles weren’t as intelligent as their elders, but it was hard to imagine how they could have missed the following slaughter.

Methodically, Carmack and his party selected isolated wyrms and put them on the blade or stone spike. On occasion, Carmack even erected walls so that other wyrms wouldn't see the death they bestowed, but it made no difference. The wyrms were too cowardly to attack or even flee from their mother's nest.

Once engaged, they were no pushovers, though. Besides Carmack and Gorn, no other amongst the party would have been able to take down one of the juvenile wyrms alone. And had he left the guardsmen to do it themselves, Carmack had no doubt they would come back with casualties.

A decent chunk of experience these feathered beasts are providing, Carmack thought as he impaled a wyrm with a stone spike. This bit of killing would help push him further, but it was also a reminder that he needed to find stronger monsters to kill when he had time.

As a group, they engaged one of the wyrms, with Carmack taking its aggression while the guardsmen stabbed at its sides with spears. He was happy to allow the guardsmen to take a few kills; well, they needed experience as much as anyone. But when it was almost dead, another wyrm that was barely a couple of meters away attacked.

Even as close as the wyrm had been, they didn’t expect it to attack first. Seeing his men in danger as the beast charged their flanks, Lieutenant Gorn stepped in. First, he flung his spear at the beast, piercing its neck and redirecting its anger toward him. Circling the beast, he never settled his footing, and when the wyrm charged him, he remained light and evasive, dodging several nasty beaked attacks.

Carmack didn’t want to risk any death on this mission, but the way the lieutenant carried himself gave him pause about getting involved.

Bouncing back and out of range as the wyrm’s sharp beak bashed against the rocky ground, Gorn found his openings, flashing forward and stabbing with his sword before hastily circling back out of range again. This dance continued for almost a minute, and Carmack ordered the other guardsmen to keep out of the fight.

Every failed attack and riposte from Gorn left the wyrm with new wounds and less stamina. Before long, it was obvious that the fight would end one way. And the blood-covered beast slumped down, its life draining from it.

“Well fought, beast,” Gorn panted as he put the wyrm out of its misery.

I see how he managed to reach the final bracket of the tournament. He is undoubtedly a skilled fighter. Not only that, but he refrained from using his skills.

Carmack was certain that Gorn was at least Iron rank, and no doubt had at least one or two skills available. He could have finished the fight sooner, but he didn’t need to expend mana and stamina that could come in handy if they were ambushed.

He’s intelligent and measured. Traits a commander needs.

Carmack surveyed the bloodied and feathered carnage of the battle. Surprisingly, he had gained two additional levels from these beasts and put the points straight into Mental.

He also gained another skill point to spend, but that would take a little time to consider. For now, he had a mission to finish and took a quick glance at his status page before continuing.
