The tornado rips at Noir’s wings and he sensibly folds them tightly to his body, giving the winds less to hold onto. It has the consequence of making him fall faster, arrowing towards the water below.

I race the point of the tornado to reach him first, pouring air-magic into my wings to propel me. The only good aspect is that the tornado seems to be acting almost automatically – the winds around me neither aid nor hinder. Either the roc doesn’t know I’m here or doesn’t care about such a small, puny being.

As the tornado suddenly darts for Noir, the talons of a massive bird of prey reaching for its dinner, I’m increasingly convinced the roc knew it would get there first and perhaps cruelly wished only to give me false hope that I could save him. Noir’s descent halts and then reverses with dramatic speed.

For a moment, he is level with me, his terrified thoughts screaming through our Bond, and then he’s above, the tornado sucking him up almost as fast as he had been descending before.

I wrack my brains for something to do. I refuse to lose Noir – not now, not ever.

Use Stealth, Disappear, and Quick Grab, in that order! I yell at Noir down the Bond, trying to drown out his own screaming thoughts with my instructions. At the same time. I pull a coil of spider-silk rope out of my Inventory and throw the end up to Noir. Guided by my own air-magic, it flies up in the air faster even than the alcaoris.

Instructed by my thoughts and impressions, Noir reaches for the end of the almost-invisible rope. The rope jerks tight and I’m yanked upward, spiralling around the funnel like a hooked fish. It’s only by pouring air-magic into my wings that I gain even the slightest degree of control over my movement, but I can’t stop my sickening orbit. I thank my increased Constitution that I only faintly feel the nausea.

I trigger my own Stealth and Fade in rapid succession – Skills I haven’t used in a while. The Tier four might be able to see through them, but we’re drowning men grasping at straws; that’s all we can do.

Grimly, I pull myself towards Noir hand by hand and feel him pulling me up too. I’m still not entirely sure how I’m going to get Noir out of here, but I’ve still got time to figure that out. A little.

I don’t have the leverage to pull Noir out of the tornado. And I don’t want to end up in it myself. But if we can just get close enough for him to enter my spatial tattoo….

I consider different options, dismissing them one after another. Creating a thin layer of glass to disrupt the tornado – too mana and time-intensive. Having him extend his wings and angle them so he’s driven out of the wall of the tornado – too much risk of his wings being shattered instead. Using my own air-magic to take brief control of the tornado – highly unlikely to succeed.

Then I feel like slapping myself in the face. Stupid! Your new Skill!

I have a Skill that is tailor-made for this kind of situation, and it’s only just occurred to me. I blame it on the fact that this is the first time I’ve actually needed it.

Letting the spider-silk line play out until I reach the end again – there’s no point in getting Noir out of the tornado if he’s pulled straight back in as soon as he reappears – I activate Bond Teleport. Focusing on Noir, I feel the Skill drag mana out of me, far more than it took when I was summoning Ninja during our practices. Noir’s Class? Or the desperation of the situation?

It also takes longer, and my heart rises in my mouth as the giant roc looms massive in our vision, its beak wide open in preparation to consume him. Inside its maw is a cavernous, unnatural blackness. It clearly does more than just eat those that enter inside. I feel existential dread seep down my Bond with Noir, something far more powerful than simple fear for his life.

Finally, I feel the jerk as Bond Teleport activates. Even then, the second it takes seems like an eternity as Noir reaches the end of the tornado and enters the danger zone.

The roc’s beak snaps shut.

And then his form blinks into place right next to me and relief seeps through my bones. He’s shaking, his eyes wide and staring with primal fear. Whatever he saw in the roc’s mouth in that fraction of a second he was there, has shaken him deeply.

But we don’t have time to deal with it right now.

Into my tattoo! I urge. He needs no second reminder, reaching out with trembling claws and blinking away again as soon as they come close enough to the spatial tattoo’s field for him to be able to enter.

The roc screeches in fury at losing its meal and the sound is powerful enough that it sends me tumbling helplessly through the air. It turns its head, its wide, silver eyes searching for the morsel that had already been in its mouth when it disappeared.

But if it notices me even through my stealth Skills, I doubt it will pass up the opportunity of a different titbit. I need to get out of here.

The only issue is that my ship is far, far below. It took us almost half a minute to be sucked up this high; it will take me at least that to descend safely again. That offers far too much opportunity for the creature to detect and then seize me. Unfortunately, though I can summon my companions to me, they can’t do the same in reverse. If I get caught in a tornado and can’t get myself out, I’m lost.

Maybe there’s another option, I think my brain ticks over the potential advantages of what’s just happened. The roc’s attention is so focused on the ships below that it may not be too aware of what’s happening above it.

It’s a risk…but no more than hoping I can make it down to one of the ships before the roc notices me. And that would just put us back at square one again – this might offer us another possibility.

Stealth and Fade still very much engaged, I rise, trying to create as little disturbance as possible in the air currents. Being this close to the roc actually makes that a bit easier – there are strong updrafts that are keeping the roc airborne, though they’re not strong enough to keep it up in the air if it were fully corporeal. All I need to do is spread my air-wings widely and add a gentle upward push with my air-magic. Since I’m barely moving my body, Fade works well to keep me hidden. I also pull in my domain close to my body – doing the magical equivalent of sucking in my stomach – in order to not accidentally clash with the roc’s and alert it that way. Fortunately, like its air currents, most of its domain seems to be focused on suppressing those on the ships.

Rising past the roc’s shoulder just brings home just how massive the creature is. From chest to back, it’s probably half the height of my ships – not including the masts, of course. Its head is a good third of its length and its maw could consume my two-room cabin without the walls touching its throat.

And then I’m above the Tier four and staring down at the expanse of its back. I drift sideways so I’m in the quieter air over it rather than continuing to be driven into the sky. From above, I can see how the bird is in a constant flux between flesh and wind. In one moment, a section of its body might be blue or grey feathers, real and soft-looking; the next, that section has disappeared and I can see through to the water below, although the view is blurred almost to opaqueness.

It doesn’t seem to know I’m here, but I don’t drop any of my stealth measures in case it has defences that will notice me as soon as I do. But I do take advantage of the time to find out more information about our enemy.

Looking at the roc with magic sight, I see that places that are translucent are far more dense magically-speaking than places that are corporeal. I recall Kalanthia’s words about Tier 4s – that their health regeneration is so good because their mana and body are aligned. Distinctions between health and mana become even more irrelevant to them than they are to me with Flesh-Shaping. I guess that’s what I’m seeing here – the shift of mana concentration from one place to another.

It’s hard to see how that information helps me. I might be in a relatively protected and even advantageous position here, but a Tier four is so much more of a challenge than I imagined it might be. The information I gain from my very careful, very subtle Inspect doesn’t give me much more hope – its health and mana pools are vast, and though I can’t get any solid information about its Skills without digging more deeply, I can tell that they are few but deep.

I’m riding a tiger, though, and I can’t descend until it’s either heavily distracted or dead.

Staying in position doesn’t require too much concentration – I have to pay more attention to hiding my presence than I do to remain in place. It gives me a moment to reflect.

What has worked on this so far? Disrupting its wind tendrils has been of limited effectiveness. The shields stopped its magic entering, but it still succeeded in blowing gusts of wind across our decks. My own attempts to disrupt it have been equally ineffective – its control over the air is just too strong.

My thoughts are drawn back to the mast and my attempts to protect it. In the end, my efforts were worse than useless – the creature didn’t only destroy the mast but sent it slamming down on my companion and my captain.

I don’t know Tolly’s fate, and Noir isn’t in the right condition to ask. Worry grips my stomach as the captain’s gruff face flashes in my mind. Then I push it away: either he’s alive and getting help from others on the ship, or he’s dead and I can’t do anything to help him anyway. The Bond network is a constant muted rumble of activity – the air might be serene up here, but clearly there’s plenty going on below.

Which is another reason I need to get moving with whatever I’m going to do here – every moment I delay is one where people die.

The thin layer of glass that I grew over the mast stopped the roc temporarily from gripping it. More, it infuriated the Tier four. Since the beginning of the encounter, the Tier four has been playing with us, treating us as both toys and buffet.

But in that moment? It was angry. Which indicates to me that I may have touched on a nerve…a weakness.

I was right in choosing earth-magic to counter its air-magic – I’m sure of it. My mistake was that I was trying to protect something that was far more vulnerable than my method of protection could ever support.

But here….

How can I use its weakness against it? And in a way that’s enough to win the day for us?
