Chapter V.XLVII (5.47) - Blood Punishment

Kizu wasn’t particularly stressed about his upcoming battle with Ulric. He’d already proven his abilities superior to the older student back last semester. He doubted that Ulric had a fraction of the resources he had for growth. Not even money could buy a tutor as skilled as Wakino. Then, of course, there was also the fact that Kizu had access to Ulric’s other subject rankings. The older student was ranked terribly in nearly everything. The five years of education got him out of many of the F classes, but nothing was higher than 400. And his average was down in the 600’s.

No, the only thing Ulric knew how to do was punch stuff. Even then, he lagged far behind Kizu in those rankings, though that was likely a result of getting himself banned from participating after mangling Kizu’s leg. It seemed he’d been allowed back in the combat fights at the start of the semester when Arclight switched the system to conjured opponents, but he was only mid 500’s at the moment.

Finally, there was another reason Kizu knew he’d swept far beyond Ulric’s skill level. Anata. Kizu wouldn’t have been capable of a fraction of his leaps through the ranks without his niece’s blood’s assistance. Some days he felt like an absolute cheater, skipping the mandatory rest periods every other student needed. Then again, his peers had two years before his arrival on campus to work on their magic. It was the one big secret he kept from Ione. He told himself that it wasn’t his secret to share, but rather Anata’s. It was her safety on the line, not his own. If word were to ever get out about the potency of what Anata’s blood could do… well the thought made him shiver in dread.

Between all of that, plus the sheer amount of effort he put into his studies, Kizu felt confident the tournament itself would be unlikely to challenge him until the last few rounds.

Still, he didn’t want to get complacent and slip up. That meant preparation. And one thing the first challenge had taught him was the importance of preparation. That went beyond simply analyzing his next opponent.

Kizu stood on the edge of the volcano that Shinzou Academy was built at the foot of. Not far away was a small portal to Owl’s Respite that he’d set up with Ione to help bring sunlight into his plant nursery. If he’d known this would happen at the end of the semester, he likely would have chosen a different location for the gate.

Down in the volcano’s collapsed crater, a dozen different mages were hard at work, setting up a large wooden stage for the upcoming fights and bleachers for an audience. This would be the location for the tournament battles.

Yesterday, when Kizu had asked Professor Ignis during his final piano lesson for the school year, the professor had explained that every year the headmaster chose a new location on the island to host the battles. This was done for several reasons. One of which had to do with the required safety equipment they’d be wearing. The academy had too many protective measures in place to keep the equipment’s enchantments active.

The reason they didn’t just have one set stadium designed for the end of the year tournaments was because nearly every year, the entire stage was nothing more than rubble by the end of the final fight.

Based on the fact that they seemed to be building the fighting arena out of wood, Kizu was surprised it lasted more than two rounds. Though his spellsense did alert him to some rather odd enchantments placed on each of the stage’s boards.

Kizu sat on the volcanic crater’s lip and watched the mages below while he munched on a sandwich he’d snatched up from the dining hall. He wasn’t idle as he examined how the workers assembled the structure, his off-hand hand constantly at work, manipulating strands of fire. He weaved the elemental magic into intricate strands with minimal thought and effort.

Ever since his leg amputation and subsequent replacement, he found fire remarkably easy to control. Unfortunately, it was one of the few fears that Anata kept close to her, even after all these months on the surface. So, he didn’t get nearly as much practice with the magic as he would have liked. Extremely unfortunate, since he believed his affinity with fire to be the key in passing his peers in the upper reaches of the academy rankings.

He took another bite of the sandwich, savoring the blend of ham and cheese. Cheese had become far more rare since Edgeland’s invasion and this was the first time they’d offered it at the dining hall. He reflected on how odd the ripples of destruction changed daily life.

The fire formed into a band around his forearm, then snaked down his body until it reached the pant leg that concealed his monstrous leg. There, the flames blazed with new life. Bright enough, in fact, that the workers down in the crater turned in his direction and hollered up at him. Kizu couldn’t make out the exact words, but he got the just of it.

Letting the flames splutter out, Kizu stood and raised a hand in surrender to the mages. He focused on Mort’s location and jumped.

The next moment, he stood on Owl’s Respite’s deck, the owl monkey hanging from the mast above him.

Kizu reflexively pulled his hand back to protect his sandwich from the monkey, only to realize his food was missing. He stared down at his empty open palm, only a few crumbs remaining.

“Damn. I seriously forgot to take it with me? That’s so… amateur.”

Kizu sighed and wiped the crumbs off his hand on his pants. He supposed that, while it lacked cheese, he could throw something together in Taroe’s kitchen.

The vibe in the ship felt a bit off as he entered the crew’s chambers. He frowned and stared at the skeletons playing cards at the table. Everything seemed right, but the energy was off, like things had been subtly moved around a bit. It might very well have to do with Ueno familiarizing herself with where she’d be protecting. He didn’t love the idea of her snooping. He eventually decided to ask Aoi about it.

He stepped through the ship’s bookshelf gate, now lined with a growing selection of books, and into his sparsely furnished bedroom in town.

When he walked downstairs, he spotted Anata and Mae hunched over a book in their bedroom. It had a big image of a local species of bat on it.

Opening the kitchen cupboards, Kizu browsed his options. His eyes fell on a box of oats. Not nearly as good as his sandwich, but at least it required minimal effort to prepare. If he couldn’t spectate the building of the tournament stadium, then he might as well get back to practicing spells.

After getting a pot of water boiling, Kizu pulled a small book from his ring and read the history of a local rebel faction in Tross two hundred years ago. The Kemon were half of the attack force and the rebellion’s failure ended with even more restrictions on the people as a punishment, regardless of whether the specific tribes participated.

Kizu considered the odds of whether or not the current rebels would utilize the Kemon in a similar way. He wondered if there had been enough time since the last failure for the oppressed people to forget how harshly they’d been punished.

Pouring the boiled water into a bowl full of dry oats, Kizu stirred and gave the oats a minute to absorb the liquid.

The front door opened. Since Anata and Mae were already in the house, Kizu used the process of elimination to deduce it must be Taroe returning from the academy. Everyone else would have knocked first.

A glance up proved his assumption correct. The disheveled ex-Elite stopped in the doorway.

“Hey, Taroe, I had a question for you.” Kizu raised his spool and blew on a glob of oatmeal. “When the liver detoxifies—”

WHAM!

Kizu never processed what exactly hit him. Even the pain struck at a delay. One moment he was sitting, leaning back in the wooden chair, the next he was airborne. In the moment before slamming into the dining room wall, Kizu saw his spoon right in front of his eyes, the oatmeal still clinging to the wooden utensil.

The wall crumpled around him as he impacted it. It cradled him there, his body stuck in place. Every time he blinked, time seemed to skip forward a few seconds. Kizu struggled for consciousness, but as he fought, the pain in his side flared stronger and stronger in his senses.

Something had struck him in the side. That was obvious. Without even looking, Kizu knew that several of his ribs were broken.

The chair he’d been sitting in had not just toppled over, but splintered into a dozen wooden fragments. One of the table legs had given out as well, having stood between himself and the wall he now found himself stuck in. As a result, the now three-legged table slanted down. His bowl of upturned oatmeal slowly slid down the table’s surface, leaving behind a trail of beige goop like a snailtrail. Every time he winked out of consciousness, the bowl slipped further down the table.

“What….” His voice trailed off. It was hardly more than a wheeze. The pain of that one word caused him to forget his question.

Thankfully, Taroe strode in his direction. The medical professor could heal him back into prime condition again with barely more than a wave of his hand.

But… there was something in his hand. Kizu’s vision was blurry. He thought at first it was a scalpel or another medic’s tool. Then the ex-Elite halted his strides a mere meter away.

A small knife was clutched in the man’s white-knucked grip.

Kizu dimly placed the blade. But his conclusion made no sense. Why would Taroe have Anata’s engraved knife? That should be safely stored away on Owl’s Respite. Why….

Taroe slammed the knife into Kizu’s shoulder. The blade sank to its hilt.

Through watery eyes, Kizu watched the ex-Elite’s lips curl back, revealing his clenched teeth.

Kizu tried to jump. His magic didn’t come. His head rolled to the side and revealed an open antimagic metal handcuff loosely hanging from his shoulder.

Next, he pressed his monster leg against the smashed wall behind him and attempted to press himself forward. Taroe stuck out a hand, caught his shoulder, and shoved him back into place.

Kizu wondered if this was how he died. Pinned to a kitchen wall, without his magic to protect him. There had to be a way out of this.

“Why?” Kizu gasped.

“You. Monster,” Taroe growled. “I know you’re secret now. You’ve been bleeding her. Like a filthy parasite. I’m going to end you for this.”

“No, you won’t,” a new voice said. In the moments before he blacked out, Kizu saw the blurry image of the crone appear behind Taroe. The last thing he felt was fear. And not for himself.
