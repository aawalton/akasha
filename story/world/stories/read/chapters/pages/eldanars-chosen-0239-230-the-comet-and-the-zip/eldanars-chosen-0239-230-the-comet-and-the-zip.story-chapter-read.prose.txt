The freezing winter wind whipped violently across the splintered, ruined edge of the central parapet.

Josh stood at the very precipice of the thirty-foot drop, his dark steel boots resting on the splintered, jagged remnants of the wooden walkway. The deafening, rhythmic pounding of the three Bone Goliaths battering the iron portcullis directly beneath him vibrated straight up through his legs. The entire gatehouse was groaning, shedding dust and loose mortar in a terrifying prelude to a total structural collapse.

Beside him, Bhel took a deep, steadying breath. The dwarven fighter rolled his thick shoulders beneath his overlapping iron plates, his hands gripping the leather-wrapped hafts of his heavy axes with white-knuckled intensity.

They did not share any final words. They had fought together long enough to know exactly what the other was going to do.

"Josh! No!"

Carcan's voice tore through the chaotic din of the ramparts. It was not a commanding, authoritative shout. It was a raw, terrified scream of absolute panic.

Josh squeezed his storm-grey eyes shut for a fraction of a second. The desperate fear in the elven healer's voice felt like a physical blade twisting in his chest. He desperately wanted to turn around, to assure her that he would be fine, but he knew if he looked back into her luminous silver eyes, he would hesitate. And hesitation right now meant the gates would shatter, and everyone inside the city would die.

"Are you insane?" Brett yelled from further down the platform, his voice cracking wildly as he realised exactly what the two heavy fighters were preparing to do.

A murmur of shock rippled through the nearby militiamen. They stopped thrusting their spears, staring in bewildered disbelief at the towering human and the broad dwarf standing on the edge of the abyss.

Josh opened his eyes, locking his gaze on the massive, horned skull of the central Bone Goliath battering the gates below.

He bent his knees, ignoring the sharp feeling of fear building in his chest.

He jumped over the wall, out into the void.

Bhel followed him off the timber wall a moment later.

Gravity instantly took hold. The rushing wind roared in Josh's ears, deafening and absolute. For a terrifying, suspended second, he was simply a very heavy man falling towards an incredibly hard, frozen surface.

Then, Josh unleashed his mana core.

He did not attempt to cushion his fall. He actively embraced the velocity. He triggered Stormforged Adamantite, pouring every single ounce of his latent magical energy into the skill.

Raw, blinding blue lightning erupted from his heavy steel plate armour. The high-voltage static violently interlaced with the dark metal, transforming him instantly. His evolved body, possessing muscles and bones infused with the density of living metal, combined perfectly with the extreme weight of his gear. He became a literal, falling comet of crackling blue electricity and uncompromising kinetic mass.

He aimed the heavy, steel-reinforced crown of his greathelm directly at the top of the central Goliath's skull.

The impact was apocalyptic.

Josh did not just strike the monster. He crashed entirely through it. The blinding blue lightning and his immense, supernatural density shattered the thick, magically fused bone plating of the Goliath's skull like fragile glass. He travelled vertically down through the centre of the creature's massive chest cavity, a terrifying, unstoppable tunnel of destruction.

He physically pulverised the glowing green vortex of dark magic anchoring the titan, bursting out through the monster's fused pelvis in a shower of golden dust and shattered bone shrapnel.

Josh cratered into the freezing mud and the ruined alchemical pitch directly in front of the iron portcullis. The shockwave of his landing blew a ten-foot ring of dark earth outward, shaking the heavy gates on their hinges.

To his immediate right, Bhel’s descent was equally devastating.

The dwarven fighter did not possess a magical aura of lightning, but he possessed extreme, concentrated physical density. Bhel angled his fall, targeting the Goliath currently battering the right side of the gatehouse.

He did not aim for the head. Bhel brought his knees up to his chest, extending both of his heavy iron axes straight down between his boots.

He slammed feet-first directly into the Goliath's massive, exposed collarbone.

The extreme, plunging weight of the dwarf drove the thick iron blades deeply into the creature's fused ribcage. Bhel let out a deafening, battle-crazed roar. He used his falling momentum and his own dense body weight like a horrific zip-line. He dragged the heavy iron axes vertically down the monster's chest, physically cleaving the massive construct in two.

Thick, unnatural bones snapped and popped loudly as Bhel rode his blades down the Goliath's torso, tearing the dark magical tethers apart. He hit the freezing mud in a crouch, ripping his axes free in a massive spray of dark sludge and dissolving bone.

The two towering behemoths collapsed simultaneously.

The central Goliath, hollowed out entirely by Josh's comet strike, crumbled inward upon itself, becoming a massive pile of disjointed, smoking remains. The right-hand Goliath, physically split down the middle by the dwarf, toppled in two separate, massive halves, its heavy oak battering ram rolling harmlessly across the cobblestones.

Up on the timber walls of Ashenfall, the stunned silence lasted for a single, breathless second.

Then, a massive, deafening roar of absolute elation erupted from the defenders. The terrified militia members, the seasoned guardsmen, and the exhausted adventurers threw their weapons into the air, screaming themselves hoarse. They had just witnessed two men leap into the jaws of hell and casually obliterate the greatest threats to their city in a matter of seconds.

Down in the mud, Josh pushed himself up from his smoking crater.

His head was ringing violently, and every single joint in his heavy, metallic body screamed in protest from the extreme kinetic shock of the landing, but his bones were intact. The blue lightning faded from his armour, leaving the dark steel scorched and smelling sharply of ozone.

He raised his dark heater shield, his storm-grey eyes locking onto the final threat.

The third Bone Goliath, standing on the left side of the gatehouse, had stopped swinging its battering ram. The dark, centralised intelligence guiding the horde faltered. The mindless protocol of the siege had been completely disrupted by the sudden, shocking obliteration of its two heavily armoured companions. The massive creature slowly turned its horned skull, staring down at the human and the dwarf standing amidst the ruined remains.

"One left, Bhel!" Josh roared, spitting a mouthful of blood into the mud.

"I’ll take the legs!" Bhel bellowed back, spinning his twin axes in a tight, lethal blur.

They did not wait for the massive monster to regain its bearings. They charged.

The Goliath let out a hollow, rattling shriek. It dropped its focus on the iron portcullis, lifting the massive, iron-banded oak trunk high into the freezing air, and swung it in a brutal, sweeping horizontal arc directly at the two fighters.

"Get behind me!" Josh commanded.

He planted his heavy steel boots deep into the freezing sludge, lowering his stance, and triggered Titan's Anchor.

The shimmering, high-voltage dome of raw blue energy flared to life around him, instantly rooting him to the earth. Bhel slid smoothly into the protective shadow of the barrier just as the massive oak trunk slammed into Josh's shield.

The kinetic force was utterly staggering. The impact sounded like a bomb detonating. The silver runes inlaid along the rim of the Bulwark of the Fallen flared blindingly bright, actively absorbing a percentage of the crushing blow, but the sheer physical weight was still immense.

Josh was ground backward. His boots carved two deep, parallel trenches through the freezing mud. His left shoulder popped ominously, a sharp, blinding spike of agony tearing through his nerves. He tasted fresh, hot blood filling his mouth, but he ground his teeth together and held the line. The magical anchor did not shatter.

The moment the heavy oak trunk bounced off the blue energy dome, Bhel went to work.

The dwarven fighter darted out from behind Josh, exploiting the Goliath's momentary imbalance. He practically threw himself at the monster's massive, tree-trunk-sized skeletal legs.

Bhel swung his heavy iron axes with the frantic, mechanical rhythm of a crazed lumberjack. He chopped brutally at the thick, fused bone of the Goliath's left tibia. Sparks flew as iron struck unnaturally hardened calcium. He hacked away massive, jagged chunks of bone, aiming directly for the magical joints holding the leg together.

The Goliath shrieked again, attempting to lift its massive, iron-plated boot to simply crush the dwarf into the mud.

Josh dropped the Titan's Anchor, surging forward. He triggered Juggernaut's Tread, throwing his entire, steel-clad weight into a devastating shield bash directly against the creature's other kneecap.

The heavy blow disrupted the Goliath's stomp, sending its massive foot crashing harmlessly into the mud beside Bhel.

The monster retaliated blindly, bringing the heavy oak trunk down in a rapid, crushing vertical strike. Josh raised his dark shield, catching the blow at a sharp angle. He deflected the worst of the impact, but the glancing blow still drove him painfully to his knees, his heavy steel backplate groaning under the strain.

"The leg is going!" Bhel roared, avoiding a sweeping backhand from the creature. The dwarf had carved a massive, deep V-shaped notch completely through the thick bone of the tibia.

The Goliath staggered, its massive bulk listing dangerously to the left as the compromised leg began to buckle under the immense weight of its iron plating.

"We need the core!" Josh shouted, wiping a thick smear of mud and blood from his visor as he pushed himself painfully back up from his knees.

The glowing green vortex of dark magic animating the titan was located high up in its massive, fused chest cavity, completely protected by a thick cage of massive, intertwined ribs. They needed to shatter that cage, but the creature was simply too tall for either of them to reach the vital spot from the ground.

Josh looked at the staggering behemoth, and then looked at Bhel.

"Ramp!" Josh bellowed, a desperate, frantic plan forming in his mind.

Bhel did not question the single word. He understood the battlefield geometry instantly.

Josh dropped back down onto his right knee. He raised his shield, bracing the heavy steel rim firmly against his thigh and angling the large, flat surface of the shield upward to create a steep, solid metallic ramp. He locked his left arm, bracing for an immense physical impact.

Bhel turned away from the splintering leg. The dwarf broke into a heavy, thudding sprint directly towards Josh.

He did not slow down. Bhel planted his heavy, iron-plated boot directly onto Josh's braced thigh, using the human's solid stance as a springboard. He took a massive, explosive step up onto the angled steel surface of the heater shield, and launched himself violently into the freezing air.

The sheer, dense weight of the leaping dwarf nearly snapped Josh's arm, driving his knee inches deep into the frozen mud, but the Guardian held the ramp perfectly steady.

Bhel soared through the pale morning sky, flying directly towards the chest of the staggering Goliath.

The monster tried to bring its oak trunk up to swat the flying dwarf out of the air, but its ruined left leg finally gave out. The thick bone snapped with a deafening crack, dropping the Goliath heavily onto its side and completely ruining its defensive swing.

Bhel hit the creature's chest cavity with the force of a fired cannonball.

"Mountain and stone!" Bhel roared.

He drove both of his heavy iron axes straight through the thick, fused ribcage of the monster. The iron blades sheared cleanly through the dark bone, plunging deep into the glowing green vortex burning within.

Bhel twisted his wrists savagely, completely destroying the magical anchor.

The green light flared blindingly bright for a fraction of a second, completely illuminating the swirling fog of the battlefield, before winking out into absolute, dead darkness.

The massive Bone Goliath collapsed backward, hitting the ruined earth with a heavy, final thud. The dark magic holding its hundreds of stolen bones together instantly evaporated, causing the massive titan to completely disintegrate into a chaotic mountain of loose, harmless remains.

Bhel rode the collapsing ribcage down, landing heavily in the mud amidst the shower of golden dust and scattered bones.

He ripped his axes free, breathing heavily, his armour now completely soaked in a mixture of his own blood and black necrotic sludge.

Josh pushed himself up from the mud, walking slowly over to the dwarf. Every single muscle in his body felt as though it had been beaten with hammers. His left arm felt like it was on fire, the heavy dark shield feeling as though it weighed a thousand pounds.

They stood together in the smoking ruins of the three siege engines, thoroughly put through the wringer. They were battered, bleeding, and entirely exhausted, standing alone in a sea of churned mud and shattered bone.

High above them, the timber walls of Ashenfall erupted into a frenzy of joyous, unchecked celebration.

The defenders were screaming their names. The civilian militia banged their crude spears against their shields, and the veteran adventurers roared their approval. Josh looked up through the swirling smoke, catching a brief glimpse of Carcan leaning over the wooden parapet, her hands covering her mouth, her silver eyes shining with overwhelming relief.

Josh let out a long, ragged breath, a small, weary smile touching his lips. They had done it. They had broken the heavy vanguard and saved the gates.

But as the thick clouds of pulverised bone and black smoke began to slowly drift away on the freezing winter wind, the deafening cheers from the wall above began to falter. The joyous shouts trailed off, replaced by a sudden, chilling wave of horrified silence.

Josh's smile vanished. He turned his head, his storm-grey eyes scanning the clearing fog.

The lesser zombies and ghouls had stopped their mindless, shrieking charge towards the walls. The meat grinder had paused. The ocean of rotting flesh was parting once more, pulling back from the immediate vicinity of the main gates.

Stepping silently out of the swirling necrotic fog, their heavy steel boots floating just a fraction of an inch above the freezing mud, came the ancient dead. Long-dead knights clad rusted armour that reflected a chilling, spectral blue aura. Bright, piercing pinpricks of icy blue soul-fire burned from within the dark visors of their heavy helmets. They carried massive greatswords, spears, swords and shield and they moved with a terrifying, unified lethality.

Josh and Bhel were standing thirty feet below the safety of the city walls, their backs pressed against the sealed iron portcullis. They were entirely cut off from the defenders above, completely isolated on the battlefield.

And silently, with perfect military precision, several dozen of the skeletons stepped forward, lowering their weapons and forming a tight, impenetrable ring of dark steel around the two exhausted fighters.
