Nyxalraith locked the mass of machinery over her shoulders. The cloth of cloak and robe was caught tight as one side clicked in place to the concealer, while the other drilled into bark and bone. Almost immediately, a wave of revulsion rolled through her. Her instincts didn’t like her body being covered, and especially didn’t like being altered. But after a moment, it was gone. Impressively, the machine piggy-backed on the concealer’s ability to suppress that inherent distaste. That wouldn’t last indefinitely, but for now it was enough.

The pain was fleeting, and soon she found the metal frame hugged her upper arms perfectly. As expected of Ta’Malis’ handiwork.

She rolled her shoulders. Gears clicked as the mechanism flowed with her movements, never obstructing her. But even so, this was only step one.

Nyxalraith reached around her sides and clamped her fingers around the hard frame already locked to her long spinal spikes. Her fingers clenched in anticipation, yet the alloy remained flawless. As expected, it was very high-quality metal.

She pulled the clamps into her sides, guiding the parted segment around the base of her wings. Gritting her teeth, the series of tubes embedded in each side of the machine cut past her ribs as the metal locked in place. Her breaths staggered for a moment as a new passage was opened in each lung. Two needles on each side drilled through her chest until they found her descending aorta and pulmonary artery. They slid in place easily.

Nyxalraith watched the blood race through the semi-transparent tubes up to her shoulders, then down to the main weapon. The way they fed the machine with branching pipe-works, it was almost like its own vascular system. Powered by her blood, a dense array of the Technocultist’s square rituals shone from circuit and mechanism alike.

…If the weapon proved useful, she was going to redo those ugly things properly.

The almost doubling of her circulatory track was a strain, but her heart handled it effortlessly. That wasn’t to say those were the last of the needles; as she glanced at the centrepieces of Ta’Malis’ artwork, she took in the sharp points rising from one edge of a metal socket. Shaking her head, she pushed on with the second last step of the equipping process. It had taken little more than a dozen seconds to get this far, but every second was an eternity in a battle between high-creeds. She couldn’t take any longer.

Her claws slid through her fingers, and she shoved her chitinous arm down the socket of both weapons. The needles pierced the crook of her elbows, driving into the bundle of nerves along with installing another siphon of blood.

Claws clamped into place, and Nyxalraith could feel the machine hum to life. It flowed with her breaths. Pulsed with each heartbeat. And as she twisted her arms, lifting the massive weights, the weapon spun to an eager clip, feeding off her eagerness.

The weight took her off-guard. Even in the all-encompassing hold of Pink, she could feel the mass of the machine burdening her body. Despite the machinery redirecting some of the weight to her spine and tail, the long barrels demanded immense effort to simply hold them upright. Her tentacles — stronger than her arms as they were — slid along their designated gripping grooves in preparation for the weapon’s actual use. They would be necessary.

Nyxalraith gave herself a once over and slipped from her boots. She would need the extra grip when she left her stomach.

Finally, she straightened her neck and flicked a claw. Ta’Malis had gone through every part of this ‘war machine’, and Nyxalraith knew she wasn’t going to like this part.

A gas mask folded in from the side and latched tight to her face. Tinted glass covered her eyes, but offered no obstruction to her sight. There was a momentary sensation of suffocation before the machine took over breathing for her directly. Through the tubes drilled through her sides. The gas mask? That had another job.

The scent struck her nose all at once. It was sweet, excessively so. She flinched her head back, but couldn’t escape the smell that was designed explicitly to flood every crevice. To dig through every receptor she had and set them alight. She had expected it, but it was still difficult to handle the immediate, brutal hunger that washed through her. Pink trembled, and what little was left of Solan whimpered in on herself.

Nyxalraith’s mouth watered.

No, that was more than an understatement. Her salivary glands were driven past the point of overdrive and she felt it flooding faster than she could get rid of it.

Knowing what was intended, Nyxalraith parted her lips and let the stream of acid spill into the gas mask where it was then pumped along her shoulders and down to the weapons hanging from her arms. It was disgusting. Honestly, what made Ta’Malis think that making her a living saliva fountain was a good idea?

As her nose was continually agitated, the stream grew to a flood. In seconds, the tubing interior was already smoking. Not a surprise, given how deadly her acid was. But it was clear the Technocultist had taken the damage it would cause into account. As saliva rushed through the inside, her blood flowed along a smaller tube, feeding a continuous ritual along the circumference. The tube rapidly grew opaque. Apparently, material was constantly being summoned to reapply the inner lining as it was stripped away.

Nyxalraith had taken too long already. Standing tall with both cannons spinning rapidly at her sides, she ripped open her chest and immediately unleashed a rain of lead.

It took her a moment to spot the kid able to brush people out of existence, and she focused her fire on him. Eight barrels on each side whirled with killer intent. The first explosion thumped up her arms and down her spine. The next dozen raged at her body with overwhelming recoil.

But it was worth it. It was oh, so worth it.

To the Scriptures’ credit, they were ready for her. All sorts of rituals had been put in place over the twenty-odd seconds she’d vanished. Their first priority was immediately to erect a sort of ethereal barrier between her tear in reality and the two Mad kids.

O̅s̫s̋thͤ̂o̲t͛h was still in full effect, and that, she was happy to learn, applied to the shells she fired just as it would her rapier.

The boy burst. A single round struck the Mad, and it completely eviscerated him. First, by the projectile exploding, sending bloody fibres everywhere. Then the expanding cloud of acid burned through anything that might have remained.

Another fifty rounds drilled the vacated space and unleashed mayhem on the grassy lanscape beyond before Nyxalraith could even notice the boy was dead to the first.

The hammer of each shot struck her ears as much as they rippled through her body, and Nyxalraith could only be grateful for the added brace Pink offered. Each massive rotary cannon roared in glee. This was what they were made for; the slaughter of her enemies.

She turned on the girl next, but the high-creed Scriptures had already learned from their mistake and holed up their defences.

Not that it mattered.

A hundred shells slammed into the barrier in a fraction of a second, and seemingly froze where they struck. Nyxalraith continued firing, slamming the ethereal wall with more and more shells until all at once, they erupted. Streaks of light raced in a million directions. Until, of course, they formed a solitary complex structure and shattered the wall with a powerful blast of light.

Against such high evolution harbingers, even artillery projectiles would be pointless. But these shells weren’t the typical fanfare. Excessively steeped in corruption, the steel itself would cause problems for whatever it touched. And that was before it had been forged into such a shape. Like her rapier, each shell had then been imbued with nature-warping processes. A number of which left them far less stable than her own blade.

If it weren’t for Pink, they wouldn’t last a day. Ta’Malis had commented how painful it was to keep the shells stored, given the risk of destabilisation and the likelihood of attracting parasitic Spawn.

On top of all that, each round contained a ritual designed solely to disrupt other rituals. She wasn’t sure how they would normally power them, but with her blood, not many defences would survive.

The next two dozen shells exploded early. They hit nothing but air, but by the silhouettes tossed through the cloud of acid — or the reflection of the Mad girl’s eyes — she could tell it was those strange non-existent animal toys brought into pseudo-reality by Madness.

“Teddies! No!” the girl screamed.

Before her cry had fully ended, three of the strips of paper dangling from her head suddenly ignited. Nyxalraith could see in her eyes the imaginations of a darker mind trying to slip in past the childishness. The rituals embedded in her head were doing better than the chimera Nyxalraith had last come across, but it seemed even the Scriptures best work couldn’t fully contain the danger.

Her heart rate accelerated. She needed to make sure of her aim, and so she abused the faster reaction time her heart provided.

What she hadn’t expected was for the rotary cannons to accelerate with her.

Shells flung at three hundred a second were suddenly flying out each set of eight barrels at triple that. The cannons were smoking, but it wasn’t from overheat. No, Ta’Malis’ cooling system was working just as intended. As the acid tubes had to create material constantly to keep from degrading, the same system was implemented along each barrel to vaporise the heat with acid.

Nyxalraith narrowed her eyes. Sure, the heatsink vaporisation system worked, but she was sure a similar solution could be reached with rituals. Or a number of other thermal regulation techniques. And the cloud of acid sprayed across the landscape; there was no doubt it was deadly, but so were the explosions from the shells themselves.

Making Nyxalraith a living fountain was completely unnecessary.

She would have words with Ta’Malis later, but for now, the severe hunger was making it difficult to look away from the cultists.

The Mad girl’s wall of stuffed animals had not fared well against the onslaught. Nyxalraith knew that the Mad could make their fabrications essentially impenetrable so long as they imagined them so, but apparently the girl hadn’t done that. In her eyes, there was a sea of dead toys.

While that did throw the girl’s sanity off a cliff, the adult mind buried deep inside didn’t have the chance to slip out and fully unleash the horrors it knew before Nyxalraith’s new war machine left a red smear of her across the otherwise green hills. Well, for a moment at least.

In only a few seconds since her reappearance, Nyxalraith had already eliminated the final two Mad. Despite her hangups, the war machine certainly worked well. Though, given how it only took a single shell to kill the both of them, she’d gone through a lot of ammunition wastefully. Not that she needed to be conservative; Ta’Malis had given her a crate full of rounds.

Nyxalraith could only imagine what this would do against an army of cultists.

Finally, the four remaining Scriptures’ attacks reached Nyxalraith. A swarm of ravens with eyes glowing a toxic green. Sharp shimmers in the air, as if light was being warped around something small and quick. Then there was the ritual furthest from her, where a man was slicing the throats of half a dozen goats that hadn’t been there half a minute ago. He sang the final notes of a hymn in a gruff voice that grated at her ears.

Of course, Nyxalraith didn’t let herself be struck. She simply closed her jaw and opened it again on the other side of the field.

She watched as a dirty grey laser exploded from the latter ritual, burning with sweltering heat through the space she’d just been. Realising she’d teleported, he tried to angle the beam to her. Unfortunately for him, he was far too slow.

Shell after shell pierced his defences and struck his body. While each exploded with frightening force, the Scripture was not as easily killed as an unevolved kid.

What a surprise.

Pentagrams flared to life across the tenth evo’s body, causing parts of his body to explode of his own will wherever the shells would come nearest, but it seemed to be incredibly effective at triggering the shells early and redirecting the blast away from himself. What really seemed to cut through his defences was the thick vapour of acid that clung to the air around him. There was no escaping it. So while his reactive rituals somewhat stopped the onslaught of her cannonfire, the melting he couldn’t do anything about.

The man was trying to paint a ritual into the ground with his own blood — and failing, what with the acid eating it all — when his body finally gave out. He melted into a puddle of ooze now that he could no longer fight. That, too, soon vanished.

…Maybe she wouldn’t demand Ta’Malis to remove the saliva siphon. Usually, if she could kill someone with acid, it meant she was already close enough to bite their head off.

For the next short while, Nyxalraith teleported around the hills, unleashing her continuous, high fire-rate onslaught upon the Scriptures. She was so familiar with close combat that causing damage from such distances felt strange. They couldn’t even hit her before she’d moved to another corner of the landscape.

The messenger had died at some point. She didn’t know when, but he must have been struck in the crossfire. That really showed the difference between a high evo harbinger and just a standard cultist; he had popped just as easily as the kids.

After she felled another Scripture from sustained fire, her gaze slipped to the criss-crossing lines of screaming realm. They were still tearing through the hills. Ever so slowly scraping away at the surface as they spread further away.

Despite how pristine this world appeared on the surface, now that some parts of the hills had been carved away — something not even this amazing Technocult weapon had managed — she could see how hollow that visage was. There was nothing below the green hills. The world simply ceased to be; a shell trying to imitate what a real world looked like, but never able to fully replicate it.

Was that what a Null Scar was? Or just this place specifically?

Nyxalraith moved to turn her eyes back on those she was absolutely shredding, but her sight caught on something that shouldn’t be there. An eye. An eye peeked through from the underside of the rift, watching her. Watching everything that was happening on the surface. She gazed back, and the instant she began to see the extent of the being — far beyond this realm alone — it vanished. It ran from her. It had been caught peeking.

She faltered, unsure what exactly she’d just caught watching her. She hadn’t had enough time to read a name. Or much about it, really. Only that it was spread through more than this realm, which immediately indicated divinity.

When her rotary cannons’ constant explosions were replaced with clicking and a vacant sensation of pressure, Nyxalraith faltered again.

She was out of ammo?

Glancing to the crate which had been full only minutes ago, she realised it was empty. Maybe thirty seconds of unstopped fire and she’d gone through all that was provided to her.

Should she have been conserving ammunition and firing only when she had a target to hit?

Of course not! A tool of such bloodshed like this demanded to be used. Next time, Ta’Malis would simply need to give her more crates-full.

Nyxalraith twisted her arms up and triggered the war machine’s secondary mode. Technically, she could still fire shells full of her acid with the in-built material summoning ritual and forge, but they would be basic bullets. Useless against such high evolution cultists.

Besides, she still had to test out the other half of the war machine, and there was two Scripture harbingers just standing there, offering to be test subjects.

With a flex of her arms, the cannon barrels-turned-blades revved, spiralling around their new centre-point extending from her arms. A burst of fire almost knocked her over as she tested the other system, where blood collected by her sides. Nyxalraith wasn’t sure how necessary a close range mode was, considering her strengths, but she was absolutely going to enjoy this.

And finally sate that gnawing hunger.
