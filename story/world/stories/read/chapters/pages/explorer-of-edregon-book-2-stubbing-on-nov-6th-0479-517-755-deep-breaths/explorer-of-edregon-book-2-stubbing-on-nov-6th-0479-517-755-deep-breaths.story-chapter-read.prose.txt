Vin opened his eyes, blinking as he realized he was staring up at the ceiling. A quick look around revealed he was back in the room he’d been given in the town hall’s basement, the black void of the Gods nowhere to be seen. Each of his friends were in the middle of rousing as well, all of them looking to be in varying stages of confusion or disbelief.

All but one.

“I’m a Slayer!” Alka shouted, laughing so hard Vin feared she’d topple off the table she sat on. The golem held her hands up in front of her face, her fingers shaking as her green eyes flared brighter than ever before. “We did it! I’m back in the System!”

“Welcome back,” Vin smiled, parroting Benevolence as he got to his feet and helped Lumel up. “Back to level 1?”

“No, that’s the best part! I’m level 20 again, the same level I was back when I died! Though my status isn’t the same, and I've got a serious number of skill, attribute, and passive points to assign,” Alka said, her eyes narrowing as she no doubt looked over her interface. “Huh. The only thing here is my level 10 Capstone, but it’s different from before. ‘Divine Connection’. Guess they needed to borrow it in order to actually get me back in the System.”

“How are you guys not freaking out?!” Scule asked. The petian was sitting on the edge of the table with his knees pulled up tight against his chest. Reginald curled up against him and pulled him into a hug with his tail, but Scule barely even seemed to notice. “The Gods are nothing more than regular people! The ones in charge of everything aren’t any more wise or special than any of us! They’re just old!”

“Scule, we’d already pretty much established that from what Vin learned in the floating library,” Shia said. Rubbing her head, she raised an eyebrow at him. “Why are you panicking about this now?”

“I don’t know, I guess I just didn’t really think about it!” Scule snapped. “Or thought it was just a fragment-specific thing. Or that you all were just wrong and I didn’t want to say anything!”

“If it makes you feel any better, I’m sure upon ascending they learn a lot more than any of us,” Lumel said. Smiling at Vin, she squeezed his hand. “It sounds like a lot goes on between levels 60 and 100. Though to think one can become a God as low as level 80 if they’re talented enough…”

“Or refuse to become one,” Shia grinned. “I can’t wait to speak with my master again. I have so many questions for him. It sounds like Godhood might not be the right choice for me either.”

“To hell with the Gods, I’m in the System again!” Alka shouted, swinging her legs off the table and all but throwing herself to her feet. “I’m so excited I don’t even know what to do!”

“Maybe take a minute to calm down before you go dumping all sixty of your bonus attribute points into strength,” Vin chuckled, peering down at his chest. “Ovord, you there? The Gods didn’t smite you while we weren’t looking, did they?”

“They did not. I apologize for not managing to warn you about my connection with them ahead of time, but similar to the restrictions of discussions between prestige classes, I was bound. Even now, most of the restrictions are still in place. We can loosely discuss what you were told of, but we cannot go into detail.”

“That’s fine. I’m just glad you’re alright,” Vin said, surprised to find he actually meant it. One glance at the beaming excitement and happiness somehow radiating from Alka’s blank facade was all Vin needed to feel warmth spreading through his chest, and he smiled. “Thank you, Ovord. You really came through for us. We quite literally couldn’t have done it without you.”

“I told you I would. I wish for a new body more than anything else, and I will continue to serve you as you work toward that goal. Also, I feel I should inform you. Benevolence triggered your divine boon in order to save you from the chaotic whims of the mana. And you received these.”

Grand arcane discovery! 25,000 exp gained.

Grand arcane discovery! 25,000 exp gained.

Vin’s eyes bulged as he took in the two monstrous notifications side by side. He wasn’t exactly surprised to see either one of them after witnessing the intricacies of Alka’s soul and even just a fraction of the inner workings of the new System, but seeing so much experience awarded like that was mind boggling.

Though almost as insane was the fact that he’d managed to get to the point where a cool fifty thousand experience wasn’t even enough to automatically get him to the next level any more.

“Guys…” Shia said, shaking slightly as she stared at something only she could see. “I just got a ridiculous amount of experience for what we all did. I guess my Druid of Bonds class highly approved of the bonds we strengthened and used in order to pull that off.”

“I did as well,” Lumel muttered, furrowing her brow slightly while reading over her own notifications. “It seems that counted as quite the discovery.”

“I think it might have been something else,” Scule said. His nervous rocking slowly came to a halt as he joined the others in staring at something only he could see. “I’m a Rogue of Poisons. I’m pretty certain nothing we just did had anything to do with being a Rogue or poisons, and I also got a ton of experience.”

“The Gods are awarding each of you for your efforts,” Ovord said, and Vin relayed his words for him.

“Ovord says that technically, we snuck into the very heart of the Gods’ most prized creation and stole a sliver of their power for Alka. That’s probably why they were able to give you experience, Scule. I think we were sort of… off the grid for a few minutes there.”

“I’m not complaining… I’ve just never seen so much in one place,” Scule said, trembling slightly as he waved his notifications away. “Though I suppose stealing from the Gods would probably do that… Guess I don’t need to worry about that level 60 bottleneck after all!”

While Scule laughed hysterically a little bit, Vin let Reginald comfort him while he moved in front of Alka. Now that he knew what he was looking for, he leaned on Beyond the Veil and let his eyes unfocus a tad. It was hazy and not nearly as clear as it had been a few minutes ago, but he smiled when the thin line of mana leading out of Alka’s core materialized in his vision once more. Knowing how that little line of mana was connecting Alka to all of them was comforting in a way.

“So. Is it everything you thought it would be?”

“That remains to be seen,” Alka laughed, her voice lighter and more excited than he’d ever heard it before. Holding out a hand, she allowed Epli to creep back up her arm to her helmet, where the schlime vanished once more. “Like you said, I’m trying to hold off on spending all my points at once, though the temptation is strong. I can’t really complain, seeing as I’d assumed I’d start back at level 1, but even at level 20, I’ve got a lot of work to do in order to catch up to you guys.”

“With how frequently you fight and kill monsters?” Vin snorted, shaking his head. “Yeah, I don’t think that’s going to be an issue.”

Alka punched him in the arm and started to say something, but all of them turned when the door leading into their room was thrown open. Without a word, Spur rushed in with a panicked look on his face, his eyes narrowing as they locked on Vin’s own.

“What did you do?” Spur demanded, stomping over to him and thrusting a finger into Vin’s chest. “I thought you were supposed to be working on your banner for the festival! I am way too busy right now to deal with this kind of crap!”

“What do you mean?” Vin asked, for once at a loss as to what Spur might be annoyed with him about.

“That blinding flash of light!” Spur said, throwing up his hands in exasperation. “Everyone in Terra was blinded for a second, with no exceptions! It didn’t even matter if they were inside or outside! By now, whenever something ridiculous and unexpected happens, I know who tends to be responsible!”

“Hey, that’s a bit presumptuous!” Scule said, crossing his arms as he glared up at the Commander. “Anyone could have accessed the inner core of the new System and reconnected their semi-dead golem friend to the nucleus!”

Spur just stared at Scule, and Vin sighed. At the very least, it seemed Spur’s surprise appearance had been enough to snap Scule out of his mild panic. “Yeah, that’s pretty much what happened. Alka’s reconnected to the System now, and no, it doesn’t sound like it’s something we can do again. Our actions resulted in four Gods coming to have a little chat with us, so we already know it was sort of a big deal.”

“Do you think everyone saw that light?” Shia asked, tapping her chin curiously as she grinned at the thought. “Imagine if we just blinded everyone on all of Edregon for a moment.”

“I hope not. That might be a death sentence for warriors in the middle of fighting,” Lumel pointed out with a nervous frown.

“For what it is worth, I doubt that is the case. I would assume only those souls closest to your vassal’s physical location would have been affected. Presumably those within this fragment. At worst the surrounding ones as well.”

For his part, Spur managed to shake off his utter disbelief at their actions in only a few seconds. Doing his best to glare between all of them equally, he held up a hand to stop them.

“Look. Terra’s big festival is in less than two days, and I am so busy I’ve started to contemplate finding a body double and running away to take a vacation in another fragment. If you lot could hold off on messing with powers beyond mortal comprehension for just two days, I would appreciate it. Pick up some goddamn crayons and work on your banner!”

Without waiting for a response, Spur turned and left the room, slamming the door shut behind him. There was a soft but noticeable click, and Scule’s jaw dropped.

“Did… did he just try to lock us in here? Us? The twice-prestiged Rogue, the mage capable of teleportation, and Vin, who could probably figure out a way to seduce the door and ask it nicely to open with all his magical crap? Is he serious?”

“I think Spur is just realizing that planning a festival is a bigger undertaking than he first thought,” Vin said. He still recalled how the last time he’d used Dream Walk to talk to Spur the man’s dream had been him all but literally drowning in paperwork. “That said, he’s not wrong. The festival is coming up fast, and I did promise to make this giant banner of the first three rings of Edregon. Not to mention the battle for wave eight is probably going to be less than a week after that. No more adventuring for us until after that’s done.”

“Fine by me. I’m going to need some time to get used to my attributes again,” Alka said, her fingers clenching and opening repeatedly as if already working on just that. “Though you better believe I’ll be joining up with you some more after that. With all the craziness you get up to, I’ll catch up in level in no time.”

“I’m going to check in on Tabetha, but after that, I’d love to get a little more work in on your bonded artifact if you’d be willing to help, Lumel,” Shia said, causing the pulmon to jump slightly as if she’d forgotten.

“Oh, yes! That would be wonderful! Thank you!”

“I’ve actually got a fun idea for the festival I think I want to work on getting prepared,” Scule said, giving them a mildly evil grin as he rubbed his hands together. “I think it will be a big hit.”

“Sounds like we’ve all got plenty to do between now and the festival,” Vin smiled. Rolling out his large banner once more, he picked up a pencil. “I have to admit, after antagonizing the Gods yet again, I’m kind of looking forward to a bit of relax time for a change!”
