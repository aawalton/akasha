Luke’s mind raced. Countless possibilities flashed through his thoughts, only to be dismissed just as quickly. He had long assumed that Lhair would take advantage of their conflict with the Qin armies—especially in the south, where their borders pressed close.

But the news that Selis had entered the fray, despite having no prior involvement, caught him off guard. It wasn’t that he thought them incapable of exploiting chaos—only that he hadn’t expected it.

I should have seen something like this coming, Luke thought, biting his lip. Lhair’s absence from this front only reinforces my theory—the traitor must be supporting the second prince.

He chastised himself for his lack of foresight, but he didn’t dwell on it. The current situation demanded focus. They had just over ten thousand men—sleep-deprived and surviving on meager rations.

If this continued, it wouldn’t matter who was leading the siege. They would crumble.

His thoughts drifted to the system quest—the one that grew more impossible the longer he pondered it. How am I supposed to organize a retreat without sacrificing more men?

“What’s the status of our food and supplies?” Tristan asked.

The older man’s voice pulled Luke from his spiraling thoughts. It was a natural question—one any experienced officer would ask first.

The commander looked utterly drained, his face heavy with weariness. Still, he answered. “With our current rations, we might last another week. No more.”

Luke suppressed a curse. As a general, he couldn’t afford to show frustration—not when the morale of the troops depended on him.

Even with a royal decree, it was difficult to inspire confidence. Who would trust a boy barely old enough to shave—especially when their lives were at stake?

“Tell me about the Selis army’s actions,” Luke said, his tone even. “Have they made contact? Sent any messengers? I can’t imagine they want to waste manpower if their goal is simply to take Xiandong.”

Commander Bridges shook his head. “No formal attacks,” he said. “But every so often, they fire their trebuchets or loose a volley of arrows over the wall. Just enough to keep us awake and on edge.”

As he spoke, the ground trembled—just slightly, but enough to jolt both Luke and Tristan.

A glance at the three exhausted men across from them confirmed it: this was the very harassment the commander had just described.

“General…” Bridges murmured, his once-sharp brown eyes dulled by fatigue. “Do you truly believe you can get us out of this hell alive?”

The other two soldiers stirred, confused by the question—but they were wise enough not to interrupt their superiors.

Luke met the man’s gaze, and for a moment, his resolve wavered. The fault for their situation didn’t lie solely with the traitor embedded in the Marxx Kingdom.

The king himself shared the blame—for his negligence, his silence, and his failure to act.

Not only had the king allowed Lhair to infiltrate their borders, but even after receiving intelligence of their movements, he hadn’t ordered a retreat. If he had, perhaps their force of thirty thousand wouldn’t have been reduced to this.

Luke didn’t expect the man to be omnipotent—but he had hoped for competence. Yet, remembering the two advisers who always flanked the throne—one, a gluttonous, self-serving Left Minister; the other, a mysterious and dangerous Right Minister—perhaps he shouldn’t have been surprised.

“I will get us out of this place,” Luke declared.

The sudden resolve in his voice startled the three officers.

Tristan, however, allowed a grin to tug at the corner of his lips.

“But first, we need to rest,” Luke added, rolling his shoulders and stretching his sore limbs. Despite the dire circumstances, both he and Tristan were running on fumes. Exhausted minds and bodies wouldn’t serve anyone—not the soldiers, and certainly not each other.

“Ah… sure,” Commander Bridges said, nodding before motioning for them to follow.

They stepped out of the dimly lit room, leaving the other two officers behind, and made their way down the corridor. Minutes later, they arrived at a modest chamber. Though devoid of decor, it reminded Luke of his old room at the Royal Academy. Two beds, clean enough and passably comfortable.

“Wait—I never asked, General… how did you two get into the fortress?” the commander asked, scratching his head. “Was there a secret entrance we weren’t told about?”

“We scaled the southern mountain from the valley,” Luke replied, voice heavy with fatigue.

“HUH?!”

“Goodnight, Commander. We’ll discuss the plan in the morning,” Luke said, shutting the door without elaboration. He had no interest in recounting their death-defying climb—not tonight.

Turning to Tristan, he patted him on the shoulder. “Get some rest. You’ve earned it, my friend.”

Tristan gave him a tired nod before collapsing onto the nearest bed. But within seconds, a loud grumble echoed from his stomach.

Luke smirked and dug into his knapsack, pulling out a few strips of dried meat. “It’s not much, but it should ease the hunger until morning,” he said, tossing the jerky onto the bed.

Tristan’s hand snapped forward like lightning, snatching up the meat with a sudden burst of energy.

Luke chuckled softly, biting into his own piece before lowering himself onto the second bed.

As he chewed, his mind drifted. He hadn’t expected the army to be in such poor condition, nor had he predicted Selis would be laying siege.

He didn’t know much about the southern nation—only that they were younger than the Marxx Kingdom and located to the southeast. From what he recalled, this was the first time the two had come to blows.

I hope I can use this to my advantage, he thought, cautiously optimistic.

There was no blood feud between their kingdoms—not yet. And Selis had kept their casualties minimal.

If I play this right… there might be a way out of this.

It would all come down to the enemy general.

Before long, Luke’s thoughts quieted, and the weight of exhaustion overtook him. Spending the better part of a day clinging to Tristan’s back as the older man scaled the mountain had completely drained his strength.

For the first time in what felt like ages, he was granted a dreamless sleep.

Unfortunately, dawn shattered that peace.

A thunderous boom rattled the building, shaking its very foundation—the unmistakable signal of the next wave of attacks from the besieging Selis army.

Both Luke and Tristan jolted awake, robbed of any chance to rest further.

“I can see why our forces are so worn down,” Tristan muttered, rubbing the sleep from his eyes. “If this keeps up, forget lasting a week—I won’t make it through the day...”

Luke didn’t respond immediately. He was still pulling himself together after the abrupt awakening.

He could feel the irritation radiating off his friend, but it was understandable. Even with system-enhanced endurance, rest was still essential to recovery. Strength, after all, came at a cost.

“Let’s go see the commander. I want to discuss something with him,” Luke said, slinging his knapsack over his shoulder. He opened it briefly to check its contents, then stepped into the corridor with Tristan following closely behind.

They retraced their steps through the winding halls and soon arrived at the audience chamber, where the three officers still sat around the table, bleary-eyed and silent.

“G-General! Good morning.” They sprang to attention, cupping their fists in unison.Luke gave a curt nod and gestured for them to sit.

“I intend to negotiate a surrender with the Selis general,” he said without preamble, dropping the bombshell.

A heavy silence fell over the room. The three officers stared at him, slack-jawed. Tristan, though unsurprised, didn’t seem particularly pleased either.

“General, do you believe the enemy would accept such terms when they already hold the advantage?” the commander asked, choosing his words carefully.

Since others were present, Tristan addressed Luke formally, using his rank. It was a small but meaningful gesture—one that showed his understanding of both protocol and psychology.

As a fellow scholar, Luke appreciated the subtlety.

“True, we’re at a disadvantage. Our forces are on their last legs,” Luke acknowledged with a nod. “But do you truly believe the Qin Empire would sit idle after losing such a strategic stronghold?”

His words hung in the air like a thick fog. The officers exchanged uncertain glances, surprised by the logic.

Luke had grown used to such reactions.

“But... the royal decree,” Commander Bridges said softly, his brow furrowing.

“The decree instructed us to raze any fortress we hold within Qin territory and retreat to the capital,” Luke said confidently. “But if we are no longer in possession of Xiandong, how could we possibly raze it?”

His argument neatly sidestepped the burden of the king’s orders, threading a legal loophole that allowed them a sliver of dignity and survival.

Even so, the commander still looked unconvinced.

“To surrender… wouldn’t that make us prisoners of war?” he asked, voice low. “I’d rather die than become a slave for the rest of my life.”

The other two officers nodded in agreement, their movements stiff and solemn—like  chickens pecking at grain.

But it was Tristan who responded next, a brilliant smile spreading across his face.

“We’ll offer the enemy general Xiandong in exchange for our freedom. At worst, he might demand we leave behind our provisions and weapons before letting us go.”

“Bingo,” Luke said, mimicking a finger gun toward the three officers.

“Bingo? What’s that?” asked the bearded officer, raising a skeptical brow.

Luke cleared his throat and brushed past it. “Never mind. The Deputy General will act as our envoy and negotiate the terms of surrender on my behalf. We’ll distribute the remaining rations to the men—if all goes well, we’ll be walking free by tomorrow.”

A flicker of hope brightened the officers’ weary expressions. They exchanged glances, the fatigue in their eyes slowly giving way to something else—excitement.

Luke ignored their reactions and turned to Tristan—the man he had just volunteered for a potentially deadly task. Guilt stirred in his chest. He hadn’t even asked for his friend’s input.

Yet Tristan looked composed, still smiling.

“Leave it to me,” he said, pointing his own finger gun at Luke. “Bingo.”

Luke blinked, incredulous.

Seeing the older man so pleased with himself, he didn’t have the heart to tell him he’d used the phrase entirely wrong.

Instead, he stepped forward and gave Tristan’s shoulder a firm pat. “Let’s get you something to eat before you head out.”

Turning to the officers, Luke gave his next set of commands. “Gather our men. We’ll cook whatever food remains in preparation for the evacuation. Keep only what we can carry by hand—no wagons or supply carriages.”

“Yes, General!” came the unified reply.

But just before they left, Commander Bridges raised a concern. “Wait, General… the men haven’t been introduced to you yet.”

Luke waved him off. “There’s no time. We can do the formalities once we’re as far from this damned place as possible.”

That seemed to settle it. The commander nodded and left with the other officers to begin preparations.

For a few moments, only silence remained between Luke and Tristan. Then Luke spoke, his voice quieter.

“How confident are you in securing our safety?”

Tristan let out a chuckle and shook his head. “You already said it yourself. The Selis general is likely wary of Qin retaliation. If we offer the fortress without a drop of blood spilled, I doubt he’ll turn us down.”

“Mmm. Let’s hope he’s a reasonable man,” Luke said. He glanced toward the hallway and added, “Now, let’s eat. Traveling unarmed won’t be easy, and we’ll need every bit of strength for what comes next.”
