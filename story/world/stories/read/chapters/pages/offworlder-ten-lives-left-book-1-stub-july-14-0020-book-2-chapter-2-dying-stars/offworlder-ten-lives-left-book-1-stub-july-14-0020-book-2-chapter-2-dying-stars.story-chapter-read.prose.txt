Wavehaven was a city of ghosts.

The morning sun cast long shadows from the husks of collapsed buildings, illuminating streets still littered with rubble and debris. People moved through the haze of dust, their faces grim as they worked to clear the wreckage, to piece their lives back together. The silence was the worst part—not the peaceful quiet of our first few days, but a heavy, grief-stricken stillness, punctuated by the scrape of shovels and the distant murmur of exhausted voices.

Rounding a corner, I skidded to a halt. A little girl, no older than five, sat alone amidst the debris of what was once her home, her small body shaking with silent sobs.

An ache bloomed in my chest, sharp and immediate. I saw my sister’s face in hers, saw the same helpless terror I’d seen in her eyes in those final days. The world narrowed, the urgency of Rosamae’s message momentarily forgotten, eclipsed by a wave of protective instinct so fierce it stole my breath.

I approached her slowly, kneeling a few feet away. "Hey," I said softly. "Are you okay? Where's your mom?"

She looked up, her tear-streaked face smudged with dirt. She just shook her head, fresh tears welling.

Cronia? I asked inwardly. Can you find her? It’d been days since I addressed the goddess. She was out flying around, keeping watch over the island, staying alert for any threats.

Cronia?

A gentle pulse of agreement was my only answer. I felt her awareness spread out from me, a subtle net cast across the nearby streets. After a moment, a flicker of recognition. Two streets over. Near the fountain. She's looking for her.

I held out my hand to the little girl. "Come on," I said, my voice softer than I thought possible. "Let's go find her together."

Hesitantly, she placed her small, trembling hand in mine. As I helped her up, she clung to me, burying her face against my leg. Unable to walk, I scooped her up and hurried forward.

We found her mother just as Cronia had said, frantic, calling her daughter's name. When the woman saw us, she cried out, ran over, and swept her daughter into a crushing embrace, showering her with kisses.

When she finally looked at me, her eyes widened with awe. "It's you," she whispered, her hand flying to her mouth. "The one from the spire." She bowed her head deeply. “Thank you. The gods bless you!”

My cheeks burned. I bowed my head in return, uncomfortable under her reverent gaze, and hurried away before she could say more. As I continued, I felt other eyes on me. People stopped their work to stare, whispering to one another, some even bowing their heads as I passed. My skin prickled, my shoulders tensing.

They saw the fight, Cronia explained gently from within my mind. They saw the power you wielded, the light that consumed the darkness. They don't see a man, Zale. They see a prophet. A warrior touched by the gods.

I winced, the title settling on me like an ill-fitting, impossibly heavy cloak.

A familiar, joyous roar cut through the somber quiet. I looked to see Gripjaw, my massive crustacean friend, lumbering toward me from a barricade he’d clearly been guarding. He shot forward, moving with a speed that defied his size, his mandibles clicking with excitement.

Laughter burst from my lungs, a sound of pure, unadulterated relief. I met him halfway, burying my face in the rough, familiar texture of his carapace as he nudged me affectionately. This was my first friend in this world, my steadfast, loyal companion. The weight on my shoulders lifted, if only for a moment.

"Been holding down the fort, buddy?" I murmured, patting his shell. He responded with a series of happy clicks. I swung myself onto his back, the worn spot on his shell a familiar seat. "Take me to the hideout. Fast."

He needed no further encouragement, thundering through the streets toward the abandoned warehouse that now served as our base of operations.

When we arrived, I slid from his back and hurried down the cellar steps, my heart hammering in anticipation. The chamber was lit by glowing fungi, casting an eerie blue light.

Rosamae stood near the central table, her arms wrapped around herself. The world narrowed to the worry in her emerald eyes—a deep, gnawing anxiety that I knew wasn't just about the sword, but about the brother she was so desperate to get back to.

Her eyes met mine. “Zale!”

I crossed the room in three strides and pulled her into my arms. She melted against me, her hands clutching the back of my vest. For a moment, we just stood there, breathing each other in, a silent island of solace in a sea of fear. The frantic energy in my chest settled, grounded by her presence.

"I'm here," I whispered into her hair. "What is it? What's wrong?"

She pulled back, though she kept her hands on my arms. "It's the sword," she said, her voice strained. She led me to the far side of the room, where the Blade of Collapsing Stars lay on a stone slab, draped in spiritually warded cloth. "Look."

I leaned closer. The weapon still radiated a palpable wrongness, but something had changed. The intricate, glowing spirals that had once writhed across its night-sky surface, the marks of Oth’Zaelin's power… they were gone. Faded into nothing, leaving the blade looking like a simple, albeit masterfully crafted, piece of dark metal.

“The corruption…it's fading?” I murmured.

No, Cronia’s voice was sharp, a spike of alarm in my mind. Not fading. Receding. It's like…like it’s being called home.

"What do you mean?" I asked aloud, drawing a confused look from Rosamae.

Silver-white light flashed on my right, blinding me momentarily. When my eyes focused, I saw Cronia materalizing, the size of a full grown woman. Her long white hair danced as if with a life of its own, her eyes like two large pearls. “There’s something else,” she said. “Someone. A presence connected to it. Distant, but…immense.”

“It’s still calling out?” Rosamae asked.

“Yes. Though, it’s stronger now. More potent.”

A shiver raced up my spine. “Can you stop it?”

“Perhaps. With your help. Your aura control is stronger now.” She strode toward it, waving me over. “But…we might not need to.”

“What do you mean?” I asked, hurrying to her side.

“You’ll see. Now, place your hand above it and follow my instructions.”

I hesitated, then slipped on the Soul Warden's Visage, placing my hand just above the sword's hilt, not quite daring to touch it. Rosamae put her hand on my shoulder, her warmth a steadying anchor. “Reach out with your senses,” Cronia said. Closing my eyes, I extended my senses, letting my awareness flow down my arm, meshing with Cronia’s as we carefully, cautiously, reached out to touch the soul of the weapon.

For a moment, there was nothing but cold, empty space. With the power of my mask, I could—

Then, reality shattered.

A vision hit me with the force of a physical blow—not a memory, but a feeling, a presence. I felt crushing pressure, as if at the bottom of the deepest ocean, constellations appearing, wheeling in a void-black sky. I felt the cold, unyielding certainty of something ancient, and the weary weight of a thousand grim necessities.

And I heard a single, clear thought, a voice like grinding glaciers and dying stars, a voice that was not meant for me but that I heard all the same:

So close. I am so, so close.
