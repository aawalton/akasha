Lee Jia’s face fell when she heard Magus Hwang’s announcement and he quickly moved to placate her.

“Ah, now, now, it’s not all bad. Let me explain before you get all downtrodden!”

Lee Jia nodded sullenly, and Hwang cleared his throat before continuing.

“Ahem, let me start with the good news! Several of these techniques should be learnable by Miss Lee with minimal adjustment. The air element techniques in particular may actually work better for one with a Yin-alignment.”

Lee Jia’s face scrunched up in confusion.

“Air element? I didn’t think Qins cultivators recognized it.”

“Oh, they don’t, typically, but that doesn’t mean they don’t practice it. Their traditional models only recognize five elements, but that doesn’t mean they are limited to just those five. What it really means, is that they miscategorize various elements into those five categories. The Body Lightening and Path of Heavenly Ascension techniques are in fact Air and Sky element, respectively. Practiced by a Yang-aligned cultivator, they would both manifest as Sky.”

Lee Jia wracked her brain trying to recall her mana theory lessons. Her recall was perfect, but the lessons were very long-winded, and very boring.

“So...if I practiced them they would manifest as Air and...Void?”

Hwang applauded Lee Jia, as he beamed at her in delight.

“Absolutely correct, Miss Lee! Well done—you’ve been paying attention! The element of nothingness is a much better fit for a technique to reduce one’s weight, and while the Sky element is well suited to flight, it’s a complicated and esoteric element. Air should be able to accomplish similar results in a more intuitive manner, especially in combination with the other technique.”

Lee Jia couldn’t help but smile. Flight was one of the most exciting options, and the fact that she might even be better than expected at it was encouraging. Unfortunately, Hwang’s next words completely ruined her good mood.

“Unfortunately, with just the techniques you have, along with your physical attunement, there’s no way for either of you to practice both of these techniques while maintaining elemental balance. Of course there’s plenty of time to find other techniques later, but each one is permanent and the more spiritual arts you engrave into your soul, the fewer options you will have in the future.”

Lee Jia let out a forlorn sigh as she felt her dreams of soaring through the sky slipping away.

“It’s not the end of the world. There are ways of adjusting one’s elemental attunement beyond opening meridians, as you’ve already demonstrated that with your ki alignment. From the second stage onward, it is possible to do something similar with one’s aura, though it would limit your spellcasting ability.

“It may also be worthwhile to ask Ienaga Yumi about how she managed elemental balance, if she’s willing to share—it’s something martial artists have historically struggled with. Before Ienaga it was even suspected that martial artists simply plateaued before they could ever reach the so-called xiantian rank. Now we know it’s possible, but Ienaga Yumi has been rather tight-lipped about how she accomplished it.”

Lee Jia frowned as she pondered what Hwang was suggesting. It was a complicated problem, so she wasn’t surprised that it was something that might take advice from all three of her teachers.

She wished they worked a bit closer together—Ienaga had implied at one point that they had some kind of professional interaction but she’d never once seen them actually exchange words. In fact, the only time she’d seen them together was during the initiation ceremony. She groaned in frustration and shook her head.

“What if we just learned all of them? As a set, they’re elementally neutral, right? I’d just end up right back where I started, more attuned towards lightning.”

Hwang nodded amiably.

“Not the worst fate, true, but it would hamper your progress somewhat. There are always bottlenecks between the stages, but they are much worse for those who are out of balance, and none are worse than the bottleneck between houtian and xiantian. It’s severe enough that attunement to one’s natural affinity has been enough to keep martial artists from xiantian rank for thousands of years before Ienaga broke through.”

It would be a long time before Lee Jia needed to concern herself with breaking through to xiantian, and perhaps she could find another solution by then. Eui pursed her lips and slumped her shoulders slightly.

“I uh...don’t think I should stay attuned to destruction for long. It’s probably not very healthy in the long term. Which ones would I be able to learn?”

Hwang scratched his chin for a bit and muttered to himself as he puzzled out a solution.

“Hmm, most of them I think. If you cut out the Awakening of the Dragon’s Heart—which is actually a combination of fire and yin, to offset the natural Yang-alignment of its users—and the Air elemental Body Lightening technique, that should account for the composite elements of destruction: Fire, Air, and Yin.”

Eui blinked, taken aback.

“Fire? I thought destruction was a Water element, and isn’t it cold? It feels cold…”

Magus Hwang smiled kindly as he explained.

“An element is more than simply the sum of its parts. Certainly destruction would be considered among the purest forms of Yin to Qin’s traditions, which would make it a water element. However, mana theory identifies it as Fire, Air, and Yin. It is, in fact, the Yin aspect of Lightning! Quite a remarkable coincidence, is it not?”

Something about the way Hwang said that last part made Lee Jia suspect that he didn’t think it was a coincidence at all. She was happy for Eui since learning the spiritual techniques would be so much easier for her, but she couldn’t help but be envious. Overwhelmed by curiosity, Lee Jia had to ask.

“What about me? If I tried to learn as many as I can and stay balanced, which ones could I learn?”

Magus Hwang scratched his head, frowning.

“Let me see, hmm. A bit tricker, there’s no clean combination that excludes the Lightning element, except...yes, you’ll need to cut Earth Rending Sword Art, and both of the Air-element techniques.”

Lee Jia felt crushed. Those were the ones she was most interested in! Projecting weapon attacks and flight! Not to mention, that left her with all of the most complicated techniques and almost none of the simple ones. It got even worse as Hwang continued.

“Unfortunately those are also the ones most compatible with your Yin alignment. The others will require some fairly extensive adjustment before you’ll be able to practice them. Tranquility of the Verdant Marsh and Domain of the Earthen Realm in particular will no longer even resemble their originals, the elemental change is simply too incompatible. The other two should be a bit easier to manage, however.”

Lee Jia slumped in her chair and let out a defeated sigh. At least Yan Zhihao had only mastered one of these techniques by the time he broke through to the second stage. She’d just focus on one for now and maybe a solution would present itself in the future.

“If we just picked one technique to practice for now, which one would be best?”

It was the same question she had asked Elder Qin. He had answered quite confidently at the time, but Lee Jia couldn’t help but wonder if Hwang would share the same opinion.

“On that, Qin Zhao’s peerless expertise on matters of cultivation shines through. He is correct to suggest Tranquility of the Verdant Marsh to you both. The Earth and Water elements will neatly offset your shared affinities for Fire and Air, and the expressions of the technique should perfectly match the opposite elements of your martial arts. Contrary to what you might expect, opposite elements tend to complement each other quite well.

“For example, in its intended use, the Tranquility of the Verdant Marsh channels the Wood element’s powers of life and growth towards qi-based healing and recovery. Quite a match for Miss An’s self-destructive martial arts, no?”

Jia and Eui exchanged a gleeful look. That was perfect! That was such good news that Jia almost entirely forgot her own qi-related woes. Being able to heal herself would be incredibly valuable for Eui’s martial arts training, and vice versa. Though the more she thought about it, repeatedly self-injuring and healing sounded like a pretty gruesome way to train.

“What about me? What element would I end up with, and how would I need to change it?”

She could have probably figured out the answer to the first part herself, but it was simpler to just ask. Hwang didn’t seem to mind answering anyway.

“The Yin aspect of Wood is rather nasty. Instead of life and growth, it’s an element of corruption and decay. Among mana theorists, we typically refer to the element as Corrosion. Needless to say, we’ll need to do away entirely with self-applications, and figure out a way to weaponize that which the technique usually uses as a healing method.”

“Like healing in reverse?”

Hwang tilted his head back and forth for a moment, considering it before nodding. Lee Jia went a bit pale at the thought. It sounded horrifying, and yet also kind of fascinating. Exchanging a glance with Eui, she saw nothing but support in her friend’s eyes. They nodded to each other and turned to address Magus Hwang.

“When do we start?”

The weeks began to fly past as Lee Jia immersed herself in cultivation and training. She made quick progress in her martial art, but found herself struggling with the modified qi technique. For one thing, it wasn’t as simple as making a few changes. There was a constantly ongoing process of Magus Hwang suggesting a change, then Lee Jia taking it back to Elder Qin for approval and adjustments to the actual method. If Elder Qin had a problem with Hwang’s idea—and he always had a problem—then Lee Jia had to act as an intermediate, going back and forth as they discussed the alterations by proxy.

She wished they would just work together on it, but while Hwang was open to the idea, Elder Qin was insistent that their regular lessons were already more of his time than she deserved. The one silver lining to this arrangement was that Lee Jia was quickly learning about the process of creating or modifying a technique, and the subtle differences between spells as used by mages, and the techniques of spiritual and martial artists.

Even when the elements manifesting weren't completely different than expected, spiritual arts tended to express themselves differently for each practitioner. This was because they were based on one’s feelings. The elements were fundamental laws of existence, but they were somewhat open to interpretation. By contrast, spell talismans and formations used rigid rules and rote understanding of the characters and concepts used.

What this ultimately came down to is Hwang trying to create a specific effect and Qin coming up with a dozen disastrous interpretations of that effect’s manifestation. It was a painfully slow process, and after weeks they’d only managed to agree on a single move.

Eui, on the other hand, had the opposite problem. Her spiritual art training was going quite well, and she had already mastered self-healing—the first application of her technique. Her martial art, however, was fickle and volatile, and while her self-healing made it much easier to recover after practicing the technique, progress was slow when every failure was so costly and painful.

Meanwhile, now that they had their own techniques, the gloves had come off in their regular sparring sessions. All their techniques were on full display now, and Lee Jia found the experience truly humbling.

Kim Yongsun—the white tiger-headed boy that Lee Jia had now learned the name of—threw out a talisman, which exploded into a barrier of ice just in time to intercept one of Eui’s throwing knives. The knife detonated a moment later, consuming part of the barrier in a chaotic whorl of destructive ki.

Eui would be out of the fight for a moment while she recovered, but Jia took the opportunity to smash her way through the weakened ice barrier. Landing on the other side, she ran for Kim Yongsun. Guan Yi moved to intercept her, but he was too slow, as Lee Jia launched herself, following an impossible trajectory, with so many sharp turns it would make Takeda blush.

Unlike Takeda, Jia’s technique had very little control aside from where she began and ended. The rest of her movement was erratic and difficult to predict, even for her. It was enough to get past Guan Yi, and Kim Yongsun scrambled to activate another talisman as she appeared next to the startled tiger-man.

Lee Jia struck Kim Yongsun with a one-two combination of spear-hands that she had been practicing endlessly. The first strike sent lightning ki raging through his meridians, causing him to seize up and twitch like he’d just been electrocuted. The second strike unleashed a burst of corrosion qi that she had been storing up, which followed the path of damage done by the first strike, infecting his ki so that his own energies would turn against him if he tried to wield them.

With Kim out of the fight, Lee Jia quickly turned to face Guan Yi, who was already swinging invisible blades of qi at her. No practice guandao this time—each swing carried the speed and sharpness of an edged weapon. As promised, the sword energy was much faster when channelled through a metal weapon, and Lee Jia struggled to avoid it.

She was forced to try to parry one of the invisible blades, using a combination of ki-parrying and qi-shielding to deflect an incoming strike that she couldn’t dodge in time. She successfully blocked the attack, but it exploded into a haze of dark mist that Lee Jia knew she had to get away from as quickly as possible.

She growled as she escaped the mist—her eyes and throat burning as she continued to evade the unending barrage of strikes from Guan Yi. He had found a frustrating synergy between his techniques—his martial art allowed him to emit that disruptive miasma, but he had discovered that he could combine it with his signature ability to project weapon attacks.

Lee Jia wondered how to approach, but her question was answered for her when Guan Yi suddenly whirled around to deflect an incoming knife, which exploded harmlessly several feet away.

Eui had recovered and was running back into the fight, already preparing to throw another exploding knife. Because of the self-damage effect, Eui was only able to fight in short bursts before she had to stop and recover, but during that time she could not be ignored.

Taking advantage of Guan Yi’s distraction, Lee Jia ran forward and held forward a talisman. The shock spell, empowered by her lightning ki, was more powerful than it had been against Yan Zhihao—though not nearly as powerful as the lightning bolt that Dae had gifted her.

Guan Yi convulsed as the spell struck him, and was unable to deflect Eui’s second knife. It embedded itself in his leg, and Jia had a moment of panic before she saw that Eui had feinted the attack—it was just a regular knife.

Eui’s voice was frantic as they regrouped with each other.

“Jia, we don’t have much time. Rika’s trying to hold her back bu—”

CRASH

Eui was cut off by the sound of the remains of the ice barrier shattering violently, sending shards everywhere. As the dust settled, Jia and Eui saw the perpetrator approaching casually. With flowing red hair, piercing green eyes, and an otherworldly grace and beauty that would give Eunae a run for her money, Hayakawa Kaede came forward.

Jia and Eui knew that this was the end. It had been a five on five team spar, and their entire strategy had been to distract Hayakawa for as long as possible, in the hope that they could take out the rest of her team and then gang up on her. It had almost worked, but Eunae had been taken out of the fight early, her martial arts ability still lagging behind the rest.

They might still have managed, but Guan Yi and Kim Yongsun both turned out to be slippery targets. Kim’s ice barriers, and Guan’s miasma made them difficult to chase, and by the time they had caught up to them they’d lost Kasai. Now it appeared that Takeda had sacrificed herself to give Eui the chance to regroup with Jia, and it was down to two on one.

It wasn’t enough. Hayakawa made the first move, suddenly lightning fast as she closed the distance between them. Eui was targeted first, but Jia moved to intercept. Hayakawa ignored her entirely, vaulting over her head with an ethereal weightlessness. When Jia tried to strike at her, she moved just enough to avoid being struck in a pressure point. Jia felt Hayakawa’s body shift as her strike landed. It was like hitting a floating leaf.

When Hayakawa landed behind Lee Jia, she landed with the weight of the earth, causing a shockwave that sent Lee Jia reeling. In the time it took her to recover, Eui had already been knocked unconscious, and Hayakawa was facing Lee Jia one-on-one. With grim resignation, Lee Jia took her stance.

The most frustrating part was that Hayakawa hadn’t even had a second awakening. She was all martial arts, but that technique was superlative. As they went into what Lee Jia knew would be the final exchange of blows, Lee Jia focused harder than she ever had. She knew that one strike was all it would take, and made every effort to avoid Hayakawa’s blows. Meanwhile, she also knew that she could never overpower Hayakawa with sheer force, the only chance was if she could strike a pressure point.

Unfortunately, Hayakawa was also acutely aware of both of these facts. While Lee Jia had to avoid each strike entirely, Hayakawa only made tiny subtle shifts. She took every blow head-on, only dodging strikes to pressure points, and even then only enough to send the blow off-target. Each time Lee Jia hit Hayakawa, it was as if she weighed nothing, but when at last Hayakawa’s relentless offense earned her a strike on Lee Jia—

BOOM

Lee Jia was sent hurtling through the air, stars flashing in her vision. She landed hard, and felt something break—if it hadn’t already broken just from the strike alone. Lee Jia tried to push herself to her feet, but trembled as pain lanced through her entire body before she collapsed back to the ground, defeated.

Lee Jia looked up to see Hayakawa Kaede standing over her. She hadn’t even broken a sweat.

“Well fought, Miss Lee. You have a rare talent for this.”

Talent? Lee Jia couldn’t help but chuckle, despite the pain. The last thing she thought before falling unconscious was that she had just seen exactly what real talent looked like.
