I…

Okay. That…made sense. It was perfectly logical that the Unchained would be using the former site of Addersfield as their base of operations. When I separated the discovery from all emotional reactions, I could understand why. Anguis had chosen the land Addersfield had sat upon for a reason to found one of his plantations.

It was in land that bordered what House Florens had used to govern, but did not technically reside within their borders. It was, from investigations I had made in the past, not more than a few hundred miles from the eastern coast of the continent. The town itself had sat on very fertile ground, judging from the sheer variety of different items grown there.

It had it all. Location, defense, isolation, easy access to shipping. There was even the stockpile I vaguely remember being told had existed there. There was no logical reason I should hate the idea of returning to the area.

So.

Why did I?

Just the thought of returning to Addersfield, after everything I had seen, everything I had experienced to escape from it, made me want to vomit. But..

I may not have my Middle Lattice right now to dull my emotional reactions, considering it was still stuck in my true body. But I had gone through an extended period of time where I had grown used to hiding my emotions like a regular person. I could compartmentalize my feelings about returning to Addersfield, even if it was the correct decision.

There was someone else who needed my help right now. And…I had a good idea about why.

In the aftermath of my initial meeting with Bleddyn and his people, the tent gradually emptied out. I had discovered that we were only going to be loitering here for another day or so before Bleddyn gave the order for our procession to continue marching towards Addersfield. Then, all of the myriad tents would be packed up, fires would be doused, and beasts of burden roused…

To nestle in the bosom of Addersfield.

But I had stayed behind, and I wasn’t the only one. The only one who had realized what I was doing was Sylvia, and she had just given me an understanding nod and stepped outside to wait for me.

Walter had joined her. Both of them were just outside, waiting for me.

I appreciated it.

Silently, I stood up from my chair and walked over to the corner of the tent. There, I calmly sat down beside one of my best friends, and someone who’d had my back through a lot. The least I could do was be there for him now.

I waited, patiently, until he was ready…

And then he spoke.

“He’s dead,” Renauld said quietly, staring down at something in his hands. A quick glance told me it was a piece of parchment, of some kind.

I nodded slowly, staring off into a distant corner. “Marcel?”

Wordlessly, Renauld nodded, playing with one corner of the piece of parchment with a claw.

I took a deep breath and blew it out in one motion. Not quite a sigh, but something close. “How did it happen? Do you know?”

A mirthless smile crossed Renauld's lips, and he nodded. “I wasn’t there, but…Gustave was. He gave me this, and told me what happened.”

“What is it?”

Renauld continued speaking as if he hadn’t heard me. “Gustave told me everything that happened to the Ashen Bride. After Sylvia, Bella, Meia, Walter, and I stole the Ward Stone of the Astray and split the expedition between those who wanted to save you, and those who wanted to follow him, they panicked. Well, Gustave panicked, from what he said. Nyx went quiet and sad, while he…” The Gnoll laughed bitterly. “He started cursing, apparently. Loudly. Mostly about you, apparently. It was all your fault, even though you were in a cell at the time. That’s just…so him that I can’t help but laugh.”

I remained quiet. It had, apparently, been a week since the battle for Rhoscara had been lost, but I don’t think Renauld had really processed this yet. I could easily see the Healer keeping these feelings bottled up.

“And then the bombs went off, and the fire spread.” Renauld closed his eyes, as if he could imagine something he hadn’t been there to see. “That’s when the Savoy hit them. Hit the expedition. While everyone was busy arguing about what to do next, now that their way out of Rhoscara was stolen, they were ambushed. Nyx was still Nyx, so she rallied who she could to try and drive them off. She was apparently even managing until he showed up.”

“Daffyd,” I said quietly.

“Yeah, him.” Renauld’s smile didn’t reach his eyes. “The other werewolf. He broke their defenses and started tossing Nyx around like she was freshly Awakened.”

“And that’s when he killed him.”

Renauld bowed his head again to stare blankly at the piece of parchment. “Dumbass apparently stepped in,” He said bitterly. “The idiot. He never progressed much past the third breakpoint, and his foundation was weak. He hit a bottleneck in his advancement as a Cultivator like…a dozen levels in, and just stalled out. I doubt he ever gained more than two or three levels in all the time I was away. He…wasn’t a real Classer. Just someone with a decent enough level to be given the title of Commandant, but without the instinct. All our battles, our adventures these past few months? That made him forget. Daffyd apparently just…tore right through him, without even looking. Wasn’t even enough of him left to recover. Just shreds of meat and fur.”

He went quiet once more, unblinkingly staring at the parchment.

“I’m sorry, Renauld,” I said tentatively, resting a hand on his shoulder. Renauld didn’t even react to it. “I know in some ways you were trying to connect with him, these past few months. I noticed it, you know. Like…you were trying to extend one last chance to him, before you moved on.”

That caused Renauld to glance up at me for the first time, a real, if not dry smile crossing his lips. “You’re better at this shit than I am, you know?” He chuckled lowly. “Emotions. I’m the Healer, but you’ve got the better bedside manner. Honoka always gives me crap about it. Gave me crap.”

I remained unmoved by his almost reflexive joke. I knew that was just how he dealt with things. “She’s alive, Renauld. I’m sure of it.”

And I was. These days, I was fully confident that Grey and Honoka had to have survived the Skyfall.

It was just…inconceivable to me that such powerful existences would be conquered by that.

“Yeah…” Renauld breathed, breaking his gaze with me. “Maybe.”

He didn’t believe it. That was fine, though.

We’d see them again, before the end.

I had faith in Isolde.

“But…that’s not what’s bothering me,” Renauld continued slowly. He hunched his shoulders in as if he was expecting a blow, and finally uncovered the piece of parchment he’d been hiding the whole time. He raised it up to eye level for me to see, but I didn’t see much of anything. It was a single folded piece of parchment, secured by what looked like a hastily dropped piece of wax to seal it. “Gustave gave it to me. Apparently…before the Savoy hit them, he had a single moment of what he called ‘clarity’. It’s, uh…it’s for me. From…Marcel.”

I studied the piece of parchment once more with greater understanding. Ah.

This, for all intents and purposes, was the last will and testament of Commandant Marcel of the Gnollish Throng.

And Renauld had had it for the last week, and hadn’t read it.

“I see,” I said quietly. “Are you afraid of what might be in there?”

To my surprise, a brief sneer appeared on Renauld’s lips, and he shook his head. Though I don’t think it was really directed at me. “No. That’s the thing, isn’t it? I’m not afraid of anything that old, treacherous asshole had to say. You’re…right, Nate. You were right. I was trying to see if there was anything in him worth admiring. Anything in him that might point to it being worth something, to try and salvage a relationship. I-” Renauld cut himself off with a bitter chuckle. “I knew better, but I still tried. I tried, Nate, to see the good in him. If there was any spark of goodness in the man I once called Father I could possibly triage. For a while, I thought there might even be something else to the man other than selfish, self-promoting, pitiless duty. It was so, so out of character for him, to volunteer on a mission like ours. Something that was ultimately self-sacrificial, a potential suicide mission to save the world. But there really wasn’t anything else, was there? The one good thing I thought he was doing, and he tried to burn it all down and piss on the ashes. Not even for a good reason. Just to try and elevate himself.”

Suddenly animated, and with a growing resolve to his furred face, Renauld stood up from his chair. With few quick strides, he walked over to a nearby brazier, the fire having been doused but glowing coals remaining. When Renauld turned to face me once more, I could see them reflected in his orange eyes.

“I don’t even want to read it,” He said bluntly. “And that’s what’s bothering me. I want so badly to stoke this flame, and then toss the damned thing into it.”

To underscore his point, Renauld tensed his eyes and waved a hand over the coals.

The fire dwelling in them flared to life, leaving a gentle, rolling flame within the forged iron cage.

“But he was my father,” Renauld turned away to gaze into the calm, almost sleepy-looking flame in the brazier. “I don’t care what he has to say anymore that he’s gone. I don’t care what last-minute epiphanies he had, or even what his last regrets were. I don’t care…” He hissed in an almost animalistic manner. “If they involve me. And I know that’s wrong, Nate. I fucking know it is. I’ve comforted plenty of people on their deathbeds, moments before they pass. I’ve seen the pain, and the regret, and the longing in their eyes before the light leaves them. I…know what some people would give to have something left behind when a parent passes. I feel like a monster that the only thing I want, is to destroy it. It’s…kept me up at night, in the last week, thinking about this.”

Silence fell on the tent, then, as Renauld stared broodingly into the fire. He’d said his piece, and I think…he just needed to get it out into the air.

If it was said, it was real.

I stood up and walked over to join him, staring into the fire. It was…nice, just staring blankly down into it. These days, fire meant more to me than just light or warmth. It was starting to be a fundamental component of my path, of my growth as a Classer.

There was something there, I think, in the conflict between thorn and flame.

Terrestrial and Celestial.

Construction vs. Destruction.

A dividing line to ride.

Contemplating a…great many things, I stood there with one of my closest friends…

And gave my own peace.

“Tell me about the early days,” I said abruptly. When Renauld jerked out of his trance and turned to stare at me with an openly confused look on his face, I just smiled at him. “When you were young. Before your Awakening, before Marcel became a Commandant, and before your mother disappeared. Tell me…were there good days? Times when your family came together, and there wasn’t conflict? It doesn’t have to be anything complicated. Simple would be good, in fact. Something simple, and silly, and fun.”

The more I spoke, the more Renauld’s vulpine brow had furrowed. When I finished, he looked to be deep in thought, to the extent I’m not sure he even saw me anymore. “Yes,” He finally admitted, something hard-edged, raw, and angry in him softening ever so slightly. “We…had something silly like that. Doesn’t every family? We were no different. Before Mother became a Matron, and Marcel a Commandant, he…liked to bake, sometimes, with her help. He had an old family recipe for a traditional dessert of our people called baklava. And, well…” Renauld cracked an almost reluctant smile, and if there was a tinge of sadness to it, I didn’t remark on it. “Once he’d put it in the oven, he would grab Mother and I by the hands, and he would lead us in a silly little chant he learned from his own Mother. I never met her, but Grandmother apparently said it was a charm to make it rise. It, uh…look, I’m not a singer, but it went something like this.”

Renauld cleared his throat, and for the first time since I’d met him all those months ago, in a dingy island prison…

He sang.

Pretty terribly.

“Hands together,

Count to three,

Rise for us,

For family.

Not for war,

Not for gold,

Rise like stories,

Mothers told.”

If Renauld’s voice choked up at all while he was singing, I didn’t comment on it. Instead, I simply stood there at his side, close enough that I could feel his warmth, as he shuddered in place and frustrated tears carved down his face.

Eventually, he regained his composure and roughly scrubbed at his face. It was only then that I spoke. “Have you decided?”

Renauld’s eyes cut over to me, and he nodded jerkily. “Yeah,” He said in a rough voice. “I have. I’ll…keep it. Not for me, though. I don’t think I will ever open it. Instead…I’m going to hand it to Liora, after all of this is through, and we meet up again. And one day, if there was a shred of kindness in my father there at the end, that’s the only thing I want my children to know of him. I’ll ask her to give it to my children when they’re old enough to understand. That…” He sighed heavily. “I think that’s fair. Maybe a bit cowardly of me, but I don’t care anymore. It’s…the only thing I can give him. The knowledge of his existence, long after he’s gone. It’s…enough.”

I tilted my head, considering his solution. “Remembrance,” I said, almost tasting the word. I nodded. “You’re right. That is enough.”

Sometimes…all we could hope for after we were gone, was to be remembered.
