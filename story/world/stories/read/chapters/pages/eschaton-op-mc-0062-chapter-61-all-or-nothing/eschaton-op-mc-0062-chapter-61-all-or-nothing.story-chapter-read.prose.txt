Morgan and myself are tinkering with some ward designs when a call from Director Holden comes in. I hold a finger up in the universal ‘taking a call’ sign.

She’s brief as she normally is, and gives away little beyond a desire for a meeting in person. Whatever it is, it sounds important. Morgan and I save what we have so far, and she paints the portal into existence. With a surge of magic from the world’s most powerful sorceress, space itself cracks open. We both step through and into the White House’s Rose Garden.

Director Holden is there to meet us, with ramrod posture and hands clasped behind her back.

“Right this way, please,” she says coolly. She never truly betrays what she’s thinking about. It’s clear that she’s even learned to control her Aura somewhat, which is incredible for a Typical. They barely have Auras to begin with, and to learn to control one without the magical senses that come with the next stage of existence is nothing less than extraordinary.

Morgan and I share a look as we both step off the raised concrete platform used specifically for portal rituals. We follow the Director to the Oval Office, my new cloak proving its worth. No need for reinforced steel plates to spread out my weight anymore. Grasping blue tendrils of light, reminiscent of a plasma globe, writhe across the carpeted floor.

Inside the Oval Office is President Drayson, Secretary of DHS Johnathan Cook, Secretary of Agriculture Tyson Freeman, and Secretary of the Interior, Brandi Miller. All of them have grim looks.

“Before I begin, everything said in this room does not leave this room. Is that clear?” President Drayson says. When we both nod, he continues. “I’ll get right to it. The food situation has hit a tipping point. We are running out of time. By the end of the year the United States will be suffering from widespread famine.”

The silence is deafening.

“We’ve come to you because we need your help,” Secretary Cook says grimly.

“This summer is the very last harvest the United States will see from its rural farmlands. Most people have already fled to the fortress cities. We’ve been forced to bring in the National Guard to complete most of the work in many cases. Being outside the walls of the fortress cities is simply too dangerous for unarmed Typicals,” Freeman says.

“We’ve started some projects retrofitting farm equipment with automation systems, but we don’t really have much hope for them. None of them were designed with automation in mind, and we don’t have the industrial capacity to build new custom-built farm equipment and the construction equipment needed to fortify major cities at the same time. We’re stuck between a rock and a hard place,” he adds.

“Asking you is a last resort,” Director Holden says coolly, and the President winces, just barely. Only myself and Morgan probably caught the tiny twitch. “We do not have any other options. We cannot do this ourselves,” she says.

“Of course I’ll help,” I tell her. “But I’m not sure how much I can do, though. This sounds like a job for Morgan.”

Morgan runs a hand through her frizzy hair. It forms a fiery halo around her head, a nervous twitch I’ve started to pick up.

“Okay,” she says. “I’ve been tossing around an idea for a few days, but I have no idea if it’s actually viable.”

“We’ll take anything,” the President says. “When I say we are out of options, I mean it. We’ll gladly take a Hail Mary.”

“Okay. So, we’ve got lots of empty skyscrapers in cities, right? Well, while we’ve been converting a few into massive apartment blocks, I’ve got a new idea: giant hydroponic towers. Basically, we use them to house a huge array of hydroponic systems.

“I haven’t done any of the design work to actually prove this could work. I can tell you for sure that the skyscrapers we have won’t be able to support that kind of weight. We’ll need to reinforce them, probably with something like mithril. But it’d require a ridiculous amount of the stuff, I’m sure.”

“We’ll make it happen,” Secretary of the Interior Brandi Miller, says.

“We’re talking thousands of tons, Secretary,” Morgan says. “Tens of thousands per arcology. Probably. Could be even more.”

“We’ll make it happen,” she says again. “It’s not like we have any other choice.”

“We plan to announce significant rationing measures in just a few days. That gets us to the end of the year if we’re lucky. If you come up with something, anything, to buy us more time, let us know,” Drayson says. “But I should make something clear here. Both of you have blank checks to get this done. Whatever you need, whenever you need it. Someone tries to slow you down or raise a fuss, you call me. Not my Chief of Staff, me.”

Morgan whistles. “A blank check from the President is no small thing. Can you even do this? I figured Congress would have to sign off on stuff like this.”

“The Apotheosis Crisis Response Act has given the President a great deal of emergency war powers,” Director Holden says coolly. “National security has never been more at risk. We are teetering on the brink. We cannot afford food riots.”

“We need to convene the Council,” I whisper quietly to Morgan, so quietly a Typical wouldn’t be able to hear me if they were right next to us. Her eyes flick to me. She signals her assent via her Aura.

“I’m not sure we can keep this entirely secret,” I admit to the group, and they stiffen. “We’re going to want to involve the Council of Ascended. This arcology could save billions of lives all over the world. I’m not sure if you know—”

“We are aware of the Council’s existence,” Director Holden says, cutting me off. “I want to reiterate that this was brought to your attention with the assumption of discretion. We cannot have this leaking before we are ready to fully enact rationing protocols.”

“And if you want this to succeed, I don’t see any other way than involving the Council.”

“Hashimoto of Japan is on the cutting edge of true magi-tech,” Morgan says. “The two of us working together is the best chance for success. As of right now, all of the concepts I’ve had to make the hydroponic arcologies work will require magical systems—Opus and magi-tech—that we simply do not have. We don’t have the materials to even start experimenting along those lines.”

“So the Council will have to be involved one way or another,” I continue. “We will need their help if we want this to succeed. Not to mention it should be done for the benefit of all humankind.”

“You’re saying you can’t do this?” Drayson asks with a raised eyebrow.

“Not alone,” I growl. “I have not once said I would be able to save the world on my own. I don’t know how, why, or care how you all came to that assumption. I cannot do this by myself. We do not know enough about Fractures other than to make vague guesses about what could be in them. You’re asking us to take random shots in the dark and pray we get lucky. If you want us to get this done, we need far more than just me diving into Fractures for this. This will have to be a global effort.”

President Drayson sighs and shares a look with his Cabinet members. They come to an unspoken collective agreement.

“At least give us until tomorrow. If we want to minimize the chaos that comes with rationing and prevent rioting we absolutely need that time.”

“Fine.” Morgan turns to leave. “Come on Mach, we’ve got work to do.”

Morgan spends the entire night with her apprentices coming up with a very basic preliminary design, at least enough to give us a vague direction. The morning before our second Council meeting, she gives me a quick run down in her gallery. She narrates over a floating hologram, displaying the clearly very rough design.

“Look, these Hydroponic Arcologies are going to need to be effectively self-sufficient. The amount of water and power they would draw to function is simply not possible for cities to handle at this stage. They’re already overloaded as it is. So we need some kind of Opus that could produce water. Maybe some kind of crystal? Australia recovered a few heat crystals from that Cat-3 you went in with them. We’re also going to need a source of sunlight.”

“What about those light crystals I salvaged back in New York from the Lumens? The twisted light post monsters.”

“Not good enough. They don’t produce the same spectrum of light the sun does. We need something like grow lights, but those are power hungry and produce a monstrous amount of heat. Even with grow lights, the predicted growth rate still isn’t enough to feed everyone.”

She points at a blown up portion of the design, which is a series of thick plates with various kinds of generic plants growing on them. They’re ten feet tall and fifteen feet wide.

“Here, these are the hydroponic platters. Each one can feed about five people with some rough napkin math. That’s a lot, but also given how many people we’ll have to feed, not nearly enough. We need something to boost the growth rates. Maybe fertilizer made from monsters or something else, I don’t know.”

“At least we’ve got an idea on what we need,” I offer. She nods in agreement.

Later that day the Council again convenes in the same place as last time. The Aurora Borealis is still as bright and beautiful as before. I dedicate a few milliseconds to marvel in its glory.

“For what reason did you call us here on such short notice?” General Guan Yu asks. The months-long Arctic early dawn casts long shadows, though the weather is much more pleasant than before. Relatively, at least. The cold is still a brutal forty below.

“I want to ask for your collective help,” I tell the group. “America faces famine by the end of the year, and I’m sure many of your own nations face the same thing. Myself and Morgan, though mostly Morgan, have a plan to stop it.”

Morgan casts an illusionary hologram and explains the concept as well as what we’re missing. Guan Yu thoughtfully strokes his long beard.

“I will have our own labs begin working on some components, as well start preparing for industrial scale mithril synthesizing. It may be difficult to transfer it to America at this stage, but an early start on production will only benefit all of us.”

Morgan tosses him a USB stick. “That’s got everything I have on the production of mithril as well as the preliminary design. Go wild. We’ve trying to start up mithril production too, but we’re missing key elements. Namely, enough titanium. At the very least we’ve started the demolition side of the project.”

“So the plan is to dive into Fracture after Fracture in the hope that we get lucky and find what we need?” Galahad asks. “Leaving something like this up to chance does not sit well with me.”

“I might be able to help with some of that, dearies,” Pythia speaks up. “After this, would you all be kind enough to join me in my home?” She says. “When I use my ability, it’s better to be at home, yes.”

“And what ability is that?” Guan Yu asks. “I assume fortune-telling by your chosen name.”

“Prophecy,” Pythia confirms.

“Maybe you’ll be able to come up with a better plan,” Singularity grumbles. “This feels like a long shot.”

Guan Yu shakes his head. “I’m afraid this is likely the only plan that will work. Nations have inertia in their own way. America has already committed to this course of action, and to change course would be to lose more than they would gain. A mistake committed to is better than the correct course of action carried out piecemeal.”

“It’s all or nothing,” I tell them. “Time to save the world, I guess.”

Galahad snorts. “Finally. Let us earn the respect and influence we have been given.”
