“Want to bet those are friendly?” I ask sardonically. Loran gives me an incredulous look. “Yeah, that’s what I was thinking.” I push off the rail. “Go tell the crafters to pack things away.” If this ends up a fight, the last thing we need is a potion being thrown around – or explosive half-made runes.

“Yes, sir,” he agrees, then quickly hurries away, tucking his carving knife and current project into his pocket. I see Sarran coming from belowdecks holding a large brown suitcase, and signal for him to join me. He quickly strides to my side as I make for the quarterdeck.

“You heard the warning, I take it.”

“I did,” Sarran confirms, his tone as grim as my own. “We need more information.”

“Already on it,” I reassure him, mentally asking Ivor to fly towards the oncoming ships. Noir, even with his Stealth Skill, would be a bit too obvious against the sky, as would the reds and yellows of Sirocco, despite her ability to become as small as my head. But the white of Ivor will hide much better against the pale clouds above. Especially with the protective suits of camouflage spider silk that Master Marin has been able to craft for him.

I curse again – if only this had happened in a few tendays’ time. Then we’d be more prepared and all three scouts would have the camouflage suits. At least he’s managed to give every group at least some armour and our ranged fighters are particularly well-equipped.

I’m grateful for that. If this comes down to a fight, we may need every advantage we can get. And, unless this is a group determined to join our party in alliance, I can only guess that their intentions are violent.

“Lord Markus,” Captain Tolly greets me as I reach the top of the stairs. He nods to Sarran. “Expedition Steward. I take it yer here about the oncoming ships?”

“We are,” I confirm. “Do you have any more information for us?”

It’s possible – the scout in the crow’s nest is one thing, but the ship-mages have other options available to them, and I know Tolly has a direct line to them for cases just like this.

Unfortunately, I'm disappointed.

“Not much,” Tolly replies, shaking his head. “They can tell there are several ships, but not the exact number, nor the type. But given the speed they’re gaining on us, they’re powerful ships – and most likely aided with magic.”

“Alright, we’ll wait,” I sigh, then glance over at Sarran. “Will you get the other lords on the mirrors, please.”

“Yes, my lord,” he agrees quickly, setting down the case and opening it carefully. Within are the paired mirrors that allow for easy communication between the other ships. Well, I say ‘easy’, but group conference calls are even more difficult to manage than back on Earth. The connection doesn’t tend to drop and there isn’t any delay in the transmission, but each mirror can only connect to one other and it can be difficult for people in one mirror to hear what another mirror is saying. We’ve had to come up with ways around that, one of which is as simple as everyone speaking very loudly. The mirrors are also limited in range – because they’re portable, they’re far less powerful than the one in Nicholas’ home, for example.

Still, we’re lucky to have them at all – apparently portable versions have only just come on the market, and are only available to certain people. If Nicholas wasn’t both a Great Lord and on good terms with House Artifice who are managing the distribution, we’d have to find an alternative way of communicating across the fleet.

I wait semi-impatiently while the mirrors are set up and my scout makes his way across the sky. I itch to have gone myself, but that wouldn’t have been a good idea for multiple reasons. Maxen soon hurries to join us, Sarran having clearly sent for him too. With him as the commander of my fighters, Captain Tolly as the master of the ship and its sailors, and Sarran as the overall organiser of the civilian part of the expedition, all we’re missing is Mage Vellus as the leader of the mages.

Loran, when you’ve warned the crafters, please send Mage Vellus up here. We’re going to need him.

Yes, Markus, my manservant acknowledges, his mental voice tense and concerned.

Finally, Sarran calls for me. The five mirrors are on a stand that our smith made for us – Happy was thrilled when he accepted her as an apprentice. It holds the mirrors securely on a curve that enables each mirror to have at least some view on one or two other mirrors. It’s not perfect but this configuration makes it easier for everyone to hear each other.

I step forward, standing in the spot where all of them can see me. Elarion’s mirror is directly in front of me, Layton’s and Josiah’s on either side, in deference to their position as lords. Valence’s and Sor’az’s images sit to the side, their view that of the side of my face.

I greet them all, quickly skipping through the formalities in order to get to the important discussion.

“There are three vessels,” I reveal, that information having come through from Ivor only a moment before. “Big – not dissimilar to our ships.” By that, I’m referring to the ones Nicholas had made for me, not Josiah’s which is more limited in size, or Sor’az’s which is even smaller.

“Are they flying any crests?” Layton asks, his expression tense.

“Yes,” I reply once I’ve had Ivor send a picture. My mouth tightens for a moment before I respond. “The fleet is led by a Torrent and includes House Bladestrike and House Siren.”

I hear murmurs from several mirrors as people not in the image view process that information.

“Kyrian, then,” Elarion concludes calmly, his gaze calculating. “Lord Torrent is unlikely to come out on an expedition such as this.” And he doesn’t need to say that only the presence of the lord or heir of the House would justify their flag flying proudly at the top of each mast.

“That’s troublesome,” Layton comments, in a carefully controlled voice. “The Torrents are powerful water mages.” Then he sends me an apologetic look. “And given their history with you and your family, Markus, I doubt they’re here with peace in mind.”

His comment gains him several snorts of humourless agreement.

“House Siren isn’t to be underestimated either,” Josiah contributes warningly. “Nor is House Bladestrike, though if we can avoid closing, their family Skills will be less effective.”

“How much distance should we ensure between us?” I ask him.

Josiah contemplates the question for a moment.

“At least three ship-lengths. I know they have an attack that can travel two, though it loses much of its force by that point. We would be in trouble if they manage to slice through our mast or even some of the key ropes on our ships.” That’s an understatement, though we do have shields that are supposed to prevent that kind of thing.

“We’ll all make sure to keep three shiplengths away,” I quickly agree, sending a glance at Captain Tolly. He nods to confirm that he’s listening and has taken that comment on board. I notice that Mage Vellus has appeared, his face pale. I return my attention to the discussion at hand. “Is it likely to be the lord or lady of the Houses, or the heirs?”

“The heirs, I would imagine,” Josiah assesses immediately.

“Unless Lord Torrent has managed to…convince Lord Bladestrike or Lady Siren to join,” Elarion points out, his meaningful look at me making his implications clear. But Josiah is already shaking his head.

“He has influence over them, yes, enough to ensure that their heirs join him. But neither lord or lady like him overmuch and they’ve been careful not to let their Houses get into debt to him. And I heard something about Nymphbay changing territory to Azaarde – if so, House Siren would be particularly unlikely to so obviously throw in with the Torrents.”

“What?” Layton asks, craning his neck as if that would make seeing his cousin easier. “When did you hear that?”

“Before we left,” Josiah comments. “The reason wasn’t made clear, but something serious must have happened to–”

“Shall we postpone this delightful sharing of – irrelevant – news to when a fleet bearing a most-likely antagonistic Great House heir is not approaching us?” Elarion asks, his drawl cutting through the conspiratorial tone that Josiah had dropped into.

“Ah, certainly,” Josiah agrees, looking a little shamefaced. He might be a lord rather than an heir, but Elarion has at least a decade on him in age. Layton looks similarly chastised.

“I might know more about the situation,” I admit, “but I can fill you in later. Elarion is right – we must figure out what to do about these three ships because, yes, I highly doubt they are coming to share a cup of tila.”

“Do we have te confront ‘em at all?” Sor’az asks, as usual blunt and largely unconcerned about hierarchical things such as who is technically higher in status. “We’ve got these fast ladies – we could try an’ outrun ‘em.” Then he grins, revealing his yellowed, blunt teeth. “We can even drop a chum bomb behind us, see if somethin’ will come an’ take ‘em off our hands.”

It’s a point and a thoughtful silence falls between us as we all consider the option.

Honestly, although I suspect, based on his father, that there will be no getting out of this confrontation with the Torrent heir, assuming it’s him, I’d rather not do it on ground that is so clearly his turf. Just like Kalanthia is uncomfortable and weakened by being surrounded by something that isn’t her element, the Torrents will only be strengthened.

“Kyrian is a powerful water mage,” Valence points out the obvious with some hesitation. “And he is unlikely to be the only one. They can use their magic to make up for the slower speed of their ships.”

“We do have a few water mages of our own.” I look pointedly at Josiah and then turn my head to glance at Mage Vellus. “Perhaps we could use a water current to propel us ahead?”

Josiah is already shaking his head.

“My specialism is rain and weather, not water currents. I can call a cloud over the ships and pelt them with rain, or hail if Layton helps, but I can’t make our ships move faster.”

I turn to look at Vellus whose specialism is water currents, but he’s already shaking his head.

“I’d exhaust myself quickly trying to move this whole fleet for long by myself. My specialism is in detecting currents, not shifting them faster than they should go.”

I sigh with irritation. Kyrian might be gunning for me, but I’d really rather not engage with him on a battleground that so clearly favours him and his allies.

“Markus? Would you like to share your discussion?” Elarion’s irritated tones ask. “I, for one, cannot hear it clearly.”

I send him a sharp look.

“Of course I intend on sharing pertinent information with my allies. However, I’m sure you equally recognise the importance of having complete information before doing so.”

My tone is mild, but my gaze is not. This isn’t the first time Elarion has tried to push my authority over the group, and I have no desire to deal with it when we have an enemy fleet quickly gaining on us.

I wait until Elarion nods – it’s fractional, but sufficient acknowledgement for me.

I fill my allies in on Vellus’ words, watching as thoughtful expressions cross Josiah’s and Elarion’s faces. Both originating from cities that have a good deal of water near them, their ships have the greatest concentration of Water Mages. Mage Vellus may have given his honest opinion, but it doesn’t necessarily apply to all of his kind.

I’m clearly not the only one thinking along those lines.

“What if all the Water Mages join forces?” Valence asks uncertainly. As far as I know, he doesn’t actually have any of their kind in his retinue – originating from a desert on the border with Nethayar, Water Mages are too valuable to send on an adventure like this. “Would that not reduce the strain on any one Mage?”

Elarion looks like he is about to speak but Josiah starts before he can.

“It wouldn’t work,” Josiah sighs, speaking first. “Not unless you intend on leaving me behind. Even if everyone combines forces and doesn’t spare the mana, current sensing and controlling is a rather niche Skill and using more general ones for the same purpose is much more inefficient. And rendered even more so when the ships themselves are not optimised for such speeds. My old girl here has been given a new lease of life, but she won’t ever move as quickly as your own – and may be damaged besides.”

“We’re not leaving you behind,” Layton declares firmly, glaring out of his mirror as if to challenge any of us to refute him.

“No,” I agree. “We’ll get through this together or not at all.”

“Then we must prepare fer battle,” Sor’az responds practically. “How’r we gonna do this?”

It’s a good question. And frankly, at this point, I’m rather hoping it is Kyrian – if it’s Lord Torrent, then we’re all in very, very big trouble.
