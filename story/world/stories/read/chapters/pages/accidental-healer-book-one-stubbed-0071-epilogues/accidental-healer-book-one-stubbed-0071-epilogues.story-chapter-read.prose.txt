Layton (New Boise)

Martin felt like he was dreaming.

The darkness was gone, reabsorbed into the panther that called himself Mischief. The Gree-Cek were dead, defeated in what had to be less than fifteen minutes.

He’d witnessed his boy, Layton, face their leader, along with several of their most powerful mages. He and his people killed them all.

Now that the battle was over, Layton withdrew his helmet. Underneath, Martin recognized the same half-cocked grin he’d known for nearly twenty years.

Water welled in the corner of his eyes.

“Layton?” he managed to choke out.

“Yeah.”

Martin walked over to his son and wrapped him in a tight hug.

“I missed you, Dad.”

“Layton, I can’t believe you’re here.” He pulled Layton’s head into his shoulder. “Didn’t you get my message?”

Layton laughed into his shoulder and his voice came out muffled.

“I figured it was safe to come home. There was no way you were still doing Charlotte’s sprinklers.”

Martin snorted. He pushed out of the hug, hands still resting on Layton’s shoulders.

“Oh man… this is going to be hard to tell you.” His face grew serious. “I haven’t even started.”

Layton grinned broadly.

“Oh, Dad! There is someone I want you to meet!” He waved for Martin to follow and jogged away. Martin jogged after him.

“Dad, this is Mischief, Mischief, this is my dad.”

The black figure of the truly enormous feline rose slowly from the tall grass.

“We’ve met briefly.”

“Is that so?” Layton eyed the cat suspiciously. “Well, allow me to formally introduce you. Mischief saved my life—more than once. I can honestly say if it wasn’t for him, I would never have survived. He’s like a brother to me.”

Martin nodded respectfully. “Mischief, thank you for taking care of my son. I can’t thank you enough.”

“He really did need a lot of help.”

***

Martin spent the next thirty minutes or so meeting some of the other friends of his son. Nick, Durkil, Alex, Norso, Ellison, and so many others he couldn’t remember them all.

Eventually it was time to return to the city, and as they did, Martin couldn’t help but ask.

“So do you have wings?”

“I wondered if you saw that or not.” Layton laughed. “Those are actually pretty new, though.”

Martin whistled. “So… what exactly is your class anyway?”

Layton spent the rest of the walk filling his dad in on every single detail there was about his class.

“It’s called…”

Arbiter of Judgement

Constitution: +7, Strength: +9, Agility: +6, Wisdom: +16, Intelligence: +12, Sense: +7.

When law and order fail, your voice is the truth.

Aura of Tranquility—When aura is active, allies in its influence have reduced battle weariness and sharper focus. Scales with Wisdom.

Holy—A powerful attack spell that can only be used on certain enemies such as undead, unholy, chaos… Scales with Wisdom and Intelligence.

***

Martin scratched his head. “But I thought you said the wings were new?”

“Right, yeah. I have a passive skill that affects spirit tools like my robe. It causes them to upgrade with me.” He shrugged. “So technically, I don’t have wings, my robe does.”

“But you can fly?”

“Well, so far I’ve only glided, and that was with you. I’m pretty sure I can. I’m a little nervous to try, though.”

Martin listened.

He still couldn’t believe this was all real.

“I can’t believe how strong you are.”

Layton blushed. “I’m just glad I was strong enough.”

“Me too.” He wrapped his arm around his son, pulling him close. “Why don’t we go and find your mom?”

Kevin

Kevin heard the steps above him, heard the door creak open.

Earlier in the week, he heard the men upstairs talking.

They were going to execute him for his crimes. He wondered how they would do it. Would it be public, or was this it?

He didn’t want to die, but he also wasn’t enjoying being alive.

“Wow.” Kevin’s head perked up. He recognized that voice.

“You look like shit.”

Kevin sat bolt upright. It couldn’t be. Standing at the foot of the basement stairs was his best friend.

“Layton!”

He grinned, but there was an edge to it.

“Hey, man.”

“Look at you!” Kevin looked his friend up and down, noticing the grey and gold robe. “Are you a freaking healer?”

“Something like that.” Layton shrugged, his demeanor sagging. “I heard about your dad. I’m so sorry, I really loved Ronnie.”

Kevin deflated instantly, excitement fading. He sank back into the old couch, the realization of where he was returning in full force.

“Yeah, me too.”

Layton crossed the room and sat next to him. “It sounds like things got pretty bad here.”

“You have no idea.”

“I wish I could’ve been here with you, Kevin.”

Wait… that’s right. Layton was here.

“Layton, you have to get out of here! I’m not sure how you got here, but go back, take your parents if you can.” His chains rattled as he grabbed Layton by the arm. “Take my mom too, it’s not safe here!”

Layton pulled his arm away.

“Boretek is dead, Kevin.”

Kevin looked at his friend, tilting his head.

“You already know about Boretek? What are you saying? Dead?”

“He’s dead. All the Gree-Cek are dead.” Layton’s eyes grew dark. “If you’d just waited.”

Kevin didn’t like the way Layton was looking at him. His chest tightened.

“Because I’m your friend, I’m not going to let them kill you.”

He looked at Layton dumbfounded.

“But that’s the best I can do.”

Layton left him there, just like his mother, just like Martin. They all left him alone. Was it possible Layton really did kill Boretek?

The implications of that revelation weighed on him like a mountain and he began to be crushed by it.

If Boretek was dead.

He’d murdered all those people for nothing. He remembered the words of his new title.

Betrayer of Man—In a world full of perils, some choose to face the unknown with hope, unity and strength of character. You are not one of those. -20 to all stats.

Warning: Your current evolution is not compatible with the new title.

A mutation to your evolution is now available.

And Kevin accepted it.

Stacy, Badlan, and Meklin

“What do you make of it?” Badlan asked, hand resting firmly against the translucent barrier that separated his territory from Faction Layton Mischief’s.

“I’m not sure.” Melkin walked a few paces off, all the while prodding the wall with his sword. “It seems like some kind of system barrier.”

“A system barrier that covers an entire faction?”

Melkin shrugged. “I’ve heard of it before.”

Badlan had also, and it was rarely a good thing for the people inside. It usually had something to do with some kind of system-enforced punishment. But what in the worlds could such a young faction possibly do to have the attention of the system? He found it extremely hard to believe. Which meant it had to be something else.

And if it was something else, it could be countered.

“Bring me the witch,” Badlan grunted to Tenor.

“Badlan… I’m not sure it’s the best idea to unmuzzle that woman.” His voice was low, and laced with concern. “If this is a system wall, it will come down on its own soon enough.”

Badlan turned and glowered at his brother-in-law. “Bring her.”

“Her magic probably won’t even work—”

Badlan raised his hand and Tenor recoiled.

“Go get the damn witch, and bring her here.” His voice was clipped and dangerous.

Tenor eyed the raised hand, then turned and jogged away.

Meklin and Badlan watched him go.

“You know, he might have a point.”

Badlan guffawed. “What will she do?”

“She’s a witch, Badlan and a crazy one at that. Maybe we should wait?”

“All witches are crazy.” He waved a hand dismissively. “It’ll be fine, we’ll tell her the boy is behind this wall. That’ll be all the encouragement she’ll need.”

Meklin watched Tenor’s back disappear into the forest. Feeling his hair prickle on the back of his neck.

***

Several hours later Meklin returned with the rest of their forces. He shrugged the witch from his shoulder and dropped her in the dirt at Badlan’s feet.

She hit with a squeak.

“Stacy, I’m glad you were able to join us,” Badlan said, not trying to hide the contempt in his voice.

The woman curled herself into a tight ball.

“I need your help, witch. There is a wall here that I want to pass through.” He reached down and gripped her under the armpit, then dragged her up and over to where the wall shimmered. “I want you to use your magic—here, feel it.”

Badlan grabbed her wrist and forced her hand onto the wall. She didn’t try to struggle, allowing her hand to feel the smooth, buzzing surface.

“There, you feel it, don’t you? Can you break through this?”

He released her hand and she curled back into herself.

“The boy you seek is behind this wall—if you open it, I will give him to you…”

Stacy’s head shifted, and she looked up slowly with a smile that seemed too wide for her small body.

“The brown-haired boy? Just behind this wall here?” She touched the wall again, raking her nails along the surface.

“I know a spell—but it will take some time.”

Meklin stepped forward. “I’m not sure this is such a good idea. We shouldn’t let this woman speak for too long.”

Badlan waved him away.

“Then you had better get started.”

City of the Gree-Cek

Carlos sat cross-legged with the thousands of other crafters Boretek handpicked to be his slaves. Several Gree-Cek guards lounged about carelessly. It wasn’t like anyone was stupid enough to try and escape.

A single guard was enough to kill them all.

Even if they could, Carlos wasn’t sure that he wanted to. He didn’t really have much to complain about, personally. He was alive, his wife was also alive, and they were taken care of and even treated fairly, so long as they were always working.

And Carlos loved his work. He was the city’s landscaper. It was rewarding work, he made the world beautiful.

Although… he supposed he wouldn’t miss the constant fear of death looming over his head.

Carlos was pondering the pros and cons of slavery when he noticed the shadow of a bird passing over the ground.

He cocked his head.

That was a pretty large bird…

He looked up and had to shield his eyes from the sunlight. Squinting, he was able to make out two giant wings silhouetted in the sky. He followed the bird circling above, feeling a pang of jealousy.

The bird landed nimbly atop Boretek’s pyramid.

That was when Carlos realized that the thing he’d mistaken for a bird wasn’t actually a bird at all.

It was a person.
