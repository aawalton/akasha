Mane swept in through the doors like a storm.

He entered a private room at the best healing center in the world. It was perched on top of a hill overlooking the crashing waves of the Venure shores. Though located in Capital City, the healing center was nestled away from the hustle and bustle, with a small encircling forest giving a layer of privacy.

It only treated the best and wealthiest, and it was almost universally reserved for heroes who had gone through life-threatening injuries or were on the cusp of death.

Both of those things applied to Stella right now.

The second he saw her on the bed, most of her face bandaged up, tubes running through her, he nearly broke down.

She didn’t look like his Stella. The untouchable Stella. The mighty Stella. Forever the shining star, Queen of Dragons.

Of course, that wasn’t who he’d known her as when they first met. Then she’d been the woman who was drunkenly trying to flirt with him at the bar he’d worked at because her friend dared her to. His flirting back had led to a conversation where she’d spilled her entire heart out to him about how cute he was and how drunk she was and how much the day had sucked, and talking to him was the highlight of her evening.

It was flattering to say the least. Amusing also.

But mostly her honesty was refreshing.

The conversation evolved into her talking about her parents and the massive amount of pressure she was under. Before he could ask her to elaborate, she promptly threw up on his shoes, and her friend had laughingly rushed over to get her out of there and take her home.

Stella was so mortified by it that she came over the next day to apologize. That was when he asked her out on a date.

She was shocked at first, but ended up saying yes. She also admitted that he was the first guy to ask her out so openly. Of course, at the time, he still didn't know she was Stella Firebringer. He might not have mustered up the nerve to do it if he thought he was talking to the future matriarch of the powerful Firebringer Clan. At the time, he simply thought she was cute, and he liked the little gap between her two front teeth, the dimple in her cheek when she smiled, and the way her eyes fluttered like a butterfly when she was nervous.

'Butterfly Eyes' was her nickname in his head for the longest time. When he told her about it, she wrinkled her nose in that adorable way of hers.

The more time they spent together, the stronger his feelings grew. He saw her as a woman with a deep sense of duty, who knew when to bend the rules to do what was right. A woman carrying a burden yet knew how to find joy in the little things of life. Forced to grow up too fast, yet had a whimsy about her and a mischievous streak.

By the time he fully understood the magnitude of who she was and the kind of fate she’d been condemned to, it was already too late.

He was already in love with her.

And no matter how powerful she was, he was always scared that one day something like this would happen.

She had so many enemies and was constantly thrust into dangerous situations, dealing with the worst of humanity.  For twenty-five years, every time she left the home, his chest would get tight, only to ease when he saw her again. And that went for his kids, too.

He'd eventually gotten so used to the feeling that he’d almost forgotten it was there, and he’d accepted it as another part of life. He somehow managed to convince himself that it was just paranoia, that Stella was powerful and fine, and she could never be hurt too badly.

That kind of thinking had made him complacent. Now, it had led to this.

His Stella....she was...

“Mane.” His name echoed as the door opened. “The guards told me you’d arrived.”

Mane didn’t turn to acknowledge the other man. He didn’t know what he would do if he saw Jerry’s face right now. Punch it, maybe. Scream probably.

He also couldn't bear to take his eyes off Stella.

He drifted closer, reaching out a shaky hand to touch her finger. Cold. Her hand was cold.

“She’s alive,” Jerry said.

“Of course she’s alive,” Mane snapped, finally turning to the other man. “No thanks to you.”

Jerry swallowed the blow and took measured steps toward him. He was smart enough not to say anything else. Mane didn’t need any platitudes right now. He didn’t need apologies. He just needed his wife to wake up. He needed her to be back home, in his kitchen with her leg propped up on the island while the pan sizzled with prime Luvian steak, the scent of which made everyone's mouth water. She should be slicing into a medium rare piece, eagerly, the juices dripping down her fork. She should be giving him a thumbs-up and a kiss.

Not lying in bed, fighting for her life.

“It was a mana bomb,” Jerry said, “It somehow bypassed her heat signature trace. Kya was on scene and said Stella was acting erratically and was distracted, preoccupied with saving a civilian.”

“Of course she was. And Kya wasn’t watching her back.”

“She was trying her best, but a lot was going on at the time."

“Yeah, and a lot's going on in the association, too. Don't think I don't know about her little power play.”

“It’s not–”

“Can you tell me with absolute certainty that Kya Wavelost did not allow my wife to get hit so that she would have one less opposition to her hostile takeover?” He’d only met the Wavelosts on a few occasions, mostly at Association parties, and the couple of times she’d attended Ansing at his house.

The impression be’d gotten of her was that she was cold, calculating, and devious.

This practically proved him right.

“I don’t think Kya wanted Stella hurt,” Jerry said. “Because Stella wouldn’t be a threat to her leading the association.  Stella doesn't want to be president." He sighed. “In fact, earlier today, she said she was planning on leaving the association entirely."

He blinked. "Are you serious?"

“Yes, though it might have been said in the heat of the moment, during a disagreement we were having about containing a threat."

"You mean Lexie Sparrowfoot?"

"You know?"

"Yes. It's all over the news. I didn't know Stella was there, too." He hadn't had time to actually read past the headline, but that was the first thing he would do when he got home. "Let me guess, you guys wanted to lock her up or hand her to the Fae, and Stella said no."

He cleared his throat. "Something like that."

"I don't get it." Mane shook his head in disgust and anger, imagining what Lexie and Aiden must be feeling. "Why don't you guys just leave her alone? She hasn't actually done anything wrong."

"She has some Eldritch essence in her."

"So? Has she used her powers for evil? Has she started any wars or killed anyone? And I'm not talking about the criminal who trapped her in the dungeon in the first place. " Stella hadn't shared that with him, but he'd heard her talking in her sleep and had put two and two together.

"She was stuck in a dungeon for nearly a year after saving my son. Maybe the Eldritch essence is the only way she could survive there. How the hell can you stand there and blame her for that? For crying out loud, she's a child! You have children of your own! How can you even think of taking a child from her father and sending her off to another planet for them to do God knows what with her?"

"They claimed they could cure her."

"Bullshit."

"She's a threat."

"Because she's powerful in a way that your system can't control and can't account for." Mane knew that was what it came down to. It wasn't about safety. It was about power.

Jerry was quiet. At last, regret colored his features, heat crawling up his neck. "The operation might have been a…miscalculation on my part.” He brushed his hand over his face. “I wanted the threat to be contained, but the Lightlarks disagreed and wanted it neutralized.”

Mane scoffed. "I wouldn't blame Aiden if he just killed all of you and got it over with."

After Jerry didn't give a response, Mane turned back to Stella. So she had threatened to leave the association. A part of him wanted to be happy about it, but he'd learned to temper his expectations when it came to her.

The question was, would she have followed through on her threat?

Or would they only have to dangle a new, overwhelming villain or a country in need to reel her back in?

He was so sick of this shit.

“The healers said she's stable for now,” Jerry said. "She put a shield to block, but the bomb had mana-disrupting components that tore down her fire shield and eroded her fireproof ability. Not to mention, she inhaled some of the particles and got direct mana poisoning. That's what's ripping her insides apart right now, and …that will likely have long-term effects. The healer is doing everything they can to recover her pathways, but we’re not sure we’ll be able to.”

His throat got tight. “Leave.”

“Mane, I want you to know that we’re so so–”

“I said get out!”

Jerry didn’t argue. He simply activated his teleportation orb and left. Mane stood there for a few more seconds, holding himself still so as not to shatter. He couldn’t take it anymore.

He walked out, too, his chest tight like he was going to stop breathing any moment now.

The armed Firebringer guards stood in front as he tried to collect himself. They gave him looks of sympathy when he leaned against the wall of the hallway, inhaling raspily.

He instantly hated them. Not personally, but he hated what they stood for. He hated the crown that had been dropped on Stella’s head without anyone bothering to ask her if she truly wanted it. And truth was, she had never wanted it. He’d known she’d never wanted it, and yet he’d let her carry the burden anyway, trying to ‘gentle parent’ her into the obvious realization that she didn’t have to do this and she didn't owe anyone anything.

He'd tried to be supportive while subtly telling her to quit torturing herself at a job she hated.

No more of that.

If she woke up from this–no, when she woke up–that was it. He was telling her to quit. He would give her an ultimatum, use their kids, use every bit of emotional blackmail he had in his arsenal to get her to do it.

Afterward, whenever she felt any guilt for retiring from hero work, she could just blame him.

He would be the bad guy, the reason why she couldn’t save people, the reason why she had to take care of herself.

She would have to finally start listening to her own needs. Even if she hated him for it.

It didn't matter. As long as nothing like this ever happens again.

“Dad?”

Mane was so caught up in his own private hell that he didn’t even hear his son come up until Theo was right next to him, his eyes wide with panic. “How is she? What happened?”

Mane couldn’t find the words to express it. He simply shook his head as the emotion rose in his chest, filling his eyes, blocking his throat.

He hugged his son tight as the emotion poured from him, and Theo clutched him just as strongly.

When Torin showed up a few minutes later, they pulled him into the hug, too.

The system screen rang off the hook for the thousandth time, and Doyle resisted the urge to bash his head into the wall. It hadn’t stopped ringing all afternoon till now.

He’d initially been happy when they’d gotten a company line, because it made everything look and feel more professional. He was even allowed to answer calls on behalf of the company. He'd felt like a real intern for once, and that had been the next natural step in establishing themselves as a legitimate agency, after they purchased a brand new office building with the first advance Lexie had sent upon signing with them.

Doyle gaped at the check Stanley showed him; he'd never seen so many zeroes at one time in his life.

He didn't know Lexie was that wealthy. She didn’t act wealthy at all. She wore pretty average clothes, lived in a pretty average house for the area, and she didn’t have any seemingly expensive hobbies. So the fact that she could shell out two hundred thousand credits like it was nothing, allowing them to purchase this small office in the center of Brightworth...that shocked him.

Every time he walked in here to have a meeting or anything, he was pretty excited.

But not today. Today was singlehandedly one of the most stressful days he'd ever had in his life.

It all started with the video of the Lightlarks attacking Lexie at her house, trying to capture her.

It had been uploaded to the NET for a full minute before it was taken down, and by that point, it had already proliferated across news organizations and forums, all of whom had been thirsty for more information about the new it girl, Lexie Sparrowfoot. They'd lapped it up and tore it apart all day, even after it got taken down. Plus, the video still lived and breathed on the Undernet.

Now, people were reaching out to them for comment.

They didn't know what to say. The instinct was to tell everyone that the video was fake.

Because there was only one reason why the Lightlarks would be attacking Lexie Sparrowfoot–she had sold her soul to the Eldritch.

That was impossible, obviously. He’d known Lexie since she was young, well, younger.

She was quiet and kept to herself, but she was friendly and normal. She was also extremely intelligent and always experimenting. One thing she wasn't was a demon. The heroes were just paranoid because of how good she was. That was the statement they should release.

“We should say that the heroes attacked Lexie because they were jealous," Doyle suggested to Stanley. "That they didn't like that she was getting attention, and so made up a rumor for the Lightlarks to go after her."

Stanley raised an eyebrow. "So you want us to get blacklisted from every major news agency. Are you insane? Do you know what it means to go against the hero association? You don't do that, kid.”

“But if we don't, they're going to say it's Lexie's fault. They're already calling her an Eldritch spawn."

Stanley groaned and put his hands on his face.

“So what do we do now?" Doyle asked. "We have to release a statement.”

“Have you managed to reach her?”

“No. Her calls aren't going through."

"Where on Earth has she disappeared to?"

Stan was silent again.

“So what are we going to do? It's close to day. We have to–”

“Just be quiet for a second, kid, and give me space to think.”

Doyle chewed on his teeth as he did just that, wondering when the thinking would be over. Abernathy was on his way too, and they’d be texting back and forth about ideas on how to deal with this crazy situation. Social media was running hot with it. It was the only thing people were talking about. Lexie's name was number one trending this morning across Districts 8, 9, 6, 7, and 4.

"Okay," Stanley finally exhaled. "Here's what we do. We–"

“Hello,” someone said, and the two other males yelled as they spun around.

A brown-haired man was in their conference room, by the wall.

"Who are you? How did you get here?'

Doyle recognized him immediately. Lexie's dad.

“I’m told that you’re working with my daughter as her PR," he said.

“Uh, yeah," Doyle answered. How did he find them? They only just moved to this building. He'd just appeared out of nowhere, and that was more than a little creepy.

“I’m Aiden. Her father.”

“I know who you are.” Everyone knew who he was. Even back when he was powerless, he was intimidating, but now that he stood there, like a statue analyzing them, his eyes shrewd and encompassing, Doyle kind of wanted to crawl deeper into his skin.

“I have a statement you can put out,” he said. "That fully explains everything that happened today. And I want you to give that to every press you have on your call list. You're also going to tell them the truth about the association."

"Is that it?" Lexie asked. "Are we done?"

"I think so," Pvilycht said tiredly. They had been working for what felt like days at this point. It was nice they were over.

"Should we try it out first?"

She shook her head. She didn't want to waste the effort.

Besides, she wanted her having the card to be a surprise, and to practice it, she would have to move somewhere with a solar system which might draw the attention of the Fae.

"I think it's good," Lexie said. "We've gone over the calculations multiple times already. I think it will work."

She had crafted a few more cards in this space to make up for the effort, for a new deck. Surprisingly, nothing had attacked her during that time.

That was good at least.

"Any updates?"

"Yes. Another woman called me handsome today."

"That's because you are." They'd designed his disguise to be appealing, so that people might be more likely to talk to him. They would switch to a plainer disguise when he started working at the lab, though. "Now, how have you put that pretty privilege to work?"

"I'm in the lab now. I'm invisible and observing. There are some rooms I cannot go into, but I'm working on the lock."

"Great." Lexie exhaled.

"So you're going home?" Pvilycht asked.

She nodded. "But first I have one last stop."

"The Orcs."

"Yup."

Lexie connected to Yasycht and teleported to the surface of their planet. It reminded her a bit of her dungeon, boiling and red and blissfully free of Fae restrictions.

The only difference was that the sky was black, in eternal darkness.

The Orcan planet. The best place for her to grow her mana, just like Naem had wanted her to do with Ganor.

It hadn't worked then because Lexie hadn't felt any true danger in that dream, nor had she suffered true damage.

And she needed both to grow.

She let out a loud war cry to invite any orcs to battle her.
