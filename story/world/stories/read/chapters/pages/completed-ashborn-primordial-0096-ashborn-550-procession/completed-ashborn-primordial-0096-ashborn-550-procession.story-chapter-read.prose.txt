Maiya had thought that getting to Vir would be the toughest part. That once they were reunited, everything would sort itself out.

How wrong she was about that.

The airship shuttled her and Siya down quickly enough, partly due to the short distance and partly because Maiya overcharged the Wind inscriptions well past their limit.

They set down at the western edge of the ruined part of Camar Gadin in minutes, with Maiya throwing open the hatch and deploying the ladder almost before they’d touched down.

Siya followed on her heels, and the two of them crossed the rubble-strewn streets, catching up with the procession moving out of the city.

That was the first thing that was off about all this. There shouldn't have been a procession at all. Just an injured Vir and maybe a few of the revived following at a respectful distance. What Maiya saw as she came into view of the demons was something else entirely.

Vir was not just unconscious, he was being carried by Roshan—Raja Thaman’s son—held in his giant arms with a level of reverence that nearly stopped Maiya in her tracks.

As for the procession, Maiya had assumed the number was in the dozens, perhaps a hundred at most, having surely been exaggerated by the reports. The slivers from the Vimana’s burst had only struck a part of the city, after all.

But no, there truly were hundreds of them, if not more.

She broke into a run, sweeping Siya up in her arms.

Spotting her, Roshan stopped to greet her. Which made the demons behind him halt as well, triggering a wave that propagated through the entire procession.

By the time Maiya reached him, she was heaving, though not on account of physical exertion. Such things had long ago ceased to be a concern for her.

“Put him down,” she said coldly.

Roshan gave her a look that was part reverence and part something she couldn’t quite place. Almost apologetic, but as to why, she couldn’t begin to guess.

He went to one knee, slowly lowering Vir to the ground in his lap rather than on the broken stones, his hands supporting Vir’s shoulders even after he had set him down.

“Forgive me, my Lady. Our Akh Nara has not the strength to stand.”

“Then shouldn’t you, oh, I don’t know, heal him?” Maiya shot back. She dropped to her knees and pulled out healing orbs of every rank, immediately going to work restoring Vir’s arm.

“Mai,” Vir rasped. “Is that you?”

His eyes slowly opened, but his gaze was realms away.

“I’m here,” she said, cradling his face in her hands. “I’m here. Don’t worry, I’m healing your arm right now. Should be as good as new in just a few moments. What happened, Vir?”

“The core...”

Maiya froze, her thoughts jumping to the worst possible outcome. But that was stupid. If that came to pass, none of them would even be—

“It’s ruptured.”

Maiya’s heart hammered against her chest, and she found herself speechless. Not for a lack of questions, but rather the opposite. How? When? How bad was it? What was to become of them now?

Yet none seemed appropriate given her beloved’s current state.

Siya knelt opposite her on Vir’s other side, putting a palm against his chest, her face going pale.

“It is worse than I expected,” she said softly.

“What do you mean? How much worse?” Maiya asked, trying and failing to keep the panic out of her voice.

“The core is indeed ruptured, though it is currently held in check, however tenuous that state might be. Tethers connect the core to every demon he has resurrected, doing more to contain it than any orb I could ever create. They are currently draining the core fast enough to offset the prana overflowing from it.”

She glanced at the warped tattoos visible through the burned remnants of Vir’s armor. “But the damage to his inscriptions is significant. Several of his Ultimate Arts have been compromised, and I estimate Yuma’s Embrace can only function at a small fraction of its capacity. That is, if he musters the concentration to activate it, which I expect he cannot. By every metric I can measure, he is holding together with sheer willpower alone. It is a miracle he’s managed this long.”

“That’s my Vir,” Maiya said with a strained smile, gently stroking his head. “Always defying the odds. Guess it’s a good thing I healed his arm then.”

“If his willpower fades,” Siya said with the most somber expression Maiya had ever seen from the girl. “if he falls asleep, or his concentration lapses, the core will fully rupture. The ensuing explosion will likely destroy half the realm, though the true danger lies in the damage it will do to the fabric of reality. Even the areas left unscathed by the initial blast will soon be consumed by the void it leaves behind.”

“You’re sure of this?” Maiya asked, regretting her words the moment she uttered them. She knew by now that Siya would never embellish something of this magnitude.

Maiya glanced down at Vir, who’d closed his eyes again.

“We need to get him out of here,” she said. “Somewhere quiet. Somewhere he can focus in peace.”

It was a small mercy that Vir did not require rest or food like other mortals. Especially not after their time in the Ash. Even Maiya’s prana capacity had expanded manyfold from her time there.

Not in a million years would she have thought their boosted constitution would be the thing keeping the realm together.

“Our Lord has already chosen a direction,” Roshan said softly, but the message was clear. He would not listen to what she had to say. That his consideration of her was more a courtesy than anything else.

Maiya took an involuntary step back. She’d grown used to Vir’s worshippers by now. Gods knew, she had some of her own.

But there had been nothing like this. No one on this level, aside from a select few Garga Lavani that Vir had folded into the Asura’s ranks long ago.

This was different and more than a little terrifying.

“He asked us to carry him west,” Roshan said. “To a field, perhaps, or open ground. He spoke of needing space he could not damage.”

Maiya looked at Vir.

“Is that what you want?”

His eyes opened just enough to find her. He nodded, and Maiya almost missed the tiny gesture.

“West, it is then,” Maiya said. “But not on foot. Roshan, can you carry him to my airship? It will be faster.”

Roshan hesitated, looking past her toward the long column of revived demons. “My Lady. They will follow, and I do not believe it right to deny them the chance.”

“Sorry, but that’s not workable,” Maiya said, looking at the column. Five hundred demons, perhaps more, all watching her with deep-seated suspicion.

“They will, my Lady. They cannot help it. I myself feel the constant pull that bids me to my Lord. If we travel by air, they will run beneath us until they catch up. We do not require food or water or sleep. We no longer tire. It will be only a matter of time. Unless you plan to force the issue?”

Maiya gave him a long, hard look before deflating. “You know I won’t.”

She’d never use violence on these poor souls. They’d done nothing to deserve this, for one, and without knowing just how much of Vir’s power they’d gained, she couldn’t rightly say if such a thing were even possible. The last thing she needed was another powerful enemy.

Turning to the column, she activated her voice amplification orb, and with every ounce of authority she possessed as Humanity’s Saint, Prime Mejai, and the woman who had stood beside Vir through every catastrophe of the last decade, she thanked them for their devotion and empathized with their plight.

She explained that the Akh Nara required rest and privacy, and she asked them to go home. To return to their families and to celebrate this gift they had been given.

The demons listened to her every word… and proceeded to not budge even an inch.

She tried to tell them that they didn’t understand. That Vir wasn’t what they thought he was and that she needed to be alone with him.

A young revived demon rushed forward and bowed.

“My Lady, we cannot leave,” the girl said. “We know not what binds us to him, only that it does and that where he goes, we must follow. Please do not ask this of us. It pains us not to listen to you, with all you’ve done for demonkind.”

Maiya looked at her for a long moment. Then with a sigh, she turned back to Roshan.

“Carry him, then. But pick up the pace if you’re able. All this jostling can’t be good for his focus.”

Roshan nodded, lifting Vir with reverence. “Of course, my Lady.”

To his credit, Roshan listened, and Maiya was reminded of just how different these demons now were.

For Roshan bounded across the land, forcing Maiya to use Lighten Load and Haste to keep up. That was a new perk from the Earth school she now had access, thanks to gaining every affinity.

Any worries of the others being left behind quickly proved unwarranted as the entire procession sped up to match. Even the elderly and the children among them, which made for quite a surreal sight.

It wasn’t long before Camar Gadin disappeared behind them, and rolling hills appeared as they continued West into the new lands. Lands that would ordinarily have been too dangerous for most demons, what with their proximity to the Ash Boundary and the beasts that came through.

Now, however, it was as safe as the rest of the realm, all thanks to Vir. Vir, who was now suffering the worst of anyone.

They bounded that way in silence for hours, and in all that time, not one demon complained or fell behind. A perk of having infinite energy, Maiya supposed.

She wondered how the children among them were handling it, augmented though they were. While they’d been reunited with their parents, they didn’t belong here, and she resolved to have a long chat with Roshan about what these demons planned to do hereafter, as well as how they planned to take care of their own.

Having traveled what must have been several hundred miles, Roshan brought them to a stop at the same flat expanse of grassland that Maiya had been eyeing herself.

No villages or roads or structures to worry about, considering this area had, until recently, been deep within the Ashen Realm.

Roshan set Vir down with such care that Maiya had to wonder if the sun would set before he completed the task.

A small eternity later, he bowed and backed away to give her, Siya, and Vir ample privacy. While he might not obey her orders, the respect, at least, was there.

The other revived demons sat on the grass nearby, seemingly content just to watch. Not one of them spoke a single word, and none came too close.

Maiya ignored them, focusing on Vir instead.

His face was pale in a way she had never seen before, and his face was soaked in sweat. He should have been resting, but instead, he was fighting to keep them all alive, even now.

“Vir,” she said gently.

“I’m here,” he said through gritted teeth. “Barely.”

Maiya choked back what she was about to say. Even words of encouragement would be a distraction he could scarcely afford. Which, now that she’d healed his arm, pretty much meant she was just a liability at this point. That hurt her more than anything.

So instead of speaking, she put her hand on his and squeezed gently.

“Mai,” he said, without opening his eyes, but Maiya wasn’t his wife for nothing. She knew that tone all too well.

“Whatever you’re about to say, the answer is no.”

“Mai, you have to go.”

“I am not leaving you, Vir,” she replied. “Not here, out in the middle of nowhere. Not when you’re going through this.”

“You have to. You can do nothing for me here.”

“I can!” Maiya retorted, despite herself, her face flushing at the reflexive denial. She’d come to the same conclusion herself just moments prior, after all.

Still, something deep within her revolted at the idea of leaving her most treasured person here like this. Amid a crowd of strangers and fanatics to deal with his crisis alone.

“I can’t pull this off if you are here,” Vir said. “If the core ruptures any further and you are in range, I will do all I can to protect you. I will not be able to help it. If I do that, everyone dies. You, me. Siya…”

Maiya cringed with every word he spoke. Not just because what he said was true, but because of the sheer effort it must have taken him to say them.

“I’ll keep my distance, then,” Maiya said. “Pitch camp a few hundred paces away.”

“Not far enough.”

“Then tell me what is! I’ll go as far as I need to.”

“Mai… please.”

Maiya blinked back the tears welling up in her eyes. “You think it a kindness to send me away?” she said, sniffling despite herself. “You think I would prefer to remain in safety while you fight for the lives of everyone alive, just like you always do?”

Vir flinched, and she instantly felt guilty for distracting him this much.

It wasn’t Vir who answered her, but Siya.

“I can stay,” she said.

“Can you?” Maiya asked, taken aback. “Can you… help him?”

The girl shook her head. “I cannot say. But I can endure any pranic blasts that come my way.”

She knew she’d lost the moment Siya said those words. That she’d have no choice but to leave. To rule the realm in Vir’s absence. That it was the only sensible thing to do.

Maiya’s shoulders drooped.

Despite the guilt wracking her at leaving such an important role to a child, it nevertheless comforted her to know Siya would be by his side, even if all she did was offer a familiar face. If Maiya couldn’t be there, then this was certainly the next best thing.

She bent forward and kissed Vir’s lips before placing her forehead against his.

“You’ve got this, Vir. I’ve seen you kill Wyrms and merge realms. This is beneath you. Overcome it like you do everything else. And then come back to me. Because I’ll be waiting.”

There was no reply, nor had she expected any. She rose to her feet and walked away.

She did not turn back. If she did, she might very well have faltered.

So instead she walked toward Roshan, who’d established himself as the de facto leader of this ragtag band.

For there was much to discuss and so little time.
