The spark ignited the collection of dried out branches with a whoosh and brought the campfire to life, scattering the dull forest dusk with orange light. Jack blew a steady stream of air to bolster the flames, making it pop and rise high in a short minute. The flickering light borrowed a restless quality to those huddled around its warmth.

Erazza's plain, almost forgettable features were weighed down even more by the circles she wore under her eyes. Strands stuck out of her mud brown hair as if she hadn't seen a brush for a month, something not likely to change anytime soon. Though Kalsin knew her to be the harder one of the two, Áron looked positively savage with black-and-blue bruises decorating his face.

When Kalsin had asked the pair to give a thorough explanation about the cure, right after the battle, they promised to do so after catching the woman, Xil. They had scoured the forest, and returned with nothing but grumbles and empty hands a few hours later, which was appropriate considering they now had a reputable eyewitness bringing news of the murder of the capital's collector. Not only did she have their names, but in Kalsin and Jack's case, she had a much easier identification method; the scars on their faces. Even without the memory or skills required to give a description of their features in detail, the law is going to be hunting for a man with a scar between his eyes, and a woman with a jawline split in the middle by a vertical scar.

For the many thousandth time in his life, Kalsin cursed his bad stroke of luck. As if his freakish eyes weren't enough, a rotting line perched above the bridge of his nose was all the more he needed.

While the azTéls had been—unsuccessfully—hunting for the runaway, Kalsin sought solitude away from prying eyes. He wasn't sure what happened then. His memories were blurry, like a drunken haze. He'd probably wept. Based on his rough throat, most likely screamed his lungs out. But in the end, he'd managed to bottle up the guilt that always threatened to consume him after bloodshed. Guilt that someone as worthless as him, a Scarrd and surgeless outlaw, got to live yet again, while Clenn paid the price for his selfish pursuit of, what. Redemption? Penance? Absolution? Some big, grand fucking word like that.

Kalsin stared into the darkening forest, wondering if Cinder was huddling somewhere nearby in the cold, alone, loyal beyond reason to her little brother. With her knack for appearing and disappearing seemingly at will, Cinder could've made an easy career as some spy or agent for a wealthy Clenn. Hell, maybe even the Civil House.

But Kalsin knew that nothing, not even the promise of a good life, would Cinder ever let come between them.

A sharp elbow to his ribs delivered by Jack nudged Kalsin back to his immediate surroundings. "Sorry," he said. "Could you repeat that?"

"Do you have even the faintest inkling what the word 'heretic' entails?" Áron asked.

"Doesn't matter much, because you're about to enlighten me."

"No. Not really," he said. Noticing Kalsin's threatening shift, he raised a calming hand. "Hold it, now. Like we promised, we're going to tell you everything we know. But I don't want you expecting the wrong thing, Mr. Threaten-murder-to-gain-trust."

Erazza gave him a slightly berating, yet endearing look that only a lifetime of companionship could bring forth. "If you're looking for a way to start the tale, at least do it without antagonizing them," she said.

"You can take over. I don't mind," Áron replied, not unkindly. When she didn't give an answer, he just shrugged.

"What are you two, anyway?" Kalsin asked, deciding he was fresh out of tact. "The collector referred to you as siblings. Around the darkfinder, you claimed you were a couple. I don't mean to intrude, but setting the record straight would be appreciated."

Kalsin swore he could hear Cinder’s mirthful snort coming from somewhere inside the forest.

"Of all people, I'd have thought you best suited to understand the need for deception. Posing as husband and wife was a ruse," Erazza said. Her voice was annoyed, but the question didn't appear to upset her much. "Me and Áron, we're family by choice, not by blood." She didn't elaborate further.

Áron cleared his throat. "Well, that's something I didn't know I wanted out of the way."

He brushed a hand over his hair, as if to make sure he still had some remaining. "Alright. You're probably wondering why the Republic is so determined to keep people like us nice and quiet. The first rumors started surfacing 'round a decade ago, and before you ask me to pinpoint the location—don't, because I've not a clue." He glanced at Erazza, a request for permission. He continued then. "Regular people, both of us were. I was a man who took odd jobs—cutting wood, hauling goods, things like that. Erazza, she worked in a money lender's office. I had just broken through to abyss, and young and ambitious as I was, I started making connections with the overworld to find a benefactor. Help me advance to spark."

"And that's more than enough you ought to know of our past," Erazza suddenly interrupted.

Áron stopped then, but Kalsin could fill in the blanks. It was always the same. Somehow, in the wake of a tragedy or that, their spirits cracked and they became Scarrd. No longer Clenn, it pushed an aspiring abyss to the rank's Scarrd equivalent, a hive, weakening it and forever taking away the possibility of rising to overworld. Sometimes a chain of downfall followed then; despair consumed the loved ones of the fallen Clenn, their sorrow breaking them and knocking even more people into becoming Scarrd. A devilish circle.

Kalsin sometimes wondered if such was a sign of pure and unconditional love; that when your child breaks, your parents break with you. He wanted to believe that. Because then he'd feel justified in the cold lake of hatred he felt toward his parents.

Áron leaned forward then over the fire, voice becoming animated. "So there I was, one day, happening to hear some rumors leaking out from overworld circles, rumors that something unexpected happened. The fallen son of a wealthy Civil House family returned home—with his scar completely gone and his surge restored to rift."

"It was a woman," Erazza said with an eye roll. "You always get this part wrong. The seb hordozó is a woman with the rank of a prism, a step above rift. And she wasn't part of the Civil House, she was the first wife of Arch Freesol himself."

Áron waved it away. "One version I heard claimed it was a deep-fisher's son. Another swore an upstart soldier with grandeurs of a revolution on his mind. Point is, we don't know, alright?" He turned back to Kalsin and Jack, the bodyguard mirroring Kalsin's eager attention. "The news of his, or her, miraculous recovery was snuffed out just as soon as it started to spread. People panicked more than they rejoiced, which, considering it all started in the overworld's exclusively Clenn circles, made perfect sense. But some people who had no reason to uphold the status quo carried word outside, making the story trickle down the hierarchy to reach the lower rungs. People like us. People they later secretly branded, hunted and executed as heretics."

"And the healed Scarrd? This seb hordozó?" Kalsin asked. "What happened to him?"

Áron shook his head. "Nobody knows. Or at least, I don't know anyone who does. You have to understand, word of mouth is a tricky thing. It's a curious phenomenon that keeps alive events surer than the written word of a thousand-page codex, yet distorts its original meaning to a degree that, years later, it becomes impossible to tell truth from fabrication."

"She was a godling, I heard," Erazza said, hugging her legs as she stared into the flames of their campfire. "The daughter of Hadúr sent down to earth, sowing discord between men in order to bring war to the Republic."

Áron spoke even before Erazza finished. "It was a trick created by a shape-shifting mythos, putting on the likeliness of a Scarrd boy, and then returning to his family to show off his unblemished skin, trying to push mankind into chaos for the atrocities toward non-humans."

Erazza smiled. "Some say the seb hordozó was a sister of healing, pious and unrelenting. She performed the miracle on herself so that Scarrd would flock under her care, restoring their souls. She asked nothing in exchange, for she was filled with the goodness and humility of Éden."

They both shared a smile. It wasn't the expression of mirth at a secret joke, but a hopeful joy at something they couldn't quite put into words.

Kalsin gave them a moment. When none of them spoke, he picked up the thread. "That's all very interesting. But it doesn't answer how one goes from Scarrd back to Clenn."

"What in the trinity of gods made you think we had that knowledge?" Erazza said. "A lone pair on the run, barely managing to stay one step ahead of the secret services. Had anyone with a grain of intellect possessed a cure to scars, the first thing on their to-do list would be to surround themselves with devoted followers as they performed miracles. Go around healing everyone, winning the loyalty of thousands until they become untouchable."

She took a deep breath, looking apologetic for the first time since Kalsin had met her. "We cannot heal you. Don't know how to even attempt such a thing. I truly am sorry. The only balm we can give is the assurance that out there, somewhere in the Republic or beyond, our savior, the seb hordozó, is waiting for the right time to reveal themselves. Those of us, named heretics by some and believers by others, will be the first ones to join the cause."

"Until then," Áron took her hand, squeezing it, "We survive."

They didn't know.

They didn't know.

The two people he'd saved, in whose pursuit he'd irreversibly incriminated himself, had no idea about a cure. They were useless idealists no more sane than the mythos he'd just beat to death, spewing nonsense about some distant legend.

Kalsin found himself shaking with pent up anger. No, not anger. Anger always has a clear direction, something you can follow. He felt lost. As lost as you get when you realize you've wasted nearly half your life on absolutely nothing. An empty destination that makes you wish you'd never started the journey to begin with.

"Come with us," he heard Áron say, genuine concern in his voice. "We'll be traveling to Lostpoint Station north of here to join the military. Darkfinder Treyant used to serve as an officer, she gave us a letter of reference. The army is our only chance to slip outside the reach of the Civil House."

Kalsin kept staring ahead, though he saw Jack’s reply as she mimed choking on a noose.

"No, they dismiss criminal charges upon recruitment. It would be a new start. If we arrived there in time."

Kalsin stood. "I, uh. I need some time to think." He could feel Jack’s concerned gaze on his back, but she knew enough not to follow.

It was a peaceful evening. There was no trace of a brewing mid-summer storm customary around Köz of Summer, and even the winds stayed silent to let cricket chirps take the stage. If he closed his eyes, Kalsin could almost pull himself in the memory of a treasure hunt they'd played with his sister as kids.

Cinder could always come up with something that was equally as intriguing as it was ridiculous; a flying ship had just crashed, quickly, to the remnants before it's looted! A monster, inhabitant of a mysterious deep-fracture, had surfaced and collapsed dead on the shore with gems and pearls in his belly. Stupid stuff. Made up silliness that children loved so much, so that one day the grown and bitter man could look back with a smile and say, 'Those were the days'.

Men, they craved adventure of a different kind. Something they could feel and touch, something real. A purpose they embraced and hung on the wall, wistfully gazing at the beautiful image for a single minute in the morning to remember; to remind themselves the reason why they do what they do. A minute of solitude to justify it. Then shut the door, and resume toiling their hours away with back-breaking work. Repeat until repetition becomes routine.

Some never reach that goal. Hell, some never even figure out what their purpose might be. But they make do with the tools they're given, and advance when opportunity allows with steps and leaps toward their perceived fulfillment. Stumble, fall, stay down, then get up. But they never lose sight of that imaginary line far away, the one they had drawn in the sand long ago.

So where do you turn when there's no line in sight anymore? What's the point of walking, falling, and then getting back up without a destination to keep getting back up for?

Kalsin had no more leads. He couldn't scour through every single church in the Republic asking after some elusive godling, and he sure as hell wouldn't be asking shape-shifting mythos whether they just happened to be the sole salvation of the Scarrd human populace in disguise.

"I always reckoned sulkiness to be the least in the row of practical virtues," Cinder said from somewhere above. It took a few seconds for Kalsin to spot her perched between the divide of two thick branches of a tree.

"They have nothing, Cinder. Nothing but a mockery that makes fairy tales coming true appear feasible by contrast."

"Oh, come on," Cinder said, exasperated. Her feet dangled like a child’s. "We don't know any less than we did two weeks ago. What did fathe—" She bit the word off, pausing for a moment. "When the trail runs cold, a hunter retraces his steps without wasting time until he spots a fresh one. Regroups, resets his mindset. Reconsiders the marks in the ground for something he had missed."

"And what does that entail, exactly? I would appreciate something a little more specific."

"How am I supposed to know? My expertise is, uh, moral support. Sometimes, I even manage to come up with a random arbitrary piece of pissin' wisdom. You're the big plan maker."

"Okay," Kalsin said. "Okay." For the sake of his sister, he would pull himself together. His breathing, which had become quick and erratic in the past few minutes, evened out as he focused. Past his worries and his doubts, he tried looking at their situation from a bird's eye view, taking long term events into account.

Their objective was still the same. Heal himself, heal Jack. With no idea how to get closer to the cure, their immediate concern was the warrant—likely being written and distributed to settlements this very second. Like it or not, dodging the hangman's noose took precedence over everything else. They had to delay their search for a cure for now. Being Scarrd was better than being dead. Though not by much.

Kalsin rapped his bandaged fingers against Cinder's tree. "How do you think I'd look in a uniform?"

"Outright diabolical, that's how. Why?"

"Well, rumor has it, signing up as a soldier has become quite popular lately. Especially among ruthless murderers looking for safe haven."

Cinder dropped five feet to the ground, getting up with grace almost enough to prove it was an intentional move. "Ah. And they have a Clenn division as well, or so I hear."

"Nope. You're not enlisting for me."

"Yes, I am."

"No, you're not."

"Yes."

"No," Kalsin turned to walk away.

Only to be tackled from behind.

"Why, I think I will, thank you very much," a victorious Cinder said, claiming her seat on top of a sulking Kalsin. "Not getting rid of me that easily, little guy. Even if I have to sneak to the end of the world."
