Percy spent the next few hours clearing his first handful of channels and activating his new core. Nephthys explained that mind channels generally coincided with one’s nervous system, which was obviously disproportionately concentrated inside a person’s head.

In some ways, Percy was quite lucky that his new core had sprouted right next to his brain, as circulating mana to and from the organ might be slightly more efficient for him.

As much as he hated the circumstances that had forced him to activate the Void Decree, he intended to make the most out of his new asset, as it currently represented the fastest way to raise his strength, while also salvaging as much of his wasted lifespan as possible.

Bringing it on par with his other two cores was Percy’s highest priority, though he knew that there was a limit to how quickly it could advance. At most, he could feed it three half-doses of Gloomy Dawn per day and work towards an artificial advancement at full speed, but it would still take around a decade to reach Green. The rest of his time could be spent on practicing his mind magic – so that he would have an actual use for the organ.

One of the first things he did was to ask Nephthys about the strange orange colour of his mana. He’d seen mind mana many times before, and it had always sported a deep red hue. He understood that the difference was related to the blessing, but that didn’t explain why Baphomet, Phoebe, Nephthys, and all the other mind deities that he had interacted with had always wielded the regular colour. He knew that most – if not all – of those gods had mastered all the auxiliary concepts of their affinity.

‘Each auxiliary concept simply amplifies one of the primary properties of the element,’ the Amenthei goddess explained. ‘The unique colouration is typically caused by this imbalance. After comprehending all the auxiliary concepts and perfecting the affinity, balance is restored and the colour returns to normal.’

‘Can you teach me the basics of mind magic?’ Percy asked.

He had some ideas of his own, but decided that he’d rather keep his mouth shut and listen to the expert first. Nephthys might not be the most knowledgeable mind deity out there, but she’d mastered her affinity and lived for hundreds of thousands of years.

‘The three auxiliary concepts are Cognition, Illusion, and Connection,’ the familiar replied. ‘All mind magic falls under one or multiple of those categories, so I should probably explain what each one does.’

Percy sent her a mental nod to continue.

‘Illusion is the offensive branch of our affinity, and it focuses on tricking an opponent or hindering their thought process in some way. Compared to other elements, ours isn’t very deadly, as illusions can only get you so far. However, most mind mages have no choice but to dip their toes into the branch sooner or later, since it’s typically our only weapon,’ the Ruby Princess explained.

Percy quickly realized that this didn’t apply to him. He had two other affinities to fall back to, including one of the deadliest elements out there. Even when it came to illusions and trickery, he could always rely on his phantoms and Micky’s sentries, so it might not be too efficient to practice this particular branch of mind magic at the moment.

In the future, he might consider weaving a few proper illusions into his spells, but he was far more interested in using his new affinity to support his current fighting style and accelerate his training speed.

‘I know what you’re thinking, and I concur,’ Nephthys said. ‘Keep in mind that you’ll inevitably have to master all the auxiliary concepts one day, but that can wait until after you attain divinity. According to what Zoris and Phoebe had told us, you only need to incorporate the foundational concept into your internal world to keep the affinity, as well as the concept of Connection if you wish to retain your blessing. Other than that, you should specialize in the areas that you deem most relevant to your goals.’

‘What are the other two branches good at?’

‘Cognition is in many ways the most unique and powerful school of mind magic, because it’s much easier for a mind user to augment their own mental state than somebody else’s. They can also take their sweet time preparing their spells, because they don’t necessarily have to activate them during combat.’

‘That sounds a lot like mindset creation,’ Percy pointed out.

‘Indeed,’ the familiar nodded appreciatively through their connection. ‘Mindset creation is the most popular and effective application of this school. It’s technically possible to accelerate one’s thinking speed or organize their memories without a mindset, but it’s better to invest some time into implementing permanent structures inside your head that do what you need them to do.’

‘But anyone can have a mindset even without the affinity,’ Percy protested. ‘Isn’t it a bit of a waste to have this affinity instead of a different one, when you can just hire a specialist for your mindsets?’

‘Not exactly,’ Nephthys replied with a mental chuckle. ‘You are thinking of passive mindsets – which are limited by a brain’s physical capacity. Non-mind users can only fill up to a hundred percent of that capacity. Mind users can feed mana into their mindsets to push them far beyond that limit, supercharging their effects.’

‘How much stronger are we talking about?’ Percy asked.

‘It usually scales linearly with the amount of mana you provide. A Red mind user feeding their mana as fast as they can regenerate it into a single mindset can usually push it by an extra one hundred percent, bringing the total to two hundred. An Orange mage can generate three times as much mana, thus quadrupling the effect of the mindset, and so on.’

Percy’s eyes widened. This was far more powerful than expected. Wouldn’t Violets and Whites experience effects that were a hundred times more potent than a non-mind user’s?

Then again, this was basically the only thing that a mind mage with a single core could do. Their offensive prowess or mobility were practically non-existent. As impressive as the ability to think a hundred times faster was, they might struggle to compete against somebody who could burn entire forests down. No wonder the female Thess’kalan that Percy had faced in the regional tournament had relied on her physical strength and a bloodline that suppressed everyone’s magic.

At the same time, mind mages had a tremendous advantage when practicing spells or trying to comprehend new concepts. This might be one of the reasons mental-type Decrees were so common, and could also explain how Percy had run into so many mind deities, despite it being a rare affinity.

Either way, Percy was definitely interested in delving into this branch.

He’d already wanted to hire a specialist to upgrade his Enhanced Cognition into something that wasn’t mass-produced. Now, he had the option of not only personally improving the mindset, but of supercharging it with his own mana.

‘And the last branch? The one I have an actual blessing for?’

‘The concept of Connection helps with communication, smoothening out mental transmissions and managing large groups of people. The spells that belong purely in this branch aren’t too exciting, but the concept is used heavily by the other schools. For example, when granting mindsets to other people, or subjecting an opponent to an illusion.’

‘What do you think I should focus on?’ he asked.

‘You’d be most suited for the schools of Connection and Cognition,’ Nephthys replied, rather unsurprisingly. ‘Like I said, Cognition in general and mindset creation in particular are basically the crown jewel of our affinity. The only drawback is that focusing entirely on your own mental processes can leave you toothless, but that’s not an issue for somebody who has access to other affinities. As for the concept of Connection… For one, it would be a shame not to take advantage of your blessing. For another, it might synergize quite well with your bloodline, due to your clones, Micky’s sentries and my wasp subordinates.’

‘Will you teach me?’ he asked, some hope spilling into his voice.

‘Of course,’ Nephthys replied warmly. ‘However, I would advise you to experiment with the element on your own for a couple of days. You seem to have a knack for using your affinities in unexpected ways, and I wouldn’t want my own preconceptions to blind you to new possibilities.’

Percy thanked his familiar before taking a stroll along the settlement, instinctively making his way to Freddy’s stand. It had been years since he’d last gone out to grab a snack by himself. He could use some fresh air and human contact, given how intense the last few weeks had been.

As he walked, he considered Nephthys’ suggestions. He appreciated that his mentor was open-minded enough to let him try his own things, and he was excited to experiment with the new mana type by himself, yet he wasn’t sure how he could possibly accomplish something in a few days that a former goddess had failed to come up with after countless millennia.

‘I’ll have to take advantage of any abilities that I have and she didn’t,’ he decided.

There were quite a few of those, with the first on the list being the very ability that had jumpstarted his entire magical journey: his bloodline.

Clone had only ever done something useful when paired with the life and soul affinities. Pure or water mana just produced short-lived, human-shaped puppets that collapsed almost immediately. Percy had no idea whether there had ever been a person in his family that had both the bloodline and a mind affinity.

‘Could it work?’ he wondered, before shrugging. ‘I suppose there’s only one way to know.’
