Chapter 144: The Next Flavor of Roach

As the minor explosion went off in the meeting room, any metal that ended up hitting pretty much anything turned to liquid ‘sauce globs’ that slung around harmlessly.

Memoria doubled over in laughter.

“What the hell, dude?!” Lindsay exclaimed, glaring daggers at Jack, as she flung metal goo from out of her hair.

“Ewww!” Permafrost cried, who’d gotten a particularly large blast, dripping down her face and coating her hands. She only glanced at Jack, instead directing her spite toward Memoria. “Don’t you dare let Jack take the fall for this, Mother!”

“Yeah, that was her doing,” the guy with gray-blue skin said, frowning at the Archon, though he actually seemed to have avoided or blocked the metal goo entirely, pristine and untouched.

Memoria looked like she was trying to say something, but couldn’t through the laughter, instead just sputtering.

Meanwhile, a few were laughing along with her, such as Highfive. Also, Jetset, who’d dodged it all and was literally leaning against Memoria as he laughed and patted her arm. They more or less were ‘hanging’ on each other.

A brat, you say? He learned from the best, apparently.

Lattice had not reacted at all, though metal fell from her chin to the table. She almost seemed to have meditatively ‘stepped away’ in the midst of the aftermath.

Solvent merely nodded in self-congratulation as he looked up at the hammer. “Nice.”

Neex- where was Neex? Oh. There she was, peeking from up underneath the table, making sure the coast was clear.

Jack winced through it all and held up his hands. “Sorry! I did not think that would happen!”

Because Memoria didn’t seem inclined, Jack took the initiative of cleaning up, using his powers to pull the ‘runny’ metal off of people and shunting it into the corner.

With things dying down somewhat, Solvent cleared his throat and said, “A demonstration, as requested, Mother.”

Memoria, wiping tears from her eyes, sighed and nodded, with one last snicker. “Yes. Hnnk! Thank you for that, my son.”

“Can you explain what you just did, please?” Conflux asked Solvent eagerly. “That boost was clearly incredible! What happens if you boost another booster?!”

Solvent smiled appreciatively at her. “Ah, youth asking the right questions, how wonderful! To put it plainly, I borrow Allotment out of a collective and concentrate it on one member. There are caps, it isn’t one-for-one, and I can borrow more from those closer to my level or above it. Passive Allotment boosts fade immediately when switching, but I have a secondary mutation that allows ‘external’ effects to fade more slowly. In practice, I can boost some combination of two or three agents performing active powers, switching between them. If some of those are boosters themselves, we can hyper-boost one person.”

Around the table, there was a burst of excited exclamations and commentary about the possibilities this offered.

Bermuda snapped her fingers loudly, cutting it all off as her eyes swept coolly around the table. “You’ll have plenty of time to theorycraft later. We already have plenty of intel from Agent Boiler on Solvent’s synergies, so trust that it combines functionally with all of you. Solvent is already very familiar with most of our vets, including Lattice, Crash, Permafrost, and Spiral. The point is to have a very strong, versatile overall team on-site, under the sea, despite the symbolism of our youthful, less-threatening representation.”

“There’s only so much that can be done beyond the borders,” Permafrost said, sighing. “I’m not saying the aliens would do anything, but they’ll almost certainly be stronger inside of theirs.”

Memoria grinned and swept her hands over to Jack. “One of our newest members changes the game in that respect. Very few of you know, yet. Jack?”

With all eyes suddenly on him, Jack cleared his throat and said, “Right. I was told this was rare — it seems that proximity to my memorite, ideally touching it, allows enhanced performance away from Memoria. It’s eighty percent functionality, or seventy percent if not touching, declining gradually with distance.”

Mostly stunned silence answered this — other than Highfive, who was grinning triumphantly and drinking in the reactions of others with glee.

“Welp,” Jetset said, smiling at Jack winningly, “I’ll go ahead and take you on as my sidekick, then, buddy. I know, I know, it’s such an honor, but you deserve it.”

“You are too kind, Jet,” Jack replied sardonically.

Lindsay sighed grandly and shook her head. “Everyone’s gonna want a pocket Jack now.”

“Yeah, I’ll take one,” the guy with gray-blue skin said, raising his hand. “I’m Crash, by the way. I’m the resident water powers guy, if anyone was wondering-”

Solvent, face painted with excitement, lightly slapped the table with both hands as he leaned forward, interrupting rather loudly. “This is fantastic; cause for celebration! The potential outside the borders is outstanding!” Turning to Jack, he asked, “How far can you extend this influence, exactly?”

There’s a lot of table-slapping at these meetings…

Jack cleared his throat and replied, “Quite far, thanks to one of my mutations called ‘Anchors.’ It lets me make autonomous, micro-instances of my power. I can make the minimum and hand it off, and it’ll passively work over a hundred kilometers away. The instance will be inert but function for performance improvement, according to Memoria.”

Memoria added, “It’s wherever your summoned memorite is, son. Normally, it would dissipate and return to you if too far separated. But as long as it’s ‘out,’ if you will, it’s providing passive routing back to me, and thus an improved interface with the greater System. They are like moving bases with heavy-duty antennae.”

The Archon turned to the table at large. “Agent Ferro can make up to seven of these at the moment, but I prefer not to have him maxed out, so we’re going to focus on up to five separations of our teams, with one optional division saved for our core team with Jack, and the final one reserved for Jack’s personal power’s use, except in the most extreme scenarios. Think of an Anchor like a flag you should keep track of. The team leaders will have it on their person, and others on a given team need to stick around them, especially in dangerous scenarios.”

“Can other entities sense that about the Anchors?” Lighthouse asked. “Target it? I assume they’re going to be relatively fragile, not to mention potentially detachable.”

“Good question. I’d think not by default, but information specialists could detect it. The zone you’ll be in is most threatened by unknown oceanic entities of great variety and difficulty fathoming. The area around the Calm is still a relative wilderness. The land enemies, where relevant in the zone, are almost all Muties. It’s a crapshoot with them.”

“We want these Anchors well-protected and secure,” Bermuda said. “Fortunately, the form is customizable. Ferro will form the material required into a tough metal cord of a specific design that goes inside a reinforced belt of the ANP inner suit. Removing it will take a few moments to unclasp and pull out the protective housing. But, in this way, damaging the material is difficult, and very unlikely from mere accidents.

“Our five divisions of the Anchors are as followed: the core team, Team Escort, twelve members plus the escorted, Neex, under the carrier and leader, Agent Solvent; Team Horse, who’ll stay on the principle transporting submarine as potential cavalry support; Team Watcher, who’ll occupy another submarine by default sticking to the surface; Agent Jetset, who’ll technically be under Team Watcher, but will have his own Anchor for mobility response; and, finally, Agent Mantis, our long-range scout who is not actually present today. He’ll be ostensibly under Team Watcher.

“We’ll have two other teams involved without Anchors: Team Hardpoint, which will remain at Fort Elpis, and Team Homebase, here, including everyone present not actually going on the trip. That concludes all teams and divisions. They are subject to change at any given change in circumstance or gathered intelligence. As for our escorted ‘candidate’ types, their team assignment remains fluid depending on the Deucalian response. Ideally, they’ll go with Team Escort; otherwise, it just depends on our field assessment.”

“I’ll just note real quick,” Jack said, “that wherever I am, I can easily plop a bit of memorite on every member that’s within my operational range, and give the maximum benefit to.”

“Yes,” Memoria replied, “and you should do that as a priority under most circumstances. Nonetheless, Agent Solvent will have an Anchor at all times, and everyone had better get used to quickly allowing him to utilize his powers on them, by the way. Do not slow him down or gum him up with hesitation or delays. Another important note: keeping Jack conscious is critical to all of this. If he’s ever disrupted from his power use during the operation, it all unravels, and the distant memorite will vanish. So let it sink in: protect him at all costs.”

In the silence, Permafrost suddenly snorted, leaned back, and had a big grin as she regarded Jack. “Jack is our queen, then.” Scattered chuckles answered this.

Jack groaned. “Can we go with king?”

“Nope!” Permafrost shook her head emphatically even as she maintained her shit-eating grin. “It’s a chess reference, Mr. Machismo. That would be odd, now, wouldn’t it? Get over yourself already!”

As more laughter resounded, Jack relented with a wince, holding his hands up in surrender. He’d heard that some role-switched the queen’s moves and role as the checkmate target to the king — for some reason — but that was considered rather weird and crass in New Babylon. Everyone knew the queen had to be protected. That was just obvious.

For some reason, Memoria’s eyes were cast to the side suspiciously.

“What if he falls asleep?” the glowy-eyed lady, Lattice, asked.

“He can’t,” Memoria said with a sigh. “If the mission goes long enough, we’ll have to arrange a ‘re-convening’ session to deal with the issue. Let him sleep, and then redo the Anchors.”

“Can’t I keep my powers going while sleeping?” Jack asked.

Memoria frowned. “With just about any non-metamorph — yourself included — weird and dangerous things can happen like that, even if you somehow avoid the probability of simply dropping them at any given point, including immediately after falling asleep.”

“What about utilizing Calibration?”

Memoria blinked and raised an eyebrow. “Such as for minor training cementation? Well, that’s an interesting theoretical sidestep. We’ve used it before to cheat communications to me in certain unusual scenarios. I’m not sure you can manage to sleep inside of it. No one has ever tried it. If you could, perhaps that would work. Powers do ‘pause’ if in operation; it’s my option to cancel them for safety. They have to be canceled to enact actual change, for instance. For training calibration and cementation, it isn’t necessary, though.”

“I think the reason no one has tried it is simple,” Solvent offered. “You cannot be tired when you try the trance. You just fall asleep without ever entering it.”

“Ah, shit!” Jack exclaimed. “That’s true.”

“Oh, that,” Lattice said dismissively. “You can learn how to meditate into it, even when tired. It’s just a technique thing.”

Memoria nodded. “That’s true: some rare individuals learn to ignore the tiredness component, though I don’t regard it as an exact science.”

“Can you teach me that, maybe, Lattice?” Jack asked.

Lattice considered it thoughtfully before answering. “Maybe. It’s worth a shot.”

“I think this concludes our rundown today,” Bermuda declared. Suddenly, a huge packet of information assaulted Jack’s brain through the interface. “I’ve sent the rest of the mission specs to your direct or external interfaces, as appropriate; all the gory details are there. Feel free to peruse them at your leisure, or you can use the facilities here over the next hour. You leave in three days, dark and early, to cross the bottom of our planet. Use your time to gain familiarity with the mission, but most of all, to know your teammates. Training is advisable, but we leave the rest to the team leaders. Otherwise, this meeting is hereby adjourned.”

“Team Escort,” Solvent called, “let’s break off to the side in fifteen after checking over the specs.”

Muttered agreements followed as nearly everyone stared off into the middle distance, reviewing things internally. Most of those present had lived with that interface for many years, if not decades. Jack still found it a tad awkward, but he was getting more used to it by the day.

The route was indeed headed somewhat closer to the South Pole, which was called ‘west’ from New Babylon, going by the standard Antarctic map, because the location was in the ‘western hemisphere’ as separated by the meridian, which dictated ‘grid north.’ They would cross the prime meridian to hit the Weddell Coast and Fort Sledge, still just within the eighty degrees latitude band, which any land within was considered well-secured Memorial Territory. Another way he could look at their travel was as ‘counterclockwise’ if the South Pole was the center of a wall clock, and ‘12’ was along the prime meridian. In that case, they would go from around the ‘three’ to the ‘eleven,’ very roughly speaking.

Historically, the area was once under an ice shelf, and there certainly wasn’t a coastline at Fort Sledge back then, though those simulated images of gargantuan amounts of ice covering the beautiful landscapes he piloted across were difficult to believe as real.

Once finished at Fort Sledge, the route went across the ocean between, to Palmer Island, a very weird-looking landmass on the maps, long and winding, with a tail far from Memoria, nearly to sixty degrees latitude, and one of the most terrible, dangerous wildernesses on their maps. Here was where the ‘Muties’ tried irregularly to gain footholds; these days, by very clandestine operations that they were apparently getting better and better at.

Evolution at work, I guess.

There was information on them in the packet, which Jack ate up with interest, recalling the very generic versions of them being used as props for various training scenarios in intensive military training regimens, and seeing the parallels here and there. Unlike the Phanties, they were almost entirely biotech-oriented, forming endlessly custom monstrosities under certain basic patterns.

It was stated in bold: ‘Fortunately, these entities are specialized in their abilities toward intimate mass melee tactics, and may have conquered the tail of South America on this foundation. Ranged supremacy, particularly aerial, is the best policy, avoiding multiple enemy Archon-derived benefits.’

Nonetheless, they’d adapted to warfare with humanity over time, developing powerful physical, kinetic projectile launchers, and even some sort of potent chemical laser or heat ray, very capable for anti-drone warfare, missile defense, as well as an excellent general weapon option. They remained weak on ordnance capabilities and were currently stuck in the realm of ambushes, terrorism threats, and skirmishing against humanity. Recently, they’d scaled back, almost perfectly in line with Memoria winning the ‘Allotment War’ and gaining constantly in their altercations, almost on the order of a sixty/forty percent split.

It was an interesting framing. The current assessment was that the engagements were called ‘Near Zero Cost,’ which was defined as only needing ‘drone versus drone’ in ninety-five percent of cases. Conventional resource expenditures were considered irrelevant, so this was very ‘low risk’ on both sides, which specifically meant not losing significant power-users and holders of Allotment. These were used very sporadically due to the risk of losing it.

There was a known enemy entity called ‘Little Reaver’ out there, a stealth specialist they could not rid themselves of, like the world’s worst cockroach. But when Memoria discovered a typical ‘hive,’ she’d long-range bomb it into oblivion and send hunter-killer drones to mop up.

Despite a streak of dominance by Memoria and several close calls on both sides, virtually no significant Allotment had been obtained in months, and things proceeded like clockwork, though the Muties were apparently experimenting more with stealth and ‘abnormal tactics’ in the process. In any case, the enemy’s chances of successfully evading detection long enough to establish a foothold within a year were graded at just under a half percent, and Memoria had no plans of adjusting to improve that any time soon, due to other priorities.

Very interesting. Encouraging, even. They’re nowhere near us like the Phanties, and they’re getting their asses handed to them. It does worry when things feel one-sided, though, doesn’t it?

The PM of the Mutants was known as ‘Paramount Reaver,’ though no further information was provided. The Archon of the Mutants had an unusual mouthful of a name: “Amalgamation Thrumming Heart Changing Metamorphosis Spawn.” Heartspawn for short.

Ooookay. Thankfully, that didn’t form a good acronym, or we’d be suffering with another.

Jack found himself glancing at Memoria, who was in a little powwow with a few others, including Jetset and InSite. She met his gaze, grinned, and winked.

In his mind, she said, “I know that look. You’re weirded out about that name, aren’t you?”

“Oh, please,” Jack replied dismissively. “You didn’t get that from a look; you read my damn mind!”

“Regardless, you want an explanation, don’t you?”

“Yes. Amalgamation what-a-who-who? What is all that about?”

“A translation, to put it plainly. At any form of contact, we know one another to a certain degree. That includes a true name — ridiculous as it may sound to us, here on our particular wavelength of sanity.”

“Huh. Fascinating. Does that mean you’re on, like, ‘friendly rivalry’ terms, or something, with them? As cosmic alien entities, or whatever?”

“You really do have an imagination, don’t you? Hell no, Jack! I hate that revolting scumbucket with every fiber of my being. I’d like nothing more than to pry its creepy, mucousy fingers away from historical Patagonia and claim the land as our own again, while sending it to oblivion forevermore.”

“Wow. Okay, then. Is that feasible?”

“One day, perhaps. If enough of our new alchemical brew continues to persist, thrive, and bubble up higher and higher.”

“Meaning the power interactions of agents like Solvent and me?”

“Indeed. And perhaps the brew can become even more potent after we add what other ingredients Q-Loth can unlock for us. Mm-hmm! Delicious, I think!”

“Alright, enough beverage talk, Mother — it’s making me thirsty, and something tells me Agent Bermuda won’t be bringing me a can today.”

“I’ll do you a solid and cast my memory of that intrusive thought into the Sea of Forgetfulness where it belongs, Soldier, never to be mentioned again.”

“You are wise and merciful as always, Mother.”

They exchanged a grin and disconnected.

He ended up meeting Lindsay’s eyes by chance, and they simply exchanged some nonverbal, uncanny thing, a look that said something like, ‘What a life, huh? Here we are together in it.’

That was all, and perhaps all that was needed.

Heartspawn, is it? Sounds like a good name for squishing underfoot, I’d say.
