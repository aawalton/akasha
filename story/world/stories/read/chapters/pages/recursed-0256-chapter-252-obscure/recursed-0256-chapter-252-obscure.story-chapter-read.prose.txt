“I don’t know how I missed that,” Alysyra said at Nyxalraith’s side, her voice hushed. “I’ve walked this tunnel a hundred times. At least.”

This place was about the furthest from what she would expect to hide a hive of the Mad. The branching alleys of square access ports were old, but not decrepit or even unreasonably worn down. It was quiet, sure. Not nearly quiet enough to hide the entrance to what was, if discovered, likely to upend all of Coral.

Madness was, after all, the most feared and despised phenomenon.

They’d peeked into more than a dozen of the shutters lining the wide tunnel veins. Almost all were active. Most were used as storage — nothing worth taking — but there were enough that held actual active cult operations. All of low import. The people moving around inside, and the pair they’d avoided walking down the connecting alley, they were all low creeds. It was incredibly unlikely they even knew they were sitting on top of the largest resurgence of Madness in over a century.

These weren’t plants. Not unless they could somehow fool her eyes.

But despite the complete lack of indication that this was anything beyond a normal low corruption working sector, when Nyxalraith did find something, she dismissed it for how consistent it was with everything else. Only when Alysyra hadn’t followed and had seemingly ‘lost’ her, did they realise they’d found the entrance.

It was simply another branching alley. Just like any other. Considering the lacking corruption in the area, there wasn’t any visual warping to the paths they walked, yet what Nyxalraith had perceived, Alysyra had seen nothing but an unbroken wall.

A mental suppression, much like her concealer? Or perhaps a masterful manipulation of corruption?

However it was made, it was certainly intentional. And far too structurally sound to be a fabrication of what some Mad imagined into reality. That was all she needed to confirm that this really was a cult behind this and not some festering infection.

The immediate entrance was unmanned. That was typical. If you wanted to keep one of your passages hidden, you didn’t give it living guards that were more likely to give away the location to any onomastician proficient in detection, or really anyone with names good at detecting life. The Technocult lived by this. Even the Cultless knew better than to keep eyes on their entrances at all times, despite being far less impressive at actually concealing them than what Nyxalraith stood in now.

Alysyra wasn’t physically present, but Nyxalraith’s eyes pierced the girl’s invisibility and followed her expression. She twisted back and forth. She leaned forward, then took a step back. It was clear that her perception was muddled, and the fake Bodytwister was trying to force comprehension even though her mind felt the wall was impenetrable. Alysyra’s hand traced along the wall, and it took visible effort — a furrowing of the brows as if she were solving a brain-twister — to push past the illusion.

Though the entrance was unguarded, Nyxalraith highly doubted whoever was experimenting with Madness was going to leave their secrecy to this single barrier. There would be traps. Traps meant to eviscerate any hint of word getting out. And that was before she would reach the damn near army of Mad that were waiting within.

Worst case, the place was rigged to blow. All evidence would be erased, and so would Ari.

“You should probably wait here,” Nyxalraith said. “The Mad have a tendency to ignore defences. I don’t know if they’d be able to affect you through your replicas, but better to be safe.”

Alysyra took a step back as if to do as asked immediately, but stopped. “No. I can help. After my latest evolution… I think I’ll be fine even if they attack directly. Their attention will be on you, right? It’ll be much easier for me to stab them in the heads while they’re focused elsewhere.”

Nyxalraith stared. As true as it was that Alysyra’s presence would be helpful if there was another Mad that decided to contain her in a padded room or something similar, she felt it was too much a risk. The Mad chimera of man and boy she’d faced had seen through her concealer. She had no doubt Alysyra, no matter how good her invisibility name was, would be noticed.

As obvious as it was that she was trying to make up for her earlier blunder, it wasn’t worth bringing her along just to make her feel better. Not considering the risk.

Nyxalraith wasn’t even sure she wanted to go in on a mass-murder spree as she had against the Cult of the Everseeing Eye. She was certainly itching to draw blood of those that had kidnapped Ari, but she had to keep her mind clear of bloodlust. At least until that once again became her objective.

There was a reason she hadn’t simply teleported into the den the moment Euphoria had told her where it was. First and foremost: that would not go unnoticed. It would stir up the hornets’ nest and more than likely put Ari in more danger than she already was. Given she didn’t exactly know the girl’s current state — she intentionally didn’t think about the botched patchworking of the man-boy horror — Nyxalraith had to approach this with less explosive force than she’d typically like.

At least until Ari was safe. Then… well, she wasn’t about to leave a bunch of the Mad free through Coral to catch her off-guard again.

“For now, just keep tabs on the search squads. If any of them look like they’re coming close to finding Euphoria, activate the ritual I gave you.”

On top of perfecting the god’s prison, the ability to call upon her own name in rituals had plenty more uses. Most relevant of which was what she’d dubbed an ‘alert’. It was a basic ritual that reached for Nyxalraith’s name and nothing more. Considering how obvious such a use was, she would know immediately.

For now, she’d only given it to Alysyra, but she had plans to make slightly altered versions for both the Technocult and the Cultless. Nyxalraith wanted to avoid the eventuality that they were attacked while she was off hunting somewhere.

Alysyra looked like she wanted to argue the point, but gave in without a fight. “How exactly will I know when they get ‘too close’? You uh… didn’t leave the space around the god’s containment in a state I would consider legible.”

Nyxalraith shrugged. “When they start laughing.”

The corruption cradled Dark Star had twisted up Coral’s guts enough that she doubted anyone would find it any time soon. Even having felt the pulse of the Event’s creation, they would probably be looking in all the wrong places. Dark Stars were typically massive and destroyed massive swathes of Coral. Anyone hunting for one would almost certainly expect to stumble across it sooner rather than later, without any actual effort.

Compressed as Euphoria’s binding was, it might as well be invisible behind the waves of corruption it spilled into its surroundings.

Nyxalraith paused to consider that. A rogue Dark Star would usually call upon condemnation from the cults. Nobody liked their communal infrastructure being damaged, after all. It wasn’t like the response to abusing Madness — not even close — but they were usually rare enough that the other cults could extract decent recompense from the accused.

… How many had Nyxalraith created now?

In her entire past life, only two Dark Stars had formed… as far as she knew. Infrequent enough that she imagined there wasn’t even a dedicated tracking group with the proper names to find one. And if there were, what would they think about all Dark Stars she’d been experimenting with in the safety of Pink? Did any hint of those spill out when she opened her portal?

“Alright,” Alysyra’s hesitance was clear in her tone. “Don’t die.” And with that, she was gone in a shower of white motes.

Carefully, Nyxalraith moved into the hidden street. Walls of steel shutters and the bright glow of LEDs did much to counter her expectations that every corner should hide a deadly trap. A foul chained beast ready to shear flesh from bone, or moving walls ready to swallow her whole. None of that. Instead, as her shadows crept through every crevice, she found the place spotless. There wasn’t a single thing out of place to any of her eyes.

It was just another branching alley. The same as all the others they’d already passed, with no other tunnels leading outward. Really, the only difference was how quiet these rows were.

Momentarily, Nyxalraith wondered if Euphoria hadn’t been as forthcoming as he acted. The consciousness warping inflicted by the main path made her inclined to believe something was hidden here, but for all she knew it was something completely unrelated to Ari. To Euphoria, it might as well be a joke.

If the god was lying… well, he would very quickly find out about some of the more undesirable functions of his cell.

Honestly, she hoped he was lying about some things. Ari’s drawings in particular. She knew very well that the obsession with the black art had begun when Ari had drawn a portrait of Nyxalraith. It would be concerning if Euphoria’s reaction to them hadn’t just been him trying to mess with her.

What exactly had Ari been drawing?

As she moved through the third shuttered warehouse, Nyxalraith again found nothing out of the ordinary. Each of the rooms were furnished as expected. Shelves and boxes of equipment for those used more for storage, and tools, benches and other furniture in those designed more as work-spaces.

The problem was — and Nyxalraith didn’t get this feeling until she’d searched through half the rooms — they were too perfect. Each room held the visage of some of the set-ups she’d seen down other branching alleys, but not a single one held a remnant of human passage. They looked like they did, sure. A brush was discarded on the side of the table and there were ink splatters dotting the table around the inkwell. There were scuff marks where unbolted shelving racks touched the ground. But the impurities felt almost… perfect. Curated.

It took a while to be certain, but her eyes didn’t lie. They were too sharp to be fooled. This entire place was designed to appear inconspicuous. No different than any of the neighbouring halls.

The only reason Nyxalraith could think of for going through the effort of making convincing dummy rooms rather than just taking a bunch of recently abandoned ones would be so the creators could control the materials used. Which would be particularly useful if there was a highly complex ritual in play.

That… or she was just seeing things.

It was when she was searching through the sixth room that she was proven right, and Euphoria was proven not to be a liar.

She had noticed the single pinprick of blood immediately. Well, Yog did. It was so small the basic human eye probably wouldn’t have noticed it in the rough browns of the synthetic rug. This was the first sign of human passage that she actually believed was not a fabrication.

The pinprick was between the large shutter doors and one of four heavy wooden desks ,so Nyxalraith immediately began ruffling through the fake mess on its surface. As she moved a book her eyes immediately picked up the natural lines of ritual etched into the desk. They were carved to look like random scratches.

And well, as they were, they might as well have been.

A large central chunk inside the ritual lines were gibberish. They stopped the lines from completing, and truly did make the rest of the runes appear like random scribbles by someone bored and with nothing better to do.

But the shape where the form died away to trash was sound. And immediately Nyxalraith began skipping through pages in the book.

It didn’t take long at all to find. An activation sequence. Painted in dried blood on page thirty eight. All she would need to do was fold the book by its spine and press the page into the desk and the ritual would activate. There was also a sneaky alert etched into the book, but Nyxalraith already had Yog remove the unnecessary lines.

If Ari and the Mad were anywhere, they were hidden behind whatever door this ritual concealed, so Nyxalraith didn’t hesitate to press the book into the desk and activate the neutered ritual. Given her edge sight hadn’t seen even the slightest hint of another room hidden behind this one, she was very curious of where the door would appear.

The little of the ritual she’d seen carved in the table before it bled away into the wood and likely hid beneath the floors gave her at least the confidence to say it was a door. Or something to be opened.

Nyxalraith watched the runes light up across the room and in an instant, she declared never to activate rituals she didn’t entirely understand again.

A rift opened.

The back half of the room caved away only to unravel into a Null Scar. Dull grey light from a world not her own carried with it screams and laughs alike.

Ah. She finally understood. The Mad are hidden away in the confines of a Null Scar.

Her eyes pierced the window to another realm in a way the human eye couldn’t. She saw the edge of that world, and it was small. She could see the other side, where another Null Scar carved a second entrance to elsewhere on Coral. A place she immediately became aware of.

This Null Scar, and all the Madness inside, connected to the Library.

The Scriptures meddled with Madness.
