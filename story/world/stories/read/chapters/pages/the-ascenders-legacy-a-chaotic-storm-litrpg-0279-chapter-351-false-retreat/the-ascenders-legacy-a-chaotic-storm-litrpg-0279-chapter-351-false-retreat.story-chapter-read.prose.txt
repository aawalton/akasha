A severed arm hit the mud and twitched for a full three seconds before the body it belonged to realized it was dead.

No one spared it a second glance.

Those were the sights that had greeted Seamus and Darien for the past three days.

Three days of brutal battle that had passed in a blur of blood, gore, and death for the warring sects.

And for those three days, Seamus and Darien had been in the thick of it—so much so—that the scent of death now clung to their robes, and gore to their fingernails. Their eyes had hardened. Their hearts were heavy, and after three days of near-constant slaughter, they were both learning that it was one thing to watch others tear enemies apart... and another thing entirely to do it yourself, limb from limb.

But the worst part was not the killing.

It was how quickly it had become routine.

Still, they did it well. Too well.

While transmigrants subdued the battlefield with the strength of their spirits, Darien and Seamus drenched it in the fiery flames of their willpower. They never seemed in danger of running dry. They never rationed themselves. They simply hurled more willpower at every problem that rose before them.

Skills. Rules. Traps. Decrees.

Whatever came, they met it head-on, willpower blazing.

And interestingly, it worked.

So well, in fact, that Elder Ghaltris, who had been hesitant to send them out to battle all this while, was now perfectly content to deploy them unsupervised, just as he had on the first day, while completely relegating the transmigrants to the rear under the justification that they needed rest after two weeks of constant warfare.

Despite their frustration at being cooped up for the past three days, none of the transmigrants could deny he was right. They needed rest—not just physically, but mentally. Their nerves were frayed, their minds shot, and their reactions had become unnecessarily violent.

They needed normalcy.

Aodhán knew it. Nymera knew it. They all did.

Yet as they watched Seamus and Darien battle across the viewing screens, they couldn’t help but feel uneasy.

“Oh, that Seraphine is a menace,” Nymera growled low as she watched Seamus shatter Glass World with a booming burst of thunder, only for it to reform around him in an instant. “I hate that I like how her mind works.”

“Like a machine,” Sophran grimaced, feeling a twinge of pain in his spirit. “Always fucking analyzing. If I hadn’t run, I never would have survived.”

Aodhán frowned. “Seamus seems to be holding his own.”

“For now,” Sophran said, shaking his head. “Let’s hope he keeps it that way.”

“He will,” Ashscar muttered, twirling a crimson-gold feather between his fingers. “This battle isn’t just about strength. It’s about counters.” The memory of his battle against Volgrim flashed in his mind and he grimaced. “Seraphine countered you too cleanly. Seamus not nearly as much.”

Sophran gave a small shrug. Cultivating the Path of Relentless Velocity, Seamus was simply a better counter to Seraphine’s skillset than he had been.

“Still,” Sophran muttered, “I wish Nymera had killed her on the first day. Make another statement.”

“I wanted to,” Nymera shifted awkwardly, and I would have, had I not been dragged into battle with that Serenity transmigrant.” She shook her head. “I still cannot understand how Aodhán survived that.”

“I barely did,” Aodhán grunted, glaring at the screen. “But like Ashscar said, it was less about power and more about counters. I had a skill that let me hold my own against him. At least for a few minutes. You didn’t.”

“Ugh.” She scowled. “Don’t remind me of that humiliation ritual. I felt completely powerless inside his bubble. The storm could barely respond.”

“Once again,” Ashscar said with a nod, “counters.”

He glanced toward Aodhán.

“This skill that helped you resist Alaric... I take it Darien has something similar?”

Aodhán looked back to the screen, a slow smile spreading across his face as Darien clashed against Alaric head-on. He grinned. “He does.”

But they were wrong.

Darien’s centered skill wasn’t something similar. It was something entirely different.

Sanctum was not a skill Darien had gained merely to combat rage or suppress emotion. It was containment built to survive trauma. Forged to survive the rage of an abusive father.

Sanctum was an escape, both from physical and emotional extremities. It was a mental state that bled into the physical—manifesting not as force or escalation, but as cognitive sovereignty, emotional stabilization, and an absolute refusal to be shaped by external forces.

It was inviolable.

And despite Alaric’s many attempts to impose stillness and serenity upon his attacks, Darien refused to be altered.

Where Aodhán had found his center through agreement—creating a competing equilibrium that undermined Alaric’s claim to universal balance—Darien found his through refusal.

And with that refusal, he fought now, declaring with each strike that serenity held no authority over him.

Willpower surged from him in violent waves as he clashed against Alaric once more, hundreds of crackling constructs slicing forward to tear the man apart in an endless wave of force that completely ignored every attempt at moderation or suppression.

Alaric attacked with constructs of his own, teeth gritted in visible annoyance as he was forced yet again into an offensive stance.

Back in the viewing deck, Aodhán’s grin widened further. “He does have something similar.”

For the rest of that day, the battle raged without much change. Water Mythics raised floods against the enemy. Wind Mythics called down cutting gales. Lightning Mythics leveraged the unrelenting storm above them, and the shadow sect did what they did best.

Stick to the shadows and assassinate.

Seamus and Darien held their own powerfully and broken formations began to gather once more.

The war was going well. Brutal, but well.

They all expected the same to happen the next day.

But the next morning, only a stroke past midnight, something odd happened.

The enemy transmigrants were called away from the battlefield.

Enemy Mythics stopped fighting, and enemy Calamities simply vanished mid battle.

The absence was so sudden, so jarring, that for a moment no one moved.

And then—

Aodhán felt an immense rise of energy, so overwhelming it blinded his senses. He turned, eyes wide, mouth opening to shout a warning—

But he never formed the words before the viewing deck exploded.

Far away from the battlefield, deep within the roiling tides of chaotic space, a ship detached from the mothership— a mere speck beside the gigantic spearhead of living alloy and void-forged crystal that was the greater vessel.

The mothership loomed like a forged god suspended in the abyss, its hull stretching from horizon to horizon like a city wrought in starlight and anchored in vacuum; its layered districts vast enough to put entire cities to shame.

The departing vessel on the other hand was laughably small against such immensity. A splinter beside a mountain. A shard beside a god-forged blade.

Yet small did not mean insignificant.

Despite its size, the ship was large enough to house thousands of spatial Mythics, two spatial Calamities, two spatial transmigrants, and one very amazed storm agent standing wide-eyed before a viewing array as the fabric of space folded and refolded around them.

Only four days had passed in the outer world since Kivasmus found the gate. But time moved differently within chaotic space. In comparison, he had spent nearly twice that within the Space Sect itself—touring their vast futuristic city, studying sect culture, and learning the taxonomy of the entities and monsters prowling the void.

Unsurprisingly, there were many.

And there had been battle. Constant battle.

Not a single day had passed without it.

Monsters, it turned out, were beyond abundant within chaotic space. Twice, they'd been forced to weather massive hordes of void, space, and chaos-maddened creatures, several of which ranked in the high Mythic and Calamitic classes.

It was a supreme delving ground. There was no doubt of that.

But though Kivasmus had indulged in a few battles himself from the safety of a space suit, slaying epic monsters was not why Kivasmus remained in the Space Sect.

He had stayed to speak directly with the First Seat. To hand her the pendants and present his terms, or rather, the Storm Sect's terms of alliance.

And eventually, after seven days of waiting, the news had come.

The two transmigrants who had gotten the pendants had gained their familiars; epic beasts of incredible power rivaling that of many elders in strength and prestige.

The pendants had worked perfectly.

The First Seat had requested an ‘urgent’ audience with him shortly after that, and now, several hours later, Kivasmus held a signed parchment of alliance in hand—and a force of over ten thousand surrounding him.

He stood within the observation deck of the vessel now, watching the sea of shifting light and chaos all around him as the navigator inputted the Storm Sect's coordinates into the helm.

Smiling, Kivasmus turned his gaze to the two transmigrants seated beside him in the forward command tier. They had insisted on accompanying the vessel to repay the Storm Sec’s gift with their services, and though he had explained how unnecessary the gesture was, he hadn't pressed the matter.

Two additional transmigrants were a bonus, not a burden.

Still... something nagged at him.

He couldn't stop wondering how the Space Sect could simply release two of their supposed three transmigrants to fight in a war they had no certainty of winning. Even their enemies back home dared not risk such a thing.

Yet the Space Sect had done so without blinking.

Were they simply content to lose them? Or—Kivasmus thought uneasily, did they have more than three transmigrants in their possession?

“No Kivasmus muttered, shaking the thought away. “Impossible.”

Transmigrants were not resources that could be hidden. That was the reason the Balance Wars existed. Like every other affinity, the Space Sect had an Origin Plane, and any expansion of its borders wouldn't go unnoticed.

...Right?

Only uncertainty met his mental question, and with a grimace, Kivasmus turned to the captain. "How long till we reach the Storm Sect?"

"One minute and two seconds."

Kivasmus nodded slowly. "We must have been close then."

The captain glanced at him, then chuckled. “No, we were thousands of miles away. We are still thousands of miles away.”

“But—” Kivasmus frowned. “We’ve only just begun moving. Its barely been twenty seconds.”

Chuckles rang out from the others, and the captain pulled up a holographic screen displaying the distance they’d covered in the last twenty seconds.

3, 167 miles.

Kivasmus stared at the counter in shock, unable to believe what he was seeing. Gordon was a continent stretching roughly 25,000 miles from north to south. To cross three thousands of those miles in twenty seconds was insane.

Eyes wide, he shifted, glancing at the distance counter once more, noting how fast it was climbing. Less than two seconds had passed since the captain spoke and already, they had crossed the thirty-five hundredth mile.

It took Kivasmus a moment to understand how it was possible, and when he did, he shook his head in amazement. “Spatial Compression.”

The captain smiled, but said nothing else, and Kivasmus slowly turned his gaze to the viewing array, expecting to see or perhaps feel how fast they were moving.

Teleportation was a great aspect of the spatial affinity, one so many other affinities had incorporated into their skillsets. Even he could teleport. But teleportation was very different from space manipulation and the compression of hundreds of miles with a single step.

Spatial cultivators, real spatial cultivators, Kivasmus was quickly learning were incredibly dangerous.

He glanced up at the timer once again, noting that in the second he’d looked away, they’d crossed another two hundred miles.

Less than a minute was left till they reached the storm sect.

Kivasmus shook his head again, awed by the speed of space itself. For some reason, it was never spoken of. Not quite as much as light and lightning, both of which were considered one of the fastest elements in existence.

He’d never heard space celebrated as so, but at the speed at which they were moving, they could probably traverse the entire continent in less than two minutes.

And if that wasn’t insane, Kivasmus didn’t know what was.

He’d barely completed the thought before a voice echoed. “Approaching exit point in thirty seconds. Multiple reality fractures detected. Spatial instability at seventy-three percent and rising. Dimensional shear probable. Safety protocols advised. Brace for turbulent reintegration.”

None of the spatial cultivators shifted at the warning. A few even appeared faintly amused. Kivasmus, however, had no desire to test his durability against a ‘dimensional shear.’

He seized the nearest rail tightly and adjusted his footing, bracing himself as his gaze flicked back to the descending timer.

Seconds passed in taut silence.

The distortion outside the viewing array thickened, light bending inward as if reality itself were being wrung like cloth.

“Ten seconds to exit,” the voice announced again. “Reintegration sequence initiated. Structural integrity stable. Brace for compression rebound in five… four… three…”

The vessel shuddered violently.

Two.

The stars outside fractured into jagged lines of white.

One.

Darkness engulfed the ship, and for a fraction of a second, the world flattened. Sound thinned. Pressure built—not outward, but inward—as though space itself were being forced through too small a seam.

Then it tore.

The ship jolted violently as distance snapped back into place. He viewing array flickered, light returning in blinding brilliance. The distortion outside cleared—

And reality snapped back into place.

Black sky. Churning storm clouds. Forks of lightning crawling across their underside-like veins of white fire.

But one thing was undeniably missing.

The sound of war, and as Kivasmus looked down, he realized they’d arrived in war that had halted. Enemy Calamities were nowhere to be found. Mythics were retreating, and the transmigrants had vanished.

“Reintegration complete,” the ship’s voice intoned…

But Kivasmus wasn’t listening, his expression confused as his gaze roamed the battlefield in worry, wondering what had happened to cause their enemies to retreat.

But this wasn’t a retreat.

It was the commencement of a hunt.

And in that second of confused stillness, the enemy struck—not at the battlefield, but at the heart of the storm itself.

The viewing deck.

“No!” Kivasmus exclaimed as he watched the building explode outward.

Before he could fully panic though, a hand clasped his shoulders, and one of the Space Calamities smiled at him. “Let this be our first act of alliance to the storm sect.”

His smile lengthened as he slowly turned his gaze to the burning deck. “Why don’t we show you what true speed is, Kivasmus?”

He vanished then, and that was when Kivasmus realized time had been slowed.

Slowed??

His eyes widened. He opened his mouth to cry out urgently, but before he could, a voice like thunder itself boomed across the battlefield.
