[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 112 — 9:00 AM[Location]: The Lucas Estate · Private Forge

Every hour of the past five days had offered Emilia Lucas a reasonable place to set her tools down.

Each of them had been legitimate: the four hours the substrate needed to come off temperature, the overnight the spatial anchor spent seating in its solvent, the long dead stretch in the middle of the third night when the only correct professional decision available was to hand the schematics to a senior apprentice, log the handoff, and go to bed. Emilia had drunk liquid stamina until the marrow of her forearms ached and had kept her hands on the work.

It sat on the velvet now, cooled and quiet, in a forge that smelled of ozone and powdered star-iron.

A cat.

The chassis was matte black, void-forged, an alloy that took the light off the lumen-globes and returned none of it. The spatial anchors ran through the joints in a lattice that would have put a competent artificer on the floor, and the soul circuit beneath them was built, deliberately and at ruinous cost, to refuse resolution for as long as the object continued to exist.

Its eyes were gold.

Emilia had opened the file and stopped breathing somewhere around the third page. A day at the bench would have taken the whole chassis to starry black, eyes included, and closed the likeness. And then handing it over would have been a sentence said out loud, in a room, to her face.

The student had drawn a black cat, and by accident had built the only version of this that could be handed over. Everything that gave it away lived in what it did when a hand came toward it, and a hand did not have to come toward it.

On the second morning Emilia stood at the bench a while, then went back to the joints and changed nothing.

The parlors of the Inner Sea had a settled opinion about all of this. They watched an unbroken stream of priceless bespoke work travel out of the Lucas forge and into the Fifth Seat's vaults, and they read it the way anyone would: a sycophant, brilliant and pitiable, throwing masterpieces at a woman who owned twenty-seven worlds and could not conceivably want another one.

They had the client wrong.

Irene was a Collector. Everything she took she kept, and every acquisition left her hungrier.

That greed steadied Emilia. To put your life's work into the hands of someone who understood its weight exactly was the closest thing to permanence the craft had ever offered anyone.

If she could not be a lover, then Irene Habsburg was the finest friend this world had to give.

The trouble was that Emilia could not make her own heart stop at the line.

She lifted the cat off the velvet, checked the anchor seating one last time against a tolerance nobody else alive could have measured, and closed the case.

She had known the answer on Day 107, in a grading queue, off a student's parchment. The five days had been for the confirmation.

[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 112 — 2:50 PM[Location]: Ailuronia · The Habsburg Residence · Outer Receiving Halls

The doors closed behind her with the oiled, unhurried finality of a vault sealing for the century, and Emilia stood in the corridor well short of the transit anchors. Her hands were shaking, which she attributed to a hundred and twenty hours without sleep.

She had presented the box. She had watched the lid come up. That was all she intended to carry out of that room.

The tears were still coming.

I have lived inside this. She stood very still with it. I know hour by hour what it costs to want something that will not turn around.

And every year of it I have been glad it was me and not you.

The wish came up behind it, the same one that had been arriving since the summer: that the reason had been insufficiency. Insufficiency was a flaw in the maker, and a flaw in the maker could be worked. She had spent a career working one. It had closed.

The answer had been in that house since before Emilia set the first gift down in front of her, and it belonged to somebody else.

Every time in her life that Emilia Lucas had said I can make that, she had been right.

Nobody could move Ovelia.

Her hand came up and stopped short of her cheek. She found the handkerchief instead and did the repair properly, in a corridor, without hurrying, the way she did everything with her hands. Her reflection in the frosted quartz was an immaculate woman in a very good coat, with nothing wrong anywhere on her except the eyes, and she looked at it for a moment with something close to affection.

Well. The relief that came with it was enormous, and genuine. Now I know. Now I can stop asking, and love her properly, and be what she actually needs.

She had thought that sentence before, in almost those words, walking out of a different room in the summer.

She took out the communication crystal and sent three lines to the Council secretariat, courteous and complete, withdrawing herself from the back half of the session.

Then she turned away from the transit anchors and went the other way, already turning over what she would build next.

[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 112 — 3:10 PM[Location]: Unmapped Coordinates · Wellington Private Folded Space

The sky over the folded world was the color of a bruised plum and had not moved in nine hours.

Underneath it the Greed Umbrella were running live fire across scorched plain. Cecilia was working a rotation of contracted sparring partners, top-grade trainers and Arch-Witches on retainer, taking them apart in the order they were presented to her.

Evangeline watched from an obsidian dais floating well above the fray, ankles crossed. A second thread had been sitting in a crow on a cornice in Ailuronia since morning.

Emilia Lucas came out of the Habsburg residence, with three hours of session still to sit, and went home.

Evangeline smiled. It was slow, and serene, and it went nowhere near her eyes.

She had put one sentence across a table in a private booth eight weeks ago, a second-year had picked it up and built something out of it, and Emilia Lucas had chosen that something out of forty-five submissions. Emilia Lucas selected an instrument, and given what it had been grown from, the answer had exactly one available shape.

Ovelia.

Evangeline let her head go back against the air. She had been in one room, once, with both of them in it. What she had read on Irene Habsburg was restraint, and she had measured the shape of it, and the shape had been killing intent. What she had said out loud, across a table, when her little sister demanded evidence, was that the woman hated cats.

The answer Emilia Lucas took home supplied the correction, and it was a small one. Restraint of that grade, maintained that long, took more architecture than hatred.

Irene Habsburg loved Ovelia.

Emilia Lucas would stop there. Emilia had gone looking for a reason, had found one, and would spend the rest of her life being tender about it.

Evangeline had wanted to break Irene Habsburg since the first time their eyes met.

They had the same talent, which was the whole of the problem. Both of them looked at a person the way one looks down through the roof of a glass house from somewhere above it. In their first exchange Evangeline had been the one who ended up on the floor, and Irene had looked down at her from that unreachable altitude with a clinical and entirely unbothered pity, and being catalogued that completely had been physically nauseating.

She had spent the years since waiting for the coordinates of a stone.

Evangeline closed her eyes and put the High Council up in the dark. Real power in the Inner Sea pooled in four places: Finance, Defense, Personnel, Administration. Every one of them was shared. Even Ash, Third Seat, Eternal Guardian of the Border, split the functional authority of Defense with the Grand Witches standing the perimeter.

Except that the Fifth Seat held the throat of the continent's finances, and shared it with no one at all.

Evangeline rolled her eyes behind her lids. Josephine Durant sat in that chair and never held a tenth of this. The only match in the records is the Pseudo-First Seats, and every one of those had been a Second Seat holding something close to a regency. She began auditing the successions.

1987. Eve Hawkins, Ovelia's own, stepped down. Sonia replaced her, another Ghost Witch, out of the same castle. The commentary of the era had called it a masterpiece of advancing by retreat: the Ghost Castle did not lose a Grand Witch, it gained one.

Evangeline knew both women. Eve Hawkins was an anomaly among a subspecies famous for its lethargy: fiercely ambitious, with an operational tempo that bordered on the terrifying, and one of Irene's principal rivals through those years. She came off the Council the year Irene took her first world championship, and she has spent her retirement since manufacturing fresh headaches for the Fifth Seat.

Sonia was socially paralyzed and carried financial luck so catastrophic that it crossed out of economics and into metaphysics. Some years after Irene took her seat, Sonia's poverty ran deep enough that she earnestly inquired about what The Witch's House paid its floor staff in commission on drink sales. Word of it reached the apex of the Pillar, and the First Seat, who had not addressed the Fifth on any matter in a month, turned her head.

Put her on a stipend, Ovelia had said. A barmaid who becomes a Grand Witch is an inspiring story. A Grand Witch who becomes a barmaid is a horror story.

Sonia had been on Irene's payroll ever since.

Child support, Evangeline thought, and neither Irene nor Sonia had the faintest idea.

1993. Josephine Durant and Don Gil Ross stepped down in the same season. Irene and Heidi came in. The age of Casendiara holding two seats ended and Milan'thir held two instead, and the continent spent a month talking about Milan'thir. Nobody had spent much time on the detail that one of the two was the politically stranded Third Seat and the other was the bottom of the ladder.

"Sister."

Evangeline opened her eyes. Cecilia had come up to the dais and was hanging off the edge of it, silver hair stuck flat to her forehead, breathing like a woman who had scheduled her own recovery intervals and was running behind on them.

She looked at Evangeline with a small line between her brows. "You look... in an extremely good mood?"

Evangeline produced a light and musical laugh. "I thought of a very funny joke about the Tenth Seat."

Cecilia's gaze stayed on her face, and went on staying there. Her mouth opened, and she got as far as drawing the breath, and then let it out again and her shoulders came down.

"All right," she said, and dropped off the dais back toward the plain.

Evangeline's smile went out the instant her sister turned.

1999. The most recent, and by a wide margin the most contested, because when it settled the seat had gone to a Plumed Dragon Witch.

Grand Witches rarely got a good end, at least not while holding the title. The recognized exit routes were failed political struggle, lost duel, and purge.

Twelve years. Four seats. Three of them peaceful.

Evangeline nearly laughed aloud at the sky. Since when were we gentle?

And the one who had genuinely gone down in a political struggle, the single conventional casualty in twelve years, had been replaced by Paddy.

Evangeline's lip curled. Does a Plumed Dragon hold loyalty to anyone?

She did. Paddy was Irene's, comprehensively and without reservation, and the continent had never once noticed. Evangeline saw it, and she also saw that Paddy held nothing whatsoever for Ovelia.

Factions were expected. Loyalty was expected. But a Grand Witch's loyalty, where it existed, terminated at the First Seat. A Grand Witch whose allegiance ran to the Fifth instead was a standing army inside the building.

A witch had lost a seat, and Irene had gained one.

Anyone patient enough could have counted the successions. Counted that far it was a deputy being selected. Ovelia's distaste for work was the oldest joke on the continent, and every witch alive would have stopped there, because the First Seat's chair does not turn over. The other nine together could not take it from her. Every Arch-Witch alive, together, would have been a long afternoon and a funeral.

So the board had been arranged. But an arrangement is only as good as the hands you put into it, and Evangeline had one thing nobody counting seats had ever held: she had been in the room on the night the last stone went down.

The seam had opened on a murder in progress, and the woman who came through it to stop it was Irene Habsburg, alone.

Alone was the word. Heidi Lucent had put a hand on Cecilia at Babylon when nobody else on the continent would go near her, and in any rational projection of that evening the Tenth Seat came through that seam beside the Fifth. Irene would have collected regardless, because the scroll lived in her vault and in nobody else's, and that woman had never in her life walked past a favor lying on a table. Evangeline had costed it that way and been wrong.

Heidi had been cleared out of the corridor before she could reach the handle, and there was exactly one house on the continent that could send a Grand Witch home with a sentence. Then the scroll had come out, and Cecilia had bowed to one creditor. The Wellingtons would stand with Ovelia past the point of sense, and the house that owned them had sent their debt to somebody else.

Why Irene?

A debt owed to Ovelia bought the Wellingtons nothing. A debt owed to Heidi would have kept them anchored in a soft harbor. A debt at that price, owed strictly to the Fifth Seat, had exactly one use.

Lifeboat.

A lifeboat is built for a ship that sinks. The First Seat intended to fall.

On the day the continent came apart, the Wellingtons would have a publicly defensible reason to walk across and stand behind Irene instead.

Evangeline had been calling her Berenice for years, and had marked her up every year she had watched her, and had come in under every time.

Ovelia had looked across the board and found her equal. She expected to die by those hands.

If Ovelia had read Irene as an ambitious threat she could have crushed her a decade before the roots took, and talent without the time to grow is a corpse with potential. So why spare her, and why pave the mountain under her. Grand Witches sometimes got a good end, and the one at the top of the pillar never had, and a change in that chair took a war.

Ovelia had loved Irene enough to raise her own killer, and enough to go out and meet it, and enough to arrange that her most fanatical followers would survive her murder by swearing fealty to her murderer.

Gooseflesh came up both Evangeline's arms and stayed. Thank you, Emilia Lucas. She meant it exactly as it sounded. You went digging for one woman's secret and cracked the foundation of the world instead. You handed me the winning numbers to the end of an age.

Ovelia had written the Wellingtons into her will. They could sleepwalk through the coming slaughter, blind and idle and arguing about the seating, and wake on the winning side of a changed sky, because their God had already tilted the board to catch them.

Evangeline had no intention of boarding that lifeboat.

She turned the square of amethyst on her thumb and pressed its face into the heel of her other hand, harder, until the seal stood printed there in white. The Wellingtons had one master. She would set her teeth in Ovelia's own hand sooner than watch that leash change owners, because teeth are also a way of holding on.

So the counterplay was to become a debt Ovelia could not close, run so deep that the woman could not afford to die and leave it unpaid. And to tether a God to the earth, you have to see the exact shape of her design first.

Now she saw it.

And thank you, Miss Ludwig.

Every tether on that girl ran up to Irene or up to Ovelia. The Fifth Seat had countersigned a second-year's patent personally, which she did not do. And then there was the innate trait the Ludwigs had so meticulously entered into the public registry.

[Umbra].

They would have known perfectly well whose ground that word described, and helped themselves anyway, on the reasonable view that Ovelia holding a cathedral did not make the shadow her private property.

Evangeline chewed on it and let out a short, soft laugh. On any other witch alive it would be a word.

Her little sister had run the channel, which remained the funniest part of it. Every step correct, and the one sentence Evangeline had come to say had gone into the room she wanted it in.

Special, Evangeline decided, and filed the Ludwig girl under a heading she left otherwise empty. Function undetermined.

She opened the channel anyway.

"To what do I owe the pleasure, Tarnished Crow?"

"I have something." Evangeline watched her sister put another one down. "I am reasonably confident you will want it. The only open question is what you are prepared to pay."

The voice on the far end dropped and slid, a viper crossing silk. "Is that your intention? Or your master's?"

Evangeline was property, with no standing to open a negotiation on her own account, and if she claimed the other thing she was lying, because Cecilia Wellington would sooner chew glass than trade favors with Adeline.

Evangeline laughed, delighted.

"My master's, naturally," she said. "I am only the messenger."

She had gone further down than most of her kind ever get, on the night she stopped being able to pretend about her own nature, into a dark where the others found nothing but the churn.

She had found the room occupied.

"I'm listening," Adeline said.

Evangeline leaned forward, and began to deliver.
