The fanfare associated with my arrival was exactly what I had anticipated, though perhaps a bit louder. The sheer, absurd volume of aircraft I had ordered into the sky over the valley had Cinderfall collectively shifting in their armor. Fifty percent of my total aerial assets were currently deployed in this single region.

The last time the Hegemony had seen so many of my birds in the air simultaneously was during the Feast Day Massacre, when the inland strikes had devastated their supply chains.

To prevent their commanders from attempting a straight, desperate charge the moment I arrived, I had fortified the primary defensive line with rows of Mark-M MECHs. It was the first time the heavy titans had been deployed in a stationary, defensive formation on this battlefield.

The panic in the Cinderfall high command, as reported by the Shadow Order, was palpable. The enemy had no idea what was happening. To their medieval, charge-oriented minds, deploying heavy armor to the front lines alongside overwhelming air support meant only one thing: a rapid, forceful charge. They assumed the Mark-M heavy units, backed by the Air Force, were preparing to roll over them.

Defensive shields started lighting up across the Cinderfall encampment, one golden dome after another.

I wasn't actually planning to make a grand, theatrical entrance. The increased defenses and air patrols were simply for my safety during the transit. I was hoping the enemy would misinterpret it as me beefing up the fortified lines, but it appeared they thought I was going for the throat immediately.

Both sides waited. The tension stretched tight across the valley. My Air Force performed significantly more patrols, the Wyverns screaming low over the cloud deck. The Mark-M heavy units stayed perfectly still, silent monoliths of black steel.

Eventually, the sun went down. The battlefield lit up in stark contrast. The Cinderfall side glowed with the warm, magical domes of their shields, while my camp was illuminated by the cold, harsh glare of modern floodlights and sweeping searchlights.

As the evening deepened, Cinderfall finally let their shields drop, realizing no charge was coming. The bright magical light was replaced by the softer, flickering glows of their torches and campfires.

The shift gave me the opportunity to finally slip in.

I angled my descent toward the makeshift landing strip we had established next to the shore. A thick pocket of localized cloud cover hung directly above the strip, generated by the weather-control runes of a nearby destroyer, keeping the area hidden from the direct line of sight of the Cinderfall scouts on the distant ridges.

I landed on the packed ash. I slid off Kaelus’s back. The massive dragon immediately shrank down to his cat-sized form and hopped onto my shoulder, letting out a loud, exaggerated yawn.

It’s my turn to ride on your shoulder, he mentally informed me, digging his small claws slightly into my cape.

I patted his head.

I turned my attention forward. A huge number of my high-ranking officers were gathered on the tarmac.

They all struck their chests simultaneously, the unified THUMP echoing off the nearby transport carriers, welcoming me. I saw Bob standing at the forefront. Beside him was Patricia, still in her Director Nyx armor. Valen, Commodore Theramore, Captain Ivela, Sir Dalgor, and Chief Engineer Zirhad were all present.

Behind them stood rows and rows of Aegis officers, all looking at me expectantly.

Bob turned slightly. "At ease, soldiers!" he shouted.

The officers dropped their salutes and stood with their legs apart, their hands clasped behind their backs. They were waiting for a speech.

"Great work so far, everyone," I called out, projecting my voice across the gathering. "Now, return to your duties."

The officers dispersed eagerly, rushing back to their positions with renewed vigor. I didn't know what else to say. I had specifically told my retainers to keep the welcoming committee as small as possible. I sometimes underestimated how large my forces had gotten, how many people now looked to me for direction. I had come a long way from just having Bob and Patricia to aid me.

If you had just sneezed, they probably would have found some profound, tactical meaning behind it, Kaelus mused, his tail swishing contentedly against my neck.

I began walking toward the MCIC, Bob and Patricia flanking me. The other high officers fell into step behind us. I could sense Kaelus’s excitement building for tomorrow, his tail now ticking back and forth like an erratic pendulum.

Before we reached the bunker, I veered off the main path, leading the group toward the shoreline. I wanted to see the progress myself.

The Colossus-class Crawler construction sites were awe-inspiring, even in the dark. Massive floodlights illuminated the skeletal frames of the mobile fortresses. They were colossal, sprawling spider-like structures of adamantium and star-iron, each one measuring three kilometers across. Swarms of Mark III-B Engineering units crawled over the scaffolding like industrious insects, their plasma welders flashing brilliant blue in the night.

"Status, Zirhad?" I asked, looking up at the towering legs of Crawler One.

"Ahead of schedule, Lord Commander," the Chief Engineer replied, stepping up beside me. "The central reactor housings are nearly complete. We will be ready for the core installations within the week. The integration of the local forge units is proceeding optimally."

I nodded, satisfied. These crawlers were the key to holding this territory permanently. They were mobile, impregnable strongholds that could spawn an endless tide of automata.

"Keep pushing them," I said, turning away from the shoreline. "We need those operational before Cinderfall realizes they can't win a war of attrition."

We finally entered the Mobile Command and Information Center. It was a specialized, temporary command post, possessing the same functionality and processing power as the CIC aboard The Aegis, crammed into a heavily shielded, rapidly deployable bunker complex.

But before we got down to the grim business of war, there was a logistical necessity to attend to. Dinner.

The officers’ mess in the MCIC was significantly smaller than the one on the flagship, but it was warm and well-lit. I took my seat at the head of the long metal table. Bob sat to my right, Valen to my left. The rest of the commanders filed in, taking their places.

A team of culinary automata began serving the meal. It was a hearty spread—roasted meats, thick stews, and fresh bread, all prepared with the magical preservation techniques we had perfected.

Except for me.

Patricia stood at my shoulder, quietly and efficiently serving my meal herself. It was a bizarre, double-layered deception. At the far end of the table sat an empty suit of Mark VI-S armor, playing the silent role of Director Nyx. Here, the actual head of my intelligence network was pouring my water and refilling my plate with the practiced grace of a lifelong maid.

I watched the table dynamics with detached amusement.

Commodore Theramore attacked his plate with the ferocity of a starving wolf. He shoved food into his mouth, gesturing emphatically with a chicken leg as he recounted a particularly satisfying naval maneuver to a very unimpressed Captain Ivela.

Next to him, Commander Trisa’s mouth twitched in silent annoyance. She looked like she was reconsidering every life choice that had led her to become the XO for a man who ate like a feral animal.

I leaned over to Valen, who was cutting his meat with precise, elegant strokes. "I could swear Trisa is thinking he looks like a dog we never feed," I muttered.

Valen snorted softly, hiding a smile behind his napkin. "Indeed, my Lord. The Commodore’s table manners leave much to be desired. Though, I suppose it matches his tactical approach."

At the far end of the table, the empty armor of Director Nyx sat completely motionless. It hadn't touched its food.

Theramore paused mid-chew, looking around the table, then specifically at the steaming bowl of stew in front of him.

"First of all, Lord Commander, I have a complaint to file," Theramore said, leaning heavily on the table. "The food here in the MCIC, or whatever it is called, is way better than the slop in my galley. I will have Trisa file a formal complaint with logistics."

Trisa, seizing the opportunity, elbowed him sharply in the ribs, trying to stop the blabbering fool from ruining the dinner. The sudden, critical damage seemed to reboot the old man's brain.

"Ahhh... yes," Theramore grunted, rubbing his side. "As I was saying. I will be departing as soon as this meeting concludes to organize and unify the three fleets to combat the incoming elven forces."

I struggled to hold back a smile. That was the problem with dealing with Theramore; keeping a poker face was almost impossible. A deep, sadistic part of me wanted to tell him the truth right then and there. I wanted to lean across the table and whisper that the delicious meal he was devouring was personally cooked and prepared by the head of the shadow intelligence agency—a woman whose professional portfolio mostly consisted of assassinations and poisonings. The sheer, existential terror that would cross the old sailor's face would be priceless. Maybe I’ll tell him after the war.

I recovered my composure and pushed my empty plate away. It was time for business.

We moved to the central holographic table. Patricia stepped away from my side, the maid vanishing, and the Director taking over as she initiated her report on the intelligence gathered from the Cinderfall lines.

"Cinderfall, upon seeing the fortified defenses, has settled on an attack plan," she stated, her synthesized voice crisp and mechanical, piped directly from the empty armor at the table. "They will engage if the enemy ships depart to combat the incoming elven naval armada."

I explained our game plan.

"When the enemy sees the fleet depart later tonight and not return, Cinderfall is almost certain to initiate their attack after waiting for one or two days," I said, pointing to the naval icons on the map. "As discussed, Theramore is taking command of the whole fleet, except for four destroyers and three submarines. Those will stay on this shore and will be put under the command of Valen as a safety measure."

I could see Theramore pout, realizing he didn't really have full command of every ship, but the death glare Ivela was giving him prevented him from speaking out. Mirelle was right to assign Ivela as Theramore’s babysitter.

We outlined the attack strategy.

"Upon seeing the new Mark-M units, the enemy has decided to send five Tier 8 knights along with fifteen Tier 7s. The rest will make up Tier 5 or 6," I explained, highlighting the enemy vanguard. "Latest estimates indicate the initial attack force will be made of roughly one hundred individuals. All high tier. Accompanied by a thousand slightly lower-tier combatants."

"When they initiate their charge," I continued, tracing the firing lines on the hologram, "the Mark-Ms and the automated grid will begin their attack, supported by long-range snipers. The second phase is where we engage. They will redirect all their high-tier combatants. We expect them to break through to our trenches, so we are moving all our officers to the back lines, relying on swarming tactics to stall them long enough for Bob and me to finish dealing with the highest-tier combatants. We will come finish off the stragglers."

"In case the enemy chooses to send more high-tier combatants," I added, glancing at the naval commanders, "Theramore will turn the fleet back around, and Ivela will drop The Vengeance and The Retribution from the upper cloud layers."

Our plans were set in stone.

Later that night, the fleet pulled up their anchors and began their journey to face the elves.

I settled into my quarters within the MCIC and tuned into my favorite show: Cinderfall Command.

I watched in real-time on the surveillance feed as the news reached King Theron. The officers under his command rejoiced, thinking their strategy was working. Some were enthusiastically urging for the attack to take place tomorrow morning itself.

But King Theron didn't rush. He raised his hand, silencing his generals.

"Don't celebrate yet," the King ordered. "Have a few phoenix knights trail them, making certain of their speed and ensuring they have moved far enough away. We can't have them suddenly turn back."

I watched them. They were starting to actually use their heads now that their go-to strategy of brute force and overwhelming strength was failing them.

Tes, I sent the mental command immediately. Send instructions to Theramore. Limit the speed of his ships so that the enemy can't accurately estimate their maximum velocity.

Two can play at this game, I mused, watching the King scowl at his maps.
