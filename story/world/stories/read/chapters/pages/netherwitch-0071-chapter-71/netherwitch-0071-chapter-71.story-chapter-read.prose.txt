“Cut the catch and make ready for port!” Captain Schultz shouted.

A dozen gamers moved as Brianna directed, scurrying across the deck of the Enterprise. Skilled hands loosened ropes, adjusting the catch, tail, and fins. Others ducked into the hold, prepping the cargo for when they made port.

Captain Schultz remained behind the helm. Unlike the Utrecht, the wheel was sized for ordinary demons. Still, the white-haired asteri was quite short, requiring the small block placed beneath her feet. Brianna’s vivid violet eye was focused forward. The other was covered by a black patch, fabric enchanted to aid the witch in tracking the astral currents. Befitting her style, Brianna was dressed in an 18th-century Napoleonic frock coat complete with a dashing, bicorn hat.

Very classy.

Compared to the Utrecht, the Enterprise was five times longer. Its hold carried brooms, cloth, cores, and a variety of refined materials. Despite their vast reserves, it was currently forbidden to export chaos resources like space stone, void rock, and white jade. Why? Because the Cloud Island Government didn’t want anyone to deduce the existence of the treasure trove called Nemesis.

Their largest product by mass was spider silk. Bolts sold for 23 soli per drom, and Harvest Doom had phantasmal farms which produced the material by the ton. Emmy was equally enthused. Creating more profitable variations of the silk was a running project for the Department of Material Alchemy. Perhaps one day, the problem could be thrown over the wall to the Department of Dungeons. Professor Fischer would happily create arachnid horrors so they could produce higher-quality silks cheaply and in bulk.

The next most popular export, by bulk, was refined hive ant cores. Once transformed, these translucent crystals served as a psychic source commanding a price of 700 soli per drom. The best part? Hive ants were abundant, particularly on the plateau near Windroost. The only trouble was, the beasts had to be hunted. What’s more, the cores were small and crude. They had to be smelted into larger crystal blocks before use.

Then there was a curious export, the feathers of the red-eyed raven. Void element essences were extremely rare. Thus, the feathers commanded a pretty penny. A pity nobody had figured out how to farm the obnoxious creatures as of yet.

Brooms, however, edged out everything by revenue.

“I suppose you’ll be on your way, ‘Mr Swift,’” Brianna said, her head swaying toward the man.

“Indeed, milady. It has been a pleasure to sail with you, but I must go.” The warlock doffed his hat, offering a suave bow. “But don’t let the sudden absence of my charming presence leave you forlorn. Silk is easily sold, but brooms can be a challenge. You must do your duty and collect great profit from this venture.”

Sylvester Swift had a boyish face and eyes of deep red like jasper. He wore a dapper, pinstriped suit along with a fashionable top hat. To add to his gentlemanly flair, the warlock held a cane of twisted chrome and dark wood in his right hand. His hair was short, the clipped locks silver-white. At five-foot six, his stature was respectable for a warlock of the magissa genera. By broader demonic standards, however, Sylvester was quite small.

Returning hat to head, he picked up his metal briefcase.

It was Wednesday of the first week of Men-Glacius. Fourteen years had passed since Sylvester had entered the netherworld, with the first gamers crossing through the gates eleven years ago. Governor Sanchez was in the third year of his term. So much had changed. More was changing. But all this had little to do with the now. The Enterprise continued on its path, Geomun Naru coming up off the bow. The port city belonged to Jeolsung, a major plane of the Fey Federation.

Brianna rolled her eye. “Just don’t do anything that’ll cause problems for Stellar Transport. I can’t afford to lose money like you.”

With a sly smile, Sylvester raised his cane. The handle made a slow, horizontal swirl. A blue-green hole was cut into the world.

“Now, now, Captain Schultz,” Sylvester replied. “I was never on this ship. And must I remind you, we have never met. Therefore, how can you say how much money I can or cannot afford to lose?”

Without waiting for a response, the silver-haired warlock stepped back.

Voomp.

One blink and Sylvester stood in a small, empty room. On the stone floor was a large, metal ring. The space steel was lightly enchanted. It wasn’t a waystone by any means, just a cheap tool to ensure his blind teleportation found the right place.

“I still have a few hours before I meet my contacts,” Sylvester noted. He raised his cane, pounding it into the ground with a thunk. Space steel burst into flame, essence disintegrating thanks to a crude fire spell used in conjunction with the cremation hex. “Since that’s the case, I’ll do a little shopping before touring this fine city.”

Crimson eyes sharp, Sylvester chanted. “■, ■, ■■.”

The first spells were anonymous presence, then tangled weave. With these, he scrubbed clean any signature left at the site. Next he cast realm skip. The spell gripped the warlock, depositing him in a nearby alley with a pop. Sylvester tipped his hat toward the startled faun before proudly striding out into the city.

He was, of course, Sylvia Swallows.

As Esmeralda suspected, Governor Sanchez had sought Sylvia to resolve Orpheus Protocol. However, it would be utterly foolish if ‘she’ appeared in Tartarus again. The Inquisition had its eyes on Gavin and Elroy. Therefore, Sylvia, who was tied to them both, was no longer a person of minor interest. Then there was the Sapphite faction of Asmodeus’s harem, which might try to kidnap her.

Thus, she planned to enter Tartarus in the guise of Sylvester. This fiction was supported by her Three-Faced Visage, Gavin’s skill book: Art of the Con, and the legendary artifact Anbaht.

In addition to the visage, Sylvester Swift had obscured his origin. Gating into Jeolsung was one method. The government agents he’d meet later would provide another. The spooks had Sylvester’s papers along with his ‘legend’. That was what spies called their invented backstory.

With all this, the connections between Sylvester and Sylvia would be severed.

Navigating the city, the false warlock found a suitable shop. Tools Most Magical was written on the sign, the beautiful calligraphy legible thanks to the psychic intent.

Sylvester slid a door to the side.

Paper lanterns filled the dingy space with orange light. A little girl looked up when the silver-haired man entered. She sported a rounded face with adorable freckles, dark hair falling past her ears in cute ringlets. He wasn’t deceived. The souls of children rarely made it through Unus Mundus, and even if they did, most bloodlines would place them squarely in the body of an adult.

This was a three-faced witch, wearing her youngest form.

“A male witch?” the little girl commented, sitting up with sudden interest. “Awakened or born? And who’s your ancestor?”

Warlocks, male witches as some insisted, were extraordinarily rare creatures. If one hundred thousand witches were gathered, only a single warlock might be found on average. That was true even though a tenth of the souls used to make witches came from mortal men, itself a product of laziness and disregard.

All warlocks came about by one of two means. First, a very lucky few were male at the start. Born warlocks made up two-thirds of the male Hecates population. The rest reclaimed their masculinity during their first grand mutation. Such warlocks were called Awakened.

“I’m Awakened,” Sylvester lied smoothly. “And I prefer the title warlock over witch.”

“A witch is a witch,” the little girl said disdainfully. She made a chopping motion with her hand. “You should just cut it off. You’ve already lived as a proper witch, so what’s the point of clinging to pretense?”

Sylvester’s gaze darkened. He knew there was bigotry against warlocks, but he hadn’t expected it to be so bold.

“I’m here to make a purchase,” he sneered. He raised the pressure of his aura, making clear he was stronger than her. “Not to be berated by you.”

“Te-he-he,” the little girl giggled, suddenly acting cute. “I’m so sorry, big brother, I’m just not used to male witches. In all of Geomun Naru, you might be the only one.”

“Be careful with your words in the future,” he warned with a tone of disgust. “Not everyone is as kind as I am.”

“I’ll listen to big brother,” the girl said politely. “Where did you say you’re from? I’m curious.”

“I didn’t say,” Sylvester returned bluntly. “Right now, I’m traveling. Before coming to Geomun Naru, I toured the Seven Level City. Tartarus is next on my list. I was hoping to find an abyss catalyst to suit the theme.”

When practicing flux shift, he’d discovered that the art’s stamina consumption could be partially offset with abyss ether. Not only that, feeding flux shift with the element abyss slightly improved the strength of the gravitational acceleration.

This had spurred his interest in associated magics. Sylvester had invented two spells thus far. The first, black hand, summoned a palm of crushing force. Due to the scale and gravitational nature, black hand was a titan killer. Indeed, it was more destructive when striking large objects than smaller ones. The second spell was scales of the abyss. It provided a magical defense akin to transformation-level ki-armor arts.

It was an effect which was quite useful, because he couldn’t be bothered to learn such an art. Also, with two transformation-level arts, his body was near its limit.

“I happen to have one,” the little girl said. Her cute smile showed her dimples. “Seventy thousand soli. It’s right over there on the shelf, if you want to take a look.”

Eyes of deep crimson turned in the direction of the witch’s gesture. With his fingers, Sylvester made a calling motion. An arm of wind stretched out according to his will, wrapping around a disk. The byeok flew across the shop before landing in his hand. He studied the item carefully. Runes were shaped into the dark metal, running around the rim.

Then, shamelessly, the warlock used causality magic. Observe Item.

Ding!

Characteristics:* Efficiency: 90%* Max Rate: 1500 ether/second

“Heh,” Sylvester laughed to himself, looking at the System’s price. His crimson eyes turned toward the little girl. “I’ll take it.”

Lips curled in a smirk, he slapped down four gold coins. Each viginiti represented twenty glints. Glints were an alternative to the soli. Made from aetheric gold, these coins had a value 550 to 600 times greater than that of their cousins.

Seeing the coins, the proprietress glared.

“Now there, little boy,” she spat. Her body suddenly aged, growing larger and older. Her little ringlets faded into stiff gray. “You should know that eighty glints amount to forty thousand at best. And honorable shops like mine don’t deal in gold in the first place.”

“Forty-four to forty-eight thousand,” the warlock corrected. He dropped the metal plate onto the table with a clatter. “Even if we use the lower end of the float, the value is greater than that of this disk. Are you trying to cheat me, old hag?”

Jasper eyes burned right back. For a long moment, the hard, dark eyes of the hexe stared into his own. Then, the witch relented.

“Te-he, big brother saw right through me,” the woman said, changing back into an adorable girl of thirteen. “Because big brother is handsome, I’ll trade you the disk for eighty glints.”

“Then we have a deal,” Sylvester accepted, sweeping the Disk of the Depths into his soul.

The little girl gave a sweet smile, quickly snatching the gold coins off the desk. How quickly ‘honor’ vanished when there was money to be made.

Politely tilting his hat, he pulled open the door. Daylight poured in, setting the warlock in silhouette. Then – shooft – he slid it closed behind him.

Geomun Naru was the third-largest city in Jeolsung.

Storefronts faced the smooth and crowded streets. The city’s architecture had a distinctive eastern style raised to fantastical proportions. Tall buildings stood all around him, sporting classic upturned roofs and vermilion pillars. The ether light lamps were set in stone lanterns, adding to the cultural flair. Small decorative trees were placed in gardens and courtyards, blossoming with ethereal beauty.

Above it all loomed an even greater structure: The Tower.

The colossal spire floated one kilometer above the city, its tip stretching 5 kilometers higher, a finger piercing deep into the empty void. This mighty structure had one hundred floors, each an ecosystem filled with phantasms drawn from every corner of the netherworld. Sylvester had seen images of the interior, thanks to the pictures collected by the gamers who’d received invitations to climb.

It was said that anyone who reached the top would receive a single wish from the secretive hobgoblin who called the tower home. Most, though, understood the main point of the climb was to earn the goblin’s favor and become one of the servants he used to extend his power and authority into the world surrounding.

Sylvester strolled through the streets, each step made five times longer. Space twisted in front of him, a crunched column slithering through the crowd. His gaze drifted as he traveled, taking in the sights, his western suit and top hat distinctly different from the clothes surrounding.

Geomun Naru was built on the black river, Samdocheon. In contradiction to common logic, these waters flowed toward the plane’s center. As they did, the river branched out, its limbs reaching many of the great cities in Jeolsung. This alone made the city a natural trade hub. When accounting for the astral ports and the influence of The-One-Who-Owns-The-Tower, it was no surprise that Geomun Naru was one of the richest cities in the Fey Federation.

Its influence was seen everywhere. Fey of every type walked the streets. The Aos-Si, Vishnu, and Shintai lineages were especially common. Drakonis and Kategaris weren’t far behind. Of course, Nox, Hecates, Typhon, and Apep could also be found. Here, the Summer and Winter courts met without violence or contradiction, a neutral territory where differences could be discussed.

While Geomun Naru traded in many goods, its greatest trade was souls.

The city sat atop the largest soul well in the Fey Federation, the Samdocheon itself. The river drew in souls as the plane drifted through the starry void, producing thirty thousand every year. To support this huge volume, Geomun Naru had built giant facilities to examine, process, encode, and train the resulting denizens. These same facilities were of interest to parties throughout the netherworld, which ballooned the soul trade even further.

All in all, one hundred thousand souls passed through Geomun Naru every year.

One good bit of news: the Fey Federation didn’t have the Law of Acquisition. Unlike Hell, they broke even during the War of Words. Thus, souls couldn’t be indebted or thrown into slavery as a consequence of their birth. Instead, it was the government and third parties who funded the creation of new fey.

The warlock arrived at a three-story pagoda. The sign outside read: ‘Nabi Nalgae’ meaning ‘Butterfly Wings’. Delicious scents filled the air surrounding. Sylvester didn’t hesitate to claim a private room on the second floor of the restaurant.

“■■■.”

Secrets assured by magic, the silver-haired man set his hat and cane down beside him. Fifteen minutes later, he sampled a spicy soup called jjambbong, sucking in the tasty noodles.

“Clinging to the pretense,” he tutted. The warlock dipped his chopsticks into the gyeranjjim to adjust his palate.

The nail had been driven hard into his heart. The hexe was right, even if for all the wrong reasons. He was clinging to pretense. He could feel it with every waking moment. This body was wrong. So too were the gazes and the expectations of all the fey surrounding.

A dissonance gripped him. For fourteen years, the feminine had been his norm. Masculine was the oddity.

Some of that would dim with time. ‘Eric’ had faced a similar conundrum in the first few months when he was thrust into the Starlight Nether Witch Academy. Back then it was long hair and girls’ clothes that’d driven him to the point of insanity. Now, it was short hair, pants, and the tilt of his feet that had him gnashing his teeth.

These feelings weren’t the product of innate desire. They were a side effect of habit. Once his mind had time to adjust, things would feel normal again.

Only then would he glimpse how much of a girl he had become.

After finishing his soup, Sylvester dropped his private booth spell, then waved down a waitress. Soon he had an order of galbi and agujjim in front of him, along with a side of kimchi for flavor.

His guests showed up not long after.

Shadows stirred opposite the warlock. Eyes of jasper watched as a man and a woman melted out of the darkness, Nathan Wagner and Charlotte Reed. Both were dressed in local garments.

“■■■.” Sylvester wove one hundred and ninety-seven runes, creating a new private booth even as the first collapsed around him, eating the threads of causality. “It’s about time you showed up.”

He’d met these agents three years ago during the raid to retake Everwhite Pass. Then, they’d been members of the Viridian Blades. He wasn’t sure when they’d joined the government, but it had to have been a good while ago given that they were part of Operation Centralia Flame.

“I like the new face,” Charlotte commented, violet eyes dancing. “Very handsome.”

Sylvester smirked. He was, indeed, a handsome man. He’d spent a good number of hours adjusting his face to achieve that effect. And, more importantly, making sure it was distinct from its devilish inspiration. He wasn’t heading to Tartarus just to have everyone scream ‘Lucifer!’ the second they took a look at him.

“Don’t forget, I’m a married man,” he reminded, showing the silver ring on his left hand.

Charlotte was a shadow mage with hair like ink. The redhead conjurer beside her was grim, his orange-brown eyes glaring across the table. Neither was Awakened. Sylvester could tell from a glance at the ranker charts. As for their exact level? Charlotte’s was unreadable thanks to her anti-divination equipment. Nathan was pegged at 690, a fact Sylvester knew because everyone above and below Nathan on the ranker charts was level 690.

The grand mutations weren’t so easy to cross. The majority of gamers would never complete that step.

“I know,” Charlotte said with a cheeky grin. “I was there in the back gallery.”

“All of us were there,” Nathan commented. The redhead placed a leather booklet on the table. “We have your papers and a first-class ticket on the Unmyeong Naru. The ship leaves for Tartarus in five hours.”

Sylvester picked up his passport. Deep, crimson eyes scanned the contents. The papers identified him as Sylvester Swift, an Awakened warlock from the bloodline Hecates Magissa Sofos. Sofos was a rare subspecies of witch, synthesized by blending code from the prisma and the gnosi. They were most commonly found on Alfheim. His legend claimed the same origin for himself. At least, that’s where the warlock had lived until he’d moved to Jeolsung half a century back.

Missing was his history from the time before. This was normal. Sylvester was an Awakened warlock. Most Awakened warlocks went out of their way to erase their past, hating their years lived as a witch. Born warlocks, by contrast, were closely tracked by the covens. In many cases, it was easy to discover a born warlock’s ancestry going seven generations back, mostly due to the bigots. Some witches viewed warlocks as a stain. Indeed, two millennia ago, the Supreme Coven of the Nether had purged the entire incubus bloodline. Thus was erased the only fragment of the Hecates lineage that was consistently male.

Snapping the book closed, Sylvester put it inside his soul. He made a note to familiarize himself with his passport and his background on his trip to Tartarus.

“How much can I trust this?” he questioned.

“One of our agents contacted a specialist,” Nathan supplied. “It’s clean enough for Tartarus, but if you wave it in front of too many government officials in the Federation, someone will see through it.”

Hell and the Fey Federation had a cordial relationship, but this wasn’t modern Earth. There was no such thing as Interpol, nor any other form of effective cooperation between the major powers. Plus, the Federation itself was splintered into so many kingdoms that confirming anything was a mess.

“Good enough,” Sylvester accepted.

Pushing aside some dishes, the silver-haired man dropped his briefcase onto the table. With a click, both locks were opened.

The container’s inside was larger than the outside. The briefcase was a common magical tool known as a hunter’s sack. Space-based enchantments expanded the carrying capacity while other magics offset the apparent weight. What couldn’t be changed was the spiritual mass. This added a kind of inertia to every action, particularly when the briefcase matched Sylvester’s weight.

Currently, the case held ten large canisters: elemental dust covering causality and space. All in all, he had five hundred droms of it, amounting to ten thousand ether in total. Next to the canisters were three dungeon cores. Sylvester removed two, placing them on the table with a clunk.

Nathan picked up a core, then nodded. The metal conjurer slipped it into his soul. Charlotte did the same. Sylvester raised a brow. Second consolidation fey could only carry 150 droms by default. To hold the dungeon cores, these agents must’ve cleared out their entire inventory while adding a realm ring array.

“My gold,” Sylvester said, making a come-hither motion.

Charlotte waved a hand over the table, leaving behind a pair of gold bars and several rolls of coin. A bar, when it came to money, had a specific meaning. It was precisely one drom of cash. That translated to 1000 soli or 1000 glints.

Sylvester’s metal-based wealth now totaled 1.7 million soli, all in a 3.5 drom package.

“This is for an account in Mammon,” Nathan continued, adding a crystal key to the pile. “If you need more money, contact the government. An agent will deposit cash into your account.”

Sylvester nodded. Needless to say, that was for emergencies only. While keyed accounts were private and anonymous, every connection was a piece of information. Enough threads and the Inquisition could draw a picture.

And it’d be remiss to think Heaven hadn’t planted a few angels in the netherworld’s premier bank.

“Thank you, Mr Wagner and Miss Reed,” Sylvester accepted, sweeping the items into his soul. “Now, you best be on your way. I wouldn’t want your corpses to spoil my lunch.”

The warlock smirked. The agents stiffened. Heh. He’d been in the netherworld longer than them. It was easy enough to guess how they were going to transport the dungeon cores. A soul sunk into Unus Mundus was almost impossible to intercept. Tracking one through divination was just as useless.

Unless the soul was marked before it escaped, of course.

“Is it that obvious?” Nathan questioned.

“Obvious doesn’t mean the plan is bad,” Sylvester replied, snagging a bit of kimchi with his chopsticks. “Sometimes, obvious things are the hardest to stop.”

Nathan nodded. Charlotte flashed a smile.

“Good luck,” she called.

Shadow wrapped both agents. In a few minutes, they’d be dead. Raising a hand, Sylvester snapped his fingers. His private booth collapsed around him, law magic erasing the threads of causality.

The Unmyeong Naru was a giant.

The ship measured three hundred and fifty meters from stem to stern. It had five decks, the lowest set aside for cargo. The middle two served ordinary passengers, while the fourth deck had entertainment and first class. Tickets ranged in price from 100 soli to 5000 soli.

Amenities, unsurprisingly, were extra.

Sylvester was going to travel in the lap of luxury. For most fey, 5000 soli was a lot of money. For him, that was only 2 gold in cloud cash. Pocket change, really. In fact, people of his wealth rarely took ships like the Unmyeong Naru, because those as rich as Sylvester typically owned private ships.

A category which… uh… technically applied to him too.

Sylvester Swift walked along the wharf, admiring the ingenuity and the beauty. The docks of Geomun Naru were a colossal construct. Jetties stretched out into the starry void, some a full kilometer long. The harbor itself was a natural wonder, the astral currents breaking upon these shores unnaturally still.

If not for the three-hundred-meter cliffs behind, it would be true perfection. But stone was easily shaped in the nether. The fey had carved deep, cutting a cavernous pit into the rock. They’d added multiple elevators afterward to carry the goods into the city above.

After studying the design for a few minutes, he handed his ticket and his papers to a kitsune standing at the front of the first-class line.

“Welcome to the Unmyeong Naru, Mr Swift,” the attendant said, while handing over a token. “We wish you a pleasant journey. Your room will be 24-B, just below the main deck. If you’d like, we can bring your luggage there for you.”

“Thank you, but I’d rather carry it myself,” Sylvester dismissed.

Without wasting another word, he fell straight up, gravity reversing under the influence of flux shift. This wasn’t flight. This was an upward plummet. Though plummet might be too strong of a word. Compared to the waltz of flowers, flux shift had greater velocity. His acceleration, however, fell short of Earth’s gravity.

And that was accounting for his level and his Starlight Striders.

Two seconds later, he set foot on the Unmyeong Naru’s main deck. One of the servants reacted to his entrance, moving in Sylvester’s direction. The warlock waved his token, indicating that he had permission to be there. The servant backed off.

With crimson eyes, he took in everything.

At the ship’s front was a swimming pool. This was no basin. Instead, the pool was a thirty-meter sphere of liquid floating above the main deck. Fey cavorted in the crystal-clear water. As Sylvester watched, a shellycoat shot out. The man landed neatly beside his chair before grabbing a drink. His shells glistened like a coat of armor in Geomun Naru’s sun.

Awakening had been kind to the man.

Further along the path was the wheelhouse, the enclosed structure rising two stories above the ship’s main deck. Set on the starboard side of that building was a stage and a bar. A quartet of elves played, a man and a woman singing in a duet. The band’s name was written on a banner: Darkness and Daylight. Fitting, as half of the musicians were dark-skinned dokkalfar while the others were ljosalfar with a milky complexion.

“I should give them a tip,” Sylvester murmured.

He’d already set his System on record because ‘there be plunder abouts.’ One of these days, the netherworld would regret its piss-poor intellectual property laws.

Smiling to himself, he took a seat at the bar. He wiggled his fingers. With this casual action, Sylvester cast one hundred and sixty-three runes into the void. His fated request carried with it a simple intent: ‘May I enter Tartarus unnoticed’.

Thud.

Sylvester set his briefcase on the table before waving down a waitress. A pretty dryad appeared beside him, her green hair a forest of leaves clipped short in a bob.

“What can I do for you, sir?” she asked.

“I’ll start with a bowl of your scotch broth,” Sylvester said, after skimming their menu.

“It’ll be a few minutes,” the dryad replied. “If you would show your token, the bill will be put on your tab, sir.”

Sylvester nodded, flashing the block. His mind was focused on a gallery of pictures. These were images he’d snapped while touring Geomun Naru. He pondered each carefully, a virtual window floating to his left. On this blue screen was displayed a private thread from the forums.

He pinched his chin in thought.

“That one,” he decided.

Smiling, his mind turned to his status sheet. A man had to admire his greatness every so often. Especially when the blue sheet showed such beautiful numbers.

…

He was just going to ignore that unmanly class title. Also, why the heart? The hearts annoyed him more than the title. Those should be stars. Seriously, he was an asteri. How could the Devil neglect the stars? Even Emmy had clicked her tongue when she’d seen it.

“You mind if I sit here?”

Sylvester vanished his status window. Across the table from him was a boyish figure standing a little over three feet tall. Transparent, iridescent wings hung off the faerie’s back. The boy, or man, Sylvester supposed, was second consolidation. Sylvester could tell at a glance that he wasn’t Awakened. Faeries added a good foot to their height after their first grand mutation.

As for Transcendent faeries? Many were taller than Sylvester himself.

“Go ahead,” he said, gesturing.

The faerie plopped down onto a chair, looking a little sick. His giant mug of beer hit the table with a clunk. The tiny fey looked up at Sylvester with his pale-blue eyes.

“I hate astralships,” the faerie groused. “Every time I get on one, I can’t stop thinking about all the things that could go wrong. Out here in the dark, there are no anchors for your soul. I always drink myself silly to take my mind off of it. Oh, I’m Wynn by the way.”

Barring an interplanar gate, traveling the planes meant crossing the starry void. The journey could run from two to six weeks. Usually, the passage was done by way of ship. Interplanar gates were expensive, running from 40 to 100 soli per drom. And that was if an immortal could find a direct connection. If they had to take two or three hops, the price could easily double.

Back in the day, Esmeralda was able to pay this cost thanks to her cosmic ash. Even then, she only wasted those resources because she didn’t dare add them to the market. If bringing students from Earth had been expensive, the retreat from the Timeless Beryl Wilderness had been worse. They’d paid 5 to 10 million soli in consumable resources for the effort. If Sylvester included durable expenses, like the waystone of the Academy’s lost gate, the price jumped to 100 million soli.

Compared to gates, ships were a hundred times cheaper.

“Pirates are rare,” Sylvester commented. “And the Unmyeong Naru is well-armed. It has a third consolidation captain and several second consolidation fighters acting as support.”

“Facts don’t stop my stomach from turning upside down.” Wynn tilted his mug, letting frothy liquid pour down his throat. “I ran into a group one hundred years back. There’s nowhere to run in the starry void. These wings? They do nothing.”

The boyish figure spread his iridescent limbs. Motes of shimmering light fell from his wings.

Sylvester understood. Most forms of flight relied on the wind element. A faerie’s wings were no exception. Wynn could learn ki arts to solve this problem. Flux shift and the waltz of flowers both worked in the starry void. But fey from magical bloodlines rarely reached the manifestation realm. Most didn’t even cross into externalization. So, without specialized equipment, Wynn was helpless in the astral world.

“Just thinking about it makes me want to vomit. Let’s drop the subject,” the faerie decided. “What sends a man like you to Tartarus? I’m heading there to join the International Exposition of Magic. It’s going to be spectacular. Mages from all the planes will be there, sharing the latest spells and innovations. Of course, it’s no match for the Grand Exposition in Duat, but they only hold the Grand Exposition of Magic once per century.”

“Just touring,” Sylvester answered, sticking to his story. His crimson eyes flickered with curiosity. “As a warlock, I have a hard time imagining that anyone would share their spells for free.”

Wynn laughed.

“Of course not, at the International Exposition of Magic, they show off what their spells and innovations can do. If you want a piece of the prize, you have to pay for it in private.” The faerie’s pale-blue eyes danced. “There are also contests and prizes. In fact, my company will be putting on a display of our new golem cores. Some lucky fellow will even get to take a golem home with them!”

Sylvester looked intrigued.

In Hell, it was impossible to run a company of more than 100 demons without being a devil. Never mind the law, if anyone dared to get close to that threshold, a noble twit would launch a violent takeover. The Fey Federation wasn’t so restrictive over who owned or operated an enterprise. Of course, there was still a risk that random thugs would steal the business. To counter this, it wasn’t unusual for lower consolidation fey like Wynn to hire bodyguards or commission private security far beyond their strength.

This difference in culture meant the Fey Federation had a true merchant class.

“You own a golem manufacturing company, then?” Sylvester questioned.

“Not just manufacturing, we also do research and development.” Wynn’s wings fluttered with excitement, shedding copious motes. “Most fey think golems are an expensive substitute for tamed beasts or cheap labor. But golems have a major advantage. They’ll follow orders exactly without tire or rest.”

“They follow orders too exactly,” Sylvester noted, leaning back. “Which means the instruction set must be precise. However, to hold more complicated instructions demands a higher-quality psychic core.”

He had plenty of experience working with golems. They’d even managed to get a System kernel installed in a few golems several years back. Though costly, the constructs were a popular substitute for players when it came to drudge work. None of Sylvester’s holdings were involved in their manufacture. Instead, golem production was handled by a small company called Cyberdyne.

He did follow the latest research, however. Occasionally, he contributed to it.

“You know your stuff!” Wynn praised. “Getting a good instruction set is hard, but once you have it you’re gold. The real problem is the cost of the psychic core. The psychic core and the blood core are the most expensive parts of any golem. But, what if the psychic core could store more information, requiring no additional materials and no additional cost? That’s the innovation of my company. We’ll be showing it off by having a golem with a mid-grade psychic core smelt metals in an industrial furnace.”

Sylvester sat up, his jasper eyes gleaming. “A mid-grade core, you say?”

Soul Trade

Emergence of Souls

Virtually all souls enter the netherworld after passing through Unus Mundus, which separates the ethereal universe from its material counterpart. Crossing into the netherworld takes 50 years on average. Individual times can vary wildly, depending on the situation and luck.

One of these factors is the movement of the planes themselves. As the ethereal worlds slide over Unus Mundus, souls are stirred, percolating up through the phantasmal structure. Major flow paths are known as soul wells. From these locations, many pure souls are continuously produced. However, not all planes have clear soul flows, and even if they do, not all souls pass into a spiritual river. In such cases, the souls will emerge at a random location on the plane’s surface.

The War of Words had a large impact on the natural flow of souls. Material worlds controlled by Heaven, Hell, and the Fey Federation use fate nets to direct souls to the preferred plane. These souls, however, still pass through Unus Mundus. Further, the treaty requires that these nets redirect no more than a third of the souls to the desired planes.

Processing

Once a new soul has been captured, it must be processed. For large-scale, professional operations, it is common to first sort the souls by way of divination. The purity of the soul, the original gender, its size and depth, and its preferred disposition will all be determined. Errors do occur. In particular, it is difficult to judge a soul’s innate preference, often resulting in a bloodline mismatch.

Smaller-scale operations often eschew this process or carry out these actions in a sloppy manner.

The next step is to inject the soul with a bloodline code. Typically, this involves taking cells from the core of an existing demon, fey, or celestial, then implanting them into the soul. This implantation may be rejected, which can leave behind a chimeric taint. Chimeric souls may demand special processing, depending on the nature of their chimerism. In some cases, additional chimeric code may have to be added so that beast-kin bloodlines can be applied.

Once implanted, the code will replicate in the soul. A denizen can then be born 2 to 3 months later.

Training

Whether in Hell, Heaven, or the Fey Federation, all newborn immortals must be given a small amount of training before being put to use. In Heaven, where the processing of souls is monopolized by the government, newborn celestials are passed into ‘small-town families’ to be raised as children – appropriately delivered by a stork.

In Hell, it is common to be trained in a slave house, a clan hall, or by a small group as an apprentice, such as with a coven. In the latter cases, the cost is covered by the organization in question with a degree of spoilage expected.

The Fey Federation adopts a mixed approach, with large processing centers funded by government entities. Clans and other groups then pay or fight to draft souls as they are released. High-quality, talented individuals enter into these groups with favorable contracts, while less capable individuals receive less favorable outcomes. The dregs are eventually cast out onto the streets.

Trade

Uncoded souls are often sold. The owner of a soul well may decide to sell part or all of their production, depending on their interest. Loose souls collected by hunters or lucky parties are likewise sold in large volumes. This often results in situations where a plane produces more souls than it consumes, in which case the souls may be shipped to a more profitable destination.

Alternatively, they may be shipped to large processing centers, such as Geomun Naru in the Fey Federation. Once the souls are coded, they can then be brought back to locations like Hell, where the demons are then sold to interested parties.

Heaven doesn’t engage in the international trade of souls. Moderate volumes, however, are exchanged between Hell and the Fey Federation. Because souls are a strategic resource, most of the soul trade remains internal to the parent nation.
