We left the hotel the moment the zombies entered their daytime state, right as the sun’s rays lit Akihabara's streets in warm morning light.

There were several zombies still shambling around, but both their aggression and awareness had plummeted, which meant it wasn’t instant death the moment we entered the streets.

Even still, we gave them a wide berth anyway. No point taking chances.

“Yashas and the others will be expecting us at the subway soon,” Aerion said, her hand resting on her sword hilt as we walked. “Whatever this hunch of yours is, we shouldn’t take too long.”

“When have my hunches ever steered us wrong?” I said, giving her a scandalized look.

“Do you want that list chronologically or by severity of disaster?”

I chuckled. “Well, this won’t take long.”

My heart was pounding, but not because of Aerion or the zombies. “Just need to check on a store. We’ll be in and out.”

She gave me a skeptical look but didn’t argue. We’d been together long enough that she’d learned to trust my instincts, even when I was being cryptic as hell. And I was cryptic because if I was wrong, I’d sound like a nutjob. And if I was right…

Well, I sincerely hoped I wasn’t.

As we walked along Akiba’s main road, I found myself looking around with a strange mix of nostalgia and melancholy, despite the zombies.

The buildings were the same as I remembered from games set here. The buildings plastered with LCDs that even now displayed ads with anime characters, the garish colors I’d always felt were unnecessarily bright, almost comically so.

“What exactly is this place?” Aerion asked, eyeing a building covered in what had once been colorful anime character posters. “It looks like a shopping district?”

“Akihabara,” I said, gesturing at the street. “Or Akiba, for short. It started as an electronics district after World War Two—a place where you could get radios, televisions, computers, all that tech stuff. But over time, it became a destination for anime and manga culture.”

“Anime?”

“Animated stories. Usually from Japan, though the term got used more broadly. Think of it like… picture books, but in motion. There were all sorts of genres, from adventure to romance to comedy and everything in between.

Some were for kids, some were definitely not.” I pointed at a building with a faded maid cafe sign. “That was a themed restaurant where the servers would dress up as Victorian maids and treat customers like masters in a mansion.”

Under other circumstances, I would’ve enjoyed playing tour guide. But my attention kept drifting to the buildings, checking each one against my memory.

Every match tightened the knot in my stomach.

“That sounds…”

“Weird?” I said with a smirk.

“Relatable,” Aerion said, shaking her head. “I can certainly understand why one would wish to be treated like a noble.”

I just barely suppressed the laugh that welled up in me, easing some of the tension I felt. Still, I didn’t bother correcting her. She wouldn’t understand the psyche of people who were into that sort of thing. Nor, for that matter, did I want her to.

Her eyes widened as she took in the storefronts with their display windows still showing dusty merchandise, the signs in Japanese advertising goods I could only guess at from the pictures.

The sheer density of stuff that filled each shop was astounding, even to me.

“I always wanted to visit here, actually,” I said. “But international travel was expensive, and I was just a broke kid with a shitty job. Could never afford the trip.”

Aerion shook her head. “That commoners traveled across your world in a matter of hours will never cease to amaze me. Were that even possible on Axius, it would cost a king’s ransom.”

“I suppose it’d seem that way. I guess I should thank the Cataclysm for finally giving me the opportunity to visit here. Even if it is a zombie-infested apocalypse.”

I barely even registered my own words. My eyes kept scanning the street, finding match after match. Electronics stores, arcades, street food vendors. All exactly where Cosmo had shown them to me.

We walked in silence, and with each step, the dread in my stomach grew heavier.

Some had their doors broken in while others looked relatively unscathed. It was hard to tell what had happened here during the initial chaos. Whether people had fled or barricaded themselves inside.

Assuming there had even been a simulated human population when the Cataclysm materialized. For all I knew, it could’ve spawned the dungeon in with the zombies already present. No one even knew how or where the Cataclysms formed, let alone witnessing the formation of one.

As we got closer to where I needed to go, my tension started building. The street was familiar from the time Cosmo had imitated this place.

A bit too familiar, if I was honest. Not only was the layout the same, even the buildings were identical. Or was that just my memory lying to me?

On the other hand, it would absolutely be within Cosmo’s powers to perfectly mimic a place like this.

No. I was overthinking this. It was a coincidence. Had to be.

I started walking faster without even realizing it. The street ahead looked exactly like the one Cosmo had walked me down.

“Greg?” Aerion asked, jogging to catch up. “What’s wrong?”

I needed to know. Needed to confirm whether this horrible suspicion crawling up my spine was justified or just paranoia born from too many near-death experiences.

And then I saw it.

The corner shop he’d brought me to. A single-story place with unmistakable glass display cases showcasing rows upon rows of anime figures and merchandise.

The sign was in Japanese, of course, but I recognized it immediately.

My blood went cold.

“Greg!” Aerion called as I broke into a full sprint.

I barely heard her. My boots pounded against pavement, dodging past a shambling zombie that turned too slowly to track me. The store’s broken door loomed closer, and my hands were shaking.

I didn’t answer her because I couldn’t. Memories of this place overlaid my current reality as I relived that moment. I’d chosen two figures back then. One with Aerion clad in flaming red armor, and another of her in a simple nightgown.

But those figures wouldn’t be here. They couldn’t be here.

I reached the store and burst through the already-broken door, the glass crunching under my Vigor suit’s boots. The interior was empty and mostly intact. Rows of shelves lined the walls, each one filled with characters from several anime I’d seen and many more I hadn’t.

But none of them were Aerion.

I rushed from shelf to shelf through the too-narrow space between display racks, scanning the dozens of figures in each case. There were plenty of elves, of course, with pointy ears and even some with platinum-blonde hair. But none of them were of Aerion. None of them had her face or her unique half-blue, half-green eyes.

The relief that washed over me was so intense I nearly laughed. I’d just been paranoid. Cosmo had shown me a fake store, and this was the real one, and they just happened to be similar because Akihabara had a lot of figure shops and—

My eyes landed on the display case near the back.

Time seemed to grind to a halt.

“No,” I whispered.

My feet carried me forward mechanically. Each step felt like wading through mud. I didn’t want to get closer. Didn’t want to confirm what I was seeing.

But I couldn’t stop.

“Greg?” Aerion’s voice behind me sounded far away. “What is that?”

The rock sat there innocently enough. Spherical in shape and shimmering faintly, the exact same size and texture as another I’d seen before. Another rock that was intimately familiar. Except this one was a bright red, rich and lustrous. A red I’d only ever seen once before, on a set of flaming armor.

“It can’t be...” Aerion muttered.

Using the pommel of Light of the Fearless, I smashed the glass and reached in to touch it, and the moment my fingers made contact, my HUD flickered to life.

Name: Rocky the 256th

Abilities: Dormant (Inactive)

Classification: Champion of Order

Age: 25,675 years

Stats:

Vigor: Rocklike

Order: Rockish

Wisdom: Rockesque

Passion: Rocky

Grace: Rocked

Cunning: Rocket

Dominion: Rockadoo

“You’ve got to be shitting me,” I whispered, but my voice was trembling. “It’s another Rocky.”

“But that’s impossible,” Aerion said, staring at the rock in my hands. “As many Rockies as Order has out there, the chances of us stumbling upon one are incredibly low. Finding two is… that is impossible.”

It was impossible. It made no sense at all. But not because of the improbability. That was weird, but I could chalk that up to Cosmo knowing of the locations of his various Rockies and guiding us here to give us an edge. Maybe he’d even done the same for Galia, somehow, though I couldn’t conceive how.

The shelves went hazy as my vision blurred. Somewhere distant, I heard Aerion saying something, but the words didn’t register.

Because my mind was replaying every conversation I had with Cosmo, recalling his carefully chosen words and fake smiles. All the times I got that feeling like he'd guided me into choosing exactly what he'd wanted.

Remembering the circumstances around our discovery of Galia’s egg the Leviathan just happened to have swallowed. The sneak peek I had of that meeting of the gods when the System glitched out after the Convergence Trial… and now this.

I laughed. I laughed so hard I fell to my knees.

The truth had always been there, hadn’t it? Right in front of us this whole damn time.

The Cataclysms are a costly expense.

“The whole damn time.”

“Greg!” Aerion grabbed my shoulders, shaking me. “You’re scaring me. What’s wrong?”

I looked at her, and I must have looked like I’d seen a ghost, because her eyes were as big as saucers.

“It’s not random,” I said, my throat suddenly dry. “None of it was ever random.”

“What are you talking about?”

“The Cataclysms, Aerion,” I said, meeting her gaze but staring through her, to someplace a thousand miles away. “The Cataclysms are the work of the gods.”
