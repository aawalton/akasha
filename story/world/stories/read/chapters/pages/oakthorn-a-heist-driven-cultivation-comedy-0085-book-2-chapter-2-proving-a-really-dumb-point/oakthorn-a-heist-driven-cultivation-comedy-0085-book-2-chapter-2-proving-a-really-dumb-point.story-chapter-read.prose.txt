Cade had seen a lot of stupid things in his life—most of them in mirrors—but watching two dozen Lifekeepers try to contain a god-touched thief, his chaos-causing crew, and a giggly toddler deity might just top the list.

The spell circles ignited like a constellation of deadly stars, their power turning the air thick and metallic in the fading sunlight. That familiar burning sensation crawled up from his core, setting his sinuses on fire. The foreign power twisted against his destruction magic, and before he could warn anyone, molten rock erupted from his mouth in an explosive sneeze, cutting through three of the Lifekeepers’ spell circles and reducing a nearby market stall to ash.

“I suppose proper combat technique is too much to ask for,” Astrid drawled from her perch, her undead eyes gleaming with predatory amusement. “Though I’ll admit, projectile lava is a new one.”

His crew shifted into their deadly dance, each movement precise despite the chaos. Elena’s stiletto whip blade sang through the air as she covered Jer’s advance, her brother’s boots launching him at impossible distances between groups of enemies. Cade watched as Jer used his gravity-propelled boots along with his new adhesive gloves. His hands shot forward as he slid past the Lifekeepers and town guards, knocking them over like that pin game his crazy ex, Cici, had taught him years ago.

To Cade’s right, Orro flowed through the shadows like liquid darkness, his orange broken blade gleaming with otherworldly light. The sulking assassin faced four Lifekeepers at once. One of them lanced their spear forward, but Orro disappeared, only to reappear directly behind the poor wood elf. The bracelet on his wrist flickered as he phased back into reality. His daggers made quick work of the rest.

Cade sent two blasts of scalding wind at approaching guards, the percussive blast enough to send them flying into the stone wall beneath Astrid. Their armor crunched under the impact, hairline fractures spreading up the stones from where they hit.

His eyes found Rayka across the battlefield. Her enchanted necklace was finally put to good use. It didn’t turn her invisible. It merely made her enemies ignore her exact location, obscuring her from their attention. This gave her the perfect opportunity to rain precise death from above with her needle-like knives.

“You know, Astrid,” Cade called out as his bracers helped channel the chaotic energy trying to tear him apart, “most people just send a carrier pigeon!”

Nora’s massive Greatsword of Terror carved through the air, fear magic rolling off it in visible waves. A Lifekeeper raised his staff to block, only to have the blade bite clean through it.

“Now might be a good time for the box,” Elena suggested to the paladin, ducking under a spell. Cade’s blood went cold at the mere mention of that accursed object. And they thought his magic was evil.

“I am NOT starting another apocalypse,” Nora shot back, her hydrofang dagger sending precise jets of water to blind incoming attackers. “The last one was enough, thank you very much.”

The thief ducked under a wild swing from one of the guards and sent a kick infused with wind at her chest. When he stood, he immediately had to jump back as a spectral creature that charged through the enemy ranks screaming what sounded suspiciously like tavern songs. Cade followed the faun’s trajectory back to its source and found Evie standing there with a toothy grin.

“Oops. Sorry, Cade.” Evie sidestepped once, narrowly avoiding a battleaxe that entrenched itself into the cobblestone where she’d just been. The siren’s necklace pulsed with subtle power as she continued to barely avoid every strike sent in her direction.

“Oh dear,” Astrid sighed with exaggerated boredom. “Sirens are no fun. Though I’m surprised one as young as her is so attuned with her core already. Impressive.”

Lightning crackled overhead as storm clouds gathered, drawn by the chaos of clashing magics. The foreign power in Cade’s core resonated with each thunderclap, and he could feel Chubbs’ divine energy beginning to stir nearby. The toddler goddess clapped her hands in delight, and reality rippled around her like heat waves off summer stones.

More Lifekeepers poured into the fight, their crystalline focus-gems gleaming as they blocked Cade’s crew from escape.

The air hummed with contained power as dozens of new spell circles blazed to life. Above them, Astrid watched with the patient amusement of a cat studying wounded prey, her casual posture belying the deadly power that radiated from her undead form.

Cade felt Chubbs shift behind his back, her tiny fingers clutching his collar as another wave of spells lit up the square. He felt his core drain of yet more magic as he conjured a dome of fire to protect them. Chubbs screamed and clambered up his back as the Lifekeepers sent projectile after projectile into his shield. The weight of her reminded him exactly what they were fighting for—and exactly how screwed they’d be if these zealots got their hands on her.

The first hint that things were about to get worse came in a flash of silvery light that cut through three of Nora’s fear-enhanced barriers like they were paper. His heart sank as he tracked the spell’s origin: a Lifekeeper wearing the distinctive silver and gold-trimmed robes of a peak-stage silver ranker.

And he wasn’t alone.

Six more dressed just like him sauntered forward.

“Incoming!” Cade shouted, directing his crew’s attention to the new threats. “We’ve got silver rankers east and south of us, and—” His voice caught as golden light began to gather at the edges of the square. “Oh, that’s just cheating.”

At least three gold rankers were closing in on their position. Because apparently the universe decided they weren’t having enough fun already.

“Getting sloppy, little thief,” Astrid called down, and this time there was an edge beneath her amusement. “What’s your play now?”

Cade didn’t bother responding, too busy calculating odds that got worse by the second. His growing core, once a source of pride, felt like bringing a torch to a forest fire. The destruction magic within him churned restlessly, eager to be unleashed, but that would only prove them right about him being a threat.

Orro materialized at his side, the long shadows of early evening clinging to him like a second skin. “Northwest corner’s about to collapse. Elena can’t hold that position much longer.”

He was right. The twins were being pushed back by coordinated spell-fire, their usual acrobatic grace hampered by the sheer volume of attacks. Even Jer’s enhanced mobility couldn’t keep up with the gold ranker’s speed.

Gav’s growl turned into a snarl of pain as golden light seared through his mental defenses. “Their minds—there’s something wrong with their minds! Like they’re not entirely—” A blast of force sent him tumbling, saved only by Nora’s quick intervention with her blade.

The crew was adapting, but they were running out of options. Rayka’s precise attacks now had to target spell matrices instead of the casters themselves, disrupting magic that would have torn them apart. Evie’s staff carved paths through the chaos, but each spectral charge met increasingly powerful counterspells.

The real problem? The golden-ranked Lifekeepers weren’t even trying to hit them yet. They were methodically laying down spell foundations, their magic creating a complex geometric pattern that Cade’s experience with their ward book recognized all too well.

“They’re building a containment lattice,” he muttered, mind racing through possibilities. “If they complete that array—”

“We all die horrible deaths?” Jer suggested, landing beside him in a roll that brought Bunny scrambling to Cade’s shoulder instead. The dragonling’s claws dug in with unusual tension, sensing the growing danger. He put his wings protectively over where Chubbs also hid behind Cade.

“Worse,” Cade corrected, watching golden light begin to trace familiar patterns in the air. “They’ll be able to drain our cores. And I don’t think Chubbs would survive that particular procedure.”

The foreign power in his core twisted again, as if responding to the threat. The destruction magic rose to meet it, and for a moment he tasted steel and starlight on his tongue. But it wasn’t enough. Not against this many high-ranked opponents.

Astrid’s laughter rang out again, but this time it held a note of genuine curiosity. “Now this is interesting. Tell me, Stormhollow—what would you be willing to trade for a way out of this rather spectacular mess you’ve made?”

Cade knew a loaded question when he heard one.

But as another gold ranker stepped into view, their magic adding another layer to the growing containment pattern, he had to admit they were running out of alternatives.

The real question was: what game was Astrid Clover really playing?

“Not interested in deals with the undead,” Cade called back to Astrid, though the words tasted like ash in his mouth as golden light carved through the air like molten sunrise.

The silver-ranked Lifekeepers were bad enough, their spells a coordinated dance of destruction that pushed his crew to their limits. But the gold rankers—they were something else entirely.

The first real hit came without warning. One moment Elena was weaving through a complex pattern of counterstrikes, the next she was flying backward, golden energy crackling across her body. She hit the wall hard enough to crack stone.

“Elena!” Jer’s voice cracked as he abandoned his position to reach his sister. The movement left a gap in their defense, and silver-ranked magic poured through like a tide.

Nora’s blade sang as she tried to cover the breach, but even her fear magic seemed to pale against the overwhelming force. A golden spell shattered her barrier, sending her stumbling. “We can’t hold this!”

The containment lattice was nearly complete, threads of power weaving together overhead like a net of burning stars. Cade’s destruction magic roiled beneath his skin, begging to be unleashed, but he knew it wouldn’t be enough.

Rayka dropped from her perch as golden light consumed it, rolling to her feet beside him. Blood trickled from a cut above her eye.

“Cade,” she said softly, the word heavy with meaning. They both knew what happened to thieves who ran out of options.

Orro materialized from shadow, his usually fluid movements now sharp with barely contained pain. Two silver-ranked spells had caught him mid-phase, and the damage showed. Still, the assassin rolled with the blows and twisted. When he completed his turn, two daggers flew through the air, colliding with the foreheads of the two spellcasters who’d struck him first.

“Northwest is lost. South in thirty seconds.” Orro’s eyes met Rayka’s for a heartbeat, and Cade saw something there he’d never noticed before—fear, not for himself, but for her.

Gav’s mental assault on the nearest gold ranker earned him nothing but a backlash that dropped him to his knees, blood trickling from his nose. The suave telepath’s eyes began to flash with pearlescent energy. Cade knew that could only mean his body was ready to shift just to save itself, and while their favorite werewolf was making huge strides in harnessing his shifts, there was no guarantee he’d distinguish friend from foe if he changed now.

Think.

How do we get out of this? Cade forced himself to consider every option, every escape route.

But these Lifekeepers were thorough, apparently adapting from the two previous times Cade and his crew had slipped their nets.

Bunny’s claws dug deeper into Cade’s shoulder as Chubbs whimpered against his back, her face buried in his tunic. The sound cut through him sharper than any blade.

“Please, Cadey. I don’t wanna be here anymore. Please,” Chubbs sobbed. A crystalline spear slammed not two feet from where she hid, and her sobs redoubled.

They weren’t just going to lose—they were going to fail her. And if these Elysians had bad blood with him, he couldn’t imagine what they’d do when they found out they were protecting the very goddess who’d lied to them for the past five thousand years.

No. He wouldn’t let that happen.

He wasn’t Hugh. He would not fail his crew.

“Last chance, Stormhollow,” Astrid’s voice carried an edge of finality now. “My offer expires when that lattice closes. Ten seconds, by my count.”

Golden light blazed as one of the gold rankers stepped forward, magic gathering around their hands like solar flares. Through the haze of power, Cade caught the slight smile on their face.

Eight seconds.

Elena was still down, Jer crouched protectively over her as spells rained around where they hid. Nora’s blade moved slower, exhaustion finally showing through her iron discipline. Evie’s voice had gone quiet, either from strain or injury.

Five seconds.

The foreign power in his core twisted violently, as if sensing approaching doom. He threw out tight columns of searing flames and wind, but the gold rankers swatted them away like flies. It would never be enough.

Three seconds.

Rayka’s hand found his, a silent question in the touch. Trust me to trust you.

“Fine!” Cade’s voice cut through the chaos like a blade. “What’s your price, Clover?”

The lattice froze, incomplete, as a shaft of darkness stabbed deep into the array’s complex geometry. Astrid’s laughter rang across the battlefield.

“Now that,” she said, “is a much more interesting question.”

“My terms are simple,” Astrid said, rising to her full height. “You’re going to steal something for me. Something that, quite frankly, you have no business touching. But then again—” Her undead eyes flickered with ancient magic. “—neither do they.”

The gold ranker closest to her laughed. “The great Astrid Clover, bargaining with thieves? How far the mighty have—”

He never finished.

One moment Astrid was atop the building, the next she was behind him, her ebony staff a blur of motion. The gold ranker’s spell matrix shattered like glass, the pieces dissolving into mist before they hit the ground. His body followed a heartbeat later.

The square erupted into chaos, but it was a different kind now. Where before there had been coordinated assault, now there was desperate survival. Astrid moved like death itself had learned to dance, each step precise, each strike inevitable.

“Watch closely, Stormhollow,” she called out, her voice carrying easily over the sounds of combat. “Consider this a free lesson in what real power looks like.”

Three silver rankers tried to box her in with synchronized spell patterns. Astrid flowed between their attacks like water through stones, her staff leaving trails of darkness in its wake.

The first fell to a strike that somehow hit from two directions at once. The second’s shield spell might as well have been paper. The third managed to complete his incantation—only to have Astrid catch the magic in her bare hand and crush it.

“Impossible,” Cade breathed.

He’d seen Orro fight, had witnessed the assassin’s lethal grace, but this... this was something else entirely. Every movement contained perfectly controlled violence, every strike calculated to maximum effect. She wasn’t just fighting—she was solving equations written in blood and bone.

A gold ranker unleashed a devastating combination of light and force magic. Astrid’s response was almost lazy, her staff spinning in a complex pattern that somehow turned the magic back on itself. The resulting explosion painted the square in colors Cade had never seen before and hoped to never see again.

“The thing about Lifekeepers,” Astrid lectured as she danced through their increasingly desperate attacks, “is that they’re so focused on controlling magic that they forget about controlling themselves.” Her staff cracked against a copper ranker’s knee, the sound somehow wrong in ways that made Cade’s teeth ache. “The body is just as much a weapon as any spell.”

To demonstrate, she dropped her staff entirely. The next sequence of attacks was pure martial prowess—kicks that appeared to bend space, strikes that ignored magical barriers completely, and throws that seemed to use gravity as a suggestion rather than a law. Three more Lifekeepers fell before the staff hit the ground.

Cade felt his crew watching with the same horrified fascination. Even Orro’s usual stoic expression had cracked, showing a mix of professional appreciation and genuine concern. This wasn’t just combat mastery—this was artistry painted in violence.

“Running low on dance partners,” Astrid called out, standing among the fallen with a nonchalance that was almost as disturbing as the killing that put them there. The remaining Lifekeepers had backed away, their golden and silver magics dim compared to the shadows gathering around her form. “Anyone else want to question my choice of business associates?”

The head Lifekeeper raised his hand, golden light gathering into what had to be his most powerful attack yet. Astrid’s smile widened.

“Oh good,” she purred. “I was worried you’d be smart about this.”

What happened next would feature in Cade’s nightmares for years to come. It wasn’t just that she won—it was that she made it look like art. Like poetry. Like a love letter written to violence itself.

When it was over, nothing remained of the final gold ranker but a spray of blood and gore nearly thirty feet long.

Astrid retrieved her staff and turned to face Cade’s crew. Blood that wasn’t hers painted delicate patterns across her clothes, like a painter’s careful brushstrokes. Her undead eyes gleamed with something ancient and terrible.

“Now then,” she said, as casual as if they were discussing the weather, “about that job I mentioned...”

Behind her, the last rays of sunset painted the devastated square in shades of gold and crimson. None of the Lifekeepers were dead—Cade realized that with a start. Broken, unconscious, possibly traumatized for life, but breathing. She’d orchestrated this entire display without a single fatality save for that last gold ranker, whose blood now covered his brethren.

That, somehow, was the most terrifying part of all.
