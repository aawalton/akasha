This wasn't the first time Jinyo had heard an Unknown's thoughts. But never before had they been this clear. The raw anguish of witnessing his son's death—still fresh—and the bitterness that had been building since the first Heaven's Secret Surveillance all came crashing down at once. Rage consumed his composure entirely.

"What do you want?! How many more lives do you need to destroy before you're satisfied?! What does Heaven even want?!" Jinyo roared. Ever since he'd learned of the Unknown's existence, not a single night had passed where Jinyo slept soundly. He was always thinking about the future—how to grow stronger, how to change what was coming. And in this second Surveillance, beyond learning the cause of Gretel's death, there was one thing Jinyo desperately wished he'd never seen.

Either he or Rina had already been dead for twenty years in Gretel's timeline. Gretel was currently seven years old. That meant either he or Rina would die when Gretel was only eleven. Jinyo was certain the one who'd die wasn't him—it was Rina.

Rina's work was the same as Jinyo's mother's. More importantly, after saving Luciria, Jinyo had come to understand what Rina's primary duty actually involved. It wasn't standard Hunter work—hunting dangerous monsters that escaped from Gates, or clearing high-difficulty Gates.

Rina's mission was specifically to fight the Unknown. The probability that she'd die in the line of duty was extremely high. Even though the nature of their relationship was still ambiguous, Rina was someone important to Jinyo. There was no way he wouldn't be furious.

[You can hear my thoughts.]

"You're practically screaming—of course I can hear you."

[Strange.]

[You are the only one who can.]

[No—]

[If it's you—]

[—then it makes perfect sense.] The Unknown's psychic pressure multiplied exponentially. It bore down on Jinyo's brain with a throbbing, splitting pain. Jinyo screamed. He fought not to bite his own tongue to escape the agony.

[You are a dangerous being.]

[I understand now that your existence will destroy our Heaven.]

[Even if it costs my life, I will drag you to hell.]

[I will make you regret revealing yourself too soon.]

[In ten years—or a hundred—you will look back on this day and wish you had never learned certain truths. Some truths are better left unknown.]

[It will make you realize just how insignificant you truly are.]

[A pleasure to meet you, Celestial Fiend... and goodbye.] The moment the Unknown's voice faded, Jinyo returned to normal—but found no peace. A premonition told him something terrible was coming. The system window had been flashing red ever since his Hostility toward Heaven had crossed the threshold, and it still hadn't reverted to gold. It wasn't over!

"Hey, little brother—you alright?! You look like the world's about to end." Theo's face was creased with concern.

"Something feels wrong. Like something extremely dangerous is closing in. Something terrifying." Jinyo grabbed his phone and fired off messages to Rina and Naho, urging them both to leave immediately—he'd explain later.

Jinyo burst out of the restroom. The instinct coiled inside him was pulling toward whatever he sensed as dangerous. He stumbled over a cleaning robot and went sprawling face-first. Suspicious stares fixed on him, but Jinyo didn't care. He scrambled up and sprinted up the stairwell all the way to the rooftop.

The dread was coming from above. Jinyo thought if he could reach the top, he'd finally see what was making him terrified to his core. But what he found was worse than he'd imagined. Even standing two hundred meters above the ground—at the highest point with a view spanning nearly the entire city—he still felt like he hadn't climbed high enough.

When he raised his eyes to the black ocean above, where millions of stars jostled to outshine one another, Jinyo understood. The danger he feared was there. And in that moment, the Unknown's words echoed back to him.

It would make him regret learning a certain truth.

A truth better left unknown.

Because knowing it was no different from a curse that would warp the course of his life.

Something moved at a speed beyond human perception. It materialized just one building's height above Jinyo's head. Then it fractured outward—becoming a hole that swallowed everything it touched.

"Little brother!" Theo sprinted in and launched himself into a flying kick aimed at Jinyo's back, trying to knock him off the roof and away from the anomaly. But it was already too late. The hole that had spawned from nothingness consumed Jinyo—and Theo along with him.

At the medical ward, Rina received Jinyo's message. She was already carrying Gretel in one arm and Ryse tucked under the other. The woman ran with everything she had—but it still wasn't fast enough.

'Jinyo... I'm sorry.' Rina closed her eyes and allowed the black hole to swallow her—along with both children.

"You idiot! Don't stop running!" Naho lunged to grab Rina's clothes, but it was already too late.

In the chaos—examinees and staff scrambling to flee—Antonio walked calmly to the emergency panel, smashed the glass with his fist, and pulled out a fire axe. He stood ready. The black hole expanded to within meters of the exam control room before it finally halted—and vanished.

Somewhere on the Western Continent stood an elliptical, golden saucer-shaped building—home to a research organization that had been operational for over a hundred years. Hundreds of scientists passed through its doors each day, every one of them an elite mind dedicated to the advancement of humanity.

If power-users were the swords and shields protecting civilization's daily life, then these scientists were the brains and eyes—investigating, analyzing, and surveilling any and all threats. Countless classified projects were run here, never disclosed to the public. Many incidents that had occurred were never committed to record.

This was The Sect—the Organization for Metaphysical Phenomena Research.

Dr. Geo brewed his black coffee with two sugar cubes, same as every day. His job was to sit in front of a supercomputer linked to more than twenty thousand satellites in Earth's orbit. The world had already collapsed once—in 1971.

Since 1971, the once-peaceful world had faced metaphysical phenomena. Dimensional rifts appeared across every continent. Sometimes they took the form of gates. Sometimes tunnels, or spherical objects. They were ticking time bombs of destruction. The people of that era had no way to counter dimensional rifts—they only knew that anything that got close would be sucked into the void.

Scientists named these metaphysical phenomena Hollow Gates—bottomless, hollowed-out doorways of staggering wonder, their origins a complete mystery. When a Hollow Gate's timer expired, it would unleash catastrophic energy. Some detonated with the force of an atomic bomb. The only way to prevent the explosion was to enter the Gate and neutralize it before time ran out.

The Sect was the organization that studied the formation of Hollow Gates, developed detection methods, and relayed warnings to the local governments of each nation. Geo found the work tedious—staring all day at graphs that ordinary people couldn't read, logging data, and coordinating with people in dozens of languages. But he didn't hate it, because it was honorable work.

More than five specialists rotated shifts with Geo, and over ten people held the same role to cover every continent. Geo was responsible for monitoring the Southeastern Continental Region. If he neglected his duties, it could mean the deaths of innocent people.

Black coffee with sugar—his pre-shift ritual gave him the energy he needed. It was the only form of entertainment The Sect permitted its monitors. He wasn't even allowed to bring in a mobile phone. Paper and pens were forbidden as well. Every use of the computer had to be reviewed and authorized at every step, which was maddening. But since black coffee was low in calories and its only active ingredient was caffeine, The Sect allowed it.

Every two to three days, a dimensional fluctuation would occur. His job was to notify the relevant government that a Hollow Gate was about to manifest at a specific location within the coming days. That alone saved countless lives—because instead of reacting after disaster struck, knowing where it would happen reduced civilian casualties to zero. Geo stared at the screen until his eyes went dry. Then, at approximately 2000 hours, he spotted a violent fluctuation.

How violent? The waveform shot upward like a rocket. This wasn't just precursor tremors before a phenomenon—it was the sudden, instantaneous formation of a Hollow Gate. Geo's heart nearly dropped to his ankles. But what puzzled him was that the spike, which had rocketed to its peak, plummeted back to a flat line—as though nothing had ever happened.

"What the hell—that scared me half to death." Dr. Geo pressed a hand to his chest. He wasn't religious, but he couldn't help muttering a thank-you to God. This anomaly was too strange for someone who'd worked as an observer for two years. Still, Geo requested data access privileges and pulled the logs for review. He found it was far too hasty to dismiss this as a glitch or system error.

"So you're telling me a Gate opened at 20:03:48 and closed at 20:04:56? That's impossible. Equix is the pillar of humanity. Its processing has never been wrong..." If Equix said a Hollow Gate had formed and closed in one minute and eight seconds, then that was exactly what had happened. His duty was simply to report this case up the chain. That was the job Geo had been assigned.

Hours later, Geo learned that at a rural Training Center in a certain country, a sudden Hollow Gate had appeared. It had consumed examinees, their families, and staff—all of whom remained missing with their fates unknown. For now, only a handful of people were paying attention to this phenomenon. But in the near future, it would become a critical milestone that demanded the world's attention.

In a black forest reeking of rotting corpses, an overcast sky devoid of any trace of sunlight made it impossible to tell day from night. A land shrouded in ashen mist and the howling of wrathful spirits.

Green raindrops fell from the sky. The stench—like sewer water—didn't invite contact with skin. The drops spattered onto a white, unconscious body. Emerald-green eyes stared wide open, lying still as a human mannequin. Pale skin and lips white as paper. Clear droplets of water trickled into his eyes, mouth, and nose.

A crack of thunder jolted the young man awake. He gasped like someone waking from a nightmare. Jinyo clutched his chest, heart ready to burst. The agony of Gretel's death was still seared into his mind—along with Ryse's death from the first Surveillance.

If every use of a Heaven's Secret Surveillance Ticket forced him to relive the pain from every previous one, Jinyo would have lost his mind by the tenth ticket. What remained now was neurological pain from the psychic interference and a hunger so ravenous it felt like a million parasites screaming for food inside his stomach.

Jinyo looked around. Every nearby tree had withered into dry, brittle husks. Strangely, green leaves still littered the ground around the roots—proof that these had been lush, thriving trees just moments ago. He sensed something enigmatic about this land that he couldn't quite grasp.

'That black hole... was that a Hollow Gate?' Jinyo had studied Hollow Gates to some extent. A Gate-type Hollow Gate appeared as a flat plane of light or a spiraling vortex. A Tunnel-type connected directly to another world—you could see the other side if you looked in. An Object-type took the form of a three-dimensional shape visible from every angle. But the black hole he'd seen didn't match any classification in the textbooks.

'How is this possible? Is it because of us?' Jinyo's memories were hazy. He'd heard screaming. Flashes of the Training Center being reduced to dust in an instant. Many people had been caught in the collateral of that insane black hole.

Jinyo opened the system out of habit. That was when an interesting change occurred.

"Poor decisions... I just wanted to know how Gretel would die. And I also saved Luciria."

You were given the chance to see your son's and daughter's futures so you could find ways to extend their lives. But know this: giving Heaven an opening to intervene means turning good things into something far worse.] The system responded to Jinyo directly. This was likely the first time the system had ever answered him this candidly. It left a lump in his chest.

Hostility toward Heaven... it meant the degree to which Heaven regarded him as an enemy.

Exceeding the system's limit meant that a future which should have improved would instead deteriorate.

Luciria, who should still be at the exam grounds. Gretel and Ryse, whose causes of death Jinyo had uncovered through the Surveillance—bringing him closer to a cure. All of it could now mean that everyone Jinyo had tried to save would die right here, right now.
