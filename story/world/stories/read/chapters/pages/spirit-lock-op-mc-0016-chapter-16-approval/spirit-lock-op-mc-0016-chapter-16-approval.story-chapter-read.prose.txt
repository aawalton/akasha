Hayden summoned the Winged Horix. Its striking white fur and beady eyes made it incredibly cute, and its wings made it look majestic.

What a wonderful beast… she thought as she watched it fly around.

“How would you like to test me?” Hayden asked.

Briggs snapped back to the present. “I’m going to start by using the Divine Eyes spirit to ensure that you don’t have any stones or artifacts on you. It can see through your clothing, but it’s not even remotely sexual. It’ll only show your body’s core and coran channels, and that’s no more attractive than seeing a wax sculpture that shows off the human circulatory system.”

“I know how the spirit sees,” Hayden said. “You have my consent.”

Her eyebrow twitched at his dull arrogance, but she said, “Then hold still.”

She activated Trip, the name she gave to Divine Eyes' spirit, and the world became a vibrant cascade of gold and greens and blues, with gold representing cora. She expected to see a normal student’s core, but her lips quivered when she saw Hayden’s body.

How…? What?

Briggs felt the need to chain questions together—that’s how baffled she was. Hayden’s core didn’t have a gate open, but it was as bright as a C-rank rifter’s, and his circulation was almost neon gold—

And he wasn’t actively breathing!

The scene left her spellbound.

“Is there a problem?” Hayden asked.

A sharp blade of irritation stabbed into her frontal cortex, and she had a snap desire to say, Yes, there’s a problem! You’re breathing like an S-rank scion! But she forced herself to calm down, lulling her sharp pang of annoyance into a dull throb.

“No,” she said. “Let’s commence the test. I’m going to lift up my palm and summon a wind spirit. Use your horix to freeze it out. Do you understand?”

“Yes.”

“Then let’s begin.” She waited until the horix neared her and tried to summon her wind spirit. It didn’t summon—because the flying mouse was already inhaling massive quantities of cora. He wasn’t even glowing like in the video!

How’s that much cora not killing it? Briggs cried. She planned to ask politely, but she found herself challenging it instead. She had a C-rank wind spirit in the second evolution. It wasn’t much in the grand scheme of things, but its attraction capabilities were vastly superior to a D-rank beast! There was no way its attraction properties were stronger than her spirit. So she cranked her spirit to full performance to prove it.

As Briggs cranked her spirit to full performance, Hayden nominally increased Gluttony’s. The torrent entering it increased massively, leaving no cora for her spirit.

It wasn’t just her spirit that lacked cora—Galma was a cora desert.

What the hell is this thing? Briggs silently cried. She studied Hayden, who was waiting patiently, and said, “You pass, obviously. But… I’m just confused. What are you doing? Because I’ve never heard of a horix being able to freeze out a full area. I mean, if people knew it could do that, they’d pay unruly sums for it.”

“That’s true,” Hayden said. “That’s why I must respectfully decline that answer. The last thing I want is rich elites coercing me into releasing it.”

“That’s… reasonable. But…”

I’m not asking about the horix, she finished in her thoughts. No spirit’s that powerful. You're doing something.

Spirits grew stronger as they evolved, but this one was as strong as a third-evolution siege spirit right after awakening. Sure, people yielded better results with the right catalyst—but Hayden hadn’t even used a catalyst. There was no way to bring out a spirit’s potential to that degree—unless he knew something they didn’t.

I need to report this… immediately.

“Is there anything else?” Hayden asked.

Briggs shook her head. “No. Feel free to return to your dorm. Loricks will bring you to Junolyx on schedule.”

Hayden bowed his head. “Thank you, Ms. Ragger.”

Briggs watched him walk away with a complex expression. Then she contacted Loricks. “Uh, hello. This is Briggs Ragger of the RDA. I was the one sent to assess Hayden. No, there’s nothing wrong. He passed. I just have one question. What was Hayden’s reaction when he obtained the horix? Huh… Are you sure? Yes, I’d appreciate the video. Yes, thank you.”

Briggs hung up and waited for footage of the lottery. She then watched in the second she got it. The video confirmed her suspicions. Hayden's eyes widened when he was offered the horix. Then he refused to trade it.

He knew what he was getting… she thought. But why would he know how to use something so obscure?

Briggs returned to her ship and messaged her boss. The text read: “I’m sending you a video of Hayden Reskco’s performance. It was nothing short of monumental.”

Ten minutes later, she got a reply: “Inform Mayle Haya that we need to monitor him.”

“Understood.”

Briggs called Mayle in augmented reality mode. To her surprise, Mayle answered, appearing before Briggs in a chair. Mayle was so realistic that Briggs could see the lint on her green blouse. She was also intimidating. Despite being well over a century, she looked thirty at most due to her regular use of cleansing elixirs.

“Principal Haya…” Briggs said nervously. “Thank you for answering.”

Mayle shifted one leg over the other, hands in her lap. “It’s not a problem. In fact, I was just about to call you. Loricks told me that there was a special student in this year’s crop. Is that true?”

Briggs cleared her throat and blinked rapidly. “Well, he certainly is that. In fact, the RDA wants you to monitor him.”

Hayden expected people to monitor him, so he didn’t worry about it. The next day, he sat calmly in a private shuttle, drinking a cup of tea without a care in the world. Lina watched him from across the aisle with what appeared to be burning curiosity. “Yes?” he finally asked.

“Yes, he says,” Lina laughed bitterly. She turned and fell silent again, but then she sharply turned back. “Okay. I gotta know. What did you do to him?”

Hayden followed her gaze and saw Rosen. He had ear muffs and a blindfold on, and he was hugging his travel pillow like a support animal.

“That’s not normal after wide scale humiliation?” Hayden mused.

“No,” Lina said. “Not for Rosen. And even if it was, he’s nipping at you like an abused dog.”

“If you ask me, that’s a positive sign of growth.”

Lina flashed an involuntary smile and then brooded about it. “What’d you do with Hayden?” she asked.

“I lived out his life, then returned to the past to put all that naive, idealistic garbage in his head into a wood fire stove. I then put a kettle onto the fire, and made this tea.” He blew a misty veil of steam off the cup. “You want to try it?”

Lina burst into involuntary laughter and then covered her mouth, likely grieving over her lost sense of control. Her smile then simmered into a frown, and she said, “Hey, Hayden. I know it’s not going to mean much, given that I was terrible to you when you were a nobody, and now I’m changing my mind now that you’re popular, but…” She grimaced, chuckled darkly, and then sighed. “Never mind.”

“I shall absolve you of your past transgressions,” Hayden said, sipping his tea.

“Oh, how magnanimous of you,” Lina mocked. Then, she hushed. “Seriously, though. You need to chill. Just because the girls at Galma treated you like a god, it doesn’t make you one. The second you go to Junolyx, people’re gonna treat you like garbage again. And unlike Galma, everyone’s as rich as Rosen. If you pull these stunts, you’re going to get yourself in serious trouble.”

“While I appreciate your warning, I must refuse,” Hayden said. “If all people don’t revere me as a god in the near future, I’m afraid it’ll destroy my fragile soul.”

Lina snorted. “You know, I didn’t like the old you… because you had this… moody, broody… I don’t know… janky personality? And now… you’re the opposite. Good job, you’re the opposite. You broke free. But… Um… yeah. That’s also pretty….”

“Charming?” Hayden mused.

“Silence it is,” Lina declared, looking out the window. In her window's reflection, she could see a hint of a smile. She instantly wiped it off her face. Hayden saw all of it, chuckling into his cup during takeoff. It would be an amusing trip.

It was, but it was short-lived.

In the space-faring age, the thousand-mile trip to Junolyx took less than an hour. It started out a high-speed blur of pines, and then they quickly slowed, lowering into a dazzling dreamscape of pink and white trees.
