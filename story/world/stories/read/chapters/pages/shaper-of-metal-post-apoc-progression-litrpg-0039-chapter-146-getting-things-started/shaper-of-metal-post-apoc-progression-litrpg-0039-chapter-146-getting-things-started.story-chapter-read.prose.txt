Chapter 146: Getting Things Started

Jack parted ways with Inkblot, whose final point was that ‘he had next,’ whenever it might be, with firm, intent seriousness, to which Jack could hardly argue. With that matter concluded, Jack arranged for some personal training with powers. Power Park was a no-go now, which felt both good as an accomplishment and something of a loss at once.

Got good memories there… such as eating that meter-long sandwich. Ahh…. we were great together, weren’t we, Zweihänder?

On the way to an isolated chamber for training, Jack shared the news with Highfive and Bo about Inkblot so they wouldn’t stress. They both stated they’d send their well-wishes personally to him, and that was that.

Jack set about first playing with and testing out [Hot From the Forge], superheating metal into cherry-red territory. Initial tests felt like he was now a ‘lava user’ if he wanted, which was quite badass. Maximum output was Magnitude around 700, equivalent to a Level 7 or 8 primary stat, and roughly equivalent to what he produced when striking with metal. In terms of the damage it did, though, things looked a lot different.

It could set things on fire with ease, and it melted the simulated flesh of lesser creatures on contact. Anything without special protections got burned. He didn’t exactly savor using this horror wantonly on living things, but… presumably, his enemies were almost exclusively monsters, and he absolutely would. He made the bed, and he had to sleep in it; he wouldn’t let a necessity be a problem, in any case.

Heat had great ramping potential, as it tended to break down most materials with more time transferring that heat. It added extra value to his ‘tag and hold’ strategies, especially against things that wouldn’t care about being choked. He could wear down armor and toughness on anything not particularly resistant to heat.

He also — somewhat hesitantly at first — tested out his resistance to heat. Currently, he had to go shirtless for this, having learned his lesson the hard way when he slightly singed his uniform, even from inside his jacket. Murphy’s Law. Besides that, it might be fire-resistant, but it wasn’t immune, and the heat got quite intense.

After he set aside the shirt, he tested molten metal on his skin, of higher and higher intensity. Nothing. At most, he felt something more approaching cold as it heated up. A little identifying tingle, essentially. It did seem to drain the heat slightly.

Probably due to the fact that it’s being absorbed and then shunted into nowhere.

If he added a little metal somewhere in the mix, he could effectively transfer the heat from one thing to another, even if touching him to some degree. All he had to do was network metal threads to the hot thing. Heat didn’t particularly like getting to him if it could avoid it, and he got better at tweaking the metal threads to transfer it more efficiently.

It also occurred to him he should probably ditch normal clothes for metallic threads, which he could allow to heat without worrying as much about them ‘going up.’

Even before he queried Mini, she interrupted with a chime. <Your clothing will be all high-performance metallic thread, yes. This was already planned, but good thinking nonetheless, Jack.>

“Thanks, but will it look weird?”

<Not at all. Only very acute observation would tell the difference. By the way, the full kit is planned for delivery and getting used to in four hours, aside from the Jackette. This will be taken each night when you’re sleeping, for any relevant tweaks, hopefully with a final form the day before leaving.>

“Noted. I certainly plan on training with it a bit more.”

Jack next went through the gamut of basics to update himself to the 6.0 life, which had included Create, Control, and Augment increases. He performed such familiar practices as cord-work, throwing various projectiles, making walls, lifting, shifting between shapes, and so on, in something already pretty close to a routine.

He put some extra emphasis on Augment, being the stat that had seen the most percentage of change since last. It was noticeable yet understated, perhaps thanks to the nature of the stat. His materials were harder and stronger, and that was that. He was rather glad he’d increased it, though, because his materials had improved significantly beyond physical baselines. His steel wasn’t steel, but fine-grade Jacksteel.

Order yours right now for twenty percent off! Don’t miss this… steal!

Because he was rusty with it, Jack took some time with the Jackette, first simply playing around with reshaping it into different forms — like melee weapons — and progressively bumping up the stress training. He let simulations of monsters get close, which ended up with mixed results, but it was little surprise when it wasn’t his specialty. His best bet was using the Jackette to help with stalling and creating range or separation with firmer barriers.

On the note of emergency response, he practiced a little with the net launcher, too. This was intended for the moments that weren’t quite ‘oh shit, I’m dead’ like the last-ditch melee of the Jackette. It was specifically a prevention measure of someone getting inside his guard, so he’d want to use it between two and ten meters distance or so.

It worked great, though he had to get used to the somewhat slower act of pulling a trigger again. It made up for this quickly after the net fired, thanks to the complete lack of any Allotment going into firing. As such, he could follow up much more easily, collapsing into the net and working it immediately as needed. By default, this was guiding and adjusting the net’s trajectory to make sure it hit, reinforcing it, and lending pure strength into the binding of the wrap. It was wonderful for dropping and pinning down targets. In many cases, he could leave it with an Anchor instance holding a target and move on while they struggled with it futilely.

One thing he tried out was expanding the net while simultaneously making the thread into thin, sharp blades, turning the use of the net into something much deadlier. Pretty awesome! It did make it more fragile, but he could do this for additional nastiness with any cord. Thanks to Augment 2.0, general blade sharpness was much improved.

An hour and a half went by like nothing, and he got a reminder from Mini. <The group is meeting in Simulation Chamber 10 in fifteen minutes.>

“Oh, right. I guess I got carried away.” In truth, he wanted to keep going, feeling like he had a lot more ready to develop, maybe even hit a breakthrough or two, just from so much pent-up time without this sort of dedicated solo practice. “Bummer. But I guess it’s time for group play, eh?”

🌑 🌒 🌓 🌔 🌕

Augment: Absorption has improved to 0.9!

Augment: Tensile has improved to 0.7!

Control: Throw has improved to 4.5!

Create: Brace has improved to 0.3!

Wall Art has improved to 1.4!

Jackettery has improved to 1.2!

New Technique gained! Transmute: Heatwork 0.1 has been added to your stat bio.

Jack got the notices as he efficiently, hyper-speed showered and changed into a new uniform. While he was doing this, he took a quick look at the new thing.

Transmute: Heatwork: Allows various ‘tricks’ with the heat you generate and contain in your metal. Transfer heat, improve absorption or radiation, intensify at precise points or more suddenly, and so on.

Looks pretty damned handy, I’d say. That can improve my speed at heating up if focused on a smaller portion.

Getting to Sim Chamber 10 was a fairly familiar process that was eerily the same as Jack had gone through to get to Chamber 4 — the first place he’d practiced for the PACCs with Highfive and Bo. He went through a sequence of claustrophobic hallways and nondescript inner elevators, and then a hallway that felt like it led to a boiler room, but instead led through a heavy sealing door labeled ‘Chamber 10’ that opened into a big breakroom.

All of this felt a bit trippy due to the deja vu, and he would’ve sworn he was being misled and sent to Chamber 4 again. Apparently, their approaches were all separate, yet the same, or nearly so.

It has Memoria written all over it, damn it.

The majority of the team’s members were present already, most of them sitting on the extensive couch seating present that faced the vidscreen. That included their fearless leader, Solvent, sitting more or less in the center of it all on the couch, at ease with one leg crossed over his knee, giving off major dad vibes he was probably oblivious to.

“Welcome, Agent Ferro,” he said, gesturing at an open seat as if he’d just invited a kid over to his house, with dinner still being prepared in the kitchen. “Have a seat. Only a few more and we can start.”

Jack tried to shake off the weird vibes as he sat down next to Neex. He finally felt a bit of relief from his paranoia about the sameness of the location when he saw that the vending machines were different from Sim Chamber 4.

Thank the heavens for that.

Highfive was standing with his arms crossed as usual, though he was in a position where he faced Solvent. Bo — also as usual — was especially buried into the couch, ignoring everyone as she scrolled her phone.

How does she manage to practically dig a hole and nest inside every couch she sits in? No one else looks like that. It’s uncanny.

The final person to enter was Permafrost, who covered a yawn behind her hand and waved as she took a standing position near the couches, murmuring a quiet apology for the yawn.

“Alright,” Solvent declared, slapping his leg and looking around, “that looks like everyone, so we can start. Let’s all put away phones or other distractions, please.” He waited until this was done before continuing.

“Just to verify,” Lighthouse began with her hand raised briefly, and nodded over at Neex, “she is here for observation, right? She can’t participate?”

“That’s correct,” Neex replied as she smiled over at Lighthouse. “I am all but depowered so far from home, and only the bond allows me to comfortably survive by stealing a bit of my bondsmate’s Allotment. My team had preparations for the eventuality of not having a bond, but everything was blown up or stolen, and I had to expend my own reserves far more than intended. All just delay mechanisms at best, similar to packing food for a trip.”

“Your stuff was never recovered?”

“Not much that was worthwhile. Scraps at best. Some things have remained missing.”

“Damn, that sucks. Wait. Would that shit happen to us out there?” Lighthouse looked around. “In other territories?”

Jack noticed a lot of puzzled looks, but knowing ones on the faces of the veterans.

“Welcome to new realizations, newbies,” Crash commented with a devilish grin. “It won’t happen quite that way on neutral zones or frontiers, but going deeper into enemy environments would be a hell of a lot less fun than what we got going on here. Actually, ‘hell’ is a great word for them.”

“Very few have been there,” Solvent said, “but the known details aren’t at all promising. Enemy core territories would likely be difficult to exist in, and that’s saying nothing about the entities ready to go for your throat. PMs will defend inner zones. One of those would easily cream the lot of us all together, just as ours would.”

“Skyhumans are hardier, though,” Neex said insistently. “And this is your world more than any other, especially above the water. Inevitably, you will take it back.”

A brief silence answered this, and finally Crash barked a laugh and muttered, “Strangely comforting to hear from an alien.”

Neex turned her gaze his way. “Are you briefed on the species’ details, Agent Crash? We are more alike than different. We are a subspecies, and this world is indeed where we were born, too. It is our home, though we share it with you, the older stock of the land-adapted and proper descendants of the originals; unfortunately, we also share it mutually with effective invaders, many of whom make monstrous things of proper and healthy environments, flora, and fauna. They’re our enemies too, I assure you.”

Crash held up his hands in surrender. “Apologies. Short-hand. Didn’t mean anything by it, miss. If any of us sympathize with water-dwellers, it’s me, after all. I’m aware of the data.”

Neex nodded as she smiled. “Apology accepted, but it’s fine.” She glanced around. “Just wanted to make sure you all know the truth.”

Solvent finally interjected, “If anyone here doesn’t, they’ve been lax in their job. In all honesty, I think most ‘Skyhumans,’ as you call us, relish the idea of friendly aliens. It certainly pervades our culture and media. I wouldn’t call it derogatory. If you do take exception to it, let us know. In any case, we’d better move on, hmm? Neex is here to observe, absorb, and take notes. Trust that it will be beneficial down the road.”

He paused to take a sip of an energy drink before continuing. “First on our itinerary is me juicing people up. It’s best to start small and work our way up for this. The foundation and cornerstone is simply being receptive on a dime. So let’s start with that — go!” He slapped his hands together loudly, causing a few to jump. “Harness your powers!”

Out of nowhere that it was, Jack was always ready for powers. He summoned memorite, and an instant later, he felt that pulling from Solvent again. He gave in to it without much effort, same as the last, and that sense of foreign tendrils mingling with his own came, but this time, they didn’t interlace and reinforce.

This time, ‘pulling’ was an especially accurate conceptual term, as he felt a portion of his Allotment network taken and stretched away from him. It took much more effort not to intrinsically resist this and let it happen, as instinctually he wanted to defend himself.

But he knew he could do that absolutely as soon as he tried. This was for the sake of helping others.

Reinforcing that in his head, Jack let it go and felt what amounted to his ‘rebranching’ extending out, soon becoming effectively imperceptible to him, as the complexity of the construct expanded far beyond his understanding. He knew Solvent was changing his Allotment once it was conceptually further from him and closer to the new controller. He was playing with it in a more plastic form, perhaps…

More fluid. Dissolving it, essentially, to repurpose it.

When Jack checked with Mini, the reduction was nine percent off his total.

Taking a little from us lower-levels would add up to something at least somewhat relevant. I’d guess the percentage ramps with each level closer to his. Still, adding only lowbies to the big boys would be underwhelming. That’s why they have just enough vets to boost other vets when needed.

Suddenly, the room seemed to change temperatures to overly warm and sunny — even to Jack — and all eyes were drawn powerfully to Délight, who was glowing and looking especially beautiful for some reason.

“Holy shit,” Délight breathed, and her voice was like music from an angel.

Jack was tangentially aware of his resistance via Q-Loth triggering, but was having a much harder time of it now, and some of the effects were bleeding through into him from the Allotment-pumped effects. Nothing capable of overriding, but it was impossible to ignore her presence.

“Holy shit,” Jack muttered.

Meanwhile, the majority of the others were struggling with their new love affair. Highfive was practically drooling, no doubt something in his mind ‘rekindling’ some ancient desire for her. That was pure evil, but not unexpected when they had been a couple at one time.

“I… hate…. Charmers…” Permafrost said, wide-eyed, staring at Délight like a moth considering a flame. “Or… do I? Ugh!”

Even Solvent himself was watching her with special fascination, though it was more clinical than anything. “These powers… they’re incredible…”

Yeesh. Way to dope yourself, dude. What a horrible plan. Well, at least he didn’t truly perv out on her. Maybe he really is a dad.

“Stop… this… shit,” Bo said with her teeth clenched, her face twitching as she helplessly stared. “Or I… will… k-... ki…” Bo shook with the effort to get the words out. “Kiiiiii!”

Damn it, Superdad, you misjudged things. This is only going to create more friction where we didn’t need it.

Perhaps the only one unaffected entirely was the quiet man, Spiral, who had his chin propped with a hand and was simply staring at nothing, as if bored.

Délight smiled winningly at Bo — a smile that lit up the world. Cutely, she cocked her head and mused, “I’d say Memoria would want me to test your resistances to this. The equivalent of enemy Charmers do exist, after all. Don’t you all agree with me?”

A few muttered a ‘yes’ or nodded. Jack just winced, still in control of himself.

Solvent nodded, but his face subsequently twitched, and he blinked a few times, finally shaking himself as he said, “Why don’t we stress test you as well, then?”

Suddenly, the power shifted, and a relative black hole made for a silent contrast to Délight’s energy, mitigating and fighting it.

The distraction pulled eyes, and Jack saw the face of Bo light up, in a truly frightening grin that would send a razorena alpha running with its tail between its legs.

Délight’s eyes went wide. “Oh no…”

“Oh yes, bitch!” Bo cried, and the warm light flickered once, then vanished completely. The air vibrated with discord. A bulb somewhere shattered.

Jack felt the air pull slightly from his lungs, and he wasn’t the only one from the gasps around the room.

Délight once again looked exceptionally normal, unimpressive, and rather mortified as her eyes slid around to people now scowling her way.

The effect on Bo soon diminished, though she was still scowling heartily at the previously ‘enthused’ Charmer in her sights.

“Way to get into the role, Dee,” Permafrost muttered balefully, shaking her head as she wiped a bit of sweat from her brow.

Lighthouse also seemed a bit shaken, staring at the table and swallowing hard. Her face was very red. “That was on Solvent.” Her eyes flashed briefly to Bo, who scoffed and rolled her eyes, muttering something ostensibly unintelligible.

Jack was pretty sure he heard, ‘still working’ in there.

I suppose sexual attraction might’ve biologically counterbalanced Lighthouse’s other hangups with Délight here. Not sure. Highfive looks mightily affected, certainly.

Solvent cleared his throat loudly and held up his hand. His smile was apologetic. “That was indeed on me, though I, ah… didn’t expect such a quick and spirited embracing of the power. This is a temptation to be aware of, as it does affect some people. Especially those with less physically obvious, yet still offensively-oriented powers, such as psychics. And, ah, well, some other things play a part. We’d get into psychological stuff to explain any further, however.”

“I’m sure,” Bo said, shaking her head and looking away.

Yep. We’re off to such a great start.
