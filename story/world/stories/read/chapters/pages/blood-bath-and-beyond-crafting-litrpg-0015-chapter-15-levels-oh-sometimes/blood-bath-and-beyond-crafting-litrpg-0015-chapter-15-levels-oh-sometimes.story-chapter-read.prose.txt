[You have acquired the skill: Mana Manipulation lvl 1!

Most magic users eventually learn to perceive the mana coursing through their bodies during spell casting. Some learn to direct these energies outside of themselves, reshaping the world around them. You are now among their number.

Congratulations! Now try not to explode yourself!

As a Witch you have the capability to trap and store mana in objects of your creation, infusing them with your power for yourself or others to use in the future. Mana infusion is a precise and complex art with varied applications.

You have been warned! Warding circles are highly advised.]

“Violent results, thanks for the warning.”

[You are most welcome.]

I wiped some of the soap splatter off my cheek. “How am I supposed to know what objects are unsuitable or incompatible?”

Silence.

The gargoyles watched with motionless interest.

I sighed and wiped them clean as well. I asked the system: “Can you at least tell me what a [Warding Circle] is?”

[Warding is a spell. A circle is a circle, usually round in nature.]

I groaned. “Anything else you can tell me? Or would like to tell me?”

[No.]

“I miss you too.”

[Ha.]

“Do you guys know how to make a [Warding] spell?” I asked the gargoyles. Two stone shakes of two stone heads.

“That’s okay, I’ll figure it out. Experiment, I guess. Okay. Great, something else to learn. Right. [Warding]. Let’s see what do I know about it? What do [Witches] make [Warding Circles] with?” My mind drifted back to various tv shows and questionable TikTok’s. “Salt? I don’t have any salt. Ashes? Smoke? I know people burn sage, that’s supposed to be protective or something. I might be able to find some in the forest, but I don’t remember seeing any sage… Hmm…[Warding]…[Warding], where have I seen …[Warding…Oh yes!”

I rushed down to my little supply room and [Inspected] my herbs for the hundredth time. My face split into a grin. Mugwort had [Warding] listed as a property. So did Devil’s Flight. Charcoal, and eggshells also carried the attribute.

After some consideration, I decided I start by burning herbs. ‘Smudging’, that was what it was called, I remembered that. I grabbed some mugwort and stampeded back to my main room. Setting the green fronds alight, I twirled in a circle, cackling like a bona fide cartoon witch. The smoke lingered in the air, forming a circle, of sorts.

[You have acquired the Skill: Warding lvl 1!

This skill makes a protective barrier shielding its creator from unwanted spirits and energy. It sanctifies the space enclosed within. Alternatively, it can be used as a containment field. A ward is a work of art, deeply personal to its creator. Many styles, compositions and components are possible. Many materials can be used. I would not want to stifle your creativity with too much information. Plus, it’s entertaining to watch you flounder.]

“Thanks,” I muttered, sitting quickly in the centre, cross-legged with a fresh ball of soap. The smell of the smoke was not unpleasant. Here goes…

I cast the spell, mentally, neatly sacrificing the mana like I did for [Umbral Cloak] or [Curse.]

[Mugwort Smoke Ward Established. Lvl 1. Quality: Unstable. Power level: weak.]

Always good to leave room for improvement!

Inhaling deeply, I settled into my meditation.

I found my mana faster this time: a small shiny shimmer in my dimly sensed centre. More of a shadow than anything concrete. This time, I took more care coaxing it out, breathing deeply, unspooling it, encouraging it towards the soap with my thoughts. I could feel it leaving my body by the familiar tug, even as I half sensed it with my eyes and fingers. It smelt a little like ozone. Sharp. Kind of metallic? Like the scent of a summer storm, all lightning and petrichor.

Keep going… keep going. I concentrated doggedly but I was acutely aware that something felt different this time. Did that mean it was working? I squished down the excitement to keep my focus. Just a little… more… Something ripped from me, flying free into the night before I could process what it was.

The fire in the hearth guttered, blowing out with a whoosh. The smoke scattered.

[Ward Failure.]

Wind roared in my ears. My hair whipped around my face.

A spiral of flame danced across my vision, entwined with streaks of shimmering, effervescent blue. What was happening? I tried to control the spill of mana, tried to stop but instead of stopping, it rushed wildly, the trickle turned to a roar, shimmering threads dashing themselves in the wind.

I screamed into the sky, clutching the gaping hole that was my stomach.

No, no, my stomach was there, all fine, flesh and blood. It just felt like a gaping hole. I patted myself down then shrieked as elongated faces loomed out at me from the darkness. I shrank from them, hand scrabbling for my dagger. Where was it? Shadows skittered at the edge of my vision. Eyes, mouths, wind, whipping and howling, a vortex of energy and shadow and light, and then—

Nothing.

No faces, no creepy wind. Nothing but a blinding headache and pain. I fell forwards, panting, my breath coming in loud, heaving gasps. “What in the-Ugggggggg—”

[Warning. Mana deprivation alert. You out of mana. All bodily and spiritual functions operating under abject duress. Prolonged mana deprivation can cause permanent damage. Warning.]

I somehow mustered the strength to check: 0/35.

What.

The.

Fuck.

“Where did it go?”

The ball of soap lay in front of me, innocently. Through the agony I [Inspected] it:

[Soap, cleansing. Quality: very poor.]

The mana had gone everywhere but into the soap, apparently. Okay, I clearly needed a better [Ward.]

Owwwwwwww.

I lay on my back and groaned. What did I do to deserve this? Why did everything hurt? My veins were on fire. Like someone had poured liquid lead into them and lit them with a match.

Somewhere overhead I could see the gargoyles looking at me in concern.

“Too much,” said Wistful.

“Too fast,” said Eggs.

Their voices hurt my brain.

“Uggggg,” I mumbled.

I rolled onto my side and stayed there for a while, done with the universe.

After an hour or so (probably after I had acquired a tick of mana) my brain decided to boot back up again.

Okay, so fuck burning mugwort then. Burning mugwort did not an adequate [Warding Circle] make. Stupid, idiot herb for mugs. It’s right there in the name. Dimly, I was aware of the gargoyles tending to the fire (well they were throwing sticks at it from the rooftop), but I was appreciative, and warm, if not comfortable. Tomorrow, if I was still alive, I would try something else.

After a while I fell asleep.

***

The next morning I woke with all the cheer and energy of someone who had recently been run over by a freight train. I groaned and stretched and cursed (the kind that did not require mana). Since I was feeling particularly unmotivated, I examined my body, grumbling over the scars and bruises that had still not yet faded. I felt heavy, my brain foggy. My legs were still a particularly colourful shade of purple and unappealing yellow.

After a wash I felt a bit better and gave myself a pep talk.

I couldn’t leave this. I was close, I knew. Close to having magical soap. And after all I had acquired two new skills, even if so, far they had done nothing useful. My mana was halfway restored after the night’s sleep, and I knew to make sure I didn’t run out. Progress, yes, I was making progress.

“The obvious answer is that the [Ward Circle] needs to be physical,” I said, talking out loud, as had become my habit, to the gargoyles. They seldom replied, but I knew they were listening, which was nice. “No smoke this time. Okay, let’s try charcoal!”

Getting it perfectly circular took a few goes but the [Ward] of charcoal dust was only slightly more successful than the smoke. It was more stable but did not last long. Not to be put defeated, in between mad unicorn visits, I popped out to the forest and gathered more mugwort and devil’s flight. Next, instead of smoking it, I laid the herbs out on top of the charcoal line, making sure all the bits connected. By the time I was done, the moons were rising again, casting silvery light over everything which made me feel happily spooky.

I stood in the centre and looked at it. It looked nice. Decidedly witchy. But something was twitching at my memory - from the tower, from the night I had arrived.

“The Enchantress,” I said. “Was there... was it… a [Warding Circle]?” I remembered the pentagram drawn on the floor with the… the body parts strewn about it. It had been enclosed in a circle. Now I thought about it, I realised I could remember the details in shocking clarity. “What is the point of the pentagram?” I asked the gargoyles. “What does it do?”

“Lines confentrae mana,” said Eggs, popping his head over the tiles. “Maybe. Not hoff gargoyles cultifffate.”

“Okay, I can draw a pentagram, that makes sense. Kinda. Um. Did the Enchantress use… blood or what to mark it?”

“Not her own.”

“Okay,” I said. “Okay, well herbs will have to do. My blood needs to stay inside me, and I’m not going to bleed some poor creature all over the floor every time I want to make soap.”

Eggs and Wistful watched with interest as I set to work with fresh vigour. The pentagram took a few goes to get right, but eventually I was seated inside a rather nifty, and decidedly occult looking [Warding Circle] of herbs and charcoal and ground eggshell. All the lines converged on me.

“Okay, let’s go!”

[Mugwort Charcoal Eggshell Ward Established. Lvl 1. Quality: Stable. Power level: weak.]

There was the faintest shimmer in the air, enclosing me like a dome.

“Yussssss!” I said, saluting the gargoyles with a fresh ball of soap. “This one is stable!”

Half an hour later I was lying on my side groaning once more with my mana, once again at zero.

[Ward has reached level 2!]

[Mana Manipulation has reached level 2!]

“Weeeeee!” I said, weakly, from the ground.

My frustration was approaching atomic levels, and I needed to rest my body. The mana-deprivation ache was no joke. I switched gears, to work on my meditation some more, and was later rewarded with another notification:

[Congratulations! Mana perception has reached level 4! Yay, it’s not just mist in the wind. Now it’s slightly easier to spot mist in the wind! Keep going kiddo.]

I hoped the additional level would make [Mana Manipulation] easier, but again, it didn’t. I experimented more with mana infusing – sending mana into various objects with scant regard for my health or mental wellbeing. Inside the weak [Wards] I exploded the following in short order: various herbs, a dandelion, some berries, a mushroom, a rock, a bit of my own hair, two more soaps, a beetle, a stick and a piece of left over crackling.

All thoughts of weaponizing the exploding mana objects were also useless; the destruction was a wet splat, or occasionally a gentle whoomph. If I wanted to dust someone with bits of gravel or splodges of beetle perhaps it would be useful. And anyone I was fighting would have to sit and wait patiently while I meditated for an hour or so first.

“I hate this,” I said to the soap, which had rolled in front of my nose. “I hate you specifically. I hate this whole ridiculous world.”

“More patience? More levels?” said Wistful, from somewhere above me.

“Stop being so goddamned reasonable.”
