Just as he’d promised, Vin spent the next hour after Tabetha finished reading the basics about Edregon answering her many questions and explaining how the new world worked.

“I still don’t understand,” she said, holding her head in her hands as she tried to piece it all together. “The Beacon is large enough that I should be able to see farther than six miles out in any direction. Why couldn’t I see the neighboring fragments?”

“Magic,” Vin shrugged, going over it one more time. “The fragment borders don’t seem to come into view until you get somewhat close to them. Before that, you just see some sort of fake imagery of the world beyond your fragment border, but it’s not real. No idea why the Gods ended up setting Edregon up like that, but it’s how it works.”

“So… there aren’t any more divigel… at all?” Tabetha asked, her voice growing quiet as she came to terms with her situation. “I’m the last of my people?”

“Not necessarily,” Shia said, doing her best to keep her from spiraling into despair. “We’ve encountered plenty of other fragments with races from differing worlds. Hell, I think we’re up to at least four or five different fragments that have elves on them, and probably even more of that for humans. Just because we haven’t encountered any other divigel yet doesn’t mean there aren’t more out there. The old universe was a big place, meaning Edregon is quite large.”

“But they won’t be from my world,” Tabetha stated, her face going blank as she took that in. “I’m the last survivor of my entire world… I don’t even know what to think.”

“I’d say start with deciding what you want to do moving forward,” Vin said, rolling his eyes in response to Shia's slight frown. “You’ve really only got two options. You can stay here within the Beacon, living off the food that was prepared for ten thousand of your people until you die of old age. Or, you can leave the Beacon behind you and find a new home, with new people. Terra would always welcome you, but I could give you a few other suggestions as well. I bet the avisul would be happy to have you. They’re a bunch of fellow fliers who live on giant clouds without any land beneath them at all.”

“They live on clouds?” Tabetha asked, that one fact seeming enough to startle her out of her funk as she perked up. “Edregon sounds like a wild place.”

“You have no idea,” Shia chuckled. “Vin, why don’t you go check on Scule and make sure the pipsqueak hasn’t gotten himself into any trouble. I want to have some alone time to talk about stuff with Tabetha. Reginald can stay.”

At this point, the rat was curled up and napping in Tabetha’s lap and didn’t even react to hearing his name. Shaking his head at Reginald’s lethargy, Vin got up and left the nook, only then realizing he didn’t have the faintest idea where Scule had gone off to. Despite all but forcing them through an impromptu tour of the top of the Beacon, the lighthouse was massive, and they hadn’t even touched on a good eighty percent of it.

“If I were a Rogue with itchy fingers, where would I be,” Vin muttered, wandering down the stairs as he started peeking into different rooms.

Most of the rooms were similar to what they’d seen up above. While she seemed to be holding herself together decently enough for the moment, Tabetha had clearly gone a little stir crazy being alone in the Beacon for so long. Furniture was upended and the majority of the rooms he walked past were a mess, which was sort of ironic for her being a Cleaner. Eventually, he resorted to casting Sense Soul, pinpointing Scule’s location and jogging down a few levels to get to him.

“Find anything good?” Vin asked, causing Scule to jump when the Rogue was caught red-handed attempting to crack open what looked like a safe. A quick glance up revealed a sign above the door that said ‘Beacon Keeper’, and Vin could only stare at his companion in amazement. “In a giant building that is almost entirely abandoned, did you seriously pick the one room that still specifically belongs to someone to rob? Don’t expect me to help if Shia catches wind of this.”

“Hey, rule number one of looking for valuables. The more important a person is, the better stuff they have,” Scule said, turning back to the safe as he pressed an ear against it. “Besides, I can tell easily enough that this thing hasn’t been opened in ages. You heard what Tabetha said, the old Beacon Keeper used his dying breath to force his position and responsibilities onto that poor woman. Sounds like he didn’t spare a second breath to give her the combination to his safe. Look.”

Vin realized soon enough what Scule meant, as there were a handful of bent or broken tools lying all around the scratched-up safe. Snapped crowbars, twisted bars of metal, and even a handful of bent dinner knives had been tossed to the side, none of them strong enough to allow Tabetha to access the sealed safe.

“I see what you mean,” he muttered, moving closer and crouching down to watch Scule work. “And I suppose you were planning on giving whatever is inside to Tabetha once you got it out?”

“Of course I am, I’m not that heartless!” Scule scoffed, looking at Vin in disdain. “…After taking an appropriate finder’s fee, as is only natural.”

“As is natural,” Vin nodded, rolling his eyes before nudging Scule out of the way and laying a hand on top of the safe. “Deconstructive Touch.”

While it was sturdy, the old Keeper’s safe wasn’t magically reinforced, which meant the metal gradually eroded away underneath his touch. It took a few minutes, but soon enough, Vin had a hole he could fit his hand through.

“Well how about that,” he muttered, pulling out a shining key that had a bright white gemstone embedded in the side. Vin didn’t even need Beyond the Veil, his magic sense was enough to tell that the magic radiating from the key felt similar to the magic lining the Beacon’s walls. “I’ve got a good feeling I know what this key goes to.”

“An even bigger safe?” Scule asked hopefully.

“You know there’s probably a storage room somewhere filled to the brim with gold, or whatever these people used as currency,” Vin said, causing Scule’s eyes to widen at the thought. “Maybe go start there instead of Tabetha’s office?”

“Look at you, we’ll make a Rogue out of you yet!” Scule grinned, turning and running out of the room as he hunted for the real treasure. Vin decided he didn’t need to point out how most people desperately fleeing from monsters with the intention of living out the rest of their lives in a bunker probably didn’t see much need to bring any money with them. As soon as Scule was gone, he turned back to the safe, fishing out the rest of its contents.

Along with a few official-looking documents filled with numbers that he didn’t have too much interest in, he found something far more interesting.

New artifact discovered!

There we go, Vin thought, smiling to himself as he looked at the gemstone-studded belt in hand. The moment he found Scule within the old Keeper’s office, he wondered if he’d find something like this lying around. According to Tabetha, the previous Beacon Keeper had lost one of his wings during his time fighting against the increasing monster horde. But if he’d really been as important as she’d said…

He had to have come up with an alternative method of flight one way or another.

Sitting in the Beacon Keeper’s chair, Vin held the belt in his hands as he closed his eyes and slipped into Introspection. He was so excited about what he’d found that he almost screamed when he found himself face-to-face with Ovord within his dark void.

“God!” Vin shouted, discovering he could still feel his heart hammering within his chest even while inside Introspection. “You’re so much quieter than Alka ever was I forget you’re still here half the time!”

“Would you prefer if I spoke up more frequently?” Ovord asked, tilting his head slightly in question. “I was trying not to annoy you.”

“You’re fine, I don’t want to make you do anything one way or another,” Vin said, taking a few deep breaths to steady himself. “I just popped in to take a look at this belt. I think I know what it does, and if I’m right, I’m pretty excited to pull the runic formation from it.”

“By all means, don’t let me stop you,” Ovord said, gesturing for Vin to continue.

Nodding, Vin turned and reached out with his magic sense, touching upon the belt and bringing the artifact’s space adjacent to his own mana pool. As the runic formation making it up came into existence, he groaned as he realized it wasn’t going to be so easy.

“Damn it, that’s a tier-4 spell,” he sighed, taking in the mess of runes as he tried to differentiate between the different root spells. “I should still be able to learn from it and probably make a lesser version of the flying spell he had, but I can’t replicate this. It would take me a solid few weeks of effort. I suppose I should be happy to have found this at all, it’s kind of strange that a race of people capable of flight even have this magic in the first place.”

“Is it? Did your own people not have magic designed to help you move about better due to failings from old age?” Ovord pointed out, peering curiously at the runic formation. “My world certainly did. Also, is a few weeks of effort truly not worth gaining the ability to fly? I was not going to say anything, but if I am speaking genuinely, that feels like a rather ridiculous take. Pardon my honesty.”

“The problem is what I could be doing with those same weeks,” Vin argued. “There are always more fragments to explore or other, simpler spells I could be learning. And there’s no guarantee I’d even succeed in learning the spell. Not to mention I’m currently almost three weeks behind schedule because someone tried to steal my body for himself.”

“I attempted to offer my literal soul to you as an apology,” Ovord shrugged, turning from the runic formation to look at him. “Most people on my world would have isolated themselves for an entire year in order to gain such power over the Arch Mage of the Soul. I would argue a few weeks is quite the bargain.”

“Again, thanks but no thanks,” Vin said, letting go of the mental hold he had on the belt and letting the runic formation vanish. At the very least, assuming Tabetha didn’t want it for whatever reason, he could wear it and use the belt’s power for himself. He just preferred not relying on artifacts whenever possible. “And sorry about the delay on getting you your own body. Like I said, I’m running a little behind schedule.”

“I spent who knows how many centuries alone within that orb,” Ovord said. “I don’t mind waiting a bit longer. Besides, it is quite interesting seeing the different worlds through your eyes. I am curious if the divigel is going to return with you four back to Terra.”

“Glad you’re enjoying the show,” Vin snorted. “Alright, I need to head back up. I have a feeling we might be here for a few days while Tabetha figures out what she wants to do, and Shia will shove me off the Beacon if I suggest we leave and come back for her.”

“Yes, she does seem like that is something she would do,” Ovord agreed. “She is quite ruthless and forceful when she wishes to get her way. I admit, I approve of her methods. You could learn a thing or two from her.”

“Please, Golrim is bad enough,” Vin muttered. Shaking himself out of Introspection, he got to his feet and left the Keeper’s office. “The last thing I need is another dark and troublesome advisor whispering in my ears.”
