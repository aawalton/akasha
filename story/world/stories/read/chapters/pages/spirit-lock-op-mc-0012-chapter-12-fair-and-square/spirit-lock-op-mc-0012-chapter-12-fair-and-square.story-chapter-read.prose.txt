Goren tried to activate his spirit, and it whirled to life. Then, the little mouse’s body lit up, and Hurricane Fist snuffed out. The mouse dimmed, and then the wind returned. Hayden’s lesson was over. He closed the distance in two bounds and hit Goren in the stomach with a fist wrapped in raw cora. The impact was so vicious that Goren coughed up a web of phlegm as he stumbled back twice and fell to his knees.

Students and teachers looked at each other in shock. Hayden heard one student say, “Wait, did he just win?” His neighbor chuckled nervously and said, “I think so?” It was only once Goren fell face-first into the stage, and the referee yelled, “Victor, Hayden Reskco!” that it truly sank in.

The silent students erupted—hooping and hollering and screeching like a pack of wild animals. Some were hugging each other or high-fiving. One person said, “I knew it!” only to be shot down by a man yelling, “You bet money on Goren, you tool!” The area detonated with roiling laughter.

It was a celebration. Hayden had triumphed over evil, and the tournament rigging villain—Rosen—found him in a reversed position. Everyone wanted to see the slimy worm kick rocks, so every attack on him was cheered on. The most savage was a group of women who walked up to Rosen, bracelets lit red, and asked, “How does it feel to know you’ll have to face Hayden like a man?”

Rosen’s face was white as twice-bleached rice, and his hung lips told the rest.

“Bye bye, little boy,” another said, flicking her hair and cat-walking away as the spectators boomed with approval.

It was a raucous celebration—but it was far from conclusive. One man reversed the mood by innocently saying, “You think he can beat Misen? The dude’s beast at martial arts.” Then, the whole student body fell into a harsh spell of debating.

“Yeah, that is a good point. Hayden just kinda blindsided Goren.”

“Yeah, and it didn't help that the guy's a fricken moron.”

“Right? He’s just big. But Misen’s like… trained. Hayden’s just like…”

“A deep forest kung fu master?”

Groups started laughing and debating, splitting the odds fifty-fifty. Suddenly, everyone was yearning to see the match between Hayden and Misen.

Loricks wasn’t thrilled about the next match. He was dreading it. After all, what he just witnessed was terrifying.

That punch should’ve killed him… he thought. What the hell just happened?

Hayden had wrapped his fist in the cleanest aura Loricks had ever seen. In fact, Loricks had graduated from a top A-rank university, and he hadn’t even seen a professor use aura like that. Despite that, the amount of aura Hayden used should have cracked Goren’s spine from the hydrostatic shock alone. No, it should have blown his organs out of his back. Yet it ended up being the exact amount of force he needed to win. Hayden didn't just have absolute control of his aura—he had control over his body, too.

That didn’t make sense. Hayden didn’t get a scholarship to Junolyx for his fighting. He was an academic being paid to boost the school’s ranking. Therefore, his “talent” didn’t even extend to fighting—

And yet this fighting was beyond A-rank performance.

That confirmed something that others had been saying, but he always denied: Hayden wasn't the same Hayden he had known a couple of weeks ago.

Possessed by a demon? Loricks doubted that. Demons didn’t treat students with respect and limit their punches, so far as the legends would make him believe.

But manipulated by a spirit? Absolutely.

And not just any spirit—a powerful one. It gave him chills just thinking about it.

Kayline had chills, too, but she didn’t care who Hayden was, or about silly things like possessions or student deaths. She was too busy celebrating, screaming, Oh, my God, he won! He won, he won, he won! in her head.

Hayden was, without a doubt, the coolest person she had ever met—and she wanted to get to know him. That's why she felt despair over the rule changes and was recording the matches to ensure Hayden got into Junolyx somehow. But that wasn't even necessary! Hayden wasn't just holding back on his training—he was holding back on his genius. His spirit link was flawless, and his freezing skills were professional grade. Plus, he turned his “useless spirit” into a frightening freak of nature.

Yet every night, after telling herself that she was under some cultish delusion about him, she still felt like he was the real deal. Not only did Hayden annihilate a seven-foot meathead against all odds, but he did it in the manliest way possible. He gave the guy a warning and told ‘em to man up!

Good trauma comes on slow—Hayden couldn't rush it. He’d do it right, and he’d do it in a way that would send a message to Rosen's father. In the meantime, he needed to press on.

He felt bad enough that he had to knock Hayden out—but the least he could do was go easy on Hayden to make him look better. That way, they would both prevail against Rosen together.
