Levi caught the stone with ease, his vision focused on the single artifact that had brought him here. The artifact that had somehow managed to put him between two powerful synced.

A slight throbbing had begun to blossom at the back of his head, and he didn't know if it was because of the constant pounding of Ghosts' daggers on the dome, or because of how fast his heart was beating. Either way, the situation was more than clear to him. If he didn't absorb the stone, the mage would kill him.

Then again, if I do absorb the stone. Ghost will most certainly pursue me to the ends of Rinesta for it.

Neither situation actually left him free per se, but the mage's deal would... Should give him a chance. A lifeline or headstart, even if it was just a false one, was a risk he'd have to take. Especially since he had both Bree and Silas depending on him, their futures were tied to him, and even if he only had enough time to give them his hidden stash in the apartment so that they could stay off the streets, then he'd have to take this chance.

"Five seconds lef-t" The mage coughed, bloody spittle flying about as the clearly strained mage dropped to a knee. Blood seeping out of his eyes, nose, and mouth.

Strained as he was, Levi knew that the mage would still be able to kill him easily. The mage would also likely fall to those Ghosts' daggers once the dome came crashing down. Which meant that the mage shouldn't have a reason to keep him here once he absorbed the stone, and frankly, it didn't much matter to Levi, his odds of surviving this mess were extremely low, which meant he would have to risk trusting the mage. So as he stared down at the stone, he took the punt.

"Ahh," Levi screamed. The skin on his forehead seemed like it was on fire, so he reached for it with his now-empty hands, the stone having disappeared into … him.

The pain stopped abruptly as soon as his fingers made contact with the area. The skin felt whole and unmarred even. It felt soft, like that of a newborn. He glanced at the mage who was looking up at him, a bloody grin plastered on his face.

"What was that?" He asked, trying to sound unmoved, but his voice cracked, betraying his true emotions.

"That was…, The brand, well, a part of it," the mage grinned, his blood-stained teeth unnerving. "You'll get one of those every day until you complete the absorption process."

Three days, I get one three times. Or two, considering I've already gotten the first of it.

"What is it ?" he asked. "The brand?"

"That is the mark of the obsidian rogue." The mage answered through a mouthful of blood. "Listen closely, we don't have enough time for this back and forth. I need you to leave town today, not at dawn and definitely not by noon. You get that stone as far away as possible from Ghost. If you survive long enough, seek out the Black Baron in Plantrith. You'll find it to the west of here."

"Okay … but why do you so badly not want Ghost to take the stone?" Levi asked.

"Apart from the fact that he killed my entire party," The mage spat. "Ghost is bad news, horrible news. You might never have heard of him in this backward town, but I promise you he's a thousand times worse than whatever monster you fear the most, and if he wants the stone for himself, Rinesta is in trouble. Even if he was hired to steal the stone, I wouldn't trust a council seat to anyone who would hire him."

"What's to stop me from betraying you?"

"Unless you have a death wish, I don't think you will. By now, you should know what it would cost you for him to take the stone from you."

My life.

"True, what's this about a council se—"

"Listen, boy, we do not have the time for this. My mana is almost spent, and this dome will not hold for much longer," The mage wheezed. "If you truly want to live, and I think you do, then you'd stop with all these questions. If you somehow manage to survive long enough, seek the Black Baron in Plantrith and all the answers you seek will be given to you. For now, live."

Levi didn't even have time to protest before he was yanked away, the oppressive atmosphere of the dome and the constant pounding of Ghosts' daggers fading away as he was sucked into nothingness and thrust out onto the roof of the Sapphire Inn. His head spinning from whatever spell had gotten him there.

"Grak this," Levi cursed.

He was ready to jump off the roof, but before that, there was something he very quickly had to check. Creeping around the roof, he went to where he'd seen the body of the unconscious guard when he'd initially gotten up to the roof with Ghost. The body was still at the same place he'd last seen it. Unmoving as before, but this time he wanted to confirm if the body was actually just unconscious, so he crouched beside it. Bylin's ring up in the sky barely illuminated the roof this late in the night, so he had to squint at the body. Using his dagger, he poked at the body, with no reaction. The body was face down, so he had to push it to the side, a sticky congealed liquid sticking to his fingers as he made contact with the neck. The body was cold to the touch and dead to boot.

What have I gotten myself into?

He had no idea what exactly it was, but he did know that he had a small window of opportunity to get himself out of it, and Levi desperately clung to the chance that seemed to be fleeing from him. He had to survive tonight by any means necessary.

He took off like a coiled spring, sprinting and then jumping off the edge of the roof in one motion. His knees bent as he dropped to the floor, the newly Synced Rogue already rolling as soon as his feet touched cobblestone, lessening the impact of his fall as best as he could.

The adrenaline pumping through his veins made him discard the pain that shot through his legs as he got up from the ground. Pain like that was the least of his worries, so he pushed that to the back of his mind as he took off, sprinting at full speed through shrubbery and grass as he exited the same way he'd come in. His destination was his rundown apartment.

I have to see them one more time. If I'm going to die, I have to see them.

He kept repeating the words in his head as he ran, his breath coming in ragged, his thoughts a jumbled mess. He couldn't stop until he was as far away from that demon as possible. So he ran as fast as he could, using every backstreet, alleyway, and shortcut he knew of to get back into the slums of Gantus.

Their little town had very few guards, if any, so Levi went through town uninterrupted. The nightlife of Gantus centered on the Junga Tavern, and he was far from there; the majority of the residents of Gantus were indoors by this hour. Which meant the streets were deserted for the most part.

A few eyes did cross his in the darkness as he exited the area of the rich into the slums, but Levi couldn't care less. Besides, no one who'd spotted him was on his heels, which meant he wasn't considered a person of interest. His footsteps were the only thing to echo in the empty streets.

The smell of piss was his first indicator that he was close to home. Even the low light of Bylin in the sky was enough to illuminate the rubbish that was tossed around everywhere on the streets in the slums. The place was beyond dirty, but it was what he could afford on a thief's earnings without drawing too much attention to himself. The random thoughts turned into dust as soon as he spotted his apartment. He ran as though the whole of hell was on his heels, and with what he'd witnessed today, that might've just been the case.

"Silas!" Levi yelled as he barged through the door of their apartment. The rogue was bending down, hands on his knees as he struggled to catch his breath. The door to the room creaked open, but it wasn't Silas who came out; it was Bree.

"Brother Lev-i," She said, tired, her right hand going to rub her eyes.

"Bree, what are you doing up this late? You know better than to be up by this time."

"Sorry," Bree mumbled.

"Don't worry about it, kiddo, just go back to bed."

"Okay, Good night," she waved.

"Yeah, Good night." He said with a soft smile and a wave of his own. He watched as his little sister walked back into her room.

There were two bedrooms in the apartment; he slept in the common room, and Silas had the other. As soon as she shut her door, another opened; it was Silas's, and he was less than happy about having his sleep interrupted. His brother's groggy irritation morphed into confusion once he looked closely at Levi, and then his facial features morphed into one of concern.

It should be enough to get you and Bree both out of here and still buy food and pay rent in a new place for, say, six months. I'm sorry I can't afford either of your syncing fees, but you have to promise me that Bree will never have to sign a contract to get synced.”

The unfinished mark, my hood must've fallen from all the running.

He needed to get out of Gantus as soon as possible.
