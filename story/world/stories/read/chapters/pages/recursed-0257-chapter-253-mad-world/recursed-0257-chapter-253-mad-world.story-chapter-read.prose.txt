Nyxalraith swallowed herself and was gone before she could even fully process what it would mean for the Scriptures to be messing with Madness. She didn’t have time.

Tearing open a Null Scar would not have gone unnoticed. It was their own ritual, sure, but for an operation this dangerous, security would be tight. They would not ignore it, or pass it off as one of their own returning.

This was the Scriptures. Records would be kept, and unless they were expecting someone’s arrival from that heavily obscured entrance, a heavy force would already be ready to intercept.

And for something as potentially catastrophic as experimenting with Madness, Nyxalraith knew there would be tenth creeds. Possibly even the Human Tomes themselves.

Quiet was no longer an option. The circumstances had changed, and now Nyxalraith needed to adapt.

She reappeared on the surface. The massive obelisk rose above her, almost leaning off the edge of Coral. She didn’t know if the tower itself held anything of substance, but what was important was that it denoted the main entrance to the Library and the many majestic layers it held.

There weren’t many around — the surface was still unlivable from the damage of the Deific Tempest — but the few dozen that were all immediately turned to her. Nyxalraith’s swirling storm of darkness was impossible to overlook. Especially given the near Dark Star level of corruption she was unleashing into her surroundings.

Her shadows immediately crawled down the large ceremonial steps, swarming cultists and plucking their souls.

There wasn’t time for panic to ignite before an ocean of blood flowed from her veins. The first screams came as her corruption seeped into Yog and amalgamations began their horrific birthing in earnest. A thousand of the smaller variants rushed forth. As they touched the hallowed halls of the Scriptures’ heart of knowledge, each and every one was obliterated.

Above, the obelisk was ablaze with a sophisticated weave of pentagrams. They didn’t dim having wiped the first wave of amalgamations, instead glowing fiercer as if readying for more.

Nyxalraith watched the entities of inconsistent form continue to throw themselves down into the incineration field, glad to let them take the brunt of the defence’s efforts.

Meanwhile, Yog finished carving his own ritual through the air above the bloody manifestation pools below. Nyxalraith stepped forward to trigger the ritual. A short hymn. That was all it took for a thunderous boom of laughter to echo down into the descending steps of the Library, warning everyone below.

“Throwing your dog a bone so soon?” Euphoria’s grins ripped into existence like lightning, swathed in dark clouds. “If I knew we would be having this much fun, I would have leashed myself willingly.”

With something as powerful as a god under her thumb, she was going to use him. Even if he couldn’t manifest his full strength through his prison, it was still far beyond what the Scriptures’ first line of defence could handle.

She glanced between Euphoria and the amalgamation-spawning sea of blood that was Yog. “Cause as much damage as you’d like. Just keep them occupied.”

With that, her chest opened again and she was gone. Nyxalraith hardly needed to micromanage the assault for these two.

Finding herself back in that imitation room, Nyxalraith gave a brief glance over her surroundings to make sure nothing had changed before stepping into the Null Scar. A faint buzz tingled at her skin and sent her shadows afoul. It didn’t quite scatter her obscurement, but interacted with it strangely. Like compressing real-space into a flat plane.

That sensation only grew stronger as she stepped through the Scar and found herself in the most strange of places.

Green, rolling hills and a piercing blue sky. There was the occasional tree, but mostly the land was just grass. Perfectly vivid green that stretched as far as the eye could see.

Nyxalraith could only compare it to the flora of the Biovault, but there, the trees grew violently and wildly wherever the human hand hadn’t forced containment. This place wasn’t like that. It was… clean. The knolls stretched to perpetuity, always unblemished, always perfectly formed.

Somehow, this perfect landscape unsettled her more than the Embalmeds’ pyramid and its far-too-close sun.

On the hill across from her was a building. The sole visible structure for the eternity the hills stretched. It was a wooden cabin, roughly made in the most pristine of sense. No nails. No splinters. It somehow appeared rugged and sterile at the same time.

Behind the cabin was the other Null Scar, which led into a lower level of the Library. She couldn’t see the chaos above having an effect yet, but it was only a matter of time.

More importantly was the small group coming to meet her. There was ten of them. Given the cabin looked too small to house them all, and the absolute lack of any other life on this perfect surface, she assumed the hut was far larger on the inside.

Her eyes scanned the lot. Most were ninth and tenth evolution, but it was the three unevolved that were the most concerning. The others gave them a wide berth, never letting them slip out of their direct line of sight.

The Mad.

These three were far beyond the slapshod craftsmanship of the one that had hunted Nyxalraith down. She could see their souls, and the intertwining of the two was almost seamless. The answer to why was made perfectly obvious by their bodies.

There was none of that Bodytwister stitching nonsense with these three. Instead, not a scrap of skin was left free of markings. Incredibly conspicuous Scripture rituals had been carved through the skin of the children. Where they had run out of skin, they had sliced open muscle to write there. Where they had run out of muscle, rolls of parchment had been grafted instead.

Nyxalraith couldn’t see any mutations, but even without them, the hundred scraps of paper that hung from every muscle and bone stole enough of their human form regardless.

These weren’t the subjects they tossed out into Coral at a whim to muddy the waters about other cults. These were the creations of a master of scripture. The best they could make with what they knew best.

And all three of them marched towards Nyxalraith as if she was a curiosity. Some new toy to play with.

The seven Scriptures fell behind, appraising what they recognised as The Nightmare with equal wariness as they gave the Mad. Nyxalraith took that as a compliment. These were some of the strongest harbingers she’d faced, and they respected the rumours of her enough to give her equal weight as that which should never be experimented with.

“New playmay?” one of the children asked in an excessively youthful tone. Looking close, Nyxalraith realised that the girl’s proportions were off. She was as tall as a ten-year-old, but only through an ugly extrusion of her body. A toddler stretched unnaturally, and forcefully.

It was disgusting.

“Auntie Luz said you go through them too quick,” a boy whined. At least he sounded somewhat older. “It’s my turn. I want to see if this one’s any good at paint duels.”

The third nodded at something Nyxalraith couldn’t see. “Yeah, I think he did.” The girl giggled. “We shouldn’t tell him. Maybe he’ll forget about her and we won’t have to see…” she trailed off, shuddering. “That again. I don’t want to go to the iso- isow- ah… the pain room.”

As soon as she assessed that Ari wasn’t in the immediate vicinity — not dragged around as a hostage by one of the Mad that somehow knew more than they were supposed to — Nyxalraith struck. None of her defences would hold against the Mad, so it was better to kill them as fast as she could.

The distance was too great to close before they would react, even with her wings, so she opted for the alternative. Nyxalraith opened her chest, the light piercing through her storm of shadows to illuminate the ten approaching, and stepped through.

A moment later, she threw her claw through the opening gap between teeth, and let dark mist roll from her jaw.

She felt her chitin slice through soul. Instantly, her own soul was bombarded with something absolutely foul. Soul rot. That was the best way she could describe it. It felt as if the Mad’s soul was eating itself from within, and the moment she’d touched that soul directly, it had tried to infect her.

Fortunately, by the time it had settled, Nyxalraith’s core name had seemingly overwhelmed it. Or possibly consumed it? Could her bottomless pit of corruption eat Madness? If so, then maybe she had a way to strip Madness from its afflicted.

Maybe? It would require their forms be fixed, otherwise — even if it was possible — the Madness would only come back.

That was not something she could do for these children. They were tools of the Scriptures. And as the older of the two girls fell to the direct soul damage inflicted, the remaining pair’s eyes narrowed with an alertness that didn’t belong in children. They had survived the explosion of darkness that washed over their souls, despite being unevolved, but perhaps Nyxalraith shouldn’t have expected it to be that easy.

While these kids weren’t the chimera she’d previously fought, the design practice seemed consistent. There was an adult mind somewhere hidden in those rituals. It was there to control the weapons’ actions without the worse horror ever having a chance to rise to the surface, as it had in the lesser experiments.

Nyxalraith focused on the reflection of their eyes. In the twisted toddler’s, she saw an army of soft animal-shaped balls of fabric. In the boy’s… nothing was familiar. The entire realm was a painting on a canvas.

He pulled an invisible brush, and Nyxalraith immediately knew she didn’t want to be where she was.

Her portal slammed shut, and when she slipped back into reality behind him, she noticed a few of the lone trees had vanished from the horizon. While the green hills didn’t seem affected, that only made her more suspicious of this strange place. She didn’t think she wouldn’t have been affected.

Now that the very idea that someone could be cured of Madness had settled in her mind, she couldn’t help but look upon these two as victims of the Scriptures rather than true enemies.

She wanted to cleanse them, but the more she assessed, the more impossible it seemed. What little she’d deciphered of those rituals hanging from their muscles revealed so many countermeasures she couldn’t even count them. Should they fall into the hands of anyone but the Scriptures, they would explode on a scale comparable to a Dark Star. Even the first wasn’t erupting only because of the proximity to that cabin.

Once again, Nyxalraith chose the necessary option.

She despised being incapable of tearing them free of the cult after just discovering that it might be possible, but the two were too dangerous. She couldn’t leave them alive long enough for her to figure out how to save them. The best she could do for them was to promise the same thing she’d promised all the other innocents she’d been given no choice but to kill: vengeance.

Those that had done this to them would be punished as if they’d done the same to Nyxalraith herself. Given how many high creeds were involved in her death, they probably had.

Nyxalraith beat her wings and ripped through the air between her teeth, dodging the cuddle toys she saw in the toddler’s eyes. Her claws bore down on the boy’s neck. He was too slow to turn. His death would be quick, that was the best she could offer him.

Except her claws struck hard metal, not flesh. Much of her shadowy mist exploded to the side, having been struck by something that moved almost faster than Nyxalraith could follow.

One of the Scriptures was before her, blade holding back her claws. “No more sitting on the side line!” His voice was directed back at the other Scriptures, not her. Given the way his eyes flickered around, he couldn’t actually see her, yet he’d stopped her attack anyway. “Defend the assets. Do not let another perish. You know the results of failure.”
