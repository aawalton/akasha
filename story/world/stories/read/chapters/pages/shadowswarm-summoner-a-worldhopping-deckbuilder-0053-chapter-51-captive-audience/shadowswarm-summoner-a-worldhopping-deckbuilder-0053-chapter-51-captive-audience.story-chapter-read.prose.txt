Chapter 51: Captive Audience

As loved as parties were, they tended to put Luthor on edge. It was a habit from his childhood, before his mother had risen as high in the ranks as she had. Back when her company only provided escorts and security for merchants and politicians. Martha Graves knew better than most how often a celebration could go wrong, and she drilled it into her son’s head to always be on the lookout when people gathered and spirits were high.

In the case of Crag’s impromptu feast, the biggest source of conflict was their young prisoner.

“These people aren’t actually -- “ Jack started to say to a passerby, before Mulch cuffed the back of his head.

“Shaddup, kid,” the molekin said, “and don’t push your luck.”

There were no fortified corners to sit in, no places to hide or stick the kid somewhere he couldn’t cause damage. The best they could do was keep a watchful eye on him and not let him speak too much. It was a headache, but the two of them were up for it. They took up a table full of food on their own, and Mulch managed to beg off from too much company, saying he was still tired from his ordeal.

Luthor sighed and said, “We’ve talked about this at length, Jack. We’re more than willing to let you go once we’re done, but if you won’t cooperate with us before then…”

“You’ll what?” Jack sneered, pushing some grubs and leafy greens around on his plate. “You already said you’re not going to hurt me. What do you really have to threaten me with?”

Mulch exchanged a look with Luthor. The two had reached an easy camaraderie, an understanding of what it meant to be opposed to the overwhelming status quo and nearly alone in that stance. The molekin nodded, and Luthor suppressed a smile.

“Because if you don’t settle down and stop giving us a headache, we’ll give you to someone who won’t be as kind as me,” he said in his usual steady, even tone. “I don’t want anything bad to happen to you, but my friend Ambrose will be more than happy to deal with you. He won’t even have to kill you. He could bind you to a card, like he did your crew-mate.”

It wasn’t kind, threatening a kid with a fate like that. Luthor knew that. He also knew that Ambrose would probably approve of this approach, but that only made him feel dirtier.

Jack’s jaw set in what he first thought was anger, but then Luthor realized it was actually fear. It looked like it was the best, worst thing to say. “Tirgahl was a rage monster, but he was my friend.”

Luthor shrugged and took another drink of that sweet berry juice. “Maybe, but he was trying to kill us. You all were. The fact that you’re sitting here now is proof that we’re kinder and more compassionate than you people. But that has limits. Do not test us, Jack.”

They fell into a sullen silence. It was a pity. Under other circumstances, it would have been nice to load up on food and drink, wander around, and meet the locals. But at least one of them needed to be responsible, and it always fell to him to look out for the others. He didn’t begrudge them their fun, but for a moment, he wished someone else had to deal with Jack.

Mulch cleared his throat, nose wriggling in interest. “I was actually kinda curious about somethin’, kid.” He fell silent for a second as a friend approached and clapped his shoulder, congratulating him on not dying. Then, when the coast was clear, he continued, “We got our reasons for hating the Empire, but what’re your reasons for being so loyal? What’ve they ever done for you?”

Jack said nothing at first, but his face reddened. Mulch and Luthor remained patient, drinking and grabbing the occasional bite of bugs. He had to admit, they tasted better than he expected, but traveling with Raeleq during the summers had shown him that good food could be found from the most unlikely sources.

“They took me in,” Jack finally said, looking down. “We were on a contested world, and my parents…” he trailed off, trembling a little.

Mulch grunted appreciatively and slid a cup over to him. The kid gratefully took a sip and his time continuing.

“The Empire was winning. And so the people on my world, Molla, got desperate. They started targeting anyone they suspected of being sympathizers. My parents weren’t, but it didn’t matter. They killed them anyway. Some imperial agents found me, took me in, and got me justice. They gave me work, and off the world. Things weren’t perfect, but…”

His voice hardened. “But you killed the only friends I had. You turned Tirgahl into a card. If you let me go, there’s nothing waiting for me anyway. Why the hell would I ever want to help you? Maybe I don’t care if you kill me.”

Mulch sighed. “And then it’d be yer word against ours, and there ain’t a chance anyone listens to you. You’d die for nothing. C’mon kid, work with us here. We’re not out to mess with you.”

Luthor held up a hand, thinking about how to phrase things. “The Empire is in the process of conquering my world,” he said, as calm as ever. “And in the process of doing so, you know what they did? They engineered a plague that wiped out half of my world. I just found that out a few days ago, and it’s haunting me. The empire killed millions of people just to make it easier for my own people to expand there and do the dirty work for them.

“When they’re willing to kill or enslave world after world, they become an existential threat for every world. I’m sorry about what happened to your parents. Collateral damage in war is tragic. But when faced with an enemy like this, I cannot fault others for being overzealous. It’s that or perish.”

Jack stood up, fists balled. Luthor was up in a flash as well. Their height difference wasn’t great enough to make the light wizard any more intimidating than he normally was. But the unconcerned, rock-solid stoic stare stopped any further aggression.

Nearby molekin stared at the brewing fight, but Mulch waved them off with a big, fake smile. “Siddown, kid. I get it. I do. But sit down and listen for a tic.”

It took a few seconds, but Jack relented and sat down, breathing hard.

Mulch took a deep breath and let it out. “My world’s already mostly fallen, as you can prolly see for yourself. But it ain’t over yet. Not while we can do something about it. Now, I ain’t going to ask you to switch sides or anything like that. All I’m asking is for you to sit tight and not do anything for a few days. Once you do, Luthor over there’s gonna drop you off somewhere safe. And if I had to guess, he’s gonna make sure you are safe and have supplies.”

“Yes,” Luthor confirmed with a slow nod. “I’m sorry for disrupting your life, but we’ll do whatever we can to make sure you end up safe and with a real chance. You have my word of honor. Trust me, and I’ll trust you back.”

The teenager looked like he tasted something sour, but at least he thought about it. He couldn’t have been more than fifteen, and Luthor felt for him and his situation. But it couldn’t go on like this. Not if they wanted a real chance to succeed.

“If I agree, can I walk away and get away from you for a bit?” Jack finally asked.

Mulch looked like he wanted to protest, but Luthor nodded again. “Yes. If you promise to keep your mouth shut and behave, we’ll give you some slack. Act up, and we yank on the leash. Do I have your word?”

“...Fine. You have my word I won’t tell the moles anything,” he said. Without waiting for any confirmation, he stood up and stormed off, disappearing into the sea of furry bodies.

“You sure about that?” Mulch asked, looking like he wanted to go after him.

“I am,” said Luthor, pushing his plate away. The music nearby had switched to something more mellow, and it suited his mood. He sat back, leaning against the rock wall behind him. “Either he tattles, or he doesn’t. If he does, we shut it down or run. If he doesn’t, we buy ourselves some breathing room to talk and plan.”

“Mm. I guess so. Still don’t feel great about it, but I’d be a dumbass for not trusting you at this point. Then again, you were all fool enough to wander into a spider den.” He grinned widely.

“Says the mole who got webbed up along with his rebel friends,” Luthor said dryly.

Mulch let out a hearty guffaw, slapping his claw on his knee. “You got me there, friend. I wish it were safer to meet openly, but Mychele’s people ain’t exactly trusted anymore. Our kind used to get along, but now…Them and Roy are from villages that haven’t been conquered. The Mycellia aren’t big on fightin’, but they’re sturdy and good at defending. Hard to sneak up on them, you know?

“They could probably get to the bats, but they don’t need to. It’s them and a one molekin village on the other side of Bedrock Spire that are holdouts. About one third of our slice of the world. But turns out, one third of a population’s enough to throw a wrench in the works. Maybe permanently, if we’re lucky.”

Luthor let it sink in, thinking about it. It matched so much of what he’d learned, and what he and Ambrose had talked about in regards to causing trouble for the Empire. Insurgencies weren’t usually enough to topple regimes on their own, but it could make things too expensive to continue. It’s what King Ransa focused on, while presenting the mostly respectable front of a man who surrendered with conditions and earned a favorable outcome for his people.

“We can’t stay here long,” he started, “but we’ll do whatever we can to help you out here before we leave. Our priority’s getting our ship repaired, but if we can give you a boost before we go, we will. You’re sure that Bedrock Spire will be safe enough?”

“Weeeell…Yer humans, so you got that going for ya. But no, I wouldn’t call it safe. Especially not if that kid decides to screw you over. But I’m not sure we got much other choices. But if you did want to help…”

“Yes?” Luthor leaned in.

Mulch looked around to make sure no one was close enough to hear and said, “If you and yer friends can get us some combat cards from there, we might be able to make something happen.”

“We have limited resources, and we’re trying to gain strength as well, but I’m sure we can pick up a number of basic cards for you and your friends to practice with. Enough to defend yourselves better. If we can arm you against our mutual foe, then we will.”

“You promise?” Mulch asked.

Arming the locals could only work in their favor. Worst case scenario, it provided them a bit of cover after they left. It worried him to make too many promises in the middle of this scenario, but one didn’t get ahead without allies. And one didn’t get allies without being willing to extend their help.

“I promise,” said Luthor. He held up his juice, and so did his new friend. They clinked mugs, and that was that.

After eating with Raeleq, Jessica considered searching out the others and spending time with them, but she ended up wandering Crag alone, itching for something to do. As far as she could tell, her future was tied inexorably with her fellow exiles, so a bit of time on her own while she processed things and tried to find some kind of peace was necessary.

At least the nap had helped keep her on her feet, and now she walked around, slipping between the much bulkier molekin. They were friendly enough, free with greetings and a toast, but no one stopped or bothered her for long. Twenty minutes of that and she felt practically invisible for the first time in her life.

It took her entirely too long to realize why it unnerved her: Jessica was used to everyone around her knowing who she was and…well, kissing her ass to get in good with her grandfather. Not that she wanted them to, of course, but a lifetime of being related to the world’s most famous wizard skewed her perspective.

Of course, now there was the expectation that she was part of the evil empire, out to conquer infinity…but maybe that was always there, and she only just now saw it.

Once she understood, it didn’t bother her like she thought it would. Jessica made a point of enjoying the invisibility, the distance. No one bothered her, which meant she had every opportunity to listen to music, dance a little, and watch moles wrestle, completely in peace. The chance to satiate her eternal curiosity and observe others without having to be too social was a dream she never knew she had.

Every so often, she saw the others, but gave them a wide berth. Raeva and Ambrose danced, Luthor and Mulch chatted, and she found Raeleq with [Reggie] out, giving molekin children rides on the dinosaur’s back while laughing in joy. She smiled and moved on, until she got to an illusionist putting on a show.

It was a molekin woman, but Jessica had a tough time telling their ages outside of the very young and old. She had what looked like a magic wand, and directed images of a rock-covered wyrm spiraling through the air while her audience clapped their big claws together in swift, staccato applause.

“Thank you, thank you,” she said in the mole’s lazy drawl. “For my next trick…”

She waved her wand and the wyrm disappeared, replaced with a massive spider, towering over them all. It didn’t look like the Spider Queen at all, but that didn’t matter. Jessica still let out a strangled squeal and nearly tripped trying to retreat.

She wasn’t alone. Several molekin let out yelps or took cover while the illusory spider raised its front legs and hissed dangerously. The magician let out an obnoxious, braying laugh. “Don’t you worry none, folks! She doesn’t bite. Much.”

Jessica stood up straight, trying not to tremble. The real spiders were way worse than this. It couldn’t hurt her, but it still annoyed the hell out of her. She was also curious.

“What cards do you have?” she asked the performer. Her Enhancement card showed her what cards people had in their hand in battle, but not outside of it. “I thought most of you couldn’t have combat cards.”

Some of the audience shifted uncomfortably.

“I-I don’t,” the illusionist said, backtracking immediately. The spider disappeared. “I got a good ritual card, passed down from my ma from hers and so on. Anytime I paint somethin’, I can bind it like it was a real critter. It can’t hurt anything, and I’m not a danger, I promise!”

The sudden fear reminded Jessica of her role. She nodded imperiously, schooling her face to something neutrally disdainful. She thought of Ambrose, of course, and did her best to mimic his scorn. “Don’t worry, I know you’re not a danger to me. I was merely curious. I’m used to seeing more cards in play than in Crag. It’s…quaint.”

Her own voice almost disgusted her, but it worked. A wave passed through the assembled molekin, like one giant sigh of relief. The illusionist herself nodded, practically bowing her head in deference. “Only a handful of us are allowed cards. Important craftsmen, healers, our head miner…”

“Oh, so you’re important?” Jessica teased. After the spider jump-scare, she couldn’t help herself. “For being able to make pretty pictures come to life? How does that serve the empire?”

The illusionist took an unconscious step back. Her big, beady eyes filled with water, and Jessica knew she had gone too far. “I-I’m good for helpin’ the younguns learn about outside dangers and edible plants! And and…And part of the agreement was we could keep heirlooms. You’re not gonna take it away from me, are ya?”

Oh gods. Jessica inhaled sharply and shook her head. “No, of course not. I was just…curious. You have a nice home here, and quite a gift. Carry on.”

She hurried away, and the molekin didn’t seem sorry to see her go. Her face burned with shame and horror at her behavior, even if it would sell the lie better. Maybe she could apologize later, and show off her [Illusory Double] and its similarities. For now, she went to a mostly empty table and collapsed in the nearest open seat.

“Wow, you really have a gift with people, don’tcha?” a feminine but raspy voice said. Dexi’Lynn sat down across from her, pulling out her ornate pipe. She lit it and took a few puffs, blowing smoke out of her nostrils. “Are you people always this good at scaring the locals, or are you having an off day?”

Jessica groaned and buried her face in her hands. “I didn’t mean to! I was scared at first, then annoyed, and…People here are really scared of the Empire, aren’t they? I know that’s a stupid question, but there’s a difference between knowing and knowing, you know?”

“Yeah, sure,” said the kobold. “But you handled that like a spoiled brat, I think.”

“I know.” Jessica sighed and rubbed her eyes. “I know. I’ll apologize later, once things are less crowded.”

“Don’t you fuckin’ dare,” said Dexi’Lynn, pointing the stem of her pipe Jessica’s way. “If you apologize, they’ll know something’s up. I’m not saying you handled it poorly at all. You did exactly what you needed to do. It’s nice to see that only half of you are as soft as a newly hatched egg. I was starting to get worried.”

Jessica looked up, puzzled. “I really don’t understand you, Dexi’Lynn.”

The kobold waved her off. “Just Dexi’s fine, since you’re not my mother. And there ain’t much to understand. I wasn’t happy where I was, and you guys give me a rare opportunity. I know I can trust you as much as you guys trust me.”

“But we don’t really trust you at all.”

Her golden eyes lit up. “Exactly! But that’s the thing, right? You’re a bunch of soft-shells, and I’m not. You gotta worry about me, but I don’t need to worry about you. And since I don’t need to worry about you, you don’t need to worry about me. See? I’ll let you know if that changes, but for now, I wanna see that ship and how it works. You can trust in that, at least.”

“I suppose,” she said doubtfully. “But mostly, I don’t see that we have a choice. I know how to fly the thing, but repairs and how it works are beyond me. SNA-” she hesitated, unsure of how much to reveal. “Our source of information is extensive, but not always easily learned from. I just know that we need to get a part to help ships fly here better. Your ship has it, right?”

“Sure,” said Dexi’Lynn, “A fair few ships in this region do, mostly to carry cargo off-world. We would’ve been fine if you hadn’t shot us down, but I bet you still would’ve crashed. Some worlds, the laws of reality work differently, right? And flight is much harder deep underground, for some reason. I’m positive we can get what you need from Bedrock Spire. The question is…The fuck are we gonna do about all those ghouls?”

Gods, the ghouls. “I don’t know,” she admitted. “That’s a problem for later. Right now, we just need to get the part…what was it called again?”

Dexi snorted smoke. “An advanced Levi-box. But if you guys got the resources, we might be able to find something that’s a bit better for all terrain. Imagine flying into a water world and drowning immediately, just because you don’t have one.”

Frankly, she was surprised her grandfather hadn’t already outfitted the ship with one. But then, she also didn’t know how to install it or operate it, how it worked, or what it would do to the other layered enchantments and artifice in the Wanderer. Again, her mind went to the Wildcard she still possessed.

During the fight with the Spider Queen, she’d been worried about it getting damaged when Raeva finally let loose. To her relief, it had come out unscathed and even dry, despite the elemental she summoned. She knew it would be smart to equip it soon. The card would become whatever she needed. Maybe it would even give her the ability to repair and tweak the ship, and let her actually be useful for a change.

Or maybe it would be smarter to give it to Raeva, and arm their heaviest single-target hitter. Or to Luthor, where it might become a healing or shield card that would save all of their asses when the time came. But no, for some reason, they thought it best to give it to her.

The least of them.

“What’s on your mind, Jess?” Dexi asked, no longer quite as cocky or crass as before.

“Nothing,” she lied. “Just thinking about how to make things work. Hey, since we’re trusting each other…When we get back to the ship, think you can teach me everything you know about it?”

“That depends. You looking for the first chance to dump me off on someone else like you’re going to do with Jack?”

Jessica blanched. “What? No. I just want to learn. We’ve got a lot of…just a lot, waiting for us. I want to learn everything I can, be as useful as possible. We’re all planning on learning everything we can, in case something happens to one of us.”

Another lie, but she’d make it true when they got back to the ship.

“Yeah, that makes sense. Hmm. Does that mean, in my capacity as mechanic and valued friend, you folks’ hook me up with some new cards? Once we get into Bedrock Spire, I mean.” The intensity of the kobold’s weird, square pupils made Jessica shiver.

“That’s the plan, basically. We’re going to get whatever we can, and then get the hell out of here, and move on.”

Dexi took an especially long drag of her pipe and exhaled a fogbank of smoke over the table. It drifted over to the crowd, some of whom coughed and shot them dirty looks. Jessica wondered if they resented her smoking, when they weren’t allowed to drink. What else weren’t the people of Stonskarr allowed to do, to maintain control?”

“This Vanderborn guy chasing after you. That’s who you’re running from, right? I heard his name before, but I don’t know much about him. What’s going on there?” Finally, she stowed the pipe.

Of all the topics, Jessica wanted to talk about this one the least, but she owed it to the kobold. “My grandfather. He threatened us, we stole from him and ran. And if he finds us, we’re all worse than dead.”

“Mm.” Dexi nodded thoughtfully. “Well, then I guess we better not get caught. With any luck, we can be out of here by tomorrow night. Morning after, at the latest. We can ditch this hole and find someplace better to lay low. I bet Grandpa wouldn’t find us on a casino world.”

Jessica laughed in spite of herself. “Maybe not. I know Ambrose would love that, but Luthor and Raeva might say no.”

“Losers.”

In spite of herself, she found herself liking the kobold. And if there was anything to be grateful about this whole situation, possibly making friends was one she wouldn’t ignore. This entire day, in fact, was a gift she intended on enjoying.

“Hey Dexi, want to go play some games?”

“And fleece the moles? You’re damned right I do!” The kobold cackled and hopped out of the chair. She offered Jessica her arm. The mind wizard took it, and they rejoined the party.
