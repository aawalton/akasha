It was a small thing, really, to hold so much power on so large a space. At first, [Burrow Sense] had almost overwhelmed him. With so many people and relics around, the skill had blared every feeling and emotion into his senses. But even through the fog of sensation, a speck of brilliant power sat beneath a weed, practically shouting its presence at him. With another little flick to use [Shiny Snatch], his hand shot through the earth in that strange dissociative way to grab Toronto’s Tax Guild Stone. The Stone came unblemished, the skill scooping only the target.

And now Alex sprinted toward the Paladome, clenching the small, grey, perfectly round pebble, feeling the essence crashing within it just calling to be used.

[Tax Guild Stone]

[System Region: Toronto]

[Status: Unclaimed]

[Holder: Empty]

[Injecting System energy into this Tax Guild Stone will erect you as its region’s Holder. You will remain as Holder until your death. It will multiply your planar acumen, solidify your Path amongst the River of Parallels, and allow you to siphon a modifiable portion of System credits from all registered transactions under its region’s Tax Guild. Credits claimed may only be distributed for use by regional Guilds.]

[All things in the Loop revert to a balanced state. Attempts to disrupt the balance may be met with subjective inequal ramification for adjustment. Balance itself is subjective. The System itself makes no mistakes.]

What was most strange was how the Tax Guild Stone felt. The words were confusing, but Alex’s mind spiraled about them. Yet for some reason as soon as he’d grasped the powerful rock, his stomach had been immediately uneasy, and stranger yet, he was scared of it.

I just don’t like it.

“Don’t touch the Paladome!” Aria called from within the fence made of [Volt Whips]. “We’ll lift it at the bottom. Also, don’t fall into a portal!”

Portal? I don’t see any portals? Alex thought as he approached the looming construct.

His heart hammered in his chest. The Stone’s message wove through his mind. Energy? That’s what the System called essence? A solid Path amongst the River of Parallels? He’d heard of the River of Parallels. His new skill, [Relative Voyeur], had told him he’d swam amongst them and survived, giving him a version of bullet time. But a Path? What was that? And how could balance be subjective? That was quite literally the opposite of balance.

All that from a tiny rock under the grass.

The Tax Guild wanted to be claimed. He could feel it pressing its message against his brain as he ran the short distance across the field, wiggling in and practically demanding that its ess—energy be used. He saw the appeal. Who wouldn’t want to have more acumen? Who wouldn’t want to be able to change the flow of money with a thought?

“ALEX!” Aria yelped. “FUCKING PAY ATTENTION!”

Oh, shit, right.

By an act of will together, Zippy snapped off a horizontal portal Alex was heading straight into as Beepy pulled up a portion of lattice along the bottom of the Paladome. Like a fence, a lip of dangerous weaves peeled up from the ground, barely high enough for someone to crawl through. Alex leapt and landed into a baseball slide to skid along the grass and under the opening. Throwing his head back to avoid getting clipped, he stopped and sprang to his feet as Beepy slammed the lip back to the ground, sealing them off again.

Alex’s muddied thoughts cleared as he assessed his friends. Brody looked perfect if dirty as he clapped Alex on the shoulder and beamed at him. Tank waggled his tail and circled. The drones huddled close together looking up at them.

“You made it,” Alex huffed, looking around from within the Paladome. “You guys actually came. And this is…this certainly is something.”

“OF COURSE.” Beepy toned and stepped forward. “THE PALADRONES WOULD NEVER ALLOW A FRIEND IN NEED BE A FRIEND WITHOUT.”

Another scream sounded as someone ran into the Paladome and lost a foot. Looking, Alex saw the crowd of hunters staring at the Paladome, clearly afraid but curious and trying to peak in.

“Vicious,” Alex remarked. “The [Volt Whips] are bright and tight enough that people can’t look in, too. Crazy. You drones are fricken crazy, you know that?” He shook his head and turned back to them. “But we gotta go. How are we all going to get—”

He stopped and spun, remembering his other friends.

“Dandelion. Where’s Dandelion and Acorn?”

Just then, her voice rose through the din of onlookers and attackers.

“Out of the way!” Dandelion cried. “Out of the way of the Indominable Shield!”

Like an unstoppable force, Acorn Turtleshell barrelled forward with Dandelion hot on his tail, bashing into the back of Hunter’s calves. Every ram resulted in the person flipping backwards. Acorn couldn’t be stopped, and each was person easily tumbled. Dandelion had wanted to get to the group in the shining circle. So that was where he would take her. Climbing up the cliff by rope once handed didn’t stop him, and a crowd was no match for he and his [Indominable Plastique Shell].

“They’re going to run into the Paladome!” Aria yelped. “Quick, Zip and Beepy, lift a section!”

There was no need for the drones to act. Before slamming into the Paladome, Dandelion leapt over and ahead of Acorn, and rather than her feet meeting solid ground, she seemed to be swallowed by the dirt. Dandelion piped through the mud as all Gnomes could and shot out on the other side. She flipped and landed, skidding to a halt and grinning up at the group.

“Trying to leave without us, eh?” Dandelion laughed. “We are here to aide Alex, and no soil nor rock nor bleating beast shall impeded our goals,” she turned to the drones, offering a quick bow. “And it’s a pleasure to meet you three offshoots of life. Dandelion Datura, special operations of the Gnomish Brigade of Allen Gardens, at your service.”

The introduction was so formal that the drones froze, then as if coming to an unspoken consensus, posed as one with high chins and forward chests.

“WE ARE THE PALADRONES.” Beepy announced seriously with a raised fist. “IT IS WITH GREAT PLEASURE WE FIGHT AGAINST THE FORCES OF EVIL AND—"

“GUYS! Not now!” Alex interrupted, the thought about how almost all his accomplices were knee high psychos passing. “Acorn? He’s going to flow under the—"

Acorn rammed into the Paladome in a sizzling thud. Spinning together, the group witnessed the potent, burning energy of the latticed [Volt Whip]s attempt but fail to melt the unmeltable shield. Raw essence sparked and splashed in a cone around Acorn as he leaned in, his porcelain arm flaring from the heat. After a moment, the Gnome backed an inch, lowered his shield, gave a silent nod, and dropped into the earth as if it were water.

“Just needed to charge his shield,” Dandelion explained to the stunned drones as Acorn popped up through grass to silently stand next to her. She nodded laid a hand on the mouthless Gnome’s shoulder. “Acorn Turtleshell, the Unbreakable, the Indominable, my lo—”

“Guys!” This time it was Brody who gurgled a yell. “How…are we going to…get out of this?”

“The Gnomish tunnels,” Dandelion commanded. “Through earth we shall escape unimpeded. With Acorn at the helm!”

“No, no! We take The Expediter, of course, we’re not leaving it,” Alex said quickly, immediately thinking of the vehicle. “Alright. Here’s what we do. Beepy, you lift the back part, and then we sprint over, with me at the wheel. You all pile on with the drones keeping guard as we speed away!”

His and Dandelion’s excitement was met with silence. They both looked at the rest of the group for approval.

“You realize we’re the Paladrones, right?” Aria asked cockily. “You might have that motorcycle, and you might have the tunnels…but us? The Paladrones can get anywhere.”

“OPEN OUR ESCAPE, ZIPPY.” Beepy droned, clapping his brother on the back.

Zippy responded with hand waving and robot noises, gesturing towards Alex.

“YES.” Beepy turned towards Alex. “ZIPPY CAN TAKE US ANYWHERE. BUT WHERE IS ANYWHERE? WHERE SHALL THE PALADRONES DELIVER YOU, ALEX?”

Alex was stunned that everyone was waiting on his final say. Truth be told, he was pissed. Nina and Nino had sent him to Pearson to what? Get closer to the Tax Stone holder? Bribe him? Then Keanu Bereaves had died and now they were in this mess. Maybe it wasn’t them that did it, but something felt off about the whole situation. He’d trusted his employer.

I still do, don’t I?

His gut tweaked slightly. Not to them then. Not until he had a moment to fully think things through.

Home was the next obvious choice, but his tummy did a somersault at that, so the choice was disregarded. Who was he sure he trusted without question? Someone who would always have his back and was an unstoppable force in their own right.

Snu wouldn’t harm him, that much he knew, but showing up with a dangerous gang and the Tax Guild Stone?  He didn’t want to put that on her. That was a lot. Hi, it’s your adorable boyfriend, please let me rant and rave about how I’m having trouble trusting people would be a terrible look. Brody he trusted outright without question. The drones too, and Mary, and of course Jemin. But where would they be? The shop, most likely.

Emilio would always welcome him, but when he thought of his cat, his stomach clenched so hard he almost doubled over.

No, definitely not him, Alex thought, unnerved at the visceral reaction to what should be an obvious destination.

Then who?

WOOF, Tank barked, the sound screeching around the metal within him.

The thought smacked him as he slapped his own forehead with his free hand. As soon as he thought of the individual, his stomach blossomed in warmth.

“Idiot,” Alex muttered. “I’m an idiot. It’s obvious.”

“WHERE, ALEX?”

He took a deep breath and held up Toronto’s Tax Guild Stone, finally getting a good look at it and showing everyone.

It was slightly smaller than an egg and perfectly round. Coloured a matte grey without a simple mark, the Tax Guild Stone was so spherical it felt strange in his hand. And practically pouring out of the Stone was barely contained potent energy. He still felt its inert desire to be used. To have someone claim it and use all the benefits. He felt its pull himself.

A cold wind seemed to curl around his stomach, as if shouting something ominous and terrible.

“We’re going to a friend,” Alex said with gritted teeth. “Someone who’s made strides to become more than they were. Someone who needs someone to believe in them. I believe in them. And hopefully, everyone I trust, or think I can trust, will come by. There’s a lot to talk about.”

He pocketed the Stone to cast his eyes at the Expediter, barely believing he was about to leave it behind. Then, feeling for his phone to make sure it was still there, as it was about to send off numerous calls on arrival, he turned towards the group.

“You can call Petal through that weird mushroom thing?” Alex asked Dandelion. She nodded.

“I’m calling everyone when we get there. Snu, Nino and Nina, Mary, Jemin. The Stone isn’t just about taxes on credits. It does so much more. It’s…scary. I don’t like it.”

Finally, he turned to Zippy alone.

“Zip, take us to Wonderland. Haven’t seen Lord Loopy in a minute.”

Boanne was conflicted. She should be raging, soaring, casting hellfire upon the puny people around her home. It was destroyed! Her roost behind the Falls had been made into two massive steps! Her treasure was scattered, and these individuals deserved her wrath. Her draconic nature demanded she burn it all to ash.

Yet her heart stopped her. Her instinct said to destroy everything down there. Her nature, kept her coasting in the air, observing and unsure of what to do.

It was one small tidbit that had solidified her decision. She was a dragon, of course. But she was no ordinary dragon. She could fly through everything. It was all air for her. No gust or shore or steel or fire could stop her. She was a mud dragon. The only mud dragon any dragon had ever heard of.

And she held the [Princess in Waiting] Title.

That tidbit made her certain in her decision to coast the skies and think. She would keep close and find somewhere appropriate to sleep that night. She’d waited for so long, and her Title felt itchy.

It had to be a sign. The fact that her eyes had picked out those two teeny tiny beings amongst the rabble. It just had to be.

Surely, the small people who moved through mud as she did was a sign.

Surely, they would come to her soon.

She was right. The Gnomes sent forth by Emilio were already on their way. Through the mud they brought his gift.
