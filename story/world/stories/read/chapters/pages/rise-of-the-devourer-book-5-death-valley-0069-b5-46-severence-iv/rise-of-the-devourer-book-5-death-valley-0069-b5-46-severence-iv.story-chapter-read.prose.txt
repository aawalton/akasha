Severence was hovering high above the valley, watching the chaos below in case his seer friend Eileth survived, when a dragon claw slithered its way out of his storage belt and dug deep into his side.

He swatted the irritating thing, which dispersed it into raw mana, but another appeared a moment later. Sev flew higher, which did little to help. But it at least helped him feel more in control of himself.

He still needed to decide what he wanted to do with the Vessel, whether he should keep him a few years for his own experiments or hand him over to the Lunar Council right away. At the moment, he was leaning heavily toward keeping the kid, at least until he could figure out how he’d stolen Sev’s strongest path, perhaps until he found a way to steal it back.

He knew better than most, nothing could truly be destroyed. Negation, Regression, they imposed a prior state, but they were not unbreakable. Whatever the monster had done to Sev, it should be possible to reverse it.

Should be.

Sev tapped into his storage belt to pull out his prisoner.  This far up, anyone below A-rank would freeze solid within moments, and if he dropped someone from this high, he’d shatter on impact. Sure, the kid could regrow his whole body, but it would at least slow him down.

His prisoner didn’t appear.

Again, Sev tapped his storage belt to drag out his prisoner, and again, his prisoner didn’t appear. A dragon claw sliced out instead and stabbed Sev in the hand, as if in retaliation for the attempt.

This wasn’t how things were supposed to go. His brow furrowed faintly as his efforts continued to be met only with failure.

This really shouldn’t be happening.

—

Putting Noah inside an astral space was a terrible move. Noah may not have been strong enough to break himself out of the storage space easily, but Astral magic was one of his specialties.

Sev only had an item, no understanding of what it did or how, and so his attempts to remove Noah were predictable, identical, and practically trivial to circumvent. Piggybacking on those attempts to attack him with the spectral dragon’s claw was just a nice bonus.

Meanwhile, between deflecting attempts to drag him out of this space, Noah was busy getting ready to loot the place clean. As it turned out, the metal cell he’d been stuffed into was only a small part of the storage area, and it was insufficiently warded. Another terrible move.

Granted, most people would enter a stasis mode upon being placed into storage, so under ordinary circumstances, even this much would be overkill. Only Noah’s personal abilities allowed him to act freely. Combine that with Noah’s excessive amounts of time poking into and out of his own storage space to test everything and anything he could think of, and Sev had no idea what he was dealing with.

Less than a minute into his investigation of his cell, Noah became confident enough that he attempted to slip from one part of the astral space to another space just outside the steel box he’d been initially deposited in. And while Noah had seen a lot of amazing things—everything from the adventurers’ guild artifact room to Zax’s hoard to the vault of Drakonias—the chance to rummage around freely in the personal storage of an Ascendant was new.

It took only fractionally more effort to move himself around inside someone else’s space than to move himself around Erandir. A resistance pushed against him, a membrane like plastic wrap an inch thick, but his abilities let him slice through it. It tried to seal itself behind him as he moved through, too slowly to do more than delay him briefly.

A moment later, he emerged into the relative freedom of Sev’s storage space. It was spacious, tidy, and annoyingly utilitarian. Instead of piles of treasure, there were shelves of neat cubbies. The sight reminded him vividly of those cheap college stacking cubes, though these were made of some marbled grey metallic stone instead of painted composite board and took up one entire wall.

Each square was labeled with a language that Noah’s universal translation failed to translate, consisting of raised single lines in various orientations.

The first two rows of shelves contained cultic artifacts and ability tomes whose names sounded highly forbidden. Nothing ancient or mystic or legendary, but a wide variety of uncommon and a handful of rares.

[Tome of Abyssal Sacrifice (Rare)]

[Tome of Void Speech (Rare)]

[Tome of Plague (Rare)]

[Tome of Sympathic Decay (Rare)]

[Tome of Madness (Uncommon)]

None of them sounded like things he wanted badly enough to erase any existing skills for them, but he certainly saw value in depriving his enemy of them.

He hummed to himself as he summoned his spear and started slicing free one of the cubes to use as a box and started shoving tomes into it unceremoniously. Then he summoned another four copies of himself to speed up the looting process. While having each item in its own space made the place look full, once they started condensing everything into convenient crates, it only took up three.

That’s when he noticed the one drawback to his visit here. His mana wasn’t recovering at all. Not even a trickle. Which wasn’t surprising, as soon as he thought about it, but was still irritating.

He dismissed his copies before they could run through any more of his dwindling supply and looked around the half-looted space.

The sides not made up of his cell or the storage wall were silver-streaked madness, only vaguely reminiscent of his own cosmic vault’s swirling power and galaxies. Instead it looked out on a mass of fog full of distorted eyes. The eyes were dull against the bright fog, pale and lifeless concave things, as though seen from inside, and stretched like taffy being pulled. Some in multiple directions at once, others elongated, all in constant dizzying motion.

It was an entirely different kind of disturbing from most things he’d seen, but it still held a strange allure. Noah found himself intensely curious, watching. One eye, stretched to its limits, popped apart into two smaller pieces that briefly spun away from each other before the torn pupil started to slide toward the center while the outer edge ballooned out.

Both of them twisted themselves inside out to stare at Noah.

You’ve been inflicted with [Madness I]

[Devourer] negates [Madness]

He blinked at the notification and quickly looked away from the fog, but not before he saw another dozen of the eyes inverting themselves to stare in at him.

You’ve been inflicted with [Madness II]

[Devourer] negates [Madness]

“You’d think one of these days I’d learn my lesson about staring into the abyss.” Noah shook his head to clear it of the disturbing sight and got back to packing up the contents of Sev’s storage space. “Huh, it has been a while since he tried to pull me out. I wonder what he’s up to now?”

No attacks were forthcoming. Sev didn’t appear in a flash of irony to punish his complacency.

“Well, no point leaving perfectly good loot lying around.” The chaos started when Noah tried to move the boxes of stolen goods into his storage without thinking about the logistics. He’d failed to move himself into his own storage from within this storage already, so if he’d been blocked from moving the items, that would have only reminded him of something he’d forgotten.

However, instead of anything so simple as bouncing off a protective membrane, instead his entire vault appeared around him in the exact same space as Sev’s storage.

Noah’s was quite a bit larger, but dimensions didn’t quite play the same out here. The cosmic void of Noah’s backdrop and the silver mist and countless watching eyes of Sev’s item, rather than ending up inside one other or cancelling the other out, swirled violently together in a writhing blur of wrongness. Noah couldn’t look at it for more than a second without his head feeling like he was hatching a nest of hungry acid vipers behind his eyes.

But looking at the shelves wasn’t much better. They, too, were smudging and warping, the attempted transition becoming a fusion instead. The space was littered with items Noah had shoved in there at one time or another, everything from a pile of dirt and pebbles to Bun Bun’s unmoving rabbit form.

“Maybe the guy has a point, I should invest in some organization one of these days.” Noah considered this, squinting against the headache-inducing twisting of dimensions. “I can always task a few of my clones to tidy the place up when we’re free between things.”

Then, with one final wrenching warp that made Noah want to throw up, the entire place shuddered and calmed.

Storage item integrated. Cosmic Vault has expanded.

“Huh?”

Noah can add other spaces to his own, Tony helpfully reminded him.

“Oh, right. Of course.” He’d forgotten that option existed. Noah’s abilities had been advancing so fast, even he’d started to lose track of what exactly each one had to offer. Good thing at least a few of them functioned more or less autonomously by now. No need for constant windows popping up to verify the answers to obvious questions.

It took him a moment to inventory the newly added space, which turned out to have another three rooms concealed behind the shelves and cells. The front area was more of a museum than anything, display cases of cult-related works for showing off. Behind the shelves were a workroom and an interrogation room, both clearly heavily used, but still as tidy and orderly as the main room. Only more stained and worn.

The cell Noah had escaped from was just that, one of eight other cells closely packed together. Two were occupied, one by an Abyssal cultist and the other by an emaciated nobleman. Both were frozen in place, the cultist in a posture of rapturous prayer with blood dripping down his arms, the nobleman frozen mid-scream with an expression of absolute horror on his face.

The panels on the cell walls were something like a camera, a workaround for observing prisoners from the workroom or interrogation room in case something went wrong.

Insufficient mana.

Noah fell.

One moment he was inside two different dimensional spaces at once, the next he was in low orbit over Death Valley, next to a very surprised-looking Sev.

He didn’t fall far before instinct took over. He shifted into dragon form, wings tearing free of his back and flaring out to catch him.

Then things happened very quickly.

Sev lunged for him, and the weight of his aura pressed down on Noah from every side, freezing him in place and threatening to crush him to death on the spot.

Tony split himself out into a dozen eyewyrms.

Bun Bun launched himself out of the storage space and, as the ascendant’s hand came up to grab the bunny around the neck, twisted in midair to fasten his teeth around Sev’s finger.

Noah ate one of Sev’s mana crystals.

Behind the cells lay the power storage room, and the A-grade crystals Sev kept on hand made the entire crystal cave Noah had been eating his way through look like nothing. He couldn’t move, but he could teleport one into his mouth, and that was good enough for magic.

This power was too much for him to even channel, let alone absorb. But in that instant between mana overload and mana paralysis, as time seemed to slow and Noah’s thoughts raced for their life, several things became perfectly clear.

None of it mattered. Sev was an Ascendant. Six hundred levels higher than Noah and then some, a qualitative difference so vast that everything he’d been doing so far was barely an irritation. None of Noah’s abilities or skills, his familiars or scales or claws, none of it would be enough in the face of this kind of strength. Even against an infiltrator who specialized in stealing from the other cults instead of fighting. Even with Life Siphon running stronger than it ever had.

Sev’s aura promised obliteration with no restraint or hope of escape.

But Noah had one thing that wasn’t an ability or skill, the one thing he’d carried with him from the beginning. He’d never utilized it fully. Even when he’d awakened it to scare Vion into activating her dragon form, he’d not used the power. Only held it briefly before pushing it away. Even the memory of that incident remained locked away, a blank space he avoided looking at.

His shadow-clone had made the argument, almost convincing Noah to embrace the power at the heart of who he was, but since seeing the path upgrade option at his B-rank evolution, he’d withdrawn from it even more. Devourer Incarnate; the dead god stirs and the separation between Noah and the Devourer will blur.

Yes, that wasn’t the path he’d chosen, but the fact that it was there, that it so clearly laid out the potential of changing who he was, that wasn’t something he could just ignore. So he’d resisted using it. Refrained from devouring everything in sight, worried about losing himself.

But that only mattered if he was still alive. And if he lost himself, well, he’d just have to find himself again.

In that prolonged instant between life and death, Noah knew beyond any doubt that this time, Sev wasn’t going to pull his punch. He wasn’t going to overlook Noah’s draconic heart. This time he would ensure that Noah was properly destroyed or subdued.

Noah’s eyes closed, and the Devourer’s opened.
