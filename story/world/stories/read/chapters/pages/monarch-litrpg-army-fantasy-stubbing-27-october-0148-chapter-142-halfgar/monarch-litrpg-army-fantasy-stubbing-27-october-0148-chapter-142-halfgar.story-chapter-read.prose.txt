In the end, Rayne and the rest of the party stayed to watch the fire giant battle as well.

And if he had to describe it in a single word, it would be messy.

Very messy.

Rayne had fought enough monsters by now to know that taking on something like a fire giant was never simple. Even a properly trained army squad would struggle against one if they weren't prepared, and yet the arena had thrown only three fighters against it.

The moment the giant emerged from the gate, the crowd erupted into deafening cheers.

Rayne's party, however, reacted very differently. Almost everyone straightened instinctively. Their hands moved closer to weapons, eyes sharpened.

Because unlike most of the audience, they actually knew the danger of what they were looking at.

The fire giant towered over the arena floor.

It stood nearly twice the height of a grown man, its body formed from dark volcanic stone split apart by glowing cracks of molten orange. Every movement sent heat rolling across the sand. Chunks of burning rock occasionally broke away from its body and shattered against the ground, scattering droplets of lava in every direction.

Its eyes burned like two furnaces. Smoke drifted constantly from its mouth and shoulders. It was chained yet the monster looked terrifying.

Fortunately, the chains fixed around its legs and waist limited its movement considerably. Several thick iron restraints anchored it to massive metal stakes driven deep into the arena floor.

Without those chains, Rayne doubted anyone inside the arena would be feeling particularly safe. Even so, that advantage didn't seem to help the fighters very much.

The battle quickly turned into chaos.

Arrows bounced harmlessly off the giant's rocky skin. Sword strikes achieved little more than leaving shallow marks. Even the shield-bearer seemed unable to do much besides desperately avoid getting crushed.

For nearly half the fight, the three arena fighters spent more time running than actually attacking.

They darted around the giant, scattered whenever it swung its enormous arms, and constantly repositioned while looking for openings that rarely appeared.

To Rayne, it barely looked like a battle. It looked more like three chickens trying not to get stepped on.

Though, the crowd absolutely loved it.

Every near miss drew cheers. Every stumble earned laughter. Every time the giant roared, thousands of spectators screamed in excitement.

Eventually, Kesh explained why. “Most people never actually see monsters,” he said while watching the fight below. “They hear stories about them. They hear soldiers and adventurers talk about them. But they never see them.”

He continued after another round of cheers. “Most people don't travel far from cities. Why would they? Monsters live outside the walls.” He pointed toward the giant. “For most people here, just seeing one of those things is already exciting.”

After thinking about it, Rayne realized the man was right.

His own perspective had become distorted. He had spent months fighting monsters. Most citizens spent their entire lives avoiding them.

“Merchants do something similar,” Heins added from the back. “I've seen them drag smaller monsters between villages before. Charge people a little copper just to look at them.”

Rayne could easily believe that. Looking at the crowd around him, the arena was probably making a fortune.

Eventually, the battle reached its inevitable conclusion.

The giant caught the swordsman. One moment the fighter was circling behind it. The next moment he was too slow.

A massive foot slammed down, and the entire arena seemed to shake.

The crowd gasped collectively.

When the giant lifted its foot again, the swordsman wasn't standing anymore.

Before things could become worse, one of the arena mages finally intervened.

The man sprinted across the sand and launched several large spheres of ice toward the giant. The spells exploded against its chest and shoulders, forcing it backwards while arena guards rushed onto the battlefield.

Within seconds, they pulled the half-dead swordsman away and dragged him toward the healers.

That effectively ended the match. The remaining two fighters showed no interest in continuing.

Neither did the officials.

As the giant was restrained again and led away, Rayne found himself wondering why the arena didn't simply send a mage or a spellsword to fight it.

That would probably be a much better spectacle. But after thinking about it for a moment, the answer became obvious.

The tournament started soon. The arena likely wanted to save its strongest fighters for the actual event.

Besides, a powerful spellsword might accidentally kill the giant, and judging by the crowd's reaction, a living fire giant was worth far more money than a dead one.

Creatures like that were rare, especially this close to the capital.

After that, the exhibition matches came to an end, and unfortunately for Rayne, that also meant he could no longer ignore the conversation regarding being part of the tournament.

Bran clearly intended to participate, and wasn't backing down.

More importantly, he clearly expected Rayne to support him.

Normally, he would have spent at least a day thinking over such a decision. Unfortunately, he didn't have that luxury.

After the giant was led away, he learned that tournament registration would close later that evening from Bran.

Which meant if Rayne wanted to participate, he needed to make a decision now.

As if that wasn't annoying enough, Jason decided to make things even worse. Halfway through the fire giant battle, the burly soldier had become increasingly excited.

By the end of it, he had already made up his mind. “My muscles need more than training,” he declared proudly. “Let me participate too.”

That was apparently his entire reasoning.

Now Bran had support, and once Jason agreed, the old man became significantly more confident.

So when the crowd finally began dispersing and thousands of spectators started pouring out of the arena, Rayne found himself being swept along with the rest of his party.

Together, they made their way down through the stone corridors toward the lower levels of the arena.

Their destination was simple—The tournament registration office located on the ground floor.

And whether Rayne liked it or not, he needed to make a decision by then.

Honestly, it wasn't as though participating in the tournament was a bad idea.

Just a few hours ago, Rayne had been wandering through the city trying to figure out how he was going to earn more gold before eventually being sent toward the godless ruins. The tournament offered prize money, experience, and the chance to test himself against opponents he would never normally encounter.

The problem wasn't the tournament itself.

The problem was how quickly everything was happening.

If he had a choice, Rayne would have preferred at least a day to think things through properly. The format of the tournament alone needed pondering.

Three fighters on each side meant teamwork mattered just as much as individual strength. Positioning, coordination, and strategy would all play a role. A powerful fighter could still lose if his party worked poorly together, and unlike a battlefield where survival was usually the only objective, a tournament came with rules, and opponents specifically preparing for matches.

It was an entirely different kind of challenge. Still, could he really refuse when his own party member wanted him to join? Especially Bran.

“You can reject it if you want to, you know.”

Rayne glanced sideways at Bran while they continued descending through the stairs. “You're saying that after spending the last hour trying to recruit me because you want a spellsword in your party?”

Bran shrugged lightly. “I do want a spellsword in my part, but honestly, I should've talked to you about it yesterday. That would've given you time to think about it properly, but I got busy dealing with a few things in the city, and then you left the longhouse before I could find you this morning.”

Bran took a breath and continued, “Still, that doesn't mean I'm going to force you into something you don't want. You're the party leader. I'm just an archer and scout.” A grin slowly spread across the old man's face. “Besides, I'm sure Nate would be happy to fill your place.”

A loud snort immediately came from behind them.

“Fill his place and lose a few fingers?” said Nate. “No thanks. I need those for cards.”

Kesh immediately rolled his eyes. “You also need them to hold a sword and shield.”

Nate shrugged. “I don't really care about those when I'm inside a city full of guards,” he replied. “Honestly, I don't want to touch either of them until somebody forces me to.”

Jason barked out a laugh while Heins shook his head.

Rayne almost reminded the man that he still intended to drag the entire party through training while they remained in the capital, but the words never left his mouth.

Instead, his thoughts returned to the tournament and whether he actually wanted to participate.

Unfortunately, before Rayne could reach any sort of conclusion, they arrived at the registration office.

The room was much busier than he expected.

Several parties had already gathered inside, some looking like adventurers, others resembling mercenaries, or just young travellers hoping to test their luck. A long wooden counter occupied the far side of the room, and behind it sat a halfgar with a crooked nose and an expression that suggested he had already lost patience hours ago.

Despite that, the small creature worked quickly.

One party approached, answered a few questions, and was immediately waved away. Another stepped forward and spent several minutes speaking with him before their names were written down on a parchment. A third group was rejected almost instantly after a brief exchange that Rayne couldn't hear.

The process repeated over and over as the line slowly shortened.

Meanwhile, Rayne and the rest of his party waited near the edge of the room while watching people come and go. More than once he noticed nervous expressions among the applicants, which only reinforced the feeling that this tournament was far more serious than he originally assumed.

Eventually, after nearly fifteen minutes of waiting, the final group stepped away from the counter.

The halfgar looked up.

His eyes moved across Rayne's party one by one before settling on them as a whole. Then he tapped the wooden desk impatiently.

“Who are the participants? Levels? And professions? Tell me clearly!”

Before anyone answered, Bran glanced toward Rayne and raised an eyebrow.

The look alone carried the question—Well, do you want me in or not?

Rayne stared at the counter for a few seconds.

Honestly, he didn't have any time left to think anymore. The entire walk from the stands to the registration office had been spent weighing the advantages and disadvantages, and if he kept delaying, the decision would simply be made for him when registration closed.

For a brief moment, he closed his eyes. When he opened them again, the answer was already there.

Stepping forward, he placed a hand on the counter. “Rayne, Bran, and Jason,” he said.

The halfgar immediately dipped his quill into ink. “Levels and professions?”

“I'm over level thirty,” Rayne replied. “Spellsword.” Then he pointed toward the two men beside him. “Both Bran and Jason are at the first wall. Bran is an archer and scout. Jason uses an axe. We're all soldiers from the army. We're staying in the capital for a while, so we decided to participate.”

The halfgar's eyes moved across the three of them slowly. For a moment, he looked mildly skeptical.

Then he shrugged. “Fine. Just make sure you're not lying. We believe words in the arena, but if you lie about your level or profession, you can actually die out there despite all the rules.”

Rayne nodded once, then the halfgar looked directly at him.

“Can you write?”

“Yes.”

The small creature immediately pulled a parchment from beneath the counter and pushed the quill along with it across the wood.

“Fill this out.”

Rayne picked up the form and looked down. Most of it was straightforward. Name, age, profession, place of residence, party members, their information etc.

There were also several sections that needed to be marked confirming they understood the tournament rules. One paragraph in particular seemed dedicated entirely to ensuring participants couldn't blame the arena afterward.

In simpler terms, it basically said that if they got injured, crippled, or otherwise suffered during the tournament, the responsibility belonged to them.

While Rayne began filling everything out, the halfgar continued talking.

“I do believe you're soldiers,” he said. “But this tournament is going to be rough, I am telling you. More than the last few times.”

Jason immediately asked. “Why?”

The halfgar leaned back in his chair. “Because we've got some heavy hitters participating this time.”

Rayne kept writing while listening.

The halfgar scratched at his crooked nose before continuing. “Just a warning. It should be much harder than usual.”

From behind Rayne, Kesh let out a snort. “Who? You talk like the king himself is participating.”

That earned a chuckle from the halfgar. “The king's battle days are long behind him, but we've still got enough nobility joining.”

He started counting on his fingers. “Third sons. Fourth sons. Daughters looking to prove themselves. Then there's a pretty famous adventurer party that's been making a name for themselves recently.”

That already sounded troublesome enough to Rayne, but then the halfgar added one more thing.

“Oh, and there's also a party from the Crimson Guard.”

Rayne's quill stopped moving. Immediately, he lifted his head from the parchment. “Crimson Guard?”

The halfgar nodded. “Pretty surprising, right? Hear this. The firstborn son of Count Arkala is leading that party.”

The room suddenly felt a little quieter to Rayne. The halfgar continued speaking without noticing the reaction.

“I don't remember the boy's name. I have it listed here somewhere,” he admitted. “But if any of you want easy money, that's who I'd bet on.” He grinned slightly. “From what I've heard, he's good.”

Rayne stared at him for a long moment while the unfinished registration form rested beneath his hand.

The Crimson Guard, and Count Arkala's heir, that was Atticus.

Somehow, the tournament seemed a lot more interesting to him now.
