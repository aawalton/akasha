When Lee Jia and An Eui arrived back home they discovered that thankfully, the supplies that had been delivered to their dorm did include food. Though it was just rice and root vegetables, it was a luxurious feast for the starving girls. Neither of them could figure out how to activate the formation in the stove, so they broke apart the supply crate for firewood and lit it the old-fashioned way.

Once they had finished eating, Lee Jia went over the other supplies that had been provided. Aside from a few pamphlets, there was a note attached to a small raw gemstone that shone red in the light of the fire. Lee Jia marvelled at it for a moment, wondering how much it would sell for before turning to the papers and coming to a grim realization. As she hung her head in despair, An Eui looked up at her curiously.

“What’s wrong? Don’t like what they have to say?”

Lee Jia turned to An Eui with tears in her eyes.

“I have no idea, I can’t read.”

An Eui blinked once before covering her mouth and stifling a laugh.

“Pfft!”

“It’s not funny!”

After only a few minutes of teasing An Eui relented to Lee Jia’s pouting and sidled up next to her, looking over her shoulder at the pamphlets.

“Let’s see...these just look like schedules for the basic lessons and a description of the facilities on the campus. There’s a library but we’re not allowed in there, not that it would do you any good.”

Lee Jia groaned as An Eui nudged her with an elbow. An Eui continued, reaching the stone and the note.

“This last one is a message from that damn mage. Let’s see…”

An Eui read the note aloud.

Welcome to both of you. It pleases me that you’ve both chosen to stay. I know that you will not regret this decision. I hope you are getting along well, as you will need to look out for each other in this place. It is no small thing to compete with cultivators and mages as unawakened mortals, but you have already proven yourselves capable. I know you have little reason to trust me, but you are more important than you know. It is my belief that you have the greatest potential out of all the disciples gathered here. Greater even than myself or the other instructors, though I’d ask you not to repeat that to any of them. I trust that you will continue to meet and exceed my expectations.

—Magus Hwang Sung

P.S. The stone is a lesser mana stone of fire. You should be able to use it to activate the heating formation in your dorm. Simply touch the stone to the formation to activate it. That should last you until you can learn to activate it on your own. Good luck!

As An Eui finished reading the note, the girls’ gazes fell on the small pile of debris that had been the supply crate, then the fire happily crackling away in the stove, then the stone. Both of them were blushing as they exchanged a glance and wordlessly agreed never to speak of it again.

“I’m a bit surprised you know how to read, An Eui.”

Lee Jia changed the subject. It hadn’t been a common skill in the circles that Lee Jia tended to move in before coming to the academy.

“Ah, yeah. I learned it before…”

An Eui trailed off and Lee Jia couldn’t help but glance up at the brand that An Eui tried to keep mostly hidden behind her hair.

“Um, what did—I mean, how did you get exiled? If you don’t mind me asking.”

An Eui scowled as she looked away and seemed to reflexively move her hair to cover more of her face.

“I do mind you asking! I told you before to mind your own fucking business!”

An Eui crossed her arms and stood up, creating space between her and Lee Jia.

“We are not friends! You might be right about trying to learn whatever they’re teaching here, but at best that makes us allies of circumstance. You don’t know me, and for your own sake you should stop trying to!”

Before Lee Jia could respond, An Eui stormed off into the bedroom and slammed the door shut behind her. Lee Jia sighed as she slumped back into the couch—that could have gone better. Just when she thought her prickly roommate was starting to open up, too. Lee Jia stretched and looked out the window—it was still only midday and she didn’t really feel like just lazing around all day, so she decided she’d go for a walk and explore the campus a bit.

---

A boy stood with his arms outstretched, a wide grin on his face. He was older than Lee Jia—seemingly around seventeen or eighteen. He was tall and broad-shouldered, and had apparently elected to tear the sleeves off of his uniform robe, revealing his muscular arms. His long dark hair was tied into an ornamental top-knot, with the rest flowing freely down around his shoulders. He was also on fire. You’d never guess it from his easy posture and smug expression, but the flames engulfed his entire form in a blazing inferno.

Across from him, standing in a battle stance was a girl. She was even taller than her opponent, nearly six feet tall, and appeared to be around sixteen. Her sleeves—and scandalously also her skirt—had been rolled up to protect the uniform from the flames that had already scorched her arms and legs in a few places during the bout. The girl’s skin was a dark tan, and Lee Jia wasn’t sure if it was the result of a life in the sun like her own, or if it was just the girl’s natural complexion. Her brown hair was only slightly longer than Lee Jia’s, but evenly cut.

“Come now, Miss Takeda. Is this truly the best Yamato has to offer? Don’t think that keeping your distance to recover is going to work against me!”

The boy—named Yan Zhihao—formed a ball of fire in his hand as he taunted his opponent, who Lee Jia had learned was named Takeda Rika. He hurled the fire like it was a stone, and Lee Jia watched in excitement as Takeda swatted the fireball out of the air with her bare hands without even flinching.

Lee Jia had wandered around aimlessly for several hours without finding anything interesting, but a small commotion had drawn her attention here, where a group of disciples had turned a small park into a makeshift dueling ground. Lee Jia had never seen fights like this before. She’d seen fist fights and knife fights—and she had seen a few mage spells—but this was something else entirely. Neither combatant were even mages as Lee Jia knew them.

Yan Zhihao was a cultivator from Qin. Apparently some kind of nobility, he boasted that his Awakening of the Dragon’s Heart technique was one worthy of the great sects—whatever that meant. Lee Jia believed it, though—his command of fire was incredible, and he seemed to do it without any of the talismans or formations that the mages she was familiar with relied upon.

Takeda Rika, was a martial artist from Yamato. She had neglected to declare a name for her techniques, and wasn’t nearly as visually impressive as her opponent, but Lee Jia was fascinated nonetheless. Takeda’s movements were smooth and seemed to waste no movement, and she was fast—Lee Jia had only ever seen half-spirits move like that before.

There was something else to her technique as well. It was subtle, but sometimes Takeda would abruptly change direction, or catch her balance in ways that seemed impossible. Lee Jia had been paying close attention, and it seemed as if Takeda was able to occasionally push off of thin air as if it were solid.

Takeda charged forward, zig-zagging to avoid the bombardment of fiery projectiles from Yan Zhihao. As she twisted to avoid one fireball, Yan swung his other hand toward her, producing a long whip of flames to catch her while she was off balance. Takeda threw her hands down towards the ground and caught the air, vaulting off of nothing in a front flip that took her over the incoming fire whip.

She reeled back a fist as the arc of her leap sent her directly into the path of Yan Zhihao. Yan moved to dodge the incoming punch, but it had been a feint. Takeda twisted acrobatically in the air and launched a seemingly impossible heel kick directly towards her opponent’s face. Yan was barely able to catch his balance in time to lean back away from the kick, which missed him by a hair’s breadth—and sent him sprawling onto the ground anyway with a resounding crack.

Takeda was unable to press the advantage as the flaming aura reflexively lashed out at her, giving her a few more burns for her trouble. All of their previous exchanges had played out in a similar way, with Takeda briefly gaining the upper hand before being pushed back by the flame aura. Though, this was the first time Takeda had managed to land a solid blow. Yan quickly got back up on his feet, wiping a spot of blood off of the corner of his mouth with a malicious grin.

“Not bad! I’ll admit I underestimated Yamato’s techniques. That was a clever trick! But now that I have seen through it, it won’t work a second time. It’s just too bad you lacked the strength to disable me in a single blow.”

Takeda’s brow furrowed as her opponent spoke. She clenched her fists and glared at him angrily.

“Don’t you ever shut up!? Is this a duel or a debate?”

Yan’s smug grin didn’t waver, though Lee Jia thought she saw a slight twitch of the eyebrow.

“Fair point, Miss. Very well! Since you’ve shown me the full strength of your technique, it’s only fair that I return in kind. Come at me! Let’s end this in one exchange!”

Takeda didn’t need to be told twice, and was running at the boy before he had even finished speaking. For his part, Yan took it in stride, abandoning his previous strategy of bombarding her with fireballs and holding both hands out in front of his face. As Takeda approached, Yan began to inhale deeply, the aura of fire around him shrinking as she did so. Takeda’s eyes widened in surprise as she seemed to notice something. Immediately she changed the direction of her charge to avoid whatever trap Yan Zhihao had laid for her.

“Too late!”

Yan Zhihao’s shout was accompanied by a gout of flames as he exhaled. The wide conflagration covered everything in front of him, and tracked Takeda in a sustained burst. Lee Jia heard the girl scream in pain as the flames engulfed her and she collapsed to the ground.

Yan Zhihao relented his fiery assault soon after, holding out his hands and inhaling to draw the flames back to him once again. There was a brief cheer for Yan Zhihao as he bowed, and then the crowd began to disperse, the duel evidently over.

As people went back about their business—a few chatting excitedly about the duel they had just witnessed—Lee Jia’s eyes were on the defeated girl, who lay unmoving where she had collapsed. Why was nobody helping her? They were acting as if it was perfectly normal to just leave a person smouldering on the ground. Was she even still alive after that? Lee Jia released a breath she hadn’t even realized she’d been holding when she saw Takeda shakily roll over onto her back, clearly still breathing.

Lee Jia remembered what they had said earlier about the medical pavilion. The south gate? That was pretty far, and she wasn’t sure that Takeda would be able to make it there on her own. She fretted over what she should do—she wanted to keep a low profile, but this could also be an opportunity to make a friend. Not that Lee Jia was very good at making friends, if An Eui was any indication. An Eui would probably say to leave Takeda alone, but Lee Jia wasn’t sure An Eui was a person she wanted to be emulating. Or befriending for that matter, now that she thought about it.

Lee Jia made up her mind—if she was going to survive in this place, she was going to need allies. Plus, she really couldn’t bring herself to leave the girl wounded on the ground. She hopped down from the tree she had been using as a vantage point to watch the duel and approached cautiously. Most of the crowd was gone by this point, and nobody seemed to be paying her any mind. Takeda didn’t seem to notice Lee Jia’s approach—her face was twisted in pain and her breathing was labored as she trembled on the ground. There were scorch marks all over her arms, legs, and face, though surprisingly her uniform was relatively unscathed.

“A-are you ok? Is there anything I can do to help?”

Takeda cracked one eyelid open and grinned up at Lee Jia.

“Never—nng—never better…”

Lee Jia, unconvinced, knelt down to examine Takeda’s wounds.

“Do you need help? I can take you to the medical pavilion or help wrap some of these burns…”

“M’fine, don’t worry about me.”

As if to prove herself, Takeda rolled over and rose unsteadily to her feet. She attempted to take a step and nearly collapsed before Lee Jia caught her.

“Oof! Ok...m-maybe not so much. Wow, you’re a lot stronger than you look.”

Takeda looked down at Lee Jia, who was holding her up despite being nearly a foot and a half shorter. As her eyes settled on the fuzzy white cat ears, her eyes widened in understanding.

“Ah! A youkai—er, no Eunae told me. Half-spirit, right?”

“That’s right, I’m a half-spirit, my name is Lee Jia. What’s a youkai?”

While Lee Jia introduced herself, by unspoken understanding the two slowly began walking south, with Lee Jia supporting Takeda as she limped along.

“Takeda Rika. I’m not a noble so don’t worry about honorifics or whatever. A youkai is—eh, well it’s the word in my country for people like you. It’s a bit rude, though, since we use the same word for magical beasts.”

Lee Jia nodded in understanding.

“Oh, like beastkin? That’s what the Qin call us. That and a few minority groups back home, but it’s considered rude.”

Takeda shook her head.

“Not quite. It’s not really meant with any malice. Our relationship with kami and youkai is a bit different in Yamato. My roommate told me that you wouldn’t appreciate being associated with magical beasts.”

“Oh, I see.”

Lee Jia was a bit curious about that, but this didn’t seem like the best time for a conversation about cultural differences. As the pair reached the town center, Lee Jia went to proceed southward towards the medical pavilion but Takeda stopped her.

“Don’t worry about the medical pavilion, I’m not in as bad a shape as I look. Yan was holding back and martial artists’ bodies heal fast. I just need some rest and I’ll be good as new.”

Lee Jia looked up at Takeda and saw that she was indeed already looking a bit better. Her walking had grown more steady as well.

“Alright, fine. Let me get you back to the dorms at least.”

Takeda agreed and the two of them went west towards the dorms instead. With some direction from Takeda, Lee Jia soon found her house and immediately felt a bit envious. The house was nearly twice the size of Lee Jia’s dorm—it had enough room for two bedrooms and even had a private garden courtyard. As they approached, the door shot open and Lee Jia’s blood froze as she saw who came running out.

“Rika! What happened? Are you ok!?”

“I’m fine, Eunae. Just lost a duel is all. Nothing permanently damaged but my pride.”

Lee Jia trembled as Eunae—no, Seong Eunae, the very same fox princess she’d seen earlier rushed out and supported Takeda from the other side.

“Who dares? The brute! I’ll have their whole family executed!”

“Don’t joke like that Eunae! The Yan family are Qin nobility—you’d start a war if someone overheard you. Um, are you ok Lee Jia?”

Takeda had noticed something amiss when she tried to take a step forward and found that Lee Jia was frozen in place. Seong Eunae leaned forward and passed her half-lidded gaze over Lee Jia, focusing on her chin rather than her eyes. Lee Jia’s grip tightened on Takeda’s arm as she shied away from the fox girl’s gaze, and Seong averted her eyes, looking down despondently.

“Ah...I suspect that’s my fault, Rika. My family has a reputation back home and—I was a bit rude to little Jia earlier.”

“Eh? You’ve already met?”

Takeda cast a confused glance between the two girls supporting her.

“N-not really…”

Lee Jia mumbled, suddenly regretting every decision she’d ever made that led her to this moment. Seong shook her head and sighed.

“We encountered each other briefly during the initiation. I did something I shouldn’t have—a technique that I have poor control over. It was a mistake, and I apologize for it, Lee Jia.”

Seong Eunae lowered her head and Lee Jia had a brief moment of panic. A princess lowering her head to someone like Lee Jia would be the ultimate scandal. It was not the kind of attention Lee Jia could afford.

“W-w-wait no! Don’t do that! It’s fine! No harm done, everything’s forgiven, ok? Please don’t bow mist—uh, prin—”

“My name is Seong Eunae, but just Eunae is fine! I’m not my family, nor do I represent them. Here, I am just Eunae.”

Seong Eunae’s tone was urgent as she interrupted Lee Jia’s clumsy attempt to address her with an introduction.

“Now let’s get inside! Jia, I insist you join us for tea. It’s the least I can do.”

Lee Jia’s head was spinning as she was ushered inside by the other two girls. Takeda had apparently forgotten her injuries, and had to lean on Lee Jia a bit. Once inside, Lee Jia was seated at a tea table much nicer than the one in her dorm, on a much more comfortable couch.

As Seong Eunae bustled off into the kitchen—a separate room!—to prepare some tea, Lee Jia didn’t understand anything anymore. How did she end up on a given name basis with a princess? How was she being served tea by said princess? Had she fallen through some strange portal into a world where everything was reversed? Lee Jia looked up at Takeda, who was her only lifeline to normality in this bizarre situation.

“H-how did you meet Miss Seong? Do you know who she is?”

“Eunae? We were just assigned together. She’s a noble from Goryeo, right?”

“A noble!? The Seong family is—”

Lee Jia saw Seong Eunae peek around the corner behind Takeda, holding a finger up to her lips and shaking her head. A small bead of sweat formed on her brow as she tried to amend what she was going to say.

“—is...is um...famous. Like she said, they have a reputation back home.”

“Right...well she’s sweet, for a noble. A bit intense, though.”

Lee Jia couldn’t help but feel that was an understatement. A few moments later, Seong brought out a small tray with a cup of tea for each of them as well as a few pastries. She sipped her tea happily while Takeda helped herself to one of the pastries. Lee Jia just stared down at the refreshments, feeling lost.

“Please help yourself! Consider it my way of thanking you for helping Rika. Though, I have to ask—”

Seong tilted her head curiously as she regarded Lee Jia.

“Jia, how is it that an unawakened mortal found herself among the academy disciples?”
