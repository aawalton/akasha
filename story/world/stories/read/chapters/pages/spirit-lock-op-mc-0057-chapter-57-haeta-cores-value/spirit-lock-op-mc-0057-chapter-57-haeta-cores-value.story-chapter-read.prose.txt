“Don’t,” Kayline demanded.

“What? It’s gross!”

“Yeah, that’s why you shouldn’t say it!”

Lina turned to Hayden sharply. “It said, ‘No Auction Needed.’ There was an arrow pointing down toward her—”

Kayline cupped her sister’s mouth, but Hayden was already frowning.

“See?” Kayline snapped. “You ruined the mood! You should never start on a bad note!”

“What? He’s a friend.”

Kayline paused and then turned to Hayden. “We are friends, right?”

“I prefer the term teacher,” he said.

“See,” Lina said, “he’s our—what?”

“Starting today, I’ll be training you two in secret. Assuming your father said ‘yes’ to you joining the rift expedition.”

“Oh, right,” Kayline said. “He told us that it was suicidal and he’d be gravely disappointed if we agreed.”

“So we said yes,” Lina said.

Hayden smiled in amusement.

“See?” Lina said. “He’s smiling. He’s totally our friend.”

“I accept your friendship. Now back to the matter at hand. You two have special cores; I’ve rented a gym to teach you how to use them. You are not to breathe a word of it to your father. Universal leaders are now involved in my life; he has no authority to withhold secrets. Do you understand?”

“Uh, sure…” Kayline smiled wryly.

“What is it?” he asked.

“It’s just… we haven’t even thanked you for your last gift, and you’re already on the next one.”

“Yeah,” Lina said. “Galactic strike leaders are getting arrested en masse, and you’re like, Don’t get distracted by irrelevant nonsense. Focus on my profound teachings. It kinda makes me feel useless.”

“Oh, don’t worry about that. You are the key to everything. And you”—he turned to Kayline—”are the key to protecting her. If you want to thank me, take my teachings seriously—because I need you.”

Both of their eyes lit up.

“I’m in!” Lina declared.

“Anything,” Kayline said. “Without question.”

“Excellent.” He sat at the conference table and turned to Lina. “Let’s talk about your core.”

Hayden smiled and motioned for her to sit. He then summoned Gluttony and offered the spirit to her.

“What’s going on?” Lina asked.

“He’ll help. Hold him and close your eyes.”

-

Lina closed her eyes and felt Gluttony’s furry body. He was so soft and cuddly and warm. She always acted hard, but she was secretly yearning to snuggle the poor thing until the end of times.

Suddenly, Gluttony lit up with magnetic energy.

Lina opened her eyes to make sure he was okay. “What’s going on?”

“Close your eyes,” Hayden repeated.

She hesitantly complied, and soon, she felt the energy within Gluttony pulsing.

“Now activate your core.”

Hayden’s words sheathed her arms in goosebumps, and her breathing became strained. There was trauma regarding using her core, and while she could tell that her core was healed and opened by the way that cora circulated in her body, she refused to activate it.

“It’s okay,” Hayden said.

His voice calmed her, and she decided to trust him. Unlike the last time she activated her core, it didn’t well up and cause a panic. The energy streamed out of her body and into Gluttony in a smooth river, as if there were no resistance between the atmosphere and her body. Her core was also like a black hole, capturing the cora in the atmosphere in a concentrated stream that she could release with ease.

“Doesn’t feel scary anymore, does it?” Hayden mused.

“No… it feels… effortless. Like… I know this is going to sound weird, but it feels like a… slush gate opened.”

Kayline giggled. “Sluice gate?”

“Hey! It’s not like I—” As her blood pressure rose, the cora in the atmosphere warped and sucked into her core. She thought it would explode, but then Gluttony suddenly glowed, and all the cora welling within her was sucked out in an overwhelming torrent. Then, the energy in the atmosphere stilled.

“You’ll get used to it,” Hayden said.

“Are you going to explain what’s going on?” Lina demanded.

Hayden laced his fingers. “Spirits are attractors. They attract cora like magnets and expand it, allowing them to affect large sections of the world around them. The problem with spirits is they're bad at refining cora, so they’re attracted to cores to perform that task for them. That’s where we come in.”

He tapped his chest.

“You’re different. Your core is endlessly malleable and augments the attraction and refinement process. It can absorb and refine large quantities of cora before releasing it. Those advantages give you the unique ability to burn catalysts, refine them, and modify them before injecting them into another person's core.”

Lina nearly collapsed from the shock. She was expecting something special—but not that. That was far beyond what she thought was even possible!

“You can also expand gates, help with training, and do just about anything except use major spirits,” he said. “It's a giver role. Many feel cheated at first, but most come to appreciate the gift.”

“Cheated?” Lina cried. “I was just handed a ticket to pedicures and fancy dresses!”

Kayline cocked a brow. “I thought you hated both of those.”

“I do. But I love having the option to spend frivolously on expensive things I don't even care about. Plus, to have a job where people at least pretend to like me? I'll take it.”

Kayline turned to Hayden. “As much as I trust you… you aren’t just getting her hopes up, are you? It’d be cruel if you were.”

Hayden tongued his cheek. “Do you know why it’s difficult to control the size and aggressiveness of your attacks?”

Kayline’s eyes trembled when Hayden pointed out her control issues. “Who told you that?”

“I didn’t need to hear it. Your body is releasing the Halva particle that breaks raw cora into fuel that magnifies active arts. You also cannot control it, which indicates that you have a natural Halva core. Your family truly has remarkable genes.”

Kayline blushed furiously when she heard that she had good genes. She had long given up on dating Hayden, but hearing she had good genes ignited a fleeting flurry of passion within her. So she blushed, then admonished herself when she saw Hayden’s lifeless expression.

“Ummm…” She chuckled darkly when she found herself tongue-tied. “You’re right. I struggle with that. Does that mean you can… help me control it?”

“Indeed I can.”

Lina stepped forward. “What about you?”

Hayden turned to her. “What do you mean?”

“What core do you have?”

A rare smile crept on Hayden’s lips. “I have none. My core’s as normal as it comes.”

“That’s hard to believe,” Lina said bluntly. “In fact, I don’t believe it.”

“If you don’t believe that, the full truth would break you.” He pulled out a box. “Now let’s begin.”

He opened the top, and intense wind pressure crashed into the room.

“What’s that?” Kayline cried.

“A wind catalyst,” Hayden said. “Your universe mislabeled this a ‘Rank 4’ catalyst.”

“Mislabeled?” Lina scoffed. “It’s warping the air!”

“Calm yourself.” Hayden touched the stone, and the wind pressure disappeared. You can always lower the transformative pressure of a catalyst to your ideal point. The only reason that we’re using this one is because it also happens to be extremely flexible. So keep focused. We need to burn this to your core.”

“W-Wait. You’re seriously giving me that?”

“In exchange for your core, yes,” Hayden said. “By using your core, all three of us can burn it.”

-

Lina held her breath when the sheer gravity of her situation sank in. She was about to duplicate a hundred million credit catalyst for three people. Even though she was just told that, she didn’t truly believe it until that moment.

“There isn’t much time,” Hayden said to her. “So if you would kindly refrain from treating me as an altruist and perform your duties, I would appreciate it.”

He handed her the catalyst, and gluttony glowed, tempering its power as he crawled onto her shoulder. Hayden motioned to the floor.

“Sure…” Lina whispered. She sat, allowing Hayden to put his palms on her back.

“Don’t worry,” Hayden said. “Gluttony and I will aid you.”

“But what if I mess it up?”

“Your core will heal. Now close your eyes and we’ll begin.”
