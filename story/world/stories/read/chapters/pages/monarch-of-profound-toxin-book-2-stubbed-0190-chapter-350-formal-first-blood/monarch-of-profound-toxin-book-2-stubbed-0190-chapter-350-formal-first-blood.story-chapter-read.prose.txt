Aiming to set a clear and graphic example, Eik didn’t bother to seal up the wound as warm, viscous arterial blood sprayed into the air from the severed neck.

Like an amateur butcher, he was bathed in it, the now headless body of Hromlung still held high in a steel grip. As it turned out, the bigger a body was, the more blood it contained. And, boy, this guy had been a big’un. The flow of blood just seemed to never cease.

Twitching slightly in death, the corpse slowly stopped spurting its life blood as the onlookers gaped at the scene. Although they had all heard him loudly and clearly when he had promised to show no mercy if there were resistance to his demands, it somehow still took most people by surprise that he made good on that promise.

“I… No— Excuse me, Mr. Magnasen!” Imkoh squawked, his feet refusing completely to carry him even a single step closer to the murderous Supreme Divinity of Toxin. “I’m q-quite certain that you very explicitly promised us to not… kill anybody today!”

Eik grimaced at that. He probably had actually promised them that, but the day where he had visited their offices and gotten this whole thing started had been filled with an endless ocean of legal documents, stipulations, paragraphs, and whatever else, and, if he had to be totally honest about it, he had skimmed most of it at best.

It had become so normal to have Robert there to vet that kind of stuff before he signed it, but he just hadn’t been able to bring himself to force such a massive responsibility onto the young man’s shoulders. Not that he doubted for even a second that the man would have been capable of dealing with it. He was unbelievably skilled at his job, but… it would have been cruel.

He had read the stuff written in bold at least, and somewhere in the depths of his memory, there probably was a recollection of a no killing-thing as well. Well, what’s done was done.

“Sorry, Mr. Imkoh. Probably won’t happen again,” he said sheepishly.

“Probably?” the immaculately dressed man repeated. “Please see to it that it doesn’t!”

“Second Armada, First Seat Hromlung, sir!” one of the other Gohkamorians yelled, his blade sliding free of its sheath as he prepared for combat. “Murderer! You will not get away with this!”

“No, you misunderstand,” Eik told him calmly. “What is happening here is simply you not getting away with it. It’s been a long time coming.”

This Gohkamorian was S-ranked as well, albeit significantly lower on the ladder of power than First Seat Hromlung had been.

That didn’t bode well for his chances in battle, considering the resistance his superior had been able to put up against Eik—that was, none.

In that same moment, a cheer rang out from a group of Earthlings further back. It sounded more like a war cry than anything else. It was an acknowledgment of the beginning of Earth’s vengeance. For the first time, the blood of humanity’s fury had been shed in the most official of manners.

Within a few seconds, a handful more voices joined in, celebrating the Gohkamorian death. Then more. And soon, a considerable chunk of the crowd had expressed their support for the execution.

Not even all of them were from Earth. Whether it was because they were people who had developed personal relationships with humans from Forest, because they already held a grudge against the large aliens, or because Eik’s rousing words had simply gripped them so solidly, it was a heart-galloping sight.

“More! More!” came the shouts. This whole thing was becoming more chaotic by the second. “Kill them all!” another roared.

“Don’t let any of them leave!”

Even though a fair number of people had been whipped up into a good ol’ medieval, rotten tomatoes-throwing, calling for a hanging kind of energy, most were still clearly either on the fence or firmly on the side of not preferring any more executions.

Among them were the Gohkamorians.

“If we are going to be put to death here, then I, for one, will go down with my weapon raised to strike!”

“Agreed,” another Gohkamorian chorused. This one was an A-ranker and wielded a massive staff of more than three meters. Each end of the weapon culminated in a thick, polished ball of metal made to perfectly smash bones and skulls. “If I’m going down, I will make sure I don’t go alone.”

With that, he leaped through the air to the nearest person cheering for their downfall, but before he had reached the peak of his trajectory, a sharp crack echoed from inside his head and he tumbled to the ground in a lifeless heap of limbs.

Eik held up a finger upon the tip of which the tiniest of Living Manifestations was perched. Together they watched a thin streak of blood flow out of the fallen Gokamorian’s ear. Eik nodded with satisfaction.

“That was… surprisingly effective. But with someone as weak as an A-ranker, we could probably have gone even smaller than that and still achieved just as good of a result. Don’t you think?”

The Profound Toxic beast wiggled around in enthusiastic agreement.

“You bastard!” the other S-ranked Gohkamorian shouted as he lunged forward, blade trained expertly at Eik’s throat.

Moving at lightning speed, Eik slapped the cheek of the heavy blade away as if it had been made of neatly folded cardboard. With a burst of even greater speed, he stepped in close, through the S-ranker’s guard, and stabbed a small needle of blue crystal through the large man’s plate armor.

Effortlessly reinforced beyond even the most powerfully crafted pieces of armor, the needle, as thin as the prong of a dinner fork, pierced deeply before disintegrating into liquid Profound Toxin and flowing gleefully into the foreign system.

Eik didn’t bother with him and instead dove under and past his flailing arms as he collapsed from the dose of death.

Another of the S-rankers was making his move, a heavy bow of metal already pulled back fully with an arrow nocked on the string that looked more like a ballista bolt than anything else. This chaos had gone far enough.

The Gohkamorians weren’t doing their reputation any favors by outright attacking those who had turned against them so blatantly, but this could just as easily be turned into a direct result of Eik’s recklessness. He had backed them into a corner, killed one of them in cold blood, and thus forced the rest of them to lash out.

But he had to cut it short while it was still fun. Even if it might help turn public ire against the Gohkamorians, he didn’t actually want some poor sod in the crowd to sacrifice their life for it. He would make sure those gigantic assholes paid the price regardless.

Swift liquid Profound Toxin sprung forth from everywhere and instantly enveloped each and every Gohkamorian present with the exception of those further back who hadn’t been a part of the high-level retinue sent to receive Eik’s demands and hadn’t joined the hostilities. Although the majority of the targets had been Earthlings, none of them had actually gotten hurt.

“All right, I think that’s enough. I didn’t actually mean for this whole thing to get out of hand like this,” Eik spoke with amplified voice, not sure himself if that was necessarily true or not. With a calculating gaze, he looked at each of the imprisoned Gohkamorians one by one. “I am going to let you free now.”

One of the strongest of them gnashed his teeth, his muscles twitching as the immovable crystal melted away from his body. Immediately, his fingers went for his weapon again, but the spiritual pressure of Eik’s aura crushed him before he could make any move.

“And… Should there be anybody dumb enough to try to draw their weapons again or use any kind of offensive skill on the crowd, I will, of course, make sure you don’t live to see the sun set tonight.”

“You can’t do this!” the S-ranked alien shouted, a powerful stomp of impotent rage sending soil and chairs flying outward. He was desperate. Feeling an inevitable death sneaking up slowly but surely.

“No, once again, that’s where you’re wrong,” Eik said with a sigh. “You see, I can do absolutely anything I please. Now, I don’t normally walk around killing passersby at random, but that is entirely due to my exceedingly kind nature and zero desire to stand on a mountain of corpses just to feed a severely warped sense of ego and self-worth, but…” He pursed his lips as his train of thought seemed to wander.

All was silent as he thought.

“No, you know what?” he continued, a note of malevolence creeping into his voice. “If you didn’t want to get murdered by me, you shouldn’t have murdered my family, friends, and fellow humans! It’s your own fucking fault, and you should not be cursing anybody but yourselves!”

“Wh—”

“You thought you could just do whatever the hell you wanted because we were a fresh, weak world, didn’t you? That there would be no consequences because you could sweep it under the rug with influence and threats? Well, ta-fucking-da! I could annihilate the lot of you between breakfast and lunch and there’s not a single thing you can do about it! The Unified Mass is all about being strongest, isn’t it?” he spat as he lost control of his emotions and a sliver of malicious aura escaped.

“Well, I am the motherfucking Monarch!”
