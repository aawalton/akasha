Brant Borisa was furious. His son had made a mockery of the Borisa Corporation by getting suspended from Junolyx, and Brant didn’t trust that he would pass the harder reentrance exam. So he sent Rosen to a nearby remedial academy as a back door into Junolyx. All Rosen had to do was win the martial arts tournament, and he would get back in. That would be easy, right? Rosen had five years of martial arts experience, a C-rank spirit, and a powerful catalyst. Brant thought it was impossible for Rosen to lose the tournament to a podunk charity case.

He was wrong.

He couldn't be more wrong.

In fact, he was so wrong that his son was not only claiming he would lose, but that he was also the subject of a family-damaging video.

Brant listened to his son ramble for a solid two minutes before saying, “Fine! I get it. I’ll watch the damn video. Keep your link on. If I call, I don’t want the tone to sound twice.” He hung up and opened the video Rosen sent him.

It started immature, so Brant picked up his fork and started spinning it back and forth in his pasta. Not thirty seconds later, he dropped the fork with a loud clank. A waiter rushed over to ask if he was alright, but Brant barely even noticed the man. He couldn’t take his eyes off the video.

The story was exquisite—professionally edited propaganda.

Whoever ordered the video had an agenda. After all, one didn’t casually make the daughter of a Golden Belt trade commissioner the damsel of a harassment story unless they wanted to get someone arrested.

Brant called his son with lethal intent in his eyes. “Is this true?”

“It’s not. I bullied Hayden—not Kayline. She called me out for barring her sister’s path and I just said that I wasn’t.”

“Don’t lie to me.”

“I’m not lying to you. You can read my lips in the video. In fact, I was best friends with Lina until Kayline showed up. I wouldn’t be surprised if Kayline made this. I mean, this uses Lina's POV footage.”

“Why would they do this?”

“I don’t know, but I think Kayline has a thing for Hayden. It's complicated…”

Brant picked up his wine glass, paused, and put it down. “What have you done about it? Anything?”

“I did. I bribed the school into null-locking Hayden with a spirit beast. But even that seems like a mistake.”

“He has a spirit beast?”

“Yeah.”

“And he hasn’t fed it?”

“It wouldn’t matter. Hayden has a clear beast—it’s worthless. But that’s not the problem. The problem’s that the martial tournament doesn't use spirits.”

“What about the spirit competition?”

Rosen looked away.

“Speak,” Brant demanded.

Rosen looked back sheepishly. “My spirit’s not functioning correctly. It's clearly a mismatch. If you get me another spirit—”

“Absolutely not. It's too late, and I refuse to invest more into a delinquent who doesn't know how to burn his core correctly.”

Rosen's face burned with shame.

“That said, I will help you.”

Rosen looked up with hope.

“You did good by null-locking him. I can work with that.”

“What about the video?”

“You'll have to bear the shame. You should be thankful Ritrix is reasonable. If the raw footage proves your innocence, he'll scold his daughters instead.”

Rosen looked at the ground like a worthless rat.

“That said,” Brant continued, “we can't let this kid into Junolyx. If he gets famous it'll be a disaster. So I'll take care of it. Until then, I expect you to get up and shave. I refuse to let my son wallow like a fresh-cut eunuch.”

Brant cut the line and called Loricks Raskin’s private link.

“Hello Loricks. Yes, I apologize for calling so late, but… I really don't have a choice. The tournament’s tomorrow and I've just obtained evidence that one of my son's competitors is taking performance-enhancing drugs. Yes, I'm certain. I’m demanding a full investigation.”

Lina had just left the dressing room when she ran into a group of women waiting for her. One said, “Just ask her,” and Lina rolled her eyes and walked up to them. “Do you need something?”

The leader, an annoying gossip queen named Renly, grinned. “We…” she nudged her head to the others, “were just wondering if your sister’s dating Hayden.”

Lina had a prepared answer for every question—except that one, apparently. Her eyes shot open, and she blurted, “What? No. Where’d you get that impression?”

“It’s just… that video—”

“Has nothing to do with reality,” Lina interjected. “Rosen only talked to Kay once, and she was calling us out for being mean to Hayden.”

“Wait, you were there? Does that mean you’re the one who took the video?”

A cool chill brushed against Lina’s spine. “What?”

“The video. Don't tell me you haven't watched it.”

Lina hadn't. She had been heckled for bullying Hayden since the video came out, so she avoided it. Now she had a real bad feeling.

Lina pulled up the video and watched. It wasn't ten seconds before she saw the footage. It was her POV—and she knew how Kay had got it.

Hey. Didn't you record that altercation between Hayden and Rosen?

Yeah, I had to. Hayden was late, so Loricks wanted to make sure nothing bad happened to him.

Interesting… So does that mean you recorded the moment he woke up?

If you're asking for my POV—no. I'm not going to give it to you.

Lina snapped back to the present. Did she actually make that video? Why?

Renly saw Lina's puzzled expression—and pounced. “Oooh~! She totally made it!”

“Of course not!” Lina snapped. “She probably gave it to—”

“Lina!” Kay turned the hall’s corner and stormed up to her. Lina was nervous by the serious look in her eyes. It was so grave that Lina expected her to say their father was in an accident. What she said instead left her baffled.

“You know Rosen, right? Like more than the other day?”

Lina blushed and averted Renly’s piercing gaze. “Yeah, we used to talk, yeah.”

“Yeah? Well tell that pencil dick that if he returns to Junolyx, I’m going to hang him on the front gate by his testicles.”

Lina cringed when she saw Renly's bracelet light up with red light. She grabbed Kay's hand and dragged her into the locker room—the one place no one would dare to record.

“What’s going on?” Lina hissed, blocking the door with her back to prevent Renly's group from getting in.

“What’s going on?” Kay laughed. “That’s what’s going on.” She thrust a staff notice into Lina’s hands. The contents explained Kay's reaction—and then some.

Lina read and reread the notice a dozen times. “People are going to be pissed…” she whispered.

Lina was right—people would be pissed about what was happening—but Hayden wouldn't be one of them. He knew what was coming the moment he was called into Loricks’ office, and he honestly couldn't care less.

Loricks did care, so he stared at Hayden with pained eyes.

“I should tell you this before the news gets out… A certain video of you has circulated, and it’s made someone very powerful… very nervous.”

Hayden didn’t react.

Loricks grimaced. “As a result, they have filed three lawsuits to have you investigated for doping before the tournament, and an injunction on the video's creation and circulation. Normally, we would just test you and be done with it, but this person has a legal department as large as this school's population pushing it through the courts. As a result…”

Hayden remained expressionless. “Yes?”

“As a result, we needed to compromise. You can still participate, but your competitors will be able to use their spirits. I know it’s unfair, I really do, but—”

“It’s fine,” Hayden said. “If you need to compromise further, that’s fine, too. Is there anything else?”

Loricks blinked in surprise. “Uh… no.”

“Okay. Thank you for notifying me.” Hayden stood, neatly pushed in his chair, and opened the door.

“Wait.”
