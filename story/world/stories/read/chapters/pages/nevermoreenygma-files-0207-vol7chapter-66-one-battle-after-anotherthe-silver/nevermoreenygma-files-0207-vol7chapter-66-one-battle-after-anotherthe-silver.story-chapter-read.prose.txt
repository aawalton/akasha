Part 3

The situation in the Xinjiang region had deteriorated rapidly due to waves of fractus coming from the Tibetan mountains and territories near the borders with Kazakhstan and Pakistan. Reports indicated that the creatures fed on the area’s magical energy and the residual energies of ancient lost cities scattered throughout the region.

This had already been discovered, and in some ways it connected to something Mai had witnessed herself with that fire elemental in the Andes. Ley Line magic energy, even if it weren't correct to call it energy, as well as electricity from power plants, could serve as sustenance—a way for some fractus types to gain cohesion in three dimensions, though concentrated electrical energy remained their weakness.

Excess of anything is harmful, or as the old saying went: “From abundant feasts, the graves are full.” Even the fractus seemed to follow that rule.

The Chinese government had requested international assistance to contain the expansion, as local military forces could not handle the intensity of the waves.

And so Mai and her squad were deployed to a company to the desert region of Taklamakan, where the fractus had taken over the ancient city of Khotan, a historic Silk Road hub. Upon arrival, they found ruins mostly buried under sand dunes and an atmosphere dense with a strange, almost tangible peculiarity. One of the mages on the team tested the ley line map, discovering discrepancies with current readings.

No one liked the implication. Now that the hidden world had been revealed, everyone knew the magical and spiritual energy layer of the Earth—the Ley Lines. And these lines didn’t shift suddenly unless a Dark Event occurred, as Mai had experienced during her time in Japan as a rookie investigator and assistant to Miho.

On the other hand, Ley Lines in arid and desert areas were stable compared to forested areas and areas with greater life activity in general, simply because they were thinner—unless they were specific points where nomadic tribes in ancient times had used that location as a focal point for magic. Such points were known in academic occult circles as “omphalos,” although the term used to describe them varied by region.

As they advanced through the remains of temples and city fortifications, the feeling of walking over cursed land intensified. It was as if the city itself were alive, waiting. Using her echolocation along Wizard Detection Systems, Mai detected several hotspots, coming from fractus that had infiltrated the ancient underground structures.

The ensuing battles were short, but exhausting. Mai guided her squad through a zone infested with jellyfish-like fractus that moved through the air. They were easy to destroy, even with conventional ammunition, but it was better not to touch living tissue directly—something a soldier learned the hard way when a suddenly appearing creature took part of his forearm, leaving the hand hanging by tissue and bloodied nerves. A mage provided first aid, but the soldier had to be evacuated immediately, which they did.

The fights were a little different because, compared to the frequent use of firearms, they had to rely on close-quarters combat techniques. For months, various armies around the world had incorporated the use of electric batons to deliver electric shocks into their combat techniques. While one could say it was similar to an electric baton, the truth was that many units had adapted traditional martial arts techniques involving sticks and staffs to combat the small-sized Fractus. Nevermore on Siren Island had long since integrated stick fighting and the Canarian tolete and garrote into their training, but now, for those familiar with that style of combat, they had to use a weapon that could change in size from about fifty centimeters to two meters.

Mai, for her part, knew how to fight with staffs thanks to Miho. Despite her blindness, Miho had been a specialist in this type of combat, using Bojutsu, Jodo, and Tanjo. More than once, Mai had taken Miho’s attacks head-on, but she had learned from them. Later, in Whisper, she had also been introduced to martial arts during her training period in the mountains. Although she hadn’t used that style of combat very much in the real world, the truth was that it proved useful in the battle against the Fractus, even though so much time had passed.

Now, as the squads explored the city, everyone had to carry special folding staffs to pull out in an emergency if they found themselves surrounded.

The most disturbing discovery came later when they passed a semi-ruined building they had previously overlooked. Inside there was a large underground chamber filled with unknown arcane symbols. A massive fractus had merged with the area’s ancient magic, becoming a gigantic, bulbous monster. It attacked them suddenly—accompanied by hundreds of the smaller jellyfish-like fractus.

The fight was exhausting. Mai had to rely heavily on her echolocation to detect movements through walls and floors, pushing her abilities to the limit. While everyone fought the large fractus with electric weapons, they also had to protect themselves from the smaller ones with short shots of batons if they got too close.

After many hours, the squads managed to bring down the creature, and Mai ended up with a terrible headache. Fighting in areas with chambers and tunnels, while using echolocation, was quite complicated, because of the sound interference from her own squad and the echoes, which created a hellish symphony in her head.

Amid the battle’s remains, they found evidence suggesting the fractus had been drawn by a force stronger than mere invasion, as if something had directed them there. The answer to the mysterious symbols might lie in Dark Events altering the ley lines to create points of attraction. The question was, who was crazy enough to do that?

In other parts of the world, similar things had happened in the past, with unknown groups altering Ley Lines to create Ley Line harvesting sites. But doing that for the fractus in such a remote place was tantamount to feeding them.

Reinforcements arrived, and Mai’s team survived, yet the questions remained unanswered. Something unsettling lingered in the air too: What if the Fractus had originally entered Earth through the Ley Line network itself, after someone had deliberately prepared it to receive them?

By mid-June, the Fractus conflict had spread beyond major cities, reaching refugee camps in Manchuria. The waves of creatures attacked not only military forces but also the most vulnerable populations—those displaced by war.

In a humanitarian mission that later turned into a battlefield, Mai was sent to secure the safety of civilians in the evacuation camps.

The area was completely overwhelmed, with hundreds of thousands seeking refuge. Mai and her squad were deployed to establish a safe zone on the outskirts of Harbin, but what they found upon arrival was terrifying. The fractus weren’t just attacking from the borders—they were infiltrating among the refugees themselves, using them as bait to lure military forces into ambushes. The creatures disguised themselves between people, a tactic that had confounded allied forces.

But the truth was that, if one applied historical experience to this new enemy, this was yet another consequence of war—and it took the form of a plague. Just as fleas and lice had done in earlier eras, the fractus also had their tiny counterpart, with the difference that instead of causing itching, they used their size to invade people’s bodies.

After seeing some strange movements in the camp and some soldiers Mai and other captains, not fully trusting the defensive lines, ventured into the heart of the camp to search for the cause of erratic behavior. She used her echolocation to detect movements invisible to the naked eye.

As she moved through old industrial facilities converted into makeshift shelters, the weight of the horror became more apparent: the fractus weren’t just attacking humans—they were manipulating those near their power using their size.

They found several refugees whose bodies had been taken over by small fractus entities, controlling them from within. It wasn't that they controlled the person by pretending to be human, the control was more subtle. The person believed they were in control of their consciousness, but having been infected, they were receiving involuntary nerve impulses that guided them toward the fractus and, with that, also toward those who were not infected. More than a control mechanism, it was an ability of the species to expand and attract and then expand again.

There had been reports of parasites before, but this was the first time Mai witnessed it herself—or rather, through her extra sense using echolocation.

Mai and the squad began "clearing" rounds, capturing the infected by the fractus and those that had not yet fully transformed. As they fought the creatures, Mai felt her morale erode. Seeing terrified and confused civilians—some of whom had become unwitting victims of the fractus—was one of the most harrowing sights she had ever witnessed.

The horror stemmed from the fact that people didn't know why they were suddenly being singled out and subjected to interrogation. Fractus lacked consciousness like humans do, and so simple questions were enough to distinguish those who had been infected. That type of fractus was weak unless it found a host, entering through the respiratory tract. Once it reached the nervous system, it took up residence, consuming almost no energy from the host to keep it alive, but through the invasion it could send signals to increase the number of infected individuals.

This was achieved by sending weak signals that the human brain interpreted in its own way, thus turning people into bait to attract others to areas where the fractus were most prevalent.

The solution came when the local laboratory identified the resonance needed to destroy them, by wearing masks for several days and creating static in the air, they managed to eliminate them all.

Since they were extremely small, they required very little energy to maintain their three-dimensional cohesion. By isolating the patients with protective masks and saturating the surrounding air with controlled electrostatic discharges, the parasites gradually lost their stability and disintegrated without causing significant harm to their hosts. They were so weak and small that not even a nucleus could be detected upon their elimination. This process became known as the Electrostatic Decontamination Protocol.

And finally and the area was secured.

Mai had nightmares like everyone else, but from that day on, they intensified.

The thought that something could get into her nose while she slept terrified her, and for a while, she and the other soldiers could only sleep in rooms equipped with masks and EDP devices.It was nice, even though it woke her up the next day with her hair all messed up from static electricity.

The last mission of August took Mai and her squad back to the arid plains of Mongolia, where a series of strange rumors had been reported near the border with Russia.

They were there for other reasons: due to three enormous ten-meter-tall fractus that they had already destroyed, but this unusual investigation caught Mai’s attention just by coincidence.

Reports, relayed by someone found dying by border forces, indicated that the fractus weren’t the only enemies there—they were being used as pieces in a something else. The border guards thought the survivor was insane, his legs were completely destroyed from walking barefoot for days.

Things changed when mysterious corpses began appearing across various parts of the desert. Some showed evidence of fractium cores inside their bodies, but these were different from the parasitic or tick-like types. Investigations suggested that someone had created a clandestine fractus research network, or was performing experiments. All those found dying offered no concrete clues, as if wandering aimlessly through the desert for days and nights had driven them mad. Those they found were often already living corpses, walking barefoot and naked through the desert, the environment and dehydration had done the rest. But how had they ended up there in scattered points?

By chance, the special road guard found one of those ghosts wandering in the desert and brought her to a base where a psychic esper read her mind. Through the woman’s memories, they confirmed the feared truth: something that only could be described as a cult worshiping the fractus had a base in the middle of the desert.

Using drones, visions and network data, they triangulated the location.

Although Mai’s squadron had already received orders to return east, they decided to help since they had one more day. Headquarters had been ordered only to monitor the situation involving these people appearing in the desert, but it could take a couple of days for a team to arrive from the nearby front lines to capture that strange group. The desert was too vast, and that group could vanish right under their noses if they realized they’d already been spotted.

After several hours of traveling in the camo vehicles Mai’s squad arrived at an old abandoned mine in the western part of the desert, where the fractus were believed to be stored and controlled by a network of occult dark magic. Upon arrival and establishing surveillance, the air was strange, charged with static electricity. Traces of magic similar to the one found in Khotan were found by the mages, and Mai could feel the vibration in the air of something else. The fact that they were guarding a mine already made everyone raise an eyebrow. The only thing left was to find those jellyfish-like creatures again.

Every thirty minutes, a man dressed in black would appear at the entrance. After looking around and smoking a cigarette, he would go back inside. This pattern repeated itself three times until sunset.

At night they entered the place with their invisible cloaks on when the surveillance period began and they saw the man enter.

As they moved through the collapsed mine structures, the horror of the place became apparent. Inside were men and women dressed simply in black. Most of them wore black pants and plain T-shirts, and only a few of them had guns in their belts.

But it wasn't the weapons that really alarmed them.

Small, mouse-sized fractus were being destroyed, Others did indeed belong to the jellyfish type.

They were being held in an enclosure resembling small Faraday cages, and their cores extracted to be inserted into people—a training ground for mutated creatures. Under the control of an unknown group of magicians, they were being prepared for something. There were at least five in an induced coma, possibly those who were going to have the nucleus implanted. They had maps with various markings all over the globe, but no clue what it meant.

They wanted to keep watching to make sure they captured them at the right moment, so the squad split up among its members. There were a few more people than just the squad, but the weapons they saw weren’t sophisticated—they were rather conventional. It was while they were conducting this investigation to secure the area that they were discovered. The invisibility devices always caused some issues, and this could result in certain parts of what the suits were supposed to render not aligning with the lighting levels or the opacity of reality; and as they passed through some of the lights illuminating the area, they were detected.

The scythe-wielder tried to strike Mai, and she was about to fire when he suddenly stopped when he saw her. At the beginning of 2097, Mai, with a new team, was in the vast, frozen Russian steppe, in the Astrakhan region, where waves of fractus had begun to stalk the borders.

Russian forces had been overwhelmed by the speed of the attacks. These fractus seemed better adapted to extreme conditions, and the ground troops could not cope with the massive waves as expected.

Smaller ones tended to appear in warmer or temperate areas, larger ones were typically found in the cold, with few exceptions.

Mai was assigned to coordinate a containment operation at an outpost near the Volga River.

Upon reaching the front, the first thing she noticed was the cutting wind blowing from the east, covering the horizon with a thick layer of snow. The military bases were built to withstand winter, but the threat this time was different: the fractus attacked not only in large numbers, but their ability to adapt to extreme weather gave them an impressive tactical advantage.

Mai squad quickly moved into the front line, using her echolocation to detect small movements beneath the snow. She didn’t need to be told where they were—she could sense them through layers of terrain, like vibrations in the air.

The entire team, followed by three others, took up positions to set up an outpost and wait for reinforcements in a few hours to secure the position. In the distance, they could hear a murmuring sound that reminded them all of the sound of whales.

Those massive fractus, as large as elephants, moved slowly and made no sound, but apparently this was a phenomenon that occurred when the wind passed through them, creating a deep sound that echoed throughout the surrounding hills and valleys. Under those conditions, it could be useful, since even the drones lost visibility in the snowstorms.

For several hours, that sound signaled when the fractus were approaching. Their slowness was a good thing—unless they attacked in a group.

That night, as the military forces prepared for the worst, a gigantic creature emerged from the darkness—a beast with dark, glowing red eyes. It moved with terrifying speed, trampling everything in its path.

Trampling?

Its attack was swift and devastating, ramming the Russian front line, which was overwhelmed—but no one died. Mai’s squad was the first to act, leading a direct offensive against the creature. Mai guided her teammates, who managed to flank it and launch simultaneous attacks.

Nothing. The force of its wings sent them flying meters into the air.

That thing didn't look like a fractus at all. They kept that in mind when one of those fractus emerged from among the trees and charged at the creature. The black creature flapped its wings, and that fractus was sent flying through the air as if it were nothing more than a stone thrown into the sky by a child.

Then it rose, and Mai froze.

It was looking at her. Or at least, that’s how it seemed.

After a few seconds, it sped away. No casualties. Simple attack.

No. That was not a fractus.

Mai had to write the report, still a little shaken by those red eyes.

It was a legend of the occult world she had seen many times in books, reports, and stories. And also, in some strange way, familiar to her. Because one of Mai's nicknames was a sort of parody of that creature that had been mentioned so many times along the way.

But now, seeing it with her own eyes had left her paralyzed.

She had seen the Mothman.

It would have been just an anecdote like thousands of others in that war, if not for one detail: that creature was said to appear as a harbinger of catastrophes—either where it appeared or to those who saw it.
