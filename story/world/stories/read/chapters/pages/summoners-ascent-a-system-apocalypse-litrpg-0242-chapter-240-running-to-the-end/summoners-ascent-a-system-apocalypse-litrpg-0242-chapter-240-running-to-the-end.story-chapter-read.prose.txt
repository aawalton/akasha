Ashton straightened his tie, leaning against the wall next to the camp's exit. He was waiting for someone, since they were about to go out on an outing. The sun was supposedly setting right now, and they had said to meet around this time to leave.

Though he knew why his friend was taking her time; the reason they were about to head out wasn't something anyone would be exactly excited about.

But it still didn't take long for Clementine to step into Ashton's channelling range anyway. She was wearing different clothes, darker and more suitable for stealth, and was finally carrying the knife Ashton gave her in a sheath by her hip.

Not that Ashton wanted to really let her use it today, but that was beside the point. He was glad that she seemed prepared for all possibilities.

"Are you ready to go?" Ashton asked, smiling at her.

"Yes." Clementine's nod and expression couldn't contradict her more if she tried.

"It's going to be okay. I promise," Ashton put his hand onto Clementine's back. "You don't have to come if you don't want to, I can take care of it on my own."

Clementine hesitated, grinding her teeth. She stared at the metal door in front of the two of them, and opened her mouth, like what she was trying to say slipped from her mind at the last second.

With a deep breath, Clementine centred herself, and shook her head.

"No. This needs to happen," Clementine finally said, and approached the doors. As the pair stepped outside, Ashton already called on Poppy and the lesser sylph.

"Are you ready?" he asked, clicking his finger as a signal to have Poppy cast her spell on Clementine. But she looked down at herself, confused.

"Wait, you're using the speed boost on me? But running is like my whole thing," Clementine pointed out. "I feel like you should use it on yourself, otherwise I'll just completely leave you in the dust."

Ashton let out a hearty laugh. "Just humour me for now, alright? And then we can see later which of us needs the buff."

Clementine shrugged. "If you say so. But don't get upset later."

"You think I'd get upset over running slower than you? What, you don't remember all the races you forced me into when we were kids?"

"Sure, but that's when we were kids. Now you're the big, bad, powerful Ash that came back from another world," Clementine pointed out with her arms crossed.

"Fair enough. But come on, let's just get moving for now, I want to get close to the cathedral before the sun is fully down." As Ashton spoke, he channelled his internal energy through his body to focus entirely on running, at least for now. He could relax a little after a while, once Clementine saw that she wasn't as superior a runner as she thought.

Then again, it wasn't like they were going to sprint all the way to the cathedral.

"Are you ready?" Ashton asked, one last time, as he looked at his friend. And she nodded, a little more determined this time around. "Then let's go."

Ashton helped the two of them out with a gust of wind pushing against their back. It wasn't really necessary; there wasn't too much time pressure, as long as they managed to finish this tonight. But he really just wanted to get Clementine's feet moving before she had time to overthink it again.

And so, the two started moving, running alongside each other. For a while, they were keeping at the same pace, though Clementine couldn't help but look down at Ashton's legs.

"How the hell is your running form so good?" she asked, not hiding her surprise.

"What do you think?" Ashton scoffed. "When you've got a memory like mine and a century to practice, you can surprisingly pick up a lot."

Clementine stared at Ashton, narrowing her eyes. "You were one of the most clumsy people that I have ever met, to the point where we took you to the hospital because we were worried you had issues with your inner ear at some point."

Ashton cleared his throat. "Well... I learned how to not trip over everything surprisingly fast. But yeah, no, Oka made me improve my running form. They taught me to prove how well I climb, jump, swim; everything, really."

"Huh," Clementine let out. "I guess that makes sense."

"Hm? Why so?"

"Just, your running form is very smooth. You said Oka's people are really tall, so I'm guessing they're heavier, too," Clementine concluded. Ashton just nodded, because of course they were. Oka weighed around 450 pounds. "I'm sure their bodies knew to counteract it to some degree, but running can be pretty intense on your joints. So they probably did what they could to develop their form to be pretty damn efficient. So efficient that even you could run like that."

Ashton wished that he still had eyes to stare at his friend, but just let out a long groan. "Very funny. But no, you're totally right, that's basically how it is. I also learned a lot of my body-strengthening techniques from Oka. There, it's just part of every-day life. The ganta, the people of Oka's planet, have another organ right next to their heart that naturally refines something similar to basic internal energy, and they constantly strengthen their body with that energy. It happens mostly subconsciously, but skilled folk can control it."

"And Oka was one of those skilled folk?"

"Yup. I mentioned it briefly before, but Oka was actually on the smaller end as far as ganta go. It was a benefit for being a jargo-maras in some ways, but they were a lot weaker than other ganta in regard to raw physical strength."

"Huh," Clementine nodded, listening intently. Though, while she was listening, she didn't really pay any attention to the fact that Ashton had both of them speed up more and more, until she couldn't keep up anymore.

"Dude... actually how are you so fast?" Clementine forced out, and Ashton shrugged.

"Well, my speed stat is higher than yours, and even though I don't have the 'Running' skill, I've got internal energy to boost me, and far more efficiently than you can," Ashton said, smiling over at Clementine. "Using persistent skills like your Running skill is supposedly pretty hard."

"Supposedly..?" Clementine repeated, starting to feel a little winded trying to keep up with Ashton at his speed, while dodging between monsters and letting herself be pushed around by the sylph's gusts.

"Well, I never had any skills before, and I don't really use the ones I do have now, so," Ashton explained. "Constantly using up your mana can be pretty draining, especially if it's to strengthen your body. It strains your muscles quite a lot, but with your increased 'resistance' stat that won't be a big issue for the most part, unless you really overdo it."

Clementine groaned. "Seriously? They couldn't just make the system work like a real game?"

Ashton laughed. "The system isn't a game, it's a method of interpretation and guidance governing sections of the multiverse, that just so happens to function like a lot of games. We are in a sort of game because of the council though, I guess."

"Fine, pedantic bastard." With another big groan, Clementine turned around the corner following Ashton. And though the cathedral's tower would have already been in plain view for a while, now they were able to actually see the borders of the camp placed within the cathedral.

Ashton checked the time just once more, and then turned to Clementine.

"The sun hasn't gone down yet, right?" he asked, and Clementine quickly shook her head.

"No, not yet. But soon, I think it'll be down in maybe twenty minutes?" she explained. Ashton nodded, having the Backpacker give him the main part of the world fragment.

"Wait here. If something's wrong, just shake my body, that should be enough to call me back," Ashton told her, and hopped into the veil before she could even respond.

Ashton quickly moved into the cathedral through the veil, and carefully shifted his view so that he could observe the material plane without being seen himself. It wasn't perfect, but it was pretty good.

He looked around. Most of the people were inside of the cathedral's main hall, and a certain figure stood at the altar.

The transformation had seemingly continued quite a bit, because the man that was standing there didn't resemble Clementine's father anymore whatsoever. But it was definitely him; Ashton was sure of that.

He was larger; bulkier. He had lost every bit of hair, from what Ashton could tell, and the form of his skin was almost like that of stone. His complexion would most likely be deep and grey by now, too.

He looked like a statue by now, wearing the robes of a priest.

The man's voice echoed through the cathedral as he gave a sermon. It wasn't technically time for one, normally, but it was twilight; the time where this particular being had access to particular powers.

The veil shifted and shook around the God-kin as he influenced the entire cathedral with his magic.
