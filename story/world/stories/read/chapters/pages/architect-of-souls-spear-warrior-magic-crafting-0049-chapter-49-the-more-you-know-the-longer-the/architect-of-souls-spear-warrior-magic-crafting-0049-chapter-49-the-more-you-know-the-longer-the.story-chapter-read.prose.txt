Ferris awoke flailing.

It took him a couple of seconds to realize where he was. The last thing he remembered was the wave of acid rushing down on him, his skin melting off his bones.

He took a deep breath, then tried to relax. It was just his mental body that had died. He was okay.

It was an odd feeling, like waking up from a nightmare he couldn’t quite believe was just a dream.

Jade was on top of him, sending worried feelings over the bond. As Ferris calmed down, he pulled the slime into his arms and sat up, looking around the room.

Serena was sitting at the table, reading a book. After noticing him move, she put the book away and turned to him.

“Ah, you are awake. How are you feeling?”

“Like I just got dunked in a river of acid.” Ferris shakily got up and took a seat at the table.

Serena watched him, hungry for information, yet clearly holding herself back from demanding it. Ferris was required by his oath to share personal details of his soul and cultivation relating to the research, yet there was a lot of interpretation involved.

Ferris collected his thoughts, then continued.

“In Jade’s soul, there is a… wall of sorts. I believe it represents the outer layer of Jade’s body. On the outer side of the wall, there is a large quantity of acid; I was on the inside. When you cut Jade, a hole appeared in the wall, flooding the area with acid.”

“Spiritual death is never pleasant,” Serena said with a scowl. Her eyes darted to the side, likely in recollection. “I am sorry that happened.”

Ferris hugged Jade a bit closer, but didn’t respond. Melting to death was the second-worst way he had died. The slow choking death of suffocation was worse, but there was still something horrible about feeling your body fall apart around you. At least it had been quick. He hadn’t seen it, as his eyes had been closed… and then gone. Any sense of touch had turned to fire, obscuring fine details, but he could still feel the unnatural loss of weight as he flailed while his flesh liquified off his body.

Even worse, the bone spear that Ferris was so proud of had been with him. Uncle Winton had appeared impressed that he had made the imprinted construct, but the acid had likely destroyed it…

Ferris sighed.

He could make a new weapon, yet it would cost more qi. He wanted to try making another imprint anyway; this would just be sooner than he wanted. Luckily, Ferris had not taken his new crossbow with him, as he didn’t expect to fight any chain demons in the slimespace.

Eventually, Ferris was able to speak. Serena had sat in silence, sharing in the misery. From her expression, she was likely staring down her own demons.

“Well,” he began, focusing on what he could, “we have a basic starting point. Jade has a wall shaped to represent the slime’s body, made from a translucent green glowing material. Outside the wall, there is acid. When Jade is fed food, the wall loses its glow. When Jade is hurt, the wall is damaged.”

As he considered what he learned, a chill ran up Ferris’ spine. If the dome leaked acid every time Jade was attacked, what did that mean for his constructions in the slimespace? If he constructed Assimilate Object, would it simply be melted away the first time a monster took a swing at Jade?

The Grow ability was planted inside the dome’s wall itself. Did it create a weak point, where a precisely aimed ability would damage the ability?

The Jump ability was planted near the wall. Ferris knew that it would grow as some kind of tree, but was it resistant to acid? The fruit hadn’t given any clues to these questions.

The Heartfire and the Core Tree were both on top of towers, about three stories tall. That would protect them from some flooding, Ferris realized, although only to a certain height. If the acid gets high enough, it would wipe out everything atop the towers.

Ferris remembered Foreman Carter claimed that the slimes at the ranch rarely evolved or grew stronger. Ferris had been confused at the time, as the monsters were being fed all they could eat and had plenty of time. But… what happened when the ranchers chopped most of the slime’s body off? If a small cut caused a giant hole, could the entire dome disappear for worse injuries? Perhaps… they did develop new abilities, but their spiritspaces were flooded with acid every time the ranchers harvested the slime fertilizer. The flood would destroy any spiritual progress they had made each day, keeping the slimes perpetually weak.

There was at least one problem with that theory: the three fruits Jade had growing on the Core Tree after Ferris bonded the slime. If a tidal wave of acid filled the dome every time the slime was harvested at the ranch, then those three fruits would never have had time to grow. The tree itself wouldn’t have been able to survive… unless it was somehow immune to the acid?

Foreman Carter hadn’t said the slimes in the pit never showed new abilities, just rarely. There must be some way an ability could survive in the slimescape, it just wasn’t common at the slime ranch…

Further, if the healing ability was related to the wall of the dome, how did that work for the bottom of Jade’s body? Ferris had always noticed that the shape of the dome was similar to Jade, but he never saw any problem with the bottom being a dirt floor. Now… what happened in the slimespace if he fed or cut Jade from beneath? He knew the slime could eat and heal from any direction, but how did that work in the slimespace? Did the dome’s wall extend underground? He would have to experiment once he’d recovered.

“I am afraid,” Ferris eventually said, “that I am left with more questions than answers. To learn more, we will have to wait until next time.”

Serena nodded, his words breaking her free from her own thoughts.

“I have been working,” Serena responded, “on converting Revivifying Flame to an acid healing ability. What you described… sounds nothing like Revivifying Flame. It does, however, sound like learning Acid Resistance will be helpful. Do you think that we can continue these tests before you do?”

“Yes,” Ferris nodded, “I can find a way to observe without being in danger, now that I know what to look out for. I may need to learn it before getting a closer look, though.”

“Then, we will continue next time. We have stayed later than planned, so if you are feeling alright, we will end it here.”

“Thank you, for that” Ferris glanced to the side, embarrassed to have delayed whatever plans she had. She could have asked an attendant to sit with him, but instead stayed late to watch over his unconscious body. He was glad he didn’t wake up to a stranger.

“These things happen,” Serena waved a hand, but then gave him a predatory smile, “just try to be more cautious in the rifts.”

Ferris nodded, looking down. He could have been more cautious, even if it grated to be told so. In the rifts, such a mistake would be deadly, and any excuses wouldn’t matter.

Just that morning, his uncle had mentioned that death in the soul was the best teacher of caution. Ferris had nodded, thinking he had learned the lesson, only to die again later the same day.

It was humbling, on top of being embarrassing.

They said farewell, then left the training hall. Ferris and Jade got back to Lily House just in time for dinner.

Mother Lily had prepared a noodle soup. The broth had a rich, salty flavor that the noodles soaked up. The soup was filled with crunchy vegetables and mushrooms. Ferris wondered, could a plant mage grow mushrooms? Maybe it was a specialty they had to develop? Was there some kind of fungus-aspected core, or were these mushrooms gathered from a rift?

Brix was in a better mood today; apparently, she’d regained her focus on training for the delver examination. Ferris glanced a couple of times at Cyrus, curious how his first delve had gone, but the young man didn’t bring it up. He didn’t look hurt, so it couldn’t have gone too poorly.

After dinner, Ferris and Jade relaxed in their room. Ferris wouldn’t be able to cultivate for a couple of days until his mental body reformed. It was frustrating. But at the same time, he appreciated the break. He had been staying up too late recently, so a few nights’ rest would be nice. He just wished he wasn’t losing out on the qi harvest. Ferris’ Air Passage would accumulate the extra—at least what the demons didn’t steal—but now he would have to wait to see the results of overfeeding Jade yesterday.

Really, it wouldn’t be so bad… if the legacy chest wasn’t sitting just out of reach. It was driving him a bit crazy, having just received it, but not having had a chance to look through it more.

The next day, he woke up resolved to put it out of his mind. Ferris couldn’t work until he recovered, so after a lazy morning, he decided to focus on any mundane tasks that could help.

His mental body would recover in two days, and Ferris wanted to make sure Jade’s Digest River was full of qi ready to harvest. When he previously fed the slime until just before bursting, he wasn’t sure if the clams in the river had enough time to eat the dissolved food before it washed away. Maybe they had, but he wanted to be sure. Ferris decided to feed the remaining three and a half straw bundles to Jade, slowly spread out over three meals. It might be a waste, but he preferred a full river of qi. There would be time to narrow down and test later.

After pulling one of the large bricks down, Ferris had a thought. The straw was wrapped with a different kind of plant vine. It was like a string, and Jade had been eating it along with the rest.

Jade wouldn’t be able to practice eating chain demons to adapt Acid Dismantle while Ferris’ mental body was regenerating. Trying to eat the straw while leaving the string behind would be a good substitute.

Ferris explained the game, which Jade had forgotten. The slime was enthusiastic, yet the string was even harder than the chain to avoid, scoring just one out of ten.

After Jade ate the first portion, the two of them leisurely visited the first district’s merit store. The attendant was slightly surprised to hear Ferris wanted to convert a merit point into mundane currency, but was able to accommodate him easily.

Back at the sixth branch’s provisionary, Louis helped Ferris order enough straw for six days of feeding Jade to double in size, a total of forty bricks. They staggered it into two deliveries, as a Ferris didn’t think it would all fit in Mother Lily’s basement.

Ferris wasn’t sure yet if that amount of food would produce enough qi to maximize production, but he could adjust as he went. It was slightly painful to see the money from the merit point immediately disappear, but it helped to think about how much qi it would turn into.

After that, it was back to Lily House for a quick lunch, and Jade’s second portion of straw. The slime did a bit better at avoiding the string, getting a two out of ten.

With the food for the next week taken care of, he decided to visit the library once again. Uncle Winton had given him a lot to consider, and new topics to research.

Gilbert once again assisted Ferris, stepping forward to help him once he reached the front of the queue at the library’s entryway.

“Welcome back, Adept Ferris. How may I help you today?”

“It is good to see you again, Gilbert. I was hoping to learn what options the clan has available for cultivation advancement methods. Then, I wanted to continue reading through the list you helped me find before, to better understand the book on acid.”

Gilbert nodded, then led Ferris to a private room to wait. When the man returned, he had three sheets of paper that he laid on the table, while another attendant carried in a stack of books to set aside. Each sheet described a single technique, but the details were very sparse.

Ferris picked up the first one, called Body Construct Control Technique. The description claimed that it would allow Ferris to alter his mental body. He knew that the mental body didn’t have to match the real body—in his soul Ferris had two legs after all—but he had never considered taking it further. Apparently, a master could grow into a giant, and then reform their soulspace like a child playing in the dirt. Moving a large structure like the fort would be as easy as picking it up.

The second was called Synergistic Space Linking Instruments Technique. Instead of changing the body, it was focused on crafting special wand constructs. These wands somehow took advantage of the nature of the soul, using it to transport large quantities of material. This sounded like an improved and streamlined version of the qi rope transfer technique Uncle Winton had just taught Ferris, which used the bonded core to ferry large quantities of material around easily. Somehow, Synergistic Space Linking Instruments Technique took it further, making wands that could remove and place any type of construction material. Point the wand at a boulder, and poof: it disappeared. Point it again, and the boulder would reappear, magically where you wanted it.

Lastly, there was Mechanical Power Technique. This took the idea of magically powered farm equipment to an extreme. Instead of crafting small wands, Mechanical Power created huge contraptions. The cultivator could then use controls that let the machines magically move. Moving stones larger than Ferris could lift would be as simple as controlling a machine to grab and place it. Ferris had considered building a wagon. With this technique, the wagon would be able to pull itself. Interestingly, the paper specified that the machines would need manual control… did that imply other techniques could create machines that controlled themselves?

It didn’t take Ferris long to read all three sheets of paper. Looking back up at Gilbert, Ferris was a bit confused. They were all labeled as techniques, while Raging Stream Jagged Point was an advancement method.

“Gilbert, my understanding is that complete methods contain a pattern to flow energy, a way to unlock meridians, and aid in spiritual construction. I see each of these describes the aid in spiritual construction, but nothing about meridians or energy patterns.”

“Yes,” Gilbert nodded, “Clan Hollowfield takes a different stance on advancement methods than focused martial traditions. What you described works for a small clan with a single type of bond, but Hollowfield supports the growth of many paths. Each of these techniques contains a way to unlock meridians and aid in developing souls, but the energy pattern is purchased separately. Different patterns can be adapted to the specific requirements of a style, while these three methods were developed to be usable by any Adept.”

Ferris considered Clan Bluereef’s Raging Stream Jagged Point method. From his uncle’s description, the Raging Stream part would aid in spiritual construction, while the Jagged Point was the pattern it carved into the soul. He couldn’t use Raging Stream due to its reliance on the bonded water core, but maybe Jagged Point could be combined with one of these…

Then again, Uncle Winton also said the Jagged Point pattern used quick turns of flowing energy to work better. Perhaps when Ferris had met his uncle’s requirement of a few delves and read through the book, he would learn more.

“Does Hollowfield have anything specific to an acid core bond?” Ferris asked.

“We have several martial traditions that use an acid core, but their details are sealed secrets. Only those on that path may view them.” Gilbert shook his head. “Honestly, I don’t know. They may have specific advancement methods, or they may use one of these three. Likely, if they knew something generic to any acid core, the clan would include it in this list. I know of at least one technique like that available to anyone with a plant core.”

“And the price of these three techniques?” Ferris hadn’t seen it listed anywhere.

“They are standardized at 32 merit each. They are priced intentionally to encourage Adepts to only purchase after beginning to delve. My understanding is that they all require substantial resources to be effective.” Gilbert looked Ferris in the eye, then said: “Much more reasonably priced are the two introductions to soul development, Spiritual Harmony and Mental Materials.”

Ferris nodded, taking the hint. “Thank you, Gilbert. I intend to make them a priority after I have what I need for my leg.” He didn’t know how long it would take to heal his leg, but Elder Harper had mentioned that the full research project could take around a year. Ferris wanted to get Acid Resistance first, to make sure the research project didn’t get delayed. There was a chance that learning Acid Resistance would require some knowledge referenced in the manuals Gilbert mentioned, but Ferris could also ask Serena for help.

While Ferris didn’t think that he had to explain himself… he wanted to, at least a little. Gilbert was going out of his way to help him. The man could have dropped off the descriptions and left, but instead had stayed to offer advice. The more information Gilbert had, the better he would be able to help. He would normally be wary of sharing his cultivation plans like this, but Elder Harper, and by extension Clan Hollowfield, already knew. “My intention is to save up enough merit to purchase the Acid Resistance ability first, then return to Spiritual Harmony and Mental Materials.”

Gilbert’s eyes lost a bit of their sharpness, but he maintained a professional attitude. Ferris’ openness didn’t magically make the man remember some critical piece of advice, but Ferris hoped it would help in the future. At the very least, it might keep Gilbert from shunning him completely after Ferris purchased Acid Resistance instead of his recommendations.

Long-term, Ferris wasn’t sure which of the three techniques to purchase. Ferris liked the idea of Body Construct Control, as he could try to get it to work for Jade as well. It would then be twice as effective as normal. Although... perhaps the same could be said for the other two. Jade could just as easily use a wand or operate a machine alongside him—well, maybe if Jade had hands. Can a slime hold a wand or pull a lever?

Ferris would have liked to review the jade slip on Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting, but he couldn’t without his mental body.

Instead, he spent the rest of the day reading through the mundane books. None of them were as revolutionary to him as On the Preparation of Food had been. But slowly, he was piecing together a better understanding of what a chemical reaction actually was.

It was, at least, progress he could be happy with.
