Of course, I got to see that group when they all trundled into Math class. I remember my teachers talking about 'differentiated learning' back in the day. I didn't get it, not exactly. Okay, I knew what it was, at least in the abstract. It's where a teacher has students who are on different levels, and the lesson that will work for the slowest kid in class will be a boring assed review for most of the others, and most of the class doesn't have the prerequisite knowledge and skills to even engage with the lesson that would challenge the kid at the top of the class. I never wound up top of the class except in Language Arts, but I remember one of my science teachers used the surprisingly effective strategy of having the most advanced kid in class teach some of the others.

At the time I thought that was kinda lazy, while also being really impressed by it. Seriously, I'm kinda lazy myself, even if I've moved away from that just a little bit here and now. But back in the day that kind of weaponized laziness just left me staring in awed fascination as the smart kid, this big walking mountain of a dude who had read the entire science textbook by the end of the first quarter, did a whole lecture and lab thing with one chunk of the class while the teacher sat down and helped a couple kids who were having trouble with the basics of the scientific method and why it's important.

I absolutely will neither confirm nor deny said teacher describing the method as 'fuck around, find out', but that got it through to both the academically challenged and delinquent kids in ways that, as you can tell, I still remember to this day.

But I get it now. Normally we've got a couple people teaching various levels of Math at the Temple school, and I mostly handle the 'advanced' kids. I never really took Calculus back in the day, so I can't teach the kids who have mastered basic Algebra anything new, but everything up to there, including some basic Trig, I can and do teach them. Although none of those words are in use here. They don't have a separate word for Trig, it's just part of Geometry. Algebra, to the best of my knowledge, wasn't invented by some Middle Eastern guy, or if it was it's one of those things that have been lost to history here and now, or something like the whole Leibniz and Newton thing where multiple people came up with the same thing in different places at different times. But here and now in the Alliance it's called 'Variable Equations', which at least describes it pretty well. As for Calculus, I'm vaguely flattered and sheepish that Saffron named it after me, what with calling it Mor-Mimic Calculations.

Wait, no, I think I might understand the why of that now, and also finally get why one of my teachers described it the way he did. He called it 'the mathematics of change'. Which, what with me being the Mor Primordial of Change, if Calculus really is the math of how things Change, it just makes sense to name it after me.

Still sheepish about it though.

As for how I know who Newton and Leibniz are, that Epic Rap Battle was a total banger.

At any rate, I digress yet again, like it's a minimum daily requirement or something. With the kits and a few new students needing to learn basic arithmetic, a lot of the rest of the class needing more work on their Variable Equations, and Ria having that down cold and really needing me to call in Saffron or Tallulah to tutor her in Mor-Mimic Calculations, I finally understood the need for the whole 'differentiated lesson plan' thing. I also am not ashamed that I wholeheartedly stole my old science teacher's method and put Ria in charge of teaching Variable Equations for the day while I worked with the new kids.
