Fractal and Ten rejoined us a few minutes later, descending through the veil of glowing spores like a pair of mismatched spirits—one elegant, shimmering, ethereal; the other muttering curses under her breath as she pushed dangling vines away from her face.

Ten landed first, brushing her braids back with a scowl.

“Alex,” she said, pointing upward with both hands as if trying to physically drag our attention toward the endless canopy, “your realm is broken.”

“Not my realm,” I corrected.

“Okay then this realm is broken.”

Fractal touched down beside her, light feathers rustling. “It is… vast,” she murmured, “and layered like woven cloth. The canopy keeps growing above itself. There is no end.”

Cordelia pinched the bridge of her nose. “Infinite verticality. Fantastic. Just what we needed.”

“Hey,” V said, shrugging, “on the bright side, if the sky collapses we’ll never know.”

“V.” I sighed.

“What? I’m coping.”

Fallias took a slow, wary step away from the nearest tree trunk, eyes narrowed. “This place feels… old. Not ancient, not primordial. Old like… repetition. Cycles on cycles. Overgrown to the point of losing what it started as.”

“An abandoned garden.” Fractal offered softly.

Ten scoffed. “If this is a garden, then whoever built it had issues.”

She wasn’t wrong.

There was a weight in the air—something in the stillness, in the pulsing glow, in the invasive humidity—that felt less like natural overgrowth and more like intentional overgrowth.

As if someone wanted it this way. As if someone tended it once.

I didn’t like the implications.

“Alright,” I said, forcing focus back into the group. “We’ve all acknowledged that we’re in a gigantic botanical nightmare. Good. Now let’s figure out how to carve a path through it before the carts get here.”

V raised a hand. “Actually—before that, can I just point something out?”

I gave him a look. “If it’s about fate again—”

“No, this time it’s real.” He pointed to the glowing motes drifting around us. “These haven’t moved since we arrived. At all. They’re in the exact same positions as before.”

I checked. He was right.

“Meaning?” Fallias asked.

“Meaning,” V continued, “the forest doesn’t notice us. Or doesn’t care that we’re here.”

“That’s good, isn’t it?” Ten said.

Cordelia shook her head immediately. “No. That’s the worst-case scenario. If it noticed and reacted with hostility, that’s predictable. If it noticed and reacted with curiosity, we could negotiate depending on the Dominus.”

She pointed at the unmoving spore-motes.

“But when an active realm ignores you, it means you’re not part of the system. Not recognized. Not processed.”

V crossed his arms. “Basically—we’re foreign objects.”

Fallias finished the thought grimly. “And foreign objects get expelled.”

“Or dissolved.” Cordelia added.

Great.

Fantastic.

Exactly what I wanted to hear.

“We’ll adapt,” I said, trying to steady the group. “We’ve survived worse.”

V snorted. “True. But usually the ‘worse’ had limbs.”

Ten already had her hand resting on her weapon. “So what’s the plan? Because ‘infinite forest realm full of glowing nonsense that doesn’t know we exist’ is not exactly something the Academy prepared us for.”

“Step one,” I said, “find a traversable direction. The forest gets too thick in the north—that’s out. Fractal, Ten, what about the west?”

Fractal hesitated, her feathers dimming slightly. “The west feels… constricted. The air pressure changes. The trees lean inward. It feels like a throat.”

“Oh good,” V groaned. “We’re in a giant stomach and a giant throat. Love that for us.”

“What about east?” Fallias asked.

Ten grimaced. “We tried. Dead end. Literally. The vines there are so thick they could stop one of Morgan’s swings barehanded.”

“And south?” Cordelia asked.

Fractal tilted her head. “South… is spacious.”

Ten blinked. “Wait. No, it wasn’t. Did it change?”

Fractal’s mask shifted slightly, the haze forming into a contemplative shape. “It may have rearranged. The forest moves.”

I tensed. “Moves?”

“Yes. Slowly. But intentionally.”

V threw his hands up. “Well that’s just fantastic. Sentient infinite forest. Love it. Great trip so far.”

Cordelia took a deep breath. “If the realm is adjusting paths when it chooses to, then maybe it’s not fully ignoring us. Maybe it’s… assessing.”

“Or,” Ten said, “it’s herding us.”

“Toward what?” Fallias asked.

Silence.

Not comfortable.

Not safe.

Not hopeful.

I drew a steadying breath. “Alright. South is our best option for now. If it shifted to open a path, we take it. Everyone on guard. Don’t touch anything unless I tell you to.”

Fractal fluttered onto my shoulder, her voice low. “Alex… something else.”

“Go on.”

“The forest…” Her feathers dimmed again. “When Ten and I ascended, we felt no fauna. But we did sense something else.”

My skin prickled.

“What kind of something?”

She hesitated.

Then, in a tone I had rarely heard from her—small, uneasy, almost frightened—

“A heartbeat.”

Every one of us froze.

Even the air seemed to still.

“…A heartbeat?” Fallias echoed.

Fractal nodded slowly. “Deep. Far. Not… mortal. But rhythmic. A pulse that resonates through the wood. Through the spores. Through everything.”

Cordelia swallowed. “You’re saying this realm has a circulatory system.”

“Not exactly.” Fractal murmured. “More like… a single heart. A central core.”

V’s tail fluffed. “And we’re being herded toward it.”

I clenched my jaw.

“Then we need to make damn sure we reach it before it reaches us.”

Ten groaned. “This is why I don’t sign up for botany missions.”

Fallias tightened his grip on his mace. “Everyone ready?”

One by one, they nodded.

I turned south, toward the newly opened corridor—wide, illuminated by drifting spores, the bioluminescent moss almost forming a natural path.

A path that hadn’t existed earlier.

A path that felt very much like an invitation.

“Alright,” I muttered, starting forward, “let’s go meet the heartbeat.”

The path south wasn’t really a path so much as an absence of resistance. The glowing moss spread smoother here. The vines drooped just a little higher, as if politely lifting their skirts for us. The ground—still soft, damp, and too warm—felt almost… cushioned.

Not comforting. Not benign.

More like the forest was encouraging us forward.

I hated that.

“Everyone eyes up,” I said quietly, stepping first. “I don’t trust this place not to snap shut behind us.”

“Oh it will,” Cordelia said. “I can already feel it pushing inward. Like it’s breathing.”

Fallias walked at my side, her tail held stiffly behind her, scales shimmering with condensation. “If it starts digesting us, I swear I’m burning it.”

V snorted. “Please do. Maybe it’ll sneeze us out.”

Ten adjusted her bracers. “If this big leafy monster sneezes me out, I’m quitting the Walker corps and becoming a potter with Matthew.”

“I don’t think you have the personality for pottery,” Cordelia murmured.

“I do when the alternative is being tree poop.”

V sighed. “Fair.”

We pushed forward.

The corridor curved slightly, funneling us deeper. A faint pulse—so soft I would’ve thought I imagined it—thrummed under my feet. The moss vibrated ever so delicately, in a rhythm that wasn’t quite regular but wasn’t chaotic either.

A pulse.

Fractal hadn’t been exaggerating.

“Alex…” Fallias said softly.

“I know,” I answered. “I feel it too.”

“It’s getting louder,” V added.

It was.

The deeper we went, the more distinct the thrum became—not sound, not exactly vibration, but something sensed in the bones. Like pressure changes before a storm.

Ten kept glancing back over her shoulder. “Still no fauna. No insects. No animals. Nothing. This entire realm is nothing but plants.”

“That’s not natural,” Cordelia whispered.

“It’s not unnatural either,” I countered. “Otherrealms don’t follow our rules. Each Dominus shapes their domain according to their will. We’ve seen aquatic realms. Lava fields. Floating labyrinths. This could just be—”

“A singular biome realm?” Cordelia finished. “Yes. Possible. But ecosystems need components. Predators. Prey. Decay. Renewal. Movement. Cycles. We haven’t seen anything acting as fauna. Not even microfauna.”

She scraped moss from a tree, inspected it, and released it.

“No mites. No larvae. No worms. No parasites. Nothing.”

“Maybe the forest kills everything that tries to settle in?” V suggested.

Ten scowled. “Then how does it grow?”

Cordelia didn’t answer.

Because there wasn’t one.

We kept moving for another hundred meters. The canopy above thickened until the glow barely filtered down, but somehow the moss beneath us glowed brighter to compensate.

The whole realm was cooperating with itself for visibility.

Yeah. Definitely not normal forest behavior.

The pulse grew stronger.

Thrum. Thrum. Ten counted under her breath. “It’s irregular.”

Cordelia shook her head. “Not irregular. Complex.”

“The Dominus’s?” Fallias asked.

“Maybe,” I said. “Or the heart of the realm.”

Fractal made a soft chiming noise from my shoulder. “Something notices us now.”

I halted.

“So it is alive,” Ten said nervously.

“No,” Fractal corrected, “not alive the way you think. Not a creature. But a presence. A… mind? No. That’s too direct. A… sensation. Like being regarded. Evaluated.”

“By the heartbeat?” V asked.

“No. By the forest.”

Fallias exhaled slowly. “Fantastic.”

The path ahead widened abruptly. The vines pulled back just enough to reveal a circular clearing. At its center stood a single enormous tree trunk—no, not a tree. A column. Perfectly smooth bark spiraling upward into the canopy, textured like carved wood but clearly organic.

And glowing.

Faint veins of bioluminescence pulsed up through it in time with the heartbeat.

Thrum.

I stepped closer.

“It’s a conduit,” Cordelia whispered, awe creeping into her normally flat tone. “A central line. A mana artery. The forest is distributing energy from that.”

Fallias narrowed her eyes. “So the heart is deeper still.”

“Yeah,” I said softly. “But this is connected.”

Ten tapped the surface with the end of her weapon.

Nothing happened.

“Should we cut it?” she asked.

“No,” I said instantly.

Every hair on my arms stood up at the thought. The whole realm felt tuned—tight and coiled—like a massive instrument waiting to be played wrong.

Cordelia nodded. “Agreed. Damaging a realm’s central channel without knowing its Dominus’s disposition could classify us instantly as hostile Others.”

“Which is bad,” V translated helpfully. “Because then we die.”

“Thanks, V.”

“Anytime.”

Fractal drifted off my shoulder and hovered near the conduit, mask shifting with curiosity. “Alex… feel this.”

I touched the surface.

A jolt ran through me—not pain, not mana overload, but recognition.

The forest… reacted.

The glow intensified for a moment.

Fallias grabbed my arm. “Alex—”

“I’m fine.” I exhaled shakily. “I think it was… reading me.”

“Reading?” V repeated. “Like a book?”

“I don’t know. It feels like—like it just took impressions? Or… metadata?” I pulled my hand back. “It felt like the realm sniffed me.”

Ten grimaced. “That’s worse.”

I didn’t disagree.

“Alright,” I said. “Everyone step back. Something’s going to—”

The ground beneath us trembled.

Not violently.

Not threateningly.

More like a deep sigh rolling through the soil.

Then the moss shifted.

It pulled backward from the center of the clearing, peeling itself away to reveal a spiraling downward slope—smooth, natural, but unmistakably a passage.

Fallias inhaled sharply. “It… opened a path.”

Cordelia’s voice was hushed. “It recognized Alexander.”

V threw his hands up. “Of course it did! Of course the magical infinite forest likes you, Rabbit. You’re its type.”

“V,” I groaned, massaging my temples. “Please not now.”

He shrugged. “I’m coping.”

Fractal touched down on my shoulder again. “It is safe,” she said softly. “I cannot explain how I know. But the forest intends no harm.”

“Yet,” Ten muttered.

“Yet,” Fractal agreed.

The downward passage glowed just enough to be visible—a dim, greenish hum.

Fallias leaned in close, whispering. “Alex… I’m not leaving your side in there. If the forest wants a piece of you, it can go through me first.”

I swallowed.

I loved her. I really, genuinely loved her in moments like that.

“Thanks, Fal-Fal,” I said softly.

She squeezed my hand, her warm scaled fingers brushing against my furred knuckles.

Cordelia cleared her throat. “We go together. No scouting ahead this time. The forest clearly wants us all.”

V took a few steps forward, peering down the spiraling descent. “Alright then. Down we go. Into the hungry glowing hole. What could possibly go wrong?”

Ten punched his arm. “Stop tempting fate!”

“I’m not tempting fate!” V yelped. “I’m narrating the obvious!”

“That’s worse!”

They continued bickering as we descended.

And I… I couldn’t help glancing back once more.

The conduit-pillar pulsed softly.

Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.

Waiting.

Calling.

Welcoming.

Not hostile.

Not friendly.

Just… expectant.

Ready for us.

Ready for me.

I exhaled, tightened my grip on Fallias’s hand, and stepped into the spiraling passage as it swallowed the light behind us.
