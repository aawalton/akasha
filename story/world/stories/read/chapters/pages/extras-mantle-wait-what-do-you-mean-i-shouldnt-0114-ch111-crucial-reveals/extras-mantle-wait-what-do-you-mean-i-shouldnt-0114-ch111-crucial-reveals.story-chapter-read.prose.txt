Veric jumped into motion the moment Jin uttered—Multiple overmortals, traps, cultists—the man was already moving, turning on his heel and shouting down the corridor before Jin could tell the man more about the situation.

Veric turned back to Jin, his dull brown gaze sharpening into something more focused.

"I'm not a superb fighter," Veric said, direct and without false modesty. "But I’ve been in combat situations enough times in my life to get by and not be a burden. The effects of Sovereign's Indifference made the decision simple—another tool in the arsenal, another piece on the board—and Jin nodded. "That would be helpful. Do you expect us to run into mindwashed civilians?"

"Almost certainly." Veric pulled a small device from his belt—palm-sized, dark metal with a single activation rune glowing faint blue. "We have hundreds of civilians unaccounted for, and that’s just on this floor alone. With comms down, we don’t know exactly who and how many are missing, but we can expect the enemy has converted a good amount of civilians, and to think we wouldn’t even know about this if it weren’t for you."

A holographic layout bloomed into existence above the device. The detail was impressive—corridors, rooms, ventilation shafts—and the hologram shifted slightly as Veric manipulated the display with finger gestures.

"This is the base where you were with Maya and the others." Veric pointed to a central hub on the circular map, a wider area marked with reinforced walls and multiple access points. "That was the central station on Floor 31. Command hub, medical, armory. And…"

The holographic map shifted, zooming out and sliding laterally until it centered on a different section.

"This is where we are now. At the West Wing of this floor." Veric's finger traced along a series of rectangular chambers. "This wing was used as cold storage for hydroponic farming, which is the core theme of Levels 29 through 31."

He pinched his fingers together, and the map contracted.

Smaller and smaller.

Jin, even in the muted state of Sovereign's Indifference, felt something close to awe as the sheer scale of the bastion revealed itself. Judging by what the map was showing, the bastion was as big, if not bigger than, the city of Vienna itself.

"It's mind-numbing, truly, how big this place is." Veric's voice carried a note of something that might've been respect or might've been exhaustion. Hard to tell. "As of right now—or rather, in the past few years—only the Central, West, and Southwest wings have been reclaimed and put under maintenance."

"You need appropriate clearance levels to access the sealed sectors. High-level hacking, security overrides, stuff I'm not familiar with." Veric shrugged. "The bastion locks itself down when it detects threats or catastrophic damage."

"I see."

« Jin, » Angel's voice cut through his thoughts, clear and urgent. « Elenor is in the Seed Vault direction. That's all I've been able to deduce from the memories so far. The good news is that Order IV shouldn't be there. The bad news is he's busy setting up a ritual. I'll have more details soon. »

Veric's brows creased, and Jin caught the man’s expression. There was surprise, and then regret, which was quickly suppressed.

"Yes, I do." Veric manipulated the hologram again, and the map shifted to highlight a specific area labeled in small text: SEED VAULT - AGRICULTURAL RESERVE ALPHA-7.

It wasn't far from their current position. Maybe ten minutes at a run through relatively clear corridors if he was reading the map right.

Jin nodded. "Then let's get to it. Unless there's something you want to tell me?"

Veric sighed—a long, deliberate exhale that suggested he absolutely had something to tell Jin and wasn't happy about it—and he moved the map slightly, pointing to another room adjacent to the path they'd need to take.

"That room is the worker residence quarters for everyone… the survivors assigned to operate the Seed Vault and cold storage." His expression darkened. "There are around twenty-five civilians in there, all mindwashed. And there's no way to reach Elenor except by going through that room."

Jin had a feeling he knew what Veric was going to say next.

"This is also the area where we caught that cultist initially." Veric's scowl deepened. "Elenor decided to pursue when they scattered."

He closed the map with a flick of his wrist, and the hologram dissolved into fading green motes. When he turned to face Jin fully, his eyes were hard.

"And I'm certain there will be at least one or two cultists hiding in the mix."

Jin was grateful for the sovereign's indifference—without it, he'd probably feel the weight of that statement. It’s going to be a nightmare, fighting enemies hidden in a crowd of hostages who'd attack you if you got too close—at least with it, he could look at the problem with detachment.

"I'll identify the cultists and handle them," Jin said. "Can you get the civilians off my back and make sure they aren't caught in the crossfire?"

Veric looked at him for a long moment, his brown eyes narrowing slightly.

Something's off with this one. Jin wouldn’t be surprised if Veric was thinking something along those lines.

"Yes," Veric said finally. "We'd need to bring Jack and Gleeward. But yes, I can make that happen." He paused. "Not for long, though. Maybe a minute or two at most."

Jin reached into his inventory and pulled out the [Veil of the Shared Horizon]. The mask materialized in his hand—dark, featureless, smooth obsidian-black material with no eye slits, no mouth opening, just a seamless oval that would cover his entire face—and he put it on.

And a panel bloomed open in the corner of his vision.
