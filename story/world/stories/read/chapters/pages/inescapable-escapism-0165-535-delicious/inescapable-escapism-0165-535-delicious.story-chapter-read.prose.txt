My head spun as I walked along the corridor. People chatted loudly around me, but I didn’t join in. There was no one there that I knew, but even if there was, I probably would have remained silent anyway. I was too tired, and I was pretty sure I couldn’t have spoken, even if I wanted to.

It felt as though my brain was full. Too much had been crammed into it, and it didn’t all fit. The class I’d just had was too intense. It was filled with facts and information, and I tried desperately to memorise everything, but I knew it was impossible. There was bound to be something I’d forget, and I just had to hope it wasn’t anything too important.

That felt unlikely, though. It was a first aid class, after all. If anything happened, if I got injured in some way, there was a chance I’d need to know some of the information I’d been taught today, and I wasn’t sure whether I would be able to recall it. That scared me. What would happen if I couldn’t? Would I die?

Possibly. It had happened before. I’d died in other worlds, but I didn’t want that to happen at the Academy. I was too attached to the world, to the people there, and I wasn’t ready to leave it. I hadn’t spent enough time there. I hadn’t had a chance to say goodbye.

But I wasn’t dying. Not yet. Nothing had happened to me, and it would be a while before I was given an assignment. I’d only just left the induction wing, I reminded myself. I had time to learn more about how to deal with injuries, and I would. As long as I focused and studied, I’d probably be okay.

The thought of that made my head throb again, and I lifted a hand to rub my temples, hoping that would help get rid of the ache that had settled there. It didn’t really help, though. The pain continued.

It probably wouldn’t be that bad, though, I tried to tell myself. The first aid classes hadn’t been too hard up until that point. I’d had a few in the induction wing, and a handful since leaving, and they were fine. They’d mostly been going over how to deal with things like minor cuts, sprains, and that kind of thing, but we’d moved on to dislocations and broken bones. Apparently, that meant we also needed to learn anatomy, and that was the problem.

I’d always thought I was pretty good at biology. My grades had been fairly good in both worlds, and I enjoyed it. I thought I understood the subject well enough, but the class I’d just had showed me how little I truly knew. My knowledge was superficial. I knew hardly anything about the human body, and there was very little that would actually help me if I were to get injured.

That wasn’t my old teachers’ fault, though. First aid wasn’t exactly something that tended to be covered in class. Especially not in the detail we were being taught at the Academy, but I wished it had been. It would have made the lessons I was having so much less overwhelming.

A sigh slipped from my lips as I continued to rub my temples. The desire to skip dinner and go back to my room, where I could turn the lights off and lie in the dark until my headache subsided, gripped me. The temptation was so strong that I almost did it. I glanced towards the stairs above me before my stomach growled loudly, bringing me back to reality.

As wonderful as it would be to not have to think for a while, I just couldn’t do it. I was too hungry, and I wanted to see Seth again, anyway. He would have been worried if I didn’t show up for dinner, and I didn’t want that to happen. He’d already seemed concerned enough when I’d visited the world earlier.

That was another reason I couldn’t let myself go up to bed early. I hadn’t spent enough time at the Academy, and I felt bad. I’d planned on returning to it earlier. I was going to visit when I was eating the dinner my dad brought up for me and whilst getting ready for bed, but I’d been distracted. I’d barely even entered the world when my phone buzzed and dragged me back to my reality.

A slight smile pulled at my lips, despite my aching head. Duncan was the one who’d been distracting me. And Phoebe. I’d told her all about what had happened at Duncan’s house earlier before deleting the messages from my phone, just in case my mom saw them, and her reaction had been hilarious. She’d been so excited, so overjoyed, for Duncan and me, and she’d demanded to know every single detail.

And I told her everything, in between texting Duncan to organise going to his house in the morning. I’d also sent her a screenshot of that, and she’d almost called me again. It was a good sign that he wanted to see me again so soon. Apparently, that was proof of how into me he was.

I bit my lip as I followed the crowd slowly up the stairs. Excitement and nervousness danced in my stomach as I thought about what would happen when I went to Duncan’s again. I was looking forward to it, even if I wasn’t sure whether the Mario Party tournament was just an excuse for me to go over to his again.

Either way, it would be fun. I kind of knew what to expect, and hopefully, that would mean I’d be less anxious. I’d be able to just focus on talking to Duncan rather than trying to work out whether or not he was flirting with me. I’d know that he was.

And I wouldn’t have to wait for him to make a move on me. If I wanted to kiss him, I could just do it. I knew he’d probably kiss me back, and that made it less scary. It was less intimidating, and perhaps I’d even try to use tongue that time.

The thought caused a flurry of emotions to flare within me, and I felt my face start to heat up. I was blushing in both worlds, and I looked down quickly to hide it as I pushed the thought aside. I needed to stop. I had to quit thinking about what could happen between Duncan and me. Otherwise, I’d never stop blushing, and I was getting close to the canteen, where Seth should be waiting for me.

Guilt bubbled in the pit of my stomach, and I chewed my lip. Thinking about kissing Duncan when I was at the Academy felt wrong, and especially when I was on my way to see Seth. I didn’t even know Duncan in that world. It had been years since we’d last spoken, and that made me feel weird that I was thinking about making out with someone who was basically a stranger to me.

Plus, it kind of made me feel like I was cheating on Seth. I wasn’t. I knew I wasn’t. We weren’t dating, and even if we were, it didn’t really count. It wasn’t real. I’d made out with Duncan in another world, and I didn’t even know Seth out there. We’d never met before, and he might not even exist. I hoped he did, but I wasn’t sure how it worked. There was a chance that he’d never been born in my reality.

That filled me with sadness. Even though I was pretty sure the world with the Academy was the only one that I’d met Seth in, I didn’t want him to not exist elsewhere. I wanted him to be alive and happy in every world. That was what he deserved.

I shook my head, trying to push the thought away. I needed to stop thinking about Duncan, I reminded myself. Even if it wasn’t cheating, I still had to stop. It was distracting me too much, and Seth would notice. He’d realised before when I was distracted, and I didn’t want that to happen again.

Reaching the top of the stairs, I took a deep breath. I could do it. It was possible for me to stop myself from thinking about Duncan and focus on the conversation I would be having with Seth. I was pretty sure I could do that, anyway.

Doubt and concern pulled at me as I crossed the foyer towards the canteen, my eyes already searching the room for Seth. He was meant to be meeting me there, and I assumed he’d already be sitting down. The classroom he’d just been in was closer to the canteen. It was on the same floor, whereas I’d needed to traipse all the way up the stairs, and that always took a while.

I spotted him quickly. It was easy to do. Something about him always seemed to stand out from the others to me, and I wasn’t sure what it was, but it helped. Before I’d even reached the canteen doors, I’d found him.

My steps faltered, and I felt myself slow ever so slightly before I forced myself to continue moving. He was talking to someone. I’d assumed that we’d be sitting alone at dinner, that it would just be the two of us. That was how we tended to eat most meals, and I thought that was how he preferred it. It was how I did.

Although it didn’t always turn out that way. There had been a few occasions where other people had sat with us. Normally, it was when the canteen was busy, so there were fewer empty seats, and they were always kind. They seemed to go out of their way to talk to us and include us in the conversation, and I appreciated that, but I still preferred it when we had the table to ourselves.

It was just easier when there was no one else with us. Being around new people had always made me kind of anxious, and I struggled to know what to say. Even when they asked me a direct question, it was hard to find the right words to answer them. I felt like it was never good enough. There had never really been any issues, but it always seemed like what I wanted to communicate came out wrong or was misinterpreted, and that stressed me out.

With Seth, it was different. I was still a little bit anxious and worried about what I was saying, but it was nowhere near as bad. I knew him better, and it was just easier to know when to speak when there were fewer people talking at once.

I watched Seth and the guy he was talking to out of the corner of my eye as I joined the queue for food, trying to work out whether he’d be eating with us. Thankfully, it didn’t seem like he would be. He didn’t have a tray in front of him, and he wasn’t even sitting properly. It looked as though he was just perched on the edge of the chair.

That had to be a good sign, I decided. He didn’t look comfortable, and so I assumed he’d only sat to speak to Seth for a minute and intended to get up again soon. Or I hoped that was the case, at least. I didn’t have anything against the guy, but I didn’t want him to eat with us.

My brain was still too tired. I could feel my thoughts growing frantic. They seemed to simultaneously be moving sluggishly but also too quickly, and I knew that would make it even harder for me to talk to someone I didn’t know, like that guy.

I’d never met him before, I realised as the line moved forward, and I got a better look at his face. I was pretty sure of that, but that didn’t mean much. I hadn’t been out of the induction wing for that long, so there were still some people in the Academy that I’d never spoken to or even met before. It was a fairly small place, so I felt like I recognised most trainees, but a few had been away on assignments. They were in the field, and had been for some time, so it made sense that I hadn’t come across them before.

He appeared to be about the same age as Seth and me, though. Most people at the Academy were, though. All of the trainees were within a couple of years of my age, and even the tutors were generally pretty young. A lot of them had been trained at the Academy or one of the others scattered around the country when they were kids, and it was sometimes kind of hard to tell who was who, but I was pretty sure the guy talking to Seth was another trainee.

I couldn’t help but watch them as I moved closer to the front of the line, trying not to draw attention to myself. I didn’t want Seth and the other boy to know I was staring at them, but I couldn’t keep my eyes away, and it didn’t help that the conversation looked pretty intense. The boy was leaning towards Seth, and even from where I was standing, I could see the slight furrow between Seth’s eyebrows. It seemed as though he was worried by what the boy was saying to him, and that just made me more curious.

The boy wouldn’t be being mean to him. I knew that, but it was still my first concern. There was bullying at most schools, if not all, and the Academy was different. It was more than just a school, and I hadn’t seen anyone being picked on there, but I knew it must still be happening. There was no way the tutors could completely stamp it out.

As long as the boy didn’t stick around, I’d ask Seth about it when I went over, I decided. I didn’t want to come across as nosy, and there was a chance he’d lie to me, that he wouldn’t want to admit it if the boy was being cruel, but I still needed to ask. And I’d keep an eye on them. If I saw the boy talking to Seth again, I’d go over there and make sure he wasn’t bullying him.

The determination that filled me was somewhat surprising. It was the way I always felt in real life when I saw people like Ella picking on others, but I’d never really had the confidence to do anything about it. The few times when I had spoken up had just shown me that the bullying didn’t stop. They just turned their attention to someone else, and that person was normally me, but they were still horrible to the person they’d been teasing before I stepped in whenever they got the chance.

I reached the front of the queue and grabbed a tray and one of the plates that had been waiting on the warmer. The hot ceramic made my fingertips sting, but I held onto it for a moment, enjoying the sensation. It was strange. Although it kind of hurt, it also felt nice, and I wasn’t sure why, but I barely had a chance to think about it before being distracted by the buffet.

My mouth watered as I began to load food onto my plate. The meals at the Academy were always so good. There were so many options, and I wanted to try everything. Even the things that I’d had before sometimes tasted different there. The chefs must have used different seasonings or something from what I was used to because they somehow managed to make something as simple as roasted courgettes taste absolutely delicious.

Too soon, there was no more space on my plate. I started to turn away from the buffet before a scent caught my attention. I couldn’t help but freeze before turning slowly towards the metal trays again, my eyes seeking out whatever it was that I could smell. It was so savoury, so salty, and there was an undercurrent of something fried.

My gaze landed on an unassuming platter towards the end of the buffet, and I felt myself lick my lips, the movement subconscious. That was it. I’d found the thing that smelled so good, and I immediately placed my tray down again, reaching for the tongs so that I could grab some of the noodles.

They’d been stained a dark brown from the sauce they were cooked in, but the dish was dry. The dish must have been cooked off until the sauce was completely absorbed, and the noodles turned crispy. It looked absolutely incredible, and it smelled even better.

I dumped a large helping of noodles onto my plate, dropping them directly on top of the small mountain of sweetcorn. There was nowhere else for them to go. Every inch of my plate was full, and I knew that I could just grab another plate, but the thought of that made me cringe. It felt too greedy.

As I turned away from the food once more, my stomach growled. The noise lasted for a solid few seconds, and it was so loud that I glanced around, hoping that no one else had heard it. The canteen was fairly loud. There were a lot of people in there, and most of them were talking, so I hoped that would have drowned it out, but I was wrong. The girl beside me was smirking softly, and immediately, I knew she must have heard the noise my body had made.

Embarrassment crashed into me. I knew it wasn’t my fault. I couldn’t exactly control my stomach grumbling, and everyone’s tummy did it at some point, but it still made me feel ashamed. I could almost hear my mom’s voice in my head, scoffing and telling me how unladylike it was.

Luckily, the girl didn’t seem to care. She looked up, catching my eye, and her smile shifted from a smirk. It became kinder, and I saw no hint of mocking on her face as she opened her mouth to speak.

“The noodles are great,” she told me, nodding towards my tray. “I have no idea why, but just a warning: they only serve them like once a month, so eat your fill when you can.”

An awkward laugh slipped from my mouth. I was relieved that she didn’t say something mean, but I had no idea how to respond to that.

“Uhh, thanks,” I replied. “You too?”

It came out more like a question than I meant it to, but the girl didn’t seem to notice. She’d already looked away, her eyes fixed on the tray of noodles.

“Oh, I plan to,” she said as she began scooping it onto her plate.

I hesitated for just a moment, feeling like I should say something more to the girl before giving up and stepping away. My gaze returned to the tables, and I looked for Seth again as I made my way towards the drinks area at the end. I couldn’t see whether Seth already had a jug of water, though. The boy’s body was blocking the table from sight, and I deliberated for just a second before grabbing one anyway.

It wasn’t the end of the world if he did. We’d just have two on the table, and that was fine. It would get drunk, and even if it didn’t, it wasn’t a problem. It was just water, after all, I told myself as I started to head towards Seth and the boy.

They were still talking, but Seth looked up as I approached, and I saw the moment he spotted me. The troubled expression slipped from his face immediately, being replaced by a smile, and he said something to the other boy. The boy glanced over his shoulder at me, his posture straightening before he muttered something to Seth and began to stand.

“Alright, I’ll talk to you in class tomorrow?” I heard him say.

“Yeah, talk then,” Seth mumbled, looking at his tray again as the guy began to walk away.

He nodded at me as he passed, and I returned the gesture, a polite smile on my lips. I didn’t know the guy was bullying Seth, I told myself. There was no point in being rude to him until I did.

“Hey,” I said as I took a seat opposite Seth and placed the jug of water on the table between us.

I’d been right to grab it, I realised. Seth was normally the one who brought over water for us, but he must have forgotten because all he had was an almost empty cup.

“Hey,” Seth responded. “Oh, thank you. I was going to go up and get a jug a few minutes ago, but Nathan came over and wouldn’t stop talking.”

The distasteful expression on Seth’s face told me he wasn’t particularly happy about that.

“Don’t worry,” I told him, hesitating before adding, “Everything okay? I mean, it seemed like you guys were having a kind of deep conversation when I came over?”

Seth let out a laugh, but it sounded somewhat forced.

“No, no, it’s fine. He was trying to explain something Fred mentioned earlier, but he went off on a tangent.”

“Oh?” I said as I picked up my fork.

“Yeah, I didn’t understand something and made the mistake of asking Nathan about it because he seemed to get it in class, but...” Seth trailed off, a grimace on his face.

I couldn’t help but laugh as relief washed over me. Seth could have been lying to me, but it didn’t feel like he was. I knew that the law tutor often threw out a lot of terminology and information, more than the first aid tutor I’d just had, so it made sense that Seth was feeling lost. Obviously, I still planned on keeping an eye on him and Nathan, just in case I did see Nathan being mean, but I felt better about it.

“Ah… how did that go?” I asked.

Seth shook his head as he sighed loudly.

“Honestly? The legal system in the UK is a complete mess, and I think Nathan is even more confused about it than I am.”

Another laugh slipped from my mouth.

“Oh no, really?”

“Yeah,” Seth told me. “I mean… he said he understands, but there is no way what he just said is true. It can’t be.”
