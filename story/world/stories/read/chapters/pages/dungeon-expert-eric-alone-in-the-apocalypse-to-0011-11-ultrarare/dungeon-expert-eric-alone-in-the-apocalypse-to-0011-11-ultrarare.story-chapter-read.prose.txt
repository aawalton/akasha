Eric opened the rewards tab and hovered over the ultrarare rewards. They shimmered slightly gold, unlike the ordinary rewards (no shine) and the rares (silver). He clicked on the first, and a small gold box appeared before his eyes, hovering in midair with absolutely no support. It shook once, twice, then burst with golden light, and an item appeared and dropped out of the air. Eric lunged, barely catching the blurry shape before it hit the floor, then realized belatedly that there was a very good chance it was an unsheathed blade and snatched his hands back, dropping it like a hot potato. A book tumbled to the ground, landing on its edge and falling open.

Oh. A little embarrassed, Eric leaned forward and picked up the book. It closed naturally as he picked it up, the cover pointing up. He read the title, and his eyes lit up. Firebolt. A spellbook! A second spell! And more than that: an attack spell. A real spell. Not that Regeneration wasn’t a real spell, but as useful as it was, it didn’t give him an attack.

Read spellbook? Y/N

“Yes,” Eric said quickly.

The book opened on its own in his hands, and the pages flipped before his eyes. Just like the first time, words flowed into his brain without him understanding them at all. This time, they weren’t foreign words, but instead English, but for all that, they didn’t make any sense to him. They flowed without purpose or meaning, and even when he deliberately captured some and forced himself to examine them, they still meant nothing.

At the same time, his mana ran wildly though him in reaction to the spellbook. His body heated up, growing feverish. His mind began to ache; not as fiercely as the first time, but nonetheless, a dull ache built up behind his temples, pounding into his brain. Focus. Get through this. It had passed the first time. It would pass the second time.

Knowledge wiggled into his brain. One iota at a time, it squeezed into place. As it did, Eric moved his mana, obeying the urges of his new instincts. Regeneration moved his mana around his whole body, taking it out of the central repository somewhere behind his belly button and pushing it back into himself; he could then push it specifically toward problem spots to heal faster. Firebolt, on the other hand, had much simpler pathways. He shoved his mana into almost a straight line from his core to his palm, then called the spell to mind, the knowledge in the spellbook, the heat and the fire. He swirled the mana in his palm, then pushed.

A teeny mote of fire popped out of his hand and fizzled its way down to the floor, about as dangerous as a spark. Eric looked at it, then looked at his hand. He licked his lips. Let’s call it a work in progress.

It wasn’t great yet, but it was an attack spell. He had an attack spell, and no one could take that away from him. With that in mind, he opened the final ultrarare reward.

Once again, a gold gift appeared, then unwrapped itself. What looked like a clump of leaves fell out. Eric caught it, not particularly worried about razor blades in a pile of leaves. The leaves landed in his hands. They were slightly damp and a little decayed, as if they’d just been fished out of a pile of undergrowth. A few mystical symbols scrawled across the surface of the leaves. He stared at them, and after a moment, the System provided a handy tool tip:

Familiar (Druidic Spell) (Ritual)

Locate the animal you wish to procure as your familiar, then invoke this spell and bind it to you. In the event that your familiar dies, the spell will reform, allowing you to bind a new one. Animal cannot be hostile to or more powerful than the caster.

Eric snorted to himself. That’s cheery. He hadn’t even picked a familiar yet, and the spell was already expecting the poor thing to die. Folding his arms, he thought for a second. A non-hostile animal…? That ruled out 90% of what he’d encountered so far, not that he particularly wanted a cockroach or snake for a familiar. One exception stood out in his mind: the horned rabbit. From day one, it had sat on the edge of the clearing, chewing the undergrowth and paying Eric no mind. As far as animals went, it was the only one that hadn’t attacked him or at least exhibited hostility toward him since the world had changed.

It was still a wild animal, but it was all he had. He couldn’t go beg his sister for one of her pet rabbits; she was off somewhere, probably in the Tutorial. If he wanted to use the spell, the rabbit really was the best candidate he had to hand.

He could wait, he supposed. Wait until he ran into another animal that wasn’t hostile toward him. But how useful was a familiar? Was it worth waiting? Or would it only be valuable for a short time period, in which case he would have wasted his ultrarare reward by putting it in his back pocket? Besides, there were upsides to a small, portable familiar. If he made a horse his familiar (putting aside that horses were massively more powerful than humans), he couldn’t take it into tight spaces or use it to scout holes he couldn’t see the end of. Rabbits, on the other hand, could fit in his pocket for easy toting and act as a scout. Anywhere he could go, a rabbit could also go.

Let’s go see if it’s still around. He was sitting here convincing himself that it was a good idea, but he didn’t even know if the rabbit was nearby. He headed for the door… only to find that the grate had already descended. It was night, and the safe zone was closed.

Eric frowned. The alarm hadn’t played. Usually, it played a siren every night when the gate closed. Did they forgo that now that the show was on? Or was it a special service for the first episode?

No way to know, and no real point in pondering it. He went back to his nest for the night, with the intention of giving his new familiar spell a try first thing in the morning.

The clatter of the rising gate woke him up bright and early the next morning. Eric got up, got some coffee, had a nice fresh donut from the donut case (glazed, today; he was feeling basic), and headed for the door, only to find a neat brown paper package waiting for him on the welcome mat. He knelt, set his coffee down beside the package, and untied the twine one-handedly, and a jacket and handkerchief made of shiny black snakeskin tumbled out.

Right! I almost forgot in the excitement of the episode. My new gear!

Finishing off the donut, he rinsed his fingers in the gas station’s surprisingly clean toilets, then pulled the jacket on. It fit him like a glove, perfectly wrapping his body in cool leather. The long coat hung to his knees, and a hood hung around his shoulders. He tied the handkerchief around his neck and felt like an adventurer for the first time since the world had ended.

Equipped: Blackscale Coat (Hides mana, DEF +2, MDEF + 5)

Equipped: Blackscale Handkerchief (Hides mana)

Eric snorted. He’d almost doubled his defense by equipping the coat. If that wasn’t worth the tokens, he didn’t know what was. More importantly, he wouldn’t be broadcasting his mana through the forest the way all the monsters around him were. It wasn’t hugely important yet, but if any magical beasts lurked in the woods, he’d rather have the jump on them than the other way around.

Feeling a little bit safer (even if only a little bit), he wandered out into the cool of early dawn. He scanned the undergrowth with his Mana Sight, searching for the horned rabbit. A cluster of cockroaches hung out in the shade of a tree; a few birds sat high on the branches, watching him through the shifting leaves. And down in the corner, not far from its usual hideout, the horned rabbit chewed the undergrowth, regarding him with its usual lack of concern.

He withdrew the familiar spell from his inventory and checked the conditions again. Non-hostile, check. Weaker than him… hopefully check. It was a tiny rabbit. There was no chance it was more powerful than him, right?

Well… let me check my stats just to be sure. He’d leveled up twice from clearing the snake dungeon and hadn’t checked his stats yet. It couldn’t hurt to take a peek. It didn’t actually make a difference, since his stats were there whether he looked at them or not, but it would reassure him, and that counted for something.

Defense was also growing slowly, which he still didn’t have an answer for. Was he inherently a not-very-defensive person, or was it some kind of hidden drop-stat associated with the mage class? He didn’t know, and he didn’t know how to find out, so aside from keeping an eye on it, he left it be. Across two levels, he’d gotten at least one point in everything, so that was nice. With a combined stat total of 67, he probably had more stats than a rabbit. Probably.

His new Firebolt spell had registered as a spell, but he hadn’t gained the Familiar spell yet. Is it because I didn’t cast it? Or is there something else I’m missing? He did still have the Familiar spellbook, after all. Maybe he wouldn’t get the spell until the book vanished. Whatever it was, it didn’t matter, as long as he could use it in a moment.

Eric pulled the spell out of his inventory. The clump of leaves looked just like it had last night, and the strange symbols on it didn’t make any more sense now than they had then. It didn’t matter, though; that was what the System was for. Eyeing the horned rabbit, he activated the spell.

The leaves shone, then burned up from left to right, as if he’d taken a lighter to one end of them, though there was no visible flame. This time, his head didn’t hurt; instead, it felt as though someone had applied a brand to his stomach. Startled, he lifted his shirt to reveal a single ember crawling over his skin. As it crawled, it left a mark behind it that shone bright red, then settled down to black. It formed a small outline of a leaf turning over just above his hip bone. Down on the forest floor, the rabbit jolted and pawed at its belly as (presumably, he couldn’t tell through the fur) the same mark appeared on it.

The second the spellbook vanished, Familiar appeared on his spell list, though it was grayed out, indicating it couldn’t be used. Eric breathed out as the pain faded from his side and surveyed his new, slightly cringey tattoo, then looked at the rabbit. There it was. His familiar.

So… what does that do for me?

He realized, a little too late, that at no point in the process had he learned what a Familiar would do for him. He’d gotten the spell and been so fixated on meeting the qualifications to activate it that he’d never thought of what a Familiar actually did. Sure, he’d thought about it scouting for him and so on and so forth, but it wasn’t like the spell had actually said Familiars could scout. That was just him guessing, with no real information.

“Hi,” he tried, not really sure what to do. Talking to it seemed like a good start.

The rabbit sniffed the ground, then lifted its cute pink nose and regarded him with its black button eyes. “Hello,” it greeted him in a deep, smooth basso.

Holy shit, how does the tiny rabbit have a deeper voice than me? Eric cleared his throat and continued in a slightly deeper tone. “I… guess you’re my familiar now?”

I’m pretty sure that’s not what noblesse oblige means, Eric thought, but kept it to himself. Arguing with a rabbit was a bad look. “Come on. We’ve got to make it to the next safe room by dark.”
