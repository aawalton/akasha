It wasn't Vayren's death glare that caught Vir's attention, nor even the congratulatory announcement from the proctor over a duel well fought.

No, all of that was noise compared to what Vir saw in the last vestiges of Prana Vision before it cut out. That sun of lightning prana which Vir knew was but a small sliver of the overall ocean lurking deep underground, somewhere in the depths of this labyrinth.

Unfortunately, Vir soon found himself looking at mere afterimages as his prana ran dry and Maiya's inscription finally faded, burning away entirely.

Without even realizing it, Vir had walked out of the room and was halfway toward the staircase that led down when he froze in his tracks.

What could I even do in my current state? He wondered, and the answer was obvious. Not much.

Only one thing could possibly possess a prana signature so bright and large. Yet if there was a Vimana down there—one that had been powered on, no less—only the full might of Vir's entire arsenal would have any hope of actually getting there, even if all he wished to do was scout. He needed Dance of the Shadow Demon for that.

As for what this Vimana was doing so deep underground or how it had been activated? Vir didn't have the slightest clue.

Vir ground his teeth. He could either wait for Maiya to inscribe another tattoo and then wait days until he'd converted enough prana to attempt to go down there, or he could take matters into his own hands.

By now, after six tattoos inscribed upon him and after inscribing the same on Maiya's back, Vir was intimately familiar with how to etch it. Furthermore, he didn't need Lightning affinity to go down there. Rather, Lightning wouldn’t allow him to activate Dance of the Shadow Demon, which relied on Ash prana. He just needed to access that, and the inscription for that was far simpler.

He was certain that, should he be able to do it himself, he could create a tattoo that would perfectly channel the latent prana in his body, wresting it back under his control.

But Maiya was busy with her own exam and would surely be caught up in the celebrations after. Yes, she'd drop everything in the blink of an eye to help, but again, even with her astonishing improvement, Vir feared her skills would not be up to the task.

Now that he knew of the Vimana beneath their feet, he couldn't afford to wait. He needed his strength, and he needed it fast.

The decision made, Vir turned on his heel and headed for the exit. Students milled about in the corridors, some celebrating their victories while others nursed their wounded pride. A few glanced at him, but Vir paid them little mind, and they looked away.

His thoughts were already racing ahead, cataloging what he would need. The inscription ink would be simple enough to prepare, if crude. There, too, he’d learned from the batch he’d prepared for Maiya, to say nothing of all he’d learned by working alongside Saunak. The real challenge would be the execution—there was a reason even master Thaumaturges never inscribed their own tattoos. Forgetting the pain, attempting to carve one's own back was no simple feat.

But then, Vir had never shied away from difficult tasks.

As such, he quickly returned to the place Maiya had rented, sliding his mechanical key into the door lock—something she'd specifically requested, claiming it was more reliable than magic in a city where everything was powered by it—before moving in front of the large full-length mirror that had come furnished with the home.

In one hand, he held Maiya's hand mirror, using it to bounce off the main mirror to see his back. Stripping off his clothes, he braced his katar in front of the mirror, securing it so that it pointed directly away. The Artifact blade would serve as his stylus. Something he very much doubted the Imperium of the other universe had in mind when they gifted it to him. Still, its sublime sharpness made it the perfect tool for carving the inscription without cutting too deep.

Vir took a moment to evaluate his handiwork in the mirror, studying the angles and the way the light caught the reflection. This would be both painful and arduous, yet Vir felt he had little choice.

Several clocks were counting down and sometimes recklessness trumped caution.

Burning just the tiniest sliver of his soul energy to create enough prana to work with was one of the most uncomfortable sensations Vir had ever felt, reminding him of all the years he spent in his mindscape mending the damage that had once been done.

Luckily, his Heart Chakra mended whatever tiny amount of damage he did, erasing any permanent damage.

Channeling the prana into a bowl of water, the liquid took on a slightly black sheen owing to his affinity.

Then he pricked his finger to add blood to the concoction, which mixed with the deep black, and he stirred the mixture to form a very rudimentary inscription ink.

Saunak would scoff, of course, having long ago mastered the art. Vir was sure its efficiency was likely a tenth of what the genius was capable of. But in lieu of anything better, he dipped the blade in the concoction and got to work.

The pain of the cut hardly bothered Vir, considering all he’d been through. Rather, what proved more difficult was the angle and the awkwardness of trying to inscribe patterns at precise depths on a part of his body he could only see through a double reflection.

Vir fell into the task. Time ceased to have meaning as he worked through each incision, ensuring the tattoo came out perfect. His world became the mirror, the katar’s tip, and the tattoo on his back. Blood trickled down his spine, but he wiped it away methodically and continued.

Thus far, he was quite proud of his progress. It was so simple now that he understood his earlier mistake.

Vir had been attempting to solve two problems with one inscription. Conversion to the various affinities could come later, once he'd regained control of the latent prana coursing through his blood. He had passed his battle exam, after all. He wouldn't be needing to fool anyone else for some time.

No, what he needed now was access. Pure, unfettered access to the power that was rightfully his.

As he'd thought, the inscription was simpler and far easier to inscribe, and thus far more robust as well, able to conduct many times more prana. Where Maiya's affinity converter had been an intricate web of inscriptions of various depths, this was more straightforward.

And so, after enduring no small amount of effort, Vir finished the inscription for the temporary tattoo. He couldn't imagine how much more effort a permanent tattoo would have taken, piercing far deeper into his skin than the topmost layer of skin as this one did.

The tattoo itself was elegant in its simplicity. A series of interconnected spirals radiating out from a central point between his shoulder blades, each spiral representing a different pathway for his prana to flow. It wouldn't win any awards for artistry, but it would serve its purpose. Hopefully.

Tentatively, Vir ran prana through the new tattoo.

The sensation was immediate. It was releasing a flood that had been held back for far too long. Vir gasped as power rushed through him, his Ash Prana responding to his will for the first time since arriving in this cursed universe.

To his relief, he found that it functioned exactly as he intended. Just as before with Maiya's tattoo, the latent Ash Prana within his body coursed through the tattoo,returning into his body. Except this time, it was his to command.

The recycler tattoo, as he called it, worked wonders. Soon, the trickle of prana that Vir regained full control of became a stream as he decoupled it from his blood and cycled it faster and faster through his body, with each new bit of Ash Prana accelerating the conversion process.

It was many times more efficient than the tattoo Maiya had carved, and the prana it gave him was more potent, being the highest form of all the affinities.

Even then, however, Vir estimated it would take several days for him to recover that vast ocean of prana he'd stockpiled over the years. Not that this came as a surprise. Vir had expanded his body's carrying capacity well beyond anything most mortals could achieve through years of cultivation and the consumption of countless prana-rich materials. His body was closer to that of a Wyrm than any human or demon at this point.

Still, it was not lost on him that a book and a professor had been the reason for his breakthroughs.

He'd entered the Altani Academy thinking it nothing but a waste of time. An obstacle placed in his path by those who controlled the universe. Another hoop to jump through.

Now, he saw it as the opportunity it was, for he might never have learned these secrets without their help. The fundamental principles of prana generation in this universe and how it burned the soul, the mechanics of conversion… all of it had been with the aid of the Academy, even if they’d just provided the first hint.

Surely an unintended consequence of the Fateweavers' meddling. They'd sought to trap him here, to slow him down. Perhaps even break him. Instead, they'd given him the keys to unlock powers he'd never dreamed possible.

How ironic, Vir thought, that their prison has become my forge.

Or had it, and was this all part of a bigger plot on their part? He would likely never know.

It only took an hour before Vir felt confident venturing into the labyrinth again. He wouldn't be able to consume magic at the rate he was used to, but if he was careful with his use of Dance of the Shadow Demon, he'd have enough to get in and out, and if need be, even use some of his other powers. Perhaps even Balancer of Scales, though he'd need to be judicious with that.

Vir flared his prana, reveling in the feeling of prana erupting all over his skin. It had not been long since he'd lost access to his power, but it had felt like an eternity. He cycled his prana faster and faster, basking in the simple pleasure of feeling whole again.

He couldn't wait to tell Maiya of his discovery, but it would have to.

She'd undoubtedly demolished her own opponent by now and would be out celebrating with the others. The thought brought a genuine smile to Vir's face.

Let her have her fun, Vir thought. She certainly deserves it.

Maiya had sacrificed so much to follow him on this mad quest across universes. The least he could do was let her have a few hours of fun.

Besides, it was easier to travel through the shadow realm alone. As powerful as Maiya was, she’d be a liability on this outing.

And so Vir donned his armor and left his room, beginning the walk back to the Academy. To the same halls where he'd just dueled Vayren, where he'd won through luck and Maiya's remarkable handiwork.

But this time, he would go lower than ever before.

This time, he was prepared.
