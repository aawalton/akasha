Caleb grabbed a pickax off the floor using his Imperium, holding it at his shoulder with the hopes it would be enough of a disguise to fool the brain addled guards.

He tried to exude confidence as he walked, implying to the onlookers that he could in fact see when he at best had a vague awareness of the cavern around him. Thankfully as he rounded the bend he saw the flickering glow of lantern light.

“There’s no ore in this chamber,” One of the guards said to Caleb, and then to his partner added, “These stupid taken. Can hardly follow a simple instruction.”

Taken? Caleb fixated on the word. The stupid ones were taken against their will, maybe?

In any case, the ones ahead of him were not stupid, so he had to act quickly before they grew suspicious. Using the hand he held his pickax with, he threw it at the guard on the left who’d not spoke and never looked away.

He propelled the ax forward with the full force of his willpower and it crashed into the chest of the guard handle first. Caleb still couldn’t accurately throw a Imperium empowered weapon that spun as it flew  but the force was enough to bend the man over in pain.

As soon as the ax had left his hand, Caleb pushed off the mine cart rails behind himself and used them to fly at the guard who’d spoken. He was still wide eyed from the sudden attack and hadn’t gathered his wits about him enough yet to respond. Caleb covered the distance in a blink and brought the spiked end of his hammer down on his neck.

Adult Dark Archer (error) has been killed.

He finished off the second.

Adult Dark Archer (error) has been killed.

With each death darkness bubbles from the room beyond vanished. Half the room had be obscured as such while the other half was lit by lanterns. The previously lit half was filled with the villagers Magnus has spoken of. The cavern was divided in half by a large metal fence that went up to the ceiling and the opposite side was furnished comfortably for the dozens of people within. Piecing things together Caleb suspected these were the ones who couldn’t be converted to this Benefactors cause.

He reassessed as he saw it was mostly the elderly and children.

Or maybe the Benefactor didn’t want these… That would imply there’s a limit to what he can do and he didn’t want to waste it converting the weak.

The other half of the room had rows of manacled individuals secured directly to the walls.

“It’s clear!” Caleb called back up the tunnel.

“Caleb?” the familiar voice of Grisim called.

“Yeah,” Caleb said as relief at his brother’s continued existence flooded him. He turned to see his brother on the opposite end of the cavern, secured to the wall with manacles with two other men he recognized from the initialized scout group Grisim had been assigned to.

His relief didn’t last long.

“Bastard!”

“Wait, what?” Caleb asked in surprise.

“You ruined my heroic escape!”

To emphasize his point he ripped his hands away from the wall, pulling the manacles out with them.

“I’m sorry, I’ll try to get captured in the rescue attempt next time so you can save both of us.”

“That would be great actually.”

Caleb went up to Grisim and gave him a quick hug.

He quested out with his senses and probed all the manacles with his Imperium to sense the shape of them and then with a small exertion of mana he unlocked all the manacles at once while also unlocking the door to the cell full of villagers. The combined sound of unlocking metal echoes through the stone room.

“We get it, you didn’t need our help,” Kelia said, walking over to the villages. “But these people do and we need to get out of here.”

The room was lit by half a dozen lanterns, and Caleb ripped them from the wall with his magic and distributed them to the more sure footed of the villagers while Kelia went around gathering blanked and making sure those less dressed for the outside weather had enough clothing. She gave an elderly woman the cloak off her own back and another the leather jacket she wore beneath it.

Both women refused her adamantly.

“I’ll be fine,” Kelia said, causing ice to crystalize all over the snow white Frigidi clothing she’d still worn beneath the disguise.

Mettius interrogated the scouts and villagers while they all prepared to leave, and as they were heading out he gave an update to Kelia and Caleb.

“These people are the families of those who joined the rebels but refused to join themselves. The rest were forcibly turned or killed.

“My half-sister Cassandra is behind this. Somehow—probably from the ‘Benefactor’—she gained the ability to grant classes. She has a shadow assassin class and these dark archers are likely her doing. If we run into her, let me do the talking, but just know we need to kill her. Be prepared to act.”

“Why do we have to kill her?” Caleb asked.

“Because if we don’t, she’ll kill me.”

“Why would she want to do that? I thought you weren’t in the running for the throne,” Kelia asked.

“I’m not, but… the royal bloodline rewards fratricide.”

“As in the bloodline bloodline?” Caleb asked,

Mettius nodded.

Caleb thought over the implicational of that.

“How many has she killed?”

“Enough.”

At a warning look from Kelia, Caleb refrained from asking any of the follow up questions he really wanted to ask

Caleb and Mettius lead the way with Grisim, Kelia, and the last scout bringing up the rear. With even the dim light of the lanterns, the way back was clear as day to Caleb’s enhanced perception. The way out weas much faster than in, even with the crowded group of people shuffling along behind. As they neared the entrance, Caleb drew the wind into the cave to prevent the sound of their approach escaping as long as possible. Once he knew they were close to the exit, Caleb and Mettius pushed the cart out while Kelia and Grisim came behind to help in case they failed to ambush

To their luck, they encountered no other miners on their exit and they passed between the two guards outside with only a cursory inspection. As soon as the guards turned back to scan for intruders as they had before, Caleb and Mettius both attacked, each taking out a guard with a blow or spell. Quickly they put the bodies into the cart and covered it, sending the signal inside that it was all clear.

The view of the mine was obstructed by buildings and the area was not populated. They had time to gather all the captives and tell them the plan. A plan that began when Caleb signaled to Bog to light the fall on fire. It only took a minute for the smoke to be visible to the group, and Caleb saw the watchers on the wall staring in confusion at the smoke, unsure of what to do.

The wall was fully up in flames before anyone moved to do anything about it. They couldn’t see beyond the buildings but the spread of the fire slowed, and Caleb heard the commotion of many people at work to battle the flames.

“Let’s break for the front gate,” Mettius told the group.

As one, the unified group of the Benefactor’s rejects made their way through town. By then every stall was deserted as all hands were at work to extinguish the fire. They made it to the gates before they ran into anyone and two guards stood up above facing out towards the woods. Each received a magical bolt of force or frost to their backs.

Caleb regretted that they’d had to commit such violence in front of the children—especially to people they likely knew—but it was either that or let them be recaptured. As was the plan, Grisim and the other scout let the captives out the gate while the Equites stayed back to serve as a rear guard. Grisim had protested, but he was the best suite for escorting a group of people through the woods.

Well, Kelia would be best, but both she and Caleb agreed it would be best for the exhausted Grisim to not stay behind and get wrapped up in a fight.

“I leveled.” Kelia told Caleb as the villagers were filing past them.

Kelia had lamented not leveling in their training but took no joy now that it had happened. Killing a brainwashed victim was not something to be happy about even if leveling was often a occasion to celebrate.

“I’m sorry,” Caleb said, reading her mood and gave her a brief hug.

Just as the last person made it out the gate, a high tongue accented voice called out over the distant sound of fire fighting.

“Mettius is that you?”
