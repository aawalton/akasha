Lira had never interacted with the First Prince before, but she recognized him immediately.

Unlike his younger brothers, there was something deeply unsettling about him. Maybe it was the complete lack of warmth in his eyes, or perhaps it was the lazy smile that never quite reached them. Whatever the reason, simply looking at him made the hairs on the back of her neck stand on end.

The First Prince slowly surveyed the room, taking his time as though admiring the changes.

The library was nearly empty compared to the morning rush. Only Prince Lyth, his bodyguard, and the three young nobles remained. Kavard gave his younger brother a brief nod when their eyes met before his attention drifted elsewhere.

Finally, his gaze settled on Lira, and his smile widened.

He approached at a leisurely pace, taking slow, deliberate steps. Only when he stood directly in front of her desk did he speak.

"You must be the new library caretaker."

His expression was probably meant to be charming, but to Lira, it looked like a predator baring its teeth.

"Yes, Your Highness."

"Wonderful." His smile grew a fraction wider. "I must say, you've done an excellent job with this place."

"Thank you, Your Highness."

"This library has always held a special place in my heart." His voice became almost nostalgic. "When I was younger, I used to sneak away from my lessons and play here with a friend."

Lira offered a polite smile, saying nothing.

"Her name was Mara." His eyes locked onto hers, carefully studying every twitch of her expression. "Do you know her?"

"I am sorry, Your Highness, but I don't think I know any lady with that name."

Lira was careful with her wording, just in case of truth spells. She didn't know any noblewoman with the name Mara, and it was safe to assume that the prince would have been playing with nobles. Plus, her past had come to save her yet again. Years of dealing with scammers, liars, and thieves had taught Lira how to maintain an unshakable poker face, even if, internally, she was on the brink of panic.

"Figures," he replied casually. "She left before your time."

His gaze drifted toward the staircase leading to the upper floor.

"Still..." he continued. "I've been looking forward to meeting you."

He paused just long enough to make her uncomfortable.

"Especially after today."

"Today?" Lira asked, though her pulse had already begun to quicken.

"You didn't hear?" He tilted his head with feigned surprise. "Hesta's final words."

For a brief moment, Lira's heart stopped.

"What about them?"

"She claimed you framed her," Kavard chuckled as though discussing the weather.

"What?" Lira forced confusion rather than guilt onto her face.

"'The seal was hidden in the library... The new caretaker must have framed me.'" He laughed softly. "Those were the last words she ever spoke."

Damn woman. Still causing me problems even from the grave. How petty can one person be? Lira couldn't help thinking.

"Of course," Kavard continued, "the guards ignored her. They had already heard her admit that she was the one who found the dagger. Changing her story moments before her death sounded like nothing more than the desperate ramblings of a condemned woman."

He leaned casually forward, resting a hand on her desk as he loomed over her.

"But personally..." His eyes narrowed ever so slightly. "I have my doubts."

A cold chill crawled down Lira's spine.

Was he testing her? Was he just playing with her? Or did he actually suspect something?

She opened her mouth, intending to respond before the silence became suspicious...

That's when the library doors slammed open.

Prince Aric strode inside with purpose, Sir Deren following close behind, one hand already resting on the hilt of his sword.

"Brother," Kavard sighed dramatically. "Are you following me now?"

Aric didn't even acknowledge the question.

Without answering, he walked straight up to the First Prince, grabbed him firmly by the shoulder, and guided him several steps away from Lira's desk toward the center of the library.

Sir Deren, on the other hand, immediately stepped between Kavard and Lira, placing himself squarely in the prince's path.

"Paldina. Picos. Hishar. Tarasana. Kurur," Aric said, the words echoing clearly through the library.

Lira blinked. This was the first time she had seen him cast magic openly.

When Aric spoke again, no sound reached her ears.

Interesting... A soundproofing spell.

She silently committed every syllable to memory for later study.

Fortunately, Lira had a solution to counter that spell.

"Pika. Velar. Syneth... Pika. Selan. Tureth," she whispered, making sure the spell didn't reach Deren's ears. She was safe casting it herself because of a bookmark hidden in one of the books behind her.

As her enhanced perception sharpened, she focused on their lips instead.

"You are not supposed to be here," Aric said.

"What do you mean, brother?" Kavard replied with exaggerated innocence. "This entire palace is my birthright. There isn't a single room that is forbidden to me."

"You know exactly what I mean." Aric's expression remained cold.

"You are not permitted to leave your quarters unless Sir Estephon is accompanying you."

Kavard's pleasant smile twitched.

"This is ridiculous." His expression hardened. "I can't believe my own brother stabbed me in the back."

"I did no such thing."

"This entire punishment exists because you brought those letters to Father instead of me." Kavard spread his hands dramatically. "You're supposed to be my brother, my ally, my general, my second-in-command."

"Kavard..." Aric's face became noticeably firmer. "You had three maids murdered."

"So?"

The answer came instantly.

"They were peasants." He shrugged without the slightest hint of remorse. "I have always behaved properly around noble daughters. I'm not an idiot."

For the first time since entering the library, genuine disbelief crossed Aric's face.

"I can't believe you're saying that,” said the second prince. “It’s like I hardly know you anymore."

"Why not?" Kavard replied with another shrug.

He glanced toward Lira.

"Look at her." His finger pointed openly in her direction.

Lira startled a little, thinking she had been caught spying.

"Freckles, and that hideous orange hair." He smiled as though discussing livestock. "No noble would ever possess such disgusting traits."

Lira kept her face perfectly still, but inside, she was already imagining where to bury the body.

Aric stepped toward his brother and grabbed him by both shoulders, this time tightening his grip.

"Do not approach her." The warmth vanished completely from his face. "She is under my protection."

Kavard blinked, then burst into laughter.

"Oh?" He wiped an imaginary tear from his eye. "Little brother, I didn't realize you enjoyed mud fishing."

He looked around theatrically.

"I could have helped you find a much prettier catch."

"No." Aric's answer was immediate. "I have absolutely no interest in her."

Lira's eyebrow twitched.

"She is assisting me with some research." His gaze never left his brother. "So stay away from her."

"What if I don't?"

"Don't test me."

For several long seconds, the two princes locked eyes, neither moving. Eventually, something in Aric's expression made Kavard reconsider.

He looked away first. With a small sigh, he straightened his coat.

"As you wish, dear brother."

He turned back toward Lira. That same pleasant smile remained fixed on his face. He gave her a small, almost friendly nod before walking calmly toward the exit.

Aric followed him immediately, but Sir Deren turned and tried to give her a reassuring smile. However, he couldn't mask the worry in his eyes.

"Sorry about that," he said. "You know how siblings are."

Lira just nodded.

With a casual salute, he exited the library, following in his friend's footsteps.

The moment the doors closed, Lira finally exhaled. She hadn't realized she had been holding her breath. Her hands trembled beneath the desk.

Second Prince Aric's protection or not... Lira knew, beyond a shadow of a doubt, that one day she was going to kill Kavard.

Unfortunately, he was going to be far more difficult to eliminate than Thadwick.
