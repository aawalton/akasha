The morning light filtered through the church’s modest windows, casting a warm glow over the wooden interior.

Everything looked quite different in the daylight, the sun brightening up the place like a fresh layer of paint!

Starting from the simple wooden pews – now clearly revealing their age through countless scratches and faded carvings, too faded to discern what they used to be – down to the plain but well-maintained walls. Yet they didn’t lack in scribbled out ‘decor’ in places, many of them covered by simple and very childish pictures.

A smile tugged at Elynara’s lips at some of the silly stick figures there. Were they fighting dragons? And was that flower swinging a sword?

Children had all the imagination!

Finally descending the creaky stairs, the scent of fresh bread and warm broth tickled her nose. The inviting aroma guided her toward the hall where yesterday’s meal had been served.

Voices filtered in, high-pitched and lively.

As she stepped through the doorway, her gaze landed on one of the long tables, partially occupied by a group of small children wearing dull, patched-up clothes with a young nun caring for them.

She couldn’t help but smile at the sight.

The little ones chattered among themselves, their voices rising and falling in chaotic harmony. Crumbs dotted their cheeks as they eagerly scooped spoonfuls of porridge into their mouths, some clearly more successful than others.

And there, sitting at another table with Father Alaric, was a girl no older than twelve.

She hugged a white furball of a creature to her chest, its fluff so overgrown that only two cute little ears and a tiny twitching tail stuck out from the mass of fur.

The way the girl clung to it made it look like she was hugging a stuffed toy brought to life.

Ugh, now she wanted to hug it too!

Elynara was still taking it all in when a faint hum of buzzing warmth brushed against her cheek. She barely had a moment to react before her own fuzzball booped her square on the nose.

She yelped in surprise, her voice echoing louder than intended.

The sprite seemed to laugh at her in its own buzzing way, before darting into her still-messy bed hair, burying itself like it had found the coziest nest in existence.

The audacity! It’s lucky she was too hungry to lecture it!

Puffing out her cheeks, she turned back towards the room.

Her startled sound must have been louder than she thought, because the children at the table turned to look at her with wide, curious eyes.

“Who’s that pretty big sister?”

“Her hair looks funny!”

Elynara’s cheeks flushed slightly, a mix of embarrassment and amusement settling in her chest. She was about to react when Father Alaric’s voice cut through the chatter.

“Now, now, children. Let our guest have her breakfast before you swarm her with questions.”

The children’s collective groan of disappointment made her stifle a laugh. It was clear Father Alaric had dealt with their enthusiasm before.

The man rose from his seat, gently patting the little girl’s shoulder as he finished his conversation with her.

“Off you go now, Mina. And make sure to help Sister Elara with the chores before running off to play.”

“Yes, Father Alaric!”

The girl chirped back, cuddling her fluffy little creature close before hurrying over to join the others. The little beast in her arms gave Elynara a curious glance in passing.

Father Alaric’s gaze settled on Elynara, his eyes twinkling with quiet amusement.

“Ah, good morning, Elynara. I trust you slept well?”

She nodded, still feeling the warmth of the sprite snuggling against her hair.

It had been a long time since she slept in a proper bed, not that she had much memory of what counted as ‘long’ anymore. The stiffness in her limbs was fading, and the warmth of a proper meal was sure to make her feel alive again.

“Better than I expected, honestly. The bed was really comfortable.”

“I’m glad to hear that.”

He motioned toward the spot across from him.

“Come, sit. Breakfast is still warm, and I imagine you’ll need the energy for your busy day.”

Elynara took the offered seat, a steaming bowl of porridge and fresh bread already waiting for her.

“Most of the older children have already gone out. They eat fast and are usually eager to tackle the day’s tasks.”

She glanced over at the younger children, now fully engaged in their breakfast again.

“They seem energetic enough.”

“They are. And far too curious for their own good.”

Elynara couldn’t help but smile at the fondness in his voice.

Something about the peaceful morning put her at ease. As if the warmth of the sunlight and the quiet bustling of the little ones had seeped into her bones, chasing away any lingering doubts she had about the place.

Wiggling a bit in her seat, she couldn’t help but ask about the most important of things!

“What was that fluffy furball? It looked so soft!”

Honest! Curiosity! Nothing to do with her sudden desire to steal and hug it too, no siree~!

“Ah, that? Little Mina quite likes animals and plans on being a tamer. So we got her a Pufflet to care for. Skittish creatures, hard to find in the wild, but easy enough to manage as pets.”

Important question answered, her tummy reminded her that breakfast could wait no longer.

Stuffing her face, she decided to make the most of the situation and steered the conversation toward the town.

“What kind of people live here?”

“Good, hardy folk. It’s a frontier town, away from politics, far removed from the hustle and bustle of the kingdoms.”

Father Alaric paused to sip on his tea. A twinkle in his eyes betraying the clear delight he took in seeing her squirm with interest.

“Most of those who moved here were human. But the place was hardly a complete wilderness. The forest people, like the ones you saw yesterday, already dwelled here in small tribes when we arrived. And had been living here for generations, long before the town came to be.”

She listened intently, nodding between bites.

She recalled the wolf-eared individuals she had seen in town.

They didn’t seem out of place, but there had been a certain distance between them and the humans. There was no hostility, just… quiet separation.

“Do they get along?”

“For the most part. There are differences, of course – cultural, sometimes even spiritual – but nothing too severe. The younger generation mingles more, but the older ones prefer their own kind.”

She supposed it made sense, though her memories remained utterly unhelpful in this case.

Continuing her breakfast, Elynara quietly listened to what else Alaric had to say about the place. Soon enough her meal was almost finished when something he said about the forest people caught her attention.

“What about elves?”

A shadow of nostalgia crossed his face.

“Elves once lived among us, but not anymore. In my youth, you would see one every now and then, travelers passing through or merchants trading rare goods. But they’ve grown reclusive.”

His gaze turned distant.

“I haven’t seen an elf in decades.”

She let that sink in. If elves had all but vanished from human towns, that meant something must have driven them away. But whatever the reason, Alaric didn’t seem inclined to elaborate.

Even when she asked him ‘What happened?’ he merely shook his head, an unreadable emotion flickering through his eyes.

Deciding to shift topics at the uncomfortable silence, she spoke about her journey from the forest, mentioning the altar and her trek to town.

Alaric listened attentively, but when she reached the part about arriving without trouble, his expression turned mildly incredulous.

“You didn’t run into any monsters?”

She shook her head, not certain why that was surprising.

“You’re quite lucky. Even if there aren’t many, most travelers run into at least one. They’re small, usually. Stray goblins, or mana-starved beasts seeking their next prey. But to meet none at all? That’s rare.”

A slow realization crept over her. She hadn’t even considered monsters a serious threat when walking through the woods. That was a dangerous level of ignorance.

“I guess I should be more careful.”

“You’ll need money, skills, and knowledge if you plan to stay. Relying on luck won’t take you far.”

She was beginning to see that.

A quiet moment stretched between them as they finished their meals. The warmth of the church, the simple steadiness of it all, was a stark contrast to the uncertainty that lingered in her mind.

In a completely new world, with a hazy recollection of her previous life and the journey since at best… Yet between her troublesome friend of a fuzzy companion she already made, and a helpful adult willing to help, the future didn’t seem all that bad.

Elynara hesitated a moment longer before bringing up the sprite, unsure how to frame it.

“I… also saw something else in the forest.”

Alaric raised a brow, gently urging her to continue.

She glanced at the glowing figure hovering near her shoulder, still faintly pulsing with light.

“A spirit... Or, at least, I think that’s what it is.”

Alaric hummed thoughtfully.

“I can’t see it.”

That caught her off guard.

“Huh? At all?”

“Not even as a faint blur.”

He tapped his fingers against the table.

“Only those attuned to mana can sense spirits, and even then, most only catch vague impressions. Although the strongest spirits, like the ones in old legends, can be seen by all.”

She frowned, watching the sprite flicker. It had seemed perfectly normal to her, but if Alaric couldn’t see it… did that mean her mana abilities were unusual?

“It seems you have much to learn about your own gift.”

He studied her for a moment before offering a slight smile.

“But that’s not a bad thing.”

She wasn’t sure if she agreed with that yet.

A quiet moment stretched between them as she finished her meal. Father Alaric seemed content to sip his tea in silence now that she had satisfied her immediate curiosity and agreed to come back to work off her stay after the visit to the guild.

She might have had no concrete plans yet, and no clear path forward, but nobody got anywhere by standing still.

The children’s laughter still echoed from the other room, the little ones now running about under the exasperated watch of the nun. Elynara could feel a smile tugging at her lips just listening to the lively chaos.

For a place that felt so worn and frayed at the edges, the heart of it still beat strong.

More than that, it felt alive.

Closing her eyes, Elynara focused her senses, seeking out the mana around her.

It was quite thin – very thin, even compared to forest outskirts, but still present. A little harder to grasp perhaps, but nothing she struggled with.

The simple exercise of sensing it put her more at ease, her fingers twitching slightly at the sensation.

Father Alaric was right. She had much to learn about her own abilities.

But life was a journey, and all she had to do was move forward!

Satisfied with her little meditation, she said her goodbyes and went out to get her first ever quest.

Refreshed after taking a deep breath of the chilly morning air, she made her way towards the guild building.

The town looked different now. Busier.

People moved about with purpose, merchants setting up stalls and carts rolling down uneven roads. The sounds of the town waking up filled the air, a soft murmur of life that made her steps feel just a little lighter.

Focused as she was on the sights, it felt like it took no time at all to arrive at her destination.

Taking a deep breath, she patted down her hair, the little resident sprite happy enough to stay put for once.

“Well, time to get to it.”

The guild hall was just as lively as before, the murmur of voices filling the air as adventurers came and went. Elynara stepped inside, making her way to the counter.

The receptionist, Alicia, greeted her with an easy smile. “Back already?”

“I need to take a quest.”

Alicia nodded, reaching for a stack of beginner-friendly postings. “I would recommend starting small – gathering herbs, delivering goods, that sort of thing.”

Elynara scanned the listings. A delivery job seemed simple, but a gathering quest might take her outside the town, giving her a chance to test her abilities. And perhaps help her learn a bit more about which herbs and plants are worth looking out for.

As she deliberated, snippets of conversation reached her ears.

“…another sighting near the old mines.”

“Nothing confirmed, but if it’s true–”

“–don’t want to end up like those fools who thought they could handle it.”

She glanced toward the adventurers speaking, their expressions serious. Whatever they were talking about, it was clearly beyond a beginner’s reach.

Pushing the thought aside, she chose a gathering quest with something about collecting medicinal herbs near the outskirts.

Seemed simple enough at first glance. But before accepting she needed to know what the herbs the request spoke of looked like – or where they even grow.

As if anticipating her questions Alicia pulled out a little herb encyclopedia, explaining everything as soon as she bothered to ask, and what she didn’t even think to ask!

What a crazy competent woman. It was almost creepy!

Finally satisfied, Alicia took the request and marked it down before flashing her a practiced smile.

“Good choice. Now that you know what to look out for, I expect great results. Just don’t wander too far!”

Elynara nodded, determined to do her best.

She quickly said her goodbyes and soon enough was on her way out.

Before setting out of the town proper, she returned to the church to fulfill her end of the agreement.

The orphanage was livelier than expected, with children darting between the pews, their laughter echoing through the halls.

A girl with bright eyes spotted her first.

“You’re the new sister!”

Before she could react, a small crowd gathered around her, curiosity shining in their faces. Questions flew at her from all directions: Where are you from? Are you a knight? Do you have a sword? Can you use magic?

Elynara wasn’t sure how to answer half of them, but the excitement was infectious.

Soon, a few of the bolder children tugged at her hands, pulling her into their games.

She hesitated at first, but before long, she was dodging imaginary spells, leaping between ‘safe zones,’ and laughing more than she had in days.

For a brief moment, all the uncertainties weighing on her mind faded.

Though after the initial excitement died out, the children finally gave her some breathing room. She used it to say her goodbyes, only to get a bunch of ‘boos’ and ‘awws’ and more than a few pouting faces in turn.

As much as she would love to play more, she had work to do, and her first quest to complete!

She didn’t really want to stand out, and wasn’t sure about too many things yet – not to mention just plainly not wanting to dirty her lovely space satchel up – Elynara figured she would need to find some means of transport for the herbs.

Perhaps she could borrow one after helping out with chores a little?

That decided, she went to ask the kids for directions, yet it still took her a little while to find the nun she was to work under. The disadvantages of being unfamiliar with the place evident.

Still, once there it took just a few short exchanges before Elynara had her hands full of cleaning utensils.

The nun sure seemed happy to have her help, and even more eager to assign her some of the more annoying chores!

And so a couple hours later, after a lot more scrubbing than she ever remembered doing – not that she remembered much – and a certain fuzzball using her focus to play pranks on her, she was finally done.

Now free of her obligations, and with a simple basket the nun almost shoved into her hands as thanks, she set out towards the same gate she entered from.

Apparently the open fields on forest outskirts were the perfect place for her gathering needs, if Alicia was to be believed.

It was amazing how much you could miss if you didn’t know what to look out for. Simply retracing her steps led her towards a few such herb patches!

With Father Alaric’s warning in mind, she continued with her gathering crusade, alert and ready for any changes.

Soon enough it became apparent that gathering herbs was the easy part of the request. It was finding them that was proving challenging, with vast stretches of ground bearing none at all despite her knowing where to look!

Between her being on high alert, and her sprite happily messing with her, her hair, basket, herbs, and anything else it found fun, things were beginning to look quite exhausting.

At least until the little nosy fuzzball suddenly flitted ahead, pausing over a specific patch of grass and bobbing there in place.

It was the first time it separated so far away from her too!

“And what do you have there, little one?”

Taking the hint, she curiously approached and right there, between the grass, were the herbs she was looking for.

She knelt down and carefully plucked the herbs, checking them against the description in the quest notice. They were an exact match.

Looking back up towards the now smugly bobbing fuzzball, Elynara couldn’t help but crack a smile. As annoying as the green spirit tended to be, things were never boring with it around.

“You’re so smart, thank you! Think you could guide me toward more of those?”

Smug and clearly happy with itself, the sprite was soon fluttering about again, leading her to even more patches of grass.

With it helping it wouldn’t be long until the request was complete!

Suddenly invigorated, Elynara double-checked her surroundings before following after it, a smile tugging at the corner of her lips.

She still had a long way to go.

But for the first time, she felt like she was truly starting.
