Spirit stones.

At first glance, spirit stones were simple things: crystallized spiritual energy formed naturally within the earth, used as currency, cultivation resources, or fuel for formations. Every cultivator knew that much.

But Ning had learned long ago that in the cultivation world, simplicity was often a lie.

He pulled out a thin book he had found in the Scripture Pavilion.

On the Classification and Nature of Spirit Stones.

Perfect.

Flipping it open, Ning leaned against a pillar and began reading.

Spirit stones were divided into four grades-low, middle, high, and top-based primarily on the purity, density, and stability of the spiritual energy contained within them.

Low-grade spirit stones were the most common. Their spiritual energy was relatively impure and dispersed easily once disturbed, which made them more suitable for everyday transactions than serious cultivation. Middle-grade spirit stones contained considerably purer and denser spiritual energy, making them more useful for cultivation and as fuel for formations.

High-grade spirit stones, however, were an entirely different matter.

They didn't merely contain spiritual energy.

They were capable of retaining it.

Their internal structure was exceptionally stable, allowing the spiritual energy within to remain concentrated for long periods without significant loss. Because of this, high-grade spirit stones could serve as reliable sources of energy for powerful techniques and large formations, while their purity also made them far more suitable for direct cultivation.

Top-grade spirit stones took these qualities even further. They were so rare that most cultivators could go their entire lives without ever seeing one, and even powerful sects treated them as strategic resources rather than ordinary currency.

Ning slowly turned the page.

That much, he already knew.

What interested him was the next section.

Special Variants and Anomalous Spirit Stones.

His eyes sharpened.

These were spirit stones that deviated from the norm due to unusual environments, long periods of exposure to particular types of qi, or other unique spiritual phenomena. Some were unstable and dangerous, while others possessed unusual properties, such as affinities with certain elements, resonance with particular cultivation techniques, or the ability to enhance cultivation under specific conditions.

Then Ning stopped.

Recoverable Spirit Stones.

He read the passage twice.

Recoverable spirit stones were not actually a separate grade of spirit stone, but rather immature stones that possessed the unusual ability to absorb spiritual energy from their surroundings. Instead of simply losing the energy stored within them after use, their internal structure gradually drew in ambient qi, refined it, and accumulated it over time.

Given sufficient time and the right environment, such a stone could eventually mature into a high-grade spirit stone. Exceptional specimens could even approach top grade.

There was only one problem: time.

Most recoverable spirit stones required decades, sometimes even centuries, to fully mature. Before reaching maturity, their capacity was severely limited, causing many of them to resemble ordinary low-grade spirit stones or even flawed stones to anyone who didn't know what to look for.

But their value, once recognized...

Ning exhaled quietly.

"So that's what you are," he murmured.

His fingers brushed against the small pouch at his waist.

Inside was the spirit stone he had obtained during the mission.

At first glance, it looked almost identical to an ordinary spirit stone. The only noticeable difference was that the spiritual energy within it seemed slightly purer than normal. Ordinarily, Ning wouldn't have thought much of it.

Then, after keeping it for several days, he noticed something strange.

The amount of spiritual energy inside had increased. Not by much, but enough for his repeated observations to confirm that he wasn't imagining things.

Something was clearly unusual about the stone.

"This must have been the reason for that rabbit's mutation and late-stage cultivation."

It also confirmed the conjecture Ning had formed earlier. If the rabbit had remained near the stone for a long enough period, constantly exposed to the unusually pure spiritual energy it gathered and released, then its abnormal growth suddenly made much more sense.

Ning turned the stone between his fingers.

Spirit stones could, of course, be used directly for cultivation, but few cultivators at his level actually relied on low-grade stones for that purpose.

Even Ning preferred Qi Gathering Pills whenever the surrounding spiritual energy was insufficient.

The reason was simple.

A single low-grade spirit stone contained too little usable spiritual energy.

Even at Ning's current cultivation level, he would need to consume several of them during a serious cultivation session. As his cultivation increased, that number would only continue to rise. Higher cultivation meant a larger reserve of qi, and a larger reserve naturally required far more spiritual energy to fill.

Worse, the spiritual energy contained within low-grade spirit stones wasn't particularly pure. Absorbing it directly meant spending additional time refining it and removing impurities before it could properly become one's own qi.

For ordinary cultivators, the cost simply wasn't worth the benefit.

That was why Qi Gathering Pills, Spirit Gathering Arrays, and similar methods existed. They provided more practical ways to increase the amount of usable spiritual energy available during cultivation.

High-grade spirit stones were different. Their spiritual energy was both dense and exceptionally pure, allowing cultivators to absorb it far more efficiently. The problem was that using something so valuable for ordinary cultivation was a luxury far beyond Ning's current means.

Fortunately, the stone in his hand presented another possibility.

Rather than using the small amount of energy currently stored within it to increase his cultivation, Ning could use it as a rechargeable reserve.

A battery.

Whenever he exhausted his qi during practice, he could draw upon the stone to restore what he had spent. Qi-replenishing pills could accomplish something similar, but every pill consumed was gone forever.

This stone wasn't, and that was the important part.

Ning had already tested it several times. Because the spiritual energy within the stone was unusually pure, he could absorb it directly without spending much time refining it. With his current qi capacity, only a small portion of the stone's stored energy was required to refill his reserves.

The process itself took barely a minute or two.

Afterward, the depleted stone would slowly absorb ambient spiritual energy and replenish what Ning had taken. At its current immature stage, that recovery wasn't particularly fast, but it was enough to restore the energy Ning had consumed within roughly a day.

That might not sound impressive at first, but Ning didn't need the stone to provide an endless supply of qi.

He could exhaust himself practicing a technique, use the stone to restore his qi almost immediately, and continue practicing instead of waiting for his body to naturally recover. Once the stone itself ran low, he could simply put it aside and allow it to recharge.

This allowed him to practice more and subsequently make his numbers go up even more.

“As expected of a protagonist halo,” Ning sighed. “Even a spirit stone has been elevated to such standards.”

...

Ning tended to the fields. This time, he had planted two acres of Frozen Breath Plants and four acres of basic spiritual grain, while a smaller plot was reserved for other plants such as Poisonous Lilies, red barley, wild herbs, and, finally, Dragon Qi Grass.

Ning sat down beside the Dragon Qi Grass.

Its leaves were long and narrow, tinged with a faint, almost imperceptible sheen. The patch itself was small, no more than a few square meters. The stalks were thinner than mature specimens, and the qi they emitted was weak, barely stronger than that of ordinary spiritual herbs.

Ning crouched beside the plot and inspected the soil with practiced ease. He brushed aside a layer of dirt, exposing the roots beneath. They were pale, firm, and spreading evenly through the soil.

"Still stable," he muttered.

When he had first obtained the Dragon Qi Grass from Xiao Fan, it had already been harvested. What he received was a single intact stalk, several residual roots torn out in haste, and clumps of soil still saturated with lingering qi.

To other cultivators, these would have been little more than leftover scraps from a one-time-use material, but Ning was a spiritual farmer.

The first thing he had done after confirming that the plant was still barely viable was not to immediately plant it.

Instead, he observed the plant.

He monitored how quickly the qi dissipated from the stalk when left exposed and compared it with ordinary spiritual herbs. Dragon Qi Grass lost its qi noticeably more slowly, as though its internal structure naturally resisted leakage.

That told Ning something important.

The plant was unusually good at retaining qi. As long as its environment remained suitable, constantly flooding it with additional spiritual energy might not only be unnecessary but could even disturb its natural growth.

So Ning focused on recreating the environment in which it had originally grown.

He started with the soil.

Using what he knew from spiritual farming, Ning refined the plot until the spiritual energy within it was stable, neither excessively rich nor particularly poor. From his past life, he applied a simple principle: when transplanting something into a new environment, stability was often more important than abundance.

He then mixed in the soil he had collected from the boar's lair, where several stalks of Dragon Qi Grass had originally grown. Rather than treating it simply as fertilizer, Ning used it to reproduce the conditions of the plant's previous environment as closely as possible. Whatever minerals, moisture, and qi characteristics the Dragon Qi Grass preferred, that soil had already proven capable of supporting it.

Next came the roots.

The residual roots Xiao Fan had left behind were damaged and incomplete, but they weren't entirely dead. Ning carefully trimmed away the decayed portions and kept only the firm, healthy sections. He planted them shallowly, deep enough to remain moist and protected but not so deep that they would easily rot. It was all about finding the right balance.

The intact stalk came last.

Ning planted it slightly away from the root fragments, giving each enough space to establish itself without immediately competing for the same nutrients.

Then came the waiting.

For the first three months, Ning did little beyond maintaining the soil and casting the Small Cloud Rain Technique at fixed intervals. He resisted the temptation to force growth with stronger spells or excessive spiritual fertilizer.

He was giving the plant time to acclimatize to the new environment.

When new roots finally emerged, Ning began the second phase.

He introduced heavily diluted spiritual fertilizer, adjusting the amount according to the plant's response. If the roots showed signs of stress, he reduced it. If the leaves began losing their healthy sheen, he increased it slightly. This step required much more testing than the first, but he eventually got it down as well.

Once every ten days, he also used the Withering and Flourishing Technique to carefully regulate the plant's growth.

He would briefly suppress its growth and qi circulation, giving the plant time to stabilize what it had already accumulated. Then he would gradually release the suppression and allow growth to resume.

The results were painfully slow.

But Ning was the kind of man who had spent twenty-four hours braising short ribs even before transmigrating, so patience was hardly a problem.

For nearly half a year, there was almost no visible progress.

Then, one morning, Ning found a new shoot.

It hadn't grown from the original stalk; no, it had emerged from one of the residual roots.

That was the turning point.

Once the root system stabilized, propagation became much easier. New shoots appeared one by one, each weak at first but healthier than the last. Ning adjusted their spacing, removed unhealthy growth, and made sure the young plants had enough room to develop without competing too heavily with one another.

With this level of care, Ning estimated that the Dragon Qi Grass would be mature enough to harvest in roughly two years.

It was a relatively long growth cycle compared with most first-tier spiritual plants, but that was hardly surprising. If Dragon Qi Grass had been easy and quick to cultivate, Xiao Fan wouldn't have needed to venture out and stumble upon it in the first place.

"Huff… it's finally looking good," Ning sighed as he stood and began casting the Small Cloud Rain Technique.

Normally, watering all his fields this way would consume a considerable amount of qi and require pauses to recover. Now, however, he had the recoverable spirit stone. Whenever his reserves ran low, he could replenish them and continue, allowing him to cast the technique repeatedly with barely any interruption.

After finishing his daily chores, Ning walked toward the river, intending to catch some fish.

It was there that he spotted Old Zhou sitting by the riverbank with a book in his hands.

Ning narrowed his eyes.

It was his book.

"How's the book you're reading, Brother Zhou?" Ning asked, genuinely curious about the feedback.

After all, what he had written was probably the first erotic light novel in this world. He honestly had no idea how people here would react to something like it.

Old Zhou was completely engrossed and only looked up after Ning called out to him a second time.

"Eh? You're asking me about the book?" Old Zhou hurriedly sat upright and closed it before clearing his throat. "This novel is… quite special. An entirely new type of story. The characterization is excellent. And those illustrations! Quite tasteful. Not to mention all the ideas I've never seen before. Truly, very good."

Old Zhou spoke with enthusiasm, going into detail without the slightest restraint. He knew that although Ning looked innocent on the surface, he was ultimately a fellow man of culture, so there was no need to hold back.

"So," Ning said carefully, "it seems you like it very much."

"Yes, I do." Old Zhou nodded firmly before his expression suddenly darkened. "But it has one fatal flaw."

Ning's heart skipped.

"That bastard author, Tzu Sun!" Old Zhou slammed the book against his knee. "Why can't he write more chapters? And on top of that, he always stops at the most interesting part! If that bastard hadn't hidden his identity, I'd have gone to fight him already!"

"I-is that so?" Ning's smile stiffened.

Ever since his book had become popular, he had been very glad that he'd hidden his identity.

Now, it seemed even Old Zhou was no exception.

"Yes!" Old Zhou grew even more animated. "If I knew who that bastard was, I'd definitely lock him in a basement and make him write books for eternity!"

T-tell me you're joking… Ning panicked internally.

No one actually has thoughts like that, right?

After venting his frustrations, Old Zhou finally calmed down and turned back to Ning. "Speaking of which, I heard you're preparing to take the Spiritual Farmer Certification?"

"Yes," Ning replied, relieved by the change of subject. "Otherwise, I'd have to wait another year, and that wouldn't be ideal."

The Spiritual Farmer Certification was held only once a year and was overseen by the Spiritual Plant Hall. Moreover, registering for the examination required a fee of five hundred spirit stones.

Without those restrictions, Ning would have taken it long ago.

"Heh! I'm sure you'll pass." Old Zhou grinned smugly. "I was nervous too, but I still made it."

With Ning's guidance and their many in-depth discussions about the principles behind various farming spells, Old Zhou had finally managed to take that last step. Combined with his years of practical experience, it had been enough for him to successfully pass the certification.

As an old man who had finally achieved his long-held dream of becoming a certified spiritual farmer, Old Zhou never missed an opportunity to show it off.

Even the farmers from neighboring sectors weren't spared.

"I'll do my best," Ning said with a smile.

He wasn't lying. He had prepared as much as he could.
