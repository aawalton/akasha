The time to extract answers from The Fallen Shepherd was over. Luke had taken too long and would pay the price if he didn't end this right away. Neither Dot nor Leom would be much help either. Brimming with mana and frustration, Luke waited a breath, then another. Once the coming monsters reached the hospital bed where he kept Relian's mana at a dangerously low level, he activated The Healer's Moment.

Following that, Luke used Cascading Threads and saw threads of mana leap out of him and into the closest monster, a Relian-doctor replica who was in the process of being remodeled, parts of others crawling all over it. Once inside, the threads spread out, leaping from monster to monster until the still, silent room glowed with more threads than he could count. Only then did Luke force his will upon the threads, adding Needle of Life to each one. It took a tremendous amount of concentration, but he persisted. One chance was all he'd get at this. That left little room for failure.

Forming each Needle of Life into something akin to a miniature drill, Luke burrowed deep into their monster cores while draining the mana from them to keep it all going. Time resumed before he finished, and a host of monsters screamed as they descended on him. Even through that horror, Luke kept his cool. He drew the last bit of mana from their cores and forced it to remain right there in the thread to quicken the use of the final piece of the puzzle that was his plan: Weaver's Rupture.

The blasts were neither small nor controlled. For one, he needed to maximize damage. For the other, directing all those blasts was beyond him. Just activating the skill that many times while pulling in mana and holding on through the many bites, claws raking him, and scalpels cutting and slicing into his skin, muscle, and arteries with expert precision, was almost too much, but in the end, Luke managed it.

In an instant, the room fell silent as all monsters died, all except for the one still on the bed. Luke felt something happening that he had hoped never to experience again: his body falling to pieces.

No.

Luke pushed back against that pressure, holding on to the image of himself as whole. In that instant, his will clashed against The Fallen Shepherd's. This time, Luke came out on top.

"Oh, that is impressive growth," Relian said. "Won't you reconsider joining me?"

Luke snapped all instances of Needle of Life still dormant in Relian. Eyes going wild, the replica of Luke's first and foremost enemy lashed out, dark globs of liquid oozing from his eyes and nose. Corruption. Even now, he attempted to survive by taking over a new body, but Luke was ready for it.

He wove a shield of threads and intercepted the leaping heap of corruption. Using the ends of all those threads in the barrier separating him from certain doom, Luke then blasted out with Weaver's Rupture, this time maximizing the scope of each attack. It obliterated the corruption in an instant, and no trace remained.

"That won't work on me," Luke said, but Relian seemed beyond hearing.

The Fallen Shepherd had fallen back against the hospital bed, unable to draw in even shallow breaths through the severed windpipe as he bled out. Even so, he whispered something Luke couldn't quite hear.

Leaning in, on high alert to be ready for a last-ditch effort from Relian, Luke whispered back, "What?"

Relian repeated himself, and Luke felt himself draining of energy even as frustration and anger built inside him, overwhelming any sense of care. It didn't matter because the not-Relian died a moment later, and Luke pulled the monster core from him, promising he'd feed it to Smudge the first chance he got. The little voidling was still out there somewhere. He was sure of it.

Luke stood back with disgust, turned, and kicked forward into a cabinet, shattering it. "Damn!" He brought out his new staff, infused it with mana, and slammed it into the wall with a crack, creating a large hole. "Dammit!"

It wasn't enough. Luke spun and grabbed the side of the bed, lifting it with ease before throwing it against the door with enough force to bend the metal bars on the side of the bed. He let out a wordless scream before stopping himself, panting.

"L-Luke?" Dot asked.

He growled as he spun to face her. Leom stood at her side, both of them pressed against the wall, trembling with fear. "What?" he added when they just stood there, their eyes wide.

Doing his best to rein in his anger, he gestured to the fallen monsters. "They're dead! Don't you see? You don't need to be afraid!"

His raised voice didn't help, and their eyes just kept growing wider, and tears appeared in Dot's eyes. Leom kept quiet, but had a staff in her hands now, holding it out as if she thought she'd need to protect herself.

"W-what did he say?" Dot asked, her voice wavering.

They were afraid of him. It didn't take a genius to figure that out. So when Luke answered, he kept his voice as calm as he could manage, repeating what he'd just learned.

"The dungeon can only create replicas of people or monsters that exist."

Dot mulled over those words for a second before realization dawned on her: the reason for Luke's state of mind. "You don't mean?"

"The Fallen Shepherd is still alive. I didn't kill him after all." He sank to his knees, strength draining out of him. "It was too easy, too clean. What an idiot I was not to see it."

She walked up to him and squatted down next to him to sit on her heels. "Maybe he lied?"

Luke shook his head. "No, I don't think so. He still wants me to join him, even after all this. His name is still on the Singers' list."

He stood and clenched his fists. "After all that, I just figured that I'd killed him with a couple of threads? Fuck!"

"How were you supposed to know? You stopped him!"

Luke looked at her with a sneer, then pulled the expression back. This wasn't her fault. She didn't understand. Shaking his head, he repeated himself, "Except I didn't. It was too clean. What was I thinking?"

Considering the implications and finding himself unable to grasp the scope of what this revelation meant for Earth, his friends, and himself, Luke shook his head. "We have to get out of here."

He looked over at Leom, who still cowered in the corner. "You coming?"

It took her a moment to register his words, and then another to put the staff away and nod. "I still have to help you return and join us through the gate back to my home."

Dot looked over at her, then back to Luke. "What is she talking about?"

"Later," Luke said, gathering his wits about him before striding out of the room and into the corridor. There was no use trying to predict what damage Relian could have done, or what his next steps would be. All Luke could do was keep going. The one thing he had going for him was that the real Relian didn't know how much he'd grown. The replica might have been a weaker version of The Fallen Shepherd, but Luke had obliterated it. Yes, the situation had favored him, but the thought gave him some glimmer of hope.

Luke stopped dead in his tracks, another realization hitting him. "Is Alan alive too?"

"Alan is dead, Luke. You killed him," Dot said.

He turned to face her. "Are you sure?"

"You're getting paranoid."

"A pretty damn rational response to all this, wouldn't you say?"

Dot gave him a long, compassionate look. "Alan is dead. The ISG guild interface confirms that. Relian isn't human, but Alan was, despite it all. Humans don't survive the sort of damage you inflicted on him."

Considering her words, he gave a slow nod. "You're right. I would've felt his echo."

"Good," she said, "then can we please leave? I don't like this place, and there are people waiting for me to heal them."

A pair of not-nurses tried to rush past, but Luke reached out with his new staff and barred their way. Threads of mana extended from within the staff, and he drained them of every speck of mana before severing their monster cores, taking them for himself.

"One more thing. You're in Integrated Solutions Group. What do you know about the Singers? Leom here," he nodded at her, "says you're cooperating with them. In fact, she says it's thanks to you guys that they're even able to invade Earth."

Dot looked like she was about to speak, but stopped, stunned, and turned to Leom. "What do you mean they're invading Earth?"

"We're inviting you to join the Singer empire," Leom said in a warm and inviting tone as she reached out her hands toward Dot, palms turned up. "Your guild has facilitated our coming."

Dot took one look at her and shied back, as if stung by the words. "Fuck that!"
