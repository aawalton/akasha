“That was an exceptional meal, Barb,” Adabelle praised as she leaned back in her chair. Her dishes, like everyone else’s, were completely cleaned. “Trevor can keep food warm almost indefinitely, would you mind if we take some ballabroody for the road?”

“Take a bowl each, dear, but leave the rest,” the older woman allowed. “Ballabroody is a family meal meant to feed dozens. That’s why I made so much. If we had eaten at home like we were supposed to, then whatever was left would have gone to our neighbors. Our family might not be as big as it used to be, but I’m not leaving here without making sure these sailors are fed right. In fact, I’ll go let them know it’s time to eat.”

“That’s quite fair,” she replied with a smile as Barb stood and left. Her eyes landed on Trevor, about to ask if he’d be willing to take some, but he was leaning back in his chair with his hands on his stomach. There was a content look on his face, and the demonkin couldn’t help but laugh as she reached over and patted his shoulder. “Are you all right, husband of mine?”

“I am amazing,” he answered, not opening his eyes. “Barb, I’d say it was exceptional, but I think it’s a step above that. Can’t think of a word for it right now. Too busy basking and digesting.”

“So good you could stop wars with this pot alone,” Lora offered, currently in much the same position as Trevor.

“Accurate,” he agreed. “Completely accurate.”

“Now you’re just buttering me up,” Barb laughed, nudging her son as she stood. “You forgot to mention that all these polite youngsters are so charming, too.”

“That goes without saying,” Klar said with a grin. “I’ll pour you a couple of bowls, Trevor.”

“A trio of bowls?” Lora asked hopefully.

“I’ll pour you a trio of bowls,” the merchant prince corrected, more than happy to provide extra for the teleporter. He stood up to get them ready. “Then, ma and I will get out of your hair and let you enjoy your honeymoon in peace.”

“I speak for both of us when I say it’s been wonderful so far,” Adabelle said. “Even though I’m not able to enter the Isle of Horns, I’ll be carrying these memories with me forever. You and Griffin have really outdone yourself.”

“Ah, well, you two have been good to me,” Klar replied with a warm smile as he placed the three bowls in front of the courier. He slowly reached out and sent them to his inventory. “Trevor more so as we have a working relationship, but you and I through Tosa, too, Lady Adabelle. I believe that friendship is a two-way street, and that those who have the means should give as much as they receive.”

“Hear, hear,” Trevor said before taking a deep breath. He patted his stomach gently “Okay, I think I’m ready to move again.”

“Feel free to teleport yourself to your next destination. I’m going to need a few more minutes,” Lora told him.

“Take your time,” Adabelle said with a laugh.

“We’ll have to fix you up with something temporary, unfortunately,” Klar stated. “You were supposed to stay on the island until nightfall tomorrow, but if the only place you’re allowed to go is the Matador then that’s not very thrilling. Certainly not suitable for your honeymoon.”

“Where are we going next?” Trevor asked.

The merchant prince closed his mouth before he could answer. He was clearly thinking it through, however, as his head bobbed back and forth. The coins hanging from his horns clinked together.

“Alright, I suppose Lady Griffin is not here to chastise me,” he said with a light laugh. “Trevor, you had expressed an interest in taking Lady Adabelle to places you have gone previously, and we decided to have you spend the night in…”

“In?” the courier urged.

“Sorry, I’m just trying to decide how much information to give you,” Klar replied. It took him a few more seconds before he spoke again. “Tomorrow night, there is an event happening in the Northern Basid Sea, and Rabinar is the best place to see it.”

The name made Trevor sit upright. “We’re going to the Floating Isles?” he asked excitedly.

He and Rashie had gone on a quest there before. It had been claimed by one of the other merchant princes generations before, and it was the only known quarry for Floatstone. That had been their main goal, but they had also been given quests to find the scales of a rainbow fish for Corrin. Their success had allowed her to make Adabelle’s colorful horn ribbon for the wedding.

“You were so excited to tell me everything that happened after you came back from that trip,” Adabelle said, reaching over to rub her husband’s shoulder. “It’s too cold to swim at this time of year, but I’m sure we can find something to keep us occupied for a whole day.”

“Oh, definitely,” Trevor confirmed with an enthusiastic nod. “Whether it’s the park, or taking a boat out on a tour so you could really see the Floating Isles in all their splendor, or making it up to the King of the Sea, there’s plenty to do. Actually, Ilyin might be interested in seeing Clay again. He did invite us back.”

“Ilyin was the elven wizard in charge of the Floatstone quarry, right?” she asked.

The courier nodded. “Yeah, that’s the guy! He said to feel free to come back, and we might be able to get a tour of the quarry. A proper one, this time.”

“As delightful as that might be, you probably won’t be able to reach him,” Klar said, holding up a hand. “The stormy season is just about finished, so everyone will be coming back tomorrow in time for… what we’re sending you for.”

“Man, that’s kind of funny,” Trevor replied with a light laugh. “Rashie and I show up at the beginning of the off-season, then, when I get the chance to go back, we’re right there at the end of the off-season. Well, that’s okay, anyway. I’m glad Adabelle gets to see it this time.”

“Of course. Then the only thing left would be to send Lora out to get you some accommodations,” the merchant prince said, turning to his employee.

The teleporter opened her eyes and took a big breath before standing up. “Alright, yeah. I can do this,” she announced before disappearing.

“We could have waited until she was ready,” Adabelle pointed out.

“She was,” Klar replied. “Lora would have said something if she wasn’t. She’s a great worker, but I made sure she knew she had enough agency to tell me when enough was enough. It was surprisingly hard to get that through to her, actually. Sometimes I think she takes my title of prince a little too literally.”

“From the sounds of it, she came from a time of kings and magic,” Trevor offered. “So that might be the reason. Maybe some kind of return to that sort of age, human or not.”

“She has told me about her home back on Avalon. Fascinating place,” the minotaur said, stroking his chin. He was about to continue when his mother returned.

“Come and eat, everyone. There should be plenty to go around,” Barb called, leading an entire herd behind her.

Klar smiled. “Shall we leave them to it before it becomes too crowded?”

“Absolutely,” Trevor said as he stood up. Adabelle joined him, her tail carefully picking up the backpack that Clay was still working inside of so she could hand it to him. “Thanks.”

They followed the merchant prince up to the deck and put their jackets back on to combat the cold. There were a few seats by the railing that looked out over the village of Rastarore, and they sat down. Adabelle’s tail once again snaked up Trevor’s sleeve as they cuddled.

“How is Lora treating you two?” Klar asked once the three of them had settled.

“She’s fantastic,” Adabelle answered without hesitation. “Lora has been nothing but a consummate professional and has done well to ensure that we’re both well taken care of and having a wonderful time on our trip.”

He smiled as he heard her praise. “That’s good. She was excited when she heard I was going to be sending you two on your honeymoon with her as your transport, but didn’t want to meet you until the last minute.”

“I did think that was a bit strange,” Trevor remarked.

“It’s how she is. Since this was supposed to be your big, special trip, she was worried about ruining it by being too familiar. This way, she could focus on being a guide,” Klar explained before he chuckled. “Not that it lasted very long, huh?”

“Nope. We were pretty quick to say she didn’t have to be so distant.”

“That kind of friendliness is exactly why I wanted to make this such a wonderful trip for you two. Even with some events not going to plan, like my mother dragging me halfway across the island to feed you,” he said as he gestured towards the mainland. “I think I mentioned that before, but Lora was supposed to ask you if you wanted to dine in with us or have your own privacy. That way we didn’t impede on anything. Ma just can’t help herself sometimes though.”

“Hey, if she feeds us stuff like that ballabroody, she can drag you anywhere she likes as far as I’m concerned,” Trevor laughed.

“We are both very social people, Klarkartar, so we likely would have accepted. Delicious food aside, your mother is a delightful and very outspoken woman, so meeting her was fun,” Adabelle added warmly.

“Yeah, she has a good heart,” the minotaur agreed, a big smile on his face. “But what’s important is that you two are enjoying yourself, and it warms me that you are.”

“More than you know, man,” Trevor said. He was about to put an arm around his wife, but the tail coiled around it held him firm. She scooted closer instead. “Sorry to hear that some of the other princes have so much trouble with you that they messed up your plans.”

Klar waved a dismissive hand. “That’s politics for you. My rise has not been quiet or easily cowed—pun intended—and that tends to get under some people’s surprisingly thin fur. It’s nothing you’ll have to worry about, at least. I get on well enough with the Altabrae family and they know you’ll be coming to the Floating Isles, so there won’t be any more trouble going forward.”

“Does no more trouble mean no more dungeons?” Adabelle asked. It was slight, but the courier could pick up the pout in her voice that she tried to hide.

“I’m not giving away the answer to that one,” the merchant prince laughed. “Telling you that you’re going to Rabinar on the Floating Isles an hour before you do is one thing, but I’m not giving up the rest of the trip. Lady Griffin would not treat me kindly after that.”

“Speaking of, what’s happening on the Floating Isles tomorrow night that you wanted us to see?” Trevor asked curiously.

“See, now, that’s a question I want to answer,” Klar said, shifting to look at the newlyweds. “Because you can always ask whoever’s around and they can tell you. No one here but Lora and I know where else you’re going after Rabinar and thus I must hold that secret tight.”

“She has been good at keeping things a secret until the last minute,” Adabelle stated with a nod. “But you are right that we could just ask.”

“Will you, though?”

“Probably,” she answered, smiling.

“Then I suppose I have no—”

“Sir, Miss Lora has returned,” a minotaur said from the stairwell, his head popping up onto the deck. “She would come to find you, but she is unfortunately soaked and shouldn’t come outside.”

“Ah, sounds like it’s raining on the Floating Isles. I was afraid of that,” Klar sighed as he stood up. “But she’ll have gotten accommodations for herself as well, so the best thing to do would be to send you on your way so she can change.”

“Just when you were about to spill the beans?” Trevor complained.

The merchant prince smiled as he walked away. “Sorry. That’s how things work sometimes.”

“Ugh.”

Giggling at her husband’s expression, Adabelle stood up first and helped him to his feet. They followed Klar down to the first cargo hold where they found Lora and Barb. The air was already a little hot thanks to the magic of the ship, and she had stripped off her jacket and gloves. That hadn’t stopped the minotaur from putting one of her colorful scarves around the woman's shoulders, though. She really was soaked.

“Hey, I got us some rooms at the big, glitzy place!” Lora announced, offering the newlyweds a peace sign. “Had to run to get there from the boss man’s warehouse, but that was easy enough.”

“Did you forget an umbrella?” Klar asked gently.

“No, I took one,” she answered as she shook her head. Water splashed all around her as her wet hair went all over the place. “There’s just a real big storm. The umbrella didn’t do jack diddly squat.”

“Poor dear,” Barb crooned, rubbing the other woman’s back. “Would you like more stew to warm you up? I might have to beat those boys off with a stick, but I’m sure I can get you another bowl.”

“Thank you, but I’m still very full,” Lora laughed. “It’s nothing a hot shower won’t cure, and I made sure to take my usual bag to the room ahead of time.”

“Usual bag?” Trevor asked.

“Yup! Since I’m going all over the place, I have stashes of clothes, money, passports, shiny things, and whatever else I need,” the teleporter explained with a smile. “That way I can travel light and still have the essentials. Someday I’ll get strong enough to carry an extradimensional bag on me, but this works in the meantime. It also helps that the boss has a lot of places I can keep things. Like that warehouse. But don’t worry, I can teleport us directly into your suite.”

“Should probably go sooner rather than later, too. A cold might be easy enough to cure with the right medicine, but I’d rather you stay healthy, Lora,” Klar said.

“You got it, boss,” she replied, nodding as she stood up. “Thank you, Miss Barb. Do you want your scarf back?”

“Keep it for now. It’s doing more than keeping you warm, you know,” the minotaur woman said, plucking at the now translucent white shirt Lora had worn under her black suit jacket. “And don’t think I haven’t noticed that you’re padding yourself up. You don’t have to do that for me, you know. I may talk a lot, but there’s nothing wrong with being on the skinny side.”

“I prefer slender to skinny, but, yeah. I’ll keep that in mind.”

“Good. Now you be careful out there. The world’s a big place, and not all of it is safe,” Barb said before giving the other woman a big hug. She did the same to Trevor and Adabelle, pulling them both in at the same time. “And it was so good to meet you two. Congratulations on the marriage, and I’m expecting you both to visit after this whole passport thing blows over, got it?”

“Loud and clear, Barb,” Trevor said, smiling as he returned the hug.

“I’m sure Klar will let us know as soon as it happens, and I’ll check my schedule so we can make plans,” Adabelle added, doing the same.

“Good, good. Then you both be safe out there, too,” the older woman said as she pulled away. “Don’t worry about those bowls, okay? I’ve got plenty more.”

“We’ll see,” Trevor replied, knowing full well that they would be sending them back.

The courier mentally checked with his familiar and found that they still weren’t done with their project. When he floated the idea of peeking, it was met with an automatic and vehement denial. Not an angry one, just firm. He chuckled at Clay’s protectiveness.

It would still have to be put into his inventory, however. Reaching into his backpack, he allowed the earth elemental to push things against the back of his wrist before making them disappear. That way he wouldn’t be able to easily guess what the pieces were. Not checking his extradimensional space after that was a matter of trust.

Once Clay’s core was in his hand, Trevor sent the empty bag away and turned to Lora. “All set.”

“Excellent,” she replied as she reached out and took the newlyweds by the hands. “Bye boss, bye boss’ boss, we’re off!”

Barb sighed. “Asalorana, dear, I told you not to—”

The room changed into a large, opulent suite with dark wood floors and white stone walls. Pictures of the Floating Isles and large ships hung all over, and there were even a few bottled vessels as well. A finely made desk sat against one wall, opposite a minotaur-sized couch, and beside it was a glass door leading out to the balcony.

Thunder crashed and wind howled. Trevor and Adabelle weren’t sure where they were in the hotel, but they could hear the rain striking the walls and glass all over. The sound almost immediately made the courier tired. This was the kind of storm he loved to sleep through.

“Keys are in the kitchen, there’s a real big tub and shower combo, bedroom is that way, and room service can be called with the ringing of the bell,” Lora explained as she took a few steps towards the door, still slightly dripping. “Is there anything I can do for you, Mr. and Mrs. Anderson-Katine?”

“No, Lora, you’ve done plenty,” Adabelle said with a smile as Trevor retrieved Clay and their project from his inventory. “Go and get yourself warmed up.”

“Will do, thanks!” she said before disappearing on the spot.

The earth elemental padded around the suite for a moment, discovering that this one actually had a spare bedroom. They took the Anderson’s Backpack in their mouth and started dragging it to the smaller of the two.

“Have fun, Clay,” Trevor chuckled. His familiar waved before entering the room. Once the backpack was clear, they closed the door. He turned to his wife and pulled her jacket into his inventory now that it was too warm for it. “Looks like we probably shouldn’t go exploring today. I’ll be honest, this weather might just lay me out.”

“Yes, I have noticed you get sleepy when it’s storming out, son of Ander,” Adabelle said with an affectionate smile before patting him on the chest. “Come on, let’s go get comfy on the bed. We’ll read or nap for now and, if the weather calms down, we’ll go out."

“Works for me,” the man replied, changing into pajamas with his Power. He followed her into the bedroom, already stifling a yawn.
