I felt completely numb. After all, I was already being puppeted around by the System, maybe not in such a direct manner as Nate seemed to insinuate, but still.

Then there was Rowan. His lack of memories. His journals asking who everyone was. Had it not been him the entire time? Had this all just been one convoluted trick by the System to get people into the Dream?

That didn't make any sense. As far as I was concerned, The Being in the Static was all-powerful, capable of warping reality around it. If an Instance could tear me from my world, it would be trivial for The System to do so.

In a way, it had, but it stopped at just me. None of my friends, parents, or classmates had been dragged along. From how Nate made it sound, my experience with The System was far from usual.

I wanted to ask the System, but all I could think about was the skin of my mouth closing. I bit the second knuckle of my pointer finger on my right hand. Rolled the knuckle between my teeth just to make sure everything was fine.

The information didn't change anything, not really. The System had promised I would see Rowan again. It wouldn't be him if he were just a puppet. The System was many things, but it wasn't a liar. It couldn't be.

I needed more answers, then I needed to get the hell out of this Instance.

How to deal with Nate and Maggie? Should I act scared anyway? Probably.

"That's uhh," I paused and breathed out shakily, "a lot."

A deafening silence filled the room, and I saw Nate and Maggie looking solely at the ground. The lack of noise was oppressive, and I could see Nate's face twist into something darker with each passing second, "Yeah. It's a lot." It seemed he disliked where this conversation had gone just as much as I did. If these two turned out to be fine, I'd have to ask them about their stories.

I fidgeted in my seat, making sure to play up the discomfort I felt. "Let's– something else. The other stats, besides Presence."

Maggie picked up the slack on Nate's continued silence. "Do you even know how to look at them?" Her voice had softened considerably, but was still harsh to my ears.

"Er. No." I didn't have to fake my embarrassment. Gods, the tutorial really didn't teach me anything.

"There should be some static in the corner of your vision. Just stare at it and think about what you want to see. Items. Stats. Boons. Anything. Give it a try and list it out for me."

I wasn't going to do that. The second part, at least. They had been nothing but helpful, and it felt really nice to talk to other people in the Dream. Still wasn't going to do that, though.

I followed Maggie's instructions and stared at the static. Usually, it grew by itself when I was entering or leaving an Instance, but here and now, the more I focused on it, the more it encapsulated my vision.

[Visitor: OII-1734-3.PRMEV12]

Lucidity: 4

Assimilation: 13

Identity: 47

Continuity: 8

Presence: 525

[You have three available stat adjustments to increase or decrease any stat by 1 for each adjustment.]

The numbers were all over the place, making it hard to get a sense of. Identity was my second highest at 47, but I had no reference point for any of the numbers; I didn't even know if a Lucidity of 4 was good or bad.

Before Maggie could remember that she asked me to read my status, I asked another question. "So, do I want all of them high? I'm– I'm not sure that I want any more Assimilation." I didn't like the meaning of that word and I certainly didn't want to be assimilated into anything.

"Probably for the best … someone like you couldn't handle it. Avoid cursed items, they may lock you at a specific stat level."

I nodded along, but I was getting impatient. I tried to mask it as anxiety.

"But what do they do?" Maybe I asked a little too forcefully, but it was probably fine. As long as I was controlling the conversation.

I went to study Maggie's face for any expression change, but quickly realized that I was, in fact, currently blinded by static. I blinked rapidly, trying to clear my vision. Stupid. I had just voluntarily blinded myself for no reason.

When my vision came back, I saw Maggie nudging Nate out of his stupor. "Sorry. Got distracted," he muttered quietly.

"The boy's asking what the stats do." The older woman nodded towards me, and Nate spoke.

"Lucidity's perception, but for weird shit. Stuff that's out of place," he scratched at one side of his cheek, mulling thoughts over before speaking. "Don't go nuts with it or balance it with Identity. I knew a guy who went all in on it. He wasn't even consumed, just … vanished. One step there. The next. Gone."

"Gods, Nate," Maggie snarled at her companion. "Do you have to do this right now?"

Nate waved a hand, plastering on a larger smile while laughing, "What? Nothing like a ghost story," I laughed along, a smaller chuckle as I studied his lightly twitching fingers.

He took a deep breath before continuing, "Assimilation's also a type of observation, but for the Dream itself. Get it high enough and you can look at people's Boons… not everyone likes that. Some people won't let high-Assimilation Visitors join their group. Then there's Identity. It's Ego. It's a tricky one, some Visitors dump it to make them more resistant to psychological attacks. It's like the more you have the more of you there is to attack."

He leaned back, gesturing at me as he retrieved a canteen of what I assumed was water. "Take a guess at what Continuity does."

"Stops Instances from messing with your memories?"

Both paused and looked at me. I coughed awkwardly. "My tutorial was with this moth called Spinner. They could tweak memories."

Nate nodded slowly, but Maggie clicked her tongue and left the room.

I looked between the two Visitors and raised a brow, "What was that about?"

"Nothing," Nate sighed, "we're just both a bit of bleeding hearts. She has a few grandkids your age. Probably hates that this happened to you. Anyways," he shut down that line of questioning, "your guess is as good as any. As far as people know, it acts as a kind of resistance to forced change."

I nodded along.

"Generally everyone starts at fifteen of everything."

Holy shit. Holy–oh. What the hell. Fifteen? My heart practically beat out of my chest. I almost convulsed. It took all my self-control not to immediately shout. The shock would've been written plain as day on my face. I had to misdirect, obfuscate somehow. How?

"Including Presence?" I asked, as innocently as possible. He had a poor reaction to it before, maybe he'd forget my surprise.

Nate grimaced, "You get shit luck on that?"

I didn't nod. That would be too direct. Instead, I looked away to my right and down towards the ground. I could play it scared instead.

"What can Presence do anyway?"

"A lot, it's your most versatile tool, but it's expensive, so functionally it's just your lifeline until you're really rolling in it.. You can change 100 Presence to a stat adjustment and vise versa, but the conversion rate isn't even," he gave me a look of pity, and put special emphasis on the conversion rate. "You only get about 50 Presence if you convert it back. Don't worry about it though. Doubt you have enough for that to matter."

"Are there like. Thresholds I should be mindful of?" I really needed to get as much info as possible. Who knew when the next time I'd meet friendly faces would be? They hadn't attacked me yet, which was a good sign.

Nate stood with a stretch and a yawn before offering me his canteen of water. I shook my head. He placed it on the table beside me anyway.

"Yeah," he started. "Anything below ten and you're generally fucked. Most people try to balance all their stats at around twenty. It's safe enough to complete most Instances and lets them use their Presence more freely. Anything hovering near fifty is considered specialized."

"And higher than that?" I pressed.

"If you meet someone like that stay far far away." His tone grew cold, a mixture of frustration and fear just underneath. "They're always dangerous."

"Done with your questions boy?" Maggie asked, stepping back into the room, her tone cold once more.

"One last question. I don't know where I am in The Endless Dream right now. I've heard there's some safer places. How do I get there?"

Maggie shook her head, none too pleased. "Bothersome. It's a long journey … Right now Nate and I are stranded. We got separated from our group while investigating the upper layers. That's where we are right now. For the safer worlds you'll have to travel lower down."

I nodded, raising my hands placatingly with a forced sheepish smile. "Okay yeah. No more questions … well besides what now. We need to find the barn to clear this Instance right, or the scientist? Have you found either?"

"We have. The power line we passed earlier leads to the barn. It's swarming with monsters though, and with the Restriction combo we can't get close without dying."

Right. The Restrictions the Instance gave us for not having our own Restrictions were quite vicious. I'd definitely be getting whatever Restriction was offered when I beat this Instance, letting an Instance Holder decide for me kind of sucked.

"I have both Restrictions. I can't see them anymore and can't move when they see me." I offered.

Maggie winced and shook her head. "I didn't get any Restrictions from the Instance and Nate only has [Line of Sight], but even if he didn't it's still too dangerous. There's at least twenty of them surrounding that place."

Honestly, I didn't see the problem. They didn't seem that hard to kill. Way easier than I imagined it would be to kill a person, at least. Their attacks also functioned more like whips than knives. They fucking hurt, but it wasn't immediately life-threatening or debilitating. It didn't even seem like they were interested in killing us immediately. Nate had been pinned down by that creature for who knows how long before I arrived.

Even if it was just twenty, that should be manageable. It'd take a while, but they could be lured away one at a time or in small groups. "How long have you both been in this Instance?" I asked.

"Fucks sake boy." Maggie's voice boiled over with frustration, and I felt a seed of indignation plant itself heavily in my heart. "Do you ever slow down with the questions?"

I almost snapped back at her, but pushed the instinct down. Play dumb. Play dumb.

Nate stayed silent, not coming to my defense this time as he stared off into space, but there was a slight twitch in his back as if straightening. It only lasted for a moment before he turned to stare at the entrance to the Greenhouse. They were acting strangely. I didn't like it.

The answer would've been yes if I liked or trusted either of these people, but I didn't. Our goals might align for now, but the two of them knew each other and didn't know me. If push came to shove, I'd be the one left hung out to dry. I couldn't very well tell the older woman off, though, so I mentally sighed and started to pretend once more.

"Ah. My bad." I scratched the back of my neck and shifted my eyes to the side to sell the embarrassment. "This is just a lot. Sorry."

"We have some cots in the back," Maggie started after a moment. "Take some time to relax while Nate and I figure out what to do now. I'll make some tea. Having a third person opens up some options.

I nodded, the picture of compliance as I slunk off in the direction the older woman indicated. I didn't take the water canteen Nate had offered me earlier.

Stepping through one of the inner tent flaps, I was met with four cots. They seemed military in origin, with black metal cross bars holding up dense nylon fabric. Two of the cots had pairs of shoes underneath them, likely meaning they were taken by either Maggie or Nate. One of the two empty beddings had a small dried patch of blood – a trash bag sat at the foot of the bed with a used bandage. Taking that as a bad omen, I took the remaining bed.

I studied the room, and there were some medical kits, bags, what looked like water filters, and some MREs. For a couple of stranded people, they were well-equipped.

Was it safe to be here? Something told me no. There were thirty-four active Visitors, and I'd only met two. Where were the other thirty-two? If there were only twenty monsters at the barn, then it'd be trivial to clear the Instance by gathering the others. Then I could gain a Restriction and only have one forced on me when my countdown ran out, and I was back in the Mourning Circle. If I were lucky, there might be time to run another Instance too. I checked the clock in the corner of my vision, eleven hours and twenty-three minutes remaining.

What sealed it for me was something I hadn't expected to see. A steel red tool lay on a pile of gardening tools near the edge of the Greenhouse. It was Peeler. I felt a cold chill run down my spine. The System said it'd put Peeler somewhere relevant to the Instance. That meant that the Greenhouse wasn't just important as a safe haven but was directly relevant to whatever was happening in this place. And that relevance was ongoing. So why here and, more importantly, why with them?
