For most of the time Cass spent in the Annex, he was occupied with learning to control his own thoughts. Aside from a few times with Adya where things had gotten a little too hot, it had never really been a problem for him before.

There were a few times, particularly when it came to fighting, that an outside observer could say that Cass was overly impulsive, but this was a different situation entirely. A barrier in his mind had been taken down, and now, he was stuck trying to rebuild it.

Advanced Quest Formatting was a new challenge as he worked. His mind continuously tried to destabilize and move in a direction that wasn’t quite at odds with his previous thought, but rather, focused on a slightly different variation of the intended idea. It was a difficulty he hadn’t expected, and an unwelcome one as he’d really gotten into a groove regarding his work.

Halfway through the day, Maggie and Ren had dropped by his desk to let him know they’d be leaving soon. Cass, not quite trusting his mouth, had nodded and put on what he thought was a sad expression, but could’ve easily been seen as constipated. She gave him a tight smile and Ren a quick nod before they left him to get back to his work.

The more Quests he drafted, the greater the control of his now speedier thoughts. And by day’s end, he wasn’t quite normal, but he was better.

He’d begged off spending time with Adya using the excuse that he was tired and needed rest, which wasn’t quite wrong. Though Ana had delivered his lunch, it felt like it wasn’t enough food, and by the time he got to his room, he was starving.

The older woman dropped a second dinner in front of him, “You keep putting food away like that, and I’ll have to truss you up for someone else’s supper.”

Cass wiped a bit of grease away from his mouth. “What does that even mean?”

“It means you’re a pig,” She deadpanned. “You were extra hungry this morning, too. You feeling alright?”

“Yep, right as rain, thank-you,” Cass said a little too fast. When Ana gave him a look, he smiled. “Feelin fine.”

The moment she left, Cass moved over to his bed and spent the rest of the evening pinging Bella and doing one other thing.

Watching his notifications.

Tomorrow was a new Lottery, meaning he had the day off. It was also the day that, even while splitting experience with Adya, he should reach the second gate. After all of the new big-ticket items that had popped up over the last twenty-four hours, it was a lot to take in. He’d moved from complacency with his skills while eking out a bit of progress per week, to suddenly having entirely new thoughts to organize.

It was a big leap, and with his gate just around the corner, another one was coming.

This is a lot at once. If the second gate drops another quill-sized ability on me, I’ll need to learn to better manage my time with everything.

Cass uneasily fell asleep. Turning off his thoughts wasn’t as easy as it used to be, so for once, he woke up late as Ana was tapping at his door.

“Cass, you alright in there?”

“Yes, Ana, thank you. Sorry, I had a late night.”

“No problem. Why don’t you get showered, and I’ll get your breakfast set up.”

“Extra please,” Cass replied, the rumble in his stomach giving him orders the moment food entered his thoughts.

“Is this going to be a normal thing I need to prepare for?”

Another rumble accompanied his words, “Yes, I think so.”

“Alrighty. Hop to, the day is starting.”

Cass did as he was told, ate his double-portioned porridge, then moved through the day in a blur. Senior QuestWright Mirelle informed everyone they had the day off for the Lottery, then sidled over and informed him that his efficiency had dropped for the first time in several weeks.

More progress was made in better controlling his thoughts, and Cass was beginning to learn to hyperfocus on one thing at a time instead of bouncing between several directions. The result quickly became that, while his Quests looked the same, it was taking him half as much time to tell the System what he wanted.

Speed was now a new option on the table.

He was finishing up a large batch when Maggie, Ren, and Noah stopped at his desk. Maggie adjusted her glasses as she looked him over, “Well, Cass, it’s time we get out of here.”

“I see,” Cass replied, leaning back in his chair. “Well, I’d like to say I hope to see you in the future, but that wouldn’t be quite right.”

“You still haven’t forgiven us?” Ren asked.

Noah sighed, “Cross your nipples, here we go.”

“It’s not about forgiveness to me,” Cass replied with weight. He’d always been confused about his feelings regarding the three, but something clicked, and suddenly, he knew exactly how he felt.

“Then what’s it about?” Maggie replied.

In the back of his mind, all the disparate thoughts and conflicting emotions streamlined together into exactly what he wanted to say. It wouldn’t be kind, but it would be just.

Sitting up, Cass inched his chin down and tried not to glare, “It’s the downward slope. Yes, it’s a Quest, but you don’t have to do it. I’m a QuestWright, I would know. And that’s always how it starts, isn’t it? First, it’s a Quest, then an order, and you excuse the shitty thing you do. You think to yourself, "Well, I'm just doing what others are telling me.” Then, you move up, and you’re making excuses for why it needs to be done again. Or maybe, it needs to be a little harsher, or meaner, or more violent. Eventually, it’s just something that you do. A habit. A dirty habit that eats at your soul and burns you down until you’re someone that the original version of yourself wouldn’t  and couldn’t recognize.”

Of the three of them, Noah was the one who looked the most guilty. Cass didn’t care.

He paused to take a breath as they leaned back from him, “You kidnapped me from a city I’d just saved. Took me from everyone I loved. And brought me to a hellhole that treats me like a spy or some leftover meat put in the sun too long. You knew what this place was like before coming here, and you barely thought about me at all. Because of a Quest. I could’ve done more in Liora. Could’ve saved people. Instead, I have to tiptoe everywhere I go in Liberty.”

He knifed a hand through the air, causing Maggie to flinch. “I don’t hate you, that’s as far as it goes. It’d be easy to hate you. However, you’ve helped me a little since we’ve been here, and that doesn’t make the situation any worse.” He paused. “But forgive? Hell no.”

None of them spoke as Cass picked up the next Quest from the pile and immediately dove into it. Noah and Ren filed out first, but Maggie held back for just a moment. Cass heard her as he updated the Quest’s Outline.

“If you ever need us, we’ll be in the Capital. Look for me in the green sector.”

He didn’t reply as she left.

Cass tried to temper his emotions as he finished up his day and headed out to the front of the Guildtower. He could feel Bella and Adya as they approached, though the closer they got, the more worry he sensed from them both. The two looked at him somberly as he pulled himself up, and they rode out. Both could tell he didn’t feel like talking, and Cass completely ignored the blinking notification in his vision.

They made it to the apartment without a word said. Awkwardly, Adya hopped down first, forcing Cass to move forward in the saddle. She smiled at him.

“I wanted to give you a heads up when people weren’t around. Alan is gearing up for something big with the Dungeon. We’ve got a week off, then he said we need to be ready.”

Cass tried not to sigh, “Alright.”

“I’m not going to ask what’s going on, but I can tell you’re all wound up tight like a spring. Why don’t you spend the day with Bella and go for a long ride? It’ll do you both some good, I think.”

“It’s my only day off for the month,” Cass replied.

“Still.”

He could immediately feel how positively Bella took that, and didn’t have the heart to say no. Adya patted his leg twice, then they slowly plodded toward the gate.

While they moved, Cass pulled up the notification he expected. Only, it wasn’t what he expected.

[QUESTWRIGHT TIER UPGRADE BLOCKED]

Purge one nearby corruption

“Of course,” Cass replied hotly. “Of course, that’s what you want.”

A few people looked at him, but Cass didn’t care. The guard at the front gate let them through after he showed them his Guild pass, and then, they were outside the walls. Even in his mired frustration, Cass went through a realization.

I haven’t been outside the city in all the time I’ve been here.

Everywhere he looked, it was a riot of color. Green was the most predominant, but there were also huge portions of red as tomatoes grew from the earth, twisted vines spilling across wooden lattices, and thick clusters of fruit hanging low enough that Cass could’ve reached out and grabbed them from the saddle.

The entirety of his vision was locked into growth, life, and vibrant hues. The world of gray walls and cyantic maps was behind him. Out here, it was a boy, his horse, and beauty all around.

He couldn’t help it, he laughed. As if that were the signal, Bella pushed off the earth and rushed forward down the cobblestone road. People traveling forward quickly got the drift and moved out of the way as the two beat down the path with great speed.

Traveling down the fields took so long that Cass lost track of the time as he breathlessly held onto Bella’s reins. Eventually, the fields ended, and that wasn’t good enough for both of them. He sensed what she wanted and yelled in her ear.

“Let’s get it, girl!”

A sharp turn after a brief slowdown, then they were off again, racing the edges of the fields against the wind and with the sun on their backs. Cass laughed again as Bella pushed herself to her top speed, running until they were both breathless and finally, were forced to slow down.

He immediately hopped off her back and came around on shaky legs to put his head against her own, “Oh, Bella, I do love you.”

She neighed, and then something forced itself into his screen.

[SYSTEM NOTICE]

Your reputation with the draft-horse, Bella, has increased by 98

You’ve reached 200 reputation with Bella the draft-horse

You’ve reached 250 reputation with Bella the draft-horse

Your bond will now provide new functionality…

Immediately, a second blinding headache struck Cass in the same number of days. With eyes closed, Cass leaped to the Prestige screen.

[BELLA THE DRAFT-HORSE: 250]

0-100:

Saved by Cassio Vale, Bella the Draft-horse gains unflinching loyalty toward her bonded companion.

Unlocks rudimentary communication between man and animal.

Increases the intelligence of the draft-horse to near-human levels that will strengthen with the bond.

101-150:

Through caring actions and daily contact, the bond between man and animal has increased to a heightened level.

Unlocks location sense between man and animal.

Increases the odds of a titled connection forming between the bonded pair.

Contracted Addition:

By contract, emotions may now be shared across the bond. This will not fade even if the contract abruptly ends.

151-200:

Sustained trust and shared experience deepen the bond beyond simple awareness.

Unlocks intermittent sensory sharing between man and animal.

Flashes of sight and sound may be perceived across the bond.

201-250:

The bond reaches a state of completion through continued reliance and mutual survival.

Unlocks full communication between man and animal.

Thoughts may now be exchanged clearly without the loss of intent.

Shared perception may now be maintained at will.

Further growth of the bond is locked without System assistance.

250+:

Locked

Cass fell to his knees as the pain intensified before settling, “Why all at once? I don’t understand.”

Oh! I can answer that for you! Aye, I can indeed!

Cass fell flat to the ground as the voice screamed in his mind, “Oh my lord, that’s loud.”

Forgive me for that. Is this better?

“Yes, a little. Who’s-” Then it hit him. “Bella? You can talk!”

That I can, Cass, and you should know, I love thee as well.

“Thee? Wait.” He finally opened his eyes as the headache faded to the back of his skull. Bella’s horsy head was right in front of him. She blew back his hair with a whuff.

You look most dashing this day.

“Why are you talking that way?”

Tis my manner of speaking.

“I doubt that very much…”

Cass thought back to when he’d last heard language like this. Then he groaned, “You’re talking like the people from the play.”

Their glory knows no bounds

“Oh, my god. But-but,” he spluttered, “we only listened there for, what, 30 minutes? How did you pick all of this up?”

Old man Alan marked my delight at the play. He hath taughtst me much I knew not.

“I think that was supposed to be taught.”

Okay.

Cass’s internal monologue rapidfired through his mind, Okay, Cass, your horse can talk in your mind, and she sounds like a badly trained actor. It’s fine. You love her, and if this is what she wants, then that’s the way it is. Don’t make your first time holding a real conversation with her be negative. Now, smile!

Cass gave a wary grin.

Art thou well?

“I’m fine, Bella girl. Thank you. So why did the reputation come all at once?”

Tis simple, I have held it in restraint. The system is most kind to mineself, in that if I did not wish for our reputation to grow, it shall not.

“So you’ve been saving up the reputation all this time?”

Aye, most assuredly.

“Well, okay then, sure.” Makes sense, sure. “Do you know how to unlock what happens next? I figure if you have some kind of a special relationship with the System, maybe it’s said something about getting you a Calling. The contract implied you could have one, I’m just not sure how to go about doing it.”

Alas, nay. I know not how it is done, yet I am certain thee shall find the way. Already do I know the Calling I would most desire.

“I think it’s thou,” He waved a hand, “you know what, it doesn’t matter. What Calling do you want?”

There are many I would wish for!

“Too loud!” Cass said, grabbing his head as a migraine began to form.

Apologies.

“It’s fine.” Cass pulled some grass out of the ground as the pulsing settled in the back of his head. “Alright, well, we’ll find a way to get you a Calling and figure it out from there. Sound good?”

I have never doubted thee

They spent a little more time talking until Bella wanted to try something. Hopping upon her back, he leaned forward. “You ready?”

Aye. Hold fast.

As Bella began to leap across the edges of the fields again, Cass used another new ability they shared. Cass’s vision faded as a new kind took its place.

The wide fields and tall trees took on new and varied shades of light and shadow as he watched the world through the eyes of his closest equine friend. The world was open to them as boy and horse, bonded together, galloping across the land, finally in sync.
