Risha stood on the wall of the Scaled’s last bastion, her eyes on the bloodied swamps below.

The defeated Scaled were pale with the sudden desertion of the Spark’s power, slumped in camps that were watched by Risha’s warriors. To her eyes now, they were a defeated people, bereft of hope from a long fought war that they lost. The plans of their leaders had failed them, and Risha found herself burnt and bloodied from the amount of mercy she’d already extended.

Corpses still littered the swamps, from the magnificent dragons to the smallest of Goblins. They were a reminder of the world that Risha faced to see her dreams a reality. The sharp consequences of her people’s freedom.

“Grand Mother?”

The voice took a moment to reach her, but it brought her from the deep anger that had begun to fill her body.

She turned around, and faced the children she’d evolved into Spirits. Glo stood with his spear towering above him, blood still gleamed from its tip, but his eyes watched her with an understanding that made her heart twist.

Klaz’zks stood in her half-human form, her many legs holding onto the edge of the wall facing the city. She looked at Risha with a blood-fueled passion, eager for the Mother’s next commands.

Sun-Chaser had a long cut through the orange fur of her cheek, and she leaned against the parapet from exhaustion. Still, Risha could see some positive changes in the fox’s spirit.

Risha looked beyond them, to the mountains where Klu had disappeared.

“Mother, one of the dragons wishes to speak with you.” Glo spoke, bringing her attention to them once again.

Risha nodded, “They can wait until after we send our people to the spirit realm.”

Glo bowed, and Risha turned to Klaz’zks.

“It is time.” Klaz’zks said, “We have fed, and now we grow tired.”

Risha walked over to the spider. The  Spider Empress lowered her head, and Risha placed her hand on her hair, “Thank you. Return to the warrens. Risha will go home soon too.”

Klaz’zks gave a soft smile, showing the two fangs in her teeth. “You will soon, Risha, and you will bring with you many things.”

The woman rose, then shifted, taking the form of a spider. A sharp stomp of her feet sent a spreading sound through the battlefield.

Her people knew what that meant. Klaz’zks climbed down the wall, and those who desired sleep followed their Empress to the warrens.

Sun-Chaser waited until the spider was gone before speaking. “The builders have been working hard with the saplings to establish roads between the two cities and here. Supplies will arrive within the week, there were… many hungry Scaled here. Thankfully, our cities had a surplus in the year’s harvest. In addition, Goblin Farmers will arrive tomorrow to bring your techniques to the Scaled.”

“They won’t refuse to become mine.”

Sun-Chaser paused, and watched Risha with a small amount of worry. “Grand Mother?”

Risha looked over the pale Scaled in their camps. “They will take my hand because they have no choice.”

Sun-Chaser narrowed her eyes, “They will take your hand because you offer them food, freedom, and a home. That is better than exile, and they know it. You aren't an overseer, Risha. You know that.”

“Am I-?

“Risha,” Glo cut her off and she turned to him in surprise. “They started this, and they will not be your slaves.” He walked up to her and spoke quietly so that only Sun-Chaser and Risha could hear. “You are a path up, not the chains that hold them down.”

Risha took a deep breath, then focused her body. “Yes. Sorry Sun-Chaser. Who has been chosen to watch the city? Have we chosen members for the council?”

“Elder Oltak will oversee the city with the help of a Chieftain who first surrendered to us. There are several members we are considering for the council. The Draconic Storm and its rider are two of them.”

Risha thought back to the Drake she had evolved before the battle, his spirit had been strong, and she thought he would be a good fit for her council. “Good. It is time then. I’ll offer them the choice.”

“Risha, rest first?” Glo placed his hand on her arm, “You’re still covered in the blood of the war.”

Risha looked down at her armor, brown from the blood and guts of those she’d killed. Her cloak wasn’t much better. Her muscles ached with the power she’d pushed through them, and mud covered her boots.

Still, she shook her head. “I won’t leave them in pain any longer than I have to.” Risha turned towards the stairs down the wall, and began a long journey to the ground below.

Glo and Sun-Chaser shared a look, then fell into step behind RIsha.

She made her way to the camps, her warriors bowing to her as she passed them by.

“Sun-Chaser,” Risha said, stepping past yet another pair of Kobold warriors.

“Yes, Grand Mother?”

“I’ve been putting it off. But it is time that I meet the pack elders. I want them gathered by the time we return to the Orc City.”

Sun-Chaser cleared her throat, “Grand Mother, if I may. It is tradition that the new Alpha go to them in the Pack’s home.”

Risha gave the fox a sidelong glance, “Do you think I should? I am the Empress after all.”

Sun-Chaser didn’t answer right away, mulling over the question. “I think you should, but you need to do it right. They can’t think they can control you. Be respectful, but don’t give them any warning, and don’t let them decide the manner of your meeting. I will prepare what you need for the trip.”

Before their conversation could continue, they arrived at the first Scaled camp.

Klu stepped through the snow.

Blood.

Was it real?

Klu couldn’t remember anymore.

Another step through the snow.

He knew that he had to move forward, he couldn’t stop.

He’d promised. Even if the spirits called him to join them, he wasn’t ready. Not yet. Bodies stared up from the frozen waistland around him. Boulders were cut in two, and the mountain itself was shorter than it’d once been.

Were the bodies real?

No, the blood was still fresh, flowing from them into the once-white snow. It had been hours since the battle. The destruction though, he remembered that.

And the arm in the snow, that was once his. It lay next to the wing he’d ripped from the vulture.

He stepped past it, another step.

That’s what he had to focus on.

Just one more. One foot in front of the other.

The bodies around him flickered, their faces changing into the people he loved. His wife, his children, his brother, long gone from this world.

The sword Klu had slaughtered him with still rose from his chest.

No, Klu would not fall.

He dragged his eyes past the bodies, and took another step.

How much farther now? He’d called upon the wings of a Demon to follow the Angel through the mountains, and he’d long since lost track of where their battle started.

Down, toward the sun, that’s where he must go.

His steps continued, unstopping.

The body of a goblin lay in his path, but he walked through it, knowing that the visions were not reality. They couldn’t be.

Another orc, coughing up blood and begging for his life to be spared, for the Mystic to stop his rampage.

Klu walked past.

His foot stopped on something solid, and Klu looked down.

Wingless, eyes forever open in surprise, the head lay half separated from the body. Klu’s heart pounded with the anger that had overcome him in their battle, a desire for blood, death, destruction.

He stepped over the body, and continued down. The stump that was once his arm still bled, but he had marched and killed through worse.

He was an orc, the evolution of war. He would not stop until he was home.

Another step.

And another.

Scarlet and crimson covered the edges of his vision.

A lesser orc would die before they returned, but he was not a lesser orc. He was one of the Grand Mother’s chosen. A Spirit Mystic.

He slid and tried to catch himself with his missing arm.

He fell into the snow, the cold invading him, threatening to take his mind to the soft embrace of sleep.

He roared, the sound echoing through the snowy mountain.

Then he pushed himself up.

Another step.

And another.
