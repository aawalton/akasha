Logan’s new quest would involve meeting with Horace later at the guild hall. For now, though, he had coin to spend, and when he had asked Senna to join him at the guild market, she was more than happy.

The guild market was larger than Logan had expected. It wasn’t in the guild hall itself, but actually positioned behind it. It also wasn’t nearly as extensive as the grid of booths found in the market district, but that didn’t mean it was small.

He stood at a booth specially catered to blowguns. Senna stood at the next booth over, which had a bunch of fletching material.

“If you’re looking to deal more damage,” said the vendor as she pointed to a polished black tube, “this is your best option. It’s long and in one piece, so you’ll have greater velocity.”

Logan nodded, but he was on the fence.

The vendor’s logic was sound. Plus, longer blowguns were easier to use for early-learners. While they were less maneuverable, they provided greater accuracy.

Then again, Logan had never really depended on the blowguns for actual kills. His Measured Touch heritage boon, even though he didn’t think of it often, gave him passive improvements to manual attacks, which applied to his gauntlets. His initial pea shooter had only been a convenient backup.

He had even considered switching to a different ranged option altogether, like throwing daggers or a sling. But his goal wasn’t really to deal heavy ranged damage, because his bonuses were best usable in close quarters.

For that reason, he decided to stick with the blowgun-related items. It might sacrifice some damage output, but it worked better for his style for now.

Also, now that he knew the skills one gained could affect class selection, he wanted to be more conscientious of how he was growing. It seemed like a distance utility option paired with a primarily close-range damage vehicle would give the best potential results.

“I would rather prioritize a blowgun I can aim quicker and with one hand. What about this one?” Logan asked as he pointed to a smaller, bamboo reed.

“That,” said the vendor with a raised lip, “is a good budget option.” She didn’t sound as if she had even sold herself on that.

Logan lifted it up. “What do you think, Senna?”

Senna glanced up from where she held a dozen different fletching accoutrements. “It’s probably a better size, but you might want something sturdier. Bamboo is fine for a simple blowgun, but you could get that in the regular markets too. While it’s great for amplifying condition effects in potions, you can probably get something better.” She pointed with a thin metal shaft. “See the titanium one next to it? If you’re sticking to that size bracket, that one’s still lightweight and will do way better.”

Logan lifted the titanium blowgun and, sure enough, it was lighter than some others he had checked. He wondered if Senna just knew that off the top of her head or if her Analyze skill could give her additional details of objects like his did with enchantments. “How much for this?”

“That’s 8 silvers.”

Logan nodded and pulled out some of his hard-earned coin. He glanced once more at the price tag on the bamboo version, which was a fraction of the cost, but everything Senna had pointed out made sense to him. Plus, he remembered the sorry state of his original pea shooter. It had practically disintegrated from his occasional use of it in melee fighting too.

The metal was the right option, especially if he continued to use it in unconventional ways.

Senna wrapped up her own transaction and walked down an aisle. “Now we need to stop by the alchemist. I have some powders I want him to convert into poisons. We can use those on your new ammunition. I also have that driprot poison recipe from the gardener in Kural.” Logan remembered the specialized poison that increased in potency for a specific target. “I think I can infuse it into an oil that won’t activate its targeting properties prematurely and can increase the concentration even further.”

Her stride changed to something more brisk and jerky. It seemed like something was bothering her. It also seemed strange that she would have the alchemist mix the poisons for her.

Logan knew she had her own struggles about wanting to be an alchemist. Despite her desire and training efforts, she was more talented with crafting, so alchemy could be a sore subject. If he could help her, he would, but he also didn’t want to pry if she didn’t want to share.

“Yeah, you know more about mixing poisons than I do.”

She gave a sighing laugh. “But not enough. I haven’t gained any levels since we’ve been in Caerwyn.”

Logan knew that feeling of not being enough and he didn’t like seeing the usually chipper Senna so bummed out. “Hey, aren’t you the one who told me the longer you use a skill at a lesser level, once you bump past it, the increases are bigger than normal? That helped me when I was leveling up my Dodge and CryoPyre skills. You were right, too, because the moment I did make a gain, the both jumped multiple levels at once.”

“Eh, I was just saying that. Some people have noticed it, but other people never do and the skills just completely stop leveling so they move on. It’s not actually proven, but it’s a nice thing to hear when you’re the one stalling, so that’s why I said it to you.”

“That’s silly. If some people never make the level gain, that’s gotta be because they stopped trying. I bet if they kept it up, they would have eventually made those big gains. My Aetherlens had been in a similar position, and now it’s leveling again. I think you’re just set up for huge gains later.”

She smiled but didn’t seem convinced yet. “Maybe. But maybe I should just just be grateful I hit my threshold perk in Crafting. I should focus on that and just stop the poison and potion stuff altogether.”

Before Logan could protest, she stopped walking and looked at him with a raised hand. “Basic poisons are one thing, but not the stuff I wanted to do.” She looked down and scuffed the floor with her shoe. “You know, I’ve seen how you go out of your way for Mishki and Alden. I should be more of a team player like you, rather than pursuing the things I want.”

“Oh, please. Senna, you already do so much for us!”

“Yeah. Gear upgrades, like Bromlin’s axe, your gauntlets, and Flick’s drakla pits. And that’s what I should stick to.”

Logan put a hand on Senna’s shoulder to get her to raise her eyes. “You know it’s more than that. Bromlin would be lost without you. Plus, what about that potion that lets Bromlin and Mishki keep up with us? Even if it’s a simple mix, that’s an enormous boost to their morales. Besides, even the upgrades you’re talking about have more organic material than I’ve seen from anyone else. Like, none of these booths we’ve passed sell enhanced, exploding fruit seeds.”

She waffled, considering his words.

“And,” Logan added, “don’t think I forget about what happened beneath Gnashridge Heights. When we only had three gliders to split between four of us, you didn’t hesitate in handing those out to everyone but yourself.”

Still staring at the ground, her lips curled up in a smile, and she shook with a small, grateful laugh. “Thanks, Logan.”

She cleared her throat and shook her shoulder from his grip. “Now don’t get all mushy on me. What if someone sees this and tells Cassandra?”

“Huh?”

She laughed. “Come on. You might have a higher Perception, but that doesn’t mean I can’t see.”

Senna resumed walking at a brisk pace.

Logan’s cheeks felt warm as he hurried to keep stride with her. “Is it that obvious?”

The tinkerer just made an amused sound.

Logan sighed. “She’s got a strong seat in her family, and I’m a random ragamuffin.”

“I thought you were a champion summoned from another world.”

Logan supposed she was right, but he still felt like just a normal guy. Maybe with a bit of magic at his disposal now, but he would still rather be chasing chickens than seeking out a confrontation with the reavers. But people are depending on me. I can’t forget that.

When they reached the alchemist, Senna started talking to him about certain oils and compounds she was looking for. The alchemist nodded, and she gave him a satchel of her own so that he could go back behind his tent and get to mixing it in for her.

While they waited, she browsed some of his other wares.

“So,” Logan said, “you mentioned you got a threshold perk in Crafting. What is it?”

“It’s just the standard one. It’s called Alloy Expert. When I’m mixing different materials into weapons, I can have the base material inherit the best qualities from different metals and get rid of the worst.”

“Does everyone who has the Crafting skill get that?”

“Mostly. But some classes modify or tweak the perk. The first crafter I ever trained under was a Siege Technician, and he could also imbue the effects into ammunition as added splash damage or condition spreading.”

“What about when you get a class? Will it retroactively apply to perks?” He was actually curious about that for some of his own skills, too. Mariv had mentioned that skill-related class benefits mostly kicked in after the second perk.

“Yes, it will.” She made an emphatic fist and smiled, followed by a wink. “But what I’m really interested in is whatever my alchemy skill reaches for its second threshold perk.”

Logan grinned back at her. Good. She didn’t even have the first threshold perk in alchemy, so at least she was thinking optimistically.

The alchemist returned. “Here you go. That will be 2 silver and 15 copper pieces.”

She reached for her coinpurse, but Logan lifted a hand.

“I’ll get it. I saw what you were buying at the last booth, and I know those were darts for my blowgun.”

She paused with her hand extended partway to the seller and raised her eyebrows. “Okay, yes, you do still have that Perception after all.” She smiled and put her coin away, taking a step back to cede the floor to Logan.

“Here you go,” Logan said as he fished the money out and handed it to the vendor.

“By the way,” the alchemist said as he gestured to Senna’s bag. “You have some good-quality ingredients here. But you really shouldn’t be storing those in drakla pits. Drakla pits are great for storing potions with restorative effects because of the fruit’s replenishing nature, but not crafting materials like explosive powders. Those will be more potent if you keep them in metal. Maybe you should try something like that. You might even gain the Alchemy skill for yourself and won’t have to come to me.”

A pink tinge crept into Senna’s cheeks. Logan knew that whatever the alchemist had observed was probably making her embarrassed. Senna already had the skill, and while it seemed like the alchemist had meant it cordially, it was just pointing out to Senna how unnatural it came to her.

As they left the alchemist’s booth and headed for the exit, Senna fidgeted with her pack’s drawstring slung over her shoulder until her face returned to a normal shade. Logan pretended to scan the booths they were passing, so she hopefully didn’t feel embarrassed on his account.

When she spoke, her voice had regained its plucky timbre. “I want to make a quick stop at the guild. Mishki’s been studying a lot lately, and I wanted to give him a little machine I put together. I got some inspiration from the perk-divining rod and wanted to test my Alloy Expert perk. After that, I’m meeting up with Brummy at the inn. With the money you just saved me, what do you say to a round of Mana Matrix Scramblers?”

Logan chuckled. “Sure.” She turned left toward the door, but Logan lingered. “Go ahead without me, though. I have one more thing I wanted to buy.”

She shrugged. “Okay. But Brummy’s probably already a few drinks in, so I’ll have to catch up. I can’t promise I won’t start without you.”

“Ha, don’t worry. I won’t be long.”

Once she disappeared through the market’s entryway, Logan checked his coin to confirm he still had enough for this next purchase. Satisfied, he beelined for the booth.
