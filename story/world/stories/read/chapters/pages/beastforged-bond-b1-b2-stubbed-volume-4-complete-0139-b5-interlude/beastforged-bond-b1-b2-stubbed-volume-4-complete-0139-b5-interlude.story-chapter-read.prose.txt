Truthseeker Max

Max was quite happy with how the day turned out. The newest cadets had just arrived for the training program, and Adam Savier had been among them. Most spirit cadets were connected to Spirits through their heritage, whether through their race or their lineage. The Fire Caldera belonged to the former, whereas certain Houses of Dracos belonged to the latter with Spiritborne ancestors.

Then there were the outliers. Powerful Blessed or those that somehow caught the Spirits’ attention. Adam Savier belonged to both categories yet neither at the same time. Humans were connected to the Spirits through their past, but the kind of connection was far from positive either way.

Putting all that aside, Max was happy to see Adam. He’d been worried when the young human hadn’t been there at the last training program and had promised himself to look for him if he hadn’t come this time around either.

Fortunately, Adam arrived, leaving Truthseeker Max with only one problem: he had to make sure the Head of the Spirit Guard Temple would acknowledge Adam and accept him into the ranks of the Spirit Guards. As a high-ranking officer, maybe even a prospect Knight if possible. That may not be what Death had asked from him, but it was the best for everyone.

So he came to visit the old man. Glacia’s Knight stood near one of the Temple’s balconies overlooking the courtyard, arms folded behind his back as he watched the Squires train below. Frost drifted lazily from his robes despite the pleasant weather, making Max wince. The air around the old man always seemed cooler than it should have been. Blessed by Glacia, while also being one of the coldest races residing in Razarn, that did make sense though.

“What are you doing here, Max?” Glacia’s Knight and Head of the Spirit Guard Temple asked without diverting his gaze from the Squires. “Don’t tell me you want me to watch the cadets all day. Has your special recruit arrived?”

The mockery in his tone made Max frown. “You know that Death told me to invite him. She’s interested in him.”

Glacia’s Knight, also one of the ancient Emperors, rolled his eyes at that. “If Death had been so interested in him, she would have approached him herself. Clearly, she did not, or a new Vowed would have joined the Squires weeks ago.” He finally glanced over, eyes blue as the ocean and cold as ice meeting his. “You know you can’t always use the Spirits as an excuse.”

The Truthseeker stared blankly at the old Emperor.

Did I just hear that correctly? he asked himself incredulously. I did that one time. ONE white lie, and that’s all he remembers for the last hundred years. How petty can you be!?

The old man was blessed directly by Glacia herself and commanded the largest Spirit Guard Temple on Razarn. He had served the Spirits for centuries, yet somehow, he remained one of the most irritating beings Max had ever met.

Then again, some of the things he said did make sense. He had never seen the Spirits fight. Nobody ever had, yet nobody was as dismissive of the Spirits’ authority as Glacia’s personal Knight. But maybe that was the problem. Glacia did not bother with him even though he was her only Knight. He had served the Spirits for so long, only to be dismissed. Because he dismissed them first. That was what it looked like to Max, but how would he know? He wasn’t an Emperor and would likely never reach that far. He would ascend to become a Ruler and join the Rankers. That, however, was the extent of his talent.

The more Max thought about it, the more confusing the Spirits were. Glacia could have taken away her Knight’s power, though she never did.

The Spirits’ power was obvious. Their influence was undeniable. Entire civilizations revolved around them. But to an ancient Emperor who had witnessed countless wars as well as the fall of the Realm, perhaps that wasn’t enough.

“If Death specifically–” he repeated himself, only to cut his own voice as a deep rumble echoed through the halls.

The floor beneath them vibrated. It was not enough to shake the temple, but enough to be noticed. Glacia’s Knight went still and the casual boredom plastered all over his face vanished. Power rippled through the air and the old Emperor disappeared.

“Hey!” Max hissed, feeling like he knew what was about to happen. He could tell where the power came from, but that was painfully obvious at this point. The cadets’ hall. “At least take me with you.”

That said, he moved on his own. The temple blurred around him as ether coursed through him, and it wasn’t long before he caught up to Glacia’s Knight. And, for the first time in many years, Max saw genuine surprise on the old man’s face.

“Is that Zegrath?” he asked, clearly sensing the Truthseeker come up from behind.

Max stopped beside the old man and smiled at the familiar sight unfolding before him. The hall reserved for the cadets’ training program was no longer an unblemished masterpiece of craftsmanship. It looked more like a battlefield. Several pillars had been shattered – no, they had been cut apart. The floor that should have been strong enough to endure the force of an average Grandmaster was cracked open in several places. And at the center of the destruction stood two figures. One was the Spiritborne of Volca, Zegrath, and the other was a young cadet bearing familiar features.

Adam Savier.

“Yes, that is Zegrath,” Max chuckled.

“And she is getting beaten up by one of our newest cadets?” Glacia’s Knight asked, even though they were both seeing the same thing.

“She sure is.”

Max knew that Adam was strong. He had felt it before, but the scene before him was so much more than he had expected.

Appendages adorned with razor-sharp blades grew out from his lower back. They carved through the floor and pillars as easily as through hide and flesh. It was likely only possible due to the semi-translucent grey sheen covering the blades, but that little detail hardly mattered.

Adam warped the space around him and teleported to Zegrath’s side. She whirled around but froze for a quarter of a second as Adam’s eyes flashed golden. The bladed appendages tore through her armor and left two gashes before Zegrath regained her bearing, but even then the situation didn’t change. They exchanged wounds after the first clash. Zegrath was clearly stronger and her blade cut deeper into Adam, but that also did not seem to matter. Adam’s wounds recovered as the fight continued. His body knit itself together at a visible pace, which only seemed to accelerate the longer the fight went on.

Then, as a ripple of ether flowed out of Adam, the ground tore open where Zegrath intended to step. She tripped, stopping the attack she had planned in its tracks. Zegrath caught herself with a burst of fire, but that cost her dearly. She lost track of Adam for yet another quarter of a second, which he used to teleport into the air and kick her in the face.

Next up were the illusions. Illusions of Adam, albeit not nearly as perfect as they could have been, appeared all around Zegrath, all while spikes made of the Temple’s floor shot toward her calves and thighs. She did notice them in time and twisted her way out of the worst, but doing so forced her right into Adam’s trap.

A concealed blade dipped in the greyish coat was waiting for her, and she leaped right into it.

Adam accelerated after that. He was not stronger, yet his speed seemed to pick up while Zegrath slowed down. That was Adam’s doing – one of his traits, to be precise – but the details eluded Max. He could only see Adam’s fluidity, evading Zegrath’s attacks while his counteroffensive strikes landed more and more often.

As the fight went on, it grew more and more apparent that Zegrath was actually losing. And that irritated her enough to burst into flames.

Volca’s presence erupted from within her, even though she should know that she was not yet ready to do so.

“What a fool,” Glacia’s Knight muttered, yet he did not make a move to help Zegrath. She was burning herself to the ground just to defeat Adam, and the old Emperor was just watching, the corner of his lips curled into a shit-eating grin.

Disgusting bastard.
