Luom's lack of resistance had come as somewhat of a shock to Luke, who hadn't expected her to fall apart with so little effort, even if he knew she wasn't used to fighting. A bellow from Perwen signaled his attack, a wide, frantic swing from a longsword, followed by a step forward and a push with the shield he carried. The attacks were uncoordinated and simple enough to dodge. Almost too simple. A bolt of ice slammed into his shoulder, penetrating almost an inch as it threw Luke forward. Rather than keep up the attack, Perwen opted for a spell that Luke knew he'd seen before, despite not having had access to the ability to sense spellweaves. Their barrier mage spun up a barrier around their party leader, but that wasn't enough to stop Luke.

Destabilize Spellweave [Active]: Destroy spellweaves with Threads of Mana.

While magical constructs like barriers were more difficult, this one posed almost no resistance due to its panicked caster. Luke wasn't sure why they were unraveling when they'd been so focused and coordinated during their previous fight, but he'd take it. Both Perwen's resurrection spell and the barrier protecting him fell apart as Luke charged under and past Perwen's raised arm, aiming to reach Lei and put a stop to her spell casting once and for all.

"Stop!" Perwen shouted, grabbing Luke's shoulder with a massive arm and throwing him aside.

Weavestep brought Luke close enough to grab Lei's wrist with a thread of mana, pulling her down with him. Tumbling to the ground, he reached into her Meta-Heart and drained mana from her with everything he had, intending to end her before she could cast another spell. Through it all, he kept Weaver's Perception primed on Perwen. If he attempted another resurrection spell, Luke would need to shift his focus. If he got to bringing his party back from the dead, things would turn in their favor, for obvious reasons.

"Please stop!" Perwen shouted again, and Lei held up her hands, as if to show she surrendered.

Luke, against his better judgment, paused for a moment. The threads were still in Lei, taking her mana and making it his own, since he needed it to keep Guardian Weave going around the speck of void mana trembling in his own Meta-Heart.

"Let me resurrect the First Chair's daughter," Perwen said as he positioned himself between Luom's body and Luke.

"Why. Would. I. Do. That?" Luke asked, struggling to talk through his gasping breaths. Fighting all these opponents was putting a strain on his body, as well as his mind.

Perwen's plate helmet disappeared, showing an expression of dread and worry on his face. "Her father will crumble if it becomes known she died on an unauthorized outing, and his choir will fall without him."

Having caught his breath, Luke eyed the Singer. "Sounds like it'll bring discord to you, Singer. How is that bad for me, exactly?"

"Without their support, the majority will shift," Perwen said, removing his shield and sword as if in an attempt to show his earnestness. "That means those who are in support of your continued existence here on Earth will fail."

"Politics?" Luke asked.

"Politics regarding your future."

"If she dies, we all die?" Luke asked.

Perwen glanced down at the ground, his fingers twitching. "Let's just stop this. Let me resurrect her now, or it will be too late. I'll even allow you to leave this place with your life. You have grown stronger, Lifeweaver, but so have we."

"Why did you bring her?"

"I knew you would survive the initial assault and wanted to bring you over in accordance with the agreement. Luom wanted the same, so she ordered us to let her join. She is too kind a soul for the position she has been placed in."

Luke considered for a moment, an uneasiness spreading over him. The situation was strange, not what he expected. He breathed in deep, checked his mana levels, then shook his head. "No."

"No?!" Perwen bellowed, his armor and weapon reappearing in an instant. His entire body took on a glow as several spellweaves spun into existence around him. "I judge you unworthy to join our melody!"

With a twitch of his threads, he ended Lei's life, taking her Meta-Heart, which he hoped would make resurrection impossible. Zelaui, having returned to life, burst from the bushes he'd known her to be hiding in. Perwen hadn't even seemed to care about the death of his wife, but Luke was wise to their deception. Her resurrection happened almost right away, as soon as they engaged in combat. Rather than chase after Curtis, she'd turned around and waited for her moment to strike at Luke.

What he hadn't expected was that Smudge would explode from his Stable spot and meet the Singer Navigator in the air. Luke's companion opened his jaw, and his head seemed to expand as the two collided. From one moment to the next, Zelaui became nothing more than a shower of gore, all of it sucked into Smudge's personal void, all except her Meta-Heart, which he crushed between his teeth before swallowing it almost whole.

This time, Perwen reacted, and he finished the spells he'd been preparing with a cry of anguish. Light slammed into Luke from above, even as a faint glow appeared within Smudge. The spell was a metaphysical one, targeting not only the physical body in an attempt to disintegrate it but also the mana channels from the top of his head all the way down to his feet. It was powerful, but not powerful enough. Luke's healing kicked in, with healing mana rushing out from his Meta-Heart into the rest of his body. In most cases, that beam would be an instant kill, but Luke found that he could deny some of the damage with nothing more than a push from his will. That moment was the first time in a good long while he'd felt powerful in the true sense of the word. The mere idea that he could will something not to damage him was ludicrous, but here it was, plain for him to see.

Even so, the damage was building, and more spells were coming. A flaw in the spell, or perhaps just a product of how it was farmed, meant it could not move. It was a straight beam anchored in place. All he had to do was step out of it. He did so, singed but still very much in the fight.

A sword-swing met him, but Luke was ready for it. Rather than risk his staff breaking, he used The Healer's Staff to block the blow, and Perwen's weapon came to a jarring stop. Before the Singer had a chance to pull it back for another strike, Luke extended threads of mana from the staff, pushing them into Perwen's mana channels, around his limbs in an attempt to hold him in place, and one more around his neck.

"How did you survive that spell?" Perwen asked with a stunned grunt, thrashing against the restraints.

The Singer did his utmost to push Luke's threads out, but lacked the will to force his version of what should unfold into existence. Threads holding, Luke pulled mana from his opponent and used it to heal himself.

To their side, the light inside Smudge's void faded away. Perwen had failed in resurrecting Zelaui. Another spellweave formed right in front of the Singer, but Luke tore it apart. "No more spells."

"Let me resurrect them," Perwen said as Luke pushed him to the ground. The mountain of a Singer felt weak in Luke's threads, like he'd given up. "It's not too late."

That was when Moonad, who'd stood there in stunned silence, broke and ran, heading for the column of Singers downslope and in the distance.

"Smudge," Luke said, and the pet loped after her.

Turning back to Perwen, Luke couldn't help but feel like a villain when he shook his head and answered, "It is too late, Perwen." He gave it a moment for the situation to sink in, then asked a question of his own. "You mentioned something about how some Singers want us dead, and some don't."

"You will all perish due to the choices you have made here today."

Luke looked around them, taking in the corpses of dead Singers strewn about the small area. "I'm not so sure." He got down so his face was right in front of Perwen and added. "You will tell me everything you know about Luom's father and his allies, and make it quick. My friends need me."

"After what you have done?" Perwen asked, staring at the blood spatters on the ground that were all that remained of his wife. "No, I don't think so."

"You decided to invade Earth. That was your decision. This is your doing. You will tell me what I need to know. It wasn't a question, but a statement." Luke sighed. "I am not a monster, Singer, but before I'm done with you, you won't know the difference."
