Sleep didn’t last. Couldn’t. Not with the forest watching. Not with something always moving out there, just far enough you couldn’t see it, but close enough you could feel it.

We made camp again the next night, this time in the hollow left by a fallen tree, its uprooted base forming a half-wall of tangled roots. Shelter and cover, but not safety. As Kell handed off watch duty to Brigg, something small and fast skittered from the shadows. A blur of claws and teeth. Not deadly, but enough to rattle us. It hissed, struck out once, and vanished into the underbrush.

None of us slept easy after that.The night dragged. Every crackle of leaves made our hands tighten around hilts. I noticed Brigg glancing over his shoulder more than usual, like he felt eyes on his back. That wasn’t like him. Brigg was usually a rock.

Brigg ducked behind a tree to relieve himself, muttering something about needing two minutes of peace. He had just started when a snarl tore through the quiet. Something lunged from the brush. Low, fast, all teeth and fury.

He stumbled back mid-stream, nearly tripping over a root. With no time to draw his weapon, he kicked out hard. His boot caught it in the snout with a crack, and it yelped, scrambling back into the brush. Blood streaked the ground, and his shin.

No one made a joke. Not even Kell.

We took turns on watch, backs to trees, blades in reach. Sleep came in scraps, shallow and haunted. The Hunt wasn’t just wearing us down. It was getting under our skin.

By morning, we were flinching at harmless sounds. A snapping branch. Wind tugging at leaves. Brigg swore he saw something crouching by a log, only for it to vanish when we approached. Tannis nearly launched a spell at a shadow that turned out to be Kell.

"We're jumping at ghosts," Kell muttered, but even he kept glancing behind.

Funny. A few days ago, we were whining about having to hunt the damn things. Now they wouldn’t leave us alone.

Each hour brought something new to keep us on edge. I heard a low growl once, circling the perimeter. Another time, a clatter of stones that made us all spin toward a ridge, blades half-drawn. Only to find nothing. I spotted faint footprints by the trees, but when I followed them, they ended mid-step, like the creature had vanished into thin air.

It started to feel coordinated. Measured. Like something was testing us, learning our rhythms.

Once, a monster’s shriek echoed through the woods, too far to be an immediate threat. But it didn’t stop. It went on for nearly ten minutes, a piercing wail that seemed to bounce from tree to tree. We couldn't block it out. By the time it faded, Rowler’s hand was clenched so tight his knuckles had gone white. I knew what that meant.

Later that same night, Tannis bolted upright with a strangled cry.

“Eyes,” she gasped. “Above us. In the canopy. Watching.”

Everyone scrambled up, weapons drawn, eyes scanning the dark treetops. But the forest swallowed what little moonlight there was. The trees were too high to climb, and it was too dark to see anything.

So we started a fire.

Flames flared. Shadows twisted. Smoke curled toward the branches. I saw nothing. No eyes. No movement. Just the crackle of burning wood and our own heavy breathing.

Tannis sat with her back to the flames, arms wrapped tight around her knees. “I saw them.”

I waited for someone to contradict her. No one did.

We didn’t sleep. Just sat with our backs to the fire, eyes locked on the branches. And for me, that was the moment things truly cracked. Not from fear. Not even death. But from doubt, a steady wearing-down of certainty.

By the fourth day, I could see the strain in all of us. Red-rimmed eyes, ragged tempers. Our boots dragged through moss and roots. Even Brigg’s breathing had gone shallow. He was favoring his left leg.

Tannis glared at me after the trail looped back on itself. “Brilliant navigation,” she muttered. I didn’t bite back, but the words were there, ready to cut. Getting lost wasn’t the worst thing that could happen.

When Brigg stumbled, she didn’t help him up. Just turned away and kept walking. We were still walking together, but it felt like each of us was pulling away, step by step. Like the Hunt was peeling us apart. The rest of the day, she barely spoke, lips pressed in a thin line.

We adjusted our formation to cover Brigg’s limp without a word, but the change cost energy. Every step took more out of us.

The attack came just before dusk. Branches snapped and leaves exploded outward as two Grubtusk Devourers stormed out of the treeline.

Brigg barely raised his shield in time. I carved into one’s flank, but it kept coming. Tannis shouted a quick incantation, casting a burst of speed into Kell and Brigg. The spell landed late, just a fraction too slow, and they barely moved in time.

We brought the monsters down, eventually. But the fight left us winded, scraped, and rattled.

And then we noticed the supply sacks were gone. Food, water, wound salves, spare clothes. All of it. No mess. No torn seams. No tracks in the dirt. It was just... gone. Like it had vanished. It didn’t feel like monsters. It felt like someone had taken them. Quietly. While we were fighting.

Rowler’s voice was quiet. “We head to Copperpine. Now.”

The team nodded. No one argued. We were out of strength, out of time.

That night, we were moving through dense woods when a predator struck.

It was fast. Just a flicker between branches. Tannis raised her hands, but the spell fizzled. Claws found her throat before she could scream.

She fell without a sound. Just a soft thud in the leaves.

I moved before I could think, Kell right beside me. His blade found flesh. The thing shrieked once, then Brigg’s shield came down, cracking its skull. The fight was over in seconds, but too late for Tannis.

I should’ve seen it coming. Should’ve been faster. But I wasn’t. And now she was gone.

Rowler stepped toward her, words starting to form. Then he stopped.

There was no point. No prayer that would fix this.

“You let her die!” Kell snapped, voice breaking.

Brigg turned on him, face red with fury. “You were closer!”

“It came from Hark’s side!”

I didn’t answer. I was watching Rowler. The man stood frozen. Jaw clenched. Shoulders tight. But nothing came out.

His silence said everything.
