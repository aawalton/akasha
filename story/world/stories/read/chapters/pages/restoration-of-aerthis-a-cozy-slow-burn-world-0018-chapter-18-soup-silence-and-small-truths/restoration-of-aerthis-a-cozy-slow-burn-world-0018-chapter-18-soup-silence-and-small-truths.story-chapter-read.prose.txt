Warmth wrapped her from all sides. Thick blanket, thick mattress, thick quiet. Something fuzzy was nudging her cheek in small, insistent taps.

Evening.

A faint trill brushed her ear.

Starpuff hovered beside her face, tail flicking with a steady glow of concern. Its nudge returned, firmer now.

“I’m up, I’m up,” she muttered, voice hoarse.

She tried to push herself upright and immediately regretted it.

Pain flared across her ribs, and her arms felt like they had traded places with raw canvas. She winced, easing back down. Starpuff puffed up in alarm, flitting back a few paces before slowly edging in to pat her forehead with its whole body.

She let out a breath somewhere between a groan and a laugh.

“You know, for a little puff you sure have a big heart.”

The puffball pulsed bright pink around the edges and spun in a proud loop. Of course it did.

Elynara lay still for a moment longer, letting memory catch up. The forest. The monster. The spell. Gods, the second spell–

She had hit it in the mouth.

And it worked. She was still alive. Bruised, scuffed, exhausted. But alive.

She wasn’t sure how she had gotten back. Someone must have carried her after she got out of the forest. She didn’t remember. Just the warmth, the voice, the embrace–

And now here. Safe again.

The ache behind her ribs tightened for a second before easing. The danger was over. Probably. Maybe.

But what if–

Her hand twitched beneath the blanket.

No. Not now. Not until she checked with the others.

First, she had to get up.

Slowly, she pulled back the covers and sat up, hissing quietly. Her legs complained. Her shoulders joined in. The whole orchestra of consequences.

Starpuff darted in immediately, its colors flickering into irritated pink and offended green as it zipped around her head.

“I’m fine. Just sore.”

It puffed up like a furious dumpling and began orbiting her like a mother hen on caffeine. She almost laughed again.

“Alright. You can yell at me after I put pants on.”

That helped. A little.

Carefully, she stood up. Someone had changed her clothes, leaving a fresh set near the bedside. Rina, most likely. Tunic, belt, soft trousers, all neat and folded with a care that made her chest ache a little more.

Ten minutes later, dressed and moving like a creaky door, she stepped out into the hallway.

A voice shouted almost immediately.

“You’re up!”

Finnie came skidding into view, face flushed with excitement. He bounced in place like he had been waiting for this exact moment all day.

“You’re okay! You really are! The monster was huge and Papa said you shot it like fwump-boom and then went whack–wait no, it was more like wham!”

Elynara raised a hand halfway.

“Hi, Finnie. I’m glad to see you too.”

“Oh! You gotta eat! Rina said no running and you gotta sit still and eat this whole thing before she yells!”

He shoved a large bowl into her hands with all the seriousness of a sacred duty. It smelled like stew. And triumph.

Her stomach grumbled loudly enough to answer for her.

“Thanks, Finnie.”

He grinned.

“And the big thing? Papa and Aren dragged it back! With help! It’s so ugly! They said it might be worth something too!”

“Hopefully not for decorations.”

Finnie’s eyebrows wiggled.

“I dunno… the teeth were really cool.”

Starpuff snorted in the air beside her.

With a sigh, Elynara sank into the nearest chair, letting the warmth of safety and stew finally begin to settle into her bones.

Elynara chewed slowly, and let it all in. The light from the kitchen window was faint now. Shadows long, warm, stretching wide across the tile.

She smiled faintly, and nodded once.

Everything was still okay.

Finnie barely let her step through the door before the barrage started.

“You really exploded it, right? Not just burned? ‘Cause Papa said there were scorch marks way up the trees!”

Elynara blinked, already halfway into a chair at the kitchen table. The bowl in front of her was still hot, and smelled of sweetroot and garlic. She reached for the spoon more out of instinct than hunger.

“It was... pretty explosive,” she murmured, not quite meeting his eyes.

“And the ground! It was cracked like whoa! Did it roar after, or just flail?”

“I wasn’t keeping notes.”

“But what if it was a cursed beast?! What if it cursed you! Can I check? My sister says you shouldn’t touch cursed blood but I touched a frog once and it was fine.”

Elynara stuffed a bite of stew in her mouth to avoid answering. That did the trick… for a bit. Finnie paused just long enough to reset his thoughts, then launched into a theory involving tusk angles and magic bones.

She chewed slowly, giving small nods between spoonfuls. Not because she wanted seconds, as her stomach still felt like it had forgotten how to be hungry. It simply gave her something to focus on while her brain slowly caught up with itself.

Eventually, mercifully, Finnie ran out of steam. His last question trailed off into a yawn he tried to hide behind both hands. Rina called him from another room and he vanished like a dropped hat.

Elynara let her spoon clink into the bowl and exhaled.

Quiet. Finally.

Getting up, she oriented herself toward the source of the voices and sounds of work, ones she certainly didn't hear when Finnie was talking her ears off. Venturing out, she noted this was where they had previously worked on her earlier monster catches.

The walk to the dismantling yard felt longer than it should have. Her legs moved, but her steps dragged. The sun had dipped behind the barn, casting long shadows across the grass. She followed them.

The area behind the coop smelled like smoke and metal and blood, but less than before. The work was nearly done.

Theren was hunched over a haunch the size of a chair, knife moving with slow, practiced strokes. Aren stood beside him, knotting cord around a packed crate. Two other adults she didn’t recognize helped with the sorting. One had a hammer. The other, a hook similar to Aren’s.

“Well, if it isn’t our little hero!”

“I just–there wasn’t a choice.”

“Didn’t need to be a choice. You still chose to do it.”

Someone clapped her shoulder gently. Another offered her a drink from a small jug. She accepted out of politeness, but the warmth in her cheeks had already set.

“I really just… reacted,” she said, quieter.

The nods came all the same.

They spoke for a while longer about the creature, the damage, and the cleanup involved. Unfortunately, no one could name the beast. Theren thought it might’ve been a malformed forest elder, but its bones were wrong. Someone else suggested it came from further east, deeper forest regions. No one knew for sure.

The only certainty: nothing else had shown up. The woods were quiet. Clear.

That, at least, settled some weight in her chest.

“You’re not,” Rina cut in, softer now. “You’re allowed to not be.”

Elynara didn’t argue. Not because she agreed, but because the act of sitting really was helping.

Starpuff, unbothered by the smell or mess, plopped itself onto her lap and flicked her belt.

She raised an eyebrow.

She would be ready.

Elynara let her eyes close.

The pillow smelled like clean linen and sun-warmed wood.

Sleep came before the next thought could follow.
