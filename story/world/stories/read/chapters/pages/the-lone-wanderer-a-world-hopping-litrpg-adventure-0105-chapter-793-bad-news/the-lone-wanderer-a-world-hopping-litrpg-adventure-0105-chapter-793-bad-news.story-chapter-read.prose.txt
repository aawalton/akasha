Percy was about to resume training when his girlfriend suddenly rushed into his familiar’s cavern, all sweaty and out of breath.

“Nesha? What’s up? Don’t tell me I missed our date night?” Percy asked half-jokingly, knowing full well that he hadn’t.

Despite how swamped with work he and his girlfriend had been since their reunion, they’d still tried their best to spend at least a couple of hours per week with each other. They’d mostly managed to stick with it too – except for whenever one of Percy’s projects required his undivided attention for several days straight.

Losing a few hours of training per week during a war might have sounded like a bad idea, but Percy and Nesha had found their time together so refreshing and enjoyable that it had made them more productive during the rest of the week.

Seeing his girlfriend unable to reciprocate his smile, however, Percy realized that something had gone seriously wrong.

“Archibald and Orin… are waiting for us in our residence…” she managed to say between pants. “It’s better to discuss this in their presence.”

Gently wrapping Nesha in his willpower, Percy carried her back to the surface much faster than she had descended. Micky didn’t physically join them, but he did peer through the connection to participate in the meeting indirectly.

Unfolding his sensory domain before he even reached the building, Percy found his grandfather and mentor sitting in his living room with grim expressions. Nesha hadn’t bothered to treat them to any refreshments, nor did anyone look like they were in the mood to eat.

“Houses Apemosyne, Momus, and Achelois were destroyed a few hours ago,” his girlfriend said right as they approached the others. “There were very few survivors, and they were probably left alive intentionally by our enemies to spread the news and hurt our side’s morale.”

Percy’s eyes widened in shock. Those were families that had joined their alliance a few months back. Wiped out within a single day.

“How the hell did this happen?!” he demanded. “I thought we had dozens of scouts and informants tracking the movements of the enemy armies! And weren’t we on the verge of crushing the bastards with sheer numbers?”

“We were,” Archibald admitted with a sigh. “Past tense.”

Percy flashed his grandfather a questioning look, though it was his mentor who decided to elaborate. “The attack wasn’t carried out by our known enemies, but by Houses Drachma, Laurion and Amphitrite. They each chose one of our allies to crush, before declaring their allegiance to Machaon and retreating to their side’s fortress.”

Percy rubbed the bridge of his nose, not sure what to make of this. Houses Drachma, Laurion and Amphitrite weren’t just random noble families – they were the remaining three Great Houses that had kept themselves neutral up until now.

“But why?” he asked bitterly. “I get the Divine Root and House Ypnos standing against me because of past grudges, but I’ve never interacted with the other Great Houses one way or the other. Why would they choose to ally with a piece of shit like Machaon at a time when I’m handing new cores and affinity-granting treasures like candy?!”

“Not everyone has ambitions of attaining divinity or growing stronger, Percy…” Archibald explained. “Some people care more about their social status. And you’ve done more than enough to disrupt that.”

“They can all see the writing on the wall,” Nesha added. “Even if you have nothing personal against their families, nobody knows whether there will be a place for Great Houses in your new world. Even if there is, you are more likely to favour House Etna and the other families who joined your alliance early on. These people have stayed in power for so long that they can’t stomach the idea of falling from grace.”

“Not to mention how much the changes you have stirred have affected their business…” Orin muttered.

Percy raised an eyebrow, trying to figure out when exactly he had done that.

House Laurion had achieved their lofty status by owning the largest collection of mines on Remior. Now that Percy’s people had received permission from the Starry Wasps to extract whatever magical minerals they needed from the hive, the Great House had probably lost a lot of money.

House Drachma possessed the Minting bloodline, which allowed them to condense their mana into metal of various colours depending on the caster’s grade. The material itself was inert and useless in combat or crafting but had the advantage of being unable to be replicated through other means. The Great House had used their bloodline to create and guarantee the authenticity of the colour-coded coins that the Alchemists’ Guild and the rest of Remior used in their transactions.

The problem was that Percy’s subordinates had been directly exchanging Aurora Dew and regular elixirs for alchemical reagents lately. There was no point in using money in those trades, because the resources that Percy was giving away were too precious to put a price tag on. Unfortunately, that had probably impacted everyone’s dependence on the family’s currency negatively.

“What about House Amphitrite? I don’t think I’ve ever touched their interests,” Percy protested.

House Amphitrite basically belonged to Poseidon, one of the thirteen gods who currently led the Divine Order. While most other deities sent their descendants to the Divine Root, the promiscuous god’s harem was too large to mingle with the others.

He had created a special family to place them all in, and tasked them with monitoring Remior’s ocean for rare resources and new species of aquatic beasts. Percy had thankfully avoided running into their members during his self-imposed exile, because the sea spanned half of the planet’s surface and many of their mages lived deep underwater thanks to their famous Waterbreathing bloodline.

“You didn’t affect them directly, but all the Great Houses are deeply embedded into our world’s economy. Destroying one of them would have a major impact on everyone’s pockets. Eliminating three would be an unprecedented crisis. Meanwhile, eradicating five out of seven as you are currently poised to do…” Archibald didn’t bother to complete that sentence because he didn’t have to.

Percy took a deep breath to calm himself down and let the information sink in before saying anything. “So… where does that leave us?”

“At a very bad spot,” Orin replied. “We had managed to gather twenty noble families including yours, which would have probably been enough to launch an attack on our enemies’ stronghold. Unfortunately, three of our allies just got destroyed, and five more – who have yet to sign your spectral contract – have stopped responding to our messages. I doubt we’ll be hearing from them again.”

This… was even worse than Percy had expected. With a single move, Machaon’s side had essentially doubled their manpower while nearly halving Percy’s!

“I don’t think there will be any new Houses joining us, moving forward,” Nesha said, piling onto the bad news. “The Moirais’ Decree and affinity-granting treasures are mouthwatering prizes, but nobody in their right mind is going to willingly side against six Great Houses. In fact, I wouldn’t be surprised if their side starts growing even faster than ours did last year.”

“What about the hive? Has everyone forgotten that Nephthys’ life is tied to my own? Or do they no longer want cleansing resources?” Percy asked.

“They addressed that in their announcement, actually.” Archibald sighed. “They’re working together to create a second, artificial hive. Their Starry Princess is in good health and has finally advanced to Blue, so she’s only one grade away from being able to spawn her own Starry Breeders, Drones, and heir.”

“They don’t have the environment for it!” Percy protested.

“Not yet,” Orin corrected him. “Apparently, there’s a second major focal point where Remior’s underground currents of ambient mana meet. It’s at the bottom of the ocean, which isn’t exactly a great spot for the Starry Wasps, but House Amphitrite is currently constructing a giant pillar to funnel ambient mana to the surface. Between that, some mana-gathering arrays, and Green beasts harvested from across Remior, they hope to build a platform suitable for their bugs. The second hive will never grow as large as the original, but they hope that their stashed elixirs and careful rationing will help them keep their nobles fed until they kill or capture you and move their Starry Queen back to the Fungal Spire.”

The more Percy heard, the more he felt his blood boil. They should be spending this time rebuilding their world and preparing for outside threats instead of escalating this stupid war.

Would Phoebe finally intervene if he explained what had gone down with Baphomet?

There was no harm in trying to reach out to the titaness, though Percy doubted that it would matter. The truth was that the overwhelming majority of mortals on the planet would be useless against Catharis. A few millennia would barely be enough for a fraction of the Order’s demigods to attain divinity, and the new deities would still be too weak to make a difference against a greater spring. Even the strongest Yellow-borns would need a lot longer than the Holy Children to reach the Concept realm.

The only person who was currently involved in the war and whose strength was growing fast enough to potentially matter was Percy. As long as Phoebe’s ability to obtain enough Aurora Dew for the Green-borns wasn’t affected, she was more likely to double down on her plan to use this crisis as a training opportunity for him.

“Send word to our remaining allies to leave their territories and move to the Fungal Spire immediately,” Percy ordered. “Given how things are progressing, it’s way too dangerous to keep our forces scattered.”

Abandoning their provinces and locking themselves around the magical mountain would cost them a lot of money and awareness of their surroundings. They would lose their ability to track what their enemies were doing, and they would also have a much harder time growing enough secondary ingredients for their alchemical needs.

“We already gave the order,” Orin said, though he didn’t look pleased about that in the slightest. “We can probably cultivate the land around the Fungal Spire to produce enough reagents for the allied families and the Guild’s alchemists, but we won’t be able to supply the rest of the planet with cleansing resources.”

Percy nodded.

He’d done everything in his power to avoid hurting the neutral families, but they hadn’t left him a choice. If they wanted to keep receiving Aurora Dew, they should have joined him to eliminate that traitor years ago. Unfortunately, Percy no longer had the luxury of worrying about both the safety of his allies and the bottom line of the undecisive nobles.

“This is the sensible move, and I personally agree with it, but keep in mind that this will drive the remaining Houses into Machaon’s arms even faster,” Nesha warned. “Losing access to their cleansing resources will put pressure on them to see the war end as soon as possible, and the enemy alliance is currently looking like the winning side.”

Percy sighed.

“I… need some time to think,” he said. “Keep managing our people as best as you can. I’ll figure out a way to end this war myself… somehow.”
