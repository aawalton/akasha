A blistering wave of heat engulfed Aodhán the moment he stepped into the building, a layered wall of furnace breath rolling over him from dozens of forges burning at intensities that would have reduced an ordinary workshop to slag and ash.

It felt as though he'd crossed from open air straight into the throat of a furnace. His skin prickled as sweat beaded along his brow and slid down the back of his neck, but despite the discomfort, Aodhán's gaze was wide with amazement.

The inside of Elder Gumi's workshop was the very image of intellectual opulence. The wide hall was decked in gold—the runes, the pillars, the forges, and even the floor—all of it golden, yet it somehow managed not to look gaudy.

The power of runes hung heavy in the air, but even more oppressive was the density of energy within the hall. The latent auras of dozens of Mythics pressed tightly against his senses—each one heavy enough to drown him in its own weight.

Aodhán swallowed.

He had never seen such a dense cluster of Mythics in one place before, and unsurprisingly, his entrance didn't go unnoticed. Several Mythics paused in their work to glance at him, their expressions tightening into faint frowns.

Nervous, Aodhán bowed deeply. "Good morning, Elders. I'm Aodhán Brystion, and I'm here to see Elder Gumi."

Flickers of recognition flashed across the faces of many, and an elder immediately floated toward him, wisps of wind essence escaping from beneath his robes.

"Welcome, Aodhán." He smiled brightly when he reached him. "I am Elder Lou, third-circle apprentice of the revered Elder Gumi. I was told to expect you." His smile widened as he leaned forward. "You must be truly outstanding in rune work to capture her attention so quickly. How did you meet the Elder?"

Aodhán frowned, realizing now that Elder Gumi hadn't conveyed the circumstances surrounding their meeting to her apprentices—perhaps not even the nature of their project. And if that was the case, then he couldn't afford to do so either.

Chuckling awkwardly, he replied, "I wouldn't label myself as outstanding, Elder Lou."

"Well, you certainly are modest," Elder Lou chuckled. "But I heard of your actions at the Trial Mountain—and they were outstanding."

Aodhán accepted the praise humbly. "I try my best, Elder Lou. Can you please lead me to her?"

"Of course," Elder Lou smiled and waved him forward. "Come with me. I believe she has been expecting you for some time now."

Aodhán followed Elder Lou deeper into the blistering forge, bowing as he passed several impressive Mythics, many of whom returned his greeting with polite nods of their own.

However, the deeper they ventured into the workshop, the hotter the air became, and by the time they finally entered a side corridor branching from the main hall, Aodhán was drenched in sweat.

A few minutes later, they stopped before a massive metallic door, and Elder Lou tapped lightly against its surface, the sound echoing through the narrow corridor.

For a second, nothing happened. Then a peephole slid open, revealing a familiar face, a sardonic edge lacing his tone as he asked, "Yes?"

The man's gaze shifted to Aodhán a moment later, and his expression fell. "Ah. I see my prayers have gone unanswered."

Aodhán smiled. "Good morning to you too, Elder Luseph. What were these prayers of yours that have gone unanswered?"

"They don't matter anymore." Elder Luseph sighed, locks clanging in succession before the door finally slid open. "Come in. You're here already."

"Perhaps I can come in too," Elder Lou added immediately, but Elder Luseph only smiled at him.

"I'm sorry, Lou. Elder Gumi specifically asked for the presence of everyone already inside, and she hasn't asked for you."

Elder Lou's expression faltered, but he caught it almost instantly. "Right. Of course. I understand. Let Elder Gumi know I'm glad to be of service for anything she needs."

"I will," Luseph replied politely, then glanced at Aodhán. "Come in."

Aodhán obeyed and stepped through the entrance, but he was vastly unprepared for the sight that met him. He had always carried a vision of what an epic runic workshop should look like, yet his imagination paled in comparison to the reality before him.

The private workshop was smaller than the grand hall outside, yet it felt infinitely bigger. Heat still dominated the space, but it was controlled, with several ice arrays pouring steady currents of cool air through the room. Aodhán barely noticed the temperature, though, his gaze captured instead by the three mythic forges dominating the center of the chamber.

Pure white flames jutted from the first, blue-orange from the second, and black from the third, all three converging to heat a floating slab of metal he vaguely recognized as orichalcum.

Hundreds of runic tools and forging instruments lined the left wall, each gleaming with the telltale sheen of highly advanced items, while more unfamiliar equipment occupied the opposite side, only a fraction of which Aodhán could identify.

A long reinforced bench rested in one corner of the workshop, and atop it lay hundreds of treasures and materials, scattered in careless disorder like junk. Two Mythics stood by the table, calmly sorting chaos-affinity items into a small metal tray.

They barely glanced up at his entrance.

But they weren't the only ones present in the room. Aside from himself, Luseph, and the two Mythics by the bench, two other individuals stood at the far end of the workshop, piles of torn parchment littered around them.

The first was an obviously powerful Mythic, his stance confident and core blazing with enviable intensity. His partner, however, was the one who caught Aodhán's attention, and the moment their gazes locked, Aodhán bowed. "Good morning, Calamity."

The man regarded him for a long moment, runic irises narrowing slightly before he gave a restrained nod. "I suppose you'll do. If my Gumi is to work with an advanced-class cultivator, at least it isn't some entitled weakling from the outer sect."

"Be nice, Shen," Elder Gumi said as she stepped out from behind a compartment, her core entirely concealed until the moment she revealed herself. A faint smile touched her lips when her gaze settled on Aodhán. "Pay no heed to Calamity Shen, Special Disciple Brystion. He was just leaving."

The Calamity snorted softly, placed a brief kiss against Elder Gumi's forehead, then turned toward the exit, his steps gentle as if he couldn't destroy this entire workshop with a mere thought if he so desired.

Aodhán shivered as the man stopped beside him, swallowing once before forcing himself to meet the Calamity's gaze.

The Calamity smiled, though the expression held no softness. "Be on your best behavior."

Aodhán nodded immediately. "Yes, Elder. I'm only here to learn. I won't cause any trouble."

"Good," the man replied, and this time the smile carried a trace of genuine warmth. "Then we shall have no problem."

He left the workshop a moment later, and though Aodhán had been the sole focus of the Calamity's attention, a subtle breath seemed to pass through the room once he was gone.

Elder Gumi chuckled at his expression, shaking her head. "Calamity Shen will not hurt you even if you blow my workshop up. He's just bluffing. You need not worry about that."

Aodhán let out a shaky smile. "That's good to know, ma'am."

"It is I you need to worry about," Elder Gumi continued casually, her smile still in place. "If you do blow up my workshop, you'll be answering to me. And though Shen Gu is a calamity, I wager you would pray for him instead of me should things go wrong."

Aodhán stiffened, then nodded. "I promise to be on my best behavior. I just want the experience."

"That's good." Her warm smile returned. "Genuine interest in runic engineering is rare among younglings these days. I'm happy to have you here."

She turned away to speak to the other Mythics, and Aodhán let out a heavy breath he hadn't realized he'd been holding. Beside him, Elder Luseph chuckled softly. "I bet you wish you'd stayed at home now."

Aodhán glanced at him, a faint grin tugging at the corner of his lips. "I don't want to sleep; I want to learn." His gaze strayed to the exit, and after a moment of hesitation, he asked, "Has anyone blown up the workshop before?"

Luseph's smile widened into a full grin. "Well, let's just say there's a reason all her apprentices are Mythics."

Aodhán swallowed again, shaking his head slightly. "What about you? Are you an apprentice too?"

Luseph shook his head. "I'm just here to observe," he said easily, his smile curving into a faint smirk. "And to protect you, should I have to, though I doubt that will be necessary."

Aodhán frowned, uncertain whether Luseph was being entirely truthful. The sect hadn't bothered to assign him a bodyguard when he'd taken on the void rift yesterday, yet now Luseph was supposedly here to protect him?

The thought left him faintly uneasy, but before he could press further, Elder Gumi called out, "Could you give me your interface, Aodhán? I need a moment to analyze it."

Aodhán glanced briefly at the other Mythics before hurrying over, removing the pendant, and handing it to her.

"Thank you," she muttered as she accepted it from him and began examining it closely.

Now standing beside her, Aodhán allowed his gaze to drift to the piles of parchment scattered across the floor, scanning their contents as he tried to piece together why they'd been discarded. The parchments were covered in intricate sketches of the pendant, cross-sectional diagrams of its internal structure, and a dizzying array of runes mapped across its surface in varying configurations.

It was incredibly complex, far more intricate than anything he had ever seen before, and as his eyes traced the layered inscriptions, he realized—

"I was right," Elder Gumi exclaimed loudly, cutting into his thoughts as she handed the pendant to the elder closest to her. "Take a look, Sedgwick. There are no chaos runes inscribed on the surface of the pendant, only storm and space runes."

"Where then is the chaos rune or array?" Elder Sedgwick frowned as he accepted the pendant, lifting it closer to his eyes. "For the pendant to filter and translate chaos as you claim, it must possess a chaos rune."

"And it does," Elder Gumi replied, her expression triumphant. "The runes are, however, within the pendant. Inside carving, Sedgwick."

The mention of inside-carving caused Sedgwick to gasp in excitement, while the two by the treasure table glanced up sharply. Aodhán, however, was still trying to wrap his head around the fact that there were runes carved beyond the visible surface of the pendant, and he had never once noticed.

"This is great, Elder Gumi," Sedgwick called out, unable to mask his excitement. "This is the first functioning item we've seen with inside carving. If we're able to understand it—"

"We might finally be able to embed far more runes into any item without destabilizing it," Elder Gumi finished for him, her smile almost blinding. "Whoever created this pendant is most certainly from Attilan. They have always been obsessed with concealing their runes."

The mention of Attilan made Aodhán tense, and in a room full of Mythics, the shift did not go unnoticed. Elder Gumi glanced up at him. "Is there a problem, Aodhán?"

"No," Aodhán shook his head. "It's just… my home continent did not have the best relationship with Attilan. They fought several wars."

A beat of silence followed before Elder Sedgwick muttered, "Well, that explains how you came into possession of something so sophisticated. It must have fallen from one of their soldiers during their campaign against your continent."

"Unbonded?" Elder Gumi frowned in mild skepticism. "That's very unlikely. Most likely, the bearer died, and so the bond was dissolved. Nevertheless, finding this was incredibly lucky."

"Right," Aodhán nodded, suddenly feeling uncomfortable. The merchant he'd bought the pendant from had said it originated somewhere in Calodan, and with the limited knowledge he'd had of Lutia at the time, he hadn't bothered to investigate further.

But now, it seemed he'd been wearing the necklace of a dead Attilan around his neck all this while. The realization made him uneasy, and he grimaced faintly, the idea of inheriting the item of an invader—one whose continent had oppressed Lutia for over a century—causing his insides to churn queasily.

He pushed the emotion aside before it could fester, though, and glanced at Elder Gumi.

"So," he asked, steadying himself, "how do we recreate it?"

Elder Sedgwick glanced at him, then at Elder Gumi, who walked back to the desk, the pendant still resting in her palms.

"Before we begin discussing how," she said calmly, "it is important that we establish what we are about to attempt." She gestured for the two Mythics by the treasure tray to join them before continuing. "The pendant is an interface designed to filter and translate the whispers of chaos for its bearer. This particular one is attuned to storm essence, but I suspect an interface could be created for any affinity if the bridging array were properly adjusted and aligned."

"But we are not going to do that," she added, a knowing smile spreading across her face. "Because if we succeed in replicating this properly, we will be ushering in a new age of power, one we would very much prefer to enjoy for at least a century before the other sects manage to grasp its significance."

Everyone nodded in agreement, and she continued.

"That being said, we must tread carefully. Inside rune-carving is not a simple process, as you all know, and I will require each of you at your absolute best."

"We will give it our all," Sedgwick said firmly, and the other two Mythics nodded.

"We won't fail you, Elder Gumi."

"Very well." Elder Gumi stood up, her gaze traveling to the tri-forge. "Then let us begin."

The next few minutes passed in a blur of words Aodhán barely understood as Elder Gumi tasked everyone present except him and Elder Luseph. Equipment he didn't recognize was brought out, and instruments whose functions he could barely imagine were laid out.

Aodhán didn't blame Elder Gumi for not giving him a task. With three Mythic apprentices, what would she need him for? The lack of a task, however, gave him a chance to sit and observe, and Aodhán was determined not to waste the opportunity.

Moving to one corner of the workshop, he created a stool for himself, took out his notepad and pen, and prepared himself to begin jotting. Elder Gumi noticed his stance and smiled. "Have you ever created an item before?"

"Yes, Elder Gumi," Aodhán replied. "But never on my own. I cannot boast of much experience."

"Neither can I," Elder Gumi chuckled. "Compared to some other monsters of this world, I am merely a child. But if you've done this before, then surely you must have a title or two to prove it."

"I do," Aodhán replied excitedly as he opened his screen and shared his titles with her.

Novice Runesmith, Amateur Rune Scholar.

Elder Gumi stared at the screen for a moment before nodding. "A novice at such an early tier is commendable." She glanced at one of the Mythics who had been manning the treasure table earlier and said, "Kobi, let him join you to melt down the chaos treasures. You can leave the sieving to him. It should grant him some experience."

She glanced away, dismissing them to give an order to Sedgwick. Aodhán glanced at the man he had been paired with and bowed. "Nice to meet you, Elder Kobi."

He stared at him for a moment before sighing. "Nice to meet you too, Aodhán. Come with me. Do you have any experience melting chaos treasures?"

"None."

"Well, you're about to." He led Aodhán to the treasure tray, and Aodhán's gaze widened as he got a closer look. The tray wasn't a simple platter of steel as he'd assumed. Instead, it was a layered array of interlocking black glass rings shot through with violet veins that held over two dozen chaos treasures unmoving like metal to a magnet.

Kobi pushed the tray into his hands and continued. "The first thing you must know is that chaos metals don't melt, not in the real sense. Instead, they unravel, and if they unravel too fast, then you get a detonation instead of a solution."

"That'll be bad."

"It usually is," Kobi chuckled. "But the paradox still helps to reduce the risk." He motioned Aodhán to a rectangular containment vat made from translucent void crystals and dumped a handful of chaos treasures into it. "This is the paradox still. It will break down the treasures into a semi-liquid state, basically forcing them to lose cohesion. Your job will be to manually remove any impurities you find as the liquid essence is pushed out."

Aodhán frowned. "Sounds simple enough."

"It is," Elder Kobi nodded. "But it requires focus and precision. The unraveled chaos essence will cool quickly, forming a thin solid base for the inside carving. You want to remove every impurity from it before that happens. If not, you'll have to melt it again, and that carries a higher risk of explosion."

Aodhán grimaced. "Okay, show me how to turn it on."

Elder Kobi placed a hand on the paradox still and channeled raw lightning into it. The vat came on immediately, and the chaos treasures he'd placed into it earlier suddenly began to unravel, pebbles and crystals losing cohesion until they became a sludge of thick black essence.

The problem, however, quickly revealed itself when Aodhán saw the number of impurities floating within it—threads of foreign essence, clumps of fossils, and remnants of the original material before its transformation into an item.

These impurities floated within the bubbling sludge—seemingly infinite—until Kobi began pulling them out with a translucent tool that looked like a fork. He made short work of the impurities, picking them out with admirable speed and precision, and by the time he was done, the sludge had solidified into a thin strip of iridescent solid very similar to a metal.

Kobi picked it up and pushed the treasure tray back into his hands. "Do the rest. This should be enough for our first prototype."

He left then, and Aodhán followed him with his gaze, realizing that he wouldn't get the chance to watch the prototype creation unless he finished up here quickly. He glanced at the crafting table where the Mythics were gathered, shaping metals and carving complex runes, and decided the faster he began, the better his chances to catch the tail end of this inside-carving process.

But he also couldn't afford to ruin the first task out of impatience, which meant he needed to give this his full attention. He glanced at the tray in his hands, grabbed a handful of chaos treasures, and shoved them into the vat. He grabbed the translucent fork, placed his hands on the paradox still, and channeled raw lightning into it.

Just like it had done with Elder Kobi, the chaos treasures began unraveling immediately, and a few minutes later, the impurities revealed themselves. He acted immediately, picking them out as quickly as he could, but he didn't have Mythic speed, nor did he have the decades of experience Elder Kobi did, and by the time the sludge solidified, Aodhán knew he hadn't gotten them all.

Unlike Elder Kobi's batch, this one was an uneven strip of iridescent solid, its surface riddled with cavities and swollen ridges like a malformed ingot poured into a cracked mold. It was imperfect and had to be melted again.

Aodhán had no idea what a chaos explosion might look like, but he knew he didn't want to witness it.

He glanced up at Elder Kobi, about to ask for help, and that was when he noticed just how silent the entire workshop was. Elder Gumi was already working on the inside carving, and the other Mythics stood around her, including Elder Luseph, their gazes so intent in concentration, Aodhán feared a sound might do more harm than good.

He wanted to join them, to see this new style of rune inscription that captured the attention of Mythics this much, but he also knew doing so would only discredit him. Melting the chaos treasures was his task, and if he left it unattended, he would be labeling himself as unreliable.

He glanced down at the uneven strip of solid chaos in the still and decided he would have to risk it. If it exploded, then that would be just terrible. But if it worked, then he would earn himself reliability points, and if he was to ingratiate himself to Elder Gumi and become a potential apprentice, he had to prove himself as capable.

Face hardening in determination, Aodhán placed a hand on the still once more and channeled raw lightning into it, praying silently that it didn't blow up in his face.
