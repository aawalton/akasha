Ryland found it rather difficult to get into the actual Crypts. Not because his way was barred or anything of the sort. As the competition had started, they had thrown open the ruins’ wide doors to let everyone descend into the darkness. All the other academies had already made their entry. All but Arcoryx.

Most of whom were huddling a little too close to Ryland as though being in his presence would automatically ward off whatever they were frightened of.

“Oh, come on, you lot,” Tyra said, clearly exasperated. “These Crypts aren’t haunted. There’s no such thing as ghosts! High Academ Zevo was just messing with your minds because he’s part of the opposition. Don’t you understand? He wants you all to be too fearful to act!”

That was certainly a plausible theory, though Ryland didn’t think it was true. He agreed with Zevo. Ghosts were certainly real enough. They even employed some in the form of Remnants at Arcoryx Academy. Though that left him curious as to what the mechanism behind the Crypts’ ghosts were.

Eventually, they seemed to understand that it was rather embarrassing for them all to be huddled up so close. Especially when their opponents had already delved in and had begun the hunt.

When Ryland finally managed to get inside, he had to admit it was rather spooky.

The rocky walls were lined with pockets where bodies mummified in clay had been stuffed in. They were in one of the lowest chambers despite having just entered, for the entrance itself sat very close to the lowest section of the Crypts.

“This is spooky,” one of the Arcoryx students murmured, like he was afraid of ghosts overhearing him.

“And dusty.” Acrypha waved her hand in front of her face to push the dust away. “Maybe the ghosts live off the dirt when they can’t get their hands on people.”

They forged onwards, still stuck in basically one group. Ryland was slowly coming around to the idea of gifting every student their own barrier with [Eternal Bastion] if that was what it would take them to de-coagulate.

Because Tyra was right. If they wished to get their hands on the Dreadlich’s ash, then they needed to spread out and search as wide an area as possible.

His plans were cut short when the children at the head of the group screamed.

“Ghost!”

“Ghost!”

“Run away!”

There was a mass stampede of small feet all around Ryland. Before he could verify as to whether that really was a ghost or not, Tyra had waded through the river of children and stomped over to the supposed spectre spooking the students.

“This,” High Academ Tyra said, whirling around to glare at the students. “Is a priestess.”

The pale woman clad in a white tunic waved at the students with a warm smile.

At some point, the children stopped running long enough to remember to feel shame. Slowly, they began returning to their original location, though Ryland remembered to move away so they couldn’t gather around him like sheep next to their shepherd again.

“Priestess,” Tyra said exasperatedly in accented Rhellian. “Can you please tell my wayward students that the ghosts in your Crypts are nothing to be scared of?”

“Why, of course!” The priestess’s smile widened. “If you are well-behaved children who have never done any wrong, the ghosts will never harm you! They might occasionally get drawn to your warmth or moan about all the things that made them sad, but really, they’re just poor, lost dears. You have nothing to fear from them.”

That didn’t seem to reassure most of the students, but Tyra decided to run with it anyway. It was obvious she wasn’t here for reassurance.

She just wanted to win.

Especially since it was starting to look like the other academies were getting a head start. They really needed to catch up soon.

“Alright.” The High Academ clapped her hands. “We’re all going to separate into different groups. I’m going to organize it all, and I want no complaints. Once we’re done, I want to see all of you heading in different directions. You can ask the priestess for guidance on where to go and what to look for, but do not pester her. Understood?”

Ryland had to admit this was one side of the High Academ he had yet to see. But it made sense. She had been a teacher for a long time now. No doubt, she had means of dealing with students she had honed over the decades.

The priestess had some choice advice for them.

“Be wary of the dust,” she said with that sweet smile of hers. “And be careful of getting lost. And try not to veer off into paths you can’t see the end of. Oh, and don’t always pay attention to all the sounds. And don’t always trust everything you hear, of course. And…”

She had been looking deeply at them all in turn, and her eyes finally landed on Ryland.

That was when she decided to stare at him like he was the ghost here. Her eyes widened, her pale face turned the colour of milk, her mouth falling open in momentary shock. Seeing her expression, the others quickly turned in fear to see if there was a ghastly apparition attacking them, only to find Ryland blinking back at the holy woman.

“Erm, as I was saying,” she continued, admirably controlling the tremor in her voice. “You must remain wary of any disembodied calls, anyone who appears to be walking alone or calling out to you if it isn’t a priest or priestess, and…”

Deciding it was best to leave everyone’s attention on the priestess, Ryland slowly backed away. Linsby and Sint quickly followed once they noticed he had indeed disappeared like a ghost. That had been a bad move on his part.

High Academ Tyra had ensured none of the three grad students would be joining any of the normal students, so the trio of them would be going together.

“Hey!” Linsby called out. “Hold up a moment.”

“I’m afraid we’ve already given up quite a bit of time,” Ryland said. “It’s best we not waste any further time.”

“It’s so dark,” Linsby said. A Glyph of Illumination came up a second later, turning the whole tunnel a little radiant.

“Are you actually intent on winning?” Sint asked.

“Aren’t you?” Ryland shot back.

“Well, I don’t particularly care. It’s just a silly little competition between the junior students. I don’t care who wins. The most I care is to ensure no one gets hurt.”

“I suppose that’s fair. That said, I do harbour a certain amount of ambition and pride in Arcoryx and would like to ensure that it comes out victorious from this endeavour. Thus, I’m putting in some effort.”

Sint grunted but didn’t actually lag behind as Ryland led the way forward. “What was that about with the priestess back there? Why did she stare at you like a ghost?”

“I’m not quite sure,” Ryland said.

That said, he was strongly reminded of his run-ins with the Oracle and everything that had happened afterwards. Had the priests and priestesses here read Fate and seen him?

Or did they know about his past on Rhel?

“Ambition and pride are all fine and good,” Linsby said, returning to their earlier topic. He was looking around suspiciously, like a ghost was going to pop out of the walls any second now. “But do you know where exactly we’re supposed to be going?”

“Admittedly, this… Dreadlich is new to me,” Ryland said. “And the Glyphs I’ve been attempting to use aren’t having much luck. For instance, I tried my Glyph of Questing, but it failed to latch onto anything properly concrete.”

He was clearly travelling with rather smart individuals, since they caught his implication. Well, they were graduate students. They had to be smart.

“And the rest of this whole Crypts business isn’t new to you?” Sint asked almost suspiciously.

Ryland shook his head. “Not really.”

“Which suggests you have a plan of getting through this, despite conventional magic not working,” Linsby said.

“Perhaps…”

In truth, Ryland did have some idea on how to proceed. Because he suspected that the Dreadlich wasn’t secreted away in some hidden nook and cranny of the Crypts. Rather, the chamber existed in some extraplanar pocket whose entryway was in this enormous underground burial chamber.

For it wasn’t just a cemetery for the good who had departed on Rhel. It was also the graveyard of evil.

It was just that some kinds of evil refused to remain dead, or only pretended to be so. Which was another one of the reasons for the way the Crypts were constructed with material that could conduct mana, even manasouls.

By drawing away the driving force behind all kinds of threats, it essentially acted as a powerful prison for all sorts of entities such as the Dreadlich. It might appear dangerous to let a group of academy students enter the barricade to retrieve some ash, but Ryland figured Rhel either had powerful safeguards in place.

Or the actual intentions behind this whole exercise went far higher than any of them could fathom.

He had a feeling it might be the latter.

Which meant it would be best if he could get to the bottom of this before anyone else reached the Dreadlich and something… not necessarily beneficial happened.

Kindly Mavar certainly wasn’t evil. But his single-mindedness and his vision combined to lend him a perspective that occasionally lost track of any disasters along the way, so long as his end goal was as perfect as he saw it.

Which was why Ryland ought to act. Fast.

“What exactly are you attempting now?” Sint asked, clearly unbothered by this whole business. Then again, Ryland hadn’t explained all his suspicions yet.

“I suspect the room that contains the Dreadlich isn’t exactly located inside the Crypts,” he said. “So for now, I’m simply trying to find different aspects of it that may be connected to the Crypts, such as the entrance, or perhaps the exit. [Rift Seeker].”

Tiny golden cracks threaded through the tunnel. They probed and poked at everything, from the walls, floors, and ceiling to even the seemingly empty air.

The problem was that the tunnel walls blocked mana quite easily. Ryland’s magic was simply absorbed into the conducted stream passing through the earth, which made the effect of his spell circle dissipate to nothing.

“Is it actually working, though?” Linsby asked.

He had his hand pressed against one wall, a glow beneath his palm indicating the existence of a spell circle there. It seemed his magic was being absorbed too.

“Direct magic isn’t going to work, yes,” Ryland said. “Unless I overpower it, but that might break things in a way where even if I fix it rather fast, it might let out… unpleasantness we don’t need to deal with. Rather, I think I should try a more subtle approach.”

He focused for a moment, finding the right Subglyphs to form a different spell circle in moments.

“[Chthonic Battalion].”

More rifts popped up. These were tiny ones, dotting the area around Ryland with tiny dark glimmers. From each rift, a small army of glimmering ants emerged in straight lines, slowly wending their way to the walls, where they then began to dig.

“What in all the Realms…?” Sint said, unable to come up with what was going on.

Ryland smiled. “These are my helpful little critters. The tiny bit of mana they use at the beginning is to glean my commands, and then they’ll be fully independent.”

“So you’re going to order ants to find this Dreadlich?” Linsby asked.

“Ants that eat mana and possess the strength to dig through solid rock, yes. They have to build residences entirely out of metal on Realm 16.”

Linsby and Sint exchanged quick glances. Soon enough, they began following Ryland, who was already going along the trail that his summoned ants were leaving.

“You sure they aren’t just going to start eating the decomposing corpses?” Sint asked.

Ryland nodded. “I have ordered them not to, yes.”

It wasn’t long before they saw a figure in white in the distance. Linsby started quaking, and even Sint was hesitating, but Ryland soon went up and found it was merely a priest. One who was shaking in his boots in turn at Ryland’s appearance.

“Not this again,” he muttered.

Sint was throwing a soft glare at Ryland. “Stop scaring the poor fellow, Ryland.”

“I have done nothing at all besides exist,” Ryland said. He looked over at the priest. “Do you mind explaining what exactly has you so scared, my good fellow?”

“H-he’s talking,” the priest stuttered. “H-he’s talking with m-me!”

Sint got in front of Ryland before he actually made the poor old priest lose consciousness entirely. “It’s alright. He’s a little odd and a little too strong, but he’s not that scary.” She paused, apparently reconsidering that statement. “Well, he’s not a ghost.”

“I certainly am not,” Ryland said.

The priest, however, was turning catatonically silent. A few more minutes of this and he’d probably be scared out of his wits.

Ryland sighed and decided it was best to get going. He didn’t want to reduce the poor man to a blubbering mess, after all. Sint was already shooting him little glares and even Linsby was starting to get suspicious now.

But before any of them could get going, they heard wild yells coming from a different corridor.

Ryland and the other graduate students hurried over. He didn’t need long at all, reaching the students faster than his companions thanks to his powerful Inscriptions, though his quick and sudden appearance might have had the opposite effect of reassuring the scared students.

A trio of Arcoryx students, including Bulgon, were running from a different group of cackling students from Eviliyen School. They all came to a stop as soon as Ryland arrived.

“Sir!” Bulgon got so close, he looked like he was going to grab Ryland’s robes like a drowning person grabbing a floating log. “We’re being haunted! Those idiots got possessed!”

The Eviliyen group all laughed together.

“Stop running, you cowards,” one of them yelled, shambling over in a very good approximation of a lifeless ghoul. “We just want a pound of flesh to let you pass!”

“You can’t get away,” said another with an artificial spooky note. “You can’t leave without first paying the price of blood!”

They all laughed again as the Arcoryx students shuddered and quaked.

Sint and Linsby arrived a moment later. Linsby was just confused, while Sint looked like she couldn’t decide who to be annoyed at. Eventually, she decided to glare at the Arcoryx students.

“You really got scared by these idiots?” Sint asked.

“Hey!” one of the Eviliyen boys protested. “We’re not idiots. We’re not the scared ones.”

Sint just continued glaring at the Arcoryx bunch.

“We’re not joking!” Bulgon said heatedly. The others nodded, their faces still pale in fear. “They really are possessed!”

“Oh, come on,” Sint said.

Before she could say anything further, Ryland was stepping forward. He squinted at the Eviliyen student in the centre, his magic reaching out.

“No,” he said. “I think Bulgon and the others have a point.”

The tone in his voice cut off the laughter from the Eviliyen students while reinforcing the fear that the Arcoryx students had arrived with. Then the student, the tall girl Ryland was approaching, shuddered. Her body jerked, her eyes glowed, her hair starting to float on its own. Both of the other two Eviliyen students screamed and fell back on either side.

Ryland set his expression and approached. For all that High Academ Zevo and the priestess had warned about ghosts and haunting, possession wasn’t supposed to be an issue.

And yet, here was a student whose soul had been nabbed by a ghost.

What exactly was going on in these Crypts?
