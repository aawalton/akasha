Day 328, 1:00 AM

“Hello, Edna.” She jumps, rage burning in her eyes as I grab her by the throat. I had coated myself in magic which shields me from detection, passing through her sphere of supernatural awareness without raising any alarms.

She opens her mouth to say something, but I won’t give her a chance to sing. Mana surges out of her, but I match its colors and nature, dispelling whatever she’s trying to do before it happens. Then I exert another portion of my will and my mana crushes her defenses and floods her body; the spell is aimed to undo all changes she’s made to her physique, all of them, while keeping her alive no matter what happens.

Her eyes grow darker, gray streaks her hair, her perfect features change, her nose growing crooked and more aquiline. She’s no longer a perfect doll, but a human being. A very old human being. Slowly, as I work on her altered neurons, the sense of fear returns to her, and Edna’s rage shifts into terror. She no longer tries to cast spells, her lips mouth pleas as she begs, aware I hold her life in my hands.

Finally, after what must have been the two most trauma-inducing minutes of Edna’s long, traumatized life, I stop, my mana keeping the old woman alive as it circulates through her body.

“You have altered your brain too much, you have become a monster,” I tell her in a calm, soothing voice. “I have undone all the changes you have ever made to your body, so you can start fresh.”

My hand releases her throat.

“Please, don’t make any neural changes, you don’t understand how the brain operates, and you’re going to descend into madness again. Honestly, if I were you, I would only tamper with the skin, muscles, and rejuvenate my organs to ensure no failures happen.”

Edna nods.

“I wish to help you. You have saved my life, you have helped me adapt to this world, and I am grateful, but we were getting closer and closer to the point where I would have to kill you, and I don’t want that.”

I don’t know if she believes me or not, but this is the last chance I’m giving her.

“There is something very wrong with the dungeons. I’m an archmage now and my mana pool can’t be much worse than a wormlord’s. I even suspect it’s greater, but the dungeon can still refill my mana in a day. That should be impossible without sucking the wormlord dry of their mana, and then they should die. I don’t think dungeons are what we think they are.

“Then there are other problems. I have delved to floor ninety and beyond. The amount of attribute points I should have received should have been staggering, but all I got was a single point per floor. The points are probably a trap, or possibly your ancestors made the dungeons to empower themselves with attribute points, but something went wrong.”

She listens to me with dismay, probably not processing half the things I wish to convey.

“Fix your body, then we’ll take what army you have made so far and head for the church’s headquarters. I have a feeling we’re going to find our answers there. I suspect the church knows a lot more than we do. About the nature of wormlords, abominations, possibly even magic.”

“Can I heal myself now?” Edna asks after I stop talking. “Everything hurts.”

I gesture for her to start freely, and she sings her song of rejuvenation. The ravages of age disappear, then she corrects the imperfections to her appearance and changes it according to her preferences.

My two “visions” skills tell me how she’s evolving, but even without them, I’m keeping a watchful eye over her transformation, focusing mostly on her neurons. She makes no changes to them, nor her important glands, and slowly, her song reaches its end.

“Can you pay attention now? Do you understand what I have done and why?”

“Yes, thank you,” she looks down at the ground with shame.

“How many tree soldiers do you have?” I estimate the number at around five hundred, based on the scant information she gave me before and how much time has elapsed since.

“Six hundred and twenty. Not enough to besiege a city.”

“With you and me to provide support, it will be enough. We can always retreat if our forces prove insufficient.”

“What about the others?” Edna’s tone no longer seems as cold when she mentions the additions to our original party of two.

“I would keep Elie and Millie here. Millie hasn’t even reached the higher levels of the journeyman mage, and Elie has enough patience to instruct her. As for the girls, they will soon become first level mages, and we could take them with us. The journey to the capital will take another month, and we could create treants, the tree soldiers, throughout the night to bolster their numbers. But I think we won’t need them. I have taken part in a lot of sieges, and the best tactic is to strike during the night, eliminating the leadership in one decisive swoop.”

We discuss the plan for a while longer, I once more warn Edna not to do anything stupid, just in case, then we head for the mansion.

I bluff the door and enter before heading to the kitchen. The girls aren’t there, which means they are probably asleep. I leave them be and head to Hadriuse’s bedroom, but the journals aren’t there, Edna had taken them in the final loop involving those tomes.

I’ll ask her about them later.

With nothing better to do, I take a shower, then head down to the research lab and search for any notes potentially relevant to questions breeding in my mind. The more I know, the more I discover, the more questions block my path. I have a feeling there’s a deep secret regarding the wormlords, not just the fact that they are a mage upgrade class on par with the archmages — no, the dungeons, the Guide, everything is connected, and I’m missing one final piece, and that piece is in the church’s headquarters.

Around four in the morning, I head into the kitchen and wait for Gila to come and cook breakfast for everyone. I close my eyes and draw as much mana as I can, distilling it for later use.

An hour later, Gila appears.

“Good morning, Griff.”

“Good morning, Gila. Is Lucy awake?”

“She’ll be here in half an hour or so.” I don’t need to say anything, but she can already tell I want to speak with the two of them. “Anything you would specifically like for breakfast?”

I shrug. “The food you prepare is sublime ever since you’ve figured out how to incorporate magic into cooking.”

She says her thanks without an ounce of false modesty, and starts preparing breakfast for five.

Twenty minutes later, Lucy comes in. She blushes when she sees me, and I blush back, sensing the life growing inside her womb. I’m not sure how I feel about having a descendant in this world, it almost feels like Aboy all over again.

“We’re going to overthrow the church. Do you want to come with?” The words I use are almost comical, but I guess that’s just how confident I am in our victory. “I have healed Edna of her madness, so she shouldn’t pose any danger to you. I have also cleared the dungeon more thoroughly than usual, so Elie and Millie will be able to handle things for at least a while.”

I can already tell Gila doesn’t want to leave this place. She’s a woman seeking comfort, a rather smart woman, given the state of the world she lives in, but Lucy jumps at the opportunity.

“I want to come!” She’s overly excited, the chemicals in her body already doing strange things to her mind, but she would have said the same even if her condition were perfect.

“I will come as well,” Gila grumbles. “Someone has to watch over these two while you are playing soldiers.”

Her glare tells me several things. Lucy knows, Gila knows, and she knows that I know about Lucy’s condition as well as the fact that Lucy is keeping it a secret from me.

“You need to take responsibility for your actions. Lucy grew without a father—”

“Gila!”

“He needs to know, you stupid honeygrub.” Gila looks at me. “Lucy’s dad went into the dungeon and his party never returned. Her mother raised her alone, and you have no idea how tough that is. I don’t know who or what you are, but I definitely know you’re not like us. You might not even be human.”

I knew she was smart, but her wits are sharp. I think she started with eleven intellect, and even with such a low number, she was a good judge of character and circumstances. Let alone now, when she’s around twenty-five.

“I don’t mean you any harm.”

She rolls her eyes. “I know. That’s why we’re going with you. I wouldn’t have let my best friend follow you on a suicidal mission, which only makes the whole affair even more absurd. You’re heading to fight the most powerful people in the world, as relaxed as one can be, and no matter how crazy it sounds, I’m certain that you’re going to win and the safest place to be would be somewhere near you.”
