Chapter 17

Scary

I’m awakened in the early morning by a bright flash of light. Not a real flash of light, mind you. I personally made certain our tent - better described as a fold-out fabric cabin - is sufficiently blacked out so that nobody outside can make out any details from the interior even if we have lights on, and the light level from the outside can’t disturb us if we need to sleep through it. There are, of course, zippable windows if we want airflow or something, but what wakes me isn’t from anything coming through them, either.

It’s coming from a blue window being broadcast directly into my brain.

NEW TRAIT ACQUIRED

DUNGEON MASTER

----------------------

In the vein of the ancients, you have successfully bonded with a dungeon core and customized its space to your preference. Your understanding of a dungeon’s operations exceeds that of all but the most erudite experts. Your connection allows you greater insight into the functions and status of any dungeon you enter.

Be aware that accessing this information for any dungeon you have not bonded with will require access to that dungeon’s core.

Grants access to a dungeon’s technical data

Decreases difficulty establishing bonds with dungeon cores

Increases influence with unbonded dungeon cores

Enables Dungeon Summon

You may demand dice rolls from your party members

They are permitted to assume you are crazy and ignore you

I’m not one to be slow to rise in the morning, but the more I read of this, the more it’s hitting me like a shot of caffeinated adrenaline right into my arm. Each time through, I get more and more excited. That this is happening must mean that the dungeon has finished upgrading, and we were successful! I even hum a little victory tune from an old RPG series as I pull myself up.

And then there's the new ability, Dungeon Summon. I pull it up too, and can't entirely suppress a giggle as I read it. Another hidden skill, and this one's going to blow everyone's minds!

I want to rush to get everyone up, but even as I turn toward the others, Ayre is already stirring. He looks a bit uncomfortable, but then he stills as he opens his eyes. He’s facing away from me, but after a long moment of silence, he speaks.

“... Remmi, what did you do?”

“... Nothing new,” I answer defensively. “Why?”

He rolls over to face me and sits up under his blanket. “Because I just got woken up by a notice that only said, ‘Remmi did something again,’ with a token five hundred points, like it just wanted the excuse to let me know.”

I scowl as I cross my arms. That’s the second time that I know of that the System’s tattled on me. “Well, like I said, nothing new. I got a notice too, and I think it’s happening now because the dungeon has probably finished. We should get some breakfast in us, pack up camp, and go check it out.”

At the confirmation that, indeed, nothing he hasn’t already been made aware of has transpired, he lightens up considerably and nods. “Yeah, that’s a good plan. We’ll need to confirm everything didn't blow up in our sleep.”

I ignore how he giggles at my pouting expression. “Oh, come on, if it had, we wouldn’t be waking up to find out!”

“That’s fair,” he agrees, still cheerful as he gets to his feet as well. “We’ll be able to tell quickly if it’s finished or not just by seeing if the fog has cleared.”

It’s just a minute or so later when we all step out of the tent and into the early-morning air. Ayre and I are alert, but Korrigan is still half asleep and yawning, and still carrying Kyuuga like a giant stuffed animal. Who seems to have accepted this, the little punk.

Sure enough, the direction of the Forest Cavern dungeon is as clear as anywhere else, just the same cold, ankle-height mists that fill this area. The kind that’ll clear out like ghosts the moment the sun finishes its coffee. But even though that’s what we came out to see, it’s not what immediately catches our attention.

Our campfire is crackling, and a teapot and a kettle of porridge are heating above it. It wasn’t the carriage man; I suspect he’s still asleep in his own tent. No, the culprit is still sitting there, as if belonging here, as if she’d been part of the expedition all along, and the warm, motherly smile she greets us all with sends a chill down my spine.

“Good morning, everyone,” Sacred Yorin opens as if she had been waiting for us to take notice of her before speaking. “I hope you all slept well.”

This is finally motivation for Kyuuga to kick free of Korrigan in order to rush over to the high priestess, who beams down at him and gives his ears a good scratch once he collides with her lap.

“S-Sacred Yorin?!” Ayre has to swallow before he can continue. “Wh-what brings you here?!” And then, reflexively, the words, “No, wait, you’re here to kill Remmi, aren’t you?!” suddenly get tacked on at the end.

I’m knocked out of my own shock to give my best friend side-eye. Isn’t it a little too early to be killing off the protagonist so casually?!

Yorin acts as if that second question was never spoken. “Oh, well, I sent you all on a very strange mission of great importance, one to which many other adventurers have failed to find a solution. When I went all day without hearing anything, I thought perhaps I should come and check on you, to make certain that everyone was alright.”

“It's funny how that almost actually sounds like the truth,” I note. She ignores that, too.

“Come,” she says instead with a wave of her hand as if inviting us into our own camp. “Sit. Eat. Tell me what you have learned of the dungeon's troubles. Have you discovered the problem it faces?”

The three of us share a look among each other, but we don't really feel like we have a choice in the matter, so we do as she says and move to take seats.

“Remmi did it,” Ayre immediately throws me under the carriage as soon as his bubble butt hits the log.

That makes me freeze halfway through my own sitting, but Yorin is unfazed.

“I am certainly detecting some guilty consciences here, yes,” she agrees with a velvety smoothness that puts my teeth on edge, “but I am certain we will get to that in due time. For now, that does not answer my question.”

There's a moment of silence that Yorin seems perfectly content to wait for it to be filled.

“... Yes,” I finally say. “Yes, we did find the problem. Immediately and undeniably. So much so, in fact, that it's hard to believe you hadn't already heard about it from the reports of the other adventurers.”

She just steeples her fingers and leans forward. “Enlighten me.”

I can't tell if she genuinely doesn't know or just wants to hear it from me. “It didn't have any monsters to cull. Or, more accurately, it has only one that you can't cull.”

“And for what reason can it not be culled?” she asks. “Surely, even focusing all of its meager power into a single monster, the Forest Cavern could not create a foe to surpass a Hero’s team on the cusp of achieving Gold rank.”

I shake my head. “No, I think she could probably be killed by groups a whole lot weaker than ours. The problem is, how do you do that to a kid?”

That causes Yorin’s eyebrow to raise, though, unlike Ayre’s expressive appendages, her ears remain perfectly unmoved. “The monster is a child?”

I hold my breath for a moment as I consider whether to go forward, but I’ve already said too much not to, and Yorin wouldn’t let anything bad happen to Angel once she understands. “Yeah. The dungeon didn’t use all of that power to make a defender. It made a mascot.”

Her smile thins as she gives an, “Excuse me?” Which is kind of her version of an intelligent, “... hah?”

“Mascot, a character that is representative of a piece of entertainment for the sole purpose of making it memorable and making lots and lots of money off of its marketable plushies,” I explain.

Ayre’s eyes roll over crossed arms as those stupidly fabulous legs fold one over the other. “We know what mascots are, Remmi.”

Korrigan, meanwhile, bounces her skull quickly enough to do a pretty good impression of a bobblehead. “Uh-huh, yeah! Like Cheeky!”

The name pulls Ayre’s eyes wide upon hearing it, and he unfolds his legs to turn to face the younger girl. “You know Cheeky, Korrigan?!”

“Yeah,” she nods back excitedly. “She's really cute!”

“Right?!” Ayre gushes back. “With those chubby little chipmunk cheeks! Just seeing one of her dangling charms brightens my day! And all of the expressions her ink stamps come in! It's like you could have an entire conversation just with them!”

“I like the seasonal tins, they're really pretty,” Korrigan admits. “My grandmother had the whole set!”

Suddenly, Ayre’s whole body freezes like it's been turned to stone, his smile thinning like it isn't supposed to still be there but hasn't realized it yet. “... Wait, what?”

“Aaaaaanyway,” I drawl, swooping in before Ayre’s existential crisis over realizing kids think his hobby is for old women can go supercritical and focusing the conversation back on Yorin, “maybe it’ll be faster if I just introduce you to her!”

With a wave of my hand, I activate my new Hidden Skill. In a great column of shifting light that I can’t decide looks more like digitization or a transporter beam, a girl’s feathered form materializes before us.

Angel’s eyes are wide and owlish as she blinks several times, still processing the change in scenery, then they recognize that I’m standing in front of her.

“Remmi?!” she squeaks in shock. “When did you get here?!” And then she’s looking around in every direction. “No, wait, where are we?! Ayre and Korrigan are here, too?! And Mister Rabbit!”

I’m outright grinning at the looks on everyone’s faces. Ayre and Korrigan, sure, but Angel, too. “We’re outside of the dungeon, Angel. It’s only for an hour, but for that time, you won’t be restricted to the Forest Cavern!”

Those eyes go wide again. “Wow, really?! So I can go where you all go?! I can see the places you’ve been?!”

I nod. “More or less. You can even fly in the sky!”

That drives her to turn her gaze upward, at the endless dawn expanse above us. She staggers for a moment, her jaw slack, as she tries to take in the scope of a world without a roof on it.

It’s a charming display of innocent wonder, and I wish I could enjoy it while it lasts, but I can’t do that.

“... Remmi …”

That voice reminds me that there’s someone’s face I haven’t been watching, and its threatening, dangerous tone draws everyone’s attention to it.

Angel takes one look at Yorin and forgets all about the sky, electing instead to jump behind me for shelter as she peeks, trembling, around my arm. “Ah, Remmi! That person’s scary!”

In the moment, I think I know exactly what she means.

The Sacred Priestess isn’t looking at Angel, and Ayre and Korrigan could cease to exist without their absence being noticed. Her eyes are locked firmly on me. She says nothing more, only pointing one pale finger at my chest, then turning it over to curl it repeatedly toward her.

… Ah, I probably should have seen this coming …

I turn around to guide Angel by the shoulders over to Ayre. “Say, Angel, why don’t you work on your language skills with Ayre and Korrigan for a bit? I’ll be right back!”

“... Bye, Remmi,” Korrigan says in a way that makes it hard to swallow.

Darn it, don’t build me a coffin already! Even if I’m probably going to need it …
