The rain did not stop. It kept drumming on the roof, seeping through cracks, and dripping into two old bowls Gilbert had found in the same house and placed under the most shameless leaks. Pampli lay under the awning by the wall of the house. Sometimes Sakura saw his tail twitch irritably in the mud, and she understood that the beast was not happy about another stop either, though this time because of the weather.

Sakura and the mage settled in the driest corner, closer to the stove. The leaks were behind them and to the right, and between them on the floor lay an old board Gilbert was using instead of a table.

She was still looking at her elbow, where the scratch had disappeared. The skin there was still red, but there was no pain anymore. Not even the unpleasant pulling sensation that usually remained after any healing.

That did not make Sakura feel any calmer. Gilbert had already hidden the salve in a small leather case. At that moment, Sakura thought that Gilbert probably had a lot of space in his Inventory, more than she did, if he could carry so many small useful things with him.

"And what are you going to do now that you know this salve works strangely on me?" Sakura asked.

Gilbert looked up.

"I was going to say that it should be used carefully."

Sakura looked at him, feeling that her companion was not saying everything he was thinking. Gilbert held her gaze with a calm that felt almost insolent to her.

"Fine," she said. "Carefully it is."

He gave a slight nod, as if that small argument was already over, and took a folded cloth out of his Inventory. On it lay a piece of coal and an old metal needle.

Sakura tensed.

"Now that already looks worse than the salve."

"This is part of the conversation about the Castle of Justice."

She sat up straighter. The exhaustion immediately moved somewhere to the side, leaving behind a cold attentiveness.

"You decided to tell me the plan?"

"The foundation."

"Fine. Let’s call it the foundation."

"A full plan inside the Castle cannot be built in advance," Gilbert said. "Too many variables. The schedule of the mists that surround and protect the prison. Guard rotations, Lir’s condition, runes, current orders. We need an action framework that can withstand changes."

"That sounds like what we have is not a plan, but only the desire to survive."

"The desire to survive usually has no structure. We have structure."

Sakura wanted to make a sarcastic comment, but changed her mind. Gilbert spoke dryly, almost in a teacher’s tone, and that was exactly why it became clear that he knew what he was talking about.

Gilbert took the coal and drew an uneven circle on the old board.

"The Castle of Justice was built as a place no mage is supposed to leave, even if he still has half his strength and an entire lifetime to prepare. The outer wall is not the biggest problem there. The problem is the runes."

"Blocking magic?"

"Active magic, Inventory, spatial links, some class skills, and almost all external artifacts. Casting there is almost impossible. But the runes still read magical signatures."

Sakura exhaled slowly.

"So everything we normally use cannot be used there."

"That is what the builders counted on."

"I hate it when enemies have good architecture."

Gilbert did not smile, but the corner of his mouth twitched.

"That is why I will enter through the legal entrance."

Sakura was silent for several seconds.

"As a..."

"Prisoner."

Now she really felt cold. She remembered that Gilbert had already said those words before. But it had not become any easier since last time.

"You want to get yourself arrested."

"I want to be brought inside."

"Those are different words for the same disgusting idea."

"The difference is significant. An arrested person looks helpless. A person brought inside ends up on the other side of the first three defensive circuits."

Sakura stared at the circle drawn in coal.

"And they won’t recognize you?"

"If I don’t enter as Gilbert, then yes. They won’t."

Gilbert took out a small sealed case. He did not open it, but Sakura understood what was inside anyway. She felt the energy coming from it. The energy of the dead mage’s core, whose people they had left behind in the canyon.

Gilbert placed the case between them, then nodded toward the needle.

"I’ll stitch it into myself."

Sakura looked at him as if he had just calmly suggested carrying Pampli in his arms through a burning forest.

"Under your skin?"

"Deep enough for the runes to read it as part of my magical background. Close enough to the magic flows for it to contaminate my signature."

"You’re talking about another person’s core like it’s dirty paint..."

"The comparison is crude, but the idea is correct."

Sakura clenched her fingers.

"Explain it properly."

Gilbert was silent for a moment, choosing his words.

"Almost every mage has a signature core. In an ordinary person, it is soft, loose, sometimes barely formed. In a mage, it is denser. In a strong mage, it can resemble a small stone: dark, amber, milky, transparent. The color depends on the nature of the magic and what the person has lived through."

He spoke evenly, like during a lecture. Except lectures were usually not given in abandoned houses while an obsidian beast lay outside the window and a piece of a dead person was stored in a case between the speakers.

"It does not appear already formed," Gilbert continued. "A child has only a weak seed of it. Then magic flows through the body for years, settles, condenses, repeats familiar patterns. Rituals, class, surges of power, training, wounds, artifacts. Everything leaves a trace. The core becomes the sediment of lived magic."

Sakura involuntarily looked at the case.

"Sediment?"

"Yes. It is not the soul. Not the entire reserve of mana. It stores a personal signature, a name-imprint, a trace of the class, the habitual pattern of spells, residual emotions, and the body’s memory. The magical habit of being a specific person."

"That sounds like a soul. Just a mage’s soul."

"They say the soul is what stores our identity. But if you remove a mage’s core while they are alive, they will not die, lose their memory, or lose their character. They will not become soulless. So it is not the soul, but it is a very important part. Unlike the soul, it does not die with the body immediately. That is why we were able to extract it."

Gilbert said it without trying to soften anything. Sakura might even have appreciated the honesty if she had not wanted to hit him with something.

"When I stitch the core in, the System and some of the verification runes will start seeing me differently," he said. "The old layer will remain, but a fresh foreign imprint will appear over it. The name will distort. It may come out as a merge of mine and his. It may produce something in between. I won’t know in advance."

"So you don’t even know who exactly you will become to the System."

"The System does not like such experiments, so the result is unpredictable. But it is temporary."

"Gilbert."

He looked at her.

"Temporary," he repeated. "While the core is fresh and while the body has not pushed it out completely."

"And if the body starts pushing it out in the prison?"

"Fever. Nausea. Distorted perception. Magic will malfunction, but that does not matter, since the prison will suppress it anyway. Sharp pains, hand tremors. If the rejection happens quickly, I will have to cut the core out ahead of time."

Sakura opened her mouth, then closed it. Because the first phrase that came to mind was too rude even for their circumstances.

The second was not much better.

"You are completely insane."

"No. I just listed the risks."

"You listed the risks in the voice of someone talking about bad weather."

"The bad weather is already here."

Sakura stared at him.

"Very funny."

"I wasn’t joking."

Gilbert lowered his gaze to the drawn circle.

"If I go in without the core, they will recognize me at the first inspection. If we try to infiltrate from outside, they will notice us before we find Lir. If you go with me, you will be identified faster than I will. The mark, Pampli, the trace of your class, all of it is too noticeable. The Castle of Justice was built for mages, and now it is full of people waiting for exactly those mistakes."

Sakura clenched her teeth. She wanted to argue simply out of stubbornness. Simply because the thought of Gilbert going there alone, with a foreign core under his skin, was disgusting.

But she was not stupid. In the prison, they really would recognize her. She was the Obsidian Hierophant, they were looking for her, and that was already common knowledge.

"I understand why I can’t go inside," she said at last. "I don’t like it, but I understand."

Gilbert nodded.

"Your task will be outside."

"What exactly?"

"Cover the escape route at the Six Mists."

Sakura frowned.

"What exactly do you mean?"

"The Castle has six outer mist zones. Each mist has its own composition, color, and method of passage. For each one, the guards have a separate protective suit. A mistake in the suit will simply kill a person."

Gilbert drew six short lines around the circle.

"Inside, I must learn which mist they use on the day of extraction and where the weak point will be. Outside, you will need to hold the path open long enough for us to get out."

"'Hold the path open' sounds like the part where you are not saying everything. More precisely, barely anything."

"Because I do not yet know which of the six paths we will get. And because we will discuss that part of the plan again. Later."

"At least tell me when you’ll know which mist it will be."

"Inside."

Sakura tiredly rubbed her face with her palm.

"Of course. Where else."

She was silent for a moment, then added, "How will I even know which mist to go to?"

"By a signal."

"What kind?"

"I will explain that part later too."

"And how I’m supposed to not die from deadly mist without a suit, you’ll also explain later?"

"Exactly," Gilbert answered.

Sakura barely restrained herself from smacking herself in the face from an overload of emotions, while Gilbert continued:

"We cannot rely on Inventory there. Everything I may need has to be physically on me. Some things will have to be hidden in the body."

Sakura slowly looked at him.

"Are you talking about lockpicks?"

"Among other things."

"In the body."

"Yes. Small ones. Hard to notice."

"Are you sure you’re not a laboratory maniac who simply chose a noble goal for himself?"

"If I were a laboratory maniac, we would have better tools."

Sakura involuntarily snorted. Otherwise she would have had to keep sitting with a stone face and imagining Gilbert stitching a dead core and bits of metal into himself.

"Inside, I will not break the prison," he continued. "Breaking prisons is stupid. They are built for that. I will look for human cracks."

"Human cracks?" Sakura arched an eyebrow.

"Who is lazy. Who drinks. Who checks locks twice. Who never checks them at all. Every system has people who make it worse than it was designed to be on paper."

"You’re going to escape through someone else’s laziness?"

"Through laziness, fear, habit, and overconfidence. Usually, that is enough."

"And if it isn’t enough?"

"Then we will need a crisis."

Sakura went silent. She did not like that word either.

"What kind of crisis?"

"The kind that will look natural for the Castle. A fire, a malfunction, a fight, an unscheduled inspection, the arrival of an important official. I will choose whatever creates the least direct connection to us."

"You say it too easily. As if you’re just choosing a door..."

"Ideally, I will choose a door. With luck, it won’t even have to be broken open. Sometimes taking a door off its hinges is safer than touching the lock. The lock will be checked afterward. The hinges will not."

Sakura looked at him with unwilling respect.

"That is so disgustingly reasonable that I don’t even know how to argue."

"You don’t have to argue."

"Too bad. I’ve already gotten used to arguing with you."

Gilbert finally looked at her more attentively.

"I am not planning to arrange a slaughter."

Sakura did not immediately realize that he was answering a question she had not yet asked out loud.

"Because you feel sorry for the guards?"

"Because corpses raise the alarm faster than tied-up people. An unconscious guard gives time. A dead guard closes the gates."

"You have a way of making mercy sound very practical."

"Practicality does not cancel the result."

Sakura looked at his hands again.

"I’m not afraid that the plan is dirty," she said more quietly. "I understand where we’re going, and that Lir can’t be pulled out with pretty words and clean hands. But you talk about yourself as if you’re not a person, but a tool. Core here. Lockpick there. Fever is acceptable. Pain is acceptable. If needed, I’ll cut it out ahead of time..."

Gilbert said nothing.

"That is what pisses me off," Sakura said. "Not the plan. You. So much risk. You don’t think about yourself at all."

"The risk is justified. We need efficiency, not caution."

"No. We need you to not heroically fall apart in the middle of the prison."

"I am not planning to heroically fall apart."

"Great. Put that in the plan as a separate point."

He exhaled wearily. For the first time in the whole conversation, something human flickered through him, not hidden under calculations and dry wording.

"Sakura, if I start thinking of my own body as something untouchable, Lir will stay inside."

"I’m not asking you to be untouchable."

"Then what are you asking?"

She did not answer at once, because she did not fully know herself. Outside, Pampli quietly snorted in his sleep or in irritation. The rain kept knocking on the roof.

"For you to stop pretending that nothing is happening to you," Sakura said. "And if the core starts breaking you faster than you calculated, I want you not to play hero with your own body until the last second, but to do something, even if it creates a risk for the plan. Immediately."

Gilbert looked at her for a long time.

"All right."

"This is not the kind of 'all right' where it means 'I heard you, but I’ll still do it my way.'"

"I understand."

"Gilbert."

"I will do it immediately," he said more clearly.

Sakura nodded. It did not make the plan safe, but now she felt as if she had a little more control.

Gilbert put away the coal, folded the cloth, and took the case with the core.

"When?"

"Not now," he said. "I need to prepare the spot where I’ll stitch it in, and the tools. And you need to sleep."

"Me?"

"Yes. The person who almost drowned today, walked through a downpour, made medicine from laboratory slime, and received a personal warning from the System."

"But you’re that person too. You did all of that with me," Sakura objected.

"I will sleep too. But first, I still need to work on our plan."

"You are the kind of person who will definitely die at work. One day."

Gilbert looked at her, and this time he really did smile faintly. She lay down closer to the wall, wrapped herself in the half-dry cloak, and for a long time watched him sit by the fire, checking the tools. The case with the core lay beside him, radiating energy. That object still felt like the beginning of some unknown disaster.
