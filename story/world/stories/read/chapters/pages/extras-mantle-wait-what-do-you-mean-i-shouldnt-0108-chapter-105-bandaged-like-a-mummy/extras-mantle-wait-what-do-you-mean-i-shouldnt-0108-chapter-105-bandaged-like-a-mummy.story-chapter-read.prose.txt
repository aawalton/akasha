Jin woke with a violent start, gasping for air like he'd been drowning.

His body screamed in protest… every part, every muscle throbbed with pain. He tried to sit up on instinct and nearly blacked out just from the effort alone.

"Whoa, whoa! Easy there, hero."

Hands pressed him back down—gentle but insistent—and Jin blinked through the haze to see a woman leaning over him. Dark hair pulled back in a messy ponytail, sea-green eyes ringed with exhaustion, and a scowl on her face as if Jin’s presence annoyed her.

"You push yourself any harder, and you'll tear open everything I just spent the last few minutes patching up." She adjusted strips of bandages around his torso—a little too tightly—and Jin winced from the spike of pain. "See! That would have… just with way more dose of pain!"

What the hell! What sort of madmen… madwoman is she!

Jin's mind scrambled to catch up. The last thing he remembered was... Orodain and all remaining good vanished from his mind as he was once again haunted by that—the cold, bitter truth that he'd killed innocents because he hadn't checked, hadn't taken the time to verify they were actually enemies, hadn't—

"Arghh... my head." The woman winced, pressing fingertips against her temples. "Can you not do that! Whatever trauma spiral you're having, go suffer alone in a bathroom or something."

“Isn’t that what you all men do?” She said, still scowling.

trauma?… and bathroom… What is she on?

Jin blinked, pushing past the shock to make sense of the baffling situation. "What?"

"Your emotions," she said irritably, dropping her hands with a sigh. "They're loud. Like someone's screaming inside my skull. Usually, I can filter it out, but you're on the way to a dark path, dude! I can’t—"

She stopped mid-sentence, eyes going wide.

This time, Jin knew what had caused her reactions—just like… what had happened in the fight, a wave of cold clarity passed through him—as he felt all the rushing emotions, the guilt, the self-blame, the horror, all just muted.

Like someone had turned down the volume on a radio that had been blaring at max.

"Oh!" The woman stared at him with something between fascination and alarm. "What the hell did you just do? One second you're drowning in godknowswhat! and the next it's just... gone. That's not normal. That's really not normal."

Jin ignored her for a moment and looked at himself. The woman had done good work if his injuries were really that severe.

Because white bandages covered most of his chest, his left arm, and his right hand, where Orodain's sickle had slid through. He could feel more wrappings around his legs under the rough blanket someone—probably her—had thrown over him.

Jin sighed in relief, feeling his spatial ring was still there, his mind was—although now much more stable—still a mess.

Unknowingly, he instinctively reached for it mentally and pulled out a potion—

A firm hand clamped down on his wrist before he could manifest it.

"Oh, no, you don't." The woman's scowl deepened into something almost frightening. "What are you, an idiot? Just how many potions did you take during..." She gestured vaguely at his bandaged body, then toward the corridor beyond. "...before your fight with that damn cultist?"

Jin froze. Oh no.

He had truly consumed potions—courtesy of the field alchemy injector—in bulk; if nothing else, he had consumed at least 5-7 healing potions since “Trish” happened.

He shivered as he sent a mental probe to his body, finding it filled with potion wastes and toxins.

Oh Shit.

« Indeed. “Oh. Shit” »

The narrator's voice echoed in his mind.

« Jin, your body is literally in the state of 'shit' »

Jin leaned back and let out a long sigh. Yeah, well, mistakes were made.

"That many, huh?"

The woman’s voice brought Jin out of his daze as he looked at her to see her shaking her head. "Your liver's probably staging a protest as we speak. One more potion right now, and you'll be dealing with essence reversal and severe toxicity of blood on top of everything else. Trust me… You don't want that."

Jin's lips curled up just ever so slightly, some emotions bleeding through the coldness. He was, quite frankly, more amused at himself.

He nodded at her and tried to speak.

"How long?" His voice came out rough, cracking on the second word.

"See? That's what I'm talking about." But she smiled slightly. "Your throat's also shredded from essence strain. And it's been closer to ten minutes since the twins hauled you in here like a sack of potatoes."

Only ten minutes?

That was... good. At least he hadn't been out for long beside—whatever his situation be—He… no, they couldn't waste time lying around.

"Thank you."

The words were so quiet Jin almost missed them, and for a moment he was confused if she was expecting him to show her gratitude when he looked at her, and no, she was the one showing her gratitude.

The woman—healer, he really needed to get her name—was looking down at her hands, fingers twisting together in a gesture that screamed nervousness.

"For what?" Jin asked slowly.

"For killing that cultist." She looked up, and there was something in her sea-green eyes. "We... some of us saw part of the fight. The end of it, anyway. That silver..." She shivered, wrapping her arms around herself. "I can still feel the heat from here. And I just... I don't think even Eleanor could have won that. Not without—"

She cut herself off, shaking her head hard like she was trying to dislodge an unpleasant thought.

A moment of silence stretched between them. Jin watched emotions play across her face—fear, relief, something that might have been awe—and wondered if…

No point. Jin shook that thought. He was no hero; he was someone who needed to get things done.

"And besides," she continued, voice steadier now, "the information that... that most of the enemy forces in the Bastion are actually our people..." Her hands clenched into fists. "That's going… We and others have been killing our own, and we didn't even know it."

"Yeah," Jin said quietly. "I know."

Another beat of silence. Then the woman seemed to shake herself, straightening up with visible effort.

"Where are my manners?" She raised her hand in a mock salute that looked more tired than crisp. "Maya von Hawkins. Newly enlisted member of the 'Volunteers'—though apparently I’m sure it's more of a 'Forced Volunteers' because your twin friends have a terrible sense of humor."

Jin felt his lips twitch toward a smile. "Jin Winters. Pleasure to meet you, Maya."

"Likewise." Maya settled back on her heels, studying him. "Though I gotta say, you look like hammered shit. No offense."

"None taken." Jin started to rise, ignoring the immediate surge of alarm that crossed Maya's face. "I've felt worse."

"That's a lie, and we both know it," she said flatly, but she didn't try to push him back down this time. Just watched with narrowed eyes as Jin managed to sit upright without passing out. "You're Order III now, though, newly ascended, aren't you?"

Jin nodded slowly, testing the movement. "Yeah. Ascended during the fight."

"Of course you did." Maya let out a breath that was half laugh, half disbelief, and then turned a bit serious. "Besides, even with your new regenerative powers and aura… if you keep getting this much damage.” She gestured at him with a wave of her arm. “There’ll be lasting scars… permanent if not.”

"And I, as a new doctor, wouldn't recommend it."

"I'll try," Jin nodded.

Maya just shook her head with a chuckle.

Jin flexed his shoulders and neck, feeling the bones pop with satisfying cracks. The pain was still there—and would be there for hours, probably days if he were to go without more potions—but it was manageable.

His new rank meant enhanced natural recovery, and his aura was already working to flush toxins and repair damage from the inside out.

He could feel it, actually. The way his essence moved differently now, denser and more responsive. The way his body's baseline healing had accelerated beyond what he could previously manage.

Small mercies.

Besides, he had ideas on how to potentially use his aura and his chains to try to harvest the toxins out of himself.

After all, there wasn’t any fact with how he had used his mantle till now that would make this feat impossible.

« Seriously… Already thinking back to harvest! And this time, yourself! »

He leaned back against the wall, choosing to take in his surroundings rather than to get into an argument with the narrator. He knew it—he—was getting more and more humanlike, taking in from his persona, and as such, he would not want a grumpy narrator in his mind.

Jin found he was in some kind of recreation area… tables pushed against walls, chairs stacked in corners, sports equipment shoved into makeshift barriers. It had been hastily converted into a triage station, with sections curtained off for privacy and supplies organized on every available surface.

Maya scribbled onto her notepad, going through drawers from drawers that were on his left and finally, with a sigh, settled into a chair beside his bed. "Not much, but it works. We set this up about twenty minutes ago."

"Where are we?" Jin asked.

"Thirty-first floor, recreation area." Maya gestured vaguely toward the curtained sections. "The storerooms down the hall are acting as temporary holding cells for the... changed people we've managed to subdue. Can't exactly kill them if there's a chance they can be saved, right?"

Jin felt something unclench in his chest. "Yeah. Right."

"Your fight burned a hole straight through three floors, by the way." Maya's voice carried a note of awe. "Twenty-ninth to thirty-first. We're literally sitting above a vertical shaft of melted stone. Whatever that silver fire was... I've never seen anything like it."

An experiment and extreme heat. Jin thought distantly. Glad it worked.

"The twins got you here after we were sure you weren't going to explode or something," Maya continued. "Brocki was convinced you were going to detonate with how high your temperature was. Kept yelling at others to shove it and pour water or any type of skills that could cool you off."

"They're good men," he said with a chuckle. "Brocki and Flocki."

"Oh, they're something." Maya rolled her eyes. "They showed up yelling about 'recruitment' and 'shiny armor for everyone' if you helped.”

"I’m glad it worked," Jin said, unable to keep the smile out of his voice. He had done the very same thing to the twins just a couple of hours earlier.

"Yup!" Maya grinned. "And it worked. Look—"

She stood and stepped back, showing off the armor she was wearing.

It was one of the sets Jin remembered he and his party had raided from cult outposts weeks ago—light plate over reinforced leather, enchanted for basic protection and durability—It fit her reasonably well, if a bit loose in the shoulders.

"Not exactly custom-fitted, but it beats the cloth robes I was wearing before." Maya did a little spin, and the armor clinked softly. "Brocki and Flocki have been handing these out like candy. Where'd you even get this many?"

"Well," Jin shrugged. "Some poor souls had too many, and we thought if they lost some, it wouldn’t hurt them.”

"I like it." She said.

Someone called Maya's name from beyond the curtain—a man's voice, urgent but not panicked—and she sighed, exhaustion bleeding back into her features.

"Duty calls. Try not to do something stupid while I'm gone, yeah? I just patched you up."

She turned to go, and Jin caught the tail end of her muttered curse: "—I swear, if one more person complains about the rations I'm going to—"

The curtain swished closed behind her, leaving Jin alone with his thoughts for the first time since waking.

Delegate your tasks, Jin thought, remembering one of Joe's favorite lessons. You can't do everything yourself. Build a team. Trust them to handle what they're good at.

Good advice. Even if it felt weird to watch other people organize while he sat here wrapped in bandages like a mummy.

Narrator? Are Comms back up?

« Not yet. The interference is only growing stronger. Someone's actively working to maintain the disruption… likely our mystery mind mage. »

Jin frowned. That's not good. We need to get rid of him fast. Apart from “Trish,” that person is likely the only high ranker the cult had invested in here.

« It is indeed not good.»

« Jin, there are some very important things you need to see. As soon as possible. »

Hmm? What kind of import—

"BOSSMAN!"

The curtain rippled violently as Brocki and Flocki—Jin called it—ducked through, both looking worse for wear but alive and grinning like idiots.

Brocki had fresh bandages on his shoulder where one of Orodain's cultists had stabbed him. The wrappings were clean, but the way he favored the other side suggested it still hurt like hell. Flocki sported a spectacular black eye, various cuts across his face, and what looked like claw marks down his left arm.

But they were grinning.

"Yer one crazy sonvabitch, you know that?" Flocki's voice carried genuine admiration mixed with disbelief. "What do you mean you just became an Overmortal ranker and beat the shit out of that cultist? If all it takes is getting angry enough to ascend mid-fight, I'll take—OW!"

Brocki's hand came down on his brother's head with a solid thwack.

"Don't," Brocki growled. "Don't”

"I was just saying—"

"You were being an idiot."

"Takes one to know one—"

"Boys." Jin's voice cut through the brewing argument, and both twins immediately snapped to attention. "You did a good job. Both of you."

He meant it.

The temporary base, the organized survivors, even the armor distribution, all of it showed the initiative and competence he hadn't expected from two guards he'd only known for a few hours.

"Aye," Flocki said, straightening with visible pride. "We did what we could, boss. Your fight with that cultist went on for over an hour. We stuck around for the first few minutes after you gave the order, but..."

“An hour!” Jin's eyes widened.

"But it was pretty clear you had it handled," Brocki finished. "Or at least, clear you didn't need us getting in the way. So we followed orders and started gathering people."

"We were ready to haul your ass out of there if things went south, though," Flocki added quickly. "Had a whole extraction plan and everything."

"We weren't supposed to talk about that!" Brocki elbowed his brother. "That was classified!"

"Oh, classified. Listen to you with the big words."

"I know big words! Do you know of lots of big words?"

“Of course, I do.”

"Name three."

"Uh... classified. And... tactical. And..."

"Incompetent?"

"Hey!"

"Boys." Jin raised his voice just slightly, and they both shut up. "I appreciate the concern. And the extraction plan. But right now I need you to tell me exactly what happened while I was out. Slowly. One at a time."

The twins exchanged a look, and Flocki nodded to his brother.

"Right," Brocki said, settling into a more serious tone. "So after you gave the order, we started sweeping floors looking for anyone with normal eyes. People were... well, they were terrified. Some hiding in vaults, some barricaded in offices. But once they saw our gear, which was better than the guards', and our eyes were normal, and realized we were organized, most were willing to listen."

"The armor helped," Flocki added. "People trust you more when you look like you know what you're doing. Plus, promising safety in numbers and actual equipment? That sealed the deal for most."

"How many?" Jin asked.

"About forty people now," Brocki said. "Mix of guards, support staff, even some civilians who knew how to fight. We've got them organized into three groups—combat, support, and scouts."

"That's..." Jin paused, genuinely impressed. "That's smart thinking. Good organization."

Brocki's expression darkened slightly. "Not everyone was exactly... voluntary about joining, though."

Jin sighed. "And?"

"Some of the civilians just wanted to hide," Flocki said. "They wanted to barricade themselves in a room and wait for official rescue. But with cultists still active, with that mind mage somewhere in the building..."

"We made it clear that hiding wasn't an option," Brocki finished. "Told them they could contribute to defense or get left behind when we moved. Most saw sense after that."

"Wow, you guys pulled the exact move I did on you," Jin said. “So what happened to those who didn’t agree even after the bait?”

"We, uh..." Flocki rubbed the back of his neck. "We sort of knocked them out, tied them up, and gently deposited them in one of the holding cells. For their own safety."

"Gently deposited," Brocki emphasized. "We were very gentle about it."

Jin stared at them. They stared back, completely unrepentant.

"You did what?" Jin said slowly.

"We rescued them from their own poor decision-making," Flocki corrected. "There's a difference."

"Is there?"

"Absolutely. One sounds way better than the other."

Jin felt a laugh bubble up. It came out half-wheeze, half-chuckle, but genuine.

"You're both insane," he said.

"Thank you!" Flocki beamed. "We try."

"That wasn't a compliment."

"We're choosing to take it as one anyway."

Brocki rolled his eyes at his brother, then turned back to Jin with a more serious expression. "Boss, you okay? You're looking pretty pale. Paler than usual, I mean."

Jin's head throbbed in time with his heartbeat, and his newly enhanced stats were no doubt working overtime to repair the damage.

But without more potions—and Maya had been very clear about not taking more potions—this was going to take time. Maybe a quick harvest would flood him with the required vitality?

« JIN! »

"I'm fine," Jin started, then caught himself. "No. Actually, I'm not fine. Obviously. I just... need a minute. To think."

The twins nodded, understanding or at least pretending to.

"We'll be outside," Brocki said. "Organizing the next sweep. The levels are way too large, and we've only covered maybe a third of them so far."

"Call if you need us," Flocki added. "For anything. Even if it's just to complain about your brothers-from-another-mother being too handsome and competent."

"Get lost!" Jin said, but he was smiling.

They ducked back through the curtain, still bickering about something, and Jin was left alone again.

Well. Not quite alone.

Maya slipped back in a moment later, crouching near a set of drawers to Jin's left.

"They're good men," she said quietly, organizing and taking the required medical vials. "But desperate times make people do desperate things. Don't judge them too harshly for the kidnapping."

"I don't," Jin said. "They did what they thought was right. That's... more than most people would do."

"Yeah." Maya glanced at him, and something complicated flickered across her face. "You're different from what I expected. When they first mentioned you, I thought you'd be more... I don't know. Arrogant? Most geniuses are."

“Geniuses,” Jin laughed and shook his head. "I'm too tired to be arrogant right now or ever."

"Fair point." She finished organizing and stood. "You need anything? Water? Food that isn't ration bars?"

"Just some time to myself," Jin said. Then, seeing her expression shift toward concern, "Not like that. I just need to think. Sort through some things."

Maya studied him for a long moment, and Jin had the uncomfortable feeling she was reading far more than he wanted to show.

"Alright," she finally said. "But if you start spiraling again, I'm coming back in here to smack you. Fair warning."

"Noted."

She turned to leave, then paused at the curtain. "Hey, Jin?"

"Yeah?"

"Do you... Need the bathroom? I've heard that's where men—"

Maya shrugged, completely unrepentant. "Just figured I'd offer. You know. In case you wanted some privacy for your emotional crisis."

"I'm fine."

"Sure you are." She grinned. "Everyone who's 'fine' looks like death warmed over and bleeds through their bandages."

"Get out."

Finally. Finally alone.

Narrator. What was the important thing you needed to show me?

SYSTEM INITIATING...

TRIALS OF THE ETERNAL ONE: 1ST TRIAL CLEARED

Narrator... the system?

« Yes and no. »

«From what I've been able to gather, whatever boon 'The Eternal One' gave you had certain... hidden conditions attached. Trials, apparently.»

And I just cleared one. Jin's mind raced through possibilities. Killing a higher-ranked enemy? No... probably reaching Order III. Breaking through to Overmortal rank.

What does this change? What can the system actually do?

You are getting petty.

When did that happen?

The fight with Orodain and that moment of terrible clarity when he'd realized he'd killed... and the world draining to grey.

So I formed the final star of the cultivation path?

Why does it say I'm Human with question marks?

What does your presence actually mean? System? Is that what you are, and what can you do?

Zenith Ascendancy Continuum.

Nothing. Not even a whisper.

Yeah. And those memories are reliable because they were my escape.

If something called the Zenith Ascendancy Continuum existed in this world's lore, I would have noticed it.
