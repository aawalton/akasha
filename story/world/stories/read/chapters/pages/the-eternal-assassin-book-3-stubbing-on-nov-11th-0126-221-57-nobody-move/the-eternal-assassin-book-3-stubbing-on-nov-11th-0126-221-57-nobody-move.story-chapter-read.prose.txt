With an eerily fuzzy egg in his Personal Rift and a mildly annoyed deer-like creature glaring at him as it continued eating flowers without a care in the world, Asher sat within his field of scattered shards. At first, he’d felt a little bad about stealing an egg from such a docile, harmless creature.

Right up until he looked back at the pile of eggs and realized the missing egg had magically been replaced somehow.

“Stupid wild realm,” he muttered, shaking his head as he decided it wasn’t worth thinking about. He hadn’t exactly been keeping track of time while running for his life from one event to the next, but if he had to guess, he’d probably only been in the wild realm for about ten minutes or so. That meant he still had the majority of an hour to kill.

And that was assuming Richard actually picked him up when he said he would.

Resigning himself to the long wait, Asher sat there, fidgeting with one of the many shards he’d tossed out to bring a small slice of order to the world of sheer chaos. He’d only ended up throwing out about a hundred shards, but those hundred shards were proving to be one of the best investments he’d ever made. Even now, he could imagine the insanity of the wild realm shifting around him, transforming from one wild thing to the next with seemingly no pattern.

The world on the edge of his field of shards slowly morphed and changed around him, and he watched curiously all the minute changes one after another. The flowers that the fuzzy-deer were eating gradually became thicker, turning more into tiny shrubs than anything someone would ever call a flower as they pulled their petals inward. In response, the deer grew longer legs, their hooves curving a bit to allow them to better dig out the shrubs and eat the roots buried underground. Asher started as he realized the ecology of the wild realm was literally evolving right before his eyes, both the plants and the animals in response to what the other was doing.

Even more curious was how the land at the edge of his field of shards that his awareness covered wasn’t changing in the slightest. One of the newly altered deer took a tentative step into his tiny flower field, staring at the unchanging flowers with what Asher could only think of was a look of confusion on its face. It briefly poked at a flower with its rounded hooves, bleating sadly before turning and leaving his field behind once more.

Asher couldn’t help himself. Getting up, he walked over to the abrupt change in terrain. Crouching down, with one foot on the flower field that remained static in space and one foot on the green field of tiny shrubs that had once been flowers, he hummed, his eyes bouncing back and forth between the different zones. Wanting to test a theory, he manifested another few shards in his hand, scattering them around him and ensuring a chunk of the shrub-infested land was now covered as well. As he did so, he tried to feel what was happening, even going so far as to activate Spatial Lock around himself with absolutely no restrictions to the barrier for the first time. He wanted the wild realm to do its thing.

The shifting of the wild realm was hard to predict, because it never actually happened to the space he was immediately surrounded by. His hope was that by utilizing both Shard Detection and his Spatial Lock, he might be able to figure out if the wild realm had any sort of tell.

Asher now had three different zones encompassed within his Spatial Lock. The field of flowers he’d originally landed in, which was covered in shards and completely static. Then he had a chunk of land covered in shrubs, now also covered in shards. It had been changed quite a lot, but it wouldn’t be changing any more. And lastly, he had another chunk of land covered in shrubs, this one not covered in shards and at the complete whim of the wild realm. He was only able to fit a tiny sliver of each section within his Spatial Lock, but that was fine.

It proved to be plenty for his purposes.

Sitting down in the center, Asher closed his eyes despite the danger and focused on the land within his Spatial Lock. After a moment, he even pulled one of Moxy’s potions of Sensitivity out of his rift and chugged it, silently promising to buy the warrior a new one later. Moxy had been intending to use them to better feel the light touches of air against her skin.

Asher wanted to better feel the subtle changes of the fabric of reality all around him.

Upon drinking the potion, he shivered as what felt like the finest, softest curtain in the world was draped over his body. In theory, seeing as he was in the Stage of Honing, Asher’s Spatial element should have begun having some subtle influences on his body at this point. He had a few ideas as to what those might have been, but he was hoping that would also mean letting him better feel the changes in the wild realm around him.

At first, he didn’t really feel anything. Deciding to go all in on his test, he kept his eyes closed and released his Spatial Lock all together.

The moment he did so, he gasped.

He could feel the fabric of reality draped over him begin churning and shifting in the direction of the shard-free area to his side. Without his Shard Detection skill keeping tabs on it, or his Spatial Lock laying claim to it, the realm was free to once again move about and alter itself however it saw fit. Asher focused on the shifting of the realm before him, entering almost something of a meditative state as he watched the changes of reality with his mind's eye. He couldn’t actually see what the wild realm was doing, but he could directly compare it to the other half of reality that was stationary and holding firm, kept still and unmoving thanks to his presence. Seeing the difference between the two halves of the world around him: one frozen in place and content as it was, the other constantly moving, always looking for something new, was jarring. In fact, he wasn’t really a fan of the churning of reality at all. It would be a lot more convenient if the world around him were more…

Permanent.

For the first time, Asher felt a strange tugging sensation in his core, almost like something was trying to be pulled out of him. It didn’t feel bad, so Asher let it happen, gasping as a hundred thousand shards were drawn from him in an instant. The moment they were, the shifting fabric of reality dancing on his skin froze, matching the other half that represented the zone he’d covered in shards and was keeping an eye on with his skill. Barely believing what had just happened, Asher opened his eyes and stared in shock at both the world, and the notification before him.

Permanence (passive/active) - Lvl 23

Tier 1: Lock a status effect in place for as long as you will it

Tier 2: Bring permanence to the world around you

Shards: 13,020,732

What in the… Asher stared at the notifications, unable to believe his eyes. Somehow, Permanence had leveled up and evolved without asking him if he wanted to spend the one hundred thousand shards like every other skill, simply pulling them from his core to fuel the evolution. And if that wasn’t crazy enough, after the evolution and being placed at level 21, it had then leveled up two more times, all on its own. He could only assume it was some unique quirk of being in an entirely different realm, one not only completely cut off from the material realm, but also regarded as less ‘stable’ than the material realm. He still didn’t quite understand the full implications of that, but one thing was for certain.

The system in charge of elements and skills didn’t work in quite the same way here as it did back on the material realm.

Putting that matter aside for the moment, Asher looked around, grinning at what he found. It seemed his newly evolved skill did exactly what it advertised. His presence appeared to completely lock down the current state of the wild realm, preventing his surroundings from shifting or changing on a whim. Even the area directly behind him was finally stable, preventing him from needing to constantly spin around to keep things like cliffs or monsters from spawning where he couldn’t see them.

While his new skill was exactly what he needed, it wasn’t as if his presence was nullifying the wild realm completely. It was a whole realm of existence, after all, and he was just one person. As far as he could tell, his influence on the ever-shifting realm only seemed to stretch out about a quarter-mile in any direction. That was still an incredibly large space for a passive skill to have influence over, but it meant the distant landscape was still constantly being replaced or evolving before his very eyes.

I wonder how this second-tier effect is going to function back on the material realm… Asher frowned as the thought crossed his mind, scratching his head at the realization he may have just shot himself in the foot. Did evolving a skill in a different realm of existence make that skill completely worthless back in the material realm? Nowhere in any of the books he read could he remember any realm wanderers discussing having evolved their skills mid-expedition.

Still, that was a problem for later. Now that the world was static around him once more, he could simply wait for Richard to bring him home. Gathering up the shards he’d scattered about and petting the fluffy deer as they bleated at him, he pulled out the artifact Richard had given him and pressed it.

Then he pressed it three more times, just to make sure the point got across.

I wish I knew Morse code so I could shout a few threats through this thing… Not like Richard would understand anyway.

Making a mental note to commission a pack of cards from someone to keep in his rift so that he could at least play Solitaire in the event something like this ever happened a second time, Asher decided to kill time by seeing what Samantha was up to. She had already given him permission to use his scrying ability on her pretty much whenever he wanted. Though even with her blessing, he didn’t use Eternal Mark’s second-tier effect very often, as it still felt like a massive invasion of privacy. But seeing as he didn’t really have anything better to do, he closed his eyes and focused on her mark.

Asher frowned as, rather than have his mind zoom over to his mark like he was used to, the skill felt slow and sluggish. Just like when he’d tried to use Recall earlier, the connection was noticeably different when trying to use the skill to reach back to the material realm from his current location. However, unlike with Recall, he wasn’t trying to teleport his entire body from one realm to another, and Eternal Mark had already been evolved to the second tier. Moving just his awareness between realms proved to be a more easily accomplished task than his full physical form. As he strained against the invisible barrier, it wasn’t long before he felt something finally give, and his mind tumbled forward.

Almost without warning, his mind was projected all the way back to the material realm, somehow being protected by his own tritessence as it passed through the void and reached his intended target. Despite being separated from his body, he somehow knew that his physical form was panting right now from the effort it took getting his skill to activate. Mentally wiping his brow, he peered at Samantha.

The hunter was busy practicing with her bow, firing arrow after arrow at presumably a target off in the distance. Asher was only able to see a small distance around his mark, so while he could make out the grass under her feet and a bee buzzing a couple of feet to her left, he couldn’t see much more than that.

Smiling at the look of concentration on her face, he watched her for a couple of minutes before deciding that was probably long enough. Going to end his skill, he paused as he realized something strange.

The heavy, difficult to pass through connection he’d struggled to force his way through in order to view Samantha from an entire realm away, suddenly felt a bit less restrictive. He wasn’t sure if having his physical body on one realm and his mind on another had stretched it out, or if he had improved some sort of invisible muscle needed to make the jump, but either way, this discovery had one exciting application. If he could make the transition easier just from doing it once…

What would happen if he kept practicing?
