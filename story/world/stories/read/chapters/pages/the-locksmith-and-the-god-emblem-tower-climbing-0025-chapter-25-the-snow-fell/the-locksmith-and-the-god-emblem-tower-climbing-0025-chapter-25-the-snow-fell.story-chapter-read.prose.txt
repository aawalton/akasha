Harry's heart pounded. Pulsating, it dictated his steps and prevented him from keeping a clear head. Since the day he left the library and never looked back, a plethora of nightmares and inner demons infested his sleep. He would wake up sweating. But since entering the Tower, none of that seemed to happen. Of course, he only had about two nights, and one of them was forced upon him. Either way, the nightmares didn't do it.

The Locksmith walked through the stone corridors amidst the dark, seeing some lights being produced by a few Metas as they broke the penumbra. He didn't know the reason for the light, but didn't question it either. They looked like fireflies.

Getting near one of the entrances, the sound of rattling and crashing became evident. The rocks... Harry then looked at the outside, feeling only the cold of the continuous blizzard. The young thief thought of putting his left hand outside to verify the truth of the danger that had announced itself but quickly shook the idea off. Missing one hand was rough enough.

He closed his eyes and embraced the moment for a second; strangely, it was comfortable. Even more so than when he slept.

Crack! The Locksmith quickly turned around, ready to use his doors. It was Prism, who just stepped in a piece of rubble.

"...Harry." Prism began.

"Don't fucking call me that." He distanced himself from the blizzard, determined she wanted to kill him. "What are you doing here?"

"I... I don't know." She blurted out as she fidgeted with her hands. "I just... I guess I wanted to see how you were doing. Is it all that bad?"

"What?" Harry closed his eyes for a second. "Prism, you—"

"Call me Estela."

"...Estela, you robbed me. You took my journal for whatever reason and left us to die in the hands of Dracno. Why?"

"They made me do it, okay?" She said, each word an enigma to Harry's mind. "The masked guy told me to get it from you. He never said why, but he told me he'd help me and the group get to the last floor."

They? Who's they? The young thief added inwardly. Masked? He talked to me in the first floor...

"By the group," He began. "You don't mean our group, right?"

"Yeah, right." She simply replied.

"In what world could I ever trust you again?" Harry said. "You are sick, Estela. You are sick and you know it. You're a sick individual."

"Oh, so it's alright for you to rob others, but when it happens to you then it's a cardinal sin!"

"Fuck off!" He took a step forward as he pointed to her. "You don't know the first thing about me."

"I know you're still a needy child who shut down to the world because it hurt you." She raised her hands and looked around. "But guess what? We're all hurt, Harry. In the end, we're all equal not because of our virtues or our moral sense, declaring beauty to the world, but in our mortality. Pain makes us all equal. You're not the only one who's suffered. I—"

"You what? Tell me. Tell the world what hurt you so badly that you left us to rot in the hands of a tyrant while you were robbing and tripping us over—"

"I lost the same thing you did." She declared, making Harry take a step back in confusion. "What? You think I can't see it? We share the same loss, pretty boy. But instead of you, I'm not sacrificing myself for a bunch of strangers. I don't yearn for companionship or connection. You know why? Because I have a fucking job to do. I have real people who depend on me."

"And you think that makes you a better person?" The Locksmith inquired. "When you turned into a survivor, the rest of us became tools, right? We both know that's not how it works. Civilization evolved because of trust, not the lack of it. Estela, they are all real humans. This isn't an experiment. Just because we're in a cage, doesn't mean it isn't our cage. We can still care about each other—"

"Care about each other!?" She guffawed. "What the fuck, Harry? Where is that coming from? When we first met, you put a dagger in my throat! It was actually kinda hot..."

The young thief ignored the last comment.

"We change, Prism." He added after a moment as he noticed a faint light in the distance. "We adapt."

"Yeah, right..."

"That's why you're here, isn't it?" Harry felt her entire stance tremble.

"No. I... lied."

This time, Harry chuckled. "Of course you did. Come on, Prism. You can't lie to a liar."

"I can do whatever I want." Prism then looked back, fearing someone could pass by.

"Relax, no one can hear you; the rocks are muffling the sound." The young thief then held his crutch tightly as he remembered the state in which he was. If Prism wanted him dead, he wouldn't be able to do anything against it. The fact that he wasn't told him a different story, though.

"...You stole my journal while we were all fighting against Dracno?" The Locksmith asked without looking back. "That was smart."

"Stop being a sycophant; it doesn't suit you." Prism replied. After a few moments, she added. "What is in that journal anyway? Why would that... thing want it?"

"The masked fucker?" Harry's comment brought a smile in Prism's face that quickly faded. "I don't know. It's nothing important, speaking in utilitarian terms. Plenty of sketches, a few diary pages and another round of sketches. A lot of them."

"Sketches of what?"

"None of your business." Harry replied sharply. "Wait, you didn't even see them?"

"I don't question the job, Locksmith." She said with a bit of proud. "It's one of the perks of working with me."

"I have yet to see the others, then." The young thief said. "I assume discretion is guaranteed?"

"You assume right."

They then watched as the blizzard took over the mountain. Glimpses of black stains contaminated the monochromatic snow storm, some big, others not so much. Apparently, they only fell from the sky in a straight direction. If it were the product of an Emblem user, which Harry doubted, it wouldn't be so random. But again, it was unlikely.

"The last floor, huh?" Harry inquired once again. "What's that like?"

"...Are you stupid?" She replied. "If I'm here, it means I'm not there, which means I don't know!"

"I assumed the mysterious figure told you about it. Or perhaps informed your group. Since you already gave the journal, he would have no reason to maintain his words. Your team is either dead or on the last floor, ready to get out of the tower by any means necessary."

"They are..." She took a while to continue. "They are waiting for me on the next floor. I know it."

"So you trust them." Harry glanced at her with a smirk.

I do. That's what Prism wanted to answer, only to wash away that ridiculous grin out of the young thief's face. She found it hard to do so, though. Perhaps because it wasn't any of his business, or maybe because she knew that it would be a lie.

"I took long enough already..." Filled with an unending fury, Prism took a dagger out of her coat as she took a step forward, ready to slice Harry.

"Mr. Locksmith?" Vela's voice came from behind them. Before the monk could spot Prism, she went invisible and hid in one of the cave's many hidden tunnels. "What are you doing here?"

Harry closed his eyes and sighed to himself. His hands were shaking.

"Prism was here." He told the man. "She was ready to kill me. I could feel it."

"...And you didn't do anything to stop it?" Vela didn't seem so alarmed, but he was also a man known for hiding the anxiety from his voice.

"What could I do?" He turned around, holding his crutch. "I either dodge but get thrown out there. I was confident in the beginning, but... Anyway, thank you, Vela. You saved me. Don't worry, I think she's scared of the group, especially Daniel and Goldman. I shouldn't have wandered so much, though."

"...You shouldn't." Vela simply replied as he stood beside the man. "But you did. And you know not to do it again. Life is about learning but also accepting, don't you think?"

Harry closed his eyes again for a moment.

"Yes."

They watched the snow fall.

Morning dawned again, the traces of the storm quickly fading. Goldman checked the cave's surroundings for discrepancies from its original state but found nothing. He ensured everyone was safe and went to speak with Petrikov.

Brun kept looking at a white piece of paper, mumbling to herself. On the other side, Daniel taught an impatient Manes about the art of sculpting. When did these two became friends? Harry thought to himself as he then saw Madeline playing with her brush.

"Hey!" He yelled in her ear, causing the young woman to ruin a brushstroke. "How are you doing? Sleep well?"

"God, Harry..." She chuckled. "No, I didn't. I don't think anyone slept well enough. Doesn't matter anyway, the loop restarts soon, doesn't it?"

"I believe so." Harry then paused as he glanced outside. "I have a weird feeling about tomorrow. I mean, today. I mean, on the next loop... You know what I mean."

"What weird feeling?" She asked, curious.

"More of a presage. It's just... I'm glad things turned out fine until now, but this omen keeps telling me it'll change. Maybe nothing will happen, but just so you know it."

"Oh, I see." Madeline grinned. "So you're warning me against a feeling you had about how things might happen. Which, in turn, has no substantial evidence for it."

"Fuck off..." The Locksmith grinned in response as he looked away. "Just be careful, okay?"

"I hear you." She replied with a more serious tone. "You... be careful as well."

"Me? Oh, no. I'll be just fine. I'm just warning you because of your lack of skill in many areas, such as—"

"What skills do you have?" She inquired, still maintaining a grin. "God knows how many times I've seen you tumble on your own doors. Remember that time on Berbil Street? You kept falling for minutes until you regained your senses and stood still—"

"I was drunk!" He pointed a finger at her. "Which... now speaking, isn't much of a good answer. It is the explanation, though."

"Alright, guys!" Goldman announced. "Use your C-Coins again and be ready. We'll begin the climb as soon as the loop restarts, okay? Remember the formation: doubles only..."

He continued to inform them about the plan as the climb would soon begin.

It was on.
