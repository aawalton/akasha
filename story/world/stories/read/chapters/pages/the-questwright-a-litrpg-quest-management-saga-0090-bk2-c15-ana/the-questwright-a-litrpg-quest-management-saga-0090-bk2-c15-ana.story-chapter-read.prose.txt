Only ten minutes after putting the finishing touches on the third ring’s Field Standards, Ana visited with his dinner. For a brief moment, he’d been so wrapped up in his work that he’d forgotten not only that she was dropping by, but that he’d developed a serious appetite.

“Oh, thank god,” he said as she set down a black bag while picking up the one from the afternoon. “It feels like my stomach is eating itself, and all I’ve done is sit here!”

“A QuestWright’s ways are mysterious, sir,” Ana said demurely. Then, she began to scoot toward the door with speed.

“Hey, where’re you going in such a hurry?” When she turned back with an anxious look, he put his hands up, “Not that I’m trying to tell you what you can or cannot do. I’m just curious what your plans are for the night.”

“Sir, I plan on retiring early to my room.”

“Ah! Was it a stressful day? You can tell me about it.” Cass looked around the empty room, “There aren’t many people around to hear it other than me. Come on, spill.”

She cast her eyes down, “If that is what you wish…”

Something’s off…

Rather than let it open naturally, Cass flipped the top off the bottle in a rush, “What’s going on? You’ve been acting weird since lunch. Is it me? Did I do something wrong?”

“No, it’s not that.”

“Then? I promise I won’t be mad.” Cass laughed, “Sometimes, I start to believe what my sister says about me.” He bunched up his face in a not-at-all accurate depiction of Janine, “Cass, you’ll never understand women. You’re a simple boy and shouldn’t try to push beyond your means. But then I remember that she’s a pig, and everything is okay.”

Ana’s face hardened as she seemed to come to a decision, “It’s the gift, sir, the one you spoke of earlier.”

She paused and looked around the room, confirming again that they were alone. Stepping closer, she whispered, “Every QuestWright is different, Cass. Some are like what you seem to be. A nice kid given a light charge, and doesn’t quite know the power they hold. Others…others have a less positive view of the servant Calling.”

She looked him in the eyes before looking away quickly, “The worst are those who seem like the nice kid, only, something darker is hiding beneath.”

“What are you talking about?”

Leaning forward, she continued speaking in a low voice, “There are stories that get around. Servants being asked to do things that no sane person would request, or seeing things that they know they need to keep quiet about.”

“What kinds of things?” Cass asked, speaking in the same tone. “And why are you whispering?”

“Some walls can hear, Cass. If you keep that in mind and never say anything you wouldn’t want to be used against you, you’ll be fine.”

Standing up, she looked at him with a wry smile. “I’ve only known you for two days, so this may be hubristic, but I’m gonna trust you. You seem like one of the good ones. Okay. What’s this gift you mentioned?”

This time, Cass was the one to look around. “You said some walls have ears?”

“Can hear,” She corrected.

“Well, any other senses I need to be worried about? Like, can anyone see in here?”

Ana shrugged, “Not that I know of. It doesn’t mean that it’s not possible. We only know for certain that there are Callings that enhance hearing.”

Like mine, Cass thought to himself. But I don’t think I can hear through walls. Must be a specialty.

Cass dove into his screens as he looked at the wall. If she was gonna trust him, and that seemed to be a big stretch for her in a relatively short amount of time, then he’d return that in kind. Mr. Moore and even Kara would probably tell him that he was being naive, but he didn’t mind that. Sometimes you had to roll the dice.

After all, in the history books he’d read back in Liora, it was those with good intentions and a heavy dose of bravery who’d changed the world.

Making sure everything was set after turning on Investigation but leaving Message off, Cass set the outline and looked at her, “How much experience do you generally get per day?”

“Oh, is that all it is? A Quest?” She smiled, “Well, that’s certainly welcome. Servant Callings have a daily, though five experience never goes very far."

Cass nodded, knowing now that this was the right move, "I’m going to show you something right now, and I really need you not to freak out. Can you do that?”

She took two steps back with a guarded expression, “Was I wrong about you?”

Tilting his head at her, he said only one word, “No.”

Cass caught the Quill as it entered the world.

Just as on the first day he’d unlocked it, a sense of awe pervaded him as it further lit up the already bright room. The feel of it was the same as always. A lighter-than-air object that seemed ghostly incandescent and impossible to hold. And yet, he held it in the palm of his hand.

It came from a desperate choice, forced upon him to see his priorities take action upon the world. It was his, uniquely as far as he knew, and would help him bring hope to those who had been forgotten. Cass was leaning quite heavily on the Quill to help him far into the future.

Ana, on the other hand, did not seem to share his feelings.

“What is that! Where did it come from!” She took two steps back and toward the door.

“Wait, please, please don’t open it.” Cass pleaded, “This is a secret, and one I don’t want the rest of the world to learn. If you open that door and people look in, they’ll start asking questions, which will ruin a few of my plans.”

“What plans?”

He smiled, “One secret at a time, Ana. Now, as for the gift?”

Focusing, Cass was forced to attempt what he’d failed at the night before. Using the Quill, just like when drafting a Quest normally, required him to control his emotions. But as the tutorial had shown, and unlike the simple act of drafting, the Quill offered more options.

Gotta be careful about this. Ana’s already scared. If a hodgepodge of golden writing begins spilling out of the Quill and rushes her, she’s probably going to bolt for the door without thinking. Honestly, I would do the same, especially when that inky blackness starts coming out of me.

I bet she’s thinking, “Here’s a weird kid. Shows up out of nowhere, says he’s from a different city, tells you to trust him, then starts pulling things from nowhere into his hands.” She’d be right to run; I’d do the same. So this needs to be done just right.

That meant he’d have to attempt to not only control his emotions enough to draft her an Exponential Quest that offered an explanation, but also make the Quill’s writing invisible. It was a task that was easier said than done.

Clearing his thoughts, Cass tried to split them. Holding two ideas in mind was difficult, as he’d learned in his failed attempt last evening. It reminded him of when he and Gary used to try patting their heads while rubbing their bellies, swapping hands and motions on demand. They’d laughed then, finding themselves unable to do so, but Cass wasn’t laughing now.

This was a skill that didn’t come from the System, but rather, from him. Something that needed to be developed over time. But a promise was a promise, and he always kept those.

His first train of thought cemented itself.

This is a Daily Quest. It will appear at the start of each morning, with increased experience for each completed. Don’t tell others. It’ll be different every day. It’s a challenge. Complete it to the best of your abilities.

It was the second thought that was hard.

Don’t show yourself. Hide away from the world. Be invisible! In-vis-i-bull!

Placing the Quill against the air as he focused his intent, he felt it cement itself against nothing at all, and suddenly, it was material. Taking a breath, Cass began to write. The two ideas in his mind bounced against one another as he fought to control his own mind. The moment he managed a solid hold on one, the other slipped away. It made him worry about what might happen if he ever focused purely on what he wanted the text to do, losing out on what it might be.

Expectedly, it came out golden.

Unexpectedly, Ana Didn't run. She stood there with wide eyes as the words floated mysteriously across the ceiling, “Wow. That’s so beautiful, Cass. Look at it. I never thought I'd see something like this. It’s like-”

Cass's shoulder's slumped as the writing finished. Then, the words hurled themselves at her chest. The older woman paused for less than the count of one as she screamed and ran for the door.

"Wait!" Cass yelled out, his hand already reaching out though it was a futile gesture.

Opening the door, she took one step into the main room of the Scriptorium just as the words combined, entering through her back. Standing just beyond her was…an empty room.

Turning around, she looked at Cass in confusion, "What's an Exponential Quest?"

Cass sighed, then gave her a smile.

Once the door was closed again and he explained everything to her, Ana's face lit up, "That's amazing! And it gives more experience each day?"

"That's what I think will happen. I've only had it for two days myself, so things are a bit up in the air at the moment. I knew it wasn't anything dangerous, and there wasn’t much else I could give you. So, this was the only thing that naturally came to mind."

"Well, young man." She laughed to herself, "You gave me quite the scare."

Cass had put away the Quill the moment she'd opened the door, but now that he was sure she wouldn't scare so easily, he pulled it out again. A flick of the wrist for show, and there it was.

"I think this was given to me to right some wrongs in the world, Ana."

"That's a pretty heavy duty to bear for someone your age." She commented, "When I was as old as you, most of my time was spent trying to find enough money to eke out the rent."

Cass looked at the Quill closely before letting it fade from the world, "What's that like? I kind of went from my parents straight to the Guild. My living situation has always been handled."

She slapped her thigh and laughed, "You’ve got a bit of a silver spoon situation. Let me tell you, this world is so different from the one I came from.”

Ana looked off in the distance, her eyes seeming to see even further, “This was a great country once, you know. Before everything changed and the three Guilds rose. We used to be the United States of America. A great country, though not one without some severe issues, both in the dark and the light. Now, it's the United States of Nothing and Nobody."

"What'd you do before?" Cass asked as he sat back down, ignoring the map as he gestured across from him.

Nodding, Ana sat down, her legs canted slightly to the side, "I was a waitress, but not always. Initially, I tried to go to college. Got through two semesters until they raised the tuition costs, and I couldn't get enough hours in at work. Had a husband too. Bob. He was a goodish man."

Speaking softly, Cass asked, "How'd he die?"

"When the Reshaping happened, we were both living in a small city at the top of O-K. That's Oklahoma. Boy, that name has no real meaning anymore. Sad.” She shook her head, “One day, I was heading into work, when out of nowhere a car goes tumbling past. I can still remember the people inside of it screaming to high heaven.”

She put her head down as a tremor ran through her, “I had an old beat-up junker I was driving that never was good for much. But when I saw that and put my foot to the pedal, she jumped faster than I ever thought possible. To the left and right, things just kept popping out. Even the sky wasn’t safe. Big goddamn bird-looking things, just, coming down as if it were the end of the world. Turns out, it was.”

Another shiver ran through her frame, “And the people! They didn’t know what to do. Pulling out guns and firing away, running. Screaming. The in-fighting and politicking of just trying to survive. We fought a lot, back then, but what really did us in were the monsters. There were simply too many of ‘em. It was terrifying.”

Cass didn’t touch her, knowing she was back at the beginning of the Reshaping in her mind. Instead, he asked, “What happened to your husband?”

“He died. And he wasn’t saving any school bus full of kids, let me tell you. I got back to our apartment and found pieces of Bob not a hundred feet from our door, a bag by his feet. I sometimes fool myself into thinking that he was packing for two, but I knew him better than that. I do think he loved me, but he was a panicker. People can be bastards when everything comes crashing down on their ears.”

“Isn’t that true of all of us?” Cass asked, tapping on the desk when she looked up. “I don’t know what you went through; how could I? You called me a kid, and you’re not far off.”

He sighed and leaned back a little in his chair, “This has always been my world. But I want to believe that, as you said, he was packing for two. We’ve gotta hold that within our souls, I think, even if there’s a wiggle that we’re wrong. Put it somewhere so deep inside that the darkness of the outside world can’t touch it. Live lives that matter, you know?”

She snorted, then put her elbows on the desk, resting her face in the palms of her hands. With one finger, she pointed at Cass’s bad eye, “There’s a story to go with that, I bet. Wanna tell me about it?”

“That’s another one of my secrets,” Cass said with a smile, “There’ll be a toll to pay.”

“Like what?”

“Like, how about next time, you bring dinner for two…Don’t give me that look, I’m not hitting on you.”
