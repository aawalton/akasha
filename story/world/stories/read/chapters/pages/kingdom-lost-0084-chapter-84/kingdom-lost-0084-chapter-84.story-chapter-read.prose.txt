CHAPTER 84

The main floor was quieter without the dead raiders in it.

Riley moved through the room slowly while her soldiers worked around her, lifting crates and rolling bolts of fabric toward the door. She had given the orders and now she let the work happen, she had learned to trust what her soldiers produced, but nothing replaced her own eyes.

The glass bottles were stacked in a wooden crate near the far wall, packed in straw, each one stoppered and intact, a welcome bonus. She lifted one and held it up. The glass was thick and well made, the kind that could hold pressure. She set it down carefully and looked at the clay pots beside them. They were sealed with wax that was dense. They varied in size from fist size to a small bucket. She picked one up and turned it in her hands and said nothing, but her mind was already somewhere else entirely, somewhere outside this room, and into the open ground where the Clawborn army was probably marching. She wondered what direction they were headed.

She quickly set it down and kept moving.

The fabric, the tunics, the plates, the candles, the empty ledger books, all of it was useful and none of it was what the others were excited about. She heard them talking as they worked and she did not interrupt it. In the past she would hear the talking as background noise almost inaudible. Why are they talking at all, she thought. The thought started to distract her from what was in front. She shook it off and went back to her mental checklist.

She was reaching past a box of candles when her fingers touched something that didn't belong. She pulled it free. The cover was dark leather, unmarked, the spine cracked from use. She opened it to a random page. The script was dense and small, written by someone who had a lot to say and cared about the space a page could hold. It was in English. That fact registered before the content did, and she stood there for a moment just taking that in.

Then she read the first full paragraph. It was a passage about the founding of the great houses, written not as legend but as record, in the flat declarative tone of someone who had been present or had spoken to people who were. Names she didn't recognise. Maybe the war she had only seen in fragments in her dreams. A kingdom that had stood where something else now stood.

She closed the book. She tucked it under her arm. This was something she would bring back herself.

***

The soldiers went down first.

Riley stood at the top of the stairs with Thorne and listened. Two sets of boots on the wooden steps, careful and slow. Then the creak of the bottom stair, then nothing but the faint movement of men working in a confined space below. She counted her breaths without meaning to.

"All clear," one of them called up.

She went down.

The basement stairs were narrow and the air at the bottom was cool, carrying the particular stillness of a space that seemed separate from the outside world. Riley came down last, after Thorne and two soldiers, and stopped at the base of the stairs to let her eyes adjust.

The torchlight found the dead raider first. He was on his back near the largest chest, both arms bent, his hands claw shaped, stuck in a coiled whip around his neck. Dark leather, long, its tail looped a number of times and drawn tight. The expression on his face told the rest of the story. Whatever had happened had not been quick enough to be merciful and had constricted him from using his voice.

Riley looked for a moment at the scene, then the chest the body laid next to, then Thorne.

Thorne was already looking at the gold.

The nearest chest sat open, its lid thrown back, and the torchlight lit the contents and reflected the whole corner of the basement with a warm glow. Gold coins and loose jewels in a shallow tray, the accumulated proceeds of a raiding operation that had been running for some time. Thorne crouched beside it with the expression of a man who had just remembered why winning mattered. He lifted a handful of coin and let it fall back through his fingers.

"Riley," he smiled.

"I see it," she said.

Riley walked toward a series of shelves and her eyes fixed on a coil of leather that only took shape once the torchlight hit it properly. She picked up one end and it unraveled. A belt. She looked down at the makeshift rope loop tied at her waist, then back at the belt. She tried it on and adjusted it until it sat right. There were a few loops, one on each side of her hips. She slid the hatchet in, then turned and caught Thorne's approving eyes.

She crossed to the table against another wall and sorted through the documents bundled there. Most of it was inventory. Ledgers, tallies, correspondence about shipments, routes and times. She set those aside, then her hand stopped.

The scroll was sealed with dark red wax, the impression in it clean and deliberate. She had heard enough from Zelgra to recognise that mark without being told what it was. Corvessa. She turned it over once, feeling the weight of what it might contain, and slid it inside her coat without breaking the seal. That was a conversation for later with Zelgra in the room.

She was turning back toward the shelves when one of the soldiers called out.

"Warden. This one's different."

The chest was against the back wall, partially obscured by an empty weapon rack. It was medium sized and iron bound. The lock heavy in a different way than the other chests. Riley crossed to it and crouched in front of it. The lock was cold under her fingers. She gave it a pull, and the lock held firm.

The soldier raised his sword.

"Stop," Riley said.

He froze in place; his eyes trained on Riley.

She looked at the chest for a moment longer. Something about it sat differently than the other chests down here. The other chests had been accessible, protected by traps but not by locks or just left open.

"Thorne." She waited until he looked up from the gold. "You take charge of this one, you're responsible for it."

Thorne crossed the basement and looked down at the chest. Something shifted in his face, not dramatically, just a small change, the difference between looking at an object and recognising one. He picked it up and gave a nod to Riley.

"Someone remove that whip," Riley said, gesturing toward the dead raider without looking at him directly.

The nearest soldier crouched beside the body and took hold of the whip's coil. He pulled. Nothing happened. He repositioned his grip and pulled harder, putting his weight into it. The whip did not move. He looked up at Riley with an expression that had no good explanation in it.

Thorne handed the chest to the second soldier, removed his gloves and crouched. He worked his fingers under the coil of the whip and pulled with both hands. His knuckles whitened. The leather did not shift.

He stood up. "It's on there like steel."

Riley looked at the whip for a moment. Then she looked around the basement, at the shelves, the cleared chests, the documents she had already collected. "Leave it. Let's get everything upstairs."

The soldiers filed up the stairs. Thorne went last with the chest, ducking his head at the low beam. Riley stood at the base of the stairs and did a final sweep of the room with the torchlight, running the beam along the walls, checking the joins between the stones for anything that didn't sit flush. Nothing. She took one last look at the shelves.

"That's all I guess," she said, to no one.

She turned around.

The dead raider was still on the floor, the whip coiled around his neck, and she stood there for a moment looking at him. She walked over and crouched beside him and took hold of the whip.

It came away in her hand like it had never been attached to anything at all.

She stayed crouched for a moment, the whip loose in her fingers, the leather warm in a way the basement air could not justify. At the very edge of her vision the HUD pulsed once, softly, no text, no notification, and then it was still again.

She stood and coiled the whip, then fastened it to her hip and went upstairs.

Thorne looked at her hip, then her face and said nothing.

***

Rayan was waiting near the gate, doing his best to look like part of the loading operation while contributing nothing to it.

Riley stopped in front of him. "Go," she said.

His face opened with relief. "Thank you. I..." He looked around at the tree line, at the road beyond the gate, at the general vastness of the world outside the compound walls. "It's just, I don't have a horse. And the forest..."

"Kill him," Riley said.

The nearest soldier moved. Rayan was through the gate before the man had closed half the distance, his feet finding a speed that suggested he had been calculating the exit the entire time he was standing there performing gratitude. In a moment he was gone. Riley raised one finger still focused on the HUD and the soldier stopped short of the gate.

Thorne opened his mouth.

"Load the rest," Riley said. "Burn everything, even the raider bodies. We leave as soon as the horses are ready."

He closed his mouth. He went back to work.

***

The fire finished the watchtower first, then spread to the main building with the unhurried confidence of something that had been waiting to finish the job. Riley stood at the edge of the yard and watched it go, the history book under one arm, the sealed scroll loaded in her saddlebag, the whip on her hip. The warmth reached her face from across the yard. She took it in.

She was not thinking about the gold.

She was thinking about clay pots and glass bottles and the Clawborn army. She was thinking about what the gap was between what she had and what was coming and whether anything she had found today closed it by enough to matter. She was thinking about the soldiers moving crates across the yard and whether they felt the heat on their faces the way she felt it and whether that question was one a warden was supposed to ask.

She kept thinking it anyway.

The horses arrived while the main building was still standing. They came through the gate in a loose column, the soldiers who had escorted the dead back riding easy now, ready for the return load. The loading moved quickly and Riley watched it from horseback without moving.

Thorne was near the gate with the locked chest set down beside him when one of the returning soldiers stopped to speak to him. She was not close enough to hear it. She watched Thorne's face instead. The brightness that had been on him since the basement, the gold, the victory, was gone. She watched him ask something and the soldier answer. She strained without moving and the distance gave her two words and nothing else.

Collapsed. Not awake.

Thorne picked up the chest and walked toward her. She watched him come the whole way across the yard, past the loading soldiers, past the fire, through the smoke, until he stopped in front of her.

"I need to tell you something," he paused.

"What..." Riley asked.

"It's, Valrik."
