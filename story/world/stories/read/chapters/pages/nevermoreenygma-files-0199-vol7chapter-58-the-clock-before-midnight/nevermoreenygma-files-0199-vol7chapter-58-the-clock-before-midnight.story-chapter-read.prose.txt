Chapter 58

The Clock Before Midnight

In 2084, the Nevermore Initiative received a formal notice from the newly founded Tripartite Council.

The notice had the weight of a revelation capable of changing the course of history. However, the official announcement did not come directly from the Council, instead, it was made public by the United Nations worldwide in 2085. The damn statement that finally broke the silence which had endured for decades.

Mai knew this was not merely a shift in public narrative. The espers had existed for a long time, and the world finally had to face reality. For years, their existence had been a closely guarded secret, dismissed by many as the fanciful invention of science fiction and fantasy writers.

But that was no longer the case.

Many espers had lived hidden lives, some didn’t even know they possessed abilities, while others had been hunted, feared, and in some cases, exploited throughout history.

The revelation, however, was presented to the public as a new phenomenon, something arising from genetic mutations, caused by the Dark Events, starting in 2060, though there might have been a few exceptions in decades and centuries past. According to reports, these mutations had led to the emergence of espers, who now needed to be identified and recognized as part of humanity.

The public campaign, “Are You an Esper?”, became a media sensation, encouraging people to question whether they had ever had special abilities beyond the ordinary. But Mai knew the truth was different. The world was not being told the real story behind the espers. Their existence was not a product of recent mutations, it was a fact that dated back far earlier.

Espers—psychics, telepaths, telekinetics, and all those with extraordinary abilities—had been present in humanity since ancient times. And the real secret was that originally magic was related to it too, since the practice of magic was a way to control those abilities. The term “esper” had long been used in occult and parapsychology circles in the previous century, but now it was a common term, accessible to everyone, ignoring the true complexity behind such powers.

Mai had heard whispers of the possibility that the rest of the hidden world would one day be revealed, but she never imagined it would happen so quickly.

Those cheaters in the Tripartite Council had played their hand with caution, revealing the existence of espers gradually. People could now learn the truth in a way they could never have imagined—but that didn’t mean everything was clear.

The factions controlling humanity’s secrets, those who had always operated in the shadows, would continue their work with the same discretion as before. Though the announcement was public, the true scale of what was being unveiled could not be fully understood by the world for many years—much like archaeological discoveries.

Mai understood that clearly, and of course she knew the impact of this revelation would far exceed what any media campaign could predict.

For espers and other hidden entities, the world would change—but not in the way the masses expected. Nope. Humanity could now be informed of espers’ existence, but that didn’t mean they would stop being hunted, labeled, or even used as pieces in a larger puzzle, for example strategy or pressure weapons.

Human history, intertwined with mutations, psychic abilities, and magical powers, remained far more complex than any public announcement could explain.

And the question lingering in Mai’s mind was the same she had asked herself for years: Was humanity ready to handle what it truly meant to live in a world where espers were no longer a secret?

***

In the following years, Mai continued her missions alongside other espers, feys, and mages, always under the directive of Nevermore and the interests of the Tripartite Council. The missions varied, like always, but all shared a common denominator: the need to maintain order in a world increasingly altered by the revelation of espers and Dark Events.

One of the most challenging missions occurred in 2087, when Mai was assigned to collaborate with a team of mages to dismantle a secret network trafficking magical artifacts. The network operated deep in the city of Geneva, where the artifacts were believed to enhance the powers of dark mages and rogue espers using them for destructive purposes.

Mai, alongside a group of energy-control specialists, infiltrated the warehouse where the artifacts were stored. The operation was tense and dangerous, and also poorly planned… by both sides. facing defenders trained in black magic and other espers who tried to counter Nevermore agents’ attempts. The problem arose when the Geneva special unit failed to notify the police special unit to coordinate efforts. That nearly ended in disaster when the police and two riot control vehicles arrived on the scene. Between the members of Nevermore, the special unit from Geneva, the traffickers and the police—who didn’t understand what kind of war that was—it ended with fifty people in jail. Mai included, who had to share her cell with a couple of her comrades and two homeless people in the infamous ‘drunk tank’ Fortunately, five hours later, the Nevermore members were released amid a very angry army commander, the police chief, and the diplomatic section. It was classified as a secret Interpol mission—something Interpol didn’t take too kindly to the next day.

Another memorable, to put it in way, job took place in 2088, when Mai and a small group of espers were sent to South America to investigate an energy anomaly reported in the Amazon rainforest. For weeks, amid mosquitoes that feasted on them and the stifling heat of the jungle, they explored a vast area where the boundaries of reality seemed to blur in a very literal sense.

The trees bent in impossible shapes, and rivers flowed in contrary directions. For Mai, it was a perplexing and terrifying mission. They encountered an enclave of natives who had lived there for centuries and had seemingly synchronized their psychic abilities with the environment. This was not merely a natural anomaly but the result of an ancient civilization that had developed psychic powers to extreme levels, creating a completely altered ecosystem. Despite her fears, Mai and her team managed to gather valuable information and preserve peace with the natives, who agreed to cooperate with the organization. The issue was whether to notify anyone, and if so, whom. That tribe had lived peacefully in the area, and in fact, it was only the occurrence of the Dark Event that had revealed their existence. Just to be safe, the situation would be kept secret for a while, and only the Brazilian branch of Nevermore in Rio was notified of the event and they will keep an eye in the situation.

As for Mai, she just figured she wouldn’t be going to the jungle for a while. The bats and other insects had nearly driven her crazy at night in that area. Several nights she had had to sleep with a helmet to prevent her antennae from picking up signals from the wildlife. It was funny because she could live perfectly well in the city, but when she went to places with more nature, that changed completely. It was as if something suddenly activated inside her. It didn’t usually happen in the winter, but in the summer it was much more active.

***

In 2089, Mai detected something happening both in the world, and on the island. People from a wide range of fields, previously unseen, began to arrive: game designers, marketing specialists, even famous film directors.

Something was cooking there, and smelled fishy, with the pardon of sirens and mermaids.

She also saw prominent fey leaders from communities she had known in the past. Something was happening, although she didn’t know the full extent, and wasn’t on the island much due to search and infiltration missions.

Even so, this became increasingly clear as the months went by.

By 2090, she noticed a global wave of fashion centered around fae beings, legends, and magic.

Shortly after, Rein explained what it was about: a secret project that was planned since the esper revelation to the world. A media campaign had begun to finally introduce feys and the world of magic to humans. A social experiment to introduce a kind of positive primacy, with a view to a possible revelation of the fey world in the future.

Mai remained on edge, unsure of what to think.

Years had already passed since the Dark Events, then the espers. Now it was time for the remaining part of the hidden world to be revealed. It all seemed too fast. Yet those involved said it would take at least a decade to establish full communication with all fey groups.

The Tripartite Cuntcil—composed of humans, feys, and aeons—had moved their pieces once again, and talks with the United Nations had begun. Leaders and other sectors would have to reach agreements, but it would be a slow process. For now, the Nevermore Initiative continued its mission: investigating Dark Events and other paranormal occurrences, while assisting feys, magical creatures, mages, and espers in need.

Rein had already told Mai not to use that word in public when referring to the Council, but since she had met a few members of the group in the past, she didn’t care for it much. In fact, Leon and Fu Xian were almost the only ones she knew, along with Travis, that human alchemist.The rest might have good intentions, but she’d been through too much to blindly trust such a centralized power, and the Council was growing far too quickly.

***

In 2093, Mai was assigned a rescue mission in Central Europe, where a group of espers had been captured by an extremist faction seeking to exploit them for its own ends.

The rescue team consisted of Mai, Kaz, an esper with telekinetic abilities, and a fey who controlled water, Milla. The operation took place in the Carpathian Mountains, where the captives were being held in an old monastery within a town taken over by enemies. The mission was extremely tense, as Mai and her team faced multiple opponents using advanced technology to neutralize their powers. However, after a grueling battle, the team successfully freed the prisoners and returned them to the nearby Nevermore base. It was a successful mission, yet Mai couldn’t stop thinking about the harsh reality. The spirits were already known, but things in the occult world remained the same.

Over the years, Mai realized that each mission grew more complex in technological terms. The world, once chaotic, was increasingly fractured by revelations, secrets, and the hidden agendas of those seeking to control the power of espers and the rest of the hidden world.

Although Nevermore agents worked tirelessly to restore some kind of balance, now in many regions linked with global security agencies, Mai couldn’t help but feel that the world was changing irreversibly, and that the peaceful life she once knew might never return.

But at the same time, she laughed at herself inwardly when she considered her own perspective. What did she call a peaceful life, when she had been running from place to place for so many decades?

What did she expect from the future?

What did she want to do with her life?

Yet, despite everything, her determination remained unshaken. She was doing something with her time. Every mission was an opportunity to learn more, to protect her own and others, and to keep at bay the forces threatening to unleash chaos. Even if that meant getting her hands dirty.

In that sense, being in Nevermore hadn't tired her as much as being in Whisper. Something obvious about that was that her hair rarely turned silver, most of the time it was dark. This was a sign that she wasn't suffering from any stress, even under pressure and danger.

That could be it. Or it could be that after so many years, she took the action as something natural.

***

It was a November night in 2094.

Mai had just completed a mission in which a group of feys, along with herself, had captured a group of antiquities traffickers involved in the illegal trade of mystical artifacts. The operation had gone smoothly in the morning hours: the perpetrators were handed over to the responsible division, and the team returned by road to their temporary base on the outskirts of a remote coastal town in Western Australia, along the Eighty Mile Beach.

It wasn't much, but a few hours' drive along the coast was nice. Nevermore Australia was located more toward the center of the continent and consisted mainly of archaeological teams, so the agents active in high-risk missions were based in and around Sydney. Mai, for her part, hadn't been to Australia very often, and on the rare occasions she did go, she took the opportunity to learn a little more about the place. After all, Rein believed that the origin of life on the planet might hold a clue there. Whether that was true or not, Mai just enjoyed the view—as long as no spiders came near her.

The team stopped and got out of the vehicle to have dinner with the night views.

Mai carried a tablet with her report and extra mission details to deliver to Clerk via the Quantum Link of The Network, the aeon in charge of coordinating missions in the region.

The atmosphere was calm, the sound of waves was gently lapping against the coast, only enhancing the relaxed mood after the operation. Yet, as Mai walked toward where the rest of the team was gathered, she noticed something unusual.

Kazuo, one of the support agents, held his communication device in hand, his face tense. He stared at the screen with concern, frowning.

“Everything okay?” Mai asked, approaching him.

Kazuo turned, surprised by the question. “Uh, I don’t know… there’s something weird in the news. And a message on the internal network. Seems like something important is happening.”

Mai raised an eyebrow, confused. “What? What kind of news?”

“I’m not sure. Actually, they were asking if we knew anything,” Kazuo replied.

Just then, Noki approached and overheard the conversation. “What’s going on?”

“They asked if we had seen the news. Haven’t you heard anything? Uh… What?” Kazuo looked back at his device, and for a moment, seemed disconnected from the net.

Mai looked at hers. She had just lost her satellite connection too.“Hmm?”

Mai noticed Kazuo eyes fixed on the sky.

“No, what news?” Noki asked, equally puzzled.

Mai felt a chill run down her antennae and spine and turned to look eastward. Towards it stretched a plain that merged with the sky, broken only by a few nearby hills and rocky mountains.

Then all three stopped, looked up at the sky, and the atmosphere shifted instantly.

Mai looked at Noki and Kazuo. Noki’s gaze was unfocused, and she looked around as if searching for something, while Mai’s antennae quivered, picking up something in the air.

The previously soothing sound of the waves vanished, replaced by a strange buzzing in the air. Mai frowned and glanced at the sky above her, which was taking on a darker, almost unnatural tone, as if the atmosphere itself were distorting and… some stars had vanished from the night.

It was as if something were altering the environment at high altitude. They felt slightly dizzy for some reason.

Mai glanced at her tactical compass in her backpack a few steps away and saw the needle spinning wildly.

“What the hell?!” Noki exclaimed, pointing toward the distant horizon.

There was something there that was not before.

A strange vortex, a kind of opening in the sky, began to form, emitting iridescent hues and lighting up the night and the ocean. It was like a fracture in the very sky, and from the iridescent hues a dark whirlpool that started to spin and expand.

“What… is that?” Mai murmured, with a sense of unease creeping over her back.

Kazuo didn’t take his eyes off the phenomenon, and for a moment, it seemed as if words were caught in his throat.

Although Kazuo was the youngest of the group, all three had seen enough in their lives. But that.. that was something different. They didn't know why, and they couldn't explain it, but it was the same feeling.

The group began murmuring among themselves, unsure how to react. Noki stepped back, staring in disbelief.

“We’re all seeing the same thing, right?”

At that moment, one of the other agents, who had been looking at his device, finally lifted his gaze, visibly alarmed. “This… this is on all transmissions of TV. The news were saying it’s happening worldwide. The sky… it’s changing.” The agent looked at his device, but now it had frozen, the transmission buffer had been cut off. “At least... it was, before the signal died.”

Mai tried to process what she was seeing, unable to find any logical explanation. “Are we witnessing a Dark Event? Right now?”

Kazuo took a deep breath, pale as a corpse. “I don’t know…”

The vortex in the sky expanded further, and a cold sweat ran down Mai’s back. It felt as if the air around them was shifting, a strange pressure gradually intensifying. She couldn’t look away from the phenomenon, her mind was struggling to find an explanation. But she knew something unprecedented was happening if it was occurring worldwide.

“This isn’t normal! We have to move!” Noki exclaimed, a little agitated. Kazuo didn’t respond immediately, his gaze still fixed on the sky.

“What the hell is all this?” Kazuo murmured, as if the words were a prayer.

Mai swallowed hard, watching the vortex take an even more defined shape. That thing… how big was it? Was it in space? But they were sensing something. Was it the atmosphere? Could it be in space? What the hell had happened to the signal?

The group began organizing, moving quickly toward the support vehicle, though none of them could tear their eyes from the sky. Kazuo took the wheel and started the engine. A strange unease settled in their stomachs, as if they all sensed that what they were witnessing was just the beginning of something far larger and that did not bode well.

As they drove away, the vortex continued to expand, and a profound sense of discomfort washed over them.

Something was about to happen.

Suddenly, without warning, the moonlight vanished abruptly, as if someone had switched off a lamp in the vast void of the universe. Mai, along with the others, watched in astonishment as a series of bolides streaked through space.

From where they came from?

“What?!”

They were not ordinary meteors, but massive rocks hurtling through the atmosphere at incredible speed, moving along a diagonal path from east to west. The glow of their passage lit the sky momentarily like the southern lights before they partially disintegrated in the air. There was no southern lights in that latitude. At least it was not predicted.

The silence that followed the phenomenon was absolute, broken only by the ragged breaths of the team members. Kazuo, still holding his communication device, tried to reestablish contact with the base, but it was useless. The satellite signal was gone. Mai tried the redundant channels, but it didn't work. Was the entire satellite network affected?

“What the hell is happening?” Kazuo murmured, staring at his screen in frustration.

The ground began to shake beneath the vehicle, as if an immense invisible force were rattling the world itself. Looking out to sea, it seemed as if the waves breaking against the shore had become more violent.

The team gazed toward the horizon. Suddenly, the atmosphere seemed to shift again, and a colossal shadow materialized in the sky. It was a massive piece of land, or rock, floating between the Earth and the Moon, a dark, irregular, monstrous body hovering there like a satellite that had appeared instantaneously.

That explained the disappearance of the stars, those things appearing must have been large enough to cover parts of the night sky, and parts of them were falling into the atmosphere.

“What is that?! What is that?!” Noki exclaimed, eyes wide, pointing at the phenomenon in the sky.

No one answered her, the words stuck in their throats. Mai felt a growing, strange unease, as if something gigantic and unknown were unfolding right in front of them, something beyond their comprehension.

They looked again at their devices, attempting to restore communication, but the interference was total. The network had fallen into absolute chaos, replaced with a red alert but that didn't especified the anomaly.

“We have to contact the base! Something’s happening!” Mai said, even though she knew there was nothing they could do at that moment. Every attempt to send messages failed.

Finally, Kazuo lifted his gaze from the device, staring toward the horizon once more. “No… it’s not just happening here. The transmissions… there are anomalies appearing all over the world.” Anxiety gripped his voice. “Everywhere!”

The team fell silent, watching as the first rocks disintegrated in midair and crashed to the ground, followed by others. In the sky, the enormous floating fragment of earth remained. It was not an illusion.

“This… this isn’t… happening, right?” Noki murmured, trembling slightly. Mai shared the same indescribable horror.

But this… this was on an entirely new scale.

It was at that moment that blood splattered across her, and the vehicle gradually came to a stop.

Beside her, half of Kazuo’s body hung lifeless, while the other agent clutched his stomach, screaming in pain.

One of those rocks—or whatever it was—falling from the sky had killed Kazuo and injured the agent behind him. Noki screamed something, but Mai, covered in the still-warm blood of her teammate, had yet to process what had just occurred.

To Mai and great part of the world it started that day.

The days of hidden missions, of operating in the shadows, of maintaining order in the unseen corners of the world, had come to an end.

Within a few hours, the old methods of communication would be dusted off, and the news would spread around the world.

The legends, theories, and possibilities were true and real after all.

They were not alone in the universe.

That night, the Fractus arrived.

And with them came the war against something far greater than any threat they had faced before.
