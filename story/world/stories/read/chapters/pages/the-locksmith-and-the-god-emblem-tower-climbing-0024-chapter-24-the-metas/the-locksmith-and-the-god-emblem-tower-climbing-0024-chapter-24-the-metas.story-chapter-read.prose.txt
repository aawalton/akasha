Harry's leg throbbed as he walked through the irregular floor of the cavern. Like a natural studio, the place echoed their voices as a bright ray of light illuminated it all. From the light, one could even see specks of snow falling through, just like a winter breeze.

But what got the group's attention wasn't the convenient cave inside the mountain, but the so called Metas. Their body, hourglass shaped, was sharp at the corners and with a rocky texture all around. They had dozens of eyes that circled the inner part of the hourglass, right near the intersection. Some were latched onto the wall as others rolled around like a strange wheel, their eyes going from one side to the other. Just seeing the way they moved made Harry wonder. Don't they get nauseated?

Daniel then looked at the water reservoir and took a step towards it.

"I wouldn't drink that unless you want a stomachache." Petrikov alerted, which left Daniel in a dilema.

Should he drink to prove that it doesn't affect him, or should he follow the elder's advice? From baby troll to elder was a concept that quickly left his mind muddy.

"I... yes." Daniel grunted, leaving the group half confused.

A Meta rolled in front of them.

"What... are these things?" Brun was the first to ask, visibly mesmerized. "We never saw them in the previous floor."

"Oh. But I am sure you did." Petrikov said as he showed them around. "If I remember correctly there seemed to be a type of worm creature that cleaned the aisles and cells of the first floor."

"...You're right." The old woman replied, remembering. "I forgot about the worm. But the second floor had none of them."

"Maybe Dracno ordered all of their deaths?" Goldman added.

"There was one." The Locksmith said. "It was on top of the church. I don't know if the flame and the other cabin parts were a type of Meta as well, but the face was. It blinked at me."

"The church blinked at you?" Madeline took a step back, horrified. "And you think that wasn't worth adding?"

"It wasn't the church, per se... But yeah, the mask on top of it." The young thief explained. "I was exhausted, okay? That whole talk with Dracno—I mean, the non verbal talk with Dracno took the life out of me. As if I wasn't tired enough from... this." He pointed to his body.

"Why did it only blink to you!?" Manes frowned. "I was there for a whole... I don't know, but I've been on that floor for a long time! Not once did I saw it move or do something!"

"Well, if you actually left your room you could've seen it."

"Haha. Nice one, Locksmith." He narrowed his eyes.

"...Why did you phrase it like an insult?" Harry looked around. "You guys saw that, right?"

"Anyway..." Petrikov broke the discussion with a chuckle. He was clearly enjoying this. "Don't bother the tiny entities, would you all? As I've come to know the little wheel guys, I've come to understand their nature. They evolve beyond the loop."

"What do you mean by beyond the loop?" Goldman questioned.

"They aren't affected by it." Brun was the first to answer. "That's why you told us not to drink the water; it's infested."

"...You're a sharp one." Petrikov grinned. "Yes, that is correct. Although I may have exaggerated on the matter. If you drink accordingly, it becomes merely an inconvenience. When you're stranded for too long on this mountain, however, inconvenience no longer affects you."

"How many of these are there?" Goldman suddenly took interest on the discussion. "Are any of them aggressive?"

"What do you think?" Petrikov answered with a smile and then sat down next to a Meta half his size. "No, they're... I mean, with their adaptation skills, I wouldn't want to be a carnivorous one, for example, but mine are—I mean, these ones are peaceful. There wasn't any need for them to resort to violence. This one I call Larry."

The Meta then turned to him oddly and let Petrikov cuddle his arms near the base of the creature. Although it couldn't talk, Harry could vaguely sense warmth in the dark puddle of his eyes. Like that of a child.

Goldman's question rang a few bells in everyone's head, but it was necessary. Although the Metas appeared to be inoffensive, one could already imagine what they could turn out once released for a couple years in a more competitive environment. Here, thankfully, they only drank water and convert energy from the sun for their survival.

"Are they edible?" Daniel asked for the first time as he studied a small one near his feet. "I am sure that behind this thick crust lies a juicy piece of mea—"

"What the hell, man?"

"Oh, dad, please..."

"Read the room, Mr. Daniel."

"I was thinking the same thing, actually..."

The majority of the group condemned the man's actions, except for one who would grow to be exactly like the viking, if he had the opportunity. The defense left Petrikov relieved as he got up, smiling.

Apparently, Daniel's weak guts could be fixed with hungry ones.

I bet you and Goldman will become great friends. Harry then watched as the group started to bicker over the Meta's nature.

Harry then approached the water, choosing a place less concentrated in tadpoles.

"Are you sure you wanna to drink that?" A red-haired woman asked raising an eyebrow. "Doesn't seem that safe to me."

"You won't live it until you risk it." He then gulped.

"What's the taste?" Madeline asked, curious.

"...What you would expect from a water filled with tadpoles." Harry made a strange face, almost as if he was about to puke. Well, turns out that the effect was almost immediate as the Locksmith felt his intestine rumble. After a few moments, however, it subsided.

"They are kinda cute, aren't they?" Madeline said as she eyed one of them hugging on a wall. The Meta looked around with his minuscule eyes and even tinier mouth as if he were yawning.

"I wouldn't say cute." Harry got up and spat on the ground. "But they have a certain... charm to it."

"They remind me of my sister." She said with a more serious tone. "They do have that child-like demeanor to them, don't they?"

Harry failed to answer as he simply stared at the creatures. Adaptive and marvelous creatures that could only exist in this loop. Their whole existence, after possibly hundreds of years, defined by this specific loop. This mountain. If any of them got outside, they wouldn't last a day.

"They're like us." Harry said. "They adapt."

"You can use your, um, what do you call them, C-Coins? You could sleep here tonight, set it up, and climb it first thing in the morning. Well, not morning per se, but as soon as the loop restarts, which should still be hours away."

"It is our only option, I'm afraid." Goldman said after a moment. "But I wish we could do this faster. How tough is this rain that you mentioned? You've seen our emblems and what we can do; are you sure we can't climb it even then?"

"Trust me on this," He began. "You won't be able to survive minutes outside. Especially in the middle of the rock rain. After a few hours, snow starts falling consistently as well, which can impede your vision severely. And that's not even talking about the crash..."

"The crash?"

"My guess is that there is a huge rock constantly falling from the sky, and near the end of the loop, is when it enters in contact with the mountain. I don't know if it is the Metas that keep this cavern standing or if they only found it like I did. Either way, we are safe as long as we stay inside. And now it is already too late to start the climb. Can you hear it?"

And as Goldman focused on a specific point, letting his ears attentive, he heard it; the sound of falling rocks.

"That means the hotel is long gone?" The pirate asked.

"I'm afraid so. But don't worry, it'll be fine by the next loop."

Being forced to see the Locksmith so close to death, together with how they ended up as prisoners on the second floor gave Goldman a new sense of responsibility. The faster they climbed, the faster they could go home. Throughout all of his apprehensive thoughts, he understood it wouldn't be feasible to get all of the group on the place. If they went one by one it would be even more dangerous.

"...Okay." The man replied. "You heard the man, guys. We will leave first thing after the loop resets, on the next cycle. Stay close to each other, don't mess with the Meta unless Petrikov allows it and don't go outside. Hey, do you know if there is a comfortable place where we can spend the night?"

"Um, yeah, follow me." The rest of the group remained still, a few looking outside as others walked around, leaving their belongings on the floor and tidying up stuff.

This feels too easy... Harry thought. Although the previous floor was tense because of Conrad, the place itself was quite serene. The third floor, on the other hand, was the opposite; no Emblem users but a harsh area. Not that it would be any easier climbing the rest of the mountain, but it wouldn't be an impossible task. It'd be much easier than fighting Conrad.

That's what the young thief thought.

Harry then watched as Petrikov led Goldman through one of the cave's many paths, smiling as he talked to someone who was willing to listen. Someone had been stealing common people and stashing them in the tower for some reason. It wasn't accidental. It couldn't be. Was it a means to secure people? Harry remembered the ruckus that was starting to roam through the streets about the moon and how long the world had. Prophecies were starting to be created as the apocalypse came close. The young thief always saws them as nothing but stunts of the government to get more money from the nobles, but what if it were true?

After a few minutes, Goldman came back and led the group to the place where they would sleep. The world was now getting darker as they settled.

"Um, Goldman..." Prism said with a low voice while scratching her arm. "Do you think that's necessary? I mean, we've seen a good portion of the mountain and checked the hotel before it fell. We only have Metas and, no offense, a Petrikov. We're not exactly on the verge of a volcano here."

She talked about the security routines they had set up so they could sleep ensuring their own safety. All while taking turns, of course.

"I understand your point." Goldman answered respectfully. "However, as I've said previously, I prefer to see you safe. I trust Petrikov and I think the Metas won't be a problem, but what happens if one of the wackos of the previous or next floor come down? I can't see how that system could ever dull down our skills and abilities."

It will if I'm fucking sleepy... Prism added inwardly as she simply smiled and nodded, receiving a sincere smile from Goldman.

Although sleep wasn't necessary since their bodies would simply reset along with weariness and energy levels, it maintained morale. It was weird enough to be teleported every loop and have yourself readjusted once again. Sleeping maintained that sense of normalcy that, at least Goldman, thought could be of use, especially on this third floor.

"Where will we sleep?" Madeline asked, confused. There were only rocks everywhere.

"Oh." Petrikov grinned. "I know just the thing."

Waking up from a dream, Harry blinked his eyes as he remembered where he was. Relief washed all over him as he looked around, almost everyone sleeping except for Vela and Daniel; their shifts hadn't ended yet, and wouldn't end for another half an hour.

All around him, the group slept using Metas as pillows. Turns out the creatures had a soft spot that took control of their skin as they came to sleep as well. Some, larger Metas, served as pillows for both Goldman and Daniel, while smaller ones could fit Brun and Madeline with no problem.

Looking at Manes, he saw a few bandages slipping out of the boy's wrist in a weird frequency, almost as if it reacted to his dreaming. It's very dark in here, that's why...

The only thing missing is a fucking blanket... He shivered as Vela's flame still held on the middle of the cave, an ambiance light and source of warmth. Well, they're in deep sleep, and Vela won't be back for another couple minutes. Maybe I can check what this rock rain is about before we feel it...

And Harry got up with his crutch.
