Chapter V.XXXIII (5.33) - Wakino’s Lesson

“You lack offensive capabilities.”

Kizu blinked at the blunt accusation. He stood across from Wakino in a grove in the forest near the academy. Birds squawked overhead and he spotted several tortoises lounging nearby. It was far from a private location, but she’d declined his offer to bring her to Owl’s Respite or Comet’s stable.

Instead, she’d jumped him here. She was dressed in her usual cloak. Today its hood was pushed back, revealing the gaping warped space where half of her face should be.

“I know a few offensive spells,” Kizu replied. “I can snap space to fling objects and can jump into opponents. Also, outside of spatial magic, I have a natural affinity with fire. Not to mention my collection of potions.”

“An affinity with flames. Yes. I can understand how that came to be.” Wakino’s single eye lingered on his monster leg. “Those might exist in your arsenal; however, I watched your performance during the last tournament challenge. Clever tricks, but not remarkable fighting. You will lose the bracket with your current level of offensive skills. Your fire isn’t anything particularly special. Fireproofing with enchantments and quenching it with water remains extremely viable as a counter. And compressing space to make objects move faster is hardly the most effective means of using spatial magic offensively. Not to mention the dangers of jumping and displacing space on your opponent with yourself. That leaves you far too prone and any half-proficient combat mage will dispatch you immediately.”

“So… do you have a recommendation?” Kizu asked. He was eager to learn whatever she had to offer. He’d defeated plenty of enemies with his current set of spells and hardly thought of himself as inept when it came to combat. However, Wakino’s standards were doubtlessly a great deal higher than his own. She was respected by nobility and had been married to an Elite of Hon, after all.

“Yes.” Wakino lifted the limp sleeve of her cloak. It made a diagonal chop, the end of the sleeve flopping to the side with the sudden motion.

A nearby tree fell. It collapsed into a neighboring tree and rained down nuts and loose branches onto the forest floor.

“You sliced it with a sharpened barrier of space?” Kizu guessed. He hadn’t been watching well enough to see the actual barrier, but he’d heard Wakino mention the technique before and figured through context that it must be that.

“Correct,” Wakino responded, her voice flat.

“I’ve seen elementalist mages do something similar. A mage I met in Hon used blades of ice to slice through his targets. And I’ve seen blades of wind as well.”

Wakino seemed to consider his statement for a moment . Then she nodded slowly. “Yes. I suppose it is similar to an elementalist technique. Should you master my proposed spell, perhaps you could perform a similar feat with fire magic using it as a template. Though I fear such an attack would immediately cauterize any wound that you inflicted.”

Kizu hadn’t even considered trying to create a blade of fire. Like Wakino said, it would sear closed all wounds cut and, like when he’d learned his fire fist spell, it wasn’t as effective with limited contact. Arclight had taught him fire was best used in a grapple. Maybe though if he needed something to burn in the aftermath it could be useful?

“If it cauterizes the cut, then it could be a nonlethal method of taking down an opponent,” Kizu mused.

“There is truth in that. Though my speciality lies not in flames. Let us refocus on the subject immediately in front of you.”

“Space,” Kizu agreed. He reached out and created a small barrier over his fingers. Every person’s barrier had their own slight deviances in its appearance granted by the caster’s personality. His own had the linked branches of a canopy embedded into the translucent hardened space. A reminder of the safety he’d once felt while under the jungle canopy in Hon. The sight of it brought him a measure of peace as he studied the barrier.

“Thinner,” Wakino instructed. “Unless you plan to bludgeon rather than cut.”

“Bludgeoning is good too though, right? There are a lot of effective bludgeoning weapons.”

“Would you rather have a book spine or a knife in a fight?”

“Point taken.”

Kizu narrowed his barrier to the thickness of paper. It wasn’t much of a challenge. He’d used thin barriers in the past for things like slowing his fall.

Wakino’s empty sleeve shot forward and smashed through the barrier, shattering it. And she’d struck the edge of the barrier, not the flat end.

“Weak,” she said.

Kizu narrowed his eyes and tried again, understanding what she wanted from him without further instruction. He needed to create a barrier more akin to a knife. A flimsy piece of thin metal hardly had the support to slice something.

This time, he recreated it with more subsistence.

With a mere glance of her eye, a nearby crooked stick raised up from the forest floor and flew in Kizu’s direction. He countered it with his barrier, placing it between himself and the attack. While the small branch did bend and leave a gash as it impacted Kizu’s new barrier, the spatial magic was once again dismissed as the force overwhelmed it. The battered stick knocked into Kizu’s leg.

“Weak.”

It continued like that for an hour. Then two. Wakino gave no suggestions and her only commentary was that single word, repeated ceaselessly. “Weak.”

Kizu, wiping beads of sweat from his brow with his uniform jacket sleeve, resisted the urge to snap at the spatial mage. It was clear that her attention was quite literally split between here and wherever else the majority of her body currently resided.

But, while a terrible teacher, he had to admit that the information deficient required him to experiment with hardening space into barriers. In the past few hours he felt he’d learned more about space than a day’s worth of studying the subject in the library. If she’d just told him how and where to increase the barrier’s density, he could have doubtlessly gotten there significantly faster, but understood far less.

Sticks were scattered all around his feet. Some cleanly sliced through, others only notched. It had been slow progression, but Kizu had noticed that as he managed to chop the sticks, Wakino had subtly increased the width of the wood she chose to hurl at him. Little by little. He could now reliably create a blade thick enough to chop through wood logs the size of his bicep.

Strangely, the next stick to rise up from the forest floor was significantly thinner than the last. Less than half the width.

Grateful for an easy target, Kizu prepared a new barrier for the next projectile and readied himself. He waited. It didn’t come at him, instead just hovering in place. He glanced at Wakino, thinking that the spatial mage must have gotten distracted by something.

“Opponents won’t usually run full speed into your barriers,” Wakino said. “You must command your barriers to seize the initiative.”

Kizu chewed on the inside of his cheek. He’d known this would be coming eventually. He pushed his barrier forward at the greatest speed he could manage. It slammed into the side of the suspended wooden rod.

The stick fell to the ground. In one piece.

“Again.” Wakino’s lackluster instruction continued. When she ran out of decent sized sticks on the ground, she began slicing branches off the tree she’d felled. These were even more difficult for Kizu to split as they were green, not dry like the sticks scattered on the forest floor.

Once Kizu finally managed to consistently chop sticks, Wakino moved on to a new exercise.

A nut the size of an eyeball smacked him in the side of his head.

“Ouch!” He instinctively raised a hand and pressed it against his stinging face. The flesh by his ear was tender to the touch.

Another nut flew at him and he managed to deflect it with a barrier before it made contact. It dropped to the forest floor and Kizu turned to face Wakino.

“What was—agh!” Another nut smacked into his forehead.

“Cut them,” Wakino said. “Don’t deflect.”

Kizu glared at the spatial mage. When the next nut flew at him, he managed to nick it with a spatial slice. Knocked off-course, it still hit him, but it painlessly smacked into his chest rather than the open skin of the other two that had hit his face.

The first exercise had been to develop the proper density of spatial barriers. The second one to practice swift movement. And now this was to train him in precision. Chopping nuts out of the air was not something he could have possibly achieved before this lesson.

While he understood the exercise’s purpose, it was the worst one yet. He managed to parry half of the projectiles, which meant red welts all over his hands and face. Kizu desperately wished he’d had the foresight to wear gloves today.

With a pop, Mort jumped to his location and then took great pleasure in attempting to catch the severed nuts as they fell. He gleefully darted around in the grove’s grass while Kizu was peppered with projectiles.

“I believe that is enough training for the day,” Wakino said, abruptly stopping her onslaught. “Continue to practice in your own time. Should you prove adequate, next I will train you in manipulating space to move objects with precision.”

Then, without giving him a chance to speak, Wakino’s form disappeared. It was as if she had never been there at all.

Kizu looked around, now abandoned in the forest. Mort nibbled on a nut. The other wildlife had long since abandoned the clearing during his training session.

Without training in long distance jumps, he’d be completely stranded in the wilderness.

“Good think I can locate the academy’s beacon right now,” Kizu muttered.
