They did get the wheel back on, but Sakura noticed the repair was sloppy. The workers were irritated and clearly wanted to finish this faster, while the senior guard on duty stood nearby with a face as if this entire wagon had become his personal enemy.

Sakura signed the papers about the damaged wheel. Then the guard on duty reluctantly signed too. He very much disliked being dragged into this paperwork mess, but he liked the idea of being left with no papers at all even less.

"That’s it," he muttered. "Now open the cargo."

Sakura clenched the reins and forced herself not to tense too visibly, because she had reached another delicate point in her plan.

As long as the wagon was closed, it was considered an ordinary delivery. But as soon as the tarp was lifted, people would see the cargo, start checking the bundles against the lists, and ask unnecessary questions. And if someone decided to run a proper inspection, Sakura would not last even a couple of minutes.

A worker climbed toward the rear edge of the tarp, and Sakura jumped down from the driver’s bench.

"Careful," she said sharply. "Because of this breakdown, everything probably shifted inside."

"Yeah, we figured that out already," the worker snapped back.

Sakura wanted to answer, but at that moment she heard a quiet, short whistle. It was almost lost in the noise of the yard and consisted of four parts: short, then longer, then very short, and the last one high and sharp. The whistle imitated the song of the local rempik birds.

Those small gray birds really did live on the roofs of the service buildings and in the Castle’s gutters. Sakura had already heard them that morning while waiting by the wagon. They whistled strangely, as if mocking dripping water, and none of the locals paid attention to them.

But this whistle was too even, too close to the instructions. Four sounds of different lengths. Exactly the way she and Gilbert had agreed before the operation began. Back then, discussing bird whistles in the middle of a prison escape plan had seemed stupid to her. Now it was a lifesaving necessity.

Gilbert was nearby. Sakura did not turn her head and did not try to find him with her eyes. She dug her fingers into the edge of her sleeve and forced herself to breathe evenly through the filter, showing no reaction. But inside, she grew calmer. She almost wanted to smile. After all, Gilbert had completed his part of the plan. Now he was somewhere nearby, as Sakura wanted to believe, with Lir, and ready to get into the wagon she had prepared for them.

And for that, she had to do two things: give the cargo an official reason to go back, and leave the rear door free for at least a few seconds.

The worker unfastened and folded back part of the tarp, and the sharp smell hit almost at once. It was so sour and caustic that two workers recoiled at the same time.

Sakura looked inside together with the senior guard on duty and saw exactly what she was supposed to see: the bundles had slid to one side, a couple of sacks were soaked through, and a cloudy greenish liquid was spreading between the boards. The clay flask Sakura had deliberately placed unsecured between the upper bundles had broken when the wagon tilted. The chemical solution had spread across the floor, touched the dry fabric, and reached the lower rolls.

"There," Sakura said sarcastically, planting her hands on her hips. "Great. Just great. This day keeps getting better."

The guard on duty frowned and looked deeper.

"What the...?"

"The treatment solution for protective fabric leaked. See? Over there, under the upper bundle. It already ran across the boards and touched the dry rolls."

"Why is treatment solution even riding in a wagon with a finished shipment?"

"Because repaired green sets now come with a small supply kit," Sakura answered irritably. Another well-rehearsed lie. "For repairs and re-treatment of seams, patches, and filter edges after receiving. You’re asking me like I made up these rules myself."

The guard on duty narrowed his eyes.

"A small kit isn’t supposed to be lying between bundles where it can break at the first tilt."

"Of course it isn’t. It’s supposed to calmly reach its destination in a wagon whose wheel doesn’t decide to start a new life away from the axle."

One of the workers snorted quietly, but immediately fell silent when the guard on duty looked at him. The guard looked back at the cargo.

"Maybe it didn’t touch everything?"

"Maybe. Or maybe the solution already leaked between the bundles and soaked lower. I’m not going to guess which sets are still good and which ones will later burn someone’s face off."

"Don’t get smart."

"I’m not getting smart. I’m trying not to hand you a shipment that will make someone die or get burned. Do you know what this solution does? At the workshop, one guy’s glove cracked, and the skin came off his fingers before they even got him to a healer. And if this filth gets into a filter, someone will breathe it in. Want to check how long he stays on his feet after that? I don’t." Sakura was deliberately frightening him more than necessary. The nastier the solution sounded, the less the guard wanted to climb into the cargo and check everything himself.

The guard grimaced. With every minute of this conversation, he grew darker. The man turned and looked at the workers. One had already discreetly stepped back from the rear side, and the second was holding his hands as if he had suddenly remembered how badly he needed his fingers.

"Close it," the guard finally said.

"And what am I supposed to do with this joy?" Sakura arched an eyebrow.

"Take it back. To the workshop. Let them check it again, dry it, and file the papers there. I’m not accepting this here."

Inside, Sakura was almost screaming and jumping from happiness. It worked. Another dangerous turn of her plan had worked as it should. The guard on duty was not stupid, but not too smart either. On top of that, he was terribly afraid of the very thing Gilbert had predicted before the operation began: the Castle’s bureaucracy.

But not everything was done yet. Sakura had to give Gilbert and Lir a way into the wagon and make sure they got inside. That was exactly why she looked at the guard on duty again and asked:

"And the paper?"

The guard on duty grimaced as if she had spat in his soup.

"You’ll get your paper."

The senior guard on duty stepped away to speak with one of the assistants. Good. Sakura made sure the rear tarp was still not secured and did not lock the wagon door. Instead, she moved away from that part of the wagon and crouched by the front wheel. Sakura jabbed a finger at the wet hub.

"This one’s warped too. Hey, you lazy lumps, come here and look!" she shouted to the workers, who were standing not far from the back of the wagon.

The workers exchanged glances. One clearly wanted to say that the front wheel had nothing to do with this, but after the talk about the solution, his desire to argue had weakened. The second definitely wanted to move farther away from the rear door, as if death itself might spill out from there at any moment.

"Well?" Sakura tapped her fingers against the hub. "Do I have to crawl in and fix it myself? I’m a driver, not a master of every trade. And I hope you’re not just here to admire the mist."

"We’re coming already," one of the workers muttered.

They approached the front. Sakura deliberately stood in a way that blocked part of the view of the back of the wagon. Then she jabbed the wheel again, this time in another spot.

"Here."

"Where is ‘here’?" the worker frowned.

"Here. See how it’s bent?"

"It isn’t bent."

"I’m telling you it is. Look closer. Maybe you need a magnifying glass?"

The worker made a face and bent lower. The second came closer too, peering from the side. Neither of them saw anything wrong with the wheel. Because there was nothing wrong with the wheel. But Sakura needed to distract their attention, so she kept arguing, occasionally calling them blind, kept poking the hub, and kept demanding that they look more carefully.

Out of the corner of her eye, she caught movement by the rear door. It looked more like a barely flickering shadow. And that was a very good sign. So Sakura kept talking even louder.

"If you want, I can go like this. And then, when I get stuck in the middle of the mist without a wheel, who will answer for that, huh?"

"There’s nothing wrong with this wheel. It’s standing steady. It won’t fall off."

"And if it does fall off? I don’t want to risk my life because of your carelessness."

"It definitely won’t fall off! It’s fine."

The tarp at the back shifted barely noticeably, something that could have been mistaken for a draft, but it was definitely not a draft. Sakura knew what it really was. Her heart seemed to hit somewhere in her throat. The wagon swayed almost imperceptibly, as if the weight inside had changed, and at that moment Sakura kicked the wheel hard with the toe of her boot to draw the workers’ attention. She almost squeaked from the pain in her foot.

The worker sharply lifted his head.

"What are you doing?"

"Checking," Sakura ground out, trying not to look at the rear door and hiding the pain in her voice. "I want to make sure it won’t fly off on the first bump."

The worker exhaled irritably and leaned in very close to the hub now, as if he genuinely wanted to find some kind of defect there just to prove Sakura was not simply messing with his head. The second also peered from the side and ran a hand over the wet rim.

The wagon behind their backs swayed barely noticeably again. Sakura almost stopped breathing.

Then the tarp went still, and there was no more movement. Sakura exhaled with relief.

"Deal with the wheel, and I’ll go check whether this wreck is locked. If the cargo starts spilling out of it on the way back too, we’ll all be writing papers here until evening."

Sakura could not openly open the wagon doors. That would have been too risky. But she looked through the crack and noticed shadows that had not been there before. Lir and Gilbert. They were inside. Gilbert had to know where the hidden niche was in the wagon, the one where the girl had hidden the protective suits so the solution would definitely not reach them. They should be changing right now, and all Sakura had left to do was not ruin everything at the last step.

The senior guard on duty returned with a small board holding a sheet of paper. The ink was already starting to blur from the damp air, and the guard himself looked as if he dreamed of blurring along with it.

"Write," he said.

Sakura rose from the wheel.

"What exactly?"

"That the shipment is returning to the workshop because the cargo was damaged during delivery. What else do you need?"

"So no one later says I decided on my own to take the cargo back," Sakura snapped.

"My mark is on it."

Sakura took the board. There really was a crude service mark on the paper. She pretended to read the text carefully, though in reality everything was swimming before her eyes from tension. Then Sakura put down the careless signature that was not hers, the one she had practiced for two evenings in a row, and handed the paper back.

The guard on duty glanced at the scrawl, then waved his hand.

"That’s it. Turn around to the workshop. And I don’t want to see this wagon again today."

"With pleasure."

"And drive carefully. If that wheel comes off again..."

"Then I’ll come back, and we’ll all discuss together how well you put it on."

The guard on duty looked at her as if, in that moment, he especially regretted the rules forbidding people from hitting delivery drivers in front of witnesses.

Sakura climbed onto the driver’s bench, and her fingers settled on the reins in the now-familiar way. The horses shifted their hooves, also tired of the noise and other people’s shouting. Under the green tarp, it was quiet. Sakura hoped Gilbert and Lir had managed to change and hide. Sitting in a wagon where chemical solution had spilled was not the best idea, but for a while the filters would save them, and later they would simply get rid of everything unnecessary and throw the rear doors open to let in air. Even later, most likely, they would move up to the driver’s bench with her. Right now, that no longer mattered as much. The most important thing was getting them beyond the gates of the damned Castle.

As soon as she thought about them, the gates ahead began to open. As at the entrance, the gates opened slowly, heavily, and with an unpleasant iron scrape, but right now, it seemed like the most beautiful sound in the world.

Sakura snapped the reins, and the wagon rolled forward. The rear wheel, put back on by someone else’s hands, creaked pitifully. Sakura forced herself not to push the horses into speed right away. She could not bolt from a standstill. She had to look like an angry worker being sent back with spoiled cargo, not a fugitive carrying two prisoners out of the Castle, one of them a valuable prisoner and the other the most wanted mage.

Very little remained. The wagon had to pass another couple dozen yards to the gates, and then the green mist would swallow it. Sakura prayed to every god that the gates would let them out beyond the prison. She looked at them like a sacred relic, slowly drawing closer. Yard by yard. A little more. Just a little more.

And then, from the direction of the outer road, a guard ran out. He appeared from the mist too sharply. His protective suit was wet and dirty. The guard breathed heavily, as if he had been running for a long time on adrenaline alone.

"They found the green delivery wagon from the workshop on the road!" he shouted. "It was attacked by a creature!"

For one short moment, the entire yard fell silent. Sakura did not turn around, but she felt the air change on her skin. Too many people had understood at the same time that something was wrong.

The senior guard on duty slowly turned his head toward her green wagon. The wagon with the delivery from the workshop. Only not that one...

"Close the gates!" he barked. "Stop her!"
