Risha watched as her skill washed over the enemy. She wasn’t sure how well this would work, but as the power flowed through them, the fighting had slowed to a near stop.

The coloring of their scales shifted, losing their sheen. They stumbled, as power they’d wielded fell away from them.

Risha lowered their leader's head, then tossed it to the ground. It rolled in silence as the enemy still recovered from Risha’s skill.

Her own people took advantage of the faltering enemy, disarming them, easily plowing through them as Risha’s warriors surged forward.

They surrendered in droves, their broken bond with their leaders leaving them unable to resist.

Some ran, retreating to the depths of their swamps, while others just lay there, bereft.

Risha let out a breath as their army broke.

She wasn’t sure it would work, and it had only worked after the main catalyst of their leader had been killed.

As Iris had told her, the scaled warriors were bound to each other by more than just emotion. Their “Great One” had taken control of a powerful structure of magic to have them do its bidding. One where each warrior was bound to their leader’s will. She was uniquely capable of destroying such a structure.

She watched her people surge forward before her gaze moved to the distance. Now that she knew it was there, she could feel it; the power of a Spark.

Despite her overwhelming victory here, she feared what lay further in. The lizard had called himself the lowliest of “his” servants, and she did not doubt those words.

In front of true power, she still had so much farther to go.

Heat filled her as Igni made himself known in her veins.

She attempted to summon the spirit to her hands, only to find her spirit empty. Her oath-breaking skill having used every last drop within her.

She lowered her hands, then stumbled on the wall of webs and earth, realizing how empty she was.

Kliks was by her side in a moment, holding her up.

Risha ran her hands through the fur of her spider, feeling the strands against her fingers.

They’re so soft.

The waters below were bloodied, the results of war still clear for her to see. She let out a sigh, her eyes closing.

The bloodied waters still filled her vision as she lost herself to sleep.

***

Again, the army put a concerted effort into pulling their own people out of the depths. Trains of captured scaled warriors walked through the camps, tied together as they were organized into rows to be watched. Nothing could be done to them until the Grand Mother woke from her sleep.

So for now they waited quietly, destroyed by their sudden loss of the bindings that had been with them through their lives.

Glo organized everything, until on the horizon, a caravan led by a Sunset Fox made its way towards them.

***

Risha rose slowly from her covers. Several small spiderlings complained as she disturbed them with her movement. She smiled as she gently helped them back into their sleep.

Someone had stripped her armor from her, leaving her in her silk underthings. She didn’t have any worry about it, as her spiderlings had no doubt been with her through the whole thing.

She found some furs, and a silk cloak, placing them over herself. The small cut on her stomach twinged, still unhealed from the power of the spark that had attempted to poison her.

The air outside her tent was brisk, a cool breeze flowing over her as the sun set on the horizon.

Igni flowed through her, driving the chill away as she walked through the camp. They celebrated their victory as they always did, with outrageous parties fueled by alcohol and the need to forget.

She made her way through them, her people cheering and howling even louder as she passed them by. She made her way to the largest tent at the center of the camp. Here, there was less partying as those still on duty hurried through the command center, in and out of tents on various missions.

A kobold and an orc opened the large tent flaps of the command tent, revealing Glo, several of his lieutenants, Klaz’zks, Caleb The Draconic Rider, Sun-Chaser and the few Elders who hadn’t joined the partying.

They bowed to her as she walked in, their conversations quieting.

“Grand Mother, did you rest well?” Glo asked, his eyes scanning her and looking for more injuries.

RIsha smiled, a surprising amount of exhaustion filtering into the expression. “How are the prisoners?”

Glo looked to the back of the tent, where an old goblin sat with her staff across her lap.

Tya harrumphed. “They are quiet, like lost spiderlings, unsure of everything. Whatever you did to them, it worked.”

Risha took a deep breath, then nodded, “I need to see them.”

Tya grumbled something as she moved off of her chair with a surprising amount of agility.

She’d evolved since Risha had last seen her, looking younger, but she still seemed to be getting more grumpy by the day.

Tya narrowed her eyes at Risha, and Risha looked away from the old goblin’s gaze. Some small part of Risha feared that the Healer’s evolution had allowed her to read minds.

Thankfully, the old goblin simply continued past her and into the camp. Risha nodded to the others, then followed Tya to the prisoner camps.

Risha’s prisoners were well taken care of, and thanks to the arrival of supplies, they were fed and healed of any of their injuries.

Still, Risha could feel the morose energy of them as she walked through them, a stark contrast to the partying of her people.

Among the prisoners, large wyrms and drakes sat in the swamps, bound by web, their heads lowered as spiders took turns watching them. The normal warriors watched Risha with warriness, not one failed to recognize her.

She was the one who brought them to this state after all.

Risha wasn’t sure what to do as she looked over them. Some part of her knew that she could bind them to her as they’d been bound to the Spark, but something about that didn’t sit right with her.

Yes, her children followed her, but they did it willingly, and their oaths were made without a takeover of their will as the binding of these scaled warriors would create.

Still, they looked pale, defeated, and they needed something.

Risha was used to the gazes of many, but the looks they gave her were so hopeless in comparison to her own people.

“I am Empress Risha, Mother Of Monsters.” Her voice spread over them, the tiniest bit of spirit amplifying her words so that even the farthest of prisoners could hear her. “To stand before you, I have done many great and terrible things. I protected my people, I guided them, I freed them from their slavery. I slaughtered my enemies, I ripped their throats, and I executed those who would not join me.” Risha showed her teeth, her eyes hardening under the strange gazes upon her. “Risha is the goblin who decides your fates, and if she chose, she could bind you to her as the Spark did.”

That brought emotion to their attention, curious and terrified gazes watched her, unspeaking.

“But I do not create slaves.” Risha said, “So again, you must choose. Pledge yourselves to me willingly, or face whatever fate waits for you when this ends; death, or exile.”

Again, Risha held monsters’ fates in her hands. Again, she offered them a choice, the only choice she could give them with the coming of evil on her horizons.

They did not answer right away, until finally, a Lizard warrior, one that she had pulled from the swamps herself, bowed his head.

“I follow the Mother Of Monsters.”

His scales rippled, taking on a purple sheen.

A large serpentine monster she’d also pulled from the waters, bowed its head.

“I follow the Mother Of Monsters.”

Its scales too, rippled as they took on a purple sheen.

First, it was those she’d pulled from near-death in the swamps, then it was the monsters she’d healed with [Mother’s Care], and finally, the rest came.

Many refused, as they always did, and she would give them more time before a decision must be made.

Risha ordered the spiders to release those who’d pledged themselves to her. They only hesitated for a moment before following her orders, then Risha led them to the partying monsters.

They quieted at the sight of the Scaled Warriors they had been fighting just hours earlier, but Risha led them, and the purple sheen to their scales was easily spotted. To her people, these were different monsters, these were Risha’s Scaled Warriors, after all.

Under Risha’s eyes, they were welcomed into the campfires of Risha’s army, first by the Goblins, then the Orcs. Humans and Kobolds were slower to warm, but Risha didn’t expect them to be as quick.

Tya laughed from where she watched besides her. “You have created quite the headache for Glo.”

“Did I?”

“Blood will be spilt before they are fully one people.”

“My monsters will accept them.”

Tya hummed, “It does help that they take on the goblin color, your color.”

“It does. And they will bond as they spill blood together.”

“You are as foolish as ever. But somehow it works when you will it.”

Risha felt a rising of anger at those words. People died under her watch, and she knew all too well what it was to fail her people, to be too late, and see her people dead.

She squashed the anger, and instead let the fire fill her veins in silence.

Risha let out a breath, “Do not say that, Tya. I already feel that I am forgetting who Risha is.”

Tya turned to Risha, “What do you mean?”

“...Never mind.”

Risha turned back to the command tent. Now that they knew what they faced, they would have to prepare. Had Glo heard that it was a Spark that powered their enemy? Risha couldn’t remember from the heat of the battle. Either way, they must fortify themselves for a bloody war. This was far from over, despite their victory.
