Charlie

Charlie sat on the cold pavement of the tower’s floor and pressed an ear against the door to the stairway he’d entered with Vetica. Most of the noises from the room had been muffled since they’d entered, but he’d heard bits and pieces of the exchange. Orb floated next to him, pressing his dungeon core body against the door as well.

The door they’d entered as part of the trial requirements had led them into a short, rounded stairwell. It was dark, with only a few torches lighting the passageway, and the walls and floor were cold to the touch. Vetica had set to work immediately, looking for a weak point she could exploit on the door leading higher into the tower. So far, she’d come up short in her efforts, but that didn’t stop her from trying.

“Maybe it’s because I don’t have ears, but I can’t hear a thing. What about you, Charlie?” Orb asked.

“It sounded like…Merlin just used some kind of magic sword! He was like whoosh, and then it was like whoosh whoosh!” Charlie said. “I think he finally used magic! He beat Obie!”

Vetica turned to glance at him over her shoulder with a frown on her face. “Are you sure you aren’t hearing things? There’s no telling what kind of tricks the tower has in store. Why don’t you try using your powers to break through here? We can’t afford to wait here much longer. There’s a battle raging on outside, and every moment we delay is more time for Brandt and the other knights to arrive.”

Charlie thought for a moment. “I know, but I believe in Merlin!” But could she be right about the tower playing tricks on his ears? It was certainly possible. There really wasn’t any way to know. But he didn’t think Obie would resort to tricks. Despite how they might feel about the other knights, Obie was a good person. Charlie truly believed that.

Even if he had heard wrong, even if Merlin hadn’t had some sort of breakthrough, Charlie believed in his friend.

As soon as that thought crossed his mind, the door in front of Vetica slid open. She jumped back in alarm at the sudden movement, drawing her dagger instinctively.

“The first challenger has won. The second and third challengers may proceed.”

Orb bounced in the air. “Well, well, well, it seems Charlie was right. I guess the old conman really did have it in him. How do you think he did it? Doesn’t matter, I suppose…Merlin almost certainly gave his life to buy us this opportunity. We should presume him dead and leave town as soon as we save Mary without looking back. It’s what he would want.”

Charlie grabbed Orb out of the air. “Come on! Let’s go see the next room!”

Orb made a scoffing noise in Charlie’s head. “I’m going to start charging for rides. Three gold coins every five seconds.”

“Nope!” Charlie said.

“You can’t just say no!”

Charlie stuck his tongue out.

Orb flew up toward Vetica and paused. She was staring at the open doorway quietly. Charlie tilted his head as he hung. Vetica’s arm was shaking. He noticed it only for a second before she caught herself and turned to look back at them. “Ready?” she asked, a forced smile on her face.

Charlie didn’t want to let on that he’d noticed and embarrass her. He smiled back. “Yes, I’m ready!” She took him into her arms and nodded. It was possible she was just cold or something. But deep down, he worried if this had anything to do with the talk they’d had. With her doubts surrounding this new power of hers.

“Good, we’ll make quick work of this next round,” Vetica said, sounding more like herself.

“You noticed that too, right Charlie?” Orb thought-spoke only to him.

“Yeah.”

As they crossed into the next round’s room, they turned their attention to the second phase of the competition.

Beyond the doorway waited the next room in the tower. This one was nowhere near as large as Obie’s playroom. Where that room had been long and wide, this one was much smaller. It made sense, seeing that the tower would only decrease in size as they reached its top, but still, it was a dramatic change after seeing the previous room not so long ago. It was about the size of a bedroom in an average-sized house. However, what stood out was the unusually high roof. The room was two or three times taller than it was wide. Handmade paintings adorned much of the walls, leaving only a few inches of white in between. He wasn’t even sure how some of the paintings had been hung up. You’d need an extremely tall ladder to reach the roof of this room to hang paintings. The white painting on the walls was the other thing; these walls weren’t stone, like the rest of the tower. It looked as if someone had covered the walls in some other material and then painted it a fine white. It looked more like an art gallery than the room of a tower within a fortress.

But the most pressing concern was the man standing in the room’s center waiting on them.

Sir Thespin.

He stood there watching them as they entered and surveyed the room. His eyes fixated on Vetica immediately, then slowly lowered to acknowledge the baby in her arms. His rapier was sheathed at his side, and the look on his face was a sorrowful one.

He lowered his cavalier hat to hide his face for a moment. “So it was true, Sir Calda was right. The child really is the one Sir Brandt is after,” Thespin said, his voice strained. “I did not want to believe it.” He looked away and gripped the handle of his rapier tightly. “Why Vetica? Why have you chosen to side with evil?” Thespin looked back, as if it pained him, and stared her in the eyes. “Why would an innocent woman get involved with a child such as this? They say he controls beasts and manipulates the earth like a powerful mage! Surely you wouldn’t serve a devil such as that!”

Vetica gritted her teeth and scowled. “A devil? You’ve spent time around him. Obie has spent time around him. Did he strike you as a devil even a single time? Did you notice even a hint of malice in him? Or do you let Calda and Brandt do all the thinking for you?” Vetica sneered. “You’re just another mindless knight following orders. It disgusts me.”

She approached the third door and waited for it to open. It slowly grated open, revealing a second stairwell, the path leading to the final floor of the tower. The floor where Mary was also certain to be waiting. She sat Charlie down in front of it and then turned back to face the knight who had angered her. She drew her daggers and stared him down. “You don’t know anything about me, knight.” She uttered the last word in absolute revulsion. “The side of evil? Serving a devil? What a hypocrite you are to spit out such drivel while serving a man like Brandt. A man who condemned an infant to death for no fault of his own. What’s more, you presume to know me?” She cackled. “You called me an innocent woman? Well, I’ll have you know, hex knight, I’m not the woman you think I am. My name is Vetica Rawlin. Perhaps you’ve heard of my family.”

Even though she was no longer an official member of the Rawlins, since her father had stripped her of her family name, it seemed she wanted to dispel Thespin’s thoughts of her once and for all.

Thespin looked torn. “I don’t understand.” He eyed the dagger in her hand. “A Rawlin? You?” He shook his head before lifting a hand to cover one of his eyes. “That’s…impossible. There’s no way. My sweet Vetica is an assassin?”

Vetica’s hand flashed, and she took advantage of his covered eye to throw her dagger towards his blindside. He didn’t react. The dagger froze in the air mere inches away from his face and hovered there, as if caught by a veil of wind. It wasn’t surprising. It would never be that easy to take down a hex knight, least of all one of this level. After all, he’d been chosen to protect the tower for good reason. Sir Thespin was incredibly strong.

Sir Thespin removed his hand from his face and flicked it toward Vetica. Her dagger returned to her, skidding on the floor harmlessly and landing at her feet.

“I understand now,” Sir Thespin said. “I have heard of the Rawlins. How they raise children from a young age and turn them into deadly assassins to serve their house. Perhaps the Rawlins have tasked you with protecting this child, or perhaps what you say is true, and there is more to the situation.” Sir Thespin reached for his rapier and slowly unsheathed it. “That is all the more reason for me to draw my blade today. I wish to save you, Vetica of the Rawlins. But to do so, I must defeat you. I’ll demonstrate that you need not worry about retribution from your family, and if the child truly isn’t a danger, I’ll investigate myself. But today, I shall prove my strength and show you that I, Sir Thespin, can protect you until the end of my days!” He pointed his blade at her with conviction.

Orb sighed. “This guy’s an idiot. Are we good to go?” he asked.

Vetica turned to look at Charlie and his dungeon core companion. “Go, I’ve got this. Save Mary, okay?” she said.

Charlie nodded and turned to crawl through the door leading to the bottom of the stairwell. He remembered her shaking arm and turned to look at her one more time. “Are you sure you’re okay, Vetica? I don’t have to leave you alone. We can fight him together. I’ll find another way up the tower if you don’t want me to leave.”

Vetica blinked in surprise as his words. She walked over, and knelt before Charlie, patting his head gently with a hand. “You’re really sweet, Charlie. But I’ve got to do this. I’ve got to prove something to myself,” she said.

Charlie smiled. “Okay, I’ll be waiting. And Vetica…I believe in you. You’re going to win this! Just like Merlin!”

Orb sighed. “I’m still pretty sure he’s dead, so maybe don’t compare the two of them.”

Vetica smile deepened. “Of course I am. I won’t let you down, Charlie.”

Charlie smiled and turned to crawl towards the stairwell.

“And Charlie,” she called out as he did so. “We both know who you’ll find waiting for you at the top of the tower. Sir Gregory is strong. You can’t afford to hold back. Give him everything you have, get Mary, and get out of here. We’ll be right behind you, I promise,” Vetica said. With that, she turned to face her opponent.

The door to the stairwell began sliding closed as soon as Charlie had left the room. As it finally shut, he heard the voice of whatever was overseeing the matches.

“The Second Round of the trial will now begin.”
