As Leiande fell back, the whole rest of the army line charged on both sides. The central block of elites surged forward faster than the rest and clashed immediately. Spellcasters unleashed on both sides, and counters ripped away the worst, leaving AoE destruction that slew mostly the weaker on the flanks, breaking up the lines. On the Dominion side, the liches were slinging out the bulk of magic, but behind the lines, from afar, other wizards struck, too.

Hidden somewhere nearby, Dax’s [Battle Manipulation] flared, twisting fate and aiding the weapons of their side against their enemy, and far more survived the magic of liches and wizards than should have.

Above the fighting, the aerial battle bloomed, too, as the flyers clashed, the alliance side burning through undead and gargoyles with bursts of light magic and the charges of powerful mounts, including Azure and Deathsting leading and carving the way, on a collision course to the bone dragons, who were bound to make their move forward.

Samantha led with single-target spells flung at the undead champion, but each time, they were absorbed. She did her customary [Spellfyr: Unleash] at the end, but it faired no better. The substance of the creature was unharmed, as was his armor, though it seemed to smoke. From this, she got a new perspective on the defense waged — not absorption, but a shunting of some kind.

Others attacked him, but their weapons passed through. The Radiancers hit him with a volley, but it was no different than any other spell, getting shunted one after the other. It wasn’t customary intangibility, as that would not be enough against the weapons of numerous champions.

What is it and what does it mean?

Meanwhile, the champion tried to push forward, swinging his blade to create space as blockers were pushed back, and using his whip like a long-ranged sniper. He was trying to hit another Radiancer, but couldn’t get there, so he angrily and almost dismissively snapped down the whip ends on a knight. The ends came down in a rapid sequence, ripping through his shield, armor, and then him.

Shit, that thing is horrifying.

Zadkiel changed things up, punching his armor plate instead, deliberately — this certainly did something. The armor was unharmed through some holding enchantment, but there was a gong-like sound, and the undead champion was nudged backwards.

“Keep hitting the armor on that bastard,” came the order from Dax before Samantha could do the same. “It’s doing something, I think, and at least slows him down.”

Samantha was about to dive in and engage with her staff when numerous members of the Brain Trust practically screamed vetoes to the idea. Jeeves articulated over their screeching voices, “We’ve assessed you can’t help any more than the best martial champions in that fight, not without expending auto-successes. We need you against the liches.”

“What if that’s what is required?!” Samantha asked, still moving. “Auto-success is precisely why I’m a problem as a duelist.”

“It’s too valuable, and not guaranteed to defeat him. Not without more knowledge, and the Dominion is shrouding that from us as much as possible. Besides, there’s a connection with the liches.”

That last certainly made her hesitate. “A connection? To the champion?”

“His armor enchantment. Investigation is ongoing, however.”

Puzzling as it was, Samantha reluctantly obeyed, scanning the liches, using her actions to counter spells as she assessed and got a feel for when and where to strike.

She made another quick assessment while doing so, too. It was about time to give her own reinforcement of the primary line, which was taking heavy casualties, even among the elites.

Samantha zipped across space, falling back as far as she needed to — about 300 meters from the line. She then pulled out Contempt For the End, the ‘super buff javelin,’ and hurled it, causing it to erupt with light and emit its spirit-lifting drumbeat all down the line, its AoE divine buff spawning a cone downward for that entire depth of the troops.

At the front of the line, there was an immediate surge from the sudden toughness boost, which also included a special resistance against the number one issue up there: AoE magic. Their lines began to hold together and push against the undead discernibly.

This done, Samantha zoomed back to the chaotic scene at the front line elite melee, and then over and past it, to deal with the liches — or, likely, one singular fucking lich, because they weren’t going to go down easily. She sighed internally. It was going to be a bloody grind.

✦•············•✦•···········•✦

Riding her wyvern, surrounded by a new destructive aura, Azure first watched the flyers she passed by — effectively passed through — seize up as the energy raked them and disrupted their flight. The first sweep felled many undead out of the air. The second sweep caused them to avoid her entirely. All that could endure the aura presently were the more limited in number gargoyles, and these she either destroyed with exploding spell daggers, or they got ripped apart by Deathsting as they passed.

Routine stuff.

But the bone dragons were finally moving forward. Once a path was feasible to them, Azure, in coordination with Angels and griffin riders, surged toward one, intending to take out multiples before they reached the front lines. She had to take out at least two. Their aerial forces were rather scaled back to focus on the ground game.

The undead beastie was so much bigger up close — dwarfing her size, and even Deathsting to a degree. Nothing but thick, yellow bone but for eyes of glowing green, its wings flapping as if they helped, but its flight was purely magical. It undulated through the air, and not very fast. She flung spell daggers at it from afar, scoring surface splinterings of bone, but hardly doing much. In retaliation, as they got within range, the bone dragon opened its maw, and a cascade of acrid green smoke enveloped them, without much chance at avoiding it.

Azure grew over a layer of protecting crystal that was promptly burned through like acid by the Entropic damage. It ate further through, burning wound negations and then making its sting felt on her crystalline form underneath. It stung, and hit her with a minor reductive malus. Messages popped up about poison and disease. Good thing she was immune anyway!

We don’t want these fucking things breathing on our troops, that’s for sure.

Deathsting fared a little worse to absorb the damage, snarling as it ripped at his skin and muscle. He shuddered from head to tail but did not stop moving. Similar to her, the poison and disease stuff were useless against a living artifact.

They cleared the smoke and found themselves flying up to the bony form of the beast, now looming in front of them.

My turn.

She flung another special concoction of a potion she’d made before this battle, specializing in AoE-style fucking up undead. She’d been tempted to use it before, as it would take down a sizable cluster of the bastards, but with the endless hordes out there, she’d decided it was pointless. She was glad she’d saved the two she had, now.

The hurled potion exploded on the bone of a wing and erupted in a huge aerosol-like spray of anti-necromantic alchemical fluid, effectively acting like acid. The wing was all but disintegrated and dissolved, and the rest of the dragon’s back took a terrible scouring, the bone sizzling and smoking in continual deterioration. Disoriented by the lack of a wing and the rest of the damage, the dragon began to spiral downward.

Briefly, Azure vaulted off her mount, spinning to fling daggers at opportunistic gargoyles suddenly swooping in. Meanwhile, Deathsting went for the head of the weakened, staggered, and vulnerable bone dragon.

The wyvern’s jaw found a splashed portion of a neck vertebrae and he chomped down hard. The neck separated, and the bone dragon briefly let out a high-pitched screech before falling to the ground as two halves.

Deathsting quickly swooped back to pick Azure’s floating form back up, already smoothly going in the direction of the next target. They heard another screech and could just make out a downed bone dragon in the distance, and what she thought was probably the Naugite team, as led by the sorceress Odleian, one of Bast’s many lovers.

He may be lucky in that way, but I have the most desirable of all in my bed.

The bone dragons, despite their relative slowness, were getting closer to the front lines. By appearances, they were trying to converge on the elites.

Oh, hell no!

“Step on it, Deathsting,” Azure said, squinting and glaring at the nearest, unchecked target they were speeding toward.

“Cease your pointless demands!” the wyvern admonished. “You know I want to get there as fast as possible already.”

As they moved through the air, a waiting Resemblant intercepted, just long enough to touch Deathsting and heal his injury. Mostly. It was Entropic, so a lingering Minor Wound was not healable by the simple magic.

Worrisome, but as they came up on their target, it was from behind, and it had already been engaged by Angels, a few of whom it had injured with a breath attack.

As the Angelic charging flight passed overhead, Azure let Deathsting get as close as he could. Just as the bone dragon was turning its head backward and adjusting to a felt presence, Azure tossed her potion in the middle of its tail. Not ideal, but it was a free hit.

The explosive spray of acid raked it and made it writhe and twist up in midair, now trying to turn around to face them. Azure followed up with a slurry of continuous daggers into its face, even as she once again leapt from Deathsting.

The wyvern went for the throat again, this time facing some grappling resistance from the dragon’s claws as they tumbled downward through the air.

Aiming carefully this time, Azure timed a dagger throw for its swinging jaw. It hit, exploded, and pushed the head away at an opportune moment. Taking advantage, Deathsting’s jaws finally closed around the throat vertebrae. He was latched on, and the bone dragon could only try and rip him off, but failed, only scoring a claw rake over the wyvern’s body.

Several almost crocodile-like, powerful rips later, there were several snaps, and the head and separated body fell into the ranks of the undead army.

When she looked up, one bone dragon was breathing death out on their elites ahead. Azure cursed herself inwardly, though they could probably handle it.

Only two. If we had gotten three, it wouldn’t have happened. Great job, Azure.

Despite her frustration, her eyes shifted back to gaze at the tower. She could feel her affinity for that place growing. The enemy would no doubt think that was a good thing for them.

I guess we’ll see, won’t we?
