Darkness.

Not quiet-dark, not night-dark, but full blackout stage curtains dropped on my soul dark. It was thick, weighty, like being swallowed whole. No shape, no edge, no breath.

Then—splshh.

A rush of something slick hit me, soaking everything, or maybe I was the one being soaked. Water? It clung and pressed, shifting me. My—feet? They wriggled, twitching without command.

Ew. EWW. Did I just faceplant into a puddle?

Panic flared. Last I remember was hiking in the little woods behind my house. I had been so proud, a self-declared adventurer, stomping around with a stick I’d called a staff. Then my ankle rolled on a root. Pain shot up, white and hot, and I remembered falling—dirt in my mouth, leaves scratching my cheek before blacking out.

And now here. With an ankle twist. But this wasn’t an ankle twist.

Because my feet… wriggled.

My “feet” writhed in a way no human toes should. They pressed downward, searching, and with a strange satisfaction they slipped into the soil. The sensation was so alien.

Oh god. Brain damage? Coma hallucinations? I did hit my head, didn’t I?

I remembered Aunt Linda at Thanksgiving, going on and on about some soap opera where a guy was in a coma for ten years. I’d laughed at the time—“who wakes up after a decade and just… walks it off?” Well, apparently me. Except instead of walking it off, I’m planting it down. Thanks, brain. Great coping strategy.

...Okay, okay, deep breath. I still have feet. Right? Right?

The wriggling of my maybe feet didn’t stop. I didn’t want to think it. Didn’t want to accept it. But the longer I lingered, the clearer it became: my “toes” weren’t toes at all.  Thin things, stringy and eager, curling and stabbing into the ground like worms.

They burrowed, wove, dug. I felt the soil part for me, cling to me, suck me down. It should have been horrifying—okay, it was horrifying—but also weirdly… satisfying. Like scratching an itch I never knew I had.

Water. Wriggle, wriggle. Go deep. Deep. Roots. I was taking root.

Those weird thoughts surfaced again but I shoved them at the back of my mind.

I tried to lift something—anything—but there were no arms. Tried to scream—but no throat. Panic bubbled, sticky and hot, like boiling sap.

What is happening to me?!

And then—warmth came next. Gentle. Golden. It brushed across me like a blanket pilled over chilled skin. Light. The light.

Am I… waking up? Walking toward the light? Wow, even my coma hallucinations are cliché.

Something long and flat—some extension of me, not quite an arm—pushed upward, trembling, but something resisted—a skin, a shell. With effort, I pushed. A crack split through  my dark prison. Then another. And suddenly—I burst through.

Shell, skin, pod, soil. Whatever it was—it split, light bleeding in. Warmth poured down, hot and golden, threading into me.

I opened my eyes but pain hit me like a migraine. At first it was a blur, a nauseating swirl of brightness and shape. My mind reeled, trying to stitch the chaos into meaning. The disorientation made me dizzy.

But slowly, painfully, things began to focus. It wasn’t sight, not exactly—More like a sphere of sense, smeared and tilting in all directions at once. Too bright. Too loud. Too alive. My head spun—if I even had one.

Okay, what… what the actual hell.

Talk about a 360-degree view. I wasn’t looking around so much as existing in every direction at once.

A green roundness to the sky. A smear of green beside me that sharpened into a leaf—no, a giant leaf blade, so large I could curl my whole self under it. Everything was too big, alien, towering.

Never knew any blade of leaf could reach that height.

I looked—no, focused—down. At my body. And instead of hands and feet, I found… a stalk. Thin. Green. Flat. Rooted in dirt. Another blade rose beside me, wide as a sail, gleaming in the light.

Oh. Oh no.

…I’m—grass?

Silence. Then the thought hit harder.

Grass.

Do people hallucinate as grass? Is that a thing? Should I call medical journals from my coma?

But the thoughts of soil pressing against me, the strange awareness of roots digging deep—that this wasn’t imagination. It was too sharp, too coherent. My brain could never make up this.

I twitched. Tried to move, desperate to prove I wasn’t a prisoner of the soil. Whatever that means. And to my shock, that one narrow part of me—thin and flat, stretching into the air—quivered. Just a twitch, but it was movement.

You Learned a new Skill!

[G] Leaf Flutter Lv. 1

[ Sacrifice Evolution Credits for Complete System Connection: 1 / 50 ]

A little green screen flickered into existence, glowing in front of me.

…What.

No, no, no. This was way too close to those isekai RPG setups. Reincarnation, stat screens, skills. Was I actually doing the “other world” thing, or had I officially lost my mind?

The box just hovered there, smug and silent. I glared at it—mentally, since I didn’t have eye, Alright, green box, how does this actually work? Do I click you? Tap? Do I… blink at you really hard?

Nothing.

Oh great. My legendary starter skill.

There was still no response to my bootlicking. Maybe sarcasm will work?

Flutter. Amazing. Really strikes fear into the hearts of my enemies. What am I supposed to do, wiggle menacingly at them?

The leaf twitched again, more deliberate this time, like waving a finger. Strange, but also… grounding. I wasn’t just stuck. I could move. Kind of.

The green box pulsed again, glowing lines bleeding into my sight.

[G] Leaf Flutter: Move your leaf blade in response to stimuli.

[ Sacrifice Evolution Credits to establish Basic System Connection: 1 / 50 ]

Beside me, the massive neighbor-blade swayed gracefully, catching sunlight like a cathedral window. I couldn’t tell if it was a neighbor, a rival, or simply another unlucky soul like me, fellow victim of plant-ification? No clue.

Does it also have [G] Leaf Flutter? Still no clue.

I tried glaring harder, whispering encouragements, then sarcasm. Nothing. No extra menus, no magical tooltips. Just the same glowing text. Establish Basic System Connection? What did that even mean?

It felt like the system was dangling a questline in front of me without a walkthrough. Great. I’d read enough novels and played enough games to know how this was supposed to go. Except… wait. Maybe this was still Earth?

I tried again to reopen the green box, to force some kind of menu. Nothing. Maybe I was missing a magic phrase. Maybe I just needed to… wait. My leaf dropped as I cancelled the third time I used the skill. All that excitement of Fluttering my leaf had left me sleepy.

Fine. Rest it was.

Warmth seeped deeper, and the soil cradled me tight. Against all odds, against all panic, a strange calm spread. I was alive. Changed, tiny, ridiculous—but alive.

Naturally, that was exactly when the ground trembled.

At first, it was subtle. Vibrations shivered through my roots, jostling me in ways I didn’t know a body could be jostled.

The ground trembled again. Then came sound. Heavy. Rhythmic. Like thunder with knees. Thoom. Thoom. Thoom.

Panic flared. Earthquakes? Animals? Please not animals. Please. I’m fragile, I’m a literal lawn clipping right now.

At first, I thought it was just the world trying to shake me loose again—Mother Nature’s way of saying “you don’t belong here, lawn weed.” But no. The vibrations had a pattern.

And then I saw them.

Figures loomed through the blur of light. My newborn sight struggled, everything doubling, edges glowing. But slowly, agonizingly, the haze sharpened, and I realized the shadows weren’t landslides or animals.

People. At least two.

Oh. My. God.

Giant people.

Weirdly familiar people. One boy and one girl.

Except not giants. Just… regular-sized humans. It was me who was ridiculously tiny.

Robes fluttered around them—loose, elegant, ancient-style. Like something from those dramas my cousin, Katty binged at 2 a.m, the ones where everyone spent an entire season raising an eyebrow at each other over tea.

I remembered Lunar New Year, getting dragged to Chinatown, some dude trying to sell me a “genuine immortal-grade robe” for twenty bucks. I laughed then. Not laughing now. Because these robes? These were the real deal. Flowing sleeves, knotted sashes, embroidered hems. Sword-era, immortal-era. Definitely not Walmart-era.

The language they spoke rumbled the air, strange and heavy, syllables twisting against my mind. Almost recognizable, almost like Mandarin—but warped, stretched, and bent until the words were lost to me.

Still. Human. People. Real people. The recognition hit me like a sucker punch: these weren’t monsters. These were people. Human beings.

And I was… a plant at their feet.

Lucky me.

Thud.

The ground quaked as the boy stepped closer. His boot blotted out the sun, and in the next instant—

CRUNCH.

I braced for agony, shut my strange new sight—but no pain came. Just a rush of wind, my leaf fluttering wildly.

Peeking again, I saw my neighbor. Tall. Green. Smug. Bent nearly flat, folded in half under the heel. Crooked now, but alive.

“Oh-ho-ho,” I wheezed in my head, delirious with petty joy, trying to ignore how close I was to death. “Not so tall now, are you, buddy? Guess we’re both sidewalk stains today.”

The grass didn’t answer, obviously. Just swayed in the breeze, looking annoyingly dignified for someone who’d just been steamrolled.

I thanked every pantheon of gods that their path angled just a little away, their feet passing only inches to the side of me. Too close, too dangerous—but safe. For now.

The boy finally chased after the girl, panting. “#X&#*$GH—!”

Bye bye, giant nature destroying people.

The girl laughed harder, and with a playful shove, she pushed him back.

He stumbled.

Straight. Back. Toward. Me.

I didn’t even have time to process before—

CRUNCH.

One sandaled foot came down. On me.

Pain screamed through my blade as it bent, midsection snapping with a sickening sound only I could hear. My vision stuttered, fractured by agony.

Warning: Severe Damage.

Yeah, thanks, green box. Really on top of things. What’s next? A pop-up tutorial on how to stop hurting? Maybe a “Did you know your body is currently mangled beyond repair?” tooltip? That would be helpful.

Again, there was nothing from the glowing box.

The boy stumbled on, oblivious, still chasing his companion, their laughter fading down the path.

I remained behind, crushed into the soil, roots clawing desperately to hold me together.

Pain flared white-hot. My blade-body snapped halfway, like someone cracking a pencil.

My mind shrieked with it, all sensation screaming red, overwhelming, and then—blackness.
