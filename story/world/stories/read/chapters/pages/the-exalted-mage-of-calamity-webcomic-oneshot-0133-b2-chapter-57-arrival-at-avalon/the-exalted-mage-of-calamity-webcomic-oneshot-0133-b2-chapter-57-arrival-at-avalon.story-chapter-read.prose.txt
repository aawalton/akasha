The world changed drastically around them. No longer was there a night sky full of shining stars above, with forest trees hidden below. Instead, they hovered over a vast ocean, the scent of salt and sea air filling the wind. It was daytime, or at least, that was what it felt like. Thick blankets of mist covered everything they could see, shrouding the world around them except for small hints of blue water beneath the ship.

“Where… are we?” Gwen asked, walking back toward them and looking around with wide eyes. She stepped over to the nearest bulwark and looked down. “There’s water under us.”

“Is this Avalon?” Theo asked, looking around just as shocked. “Why is it so misty?”

Veronica looked around as well, noticing that nearly everything was covered by the white mist. Unlike before, where The Mirabelle had been floating a few thousand meters in the sky, they were now hovering only a few hundred feet above the ocean.

“This… should be where the Isle of Avalon is. But I don’t think we’re there just yet. We should be close.”

Nasha went to the bow of the ship and looked ahead. She squinted, but saw nothing of note.

Orban took a quick look behind them and furrowed his brow. The portal that had allowed them to enter was already beginning to close.

“Yer still got enough material ta open an exit, right?” he asked.

“Yeah. There’s enough for a full round trip, so we should be fine,” Veronica replied. “Gwen, go ahead and fly us straight forward.”

Gwen turned around. “You sure? We can barely see anything ahead of us.”

Veronica nodded. “Just fly slowly. We shouldn’t be too far from the actual island. The mist should start clearing once we get close.”

She nodded and ran back up the steps to the helm of the ship. The Mirabelle began moving forward, while everyone else kept an eye out for anything in the distance.

It wasn’t too long before they spotted it.

“Captain! I think I spotted it!” Gwen shouted.

“I’m standing right next to you,” Veronica said, clearly standing beside her.

Gwen smiled. “I just always wanted to say captain.”

Ahead of them, the mist began clearing significantly. Once they broke through, they spotted the island. Or at least, they spotted a portion of it.

The Mirabelle stopped as Gwen and Veronica approached the front of the ship alongside everyone else.

“This…” Orban murmured. “This is the largest barrier I’ve ever seen.”

They spotted the outer shore of an island, presumably Avalon itself. However, the entire isle was covered in a massive, opaque barrier. They noticed a few faint colors along with what seemed to be the ground and several trees, but everything beyond that was too difficult to discern.

“Does this cover the entire island?” Gwen asked.

“Seems like it. But the mist is making it hard to tell how wide this thing stretches,” Nasha said.

“So? What do we do here?” Orban asked, turning to Veronica. “Can we pass through just like this?”

Veronica turned to Nasha. “It’s your turn now.”

“What?” Nasha asked, looking at her in confusion.

“This barrier can be broken forcibly, but no one here is strong enough to do that. It can, however, be opened with pure spiritual energy.”

“Hah? Pure spiritual energy?” Orban asked, looking between them, almost offended. “How the blazes do you have something like that?”

“Got it,” Nasha said, already running down the deck to grab her bow. A moment later, she stepped toward the front of the ship. She drew an arrow and nocked it, taking aim.

Orban stood next to Veronica with his arms crossed. “Yer party is damn well bogglin’ my mind. First, ya got an airship from an archmage, and now ya got pure spiritual energy? How’d ya manage that?”

Veronica smirked. “Trade secret.”

Orban grumbled but didn’t press any further.

Using Selene’s Tear, which was set inside the necklace Veronica had given her, Nasha channeled its power into her arrow. The bow gifted to her by Viscount Leopold already had the ability to channel her aura into it. Now, she just needed to do the same with spiritual energy.

The tip of her arrow vibrated with a faint reddish energy before a clearer white energy traveled from Nasha’s fingertips and up the arrow’s shaft. With a release of her fingers, the arrow shot forward, whizzing through the air.

It crashed directly into the island’s outer barrier, causing a large cascading ripple across its surface. The arrow stuck firmly in it, suspended motionless in the air, as small cracks began to emerge around it.

“Gwen! Fly us forward!” Veronica commanded.

“Got it, captain!” Gwen shouted, running as fast as she could back to the helm.

Slowly, the entry wound caused by Nasha’s arrow expanded across the barrier. It eventually cracked open, spreading like fire through a field of grass. The arrow fell toward the water as Gwen piloted The Mirabelle forward through the widening gap.

More and more, the barrier quivered as it opened, froze, and then slowly began to repair itself and withdraw again. Before it could fully close, The Mirabelle successfully passed through as Gwen jolted the ship forward. This inadvertently caused everyone on the deck to stumble and fall over.

“Argh,” Orban mumbled, trying to stand back up. He grabbed onto the bulwark and lifted himself, only for his eyes to widen. “By the gods…”

One by one, everyone composed themselves and looked ahead. And for a moment, none of them spoke.

The mist had completely disappeared, and the island appeared before them in its full, grand entirety.

Veronica took a closer look with her augmented vision.

It truly appeared like a myth from old fairytales. Although it was called an isle, Avalon was not, in fact, a small island. Flying overhead, she spotted rolling fields of green forest, thick with large, ancient trees. Winding rivers stretched through the island like white threads. Cliffs of pale stone rose high and low, while fields of exotic, vibrant flowers spread across the land, some patches red, some white, and others… gold?

Farther inland was a grove of white trees that swayed gently. Their branches drooped in low arcs like willow trees, heavy with blossoms and yellow fruit. Petals drifted around them, dancing in the wind. They seemed to vanish halfway through the air and reappear elsewhere, as if they were not allowed to touch the ground.

Toward the center of the island stood a massive white castle, covered in overgrown trees like a shield. There was even a small drawbridge in the front.

Gwen stepped close to the railing. “This place… it’s beautiful.”

Theo’s mouth was wide open, yet he didn’t say anything, completely mesmerized. Even Orban, the mouthy dwarf, had no words.

“Are those… unicorns?” Nasha asked, looking down low. Near the shore of the island, there was a sandy beach that merged with grassy fields. And near the sands were horses with peculiar, large horns on their heads.

Gwen gasped, pointing at them. She looked toward Veronica. “Unicorns! There are actual unicorns here! Oh my god!”

“Alright, alright,” Veronica said with a slight chuckle. “Let’s calm down. We have to find a place to land. We can’t stay up here forever.”

After finding a clearing near the top of a large cliff, Gwen landed The Mirabelle. Everyone disembarked, carrying their prepared gear.

“Where to first?” Nasha asked, adjusting the gloves on her hands. “Are we heading to the castle?”

In front of Veronica, a transparent panel appeared. On it was a two-dimensional diagram of the entire island of Avalon. After reaching the fourth tier and obtaining the demon slayer system, Sage’s processing capabilities had been greatly enhanced. Using all the information she had read from Morgan’s book, along with Veronica’s brief inspection of the island from above, Sage could now display a map. If she wanted, she could even convert it into a three-dimensional display.

She looked at where they were, and her eyes moved to the right, toward a patch of the strange white willow trees. “No. Let’s make the castle the last priority. According to what Morgan said, the castle has many traps, so it’s probably the most dangerous place to go. Let’s explore the rest of the island first before heading there.”

“Then where to? I ain’t interested in nothin’ but this legendary forge you said should be here,” Orban said, arms crossed. Along his hip was his blacksmithing hammer, while a metal shield was strapped across his back.

Veronica smirked. “We’ll go to the place with the white trees first. Merrywind Grove. And you might change your mind, dwarf, once you see what kind of fruit these trees bear.”

“Hmph. I’ll be decidin’ that.”

With everyone prepared, they began marching forward. First, they had to pass through the forest in front of them. According to Morgan’s book, it was a place called Faewood.

It didn’t take them long to understand why.

The moment they stepped into the shade of the trees, the air around them changed. It smelled sweet and light, with a floral scent lingering underneath. Sunlight filtered through the canopy, shining upon small motes of light that floated between the branches.

At first, Veronica assumed they were insects. Then she saw one stop for a few short moments. It had the faint outline of a tiny humanoid figure. The instant it realized she was staring at it, it vanished behind the leaves.

More and more, she spotted brief flashes between the trees. Something small was following them, spying on them, disappearing just as quickly as they were seen.

“Um…” Theo glanced around. “Does anyone else see those?”

A yelp came from behind them. Immediately, everyone shifted, preparing for battle. Nasha drew her bow, while Orban wielded his hammer.

The scream had come from Gwen.

“H-hey! This isn’t yours!”

She had both hands wrapped around her staff. Something roughly the size of Veronica’s hand was pulling on the crystal set into its head.

It looked vaguely human. Female, perhaps. But the proportions were slightly off. Its arms were thin, fingers longer than expected, and two translucent wings fluttered behind its back. They beat with enough force that Gwen was actually being dragged across the ground despite their difference in size.

“Let go!” Gwen snapped.

The creature bared tiny teeth at her and pulled harder.

Veronica raised her finger at the small creature. A bolt of lightning burst through the clearing and struck it directly. Static erupted as its entire body convulsed with electricity before it fell to the ground, completely stunned, its wings twitching.

The sudden release caused Gwen to stumble and fall backward.

“Ow…” Gwen said, groaning as she tried to stand up while rubbing her backside.

Nasha walked over and stared down at it. “Is this a fairy?”

Theo offered Gwen a hand to help her up, while the others approached the fairy.

Veronica looked at it closer, and her status screen appeared beside her. Information about the fairy surfaced as Sage compared the creature against Morgan’s records.

“This is a greenwood fairy.”

Theo glanced toward the pale lights watching them from between the trees. “There are different kinds?”

“Several, apparently. Greenwood fairies live throughout this forest.”

“And they attack travelers?”

“Steal from them,” Veronica corrected. “Morgan says they have a particular fascination with gemstones, polished metal, and magical objects. Basically, anything shiny.”

Fully standing now, Gwen clutched her staff closer. “Wonderful. Now we have to watch out for little thieves.”

“What should we do with it?” Nasha asked. She reached down and pinched the back of its purple dress, lifting it up.

One of its eyes cracked open, but immediately closed again.

Veronica stared at it. “How about… we eat it?”

Suddenly, the little fairy jolted awake. “No.”

Veronica raised an eyebrow.

The fairy briefly glanced between the five of them before settling its gaze back on Veronica. “I taste bad. Really bad. Like, bitter-bad.”

“Really now?” Veronica asked, not buying it.

“I won’t steal anything else.”

“Uh-huh?”

The fairy began looking a bit more panicked. “I-I promise. I won’t steal from her again.”

“You just admitted you’re willing to steal from anyone else, then,” Nasha interjected.

“Oh…” the fairy said quietly, before pursing her lips. “Fine. I won’t try to steal anything from any of you.”

“That doesn’t really give us much reason to let you go,” Veronica said. She glanced around, noticing that there were still more fairies spying on them. “Especially with everyone else staring at us.”

“I’ll guide you then! I’ll make sure none of them approach you. I’ll take you to Merrywind Grove!”

“Hah? How does it know we’re trying to reach there? This thing spyin’ on us?” Orban grunted.

“You won’t reach Merrywind Grove if you go by yourselves.”

“And why not?” Veronica asked.

The fairy’s gaze flicked to their right, deeper into the forest. “You’ve been walking inside Faewood for eight minutes.”

“Yeah, and?” Orban grunted.

“Do you think you’re actually moving anywhere?”

“You sayin’ we’re stuck in this place?”

The fairy nodded its head. “The forest’ll trap you here forever if you don’t have a guide.”

Orban looked up and glanced at the others. Everyone else had the same expression.

“You know the way out, then?” Veronica asked.

It nodded. “Yes. If you promise to let me go, I’ll take you to Merrywind Grove.”

Sage. Any signs that it may be lying?

After a few moments of deliberation, Veronica agreed. “Fine,” she said. “But you have to lead us all the way. And help us determine which fruits are dangerous.”

“Deal!” the small fairy said.

Veronica nodded toward Nasha.

She let the fairy go.

It dropped a few inches before its wings fluttered, catching the air.

Orban narrowed his eyes at it. “This thing got a name?”

The fairy looked mildly offended. “Perripippa Ferripepper.”

“Perri, then,” Gwen said flatly.

The fairy frowned at her. “Perripippa.”

“I’m callin’ it bug,” Orban said, lowering his hammer.
