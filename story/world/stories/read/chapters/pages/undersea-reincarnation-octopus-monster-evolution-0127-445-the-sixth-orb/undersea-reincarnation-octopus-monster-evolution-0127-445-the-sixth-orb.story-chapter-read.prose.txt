Following the path toward the orb throughout the structure wasn’t much of a challenge, which Henry was grateful for. It gave him the opportunity to just relax and explore the decrepit corridors and the occasional chambers the two came upon, meandering their way down the floors and dispatching the occasional blob of toxic-green jelly. The path to the orb was pointing straight down, but Henry and Maurice were in no big rush. Whether they got the orb in five minutes or an hour wouldn’t make a big difference either way and honestly, Henry could use the time to center himself before heading down the dark depths. Occasionally, they attempted some short-range scrying casts. They had mentioned to Zerathstra their theories about recreating the turtle’s teleporting ability, and the ape had confirmed that such skills existed. They just had to keep working on it, though this spot might not be the best for it.

“Those things really ate everything, didn’t they?” Maurice said, lifting one of the B-rank, soccer-sized orbs of quivering jelly with Telekinesis while the rest scurried away. “This clearly had people who lived here, but there isn’t even a piece of cloth left.”

Henry agreed with the assessment. He had even located some spots where he believed plumbing had gone through the stone, but everything had been smoothed out. There was not one bit of metal, wood, cloth, or anything aside from the oddly smoothed out stone, which was obviously the work of the slimes as they ate their way through the structure.

“Clearly, the stone’s not their least favorite meal, as everything else had been cleared first.” Henry said, eyeing the odd creature as it recoiled from him. Speaking to them didn’t seem productive, as the slimes only flinched when he attempted communication, so after a couple minutes, the two put the blob down and continued their delve, going floor by floor, exploring the desolate, abandoned building and looking for anything other than mere gray stone.

Eventually, they found something. Near the exit of one of the many entrances, some decoration had survived, which Henry attributed to the slimes not wanting to be too close to the outside world. Robed figures adorned the wall, engraved into the stone to look down on whoever passed by, like sentinels staring down the newcomers. From that side, the tunnel led outside, but from the other, it led to a small antechamber with a high and wide doorway, leading into a massive, dark circular room with what had either been a basin of some sort, or an amphitheater. It was hard to tell, considering everything had been melted and eaten away by the slimes and right now, a handful of them were rolling into whatever water had collected through the cracks from above, splashing into the brackish water without a care in the world.

“What do you think’s behind it?”

The question pulled Henry’s attention away from the slimes and back to Maurice, who was staring vaguely at the doorway that led into the chamber. Henry blinked for a second, a little taken aback by the random question, then realized what the crab was speaking about.

Henry had been paying more attention to his own psyche and trying to readjust now that his life wasn’t at as much risk as it had been, but even then, he couldn’t really forget what he’d seen. This doorway was like comparing a cardboard box to a marble-walled mansion, but the principle of the thing was still attainable, and it reminded Henry of the massive door that had quarantined this… world? Reality? The door itself had called it a Sector, and while Henry knew the definition of the word, technically, he didn’t know what it meant within the context of multiple worlds and gateways into other universes.

For a moment, Henry considered what might be beyond the door. For starters, as the text had recognized that he was not of this sector himself, Henry’s old Sector would be somewhere behind the door. And while a quick visit might be nice, Henry found himself a bit more interested in what else was there. Other worlds just like this one, he assumed. But how many of them would there be? Hundreds? Thousands?

“Plenty of interesting places, I’m sure,” Henry said after a moment, and while he hadn’t fully shaken the funk he’d experienced yesterday just yet, he felt a bit of excitement at the thought of traveling to other worlds. It was a fun prospect. More fun than just blindly looking for the next big emergency to take care of so he wouldn’t let himself sit down and think. Henry wondered if his Avatars would eventually allow him to hop from one world to the next. Unfettered Echoes would need to attain a level beyond A-rank, at the very least. Which might be a bit of a challenge, if he were to believe Zerathstra. Doing so—if he managed it—might mean he’d ascend, which would have some dangerous implications none of them knew much about.

And speaking of implications, Henry thought, considering a different angle of their current conversation, only for Maurice to beat him to it.

“You think the System would let us? What about the people that made the System and the door that guards this sector? Do you think they’d let us through to explore? They must be strong to have created the System and the door. Do you think they created this world too?”

Without realizing it, Henry found himself chuckling at the nostalgic barrage of questions.

“Oh right. Do you need me to ask them one at a time?” Maurice asked, putting a gentle claw on Henry’s arm. “Was that too fast for you?”

Henry swatted at him with an arm, only for the crab to cackle as he Blinked out of the way.

“Too slow!”

Henry shook his head at the provocation, then considered the questions for a moment. “Well, whether they—or the System—let us through or not, it’ll be hard to guess until we speak to either. But there will likely be a process of some sort. The creators of the door obviously care about who goes where, else there wouldn’t be a massive door locking this sector out. As for whether they created this world or not, I’m gonna say likely not, though who knows. Remember how Attal mentioned that the System hadn’t always been around? And the mantises called it a Graft, which means it had been attached to this world, somehow. The creators of the door and the System are likely the same, but they did not create this world. Only… integrated it. Then locked it away, from the looks of it.”

“If they could create something as powerful as the System and the door that’s blocking the insects, why can’t they just beat them up?”

Henry eyed the bare stone walls around them. “That’s the question, isn’t it? I’m probably missing a lot of context, but I’m thinking if they went through the trouble of implementing the System, then bailed and locked it away at the first sign of trouble, then the answer to the question might be unpleasant. For us.”

Maurice floated up with flight, then settled on top of Henry’s head. “Hmmm. Maybe we’re not that important?”

Henry nodded. “It’s one possible answer. Defending this world might be too costly. Maybe the “trouble” of grafting the System isn’t much trouble at all, and they don’t want to deal with any of this. Maybe they’re in trouble themselves, either from these mantises or from something else. At this point, we’re just speculating without having the faintest idea of what’s out there or what the System’s creators can do,” Henry said, carrying them closer to the orb. “There are too many pieces of the picture missing, but we only need a couple to guess that the picture itself will have some dark corners.”

***

The search in the ruins had been fun, but eventually, the mostly eaten dark stone walls got old, and Henry and Maurice were ready to move on. Following the path the anchor ring provided them, the duo arrived in a wide room where in the corner, the black orb was being munched on by a slime, while a couple of others watched from the corner, waiting for their turn. Naturally, this had the sole effect of keeping the dark, obsidian-like orb clean of any dust or muck, as not even an A-rank could damage the things, much less the slime and so, with the sixth orb tucked into his Maw, only one was left. Exiting the ruins with Maurice wrapped in one of his arms, Henry glanced through the eyes of one specific Avatar as it continued to be battered by rain and giant waves. The storm had only grown wilder since he’d located this spot.

Taking a deep breath, Henry was considering how to approach the subject and obviously, this gave Maurice plenty of time to counter.

“I’m coming with you,” Maurice said, and his tone was firm. But Henry hadn’t said his piece yet, and so, he brought them both to the location in question and as wind howled in their ears, Henry laid it out. Whatever was down below, it had put even the Depth Lord on edge. Whatever it was, it would be dangerous, and now, the two of them were very much aware that Henry’s teleportation could be blocked. Stuffing Maurice into his Maw was a potential solution, but they had also observed that skill-suppression was a possibility. If Henry’s Avatar died, or if he himself died, they’d all be fine. But Maurice was another matter altogether.

Maurice listened, and Henry could tell he accepted all these facts readily. But his stance was not moved at all. The crab tried to shout his response back at first, but nothing but the rumble and crash of the waves could be heard. So the crab switched to telepathy. “[This isn’t the first time we’ve gotten into a dangerous situation, and I know you’ll keep me safe. So I’m going.]”

Henry stared up at the crab he was holding upside down, and Maurice’s gaze didn’t falter. The crab was resolute, and sensing his mood, Henry knew he there wasn’t much he could say that’d change his mind. Which… surprised him a little. “Why?” Henry finally asked. There must be something. Maurice didn’t usually take such hard stances out of the blue.

Maurice stared at him for a moment, then seemed to deflate for a beat before he started rubbing his claws together, peering past Henry at the roaring waves below. “[Because you’ve been off since we got back, and now’s not the time to go down somewhere dangerous alone. Plus, you know. It’s fun. A lost city and a big monster. What’s not to like? I want to see stuff like that. I love stuff like that. But also, we’re friends, and I wouldn’t be a good friend if I acted like nothing was wrong, right? I don’t know how to help, but at least I’m here if I figure it out. Or if you figure it out. Whichever comes first. You know?]”

Henry listened quietly, eyes glimmering in the storm. There was not much else to say, was there? So Henry gave himself a couple of seconds to recover as his breath had gotten a little rough, then the two of them dropped out of the sky and into the sea for yet another adventure.

“[Thanks,]” Henry simply said as a school of sharks scattered away from the hulking kraken, and Maurice tapped him on the arm.

“[No problem! Now, how long do you think it’s gonna take us to get down there?]”
