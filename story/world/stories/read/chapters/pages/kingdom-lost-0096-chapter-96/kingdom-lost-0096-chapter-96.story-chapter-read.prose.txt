Chapter 96

Nami had been crossing the yard since the soldier had come to the bunkhouse door.

He had not said much. He had said that the Warden wanted to see her, and he had said it in the tone soldiers used for things that did not require explaining, and Nami had set down the bowl she had been washing and reached for her shawl and told Mali to stay with the older girls and to be good. Mali had asked who wanted to see her. Nami had said the Warden. Mali had nodded as if that meant something she understood, and gone back to her doll.

The yard was busier than usual. Carts moved past her with timber and with sacks she did not recognize. Soldiers stood in clusters near the gate where none had stood earlier in the week. She kept her eyes on the door of the tower and did not let them wander.

She had crossed this yard many times, but she had never crossed for something like this.

At the door a soldier stepped aside and let her through without speaking. The ground floor of the tower opened into a long room with a heavy table set near the middle of the room. Chairs were pushed back from the table at angles that said the people who had been sitting in them had stood up not long ago. The air still held the warmth of a closed room that had been full of people. Nami noticed it the way she noticed weather.

Riley was standing at the window with her back to the room.

She turned as Nami stepped in.

"Close the door behind you."

Nami did. The latch fell into place with a small sound.

"Sit."

Nami crossed to the chair Riley had indicated, the one nearest the head of the table, and sat down. Her hands found each other in her lap and laced together without her looking at them. She did not know what to do with her face, so she just kept it still.

Riley did not take her own seat. She came around the head of the table and sat on the edge of it instead, half facing Nami, one boot on the floor and one swinging slightly. Closer than Nami had expected.

"There is a problem I have been carrying for a while," Riley said. "I have not done well by you. I am going to fix that this morning."

Nami's hands tightened into each other.

"You came here with Mali and you asked Nikola for work. You have been given work. You have done it without complaint. None of that work really challenges you or shows what you are truly capable of. That is on me. I knew it the day you walked into this settlement and it is time for you to show the community what you are truly capable of."

Nami did not speak and her brow furrowed.

"Do you know what we used at the compound—the fire."

"I heard," Nami said. Her voice was even and quiet. "The soldiers spoke about it when they came back."

"The mixture goes inside clay pots. Each pot needs a wick and a seal. The pots have to be made the right size and the right thickness and the right shape so the fire breaks them where we want them to break and not before. We will need many of them, they will be key to this community's future."

She paused.

"I want you to run that, from end to end. The mixture, the pots, and the entire assembly."

Nami did not move for a moment. Her hands stayed laced.

"Me?"

"You."

Nami looked down at her hands and then back up. The expression of her face did not change but something behind it had shifted.

"I have never run anything," she said.

"You ran a child through a forest across a country that wanted to kill her, and you did it without losing her, and you walked into a settlement of strangers with nothing on your back and asked for work the next morning. You have run more than most of the people in this settlement. You have what you need inside."

Nami sat with that.

"The clay comes from the river. The riverbed has a band of good clay that runs the length of the south bank. You will need labor on the bank with shovels and you will need carts to move the wet clay back to the kiln site. The kiln site goes outside the wall."

"Outside?"

"A kiln runs hot enough that if one goes wrong inside these walls we lose half of what we have built before the buckets reach them, and the clay is at the river. The kilns go where the work is and where the fire cannot reach us. Soldiers will rotate through the site for security. That is not your problem to solve. That is Thorne's and Valrik's. The site is yours."

Nami nodded slowly.

"The labor," Riley said. "The Hoshin who came with you and the Hoshin who have come since. Those are your people. You know them. You know which of them work clay already and which of them do not. You will lead them. They will answer to you and you will answer to me. The soldiers who come to the site for security answer to Thorne and not to you, but they will respect what you are doing there and they will know you run the site. I will make that clear myself."

"How many?"

"As many as you need. Start with whoever you trust and grow it as fast as needed. I will not put a number on it. You will tell me what you need and you will get it."

Nami's hands had finally unlaced. They rested flat on her knees. She was looking directly at Riley now, the still careful look Riley had seen across the firelight in the longhouse the night the villagers had chosen.

"What about Tama?"

Riley was quiet for a beat.

She had not been ready for that question and she did not pretend she had been.

"What about her?"

"She comes in the next migration. Or the one after. She has been the leader of the Hoshin for as long as I can remember, and when she arrives and finds her people working at a kiln site she did not choose, with one of her own running it, she may feel I am challenging her for that leadership."

The pause held.

Nami did not break it for a moment. When she spoke again it was slow and careful, the way she had spoken on the bench in front of the bunkhouse all those weeks ago when she had decided to stand up and find Riley.

"Tama is the leader of the Hoshin. The ones who are here are still Hoshin. If she comes and asks me what we are doing, I will tell her. If she asks me why I am the one telling her, I will tell her that too."

"And if she does not like the answer?" Riley asked.

"Then I will say it again."

Riley looked at her for a long moment.

"All right," she said.

"It will not be easy," Nami said. "Not at first. But I cannot do less than this because Tama might be uncomfortable with it. Mali is here. The Clawborn are coming. If clay pots help, then I will make clay pots."

The room was quiet around it.

"You will talk to three people today," Riley said. "Nikola about labor, carts, and the kiln site. Landryn about what goes inside the pots, how much, and when. Thorne about the security rotation at the river. I will speak with each of them before you do. They will already know you are coming."

"Today?"

"Today."

Nami drew in a slow breath. Her shoulders settled and her chin lifted a fraction.

"I will not let you down."

Riley smiled. It was a small smile and it did not reach all the way through her face, but it was a real one, the first of the day.

"You better not."

Nami stood. She smoothed the front of her skirt with both hands, looked toward the door, looked back at Riley once more, and crossed to the door without hurry. The latch clicked open and shut behind her, and the room was quiet.

Riley sat on the edge of the table a moment longer.

Then she stood and walked back to the window.

Nami was already crossing the yard. She did not walk faster than she had on the way in. She walked the same speed. But her shoulders sat differently, and her hands swung at her sides instead of clasped at her front, and she was looking at the yard around her as she crossed it instead of holding her eyes on the door at the far side.

Riley watched her until she reached the work yard and turned out of sight.

Then she turned from the window and went to find Nikola.
