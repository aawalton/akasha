Around two months after Nahar’s death, in a small settlement that had miraculously gone nearly untouched by the disaster, two men sat outside on a bench, smoking and chatting away. “The rumors say he’s the one who did it.”

“Freddy?”

“Yeah. He’s the one who finished him off.” The man laughed. “As for how much he did personally, it’s hard to tell. The rumors make it sound like he almost single-handedly killed Nahar, but that’s absurd.”

The other man snorted, looking away with a conflicted look in his eye.

Harold looked over at Mark, his serpentine gaze flickering with its usual blend of amusement and cruelty. “He’s got a lot of potential, you know,” he goaded. “Regardless of how much truth there is to the rumors, he’s definitely helped some. And for a two-star to stand up to a four-star and live to tell the tale… That’s not something you see every day.”

“He’s under a Triple Block,” Mark reminded. “And he’s got soul damage now, too. He won’t even reach the third star, let alone go past that.”

Basilisk shrugged. “You didn’t think he’d be coming back alive from that mining operation, either. Say, remind me what happened with that again?”

Mark snorted. “Right. I think this is a bit more extreme.”

“Anyway…” Harold steered from the topic. “Things are starting to calm down again. It won’t be long before we’re back to business as usual.”

“Business? What business?” Mark scoffed, nerves clearly stretched. “Everything has been razed to the fucking ground. We basically have to start from scratch.”

Harold smiled. “Have you ever heard of serotiny, Mark?” He got up to his feet, eyes glimmering as he looked over the snowy landscape. “I’ll show you what happens to pine cones after a fire.”

Days passed by. The Northern Belt was still a wreck, but slowly, life was starting to return to it. With the head of the disaster being cut off, it wasn’t much longer until the horde was officially declared as over, and the two were free to move again.

Repentawa was an obvious choice. It had rapidly become the focal point of the entire Belt’s future. People were migrating there even from distant settlements. However, Basilisk had no intention of moving to the city any time soon. His enmity with Freddy was well known at that point, and right about everyone would be looking to step on his toes to try and look as good as humanly possible.

As was often the case in life, the center of attention wasn’t the best place to be—being right next to it was better.

There was only so much real estate inside the Repentawa walls. Most of it was already filled up. Expansion was already well into the phase of getting vertical, be it up or down, and before long, people would start turning to internal expansion to keep the space growing.

At that moment, those who had already settled in the city were going to benefit greatly, while those who hadn’t would have to pay out their ass to get a single foot in.

Basilisk wasn’t going to be on either side of that game.

The disaster aside, the Northern Belt was still just the Northern Belt. Hordes like that were rare, and with the massive monster purge that happened with the mass migration this time around, it would be a long time before it was actually dangerous to live in an unwalled settlement.

Simply put—there was nothing wrong with living close to Repentawa. In, say, one of the many settlements that was starting to crop up. But that wasn’t enough.

After several days of flying above Repentawa with the carriage, they got a much better idea of what the future development of the area would look like. There were places that were more convenient, and places that were less convenient. Over time, parts with relatively easy access and quick routes would explode in growth, while those who were just trying to get as close as possible would stagnate.

Before long, they settled on an area that was a bit further than where most were settling. It was a large valley, tucked behind a massive mountain. In the eyes of many, that made it a horrid place to settle, as having to go around the mountain made routes too inconvenient.

That was a problem that was easy enough to solve for two nearly-peak three-stars with the earth affinity.

Around a week of work later, there was a brand new, massive tunnel leading straight through the mountain. Harold and Mark had carved it out entirely by themselves, in order to keep it a secret.

The empire had a lot of unclaimed land. When it came to claiming it, the process was relatively simple in theory.

As long as you paid your taxes, you could claim literally any amount of land you could physically develop and protect. That being said, there was a catch. When one claimed land, one became subject to the nearest official.

For non-officials, this could get a bit loopy. For starters, they were subject to the nearest Lord-appointed official, who was subject to said Lord, who was subject to whatever Overlord the area was under, who was subject to the empress.

Now, theoretically, if everyone involved was a sane, rational individual, this shouldn’t be a problem. Everyone up the chain could set reasonable terms, and voila! Maximum development and profit for everyone!

In reality, the Lord-appointed official was usually a power-crazed lunatic, the Lord was a greedy bastard, and the Overlord was a complete maniac.

There were fifteen layers of wacky taxes, a mountain of bureaucracy, rules upon rules, too many of which resulted in getting the death sentence, and of course, none of this shit was actually applied with any degree of seriousness, and whoever was up the chain could, and often did, make arbitrary demands of everyone below them, applying said headache-inducing rules entirely by whatever mood they were currently in.

But this was the beauty of subordinate territories, and the main reason why Basilisk decided to go there.

All subordinate territories were directly subject to the empire. And the empire’s rules, while harsh, were at the very least applied consistently, which massively simplified the entire process.

They got to work as subtly as they could, recruiting workers from neighboring settlements, locating the nearest passages, building infrastructure, developing land, setting supply chains, preparing marketing, and of course, killing anyone who sniffed out what they were doing and tried to capitalize on it without cooperating with them.

For a lot of the work, the two of them did it themselves. Just as with the tunnel, they put their earth affinities to work, which drastically sped up the progress.

Mark’s second talent wasn’t quite Telekinesis, but it wasn’t… not Telekinesis, either. It could be used as such, and that was enough. The two of them got down and dirty. Mark moved snow in bulks, and Basilisk used an ability to turn vast chunks of earth into slithering snakes, which then distributed themselves into depressions to flatten the ground as much as possible.

Once the bulk of the property was flat and etched out into plots, they set the roads and sidewalks, then hollowed out the path of the future sewers, which would take to the nearest river.

It wasn’t too long before Mark started bitching. “Why the hell are we doing this shit, anyway?” He barked, wiping the sweat off his forehead, the strain of abusing his talent to the limit catching up with him.

“What’s the problem?” Basilisk provoked with a smile as he used another ability to turn earth to snakes, then made them move out of the way, widening the storm drain channel even further.

“I think we have better things we could be doing, don’t you think?” Mark crossed his arms. “I understand the tunnel and the need to keep that a secret, but shouldn’t we be leaving this work to non-combatants?”

“There is no such thing as a non-combatant, my boy,” Harold said sagely, smirking. Then, he suddenly turned serious. “There are two kinds of decisions you can make in life. Those that earn you power, and those that squander it. You should rid yourself of that kind of mindset as soon as possible. Nothing is below you, Mark, and you’re not above anything.”

Pointing to the workers who were working on the foundations of the city-center skyscrapers, Harold said, “See those workers over there, Mark? Those are some of the most important people who will ever set foot in this city.”

Mark scowled. “That’s a bunch of construction workers, Harold.”

“Exactly.” Harold cast another ability, further widening the channel. “See how they turn to look at us every once in a while? That’s why we’re the ones doing this. They’ve noticed how good I am at building infrastructure. I’ve impressed them, even. In their eyes, they don’t see just another vulture trying to capitalize on the Repentawa craze—they see a diligent, hard-working pair of responsible leaders, who are willing to do what it takes to ensure that the city that will once stand here is built on solid ground.

“Such people talk. And that talk will set the tone for this entire city’s future. There’s an entire swarm of greedy idiots rushing to be Repentawa’s biggest neighbor, and they’ll be cutting corners everywhere they can. In contrast, we’ll look stellar. That inspires trust, and trust fast-tracks the process of legitimization. Legitimacy brings people, people bring business, business brings money. It’s as easy as that.”

Mark frowned. “I’m sorry, but I don’t buy it. I know you. You’re a rotten bastard to the core, and this seems a little too—”

“Fair? Proper? Just? Legal? Ethical?” Basilisk smiled. “Is that really the only thing you’ve learned from me so far? That cheating is the best method available?”

“Yes.”

Basilisk laughed. “Then you weren’t paying enough attention. Listen to me, and keep this in mind with anything you do in life—business has nothing to do with moral values. This doesn’t mean that you have to maximize immoral tactics. In fact, the moral choice is very often the best one you can make, for everyone involved, and that includes you. Don’t be immoral—be amoral.

“Being a prick for the sake of it is a kind of ethics in its own right, and ethics clouds your judgment. There are things that benefit you, and things that don’t. That’s all there is to it.  Now back to work, Marky boy. We’ve got snow to plow and drains to dig.”

Mark remained stunned for a long moment, a conflicted look on his face. He slowly nodded, and they continued work.

For a couple of months, everything went perfectly fine. The yet-to-be-named settlement attracted investors, and once Basilisk had a legal chokehold on the biggest players, they revealed the tunnel. Within weeks, the population exploded.

Of course, never half-assing his methods, Basilisk went on a shopping spree, buying out failed investments in many of the other project settlements, and then instating policies that further drained said settlements of life, which accelerated the rate at which businesses migrated to them.

With the pieces slowly falling into place, Harold slept like a baby at night.

Until one night, he had something of a nightmare.

He opened his eyes, finding himself standing within a dark space. Before him was a tall figure shrouded in darkness.

Basilisk grinned. “You know, I always wondered why it was that you were taking so long. Did I not give off the impression of a sellout? I’m a real sellout, you know. The man with a price. Can you read my mind? I’m pretty sure that should be impossible, but on the off chance you can, you should know what I’ve done to my parents. If I did that, imagine what else I’d be willing to do!”

The creature showed no reaction. After a few long moments of silence, it said, “I know your tendencies well enough, snake. Quite the opposite—I merely set you aside for when the need arose.”

“I’m flattered. So what can I do for you? Keep in mind, my soul’s already reserved for the devil, so I can’t give you that. But maybe more importantly…” He playfully twirled his fingers. “What are you willing to part with?”

“I need you to kill Freddy Stern.”

Basilisk froze. Then, he burst into laughter. “Seriously!? That wasn’t even in my top twenty guesses for what you’d ask of me.” He turned serious. “Why?”

“That is none of your concern. Either you take the deal, or not.”

“Is he that good? He totally killed Nahar, then. Are you afraid he’ll grow in the future and fight back?”

“Do not play your games with me.”

Basilisk paused. He was putting on quite a bit of confidence, but frankly, even he was a bit freaked out about whatever this thing was.  “Fine then. I’m not… against doing it, but you see, ah…” He sucked air through gritted teeth. “That’s gonna be a bit of a problem. He’s become something of a bigshot in the government, and killing him will be a massive chore, you know what I mean? I’d agree to, say, a second talent?” He suggested teasingly. “The kind you gave Mark, tho. None of that shit you did to Nahar.”

The figure snorted. “That is a limited resource, even to someone such as I. But do not worry about payment—I have something you’d be far more interested in.”

“Than a second talent? That’s a big claim to make. What could possibly be more valuable than a second talent?”

“Information about the next step of interspace integration.”

Basilisk turned deathly serious. “You’re gonna have to give me some kind of guarantee that whatever you say is the truth.”

“That is simply part of how this deal works. For when we agree to something, neither will be able to renege on the deal. You will do as I say, and I will deliver on my word.”

He remained silent for a few minutes, mulling over it. This was a damn big deal.

Interspace integration didn’t end with the Rift. It was well-known that, over time, things have been gradually changing, and that wouldn’t stop being the case. Access to Faralethal, Great Anomaly appearances, shifts in ether density—knowing the next big change was an advantage whose value couldn’t be measured. It was, as the thing said, a better offer than just a mere second talent.

“I just have one question,” Basilisk said, pointing at his face. “The eyes. Will my eyes change?”

The creature nodded. “For as long as your part of the deal has not been delivered, the mark will remain. That is a trait that cannot be altered.”

“But I’ll be able to hide it once my job is done?”

“Precisely.”

Basilisk nodded. “I’ll take the deal, but not today. You’ve no doubt been using this crap forever, so you should already know that everyone’s checking for freaky eyeballs. I’ll prepare, and when I’m close to delivering my end of the deal, we’ll talk again.”

“It is always a pleasure to work with the wise. Very well. Until the day arrives, prepare. Wish it, and we will meet again.”

He woke up. Grinning, he got up, eyes full of vigor.

Since that night, he did something truly unusual—he put in actual effort into fighting. He delved day after day, taking on some serious challenges.

When one’s star was reverted, they went back in progress. That progress could be earned back relatively easily by gathering. One could only regain exactly what they lost, however. Only if one decided to sever would they be able to change the outcome of the ascension. But that was naturally far more difficult.

Mark got suspicious soon enough, but to his credit, he didn’t actually ask. It was clear that the kid didn’t exactly like Basilisk, and if he was eager to get back to the fourth star, which would get him kicked out of the Northern Belt, Mark was only too happy to cheer him on.

It almost broke his cold, dead heart to see the kid filled with such hatred. But oh well.

For nearly an entire year, Basilisk grinded away at earning back his lost progress. He could have done it a lot faster, but he tried to keep the risk to a reasonable level. Regardless, he made it.

One day, he woke up, and met Mark. The kid smiled broadly at him. “Back to the fourth star already?”

Basilisk shrugged. “Yup. They’ll be dragging my ass away later today, and I’ll be forced to go to the frontlines for a while. Getting that Lordship back won’t be easy.”

“Interesting you decided to go back so quickly,” Mark noted. “I expected you to take much longer.” The hidden question was obvious.

But Basilisk simply ignored it. “This place is too cold for me,” he said jokingly.

And that was the last they talked.

As expected, Basilisk was hauled away. He was brought before a minor council in Nova York, where they decreed that he would be sent to the Caribbean archipelago to serve until he could be reinstated as a Lord. It was a lovely place to be sent to war, and it was clear that the empire was being very lenient with him.

His service was as uneventful as he expected. There was some passage clearing, a bit of rebel subduing, and the slaughter of several islands’ worth of dissidents. Nothing out of the ordinary for a Lord trial. Far easier than the last one he was subjected to.

Before long, he found himself at a place he’d been seeing in his dreams. The absolute frontier of human progress, and the location of his target.

He found his way to Starhold.
