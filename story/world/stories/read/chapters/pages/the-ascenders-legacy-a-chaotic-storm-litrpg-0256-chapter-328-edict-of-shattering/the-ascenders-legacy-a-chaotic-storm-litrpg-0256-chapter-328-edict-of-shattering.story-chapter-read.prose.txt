What is Authority?

Well, it wasn't power, as Aodhán had very recently learned. Power could be resisted. It could be overwhelmed, countered, or shattered by someone stronger.

Authority was something far more insidious.

It was an invisible agreement between reality and the one who spoke it.

A king did not rule because he was strong. He ruled because when he gave a command, the world behaved as though he had the right to do so. A judge did not end a life with strength but with recognition—recognition that his words mattered more than the will of the condemned.

That was authority.

And as Aodhán tested out his new skills against the wall of his training room, he realized that he was treading a path no known person in existence had ever trod. Every other path either bent the storm to the cultivator's will or the cultivator to the storm's. Some even became the storm.

But this path would lead him in a more intricate direction.

He wouldn't just be commanding lightning. He would be deciding who was allowed to command it at all. And he couldn't help but wonder just how far he could push it.

So far, he'd been able to completely overpower Darien, who was more than a dozen tiers above him, and completely restrict Crixis from accessing his abilities at all.

It was mind-blowing what his newfound authority allowed him to do.

But slightly troubling was the uncertainty of how far he could push it. He needed to test himself against a stronger opponent. He needed a life-and-death battle to understand his full limit.

And he knew exactly how to go about it.

It was time he went rift delving.

Thankfully, the sect never slept. Nor did their rifts.

He glanced at Varéc, who was coiled up in a corner, brooding—he had been doing that a lot these days—and smiled. "How would you like to go on an adventure, broody?"

Varéc looked at him without expression, but the excitement simmering within their psychic bond couldn't be denied. Aodhán's smile widened. "Come on then, we don't have all night."

They stepped out of the house a moment later, shut the door behind themselves, and flew toward the rift verge, Varéc's silhouette stretching across the earth like a living storm cloud.

Aodhán had never seen the Verge before, but he'd heard enough about the place that finding it wasn't hard at all. The Verge was a collection of stabilized rifts, located beyond the residential area of the sect, barricaded by a huge, rune-reinforced wall to keep any rift break in check.

The rifts themselves were very different from dungeons. Rather than portals, they were scars in the fabric of reality leading to worlds and realms unknown. They weren't color-coded like dungeons usually were, but each rift was wrapped in rotating rings of spectral light whose number, thickness, and shifting hue served as a living metric of class, affinity, and danger.

There were about thirty of them, Aodhán noted from his position in the air as they drew closer to the Verge, each one at the advanced class, which meant the weakest creature he would find in any of them would be at the 25th tier.

That was fine by Aodhán, and with a determined expression, he descended to the verge's entrance, where an elder stood guard, their obsidian robes not quite as shiny as those of the other elders, ranking him as a low-level Mythic.

He had most likely been promoted straight from the outer sect to the elder sect following his evolution to the Mythic class—and his strength, in all likelihood, was lacking. That didn't, however, mean he was undeserving of respect.

"Good evening, Elder," Aodhán bowed as he climbed down from Varéc.

"Evening, special disciple Brystion," the elder greeted him with a smile. "You've come to delve?"

"Yes." Aodhán presented his ringed finger to the man and paid the entrance fee of 20 SP. "Can I just pick any one?"

The elder nodded. "Within reason. Every rift you see in there is coded to a different affinity, with some even containing sub-affinities, and they all range from the 25th to the 99th tier."

"But?" Aodhán asked, pulling his arm back.

"But as you well know, tier isn't the only definer of power. The creatures in some of these rifts are harder than others to deal with due to their nature, their environment, or their affinities. So yes, you can indeed choose any rift you like, but caution is advised."

Aodhán nodded in understanding. "Do I get an emergency exit item? Like an ejector?"

The elder shook his head with a chuckle. "Transmigrants are expected to be smart enough to know when to turn back. Those who rely on crutches never survive true battle."

Aodhán grimaced slightly. He had hoped things would be like they had been in Lutia, where he could simply eject himself when the going became impossible. Having to rely on himself was, well… unreliable.

His grimace tightened, but he nodded regardless. "I'll be careful."

"Try to be," the elder said as he gestured for Aodhán to go in. "The sect would dearly hate to lose you, but it's not our policy to be overprotective. Chicks must learn to fly, after all."

"And if they fall in the process?" Aodhán asked.

The elder regarded him silently for a moment before speaking. "They won't fall. Now, come in. Which rift would you like to delve?"

Aodhán stepped into the verge, breath hitching as he walked past the barricaded wall into the Verge proper.

Thirty different rifts stared back at him.

Each one a different promise of death, growth, or both. Varéc followed behind him, his scales gleaming under the light of the rifts arranged before them in rows of ten.

"So, which one would you like to pick?" the elder asked again, a proud smile on his face as he gestured to the first rift. "Can you read their affinities?"

Aodhán nodded. The first rift was a fire rift, its fiery red rings circling its entrance gently. It would be interesting to delve into a fire rift, but Aodhán wasn't inclined to deal with fire-breathing creatures at the moment.

The second was a wind rift, the third, a space rift, the fourth, a blood rift…

A few seconds passed in silence as Aodhán walked past the first row of rifts and then the second, eyes narrowed as he scrutinized each one carefully.

The rifts were all in the same class, yet they radiated different levels of power and danger. At first, Aodhán thought perhaps the theory that certain affinities were stronger than others would finally be proven, but it turned out that wasn't the case.

Those rifts simply contained more monsters.

"Swarms?" he asked as he stared at the nature rift.

The elder chuckled. "And hordes. These kinds of rifts are usually more challenging, but they do grant a wider variety of opponents."

Aodhán nodded, regarded the rift a moment longer before moving on to the next—a deep purple rift with crimson and black spectral rings rotating around its crackling entrance.

It was a void rift, and though he had very little experience fighting void creatures, Aodhán felt his spirit hum in agreement.

"I think I'll delve this one," he said, and the elder smiled.

“That’s a good one. You have five hours. If you're not out by then, you'll owe me another 20 SP."

Aodhán chuckled. "I'd rather not do that."

The elder snorted. "That's what you all say. Be safe, special disciple Brystion. I'll expect you in five hours."

He left then, leaving Aodhán and Varéc alone, and with a steadying breath, Aodhán glanced at Varéc. "Are you ready?"

Varéc grunted in response, and Aodhán smiled. He climbed back on Varéc, and with a look of equal parts excitement and dread, they flew into the rift.

Their arrival was instantaneous.

One moment, they were in the Verge, and the next, they found themselves in a world of decay and nothingness. The sky was a hemorrhaging wound of crimson and void-black, the remnant of what seemed to be an apocalypse of great proportions.

From those cracks poured streams of absolute darkness, cascading downward like inverted rain, eroding the obsidian ground wherever it touched. There was no sun or light within the realm save the fragmented gleams of crimson and void light shimmering from the cracked sky.

The air hung heavy with void essence so thick it felt like breathing in poison, and with each breath Aodhán took, his lungs burned. Eyes watering, he scanned the terrain below—endless cracked stone, ash-choked flats, and half-melted pools of thick black liquid that hissed softly as they devoured the ground around them.

There were no hills. No ruins. No shelter.

Just a flat, collapsing wasteland that offered nowhere safe to land. So Aodhán decided he would have to create one for himself. With a simple flick of his fingers, a massive platform of compressed storm condensed beneath them, crackling with restrained lightning, and they settled onto it as the void gnawed uselessly at its edges.

Whatever creatures lived in this rift couldn't be easy prey. But that was one of the reasons he'd chosen the rift in the first place. He had too little experience with void creatures, and he needed to rectify that.

With another shuddering breath, Aodhán expanded his core sense to its maximum range, needing to get a better understanding of the rift's layout and the creatures within it. He'd been more than prepared to find a few creatures lurking around him. He was in a rift, after all.

What he hadn't expected, however, was the explosion of blazing cores that appeared in his mind as core sense expanded. There were hundreds of them, all between the 25th and 50th tiers, invisible to the naked eye, and gathering around them.

Aodhán realized what was going on immediately, and he smiled. "Oh, that's clever. I wouldn't have noticed them at all if not for core sense."

Varéc growled low, scales arcing with lightning as he also sensed what Aodhán did. But rather than fear, Aodhán only felt excitement bubble within him.

This was an opportunity for him to add a few more bodies to his Dread Tempering Counter.

But beyond that, it was time to put his new path and skills to the test.

With a wide smile, he raised himself higher into the air, lifted his hands to the shattered sky, and summoned a colossal storm into existence.

It was time to test just how much authority he wielded, and what better way to do that than by using one of his oldest and favorite skills?

Varéc roared, thunder boomed, and the creatures below screeched, but Aodhán's smile only widened in excitement as he channeled a ton of energy into the skill and whispered,

{Lightning Descent}.

***

Thunder boomed across the shattered sky as red lightning rained down in streams so large it would have left anyone in the advanced class speechless. Even Champions would have had to pause, not because they couldn't do the same, but because it was one thing to create so much lightning and another thing entirely to actually control or direct it.

But Aodhán wasn't just controlling it perfectly—he was doing so seamlessly, his face a mask of excitement as he rained down destruction on the creatures below. Varéc joined in immediately, a roar of excitement ripping out of his throat as he descended on the invisible creatures, a condensed beam of black electricity spilling out of his maw in a deluge of decay and destruction.

But the creatures were not so easily defeated. Though invisible, the wave of nothingness spilling out of their bodies was anything but, and as both lightning attacks fell—skewering some and simply ripping others apart—a large portion of them were swallowed up by the wave of nothingness spilling out of their bodies, simply eradicated into a void.

An attack that should have killed hundreds only killed dozens. But Aodhán had more than enough willpower and control to spare.

Eyes narrowing in determination, he raised his arms to the storm once again, chaos and willpower gathering within his spirit. His last attack had been unfocused, wild, and untamed—traits common to those following the path of tempest and unpredictability.

But he was no longer that person. He was now a follower of the path of ruinous authority, and he needed to start fighting with the authority he bore. His gaze hardened as more and more of the invisible creatures rushed into the range of core sense, gathering at the sound of battle.

But Aodhán was unfazed. With perfect will imbuement, he channeled two dozen strands of chaos-energized willpower into {Lightning Descent}, infused them with a thread of quintessence, and pulled his arms down in a single swift motion.

It wasn't an activation.

It was a command. And the storm broke to obey.

Thunder cracked across the sky with a sound so loud Aodhán himself was temporarily deafened. The storm shattered, and as if the sky itself had broken open, lightning poured down—not just red, but also black, green, violet, white, and even silver.

It slammed down into the creatures like judgment itself, and this time, there was very little their anti-magic auras could do to stop it. Screeches rose into the air in dying pleas, but they only lasted a moment as lightning surged through their ranks, unrelenting until every single creature was utterly vanquished.

Even for Aodhán, who had unleashed the attack, it was awe-inspiring to see just how much his power had grown in less than a year. When he'd first created this skill, it had barely been able to kill a tier 1 Rithclaw on its own. But now, it had just wiped out hundreds of creatures in a single wave.

Every creature within Core Sense's range was dead. Gone. Ripped apart by lightning. It was his doing, but it also wasn't.

He hadn't controlled the lightning or targeted any single creature. He had simply commanded destruction, and the storm had obeyed. This was Authority. This was his new path.

A wide smile broke across his lips as notifications pinged in his mind, but Aodhán dismissed them, not ready to view them yet. Instead, he glanced at the bodies of the creatures that had attacked him, their invisibility gone now that they were no more, and grimaced.

The creatures were abominable—lean, hunched beasts shaped like massive hyenas with tiny batlike wings jutting out of their spines. They weren't like any other creatures Aodhán had seen and instead looked like the results of a chimera experiment gone wrong.

Varéc descended to the ground, his claws ripping one of them apart to reveal their void cores.

Aodhán smiled. "Well, I'm sure we can sell that somewhere."

He bent down to do the same, a knife construct appearing in his palms to slice the nearest creature open. Before he could, though, a rumble thrummed through the earth—a furious vibration whose intensity grew with each passing second.

Aodhán looked up, eyes scanning the horizon for the source of the sound. He found nothing, of course, but he only had to wait a moment before he sensed them—a massive horde of the same creatures he had just dealt with, only larger and far more powerful—not even in tiers, but in the blinding radiance of their cores.

The creatures he had just defeated may have been strong, but it seemed he was just about to face their elite members. And this time, Aodhán didn't think a single skill would be enough to do the trick.

He and Varéc floated into the air immediately, trying to secure themselves an advantageous position, but that plan was immediately dunked when he sensed hundreds of cores taking to the sky too.

These creatures could fly.

"Okay, new plan," he muttered urgently to Varéc and stretched his arms wide. Varéc understood him immediately, and with a roar of excitement, he flew back into Aodhán's spirit just as Aodhán triggered his Merge perk.

Large, draconic wings jutted out of his back immediately, and gleaming scales covered his skin. His nails grew into claws, curved horns jutted from his head, and his eyes grew slitted, sharpening to crystal-clear clarity.

An animalistic fury surged within him, urging him to dive straight into the incoming creatures and rip them apart with his claws, but as nice as that would have felt, Aodhán had a much different plan—one that required the enhanced area of influence Varéc provided.

The mere thought of his plan caused chaos to surge into his spirit in an overwhelming tide, soaking every part of him in its iridescence. Power burned brightly within him, so dense it cloaked him in storm light as he rose even higher into the air.

The creatures were close now, having circled around him to box him in, their snarls and screeches riding the current of wind in a furious cacophony. But Aodhán only smiled, revealing sharp fangs as he spread his arms wide and waited for the creatures to come as close to him as possible.

The moment they did, he channeled all twenty-five chaos-energized strands of willpower he was allowed into {Edict of Shattering} and simply watched as the rift threatened to rip itself asunder.

The skill reinterpreted the existence of all storm phenomena within his area of influence to facilitate the destruction of every target within it. But as the world boomed, as lightning gathered—not just from the storm but from everywhere at once—as pressure points collapsed inward, and ionized particles gathered, Aodhán realized, not for the first time, just how little he truly knew about his affinity.

Storm phenomena weren't just rain, wind, and lightning. They were any natural process governed by atmospheric electricity, pressure systems, ionized particles, and large-scale energy movement.

Simply put, if it existed because the sky was angry, it was a storm phenomenon.

And as all those phenomena began reinterpreting their existence for destruction, the rift screamed. Not in sound, but in pressure. In direction. In the sudden, violent certainty that everything within his area of influence had been given a single unquestionable purpose.

Ruin.

The cracked heavens convulsed, and Aodhán did the same as willpower drained out of him like a dam broken open—so dense and tangible it warped the air around him and slammed his mind with a sudden wave of dizziness.

The edict built quickly, though, almost instantly, and as the charging creatures suddenly began to retreat in fear, it shattered—a crushing convergence that ripped through the horde in a wave of utter destruction like ruin given form.

Cries and screeches filled the air as the edict ripped the creatures apart, canceling their invisibility to tear their forms open even as it shattered bone, skulls, and wings all across the horde.

That one activation had cut the horde in half, but there were still hundreds of the creatures remaining, and with their invisibility now gone, Aodhán was finally able to identify them.

{Elite Void-Gnashers}

He grimaced at the name, not quite sure it fit the ferocious monsters well, but that was the least of his concerns with hundreds of them still rushing toward him. Another edict could probably wipe them out, but Aodhán wasn't willing to use that skill one more time.

When the system had said the skill was willpower-intensive, he hadn't quite understood and had lost nearly half his willpower pool in a single activation.

He had to be smarter about this, and as the winged void-hyenas rushed toward him, utter fury in their eyes, Aodhán decided it was time to level the playing field.

Wings unfurling, he activated his Stealth perk and smiled when the Gnashers suddenly paused, their furious gazes darting about in confusion. His smile widened in excitement. He had leveled the playing field, and now it was time to rip it apart.

And he knew just the skill for it.

He teleported immediately, hands reaching out to touch the nearest Gnasher just as Tidal Rupture activated.

His hands found hide. Water bubbled. And an instant later, the Gnasher erupted in a spray of bone, blood, and sinew so violent broken bones stabbed into its comrades.

But Aodhán was already gone, hands reaching out to the next and the next and the next…

It was chaos—bloody and dirty. Every Gnasher Aodhán touched died instantly, their bodies erupting violently. Some of the creatures tried to run, void essence coating their wings to aid their flight, but Aodhán simply chased them down, basking in the swiftness and raw control his path offered.

But that excitement didn't last long.

Only a few minutes into his massacre, a new horde of Void Gnashers entered his range, their cores blazing with an intensity rivaling that of Champions. Aodhán turned his gaze to the horizon immediately, expecting to find nothing, but this horde didn't care much about hiding themselves.

They filled the horizon—hundreds of them, all between the 40th and 75th tiers. Aodhán focused on the text floating above the head of the largest, and his eyes widened.

{Super Void-Gnasher—Tier 75}

The Gnasher met his gaze, crimson eyes burning, and before Aodhán could so much as blink, the Gnasher was before him, chaos erupting out of it as it teleported through chaotic space. Aodhán backpedaled immediately, flying backward, but he was too slow, and an instant later, pain slammed into his skull as he crashed into the earth, void magic eating at his face.

The Gnasher was above him before he could even think, and Aodhán realized he had to decide in that moment whether to stay or fight.

He raised his gaze to the crimson one of the Gnasher and decided to stay.

His first real challenge had just arrived. He would be a coward to leave now.

And he was no coward.
