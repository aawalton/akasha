Icons were the ultimate manifestation of a cultivator's path—the crystallization of their ideals made real.

The final argument in any battle. The ace of spades.

Yet the power of icons wasn't fixed. They scaled—evolving with tier, deepening as understanding sharpened, expanding as authority grew.

And at the Calamity Class…

They were so powerful that calling them Icons undersold their immense power and scale. Not to mention the sheer destructive potential they possessed. Perhaps, a better word to describe them were AXIOMS—fundamental truths imposed upon existence. Unanswerable. Undeniable.

Yet even that, Darien thought to himself, felt insufficient in the face of the catastrophe unfolding above him.

It wasn't just light or blood or lightning. It wasn't even just energy.

It was pressure. Ontological pressure.

The kind that pressed down on the world like a divine hand, forcing reality to splinter and crack from the weight of it. The air thickened, compressed until it felt solid—unbreathable. Space heaved around him, fractures spreading visibly as the pressure of stacking icons tripled with each thunderous announcement.

The sky blazed—not with flame, but with faultlines—great cracks carving across the heavens like a structure pushed far beyond its limit. Color bled out of the world and returned wrong. Reds too deep. Blues bruised into violet-black. Contradictions erupted in cascading waves as the heavens themselves glitched, illusions clawing at reality to rewrite what already existed.

Boiling blood churned above the shattered firmament. Sky shards descended like jagged glass. The earth had split open like a maw, hungering darkness spilling upward in waves.

And at the center of it all, Darien floated—unable to move. Unable to breathe.

But even if he could, he knew there was no surviving this on his own. None of his skills, no matter how much willpower he channeled into them, could hold a Calamity's icon at bay.

Let alone this.

But he couldn't give up either. None of the other specials would ever give up.

So Darien did the only thing he could, despite the futility of it all.

He raised his gaze to the descending storm of destruction, inhaled deeply, and activated his own icon.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: SANCTUM ABSOLUTE}

But even as the world announced it, even as reality was forced back into order around him—a bastion of absolute calm spreading nearly a mile wide—Darien knew it would fail.

The icons struck an instant later. Sanctum held for barely a second.

Then it shattered. Pressure engulfed him. Then light—painful, blistering light.

Darien let go. He had tried. He had given it everything despite the futility of it. He hadn't died a coward.

His father would have hated that.

Oblivion crept in. A sorrowful smile touched his unraveling face. And then—

Another Icon activated.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: VOID OF INFINITY}

Darien barely heard the declaration over the agony consuming him. The pain was so immense—so total—that he failed to notice when he stopped dying. Or when hands wrapped gently around his ravaged skin, an unfamiliar voice whispering close to his ear.

"Allow me to correct my partner's error."

He barely registered anything save the pain of destruction, and the seeping embrace of darkness.

But just before his consciousness slipped fully into oblivion—as it fled the ruin tearing through his body—Darien felt a sense of warmth. Sanctuary. Engulfing him from every direction at once.

The blistering agony receded.

He was alive. Against all odds, against inevitability itself, he had survived.

Unfortunately, Seamus was not so fortunate.

While Darien had been displaced by space—reappearing at the center of the battlefield—Seamus had been displaced by time—reappearing exactly two seconds before the Icons descended.

Two seconds too late to stop the teleportation.

Two seconds too early to belong to the present.

He'd been offset from the natural flow of time, unable to do anything but watch as catastrophe advanced toward a future he had not yet reached.

He screamed and struck at the air around him, but nothing responded. His attacks passed through events that had already occurred. His voice altered nothing.

He could see the world moving. Advancing. Continuing. And he moved with it.

Yet he was not a part of it.

To those in the present, he did not exist. He was trapped two seconds behind the world. Time advanced. So did he. But the gap between them did not shrink.

It remained constant. Unchanging.

And as time marched forward without pause, Seamus realized the gap would never close.

The world would continue and he would remain forever two seconds behind.

If nothing interrupted this perfect symmetry… he would never arrive.

He would simply cease to exist.

In that same moment, far from the battlefield, Aodhán and the other transmigrants stood trapped in a different kind of prison—one of perfect safety that felt like torture.

They watched the world crumble beneath the weight of competing realities, unable to leave or help or act, while the elders argued all around them, voices sharp with fury and fear as they debated what a fool the Space Calamity had been.

But even if they were allowed to leave, Aodhán wondered what use they would be in a situation like this.

For all their power and talent, they were still advanced class cultivators. What could they do against the icons of Calamities? Let alone this storm of stacked Axioms that even Calamities approached with dread?

With a shudder, he raised his gaze to the burning sky, swallowing as the energy of the converging destruction swept over him.

It hadn't even reached its full potential. And already it was cataclysmic.

"Koh's kar tel, rah min sco fe lan des." Ashscar muttered shakily beside him, the unintelligible prayer barely a whisper on his lips. Aodhán glanced at him, noting the fear etched across his face—across all their faces—and shuddered again.

In the weeks since he'd known Darien and Seamus, he had grown to care for them greatly. And though he hadn't known them as long as the others, he didn't want them to die. They were his friends.

His family.

And now they were beneath a storm of energy about to rip the world apart, and he couldn't do anything about it.

That helplessness ate at him—at all of them—as they watched the catastrophe unfold, willing Darien and Seamus to clear the battlefield before the icons struck. But with each passing second, that possibility narrowed. Space was too unstable. Time too warped. Distance too unreliable.

All around the battlefield, Mythics fled for their lives, zipping through the air with desperate speed. Many would make it. But Aodhán feared that for everyone who did, hundreds wouldn't. Thousands.

All because a Calamity had been icon-happy. All because someone with the power to reshape reality had decided that saving four transmigrants was worth triggering an apocalypse.

It was stupid. Even Aodhán knew that, and he’d barely spent a year in this world. Icons were last resort—final cards played when every other thing had failed. They shouldn’t have been activated this early into the war—not with the lives of so many at stake, not when the entire purpose of the war was to restore balance, not obliterate everything in pursuit of it.

Around him, more whispers rose as Nymera and Sophran joined Ashscar in prayer, but Aodhán couldn’t bring himself to join them. He was too afraid to pray. Too angry. Too aware that the gods of AEFLYM had never saved anyone before and weren’t about to start now.

Still, he needed a miracle. If the Calamities didn’t find Darien and Seamus soon, it would be all over.

And he wasn’t sure he could handle the death of another friend. Not after Baxter, and Brennon. Not after Mirelle.

He needed a miracle, and so despite the futility of it, he forced himself to pray, pleading for any god who was watching to intervene.

However, he’d barely begun before things took a drastic turn for the worse.

Reality cracked from the strain of holding so much power. Space unraveled. Time fractured, and the heavens caved, rupturing alongside space, time, and reality. Fracture lines of incandescent light spiderwebbed across the firmament, and through those cracks, the Icons began descending, obliterating reality as easily as a blade through parchment.

“No,” he whispered urgently, searching the skies of the battlefield for any sign of Darien. Of Seamus. Of anyone at all.

He found none.

“No,” he whispered again, his voice breaking as grief began to stir within him. “No, please—not yet.”

But the descending catastrophe neither heard nor cared for his feelings. It came like the end of all things—burning through space, warping time, shredding reality itself, and consuming everything in between.

No one could survive it. No one.

Grief surged through him like a wildfire, burning through his spirit with a near tangible intensity, but Aodhán refused to cry. Refused to blink. There were only two seconds left before the catastrophe struck, but there was still hope. There was still—

One second.

Yet Aodhán kept his eyes open, hope warring with despair in his chest. And then, like a ray of sunshine through a cloudy morning, that hope bore fruit.

A bastion of calm spread out from the center of the battlefield and another icon thundered into existence, stalling the catastrophe for a moment.

It was weaker. Smaller. Yet familiar in a way that made Aodhán’s breath catch.

{ICON MANIFESTATION: SANCTUM ABSOLUTE}

“That’s Darien,” Sophran called out urgently, his prayer temporarily forgotten as he pointed to the battlefield. “That’s his Icon. He’s still alive.”

Aodhán felt his body move instinctively, feet carrying him forward before thought could catch up. Fortunately, elder Ghaltris stopped him in time. “Stay where you are, the Calamities will find him.”

And true to his words, they did.

Another Icon thundered into existence—{ICON MANIFESTATION: VOID OF INFINITY}—cutting through space with an expanse of void that stretched the distance between the descending icons and the earth into infinity.

The defensive Icon barely lasted a second—unable to hold such immense power at bay for too long, but for that moment—that fraction of a second when it had ruled, space-time stabilized.

And no one wasted it.

Calamities moved, teleporting thousands of Mythics out of the battlefield in an instant. Space Mythics did the same, saving as many as they could

Thousands would still die, but that number had been reduced from all to thousands.

And then—

The second space Calamity appeared before them all, a bundle in hand.

They rushed toward him immediately, relief surging through them as the man laid the bundle down to reveal a face Aodhán would have recognized anywhere.

Darien.

He was severely injured with nearly half his shoulder and face unraveled as if reality itself had tried to pull him apart thread by thread, and had only barely failed. Several parts of his skin hung in strips. Bone showed through gaps that shouldn’t exist, and both his eyes were gone.

But he was alive. He was alive and breathing.

Aodhán’s eyes watered at the sight, relief and pain ripping uncontrollably through his spirit. It would take Darien a while to regenerate from an injury like this, but at least he was alive. He was—

Aodhán froze. His eyes widened. And that was when he realized what they had missed in the relief of the moment.

Where was Seamus?

Sophran came to the same realization immediately, and together, they turned their gazes back to the battlefield, eyes wide in panic.

But it was too late.

Before anyone could move—before the horror could even dawn—The Icons struck the earth.

And the world went white.

Far away from the battlefield, far even from the viewing deck of the enemy, elder Malverin and the other elders watched the world burn with equal parts horror and terror.

The earth beneath their feet shuddered violently, cracks and fissures spreading in jagged arc as the icons obliterated the earth, consuming everything that had once been the battlefield.

Even from the distance, Malverin felt the shockwaves hit her like an avalanche—the compression of reality so sudden and absolute that her ribs creaked, her lungs flattened and her core compressed within her like a star collapsing in on itself.

In only a second, the entire battlefield was gone. Hundreds of miles of earth—obliterated as if it had never existed. And in its place was a smoking crater so deep, it spilled the essence of the abyss into the atmosphere.

It rose like smoke, burning and shredding through the immediate reality of the crater like oblivion itself—darkness gathering across the crater like a wound seeping dark blood.

The earth groaned—the sound not unlike that of an injured animal at the edge of death. And it looked no different. Scars and fractures covered every inch of ground outside the cratered site. Blood covered the earth in flowing streams. And bodies—or what remained of them lay scattered like broken dolls across the devastation.

It was destruction beyond anything she had ever seen.

And it had been their doing,

Beside her, elder Sang-Hoon stared at the destruction in horrified shock, his usual togetherness gone in the face of the catastrophe they had just witnessed.

The catastrophe they had helped propagate.

His pain was realer than hers, Malverin knew. While she could only see the wound in the earth, he could no doubt feel them—feel the devastating extent of them. And then, there was Aspwick who could feel the blood—the souls of all those whose life had been lost in the attack.

There were thousands of them—both allies and enemies—those who hadn’t made it out fast enough. Their blood stained the earth now, pooling into fissures and boiling from the scorching ground.

And though they may have lost fewer members, Malverin knew this was no victory.

It was a holocaust. A shared grave.

There was only one thing that would make it all worthwhile, and that was—

“The transmigrants,” elder Clearfall asked, echoing her very thought. “Did we do it? did we kill them?”

Malverin turned to glance at elder Mournwind, her expression hopeful, but the Serenity elder shook his head.

“No. They are still alive.”

Disappointment crashed down on her like a tidal wave, and with a heavy sigh, she glanced back at the destruction wrought—at the cracks in reality and fissures on the earth—and realized it had all been in vain.

Swallowing, she asked. “Is the Origin Plane of Storm still expanding?”

“No,’ Elder Mournwind replied, and she nodded.

“Is it shrinking?”

This time there was a pause—enough time for her to hope. And then—

"No," Elder Mournwind shook his head again. "No. It's not shrinking."
