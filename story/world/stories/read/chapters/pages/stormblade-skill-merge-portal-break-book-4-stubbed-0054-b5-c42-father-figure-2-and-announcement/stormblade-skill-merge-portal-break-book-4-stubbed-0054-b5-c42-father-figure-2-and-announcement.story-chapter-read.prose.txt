Had I made Dad proud?

I thought I had, back when I’d won the Fallen Delvers Tournament. That victory had felt like something just for him. I’d claimed it inside the portal below his tomb, after all. And the portal had put me face to face with that most terrible moment once again. This time, I’d been ready.

But I didn’t know for sure.

Dad sat across from me. He looked at the chessboard, unable or unwilling to meet my gaze. It was still my move, but the pieces blurred and twisted as I looked at them.

Then the Stormkin Avatar nodded.

“Kade, you’ve made me proud every day since I married your mother. You never had to make that promise, and even if you’d done nothing but take care of Jessie, that would have been enough. Instead, you’ve gone far above and beyond…”

Silence. This time, it felt different. I glanced down at the chessboard again.

“Kade Allen Noelstra. Yes. Yes, you’ve made me proud. Nothing can ever change that.” Dad stood up and spread his arms wide.

I stood to embrace him. His arms wrapped around me, and where they should have burned my skin as electricity arced between us, I only felt a hug I hadn’t felt in a year and a half. It was…in a way, it was more comforting than anything I’d had since. Jessie. Ellen. Anyone else. Nothing compared to Dad’s embrace. It had been there the whole time I grew up, after every schoolyard fight, every brutal sparring practice, every beat-down I’d suffered on the chessboard.

My eyes widened, and I pulled myself free to stare at the board.

“Wait.”

My fingers touched a piece. I moved the pawn one space forward. The move exposed both my bishop and rook to being taken, but that didn’t matter. I could lose either of them and still…

“Mate in two moves. I don’t think I can get out of that,” Dad said after a moment. We played it out, his turns taking almost a minute each as he searched for outs. Then, with a smile, he knocked the white king over. “Well done, Kade. I’m proud of you for that, too.”

I squeezed my eyes shut against the tears.

Something was wrong. Ellen could feel it.

Law-Learning visions were supposed to be fast. Instant. One moment, a delver closed their eyes with a lower-ranked skill. The next, they opened them with the upgraded version. Simple.

But Kade’s Law-Learning vision wasn’t proceeding like that. The seconds ticked by into minutes, and he sat on the floor of their borrowed, white-walled room, sweat pouring down his brow as he twitched and shivered.

She wasn’t worried about that. Kade would be fine. Whatever was happening inside his mental world, he’d been through worse, and there was no one Ellen trusted more than Kade when it came to getting through bad situations. And he hadn’t stopped the Law-Learning process himself, so it couldn’t truly be that bad.

No, the issue Ellen was concerned about was inside her own core.

It had started…the closest she could get to a description of what was happening was shaking…just after Kade closed his eyes. Vibrating. The sensation was wildly uncomfortable, and she had no idea what was happening. All she knew was that it had something to do with Kade, and that the longer his attempt to learn the final Law of Stormbreak lasted, the worse it got. The sensation of her core—or her very soul, in a way—vibrating was almost painful, but not quite. Different. Somehow, it was different. And it went on and on, on the edge between pain like being stabbed and discomfort like a period cramp.

Ellen wasn’t a stranger to core problems. She’d experienced Mana Burn before, and she’d pushed her core to the breaking point in the closing hours of the Siege of Phoenix. Those had both hurt. But this…this was strange and unfamiliar in so many ways. She’d learned how to handle pain. But this…this wasn’t something she’d ever prepared for.

Then it stopped as suddenly as it had begun, and darkness began to build inside her core.

I opened my eyes to a silently spinning storm wall.

Dad’s last words to me stuck in my head as I got my bearings. I’d beaten him. For the first time, I’d beaten him. That he was likely nothing more than a memory of the man he’d been didn’t matter. At the end of the game, he’d knocked his king over for the first time, and he’d told me he was proud of me for winning—and for everything else. I’d become the man he’d worked hard to help me be.

That was almost enough to end the Law-Learning process right there. But not quite.

Stormbreak. S-Rank. That was why I was here. One more weapon to use against the God of Thunder.

And ‘here,’ in this case, could only be the inside of the Eye of the Storm, or something so similar I couldn’t tell the difference. The storm wall had to rise fifty thousand feet—it was so tall I could see neither top nor bottom. White and black clouds flickered by in a chessboard-like pattern that blurred into gray even as it churned.

But the most shocking part of it was the sheer silence.

In all my Law-Learning attempts before this, the storm had always been a physical and audible presence. The wind had always howled, thunder had boomed, and even the falling rain had tapped against my skin and the ground. My duel against Queen Mother Yalerox had been loud, too. She’d been mustering the Eye of the Storm itself, and the center of that hurricane had been deafening. So had the magic we’d thrown at each other. And Stormbreak…Stormbreak was never silent.

But this was.

Lightning cracked across the endless bank of clouds. Rain fell all around me. The wind threatened to tear me limb from limb. And yet…silence.

I was suspended at the center of the storm, miles from the storm wall.

What was the lesson to be learned from this?

I bent my mind toward understanding. The previous Laws of the Unbroken Storm had been about sacrifice, dedication, and giving my all to the storm’s fury. They’d been demanding—not just the wording, but the situations themselves.

This felt different. Silent. Isolated. Alone.

The God of Thunder wanted me alone. For a short few seconds, I pondered that. Stormbreak, after all, was one of his Path’s skills. A small part of me wondered if this was a trap set by the SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon himself—a trap hidden in his Path.

But I discarded that theory quickly. The storm wall wasn’t here to destroy me, and when I reached out for my Dual Skill Progression bond, Ellen was still close by. So, if it wasn’t the God of Thunder’s trap, what was it?

Instead of focusing on him, I turned my mind toward my own Path. The Stormblade Path’s steps materialized around me, just as they had when I’d first blazed them during my push to S-Rank. The different Laws—the ones I’d learned on the Stormsteel Path, the places I’d deviated, and the new steps I’d taken along my route to power after that final deviation.

Every one of them had been accompanied by pain and suffering.

I’d suffered injury after injury, some so grievous they should have killed me, while walking my Path. All the way from my first Stormbreak to my core-broken battle against Yalerox—from my fights with Deborah Callahan to the Placid King’s assault on the Lightning Tower—I’d fought, and I’d suffered.

That was part of it. But it wasn’t the whole thing. Suffering and pain weren’t ends to themselves. They were a consequence, or a price. And what that price had bought me was…

Power.

The power to keep promises. The power to protect the people I loved. The power to change things. The Placid King and the God of Thunder both bought into a belief whose end result would only be death and destruction. Even the Crone—who, if she could be believed, fought her every instinct to find a better way—was a part of that same system.

With the power the Stormblade Path offered…with Stormbreak’s power…I could start to change that. That wasn’t enough, though. Pain and suffering. Power. It was part of this final Law, but not all of it.

How?

How did I want to change things?

Stormbreak, at this rank, wasn’t about the simple transfer of power. It couldn’t be. For a long time, it had been more or less the same ability. Positive and negative zones. Mana drain. Lightning blossoms. Pulses of electricity that ripped through everything around me. But, like my other skills, S-Rank promised something more. The Stormblade Core hadn’t awakened yet. It hadn’t started to change the skill. But maybe it didn’t need to.

Change. The future. I struggled to wrap my mind around it. Just defeating the God of Thunder felt like such an impossible task, even with my weapons and allies and the power of the Lightning Tower, Void Garden, and Eye of the Storm, that I couldn’t fully grasp what would have to come next.

I had to try, though. The final Law of the Unbroken Storm was about how the storm shifted, and how its power changed as it moved, grew, and darkened. I needed to understand that about me, too.

Stormbreak. What was it, really? The shattering of the storm itself. The lightning and thunder erupting to break an opponent.

Why couldn’t I apply that power, instead of an opponent, to something larger? Not to the God of Thunder, but to his very source of power? And if I could do that, why couldn’t I go even further? Instead of breaking the God of Thunder or the Stormsteel Path, why couldn’t I break the very system that trapped him, the Crone, Retan, and all the others over S-Rank?

Could Stormbreak achieve that?

I didn’t know.

But the Law was starting to come into focus. It was about power. About suffering. About dedication. Those were the hallmarks of the first four Laws of the Unbroken Storm. I’d given everything I had to those Laws. Gotten over my fear of pain—both my own, and the pain I’d cause others with Stormbreak. Harnessed its power, and that of the Eye of the Storm. Worked hard to gain the strength I’d need to hold the storm’s reins.

If the other Laws were about the process, this final one could only be about the result.

I had sacrificed.

The storm’s full, unbroken potential had to be mine now.

There was no other way.
