Princess Tiyana Matali was currently sitting in a rickety wooden chair, raptly listening to an old woman speak.

A woman who’d known her mother. A woman with an excellent memory, who had tales to tell.

Almost an hour later, Tia found herself lost in old memories of stories she’d mostly forgotten.

“She came through here when I was just a girl,” Subhi said. “Before the Ash took the lands to the east. She rode through with your grandfather’s retinue, and she stopped in the market to look at some fabric, I think. Yes, fabric. I remember because a child there spilled a bowl of dal soup on her riding dress. Her parents nearly fainted from shock, yet your mother showed no anger at all. She just knelt and asked the child what color she liked best. The child said red, and so she bought a length of red silk from the stall and gave it to her.”

“That sounds exactly like what I remember of her,” Tia said with a smile. What that child would’ve done with silk like that, she had no clue, but she bet her mother hadn’t thought twice about it.

“I am the only one in this town who remembers, my Lady. I wanted to tell you before you go. Your mother was a kind person, and I don’t mean in that superficial way people are nowadays. She cared about us, genuinely. The realm lost a bright star the day she passed.”

“Thank you,” Tia replied, a pang of longing flaring up in her chest, even after all these years. “I’ll try to do right by her memory.”

“You already are, my Lady,” Subhi said, patting her hand with her dry and papery fingers. “And thank you for gracing us. Truth be told, we did not expect you. We were told you would be out touring this week.”

“Canceled, I’m afraid,” Tia said. “My brother needed me in Falguna instead. It was a happy coincidence.”

“Indeed,” Subhi replied. “With your help, Matali might well reclaim its former glory once again!”

“That is my aim,” Tia said, rising from her seat.

Yet more meetings had been scheduled, and even this hour had been a luxury she couldn’t truly spare. With what she hoped was a reassuring squeeze of the old woman’s shoulder, she turned and headed for the door.

It was only when she’d opened it and stepped past Vason and Haymi that she noticed the silence.

The hustle and bustle of the market beneath the window had ceased, and the only reason she noticed was thanks to her eternity in that blighted wasteland of ash. It was exactly the sort of silence that came as lesser Ash Beasts fled to safety, leaving only emptiness behind.

The sort of warning she’d learned the hard way to listen to, nearly losing her life in the process. Nor did Vason and Haymi miss it either.

Even still, she didn’t even think of running away. While Tia might once have done exactly that, thinking only of herself, she wasn’t that person now. Not anymore.

“Stay here,” she told Subhi. “Bar the door behind me and don’t open it for anyone except me, Haymi, or Vason.”

The old woman nodded, retreating into the room.

Tia withdrew a B Grade Fireball from her belt and slotted it into her aiming bracer, taking the stairs two at a time, Haymi and Vason following close behind.

They’d just made it to the street when a shriek pierced the air.

It did not take long to find the cause.

A dozen demons were moving through the stalls in close formation, killing civilians without rhyme or reason.

Iksana ghaels flitted between the shadows, stabbing daggers into backs while red demons attacked with talwars and spears. They were supported by three Aindri Warriors who rode around atop their War Ash’va mounts.

The results were about as could be expected when a group of elite demons raided an unsuspecting human town.

Merchants lay dead at their stalls, their throats gaping open, while a child was sent tumbling after being punched in the face.

Tia’s head grew hot despite the cool breeze. The very same fury she’d felt ever since demons had murdered her parents before her eyes. Her old friend that spread from her chest to her head and down to every part of her feet until she felt like she was ablaze.

Two more Fireball orbs were in her hands before she knew it.

The rage coursed through her, and it was only with immense effort that she managed to suppress it.

Just as she was no longer the sort of princess who looked out for herself, she’d learned to control that hatred too. And so, rather than activating her twin orbs that would have caused untold mayhem, she called upon Haste instead.

Haymi hung back while Vason surged forth, joining Tia in a pattern they’d practiced so many times in the Ash that she knew it better than her own name.

Aided by her burgeoning reserves of prana, the world slowed to a crawl. To the world, she became far faster than any enhanced demon physique could hope to track.

The first ghael had a prana-laden net half-deployed when Tia’s shoulder caught him in the chest, surging prana into his body and instantly rendering him unconscious.

Her second victim was a red demon dragging a bound merchant toward an alley. A simple strike of her spear’s shaft on the back of his skull was more than sufficient. Aided by her superhuman strength, the impact sent him crumpling well before he had a chance to react.

The merchant would have to wait, however, as two ghaels fired a wide prana-laced net from above at that instant.

Tia responded with a precharged Fireball, and the ghaels who had been holding the cord—the now very much burning cord—slunk back into their shadows.

Just as a mounted Aindri Warrior charged up to her, his bare arms glowing with tattoo arts.

No doubt he hoped to take her by surprise with his enhanced mobility, but unfortunately for him, she was so much faster that he might as well have been trotting.

He swung his talwar, but she easily dropped beneath the strike, driving her spear up into his ribs with every ounce of strength she had.

Which, thanks to the prana that infused every corner of her body, was quite a bit. So much so that her attack not only connected, it pushed him up and out of his saddle, his neck slamming against the cobblestone with a sickening crunch.

He did not get up.

The second Warrior lasted slightly longer, though she overestimated even him. If she’d known she could’ve disarmed him so early, she wouldn’t have bothered to fire an Ember spell fired at point-blank range.

The third and the fourth both tried to back away, only to have their Achilles’ tendons cut, leaving them open to be clubbed unconscious.

Twenty seconds and half of the raiders were on the ground.

She took a moment to pop her head up and inspect the battlefield, and it was then that she got a good look at the gear they carried and their implications.

The cord on the merchant glowed with prana, as did the nets the demons carried. Now that she thought about it, the first Warrior had seemed oddly restrained in his use of demonic arts.

Not that it would’ve done him much good.

Still, it was clear they were trying not to kill her, but it wasn’t out of some deference for her status.

No, this wasn’t a raid at all. They’d come here to capture someone, and it was beyond obvious who.

“Princess Tiyana Matali,” a smug voice said from behind.

Tia turned to find a tall red demon in dark armor approaching her.

His arms were bare to show off the Aspect tattoos inscribed all across them, and while he wielded a talwar like the rest, his was longer and more ornate.

By the fact that it remained in its scabbard, Tia deduced he was their commander.

“I had hoped to take you without resistance,” the demon said with an exaggerated bow, “though I now see I have lost that advantage.”

“Why me?”

“We had several objectives. You were simply the last among them.”

The grak does that mean? Tia wondered, looking at the wanton carnage. If there was a means behind their madness, she certainly didn’t see it.

The anger boiled within her once again, and this time, she channeled it into something useful.

Activating Haste again, Tia slotted a precharged B-grade Fireball into her aiming bracer. She’d show no mercy for this one.

He they clashed at the center of the square, and she instantly understood that he was not like the others.

While he wasn’t nearly as slow as the others, she still held a sizeable advantage in speed over him. Despite that, he still somehow managed to read her movements.

She flared Haste to its maximum, unleashing her Fireball as she closed within range. He predicted the move, breaking her stance with a palm strike that almost required him to know where she’d be.

The strike didn’t even come close to wounding her, but it did force her to shift her weight, sending the Fireball wide.

She tried again, this time bringing her spear to bear, but he somehow disrupted the strike again. Every time she struck, he was already moving to intercept, despite lacking any ability to see the future. From what Tia had learned of demonkind in her time back, only the Iksana clan had that ability, and they didn’t look anything like the red demon she fought now.

As for her orbs, unfortunately, her speed was hampered by her magic in this case. Fire was the slowest of the magics—a reason why she only used it up close.

He knew that as well as she did, and he had the skill to use that limitation to his advantage, constantly moving and somehow staying just outside her range despite her speed advantage.

His Aspect tattoos flared again and again, and he moved in patterns she could not predict.

Granted, she’d spent the past ten years fighting relatively unintelligent creatures rather than humans. Strong creatures, yes, but nowhere near as cunning as a being that used advanced tactics.

Rather than frustration, however, Tia found herself enjoying the challenge. He was reading her like an open book, and she grew more and more absorbed in the fight as she tried to figure out how.

It was for that reason that she didn’t notice the Order operative until it was too late.

He’d appeared from a side alley, moving fast for a demon, but not unusually so. A small red demon without armor and no visible Aspect tattoos.

Which was why Tia ignored him. The commander was the clear danger here, even if that danger was slight to her as she was now.

She’d deal with the other demon after. She returned her attention to the dance the commander had her in, moving in for what she hoped would be a killing strike.

Perhaps she hadn’t quite expected it to work, because it wasn’t satisfaction but rather shock that she felt when her attack connected, piercing the commander in his flank.

It was only when the cold of metal choked her neck that she realized her mistake.

The collar locked in place, settling around her throat, and she knew she’d been played even before she felt the drain.

A subjugation collar, the kind used throughout the realm. Mostly for demons, but also humans in certain countries.

The collar did not simply block her prana. Rather, it sucked it out of her body like a hungry or, and that too at a rate that should have emptied her reserves in seconds.

Her prana kept cycling, but her reserves reduced with each passing second, siphoned away far faster than she could hope to gather it from the surrounding air and earth.

Haste failed and the Fire orb she’d been charging dimmed as the prana feeding it thinned to a trickle.

The commander stepped back, clutching his ribs in pain as he lowered his sword. “Well. I must say, I do not know how much longer I could have lasted against that. You fight like a demon, princess.”

Haymi screamed her name from somewhere behind. Her friend was running to her, preparing her magic. So was Vason, plowing through demons trying to slow him down. But he was over twenty paces away and Haymi was even further. More operatives had appeared from the alleys, clogging their paths and threatening them both with binding nets.

Tia shut her eyes, forcing herself to concentrate.

Were this before the Ash, she would have crumpled on the spot, drowning in panic. But it wasn’t only her vast ocean of pranic reserves that had kept her standing.

When one fought Ash Beasts for as long as she had, panic became a distant, foggy memory.

She’d faced worse crises than this, and while she might not have had the vast prana of the Ash to pull from, her mind remained as sharp as ever.

The collar pulled harder and her prana left her in waves. Her knees began to buckle.

But underneath it all, she held.

The Pagan Order’s reports had been based on the Tiyana Matali of before. They had not accounted for the fact that five years to them had been more than ten for her, and they had certainly not accounted for all she’d endured during that time.

The collars had never been designed for someone with this much prana.

The metal at her throat began to whine softly with the strain.

She opened her eyes. The commander was watching her with a satisfied smile, believing his work done.

In fact, it had barely even begun.

Tia roared, full of defiance and spite.

She forced her prana to push through the drain. To break through despite it.

What reached her hands was a fraction of her full capacity… but it was enough.

She grasped a precharged C-grade Ember orb. Weaker than a Fireball, but deadly enough. Especially at this range. She fired the spell at the commander, right in the chest, sending him reeling back in shock.

She followed up with another. And another, and by then, he wasn’t smiling any longer. With his body on fire, all he could do was scream.

The second operative was in the process of deploying his net when Tia activated Haste. Time barely felt like it slowed at all, but it was enough to let her cross the distance and put him on the ground with an Empowered strike.

The third ran. Which was just as well, because with the collar continuing to drain her, she didn’t know if she had it in her to take on anyone else.

She turned back to the commander, heaving.

He was on the ground, rolling around to douse the flames, and while he might have been successful in that endeavor, it was too late.

Burns covered his entire body. Without A Grade healing, he would not survive, and Tia wasn’t in the mood to ask Haymi for it.

Instead, she pinned him to the ground with her knee.

“Well fought… princess,” he rasped, his words coming in spurts. “And yet, none of this matters. For we have already won!”

“What do you mean?” Tia asked, shaking his shoulders. “What was your goal here? To capture me? You could’ve attacked in my sleep. Or laid an ambush while I was alone with Subhi. Why now?”

She realized she was shouting—almost screaming—at the dying demon, but it didn’t matter. She needed to know.

“Justice,” he replied. “Not just here. Across the realm. Attacks everywhere. You are too late.”

“Where?” Tia shouted, but received no response. The demon had breathed his last.

She dropped his corpse in disgust and rose to face the remaining demons, but there was no need.

They’d fled the moment their leader passed, with the ghaels sinking into the shadows carrying red demons with them.

Not a moment after they disappeared, Tia collapsed on the cobblestones. The collar continued to suck her prana even now.

“I’m so sorry, Tia,” Haymi said, finally reaching her. “I’ll heal you now.”

She pressed a B grade Life prana against her skin, and Tia felt energy flow into her. “I can do nothing against that collar, however.”

“No,” Vason said, clutching the Artifact. “But I can.”

Vason grounded his tower shield and gripped the collar with so much force, the veins on his arms visibly bulged.

As much as Tia had grown her prana reserves, it was Vason who had benefited the most. Every ounce of power had gone into enhancing his own body. Mastering new Talents all for that goal, his raw strength easily rivaled Ash Beasts at this point.

Which was why Tia was more than a little embarrassed when she found herself surprised when Vason’s fingers crushed the collar, shattering it in his bare hands.

“See?” he said, grinning as he held up the remnants. His face was drenched in sweat and he heaved for breath… but he’d done it. “I’m as strong as the Gods now!”

Haymi rolled her eyes, while Tia snorted, the inrush of ambient prana already bolstering her strength. “You sure are,” she said, before wiping the smile off her face. “It seems there were other strikes across the realm. We need to get messengers to Erhan. And Maiya, if the crystal network is functional. She needs to know. And summon the city guard. We need to bind the demons I left alive before they wake up.”

Vason nodded, his joviality gone without a trace. “It will be done,” he said, exchanging nods with Haymi before rushing off to fulfill his mission.

Tia tried to get up, but Haymi forced her down.

“Rest,” she said. “I’m not done healing you.”

Tia sighed, knowing it was futile to argue with their healer. She didn’t even bother mentioning that her injuries were trivial.

“I see you didn’t kill them,” Haymi said softly.

“Not all of them, anyway,” Tia said.

“But not just to question them, yes?”

Tia chuckled. “Would you believe me if I denied it?”

Haymi said nothing for a moment, causing Tia to look up into her eyes. To her surprise, what she found wasn’t amusement, but something else entirely.

“You’ve come a long way, my princess. Your mother would be proud of you. We all are.”

Tia did not respond. She averted her eyes, staring at a distant building.

Because otherwise, she couldn’t have hidden the tears that threatened to stream down her face.

Riyan had been discussing the concessions he’d have to make to the local Sawai lords for raising taxes in Kartara with his advisors when one of the several communications crystals on the long table behind him lit up.

Kindly gifted to him by Maiya, the crystals connected to every city and major town in Sai, and the one that lit belonged to Kaia’s harbor administration.

Arjun Nair, lord of Kaia, was the only person on the other end, and he was not one to idly contact Riyan.

He held up a hand, cutting his advisor off mid-sentence. “Out. Both of you.”

They obeyed, leaving without a word.

Arjun’s face appeared in the projection. He was breathing hard, and there was blood on his collar, though Riyan could not tell whose. Behind him beyond the edge of the projection, came shouting and the telltale flashes of orb discharges.

“Sire!” Arjun said straight away. “Demons are attacking!”

“Elaborate,” Riyan replied, his worry growing with every word Nair spoke. “How many? From where?”

“A coordinated strike force, sire. They came at dawn. The harbor is…”

The crystal went dark.

For a long moment, Riyan stared at the empty space where Arjun’s face had been.

He did not need to speculate long about the poor lord’s fate.

A myriad of thoughts went through the mind of King Riyan Savar of Sai at that moment. Even still, he wasted no time in calling for his advisors.

“Issue a full military mobilization to Kaia,” he commanded. “Send word to Matali, to Sonam, and to every territory we have a crystal link with. Anyone with eyes on Kaia is ordered to send couriers out immediately. I want to know what is happening there within the hour.”

“Yes, Sire,” his advisor replied. “Shall I convene the War Council?”

“Yes, tonight,” Riyan replied. “And get a hold of Maiya.”

His advisors bowed and moved for the door.

Riyan glanced out the window. The sun was rising on the frigid lands before him. Another peaceful morning in Kartara.

And yet, to the south, Kaia might well be burning. It was not difficult to imagine. Not since Ksaia had burned.

One city razed was enough. He would not allow another.
