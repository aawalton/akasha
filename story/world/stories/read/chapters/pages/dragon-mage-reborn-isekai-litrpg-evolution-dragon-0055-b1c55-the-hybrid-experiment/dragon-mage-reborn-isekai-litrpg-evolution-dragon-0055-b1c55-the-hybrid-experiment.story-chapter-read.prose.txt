“The core!” Lun shouted, pointing with his scepter. “That’s its power source! Target that!”

I dashed in and used my [Cyclone Shield] to spread the free rocks further as I noticed it trying to glue itself back together. My shield drew the golem upward, and I was able to stand underneath the monster as it lifted into the air and got snagged in my cyclone. The tail end of my skill was a burst effect that separated the rocks. Lun ran behind Zaiden’s bubble to protect himself from the free rocks, while Nolan stayed behind that boulder with his Scarlet ready to fire. Shockingly enough, I didn’t need his help for this golem, because as soon as I separated its rocky bits and melted the core with my breath weapon, it was adios to monster numero uno.

“Great,” Nolan commented as he walked over to me, but his eye was pointed behind me. “One down, two more to go.”

“You best make this quick,” Lun said as he rushed over. “My amplification spell won’t last much longer. It’s not meant to extend beyond a few minutes’s time.”

I turned to face the remaining golems. The mallet-wielder had partially reconstructed its damaged arm, while the sword-golem was dragging itself forward despite missing a limb. The luminous cores in both monsters pulsed, visible through the acid-eaten holes in their stone bodies.

“Go for the cores directly,” Nolan instructed. “I’ll create an opening.”

These stone golems weren’t exactly one-trick ponies. After they noticed how I fought, they altered their strategies. More defense, less offense. We got to see more stone shields, stone mines, and stone barriers. They were learning, adapting to our tactics. The mallet-golem slammed its weapon into the ground, but instead of creating a simple fissure, dozens of stone spikes erupted in a wave heading straight for us.

I darted between the larger spikes, but kept my distance. Nolan mentioned that one hit from those golems would delete me. The disarmed sword-golem followed up its comrade’s attack and plunged its fist into the earth. The ground beneath my feet suddenly softened like quicksand, threatening to suck me in. Lun had me covered when he converted that sand to water.

“I’m going to need another mana potion, stat!” he insisted, but he didn’t have to worry. Together, Nolan and I wrapped up the fight, weaving between their defensive moves to break those rock-heads down.

After the trio was down for good, we collected much needed loot from the fallen golem monsters. Despite our gargantuan need for mana restoration vials, these stone golems barely had enough of those to go around.

“These Stone Fists should be useful. Works like a glove, boosts strength and flame resistance.” Nolan turned to Lun. “How is the rest of our inventory looking?”

“Depressing,” Lun replied as he crouched down over the loot. “But, we have no choice but to make do with what we have.” He then looked over to me curiously. “Hey, you’re a multi elemental dragon, but most importantly, a dragon that has the ability to grow her thrall. Seeing as you’re high-leveled, why haven’t you summoned and grown your thrall yet?”

“She does have thralls,” Nolan answered for me. “They are just hidden. That location is a secret.”

“Not much of a secret as you think. We are partymates, remember?”

“So then, this conversation is over, isn’t it?”

Lun shrugged. “Still doesn’t answer my original question. If you can summon your thralls, why aren’t they here, helping us beat this dungeon? The little you do have would still be useful to us.”

“Don’t you think if she could use them, they’d be out doing their jobs?”

Lun looked over to me before turning to Nolan again. “Look, I understand that Freya is a special case. Hell, I haven’t seen a young dragon this strong in my entire lifetime! Even so, she still requires all the help she can get. If she needs help conquering subjects for her thrall, or summoning her thrall from her domain, as her codex calls it, then that’s something she should seek professional help from the very best of the best. You know, there are dragon acolytes in the southern lands of Humbok. I’m sure she’d benefit from all of the kn—”

“She’s not going to Humbok,” Nolan interjected. “Humbok is filled with dragon hunters disguised as allies,” Nolan finished with a sharp edge to his voice. “They’d strip her scales and harvest her organs before she could even meet these so-called acolytes.”

“You’re being paranoid,” Lun countered, twirling his scepter idly. “The Humbok Sanctuary has protected dragons for centuries.”

“Protected them right into extinction, you mean,” Nolan snorted. “Strange how all these ‘sanctuaries’ keep popping up while dragon numbers dwindle.”

Lun sighed as he got back on his feet. “Very well. This earth stone would have to do.”

“The earth stone must be a dungeon progression-specific loot,” Nolan added. “There is no functionality associated with it, according to my codex.”

Lun grunted. “None of these items do much of anything! All cosmetics!”

Nolan scoffed. “Wishful thinking that a tier five dungeon would give us an abundance of useful loot.” He looked down at the stone and studied it closer. “The stone is engraved, with the same mountain symbol we saw on the door.”

“This might be a hunch, but I think this is a key to go back to the main dungeon area, you know, where the king is keeping Typha.”

“Great, then how the hell do we get back?”

‘Follow the glowing green pathway,’ I said inwardly as I noticed a faint green trail on the ground. The trail was narrow and long, fading into an outlook filled with mountains, with its source coming from the stone in Lun’s hand.

“Seems terribly long,” Lun winced. “This might very well be a test of endurance.”

Nolan grunted, and when I looked back, I noticed Lun’s bubble had faded and Zaiden was back to being over the gunslinger’s shoulder again. “Let’s get a move on,” Nolan muttered, adjusting Zaiden’s weight. “We don’t know how much longer Typha has.”

The path stretched before us like a glowing green ribbon winding through the vast wasteland. Each step we took felt like we were walking further and further into nothingness. The journey seemed endless, the mountains around us barely changing despite how far we walked. Not only that, but it was getting dangerously cold for Nolan and Lun.

“How… much… longer?!?!” Lun muttered between chattering teeth.

‘Hold on, I see something up ahead,’ I called out to Nolan, but when he didn’t answer back, I looked over my shoulder and noticed he was on his knees on the snowy ground, wrapping up a frost-bitten Zaiden with his cloak.

I rushed over, my eyes catching the numbness in Nolan’s fingers.

“This trail is meant to eat away at our endurance,” he said in a low defeated voice. “I don’t think...” he paused, looking down at Zaiden’s poor condition.

‘No, we will make it,’ I said, then dashed over to an area of trees. I used my [Astral Blast] to fire at the trunks until they snapped off, and then used my [Vine Slash] to haul away pieces of bark to my comrades.

I was the only one immune to this cold, and honestly, the climate was giving me a natural boost in all of my stats. I was stronger, faster, and my endurance was higher.

After I collected all of the pieces near Nolan, I set them on fire, hoping to warm my team up.

Lun gathered around too, the heat from my flames thawing away those icicles down his nostrils. “Say what you want about dragons, they make great comrades for mages,” Lun said, his voice steadier as he warmed his hands.

“The cold will only grow worse the longer we stay. These trials are designed to exploit weaknesses,” Nolan added as he adjusted Zaiden’s limp form closer to the flames. “The earth trial isn’t just about fighting golems. It’s about endurance against the elements themselves.”

Lun scoffed. “Not even a dozen mana restoration vials between us, maybe a handful of health restoration potions, and now we are freezing our butts off!”

“If my resources weren’t so laughable, this would have been a lot easier.” He turned to Lun. “I know you can’t use up too much mana, but there is no point in saving your gauge if we end up dead anyway.”

Lun scoffed. “I don’t like what you’re suggesting, here. I hate being caught with my pants down!”

‘Do you guys need a bigger fire?’

‘No, Freya. You’ve been great. This is fine. It’s just… Zaiden’s heart beat is doing worse. And I don’t want to bury someone in a dungeon.”

‘What do you have in mind?’

‘These harsh conditions aren’t making it easier on him. I was thinking life force tethering, but the amount of mana that would require surpasses what Lun has in his gauge. Not only that, I doubt the lizard would want to tether his life force to a complete stranger. He could direct the tethering to someone else, but it’s… risky.’

“Mind sharing the conversation with the rest of the class?” Lun said cheekily. “For all I know, you two could be plotting against me. Set me over this fire, and consume me for energy.”

Nolan smirked. “You’re the last person I’d cook for energy, seeing as you’re our life support right now.”

Lun nodded proudly. “And I’m glad you know it!”

“We were just considering our very limited options here… with Zaiden.”

“Well, if you’re thinking about a life force tether, you’ve got me confused for another rascally lizard mage. I’ve spent too much mana on him as it is to forfeit my life for his,” Lun said, poking at the fire with a stick. “I’m a pragmatist, not a martyr!”

Nolan rested his hand on Zaiden’s forehead. “But we need something. His condition is deteriorating.”

I moved closer to Zaiden, examining his ashen face. His breathing had grown shallow, the frost clinging to his eyelashes despite the fire’s warmth. All of a sudden I felt protective of him. He was a hybrid, half fae, half human. He looked to be my age when I was back on earth, with light hair and thick eyebrows. With those soft and gentle features on his face, it was hard not to want to help him. He was fighting the best he could, he just needed a push from a dragon….

I noticed myself catching feelings for an unconscious guy! Something within me stirred, an idea, but not exactly a good one. I lowered my head and concentrated, feeling a strange warmth building in my chest that had nothing to do with my fire breath. I had tried this only once on Lun, and it backfired terribly. Even so, the conditions were much different, so I shouldn’t let that deter me from trying. For one, Lun was conscious, and two, Lun was a powerful mage.

‘I want to try something,’ I communicated to Nolan.

‘Try what?’

I turned to Nolan and gave him a look. Right then and there, he knew what I had in mind.

‘Are you sure that’s a good idea?’

‘No, but I want to save him. He would be safe there.’

‘Safe and stuck. You haven’t really had any successful attempts at bringing your thralls back out again.’

‘More reason for me to figure out why and fix it.’

‘Zaiden isn’t a monster, Freya. If you can’t get him out of your domain, he’ll starve to death. Think about this.’

‘Nolan, I was hoping you’d have some faith in me, here!’

‘I do. But I want to be the one who sets realistic possibilities. And it’s not always going to be pretty. I understand that you want to help, but if you can’t fish him out in time, his death will be on your shoulders.’

‘Well, I’m a necromancer too, aren’t I?!’

‘You aren’t seriously considering reviving humans, are you?’

I went silent for a second. I couldn’t lie, the thought had crossed my mind a couple of times. If I lost Nolan, I always knew I’d reanimate him….

He looked at me seriously. ‘Freya….’

‘I am going to do it, Nolan. What’s the point of having this power if I can’t use it to save people?’

‘It’s not going to work anyway. He has to be awake in order for you to use [Conquer].’

‘I feel like I could wake him up another way. Remember what you’d told me? That magic affirmed creatures are connected to Etheryn and each other in a special way.’

‘Yeah, within their respective races. You’re not fae.’

‘I don’t have to be. Nolan, ever since I came here, I’ve felt a connection with this place, shown in many forms. From the dungeon I awakened, to stirring the pot of an evil cave demon.’

‘What?’

‘Back when I fought the Dark One, I didn’t recite a prayer. I… channeled him by my presence alone. The point is, I’m more than just a multi-elemental dragon. I don’t know what any of this means, but until I do, I’d use all of its advantages to do my job. And my job is to preserve the wellbeing of not only the people from my world, but the people of yours, too. Can you get behind that?’

‘I can, but not like this.’

‘I… really need you to be with me on this, Nolan.’

‘Freya, this is different from inducting a monster. You convert him, and there is no turning back. Putting him in your domain is the same thing as enslaving another being. Let me be your voice of reason. There are so many ways this idea could go south. You know I’ll always be your biggest supporter, but this idea fucking reeks. Take a true moment to think the consequences through. You’ll see how bad it is.’

I hesitated, unsure of how to answer. The truth was, I didn’t fully understand my own abilities yet. Every thrall I’d taken had been a monster, and I was never able to release any of them. The mechanics of my power remained frustratingly opaque.

‘Tell me, Nolan, do dragons have the ability to release their thralls? From captivation?’

He turned away from me and sighed. ‘Yes, but don’t rely on—’

‘That’s all the confirmation I need,’ I said, interrupting him. I inched closer to Zaiden, placed a paw on his chest, and closed my eyes.

“Freya, don’t—” Nolan continued to refuse.

“Don’t?” Lun asked, confused. “Don’t what? What’s happening?!”

I reached deep within myself, searching for that connection I’d felt earlier when I’d been in the Dark One’s lair. All of the sensations felt similar. There was a spreading feeling in my chest when I reached out to the demon, that same feeling when I’d activated the dungeon. In both instances, there was not only dungeon core magic, but real-world magic. Zaiden might have been unconscious on the outside, but he was very much awake on the inside.

I focused on that internal flame, that consciousness I could sense flickering inside him. My mind reached out, not to conquer, but to connect. Something warm and electric passed between us, like webs of light weaving together.

“She’s trying to add him to her thrall,” Nolan explained tersely to Lun. “Without his consent.”

“That’s... bold. And potentially disastrous.”

I ignored them both, concentrating on Zaiden’s essence. His life force was weak but distinct.

I could feel it—a spark unlike any I’d encountered with my monster thralls. There was resistance, but not rejection. His consciousness wasn’t fighting me; it was reaching back, desperate to break free from the cold void threatening to claim him.

‘I’m trying to help you,’ I projected, unsure if he could hear me. ‘Can you feel me with you?’

A faint response echoed back, wordless but clear—acknowledgment perhaps? While I could feel him, I still felt limited. So I warned him, and told him that everything was going to be okay. I wasn’t here to make him my slave, but to keep him safe. His aura, I felt how unsure it was. Regardless, he submitted to me, and a faint grunt escaped his lips.

“Holy shit, she is really doing it!” Lun gasped.

‘Latch on. I’ll do my best to keep you safe.’
