7th of Peregrinor

Heaven save me from the stupidity of samuran hatchlings and raptorcats! Or maybe, since it’s the month of Explorer and Diplomacy, I should be swearing at the former and praying to the latter for strength. (I’m still not convinced that these gods have any real influence on our lives even if I can’t deny their existence, but it is sure that the samuran hatchlings have been particularly difficult over the past few days due to wanting to explore everywhere.)

It’s not as though we’re trying to keep them confined to a room – we gave up on that almost a month ago – but the limits we give them are there for a good reason. Not that they seem to realise that – or care if they do!

Three injuries! Today! And all so easily avoided if the damn hatchlings would just stay where they’re supposed to be. I had the chef complain to me today about it. Apparently the hatchlings like sneaking into the kitchen most of all – for obvious reasons – and two of the injuries were from someone tripping over them when they moved at the wrong moment. A kitchen is hardly the safest place for people to unexpectedly trip. We’ll have to figure out a way to keep them out of the area.

At the same time, we need to improve safety on the top deck – one of them has fallen overboard twice today.

That last, I place entirely at Trouble’s door since, according to what I heard of Ninja’s scolding, he had been tasked with watching one of the most notorious troublemakers while the hatchlings were being allowed out on deck – a necessity, we’ve found, if we want any hope of calming them enough for lessons later in the day.

Either he wasn’t paying sufficient attention, or he failed to appreciate the risk. And Spot and Blaze weren’t much help – at the time I came onto the top deck, they were curled up nose to tail in the sun and more than half-asleep. Lazy male raptorcats!

The hatchling in question – newly named Leaps-overboard – had, true to form, done exactly that. Again.

For reference, samurans used to name their hatchlings after the first action they took on their naming day, which led to very incongruous names from time to time. I made the suggestion that they instead choose more significant ones that reflect the hatchlings’ character. That suggestion sparked quite a debate among the adult samurans – whether to stick to the tradition of a single naming day, or to focus on names that truly fit. In the end, they decided that a new world deserved slightly new traditions and have decided to go with my suggestion. Leaps-overboard will regret his youthful impetuousness one day – and I can’t say I feel guilty in the slightest.

Fortunately for him, Ninja has her new Shadow Projection ability and, unlike the official guards, was paying attention. She was able to dive after the offending samuran, retrieve him, and return to the ship before either of them suffered more than a thorough soaking.

Still, I swear, these samuran hatchlings are going to be the death of me. Or I’m going to commit genocide one of these days. One or the other – there can be no in-between.

Honestly? I’m beginning to understand why the samurans in the other world pushed them out into the forest at the earliest possible opportunity. At least there, they could get up to whatever nonsense they pleased without endangering everyone else.

11th of Peregrinor

Descendants, (if I do eventually decide to include this journal in the family grimoire library) if you have not yet learned how to access your Willpower challenges through Dominate, then I strongly suggest that you do so as soon as possible.

For reference: I accessed this space by using a reflective surface and triggering Dominate while looking directly into my own eyes. This brought me into a mist-filled environment reminiscent of a Battle of Wills, in which I was offered the opportunity to undertake a Willpower challenge. And, just so you are forewarned, the similarity of the space to a Battle of Wills is the biggest warning you will have as to the nature of the challenges therein – which are, indeed, challenging.

They are also well worth doing.

A successfully completed challenge grants both direct increases to Willpower and a percentage-based improvement. It does seem to consume some of my Energy store to give me those benefits, but far less than I would expect given the level of my Willpower now. I strongly suspect that the Titanbend who created this Skill found some exception to the usual cap of twenty points earned by effort.

To give you an example just to illustrate how useful these challenges are, after recently completing a level four challenge with an A grade – not my best, but I wasn’t joking when I said that these are not easy – I earned four additional Willpower points and a four percent increase in effective Willpower. In total, that translated to an increase of eleven points.

Now tell me that you would turn down the equivalent of nearly two levels’ worth of progress for the cost of a few days’ effort. No? Exactly.

And yes, that wasn’t an error in my writing – days.

While inside the challenge, the passage of time does not correspond perfectly with reality. Ensure that you have eaten, drunk, and relieved yourself before beginning, and that someone is aware of what you are doing. Ideally, have them check on you from time to time and provide water if necessary.

On a related note: a sea voyage is not an ideal time to undertake such a challenge. However peaceful things might be when you enter the trance, that is no guarantee of what you might find when you wake up. I was lucky – my crew had the massive Tier three fish accompanied by a shoal of others well in hand. However, if something stronger had attacked us, I might have woken up at the bottom of the sea – or not at all.

Be warned.

Also, bear in mind that you cannot take one challenge immediately after another – there’s a necessary delay between attempts. I theorise this is to let the previous challenge settle into my soul. In my experience, this delay increases with each level. It has been months since I last completed one, and I have been checking periodically to see when it would become available.

As for the nature of the challenges themselves…. I cannot say for certain because Nicholas, the one who gave me my Class didn’t actually know about this feature and so I was unable to compare. However, I can give an idea of what I’ve experienced and what I expect is coherent across anyone using the Skill in this way.

They are, in essence, confrontations with yourself.

In previous trials, I faced my own self-doubt and the lingering effects of past traumas. This time, the challenge focused on my ambitions – on what I aim to achieve – and seemed intent on dissuading me from pursuing them at all, forcing me to shore up my determination to do so.

Facing yourself in this way is far more effective than any ordinary attempt at self-reflection. Once you have confronted your fears and doubts within the challenge, they lose much of their power over you – as long as you don’t give it back to them.

So, again, if you have not yet discovered this aspect of Dominate, I urge you to experiment.

Though, please, for the sake of the family, take precautions first.

17th of Peregrinor

I’ve now seen it all.

Flying fish, I had heard of.

Flying jellyfish? This world is ridiculous.

I thought the samurans’ world was bad enough, with its dinosaur-like creatures roaming freely, but this…. I still can’t quite process it.

Fortunately, they weren’t trying to attack us. Even so, the newly-promoted Captain Waveborn ordered all non-essential personnel below decks – their dangling tentacles are known to be either venomous or imbued with other magical effects that would cause a good deal of unnecessary injuries.

Valence was all for attacking them – many were at least Tier two and likely carried Cores that could have been useful. I was tempted, but Josiah informed us – and several sailors confirmed with varying degrees of fear on their faces – that attacking one makes the rest swarm.

Given there were hundreds, possibly thousands, of the things, I decided it wasn’t worth the risk. It’s probably pheromones or the magical equivalent, but without being confident that Air Magic would blow the scent away, it wasn’t a fight I wanted to provoke. I guess there’s a reason these creatures can float through the air unmolested.

I was on tenterhooks the whole time we were passing the…what do you even call a load of jellyfish? A shoal? A swarm? A float? Whatever – I was concerned that some idiot would be greedy enough to set off the attack, so I kept a sharp eye. Fortunately, it seems all of the other smart ones were also keeping the idiots out of the way.

I also asked all of my companions to stay in my tattoo or below decks – no slight meant to them; I just didn’t want their instincts accidentally setting off something serious.

Still…flying jellyfish.

It’s probably tempting Fate even to write this, but surely nothing else in this world can surprise me now.

18th of Peregrinor

It turns out that the lords of Moriax are capable of being unexpectedly relaxed when alcohol is involved.

I am, of course, entirely unsurprised that alcohol exists here. Humanity, regardless of the world, will always find a way to create some mind-altering substance and call it recreational. Actually, thinking about it, the samurans did that too, though their substance only tasted tolerable after the second bowlful, and by then the drinker was more than four sheets to the wind.

That said, I now feel like I’ve had enough experience to say that the quality here in Moriax varies wildly.

Some of what the crew drink is…rough. Functional, I suppose, but certainly not enjoyable, though they seem to quaff it easily enough. But the lords brought far better stock – as expected. Elarion, in particular, produced a liqueur that went down very nicely.

Far too easily, in fact – it tasted of something like a mixture between a limoncello and jaegermeister, but had an impressive kick even for people with enhanced Constitutions. If it weren’t for my Flesh Shaping, I would be suffering for it now. As it stands, hangovers are largely avoidable – one of the many advantages of magic that I thoroughly appreciate.

I was, however, relieved that nothing resembled whiskey. I suspect that would have brought back memories I would rather not revisit.

Still, what I realised I had missed – long before arriving on Moriax – was not the drink itself, but the camaraderie.

The gathering was entirely impromptu. Layton invited me and Josiah to his ship. After a couple of drinks, it seemed a shame to leave the others out, so we invited Valance, Elarion, and Sor’az. I would have liked to invite Garous since he’s a captain, but decided that everyone would be just too uncomfortable to relax. Hopefully things will have changed a little by the time you read this, descendant. The same with Sarran – given the difference between our ages and experience, it would have felt like inviting our father or something.

Only Captain Soros declined, which is understandable. He is neither a lord nor in a position to leave his post.

We decided not to invite Tiria or Keshaka either, despite them being both nobles and around the same sort of age as us. It might be a bit sexist, but I don’t regret keeping the group small. I also don’t regret Tiria in particular not being present when I was probably embarrassingly drunk.

What followed was highly enjoyable, though I can’t recall everything completely clearly – as it turns out, even magical healing can’t bring back a memory that was never clearly formed in the first place. It feels far too long since I just relaxed in the company of people I like. Well, by and large. I’m still uncertain about Elarion.

Though, I was surprised to find out that he was something of a hellion in his youth – he certainly doesn’t seem the type to sneak out of his family estate to chase magical phenomena or speak with travellers passing through nearby villages.

Valence contributed a number of scandalous stories from court life, which I listened to with a mixture of amusement and disbelief.

My tales of what life was like for me on Earth were met with just as much disbelief and amazement, though I did my best to keep them light-hearted and easily understandable.

One of the others, Layton of all people, spoke of fighting on the front lines with the Nethayar. That sobered the mood for a time, though Josiah soon revived it with exaggerated tales of the sea and the various terrifying beasts that sailors like to talk about when they’re more than a little gazeboed.

Which, in turn, became less amusing when we remembered that we are currently sailing on said sea – and may yet discover that those tales are not exaggerated at all.

The last part of the night is something of a blur.

Still, I am sure nothing too disastrous could have happened.

…Probably.
