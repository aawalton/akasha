When Jianyi entered the kitchen to grab a snack before seeing if he could fix his cultivation, Minghao was busy preparing what looked like a wide variety of food, more than they would usually eat in a week.

He considered asking what this was about, but his cultivation chamber was more tempting, so he waved at the retainer, eager to be back on his way.

Minghao had other plans, though. “Elder Sun would like to have a word. We should go and see him right away.”

Jianyi looked longingly in the direction of his chambers as they exited the kitchen, but in truth, he was always happy to see the elder—there hadn’t been a day in which he wasn’t grateful for the help and support of his teacher, even more so since the duel with Elder Fang.

With a small smile, he admitted that on the day that Elder Sun had talked him out of using a simple pill to open his dantians, it had been more difficult than usual to access that gratefulness.

With a knock, they entered Elder Sun’s study—and to Jianyi’s surprise, Minghao joined them as well, which had happened only once or twice in the half a year or so that Jianyi lived in their household.

“Ah, Jianyi, my young friend. Have you been able to buy all that you wanted?” the elder asked while slowly tapping his desk with one finger.

The absence of his usual smile worried Jianyi. “I have, teacher. With a little luck, I should be on the path of body cultivation shortly.”

“Good, good. I always want to be honest with you…”

Jianyi nodded; never had he had the impression that the elder was needlessly withholding information from him or, even worse, lying to him.

“… Shiqiu City seems to be a bottomless pit of depravity and the sect…” the elder’s finger paused for a moment in its rhythmic tapping. “The sect is not what it should be. I need some time to sort things out. Second, I am keen for you to progress your cultivation, body and spirit, quickly, but if you do that here and now, it could garner you more attention than you want.”

Jianyi grimaced as his gut churned. He was sufficiently self-aware to know that he had not processed the death of Jun and everything that had come out in that context. It had left a mark on him and the small group of people he had resolved to call friends.

That demons and their human collaborators were searching for him—all cultivators with dual affinities—angered him, no, infuriated him, but the anger went hand in hand with a tiny bit of fear. Never again did he want to lie on a table to be killed for the Core Card in his spirit.

Elder Sun had paused to give Jianyi a moment to process what had been said before continuing: “But… Misfortune is what fortune depends on!”

Jianyi nodded and completed the proverb in his head, ‘Fortune is where misfortune lurks.’

“Minghao told me that you have no experience living, surviving, thriving in the wilderness—that is unacceptable, and no student of mine should continue with such a large gap in their education…”

Jianyi’s heartbeat accelerated slightly. As much as he had always enjoyed the stories of cultivators braving the wilderness, he easily admitted that he was a child of the city and not much good had come out of his trips into nature so far.

“There could be no better teacher than Minghao, so he graciously agreed to spend some time with you out there,” the elder said, now with his customary predatory smile and a wave in the direction outside of the sect.

“You have one month until the mid-year spear assessment. Your goals until this time are simple: advance to the third stage of the Qi Gathering Realm, the second stage of the Body Tempering Realm, be at least at basic proficiency of woodcraft and survival, and advance your spear abilities with some real-world experience. If you do all that, and also perform well during the mid-year assessment, a reward might be in your future.”

Jianyi looked at the elder with an open mouth, struggling to process everything. One month to advance in his spirit cultivation was already a challenge, given that he currently couldn’t make any progress at all with the state of his Celestial Core Card.

On top of that, advancing two stages in body cultivation, even though he hadn’t even started yet, seemed absurd. Especially in the wilderness—he didn’t even know what woodcraft was.

“Teacher…” he paused, trying to come up with a polite way of sharing his befuddlement. “Teacher, I just spent all my contribution points; I can’t buy all the potions that I will need for the body cultivation advances…”

“Ah, Jianyi, you are a burgeoning alchemist, are you not? I don’t see anything that a resourceful young man like you can’t overcome,” said the elder cheerfully.

“But, Elder, I can’t even control three threads of qi, yet!”

The elder clapped his hands, got up, and pulled Jianyi out of his seat. “As I said, I have a lot of faith in you.”

He winked.

Jianyi was speechless. His answer to a clearly impossible challenge was to wink at him.

He looked at Minghao for help, but the retainer just smiled and nodded for him to leave the study and followed him outside.

“Minghao, what just happened?”

“What do you mean?” the older man said, not even stopping on his way back to the kitchen. “Are you not interested in going on an adventure with me?”

Jianyi sputtered. “That’s not it at all! I think that would be incredible.”

He paused. It did actually sound incredible. In a few months, Minghao had revolutionized his fighting abilities. Jianyi had no real idea what abilities he was lacking when it came to survival in the wilderness, but he could easily see how learning them from the experienced retainer would be a massive boon.

But the cultivation? He genuinely didn’t see how any of that would work. Then again, Elder Sun knew exactly what it would take to achieve the goals he had set. Either there was a way to make it, in that case Jianyi just had to go with the flow and work hard, or there wasn’t—in which case there was probably still a lesson to be learned.

“When do we leave?” he said, resigned to his fate.

Minghao turned around, grinning at him. “In one hour. You better get ready.”

***

Jianyi stood in his bedchamber, his body frozen.

He needed to pack.

What?

He could run to the Library for some last-minute research.

Did they have scrolls on woodcraft?

He could start integrating the Cinnamon Tree Sprout card.

Was there enough time?

He took a breath, his muscles slightly loosening up.

Sitting down at his desk, he pinned a note.

Shuhua,

I thought it necessary to inform you that my teacher is sending me on a month-long excursion. You should not misinterpret my absence from our weekly training.

Based on the last session, I would recommend spending some time on Piercing Fang with Hong; it is the most basic of attacks, after all. But—as I am sure you know—basic does not mean weak or unimportant; it means that it is the basis of your offense. That’s at least what my teacher told me. I am not sure somebody has told Hong yet; maybe you could.

I will return prior to the mid-term spear assessment and join your weekly sessions then. If you will have me, that is.

Your sect brother friend

Jianyi

He wasn’t sure whether it was appropriate to end a note on these terms. He wasn’t even sure why he had written the note in the first place. It seemed a bit like a waste of time, didn’t it?

But done was done.

He scratched his head, wrote another quick note to the tailors about his absence, and then quickly started packing.

It turned out, when one had a storage ring and very few possessions, it wasn’t that hard.

***

Surprisingly, it wasn’t Jianyi who was late.

Minghao was still patiently cooking in the kitchen when Jianyi was done, and time had run out.

“Are you going to take this along for our trip? I actually never tried to transport something hot in my storage ring. Does the food stay warm?”

“Yes, it does. Things are kept in stasis—it is apparently one of the reasons why living things can’t be transported in a normal storage ring.”

Jianyi nodded. “Anyway, this tasty-smelling food alleviates one of my worries for the next month.”

Minghao looked at him, eyebrow raised. “I don’t want you to be too disappointed later on, so let me be clear: this is my food. You will have to make your own.”

“Oh…” Jianyi looked at the retainer despondently, then shrugged and went to the pantry to get some supplies.

“Sorry, you are only allowed some spices if you want to bring them. No food.”

“I see. I guess you expect me to hunt for my food?”

“Hunt and forage, yes.”

He understood, really. It didn’t mean that he was looking forward to it, but he was sure that it would be helpful somewhere down the line.

That didn’t prevent him from taking the last two steamed buns he had prepared that morning when Minghao wasn’t looking.

Half an hour later, they were walking down the mountain towards the north.

Jianyi, happy to be wearing his coat, turned to Minghao. “How did you learn about survival and woodcraft?”

“I was born in a small village, and my father was a hunter. He took me into the forest from a very young age. Also, it is not like the sect won’t teach you the basics as well; it just happens later than Elder Sun would like. And lastly, even the least interested will either learn or perish when deployed on the frontlines…”

“Why on the frontlines? Aren’t there big camps where people live when not fighting?”

Minghao chuckled sadly. “I know that in the stories, the frontlines are either about a single, powerful cultivator that fights through endless masses, or about huge battles with thousands and thousands of cultivators clashing against the millions of demons and demonic beasts.”

Jianyi nodded, because that was how he imagined his future to be.

“It’s not wrong. Once you have seen one of the old monsters take to the battlefield, you know where those stories come from. It is, in many ways, more frightening than the big battles that get periodically launched to push the demons back.”

Jianyi shivered, thinking that his father must have died in one of those fights.

“But it is far from everything that happens up there. You see, the frontlines are way, way too long to be cordoned off completely. There are camps opposing the large gates, but demons try to vanish into the wilderness or flank us all the time. That means there are always missions to man camps in the wilderness, eliminate pockets of demonic beasts, or, and those are some of the most dangerous, to scout the corrupted lands that have already fallen to the demons.”

He knew, with absolute certainty, that this was his destiny, his purpose, but he was honest enough with himself to acknowledge that the feeling in his gut was not just excitement, but also fear. In light of that, this trip seemed even more important. He would need to get stronger so that he could face his future with his head held high.
