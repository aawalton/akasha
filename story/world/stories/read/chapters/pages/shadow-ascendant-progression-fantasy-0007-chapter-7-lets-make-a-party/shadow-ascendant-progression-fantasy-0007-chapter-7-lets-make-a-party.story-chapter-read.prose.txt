I hadn’t fully regained my self-consciousness yet; half my brain was probably still asleep. I took a deep breath and rolled to the side of the bed, pushing the blanket off my legs.

Rose…? Ah, that must be the perfume she’s wearing. I swung my legs over the edge and stretched my right arm. "Thanks for waking me up, Miss Weeble," I said politely, extending my hand toward her—and, for some reason, dropping to my knees.

My hands got slapped though.

"Idiot" she murmured, her blue eyes rolling back, "Drop the formality," her voice softened as she continued, "The matchup for the fest has been decided."

I got back on my feet and crossed to the water jug on the table. "Who are you fighting?" I asked, curious.

"Ahh, it’s a guy named Vermos, someone from Wiz dorm with an affinity for fire. I’ll beat him though, easy peasy!" Mabel replied, making a victory sign in front of her face with a grin.

Water almost always beats fire, and considering Mabel’s mastery of both ice and water, I’d give her fifteen wins out of ten. In short, she’s practically unbeatable.

Well… except for that 0.1 percent chance.

Placing the jug back on the table, I walked back to her. "Am I in your column?"

She shook her head; "you are not, though there’s Marcus in mine… Emily and Seraphina are in your column."

"Ohh… not that it matters," I chuckled. "I haven’t even collected 150 credits yet."

"You have… not?"

"I have… not."

Mabel stared at me for a good few seconds. It felt like an eternity to me, though. Then I swear she jumped at me, grabbed my shoulder with her oh so sharp nails and shook me, the way you will shake any kid and they will make the ahhh Owo aaah sound.

Adults don’t usually make that kind of sound, at least the ones I know don’t.

"Ahhh Owo, aaah," I squeaked, like those cushy toys that screamed when choked.

"Ohh…" Mabel stepped back, releasing my shoulder, "Sorry."

"Don’t worry, it’s not a big dea—" I flicked my hand as if to swat the thought away. But before I could complete it, her expression changed.

Mabel’s blue eyes narrowed, her thin eyebrows making a V, "What do you mean by it’s not a big deal? Getting 150 credits before the tournament is absolutely necessary!" She barked.

"I… ahh, I should reach my goal by the 11th, so most probably I’ll be safe."

"How short are you currently?"

"Ahhh…" I looked up, closing my eyes. "It's eighty-six, so like I am sixty-four points short, I should be able to reach my goal in eight to eleven days. I’d have a day or two to spare, I think."

"You think, huh…" She murmured, looking to her side, as if considering something. Were you able to find a team to join from the Wiz dorm?

"25 percent credit is not something people think I am worth," I smiled. "If only there weren’t any penalty on an additional party member, well if that was the case maybe someone from cryz might have picked me too? I mean, having an additional member is always a plus, right?"

Mabel didn’t answer immediately. She stared off into the distance, eyes unfocused, lips slightly parted. It was strange—she wasn’t usually the type to go silent, yet here she was, clearly lost.

"You know what, Arnos?"

"I know what?"

"…Don’t interrupt me in between!"

"Sorry"

"Ahem." Mabel cleared her throat, her blue eyes now focused on me.

"Let’s get into a temporary relationship."

I blinked, then being the optimistic person I am, filtered the temporary part, and emphasised the relationship part. Wait whaaaaatT! I yelled inwardly, clearly flabbergasted.

Never had I ever expected I would get proposed to by such a good-looking girl, "ye—"

"Like you and I could make a party. Since Emily has left for the royal capital, we haven’t really been going to the dungeon recently. Marcus says, going without her doesn’t make any sense, since we can’t really go any deeper than floor eight without her."

"Ahhhhhh," I murmured, "so that’s what you meant by temporary relationship?"

"Yeah," she nodded.

"That sure sounds good… I think."

Mabel’s eyes narrowed. "Why do you sound disappointed?" she asked.

I shook my head. I wasn’t disappointed or anything… it’s not like I was even expecting something from her.

I mean, it would’ve been good if my thoughts ended up being true and she was indeed proposing to me, but it still would’ve taken me a lot of time to see her as anything but a friend.

"I don’t sound disappointed!" I replied, forcing the widest smile possible onto my face. To show my uh, non-disappointment.

"Bleh." She stuck out her tongue, scrunching her nose at me like I was rotten food. "Don’t force a smile on your face; it looks ugly. Like—orc level ugly."

"Didn’t you once say orcs are pretty handsome if you remove their brai—" I continued.

Fair to say, I deserved the pillow Mabel threw at my face.

"Ouch… don’t throw pillows at me."

"It was a phase; forget it."

"Hahh, you say it was a phase, huh? How cute," I smirked.

Moments when I managed to get the upper hand with her were rare. Not because I was dumb or anything, but because she was too shameless to be fazed by anything.

"Didn’t you say orc commanders look better than kings cause they aren’t hairy?" I threw another jab at her, having no interest in backing down.

This was the true form of Arnos the… ahh, nothing? I mean, I don’t really have a cool nickname.

I looked at Mabel. she for some reason, was hiding her face. Was she weirded out by the words of her past self, maybe? But I think she should man up and face it like real Mabel would… Wait, is she an imposter?

"Stop, or I’ll—" Mabel paused. She turned her eyes around the room searching for something, then walked to my study table with loud steps, like an angry toddler. She grabbed a black fountain pen from my pen stand, removed the golden cap, and carefully placed it inside the stand.

Then she instantly turned around, holding the pen like a delinquent gripping a knife… in the reverse grip! "Say it once again and I…" she paused, her angry expression easing into a calm one.

"Hey Arnos, is it better to use a lance to pierce your eyes or a pen? Like, which would carry the lighter sentence?"

I paused, murmuring, "hmmm." Like some wannabe genius, I sat down on the bed, crossed one leg, and propped my chin on my palm, using my knee for support. Fair to say, it didn’t work. The pose was ridiculously uncomfortable.

"Man, these beds suck!" I yelled. Looking at Mabel, she nodded, perhaps understanding my pain.

I stretched out my left arm, grabbed the cushy yellow pillow, and set it on my lap. Then, I carefully placed my left hand over it, rested my right elbow on the back of that palm, and tucked my chin into my hand. Just like the detectives in those novels traveller Adam wrote, YaY.

After a moment of serious thinking. I finally had my answer. Crossing my arms, I looked up at Mabel.

"Stabbing with a pen carries less punishment. I mean, there are stricter rules for magic, right? Also, if you have to stab someone, I think you should collect some blackmail material beforehand," I replied.

"You should know this already, shouldn’t you?" I added.

"I was just confirming," Mabel replied, with a clumsy smile. "Okay…" She cleared her throat. "If you say orc once more, I’ll stab your eyes."

"Sure," I replied.

"Good choice," Mabel nodded quickly, walking back to the table. Her eyes widened suddenly as she stared at me. "That reminds me, mess food might finish soon."

My eyes widened with shock. Going to sleep without dinner was something I feared. How could I ever have let myself forget about that?

Swiftly, I jumped up from the bed and rushed for the door. "I’ll be going!" I shouted. Casting a brief glance at Mabel, who for some odd reason looked fascinated with my pen collection.

They were all simple, inexpensive fountain pens, though yes my collection was a work of an art.

"I’ll be going then, you… uhh."

"I’ll shut the door when I leave, don’t worry!" Mabel replied eagerly, though I was more troubled about her staying in my room than it being unlocked.
