I’m already concerned that the gentle curve I’ve created from my internal matrix to the new placement of the Skill will badly affect things; how much more so will that be true if I change more than just the position of the threads.

Yet I can’t help questioning whether a knot here is right. The feature later where one thread becomes two is clearly vital. But this one here….

With the knowledge that there are no crises currently demanding my presence, I feel no guilt at spending time contemplating the situation. This has to be done right the first time – I doubt there is much margin for error when it comes to a Skill like this. The last thing I want is to teleport one of my companions and have them arrive with their heart two millimetres out of place.

But what if the Skill has errors in it? Though this came from a Master Skill stone, I have enough Skills at Master to know that it doesn’t mean the Skill is perfect. This knot could reflect an error in understanding of the one who imprinted the Skill.

At the same time, who am I to know what is error – or maybe ‘inefficiency’ would be a better term – and what is correct? I’ve only had the Skill for a few months and I haven’t had time yet to even get it past Novice.

I wish I had a lip to chew – I need some way of releasing the tension I’m feeling here. My instincts are screaming one thing at me; my brain is telling me something else.

I actually pull out of my Core space and gaze thoughtfully up at the partially clouded night sky, appreciating the solid weight of Rock Fenrir’s body against my back – but missing the real one with a strength that surprises me.

I even lightly brush the Bond between me and the lizog, but feel that he’s sleeping peacefully. Sighing, I lean more heavily against Rock Fenrir. Though I know that he wouldn’t hold it against me, I would feel bad at disturbing Fenrir’s rest. He spends his days guarding one or other of the pack – the least he deserves is a quiet night. Anyway, it’s not like he could help me if he were here. So why am I feeling so guilty?

I push away the thoughts and concentrate on the question at hand.

Staring up at the stars, I find myself thinking about all the people over the centuries who sought flight by any possible means. Creating gliders, trying to mimic birds with wings, being shot from canons, and then finally culminating in the first biplanes. And humans were not satisfied with that – when the skies were no longer unachievable, Space became the next goal.

Those people didn’t succeed because they were content to remain with what everyone else had done before. They succeeded because they sought innovation at every turn – learning from the past, but not being bound by it.

I have the skills. Out of everyone here, I might be the only one capable of redrawing a Skill; the only one capable of changing one. I must not let fear get in my way – even if I don’t manage to succeed in this moment, what I learn will no doubt help me in the future.

Anyway, even if I mess this up irreparably, it’s not the end of the world. With my experimentation in growing a new strand of my internal matrix, I’ve learned enough that I’m sure I could cut all strands leading to Bond Teleport – and anything else I create. And, although Bond Teleport Skill stones are not exactly two to a penny, Nicholas might be willing to give me another; even if he won’t, I’ve done well enough without it.

I feel a swell of determination as I make my decision. Yet it would be foolish to dive into this without any preparation. Just because failure is an option I can live with doesn’t mean I want to court it actively.

Closing my eyes, I recall my many instances of using Bond Teleport. Training with Ninja. Saving Noir from the roc. Pulling Sirocco out of the flock of birds. Teleporting Aingeal away from Nessie’s attack – at great cost. And, most recently, pushing Bond Teleport to its limits – and beyond – to save Kalanthia.

The recall offered by my increased Intelligence enables me to feel like I am almost back in the moment, yet also allows me to dig deeper into my memories with each repetition. Again and again I relive the sense of mana gathering and then rushing out of me. Yet, as I press my memory more, I realise that it is far more targeted than that – it’s a teleportation Skill, not an explosion.

Moreover, I feel a dawning realisation occur to me as I understand just why this is considered one of my Class Skills and not a general one – it uses my Bonds.

That might seem like an obvious statement – of course it uses my Bonds; I can’t just teleport anyone. But it’s more than that. My Bonds don’t just act as a means of targeting, or a means of overriding someone’s autonomy over their own body. The Bond actually channels the mana to my companion’s side to create the second point of the transportation…string, I suppose.

It’s reminiscent of the gate that brought me over to Moriax from the samurans’ world – that needed both Nicholas and me to be actively supplying Energy from either side. So does this. Except, my companion doesn’t actually supply the mana themselves – I do, part in my own body, part in my companion’s, using the Bond to make that possible.

When I realise that, I have to stare up at the stars again, left awed by the implications of what I’ve just learned.

With this knowledge, I might be able to improve the efficiency of Bond Teleport according to the strength of the Bond between us – because a stronger Bond can channel more mana and more quickly. Maybe that’s why the Skill ended up burned out – the person who created it might not have had the kind of strong Bonds I do with my companions, and therefore the amount of mana that I could send to Kalanthia, that I needed to send to her, was just too much for it to handle – especially at Novice.

I know that my approach to Taming is unusual. Nicholas was carefully neutral when I told him about how I don’t want to Bind anyone forcibly without a very good reason, and his response suggested that he thought it was a moral scruple I would lose in time as the reality of the world came to bear. And his words came after generations of Titanbends – the ancestor whose grimoire Nicholas has lent me aside – who almost all seemed to consider Bonds tools to enforce their will, whether the bludgeoning tool of Dominate, or the more subtle coercion of Tame.

But this is validation of my alternative method, if I needed it.

I already knew that willing companions are far easier to manage. The amount of focus I had to put into holding the unwilling Tier threes’ Bonds to me during the battle with the parasite controller made them almost not worth having if just neutralising them hadn’t been a major part of our battle strategy. Kalanthia and Bastet’s Bonds are a breeze in comparison.

Of course, that’s partly because of where I have taken Dominate. Now at Master, all of its rank ups have all gone towards supporting me in my attempts to convince the other side to accept the Bond, rather than enabling me to more easily force it on someone unwilling. No doubt, those other Titanbends with different approaches would have, at my level, found controlling those Tier threes far easier.

However, if I’m right about Bond Teleport, I may have found another advantage to my method, if certain Skills could be more powerful or more efficient based on the strength of the Bond between us.

I’m tempted to go and look at my other Class Skills – at Management that allows my ‘subordinates’ to gain a percentage of my Wisdom and Willpower; at Tame that allows my companions to share abilities with each other. But that’s getting a bit ahead of myself. First, I need to sort out Bond Teleport. If that works the way I’m hoping it will, then I can see about perhaps improving my other Skills.

Guided by my sensations of using Bond Teleport, I dive back into my Core space. Approaching close enough to the blackened pattern of Bond Teleport that it is all I can see, I contemplate its twists and curves.

Immediately, several places stand out. Some of them I’m certain are absolutely necessary. I’d already noticed one of the key elements – the thread that splits into two and curves away from each other to form the shape that encircles the single thread. But now I look at it, I see that those two threads wind together at a later point and then split again to go in two different directions, leaving the Skill pattern entirely. One goes into my internal matrix. The other sneakily dives through my internal matrix and joins up directly with Dominate.

When I follow it into my Dominate Skill, I see that the channels making up that pattern are multiple times thicker – understandable since it is a Master Skill. Equally, I note that the Bonds that spin away from Companion Bond’s section of the Skill are all of different thicknesses. Kalanthia’s isn’t as thick as Bastet’s, which is right next door, but it is far thicker than any of the threads in Bond Teleport.

Perhaps, even if this connection and these aspects of Bond Teleport’s pattern are necessary, they aren’t optimised. The two threads that split apart are thinner than the original. That’s perhaps logical – a thread split into two can’t be the same thickness after the split as before. At least, not when dealing with a physical thread. An Energy channel is very different. Perhaps this thin thread is another reason for Bond Teleport burning out.

Other elements are just as clearly not necessary, or optimised. Here, I see a curve that I’m sure would be better as a straight line, but is forced into a curve because of another, equally unnecessary, doubling back of a thread. There, I sense a knot of thread curling back on itself several times that supposedly helps accumulate mana for the teleport, but serves only to throttle it.

And that knot that drove me into this contemplation? Definitely not essential. I sense what it was designed for – to prevent backlash from the Skill entering my channels. A sensible idea, especially for someone transporting potentially struggling beasts, but not for me. Anyway, I sense that this would be far better as a tight spiral than a chaotic knot – the energy would flow much better that way.

There are a few places that I’m less certain about – a sharp change in direction that clearly loses some mana, but that might be important; a point when the thread actually does a full circle around the whole pattern for some reason that I can’t determine. I decide to leave those alone for now. Better not risk pulling out the wrong jenga block and sending the whole lot tumbling down, not when there are several places that I’m very confident I can improve.

Given how much I see that needs work, I take some time to plan out the changes I will make before I dive into redrawing it.

And then, with determination and inspiration giving me as much energy as I need, I grab chunks of mana and pull them behind me as I paint with golden light.

With each inch that grows, I become more confident, competent. The internal matrix and luminous blackness that previously resisted me relax their grip, allowing me to create and expand new threads with increasing speed.

I lose track of time entirely – Rock Fenrir had better be able to deal with anyone who tries to attack me, because I doubt I’ll even be able to sense him trying to get my attention. All that matters is my mana and the channels.

I widen, strengthen, straighten, simplify, curve, and connect. The trickiest point is that crucial moment – where both threads separate. Somehow, I sense that I need to complete the two threads that result simultaneously or it won’t work.

That tests my ability to multi-task to the extreme, but I succeed. I even widen the thread directly before the split to create a small ‘reservoir’, and ensure that each thread is the same width as the original single one.

And then finally, I’m done.

I check over every inch of the new Bond Teleport with assiduity, checking with all my available senses for anything that might cause a problem. For any kink, for any stretch of channel that feels even slightly wrong.

And then, when I’m confident, I move to the beginning of the old Bond Teleport, where the gold shifts into black.

There, I hesitate. I can’t affect the black, but I can affect the gold. And having the old, burnt Bond Teleport hanging around can’t be a good thing.

Moment of truth.

Tracing back from the old Bond Teleport to where the connection to the new version branches off from my internal matrix, I slice through the gold. Pulling the mana out of the now unnecessary connection, I watch it crumble into dust. The black outline of Bond Teleport doesn’t move, but now there is no indication among the gold channels that it was ever connected.

Moving over to the two other connections to my mandala and Dominate, I do the same to them.

I half expect the blackened Bond Teleport to disappear as soon as it’s disconnected, but it doesn’t. It just hangs there, unmoving. But it’s disconnected from my internal matrix, so hopefully it won’t cause any issues on level up.

I’ll have to find out.

But first, I need to test whether the new Bond Teleport works as intended.

Pulling out of my Core space, I’m surprised when the first thing that registers is warmth and slight softness to the surface on which I’m leaning. Has Rock Fenrir become more alive? I wonder for a moment before I turn my head and I realise the true cause.

Rock Fenrir is sitting off to the side, his back to me, his focus on the forest. What I’m leaning on is the real Fenrir – and his Bond makes it very clear he’s not happy.
