“Wait, hang on,” Ciro said, licking his lips. He eyed the loot of the fallen. Several spears lay sprawled on the ground. It seemed everyone had forgotten about the gun the big guy carried. It lay just beside the stocky green-haired man.

The sniper had taken a glance at it, but nothing more.

Picking the weapon up, he aimed it at Kaisu, pretending to fire it. “Bang, Bang!”

Kaisu’s silvery skin turned a darker shade. “Put that thing down!” She hissed.

Lowering the weapon, Ciro gave her a puzzled look. “I’m just playing around,” he caressed the weapon with his fingertips. “With this we can take out all the Krovalith we need. We gotta kill about four each, if we want to live–”

Before he could even finish his sentence, Kaisu charged at him, with gritted teeth. Her gills hissed, releasing air that turned to vapour in the humid jungle. She tackled him to the ground, both sliding in the mud, picking up dust.

Peeling the gun out of his hands, she grabbed her spear, smashing the weapon with the spear’s shaft, as if it were a hammer.

Ciro’s pupils dilated, he pushed himself trying to grab the spear off Kaisu. “Stop! Stop! What’s wrong with you, that’s our one-way ticket out of here.”

As soon as his hands made contact with the spear, Kaisu grabbed him by the collar and threw him away. By the time she was done the gun was left in fragments. Ciro crawled towards the remains of the weapon. He clasped his hands in prayer, “I’m so sorry.”

He got up off the ground, pointing a finger at Kaisu’s face. “What’s the big idea?”

Kaisu only grunted in response, Ciro could see the little grin of satisfaction on her face. She had doomed them. All Ciro wanted to do was get out of this jungle alive, and she ruined it all.

“Such a weapon shouldn't be used,” said Kaisu. “Let us move out, we have dawdled for far too long.”

“Ugh! I can’t believe this,” Ciro clutched at his head in frustration. He couldn’t let this go. He needed to at least gain some spoils from all of this. Having lost his eyeballs and no high-tech weapon. He looked over the sea of corpses, the multiple spears on the ground. I guess one of those will do, Ciro thought.

Then his eyes became stuck on the large green-haired man. Ciro grinned, looking over his clothes. Smothered in dirt, the suit was no longer gray but closer to brown. There were tears in all sorts of places, from all the fights he had been in.

He moved over to the corpse, pulling the clothes off its body.

“You’re not taking his clothes.” Kaisu watched him from a distance, ready to leave with her spear in her hand.

“Watch me,” Ciro smirked, taking off the gray combat pants of the carcass. With all the clothes taken, he pulled at the collar of his suit. Before he took it off, he looked over at Kaisu. “On second thought, don’t look.”

Kaisu raised a non-existent eyebrow, “I have no interest in your pitiful body.”

He didn’t know whether to be offended or not, but it didn’t matter anyway, she was an alien after all. He pulled the suit over head, surprisingly coming off with ease, quickly putting on his new garbs. The clothes didn’t fit him completely. They appeared slightly baggy on him, but they were much more comfortable than his old suit. Plus, it was stylish.

“You seriously watched me change?”

“There was nothing to see.”

“Whatever,” Ciro grumbled, grabbing a spear off the ground, before pushing past Kaisu as they entered through the maze of trees.

He wasn’t in front for long, with Kaisu deciding to lead instead. “You follow my lead, Otherworlder.”

“The name’s Ciro, you know this,” he rolled his eyes.

“You have proven yourself to be dishonorable scum. I will not utter your name.”

Ciro shook his head, he didn’t know how it all worked out, but he teamed up with an alien. The sniper's words kept ringing in his mind. He looked ahead towards Kaisu. Is this the right thing to do? Are the aliens my enemy? He pondered. Stifling a bitter laugh, he found irony in that the beings that threatened to kill him so far were his own people. Humans.

It was to be expected, Ciro pushed away all thoughts and focused back on the hunt. Alien, human, it no longer mattered. Survival was of the utmost importance.

They trekked further into the jungle, unsure where the Krovalith lay. Suddenly Kaisu stopped in her tracks, raising her hand as a signal.

“What is it?” asked Ciro.

“I can taste blood.”

“Are you sure you didn't bite your cheek?” Ciro said, twisting his lips.

“Someone has died, multiple people.”

Ciro scanned the area. His heart rate rose. She's got me on edge, he thought. Surrounded by trees and the humming of big insects flying around them, it was hard to believe there was anything out here. “Damn it, this spear better be useful.”

“This way,” Kaisu moved, motioning Ciro to follow. Pushing aside a curtain of thick, waxy leaves, he followed her into a small clearing where the air felt colder. His eyes traced the uneven rock face that loomed ahead, half-swallowed by moss and creeping vines. At first, it looked like just another tangle of stone and roots, until his gaze settled on a dark seam near the base.

A cave full of all-consuming darkness.

Ciro swallowed, he looked towards Kaisu. “We're not going in there.”

Kaisu didn't respond, her eyes more focused on the outside of the cave. Three bodies littered the entrance, along with broken weapons. Ciro wanted to vomit at the sight. One body had their entire neck twisted, its corpse was lying flat on its stomach but its head watched the sky.

They slowly walked towards the corpses. The stench grew stronger with each footstep. A rancid smell of iron and rot that clung to the back of his throat. He grimaced, trying his hardest not to throw up.

They were mutilated, the best way Ciro could describe it. The other two bodies were in an even worse state than the first.

Laying on a large boulder, the second body's torso was completely ripped open, as if zipped down like a jacket. The large alien insects fed on its organs, barely anything of its heart remained.

Ciro soon realized they weren't humans. Both bodies had a blue tint to them. Their eyes were large, only made more horrifying by their dead stares. They each had a small horn on their heads that seemed ready to crumble at the slightest touch.

Entrails spilled across the forest floor in tangled heaps, glistening in the filtered sunlight. What remained of the face was crushed bones crushed inward, as though a massive hand had closed around the skull and squeezed until it popped.

The third was the worst.

She lay sprawled over a patch of flattened grass, her spine bent backward until her body formed a grotesque arch. Her body was petite compared to the two men she more than likely died beside. The skin was marred by rows of deep punctures, as if jagged teeth had chewed through her from shoulder to thigh. Chunks of flesh were simply gone, ripped away in uneven patches, leaving raw muscle and shattered bone exposed to the air. One hand still clutched a broken knife, the blade snapped and smeared with blackish blood not of her own.

Ciro's hand hovered over the knife. He pulled it back, feeling some disgust in his actions.

Crouching down and inspecting the bodies, Kaisu spoke, breaking the intense silence. “Cryori. They're a founder race. I can't imagine a single Krovalith doing this to them.”

“Look, let's just get out of here and look for something that won't maul us to death. If they were just as trained as you and died. We'd be screwed.” Ciro pleaded as best he could.

She didn't respond, pondering as she continued to look at the corpses. “We should leave.” She finally spoke, glancing towards the ominous cave ahead.

“Thank you,” Ciro huffed, making his way back the way they came. Just as he was going to take his last step and leave the small clearing he heard rustling to his side.

He looked towards Kaisu, she already had her eyes fixed on the location of the sound. Her spear pointed in its direction. He nudged his head, motioning for her to look.

She rolled her eyes. Ciro could've sworn she whispered some sort of curse aimed at him. She crept towards the rustling, which grew louder and constant.

A bush covered the source of the noise. Quickly Kaisu pushed the bush aside. Ciro saw something big and bulbous sticking out from the ground, wiggling erratically.

Just as Kaisu was about to impale it, Ciro pulled her away. “Wait, what is it?” he hissed.

“I don't know.” Kaisu admitted, “but anything unknown should be killed.”

Ciro stepped towards the unknown object. “I think it's an ass.”

“Excuse you?”

Ciro poked at it. “Yep definitely an ass, look it has small little legs.” He pointed towards the small spindly legs that swung violently in the air.

Kaisu stepped forward, looking more closely right beside Ciro. “It's wearing the standard issue Exofront suit.”

Ciro nodded, unsure what to do. “It's an alien.”

“An Otherworlder.”

Ciro groaned, “please don't call it an Otherworlder. You're putting it in the same category as me.”

“Why would it matter?”

Ciro pointed at it once more, “look at it, it's most likely the size of a football, at most a child. We're not the same.”

“I see no difference.”

Before an argument broke out between them, the alien butt began mumbling through the soil, its legs’ still thrashing in the air.

“What do we do?” Ciro asked, turning back to the cave. “We don't have any time, and whatever did that could still be out there.” He pointed towards the three horned alien bodies.

“You pull the left leg, I pull the other,” Kaisu sighed.

Ciro clicked his tongue, grabbing the small little foot he was assigned. “I can't believe we're doing this.”

“One, two, three.”

They both heaved, whatever mass they pulled, slowly emerged from the soil. Ciro and Kaisu were both pushed back once the unknown entity popped out.

Ciro grew to regret their decision even more.

It was the ugliest thing he'd ever seen.

It wore a leathery green skin, yet it was slick that reflected the sunlight. Thick, muscular arms hung too long from its stunted body, the fingers tipped with blunt, swollen knuckles.

Barely reaching Ciro's chest, most of its size came from its bulbous head. Framed by ridges that ran across its scalp like melted wax.

Four milky white eyes blinked in unsettling unison, each one set at uneven angles across its face, independently twitching towards different directions. Its nostrils flared and pulsed, drawing in air with wet, slurping sounds, as if it was tasting Ciro's very scent.

It spoke with a gurgling noise, like a child imitating an adult's speech. Words slipped from its crooked mouth, half-language, half-gurgle, punctuated by sharp, breathy gasps that made Ciro’s skin crawl.

“Thank you, oh great one's,” it said, bowing its head on the ground. “For you have rescued Peto.”

“Peto?”

“It is this one's name,” it gurgled.

Ciro looked over to Kaisu, “look it even has a name,” Ciro smiled sarcastically.

Kaisu stood up. “Good luck Otherworlder. We are mid hunt, we shall leave you now.” Kaisu stepped away, looking around deciding where to go.

“Peto wishes to return your kindness. Peto shall aid in your hunt.” The booger given form smiled warmly at him. Ciro shivered.

“Listen…uh Peto, we appreciate the help, but I don't think you'll be of any use.” Ciro placed a hand on his small shoulders. “Just try to survive.”

“Peto doesn't want you to end up like horny beasts.”

Horny? Who's horny? Realization dawned on Ciro, he looked back towards the corpses, then a cold dread washed over him. “What happened to those guys back there Peto.”

“Horny beasts use Peto to find alpha. Peto find alpha. But alpha strong, kills them all. Peto smart. Peto run. But Peto head too big. Peto head got stuck in ground.” The alien twiddled his fingers in embarrassment.

Ciro and Kaisu shared worried glances.

“Uhh Peto, little buddy. You can lead us to some Krovalith right?” Ciro asked, hesitating slightly.

“Peto sense Krovalith mostly dead. Only alpha remains. Peto will guide, help saviors.”

Ciro grabbed Kaisu, pulling her towards them. “What do we do,” he hissed

“His sources may not be entirely accurate.” Kaisu responded. “Tell me, Peto, where is this Alpha right now?”

Peto paused for only a second. All four glassy eyelids stared into Ciro’s soul.

“Alpha is coming here now.”
