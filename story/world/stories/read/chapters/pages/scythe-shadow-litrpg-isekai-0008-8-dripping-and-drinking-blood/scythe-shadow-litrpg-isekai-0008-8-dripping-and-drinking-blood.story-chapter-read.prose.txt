I sat down at the end of the jungle bar, leaving several empty stools between me and the next Reaper.

It felt like everyone was looking at me, the clueless Amber. But every time I glanced over my shoulder, they were all preoccupied with their own drinks and conversation. Nothing suspicious amid the music of clinking glasses, laughing, and hushed stories. No different than any bar back home. No one paid me any heed, waiting for me to turn so they could stab me in the back.

Apparently, the paranoia of Zeus' betrayal hasn't worn off yet. Bastard.

Thinking about it, I didn’t notice any scythes in the entire room besides my own. I glanced over my shoulder again to verify.

That seemed odd.

“Ahem,” a voice said from behind the bar. The barkeeper leaned over, elbows on the counter, staring at me. She was a short middle-age woman with a red vest over a black shirt and trousers. Her reddish-graying hair was tied up into a neat bun.

“Hello,” I said.

She gestured with a tilt of her head at the scythe in my left hand. It stretched up over my head like a fucking spotlight. Here’s the idiot noob!

“Would you mind putting that away?” she said.

“Away…?” I scanned the room for any sort of closet or rack to hang it on. Nothing.

“Is this your first day, honey?”

“I… Yeah. What gave it away...?”

“Well, you waltzed in here waving a deadly weapon around like a flag, for one.”

“Is there someplace I can—”

“Judith, my love!” Jericho danced his way over to the stool beside me.

The barkeeper—Judith apparently—rolled her eyes. “Jericho… Haven't seen you in a while. This one yours, then?”

“I’m afraid so.”

“Haven’t you taught him a darn thing yet?”

“You know, he just won’t listen.” He pointed a scolding finger at me. “Bad Nameless Amber. Bad!”

“Wow, gee, thanks,” I muttered. “Throw me under the proverbial bus, why don’t you?”

Judith pulled a white cloth from her belt and wiped a red splotch from the bar top. “Why don’t we start with stopping the six-foot, bladed murder weapon from dripping blood all over my bar, hmm?”

Jericho looked up at my scythe, which was—as she noted, however exaggeratedly—dripping a few drops of blood on the bar. “Yes. It is rather unsightly, isn’t it?”

Nuzzle stuck his tongue out and caught the next drop, which sizzled on contact.

“Would someone please just tell me where the hell I should put it?” I interjected.

Jericho held up his carapace-covered arm and pointed to the inner forearm.

I didn’t get it. Was this some kind of Amber hazing?

But his scythe was nowhere to be seen, same as all the other Reapers in the tavern.

“The scythe is an extension of you,” he said. “You really just have to think about retracting it.”

“Retracting? What?” I stared at my own forearm. “You’re kidding, right?”

Jericho held his arm out, then opened his fingers as if ready to catch something. The bluish-black carapace on his arm shifted, slowly at first, then quicker, like a viscous liquid. The scythe’s blade flicked out the back of his forearm, extending well beyond his elbow. Bubbles of carapace crawled over his arm to the large fingers at the end, melting out of his palm, forming the scythe’s shaft both down and up from the hand simultaneously. All the while, the blade itself slid past his wrist, over the back of his hand, and glided up to the top of the shaft. The last tendril of carapace detached and the end of the scythe handle tapped on the ground as it solidified.

Jericho curled his fingers around it.

“Well fuck,” was all I could say.

Judith laughed as she ducked under a tree branch and stepped to the back wall. “I’ll get you a drink. First one’s on the house.”

“Alright,” Jericho said. “Your turn.”

“So I just…”

“Tell it what to do with your mind."

I stared at him blankly.

He sighed. "Did you use an ability during your battle?"

"Yeah. I guess. I just kind of moved really fast for a short burst."

"Ah. Aero Shift probably. Not a bad skill to start with, though doesn't really help your offensive output much."

So, it has a name. Aero Shift. I liked the sound of that.

"Regardless," Jericho continued, "you can control your inventory in the same way you control your ability. Just think it."

Of course, using my ability had been a complete accident. Maybe I can 'accidentally' put this thing away too.

I looked up at my scythe’s blade, not convinced the razor sharp, three-foot slice of metal was going to fit inside my arm somehow, even with the blackish shell around it. But I also hadn’t woken up that morning thinking I’d be in the shadow realm hunting monstrous human Sins a few hours later.

With a shrug, I instructed my scythe to retract, commanding it to move inversely to how I observed Jericho’s deploy. To my surprise, it listened without hesitation.

It was an eery sensation. While the outer exoskeleton had no feeling, my muscles and nerves beneath felt the tingle of motion as the scythe’s handle melded into the arm and integrated seamlessly. The blade was another matter. Once on my forearm, it rested there, as if waiting for further instruction. I obliged, telling it to retract. In a blink, the blade vanished into my arm with the force of a shotgun, jolting it against my torso.

I held up my arm, examining it. I never would have known there was a scythe hiding within if I hadn’t witnessed it myself.

“I’m not sure that will ever get old,” I said.

Jericho retracted his scythe in a fraction of the time it had taken me. His arm also recoiling as the blade returned home. “Oh, it doesn’t,” he said with a smile.

Judith came back and slid a pair of drinks over to us.

A Bloody Mary.

She winked at me, then wiped the bar clean one more time and went to deal with other customers.

I picked up the drink, eying the cloudy red liquid. “Not funny.”

“It’s a little bit funny,” Jericho said, rainbow nailed fingers curling around the glass as he sipped his own. “And delicious.”

I picked the celery stalk from the ruddy glass and held it up to Nuzzle. He sniffed it once, then wrapped his tongue around it and sucked the entire thing down his throat in one gulp, a feat that was almost as impressive as sliding a murder weapon inside your arm.

He burped a little ball of smoke, which puffed out onto my cheek. Then he jumped onto the bar and gagged like a cat trying to hack up a fur ball. After a few attempts, he finally succeeded, vomiting a charred, shriveled celery stalk onto the bar counter.

Judith was busy helping another customer and hadn't noticed yet, so I quickly grabbed a cocktail napkin and wiped up the disgusting regurgitation.

"She'll never know," Jericho whispered.

"I always know!" Judith shouted from down the length of the bar. She came back moments later with another cloth and wiped away the rest of Nuzzle's vomit residue.

"Thanks," I muttered.

Judith grunted. "I've seen it all, kid. You wouldn't believe."

"You're not a Reaper though?" I asked, noting she had no cloak or carapace-armored arm.

She glanced to Jericho. "This one's astute," she said, nonplussed.

"He gets that from his mother's side, I assure you," Jericho said.

I rolled my eyes, and found Judith doing the same. "Did you get here the same way as us?" I asked her. "Dissection by skeletons and all that?"

"No clue." She continued wiping the bar absentmindedly. "No memory of anything before I got here, same as all the other non-Reapers. Just woke up out in the snow one day. Eventually wandered to a small village of others like me, got a job here to make a life." Her face turned to a frown. "Don't give me that look, Mister I-Just-Got-Here Amber. I know what all this is. Been doing this long enough. Talked to enough Reapers, overheard even more."

I nodded, trying to hide any pity from my expression.

"I suppose I must've had a life in Vitria before," Judith continued. "Can't miss what you don't remember though, right? I'm happy here. I'm glad I can contribute in my own small way to what you lot are doing. Hopefully it makes Vitria better for those I left behind."

Whatever pity I'd been holding on to completely evaporated. "That's actually a really impressive outlook."

Judith clicked her tongue and eased her way down the bar, wiping the surface as she went. "Brown nosing won't get you more free drinks. Worthy effort though." She began chatting with a group of other Reapers out of earshot.

"Don't worry. I can buy you another drink," Jericho said, leaning into my shoulder.

"I'm not looking of free drinks!" I hadn't even touched my first one yet.

"Really? Dissection by skeletons (your words, mind you), isn't driving you to empty a keg?"

"Well… I was already a bit hung over when all this happened."

"Mmm. Dumped, eh?"

Nuzzle gave me a lick on the hand, eyes mirroring the sympathetic look Jericho gave me.

"What? No! The opposite, if you must know. My bachelor party."

"Oof, that's a rough way to go, my boy. Whose the lucky lady? Actually, never mind. System won't let you say it anyway. But honestly, bachelor party? It's a miracle you remember a thing from the transformation."

"Not sure I can ever un-remember it. The monster killing me was terrifying sure, but the skeletal doctors cutting me and injecting me. That was pure horror. And such a contrast from the angel."

"The what now?" Jericho asked, his drink paused halfway to his mouth.

"I mean, he probably wasn't an actual angel, but it's the best description I've got. You know. All dressed in white and gold. Faceless mask. Glowing eyes. Fancy crown."

Jericho put his drink down without taking a sip. "Now that's one I haven't heard before."

"There was no one like that there for you?"

"Most certainly not. And you're one order of magnitude off, by the way." He grinned. "That wasn't an angel. You met a god."
