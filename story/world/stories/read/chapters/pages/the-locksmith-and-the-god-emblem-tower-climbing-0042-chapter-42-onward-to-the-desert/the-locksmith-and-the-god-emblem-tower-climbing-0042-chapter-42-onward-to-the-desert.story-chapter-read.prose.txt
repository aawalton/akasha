Like a gelatinous form, the physical form of time seemed to want to rip him out of the tower again, but in a nanosecond, it restrained itself and threw him back where it had found him. Harry then, distancing himself from his companions — still extremely confused every time the loop reset — found himself with his face covered in sand.

With his heartbeat slowing, he blinked slowly as he turned upwards. Staring at the false blue sky, Harry stopped himself from perceiving the visual imperfections and hugged the infinite azure. We made it.

With his hands behind him and relaxing on the grainy texture of the sand, Harry relaxed after an intense cycle. He breathed in and out deeply, almost as if meditating. His "meditation" was quickly interrupted by the surprised exclamations of everyone involved.

Next to Harry, Goldman stood up and began to gently massage his stomach. Thank the gods! He was grateful for the relief of his stomach pain. Noticing Harry's unburdened state, Goldman decided to greet the others and try to explain the events that had occurred.

Was it strange to find peace in moments like this? Harry certainly thought so. Nevertheless, he allowed himself to ignore the tremors in his heart and, for a moment, appreciate the absurd world he lived in. A distant sense of happiness that, however close it seemed, was getting further away; he needed to cling to these good moments before they disappeared. Little did Harry know, however, that true happiness is not momentary.

Then, through his closed eyes, he felt a shape shadowing him. Confused, he allowed himself to abandon his relaxed state only to be face to face with Madeline. With an indecipherable expression on her face, the young woman and former thief simply stared at the Locksmith. Harry then sat down on the sand.

"Maddy? Are you alright? What are you..." And the girl, without warning, knelt on the sand and hugged him.

He heard sobs, which he judged to be muffled crying. Did I say something wrong? he wondered as the girl continued to cry. He then understood the gravity of the situation, and afraid to say anything, possibly worsening the woman's clearly fragile state, Harry hugged her back.

Now holding her even tighter, Madeline seemed to stop sniffling. Pulling away, with her hands on Harry's shoulders, Madeline tucked her hair behind her ear while smiling, tears streaming down her face.

"I..." Understanding the situation a little better, Harry stopped himself from speaking. Whatever had happened while Harry was away, Madeline and some of the others were clearly the most affected. He still remembered Madeline's body and how vulnerable she looked. She could have died.

Although the loops reset physical conditions to the moment they entered the tower, certain psychological impacts could not be erased. Given Dracno's actions and the damage Harry had inflicted on an already broken man, Madeline had certainly had a traumatic experience.

Her red hair, affected by the wind coming from behind Harry, gave even more space to her soft features and empathetic eyes. At least you're okay. This struck Harry with a new sense of relief he didn't realize he needed.

"...Couldn't you have painted a healing potion or something?" Harry joked as he smiled back at her.

"Oh, shut up..." Madeline lightly punched his shoulder as she chuckled. "How could you joke in a time like this?"

"I'm sorry, I'm sorry...." He laughed as he looked away, scratching his head. "I'm glade you're safe, though. I really am."

"I know." She simply answered.

"...But at least you didn't had to smell Alexander's rotting body"

"Okay, now you just ruined the moment."

When everyone reunited, not a single member with permanent damage, Harry recounted what had happened, from the clash with Alexander to the shelter behind the doors. After repeatedly confirming to Brun that Alexander was dead, the tension in the air finally seemed to have eased.

Harry then decided to be transparent with the group about how he managed to avoid the explosion at the lighthouse. He told them about Raven and the stopped time. He added an even more mysterious layer to the figure. Despite this, he omitted the conversation about the God Emblem, since that could generate unnecessary intrigue among the group, who already had a lot on their minds. You don't need to get into that... It's too dangerous.

They took a few minutes to discuss their next steps. Despite being certain Alexander was dead, they couldn't say the same about Cal and Gale. I wonder if they managed to escape... Although the third floor isn't that much better.

"Why were we underneath the island right after I got back?" Harry asked, the group in a sparse circle.

"After the... explosion," Brun began. "We... It all happened so fast I don't even remember exactly what happened, but I know Goldman and Madeline saved us. They stopped us from falling to certain death while we started chasing Alexander. Apparently, that had always been his plan, to go down there and destroy everything. Despite that, I imagine the exit is still down there under all that dust and debris. I don't think even he believed he could jump an entire floor..."

"I see..." Harry said thoughtfully.

After that, Goldman, Madeline, and Brun decided to go down to the bottom of the island again, to the only dry spot surrounded by a thirsty sea. Right below that spot, the hatch remained.

With that, Daniel volunteered to check if Cal or Gale were alive and if they could chase them to the next floor. Manes, for some reason, decided to go after Daniel, who didn't refuse company. According to him, he wanted to "learn something" from the brute. As the two walked through some bushes, Harry stared at Manes, tilting his head slightly. Since when are they friends?

"Vela." Harry turned and said, shaking off the strange thought about Manes and the Viking. "It's been a while since we talked. You've been pretty quiet these last few days—I mean, cycles."

"I imagine so." The candle man replied with his characteristical candelabrum. "I've just been thinking, that's all. About my usefulness in the group and about... other things. We have all the time in the world, but it seems like even that isn't enough. Do you understand what I mean?"

"...Surprisingly, yes." Harry sighed deeply. "Just remembering Estela's eyes on that cold floor... Fuck, man."

"Prism's death wasn't your fault," Vela said. "You know that, don't you?"

"I..." He paused for a second before answering. "I don't know anything anymore. It seems like with each floor we go down, I know less about who I am and what my purpose is in this damned Tower. I keep telling myself things will be alright but..."

"But what?" Vela asked in a calm voice. "Are you afraid things won't happen the way you want? Seizing control of your own fate isn't about forcefully narrowing the paths to the one you seek, but rather accepting that how you get there is more important than where you'll end up. I'm not saying you shouldn't fight for your own life, but that some things are outside of your control. The more you try to change them, the more you'll get hurt."

"And how do I know which things can and can't be changed?" Harry felt a growing anger coursing through his body, a rage that screamed at his disagreement with Vela. Still, he tried to remain calm.

"You learn from your own mistakes. Your errors." Vela said, suddenly melancholic. "I suppose it is the only constant in life. I have found that meditation helps."

Harry noticed Vela's features and sighed deeply. He just wants to help. Despite being interested in the former monk's life, Harry only gave an apologetic smile and looked at Madeline as she created platforms for the group to descend at Brun's request. He turned to Vela and gave him a smirk.

"...Was this really an advice or are you just trying to get a meditation buddy?"

"I..." Vela looked at Harry confused, but upon noticing his smile, the monk's demeanor changed. "You got me, Mr. Harry. Nothing goes through you, huh?"

"Fuck yeah!" He yelled to the sky, startling Madeline, who simply laughed as she saw the apparently unhinged man. "Now come on, let's give backup to Daniel. If he finds Gale one more time, he'll probably kill him."

"Are we ready?" Goldman said as he stood on top of a hatch, the waves avoiding the man in a circular spot.

"Yeah..." Harry said, his stomach making strange noises. "You can go first, I'll go after..."

"Are you okay, Harry? You look like—"

"I'm great! Just you guys go, and I'll go in later."

After Brun analyzed the location with her Emblem and confirmed that the loop had just been reset, the group members jumped inside. The first to slide through the gate to the next floor were Madeline and Daniel. Besides them, the rest already had their C-Coins ready to use when they arrived.

"Only you left, boyo!" Goldman said with a contagious smile, but Harry was too unwell to answer him. He swallowed and moved to the next floor.

Carefully, he balanced himself with the change in gravity while looking around, his stomach growling.

Apparently, the fifth floor was a wasteland?

In the middle of the desert, however, relatively far away, a coliseum appeared, with what seemed to be fires and explosions visible coming from the top. Goldman then arrived and the hatch closed, once again becoming overflowing with sand.

Before anyone could say anything, Harry then vomited violently on the ground to his right, much to the displeasure of the entire group. Daniel, to their even greater displeasure, had his shoe nearby.

"WHAT THE HELL IS THIS?!" Daniel yelled as he jumped back, kicking his foot in the sand. "Locksmith!?" The man, startled, even summoned an arrow.

"I think it's that piece of Meta... I..." And he vomited again, sounds of disgust coming from the back of the group. "...I feel better now." And he really did. Despite the last half hour being torturous, it seemed he had thrown away what was left of the Meta. At least that's what he wanted to believe.

Then he felt another vibration, this time not coming from his stomach, but from the ground; it felt like a small earthquake.

Already?! We just got here! Harry thought. Please, anything but an earthquake... I don't want to have to use my doors anytime soon. If I throw up in the middle of it...

If he'd known what was coming, he would have wished for an earthquake.
