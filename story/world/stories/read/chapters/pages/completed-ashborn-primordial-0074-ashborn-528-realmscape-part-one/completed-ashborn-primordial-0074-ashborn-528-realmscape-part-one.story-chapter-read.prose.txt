The wolves had arranged themselves along the far wall of the massive chamber, as though in understanding of the gravitas of what was to come.

Shan sat at their center, stoic as always, his keen eyes noticing every movement.

Vir didn’t know how they’d found their way down here, but he suspected Janak had something to do with it. He was glad they had. Though he was not as close to the others as Ashani was, Shan had been his faithful ally and trustworthy friend for so many years now. Even if just a little, their presence helped ease the weight of what he was about to endure.

Ashani stood to one side of the machine, looking as serene and godlike as ever, though Vir had learned long ago that she could shut off her facial emotions as she wished, and the fact that she was doing so now said everything about what she must truly have felt inside.

Janak stood before the machine itself, studying the contraption he’d spent millennia building. Vir would give anything to know the god’s thoughts at that moment.

Maiya was, of course, by his side, her hand in his. She hadn’t spoken once since leaving their chambers.

“The process will be identical to your practice sessions,” Janak said, turning to face them. “My predecessor designed the system that way to maximize the probability of success. The only divergence will come at the end, when I introduce my… modification.” His gaze settled on Vir, and as always, Vir felt that the ancient being was seeing into his soul. “You will know when it is time.”

Vir nodded, though it was more for himself.

Janak was quiet for a moment, as though deliberating whether to speak his next words.

“I must thank you,” he said at last. “For this sacrifice. For everything you have given to reach this moment.” His voice was calm, but Vir didn’t miss the slight quiver in his words. Was he nervous? Or was that something else in his voice? “Truly. You cannot know how proud I am. Not of my predecessor’s reincarnation magic. Of you. Of all that you are. Against our favor though the odds may be, we must do all we can. I could not have asked for more from any incarnation than I have received from you.”

“Then don’t,” Maiya snapped. Vir turned in shock, and though he found tears welling in her eyes, her face was as set as if she were about to take on an Ash Beast.

“Don’t stand there and thank him!” she said. “You ought to be ashamed of yourself. You have had millennia to devise something better. Millennia! And this is all you could do?”

The tears spilled over, and she didn’t bother to wipe them. “If only the original Janak had survived… Shame on you.”

The chamber went silent. Even the wolves stilled.

Vir stood with his hand in hers and said nothing. Part of him wanted to speak, to soften her words, because they were unfair and they both knew it.

Janak had not chosen to spend five thousand years fighting this war, trapped inside a dead tower, his people gone and his daughter transmuted into a horror he had to stare at every day.

But another part of Vir, the much larger part that cherished and loved Maiya, forced him to say nothing in the deity’s defense.

But when Janak finally spoke, it was not with admonishment or even a defense of his situation.

“You cannot know how I have longed for my predecessor’s memories of the moment where he looked beyond the pale and glimpsed their power,” Janak said quietly. “The truth of things, as you will glimpse yourself, should our gambit succeed. But my memories were imprinted prior to the Fall, and the enemy ensured any records he left of that experience were wiped as thoroughly as if they had never existed. From the beginning, I have been crippled and blind. I have known it every day of every year I have spent in this accursed tower. Every moment, I am reminded of my shortcomings and what they cost me and the realms. And yet, what can a soul do but try?”

Ashani moved to his side and placed her hand on his arm, the innocent gesture nonetheless so full of love.

“He has toiled and suffered more than any of us,” she said, looking at Maiya without reproach. “It is not fair to blame him.”

“You know I'm no stranger to blame. Or to sacrifice. I've been a queen for years. But fair or not,” Maiya said, her voice breaking as she spoke. “It’s my Vir who has to die. ”

Ashani’s expression didn’t change. “And my Janak as well.”

Their words were like a seric blade to Vir's heart. Their sacrifice would not end with their deaths. It would live on in the hearts of all who loved them.

And yet, despite the tragic nature of his very existence, Janak had not lost his mind. Had not given up or rotted away. He’d found a way to reverse engineer what his predecessor had glimpsed in that moment of epiphany, and he was ready and willing to lay down his very soul for even the slightest hope of saving his predecessor’s daughter and restoring the realms to their former glory.

Could Vir have done that? He thought not. No, he was certain he couldn’t.

Thankfully, Janak broke the awkward silence. “I know what I am asking of you,” he said, and his eyes moved to Vir. “It is easier for someone such as myself to contemplate my end, having lived so long, wretched life though it might’ve been. For one so young as you, it is another thing entirely. I do not pretend otherwise.”

“Then don’t do it!” Maiya’s voice cried, echoing through the chamber, and this time, she was shaking. “Why do it if you know it will fail!? I’d rather die together with Vir than lose him now!”

Maiya turned to Vir, the tears running freely down her cheeks. “I’m sorry, Vir. I tried. I tried to support this. I truly wanted to! I know it’s probably the right thing for the realm, but I can’t.” She pressed her free hand to her mouth for a moment, then dropped it. “I can’t lose you.”

That was the final straw, and Maiya truly fell apart then. Had it not been for Vir pulling her into his chest and holding her, she might’ve collapsed into a sobbing heap on the floor.

He said nothing as he held her, feeling her whole body trembling against his chest. He said nothing because there was nothing left to say. They’d laid their feelings bare in their chambers and in the long quiet hours after. He knew her feelings, and if he was honest, he didn’t disagree. If it were only his life on the line, he might very well have abandoned this whole plan to take refuge in some alternate universe as she said.

But Vir couldn’t stand idly by and watch his world die, and Maiya knew that better than anyone. It was why she’d directed her words at Janak and not Vir. They both knew that if there was even the tiniest chance of success, they had to take it.

And so he would.

“The burden you bear is a heavy one, Maiya,” Janak said. “As the one who lives on.”

Vir wasn't sure if she'd heard him, but he certainly had. “What do you mean?” he asked, even as Maiya trembled within his arms.

“Even if the machine functions and we succeed in creating a universe, do not believe for an instant it will be free of their influence. What we are doing is simply turning back the clock, but not to the beginning. You've seen it yourself in your mindscape, yes?”

Maiya frowned, looking up at Vir. “What does he mean?” she asked hoarsely.

“It has to do with the souls I'm to ferry across. I... can't tamper with them. I tried in the mindscape and failed every time. Our enemy's tendrils run deep, and to remove them would be to irrevocably scar the soul forever.”

“What does that mean!?” Maiya asked, panicking.

“It means that, while the realm will be quite safe from Ash Tears and the like, our enemy will still pose a threat. A threat that might well take decades and centuries to manifest, but you must remain vigilant, Maiya. Through you and their existing agents, of which there are many, they will seek to alter Fate through subtler means, yet no less insidious.”

Maiya said nothing, and Vir didn't know if she'd even heard his words.He held her until she stilled, touched at the thought that she'd fight for his life in the face of all that was at risk. It was, he knew, a decision she'd make in a heartbeat were she in his shoes.Just as he knew he'd have fought tooth and nail for her life in that scenario as well.If Janak and Ashani were at all impatient, they said nothing. They simply held each others’ hands in a much more reserved display of affection. They’d likely said their goodbyes in the same machine Maiya had him enter. Vir couldn't begin to imagine what it must be like to finally be reunited with a lover after so impossibly long, only to have them perish.At least he'd enjoyed the past five years together with his beloved.

Vir began to stroke Maiya’s head at some point, like he’d always done when she’d come crying to him about some issue or another back in Brij. She’d progressed so far from the girl she was back then, rising to be the most powerful human in all the realms. Indeed, the de facto ruler of the Human Realm.

Yet now, with her trembling against his chest, she felt exactly like that little girl who needed consoling. It was a side of her only he knew, and it comforted him to no end.

Eventually, Maiya pulled away, pressing her forehead against his chest before stepping away. When she did, there was no trace of that weakness. She’d put herself back together like she always did, and when she looked up at him with her piercing amber eyes, she looked every bit ready to take on the burden of leading the new realm in his absence.

He took her hand one final time, and together, they walked to Janak and Ashani.
