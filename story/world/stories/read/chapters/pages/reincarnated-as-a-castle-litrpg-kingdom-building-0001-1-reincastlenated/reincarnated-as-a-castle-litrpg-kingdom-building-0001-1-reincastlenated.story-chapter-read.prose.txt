It was a quiet Sunday morning.

As a newbie webnovel author, I was diligently doing “research” for my writing job.

Or at least, that was what I told myself.

In reality, my so-called research involved lying flat on my unmade bed like a stranded whale while binge-watching the latest isekai anime from sunrise to sunrise. A half-empty bucket of chicken nuggets sat beside me, and my laptop balanced precariously on my knees as its fan whirred loudly in protest.

Honestly, life felt strangely peaceful.

At least, it did as long as I avoided thinking about the ten-thousand-word deadline hanging over my head.

Then a sharp notification sound cut through the room.

DING!

My phone.

I rolled onto my side with all the grace of a dying walrus and lazily reached for it. I fully expected my editor to be calling, ready to scream about why my useless ass was still horizontal instead of chained to a keyboard.

But… surprisingly, it wasn’t that.

The message glowing on my screen was something entirely different.

[User “Mister X” has rewarded your novel with 1000 Luxury Castles — worth 5,000,000 Coins!]

I froze mid-chew.

(Huh…?)

(Five million… WHAT?!)

…

Yep.

Believe it or not, that was the exact moment I died.

Some lunatic had just thrown thousands of dollars at my trashy catgirl smut novel. I was so overwhelmed with joy that before I could even think about spending a single cent, I choked on a goddamn chicken nugget.

…And died.

Honestly, it was a pretty pathetic way to go.

Thankfully, death wasn’t the end.

After the darkness swallowed everything, I woke up once again and discovered that I had been reincarnated into another world.

But I wasn’t reborn as a human, a mighty hero, or even some overpowered monster.

Instead, I became… a castle.

A damn castle sitting in the middle of a wild forest.

Reincarnated as a Castle — Day 1

Castle Points: 0

For what felt like hours, my mind spiraled in absolute chaos.

Eventually, I managed to calm myself enough to take a few deep mental breaths. Mental only, of course. Since I was now nothing more than a pile of stone and mortar, I didn’t exactly have lungs anymore.

Though honestly, calling me a “castle” felt a little generous.

I looked more like a small manor house or, at best, a miniature keep.

The main structure was a simple rectangular building surrounded by thick stone walls roughly eight meters high. Each corner held a short turret capped with pale-blue slate roofs meant to keep out the rain.

The entire structure covered only around two hundred square meters, split between two floors and a cramped basement.

Add in the small front yard overgrown with weeds, tangled vines, and cracked paving stones, and the whole compound barely reached five hundred square meters.

Around it stood a moss-covered three-meter stone wall, along with a heavy iron gate fitted with a portcullis that could be raised and lowered.

But… that was basically it.

A plain little castle with a painfully simple design.

Honestly, it was kind of pathetic.

Still, there were at least a few interesting things worth mentioning.

First: my vision.

I had two different kinds of vision.

The first was external vision. It felt like controlling an invisible drone hovering high above the courtyard, giving me a bird’s-eye view of the surrounding area. Through it, I could see the dense forest around me, distant hills and mountains, and even a narrow river nearby.

Most of the trees were towering oaks and firs, thick enough to make the entire forest feel dark and ominous. The kind of place where magical beasts or monsters definitely lurked.

Although so far, thankfully, nothing had shown up yet.

The second type was internal vision.

It felt like having CCTV cameras installed throughout the castle. I could see every hallway, staircase, room, and even the gloomy basement all at once.

Naturally, processing that many perspectives at the same time should have been overwhelming. Imagine trying to watch dozens of scenes simultaneously. A normal human brain probably would’ve melted like grilled mozzarella cheese.

Luckily, I didn’t have a brain anymore.

So after struggling with it for a while, I gradually got used to the strange sensation. Eventually, switching between views became almost natural.

As for the courtyard…

It was a complete disaster.

Grass had grown wildly out of control, vines crawled over everything, and half-dead shrubs tangled together into a horrifying mess. Cracked paving stones barely poked through the overgrowth.

Thankfully, I didn’t have OCD.

Otherwise, this place would’ve driven me insane within ten minutes.

The inside wasn’t much better either.

Every room was completely barren. No furniture. No decorations. Nothing.

I mean, fantasy castles were supposed to have paintings, chandeliers, giant stone statues, and all that fancy stuff, right?

At the very least, there should’ve been some broken furniture lying around to make the place feel abandoned.

But nope.

Just dust and empty rooms.

Well… I did have a few decent windows that let sunlight in, along with several small wall torches.

That was pretty much the extent of my luxury.

As for what I could actually do…

Well, the obvious limitation was that I couldn’t move.

But I did have a few small tricks.

First, I could freely open and close every door and window within my walls.

Second, I could light or extinguish any torch or candle inside the castle whenever I wanted. Pretty useful for nighttime, or whenever I wanted the place to feel slightly less haunted.

And… yeah.

That was mostly it.

Oh, right.

There was one more thing.

Like every proper isekai protagonist, I also had… a System.

Whenever I thought, System, open, a glowing blue screen appeared in my vision.
