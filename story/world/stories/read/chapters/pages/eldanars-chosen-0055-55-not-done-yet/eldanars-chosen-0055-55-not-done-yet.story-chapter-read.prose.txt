From his position near the tree line, Brett saw exactly what Josh was doing. His friend had isolated one of the orcs, throwing himself into the fray before both could reach the line. It was reckless but tactically sound. If both orcs hit their position simultaneously, even Josh and Bhel would struggle to hold the ground.

Brett sucked in a sharp breath, forcing down the surge of panic that threatened to cloud his mind. Fireballs were too risky now; one misstep and he would roast his own allies. He switched spells, thrusting his staff forward and channelling mana into the mud beneath the second orc’s heavy boots.

“Ensnare!” he shouted, his voice cracking and hoarse.

The earth split. Thick, gnarled roots burst upward, coiling around the orc’s legs like starving serpents. The creature bellowed, hacking at the wooden binds with one of its axes. It splintered bark and tore free chunks of root, but the magic kept reforming, twisting tighter with every desperate movement.

Brett gritted his teeth as the spell strained his focus. The orc’s sheer brute strength was testing the limits of his control. Sweat rolled down his temples, stinging his eyes. He forced more mana into the spell, ignoring the rhythmic pounding in his skull.

When the orc finally slowed, snarling in frustration, Brett changed his tactics again. He pulled his focus into his palms, channelling smaller, more precise bursts of fire.

“Fine,” he muttered. “Let’s see how you like this.”

He began hurling firebolts. These were not grand explosions but sharp, concentrated blasts of searing flame. Each one slammed into the orc’s torso or arms, leaving smoking burns and blackened flesh in its wake. The monster roared in pain, stumbling against the roots that still clung stubbornly to its legs.

Perberos’ arrows started to streak in beside the firebolts. The pair worked almost in sync; the fire lit the target, and the arrows punched into the scorched flesh a heartbeat later.

Brett felt his mana draining fast. The edges of his vision flickered with a grey haze. But he did not stop. He could not stop while that thing was still moving toward Josh.

“Stay there, you bastard,” he hissed through clenched teeth. He launched another firebolt that burst across the orc’s shoulder and sent it reeling backward.

For the moment, the plan was working. The orc was slowed, bleeding, and half-blind with rage. But Brett could feel the pressure mounting in the air again.

The orc came again. It swung its weapon in a brutal arc that crashed into Josh’s shield and nearly tore the straps from his grip. The force shuddered through his arm; his wrist screamed in protest. Josh gritted his teeth and drove forward again. He hacked low, catching the orc’s thigh. The creature snarled and retaliated with a backhanded swing. The edge of its cleaver scraped across the rim of Josh’s shield and sparked off the metal.

A goblin lunged from the side, stabbing with a rusted spear. Josh barely had time to shift his stance before the creature’s skull exploded; Perberos’ arrow had found its mark. Another goblin tried the same a heartbeat later, only for its head to snap back as its throat was pierced clean through.

Josh did not look back. He trusted Perberos to keep his flanks clear. He pressed on, shield-bashing the orc again and again to force it to give ground. The beast swung wildly with its right arm, raw strength keeping it in the fight even as its movements grew slower and heavier. Every swing came with a guttural roar. Every miss arrived with a growl that shook the very air.

Josh feinted left, then drove his sword up under the orc’s guard, catching it in the ribs. The blade slid between cracked armour plates. The orc bellowed in rage and pain, its left arm falling slack by its side. It stumbled back a step, then two, clutching at the wound with its weakened hand. Josh did not give it the chance to recover. He surged forward, shoulder-first, smashing into its chest and driving the monster down into the dirt.

They hit the ground hard. Josh drove his knees into the beast's chest, the air around them filled with dust and blood. The orc thrashed, trying to bring its cleaver around again, but Josh pinned its wrist with his shield and drove his sword down. Once, twice, three times. The final strike buried itself deep in the creature’s throat. The orc twitched, gargled, and finally went still.

Josh staggered to his feet. His chest was heaving, and his sword felt slick and heavy with blood. All around him, goblins still screamed and died, but the centre of the field had gone momentarily quiet. He looked down at the fallen orc; its chest was a mess of gore, and its eyes were glassy and wide.

“Stay down,” Josh spat, yanking his sword free with a wet squelch.

Another roar echoed from deeper in the mass of goblins. It was louder, closer, and angrier than the ones before. Josh turned, raising his shield again as Bhel’s laughter rolled across the field.

“One down!” the dwarf bellowed. He stomped through the goblin corpses toward Josh. “Let’s see how the next one fares!”

Josh nodded, catching his breath. The fight was not over. It was not even close.

The ground trembled as the third orc shoved its way through the thinning ranks of goblins. It sent smaller bodies flying like leaves in a storm. This one was larger than the others. Its armour was scorched but still intact, and its tusks were wet with blood and foam.

Josh tightened his grip on his shield, glancing once at Bhel. The dwarf’s eyes gleamed beneath his soot-streaked brow, and he gave a single, sharp nod. That was the only signal Josh needed.

He took a deep breath and then sprinted forward.

The orc bellowed and swung one massive axe in a two-handed arc. Josh ducked under it. The wind of the blow raked across his helmet. He slammed his shoulder into the creature’s gut. The impact jarred his entire body, but the momentum carried them both a step back. The orc snarled, spitting flecks of blood, and slammed its foot forward into Josh’s ribs. The air rushed out of him, and pain flared bright in his side, but he did not stop. He drove his sword up, catching the orc across the chest and leaving a deep, angry gash.

The beast swung again, catching the edge of Josh’s shield and splintering part of the rim. Josh stumbled, his boots skidding in the blood-soaked mud, but before the orc could follow up, a flash of steel came from the side.

Josh turned in time to see Bhel had closed the distance. Both of the dwarf's axes were raised. Bhel let out a wordless roar and brought one axe down into the orc’s ribs; the sound was like splitting seasoned wood. The orc tried to retaliate, but Brett’s next firebolt caught it square in the side of the face, turning half its head into a charred ruin.

Bhel tore his weapon free and struck again. The second axe drove deep into its chest. Bhel’s blade bit into the orc’s thigh, carving deep into the muscle.

The creature howled and turned, its attention torn away from Josh. That was the only opening Josh needed. He stepped in, raised his shield, and slammed it into the orc’s face. The strike sent teeth and blood flying, leaving the beast stumbling.

Josh’s blade drove deep into the orc’s gut. The impact jarred his arm and rattled his teeth. The creature let out a guttural roar, its hot breath washing over him as it swung its crude axe downward. Josh barely brought his shield up in time. The blow crashed into it with a bone-shaking thud that numbed his forearm. He gritted his teeth and shoved forward, booting the orc square in the chest to wrench his blade free.

Bhel was already moving. The dwarf barrelled in from the left, his axe biting into the orc’s exposed flank with a sickening crunch. The creature staggered, bellowing in pain as blackened blood poured from the wound. It tried to bring its axe around in a wide arc, but Josh ducked low and slammed his shield into the orc’s chest again, knocking it further off balance.

The beast fell to one knee, snarling and swinging wildly, but it was finished. Blood poured from its multiple wounds. Bhel stepped in, planted his foot against the orc’s thigh, and hacked downward. The axe split through the collarbone and buried itself deep in the monster’s chest. The roar that followed turned into a wet gurgle before the creature toppled backward, shaking the ground as it hit the earth.

The day had devolved into absolute chaos.

From somewhere deeper in the camp came a string of deafening explosions. These were sharp, concussive blasts that sent flocks of birds scattering from the trees. The ground trembled under Carcan’s boots; the sound rolled through the forest like thunder. Flashes of red and orange tore across the tree line, followed by cheers, screams, and the wet crunch of steel meeting flesh.

It was impossible to tell who was winning.

Carcan’s pulse pounded in her ears as she tried to focus. The noise was relentless: the whistling of arrows, the dull thud of impact, and the shrieks of goblins dying in the dark. She could hear her own breath hitching as she turned, scanning the tree line for movement. Every rustle of leaves made her heart jump.

“Where the hell is that coming from?” she muttered, half to herself. She tried to catch sight of another party through the trees. She caught glimpses of silhouettes moving between flashes of flame, but she could not tell if they were allies or enemies.

A sudden boom ripped through the camp again. It was closer this time. Dirt rained down from the canopy. Somewhere to the left, someone screamed, and the sound was cut off in an instant.

Carcan’s stomach twisted.

The chaos was getting to her. The uncertainty was worse than fear; she did not know if the others were winning or dying, if more enemies were coming, or if they were already surrounded.

“Focus,” she hissed, forcing the word out through clenched teeth.

Carcan squinted through the haze. The heat from Brett’s fireballs still rippled in the air. Shapes moved in the smoke: shadows twisting, blades flashing. Then she saw it. Josh was bloodied but unbowed, driving his sword up beneath the orc’s ribs as Bhel’s axe came down across its chest in a brutal, splitting arc. The beast gave one last shuddering roar before collapsing in a heap. Its lifeblood soaked into the churned mud.

For a heartbeat, there was silence in their corner of the battlefield. The last few goblins—those still shrieking and flailing—were quickly cut down. One tried to flee, its thin limbs pumping in panic, only for an arrow to whistle past Josh and bury itself in the back of its skull. It fell mid-step, twitching once before lying still. She did not even need to look to know whose arrow it was. Perberos.

Her chest swelled with a fierce, exhausted pride. They had done it. Against overwhelming odds, her party had held. The fires still crackled, and the ground was littered with corpses. Her mana was nearly spent, but they were alive.

A shaky smile tugged at her lips as relief began to creep in. Then the wind shifted. The distant sound of clashing steel, guttural roars, and panicked screams rolled through the trees. The smile faltered.

The fight was not over. Not yet.

Carcan tightened her grip on her staff, drawing in a slow breath as she steadied herself. “We’re not done,” she whispered, half to her party and half to herself. Her eyes flicked toward the chaos still raging deeper in the camp.

Carcan exhaled slowly, forcing her trembling hands to stay still as she gripped her staff. The fear, the exhaustion, and the chaos all pressed down on her like a mountain, but beneath it, something stronger began to stir. Determination. It was not loud or fiery like Brett’s magic; it was quiet and steady: a heartbeat that refused to falter. They had come too far to stop now.

She glanced at her potion pouch and pulled out her final vial. The glass was slick with grime, and the liquid inside glowed faintly blue. For a moment she hesitated. She knew the risks of taking too many in a short time, but then she nodded to herself, her jaw tightening.

“To the end,” she murmured, and tipped the potion back.

The burn hit instantly. The warmth spread through her throat, then down her chest, pulsing through her limbs like liquid fire. Her fingers tingled. The haze in her mind lifted, and strength flooded back into her. Her staff felt lighter. Her breath was steadier. She straightened, the weight of doubt peeling away with every heartbeat.

When she turned, she caught sight of Brett a few paces away. He was doing the same: downing his own potion and wiping his mouth with the back of his sleeve. His face was flushed from the heat and magic. Their eyes met through the smoke and the flicker of flame.

He gave her a tired grin, and she returned it with one of her own. No words were needed between them. That look said everything: We’re not done. Not yet.

With renewed strength, Carcan adjusted her grip on her staff and faced the burning camp once more, ready to push forward into the storm.
