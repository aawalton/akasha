Chapter 113

Fourth of July

July 3rd.

Downtown Cleveland.

30 days, 10 hours, and 13 minutes until Phase 2 of the Gods Game commences…

Life hits a little different when you wake up in a new penthouse apartment. Not because I’m any different. I’m not. Still the same, simple gym junkie. Hell, the apartment would be sad as hell if it wasn’t for Labonte and Harvest Guild furnishing the whole place. But because the view outside my windows doesn’t include my parents’ backyard, or the neighbor’s trampoline, or my childhood memories staring back at me like an accusation. Why are you here? Because you’re a failure. I don’t feel like so much of a failure now. It’s a nice feeling, though still hard to square away in my head.

My new apartment is smack dab in the middle of downtown. Not too far from Playhouse Square. A building with an all-white exterior and a lobby with polished marble floors, a fountain, and a doorman. A private elevator takes me to my floor, which only has four apartments. When I made my demand to Labonte, I was not expecting… Well, this!

The apartment is massive. The place is already set up like someone staged it for a real estate listing. A fancy sectional couch, together with a Persian rug and all-glass coffee table sit in the center of the living room before a fireplace and large television (Jelly Boy will love that). Modern art—at least, that’s what I think it is, otherwise it looks like a printer error—sporadically fills the walls. The kitchen island has more surface area than all counter and tabletops in my parents’ entire house. Floor-to-ceiling windows line the back wall of the main living space, providing for a lot of natural light to the space.

Of course, even if it’s furnished, I still have a decent chunk of my own junk that needs to be moved in. A storage unit’s worth of it, in fact, left over from when I first crawled back from NYC like a wounded animal. Boxes of clothes, a cheap desk, my old blender and a random assortment of kitchen crap (I think I bought this knife as a Christmas gift for Sarah…). A couple duffel bags worth of miscellaneous gym equipment and accessories. A sad truckful for the movers.

The movers who were supposed to handle it. They couldn’t get out until next week.  So of course, I do what I always do when faced with logistical inconvenience. I brute force it.

Kyle is here, because the universe is also occasionally kind. My jiu jitsu instructor—the Wind Dancer with cauliflower ears and black beard as thick as night—had offered to help me yesterday after training.

“You’re moving?” he’d said, towel around his neck. “I can help.”

“Er—Really? You don’t have to!” I stammered, cheeks flushing. I had mentioned the movers’ delay as small talk, not because I had been fishing for him to offer to help me.

“No one should move-in on their own,” he had said. “And after all, that’s what friends do.”

“Friends?”

He raised an eyebrow. “We’re not friends?”

“Er… No, of course! Friends! I just… Didn’t think you’d want to be friends.” I nervously laughed, wishing to be anywhere else and wondering if the System had a self-destruct function.

Kyle laughed. “You’re too easy to mess with. What time do you need me there?”

And now he’s carrying a boxed-up gaming set-up like it weighs nothing, strolling through the penthouse to place the large box into the second bedroom I plan on using as an office.

“This place is insane,” he says, looking around, voice echoing slightly in the vast, high ceilinged room.

“It’s… yeah,” I admit, setting down a box labeled MISC that I’m pretty sure contains multiple unopened bottles of liquor from my old bar cart from New York. “Harvest Guild hookup.”

Kyle whistles low. “They must really like you.” He walks the box into the office.

“Or he wants to keep me close so I can’t run,” I mutter.

Poking his head back out, he says, “Any chance they need more Martial Classes?”

I chuckle. “I’ll put in a recommendation. They seem to like oddballs like me though… You may be too good at simply kicking ass.”

Kyle laughs.

With our Strength and Stamina, moving is almost comical. We grab furniture like it’s cardboard. We lift boxes two at a time, hoisting them onto our shoulders. We don’t bother with the freight elevator, deciding it’s quick to take the twenty flights of stairs instead. We don’t even break a sweat after the third and final trip.

Kyle drops a box near the bedroom and nods toward the windows. “What a view!”

“Apparently I also have access to the private rooftop,” I say, like I’m casually mentioning I own a bicycle.

Kyle’s eyebrows lift. “No way.”

“Way.”

“That must be sick.”

“Wanna check it out?”

“Hell yeah.”

We find the access door. Using the fob attached to my apartment key, I open the door, which leads to a short flight of stairs. We step up onto the rooftop.

The roof is nice. There’s a shaded area with clean-lined furniture. A full sink and kitchenette setup tucked along one wall. A wide flat-top grill, already attached to a full tank of propane. Tables for eating, tables for lounging, and lighting that makes the space feel like an upscale rooftop bar, as opposed to a private downtown patio. The back edge is covered in an artificial turf, which helps keep that portion of the roof cooler since it’s not as shielded from the sun.

Kyle walks out to that edge and looks down over the city. Then, he points.

“You’ve got the perfect view of the stadium,” he says. “You’ll see the fireworks tomorrow night, easy.”

I follow his gaze and yeah, he’s right. My rooftop presents a straight line of sight into the city’s major league baseball stadium and, being the tallest building nearby, a wide canvas of sky under which the stadium sits. A perfect view, as Kyle had noted.

And that thought sparks something in me. A little reckless flare. A stupid idea.

I turn to Kyle. “Hey… if I have people over tomorrow—like a housewarming thing plus Fourth of July—would you drop by?”

Kyle looks at me, straight faced. “I would, if we were friends.”

I throw a hand over my chest in feigned pain.

Then he smiles. “I’ve got plans earlier, but yeah. I’d join later. Definitely in time for fireworks.”

I nod, feeling momentum build in my chest like I’ve already committed to the dumb thing. That’s when the anxiety rushes in. Did I just commit to having a party?

“It’s decided,” I say, already half doomed.

The words leave my mouth and instantly my stomach drops.

Regret hits so fast it might as well be a Skill activation.

“Wait,” I mutter, staring at the skyline like it might offer me an escape route. “Is that a dumb idea?” Who would even show up to a last-second party?

Kyle chuckles, claps me on the shoulder, and looks over the rooftop again. “You’re a guy who lives in a place like this now,” he says. “That guy tends to draw a crowd.”

I groan.

“Great,” I say. Now, I just have to become a person who hosts.

July 4th.

Joe’s Rooftop, Downtown Cleveland.

29 days, 3 hours, and 48 minutes until Phase 2 of the Gods Game commences…

I wake up on July 4th with the kind of mild panic you get when you remember you told people you’d “host” something and now your apartment is the venue and you have no idea what possessed you to do something so ridiculous. After spending yesterday texting people with very casual invites, and enough people saying yes, I spent the remainder of the afternoon and evening stocking up for the holiday festivities. And then the entire night stressed that almost no one would show. That the party would be lame. That no one wanted to talk to me after wasting their time coming to his dumb rooftop cookout. That people would think he was an asshole trying to show off his new place.

What happened to you, man? I think, rolling out of bed. Jelly Boy is still snoozing on the other pillow, his gentle vibrations reminding me of snoring. I wasn’t always like this. In fact, Sarah and I had hosted people often in our Manhattan apartment. But she always had a way of making sure everything was perfect. Telling me what to wear, what to get for our guests, where to stand, even. I sigh. Just going to need to get over it and put your big pants on, Joe.

By noon, I’m on the rooftop with coolers full of drinks, a mountain of meat, and a pre-heated grill, glistening in the afternoon sun. I transfer the meet to the rooftop kitchenette’s fridge, and crack open a cold one. I take a sip.

“Okay,” I tell myself. “Now, just need the people.”

By one o’ clock, people start showing.

And somehow—somehow—my last-second cookout turns into an actual thing. Just about everyone I had texted makes it.

My parents arrive first. Because if there’s anything my dad loves, it’s grilling or standing around a grill while someone else grills. He takes one look at the rooftop setup, nods in approval. But instead of taking a position in front of the blacktop, he moves to one of the tables, taking a seat like he’s claiming his territory.

“You look tired, pops!” I call over from the coolers, kneeling down and snagging another ice cold beer.

“Long week at work! Grocery stores were crazy yesterday with the holiday!”

My mom steps onto the rooftop after having thoroughly inspecting my apartment. She’s accompanied by the Slime Bros—Tom, Jax, and Other Tom—who immediately attach themselves to my dad. One pulls out an umbrella, popping it open and shielding my old man from the sun. One takes the liberty of cracking open my dad’s beer. The other one is massaging my dad’s calves.

My dad doesn’t even flinch.

I raise an eyebrow. “That’s… odd.”

“Oh hush!” Mom says, slapping me on the chest. “They just want to show their appreciation. And your dad doesn’t trust them with yardwork, so they do what they can.”

“Tom,” my dad says, pointing casually in my direction, “I’m fine. Make sure Joe gets some shade. He’ll be in front of that grill.”

Tom plorps away like a proud jelly butler, the soles of the slime-formed feet sticking to the hot floor of the rooftop. Each step is accompanied by a squelching noise as he pulls his feet away. He’s carrying the open umbrella over his head.

My dad nods in approval. “Good work.”

Tom vibrates in joy like he just earned employee of the month, taking up his position near the grill.

“Jax, can you go help the Missus with the side dishes from the car?” Dad asks.

The slime and Mom head back down. When they return, Liv is with them, having just arrived. The three of them are carrying more sides than should be physically possible in one trip. Potato salad, pasta salad, deviled eggs, fruit tray, chips, dips. They look like they’ve just robbed a deli.

“I didn’t know what you’d need,” Mom says. “So, I brought…”

“Everything,” I say, awed. “You brought everything.”

“I always bring everything,” she says, already setting up a spread with practiced motions, knowing exactly where everything needs to go.

Liv invited a few of her friends, who show up, hauling their own cooler of beverages to throw alongside the coolers I purchased yesterday. Two girls I barely recognize from growing up (I think they went to high school with Liv) and a guy who looks like he’s auditioning for “nice boyfriend #3” on a low budget sitcom. They see the Slime Bros and freeze in the doorway.

Liv casually waves. “Don’t mind them. They’re friendly.”

Other Tom does a little wave back.

The girls squeak.

The guy whispers, “Is that… safe? A monster?”

Liv smiles too sweetly. “Mostly. Just don’t share any opinions on The Bachelorette and you’ll be fine!”

I start grilling some meat, filling the air with scents of standard American fare: all beef franks, seasoned burger patties, and juice cheddar brats.

Jordan arrives next, wearing sunglasses and athletic gear that she somehow makes look like it’s meant to be absolutely stylish streetware. She takes one look at the grill, then at me, then at the rooftop view.

“You live here?” she asks, like she thinks I’m pranking her.

“Harvest Guild,” I say. “New job, new place.’

Jordan makes a low impressed sound. “Okay, moneybags. I may need to stop the complementary sparring sessions after your workout classes.”

We laugh. She grabs a drink and joins Liv and her friends in conversation.

Kyle shows up later, true to his word, with a six-pack in one hand and a large watermelon in the other. He walks out, takes in the crowd, then nods slowly.

“This is a good rooftop,” he says, like he’s blessing the structure.

“Thanks,” I reply. “Same one you saw yesterday.”

Kyle laughs. “Looks better with people.”

Veronica arrives with a bright smile and boxes of cannoli from Little Italy. She’s dressed casual but sharp, like she could go from “fun barbecue” to “stab you with a quip” in half a second. Actually, she looks like she’s almost dressed for a date, I think. But I don’t comment on it. I just smile, greet her, thank her for coming and offer to take the bakery boxes off her hands.

Clyde shows up too, which is honestly surprising. He walks up and we clasp hands in strong handshake. I can feel our auras clash, rippling out from us. The other System Users on the rooftop look our way, sensing the quake of energy shimmering in the air.

“Nice place,” he says.

“Nice aura,” I shoot back.

He snorts. “You too.”

“Any word from Chase?”

“Not yet, but I’m pretty confident I’ll be getting the offer.”

“Does that mean you’re already looking at new places in Columbus?”

“Don’t sound too heartbroken, bro. It’s only two hours away.”

I chuckle. “Fuck you… Guess we won’t be tackling any Gates together anytime soon.”

“I’ll be worrying about your wellbeing without me leading you and watching your back.”

“Did I mention, ‘Fuck you’? Remember who saved who last time!”

“Yeah, yeah… In all seriousness, a change of scenery will be good for me. Columbus isn’t too far away, but Cleveland has a lot of… Tough history.”

I nod. “I get it, man.”

Clyde smiles, but there’s something distant in his expression. “I see Veronica’s here too. I’m gonna go say hi.” He claps me on the shoulder before grabbing a beer from one of the coolers and walking over to Veronica.

Labonte is the next to arrive. Yes, for some reason I thought it would be a good idea to text my new boss and invite him. To my surprise, he texted back, noting that Fourth of July is a dumb as hell holiday, but that he would be in attendance.

He steps out onto the rooftop in his typical full suit, but the fabric is clearly lighter, like he’s trying to respect the concept of “summer” without committing to it. Still, his pinkish skin is covered in the sheen of sweat. His bald head gross progressive darker, turning to a bright red at the top. He’s wearing sunglasses, which on him makes him look like a grumpy mob boss of  Candy Land. I sure hope Lord Licorice doesn’t owe him anything.

Jerome follows the Guildmaster, looking like the human embodiment of “competent” and cool. He’s traded in the full business attire for something more casual. A short sleeve button down tucked into a flowing pair of linen pants. A gold watch on his right wrist, and gold chains, matching the clasps on a sleek pair of loafers. He also has a pair of shades on, the dark lenses a brownish shade. He scans the rooftop and the attendees like he’s assessing security risk and snack quality simultaneously.

Labonte takes one look at the spread my mom set up and his face softens for half a second. “Now this,” he says, “is civilized.”

My mom beams. “Would you like potato salad?”

Labonte shakes his head. “No, but what are those?”

Mom points to cups full of striped red and blue Jell-O, topped with a crushed pretzels tossed in melted butter and sugar and whipped cream. “My Fourth of July Pretzel Jell-O Salad Cups?”

“I’ll take three, thank you,” Labonte says with a smile. “The sweeter, the better.”

Jerome takes a chilled bottle from one of the coolers. He approaches me and we clink drinks in greeting.

“Thank you for having us,” he says.

“Thank you for coming,” I reply. “I thought you two would have better places to be. Perhaps on a yacht on the lake.”

Jerome smiles, turning his head towards Labonte, who’s animatedly chatting away with my Mom. “Mr. Labonte is a more lonely man than you’d think. As for me? I like to take the opportunities to get to know our new recruits on a more human level. It’s good for integration and buy-in.”

“… Right… Well, make yourself at home!”

Jelly Boy, of course, is the life of the party.

At some point he re-equips those ridiculous legs and struts around the rooftop like he’s hosting a runway show between the tables. Liv connects to the Bluetooth speakers installed throughout the rooftop, filling the air with music, which the slime loves. He carries drinks for people by balancing cans in little indentations in his slime body like a wobbling tray. He “helps” with grilling by hovering too close and vibrating enthusiastically every time meat sizzles..

Jordan watches him do a little spin and deadpans, “That slime has better footwork than half my students.”

Kyle, chewing a burger, nods thoughtfully. “He’s got hips.”

My dad, completely unfazed now, points at Jelly Boy. “Jelly. Tell Jax to bring me mustard, please?”

Jelly Boy extends a pseudopod from his body and salutes. Jax slinks off and returns with mustard like he’s presenting a sacred artifact.

Labonte, who’s eating a cannoli while sitting beside my dad, sighs dramatically. “If this isn’t happiness, it sure as hell beats attending my Board meetings!”

Jerome sips a drink and murmurs, “That is a low bar.”

Clyde ends up talking with Liv’s friends, none of which I think are System Users. At one point I hear him say, completely straight-faced, “And that’s when the dragon’s lightning breath tore right through Veronica’s shoulder. So, it was on me to take it down, one-on-one!”

My mom is walking around, trying to snap “candid” photos of everyone. But she keeps telling them how to pose, or to smile more.

As the sun lowers, the rooftop lights flick on and the whole place shifts into night mode. The warm string lights are accompanied by the city glow, and the distant hum of Cleveland like a living thing below us. The fireworks are supposed to start soon.

I’m done grilling but am still in host mode. Still doing that frantic “is everyone having fun?” scan every thirty seconds, when I finally spot Veronica near the edge, leaning on the railing with a drink in hand.

I walk over, a beer in my own hand, and realize with mild surprise that I actually feel… a buzz. Did I drink more than I realized while grilling? I must have. Thanks to the System, my tolerance is through the roof, the alcohol being cleansed like a toxin from my body. No… Perhaps it was just the exhaustion of coming down from hours of being so anxious.

“Hey,” I say, sliding in beside her.

Veronica glances over and smiles. “Hey.”

We make small talk at first. All dumb stuff: the view, the crowd of people moving up and down Euclid Avenue, how Labonte somehow ate nine more of my Mom’s Pretzel Jell-O Pie Cups. Then, Veronica’s smile fades a little as she looks down at her phone.

“Damien was supposed to meet me here,” she says. “But he got caught up in something.”

Her thumb scrolls. Her frown deepens, just slightly, and she closes out of her phone, pocketing the device.  She tries to play it off, but her shoulders slump just a fraction.

“Bummer,” I say quietly.

“Yeah,” she admits. “Kind of.”

She looks back at the rooftop—at Clyde laughing at something Kyle said, at my dad being served like royalty by slimes, at Labonte gesturing grandly while Jerome calmly eats potato salad, at Jelly Boy prancing like he owns the world.

Then her eyes land on me.

And she squints slightly.

“You look…” she pauses, searching for the word, “…happy.”

I blink, caught off guard.

Because she’s right.

And I didn’t even notice until she said it.

In the now-dark sky above our heads, fireworks begin to blossom in bright bursts of color, accompanied by deafening booms. Veronica turns her face upwards, face illuminated by the display. I join her, watching the explosions and trying to remember this feeling of peace and joy.
