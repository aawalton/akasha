We walked slowly toward the place where, according to Duchess, Farhad’s group was setting up camp. We moved carefully, pausing often to let Duchess listen for patrols. After several hundred feet, we reached a good spot to peek into the clearing.

There they were — we count 17 people, which was most of Farhad’s group. They were setting up tents, cooking stew, and looking annoyingly relaxed. One Asian guy I didn’t know was even strumming a guitar next to the fire like this was a summer camping trip.

They were only about an hour and a half’s fast walk from Kamatouu. For us, I mean. For them — unfamiliar with the terrain, slow-moving, and in a big group — it would probably take more like four hours. So if they marched at sunrise, they’d reach Kamatouu around noon tomorrow.

“Let’s head back,” I said. “Alert the others and come up with a plan.”

We hurried to Kamatouu, found the others quickly, and gathered in Juliette’s room.

“So we’ve got until tomorrow noon?” asked Juliette. “To arrange guards, and call the goblins?”

“No,” said Hyun-woo. “We should attack them at their camp. And we should do it tonight.”

We all looked at him.

“We know exactly where their camp is,” he explained. “That gives us the initiative. If we wait, they control the time and place of engagement. If we strike now, we catch them unprepared. That gives us the edge.”

“I like it,” I said after thinking. “Juliette?”

“Yes, that makes sense,” she said. She looked stressed, but I knew she could handle it.

“I’ll go to the Baron and the captain and arrange the guards. Geshem, take Santiago and go to the goblin camp — fast. Samar, take Kim and arrange Dubadilo and… er, Dubadila.”

Juliette was our decisive leader like that.

We headed into the forest right away — time mattered.

I liked the idea that Farhad’s group thought we’d be hiding in a room, trembling, while they attacked. And what was really going to happen, is a nasty surprise for them. Goblins, guards, care-bear armadillos, and the Band of the Stormcrows — all crashing into them while they slept.

But for that to happen, a few things still had to go right. Like the goblins not pulling anything funny.

We reached the goblin camp, and I was stunned. I hadn’t been back since my first visit, and the place looked nothing like before — it was a proper stronghold now.

A palisade wall surrounded everything, with three tall watchtowers and a fortified gate guarded by sentries. Inside, I saw four large longhouses, a main hall that looked bigger than before, storage sheds, and even proper workshops—one was definitely a tannery, another a blacksmith.

A lot of that rapid development was definitely thanks to the tools and supplies me and Vel’nari had arranged.

Rul’vak and Vel’nari spotted us and came over.

“Greetings, humans,” said Rul’vak. “What brings you here?”

“Farhad’s group,” I said. “They’re camped near our town. They’ll probably launch an attack tomorrow. We want to catch them off guard and hit tonight.”

Both Rul’vak and Vel’nari smiled — sharp teeth showing.

“A surprise attack,” said Vel’nari, pleased. “I like it.”

“I’ll get the chief,” said Rul’vak, and ran to the main cabin.

Vel’nari leaned in close and whispered in my ear while scratching my hand with her claw.“After the battle…”

I turned red.

Then came the loud sound of a horn. Chief Rash’gul stood on a tall platform, and every goblin dropped what they were doing to gather.

“Tonight,” he shouted, “we go to war!”

The cheer was loud. Apparently, goblins thought war was awesome.

“The humans known as the Farhad group are camping nearby,” he continued. “They’ve killed our patrols and plan to take our forest.”

Angry shouts followed.

“They sleep peacefully in their tents, not suspecting a thing. But tonight—” he bellowed— “our mighty army shall charge!”

He screamed the final words: “We will SLAUGHTER and KILL THEM ALL!”

Even louder cheering followed.

And I took a moment to think: something here wasn’t exactly going to plan.

I had mostly been worried the goblins would bail on us. I hadn’t considered the opposite—that they’d be too enthusiastic.

Our goal in this attack was to scare the Farhad group. Maybe one or two kills would be unavoidable, but we mostly just wanted them to leave us alone. We definitely weren’t planning a massacre.

I didn’t know what to do. I couldn’t exactly start arguing with the Chief right then, in front of his whole army.

Oh, right—I wasn’t the leader. I’d just let Juliette handle it.

“Was… it supposed to go like this?” Santiago asked, clearly shaken, as goblins started pounding on war drums.

“I don’t know,” I said. “It’s not like I lead goblin war charges every Tuesday.”

Rash’gul arranged his troops,ordering thirty goblin soldiers to join us—about half the camp. A solid force. They moved quickly, and within ten minutes we were already on the march.

And suddenly, I felt… good. Weirdly good. I was marching side by side with a goblin army. Torches flaring, spears glinting, war drums pounding. I felt cool. Powerful. Like I was part of something big.

No need to worry about scorpion-suricata tribes or hippo territory — Any monsters we passed ran the other way. Nothing wanted to mess with us. We were scary as shit!

We arrived at the forest clearing near Elrik’s cabin — the meeting point we’d agreed on with Juliette.

Juliette was already there, along with Gordon the guard captain, and twenty armed guards, just like the Baron had promised.

The guards shifted uncomfortably when they saw the goblins, and we tried to stay as far from them in the clearing as we could. The goblins also seemed uneasy around the soldiers.

A little later, Samar and Kim arrived with both care bears—both wearing the makeshift leather armor I’d tailored for them. Dubadilo even held a sharpened spear, looking absurdly heroic for a lazy armadillo.

I also saw Charlotte, Nayeem, Duchess — even Hyun-woo.

He agreed to come to help with battle strategy, but said clearly he and Samar wouldn’t actively fight.

Not that Samar ever listened to anyone. She’d probably do something reckless anyway.

It was already night. It would take us about two hours to reach the enemy camp. By then, most of them would probably be asleep.

We weren’t tired at all—with all the battle adrenaline, the idea of sleep felt comical.

Juliette came to me.

“Good job arranging a goblin army!” she said, clearly impressed.

“Yeah, about that… there’s something I should mention,” I said.

She raised an eyebrow.

“The goblins… kind of plan to massacre everyone.”

Juliette didn’t look too phased. “You mean, in case we win,” she said.

I got it—the winning part mattered more—but still…

“Yes, in case we win,” I repeated. “we don’t want to kill them all, right? In the heat of it, we might not be able to stop the goblins.”

“The ideal outcome,” Juliette said, “is to kill a few, hurt them enough, and make the rest flee. But… Geshem, we need to hurt them. Badly — so they don’t retaliate.”

“I get that,” I said. “But most of Farhad’s guys aren’t even bad people. They don’t think attacking Kamatouu is a big deal. They’re just… you know, kids. Like us.”

“Kids who plan to attack our home,” she said coldly. “If this is a game for them, they’ll bleed for it.”

She looked away. “Look, if we do win, and the enemy stops fighting, I’ll keep the guards from killing anyone else. And… the goblins too, if I can. Is that enough?”

“Yes,” I said, “Thank you.”

I was surprised by Juliette’s resolve. I hadn’t thought of her as ruthless. She also used the phrase ‘attacking our home.’

I guess from a leader’s point of view, it really did feel that way. She felt responsible for everyone.

I hoped we’d win. And if we did… I hoped it wouldn’t turn into a bloodbath.

We began marching toward Farhad’s camp.

In front were me, Duchess, Samar, Kim, Nayeem, and a small goblin named Vil’vish — apparently their best scout, with a great sense of smell.

Behind us came the goblin army. At a respectful distance behind them - the guards, with Juliette, Charlotte, Santiago, and Hyun-woo.

A few miles from the camp, we slowed down.

We were now in range of their night sentries. We moved carefully, hoping Duchess or Vil’vish would spot them before they spotted us.

Sure enough, just half a mile from camp, Duchess signaled a halt.

“Two sentries,” she whispered. “Stupid Naked-Nula. They’re chatting with each other — easy to hear them.”

Idiots. Probably thought their job was just to watch for monsters, not people.

Still, we had to deal with them.

We fell back to consult Juliette and the goblin chief.

“I’ll sneak up with three more archers,” suggested Rul’vak. “If they’re busy talking and stupid, we can make them full with arrows before they know something is up.”

“Good,” said Rash’gul. “Go.”

I noticed Juliette didn’t object.

“If this fails and they make noise, we all charge,” said Rash’gul.

“Agreed,” said Juliette.

The goblins crept forward. Silent. Professionals. I could barely see them — they could’ve been right next to me, and I wouldn’t notice.

Let alone those talking sentries morons.

Still, I held my breath.

When the arrows struck, the sound wasn’t a scream—just a wet gasp. A sharp inhale cut short, replaced by a bubbling hiss of air through blood.

A few seconds later, a soft thud—the goblins probably slit their throats.

They returned and signaled: all clear.

Two lives gone, just like that. It made me sick, but I pushed the feeling down. I knew there was more to come.

We reached the edge of the clearing. Hidden behind a thicket, we could see everything.

Tents. Bags. Equipment. Smoldering fires. Leftover stew.

Everyone sleeping peacefully in their tents. Not suspecting a thing.

In a few minutes, we’d scream and charge like madmen — wake them into a living nightmare. Panic and confusion would give us the edge.

But before we charged, we had one last trick up our sleeve.

I’d talked with Hyun-woo and Juliette earlier, and suggested fire-arrows to open the attack. Max out the chaos.

I’d prepped thirty arrows in advance. Wrapped the heads in cloth soaked in tar and oil.

I called out to Rash’gul. “I brought fire arrows. Let’s open the assault by burning the tents. Cause chaos before we even charge.”

Rash’gul gave me a terrifying smile. “I like that, human. I like that a lot.”

He called over ten archers — each took three.

I watched Juliette give the final prep talk to the captain. The guards lined up.

I lit a torch, and each goblin lit their arrow.

They raised their bows.

“Ready…” shouted Rash’gul.

“Fire!”

Arrows arced through the night — tips blazing.

They struck canvas. Muffled thuds.

And then — fire. Orange light exploded across the camp. Tents roared to life.

Men inside woke choking, some screaming, blinded by smoke, as fabric collapsed in flaming sheets around them.

Rash’gul turned to Juliette.

“It’s time. Would you like the honor, human leader?”

Juliette nodded.

She took a deep breath, stepped forward, and shouted at the top of her lungs:

“CHARGE!!!”

We were charging then, screaming our throats out. NPC guards and goblins together, running like maniacs.The goblins were first—snarling, yellow eyes gleaming in the firelight. Their small, twisted bodies surged forward with a speed that didn’t match their crooked frames.

A half-naked guy stumbled from a blazing tent, still clutching his scabbard. I recognized him—Emeka, the one who made that amazing stew from Arjun's group. A goblin leapt at him, stabbed deep into his arm, and another split his skull with an axe.

From another tent, a man I didn’t know staggered out in his underclothes, blinking at the dark. He raised his hand to shield his face—confused, slow. A human NPC guard swept in and hacked downward. The sword cut across his collarbone and deep into his ribs. He crumpled sideways with a scream that was cut short by another swing. The NPC attacked coldly, mercilessly.

A third figure—a girl—stumbled through a tent flap, coughing from the smoke, rubbing her eyes, shouting “Help!”She barely got the word out before a spear punched through her chest. The iron tip burst from her back. She made a wet, animal sound and sagged forward, blood spilling down the shaft. The goblin holding it grinned, twisted the weapon free, and stabbed again and again until she collapsed into the bedding.

The noise swelled—shouts, screams, the occasional clang of steel on steel—but that was rare. Most of them never managed to fight back. They spilled from their burning tents in chaos, eyes wide with terror.

I heard Arjun shouting, trying to rally his comrades. “Form a damn line! You’re level 2, dammit!” He waved his sword above his head—but his voice cracked with fear, and his men dissolved before they even formed a line.

Then he turned his head and looked straight at me. I wasn’t even close, but he recognized me—hatred followed instantly. Unbelievable hatred. I’ve never seen anyone look at me like that.

Then I heard Farhad’s voice: “Wear your fucking armor!” he shouted. “To me! Regroup!”For a moment, Farhad managed to gather half a dozen men around him—blades raised, shields trembling. A desperate pocket of resistance.

But when the next rain of arrows hit and goblins swarmed the flank with shrieks like feral dogs, their courage broke.

Screams, smoke, and the stink of burning canvas filled the clearing. Tents toppled. Goblins leapt from shadow to shadow, grinning, red with blood.The human NPC guards pressed forward in a solid wall, blades rising and falling in steady rhythm.

The enemy line—if there ever was one—crumbled to nothing.

The clearing was full of running shapes, stumbling shadows in firelight. One fell to arrows in the back. But most of Farhad’s group had already vanished into the trees, their screams echoing until swallowed by the night.

It was over. In maybe five freaking minutes, it was all over.

When I had planned this, thought about it, imagined it—none of it looked like this. Nothing so fast. So bloody and chaotic. No way I could’ve prepared myself for something like that. The real thing… the real thing was something else entirely.

The goblins gave a loud, guttural cheer. They won. We won.

But we didn’t cheer.

Farhad’s group weren’t soldiers. They were just playing at being soldiers. They never saw real combat, not like the goblins had. They were kids, who not long ago, only saw violence through video games.

So when the battle came… they weren’t even remotely ready for it. I wasn’t ready either, and I was on the winning side.

In the end, seven of them were killed—five in the camp, two scouts earlier. More were injured but managed to escape. I hoped they lived. We’d done enough.

On our side, no one died. A few goblins got minor wounds, but nothing serious.

I looked over the battlefield—and immediately felt sick from the sights and smells.

The bodies started disappearing one by one. Some lingered longer than others.Only one didn’t disappear at all.

I forced myself to go to him. A brown-skinned young man, maybe my age. Still alive, but clearly dying.

His abdomen was cut open, and he was trying to hold it closed with his hands, mumbling, “Mama, mama,” over and over.

It was heartbreaking. I didn’t know what to do.

Rash’gul walked up beside me.

“You are not a soldier,” he said. “This is hard for you.”

“Yes,” I whispered.

He handed me his spear.

“You do it,” he said. “You have the magic that makes you stronger by killing. You can get this… XP.”

“Yes, but…” I mumbled. “I don’t want to. I can’t.”

“It must be done anyway,” the goblin chief said. “This human is suffering. Help him.”

I took the spear. My eyes watered, and I started to hyperventilate. I raised it, forced myself to focus—and then shoved it into the kid’s throat with all my strength.

“I’m sorry,” I whispered, “I’m so sorry…”

Rash’gul put his large hand on my shoulder.

“It gets easier the second time,” he said. “After the tenth, you’ll start to enjoy it.”

I somewhat doubt it.

Then the body disappeared—and I felt the XP rush into me. Like when I killed the Care Bear Armadillo… but this time, it didn’t stop. It just kept coming.

My level two well was filled to the brim. I had leveled up.
