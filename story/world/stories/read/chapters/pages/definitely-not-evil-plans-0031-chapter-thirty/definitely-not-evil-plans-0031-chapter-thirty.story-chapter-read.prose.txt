The ‘place’ Scott knew of was a Vietnamese restaurant called Pho King Good, which should have been my first warning sign.

“It’s a pun,” he explained standing outside and staring up at a faded sign. “Pho King Good, get it?”

“I’m genuinely concerned you didn’t think I got it.”

He looked at me with a smile so wide and crooked that his eyes crinkled at the corners an a single dimple appeared on his left cheek. Then he grabbed my hand, “Come on. I’m starving. My buddy Christopher swears by their spring rolls.”

We went inside. The restaurant was nearly empty, at just before one in the afternoon, that should have been my second sign. A bored looking waitress led us to a table by a window. There we were immediately assaulted by the smell of something burning.

“That’s…probably the kitchen.” Scott said.

“I don’t know if that’s better or worse than it being something else burning, not in the kitchen,” I countered.

The menu was sticky and I wrinkled my nose as I tried to open the two pages futilely. “

The waitress came back over and set two water glasses on the table unceremoniously.

“Can I get you all started with an appetizer?”

Scott looked at me and I shrugged then back to the waitress. “We’ll take the spring rolls please.”

“Got it. I’ll be right back.”

After she walked away I raised my eyebrows. “Where did you bring me? This place is…”

“Not what it used to be,” he finished, “but maybe the food makes up for it?”

I picked up my waterglass which had a lipstick mark. “You know, I’m not so sure about this.”

The waitress was back within just a couple of minutes. She dropped the plate of spring rolls on the table and two small plates. “Are you guys ready to order?”

“Um, a few more minutes please, also this glass is dirty.” I showed her the glass.

She grabbed it and looked at me with narrowed eyes, which I assumed was her making sure it didn’t match my lipcolor.

“Sorry about that ma'am. I’ll get you something else.” She spun on her heel and left without asking me what else I’d like.

The spring rolls as it turned out were a culinary achievement I didn’t know was possible outside of hotpockets. The outside was burnt and the inside was both hot and frozen.

Scott took one bite, made a face and set down his chop sticks.  “Okay,” he said. “I owe you an apology and also some antibiotics.”

I burst out laughing. I couldn’t help it. The whole situation was so absurd, so perfectly terrible after my absolutely awful morning, that something cracked. It took a couple of minutes before I stopped laughing.

“This is the worst restaurant I have ever been to.” I said between laughing. Tears began to run down my cheeks and I wiped them away with the back of my hand.

“Christopher is dead to me.” But Scott was grinning too. “I promised you a nice lunch and I’ve delivered you a couple of health code violations instead. Let me make it up to you.”

“How could you ever top this?”

“Do you trust me?”

His smile was infectious and I hadn’t laughed since Halloween. So, I decided to give in to the absurdity of the universe.

“Okay, Aladdin. But if the next place has rats, I’m feeding you to my tiger.

He left a twenty on the table and we left before they had the opportunity to bless us with any additional amazing customer service.

“Okay,” Scott announced. “This place has never let me down. “This is Retrograde.”

We stood in front of an arcade. This wasn’t a weird kids place, or one of those overhype bar-restaurants. This was an actual arcade.  There were rows of vintage machines, rows of new machines, no shortage of flashing lights and the cheerful chaos of digital explosions.

“I can guarantee it’s rat-free.”

“That is a very low bar you’re setting for yourself.”

“After Pho King Terrible  I feel like I need to.” He held the door open for me. “Come on, let me destroy you at air hockey.”

“Bold words for a man that can’t walk past a coffee table without causing a ruckus.”

“My clumsiness is limited to stationary objects. Moving objects I’m great at. Like a t-rex, or The Predator.”

Inside the arcade was everything that the restaurant wasn’t, clean, bright, everything esteemed to be the appropriate temperature.” The walls were a mix of vintage game posters and neon signs. A group of college age kids clustered around a fighting game, shouting encouragements at each other. The happy cacophony  of electronic music and arcade sound effects  was strangely welcoming.

Scott led me to an air hockey table, he seemed to already know exactly where it was.

“Best two out of three,” he said, dropping tokens into the slot. “Loser buys dinner.”

“Wait, when did you get tokens?”

“I had some from the other day.”

‘Hmm… and you assume I’ll have dinner with you?”

“Oh, I’m confident after you see my arcade skills you’ll be asking me to dinner.”

“You’re on.”

“I hadn’t played air hockey in a very long time. As it turned out there are more things that come back to you no matter how long it's been, you know, other than riding a bike. The puck shot back and forth between us. It clattered against the sides and rapped my knuckles. I winced and for the first time in what felt like forever, I was in the moment, not thinking about work (or lack there of), secrets, or anything really other than just playing the game. Having fun.

Scott was good, which I should have expected considering the tokens in his pocket. He scored the first goal by bouncing it off the wall with some sort of trick shot. His victory dance was so ridiculous that he almost scored again while I was busy laughing.

“One-nothing,” he announced. “Prepare to be skunked.”

“In your dreams.”

I focused as the puck came toward me and with a sharp snap of my wrist I sent it straight back into his goal.

“Lucky shot.”

“Nothing but skill,” I countered.

We traded goals until we were tied six-six. The deciding point came down to a stand-off. Both of us were hunched over the table paddles ready. Scott feinted left but I didn’t fall for it. He shot right and I blocked it. The puck shot back over toward his goal, he dove for it and ended up knocking backwards into his own goal.

I threw up my hands in victory. “Ha! Who’s humiliated now?”

“Okay okay.” He held up his hands in mock surrender. “I bow to the queen. But, I demand a rematch. Different game, though.”

“Alright, name it.”

We played space invaders where Scott was definitely more skilled and beat me by an unmentionable amount of points. We played Pac-Man where I got eaten by so many ghosts that Scott began providing commentary as if it were a nature documentary.

“And here we see the infamous Pac in its natural habitat, he is grazing on a breakfast of pac-pellets. Oh, what have we here? The pac seems to have made a catastrophically poor choice by allowing itself to get cornered without a power pellet in sight.”

Then we found the carnival games.

“Ring toss,” Scott said. “I’m feeling lucky.”

“After you.”

He paid for a round and proceeded to miss all three throws spectacularly. One ring bounced off the bottles and back toward us, one landed on the floor and the other got caught up in the stuffed animals on the wall behind the bottles.

“The physics of the game are broken.” he declared.

“Mmmhmmm.” I raised my eyebrow sceptically.

“Fine you try it then if you don’t believe me.”

I picked up the rings and felt their weight in my hands. Such simple objects, activating my code vision I could see the materials the bottles and the rings were made from. The hard plastic was meant to create high rebound energy causing the rings to deflect, bounce and otherwise not go on the neck of a bottle. I nudged the material to be more rubbery and I hoped to make it absorb energy rather than reflect it.

I tossed the first ring and it landed perfectly on a bottle neck.

“Okay, beginner’s luck.”

Second ring, and another perfect landing.

“Are you a secret ring toss champion? Did you intern in the summers as a carnie?”

I chuckled,  and tossed the third ring low like a frisbee. It settled onto the bottle with a satisfying clink.

“What the hell?” Scott stared at me. “How did you do that?”

“Natural talent,” I said, accepting my prize. It was an over-stuffed narwhal with two tiny eyes that were too far apart and a mouth that was just a line. It was adorable in a dopey kind of way.

“You hustled me. You’re a professional ring-tosser.”

“That is not a real thing.”

“I bet it is.”

I hugged the narwhal to my chest, grinning. I’d cheated but I felt like it was worth it. It was a small harmless thing and the look on Scott’s face was priceless.

We played a few more games and I won a few more prizes by subtly turning the games in my favor. By the time we left. We had a narwhal, a rubber duck and a small stuffed alien that Scott insisted was named, Jay-C.

“Wait, why is the alien Jay-C?”

“Oh, it seemed like a good name. And the narwhal is… Cobalt.”

“Cobalt is better than plain old blue.”

“Jay-C the alien. Cobalt the narwhal. How very sophisticated.”

“This was the best first date I’ve ever been on.” Scott said as we left the arcade. “Food poisoning not withstanding.”

I stopped, “This was a date.”

“Was it not?” Scott said, looking uncertain. “I mean, it doesn’t have to be. It could just be two new friends hanging out. I’m flexible.”

“No, I…” I clutched Cobalt the Narwhal, suddenly feeling a little shy. “I’d like it if it was a date.”

Scott’s smile was like a sunrise and lit up his entire face. “Then it was definitely a date. The best worst date in history.”

“The restaurant was terrible.”

“Pho king terrible, if I remember correctly.  But the company was excellent.” He held out his hand. “Can I walk you to your car, or bus stop or whatever form of transportation you used to get to this section of town.”

I took his hand. His fingers were warm, slightly calloused and felt nice. Normal and perfect.

“I drove,” I said. “But I wouldn’t mind walking around for a little longer first.”

We walked and talked about nothing important. Favorite movies, childhood haircuts, whether hotdogs were sandwiches, and favorite instant noodle.

His: The Fifth Element, the ‘Jonathan Taylor Thomas look from the 90s’, yes, and chicken nissan.

Mine: The Princess Bride, the ‘spiked in the back can I talk to your manager cut’, no, and easy mac.

By the time the sun had set and the streetlight came on I felt lighter and almost normal.

“I’d like to do this again,” Scott said. “Maybe somewhere with actual edible food next time.”

“I’d like that too.”

“Friday?” There’s this book store I know. There’s a cafe next door that has great pastries. We could browse, get coffee and argue about literature.”

“That sounds perfect.”

He squeezed my hand in front of my car before letting go. “It’s a date. A real one this time. No food poisoning. Promise.”

I drove home with Cobalt the Narwhal in the passenger seat and felt something that was dangerously close to hope blooming in my chest.
