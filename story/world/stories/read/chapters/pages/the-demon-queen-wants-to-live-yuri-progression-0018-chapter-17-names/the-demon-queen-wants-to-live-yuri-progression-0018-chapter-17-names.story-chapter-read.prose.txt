It took one day for me to start moving. In the morning, I returned to my spot by the low wall. This patch of earth now bore several holes from my earlier failures. It was my throne, such as it was.

I moved gingerly. My ribs still ached when I breathed too deeply. I had trained through worse, long ago. I would not let any of this stop me. I had only left after setting the egg on a secure shelf, and I had checked on it before leaving. Just to be sure.

I had two of these Lines now, one on each arm. The Requiem was black, like a cut in the skin that someone had filled with ink. The Cradle was white, and the whole of it glowed in the light.

The first thing to do was to test these new marks. I chose the Requiem first. There was a suitable target, a large stone some three paces away from where I stood. I raised my right hand and channeled mana. Black flame crossed the distance and the stone greyed where the fire hit. Cracks spread, and the rock turned into powder. Seconds more, and that powder turned into nothing at all.

And the power carried with it an impression. One that was faint, so faint I almost missed it entirely. The stone had been content to be a stone. Then I had erased it. I glared at my own arm. I would not feel something for a rock.

I tried the attack on another stone, only stopping when the rock turned into powder. I could control the Requiem that far at least.

This new attack was far, far stronger than when it had just been a Spark, and far more useful. I tested the range. It was three meters. By four, the black simply did not go further. I tested the duration and the mana cost and the rate of decay against different materials. It was better in every conceivable way.

There was one problem. It was taking too much mana. Far more than it had before. I only had this world's thin mana left inside me, and it drained like water through cupped hands. The Requiem drank twenty times the amount it had before. My mana core had been absurd in my time, and it might have been absurder still here, but there were still limits. It hadn’t even fully refilled yet -even that was slower now.

Now, it was time to test the Cradle. I turned my left hand toward a dead patch of grass nearby, pushing mana through the Cradle. White fire left my hand, so bright it almost made the sun look dim by comparison. There was nothing for three seconds, then, there was green -a small circle of it, spreading outward from where I had aimed. The dead grass straightened. Color returned to it in a wave, a little pale at the edges and deepening toward the center.

With this, came another impression. Of a thing that had been whole once, and had wanted to be whole once again. It had accepted its eventual death, and wasn’t sure what to do with its new life.

“Tsk,” I clicked my tongue. Was this how it was always going to be, whenever I used these powers?

I stared at the greenery. Then, I used the Cradle again, forming a second circle of green, adjacent to the first. Then I formed a third, then a fourth, and then a fifth, for no reason at all. The range was just as long as the Requiem, but the mana cost was higher. With this world’s mana, the cost was roughly twenty five times what it had been before the phoenix. Damn it.

Still, I could now project both of my marks beyond my hands. Some part of me had known this when the marks had grown. Only a fool didn’t confirm gut feelings.

The mana aside, I now had two new techniques and now I was at something of an impasse. What was I to call these two new abilities? In the old world, it was not even a question. The System named everything, from spells to skills, everything bore a title in brackets that told you what it was. It was one of the few things about the System I might have liked.

There were no brackets now. Now I had abilities, and I would have to name them myself. The first name came easily, so easily I almost did not have to think about it at all.

“Requiem's Torch.”

I said the two words slowly. The name had an appropriate amount of syllables. Requiem was the name of the mark itself, and torch was what the power did. It was an acceptable name. It was also deeply boring.

I would not give the Requiem's new power a boring name. This was not vanity. All great powers deserved names to match them. When I had been Queen, every spell I had possessed had struck fear far and wide. Sometimes, their name alone had been enough to decide battles before they were even fought. [Oblivion’s Decree]. [Balefire]. [The Ending Sky]. [Dirge For The Sun], [The Truth That Lies Beyond Death].

These were names that made Gods flinch. 'Requiem's Torch' made nothing flinch. Also…I just simply did not like it.

I began to consider a new name. “Black Ruin.” I said eventually. I shook my head a moment later. The name was both simple and obvious. Any brute could name their attack “Black Ruin.” In fact, I bet plenty had.

“The Unmaker’s Touch.” That was better, but it was referencing touch, and I did not need to touch any longer.

“How about…Entropy’s Lament.” I said finally. Entropy was a good name for what the black flame did. And the word Lament itself implied a sorrow, a destruction that was more than just-

I sighed. I would need a different name, one that wasn't so…pretentious. And so I considered again. This naming was far harder than I had thought it would be. Perhaps the System had been good for something after all.

“Um, who- who are you talking to?” I froze and turned.

Sara stood behind me, holding what appeared to be a bundle of flowers. She looked confused. How had she snuck up on me at all?

"I am naming my new techniques, girl. It is a matter of much importance." I said. My voice was calm, which was very good.

Inwardly, I wished for the ground to open and swallow me. To get caught naming one's techniques was surely entirely against the point of naming them in the first place.

“Oh! I get it!” Sara sat down beside me without invitation. She placed the wildflowers in her lap and began to pull the petals from one. “Um…what’s a tech-nique?”

It seemed she did not get anything.

"Techniques are…." I paused. How exactly was I to explain this to a human child? "A technique is…something you do with mana. Look at that." I gestured at the row of pebbles along the ground. "Those pebbles were once a great boulder. I did that with my power. With a technique."

Sara looked at the pebbles and then looked at me. “You broke the rock?”

“No fool child, I decayed it. Decayed.”

“It just looks broken to me.” Sara said, head slightly tilted.

"It is not bro-" I paused and caught myself. I took a deep breath. "The only thing you need to know is that I require a name for this power." Why was I bothering to explain this to her?

For simple demonstration, I raised my right hand and let a tiny wisp of black fire lick at the air. "This is the power I wish to name, girl."

Sara pulled another petal. She took one look at the tiny wisp. “What about ‘the black thing’?”

I paused. “Absolutely not.”

She tilted her head. “The zappy thing?”

“My power does not zap.”

That was not enough to deter the girl. She nodded her head quickly. “What about…Ruin?” She said, “Like…you ruined that poor thing!”

I opened my mouth to protest. Ruin was too simple a word. It was only one syllable, and had no weight at all. A proper name was supposed to- Damn it.

"Perhaps that name is ... acceptable," I said finally. The girl beamed at me, the way I had seen her beam at Ash. "You have no reason to look pleased. I said the name was acceptable. That does not mean it's good." The girl wasn't looking at me now, instead she was smiling at her flowers.

Fine then. It seemed the Requiem's power had been dubbed this Ruin. Now I needed to find a counterpart to this power for the Cradle. As Ruin was simple, this new name had to be simple too. I considered this and extended my left hand towards another dead patch of grass. The Cradle shot white fire and green spread.

Sara stopped playing with her flowers and looked at the green spread. "Ooh!"

"This one creates instead of decays," I explained to her. "It is the opposite of the other."

"Bloom." The girl's voice was barely above a whisper. I almost did not hear her at all.

I stared at her. Bloom was a very simple word. A too simple word. It was also only one syllable. Ruin and Bloom. Some kind of lump had lodged itself inside my throat. I had to swallow past it. “These are adequate names,” I said.

Sara smiled again. This time, I did not discourage her.

It took some time to find the Hero. I found her in the clearing I had seen her in before. The Hero sat on a rock at the center of a ring made out of a dozen split stones. She had been busy with her power then. Right now, she was polishing her blade.

“Ruin and Bloom,”

Ash looked up. “What?”

“I have named both of my techniques. Ruin, for the Requiem’s black flame.” I raised my right arm. “Bloom, for the Cradle’s white.” I raised my left. “They are excellent names.” I said, folding my arms just beneath my chest.

Ash stared at me for a long moment. Her lips pressed together and trembled. “Did…did you come all the way out here just to tell me that?”

Why was she saying it like that? “I came to inform you of that, yes. It seemed courteous. Given that we are, regrettably, allies of circumstance.”

“Ruin and Bloom.” Ash repeated. Something in her expression softened. “Were they from…Sara?”

My silence was answer enough. She grinned and turned toward the river. “They’re good names,” she said smirking. I chose not to acknowledge the strain in her voice. Then, something shifted on her face. “Cradle and Requiem, huh? That’s what…that thing told you?”

“Yes.” I said, matter of factly.

Ash held my gaze for a long moment. “Those…Those make sense.”

I did not argue, though I wished to. I did not feel pleased, though I did. I moved closer and my gaze settled on her arms, and I paused. The black mark on her left forearm had changed. It was no longer a Spark. It was now a thin, dark line that ran from the mark toward her elbow. The Cypher on her right arm had changed too. It was still a Spark, but the edges were softer.

“You advanced,” I said. Ash looked down at her arms, as if she had not decided whether to show me. Then she nodded. “When?”

She paused. “After the phoenix. While you were....” She shrugged.

She meant when I had been unconscious. In the three days she had been doing…what it was she had done. Her mark had grown, but I was sure there had been no combat. At least, I had heard no mention of such yet. Still, I supposed there was now a greater question. “And?” I pressed. “Have you named your techniques?”

Ash ran her thumb along the flat side of her blade. The gesture was slow and thoughtful. “Parting.” She said without looking at me.

“Parting?”

“It's the name for the Sever's power…Sever is my mark.” Ash lifted her sword and sliced the air in the direction of one of the stones around her.

The stone split, and its two halves fell silently onto the grass. The Hero had range to her power now.  It seemed all marks grew along the same general direction. At least, so far. “I am calling it Parting,” Ash repeated. “Because that is what it does.”

Sever. So that’s what her mark had been, though naturally I had assumed it had something to do with cutting. That did sound like a Hero’s mark. Parting, however, was a truly simple name, for a truly simple woman. Ruin and Bloom were decidedly better, as was to be expected. “That,” I said carefully, “is a very boring name.”

Ash smiled. “I know.” There was something different to her gaze now when she looked at me. It was a slight thing, a softening of her eyes. Odd.

“And the other one?” I asked, pointing at the purple Spark.

Ash hesitated. She hesitated for so long that I did not think she would answer me at all. Then she met my gaze. “The System called it Veil.”

Veil. The power to conceal then. That made sense. I had seen this power conceal something when Ash had been helping the villagers before. And given what I knew of the Hero's past, it made even more sense. It was a useful power, one that was-

The Hero's shoulders had tightened. She pressed her left hand over her right, as if to block the purple Spark from the world.

I said none of what I was thinking. "That name does not suit you," I said instead. Ash flinched, in a gesture so small I almost missed it. "A veil is for those who have something shameful to conceal. You are not-" I knew what I wanted to say, but I did not know how to say it."You are not that," I finished.

Ash stared at me. For a moment, I wondered if perhaps I had offended her. Then, she laughed, and it was the same laugh she used with the children. I found myself looking away from her. It should have felt like she was laughing at me. My lips twitched upwards, just for a second.

We stayed in the clearing for a time.
