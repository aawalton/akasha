Kana ran.

Her boots hammered against the stone floor as the tunnel spat her into the chamber’s rear entrance. Her lungs burned, her mind racing faster than her legs. Every turn she’d taken since escaping had been a gamble — instinct and guesswork layered over memory — but now the sounds of combat echoed ahead, sharp and unmistakable.

She burst through the archway.

The chamber exploded into motion before her — spells flashing, walls cracking, magic roaring like a storm caught underground. Zephyr stood at the centre of it all, cloak whipping through the air as Kaelin, Lena, and Aria pressed against him from three sides.

And Aric.

Aric stood at the side while Zephyr defended him.

Kana didn’t slow. She sprinted straight toward them, skidding over shattered stone.

“Zephyr, stop!” she shouted, voice tearing through the clash of magic. “You don’t know what he’s doing!”

Everyone froze for half a heartbeat — just long enough for her to slide between falling debris and plant herself behind Aric. Her chest heaved, hair plastered to her face with sweat.

Zephyr’s eyes widened. “Kana—? Where the hell have you been?”

“Trying not to die,” she snapped, glaring at Aric’s back. “And figuring out what he’s been hiding.”

She stepped forward, raising her voice so all of them could hear. “I found the border — the edge of Erundal. Luneah’s near the coast. We’re not inland like they told us. We’re on an island.”

Silence rang out in the hall like a piercing screech.

Kaelin’s head snapped toward her. Lena blinked in confusion. Even Aria’s calm expression flickered with surprise.

Zephyr frowned. “What are you talking about? That’s—”

“It’s real,” Kana insisted. “Outside the barrier, everything moves faster. Animals, birds, even rocks fall like time’s being accelerated. Organic stuff can’t pass through. Something’s wrong with the whole damn place.”

Aric moved before she could finish.

His hand lifted slightly — not even a full gesture — and the rock underneath her flipped, throwing her into the wall. The air left her lungs in a sharp gasp as she fell to the floor.

“That is enough,” he said quietly.

The temperature in the room seemed to drop.

“Do you really think,” Aric continued, turning slowly to face her, “that shouting these truths will change a thing? What makes you think I even had anything to do with it?”

Kana dusted herself off as she rose from the floor, still pained by the sudden hit. “You don’t. You didn’t make the barrier,” she replied calmly. “You’re not strong enough alone. But you’re using it for something — for your plan.”

“You always have been rather observant,” he murmured. “Unfortunately, for you that is, you’ve chosen the wrong moment to speak.”

His hand rose higher.

Kaelin could sense the magic coiling around his fingers aggressively like lightning.

The ceiling shattered.

Stone exploded upward in a thunderous crack as a massive force tore through the chamber’s roof. Dust and debris cascaded down like rain, and a blinding light shone through from above.

Kaelin raised her hand to help shield her eyes from the glaring beams of light which had suddenly appeared in the dim chamber, but soon enough it was all around her. Aric had raised the earth beneath their feet to bring them to the surface.

Cold air roared around them, carrying with it the distant screams of students fleeing across the academy grounds above. Sunlight speared through drifting dust, illuminating floating shards of stone and splintered beams.

Kaelin barely had time to process it before Aric moved.

Time twisted around him in jagged spirals, distorting the air like a mirage. Zephyr stepped forward beside him, shoulders tense, eyes flickering between Lena and Kaelin, conflicted.

And then the battle began.

Aric struck first.

The ground beneath Kaelin cracked apart as a wave of earth surged outward. He set spiked rods on the outside to try to give her no escape. Kaelin jumped back instinctively, slowing the world around her just enough to slip between falling debris.

“Spread out!” Aria commanded, already moving. Although she couldn’t fight, she was a great strategist.

Lena surged forward before anyone else could react.

Shields burst into existence around her, a large wall attached to her arm made from vines, twisted in a tangled mess to prevent penetration. She raised her weapon in her other hand, posture low and grounded, placing herself directly between Aric and the rest of them.

“I’ll hold the line!” she shouted.

Kana darted to the side, weaving through broken columns, vying for a way to keep Zephyr’s attention while the other two focused on Aric. Aria stayed back, with her strategic abilities, scanning the field from the backlines gave her more space to contribute how she could.

Kaelin stepped in behind Lena, time threads gathering around her fingers.

Zephyr moved next.

Vines exploded from the cracked earth at his feet, twisting into whips and jagged spears. Mud surged upward, wrapping around his arms and torso, forming rough armour that hardened with each breath. He slammed his hands downward and the terrain responded — a ramp of stone lifting him to higher ground while walls of earth erupted to block incoming attacks.

He looked like a fortress in motion.

“Zephyr,” Lena called desperately, parrying a vine strike with her shield. “You don’t have to do this!”

His jaw tightened. “I’m trying to stop you from getting hurt!”

“I don’t need you to tell me what I’m allowed to do!”

Aric laughed softly behind him — a sound Kaelin hated more than anything.

Lena threw a mace out in front which she had made from the stone at her feet. Kaelin jumped out from behind her shields, grasping the mace in both hands, taking a large swing at Aric.

He dodged, with what seemed like ease. He was moving faster than she’d seen anyone do so before.

She swung, he countered.

A kick, thrown straight into her side, put her off balance. Pain flared through her ribs, stealing her breath. She rolled to a stop against a fallen pillar, ears ringing.

For a second, the world narrowed to the sound of her own heartbeat.

Get up.

She forced herself to stand, ignoring the ache spreading through her side. Ahead of her, Kana and Zephyr clashed in close quarters with Aria helping out in the back lines when Guy Everette suddenly vaulted into the battlefield from above.

Kaelin blinked in shock.

Guy landed beside Lena in a spray of shattered stone, vines already coiling around his arms like living gauntlets. His nature threads flared bright and vibrant, his gauntlets pushing back against Aric’s constructs with a similar force.

“Guy?” Aria called, clearly startled. “Why are you still here? Everyone else evacuated!”

Guy slammed a fist into the ground, roots erupting upward to block a strike from Aric. “Someone has to save people!” he shouted.

Kana snorted despite the chaos. “There’s literally an evacuation order!”

“And the head chef is still inside the building!” Guy added urgently.

There was a collective pause — a beat of disbelief even mid-battle.

Kaelin groaned. “You stayed… for the chef?”

“We each have our priorities, just stop sitting around, hurry up and help!” Guy shot back, already reinforcing Lena’s shields with layered barriers of root and bark.

Aria dragged a hand down her face. “I cannot believe this.”

But none of them told him to leave.

Because he was strong — incredibly strong — and the moment he joined the frontline, the pressure against Lena eased. Together they formed a solid wall, Lena’s shields overlapping with Guy’s nature constructs. He fought aggressively, shaping his tree-like roots into thick weapons built for bludgeoning, while raising barriers and launching vines beneath Aric’s feet to disrupt his footing.

Lena moved like a storm at his side — shield first, weapon striking in tight, controlled arcs. She never stepped back, never let Aric push through their defence.

Kaelin exhaled slowly, regaining focus.

She accelerated herself again, pushing forward to support them, slipping through openings created by Aria’s precise attacks and Kana’s disruptive strikes against Zephyr’s concentration.

For a moment, they had balance.

Aric snarled softly, frustration flashing across his face as he struggled to break through the frontline. Zephyr shifted constantly, torn between defending Aric by keeping Aria and Kana at bay, and running over to alleviate the pressure put on him by the others.

The battlefield became chaos.

Nature magic tore up the ground. Time magic warped the air. Stone exploded into fragments beneath relentless force.

Kaelin darted in for another strike — and misjudged.

Aric moved faster than she anticipated. An uppercut caught her squarely in the chest, launching her upwards, Aric leapt up and slammed her back down. She crashed into the ground, the impact knocking the breath from her lungs completely.

Stars exploded behind her eyes.

She stayed limp, flat on the ground, hands shaking as she tried to push herself upright. Pain radiated through her ribs and shoulder, every breath sharp and shallow.

The fight continued without her for a moment — Lena and Guy holding the line, Aria pressing from the flank, Kana striking whenever Zephyr faltered.

Kaelin forced herself to look up.

They were holding… but barely.

Aric was adapting faster than any of them. Zephyr was growing more desperate, pushing harder, his illusions flickering unpredictably as he tried to distract Kana and Aria at the same time, despite how little talent he held in mind threads.

She couldn’t stay down.

Not now.

Kaelin drew in a slow breath, ignoring the pain, and pulled time around herself once more — carefully, deliberately — giving her muscles the extra strength she needed to stand.

But before she could, with the short opening which her absence gave Aric, he slammed a hand forward.

Quickly, Lena’s living walls seemed to die, the life leaving them in pulses. That’s when Kaelin noticed the thread poking out from the shield itself. Aric could use essence.

Lena’s shields fell, the energy itself having been drawn out from it.

“Lena!” Kaelin shouted.

Before anyone could react, Aric was already inside the gap. He moved with terrifying precision, slipping past Guy’s roots and Aria’s counterstrike as if he’d predicted every motion seconds in advance.

His hand closed around Lena’s wrists, clasping her hands together as if praying to some higher power.

She tried to pull back, raising another shield instinctively — but it flickered weakly, lacking energy from seconds before.

Aric yanked her forward.

“Sorry,” he said almost casually, glancing over his shoulder toward Zephyr. His voice seemed half sorry, and half regretful.

“He says we can’t beat all five of them like this.”

The words tilted strangely, as if someone else spoke through him for a fraction of a second.

Then his tone hardened.

“Blame yourself for being so weak.”

Kaelin’s world slowed to a crawl.

She lunged forward, moving at maximum speed, all her energy put into this moment — but she was too far away.

Aric drove his right hand forward.

Lena gasped sharply, her body jolting as the blow landed.

Everything went silent.

Kaelin froze mid-step, her mind refusing to process what she was seeing.

Lena’s weapon slipped from her grasp, clattering uselessly against the stone. Her weakened shields dissolved into fading sparks around her as her strength faltered.

Zephyr’s face went pale. “Aric…?” he whispered, voice cracking.

Lena’s eyes found Kaelin’s.

They were wide — not angry, but afraid. Desperate.

Her lips trembled as she forced words out between shallow breaths.

“Do… your thing…” she rasped. “The one you said would help us… please… Is this… not bad enough…? I’m—”

Aric’s left hand shot up, gripping her head sharply, cutting her off.

Kaelin stumbled forward, panic clawing at her throat. “Stop—!”

Aric didn’t even look at her.

He pulled his right hand out in one swift motion.

Lena’s body went slack instantly.

For a split second, time felt like it had frozen completely — the battle, the noise, the chaos — all swallowed by a heavy, suffocating stillness.

Then Aric tossed her aside like she weighed nothing.

She hit the ground several metres away, unmoving.

The sound echoed throughout the courtyard amidst the silence.
