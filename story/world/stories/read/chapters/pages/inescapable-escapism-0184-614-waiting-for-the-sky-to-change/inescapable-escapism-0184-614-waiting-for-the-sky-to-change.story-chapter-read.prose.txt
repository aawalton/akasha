Panic pounded in my chest as I fell, unable to see anything. I could feel my body flailing wildly. I thrashed as I searched desperately for something, anything, to grab onto, but my hands closed around air alone. There was nothing to catch me, nothing to stop my descent. I was free-falling, plummeting endlessly downwards towards something that terrified me even more than the fall.

What was it that I was speeding towards? Another world awaited me there; that much was clear, but I knew nothing about it. I couldn’t even work out whether the fear that filled my body, turning my blood to ice, came from the sudden departure from the Academy or if it was coming from wherever I was heading, and that thought made my heart race even faster still.

I couldn’t escape, though. I couldn’t break free from whatever world had sunk its claws into me and was reeling me in. I tried once more, throwing my arms out in the hopes of finding something, but the void I fell through was seemingly empty. If there was something there, it was avoiding me, letting me be dragged away.

My panic and desperation grew stronger. A helpless sob slipped through my lips, but it was immediately whipped away by the wind that assaulted my face and stole my tears away as they flowed freely down my cheeks. I opened my mouth, unsure whether I intended to shout or cry, but neither came out.

Instead, I heard a question cut through the rushing whoosh of air that obstructed my hearing.

“What?”

That was my voice. The word had tumbled from my lips, but I had not meant to let it. I did not know it was going to escape, and I had not meant to speak at all. From the moment I saw the boy, just seconds before, I’d planned on remaining quiet. I wanted to stay silent and slip away before he had the chance to notice my presence, but now it was too late.

“I said, you shouldn’t be here,” he repeated, his voice seeming to fill the large courtyard we were in.

He didn’t even look at me, though. He had remained seated on the low stone bench in the centre of the square, and he didn’t even bother to turn towards me, I realised. Instead, he just stayed where he was with his head tilted back and his attention fixed on the darkening sky above us.

Is he able to look away? Or simply unwilling? I’m not sure, and I can’t work out what he’s even looking at. The sky is cloudy. Dark shadows are blocking the setting sun from view, and there are no stars scattered across the vast emptiness above us. He is staring at nothing.

My body jolted as a sudden realisation slammed into me. I was no longer falling, and the world around me was in focus. I could see clearly. My dizziness was gone, and the only thing that remained was the unsettling awareness that I was in another world, one that I had never been to before and did not know.

Fear tightened around my throat, beginning to choke me. Instinctively, I reached out for the dizzinesses that normally clouded the back of my mind, searching for an escape from this world, but they danced away from me. I tried again, lunging towards my reality, the Academy, Mitch’s world. I would have gone anywhere just to get out of the world I was in, but they continued to evade me. The harder I tried, the further they fled from me.

I was stuck. Trapped.

My heartbeat pulsed unsteadily in my ears, and I tried to swallow, but my throat had turned so dry that I choked. I looked around, searching for a bottle of water or something to drink, but I found nothing. I didn’t bring a bottle with me. I didn’t even have a bag that one could be stashed in, and there was nothing around us. The courtyard was empty. With the exception of the boy and the bench he was seated on, the only things I could see were the walls that surrounded us and the towering buildings on either side.

I forced myself to swallow again, hoping that the meagre amount of saliva that I’d managed to conjure up would help my spasming chest. It did a little, but the urge to cough remained. I could feel it in my chest, threatening to escape at any moment.

When was the last time I had a drink? The question flitted into my mind before drifting away once more. I was parched, dehydrated, and desperate for something to drink, but I could not recall the last time I’d had any. I couldn’t even remember the last time that I’d seen water or anything that wouldn’t have poisoned me, but it felt like it had been a while.

With a shaky exhale, I looked around again, trying to take in my surroundings, but it felt like my brain was rejecting what I was seeing. It refused to take it in, refused to process it, and I truly had no idea where I was. I tried searching my memories again, but that didn’t clarify anything. Even the version of me that had existed in the world before I arrived didn’t know where I was or how I’d gotten there. The last thing I remembered was…

Fear. Fear, panic, and desperation. I’d been crying, sobbing uncontrollably, but my tears had dried on my cheeks, and I couldn’t recall what had caused it. I had no idea why I was so petrified, but I knew that was what had brought me to the world. For some reason, those emotions had called out to me and ripped me away from the Academy, but why?

“Where am I?” I asked, finally gaining control of my voice again. “How did I get here?”

The questions had come out far more high-pitched than I’d intended them to. My fear was audible, but the boy seemed unmoved. He still did not look at me.

“I don’t know,” he sighed.

He seemed distracted, annoyed by my presence. It felt like I was interrupting him from some very important task, but I didn’t know what. It didn’t appear as though he was doing anything, from what I could see. I didn’t want to move closer to check, though. That felt too dangerous.

I didn’t know how I’d gotten into that place. My memories were inaccessible to me, too hazy to make sense of, but I had not been there long. I could feel that. That meant I must have still been near the entrance. The courtyard wasn’t particularly large, but I was scared that if I walked any further inside, I might get lost. I may never find my way out again.

“What do you mean?” I asked. “Surely you know where you are. How could you not?”

“A good question, one would think,” the boy said. “I only know this to be the Observatory. That’s what… someone used to call it.”

My fear was starting to be overshadowed by irritation. What did he mean by that, and why was he being so intentionally vague?

“Who used to call it that?”

A sigh reached across the courtyard towards me, but still, the boy did not look away from the sky.

“Why must you ask so many questions?” he said, but it sounded more like he was talking to himself than to me. He seemed to be pounding the question, musing it over rather than demanding an answer. “I don’t know. They’re gone now. Left… a while ago.”

I stared at the back of his head, feeling my eyebrows curl together. My shoulders felt tight, and I knew my hands were curled into fists, but I could not do anything about that. I was too tense, too annoyed by the boy.

It seemed as though he’d forgotten how to have a normal conversation with a person. Perhaps it had been too long since he’d had any practice, and that was starting to show. Nothing he’d said since I’d arrived in the world made any sense to me, and he had to be doing it on purpose. There was no way he truly did not know the answers to the many questions I’d asked him. Surely, he was just trying to be unhelpful, but why?

I took a deep breath, trying to steady myself and calm down, but my thoughts continued to race as I watched the boy. What was the Observatory? I turned the question over in my head, trying to figure it out. I’d heard the term before, obviously. I knew an observatory was a place where scientists, astronomers, studied space and the stars, but the way the boy had said it made it sound more important than that. It seemed more like a title than just… a descriptor.

He didn’t look like a scientist, though. I couldn’t really see him from where I was standing. I’d entered the courtyard directly behind him, so I couldn’t see his face at all, but he didn’t have any equipment around him. There was no telescope standing before him, nothing to help him see the stars. He was just looking up at the sky, and it was cloudy. Even if he had unnaturally good eyesight or something, there was no way he would have been able to see anything.

Shooting him one last suspicious glance, I looked around again. Sandstone walls surrounded us on two sides of the courtyard with giant buildings at either end. They had seen better days; the walls were starting to crumble, but they were still too tall for me to see anything over other than the late afternoon sky. Either there were no other buildings around us, or we were up high. That would make sense if the place truly was an observatory.

Hesitantly, I allowed my eyes to fall upon the building to my right before peering at the building on my left. Both were practically identical and in a similar state of deterioration. It seemed as though they’d once been grand, an extravagant spectacle to behold, but now they appeared a few decades away from ruin. The decay was already beginning to show.

They were still impressive, though. Both buildings reached up high into the clouds around us, and they were adorned with stained glass. The windows seemed intricate, but it was too dark to make out any detail, and very little light shone through them to assist me. I could only see dull glints of colour.

It looked more like a church than an observatory, I realised as I stared. There was something strangely sacred about the buildings. Even just the sight of them stirred something deep within my heart, something that longed and demanded to be revered.

Tearing my eyes away from the stained glass with great difficulty, I looked back at the boy. I was both relieved and annoyed to notice that he had not moved. He was still sitting on the bench, where he’d been since I’d arrived, and that meant that I had to do something. I could not remain where I was, hiding in the shadow of the wall forever.

I needed to move closer, to take a step towards the boy. It would be easier to talk to him if I was nearer. We wouldn’t have to call out to each other across the courtyard, and then I might have been able to get a proper sentence out of him, rather than half-answers and riddles. I needed more than that. I had to have actual information, something I could use to figure out where I was and how to get home.

My knees trembled as I forced myself to take a step forward before freezing once more. I couldn’t help but glance over my shoulder, my eyes scanning the wall behind me in an attempt to find some kind of seam or hidden door that I might have crept through to enter, but I saw nothing.

Glancing up, I peered at the top of the wall. Could I have climbed over it? I had no recollection of that, and I wasn’t sure whether the version of me who resided in that world would have even been capable of that. The wall was too tall, and some stones jutted out, creating natural places for my hands and feet to go, but there weren’t enough.

If I had climbed the wall, I realised, my clothes would be covered in dust. I didn’t even need to touch the sandstone to know that brushing against it would have left a trail on any fabric, and I looked down at myself. My clothes were dirty and faded from wear, but the light-coloured dust would have stood out, and I saw none.

That meant I could not have climbed over the wall. I must have found another way into the Observatory, and if I did it once, I would be able to do it again, I told myself. Therefore, it did not matter if I moved away from the wall. That would not destroy my only chance of getting away from that place.

Taking another deep breath, I walked forward, turning the questions that I wanted to ask the boy over and over in my head. There were two. Two questions that I needed to know the answer to, and both may have prompted him to speak more, to tell me something of use, but I wasn’t sure which to ask first. They both seemed equally important, but one pulled at my curiosity more.

“What are you doing?”

My voice sounded a little stronger than it was before. There was no tremble to my words; my fear was slightly less audible, but the boy did not seem to notice. He didn’t even reply, and I wasn’t sure whether he’d even heard me. He must have, though. He’d heard my questions before and responded to them, and I had been much further away than I was when I’d spoken again.

I was closer now. More of his face had come into view, and I couldn’t stop myself from staring at him. To my surprise, he did not seem much older than I was. There was a boyish roundness to his cheeks that hadn’t had enough time to fully melt away yet, but there was an air about him. Something seemed to hint that he was much older than he appeared, that he’d already been forced to suffer more lifetimes than anything or anyone should ever have to endure.

And there were flecks of silver in his hair, I noticed. I had assumed he was just blonde at first, but as I drew closer, I realised I was wrong. It was strange to see someone who looked so young with grey hairs, especially when they didn’t appear to be dyed. He had so many, though. They almost outnumbered the rest of his hair, which glowed a faint gold, making me wonder how old he truly was.

“Waiting for the sky to change,” the boy said, finally answering my question.

His words made me feel as though the floor had dropped out from beneath me. I was taken aback and unsteady, but I didn’t know why. I didn’t understand what he meant. It made no sense to me.

Did he mean it literally? Was he waiting for the clouds to part for some reason? That would be the sky changing, wouldn’t it? But that didn’t clarify anything to me. It seemed so strange, and I couldn’t think of a single reason why that would matter. Why? Why would anyone be waiting to see the sky again?

Unless that wasn’t what he meant, a small voice at the back of my head whispered, causing my stomach to sink slowly. His answer felt significant. It felt important. I just didn’t understand why, but I needed to. I had to know why he was staring upwards with such rapt attention.

“What do you—” I started to ask, but that was as far as I got before the words died on my lips, and my voice failed me.

I had finally drawn in line with the boy. I hadn’t even noticed that I’d walked closer to him when he spoke. My body had done so without my consent, and suddenly, the boy had blinked. He’d looked away from the skies, his gaze finding mine, and the oxygen had been torn from my lungs as I stared back into his eyes.

My body moved closer again. I could feel myself stepping towards him and sinking down slowly. The cold from the stone bench radiated through my clothes, seeming to reach all the way into my bones, but I didn’t care. There was nothing I could do about it, anyway. I was compelled to remain there, powerless to ever fight back, and I was fine with that.

It was his eyes. They had commanded the attention of my thoughts, bringing them to a halt, and they were holding me captive, but I could not look away. All I could do was stare into the inky darkness, leaning closer as I attempted to see more.

There was light in there. Tiny flickering specks of gold seemed to flicker within the depths of his eyes, and I studied them. My own eyes seemed to trace a line between the glowing dots, and a strangely muted surprise tugged at me as I realised that I recognised the shapes I was seeing.

It was the constellations. How the boy’s eyes held every single constellation within them, I did not know, but I was certain that they were what I was seeing, and I needed to know more. I had to understand.

“How long have you been here?” I asked, my voice breathy from emotion.

That emotion was no longer fear, though. That had left me, drained out without me even realising, and something else had taken its place. I did not recognise it, though. Or perhaps I just did not want to.

The boy laughed, a hollow, humourless sound.

“An eternity,” he told me. “An eternity spent waiting for the sky to change, and my fate to be rewritten. A never-ending lifetime of nothingness.”

Sorrow burrowed into my heart, making my eyes sting with tears. I did not know the boy or understand what he was talking about, but I could feel my heart breaking for him, and I yearned to reach out and embrace him, to provide him some level of comfort. I couldn’t, though. My body was frozen and would not move.

“Why?”

The boy let out a breath, his body sagging slightly, but he didn’t respond immediately. He seemed to be thinking about something as his gaze flitted back and forth between my eyes, debating whether or not to say something.

I longed to know what he was grappling with. The urge to speak, to ask him what he did not want to say, bubbled up inside me and fought to escape, but I could not let myself. It seemed as though he was just starting to open up, and I was scared of pushing him too far and ruining everything. He could shut down again, go back to telling me nothing, and I didn’t want that. I couldn’t let that happen.

“Because I am here.”

Frustration pulled at me again. I felt disappointment surge within my heart at the short, unhelpful response, but at least the boy hadn’t looked away yet. He continued to hold my gaze, and that was all that mattered. As long as I could continue to marvel at the stars in his eyes, I did not mind.

“But, why?” I heard myself ask. “Why does that matter?”

He seemed just as disappointed as I was. I could see it written across his face, and immediately, fear flared in my heart. I did not want to let the boy that I had only just met down. I needed to do better.

“Because it does,” he said sadly.

My eyebrows drew together, and I tried desperately to come up with something to say. There had to be something that I could ask him, something that I was missing. I just needed to figure out what.

Something began to pull at me. I could feel a tugging at the back of my mind, but I pushed it away. Whatever it was did not matter anymore. There were other, more important things that I needed to attend to.

“Why, though? Is it something about this place?” I asked, grasping at straws in an attempt to understand what the boy was saying. “About the Observatory?”

It had seemed important. He had put too much emphasis on the name before for it to not be important, I tried to tell myself in an attempt to justify my question. The place had to matter. It needed to be to blame for whatever was plaguing him, but I didn’t know why or what that even was.

Something flitted across the boy’s face too quickly for me to take it, and he cocked his head to the side.

“In a way,” he responded, his words slow and hesitant.

I was getting closer, but I was still missing something. Maybe he was trapped there for some reason?

“Can you leave? Is that what it is? Is someone stopping you?”

I hadn’t noticed anyone before. The buildings had seemed fairly empty, but I hadn’t actually been inside any of them, so maybe there was someone lurking in there and watching over the boy. They could be watching us both.

Goosebumps crept along my arms, and the hairs on the back of my neck stood on end. I longed to look around, to check whether I could see anyone, but I could not look away from his eyes.

“Not someone.”

My breath caught in my throat.

“Something then?” I asked. “There’s something stopping you from leaving?”

“Yes,” the boy said, but it was more of a whisper, a confession that had been pried from his lips.

“What is it?”

“I cannot leave,” is all he says.

“But why not?” I pressed.

“There needs to be someone here. Someone must keep watch.”
