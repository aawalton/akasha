Eola’s elbow and wrists jarred painfully from the impact. She took an awkward half-step back, pushed by the hulking monster’s weight and panicked by its sudden appearance. She barely deflected a second sweeping blow before her feet finally found solid purchase on the wooden floor.

“Eola, what is that!?” Colin yelled from below.

But she couldn’t respond. The book monster had found her again.

It had to be the same monster. It had to be. But it had changed. Its body was…not bigger, but more defined. Where it had been a swirling whirlwind of shredded pages, its form was now clearer. Almost man-like from the waist up. Long, sharp claws. The same book for a head, but the purple glow at its core was all but hidden by layers of paper. Still almost, but not quite, a feral book. Too solid—and too strong.

“Hold on! We’re on our way!” Patrice said.

Eola’s smallsword popped into High Guard, and her off-hand wrapped around her wand. She parried and sketched, and a moment later, a Safe Shield manifested mid-air, blocking the book monster’s progress for a moment. That gave her time to think.

“I’ll come to you!” she yelled back.

Atta was already moving. She leaped from the wooden walkway, body flailing mid-air as she got her feet under her. A burst of magic erupted from her; she slowed as she hit the ground, feet churning almost comically and fur out as far as Eola had ever seen it. Under other circumstances, it would have been funny. Under the current one, though…not funny at all.

The book monster’s claws slammed through the Safe Shield as Eola started ‘purifying’ Mana. The library-dungeon’s Mana wasn’t right—she could feel that in her soul—but it was functional. Her soul slowly started to refill as her enemy pressed her back, back toward the edge of the platform.

One of its clawed hands stopped hammering on her smallsword. Eola grinned—an opening? But before she could take advantage of it, one long, clawed finger started to sketch mid-air.

The book monster was a mage?

A shard of freezing ice as long as Eola’s arm and needle-thin hissed toward her, cutting through her cloak and tearing at her arm. Pain rippled out from her bicep; it was only grazed, but it still hurt. Still, it was an opening. She dropped into a stop-thrust, smallsword punching toward the monster’s purple Mana mass. A clawed hand caught the blade and knocked it aside—a few scraps of yellowed parchment floated away, but not enough to be real damage.

Down below, Colin was mid-ritual, muttering mnemonics and sketching as fast as he could—which wasn’t very fast at all. Atta hissed and spat next to him, completely ignoring the weasel wrapped around his shoulders. Eola took another two shuffling steps back. Space. Space and distance—she needed to buy time for herself and her friends. Her wand moved, sketching another rune. This one was slow. Painfully slow.

The paper monster didn’t give her time to cast it. It bunched up, cat-like, and launched itself at her. She brought her sword up, and it punched through layers of paper but barely slowed the thing’s movement. Claws scrabbled across her breastplate as it knocked her to the wooden floor, weight landing on top of her.

The Lighting Wing spell-chain faded, half-formed. “Y’aer save me!” Eola screamed. She got a booted foot between her and the monster and kicked; the thing was shockingly light, and the effort was enough to send it flying. But she’d barely regained her feet before it recovered and whirled back toward her, one clawed hand drawing another rune. This time, Eola recognized it: Ice Touch at its core, four modifying marks arrayed around it.

Frost Lance, a First Order spell.

Eola couldn’t stand up to the paper monster. She needed more time. More distance.

Below her, Atta yowled.

Yes, down. Down was the best way. She steeled herself and half-leaped, half-stumbled off the room’s second floor. If she was lucky, she’d hit one of the armchairs in the study circles.

She wasn’t. Eola’s body didn’t break on the tile like it had when she’d fallen from the third floor, but it hurt. Her still-blooming bruises sent fresh waves of pain across her side in protest, and air rushed from her lungs. She coughed, and her vision narrowed, but one look behind her at the approaching horror was enough to force her to her feet. The book monster was in the air. It descended rapidly, closing the gap until—

“Duck!” Colin shouted.

Eola threw herself to the ground, body protesting, then rolled to face the monster as it hit the table behind her. Her wand was already moving to sketch a Bright Ball—the Hasty, Echoing kind—when a gout of flame passed over her and crashed into the paper construct’s body. The Bright Ball went off an instant later. Light poured across Eola’s vision, then darkness, faster than she could even blink. The monster didn’t make a sound as fire and light bombarded it. Eola got some distance, and Patrice rushed to her and pulled her to her feet.

But when Colin’s ritual ended, the monster shed a layer of ash and half-burnt paper and refocused. Its leather face turned from side to side, trying to find a target. “It’s dazed,” Eola muttered, voice low.

“We need to leave,” Colin said. “Find a professor or librarian, or head for the exit.”

“No! We won’t make it that far,” Eola said. “We need—“

“Do you like my familiar, girlie?” a new, but familiar voice asked. Eola’s spine shivered, and her knuckles whitened on her sword’s grip as Eddarn walked up a flight of stairs and stepped into the floating room. “You’ve met it before, but I’ve been finding study materials of my own to improve it.”

The Scholar’s Cult was here.

~Eddarn~

Victory was Eddarn’s.

He’d been planning this for a long time. The day he’d met ‘Tabby’ on the third floor. The day he’d noticed that Y’aer-cursed blue tiger had bonded with her. Now, he’d done it. The plan had gone off without a hitch, right from the fools fighting the professors and librarians all the way to his ambush.

It was impossible to follow someone through the third floor on foot. Couldn’t be done. But the Scholar had people on the inside. They’d known exactly where the books ‘Tabby’ was supposed to find were. From there, it was easy. Between books number three and four. A perfect ambush room. Lots of places to hide, not many places to enter or exit. He’d set himself up. He’d gotten his familiar in position. Then he’d waited.

Everything had happened just like Eddarn hoped. The others’ attack had drawn off the professor and librarian. ‘Tabby’ and her friends had let their need to pass the Library Exam overtake their common sense. The Y’aer-cursed paper monster had done its job—pulled them away from the stairwell so he could step between them and their escape route. Now, he had the blue tiger and the girl right where he wanted them, and even better, its collar was keeping it suppressed.

From here, it’d be easy. Take the two of them in, induct ‘Tabby’ into the cult, and get that blue monstrosity under their control.

He’d have to get rid of the other students somehow. The Scholar’s Cult didn’t have that many openings.

That was fine, though. He’d brought his hunting dog along for a reason.

Eola stood, frozen, for a moment.

Roth had no such hesitation. He went for his flute—the silvered one—and started to play a spell. The melody formed, a distracting, twisting one that filled Eola’s ears and took all of her focus to follow. She found herself drifting away in it until something pricked the back of her neck. When she looked, a pair of narrowed yellow eyes greeted her.

“Thanks, Atta,” she said as the song’s grip on her loosened. Roth’s fascination countermelody was rough. She rushed to Patrice and slapped her across the face with her gloved hand, as hard as she could. The tall girl’s eyes refocused, and she shook herself.

“Find somewhere safe!” Colin said.

But there was nowhere safe. Eddarn stood between them and the only current stairwell, and the paper construct had cut off their retreat upward into the room’s higher sections. Both were, for the moment, fascinated, but though they’d experimented with breaking Roth’s spell and found that sharp, quick pain was the best way, any contact could interrupt the effect.

Worse, Roth couldn’t maintain this song for long, especially with his current Mana.

She only saw one option: the study rooms. If they could hide, they could buy time to come up with a plan.

“There!” she broke and ran for the first door, then shook her head and opened the second. A handful of wooden chairs and a single round table greeted her, along with a tapestry that should have covered a wall but instead, flapped slightly in the third floor’s open air. It depicted Y’aer granting magic to the world on one half, and monstrosities erupting from their stomach on the other. Eola shivered, but there wasn’t time for a less disturbing room.

She held the door open, and the others followed her in. Roth looked tired. Faint blue shone through the veins on the backs of his hands as he played his spell; she shut the door, and he let it go, breathing heavily.

Then, before the fascination could fade, he started up another song—the same one he’d used to sneak past the Srilla. He sat next to the door, playing even as he slumped over and started to ‘purify’ his Mana. The distraction song was less Mana-intensive, and it’d be better in this specific situation. Eola grabbed a chair and shoved it under the door’s handle, then helped Patrice push the table against the thick oak slab.

It was the best they could do; even as they finished, Eddarn’s dry, cracking voice echoed through the floating room. “Tabby, girlie, you ent getting away from me this time. My familiar’s got you figured out. It’ll find you and that Y’aer-cursed cat.” His boots thudded on the tile right outside the door, and Eola gasped as hinges creaked, her sword at the ready. Patrice stiffened, too, but the cultist didn’t burst through the door. The handle didn’t even move. And a moment later, the footsteps echoed again as he walked away.

“What do we do?” Colin whispered anxiously. “What do we do?”

“I’ve been thinking about you, Tabby Cat,” Eddarn continued as he walked heavily from the door to the left of the one they were hiding in to the one to the right. It crashed open, and he paused, then continued. “You ent reconsidered joining us, have you? The cult’s just trying to help.”

Patrice stared at Eola, then mouthed a single word. ‘Liar.’ Eola nodded, but didn’t say anything. Colin hyperventilated in the corner, and Eola rested a hand on his shoulder for a moment.

“We ent bad people, girlie. You academy types could learn from us, but no. We’re the enemies. The bad guys. Just give it up and surrender, and we’ll leave the rest of your little classmates alone. I ent the only one here. But no one’s got as good a grasp on their books as I do. It’s going to hunt you down, and only I can call it off.”

Eddarn kept talking as the seconds passed by, and gradually, Eola came to grips with their situation. There were two factors governing it, and only two.

First, neither he nor his weird familiar had any idea where she and her friends were hiding. And second, that their safety would only last as long as Roth’s soul held out.

Oh, and one more thing. His Mana was already being strained.

They were running out of time.

“Your teacher’s good with a wand,” Eddarn said. He’d stopped prowling the floating room. Instead, he’d found a seat somewhere just outside the room. When she peeked through the room’s window for a moment, Eola estimated he was less than thirty feet away, and he’d filled the air around him with Safe Shields, Ward Watches, and a half dozen other First Order and Child’s Magic spells. Eola doubted he could safely move from his seat without triggering them, and they certainly couldn’t launch a surprise attack from their room.

The book monster—Eddarn’s familiar, apparently—had no such problems. It moved across the wooden ceiling overhead, searching the room’s second, third, and fourth stories. Eola’s modified Bright Ball had disoriented it, and Colin’s ritual had caused some permanent damage, but from the sounds, it was feeding on the room’s books.

“I’m going to have to use the Soul Bleeding technique,” Roth said after almost ten minutes.

Eola shook her head. “We’ve got other options.”

“We don’t. This is the only way to keep us safe.”

“No, seriously. I ent seen a truemage with that much skill in a while. He’s good enough we had to send another bunch to counter him, and the librarian with him’s no joke, either. Tabby, think you could talk him down for us once you give up?” Eddarn said from outside. He sounded comfortable, and sure enough, when Eola sneaked another look, he’d leaned back in the chair and put his hands behind his head.

In contrast, Roth looked terrible. His cheeks were already sunken, and his eyes were hollow. He’d clearly pushed himself as far as the ‘purification’ process could take him, and he needed a refill if he wanted to keep casting.

She nodded. So did Patrice. Colin stared at the door, ‘purifying’ his own Mana after casting his fire ritual earlier. Unlike Roth, he had all the time he’d need.

Roth started the process, and the blue tint in his veins accelerated. Eola stared at his shirtsleeves as the glow of Mana poured through them, lighting up his corner of the room. He gritted his teeth, and his eyes narrowed into pained slits. “Y’aer curse it,” he choked out as tears ran down his face. The process went on and on, and both Eola and Patrice stared in horror as he played his flute through it, only missing a note here and there—and only then to swear.

When it finally ended, he was pale, and his arm’s veins glowed almost as brightly as a Bright Ball cast. “Second…second worst I’ve ever felt in my life. Don’t ask about the first. Ever. Y’aer's curse!”

Then he kept playing.

“Can we still…pass the test?” Patrice asked hesitantly.

Eola stayed quiet. The test…had gotten a little out of hand, and she hadn’t thought about either The Love Letters of Dame Brandwell and Duke Iverly or The Water War since the book monster attacked. It seemed so unimportant; if they wanted to make it out of here, they had to survive Eddarn, the Scholar’s Cult, and the monster he controlled first.

She reached into Colin’s satchel—earning a glare—and pulled out Varin’s cracked, nearly-empty journal. Her eyes scanned the words over and over, looking for something, anything, that might give her an idea.

“Pass?” Colin asked after almost a minute. Patrice twitched like she’d forgotten she’d even asked. “I’m more worried about surviving.”

Eola nodded slowly, but she still didn’t say anything.

“Magecraft is about finding the best solutions, not the most realistic ones. Cut away the fetters of preconceptions, believe the world is what you can make it, and magic will meet you there.’”

It hit her like a thunderbolt from above. She sat down in one of the remaining chairs like a puppet with cut strings. Her eyes locked on the quote, and she pulled her spellbook from its place on her bandolier.

“What are you doing?” Patrice asked.

For a moment, Eola ignored her friend. Her fingers danced through the pages, going up to her dry lips for a touch of moisture to make turning them easier. Then, at least, she stopped at the incomplete sketch of her newest modifying mark. She tapped her fingers against the unfinished Forsooth Script phrase.

Then she nodded. “I…think we can do both, Colin. We can get out of this and pass the rest of the test. But to do it, I’m going to need your help. We have to figure this out…and we have to do it now.”
