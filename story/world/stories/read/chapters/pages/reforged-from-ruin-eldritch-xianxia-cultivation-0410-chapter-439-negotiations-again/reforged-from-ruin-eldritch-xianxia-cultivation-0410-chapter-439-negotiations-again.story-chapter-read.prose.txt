Raika screams as loud and as hard as she can.

Considering just how big she is and just how many lungs she has, she can do it very hard and loud indeed.

The landscape shakes with her frustration. The remaining mountains surrounding her northern side vibrate with the sound, echoing it up and down into the rivers and valleys below, making the trees and grasses and puddles and ponds and rocks all vibrate in tune. The sky quivers, the surface of it out far beyond conventional sight but the effects of its spasms visible all the way down on the ground. Beneath the ground, crimson stone cracks and heals and cracks again, tectonic plates in miniature trembling with the force of the emotions on display.

Below, the trial-runners are all looking about, reacting to each other and the dangers all around. The trials don’t stop in the midst of all the chaos. Some of them do pause, or are disrupted by breaking ground and shaking walls, but the biomorphic constructs keep fighting mindlessly and with complete violence, and the environmental hazards don’t vanish either. Clouds of acid and pools of transformative enzymes splash or fill up different passageways, while spikes or vents of superheated gases shoot out towards different targets inside massive underground caves and manufactured complexes. Serpents and crabs and humanoids and insects, all ranging from miniscule to colossal, fill the bounds of the lower levels, acting as direct dangers and sources of food and natural treasures both.

Above the ground, a ruined few miles of terrain have collapsed from where they fell out of the air when Raika pulled.

If one looks at it from the right angle, they might be able to see the shape of what used to be a campus, what used to be buildings, torn open or unmade almost frame by frame. Conventional rock, dirt and plants are vanishing moment by moment, being digested away by the ground they’ve landed on, leaving just Raika and the corpses and the people who didn’t quite end up that way.

Close to a third of the spouses have died. Closer to half of the servants, be it of the spouses or the Crimson Manse itself, have been made gone. Corpses in all but form, because there is no blood or collapsing biology, just bodies with absences, pieces of them crucial for existence scooped out of them and the resulting voids simply existing.

Aria is alive. The ones she chose to enhance, who volunteered for their modifications, are alive. Others (and she hasn’t learned the names of any of them yet and part of her feels wrong about that and part of her does not care) are still alive.

And as the seconds pass, they stay that way.

Raika keeps screaming. She also keeps looking everywhere, checking on the state of every single person and aspect and piece within her.

The seconds pass, and she keeps shaking with her rage and frustration, and they stay alive.

The man who Is Not There… isn’t here.

They’re all talking. All those different voices, figuring things out, trying to communicate or understand what’s happening. Aria is one of them. Aria helped, and had a good idea, and she should probably listen to her. One Raika is, actually, but even she’s a bit distracted. All the others, though, are focused on thinking.

She can’t remember the last time she ran from a fight. For once, it’s not just because her brain is hard wired to explode itself if she tries. She thinks, she genuinely thinks, but there are no safeguards, no blocks, nothing that implies there’s a memory or part of her identity she’s restricted from accessing.

She doesn’t run from fights. Raika doesn’t run from fights. She doesn’t- maybe she’s avoided fights, or had to retreat, had to be smart, but nothing fits the criteria, nothing. She had to run. She had to run.

She should maybe still run.

She keeps ignoring all of the voices vying for her attention, letting other Raikas deal with or not deal with things as needed, instead turning to focus on her “exterior”.

The moment she refocuses on the exterior of the fold in space, a head turns to look at her in a bit of surprise.

“Oh! Hey, you’re back. Master, this is-”

She tunes Ko out entirely, instead growing several new eyes and tendrils to move them out of the branches and ground to look around.

She’s still in the crater where she took out the Feng and forced the other two to retreat. She can sense the mountain, the river, and the sea in the form of people still present, each of them still hovering above the ground in varying states (though they have made themselves more comfortable). She can still taste the encampments out further, where dozens of lesser cultivators continue to wait in safety far from her.

The sky has a few wisps of cloud, coming in from far away to fill the void the previous explosions left. The mountain remains charred and torn apart, only the colossal scale of it ensuring that it doesn’t collapse from the carved out devastation where the plateau used to be.

There is no sign of the Feng.

She takes a few seconds more, and she isn’t erased.

She closes a few eyes, makes a few empty spaces where she isn’t actively seeing things.

She still doesn’t vanish or get erased.

Alright. She has time. A little bit of time, maybe.

She should probably move again. Find a new place, relocate, hide, and-

Hide.

She hates.

“Master?”

A few hundred eyes blink, then roll and roil over to stare at Ko.

He doesn’t flinch. She actually doesn’t even sense discomfort from him. It’s…

Has she been sensing discomfort from people this whole time? Is that what that was? Its absence feels loud.

“Yes, Ko?”

“Did you… have an opinion on anything?”

“I have opinions on many things.”

“As is only right. But you were just screaming very very loudly. And you have also not answered any direct questions in a few minutes. Also, you seem a bit tense.”

“That’s accurate. Was there something you needed me for?”

“Not right this immediate second, but I am fairly certain I’m going to be violently killed sometime in the next hour or two if I don’t adequately represent your interests and prove my ability to negotiate on your behalf. I should note I am also not very good at negotiating.”

“That’s fair. Sorry you might die. Do you think it would help if Aria were here? She’s helping with the spouses right now.”

“Huh. A bit funny, that. Usually it’s reversed.”

“Oh, that’s perfect then.”

She pictures where Aria is standing and the space where Ko is standing as opposite ends of a tendon, and Flesh is Space and she pulls at it.

Aria immediately stumbles, briefly taking a breath before she gags, turns, and vomits loudly into some of the nearby bushes. Which are also Raika.

Ko, to his credit, doesn’t puke. It might be because he also has a space-like affinity with how he teleports to his sound-waves, but it might also be that she did a better job because she was more actively focused on him. He does, however, go a bit pale and get a pretty big spike in his stress-scents as he sees the view in front of him- what’s left of the Crimson Manse digesting into the landscape of Raika, with dozens of confused, terrified, and sometimes quite powerful cultivators turning to glare at him.

“What is this childish stupidity before me?” asks one of the elders.

She could probably kill him. No, she could definitely kill him. She has mountains, she is mountains, and a mountain, no matter how big, does not surpass space or the world or the idea of flesh made manifest.

Should she kill him? She’s not sure.

Is this what it’s like to be afraid? A mind constantly darting about, unable to focus? Or is it a symptom of how she’s lobotomized herself? She’s not sure.

“Just resolving some things,” she says instead of narrating all of that. “Turns out I had to fight a bunch more Fengs somewhere else and now I’m a bit tired from it. You understand.”
