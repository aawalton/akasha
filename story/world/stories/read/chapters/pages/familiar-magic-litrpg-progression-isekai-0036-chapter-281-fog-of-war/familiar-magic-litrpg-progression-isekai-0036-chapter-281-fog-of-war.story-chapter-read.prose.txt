Ethan Bishop had nearly run out of tricks, but he couldn’t let that stop him. The Follow army was here, right in front of him, their presence both a blessing and a curse. A blessing because his plan to use the Basilisk now had a chance at succeeding, but a curse because they were only a handful of days away from Ethan’s own demonic forces.

He needed to hurt them. He couldn’t believe that he could stop them, not with no real power of his own, and just a single Master rank monster–one not even under his command. But he could hurt them. Slow them. Perhaps even give them something to fear.

An odd, cold calm came over his mind, as purpose burned up indecision and worry. All he felt was clarity, his goals laid out before him, and his body and magic merely tools to achieve what was barely possible.

Ethan was rushing down the hill toward the marching army of the Followers, a small sea of liquid black demons, moving inexorably to the east. They were a more eclectic collection than when last he’d seen them. They ranged from enormous insect-like creatures, to something like twisted versions of animals Ethan might have recognized from home.

He spotted some resembling apes, and more than a few looking like enormous cats or hounds. Some even looked like dark reflections of Revan, making Ethan wish his fiery Familiar weren’t still recovering. But uniformly they all looked forward, not even glazing at the demon the Basilisk was currently devouring.

Ethan couldn’t account for the oddity, except perhaps that he couldn’t spot any actual Followers among the group. It was possible they were all out searching for more recruits, though he did see the same poorly constructed shack atop an elephant-like demon toward the center of the army. Ethan doubted they would leave their army entirely alone–doubted they could do so, and he expected their Emperor was still within.

Ethan’s right arm ached in remembered pain from his last encounter with the Master rank Follower, and promised himself he wouldn’t be caught again.

Sparse trees dotted the valley, and Ethan used them for cover as he raced toward the dark army. He could feel the Basilisk chasing him once more, and he pushed himself to reach the next closest demon before that fated gaze passed over him. His side was still slowly petrifying, and he needed no more reminder of the dangerous game he was playing.

The scorpion-like demon he reached first didn’t seem to acknowledge him as he slid beneath its many legs, and he appeared on the other side of it just as the Basilisk unleashed its power. Ethan managed to the large beast for cover, slowing only slightly to see how effective the stony gaze would be.

His own eyes widened as the demon was entirely turned to stone in mere moments, the heavy body cracking and turning to dust almost immediately.

“It’s vastly more effective on these demons than it was on me,” he said in shock. He was already moving again when Tomo spoke.

“I do not believe Potentia has ever encountered such power before, Ethan Bishop. Not only has Basilisk reached Master rank, and seemingly subsumed all its other abilities in service of its gaze, but demons do not seem to have any natural resistance to the intrusive magic.”

“I’ll take any win I can get,” Ethan said, leaping to the back of the next Twilight he saw, then almost immediately jumping away as the Basilisk's power washed over this new target.

He unleashed [Karmic Threads] all around him now, grateful that the ability was so mana-efficient. He used the thin metal wires to roughly pull him through the army, drawing the enormous serpent ever closer.

The silence of the army remained, and it was somewhat unnerving to only hear their many heavy footfalls, but someone had clearly noticed what was happening by now. The ponderous Twilight giants were all turning toward the new threat, many readying attacks.

Petrification washed over them like a wave. Dozens were turned to stone in the first minute, with Ethan darting between them like a determined housefly, always just out of reach. He couldn’t believe how well this was going. The army may be in the hundreds, but that was working against it. The demons were so densely packed that even the smaller, faster ones had no room to move.

The Basilisk was devastating their somewhat orderly lines, turning them to stone, then stone to dust as it charged madly forward. Ethan’s cold calm was on the verge of turning into nervous glee as the minutes went on. He had to keep to the edges of the army to avoid getting close to the Emperor, but he caught glimpses of the Follower leader between swinging and dodging.

The Emperor was covered in his own inky blackness, and gesticulating wildly at the colossal serpent in their midst. Ethan couldn’t even be sure the man knew he was there, as the Basilisk itself was such an eye-catching terror.

The killing was slowing now, as the Emperor seemed to be taking a direct role in commanding his forces. The demons were spreading out, and using those already petrified as cover to get close. Already a half-dozen of the cat-like demons had latched onto the serpent, claws and bladed limbs slashing deeply into the scaly flesh.

But the Master rank Basilisk barely slowed, never losing Ethan’s trail as they both weaved around the army. Ethan was approaching giddiness as his risky plan seemed to be out-performing his wildest, most desperate hopes. After roughly fifteen minutes of chaos, easily half the enemy’s army was nothing more than thick dust. That dust only made it harder to see and avoid the petrifying gaze, and Ethan was almost ready to believe they could finish off the entire Follower forces in one terrible blow.

Even the Emperor appeared afraid of approaching the terrible serpent, creating a shield of demonic flesh to protect himself at the army’s center. He stood in the middle of half a hundred Twilights moving around him, pointing and giving commands to someone unseen.

For Ethan’s part, his game of cat and mouse with the Basilisk was still draining, his mana reserves nearly spent. He was just considering whether he should risk eating up some of the tiny quantity of dimensional mana he’d recovered, when something strange happened.

He heard a roar. It was a deep, challenging sound, and it actually seemed to get the attention of the previously myopic serpent. Ethan’s eyes widened as he saw a hulking, inky-black creature charging away from the Emperor. It looked wrong, for a demon. Rounded, where Potentia favored sharp angles. Furry, where chitin and sleek was the norm. He would have sworn it was a bear–

It roared again, the only creature other than the Basilisk to make any sound at all, and the serpent answered with a blast from those cold white eyes. But rather than the expected end, the bear-like beast opened enormous jaws to unleash a blast of frost, and the two powers crashed together, a wave of fog and dust rolling out.

Ethan’s jaw fell. A bear using powers…it couldn’t be. Then he saw a figure rushing toward the Basilisk, and the world seemed to shatter around him.

The man was entirely lost beneath the inky black of a Follower Familiar, but the way he moved, and the two swords he wielded were unmistakable. Ethan couldn’t imagine how this was possible, but he could only watch in stunned silence as Prince Calevaro charged the Basilisk.

With typical speed and control–yet elevated to Twilight rank–the prince darted between demons, easily staying ahead of the Basilisk’s petrifying gauge. He moved like the wind, reaching the serpent in seconds, then running in a spiral up the scaly body. When he reached the enormous head, Ethan thought he was about to see his friend die, as those glowing eyes turned on the prince.

But just like he’d done beneath the ice when they faced Basilisk as a man, the prince managed to get his swords together. He was close enough to block the power as the yellow wave passed over him, leaving the serpent blinking in surprise.

Calevaro didn’t waste the moment he’d bought himself and his blades speared into Basilisk’s left eye. The serpent howled in pain, amplified a moment later as its right eye met the same fate. Ethan knew how quickly those eyes could regenerate–he’d tried the same thing himself–but the prince simply left a sword in each, forcing them back into place as the Basilisk tried to shake off its attacker.

Ethan was moving forward before he even realized it. All his instincts told him that he needed to help his friend–whether against the Basilisk, or freeing him from the Followers, Ethan couldn’t say. But he halted after only a few paces when he saw the Emperor finally make a move.

No longer fearing petrification, the Follower had leapt from the center of the army, landing on the Basilisk’s back. Ethan’s heart dropped as he saw inky black erupting from the Emperor’s body, and slowly covering the enormous creature. The Basilisk flailed and roared, its long body rolling over and crushing nearby Twilights, but whatever the Follower was doing, the Basilisk didn’t seem able to resist.

Everything had just changed. The army had been reduced by nearly two thirds, but gained a Master rank monster. One that could seemingly petrify anything its gaze landed on. Ethan hadn’t imagined this. The Basilisk wasn’t a demon, and it was so dangerous he’d believed the Followers would be forced to swarm and kill it. His only hope had been that it would do enough damage before its death to justify the difficulty in getting here.

But this… He could never have accounted for Prince Calevaro.

Ethan’s body tried to collapse in defeat, and it was a supreme effort of will to stay on his feet. The chaos and scrambling around him were slowing as the Basilisk came under Follower control, leaving him in the middle of an enemy army, with almost no mana, and even less of a chance at survival.

He was crouched in the stone remains of a large Twilight insect, and he didn’t think they’d seen him yet. Maybe he could make it away from the army, but with the Emperor and the Basilisk in pursuit…with Calevaro in pursuit, and his remarkable speed, what chance did he have of surviving another week-long sprint?

Stealth had failed him against the Emperor before, but it was all Ethan had. He prepared to make another desperate escape, only to notice that the army seemed frozen in place. He risked a glance back at the Basilisk, now silent, and entirely encased in black, and saw that it was staring back east. So were Cal and the Emperor.

Ethan followed their gaze, squinting into the distance in confusion. Then he saw it.

A bird? A silver bird. It was so far away that the scale didn’t make sense at first, until Ethan realized that he’d seen it before. He’d seen it here, on Potentia, years ago. It was the Familiar of the first Bonded he’d met.

The Champion Allyara had arrived.
