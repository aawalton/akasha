I jumped out of bed, backing toward the door in panic. I was… out of words, out of thoughts. Stunned. I tried to force the sleep out of my brain. This was the most fucked-up situation imaginable, and I needed to assess it absolutely right now.

I will… consult with Juliette?

“WHAT?! THE?? FUCK, JULIETTE!?” I shouted, probably woke up half the NPCs in town. If they could wake up, that is.“THIS THING! FUCK!” I continued, pointing furiously at the Scorpion Surikata. I took a short breath, trying to force my brain into functionality.

“You did it on purpose,” I said, quieter now. “You brought the creature here. Why?”

Juliette kept on smiling - but her voice was calm. And she spoke with confidence.

“First of all,” she began, “I want to apologize for lying. After we got back from the forest, yes—I cried. I was sad about Ramon. But I wasn’t panicking. I wasn’t even upset. I felt... calm. Very calm. And that surprised me.”

She looked away for a moment. “It was like everything suddenly made sense—the forest, the Scorpion Surikata. I knew it was all connected to the sting. And I knew if I told you, you wouldn’t trust me. So I waited until you were asleep... and I went back.”"You what?""I wasn’t scared," she repeated. "And the same scorpion that stung me… he was waiting for me. I brought him back." She gestured proudly at the creature sitting on her lap.

"And let him fucking sting me in my sleep!"

"Yes," she said, without even flinching.

I stared at her. My panic wasn’t fading. My brain was scrambling to make sense of this.

"What the hell do you mean, you understood things?" I asked. But somewhere deep inside, I already knew where this was going. And that part of me was terrified.

"The forest isn’t just some chaotic monster pit," Juliette said, sounding like a high priestess now. "It’s organized. Structured. Like ecosystems and territory disputes. The Care Bear Armadillos that attacked Ramon? And the Scorpion Surikata we killed? They were from the same tribe. A bad tribe.”

“What... are you talking about?” I cut her off, my heart pounding.

“Look,” she said, smiling knowingly, “This Scorpion Surikata is not from that tribe. His tribe has lived in this part of the forest for years. But recently, the bad tribe—the White Stripe Tribe, they called, started attacking them. Killing them. Forcing them to flee into other tribes’ territory where they’d get attacked again. They’re being wiped out.”

Juliette’s face was full of compassion. Way too much compassion. “I just... really want to help them. I think we should help. It’s the right thing to do.”

At this point, I didn’t even need to ask more questions. Everything already fit into place.

This was a mind-control thing. Not telepathy—something chemical. Maybe magical. From the sting. It was in her blood now. Probably mine, too.

The smart thing would be to... move fast. Grab it from her lap. And—no, I couldn’t even finish the thought.

Killing it was wrong. Hurting it was unthinkable. I knew I was only thinking that way because my brain had been tampered with—but that knowledge changed nothing. The idea of harming the Surikata Scorpion felt... vile.

Too late. It was already happening.

The creature was starting to look cute. I wanted to pet it behind those weird ear-like things. I felt calm. Focused. Strangely good.

But I kept thinking. Kept analyzing. I was used to that.

“Wait,” I said slowly. “All that stuff you said—about the tribes, and the White Stripes—how the hell do you know all that?”

“He told me, of course,” she said brightly. “Here. Touch him.”

I reached out and placed my hand on the creature’s back. No hesitation. The idea of being afraid of this thing was laughable now. It looked less dangerous than a kitten.

For a few moments, nothing happened.

Then the fur under my hand started to vibrate.

I felt a tingling sensation in my fingers.

I looked at Juliette in confusion, but then—it started.

I was receiving information. Not with words, not with sound, but through this... like pictures. Like emotions layered over images. It was surprisingly easy to understand—like hearing full sentences, just through a different channel.It was probably the system doing. Translating the creature’s hair-vibration language for me.

First, the creature presented himself. He was part of a small tribe - about twenty Scorpion Surikatas. A core family. Plus some dozens of ‘friends’ - mostly Armadillo Care Bears, some of those cow-sized six-legged creatures, and some furred snake things.Juliette and I were the only humans—and the only sapient beings—the tribe had ever had.This was a big deal for him. He was proud to have us. And that made me feel—God help me—proud too.

He showed me more.He was the younger brother of the tribe’s leader—a strong female. That made him second in command. He had a mate, and seven kids. He used to have nine.

He showed me two that died during the last White Stripe Tribe attack. He showed them fighting. Brave. Defending their home. He needed me to know that.

I pulled my hand away. I couldn’t take it. My eyes welled up with tears.

“Oh my God,” I whispered. “They... they were so young.”

“I know,” Juliette said softly, already guessing what I’d seen. She placed her hand gently on my shoulder. “But please. Keep listening.”

I returned my hand to the creature’s back.

He showed me more—their struggle, their wish to live a peaceful life, raise their children, grow as a tribe.

And then he showed why we were important. Humans—sapient allies—were rare and precious. He was counting on us. The hope of the tribe rested on our shoulders.

But... he wasn’t quite sure how we were supposed to help.

He had this vague idea that humans could build traps—he pictured bizarre metallic devices that maimed hordes of enemies. He also imagined us bringing enormous piles of food, fire things? soft pillows, and finding more humans. He somehow knew the difference between NPCs and players—and knew his venom didn’t work on NPCs. So basically, he wanted us to go back to the forest and conjure a bunch of miraculous traps.

The conversation was over. I pulled my hand back.

The truth was... the Scorpion Surikata wasn’t very smart.

Don’t get me wrong. Compared to most animals, he was incredible. He understood concepts like family, war, weapons. But not deeply. I’d say he was chimpanzee-level smart. Which, again—not an insult. That’s crazy smart for a furry mongoose size creature.

But yeah. He was wrong about how we could help.

“Juliette,” I said slowly, “can he... understand us? I mean, we can’t vibrate hair like him.”

“He understands French,” she said. “I mean, the system translates for him too. But... he doesn’t get a lot of words. It’s tricky. You should say things in different ways, touch him, and feel whether he truly understood what you were trying to say. And talk loud and close to him. I think his hearing sucks.”

“Got it.” I nodded. “But Juliette, he’s very wrong about how we can help him. He doesn’t understand our limitations.”

“I know,” she said. “We don’t have the supplies. We can’t help in a war. We’d just die.”

We both went quiet. The unspoken thought hung heavy between us: if we die, the tribe loses its only sapient allies.

"We shouldn't go back to the forest full-time," I said. "Not yet."

The surikata looked up at me. His fur vibrated. He wanted to say something.

I ignored him.

“From what I got from him,” I continued, “this war isn’t ending tomorrow. It’s been going on for a long time. Months maybe. So yes—it’s awful. But it’s not an immediate threat. What they need now is supplies. And we need better gear. I’ve been thinking: spears, full leather armor, and shields would be the most effective for us. And we can’t steal them—we’d risk jail. Which helps no one.”

“I agree,” she said after a pause. “We should work. Earn money. Maybe... two weeks? Get everything we need.”

“Exactly.” I turned to the creature. Placed my hand on his fur again. “Humans are bad fighters,” I said slowly. “Need weapons.”

He understood. Showed me and Juliette with imaginary weapons—then pictured us returning to the forest.

“Right now, We don’t have weapons. Or food. Or supplies,” I said.

He seemed baffled. He imagined us taking supplies from human homes—maybe thinking those buildings were our ‘former tribe.’

It was a slow process.

He had no concept of jobs. Or money. After about thirty minutes of weird, trial, error, and repeated words, he understood that we needed to do something to get the weapons and supplies. That it would take time. He didn’t like it. But he accepted it.

He still wanted us to come visit the tribe. To show us off, probably. We happily agreed.

Moments later, he curled up like a cat on a pillow and seemed to fall asleep.

Seriously—the cutest thing ever.Now that he was probably asleep, there were some things I wanted to talk about with Juliette—alone.

I sat beside Juliette on the floor. “Can we talk?” I asked quietly.

“Sure,” she said. “You... probably want to talk about all this… without him. Speak low. Even if he wakes, he won’t hear.”

“Juliette... you know our minds are being messed with, right? The sting didn’t exactly mind-control us—but it made us love him. Like, genuinely care.”

“I know,” she sighed. “Maybe that should be scary. But... no matter how I try to twist the idea in my head, it still feels okay. Like... we just found a new friend and bonded instantly. Yeah, in a weird way. But does the reason really matter?”

“I guess not,” I admitted. “Still... we had a mission. To get stronger. You wanted to get home.”

“And I still do,” she said. “But… maybe the goals align. We need better equipment. We want to learn about the forest. And maybe helping this Scorpion Surikata”

“Bob! Let’s call him Bob!” I said. “Please, we must give him a name.”

She laughed. “Okay. Bob it is.”

“Maybe helping Bob will help us too,” She continue. “And maybe... if we kill his enemies...”

“We’ll also level up!” I finished.

We grinned at each other.

“I think we maybe actually got lucky,” I said.

“Yes,” she whispered. Then suddenly, she hugged me. “I have a feeling everything is finally working out.”

I liked the hug. A lot.

I was still sad about Ramon. But once again, I had two important friends:

One human.One fluffy.And I loved and cared for them both.
