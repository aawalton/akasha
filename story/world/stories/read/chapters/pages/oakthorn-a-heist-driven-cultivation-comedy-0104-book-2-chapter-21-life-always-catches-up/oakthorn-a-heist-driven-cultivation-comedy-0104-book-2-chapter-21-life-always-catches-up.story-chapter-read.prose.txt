The vortex seized Destruction’s essence and pulled her downward through its swirling depths. Her form twisted while her consciousness stretched into painful oblivion before snapping back into sharp focus. Winter’s breath swept across her face, somehow both gentle and biting all at once.

She blinked, and the world materialized around her in crystalline clarity. The winter lodge stood nestled against the steep mountainside of the Cloudrift range, paradise incarnate in ancient timber and weathered stone. Massive pine logs stacked and sealed with golden resin formed walls that had sheltered centuries of whispered confessions and intertwined bodies.

A stone hearth dominated the main room where embers still glowed, casting dancing shadows across the collection of oddities they had gathered over their decade together—mortal trinkets that meant nothing to anyone but them. Death’s midnight cloak hung by the door beside her flame-red shawl, the fabrics occasionally brushing against each other when the mountain breeze found its way through the cracks. Their shared bed remained unmade, sheets tangled from the previous night’s passion, and two teacups sat abandoned on the bedside table, tea leaves settled into patterns only Death could read.

Books lined every available surface, some with pages marked by pressed flowers from realms long destroyed, others dog-eared at passages that had made her laugh. A map of constellations nobody but they remembered was etched into the ceiling above their bed, and sometimes they would lie there for days, recounting stories of stars they had watched live and die.

Outside, frost glittered across the meadow beyond the doorway, blankets of white extending to a distant precipice where solid ground surrendered to clouds below. More clouds hung above, not ephemeral wisps but tangible constructions that served as pathways for gods to tread upon when they wished to walk through the sky.

Destruction exhaled slowly outside her beloved home and watched her breath curl upward into the crisp air until it vanished. Memories flooded through her mind—countless walks taken with Death across those white landscapes, their fingers intertwined as they wandered far from the troubles of the mortal realm below, far from Creation’s vengeful wrath, far from the endless war that had consumed so many centuries.

A decade had passed in this sanctuary with her dark lover. Ten perfect years of refuge where the sting of earlier betrayals had gradually faded until only contentment remained. Peace had crept into her bones, an unfamiliar sensation but one she had grown to welcome and cherish.

The wind shifted suddenly.

Cold—true cold—bit into Destruction’s skin with unexpected ferocity. She felt it deep in her core, truly felt it in a way that startled her. This wasn’t the gentle chill that usually rolled off Death’s form when he approached; this was something sharper and fundamentally wrong. Her heart stuttered in her chest while ancient instincts screamed warnings she had long forgotten how to properly heed.

She turned, expecting to see Death’s familiar silhouette against the snow.

“YOU’VE GOTTEN BETTER AT THAT AURA OF YOURS, MY—”

Instead, gold glinted brilliantly in the winter light.

Creation stood at the meadow’s edge with her golden hair pulled back in an elaborate warbraid and metallic armor encasing her form—armor that Destruction recognized from countless battles across endless centuries, armor that shifted and flowed with its wearer’s desires like liquid metal. Her sister’s face mirrored her own in many ways yet appeared harder and colder, sculpted by hatred rather than love.

Memory tugged at Destruction’s mind. They had fought and battled across countless realms. Creation had prevailed and won and...

But surely that didn’t matter now. They were family bound by forces older than time itself. Here in this place apart from the world, old wounds could finally begin to heal.

“Sister,” Destruction began, her voice soft with tentative hope. She intentionally reduced the amount of aura she imbued into her words, desiring to keep the mood light. Intimate, even. “How did you—”

Creation’s scream tore through the mountain air like a blade. She lunged forward, a golden comet streaking across the snow with divine fury.

Destruction barely managed to sidestep the attack, calling fire to her hands through pure reflex. Divine power answered her call but responded sluggishly—years of peace had dulled her combat edges. Creation’s fist connected with her jaw and sent her sprawling across the snow. Blood, bright and shocking against the pristine white, spattered in tiny crimson constellations around her fallen form.

“Sister, please!” Destruction rolled away from a strike that shattered the frozen ground where she had lain only moments before. “I don’t want to fight you!”

Creation offered no verbal response but launched another vicious attack as blades manifested from her armor to slash toward Destruction’s face. Destruction blocked the strike, and metal shrieked against her hastily formed shield of hardened flame. She retreated steadily, defending herself without countering with the full destructive force she could command if unleashed.

She refused to hurt her sister here. This sanctuary had been built for healing, not for continuing ancient wounds.

Creation pressed her advantage relentlessly and conjured a golden spear in her hands that trailed stardust as it thrust toward Destruction’s heart. Destruction twisted her body aside, caught the shaft in both hands, and used Creation’s momentum to throw her sister over her shoulder and into a nearby snowbank.

The brief respite gave Destruction precious moments to breathe deeply and center herself for what was coming.

“WHY?” Destruction roared as tears streamed down her face. Her gaze caught sight of the cabin behind her sister where flames now consumed the structure, devouring memories and tender moments without mercy. Robed figures—Lifekeepers in their ceremonial garb—encircled the inferno and ensured that nothing would survive the blaze. “WHY CAN’T YOU JUST LEAVE US BE? WE DID NOTHING TO YOU THAT YOU HAVEN’T DONE TO US!”

Creation laughed, and the sound broke across the mountainside like glass shattering against unforgiving stone.

“YOU REALLY DON’T KNOW, DO YOU?” Her voice carried across the distance between them without the weight of Destruction’s but bearing no less power in its cold fury.

“WHAT?”

Creation spread her arms wide, and her armor glistened like a newborn star against the backdrop of the blazing cabin. Paradise burning. Memory burning. Love burning. All because her sister apparently couldn’t release some ancient grudge Destruction couldn’t even remember.

“YOU KILLED HIM,” Creation’s words fell between them like hammers against an anvil. “YOU KILLED MY SON, YOU HEARTLESS BITCH.”

Creation surged forward with renewed rage as weapons erupted from every surface of her armor—blades, spikes, and razors all aimed at Destruction’s heart. Destruction, shocked by the accusation that seemed to come from nowhere, failed to mount an adequate defense. Weapons pierced her body in a dozen places simultaneously, and pain blossomed through her like crimson flowers blooming against the snow.

“WHAT SON?” Destruction gasped through blood-flecked lips as Creation twisted a blade embedded between her ribs. “I NEVER—”

“LUCIEN!” Creation’s voice broke, her perfect face contorting with grief so raw it transcended divinity. “MY BEAUTIFUL BOY!” She withdrew one blade only to plunge it deeper into Destruction’s shoulder, pinning her to the frozen ground. “THE MOONGLADE KINGDOM! YOU RAZED IT TO NOTHING BUT ASH AND EMBERS!”

Memory crashed into Destruction’s mind with the force of a collapsing star. The Moonglade capital with its silver spires and luminescent gardens. Her fury after another defeat—how many had there been?—at Creation and Life’s hands. The week of fire she had called down from the heavens until nothing remained but scorched earth and rising smoke. How she had driven her essence deep into the ground and forced a mountain of magma to rise where the palace once stood.

It had been just before she and Death fled to this sanctuary. Her final act of defiance.

“I DIDN’T KNOW,” Destruction whispered, and then louder as realization dawned: “I DIDN’T KNOW HE WAS THERE! CREATION, I SWEAR ON THE VOID BETWEEN STARS—”

“HE WAS SEVEN!” Creation’s fist connected with Destruction’s jaw, the metal gauntlet shattering bone. She followed with a knee to Destruction’s stomach, driving the air from her lungs in a cloud of crimson mist. “SEVEN! STUDYING THE MOON FLORA! LEARNING TO CREATE BEAUTY INSTEAD OF BREAKING IT!”

Creation manifested a golden whip from her palm and lashed it across Destruction’s chest, tearing flesh and exposing ribs beneath. “AND YOU BURNED HIM! ALIVE! FOR A WEEK!” Each word punctuated by another strike. “HE CALLED FOR ME! EVERY! SINGLE! DAY!”

Agony tore through Destruction’s entire being on multiple levels beyond mere physical pain. She recognized the distinctive burning sensation spreading outward from each wound—Life magic coursing through her veins like acid. Her sister had infused her weapons with Life’s essence, the one substance truly anathema to Destruction’s very nature. But worse than the physical torment was the realization of what she had done.

“I NEVER MEANT—” Destruction struggled to form words through the waves of pain. “THAT’S WHY I LEFT. I COULDN’T BEAR WHAT I’D BECOME. WHAT I’D DONE.”

Creation’s face twisted into something unrecognizable—grief and rage transcending divine beauty. She manifested a spear of pure golden light and held it above Destruction’s heart. “YOU LEFT BECAUSE YOU’RE A COWARD. YOU RAN WITH YOUR LOVER WHILE MY SON’S ASHES FERTILIZED YOUR PRECIOUS VOLCANO.”

She drove the spear downward, stopping mere inches from Destruction’s heart. “I TRACKED YOU FOR TEN YEARS. TEN YEARS WHILE YOU PLAYED HOUSE IN YOUR LITTLE PARADISE, FORGETTING THE LIVES YOU DESTROYED.”

Destruction’s hands shot up, wrapping around the shaft of the golden spear. Once, she would have shattered it with a thought, but her powers responded sluggishly after years of disuse. Still, she managed to push back, just enough to create space.

“HE WAS INNOCENT,” Destruction acknowledged, her voice breaking. “BUT SO WAS I OF KNOWING HE WAS THERE. CREATION, PLEASE—WE CAN END THIS CYCLE.”

With a desperate surge, she kicked upward, connecting with Creation’s stomach and sending her sister stumbling backward. Destruction rolled, calling flame to her fingertips, but the fire flickered weakly—a candle’s glow where once a sun would have answered.

Creation recovered almost instantly, her movements fluid and precise. Years of purpose had honed her into a perfect weapon while Destruction had grown soft in her contentment.

“END THE CYCLE?” Creation laughed, the sound devoid of all mirth. She flicked her wrist, and golden chains erupted from the snow, wrapping around Destruction’s wrists and ankles. “THERE IS NO CYCLE. ONLY JUSTICE.”

Creation stalked forward, manifesting a curved blade that gleamed with an inner light. With surgical precision, she sliced along Destruction’s arms, opening vessels that spilled divine essence into the snow. “YOU CLAIMED IGNORANCE AS EXCUSE WHEN YOU BUTCHERED MILLIONS. THE GREAT DESTROYER, TOO GRAND TO COUNT THE COST OF HER TANTRUMS.”

Destruction strained against the chains, muscles tearing with the effort. A decade ago, she would have broken free in an instant. Now, she couldn’t even summon enough power to melt the links.

“I’VE CHANGED,” she gasped, blood bubbling between her lips. “I’VE BEEN TRYING TO ATONE—”

“ATONE?” Creation drove her knee into Destruction’s sternum, shattering ribs with the impact. She grabbed her sister’s throat, fingers digging into divine flesh. “BY HIDING? BY PRETENDING THE BLOOD ON YOUR HANDS DOESN’T EXIST?” She slammed Destruction’s head against the ground, fracturing the frozen earth beneath. “TELL ME, SISTER, HOW MANY TEARS HAVE YOU SHED FOR LUCIEN? FOR ANY OF THEM?”

Destruction’s vision blurred, memories and present pain merging into a kaleidoscope of agony. She tried to form flame daggers, a desperate countermeasure she’d once employed instinctively. Her fingers barely smoked.

Creation noticed the attempt and smiled—a terrible expression devoid of anything resembling joy. “YOUR EDGE HAS DULLED, SISTER. MINE HAS ONLY SHARPENED.” She placed her palm against Destruction’s chest, and golden light pulsed between her fingers. “EVERY DAY WITHOUT MY SON, I PRACTICED. EVERY NIGHT I DREAMED OF THIS MOMENT.”

The golden light intensified, and Destruction screamed as it seared through her flesh, burning her from the inside. Creation’s power had grown immeasurably, fueled by grief and rage and purpose.

“WHERE... WHERE IS DEATH?” Destruction gasped, reaching desperately for her lover through their bond. Surely he would sense her agony, her need.

Creation’s smile widened, something terrible and knowing in her eyes. She released her grip on Destruction’s throat and leaned close until her mouth hovered near Destruction’s ear, her voice dropping to a venomous whisper that somehow cut deeper than her blades.

“WHERE IS THAT BASTARD?” The question slithered into Destruction’s mind like poison. “HE NEVER SEEMS TO BE AROUND WHEN YOU SUFFER. CONVENIENT.”

Destruction couldn’t deny the observation. Death was indeed absent, as he always seemed to be in her moments of greatest need. She had convinced herself this was merely Creation’s manipulation—another clever attempt to drive a wedge between her and her lover. Creation certainly knew that divided, she and Death would inevitably fall to the combined might of Creation and Life.

Death must be fighting Life elsewhere right now, she reasoned desperately. He must be locked in his own desperate battle while she faced Creation alone. He would come soon. He would save their home and their sanctuary and their love.

But as darkness gradually crept into the edges of her vision and Creation’s sneering face hovered above her, the final terrible truth dawned with absolute clarity. Some part of her had known it for a while. It had grated against her resolve those five millennia she’d spent in a box. Where had he been? Why hadn’t he fought for her? With her? Where in the four hells had he been that night when their home burned and she bled into the snow?

She knew now. There’d been no fight. She would’ve felt his power drawn from the local ley lines as easily as he could’ve felt hers.

Death is a coward.

The memory fractured suddenly, and reality reasserted itself with jarring force. Destruction found herself kneeling in a cold stone tunnel with blood—her own blood—pooled beneath her trembling form. In her hand, a glowing Remnant pulsed with power unlike any other she had encountered throughout her long existence. She felt it resonating deep within her essence and calling to her like a piece of herself returned after eons apart.

Destruction spat blood onto the stone floor and closed her fingers tightly around the Remnant. It’s glow lit up the abyssal tunnel.

Two down with only one remaining.

She pushed herself upright while ignoring the phantom pain of wounds long healed and continued purposefully down the tunnel toward the final vortex. One more trial awaited her journey. One more crucial piece of herself to reclaim from the endless void.

Then she would forge Calamity at last.
