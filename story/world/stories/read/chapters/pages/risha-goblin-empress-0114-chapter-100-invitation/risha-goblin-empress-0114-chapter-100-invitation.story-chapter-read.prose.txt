“You sensed the Echo’s spirit leave?” The Ancient Shaman asked.

Risha sat with him on a fallen log inside the realm of her star. Across from them, the Red-Scaled apprentice, Ava, held the gaze of a small spirit in the shape of a squirrel. The other two Scaled apprentices waited back in the glade where the Shaman had created the spirit-gathering array.

“I don’t know.” Risha watched as the squirrel moved closer to the Scaled girl. “The Echo’s spirit had been destroyed when I entered the room, but I sensed… something when we left.”

“Hmm.” The Shaman shifted, placing his hands behind him on the log, then shrugged. “Who knows? I wouldn’t worry too much lil sis. What harm can a dead god do?” Despite his words, she could tell that he was thinking deeply on the news.

Risha snorted, but didn’t respond as the small squirrel spirit walked right up to the Red-Scaled girl. Words were said, and then The Ancient Shaman tapped the ground with his foot. Runes spread around the Spirit and the girl.

The squirrel spirit flowed into Ava, their bond complete.

Risha smiled and clapped, drawing the attention of the embarrassed girl. Jumping off the log, Risha walked over to the girl, stopping a few feet away. “How is it?”

Ava frowned with concentration, “I can sense him. Skeorus.”

The squirrel hopped out of her, and ran along her arm until it stood on her shoulder. It met Risha’s eyes and chittered at her.

“He says hi.” Ava shuffled her feet.

“Hello, little spirit. I am Risha.” Risha bowed her head in greeting, but the squirrel was already running towards Ava’s sharp tail.

Risha grinned at the cheerful freedom of the spirit. It resonated with her.

“Who were you speaking with?” Ava looked past Risha to the Ancient Shaman some steps back.

“My teacher.” Risha motioned for the girl to follow her back to the clearing. “Despite how he looks, he’s quite smart.”

“I heard that.” The Shaman stepped up beside Risha with his eyes narrowed as they walked through the underbrush.

Risha leaned closer to Ava, “He’s also really old. Risha’s amazed he can hear anything.”

Ava giggled, then looked at the Ancient Shaman with embarrassment.

The Old Brother, First Servant of the Sparks, Ancient Shaman of the Spirits, spluttered at Risha’s words.

Risha ducked under the whack of a staff, then leapt away as he tried to hit her again. She spun around and stuck her tongue at the Shaman.

He narrowed his eyes, and Risha felt the barest hint of his powerful presence,

Risha smiled despite the sudden pressure, “Risha has a lot of experience dodging Old Goblin’s sticks.”

“Tya has trained you too well.” The Shaman grumbled.

Ava laughed, then covered her mouth.

Risha and The Shaman shared wide grins.

Soon enough they reached the clearing where the other two apprentices waited. Zahra the White had a spirit snake lazily resting around her neck, while Kian the Orange lounged against the side of a large brown spirit bear.

Both of the Scaled leapt up in excitement and hurried over to Ava, asking her about the small squirrel that now rested on top of her head.

“They are quite-” The Shaman paused mid sentence, his gaze moving to the distance with a frown. Runes spread around him, drawing the attention of everyone in the clearing, “I’ve got to go.” With a flash of light, the Shaman disappeared.

Risha stared at the spot he’d been standing with a frown. That was unlike him.

“That ends our lessons too. Time to go back.” Risha’s words were hard, worry making her voice tight.

The spirits flowed into the three Scaled, then as one, they bowed to Risha. Risha gave a warm smile as the three of them left the spirit realm.

Nyx hopped from Risha, landing on the soft grass in her larger panther form. She stared into the distance, the same direction the Ancient Shaman had looked before he disappeared.

“Do you sense something?” Risha asked.

Nyx nodded. “A void god moved.”

Risha followed the gaze, expanding her spirit sense as best she could. Something was happening, but she didn’t know enough to understand what. “What does that mean?”

“I don’t know.” Nyx’s voice carried a hint of frustration in it. “They have not moved in a long time.”

“Is there anything I should do?”

“No. This is something you must leave to the Shaman.” Nyx jumped back into Risha.

Risha looked one last time towards the distance, then stepped out of the Spirit World.

Risha looked out the window of her room in the inn, watching the people in the street of Freerala as they passed by. Kliks played with her hair, weaving it like she would her webs.

“It’s a nice room you’ve got here.”

Risha turned to the Demon who sat on her bed without a care. Damien smiled at her, and gave a little wave.

Risha tilted her head, “What’re you doing here?”

“I wanted to watch.”

“Watch what?”

“You’ll see, they’re almost here.”

Risha opened her mouth, then paused as a knock came from her door.

A kobold peaked into the room, and bowed his head. “Mother, a messenger is here to visit you.”

Risha looked at Damien, but he just smiled as he pulled his hood up, hiding his face.

She turned back to the kobold, “Let them in.”

A short woman walked into the room, her muscles bulging through her clothes. A soft metallic sheen reflected off of her pale skin, and her hair was carefully braided. She wore well-fitting clothes of a material that Risha didn’t know that wrapped around her arms in spirals. It looked heavier than Risha’s own simple spidersilk and fur clothing, but was clearly styled.

The Dwarven woman stopped in the doorway, and bowed her head, “Am I speaking to the Lady in charge of the monsters here?”

“I am Risha.”

The dwarf pulled a small envelope from her jacket pocket, then bowed as she presented it. “Lord Einar invites you to a warm celebration in his home.”

Risha rose from her spot by the window, and walked over to the Dwarven woman. SHe took the letter. The envelope was made of soft and smooth paper which felt strange in Risha’s callused fingers.

Risha opened the letter, only to find letters she didn’t know. She held it out to Damien, and the Demon stood with a flourish.

He took the letter, then cleared his throat.

“To the esteemed lady from the nation of monsters,” Damien’s voice gained a haughtiness to it that was strange for the trickster.

Risha glanced at the Demon, and he smiled, his teeth showing from the shadows of his hood.

“Please join me to celebrate yet another year of peace and prosperity as an honored guest. If you choose to accept this invitation, let my esteemed messenger know. Signed, Your honored Lord Einar.”

The demon folded the letter back up and handed it back to Risha.

Risha looked at the soft paper, then up into the shadowed face of the Demon. He didn’t hint at how she should respond, but she felt that her response was important.

She turned to the waiting dwarf, “Risha would love to go.”

The Dwarven woman bowed lower, “You honor us, Lady Risha. Who will you choose to accompany you?” She looked at Damien worriedly.

The demon placed his hand over his heart as he bowed back to the dwarf, “No need to worry, little dwarf. I am but a humble friend of Lady Risha’s. I wouldn’t dare crash a noble party.”

Risha looked between the two, “You don’t want him to go?”

The Dwarven woman hurriedly looked away, “I meant no disrespect, Lady Risha. It’s simply been a… long time since a demon was invited to the Lord’s home.”

“As a guest.” Damien added.

The Dwarven woman kept her gaze on the floor.

“What does that mean?” Risha asked.

“Oh, demon servants pass through all the time, I’m sure.” Damien let out a long sigh as he sat back down on Risha’s bed. “It’s for the best I don’t go.”

“What about Siyah?” Risha asked.

“Now that, that would be hilarious.” Damien gave a humorless chuckle, “But are you ready to make that kind of statement to this Dwarven Lord?”

“I think I’d like to.” Risha turned to the bowing Dwarven woman. “Demoness Siyah, my trusted friend will be accompanying me.”

The Dwarven woman rose from her bow, “Thank you, Lady Risha. I will inform Lord Einar of your acceptance.” With that said, the messenger left the room.

“I didn’t realize dwarves were so formal.” Risha looked at Damien.

“You should meet their Old Brother.” Damien shook his head as he walked towards the exit, “I’ll leave to let you get ready for your party.”

Risha watched him as he closed the door, then stayed staring for several more seconds. She let out a sigh, then opened the door and looked at the Kobold standing guard outside of it. “Let Siyah know that she’ll be coming with me to the Dwarf Lord’s party tonight.”

The kobold bowed, then took off down the corner.

Risha returned to her room, then laid down on her bed. She was already in silks good for dancing, so she felt no need to change for the Dwarven party.

She hoped the fire would be large, and the drums loud. It’d been too long since she’d been at a good party.

Hmm. Maybe she should bring some delicious drink and a set of tiles.

That sounded like a good idea. Also, she would tell Mist-Prowler of her plans. The Coyote would find a way into the castle, even if she weren’t on the guest list, and considering the warning Maya had given her, she’d want her allies ready for anything.

Risha pushed herself off of the bed. Klu and Klaz’zks should know as well, just in case the day ended up venomous.

Risha found both of her Chosen in Klu’s room. A messenger was sent to grab Mist-Prowler, and the four of them began their plans for the evening.

Risha had a strong feeling that this invitation was anything but simple. Damien’s presence during her meeting with the dwarven messenger confirmed that feeling.

Siyah arrived halfway through their planning. She wore a beautiful dress, but Risha worried that it would be hard to dance in.

After some observation, Risha was surprised to see the demoness was moving rather comfortably in the tight-looking dress, so Risha didn’t say anything.

Soon enough, it approached the time for them to leave.

A carriage pulled up to the inn. Several Demon servants pulling the carriage greeted Risha and Siyah with smiles they did their best to hide. Risha thought they were surprisingly happy to be carrying them to the Dwarf’s home, but Siyah had a cheering effect on her own kind that Risha could appreciate.

Risha shared one last look with Mist-Prowler, then hopped into the carriage, followed by a much more graceful Siyah.
