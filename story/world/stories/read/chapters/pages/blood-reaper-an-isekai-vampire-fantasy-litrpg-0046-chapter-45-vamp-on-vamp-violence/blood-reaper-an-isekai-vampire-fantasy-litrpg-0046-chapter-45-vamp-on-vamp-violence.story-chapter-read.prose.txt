Plotting his next move, no doubt, and figuring out how to use the team to get what he wanted.

"She… she was just…” Marcus cleared his throat, gagging once as he tried to keep another surge of vomit at bay. His shoulders heaved with silent sobs he couldn't quite contain. “We were just talking about spreadsheets yesterday. Fucking spreadsheets.”

Silent as ever, even in grief.

Meanwhile, Tara's medical training abandoned her completely. Her hands trembled so violently she had to clench them into fists. She opened her mouth to speak, but nothing came out. She slumped to the ground as the full weight of the truth crushed her. When words failed, she held her face in her hands and sobbed. With her HP and SM at near-zero, she couldn’t heal anyone for now—even if there had been something left of Rachel to save.

For all their supernatural power, in that moment they were achingly, devastatingly human—united in the universal experience of loss.

Of death.

Victor, meanwhile, said absolutely nothing—he stood apart from the others, still inspecting his nails, and hooked his whip on his belt.

The contrast between Victor and the team made Ace's stomach churn.

The perpetual twilight of the Barren Highlands cast long shadows across the aftermath of their battle with the Thornhide Behemoth. Blood and sizzling bark still stained the trampled grass as the last of the Behemoth dissolved into acid and smoke. The bitter, coppery tang of vampire blood mingled with the Behemoth's putrid sap, created a nauseating cocktail that coated the back of Ace's throat with each breath. Flies already buzzed around congealing puddles of monster goop that caked the fallen leaves at the edge of the forest. The monster's horns—the only part of it that remained intact—still dripped with a venom that hissed when it touched the trampled, blood-soaked grass.

Victor had used the team as bait, and Ace wanted nothing more than to punch the motherfucker in the face.

In fact, Ace burned with anger. It consumed him, flooding his veins and clouding his mind with each passing second. Victor had gambled with his team’s lives, and Ace wasn’t about to let that slide.

The game had changed. They both knew it—and Victor had become a liability.

How much longer can I afford to keep him around?

The thought surfaced with cold indifference. To Ace's surprise, he felt nothing at the idea of cutting the man loose.

That was it, then.

Decision made.

Ace quickly closed the gap between him and the former merc. He walked with measured silence, keeping to Victor’s blind spot as he approached. The man mumbled to himself as Ace drew closer, and Ace caught something about cutting loose ties.

Through it all, Victor remained oblivious, still staring into the darkened trees, his fingers idly tapping against the handle of his whip. His posture revealed nothing but casual indifference, as if the team's suffering meant less than the dirt beneath his boots.

Five feet to go, and closing in fast.

Victor’s body suddenly tensed—a sudden awareness, maybe or, an animal instinct sensing danger.

Too late.

Ace grabbed Victor’s shoulder and spun the bastard around to face him. With no warning, Ace punched the bastard in the face. His fist connected with Victor’s jaw, followed by the crunch of fracturing bones.

Painful.

Victor's head snapped back, and his body slammed against the nearest tree. His feet left the ground as the force lifted him into the air and shot him backward. Blood sprayed from his mouth in a fine crimson mist. His eyes widened with genuine shock in the second before gravity kicked in, and he collapsed onto the forest floor in a heap of blood and wounded pride.

Ace loomed over the man as Victor glared up at him in hatred.

“You don’t use my team as bait,” Ace seethed.

With a lightning-quick strike, Ace landed another blow to Victor’s face. This time, the bastard’s nose cracked beneath Ace’s fist, and the man’s head snapped back once more. Victor stumbled, but he recovered before he could fall to the ground again. He raised his hands to guard his face.

“You’re going to regret that,” Victor said through clenched teeth. “You’re dead, Blackwell, and once you’re down, I’ll kill the others. I could use some vampire EXP Solutes.”

Oh, hell no.

With that, something in Ace snapped—the final thread of restraint, perhaps, worn thin by so many days of tension.

Fine.

No more holding back.

Victor's cruel smile widened as he sensed the change.

"Finally," he whispered, wiping blood from his split lip. "Show me what you can really do, Blackwell."

In a blur too fast to track, Victor grabbed his whip. The weapon unfurled like a living shadow, rippling and twisting of its own accord. It left trails of darkness that lingered in the air like wounds in reality itself.

Tara shouted something, but the words were distant. Muted. Lost beneath the ringing in Ace's ears. Her voice dissolved into nothing more than white noise, swallowed by the sudden shift in Ace's perception. The world around him narrowed as he focused on the threat, nearby colors sharpening while peripheral details faded. His breathing regulated automatically. His body tensed for the coming fight, and he raised his hands to guard his face.

Combat clarity—the mental refuge where emotions became fuel rather than distractions. The space where thought and action merged. He'd experienced it in every firefight he’d ever been in. He’d relied on it in every close-quarters combat situation with insurgents. In all of those moments where survival demanded perfect focus.

His senses heightened. He could hear Victor's breaths. Smell the adrenaline rolling off him. See the man’s subtle tells before each strike.

The world shrank to a single point of focus as Ace processed the threat. Victor. The whip. The danger to his team.

Options for what to do next flickered through his mind.

Duskblades were more reliable to summon—especially since he’d never pulled his Crimson Sword out of his Abyssal Armory—but against Victor's reach and that deadly whip, the daggers would put him at a disadvantage. The Crimson Sword, meanwhile, would give him equal range and more power. From the first time he’d held it, the obsidian broadsword had wanted blood. It wanted to be used.

So be it, then.

Before the sergeant could try to reach into his Abyssal Armory, Victor struck. The former merc’s arm extended in a fluid motion, the black whip uncoiling like a living serpent. The weapon shifted mid-air, its form solidifying into something deadlier—more blade than lash.

Ace ducked to the left as the whip sliced through the space where his chest had been. The metal-capped tip of the weapon snapped against the air, and a shockwave shot out in all directions. It buffeted Ace’s shoulder like a gust of hurricane wind. When the shockwave hit a rock embedded in the ground, a thin crack shot across the stone.

All from just the shockwave—and Ace didn’t want to know what would happen if it hit him.

"I’m going to enjoy this," Victor said.

Ace didn’t reply. In a fight, words were a waste of energy.

Victor flourished his whip-blade again, and the weapon sang through the air. This time, he swept it low, trying to catch Ace's legs. Ace jumped over it, but he miscalculated the weapon’s speed. The edge caught his thigh, slicing through a gap in his Thornhide Armor.

This time, he put a little more distance between him and Victor. The wound wasn't deep, but enough blood still poured from it to stain his new boots.

He needed a weapon—and the Crimson Sword was his best bet.

Ace reached into the Shadow Realm, stretching his consciousness through the dimensional barrier that separated reality from his Abyssal Armory. All he had to go on were memory and instinct as he extended his will into the void. The Crimson Sword existed somewhere in that darkness, waiting.

There—he felt a pulse of its ancient power, but the damn thing fought him.

He wasn’t fast enough.

Time for a new plan.
