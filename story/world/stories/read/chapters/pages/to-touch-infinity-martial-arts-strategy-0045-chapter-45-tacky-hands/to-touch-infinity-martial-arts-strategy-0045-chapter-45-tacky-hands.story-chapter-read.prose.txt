Compared to the loud, distracting noises of the battlefield, the forest was almost eerily quiet, even with a battalion moving through it. The soft layer of leaves resting atop the dirt path partially muffled the noise of their movement, turning the sound of thudding boots into a series of omnipresent crinkles. In place of screams, the air was full of the soft rustle of tired arms brushing vegetation aside.

Mu-cheon pushed his way down the freshly hacked escape path, quickly catching up to the back of the Tai-shan column. As far as he could see in front of him, exhausted men in oxblood silk trudged forwards, slowly grinding the layer of leaves on the forest floor to dust.

With the slower pace came time to think, but doing so did Mu-cheon no favors. Every time he blinked, he saw the jade silk of Chae-ha’s robes blooming with crimson. A spot of blood that may well have been hers was still drying on his palm, remaining tacky enough that it caused his fingers to stick whenever he made a fist.

But Chae-ha wasn’t his only worry. The young man kept casting anxious glances over his shoulder, peering into the gloom for any sign of Elder Eom. The old man had stayed behind with the hostage, but had promised to quickly follow. Where was he?

And then there was Commander Mok. Despite their differences, he was still a Tai-shan officer. Mu-cheon couldn’t help but wonder if the Vanguard Commander would be able to retreat safely, perhaps into the mountains, or if he and his strike team were just a further addition to the day’s list of casualties.

As they continued walking, the rough, uneven terrain finally improved a bit when the newly cut path eventually merged into the slightly wider, more established hunting trails. But although the ground grew firmer, their pace hardly improved. The trail was completely overloaded with retreating soldiers, who had to filter out of the way before any further progress could be made. Their escape felt agonizingly slow.

“There you are, Young Master.”

Mu-cheon whipped his head around. Emerging from the brush behind him, looking more haggard than the young man had ever seen him, was Eom Tae-seok.

The veteran general was breathing heavily. The entire left side of his face was covered with raw burns, and his left eye was completely swollen shut. A fresh, ugly puncture wound dripped blood steadily down his left shoulder. The man was leaning heavily on his Cham-ma-do, using it like it was a walking stick, but…he was alive.

“Elder!” Mu-cheon exclaimed, one weight at least lifted from his shoulders. “You made it.”

“Barely,” Tae-seok grunted, limping forwards to close the distance. “We don’t have time to stand around. The strategist got away. The Meng-shan are coming, and they aren’t far behind.”

Mu-cheon looked ahead at the line of their own troops blocking their path. “We can’t go any faster. The men are exhausted, and the trail is too narrow.”

Tae-seok grimaced, his one good eye scanning the dense canopy above. “This forest might be a terrible place for a bunch of spearmen to fight, but it’s not exactly ideal for us, either. We need to avoid conflict if we can.”

As if on cue, the sound of a new set of footsteps crunching through the twigs and leaves drifted through the woods. Mu-cheon peered back down the trail, through the twisting trunks, and felt his heart drop into the pit of his stomach when his eye caught flashes of vibrant purple silk in the distance.

“They’re almost here,” Tae-seok hissed, forcing himself to stand upright and shifting his grip on his oversized blade. “Go on ahead, Young Master. I’ll…hold the rear.”

Mu-cheon looked at the battered old man, who could barely stand, and felt a sudden, profound sense of clarity. He wasn’t going to let anyone else sacrifice themselves for him today.

The young man closed his eyes. An image of Chae-ha flashed into his mind. He saw her standing in the freezing rain, pointing a slender finger at a cluster of towering pines. “Have you ever considered a career as a lumberjack?” she’d said.

Mu-cheon opened his eyes. “No, Elder. You go on ahead.”

Before the man could argue, Mu-cheon stepped past him and into the center of the trail. He turned and faced a thick, ancient oak tree growing precariously close to the edge of the path. It was wide enough he couldn’t put his arms around it.

The Young Master drew his Dae-jik-do. He sank into his stance, took a deep breath, and engaged the Chuk-ap-gyeol.

“Gye-san-byeok,” Mu-cheon roared, his voice shaking leaves from the branches above his head. “Mountain-Rending Cleave!”

Erratic distortions in the air developed along the flats of his blade as Mu-cheon infused the weapon with sundering Ki. The edge of his greatsword sheared a clean diagonal line straight through the ancient oak, blasting bits of bark, sap, and wood pulp out into the forest. The tree was too thick for his sword to reach all the way through, so he struck again. And again. His final, felling blow landed right as their pursuers burst into view.

A loud crack sounded as the thin sliver of wood still holding the tree together ripped apart under its own weight. The Young Master scrambled out of the way as the body of the giant oak crashed down across the hunting trail, its colossal trunk slamming into the earth and throwing up a cloud of wet dirt and splintered debris. Torn branches and displaced leaves from the canopy rained down everywhere.

An impassable wall of timber now stood between the fleeing Tai-shan and their pursuers. Through the tangled branches, Mu-cheon could see the Meng-shan spearmen skid to a halt. They cursed and shouted, a few attempting to clamber over the massive trunk, only to realize that navigating the sheer volume of sharp branches and dense wood with their long spears was effectively impossible.

Exhausted, the young man sheathed his Dae-jik-do and turned his back to the roadblock. If the dense forest had been hard to pass through before, it was all but impassable now. With Elder Eom leaning heavily on him, Mu-cheon resumed the long trek down the hunting trail and back to the road. As he did, his mind kept returning to Chae-ha.

When the young man finally broke out of the forest and stepped onto the main thoroughfare, a surprisingly messy scene awaited him. People were everywhere. Some were guarding the entrance to the hunting trails, some were milling around, some were nursing injuries, and some just seemed to be sitting there in shock. To his relief, Lieutenant Shin was safe, running around and calling out orders. Unfortunately, he seemed to be struggling to organize the war-weary column for the long march back home.

Mu-cheon ignored all of it. His eyes scanned the edges of the road, glossing over their recovered supply wagons to eventually find what he was looking for: a designated triage area where badly injured soldiers had been laid out on simple wooden pallets. Members of the medical team were frantically buzzing around the wounded, their hands stained with blood. One came up to them to help take care of Elder Eom.

After handing the retired general over for medical treatment, Mu-cheon strode through the rows of wounded soldiers, his heart in his throat. He was scanning the muddy cots for anything that looked like jade silk.

He found her near the back.

Chae-ha lay worryingly still on a cheap pallet. Her oversized scholar’s robes had been hastily cut away around her left side, exposing a thick lattice of white bandages that were already stained with blood. Her face was ashen, her breathing was shallow, and her eyes remained closed.

A member of the medical squad was kneeling beside her, feeling her pulse. The man began to stand up, and without even thinking about what he was doing, Mu-cheon clamped two muscular hands onto the man’s collar and pulled him up towards his face.

“Will she survive?” Mu-cheon demanded, his grip and his tone both far more aggressive than he had intended.

The medic swallowed hard, staring up at the blood-soaked giant in terror. “I…I hope so, Young Master. She’s lost a lot of blood, and the risk of infection is high, but...” the medic gestured weakly to the rows of dying men around them. “Her odds are a lot better than some of the others here.”

Finally realizing what he was doing, Mu-cheon released the man’s tunic. His cheeks turned red with embarrassment. “Forgive me. I’m just...”

The medic nodded quickly and scrambled away.

Mu-cheon slowly sank to the ground beside Chae-ha’s pallet. He stared at her peaceful, sleeping face, so at odds with the horrors of the battlefield they’d only recently escaped. The young man reached out, his calloused fingers hovering slightly above her cheek, afraid that if he touched her, she might disappear.

“What do I do now, little sister?” he whispered into the din of the camp, fully aware she couldn’t hear him. “I made a mess of everything.”

Despite the fact that she was unconscious, he could almost hear her reply.

“Look around you, senior brother. The men are terrified and defeated. They need something to motivate them. Something to rally around.”

“Yes,” Mu-cheon whispered back to her. “I know. I feel just like them.”

“It’s because you’re like them that they admire you. Raise your voice, senior brother. If we’re going to win this war, the men need a hero more than they need a drinking partner.”

Mu-cheon closed his eyes and let out a long, shaky breath. She was right. She was always right.

The Young Master stood up, his towering form casting a shadow over her pallet. He looked down the road, where Lieutenant Shin was still struggling to get the column organized. Then he looked down the row of injured soldiers and at the medic, who was still glancing nervously in his direction.

“Do whatever you need to do to save her,” Mu-cheon ordered, his voice steadying, his shoulders straightening. Guilt could wait. For now, it was time to assume the mantle of his birthright. Without looking back, the Young Master turned away from the triage area and marched out into the center of the road. He took a deep breath to calm himself.

It was time once again to be the man his soldiers needed him to be.

Mu-cheon strode back down the road, towards the front of the disorganized throng, where Lieutenant Shin was hoarsely shouting at a group of soldiers. The Young Master stepped up beside the haggard officer and rested a reassuring hand on his shoulder.

“Save your voice, Lieutenant. Let me help.”

Mu-cheon stepped past him, turning to face the sprawling mass of exhausted, defeated men. He drew in a huge breath, letting the voice of the Great Bell ring out.

“WARRIORS OF MT. TAI, LIFT YOUR HEADS!”

The sound of his voice echoed off the surrounding trees, silencing the murmurs of the camp. Every head turned to look at the towering heir.

“YES, WE LOST A ROAD TODAY! BUT THE MENG-SHAN LOST A HIGH ELDER! SO TELL ME: WHOSE DEFEAT WAS GREATER?”

The words hung in the otherwise silent air. Virtually the entire column had retreated before Eom Tae-seok had defeated the High Elder, so few of them knew. A ripple of shock passed through the ranks, rapidly dissolving into excited, disbelieving chatter. The Meng-shan High Elder was dead?

As realization swept over the camp, Mu-cheon turned and clapped Lieutenant Shin on the back, bringing the man forwards and into the center of attention. He faced the crowd again, roaring with renewed authority.

“NOW GET OFF THE GROUND, STOP MOPING, AND FALL INTO LINE! IN CASE YOU’VE ALL FORGOTTEN, WE STILL HAVE A WAR TO WIN!”

Dozens of tired men struggled back to their feet, a small spark of pride reignited inside of them. Lieutenant Shin barked orders at his sergeants, and many of the soldiers began to fall into place. The few that didn’t comply immediately received a personal visit from a very loud Young Master, after which their attitudes miraculously improved.

With Mu-cheon taking point alongside Lieutenant Shin, the revitalized column finally turned its back on the Iron Creek junction and began the grueling march back to Laiwu. Despite the presence of hundreds of men between them, the Inner Disciple felt intensely aware of Chae-ha’s location the entire time, sleeping as she was in a supply wagon at the back of the column. But as much as he wanted to look back, the Young Master kept his eyes forwards and on the road. He didn’t want the men to catch him stealing glances.

That night, shortly before the column was about to end their march for the day and set up camp, a panicked medic tore down the side of the road. Lieutenant Shin called Mu-cheon’s attention to him; the man was stumbling and tripping over rocks and gravel in a frantic effort to reach the front. The Young Master’s heart skipped a beat when he recognized the medic he’d manhandled earlier – the one he’d asked to look after Chae-ha.

When the man finally arrived, he stood there for a painfully long moment, panting heavily with his hands on his knees. Mu-cheon couldn’t help himself; his voice came out, full of dread.

“What is it? Did something happen?”

“I thought you’d want to know. It’s the girl, sir,” the medic gasped, looking up.

“She has a light fever, but…she’s awake!”
