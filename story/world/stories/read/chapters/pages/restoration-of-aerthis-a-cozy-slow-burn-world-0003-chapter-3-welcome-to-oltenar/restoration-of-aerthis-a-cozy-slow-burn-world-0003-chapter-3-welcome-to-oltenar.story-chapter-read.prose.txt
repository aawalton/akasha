The walls of the town loomed ahead, standing tall but weathered by time. Banners hung limply from the watchtowers, their once-vibrant colors dulled by wind and dust. The gate stood open, yet only a single guard was stationed there, leaning lazily against the frame.

His armor, though serviceable, bore signs of long use, the metal dulled in places.

Elynara approached, her footsteps light against the dirt path. She unconsciously slowed her pace, slight apprehension making itself known.

The guard, clearly noticing her approach from afar, gave her a once-over before raising a brow.

“You’re coming from that way? It’s not yet time for the pilgrimage.”

She tilted her head, surprised at being spoken to so directly.

“Pilgrimage?”

His eyes lingered on her briefly before he gave a small chuckle.

“You’re not from around here, are you?”

He shifted his weight, resting a hand on his hip.

“Every so often, the church sends a small group to pray at the forest altar. It's a tradition, meant to honor the goddess.”

The mention of the altar stirred a flicker of recognition. The way mana had gathered in that clearing, the way it had responded to her… it made sense that it was considered sacred.

“They actually go into the forest?”

“Only to the clearing. Even that’s enough to make some uneasy.”

He studied her a moment longer before shaking his head.

“Well, whatever your reason, welcome to Oltenar. What’s your business here?”

“Looking for work,” she answered without hesitation.

Her memories told her money was important. And despite only two days of eating the rations, she was rather eager to change her diet.

The guard snorted, clearly amused.

“You and half the town.”

There was a small pause before he continued.

“Not much variety here. If you’ve got something to sell, the Merchant Guild might be worth a stop. If you can craft, there’s the Craftsmen Guild, though they don’t take just anyone. And if you’re looking for odd jobs or to make a name for yourself, the Adventurer’s Guild is your best bet.”

He eyed her once more, the faintest trace of a smile crossing his face.

“Can’t say I would have expected someone like you to walk out of the forest and start job hunting, but stranger things have happened.”

She didn’t really know what the decidedly bored guard implied, but despite the bubbling curiosity, it felt like she really was better off not knowing.

And it wasn’t like she could act upon any such information either, she still knew far too little of this world! The best she could do right now… was to find out more.

Trusting her gut feeling, she ignored the comment, and quickly got to the point.

“And where can I find this Adventurer’s Guild?”

“Straight down the main road. Big building, can’t miss it.”

He stepped aside, motioning her through.

“Try not to get into trouble.”

Elynara gave a short nod before stepping past the gate.

The first hurdle of getting into the town passed surprisingly easily, and she could now get a good look at what it had to offer.

The town of Oltenar stretched before her, its streets lined with sturdy wooden houses and market stalls.

The aroma of fresh bread and roasting meat drifted through the air, blending with the ever-present smell of damp stone. She did not quite care for the city smells, but the notion of good food made her gulp.

People moved in small clusters, their chatter filling the air in bursts of activity, yet there were moments of silence between them; pauses that left an odd, unsettling feeling.

Her gaze swept over the townsfolk.

Most were human, dressed in simple but practical clothing. Some were moving boxes, pulling carts, or otherwise toiling away, but despite working in groups, their chatter was infrequent, their interactions muted.

And among them were figures with wolf-like ears and swishing tails. The beastkin blended seamlessly into the crowd, working, bartering, and going about their lives as naturally as any other citizen.

As she walked, small details caught her eye – stonework that had started to crumble, signs whose paint had faded to near illegibility, patches of dirt roads worn thin from years of use.

The town still held an air of liveliness, but beneath it was something quieter, something worn.

She refocused. Whatever struggles the town faced, they weren’t her concern. Not yet.

The town wasn’t a big one, and despite her slow pace and taking in the sights, she soon arrived at her destination, with the adventurer’s guild coming into view at the end of the road.

The building stood taller than most, its wooden beams thick and reinforced, a wide entrance left open to the street. The sign above bore the emblem of the guild, a shield with staff and sword crossed.

In line with what she had seen of the town so far, the paint was chipped in places, revealing the wood beneath.

A small sign of disrepair, but one present regardless.

Thinking about what she might find inside, she paused for a moment, gathering up her courage.

Pushing the gate open, she stepped inside, and was immediately met with the warm glow of lantern light. The air smelled of aged wood and parchment, with a faint undertone of something metallic – likely from weapons and armor worn by the guild’s members.

The interior was spacious, filled with wooden tables and chairs, a large quest board pinned with various notices along one wall. A few faces turned her way, but seemed to lose interest quickly – aside from one lone man drinking sullenly in the corner.

That one seemed intent on burning a hole through her, but she tried ignoring him as well as she could.

She didn’t want any trouble, not now, when she knew so little. And probably not ever, if she could help it!

Not to mention, she didn’t feel confident in case of an actual confrontation…

Moving further in, she noticed how the low murmur of conversation filled the space, adventurers discussing jobs, trades, or simply killing time.

She couldn’t really discern anything from the chatter, and as soon as she formed that thought, her destination was before her.

At the guild counter, a receptionist stood waiting.

A sharp-eyed woman with a practiced yet polite demeanor, she took one look at Elynara and gave a small, knowing smile.

“Here to register?”

Elynara nodded.

The woman tilted her head slightly, a glimmer of curiosity in her gaze.

“You are a mage, aren’t you?”

Elynara tensed for a fraction of a second before nodding again.

The woman before her seemed to know what to expect from her long before she herself became aware of it. Being on the receiving end, it felt quite intimidating.

The receptionist’s smile widened.

“We don’t get many mages. You won’t have trouble finding work.”

She pulled out a small stack of forms, setting them on the counter.

“Just fill these out, and we’ll get you sorted.”

Elynara took the stack and a pen from the receptionist’s hands, looking them over.

By all means, she should be unfamiliar with the language, much less its written form, but it seemed whatever Celestia did to fix up her soul also included making integrating into her world easier.

Not finding anything particularly egregious in the registration terms, just the usual ‘we take no responsibility’ stuff she faintly recalled being so common in her old world.

“First time registrants can either pay the registration fee, or have it taken out of their first guild-approved request pay. Simply tick off which one it will be on the last form.”

Quickly filling the forms with the required information, she handed back the papers to the waiting woman.

“So, your name is Elynara, and you’re a mage indeed. My name is Alicia, It will be a pleasure working with you in the future.”

She nodded again, not sure how to reply to the sudden enthusiasm.

And just a few minutes and some more pleasantries later she was handed a small booklet and a wooden badge, the guild’s insignia etched onto both surfaces.

The badge was simple, but as she held it in her palm, it felt increasingly grand. Like taking a small but important step forward. After a brief flip through the booklet’s pages, she looked curiously back up, an unasked question on her face.

“Ah, that is your Adventurer’s Manual, that will give you a bit of information on each rank and what you need in order to achieve them, among a few other things,” Alicia explained. “You would do well to browse through it in your spare time.”

Elynara’s eyes slightly widened with intrigue before tucking it into her satchel for safekeeping, offering her thanks.

With that done, she turned to her next concern: lodging.

Asking the receptionist about it, she quickly realized what the problem was.

“Ah, yes. The church offers meals and shelter in exchange for work or a small donation. It also serves as a library and an orphanage. If you’re looking for a place to stay, it’s a good option.”

That was all she needed to hear and after learning its location, she was once more on her way.

As it turned out, finding the church wasn’t too hard. The town was perhaps too small to get properly lost in, but she still counted it as a win!

It stood near the edge of town, its structure much simpler than the few grand cathedrals she still remembered from before.

Flowers lined the sides of the entrance, their petals swaying gently in the evening breeze, though despite being so close, their fragrance could hardly compete with the city smells.

A shame, they looked nice, and Elynara bet they smelled so too!

Returning her gaze to the entrance, she noted how the warm light spilled from within, providing a soft contrast to the cooling air outside.

It didn’t feel like that much time had passed, yet between her morning activities, the journey here, and everything she did and seen in the town since, the day just slipped by unnoticed.

She stepped through the doors, and was greeted by the scent of aged wood and candle wax. The interior was modest - wooden pews, and an altar lined with simple carvings – but it carried a quiet, welcoming atmosphere.

A man turned at her approach.

Father Alaric. The nice but intimidating receptionist lady told her about him. Or at least she hoped it was him. It would be quite embarrassing if it wasn’t!

His robes were simple, his face lined with age, but his presence was steady, and his gaze kind.

Turning to her, he studied her with quiet curiosity.

“A traveler?”

She nodded, coming closer.

“I heard the church offers shelter in exchange for work.”

His expression softened into a gentle smile.

“That we do.”

He motioned toward a side hall.

“Come, you must be hungry.”

The meal was simple – warm bread, hearty stew, and a cup of fresh water.

After days of rations, it felt almost indulgent. It didn’t stop her from eating her fill however, munching it down happily.

As they ate, Father Alaric spoke of the town, of the people who relied on the church’s aid.

“We do what we can,” he said, a hint of weariness in his voice. “Times have been harder, but kindness should never falter.”

Elynara listened, absorbing his words. There was a steadiness to him, a quiet strength.

“Is that why you didn’t ask me anything?”

He studied her face for a moment before giving an answer.

“I am in no business of asking questions. All I see is a young lady in need of help.”

He paused, a twinkle of amusement showing in his eyes.

“Besides, people tend to be more than happy to tell me what they wish after a warm meal and a full belly. Come, let me take you to your accommodations, we will speak how you can help in the morning.”

Later that evening, she sat in the church courtyard, the sky stretched wide above her, stars twinkling against the deep blue.

Her sprite hovered nearby, its glow pulsing faintly. She reached out, threading a sliver of mana toward it. It accepted it eagerly, nuzzling against her cheek in silent gratitude.

Elynara exhaled softly, a small smile playing at her lips.

She had work. A place to stay. A path forward.

For the first time since waking in this world, the future felt less uncertain.

Tomorrow, she would begin in earnest.
