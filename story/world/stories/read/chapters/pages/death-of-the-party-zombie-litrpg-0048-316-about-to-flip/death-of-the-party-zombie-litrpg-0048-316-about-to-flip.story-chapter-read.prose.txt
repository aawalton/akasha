A sequence of explosions rippled through the inside of the vessel, blowing waves of hot air and fragmented debris down the corridors.

Sally tumbled through a doorway and struck the metal wall of the warm passageway with a thump. Droplets of blood pattered across the grated floor as the zombie pushed herself to her feet. Healing threads spiraled around her shoulder, closing off where her left arm had been torn from her torso.

“Me an’ my big mouth,” she muttered, before spitting a loose tooth to the floor.

The lumbering beast in the wide chamber she had fallen from gave a disgruntled shriek. It was like a giant crab, if the main body was made of a school bus and the claws were spinning balls of razor wire.

Two of her risen zombies clawed inertly against the monster’s tough skin. Skeleton Key had cut a few deep lines across the creature, but the odd arms had kept her too far away to get a decent attack in.

It wasn’t because she was lightheaded. At least half of the warm blood soaking through her was probably not hers. It couldn’t be exhaustion either. While it had definitely been a tiring day, she’d had worse. She wasn’t getting old.

Down the corridor, a flash of green light, quickly followed by yellow, caused a rumble to shake through the spaceship. Before she could finish her prior thought, Archie appeared beside her with a flash of soft blue.

He took one look at her and pulled a face. “We may have bitten off more than even you can chew.”

“I’m fine.” She grinned. “Not dead yet.”

“My main worry is that we will wear out our resources before we are able to get to the second ship.” Archie wrung his hands together as they crackled with arcs of red electricity. “Using so much power while I am still getting used to it is taking a toll.”

“What's the plan then?”

The former cat smiled. “Luckily for us, I have found the location of the bridge. If you'd like to take a shortcut, I can send you there and I'll finish up this mess you've been making.”

“Not like me to be a Karen but perhaps it is time for me to go see the manager. There might be some big red button I can push up there to help speed this process up.” Sally idly twirled her dagger in her hand. “I don't suppose you can create me a new arm, can you?”

Archie shook his head. “No, but I…” He hesitated for a second as further explosions vibrated through the vessel. “Here's a get out of jail free card if things get really bad.”

The zombie sheathed her dagger so that she could take the small item from the wizard's hand. It was a coin of light-blue energy, thick and dense. Even if she didn't fully understand what it was, she could still feel the immense power radiating from the object. Her system wouldn't tell her what exactly it was, despite trying.

“Neat,” she managed, pocketing the item with a grimace. “All right, I'm ready. Send me up to the bridge before this monster breaks through the doorway.”

Archie glanced through the opening at the monster, which was now regaining its composure. Blue light twirled around his hand as he held it out towards the zombie. “Give them hell, Sally.”

“Get away from me, you demon!” The evil wizard staggered around the roof of the stone tower as magical shapes circled his hand.

The shadow man paused, and a smile crossed his face. “I ain't no demon, but I suppose that's just a matter of perspective, isn't it?”

With a loud crack, a rod of lightning shot from the wizard's grip, struck the stranger, causing pulses of bright energy to wrap around him. Rather than bind the man or cause him any visible pain, the light of the attack slowly faded away as if the arcane energy was nullified and absorbed.

“I only wanted to use the tower as a means of communicating,” he countered.

“You destroyed all my servants and traps,” the wizard hissed.

“But I haven't killed you.”

The evil wizard stopped, and the breeze ruffled through his purple robes. He seemed confused and unsure whether to trust the stranger or not. Yet he lowered his next attack.

“I'm not here to judge your morality.” The shadowed man took a step closer. “I just wondered, since you're such a powerful wizard, if you could tell me what exactly I am.”

“Identification magic isn't exactly my speciality,” he murmured in return. “Who were you trying to communicate with? Perhaps that will point to an answer.”

The shadowed man shrugged. “Another world. I know that I will not find the one I seek here so I must leave somehow.”

“Hmm.” After a moment of silence, the wizard ran a hand through his dark beard. “Could it be that you are some kind of planar entity, perhaps? You seek the Land of Shadows, or a realm of darkness?”

“No.”

The stranger glanced back at the staircase leading into the upper part of the tower. He was pretty sure that his time with Nancy had confirmed that the person he sought was likely a human woman. Although that might be recency bias, there was also a feeling that it was part of the puzzle. Nothing the evil wizard said really hit any similar notes.

“If not a demon, are you some manner of undead or perhaps a shadow creature?”

He gave the wizard a shrug. “Can't say I'm too familiar with anything like that.” Aside from meeting a zombie and Death Knight, at least - but there was no sense sharing that.

“Poltergeists, Shades, Reavers.” The wizard tilted his head. “Uh, Nighthaunts, Night Walkers, Nightmare—although you're clearly not a horse—Wights...”

The shadowed man held up a hand, and with the faintest tearing sound, the robed man tensed up before shredding into darkened ash. The breeze took the pieces out over the battlements and out to the forest beyond. Silence fell around the area.

“Is it over?”

He turned to see Nancy's head poking up at the top of the stairwell. She looked pale, as if she had seen enough death and destruction to last a lifetime. Her eyes scoured the top of the tower to see if the evil wizard still remained.

“Pretty much.” The shadowed man scratched his stubbled chin before gesturing for the woman to join him.

“Were you able to get any useful information out of him?”

“No.”

Other than the fact that the wizard should have been a bit more cautious about who he let up into the tower. Complacency could often be deadly. Though he hadn't actually meant to kill him so soon, it was something of a knee-jerk reaction to…

One of those monster names he was prattling off. He looked down at his hand. Despite the flair of familiarity, he wasn't sure he was anything that the wizard had come up with. It just didn't settle properly. Felt… muddy and obscured. Distant?

“So, what now?”

He sighed and gave the woman a brief smile. “Now I search the stars for a road that I can travel. You can go ahead and loot the tower for anything you can sell or use.”

“It's going to be a shame to see you go.” Nancy furrowed her brow as she brushed some hair behind her ear. “I know you only arrived today, and it's not like we really know each other, but…”

“Perhaps once I complete my purpose, I'll come back to visit.” He gave her a nod and tipped his hat.

Her expression brightened a little. “That better be a promise, mister.”

“I am a man of my word, even if I'm not truly a man. “With a soft smile across his face, the shadow looked up at the sky and closed his eyes.

Sally blinked her eyes repeatedly, trying to get the bright flashes of failing electrical equipment out of her vision. The blood pouring from the cut across her forehead was also a problem, but one swiftly mended with a bit of thread, sewing the wound shut.

Assaulting the bridge had been a lofty proposition.

Not only was it a relatively enclosed space, but it was also filled with several Radochs higher level than her, plus an assortment of crew and Elite ranked cockroaches. Half of the large chamber was dismembered cockroach and zombie corpses, blood and death following her slow progress toward the main console. An electrified lance darted past her, exploding one of her zombie pals. Not that she was making this easy for them.

After attaching a severed arm and then blowing it up to summon more zombies, she had sunk into back-to-back duels.

More of a Humphrey or Theo thing, if she were honest. She could hold her own, but anything prolonged just made her want her zombies to take over. Her small squad of undead hadn’t fared well against the powerful Radochs. Even though her minion scaling was through the figurative roof, her pals just didn’t stand up to the top-tier aliens.

Or, at least, the best this spaceship had to offer.

A gust of energy lifted her up off the floor. Twisting, she hit the metallic wall with her feet, stepping backward quickly to front flip. Rolling away from the follow-up attack, she growled as sparks rained down from where the lance had pierced the wall. Electrical discharge crackled through the air, causing her hair to stand on end.

The Radoch standing before her was perhaps the most intimidating one yet. While most of the aliens didn’t rely on much in terms of armor or protective clothing, this one had his whole exoskeleton covered in what appeared to be metallic circuit boards. As the giant bug loomed over her and raised his lance once more, the circuits around his suit flickered with life, bringing a charge toward the held weapon - empowering it with a glowing electrical light.

Sally tried to crack a one-liner, but found her tongue unresponsive. In fact, most of her body was rather numb and combative. That helped with the pain, but was also a buzzkill considering she wanted to-

The lance flashed toward her, impaling her torso and lifting her into the air. Ribs cracked apart, just as an intense burning sensation flooded her body. The discharge of electricity from the long weapon crackled and faded away.

With an alien sneer, the Radoch leaned in closer, bringing the weapon toward him as if inspecting a marshmallow to see if it was burned just right.

The zombie’s head hung low as wisps of dark smoke swirled from her charred flesh.

“Arrogant wretch,” the cockroach spat. “Thorn you may be, but you are not beyond death. Your planet and everyone you know will be erased and absorbed by our fleet. We are the end of this universe. It is ours to control.”

With the flick of the giant cockroach’s long arms, Sally’s body flopped off the end of the lance and bounced limply across the metal floor. Blood ran from the open cavity in her torso, bones and fried organs exposed. The few remaining zombies on the bridge stumbled around blindly as if stunned, before the last of the Radoch crew cut them down in short order.

The Radoch commander gave the unmoving undead woman another sneer before pulling a radio from his belt. “Status report? The wizard is still loose; where is-”

A shudder and violent jerking motion ran through the ship, causing the bug to stumble slightly.

“Commander!” A voice from behind several long monitor-laden tables called. “There’s… a problem. The ship is moving.”

“I never gave the command to retreat.” Electrical energy crackled along his thick-plated armor as he tried to determine if it was the interlopers or his incompetent staff to blame.

“That’s the t-thing. We didn’t tell the ship to move, and it’s not responding to my input.”

The commander stepped toward the other Radoch to reprimand his underling, before stopping again. His clawed hands flexed and tightened on his lance. A pregnant pause hung in the air before the weight of a more important problem could leave his mandibles. “Where are we headed, navigator?”

Shakily, the vessel controller’s multifaceted eyes darted between the screens. “Directly… toward our sister ship.”

Before the Radoch could respond, a noise behind him drew his attention away. From over his wide shoulder, he watched the zombie woman slowly rise to her feet.

With her one remaining eye glowing bright red, Sally raised her shaky arm to reveal the glowing coin pressed against her thumb and finger.

“Heads or tails,” she growled, her face contorted with a manic grin.
