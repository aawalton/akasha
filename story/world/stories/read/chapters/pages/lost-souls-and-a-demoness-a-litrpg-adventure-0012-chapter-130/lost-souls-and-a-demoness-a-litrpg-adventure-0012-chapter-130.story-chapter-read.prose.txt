Jade leaned against the railing, watching the sun set over the crowded city. Tall buildings cast long shadows over narrow streets that teemed with people, and the noises of a bustling city filtered up to mingle with the stiff breeze blowing in from the lake. It was the evening after Jade found the mysterious note in her room, and after careful deliberation she had decided to see what it was about. Their group had taken the day to do some shopping, relax, and prepare themselves to make another push into the Labyrinth. Most of the day was spent exploring the markets, trying new food, and selling loot. It was fun and relaxing, and a much-needed break from the mounting pressure weighing down on them, but now it was time to get back to business.

Jade didn't know who or what the Ebon Eye was, but they clearly knew something about her. With her newfound focus on openness and trust in mind, she had taken the time to explain the situation to her companions rather than rushing off to investigate herself. While the note asked her to come alone, she hadn't been asked to refrain from telling anyone about the meeting. Whether or not that was the intention, the rest of her party was waiting one block over, also on the upper level of the city. If anything went wrong or if this mysterious group turned out to be dangerous, they were close enough to help.

The elevated walkways of Illysport's market ward weren't as crowded as the street level thoroughfares, but there was no shortage of foot traffic. People came and went behind Jade, few even glancing her way. She watched them out of the corner of her eye, probing gently with her magical senses, searching for…

There he is.

Jade sensed him before she saw him. A figure moving purposefully through the crowd in her direction. She tensed, ready to spring into action, but the man simply slipped free from the flow of people to join her at the railing, leaning against it beside her. He was a fit, muscular man, a little on the heavy side, but with a weight that spoke of strength. His clothes were simple but well-tailored, worn in the local style with an open jacket over a loose shirt. He looked so different than how Jade remembered him that it took her several moments to recognize the man.

"You were with Lord Ledkin, on the first floor."

It was more an observation than a question. This man had been one of the nobleman's lackeys, one of the thugs to be ordered around. Except he didn't look like that now.

"Aye, unfortunately. I'm glad to be back from that trip. And between you and me, glad your group showed him up."

"Who are you?"

"Braston. I work for an organization that has had its eye on you for some time. My assignment with Verres was only temporary."

"The Ebon Eye?"

"That's right."

Jade pushed herself off the rail, crossing her arms and turning to face him. He continued looking out across the city, not returning her gaze.

"What do you want?"'

"Not going to ask who we are?"

"I don't expect that you'd tell me. It's clearly a shady group if your way of introducing yourself is leaving a letter in someone's bedroom."

"That's a fair point." Braston grunted.

"So, I'll ask again. What do you want? I don't know if you've noticed, but there's an integration going on. My people are relying on me to take care of that, so I'm a busy woman. I'm only here because the note you left mentioned an arrangement that could benefit both of us."

"You haven't told anyone here your true race, have you."

It didn't sound like a question, and Jade narrowed her eyes at the man. A quick inspection of his status window showed that he was only level 16, so he wasn't a threat to her directly. But…

"I don't recall ever telling you that, either."

"I caught a little of what happened that night. Enough to see what you were."

Jade frowned, annoyed. But too many people knew her true nature for it to be truly secret. Now that she'd reconnected with Aylin, she needed to see if she could clear Jade's presence in Illysport as she had in Estelhelm. It would be best to sort that out ahead of time, before any problems arose.

"Is that why you're here? Because of my race?"

"Not exactly. But you proved resourceful, have unique talents, and are involved with the ongoing integration. That makes you the perfect candidate for a job my organization has.""I don't have time for any side jobs." Jade rolled her eyes. "You have no idea what's really going on out there.""The demon problem? Well, this could help with that. And it wouldn't require you to go out of your way."

Jade arched an eyebrow.

"Is that so?"

"That's right. You and your friends will be going into corespaces soon. I've been told to tell you that there's a valuable material at the heart of a core. Something called a 'dimensional anchor'. Retrieve one and bring it back to us, and you'll be compensated well."

"I'm not just going to hand over some random item without knowing what it does, or what you're going to do with it. Do you think I'm an idiot?"

Braston shrugged, finally taking his eyes off the horizon to look at her.

"All I know is that it's something that can be used to interfere with the dimensional magic the demons are using."

"And how do you benefit from this?" Jade asked skeptically.

Braston looked at her again, one eyebrow raised.

"Demons invading the Labyrinth is bad for all of us. You know we live here too, right?"

Jade supposed that was a fair point, but she could sense there was more to it than that. The man's words were sound, but there was a calculation behind them. She could taste it.

"That's not all, though. Don't bother lying."

The large man shrugged again. He put his back against the railing, crossing his arms and casually leaning against it.

"Having a weapon we can use against the demons would be quite profitable for us. You, me, and our organization."

"You want to profit off the invasion?" Jade asked, not bothering to try and hide the disgust in her voice. "If you knew me at all, you'd realize I wouldn't be a part of that."

"We'd make it worth it. Not just for you, but for your whole people. Retrieve one of these dimensional anchors for us and we'll make sure all adventuring supplies in the city are sold at cost to Earth wayfarers. They won't find a better deal anywhere on the floor. And as for you, our leader would surely be happy to provide you with some appropriate C-rank gear to help you in your quests."

That caught Jade's attention. She hesitated, torn. She didn't know anything about this group, but what they were offering was tempting. Still… handing over a potentially dangerous item to an organization she knew nothing about didn't sit right with her. If they were willing to offer this much, it must be important.

"That, and we'll make sure Lord Ledkin doesn't cause any problems for you, your team, or anyone else from Earth." Braston continued, interrupting her thoughts. "He's a vindictive sort, I'd wager he's already planning something."

"Does he know I'm here?"With everything else going on, Jade had nearly forgotten that the arrogant nobleman they'd come into conflict with on the first floor was from Illysport. She cursed her own stupidity. It would have been so easy to adopt a disguise in the city, but she'd walked straight through the front gates without even thinking about it.

"Not as far as I know. But word travels quickly, and there are a lot of eyes on you Earth wayfarers."

"Great…" Jade sighed. Silence stretched between them, the bustle of the crowd filling the air around them. Finally, Jade spoke again. "I'll think about it."

She tasted the man's satisfaction as he handed her an envelope from an inside pocket of his jacket. Jade probed again for any kind of deception but couldn't find any. The thought of charming him to probe for more information crossed her mind, but no. They knew what she was, and Jade found it highly unlikely that a group with their apparent interests would send someone to meet her without some form of protection. Coercive tactics would only antagonize a group whose influence and reach she didn't know. She accepted the envelope, tucking it away in her dimensional pouch.

"That has a description of the material we're looking for. There are also a few tips on how to extract it from the Core, but my understanding is that it's not an exact science. You may have to figure it out yourself."

"Why shouldn't I just give this powerful magic thing to the Mystic Order?" Jade asked. "Wouldn't they use it for the same purpose?"

"Probably." Braston grinned. "But I doubt they'd give you as good a deal, if they gave you one at all. Not an organization known for their generosity."

"Aylin has been good to us."

"Miss Cleska isn't representative of the rest of the council. When I heard the expedition to the first floor was going to have a Mystic Order representative coming with us I was sure she'd be worse than Verres. But it turned out she's one of the good ones. Anyway." Braston waved a hand dismissively. "Give it some thought. You have instructions on how to find us if you decide you want in."

"Wait." Jade said, catching him by the arm as he turned to go. "Why me? Why didn't you approach one of my friends? Or another Earth wayfarer?"

"Because you've got the best tricks up your sleeve. Anyone seeing you take that thing would have some questions. The Matron, our leader, seems to think you're best suited to handling any complications."

"Because I'm a succubus?"

"Aye, there's that. But you and your friends have achieved quite a bit in your short time here. That Stronghold in Lysara, the demons in Estelhelm, and now uncovering their greater plot."

"You're very well informed."

"All part of the trade. See you around, miss."

The large man gave her a grin, nodded slightly, then disappeared into the crowd. Jade remained for several minutes, her mind filled with troubled thoughts. This wasn't something she could figure out on her own. Fortunately, she didn't have to.
