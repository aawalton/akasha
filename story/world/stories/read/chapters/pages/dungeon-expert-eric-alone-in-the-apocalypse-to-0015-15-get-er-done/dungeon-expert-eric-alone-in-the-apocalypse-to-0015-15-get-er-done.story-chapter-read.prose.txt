Eric woke up the next day with a big stretch. For a moment, he just laid there, luxuriating in the sensation of having a bed and a mattress and even a whole room all to himself, and then, with a quiet sigh, he slung his legs out of bed and climbed to his feet. It was a beautiful new day. The sun shone, birds sang, and he hadn’t featured for even a second of last night’s episode. It wasn’t bothering him at all. Not even a little bit.

I fought a giant, visible wolf. I cast magic! It was awesome! What else do they want from me?

At least there was an invisible short order cook waiting for him downstairs. Eric checked on Cy, who was still snoozing on the bedside table, then headed downstairs alone. He was clearly doing something wrong, but what?

“There you are, bucko. Hey. What do you want for brekkies? Tell me fast, ‘cuz I’m hankerin’ for a ciggie.”

Eric almost leaped out of his skin. A very visible, rather broad man sat behind the café’s counter, wielding a spatula in one hand and twiddling a toothpick in the other. He wore a stained tank top that was struggling to contain all the man he was and a pair of old, baggy cargo pants with a few ragged spots and burn holes mixed in. At the sight of Eric, the man stood and slapped the toothpick in his mouth, then got started slathering the cooktop in oil.

“Er… Joe?” Eric guessed.

“Nah, it’s the Sugar Plum Fairy. Yeah it’s Joe. What do you want?”

Eric thought for a second, then decided to press his luck. “You do omelets?”

“Du fromage, even.”

“Then a cheese and spinach omelet, please.” Normally, he’d add some meat to it, bacon, maybe some ham or sausage, but he’d just come from gas station hell. His body screamed for fresh produce, vegetables and fruits, not more processed meat.

“That’s what I’m talking about.” Joe got to work behind the counter like he’d been a short-order cook his whole life.

For a while, they sat in silence. Eric stewed in his thoughts while Joe cracked eggs and sizzled at the grill. At last, Eric sighed. “Why didn’t I show up in yesterday’s episode? What did I do wrong?”

“You’re thinking about it all wrong, kid.”

“What do you mean?”

Joe scooped up the cooked spinach and slapped it into the cooking egg. “I know I told ya you have gold, and it’s true. That’s true. Getting to the Great Game early is money. But the thing is, there’s a lot of people out there who really like the Tutorial. And right now, pretty much the only people watching are the people who like the Tutorial. Half of ‘em don’t even watch the real Game. The other half consider it pointless without the background drama of the Tutorial.

“You’ve got ahold of a unique market right now, and there’s viewers who are probably already mainlining your stream as the only “real” stream out there. But most of the viewers who really love the Game and abhor the Tutorial are, uh, shall we say… not watching right now?”

“So what am I supposed to do?”

Joe shrugged. He folded the eggs over the veggies and cheese and slid the omelet on a plate. “I’d say play to market, but you can’t play to market right now, so don’t try. The main episode doesn’t care about you, so don’t care about it. Just lock in and focus on what you can do. Hone your craft. Your market is on its way.”

Eric sighed. “Basically, you’re telling me I’m not going to make it on the main show until I’ve already made it as a sideshow?”

“Ain’t that life?” Joe put the plate down in front of Eric. “Luckily we’ve got the subscriber system to play to. It’s where the money is, anyways. You won’t make much money from two second clips, no matter how many billions of views you get—2 times 2 billion is a lot, but that base multiplier is nothing exciting—but you’ll make bank pulling constant viewers.” He paused. “Well, unless you unicorn, but no one can count on unicorning. Unicorning is something that happens to you, not something you can achieve.”

“Unicorn…?”

“You know. Hit it in one.”

“Oh, right.” Eric pulled a fork and knife out of the plastic cup full of (he hoped) clean silverware on the countertop and took a bite. The omelet was delicious, the egg soft and fluffy and the cheese perfectly melty.

Joe shrugged, crossing his meaty arms. “It’ll take longer to find subscribers without making it on the main episode, but hey, having a dozen subscribers is still better than having a half-dozen clips on the episode and no subscribers.”

Eric nodded, his mouth full of melted cheese.

“But that’s not why I’m here.”

“It’s not?”

“Nah. I hate to break it to ya, kid, but I’m not the hand-holdy, tear-wipey type.”

Eric snorted into his eggs. “Figured that out already.”

“I came here to kick your ass in gear. Where the hell is that idea of yours, huh? If you’re gonna do a walkthrough, do a fuckin’ walkthrough! Get to it! Now is the perfect time to make mistakes and fuck up, and you’re wasting it trying to play it straight to an audience that would rather see you fuck someone else’s wife and get in a big sloppy slap fight about it!”

“That was a really extended segment last night, wasn’t it.”

Joe leaned forward and jabbed his spatula at Eric, more serious than he’d been all day. “To be fair? Great slap fight.”

“The thing is… I can’t do a walkthrough of a dungeon I haven’t scouted out first,” Eric pointed out. He’d realized it while he was walking and had a whole lot of time to poke holes in his previously-awesome idea. “The flaw in the concept is that I have to run a dungeon twice, and I couldn’t redo that last one because it leveled up immediately. So I’ll have to run one of these dungeons, then immediately turn around and do it again. Maybe even do it two or three times to really get a feel for it. Will anyone watch that? I mean, it’s boring, right? Just me doing the same thing over and over again.”

“Will anyone watch that,” Joe mocked in an unfairly high-pitched rendition of Eric’s voice that was otherwise startlingly accurate. “I don’t know! You figure it out. That’s your job. But you’ll never know if you sit on your ass and do fuck-all about it!”

“That’s… fair,” Eric allowed. He took a deep breath. He’d been afraid of trying it because he was afraid it wouldn’t work. But wasn’t the worst thing he could do, to do nothing and fear failure? Rather, he should try it. If it didn’t work, then he was free to either tweak it until it worked or move on to the next thing. If he just sat here in fear of getting it wrong, he’d never learn enough to figure out how to do it right… or do the right thing, if it was completely wrong.

He pushed upright. “Thanks, Joe. I’m going to get to it.”

“That’s what I want to hear. Don’t forget your passenger.”

Eric paused at the stairs. He looked back. “You’re the regional admin, right?”

“Sure am.”

“And you find Cy funny.”

“Guilty as charged.”

“You didn’t happen to put Cy near me deliberately so I’d pick him as my familiar, did you?”

Joe put his hands up. “Sometimes I do things. Sometimes fate works in magnificent ways. Look, I’m not going to say I wasn’t cheering for it once I realized my favorite rabbit was nearby, but I didn’t spawn him there. I don’t keep so close an eye on him that I know exactly where he’s going to spawn in a baby Game that shouldn’t even have any Players yet. Hell, he was there before you picked Mage, so why would I have bothered, you know?”

Eric squinted at Joe, still a little suspicious despite Joe’s words. Joe had his own ulterior motives, and he usually wasn’t telling Eric the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth… but he seemed pretty earnest right now. He shrugged, turning to the stairs. It didn’t matter either way, really. He just couldn’t help being suspicious of his good fortune in finding Cy in a game that had otherwise been unmitigatedly brutal.

Sometimes, good things happen. They’re allowed to happen. Stop fixating on the only unusually good thing to happen to you so far this whole Game.

Eric trooped back upstairs, roused Cy from his doze, and headed back out onto the porch. Wispy, early-morning mist clung to the stairs and the distant trees, but it hadn’t yet built up to the full head of thick fog he’d contended with yesterday evening. Still standing on the porch, technically within the span of the safe zone, he checked his map. There were dungeons in all directions, but the closest one was just to the right, in the opposite direction of the door. He shrugged. I’ll go scout it out. If it’s too dangerous or complex, I’ll just go check out another one. There were certainly plenty of dungeons around here.

Heat climbed as he drew closer to the dungeon, and red light reflected off the mist. The mist itself burned away, and the trees blackened. He drew to a halt as the ground melted away into bubbling hot lava on either side of the path ahead of him. Ahead, a stone gate barred his way further.

First to find dungeon! Reward granted.

Quest granted: Defeat Lavafire Dungeon.

Eric eyed the lava. Even from where he stood, the heat beat against his face like he’d just opened the oven. Forget monsters; one wrong step, and he was beyond done for. He licked his lips. You know what? I’m going to try another dungeon.

There was another dungeon immediately to the other side of the safe zone, closer to the door, down a narrow path that he hadn’t noticed branching off the main path in the thick fog last afternoon. He wandered down it, keeping his eyes open for any signs of horrific environmental dangers or whatever monster had followed after him the previous day, but it was relatively normal. Just ordinary forest around him. The mist remained thin, too, and he didn’t hear so much as a whisper of a second set of footsteps.

The entrance to the dungeon faded out of the mist as he approached: a low wooden gate opening in a low slatted fence, like a garden gate in a house’s decorative fence. He approached it and fiddled with the gate a little, swinging it on its loose latch just far enough to hear its rusty, lichen-clad hinges creak. The big gate at the entrance, the stone gate in the lava area, and now this? I’m sensing a theme.

First to find dungeon! Reward granted.

Quest granted: Defeat No Mess Dungeon.

Alright. Let’s do this intentionally. He stepped back and closed his eyes, settling his mind. Right now, I’m just scouting. I get a feeling for as much of the dungeon as I can. If I have to kill, I have to kill, but that’s not my objective. In fact, better if I don’t, because that way I don’t have to wait for it to reset. If it came to his life or a monster’s, he wouldn’t hesitate, but right now, he was pure stealth.

He touched his jacket. He’d been impressed with it from the start, but it really was his ideal equipment, wasn’t it? It hid him from monsters who saw mana, and if he managed to upgrade it, it could even turn invisible. That kind of stealth was essential to his goal of doing walkthroughs; for one, it meant there’d only be one set of clips of him actually doing the dungeon, and two, it gave him the opportunity to slip around and sneak past monsters without killing them. Right now he still had to be careful, since it didn’t make him invisible, but in the future, it would, and then it’d be truly invaluable. It was something to look forward to.

Of course, that’s assuming this works out and gets me an audience and all that. Eric swallowed, then shook his head. He opened his eyes and stepped forward, unlatching the rusty garden gate.

A blast of cool, moist air rushed out. He stepped inside and instantly felt transported somewhere else. It’d looked nice from the far side of the gate, but from within, it was truly gorgeous. Lush plants sprouted from every surface. Thick moss coated the path’s stones, softening his steps. Flowers peeked out here and there, not obtrusive or too obviously planned but nonetheless clearly a part of the garden. A little fairy door was propped up against a tree, and a mushroom-cap-roofed village clustered nearby, the porcelain kitsch the kind anyone could buy from a hobby store.

It’s pretty, but where’s the catch? Eric proceeded carefully, his mind on the snakes from the last dungeon. If he didn’t see the threat, that didn’t mean there wasn’t one; it just meant he hadn’t spotted it yet. He kept his head on a swivel, sweeping his gaze over the mossy stone and thick leaf litter. “No Mess Dungeon.” Was that a hint of some sort? Did he have to cause a mess for the monsters to appear?

Cy, who’d been dozing on his shoulder up until now, perked up. “Oh, is this one of those dungeons?”

“What do you mean?” Eric whispered back.

Cy shifted his little rabbit legs, then raised a forepaw and pointed. “You’re disguising your mana signature. Try sending a little mote of mana over there, near those creepy houses.”

Creepy? The looked more chintz than creepy to him, but what did he know? He lifted his hand, then paused. “How do I, um, make a mana mote?”

“You spoonfed System babies… ugh. I’ll do this one for free, but you’d better watch and learn for next time.” Cy lifted his paw again. Eric stared, watching him closely with his Mana sight. Gold light flowed through Cy’s body at all times, collecting somewhere around his rabbit belly. As the rabbit lifted his paw, a tiny pulse of gold energy travelled from his stomach, down his arm, and floated slowly through the air toward the little houses.

Eric copied the motions, pushing mana through his body and out through his hand. It took a few tries, his mana manipulation clumsy as a new limb he’d just learned to maneuver, but he managed to push out a little mote of raw mana at last. He sent his over a little faster, and his and Cy’s motes arrived at the houses at the same time. Interestingly, his lost more mana on the travel, a tidbit Eric tucked into the back of his mind. Cy had let his float over on the wind, more or less, while Eric had intentionally pushed his. Did that take mana? It would make sense.

The motes of mana danced over the houses’ roofs, drifting back and forth with the flow of energy on the air. Cy frowned. “Hmm. Maybe I’m wrong.”

And then the houses exploded into motion.
