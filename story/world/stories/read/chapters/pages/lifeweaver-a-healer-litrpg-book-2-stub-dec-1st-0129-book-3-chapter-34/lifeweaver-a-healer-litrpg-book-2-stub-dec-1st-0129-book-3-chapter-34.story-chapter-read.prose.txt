"Nothing?" Peter asked. "You remember nothing? Not even your own name?"

"No..." Jeff said, casting about with his eyes until his gaze met Luke's. "You."

"Me?" Luke asked.

"I remember you." Jeff pointed to his head. "In here. I saw you... I think I saw you."

"Luke healed you," Dot supplied, trying to be helpful.

"Check your interface," Luke added.

Perhaps seeing his name in there would jolt a few memories back to the forefront of his mind. Jeff's brain had been all but gone, but that shouldn't matter in this case. Luke wasn't an expert on memories or the brain, or even close to one, but he remembered the introduction to neurology class, where they read that memories were stored by the strength of synaptic connections between neurons. That alone went over Luke's head by a lot, but he figured it meant that a total restoration of Jeff's brain to its pre-injured state should have meant the memories being restored along with the physical brain. Maybe the charge between synapses didn't... uh... charge? Either way, this was beyond Luke's ability to fix or even diagnose.

"It's blank."

"What is?" Dot asked.

Jeff looked over at her. "My name in the interface."

"But you do have an interface and remember how to use it?" Luke asked.

"Yeah," Jeff said, the word coming out slow, as if he wasn't sure how he knew. "No name, though, and the class is wrong."

"What do you mean?" Peter asked, waving for the doctors, except for the one Luke assumed was a neurologist, the healers, and the ice mage to leave the room. Dot stayed, ignoring the glare Peter sent her way.

Jeff sat up. "I don't remember my name or my parents. Nothing. Don't even remember my skin being black or what my hands looked like, but it's like information about my class and skills is being pumped into my mind from somewhere else."

"The system," Luke said.

Jeff nodded. "Seems about right."

"Your name is Jeff," Dot said.

Jeff didn't seem to hear her at first, because he'd removed a long, almost spear-like but blade-topped weapon from his inventory, and he moved it about, testing its weight. "I remember this."

"Please put that away, Jeff," Peter said.

Blinking, Jeff turned to look at Peter. He put the weapon back into his inventory and shook his head. "Jeff? No, that doesn't sound right."

Peter took something from a counter near the wall and held it up for Jeff to see. It was a driver's license. "See here," he said, pointing. "Jeff."

"Maybe that was my name before, but it's not anymore," not-Jeff said, staring down at his large hands. "Jeff is gone."

"So, what's your name?" Lemira asked.

A little startled, almost having forgotten she was still in there with them, Luke nodded in agreement. "Yeah, man. What do we call you?"

Not-Jeff considered the question for a moment, then nodded to himself even as he got a bewildered look in his eyes. "Elijah. I think my name is Elijah."

"Welcome back to the world of the living, Elijah," Luke said.

"I just got word from your section leader that you are no longer in the guild, Elijah," Peter said. "But there was no notification when you left."

"I don't remember ever being in a guild," Elijah said, looking around the room.

"What are you searching for, Elijah?" Lemira asked. "My name is Lemira."

Dot gave her a look, but said nothing.

"My clothes. I need to get out of here."

While he'd calmed somewhat, the hormones rushing through Elijah's system told Luke he was still far from a normal, stress-free state, and who could blame him?

Peter held out a hand, as if wanting Elijah to reach out and shake it. "I have extended an invitation to join our guild again. Accept, and we'll get you situated. I'm sure Dr. Yemre here would like to perform some tests to see if we can get an idea of what's wrong with your memories. You have no next of kin or family in your file, but I'm sure there is someone you'd like us to reach out to?"

Elijah stared at Peter's hand for a long moment, then swung his legs over the side of the bed and got out, pulling off the pulse oximeter, blood pressure cuff, and the electrodes. Several machines started letting out angry beeps, forcing the one remaining doctor in the room to rush up and turn them off.

Standing, Elijah took a hesitant step forward, and then another, finding his balance.

Peter dropped his hand. "You can re-join later. That's fine. I understand. Should I send someone to guide you back to your room?"

"I don't remember any family or friends. No one," Elijah said, irritation evident in his voice. "And I'm not joining no guild of yours."

The room fell silent while Elijah donned new clothes from his inventory, followed by what appeared to be fur-trimmed leather armor. The leather and fur were both pure white, giving him an arctic look. "What about you?" Elijah asked, turning to Luke when he was done.

"What about me?" Luke asked.

"You healed me, yeah?"

"Sure did."

"That makes you a friend in my book. Mind if I tag along?"

Luke glanced over at Peter, whose expression was blank, then shrugged. "Sure. Can't have too many friends."

"Hold on a moment," Peter said, his expression a blank mask of pleasantness as he rounded the table and walked toward Elijah.

Lemira stepped out in front of him, blocking his path without saying a word.

"You can't force the man to join," Luke said.

"That was not my intention," Peter assured them, pulling a small, round, stone-like object from his inventory before handing it over to Luke. "Two more patients to go for you, but as promised, take this. My hope is that Elijah will regain his memories and return to his rightful place at the top of ISG, but I have full understanding of why you would want some space at this time."

Having handed over the little unassuming bead to Luke, who pocketed it right away, Peter reached out his hand again, and this time Elijah took it. "Please let me know if you need anything or if your memories start coming back," Peter said.

Elijah gave a noncommittal but not unfriendly shrug before he left the room, still with a slight unsteadiness to his steps. Luke and Lemira followed.

"Bye, Luke," Dot said just before the door closed.

Luke: "Bye."

Dot: "Be careful out there."

Luke: "You too."

The trio found a guide right outside who took them back into the lobby and then ushered them outside.

Lemira drew in a deep breath and let it out through her nose in a long sigh, as if tasting the air and finding it to her liking.

"We're starting our own guild, Elijah," Luke said, unsure how else to start a conversation with the confused-looking man who looked around at the building reaching toward the sky with an unsure expression. "If you don't want to join..."

"I'll join," Elijah said.

"Well, okay. I'll talk to my sister. She'll be the one in charge."

Looking at Elijah now, he was a little older than Luke had first thought, somewhere in his thirties, if he had to guess, but standing there on the street, he gave the impression of someone much younger. Then again, from Elijah's own point of view, he wasn't even an hour old. Weird, to say the least.

"Another soul new to this world," Lemira noted.

"What?" Elijah asked.

Luke sighed. "You're not supposed to go around telling people you're not human, Lemira."

Elijah's attention shot to Luke. "What?!"

"Oops."

Lemira put a hand on Luke's shoulder. "If we're to be in the same guild, should we not trust one another?"

"Sure, I guess," Luke said, making a helpless gesture.

"What was that about not being human?" Elijah asked, narrowing his eyes.

Lemira looked to Luke for confirmation, and when he gave her the go-ahead, she explained her origins. For a brief moment, it looked like Elijah might attack her, but it passed in a breath, and in the end, he was nodding in approval, looking between Lemira and Luke.

"You're good at attracting strays," he said.

"It seems like it, doesn't it?" Luke said, ignoring some messages from random names looking for healing. Quite a few of those had been coming in the last couple of weeks, and even more since Alan’s demise, but there was no way Luke would get anything done if he ran from place to place, healing everyone who asked. "So tell me about your level and class."

"My class has changed."

"How do you know?" Luke asked. He felt it was a fair question, since the dude had total amnesia.

Elijah frowned. "I'm not sure. Don't remember what it was in the past, except that it was some sort of fighter. What I have now is different."

Luke gave him an expectant look, urging him on.

"Reborn Adept."

"Eh," Luke began, clearing his throat. "Yeah, I get what you mean with that kind of class name. What does it do?"

"Hold on," Elijah said, his eyes going vacant to show he perused the different interface menus. After a while, he nodded to himself and returned the present. "I'm still a melee fighter type class, but with some sort of light theme, I believe."

"Phew. Another darkness or shadow type would be annoying."

"Hmm?" Elijah said.

Luke waved it away. "Nevermind. What's your level?"

"Also higher than before. Twenty-three."

"Class changes are not unheard of, though they are considered rare," Lemira said.

From somewhere in the distance, the telltale rumble of something hulking and large, causing destruction, reached them. They quietened and turned to see a... Well, a giant. Sort of. It was taller than three men stacked together, but the monster was thin as a rail. Sagging gray skin made its nude form more than a little disgusting.

Luke's mouth twisted in disgust as he pointed to it. "Why don't you test out your new abilities and show us what you've got, Elijah?"

"I was just about to suggest the same thing," Elijah said, equipping the blade-topped polearm. The weapon flashed in the sun as he reached it out to the side before setting off running toward his opponent.

"What about you?" Luke asked, half-turning to Lemira without taking his eyes off the recruit.

"What about me?"

"You mentioned your class being called Core Caller, if I remember it right."

"That's right."

Luke gestured for her to go on explaining, but she just frowned, not understanding.

"How does the class work?" he asked, suppressing an exasperated sigh. "You mentioned something about using monster cores, which makes sense."

"I summon monsters from the cores, and they act as companions with classes of their own, levels, and so on."

Luke blinked. "Wait. So you're saying you can summon anything you have the core from?"

"Not yet," Lemira said with a twinkle in her eye. "Some day I might be able to ride on the back of dragons, but that day has not yet come. There are limitations."

"Like what?" Luke asked as Elijah approached the monster.

"The number of companions I can use is limited, so is the individual companion's strength and level. Some cores won't work no matter what."

"A level seventy dragon won't work?"

She shook her head. "Again, not yet."

"What about meta-hearts?"

Lemira leveled a more than a little disturbed look at him. "No."

"Just curious," he said, trying to placate her. "So what cores do you have now?"

"None."

"None? Why?"

"I told them to guard the door to the room where you had your final discussion with my father. They put up a valiant effort, but Lemina's undead scourge proved too great a challenge."

"Oh," Luke said as Elijah reached his target. "Thanks."

What followed was a demonstration of Elijah's skills. From that distance, Luke didn't catch much more than flashes of light coming from his weapon with every slash. That light tore into the monster and killed it in mere moments. The fight was over as soon as it started.

"Impressive," Lemira said.

Luke nodded. "We'll get you more cores. I have some I kept as food for Smudge. You can have those to start."

"Thank you," she said, shaking her head. "Unfortunately, I need to harvest the cores myself, or they will not work with my class."

"Ah. Once we've met up with the others, we'll make sure to hit another dungeon. There'll be plenty of people in need of leveling."

"Is your sister a lot like you?"

Luke laughed. "More than she'd like to admit."
