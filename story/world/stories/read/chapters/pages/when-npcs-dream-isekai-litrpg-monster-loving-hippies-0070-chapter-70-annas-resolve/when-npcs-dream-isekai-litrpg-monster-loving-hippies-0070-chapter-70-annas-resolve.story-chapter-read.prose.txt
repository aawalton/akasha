Seeing the blond human girl was... really unexpected.

Agents had some controlled Naked-Nulas working for them, but they were really a rarity. Probably less than five in total on the entire continent.

I looked at her, trying to recognize her...

I knew the Ranger of the North had some girl taken... but she wasn’t blond.

This girl was probably from the smaller human group at Lithurion.

“Look!” Shooya pointed excitedly. “A Soft Naked Nula!”

“Just a Naked Nula!” I corrected her.

We waited for them to gather all the prisoners on deck. Some pirates tied the prisoners' hands and kept them at sword point, while the rest of the crew went in and out of their ship carrying the stolen loot.

“Hi! Naked Nula!” the girl spotted me and shouted, jump and try to wave her bound hands. “Over here!”

I ignored her. “Shooya,” I whispered, “you need to smell them for control spores. While the Naked Nula is obviously controlled, we need to make sure that all the others are uncontrolled NPC soldiers, and not a controlled Player in disguise.”

We went over to the prisoners, and Shooya confirmed only the Naked Nula was controlled.

“Ah, I know who you are!” The blond girl beamed at me. “Geshem and Shooya, right? Poor Agents looking all over for you. Who would guess you will use a pirate ship to escape! That is really smart!”

“Poor Agents?” I repeated, raising an eyebrow.

“Well, as you know, I’m working for them,” she gave a small chuckle, “but I guess that would change in a few days…”

“Exactly so,” I said dryly.

“My name is Anna,” she said in that cheerful, brainwashed way controlled people have, then swiveled to show her tied hands behind her back. “Sorry, I can’t shake.”

“Don’t... don’t worry about it,” I said. “So what were you doing at sea?”

“We sailed to Moeveth,” she said, “to buy cloth. For outrageous prices, mind you.”

“Yes... I know...”

I narrowed my eyes at her. I was suspicious, but it was silly. She didn’t hide the fact she was controlled and working for the Agents. And she didn’t mind telling us unimportant stuff. If we asked her important military stuff, she wouldn’t say or would lie, and since we weren’t the torturer type, interrogating her was really meaningless.

“So where are you heading?” Anna asked.

“Where are you from originally?” I asked without answering her. “Lithurion? Which group?”

“Linda’s group,” she said. “Or... she started calling them the Children of the Moon, but it was a silly name, didn’t really catch on. We were located in Kenzebeth city. It’s east-north to—”

“I know where Kenzebeth is,” I cut her off. Controlled people were often too chatty.

“So there,” she said, smiling. “But it was a long time ago. I was with the Agent from the very start.”

“Oh...” I said. “And how has it been going for you?”

“Great!” she said. “I like it, I want to serve my masters! It brings me happiness.”

“I was controlled once, by Uul,” I said darkly. “I know how it is. And it brings you a cheap high at best, definitely not happiness.”

She looked confused at that. But then she just gave me her brightest smile. She was cute, I’ll give her that.

“We will do everything we could to help you,” said Shooya earnestly. “It... will be rough, in the next couple of days—of rehab, I mean—but you will get through. I know you will.”

“Thank you, Shooya,” she said. “I have heard you have a big heart.”

“Arthropods don’t have a heart,” I corrected her.

“Rude!” said Shooya. “We do have a heart! We aren't really the insects you told me about from your planet, you know.”

“Right... sorry.” Yes, I was in a bad mood. Since I discovered I was kidnapped by pirates, I mean.

Then we heard some commotion.

It seemed like the Captain was coming to the deck, and her newly reunited daughter came along with her.

All the other pirates stopped what they were doing. They stood straight, saluting in their own ragged way, respecting the Captain. The deck fell silent as the Captain walked along, surveying the new prisoners.

The Captain turned to her crew.

“Lads! Lasses!” she shouted, striking a pose. “Another victory for the Red Feather! The sea gives to those who dare take it! We fear no storm, and no enemy! Tonight, we drink to adventure!”

The pirates cheered.

What were bird-folk pirates doing with captured prisoners anyway? I had no way to know. I hoped they wouldn’t throw them from the deck. Could they sell them into slavery? Umm... come to think about it, I didn’t think there were slaves at all in this world. Probably go strictly against some ISO standard rules or something.

“The prisoners will fetch a good ransom,” said the Captain to her lieutenant.

Oh yeah, that was an option too.

I went over to the Captain; I had a few things to chat with her about.

“We are taking the Naked-Nula prisoner into our custody,” I informed her. “We helped capture the ship, after all. We deserve a reward.”

“Aye,” the Captain nodded sagely. “The spoils go to the victor. She is yours.”

“Hmm, where are we... exactly, going?” I asked.

“To Lithurion!” she declared, pointing a feathered finger at the horizon. “To sell our goods at the bustling market! To make gold, rob those land-rat fools, and be back at sea for another adventure!”

“Turn the ship!” I ordered her, putting half an effort into it. “We are going back to Raz-Rash!”

“To Lithurion!” she repeated “To sell our goods at the bustling market! To make gold, rob those land-rat fools, and be back at sea for another adventure!”

“Well, how much time until we get to Lithurion?” I asked.

“...”

Annoying!

How come, after all this time with Lori, I still ask NPC questions looking for a straight answer?

I went back to Anna and Shooya.

“Anna will be in our custody now,” I said. “But the Captain won’t turn the ship around. They are heading to Lithurion. I don’t know how much time to get there. I’m still of half a mind to gas everyone and make the Captain turn the ship.”

“You... want to return to Raz-Rash?” said Anna. “Oh right, you’re missing Lori. She is still at Raz-Rash, I’m guessing... I heard Nemm report she was injured. Is she... is she alright?”

“She will live,” I said coldly. Even though Anna was a prisoner, I didn’t like the enemy learning important information about us.

“Good,” Anna said. “I wanted to meet her so much! Uul used to talk about her, how dangerous she was, but the other Agents didn’t take him seriously. But after she killed Hash... well, she is a legend. All the Agents talk in fear about Demon Lori. She... she is not on my side, but... I still think she must be really cool.”

“Right,” I said, ignoring her and turning to Shooya. “So what do we do? Keep pressing the Captain? We must be far from Raz-Rash already. I have no clue how long it would take...”

“Probably three days,” Anna supplied.

I looked at her.

“With this ship size, they have good wind for them. So I’m guessing three days.”

“Let's say Anna is right,” I said. “Even three days will be a huge problem. Lori is injured and won’t get help, and all our missions… Miriam too. It’s too much.”

“I have a solution!” suddenly said Anna, beaming. “To your problems!”

“You... do?” I asked, not really believing it. Controlled people were annoying and I didn’t have much patience right now.

“Yes, definitely,” she said. “We had a phone in our ship. Of course, it was taken by the pirates as loot. But you can get the phone, call the Order of the Phoenix, and tell them where Lori is hiding. They have small teams sniffing around in Sin-Irsh cities and towns. They probably could get to Lori quickly and get her out.”

“Oh...” This was actually a great solution. But... “Since you suggested it, we should be suspicious. Could be a trap.”

“But what trap could it be?” asked Shooya.

“What if they found a way to alter the phone,” I started. “So a third party could listen to conversations? If this is true, the Agents will get to Lori first and kill her. Or... I don’t know, the phone can have death spore paste on it and I will die when using it…”

“Oh no,” said Anna. “Controlled or not, I’ll never do it. Master Zylil, she is ruthless but not evil, believe me, she will never resort to such tactics.”

“Ruthless but not evil, that’s a thin line you got there,” I said coldly, not convinced at all.

“I believe her,” said Shooya. “It’s harder to lie to me, with my new Compassion skill.”

“Was it hard to lie to you before?” I asked.

“Not at all!” admitted Shooya.

I gave Shooya a look.

“You are wrong in this,” she said with some newly founded confidence. “I believe her. I also knew Zylil, and met with bird-folk under her control. They were really nice, they suggested I escape Uul, told me to believe in myself.”

“I... I don’t know...” I said.

“I just said it was alright!” Shooya raised her voice. “Get the phone already!”

Now I was just stunned. I looked at Shooya with wide eyes. I never heard her talk like this before.

“I...” she murmured, shrinking back a little. “I am a pirate now!”

“Fine,” I said.

I followed one of the pirates who was carrying one of the last crates of loot from Anna’s ship until I got to a small storage space. I started to search in different chests and crates until I noticed the pirate standing and looking at me.

“I need something from here!” I said. “Got a problem with that?”

The pirate shrugged and left me alone.

It wasn’t long before I found it. A small bag with a large stone tablet and few smaller ones, along with some wooden sticks.

It looked normal. Maybe I was too paranoid sometimes. I also needed to trust Shooya’s judgment more quickly, I mentally noted to myself.

I went back to Anna and Shooya and started tweaking with the thing. It was harder to connect now because I needed to position the thing in line with a receiver, and I didn’t know exactly where I was. I also didn’t know where Felix was. I just tried to hit someone in Liliverth; they could tell me Felix’s location.

There was also the ship's constant movement; it was a real challenge. Maybe I should invest my next skill point in 'Stupid Magic Phone Operator.'

It took me half an hour, but I connected to someone from the Order, then another half hour to hit Felix.

“Hi Felix, Geshem here,” I said, exhausted.

“Geshem!” Felix all but shouted my name. “It’s great to hear your voice! We weren’t sure if you were alive. How is Shooya and Lori?”

“Shooya is fine, here with me,” I said. “But Lori got injured really bad. She is resting in a hidden place in Raz-Rash. I’ll need you to send a small team to get her as fast as you can. It’s in a small house near the broken windmill, at the first street in the neighborhood near the docks.”

“Sure,” said Felix. “We will get to her in less than three hours. What else? Where are you?”

“We are pirates now,” I sighed. “Well, at least Shooya is. We are on a pirate ship on its way to Lithurion. If you escort Lori back to Kamatouu, I have no reason to turn back. We will just keep with the pirates.”

“But you are not in any danger?”

“No,” I said. “But I didn’t mean for this to happen—be a pirate, sail in the sea and all that. Now I’m stuck here for three days, at least!”

“But how can you become a pirate and sail the sea by accident?” he asked, but then regretted it and sighed. “No, never mind, these things are normal with you. But tell me, our spy tells us you killed Hash, a dangerous Agent officer. Is it true?”

“Lori did,” I said, the memory flashing through my mind. “If it weren’t for her, I’d have died a thousand times over. Hash was unbelievably tough; I was no match for him. But Lori could—somehow—fight like nothing I’d ever seen before. Even so, she barely made it out alive.”

As I said it, my stomach twitched at the memory of her bad injury, how close she was to death, how useless I was in protecting her... I hated this.

“And we burned probably a fifth of the Cloth shipment,” I added. “But as you know, the shipment was huge, so it was a failure. They got a lot.”

“We got another two wagons of that shipment,” Felix said. “All the fire and smoke you did... we got there after some time and found two wagons headed out of town. If you add killing Hash, it wasn’t such a bad outcome.”

“That’s good,” I said. “Oh, and we came across an Agent cloth ship just an hour ago, and the pirates raided it. The Agent escaped, but we did find a controlled Naked-Nula.”

“Really?” said Felix. “That’s rare. Who?”

“Anna,” I said. “She is from Lithurion, do you know her?”

“Yes,” said Felix. “From Linda’s group. Was one of the first that got taken. It’s really good to have her back. Good work.”

“Yeah, I guess,” I said. “How... how are things at your end, war and stuff?”

“Umm... It’s actually hard to say,” Felix sounded somewhat troubled. “We always win skirmishes, so it feels like it’s going good. But it’s really not. They hire bug-folk mercenaries all the time—some NPCs, some Players—and it’s hard for us. We are fighting in Rishuan, not in Sin-Irsh.

Its also look like they are slowly advancing in Ire-Irsh. And they are starting to adapt the hit-and-run missions like we are doing; some small team assaults in the depths of Lithurion.

So in short, we are not winning, and time runs against us. They get tons of Cloth in a lot of smart ways; we are a few weeks before they have enough for the ship. They also get help from their bamboo masters all the time—new Agents each month, diamond funds, and spore weapons via the seagull birds. So, the truth is, we are doing bad.”

That sounded depressing, really.

“So what is the plan?” I asked. “What are we going to do?”

“When the pressure bubble training and the gas masks are complete, we are going to mount an attack,” he said quietly. “It will happen in a few days. An all-out attack, with all we got. Straight to their capital and large cities. We feel like this is our last chance at winning this. We will need everyone who can fight, of course. We will need you, and... if Lori gets better...”

I didn’t want Lori to fight again. But I understood why Felix said what he said. Lori had something weird going on about her strength; she maybe was our trump card. But she could very well easily die like the rest of us.

I finished the talk with Felix. Then I called the Stormcrows at Kamatouu.

I updated Juliette on everything that was going on and got Samar on the phone.

She told me they were fine, and Miriam was fine—mostly happy, but she also asked about Lori from time to time, and then became sad. Poor Miriam!

Samar tried to get Miriam on the phone, but she apparently just got angry at the stone tablet and tried to hit it with a stick.

I truly missed her.

I finished with the phone.

Now I didn’t actually know what to do next. There was really nothing important left to do.

“I would like to rest,” Anna said. “Can we get to our cabin?”

I looked at her with amazement. “You are a war prisoner. You know that, right?”

“Oh...” she said, disappointed. “It just felt like we were getting friendly and all.”

“She is temporarily a prisoner on rehab,” corrected Shooya. “And I can at least talk for myself—I would like to be your friend, Anna.”

“That’s great,” she said, smiling. “I really like you, Shooya.”

“Me too,” Shooya said happily.

“Shooya!” I said firmly. “In the next few days, she will gladly kill us and escape to her Zylil if she could. Did you forget about everything that you have been through with me!?”

“Oh, no,” she said. “Sorry, I’ll be careful. But... I want to rest too. Can you... ah... arrange a room or cabin for us?”

“Yes...” I said.

I went to some pirate and demanded a room. I had this... status over here. Like they knew I was somewhat important or strong, and if I demanded reasonable things, they would oblige.

The pirate led the way without a word, and we followed.

It was a small room with broken crates and a single wretched bed. The air was stale, and the lantern swung violently with the ship's roll.

It would be crowded with Shooya in the bed. And I think she tended to hug me in her sleep, which is extremely annoying and not comfortable at all.

I didn’t know yet what we would do with Anna at night time.

“So what would you like to do?” Shooya asked.

Do? Like, to pass the time? I guess everything major had been settled, and we just had three days of doing nothing. Oh, except fearing all the time that a controlled enemy would stab us in the back.

“I have a Quick-Fast deck on me,” I finally said. “Would you like a game?”

“Yes!” said Shooya. “This time my Princess will enslave all your troops!”

“We will see about it,” I said, starting to deal the cards.

“Ooo, I want in too!” jumped Anna. “I love learning alien card games and this looks cool!”

“You will have a hard time playing it,” I said.

“Why is that?” Anna asked.

“Because your hands are tied behind your back!” I told her. Gosh, couldn’t she act a bit more like a prisoner?

“So release me already!” Anna said.

“So you could stab us with a hidden knife!?” I snapped.

“I don’t have a hidden knife!”

“Ah... perhaps I may offer,” asked Shooya hesitantly, “a body search?”

“Fine,” I said to Shooya. “But make it really thorough.” I turned to Anna. “Shooya is an alien bug girl so it’s really not... ah, harassment.”

“I don’t mind,” said Anna.

“Yeah,” said Shooya eagerly, “I will search her every soft part.”

“Now it does sound bad!” I told Shooya. “Stop being creepy about the Naked Nula.”

“Not my fault you don’t have any natural defense,” Shooya said defensively. “Just vulnerable, soft, smooth skin, with tiny, barely visible hairs.”

“Just search her,” I said. “If she is not armed, I have confidence in sensing magic circles and retaliating fast. So I guess it will be fine to untie her. But she will sleep in the ship brig, with the other prisoners.”

“I really don’t know the first thing about magic...” said Anna.

“Please remember I don’t believe anything you say,” I told her, “until at least four days from now.”

“Three actually,” corrected Shooya. “I can sense control spores very accurately. She probably was already a day or two without Zylil. In three more days, she will be cleared. Tomorrow she is expected to have a hard time with the rehab…”

Shooya started the search, and I turned my head aside. But I still had to imagine everything going on a few inches from me, and I blushed. Gosh, at least no one could see.

After Shooya finished, she released the rope ties, and we all played Quick-Fast for hours.

I must admit, Anna caught onto the game extremely quickly and wasn’t half bad. The game also worked great with three players, and we had fun time.

When it was time to call it a day, we escorted her with a few pirates to the ship’s prison cell, where her NPC former crew stayed.

The cells looked bad and stank of bilge water and despair, but sleeping in our room with a controlled enemy who could be a mage was a really, really stupid thing to do. Shooya got it too.

Tomorrow morning we got Anna out again, but as Shooya predicted, she was noticeably less cheerful than the day before. Sometimes she had this uncontrolled monologue about how her Master needed her. But she was friendly, too, most of the time.

The day after that was worse for Anna. She screamed a lot, shivered, and once even tried to jump off the rail and swim to Zylil. We caught her at the last moment. After that, we tied her securely and confined her to the prison.

On the last day of our voyage, she was relaxed again. Shooya said that at this state, she had very little spore smell on her and could probably win against the control effect and talk as herself. But of course, we were to wait until late evening—the date and time Shooya decided was safe. Also, it was supposed to be about the time we arrived.

That evening, Anna played Quick-Fast with us again, but she was quiet and sad.

In one of her turns she suddenly stopped. Her cards fell from her hand.

“Anna, are you okay?” I asked.

She hugged her knees and started to tremble, tears falling from her eyes.

“I’m... really not,” she said in a small voice. “I didn’t... don’t... I don’t think I like them anymore! I... don’t like Zylil.”

We looked at her. I knew how hard it was, how all those conflicting emotions tore your brain from the inside. It was torture.

“I have been used,” she said. “Horribly so, for horribly a long time!”

“Yes...” I said gently. “But... not anymore.”

“You... you don’t understand,” Anna said. “How... how horrible.”

She then pulled at her scarf, revealing her pale neck. It was covered with red, angry puncture marks. Dots.

It took me a moment, but I understood what I saw.

“She... she feeds on me,” Anna confessed, her voice breaking. “Said my blood is the sweetest.”

Shooya gasped, hands flying to her mouth, and then she began to cry too.

I also noticed my eyes tearing up. Even knowing the Agents' psychopathic behavior, this was something else.

She was cattle to them. It was truly horrific.

“I’m sorry,” I said quietly. “You had to go through so much.”

Anna curled up on the floor and cried softly. Me and Shooya were truly shaken by this.

“I want to help,” Anna finally said. She sat up again, wiping her face. There was a new strength in her tone, a resolve. “I want to help beat them. I want them to lose!”

“Sure,” I said. “I will connect you with the Order and see how you can contribute. But first, you must get better.”

“Yes...” she said. “Th... Thank you.”

Anna was really something.

Even after the horror she had been through, how traumatized she probably was, she still wanted to help—to fight the Agent. It required unfathomable willpower.

At that moment, I could feel nothing but admiration for Anna.
