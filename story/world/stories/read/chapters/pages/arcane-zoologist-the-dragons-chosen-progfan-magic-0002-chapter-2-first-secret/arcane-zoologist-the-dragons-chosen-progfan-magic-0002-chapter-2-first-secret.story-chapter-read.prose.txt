When Callie woke up the next morning, there wasn't a single trace of the dragon left. Light shone down into the cave from the opening as she looked around, trying to find any sign of yesterday's encounter. But there were none.

"Was it just a dream?" She asked herself, saddened by the thought.

But as she pulled herself up to her feet, Callie felt heat on her right forearm. Looking down, she noticed a bright pink mark glowing slightly. While the feeling was unpleasant, it was more than clear that it wasn't just a regular bruise. This was magic!

Callie squealed with delight. Did this mean that she could use magic now? She extended her arm towards the ground and called, "Grow!"

Nothing happened. But Callie didn't feel discouraged at all. It would have been strange if she could do magic that easily. Mom and Dad had to train for many years to use even a little bit of magic. She just needed practice. The mark gently glowing on her forearm was all the encouragement she needed.

"Thank you, kind dragon. I hope we meet again," Callie said into the cave before she left.

She would have liked to explore the cave more and search for a rogue scale or some footprint from the dragon. But her family must be beside themselves with worry, so Callie rushed home. As it turned out, the cave where she had spent the night was not too far from the forest path where she often searched for herbs. So finding her way home was easy.

What followed afterwards, however, could be described as Callie's worst nightmare.

As it turned out, the whole village had been searching for her since late last evening. So when one of the neighbors spotted Callie coming down the forest path as if nothing at all wrong had happened, they instantly swarmed around her. There was a lot of screaming, "Callie's back!" which was followed by frantic running and more adults swarming her from all sides.

They were asking her a thousand questions, pulling on her, checking her clothes and body for injuries. Callie was all too overwhelmed to register anything that was said to her.

"ENOUGH!" Callen's clear voice rang over the swarming villagers. "You're all scaring her!"

"Brother!" Callie sniffled and threw her arms around her older brother's waist the moment he managed to push himself through the crowd.

"It's okay now," he said soothingly and picked Callie up. Although she was already quite big—she was nine after all—her big brother was very strong and could easily lift her. "I'm taking Callie home!"

More questions were thrown their direction, but Callie hid herself in her brother's arms. To think that getting lost in the woods would bring so much attention to her. Unfortunately, when they got home, Callie was instantly bombarded with questions by their parents. Mom and Dad both looked relieved when Callen brought her back. But it was short-lived.

Once they knew that Callie wasn't hurt, they got mad. Real mad. Callie had never seen Mom get that angry in her life. Dad—yes. He was known to have a rather short temper. But never Mom.

"Why didn't you return home last evening? You know better than to stay in the forest when it's dark!" Mom demanded. "And why is your tunic torn?"

Callie winced. She had hoped that Mom wouldn't notice the tunic.

"Monsters could have attacked you!" Dad growled, but slid a plate with two eggs and a potato pancake in front of Callie.

She realized just how famished she was and dug into the food instantly, ignoring the fact that Dad had called them monsters. Although angry fire was burning in Mom's and Dad's eyes, they allowed her to eat several mouthfuls before urging her to answer.

"I… I saved a magic rabbit. It was caught in a trap. I… I couldn't just leave it there to die," Callie explained meekly, shrinking deeper in the chair as Dad's gaze somehow grew even more furious.

"A magic rabbit? Do you mean to tell me that you helped a glimmerhare?" He demanded.

"I… It was bleeding badly…" Callie squeaked out. "So I tore off a piece of the tunic to m-make a bandage…"

Mom's eyebrow twitched. That wasn't a good sign. "So you freed it from the trap. Okay. You tore your tunic for it. Fiiine. But why didn't you return home? The whole village searched for you! All the paths, the stream and even beyond millefolia grove where you were supposed to be, but you were nowhere to be seen."

"I-It was dark outside. I couldn't see the path home, but the glimmerhare lit up its antlers so I followed it…"

Mom and Dad both groaned, and Dad even smacked his forehead in disbelief. Callie shrank deeper into the seat, her nose barely visible above the wooden kitchen table.

"You followed a glimmerhare? What were you thinking? They are known for leading people astray!"

"No! It led me into a cave and there—"

"You followed it into a cave?!" Mom exclaimed, clutching her head with both hands. "Child, would you also follow a stranger if he offered you candy and a ride in his wagon?"

"What? No!" Callie protested. She knew better than to take anything from strangers. You could never trust anyone outside the village.

"Then why would you follow a monster? That is even worse. You are lucky to be alive," Mom said with a heavy sigh. Only then did Callie notice large bags underneath her eyes. As if she hadn't slept at all. Looking at Dad, his eyes looked tired too.

"She's back. That's all that matters," Callen said, wrapping his arm around Callie's shoulders. "Eat, you must be hungry."

Nodding meekly, she finished the rest of the eggs and the potato pancake. While eating, Callie wondered how to tell her parents that she had met a dragon. They had such a horrible reaction to just the glimmerhare. Imagine their rage if they knew that she had come face-to-face with a dragon and drank the magic water that it had offered her.

No, she couldn't tell her parents about that. Neither could she say anything about the mark on her forearm. Luckily, they still hadn't noticed it. It was best that it remained hidden.

"Did anything else happen?" Mom asked, rubbing at her temples.

"No! Nothing!" Callie said, shaking her head fervently.

Mom raised an eyebrow. Dad looked at Callie with a narrowed gaze.

"Nothing at all?" Dad asked.

"I'm sure Callie had a rough night in the forest. Why don't we let her rest for the day?" Callen said.

"Fine, but you're confined to home for a week! Not for helping the glimmerhare, but for following it foolishly into the forest," Mom said, standing up from her seat. "I'm too tired for this, so I'm going to sleep for an hour or two."

"Me too," Dad said, following after Mom.

Callie wanted to protest her punishment, but stopped before she opened her mouth. Maybe it had been a bit foolish to follow an unknown creature into the forest at night. But if she hadn't done that, then Callie would have never met the dragon.

This also made her realize that telling her Mom, the best hunter in the village, about a dragon living nearby was the worst thing she could do.

What if she called it dangerous and went to kill it? Like she did with other arcane beasts? No, Callie couldn't let that happen. The dragon helped Callie when she was hurt and lost in the forest. She would return his kindness and keep it secret.

As soon as both parents had disappeared into their bedroom, Callen turned to Callie.

"So what happened, really?" He asked quietly, narrowing his gaze.

For a moment, Callie contemplated telling her beloved brother the truth. He was the person she trusted most in this whole world. But what if he told their parents?

It wasn't that she didn't trust him. He wasn't a tattletale and would usually keep Callie's secrets. But Callen didn't share Callie's love for arcane beasts—he might decide that a dragon was indeed too dangerous and tell their parents out of genuine concern for everyone's safety.

"Nothing. That was it. I went after the glimmerhare and fell into a cave. I ended up spending the night there," Callie replied, not daring to look her brother in the eyes. It felt horrible lying to him. She never lied to her brother.

Callen kept his gaze on her for a long while, but eventually sighed. "Okay… I was really worried, you know."

She felt so, so bad. Not telling her brother the truth felt excruciating. She even pursed her lips tightly together, so as not to open her mouth. It was too risky. She didn't want any harm to come to the dragon. Maybe if Callen showed that he didn't view arcane beasts as evil monsters, she could tell him the truth.

Seeing that Callie wouldn't say anything more, Callen ruffled her hair and gave her a smile. "Go rest up, you must be tired. I'll go tell Granny Ethel that you'll be confined to home for a week."

"I can't even go to Granny Ethel's? But who is going to help her?" Callie asked, worried about her mentor.

"She's got Tristan, no?"

"Tristan… Pff…" Callie snorted. That boy could hardly be called an apprentice. He was nothing more than a menace whose favorite activity was bullying Callie. "He's just going to cause trouble…"

"Don't you trust Granny Ethel to keep him in check?" Callen asked.

"Well…" Callie thought about it. It was true that Granny Ethel was the only one who could talk any sort of sense into the bully. But that was on the condition that she actually managed to catch Tristan. Unfortunately, he was much faster than the old woman. "I'm still worried."

"It's just for one week. I'm sure she's going to be fine. But for today, I think it's best to rest."

Callie nodded. Their parents likely were too wound up to actually be reasoned with anyway. So after telling her brother goodbye, she went to her room and dove into her bed.

The rough linens and tough mattress suddenly seemed like the most comfortable bed in the world. Callie hadn't even realized how tired she had been. She would have fallen asleep right away, but she noticed the paper that was lying on her nightstand.

Her parents had gotten her some on her last birthday, because she had actually learned to read and write from Granny Ethel. Not even all adults in the village could do that, so Mom and Dad had been really proud. But the paper had seemed so precious that she hadn't dared to do anything with it.

But suddenly, she knew all too well what to do with the pages. She would write down everything she knew about arcane beasts and create an encyclopedia for others to read. Maybe then they would try to understand them, at least a little. Callie sat up and grabbed two pages.

Name: Glimmerhare

Location:

Beyond millefolia grove, near the dragon's cave

Appearance:

Snow white rabbit with deer antlers

Abilities:

Glowing antlers

Notes:

Released from trap, healed

Name: Dragon

Location:

The dragon cave

Appearance:

Big and long beast with horns, spikes on back and tail, claws and dark green scales

Abilities:

Grows magic plants

Healing water

Gave me magic (?)

Notes:

Never tell anyone.

Callie looked proudly at her drawings and the notes she'd written. It was difficult writing so many words, and she worried whether she had even written it correctly. But nonetheless, she was proud—it looked very similar to the encyclopedia pages Granny Ethel had written about herbs.

If I met more arcane beasts, I could definitely make an encyclopedia.

She giggled at the thought. That would be amazing. Rolling up her sleeve, Callie checked the mark left by the dragon. It was still glowing ever so faintly with gentle pink light. Thankfully, it hadn't been strong enough to shine through her tunic, and it seemed to be growing dimmer.

It made her wonder how exactly she could use magic. There certainly wouldn't be any books about it, not in their tiny village at least.

"Maybe I'll try something later…" Callie muttered, her eyes slowly closing shut as she drifted off into a quiet slumber.

The last thought she had before falling asleep was that she would definitely figure out the magic that the dragon gave her.

Settling down the agitated villagers was a pain in the ass. Many wanted to give Callie some stricter punishment for remaining in the forest after dark. Callen had to repeatedly say that she had been confined to her home and that it was an accident, as she had rescued a rabbit from a trap.

He didn't mention that his little sister had actually rescued a glimmerhare. Everyone in the village believed that monsters were dangerous, even small ones. Callie's fascination with them was bordering on obsession because of the incident with the skywolves two years ago.

Callen himself had rather mixed feelings towards monsters. On one hand, they endangered their village, especially during winter. On the other hand, his little, adorable sister believed that they were merely misunderstood.

He had no clue why the little girl developed such a strong belief, especially after coming face to face with a skywolf herself. She should be more afraid of monsters than anyone else. Yet she wasn't, so that gave Callen pause. Maybe there was some logic to what the child was saying. One didn't change their beliefs so drastically without a good reason.

Once all the villagers had been informed of the events and more or less pacified, he returned home. As he slipped into his little sister's room and noticed her sleeping in the bed with a dopey grin on her face, his anger disappeared without a trace. Callen had no clue how anyone could be mad at this adorable little girl.

Unlike everyone else in the village, Callie had beautiful ginger hair, a color she inherited from their father. While it was mostly tangled in a messy state, it actually suited her, as did the flurry of light freckles on her cheeks and nose. Her deep green eyes contrasted with her hair rather nicely, always looking around and eager to learn more about the world around her.

Callen took a blanket and gently draped it over Callie when he noticed a few pages lying on the nightstand. She had actually drawn something on them. He was surprised. Callie had been too scared to waste the precious paper, although she already knew how to write.

Curious, he picked up and studied them. The first one was a drawing of the glimmerhare along with a short description. He smiled at the image. Callie had drawn the creature rather cutely. Is this how she saw monsters? It certainly made them appear less dangerous.

Then he flipped to the second page, and his breath hitched.

"A d-dragon?" He muttered in disbelief, hurriedly reading over Callie's notes. "Water… magic…"

Callen carefully rolled up Callie's tunic sleeve to look at her forearm. Sure enough, a small pink mark was there, glowing ever so faintly. There was no doubt that it was magical in nature.

"Oh no…"
