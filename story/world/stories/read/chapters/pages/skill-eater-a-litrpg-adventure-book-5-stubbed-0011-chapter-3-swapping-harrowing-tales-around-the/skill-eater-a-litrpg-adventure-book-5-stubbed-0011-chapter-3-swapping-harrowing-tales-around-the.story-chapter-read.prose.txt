The crew eliminated six more monsters before calling it a day.

When the final nightmare made flesh breathed its last, the hunters made their way to the maze’s exit. The crew climbed the dirt ramp leading out of the lowlands, circled the cliffside, and camped beside the floating wagon they had parked in the highlands before delving the sub-biome’s depths.

After transferring a load of parts to the wagon, Edge sat down on a boulder, staring up at the sky while Jumo and Mel cooked dinner. The meal was ready half an hour later, a hearty meat stew topped with flavorful spices Trapper had purchased in World’s End.

He helped himself to a heaping bowl, savoring the delicious flavor. When everyone was seated around the fire, he turned to his friends and said, “I need to tell you what happened when I stepped through the rift. Afterwards, I want you to share what you went through while I was away, if you feel up to talking about it.”

The crew leaned in as he began his harrowing tale, starting with the dying world he discovered on the far side of the portal. “Almost all the magicytes were corrupted,” he explained. “From what I gathered, only the Ancients can use them to cast spells, leaving the flesh-and-blood monsters starving for magic.”

“That tracks with how the portal sucked up all the filth from the lowlands after you destroyed the anchor,” Sakura said. “It must have been built using the Ancients’ magic.”

“On that note,” Edge continued, “I have most of the anchor sitting in my spatial storage.” He recounted the story of his battle with the stage-four lobster, culminating with Rue dealing the final blow and saving the day.

“Someone is getting all the belly rubs he wants when we get back to Word’s End,” Melenia muttered. Edge arched an eyebrow and glanced down at his stomach, getting a good laugh for his antics. “You can have some too, but you’ll have to earn them first.”

“Flirt later,” Jumo interrupted, “and finish your story. The suspense is killing me.”

He obliged and backed up a few minutes, sharing the struggle that had ensued when the dark passenger opened its eyes.

“I was drawn into my inner world for an epic showdown with the Ancient infesting my core. Sakura’s comment about the artifacts reflecting their lord’s true nature provided the key I needed to open the tome, and not a second too soon. When her memories were restored, I was caught in the flow. I wound up experiencing the story of how Skill-Eater’s family came to this world and their first encounter with the Ancients.”

“Her?” asked Sakura.

“Yea, that one caught me by surprise. It turns out that the giant bound by a legion of chains was the monstrous form Skill-Eater assumed after being corrupted by the passenger. Her true form isn’t too different from you or me, although she implied she has worn quite a few guises over the millennia.”

Edge revealed everything he had witnessed in the vision, then finished his tale with the passenger’s defeat and its capture by the chains of oblivion. “The monster isn’t gone for good, but I should be able to evolve safely now, and Skill-Eater will have a lot more energy when I do.”

“That is fascinating,” Sakura said, “and more than a little ominous. A portion of Lore-Weaver’s power was sealed in the puzzle box, but far fewer of the weaver’s memories were restored when I unlocked it. I got the impression that the process used to seal Skill-Eater was an early prototype of what turned the rest of the lords into Unique cores. I think the memory relics were stored separately from the rest. Lore-Weaver still looks like an alien spider without an obvious gender, but it must have another form too.

“From the fragments of memory that were included, combined with what you learned and our earlier insights, when the monsters’ dimension overlapped with Ord, some of the Ancients passed through. They waged war on the lords and the rest of the twenty-one transformed themselves into cores to prevent being infected like Boundless-Well and Skill-Eater. It sounds like the Prime Ancients are even stronger. If the monster possessing Realm-Traveler can bridge the dimensions and the Primes pass through, I doubt anything can stop them.”

“If the Lords of Ord are beings of pure magic,” Sasha added, “and the Ancients are energetic parasites, where do the other monsters fit into the equation?”

“I’ve been pondering that all day,” Edge replied. “Based on everything I’ve witnessed thus far, the Ancients weren’t originally from that dying world, or if they were, they hadn’t dominated the planet when the lobster was young.

“In its inner world, the filth hadn’t wrecked the ecosystem, and the sun was its normal size. Regardless of the timeline, I think the Ancients altered the monsters’ bodies, enabling them consume Mortium and produce tainted magic, kind of like mobile terraformers and disposable minions rolled into one.”

“We don’t have enough information to unravel the full story.” Sakura rubbed her temples. “But it’s clear that the Ancients are capable of destroying entire worlds, even the very stars. Whatever they are planning, they represent a dire threat to every creature that calls Ord home, and they have been on this planet for centuries, laying plans while lurking in the shadows.”

“I wonder if there is any connection between them and the anomaly,” Jumo said, “and how the Wardens and the System fit into the picture.”

“We need to find a way to deal with the Ancients,” Trapper spoke after a long silence. “But first, we need to survive being caught between the Wardens and the Coalition and make sure Puppet Town can thrive over the long-haul. This situation is bigger than any of us. If we’re going to weather this storm, the Lords of Ord and their scions will play a critical role. One way or another, we need to form alliances with people we can trust.”

Edge rose to his feet and tossed another log on the fire. “I’ve shared what I went through. Now I need to know what happened while I was away.”

“It was bad,” Melenia spoke through trembling lips. “Things were going well at first, but then a stage-four monster appeared and blew itself up after we scored a critical blow.”

“I used your healing spellshot,” Trapper added. “But I had no idea how long the effect would last and wound up cutting it too close. The magic restored us, but not before we suffered some truly horrific wounds.”

Sasha looked like she wanted to say something, but she just rubbed her neck as the color drained from her face. Edge walked over, drew her in, and held her tight. “It’s okay. You don’t have to share the gruesome details. I’m just glad everyone is still in one piece.”

“That reminds me.” Trapper handed Edge his spellslinger. “All the rounds are still there except for Healing Sanctuary and Flesh to Glass. Here are the pills and consumables we didn’t use.” She passed over a package that was considerably smaller than the one he’d given her before jumping through the portal.

“Let’s get some sleep and worry about the big picture in the morning. But to summarize, we will do everything we can to prepare for our meeting with the Coalition later this week. When we speak with King, we will try to buy some time so Edge can digest that Epic skill and evolve before we sign on the dotted line. Meanwhile, will do our best to stay out of the Wardens’ way and support our people back home.

“Over the mid-term, we need to figure out who we can trust, form alliances to deal with the Ancients that are already here, and stop the monsters from opening more rifts. Finding the other relics and restoring Skill-Eater and Lore-Weaver’s sealed power is critically important too. Finally. our odds will improve if we can establish favorable relationships with any scion we meet, except Corpse, who we owe some payback for what she did to our people.”

“I thought our situation was fucked up before learning about the Ancients.” Jumo sighed.

Edge volunteered to take first watch, gazing at the star-studded heavens while his friends got some much-deserved shuteye. His mind was racing as all the insights and emotions he had experienced swirled through his brain. He wound up staying up all night. Since he knew he couldn’t sleep, he didn’t bother waking anyone up.

Part of the way through, Rue materialized in his arms. It wasn’t completely safe for the stage-one beast to be out here, but the Haunted Vale had been pacified for the moment, and after everything that had happened, he really needed the company.

Together, they watched shooting stars streak across the sky until the sun rose and the rest of the crew woke up on their own. After bidding farewell to Rue, everyone packed their gear, loaded the wagon, and began the day-long hike back to the free city.

They weren’t planning to do any hunting other than what Edge needed for Extraction, so they set a ground-eating stride while deploying their stealth powers. They had filled the wagon with the least-valuable parts they had collected, so they wouldn’t pose a tempting target to predators of the human variety if they encountered any bandits on the journey to World’s End.

There wasn’t a reason to push through the night, so the crew camped on a hilltop that offered a gorgeous view of the Lakelands. They watched the endless waterways reflect the radiant glory of the of the setting sun while enjoying each other’s company and celebrating the fact that they had emerged from their ordeal without losing anyone along the way.

Now that the team had shared their life-and-death struggles, the hunters were ready to discuss something a great deal more pleasant—all the awesome new powers they had earned while repelling the monsters’ invasion.

Edge had claimed a Rare skill from the stingray and an Epic from the lobster. However, he wouldn’t learn the details of the former until tomorrow afternoon and the latter for another twelve days.

Next, they shared the skills they had ranked up during the siege. He let them know what had happened when he used Elemental Blade’s Devastating Discharge for the first time, which the System had chosen after taking the power to rank five.

“The skill prevents me from taking a direct hit from the eruption of elemental energy, but I need to be careful about being caught in the crossfire if I use it on a weapon I’m holding instead of channeling it through Manifest Chain.”

Sasha had taken Flame Dash to rank two. The strawberry-blond hunter chose an upgrade that made the fire the mobility skill emitted burn significantly hotter than before. Anything that decided to chase her was in for a searing surprise. She had also advanced Solar Flare to rank three, which was incredibly fast for an Epic skill. Her strongest power could deal more damage than ever, and everyone was excited to see what happened when she unlocked Flare’s rank-four upgrade.

Silent Step and Enhance Hearing both hit rank three, and Purity of Flame had advanced to rank two, gaining a significant reduction to its cooldown time. Finally, Bulwark had reached rank two, providing a boost to its recipients’ Disruption on top of enhancing their Durability and granting their weapons an oscillating effect.

“Whoa.” Edge let out a low whistle. “That is an insane amount of skill advancement, even for me.”

“That’s what happens when you slaughter scores of monsters in a single day,” Sasha shot back, “several of which were stage-three.”

“We received credit for killing the stage-four blob too,” Trapper added. “Although technically, it blew itself up.”

“Don’t remind me.” Mel shuddered before turning the conversation back to their recent gains.

The muscular guardswoman had made some amazing progress too. In addition to earning ten cycles, Melenia had taken Second Wind and Swap to rank three, along with her Epic skill Titan’s Axe. Force Wall had hit rank two, gaining a big bonus to the damage the barrier could absorb.

Assassin’s Boon had also advanced to rank two. In addition to providing a bonus to Perception, Speed, and Control and identifying an enemy’s weak points, the aura now erased the trail its recipients’ left behind instead of merely reducing it, something that Mel had always struggled with while hunting in the field. It also dampened scent and heat signatures, making her considerably stealthier than before.

Jumo had taken Multistrike, Second Wind, and Dash to rank three. For its rank-two upgrade, Multistrike had doubled its attack speed, adding two extra hits to its lightning-fast barrage. Mirror Image had received its Ghost Army upgrade for reaching rank two, letting him create three separate clones for each person his aura protected.

Jumo had chosen the same coverage upgrade Edge picked after taking Harden to rank two, letting him shield more of his body at once. Finally, Manifest Mineral had reached rank two as well, letting him conjure more stone at a faster rate.

As amazing as all of that was, the biggest gains belonged to Trapper and Sakura, both of whom had evolved to stage three while defending the dimensional breech.

Thanks to all the high-stage beasts and monsters she had defeated with devices over the last few weeks, the crew’s leader had cleared the requirements to upgrade her specialty core from Uncommon to Epic. Combined with the cycles she’d racked up immediately after, the gray-eyed hunter had multiplied her might within the span of two days.

Trapper’s ultimate ability had received a considerable boost in the process. She could still manually arm and trigger any traps she set, whether magical or mundane, which meant her allies never needed to worry about being harmed by her devices. Now she could set complex trigger conditions too, which was certain to come in handy while hunting in the field.

But that was only the beginning. Her upgraded ultimate doubled the power of any trap that went off within a hundred feet of her position and dramatically reduced the time it took for her trap skills to manifest, making her namesake specialty even more deadly.

She had also taken Jab, Omnisense, and Blend to rank two, and thought Snipe and Manifest Trap would hit rank five before long. Jab had gained a considerable bonus to its damage and Omnisense had tripled its range. Blend had acquired an efficiency bonus when used on multiple people and could reveal its recipients’ positions to each other, eliminating both of the skill’s biggest weaknesses.

That just left Edge’s fellow scion.

Sakura began by explaining the upgrades to her ultimate abilities. Analyze, Bypass, and Probe had their cooldowns reduced and their sensitivity enhanced, making her informational powers even more potent.

“Hold up,” Jumo chimed in. “I know Analyze lets you study a spell’s effect and trace its mana signature, like when you tracked Wraith’s crew when they were trying to blow up our turrets. Probe is that awesome power that creates a model of a creature’s core, including every trait and skill in its collection, but what is Bypass?”

“Bypass lets me interface with magitech devices and alter their programming,” Sakura replied. “It’s how I hack into our Guides and use the messenger function the System disabled after putting the planet on lockdown.”

“Got it. Please continue.” He offered a bow that made her smile.

The next part made Edge’s ears prick up. It seemed that every Unique core gained a new ultimate ability when it evolved, which meant he had one coming too.

Sakura’s new ultimate was a passive called Gift of the Magi. It straight up gave her mana sensitivity, an extremely rare phenomenon that occurred when a beast reached stage one or a person installed a new core. Fortunes had been spent trying to induce mana sensitivity artificially, and to the best of Edge’s knowledge, Ord’s most powerful factions had failed to accomplish what Lore-Weaver had bestowed its scion—a potent power perfectly fitting the weaver’s nature.

Gift of the Magi radically enhanced her energetic senses and had powerful synergy with her other informational powers. It would help Sakura spot hidden enemies, suss out hostile spells and trap magic, and notice environmental hazards from considerably further out, along with countless other uses that were beyond his ability to truly comprehend.

Combined, Sakura’s ability to investigate her enemy’s magic far surpassed his own, although they worked through fundamentally different mechanics. Edge’s skill senses were intuitive, while hers was analytical, reading the flows of mana in the air and unraveling the mechanics involved. He was the only one who could enter a creature’s inner world and examine their avatars in person, granting him a unique advantage in that regard.

The scion had also advanced several skills during the fight. Disruption Shield, Redirect, and Mirage had all hit rank two. Her antimagic barrier had doubled in size, and its shape was far more malleable. Her defensive skill could channel significantly more kinetic, thermal, and electrical energy into the ground below her boots before being overwhelmed. Finally, Sakura’s illusion-generating magic had grown more efficient while making it easier to multitask when it was running, which would let her fight and create Mirages at the same time.

“I know we pulled off something crazy,” Sasha said when show and tell was over. “But we just compressed a year’s worth of gains into less than two days, plus the tokens and traits the System gave us for completing its quest.”

“I would rather not go through anything that risky again,” Trapper replied. “But we are leaps and bounds ahead of the goals we set for our meeting with the Coalition. The only exception is Edge won’t evolve in time, but he cleared the conditions and both skills he stole sound amazing. If we can delay committing to a decision, we can negotiate from a position of strength—maybe even find a way to turn down joining the faction without endangering our home.

“That’s enough chatter for one night. We haven’t fully recovered from our ordeal, and we have a lot to do when we make it back to the free city.”

Edge didn’t have to stand watch tonight, so after spending some time with Rue and the avatars in his core, he slipped into his bedroll and was out cold less than a minute later.
