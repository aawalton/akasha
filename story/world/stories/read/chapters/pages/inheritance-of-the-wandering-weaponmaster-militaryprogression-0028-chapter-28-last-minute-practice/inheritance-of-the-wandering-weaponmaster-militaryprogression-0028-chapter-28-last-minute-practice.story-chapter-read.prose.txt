Ezra’s struggling with the aura faded from his mind as he lay eyes on the camp for the first time. He didn’t know what he’d been expecting, but a camp just like the one that he and the rest of the Count’s soldiers and levies were setting up wasn’t it. It was just a lot bigger, but while he looked at the camp, he saw Chloe rush out from the middle of the Count’s army. Or, he supposed, Count Villy’s army. There would be at least another Count who’d been made to bring his levies and soldiers here, so he’d probably have to specify.

The thought of having to learn politics made Ezra’s skin crawl, so instead, he just followed the march forward, getting closer to the wagons as the sergeants began barking orders out to the soldiers and levies.

“You’ll get your tents when we arrive at our campsite!” Oscar’s voice was the loudest. “If you start pushing and shoving to get your stuff, then I can guarantee that I’ll have you planking until you puke and then for another hour!”

Some of the more eager levies who’d hoped to get an early start on rest backed away at that, and the other sergeants’ commands revolved around collecting firewood and which squads would be assigned to that what day.

“It’ll be pretty crazy here for a while,” Amy said from behind him, and Ezra turned to look at her. “It’ll calm down by tomorrow’s lunch, though.”

“Have you been on an expedition like this before?” he asked, noting that the other elite recruits stood nearby and listened for the answer as well.

“This large, no,” Amy shook her head. “This is the largest that’s happened in a long time. But there was a joint operation with Count Svana a couple years back, to deal with the Longhorn Bandits. It was much the same when we got there.”

Ezra nodded, remembering that there were plenty of more experienced people who were around to help out. That helped calm him more than he’d like to admit, so he stepped back and made his way into camp alongside the rest of the soldiers who marched under Count Villy’s banner. A few of the sergeants and other commanding officers spoke with other people who’d already been here, but the rank and file soldiers and levies were sent onward towards where they would be camping.

The camp was set up in the valley between two peaks, the sole passage that allowed entry from this section of the Dragon Wilds to the Kingdom of Fidelius. While they drew closer to their destination, Ezra asked Amy, “How do we know that they’re coming from this part of the Wilds here? Petr’s already said that this is the only pass through the mountains nearby, but how do we know that they’re coming to this pass instead of a different one? They can just go back into the Wilds, right?”

“This isn’t just on the other side of the Wilds,” Amy corrected him. “It’s still Kingdom territory on the other side of the pass. And we know it’s here because we’ve tracked them until they entered the pass. The scouts’re damn good at their jobs and there’ve been some efforts to steer them in this direction. I don’t know what exactly the specifics have been there, though.”

Ezra just nodded in response to that, unable or unwilling to understand what kinds of sacrifices would have been necessary to move a force of five hundred monsters. His steps continued forward, and it wasn’t long before he was setting up the tent with the other three elite recruits.

“What’s different now?” Boyd insisted as Ezra sat on his bedding.

“Nothing, really,” Ezra said, not for the first time. “Just trying to figure it out. And I’m going to do that tonight, if you don’t mind.”

Cord grabbed his brother by the elbow and pulled him to his own bedding, where they sat and talked quietly as Ezra and Leah both tried to strengthen their inner worlds. He couldn’t say if he was going to have any measure of success in finding what kind of aura remained well in there, but he wasn’t about to call it quits on the very first day that he learned about it being a possibility.

Instead of trying to draw ice inside himself again, Ezra moved to the side of his bedroll where he could sit directly on the dirt there. He opened his mind up to the stone’s aura, and he pulled it directly into his inner world, not trying to make it anything else first. The feeling was strange, as he’d spent months now refining the aura before he tried to pull it into his inner world, but now he was just trying to move it directly.

He saw immediate success this way, though moving that aura into his inner world made him groan in pain as he was reminded that it was already full. The stone settled in the middle of his inner world, though it fell into gravel that spread slowly over the ground, making a path of sorts. The stone lay there for a short time while Ezra breathed steadily and tried to adjust to the pain of a swollen inner world.

“That isn’t fun,” he breathed to himself, not paying attention to if any of the other people in the tent, ghostly or otherwise, were looking at him weirdly for that. Instead, he just watched the stone in his internal world, wondering if it would last longer than the ice, and if it did, if that mattered. After all, he didn’t want to be like Chloe, where she’d progressed all the way to Pseudo-Knight and, according to Jormun and her Knight teacher, had made a mistake of some sort regarding her inner world’s affinity.

Looking at the stone laying there in his inner world, Ezra tried to understand how it felt there. Everything else to do with this path to Sainthood, as Jormun called it, had come intuitively to him. The first time he’d tried, he’d touched the aura of flame, and it wasn’t long thereafter that he’d touched ice and cold. Moving and pulling a little bit of aura into himself had come easily as well, which, he was seeing now, was hard for most people.

So, he trusted his gut. And, though he hadn’t liked the thought of ice being the affinity of his aura, it felt a lot more natural in his inner world than stone did. He couldn’t put his finger on why that was, but the stone felt off. He’d have to experiment a bit more, get that affinity into his inner world by converting it and getting it in there, but he had the sneaking suspicion that it wouldn’t change that feeling of wrongness.

Another thought crossed his mind, and he cracked his eyes open to look at everyone else in the tent. Maybe thirty minutes had passed since he’d started to incorporate the stone into his inner world, and everyone had returned to sleep. The even breathing and light snores let him know that he was actually alone, and he whispered to Jormun, “How did you make your inner world steel?”

It started with stone, to granite, to iron, to steel, the grizzled ghost answered without hesitation. Don’t ask me why, because I know that’s not how it works. Even so, that’s what my mind worked with and where I ended up. Just remember, what you’re doing right now is finding something comfortable, something that feels at least partially correct. Usually, what someone uses to ascend to Adept is a simpler concept, but not always. So don’t think that what’s comfortable now is where you’ll be forever. Don’t lose sight of what’s currently possible for you while looking for the amazing.

“Everyone says I’m a prodigy, I can’t be stuck with some lame affinity,” Ezra grumbled.

Not a single bad affinity, Jormun replied instantly. Especially if you’re looking to be a Weapon Saint. Are you still thinking that’s what you want to do and be?

“Yes,” Ezra said, surprised at his own vehemence.

Then I can’t think of any affinity that’d be the best option for that. Steel was good for me, giving stronger blows, but lightning would have made me faster and given additional effects to all my attacks, to burn or to paralyze. Would it have been better for me? Fire, ice, wind, and water would all have plenty of utility, to say nothing of corrosion, lava, smoke, shadow, light, storm, sound, and more. There’s an infinite list of possibilities, and they all start somewhere. Don’t just think that what’s flashy or exotic is best. It’s a good way to find yourself lost on your path instead of wise. Steel was a common enough affinity on my home world, wherever that was. And I was the last human alive.

Jormun’s words trailed off at that, and Ezra remembered that nightmare he’d seen the first day that they’d met. Instead of continuing on that same track, he asked, “Do you know why you appeared to me specifically?”

No.

Jormun didn’t say anything else, and he withdrew on himself. He seemed to shrink from a massive, hulking man, instead lost in his thoughts as he didn’t answer any more. Ezra was about to lose himself back in his inner world when Jormun spoke again.

It isn’t just the affinity, the design of your inner world as well influences the way that your aura manifests, and, in the end, the aura is just a manifestation of who you are. Continue to learn who Ezra is, and you’ll find that you’ll do a lot better on your path to Sainthood.

Then, there were no more words, and Ezra was left alone, even as he had friends on every side.

The next three days passed quickly, with the morning spent drilling and the afternoons spent resting, with occasional reports of when the Dragonkin were estimated to arrive. Each morning’s practice was hard, with forced marches from one edge of the pass to the other, jogging with shield and spear in hand or in formation while striking out with the spear in unison. Fortunately, at this point, Ezra’s boots were well worn, and his feet didn’t complain as much. Even so, he found that, when doing these drills, he couldn’t spare the focus to try to pull aura into himself, only able to spend it to help him recover. Maybe someday he’d be able to fight and pull in aura at the same time, but Jormun thought it would be a long time coming.

By the second day, everyone was on edge, though whenever one of the soldiers or levies started to make trouble, one of the sergeants was there with a heavy hand and overbearing punishments.

For his part, Ezra desperately threw himself into strengthening his inner world, though he couldn’t find any affinity that seemed to want to stick around for long. He didn’t feel the need to share with anyone other than Jormun that he was able to get most everything into his inner world by now, with just three days of practice. The old ghost’d just sighed at that, but seemed to accept it as just something to be expected at this point.

On the second night, the last night before the battle was expected to begin, Ezra sat on his bedroll, angrily cycling different types of aura through his inner world. Nothing seemed to work, and though Ice and fire felt alright, they weren’t right, and stone was just wrong. He couldn’t see anything, the swirling mass of his inner world just… swirling. Nothing stayed, and instead got moved around and slowly dismantled into ambient aura instead.

With a flash of inspiration, he tried to think about when he’d used an aural attack a month ago… but to no avail. He hadn’t felt anything but warmth that day, and a stronger attack. Maybe some light? Nothing he could settle on. Instead of continuing to smash his head stubbornly against the wall that was his inner world, Ezra forced himself to stop, lay down, and try to get some rest. Fortunately, sleep wasn’t hard to find, though his dreams were fitful and fearful, without a form he could remember when he woke.

And when he woke, that fear transformed into something deeper, more guttural. Because horns were sounding and people everywhere were rushing around, getting ready.

The enemy army was here.
