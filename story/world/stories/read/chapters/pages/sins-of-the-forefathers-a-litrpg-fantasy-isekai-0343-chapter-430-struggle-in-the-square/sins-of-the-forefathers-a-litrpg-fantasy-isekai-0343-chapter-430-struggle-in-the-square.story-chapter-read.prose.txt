Things only went from bad to worse as we tracked the path the expedition had taken through Rhoscara. Whatever or whoever it was that had slain Nyx seemed to have remained with the Savoy forces.

There seemed to have been a notable increase in casualties among the expedition at their hands. My people’s fighting retreat looked to have turned into a rout. A bloody one at that. Expedition members from all factions of it littered the street, with fewer and fewer Savoy corpses along the way. To my dismay, I realized I even knew many of these people, and I wasn’t the only one. There were casualties from the Herztalian Army, from the various Martial Orders, from the Gnolls, and yes, even among my own Polaris Reach. I…even saw one particular death among Bella’s crew, a man who had helped me settle in on the Thorny Reef, back in the day.

The one-eyed corpse of Laryn stared up at us blankly from where he had fallen in the middle of the street. His entire torso looked to have been crushed somehow, as if an enormous fist had ground his chest to dust. He didn’t even look like he’d had time to process his own death before his soul had fled from his body.

Bella had stopped and stared at him for a moment, looking blank in a way I had rarely seen from the Pirate Captain. But she was harder than most, and this was far from the first time she’d lost men. Even ones who had served with her for as long as Laryn had. Before we could prompt her, she had reached out and closed the pirate’s unseeing eyes, in a surprisingly gentle motion…

Before urging us on, tension in every line of her body.

We were lucky, in an odd, morbid way. The tanners' district had already been evacuated before the expedition had ever pitched our camp within it. The only things Anguis’s forces would have been interested in would have been us. And with the expedition having been routed and chased through the streets by his men, there was no need to position any more within it. My squad encountered no resistance whatsoever as we sprinted through the streets, following a trail of our own dead as we did so.

But it turned out things weren’t quite as settled as I thought they were.

We heard them before we saw them. Only a few streets over, the clamor of battle suddenly reached my ears, piercing through the roar of the flames and the screams of the butchered elsewhere in the city. War cries, the clash of steel, the march of boots upon cobblestone as they rapidly shuffled in an intricate dance I knew all too well…

Accompanied by what almost sounded like thunder, clashing and howling over the din. And I do mean howling.

I swore that I almost heard what sounded like the furious screams of wolves in the distance.

We accelerated, and I don’t think I was the only one whose heart leapt in their chest. And as we crested the last corner, we saw it.

Them.

The expedition…and the forces off the Unchained.

Locked in battle with the Savoy.

I was mildly familiar with this square we had just stumbled upon, if only because of the strategy meetings I had attended for the defense of the city. This was the exit point from the tannery district and into the business district. It was huge, easily several blocks in size. Prior to the Savoy invasion, it had been heavily fortified by the Rhoscarans, as one of the designated chokepoints meant to hold the invaders off. Dozens of barricades had been thrown up to turn it into a killing ground, with nearly every building that enclosed the square intended as a sniper's spot for archers to rain death down from. To my relief, it looked like the Unchained had made a play for it when things had gone south for the now mind-controlled Dwarves of the city. They had seized the barricades and the sniper spots both, and now it was the arrows of freed-slaves that rained down upon the shields of the Savoy.

All to protect the fleeing members of the expedition.

There seemed to be far fewer of them than I wished. My extremely quick glance told me we had suffered significantly at the hands of Anguis’s forces. Hundreds dead, at the very least, and it wasn’t hard to see why.

If I didn’t know better, I would have thought every Dwarf in the city was trying to breach the defenses of this square. There must have been thousands of them trying to get inside, from the multiple entrances into it. They looked to have been succeeding, as well. Two of the barricades that faced the direction of the docks looked to have already fallen to the swarming Dwarves, their gates having been shattered. Three additional layers to the tiered barricades still fought on, while the main fortification near the back of the plaza still stood. Many of the sniper spots on the surrounding buildings almost looked to have been burned out or even bombed. Raging fires flared from those windows, and unlike many of the other spots in the square, no arrows fell from them.

But…none of that was the most important thing happening in the square. For all of the individual battles occurring throughout it, for all of the minor victories and defeats that happened every second in a fight on this scale…

There was one that was the most important.

Before the closed gates of the third barricade in the plaza, I was shocked to recognize two werewolves locked in mortal combat. There was a large opening in the battle between the two transformed, bestial existences, a circle big enough where they could tear and claw at each other beyond the reach of the rest of the battle. Unchained and expedition members both still fought on around the two werewolves, but none dared get close enough.

They would have been fried by the lightning erupting from them if they tried.

Both of the werewolves were massive, equally a match for me in my fully transformed state, and each of them possessed the marker that linked them to the Spirit Wolves. Just as Fade did, they possessed a full, impressive rack of antlers upon their furred heads, but that was where the similarities ceased. One was black of fur, and heavily muscled in a manner that reminded me distinctly of Bleddyn. Streaks of bright yellow lightning crackled up and down the bidpedal wolf’s limbs, curling around them almost like armor as it struck out at the other one. This werewolf was almost feral in its movements, its fury evident in every swipe of its claws and snap of its jaws. And yet…for all of its fury, I could detect a hint of something else in its countenance.

Betrayal…and grief.

But the other…it was almost the total opposite. It was taller, for one, by at least a foot, and leaner as well. Its body was almost gangly in its proportion, to the extent that just gazing at the creature was enough to disturb me. And yet, it was by no means weaker than the smaller werewolf. It loomed over the black-furred one as, instead of yellow lightning, this one bore bolts of purest sky blue, of a shade so similar that it almost caused my heart to hurt in longing for a sky stolen. Just looking at this second werewolf, I could tell that it was stronger than the other one. Its lightning was brighter, its control over it far more precise. Instead of writhing upon its form, this werewolf’s lightning was frozen in tightly controlled places, and the resemblance to armor was much starker upon him. It was less bestial in its attacks, as well. Instead of a wild, animalistic fury, this werewolf fought with a disturbingly familiar discipline. Every strike it lash out with was precise, every discharge of its thunder calculated, and every movement personal. To my shock, I think it was performing an almost kind of martial art. Or at least, a recognizable unarmed combat discipline.

The contrast between the two monstrous existences was immediately apparent. The smaller one had to be Bleddyn, from everything I knew.

And he was losing. Claw marks were evident all over his furred body, oozing a thick, dark blood. Patches of his fur had been outright vaporized in place, where he couldn’t deflect the other werewolf’s blasts of lightning. Bleddyn must have taken a hard blow to his right leg, too, because the transformed state of my fellow former slave looked to be limping as he fought off the other. But who was he? Everything I knew about the werewolves was that they were members of the various Mynnyd clans, sworn to Taran in some way. What member of those peoples would actually fight on the side of the Savoy? Especially to fight against the man who was meant to become the next Chief of the Thunderhearts?

It was only a matter of time before Bleddyn lost. If he was defeated by that other werewolf, he would blow straight through the other barricades, and then both the forces of the Unchained and the expedition I had come to save would be overrun.

I had to assist.

“Sylvia,” I said quickly, breaking both her and the rest of my squad out of their stunned trance. “Take the others and join the defense. I need to help Bleddyn.”

“Bleddyn? What are you-” Was all I heard from my confused lover, before I triggered Vis Maledicta Exactoris, and exploded to my full, monstrous transformed state. I flexed my reptilian wings and flapped them as strongly as I could, immediately shooting into the air. I cleared the rooftops of Rhoscara in only seconds, and for a single, solitary moment…

I gazed out at the ruin of Rhoscara.

As far as my eyes could see, the city of the Florens was burning. Not a single inch of it was untouched by the flames originally started by the very man I intended to save, but fed by the Dwarf I wanted to kill. Dwarven soldiers, either mind-controlled or not, ran rampant through the streets of the one thriving metropolis. The screams of the innocents they butchered without abandon rose above even the roar of the flames. There were too few in those winding, stone corridors fighting against the monstrosity occurring before me. Some members of the Unchained, who weren’t held up at the choke-point below me. A few squads of what looked like Elves, those sworn to Alveron, were out in the streets and ambushing Dwarven butchers. And somewhere, in the distance…

I thought I could see some squads of Rhoscaran Rangers, whose minds were as yet undominated, fighting to save their people.

But it wasn’t enough. It would never be enough.

I had to face facts.

This city…was lost. There weren’t enough defenders capable of fighting off the near-unending hordes of Savoy sworn soldiers pouring into the city. The destruction of the port hadn’t stopped them. Instead, they were either pouring over the walls, manned only by those whose minds had been stolen, or simply walking in through the front gates. In their state of control, the guardians of the gates had simply opened them for the Savoy to come slithering in.

Whatever strength we could muster…whatever defense we might pitch…

It wasn’t enough.

This city was lost. The only hope we had was to evacuate as many civilians as we could…and then flee through the Long Road. We could only hope that they wouldn’t follow us through those ancient paths.

I…don’t…I don’t think…

I don’t think I could either save Elysael from Anguis, nor could I find and kill him. Not now. Not here. He had engineered this situation too perfectly. I had no idea where he even was, and if he showed his face, what then? Was I supposed to triumph, on my own, against a Classer hundreds of Levels my superior, buoyed by two sparks of Divinity?

Maybe I could do it. Maybe I could get lucky and ambush him with The Scintillant Blade.

And then he would kill me, and the last hope of ending the Skyfall would die with me.

No.

I had to keep my priorities straight. We had to flee Rhoscara, and we had to find a way south to Smaragd. There, we could do something about Fynneas and put an end to this whole mess. I had wanted to do so with the forces of Rhoscara at my side, but I had no choice but to do it with far fewer people now. With a much diminished expedition, our mode of transportation shattered and burned to cinders.

I had to believe we could do it. Otherwise…

I took a deep, deep breath, filling my monstrous lungs with the burning smoke of Rhoscara, and didn’t flinch.

There was no otherwise. I couldn’t save Rhoscara…

But I could save Bleddyn.

At the peak of my wing assisted jump, far over the rooftops of Rhoscara, and I drew my last remaining extendable spear, and triggered it. And as I did, I activated two Skills.

The first was Might of the Wyrdwood.

Fifty percent. That was the highest I felt I could hold it without struggle. With that level of enhanced strength, I had to believe I could overcome the werewolf even now battering at Bleddyn.

The other Skill…

Was The Scintillant Blade. As the razor-sharp Oninite blade of my spear ignited into a swirling, coruscating rainbow flame, I angled it downward.

And dived, point first, toward the second werewolf.
