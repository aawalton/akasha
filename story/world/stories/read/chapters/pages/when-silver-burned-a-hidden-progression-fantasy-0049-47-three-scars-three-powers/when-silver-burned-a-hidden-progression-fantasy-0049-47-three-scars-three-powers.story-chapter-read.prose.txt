It was as if the storm itself had swallowed them whole.

Kalsin trudged through the soaked tundra, Cinder's silhouette just barely visible ahead as she cut a path straight toward the edge of the Eastern Expanse. She pulled ahead sometimes, disappearing entirely, only to resume her spot a minute later. Mud clung to his boots with each step, making their trek slower than he would've liked. But then again, he figured speed mattered little at this juncture. They had until sunup to disappear.

Behind him, the muffled grunts and mutters of his companions punctuated the constant drumroll of raindrops. The bahten snorted, their hardened fins sinking into the earth. And the wind; the cold, harsh, biting early autumn wind never let up for a moment, rolling over the dead of night and sweeping the storm across the borderlands.

It was hard to tell how much distance they'd covered by now. A hundred paces? Double that? The storm ensured there was no hope of seeing any landmarks in the gloom. Kalsin could only trust in Cinder's memory and her instincts guiding them ahead.

"I'm soaked to the bone," Ringtail grumbled at his side. She huddled deeper into her dark cloak, collar pulled up all the way to her chin. "Your friend is shit at choosing dates."

"My friend is right next to you, Ringtail, and her ears work perfectly well," Kalsin said. He didn't relish the idea of the two of them butting heads every five minutes. He steeled himself for it nonetheless. Of all his troubles, this was the easiest one to predict, plan for, and mitigate. "You still owe Jack an apology."

"Psh! I do not."

"Well then, the next time you pour your heart out to some poor old chap, don't expect her to extend any privacy to you."

Kalsin heard Ringtail sputter at that, hanging behind for a moment before drawing back in pace with them.

"...as if I would just... to anyone... clueless fucking man..." she kept muttering darkly.

Jack snorted quietly beside him. Good. She wasn't an inherently distrustful soul, but the way she had kept sending warning looks around the crew for the past few minutes somewhat unsettled Kalsin. And in turn, it kept unsettling him.

The few downsides of having a bodyguard; if the professional swordslady watching your back is getting nervous, that's when you know things are on the verge of going sideways.

"Back!" Cinder's voice rang out over the wind, her vague shape becoming clearer as she backtracked toward them. She halted a few steps ahead, staying on the outer edge of their vision still, and Kalsin had to squint to make out her expression clearly. "Too late. They closed the gap. We can't squeeze out with so little space between those men."

The fact that she even saw in this weather was a small miracle. But Kalsin wasn't too keen on looking a gift lass in the mouth.

"Where are we, relative to our barracks?" Kalsin asked.

“You mean those clay huts? They’re just ahead, a little north. We’ll brush right past them if we keep going straight,” Cinder said. “Should we veer that way? I can scout ahead if you want.”

“No. That’s the one place I’m sure we can’t go. It’d be the first spot anyone looking for me would lock down—you’d be walking into a trap.”

"But..." She started, almost looking like she wanted to argue. She shifted in place. "I..."

Kalsin felt Ringtail poke him in the side gently. He ignored it. “We’ll swing south,” he said, pointing down along where the wall should run. “You stick to the front, and if you spot a lousy spot in the defenders’ formation, that’s where we’ll try our luck slipping through.”

"Why?" Crux asked at the same time as Cinder answered, "Okay, Kal. If that's what you think is best."

Ringtail poked him in the side again.

"It is. Thanks, Cinder," Kalsin said. He turned a bit, now addressing the men huddled around him directly. "We need to break out from Republic territory. To do that, we can either go north—toward the Clenn camp—or south, where even if we stumble into some fools, at least they won't be wearing gold."

"That's all very interesting, Ghost. But still doesn't explain why we can't just push through straight ahead," Crux said, dabbing at his wet nose with his sleeve. "There's bound to be some gaps left open. 'Specially with us now missing from our spots."

Kalsin stared at the man, annoyed. "There are no gaps ahead. Were you even listening to her?"

When Ringtail's poke came again, this time Kalsin swiped her hand away irritably. "What?" he snarled.

"There's nobody there."

Kalsin just stared down at Ringtail, wordless.

She pointed a finger ahead, speaking quietly. "There's nobody there, Kalsin. Just... just rain and grass and mud. Far as we can tell, you've been talking to yourself for the past while."

Kalsin slowly followed her finger, looking at Cinder's smudged outline in the rain. Lightning flashed, showing her head bowed, staring down at the ground, blonde hair wet and matted to her skin.

“This,” Kalsin turned back, his voice low and threatening. “Is not a time for either your idiocy or your prejudice. She’s Clenn, sure—and now, so are you. All of you will be soon enough.”

They did not like that answer. Shoreline looked on with a sour expression. Cyanfish shook his head and muttered something about ‘following a lunatic’, and even Fals was somehow starting to appear uncomfortable.

They all slightly jumped at the sound of hissing steel as Jack half-drew her sword and shifted to stand between them and Kalsin.

“Oi, hey, now relax for a second, missus,” Fals held up her hands, taking a single step back and almost slipping in the mud. “We’re just, ah, a tad confused is all. Right. Right?” she glanced at Quiescent next to her. The man had his head tilted, watching Kalsin from under his hood.

“We’re all in agreement that Ghost right here is worth leavin’ for. Scars shifting around and jumping from one body to another is kind of freaky, but, uh… the good kind of freaky. You won’t hear me complain.” Fals said. She then gestured vaguely around. “Only, it’s gotten awfully dark, and I think me and the fellas would prefer not stumbling headfirst into a clump of soldiers on the eve of their desertion.”

Kalsin had expected this from Ringtail. Maybe from some of the others. But he thought Fals was better than that. “Cinder is—”

Crux nodded at Jack. “Is she Cinder?”

“No, no, the gal is named Jack, didn’t you hear?” Wardoll said.

“Cinder Jack? As in, Jack’s the name of her folk?”

“Uh, maybe? Still doesn’t make an iota of sense. She was walking right there, right beside him all along.”

“I know, I saw it too. Someone that big is kind of hard to miss.”

Deep breaths. Deep breaths, Kalsin kept telling himself. They didn’t seem to be doing this out of malice. But then why… Damn it all, he didn’t understand what their issue was. He ran a hand through his soaked hair, and after a moment tapped Jack’s shoulder. “I’m fine. They’re just… confused. Scared, I think. Too many things they don’t understand happened in too little time. But they’re my mates. They’re not going to hurt me, girl.”

Jack shot him an uncertain look over her shoulder, still gripping her hilt.

“Yeah. They’re good people. You don’t know them yet, but they’ll prove it to you if you let them.”

Slowly, she uncurled her fingers from the hilt of her sword.

And then lightning split the sky, casting a white blaze across the field and revealing the dark outlines of several men creeping toward them from the path they’d just traveled.

“Ambush!” Kalsin roared.

That was all the warning they had before everything descended into chaos.

Standing nearest to the men descending on them, Littleguy could barely turn before two figures surged forward and took him under. Displaced water sprayed in their paths, leaving behind empty spots like reverse afterimages before the rain washed them away. Another soldier burst forward, sweeping a hand to unleash a jet of flames at Fals—who surged with sudden speed, shield snapping up just in time to catch the sticky orange manifestation across its steel face. Rather than smothering it, raindrops steamed, popped, caught on fire and pooled at her feet as they passed through the flames.

His shout lost in the thunder, Pike met one attacker head-on as another lunged past to slash at his side. Quiescent appeared out of nowhere with a handful of mud clutched in his fist, smashing it into the man's eyes, grinding rocks and pebbles into his sockets. Teeth flashed white in a silent scream.

Crux wrestled with a man having caught his arm before slipping out, leaving his attacker clutching his empty wet cloak, but slipping in the process. Only Shoreline's chainmail saved him from a blocky cleaver as she lunged to cover him with her own body, making the two of them roll several feet into the dark. A travel bag ripped at the seams, plates and firewood spilling onto the ground.

Dweller had been near enough to reach Kalsin, and Wardoll seized the distraction her allies created, darting between combatants and stumbling into their circle. Within seconds the skirmish had fractured into scattered pockets around the edge of their vision. Shadows grappled in the dark, revealed only by the fleeting flash of steel and the hiss of burning flames. A sheet of constant rain blurred everything. Even Blindlight would have given a clearer view of their surroundings.

Jack shifted on a tight axis around him as she tried to position herself between him and their assailants, but it was an impossible prospect. Doubly so, with Ringtail deliberately shouldering into her and trying to do the same thing.

Kalsin ignored them both, pulling a knife from the collar of his riding boot and roughly passing it over to Wardoll. "An unstrung bow isn't much use here," he muttered. He doubted she heard him in the storm. She clutched the iron with white knuckles nonetheless, facing outward together with Dweller, the willowy woman hunched over and ready to spring with her sword pointing toward the dark. The clash of steel rang out all around them, interspersed with grunts and muffled screams that could have been theirs or the attackers.

They were in a bad spot. Close enough to the wall for the men stationed along the border to hear a larger commotion, if the sky deigned to cease growling for long enough.

"Go help the others, I'll be fine!" Kalsin shouted at Jack, struggling to be loud enough to pierce the racket of rain and combat. He swept a glance around him, eyes widening as he spotted Cinder's blonde mop by Sap's side. His sister just stood there, staring into the chaos as if frozen. "We need to—"

But Kalsin never finished his sentence because there was a blur of motion coming from the dark, faster than the eye could follow, faster than thought, racing toward him.

Jack surged. Like a lightning bolt, she cleaved a path through the rain with her shield forward, catching the man under the chin mid-surge. The two colliding forces snapped his neck inward, making his throat collapse sharply enough to look damn near decapitated, head flopping at an impossible angle. The momentum and the slippery mud forced Jack to her knees, bracing herself with the point of her sword.

A woman burst out of the storm, her surge running out halfway toward him. Her spear lent her enough reach to almost skewer his ribs anyway. But Kalsin had already raised his sword to parry as his red-haired ghost appeared out of nowhere, directing a hit where the spear-point was about to impact just a second before it happened. Her spear swung away with a clang. The woman brought it around for another thrust immediately.

Ringtail screamed, and the woman staggered.

The abyssal surge that swept over the battlefield was not directed at Kalsin, but even he could feel the crushing hopelessness that rammed against the minds of the ambushers. Ringtail's power—the fully restored power of a Clenn—was sharp, sudden, and intense in its potency. She stood with her feet set wide, sword completely forgotten in her hands, throat going raw with her scream. It didn't last long, a second only. It covered little ground, from the way Wardoll and Dweller's attackers barely paused. But it was devastating in its effectiveness.

The woman had fallen like a ragdoll with its strings cut, collapsing facefirst into the wet ground. Her sharp, panicked inhales made her choke on mud. Kalsin ended her suffering with a downward slash. She died before he'd pulled his blade free.

A flash of lightning revealed Cyanfish engaged with two men. One on each side, with his sword splitting rain and his shield catching blows by sheer desperation. More attackers stalked through the rain and the darkness, steadily converging on the center. Many were already down, slumped unmoving, coloring the puddles red. Flames spread across the wet grass, forming a wall and spitting sparks.

Kalsin didn't wait to get surrounded.

He rushed at the man skirting around Sap, catching him by surprise. He bashed his crossguard into the man's face, the stiff iron embedding itself into one eye with a slick squelch while the blade sawed at the side of his head until Kalsin managed to wrench it free. He pushed the falling body away, already turning to engage the Clenn who had crept up on him, a masked man with his sleeves rolled up, both arms red and transformed into a pair of barbed maces.

Kalsin spun, swinging back with his blade aimed straight at the Clenn. The man blocked with both his arms, catching Kalsin's sword between the spikes of his manifested hands. Metal screeched against metal as they strained, caught in a deadlock. The man leaned in, teeth bared, pushing Kalsin's own blade back toward his neck.

Seconds stretched, and Kalsin lost ground with each one passing. He kicked out, but the man was ready for it, taking it on the outside of his thigh. He couldn't let go of his sword, the blade hovering too close to his body, and he felt the raindrops bounce off the sharp edge and against his chin. His red-haired ghost flickered into existence, overlapping the man in warning—but there was no course of action Kalsin could take. A glimpse into the future is useless if you no longer have the means to change it.

He saw Jack's tall outline spin about somewhere at the edges of his vision. He could hear Ringtail's wild yells coming through the storm. Neither of them would find him in time, and neither of them were close enough to make a difference anyway.

So when a spear point appeared straight from Kalsin's chest and skewered the Clenn right in the gut, Kalsin thought someone had run him through from behind.

Except there was no blood, no pain, and he could only stare down in fascination as a hand morphed into existence straight from his own chest, holding the spear forward and pushing the Clenn's twitching body away like roast at the end of a spit.

A silver-haired woman stepped out of his body.

Like a snake shedding its skin, Kalsin saw her come into existence splitting off of him, and with enough momentum to drive the man onto his back, crouching over him and grabbing his hair to slam it roughly into the ground once, then twice, until he stopped moving. She straightened without hesitation, pulling the spear with a twist. Her braid flailed in the wind as she turned to face him. She thumped her chest with a fist, water and blood dripping down her uniform.

"Reporting for duty, sir."
