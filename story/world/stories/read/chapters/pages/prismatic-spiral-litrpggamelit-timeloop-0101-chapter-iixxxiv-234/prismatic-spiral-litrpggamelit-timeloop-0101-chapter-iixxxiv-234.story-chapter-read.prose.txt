Chapter II.XXXIV (2.34)

Sterling was definitely dead. The upper body of the cat’s corpse was nothing more than mashed bones, skin, and hair. The back legs weren’t even much better off, having been picked and pulled apart by juhyo teeth. Since the only available host for his return would probably be those same juhyo, Mitsuko doubted she’d see him again anytime soon. While she hadn’t directly asked it as a question, she didn’t think that Sterling could possess any creatures that acted as acolytes for the guardians. Maybe that changed when she banished the guardian, but she wasn’t holding her breath.

Now that she thought about it, Hrim seemed to only influence beasts with his abilities as guardian. None of the villagers had appeared to be acolytes, only the ice worm and hundreds of juhyo. Sterling had mentioned in the past that less willful acolyte subjects were easier to take control of, so she supposed that Hrim had attempted a technique where he instead controlled hoards of beasts rather than a few select humanoids. Well, he also didn’t seem to think very highly of ‘mortals’ in general. That discriminatory attitude might have also played a role in his decision making.

“It sure is cold in here,” she muttered to herself, hugging her exposed arms. Not having a shirt on didn’t help. All she wore above the waist were wraps of cloth around her chest, which definitely weren’t designed to keep in warmth.

She suspected that the island’s sage’s treasure chamber was hidden under the throne; however, Mitsuko didn’t feel like meeting the sage while half-naked. Instead, she wandered around the icy chamber.

Eventually, she found what she was looking for. A bit of ash, scattered by juhyo paws. She reached down and touched a piece of the soot. It stuck to her fingertips. She cast Mend. All the ash accumulated, burst into flame for a brief moment, then formed into her fur cloak.

She greedily tossed it over her shoulders and stuck her arms through. Not perfect, but a lot fucking better. While her spell arsenal meant she might not be at risk of frostbite and losing bits from the chill, that didn’t make the cold any less uncomfortable.

Next, she reclaimed her bag. Somewhere in the fight she’d lost it and hadn’t even realized it. As she examined the chewed through leather straps, she surmised it must have been during her charge when several juhyo had been biting her.

With that out of the way, Mitsuko approached the throne. She raised her lantern. Even with her constant temporal tampering, it was surprising the wick had managed to remain lit while she’d been mangled by dozens of attacks. She supposed destroying it hadn’t exactly been the priority of Hrim. Not when she could just Mend it back into function at a whim.

She stuffed the history book she’d been reading on the ship over here into her lantern and it burst into flames with very little effort on her part. Then she tossed the flaming book on the seat of the throne and waited. Periodically, Mitsuko Mended the book to renew its flames while simultaneously Deteriorating the ice to speed up the process.

After a few minutes, the burning book seared a hole straight down through the ice throne. Mitsuko took a step up and looked down the hole. The book’s ashy remains had fallen into a chamber below. It had thankfully been a decent sized tome and the ice was melted with several extra centimeters in a radius around the book’s imprint.

She stepped into the hole and let her body slide through the wet tunnel leading below.

Her legs buckled as she made contact with the floor. A quick Mend fixed any damage to her knees. Then, because she felt bad about burning a book like a barbarian, she Mended the pile of soot under her feet back into bound paper.

“Welcome, Champion. I am overjoyed by the sight of you.”

Mitsuko clambered back to her feet and prepared herself to face this new sage.

Instead, she found herself gaping in dumbfounded disbelief.

The entire wall before her was a spiralling mass of colors unlike anything she’d ever witnessed in her life. The closest she’d ever seen was the aurora in the northern reaches of Tross, but even that was nothing in comparison to the lively movement and beauty of the ice sealed here. This was a rainbow of twisting colors that twirled and flowed within the icy wall. For a moment, she forgot how to breathe.

“Did the guardian remove your tongue?” the sage asked, sounding amused.

Mitsuko blinked. Tearing her eyes away from the myriad of colors, she examined the sage that stood before her.

Unsurprisingly, the sage was a sculpt. However, unlike others of her species that Mitsuko had met, this sculpt lacked any snowy textures. Instead, she appeared to be made nearly entirely of a clear, crystalized ice. She was gorgeous like a master craftsmen’s statue. But it was also no wonder that Mitsuko’s eyes had immediately wandered over to the colors beyond her. The sculpt’s body was nearly transparent and she wore no clothes.

The only piece of her not formed of ice was a metal stopwatch, frozen in her breast where a human’s heart would lie. The hands of the clock remained stationary, caught in a single moment. Both long and short hands straight up.

“I see your eyes have found me. That’s progress!” The sage chuckled at her joke.

“Sorry,” Mitsuko muttered.

“No, no. Honestly, your lack of focus is an improvement over the last Champion we had. He kept ogling me like I had suddenly materialized out of some wet dream of his.”

“I… what?” Mitsuko blinked and scratched her head.

“How about we start with introductions?” the sage suggested. “I am Queen Verna Hrim, the first sculpt.”

“Hrim as in…”

“As in Eustace Hrim,” she confirmed. “My father. He crafted me from the ice you see in this very room. The essence of a Titan.” She paused. “You know, usually introductions go two ways.”

“Ah, yeah.” Mitsuko shook her head in an attempt to clear it. “My name is Mitsuko. Nice to meet you. What exactly are you the sage of?”

“Jumping right into that, are we? No further pleasantries?” Verna grinned. “I like that.”

The woman made of ice strode forward until she stood right in front of Mitsuko. She planted her hands on her hips and smiled broadly, her eyes twinkling in the lamplight.

“Pleasure to meet you Mitsuko. I am the Sage of Dilation. I grant you my boon, the great spell of Dilation to use as you please.”

“Dilation,” Mitsuko repeated. She pondered that for a moment. “You can slow time down,” she surmised.

Verna scrunched up her face, lifted a hand, fingers spread flat, and jiggled it. “Broadly correct, technically wrong! I have the ability to perceive and move at a rate faster than those around me. It does not truly slow time but from your point of view it will certainly seem that way.”

“What’s the difference?” Mitsuko asked.

“Dialation makes your perception of events and ability to maneuver faster, only targeting you as an individual. Slowing down time itself would be your spell reaching out and altering the entirety of the world, save for yourself.”

Mitsuko nodded slowly. “I think I get what you’re saying. Still.. I’m just mostly going to think of it as slowing down time.”

Verna shrugged. “Do as you like.”

“This sounds like an extremely useful ability. I’m only at the first level, though. What are the drawbacks?”

“You can only target yourself. Everything will appear around half speed. And the spell can only be held for about ten seconds of your perceived seconds for every minute. At full capacity, at least. As I’m certain you’re aware by now, there are a ton of annoying mathematical equations that go into the duration of your temporal spells. We designed the transfer of power to a balanced point where your body should never be in danger of dying of blood loss due to spellcasting. Assuming, of course, you don’t alter your body in weird ways.”

“So, if I slowed down time to like a fourth normal speed? Would it not be twenty seconds for every minute?”

“Nope. It will be longer, but not exactly twenty seconds. Like I said, it gets complicated. Experiment with it a bit next loop.” Verna strolled past her and stared up at the hole melted into the ceiling. “I don’t suppose you’ll allow me to interrupt your current deal with Juniper? It would be convenient if I could teach you in person next loop. Also, I’d really like to get out of this damn cage. My brain is turning into mush, staring at the ice for days on end.”

“Maybe,” Mitsuko said hesitantly. “I’d want to talk to Sterling about it before I agree.”

“Oh, he’ll agree. Pilroth likes me. I’m, easily, his favorite.”

“You don’t have a vendetta against him like the other sages?”

Verna was still grinning when she looked down from the ceiling hole. “Oh, don’t get me wrong, your pal Sterling fucked up big time in the last Prismatic Spiral. His over-confidence and snooty attitude only served to add additional salt to the wound when he eventually failed in his mission. But he’s not a bad guy. I like him. He likes me. We’re old pals who respect one another. Even before we initiated the Prismatic Spiral, I was the only friend he still had alive. After all, we are the only two sages who were effectively immortal. Him through his spellcraft and I through my method of creation.”

Mitsuko shivered. It had nothing to do with the cold. “Immortality must be a lonely existence.”

“That is an obstacle for you to clear after you complete your quest on the Prismatic Archipelago,” Verna replied. For that statement, her voice had dropped much of its jovial jaunt. “Put it out of your mind for now.”

“You don’t have any advice?” Mitsuko asked.

“Advice? For immortality?” Verna lifted her hand and examined it for a long minute. “Don’t let it stagnate you. I am the Sage of Dilation. I know better than all others how easy it is to take tomorrow for granted. You move through time at your own pace. However, that doesn’t make the world move differently. You’re only one, miniscule piece of the universe. Don’t let it pass you by. There are things even Pilroth Sterling Novryss III, Great Sage of Tempus, could not Mend.”
