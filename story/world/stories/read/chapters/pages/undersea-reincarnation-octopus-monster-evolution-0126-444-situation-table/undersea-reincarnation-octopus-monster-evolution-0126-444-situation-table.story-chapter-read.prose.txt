The morning was still celebratory, but it was also much, much more serious. Around the spreads of jam, breads, and smoked fish, the senior leadership of Thalis—which basically included Zerathstra, Arisia, and a couple of administrative people Henry knew from afar—sat around an improvised situation table while Henry, Maurice, and Velistraine gave a more detailed briefing of what they had seen, what they had theorized, and what Henry had done since they came back.

“That’s what the Depth Lord called the set? The Founders’ Key?” Zerathstra asked, eyes narrowed in concentration, to which Henry nodded. Zerathstra frowned deeper, blinking more and more as if trying to recall something, but then he clicked his tongue and leaned back, deflating. “I can’t remember the details. But I’ve heard of it being mentioned. Now it makes sense that I inherited one of the orbs,” he muttered.

The conversation moved on from there toward the next steps, which Henry was already preparing for. Because through the night, his scouting Avatars had reached their targets. Somewhat. And now, there were three big tasks on his to-do list—and plenty of smaller ones, like his training Avatars, but those could wait. The first of the three was the chat with Wolf’s Den’s Warden. That one had been ready to tackle since yesterday, but Henry had taken a break. Today he’d deal with it. The second task was retrieving the Reach’s orb, and right now, the path toward it was pointing at an oddly flat plateau in the middle of the forest, one that was giving the flora of the region a hard time. Trees were a bit more sparse here, but everything was still covered.

Henry suspected ruins. He’d know for sure once he sent his Avatars down.

As for the third item, it was for the orb lost in the depths. The spot in question—from the surface—looked like any other patch on the high seas. It was a bit stormy, and the waves were massive, threatening to reach him with every swell, even though he was floating nearly a couple hundred feet in the air, but the path toward the orb was pointing straight down. Now how deep Henry would need to dive, he didn’t know. But his Avatar was in position.

Staring into the tumultuous waters for a moment, Henry pulled his attention away for a moment.

“I’ll talk to the Warden first, then I’ll start scouting the area in the Reach. For Nymara and where it has sunk, I’ll be leaving that for last,” Henry decided. He didn’t know what exactly was waiting for him down there, but he’d rather have everything else handled first. Just in case the abyssal waters turned out to be more of a pain in the ass than he expected. He’d give it all of his attention then.

With all that said, the most shocking fact was that all of these scouts, all of these Avatars were well within his operating range. With how large each relay’s area of effect had become and with how many Avatars he had at his disposal, Henry could command and switch places with his Avatars all over the world, right from Thalis. Which was a detail more than one individual was picking up on. Zerathstra was impressed, while Velistraine shrugged and said it took the fun out of traveling. As for the others, they had a more calculating look in their eyes.

“Once this is all over, you’re going to get a lot of attention for this network of yours and how far it spreads. You’re going to worry a lot of powerful beings,” Arisia said, a hint of worry in her voice. “Especially if our theory about the ascended turns out to be right.”

Henry definitely could see that becoming a problem. But he also, frankly, could not care less. Maybe it was the fact that he had his arms full with more important matters. Maybe it was the end of the world over the horizon that made politics feel trivial, or maybe it was just that he was still feeling disconnected from it all, and that he could just leave any potential annoyance and hop to the other side of the world. Whatever the reason was, he didn’t care. But he didn’t say that. Instead, he simply got to work.

“The Reach first, then,” he said, giving Maurice a nod, then shooting Velistraine a glance. “Ready?”

Velistraine slapped her knees and got up, giving him a mock-salute. “Diplomat Velistraine reporting for duty.”

Henry and everyone stared for a beat, then someone spoke. “You sure we don’t have anyone else?” muttered one of Thalis’s leaders, leaning over the table, only for Velistraine to click her tongue at him, at which point the man just gave her a fake, sweet smile, but he didn’t straighten up, and he didn’t take his words back. Velistraine’s eyes narrowed, ready to argue, only for Henry’s arm to land on her shoulder and on Maurice’s shell.

“Well. See you all in a bit,” he said, stalling any further shenanigans, then teleported them out.

***

The first stop was at the Emerald Isles, where Henry dropped off Velistraine so she could send some messages around and go update Attal. While there was plenty of errands Henry could run with his Avatars with minimal effort, he didn’t have to—or want to—do everything by himself, not to mention, Velistraine wanted to stretch her legs as well, understandably, considering her last few months. And so with that in mind, Henry and Maurice found themselves hovering over the oddly flat plateau, somewhere deep within the lights of the Pillar that touched down in the Reach, and left Velistraine to tell everyone who might be able to contribute to be ready.

Well. Everyone that wasn’t in the Reach, that was.

“Give me… let’s say five minutes before I’m fully here,” Henry said to Maurice. “In the meantime, let’s start scouting.”

“You’re gonna talk to the Wolf person?”

“Yup,” Henry confirmed as his Avatar stepped out from cover and made its way toward Wolf’s Den’s entrance. Naturally, his presence was very quickly noticed, and a squad of armed and armored warriors came out to greet him. Unsurprisingly, they were a bit jumpy at the lone stranger walking toward them, and likely even further unnerved by the vibrant yellow eyes.

Henry wasn’t sure when it had started exactly, but just like Velistraine, his eyes had gained an odd sheen to them, which really made him look inhuman. Henry could have tried to mellow it down, but why bother?

He wasn’t human. And he wasn’t really trying to pass himself off as one.

The next couple of standoffish minutes went by without incident. Henry made his request to speak to the Warden, and someone went to get him without much of a hassle. As for the Warden himself, he was… easy going. He recognized Henry, but hadn’t said much about their last encounter. Henry simply told him of the threat outside of the world, what might soon happen, and showed him the proof by arraying the bodies of the creatures, which the Warden examined himself along with a couple of bookish-looking people who’d appeared along with the leader’s entourage, and then… it was over. Henry had been ready for an annoying and arduous back and forth, but the Warden had simply said that he’d be ready, and to keep him informed. He also said that he’d get in touch with the other Wardens—which Henry realized was the term for the protectors out here—and then everyone went their own way. The Warden and his people went back to their settlements, the weaker of them shooting worried glances back toward the Avatar flying away, and Henry turned his full attention back to their scouting.

“Alright, then,” he said, reviewing what his autopilot and Maurice had managed to uncover. “What’s going on here?”

“Big city and a lot of green monkeys. Sort of like the fire one, somehow. But these ones don’t have fire. They just try and grab you with the vines when you pass by,” Maurice quickly resumed while Henry reviewed what the two of them had seen. Effectively, this had been once a human settlement before the forest had reclaimed it, considering the few towers that hadn’t crumbled yet and the rooms therein, and once Henry scared the crap out of the B-ranks and slapped the A-rank apes around a couple of times, the local fauna steered clear while Henry and Maurice looked around.

Most of the city’s remains were under a heavy layer of soil and leaves, which would have made retrieving the orb a pain in the arms if they had to search. But the ring they’d gotten from collecting the other orbs was still guiding them, and as Henry and Maurice got started on lifting heavy clumps of soil and dead roots, one of the larger buildings around was slowly uncovered, seeing the light of day for the first time in at least a couple of centuries, Henry thought.

The work was fun, and it felt nice. Henry and Maurice chatted and snacked while they worked, occasionally throwing a fruit or a piece of meat toward the apes, until nearly half an hour later, most of the structure was uncovered. To Henry, it looked like a palace, or maybe a temple. It was tall and pyramidal, at least a couple hundred feet tall and three hundred wide, with multiple entrances at the base level, and many windows throughout its sides. It must have looked amazing once, before it was covered with dirt and moss, not to mention the blown-up and molten crater on its east-side facade.

“So it’s in there?” Maurice asked, eyeing the massive structures as the apes hooted and hollered behind them.

“It is,” Henry simply said, and without much of a fuss, the two floated in through the crater and followed the golden thread down the depths of the structure.
