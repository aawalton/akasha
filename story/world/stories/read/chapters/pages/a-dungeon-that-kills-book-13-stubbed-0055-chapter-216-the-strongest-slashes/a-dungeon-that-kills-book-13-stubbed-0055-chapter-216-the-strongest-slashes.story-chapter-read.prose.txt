Flying out of the hollow rock, Viktor activated a Thauma.

Water Scabbard.

Simple little thing. It did exactly what its unassuming name suggested: conjure a sheath made of water to wrap around his sword.

Well, actually, it had one more trick. At any point during its duration, he could order the water to peel away and reveal the blade underneath. As a result, his weapon could now be sheathed and unsheathed at will, requiring nothing from him but the barest flicker of thought.

The first benefit of such a Thauma was obvious enough. It saved him the precious seconds he would otherwise have to spend drawing the sword free and returning it to its scabbard. But more importantly, it allowed the Reliquary to keep recharging the whole time. He could fight while the sheath remained on, and in that state the artifact behaved like any ordinary rapier, albeit one very wet. But he could also, at any moment he chose, turn it into the blade of light that could cut through everything. And what he was going to do was this: swing the sheathed rapier at his opponent, switch it to the blade of light a heartbeat before contact, then wrap it with water again after it finished doing whatever unpleasant business it had been sent to do.

The greatest drawback of this Reliquary of his was that he needed to return the blade to its scabbard from time to time to let it recharge, which prevented him from using it continuously in a prolonged fight. But now, it stayed sheathed the whole time, only coming out for barely a second each time he swung it. Thus, he could fight for as long as his arm could hold out. A perfect solution. He was rather pleased with himself for having figured out this trick.

He closed on the Apostle, on that ruin of a body still caught in the throes of its own desperate regeneration, on the sprawling web of flesh that stretched between the scattered pieces of meat, straining to drag every last of them back into a whole. It would be very convenient if he could locate the brain amid this grotesque tangle, but that wasn’t really necessary. He would simply tear everything apart.

Of course, there was always a chance the bastard still had some other trick up his sleeve, yet another hidden power that could help him wriggle free from this predicament. But Viktor knew better than to let such thoughts slow him down. Sometimes you had to take the risk in front of you. This was, without a doubt, the best opportunity he could hope for to rid himself of this dangerous enemy for good, and he would be damned if he let it slip away.

He neared the web of flesh, now anchored to one of the floating platforms, and the mass shuddered before weaving its strands of tissue into several long, groping tentacles that lashed out toward him.

He drew a breath and then ran through the routine he had drilled into his arm a hundred times: swing, light on, light off, rinse and repeat. A rhythm that was as natural to him as the beating of his own heart. Each time his hand moved, another tentacle was cut down and fell away.

The Apostle must have realized that flesh alone wasn’t enough to keep the deadly blade off him, so he changed his tactics accordingly. The next batch of tentacles came wrapped in bone, which made them look similar to the bone whip he had wielded earlier. But they were shorter and considerably more sluggish. The man must have been severely weakened, then. Good.

Viktor flowed through the onslaught, rapier snapping up to parry the tentacles while his eyes hunted for the joints, the gaps where the bone plates gave way to something softer. And whenever an opportunity presented itself, the blade of light surged to life and severed the armored tentacle as easily as it did anything else.

One by one, the glistening limbs fell. Better still, he was killing them faster than his opponent could grow replacements. And so, bit by bit, he closed the distance to the ravaged body, which had stopped trying to mend itself, as all its strength was now focused on the desperate task of keeping him at bay.

Chitin and bone began to form around the various organs scattered throughout the web of flesh. An attempt to shield them from harm, obviously. But it was futile. If he couldn’t cut through the shell, he would just cut around it, sever the threads connecting them to the web.

His hand never stopped. Slash, and slash, and slash again. Light flared into being only to vanish again a heartbeat later, an endless blur of water-wrapped mithril occasionally interrupted by that momentary flash. And with each swing, another chunk of the web tore away, severed and sent tumbling through the air. A liver ringed with teeth. A heart encased in chitin. A hand cradled in a tiny ribcage. One gruesome trophy after another rained down onto the cathedral below.

And then, buried under layer after layer of glistening strands, he found the head. Its earlier carefree, almost mocking expression was gone, replaced by something far more serious. Was the brain hidden inside that skull, or had it relocated somewhere else within this monstrous, sprawling mass? Didn’t matter. He took the head off anyway.

However, the moment the head was separated from the rest of the web, thin threads of flesh shot out from the stump of its neck and reattached it to the greater mass, accompanied by many strands of grey hair doing the exact same thing.

Oh?

Everything Viktor had lopped off up to this point had simply fallen away like dead meat, as though its connection to the main body was the only thing that gave it any purpose. But this severed head’s behavior was completely different. And that could only mean one thing: it was the main body itself.

So the brain really is inside the skull, huh?

He allowed himself a satisfied smile as his swings came faster, severing every thread the head threw out before it could take hold. Yes, this was the best approach. If he attempted to go straight for the kill, the brain could flee to some other distant part of the web. Better to isolate it from the rest first, and butcher it after.

As his right hand kept up its ceaseless rhythm with the rapier, his left simultaneously flung sphere after sphere of roaring fire into the mess around the head. After all, in the brief moment when the body lost its connection to the brain, it stopped counting as a Reliquary and reverted to ordinary flesh, and fire should be able to deal real, lasting damage to it. Also, dozens of Templars had arrived on the scene, starting to hack into the web with zealous enthusiasm.

Suddenly, the Apostle’s head tore itself free from the web. At the same time, a pair of wings sprouted from the crown of its skull, and it flew away.

Running now, are we? Viktor chuckled. The man must have realized that clinging to this wreckage wasn’t going to save him, so he cut his losses and fled with what mattered. Sensible.

So Viktor turned as well and flew after him in pursuit.

Is he... going after the Tengu?

He was. The winged head was homing in on the nearest of the bird-men, no doubt about it.

“Celeste, tell the Tengu to be careful. Keep them well clear of that head.”

[Understood.]

The head might look small, but it could still contain a large amount of flesh, enough to spread wide as a sheet and swallow a bird-man whole, replenishing a bit of the mass it had lost.

Fortunately, after being warned by Celeste, the Tengu immediately scattered and retreated. And so the battle above the cathedral devolved into an absurd game of tag between airborne poultry and a disembodied flying head. A scene that might have looked almost comical if you forgot about the horror of the situation for a second.

Also, it was odd that the head had gone after the Tengu instead of Sebekton, who was much slower and would have been an easier catch. Then again, perhaps the Apostle had been weakened so badly he was no longer able to consume someone of the Crocodilian’s size. Either way, Viktor didn’t waste time wondering about it and went straight after the winged head.

Instead of chasing it from behind, he cut across to intercept it head-on. And once he was close, he brought the blade around in an arc of radiance, slicing cleanly across the Apostle’s face.

And the head bounced away.

What?

Instead of splitting cleanly in two, it was simply knocked back. A gash opened up across the face, but it was already starting to knit itself back together, and the man had the gall to grin at him before flying off.

Damn it!

That was his skull getting in the way, wasn’t it? If the bastard reinforced the bone plate around his brain, then it stood to reason that even the blade of light couldn’t cut through. Which meant, the Apostle was practically invincible in this form.

Seriously?

Viktor had done everything. He had blown his enemy up, dismantled him organ by organ, reduced him to nothing but a single fleeing head. Yet here he was, unable to land the one blow that would finish the job.

“Hit it harder,” that woman had said. So it comes down to this in the end anyway, huh? But how, exactly, was he supposed to hit harder than this? Khenemhotep’s beam of green light was no longer available, so the rapier was the strongest card he had at hand, and it was still not enough.

Did he have to resort to “Rekindled Ember”? Dealing himself a lethal blow to activate the Supreme Thauma and give himself the raw strength he needed to crack that skull? That might work, but...

No. There was one more thing he could try.

“Celeste,” Viktor called his Dungeon Core. “Tell Sebekton to unleash his crescent of destruction. The very one he has once used to cleave the water realm.”

[But... Master, can he really hit a target that agile?]

Viktor chuckled. “I’m not asking him to strike at the Apostle. I’m asking him to strike at me.”

[Master!?]

“Just do it.”

[...Understood.]

Looking down, he saw his Guardian shift his stance. Sebekton hauled the massive axe back over one shoulder, roared loud enough to shake the platform he stood on, and spun his whole enormous bulk into the swing. The Reliquary followed, screaming through a wide and brutal arc, and with it came the invisible crescent of devastating force.

Viktor brought his rapier up before his chest, guard raised, and activated a Thauma.

Air Parry.

It allowed him to catch the air slash Sebekton launched the instant it touched his blade and turn it aside. He had tried this technique before, only for the fun of seeing if it worked. Never once had he imagined he would actually need it for anything that mattered.

As he saw the faint shimmer rippling across the water covering his rapier, the signal that the crescent had arrived, he swept the blade. But unlike the last time, when he swatted it away without giving any thought to where it would land, this time he followed it, stayed with it, kept his sword pressed to its edge so that he could have a say in its trajectory.

The two of them, man and crescent both, went tearing through the sky together. He sent a message via Celeste to the Tengu currently being chased by the winged head, instructing him to fly toward him. Then, at the last possible moment, the bird-man veered sharply upward, pulling himself out of harm’s way just as the crescent slammed into the Apostle’s face.

And, right at that precise instant of impact, Viktor willed the water scabbard to part away, and the blade of light flared to life.

Two strongest slashes.

Landing at the exact same place.

At the exact same time.

The resistance gave way. The face crumbled inward under the combined force of both strikes. Skin and bone and brain matter vanished in an instant, boiled away between the two slashes.

Is it... over?

That was when he heard a voice. A whisper, practically breathed directly into his ear, close enough that he nearly looked over his shoulder to find the source.

“You won this round, Thaumaturgist.”

What?

He hung in the air, dumbfounded for a long moment. Then, he called his Dungeon Core.

“Celeste... did we win?”

[Yes, Master. I have gotten that power. The human Reliquary has been destroyed, without any doubt.]

Then who the hell had spoken to him just now?

A chill ran down his spine as an unwelcome thought dawned upon him.

There was a question he had been asking himself: who was the one controlling that body, exactly? The original soul was long gone, since the body had to be soulless before it could be converted into a Reliquary. So it couldn’t be it. He had then suspected it was the soul that had become the essence powering the artifact. Then again, why was it sentient, instead of staying dormant like those in any other Reliquary?

But.

What if—that body was indeed empty, with no shred of free will inside it?

What if—it was a mere puppet controlled by someone far away, similar to how he controlled the dead bodies using Khenemhotep’s ringed crosses?

What if—the man he had been fighting this whole time...

...was the Abyss itself?
