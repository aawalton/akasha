Jessie wore a suit.

It was a sign of how seriously she was taking this Governing Council meeting; she’d only suited up once or twice before. Her laptop was tucked into a bag that hung from her shoulders, her tablet rested in the crook of her left arm, and her right held her cane. She looked every inch the picture of a serious, no-nonsense sixteen-year-old.

I wasn’t fooled, though.

She’d armored herself for battle, but she was hurting. The way she moved made it obvious; this was going to be a taxing, difficult meeting for her.

“You’re sure you’re up for this?” I asked.

Her eyes locked onto mine, blazing. She shook her head. “No. But I’m doing it anyway. The guild needs Phoenix to stay stable, and you need the GC’s backing, right?”

“Right.”

“Okay. Great. My chair’s in Deimos’s trunk if I need it. Let’s go.”

Ellen shot Jessie a concerned look. I shrugged. “She says she’s good to go. Let’s do it.”

And just like that, the three of us piled into Deimos and took off through Phoenix’s busy streets. The AI-driven car handled itself, leaving us to talk business as it swerved around semis and passed minivans. By the time we pulled up outside of the GC’s tower, I knew more about the Guardians and Portal Tyrants than I’d ever wanted to know.

The Guardians were the most reclusive of Phoenix’s five major guilds. They occupied the Wickenberg fields, just past the 303 Wall from the Desert Wind’s headquarters, and they protected the farms there from monsters, portal breaks, and the Wickenberg Portal itself. The portal loomed large over Phoenix—it was dangerous to have a portal that strong less than five miles from the Wall—but every time closing it came up, the rewards of keeping it outweighed the risks. Water poured from it in a river that rivaled what the old canal from the north used to bring, and it was pure. It disappeared after a day or two, but that was more than enough time to water the Wickenberg fields.

We’d run into the Guardians’ guild leader once before. Vern Kellen. He was a serious man, and he took his guild’s mandate seriously. I didn’t want to pick a fight with him.

Jessie insisted that if we did this right, it wouldn’t ever come to that.

The Portal Tyrants were an interesting bunch. I’d worked with Terrel Young before, first as a member of the Carlsbad relief convoy, then again when we’d assaulted the caverns themselves. And I’d had my fair share of run-ins with Ezekiel Elwood, their recruiter. And then there’d been our deals, first to get Jessie the care she needed, and then regarding the core break issue. On the surface, their leader seemed like a brute of a man; he was one of the strongest fighters I’d ever seen. But the guild—shockingly—had one of the best general research teams in the city.

As far as I knew, the Portal Tyrants were the closest people on Earth to cracking the core-break problem and learning how to repair them. Granted, they’d learned what they knew from me, but even so, the fact that they understood any of what the God of Thunder had done was impressive. And they’d also done some research into Jessie’s joint problem, but to no avail.

It was that research and development focus that Jessie was hoping to target for our alliance. With Ellen’s core now fully functional, we had a second look at the core repair methods—and one that an Earth-based team might be able to replicate.

Deimos’s doors opened, and I climbed out, then helped Jessie extract herself from the back seat. And only then did I look at the Governing Council’s tower.

It was still in bad shape. Tower cranes surrounded it, and gigantic steel beams and plates had been welded all around the heavily damaged section where Deborah Callahan’s cell had been. In the last hours of the Siege of Phoenix, just before Ellen had broken it with Tides of Darkness, the Placid King had assaulted the tower, ripping her cell free from it and leaving the building gashed and broken. Repair crews were hard at work, and the tower was stable enough to use, but it’d be a long time before the centerpiece of the Fallen Delvers courtyard was restored to its former glory.

“Ready?” Jessie asked, fidgeting with her hair.

I nodded, steeled myself, and followed her into the belly of the beast.

As Ezekiel Elwood followed the Portal Tyrant into the conference room, his eyes kept flicking to Kade Noelstra.

The kid had changed.

He had a gravity about him that Ezekiel hadn’t seen the last time they’d met. It wasn’t just his aura—although static hung thick in the air around him. And it wasn’t just ego. Ezekiel had seen enough delvers with ego to recognize the difference between cockiness and confidence, and Kade was definitely the latter.

Ezekiel was the Portal Tyrants’ best recruiter, and if he was being honest, he was at least partially responsible for the guild’s status as one of the five on the Inner Council. Three, he supposed; the ramifications of the Roadrunners and Coyotes both losing their S-Ranked leaders were going to be massive. Of the eight A-Rankers backing up the Portal Tyrant himself, five were Ezekiel’s recruits. He didn’t find a ton of recruits, but every recruit he brought in fulfilled their potential. That was his real unique skill—not Power of Friendship, but his eye for talent.

He stood behind Terrel Young’s seat, hands behind his back and feet shoulder-width apart, and his eyes met Kade’s. The S-Ranker caught his gaze and held it. Only for a few seconds, though, before he shifted to focus on Councilwoman Myers as she entered the conference room.

But that was plenty.

Ezekiel hadn’t lost many fish. If he got his hooks in someone, he either recruited them or decided they weren’t worth it. They didn’t wriggle free. But Kade had, and that was a shame. Of all the delvers he’d recruited, Kade would have been his crown jewel.

He was a madman, of course. A maniac. Terry had briefed him on their drive over. The news channels only knew the basics: Kade had avenged Angelo Lawrence, he’d hit S-Rank in a hostile portal, and he’d ended the Carlsbad Portal as a threat with the other S-Rankers. No, Terry knew the truth. He, Nathan, and Sarah had lost. They’d been out of the fight, and Bernard had been dead, when Kade came in and single-handedly beaten not only the portal boss, but the thing assisting the Craftsman.

Councilwoman Mysers cleared her throat and stood at the head of the table, next to Nathan Anders. “Thank you all for coming today, Guildmasters Young and Gerald. We have quite a few points on the agenda. Defensive arrangements for the city while the 303 Wall is repaired and reinforced, the need for Carlsbad Fortress now that the portal is closed, and—“

“Councilwoman.”

Ezekiel stared at the girl in the suit. He’d been briefed on her, of course. Guildmaster Jessica Gerald. Kade Noelstra’s half-sister by his mother. She’d never have crossed his radar if it wasn’t for the deal Kade had made with Terry, but she was every bit as much a spitfire as her brother. The suit was a nice touch, too. Well-tailored, serious, but not too formal.

Kade’s hand touched her shoulder, and she nodded as the GC’s leader trailed off. “I had to interrupt your business and shift your priorities around, but we need to talk about my concerns first. The fate of Phoenix—no, of the world—may depend on it.”

One key point that Ellen, Jessie, and I had agreed on was that our biggest enemy in the room wouldn’t be the Guardians’ guild leader—if he showed up—or Councilwoman Myers. It wouldn’t be Ezekiel Elwood, either, even though I didn’t trust him as far as Jessie could throw him.

It would be time and bureaucracy.

And, to her credit, she was trying to cut through the red tape. Her strategy was pretty simple, too. She was opting for chaos and interruptions in an attempt to get inside the Inner Council’s process.

“Miss Gerald, we have procedures for a—“

“Reason, I know. But point of order, Ms. Myers. I’m Guildmaster Gerald. The Desert Wind might not currently be one of the big five guilds, but by the end of this meeting, it will be. You know it, I know it—even my pet lizard knows it.” Jessie didn’t have a pet lizard, but the point stood. “Now, we’ve all had pretty much the same debrief on what happened inside the Carlsbad Portal, right?”

Councilwoman Myers didn’t say anything. Her face was red, and her fists had balled as she glared at my sister. She was stressed out, and we weren’t helping. But that was the point. I locked eyes with her, and she broke first. A second later, Nathan Anders spoke up. “Yes. I debriefed the Governing Council—along with all three living guild leaders and the likely replacements for the fallen—on what happened. I assume Kade told you, Guildmaster Gerald, and I’m equally sure Terrel told everyone in the Portal Tyrants. We should all be on the same page.”

I didn’t miss that Nathan hadn’t referred to the Portal Tyrant by either title or as Guildmaster Young. That made sense; they were battle comrades, and while this felt like a formal meeting, Jessie’s interruption was already changing the calculus.

“Great.” Jessie sounded anything but great; her voice radiated poorly-concealed pain and exhaustion. “You’re missing some context, though.”

“And you’re going to enlighten us?” Myers asked.

Jessie shot a look her way. “Yes. After we deal with the only reason Kade’s taking the time to be here. The Desert Wind wants the Coyotes’ seat on the Inner Council. It’s necessary.”

“Unbelievable,” Myers said. She shook her head. “This wasn’t what we discussed, Jessie.”

“Guildmaster Gerald. And this is exactly what we talked about.” Jessie straightened her back with an audible crack and tiny gasp of pain, then met Myers’s eyes. “The Guardians committed nothing to the Carlsbad operation. They were supposed to assist the fortress assault and back up Angelo Lawrence, but they backed out at the last minute. They kept almost all their strength, and now, they’re undisputably the most powerful of the Phoenix guilds. The Iron Falcons might be close, but—“

“No one cares about the Falcon’s Eye from a political standpoint,” Terrel finished. “He runs Tucson, and we run Phoenix. Simple.”

“Exactly.” Jessie pulled a sheet of paper from her laptop case and set it on the table. “Here’s my evidence.”

This was the part I’d been the most concerned about. To prove that the Guardians were up to something, she’d have to reveal her sources—or at least that she could see their private communications. I’d tried to stop her. There was a big difference between spying on someone and telling the world you were spying on them. But she’d insisted that this was the best way. The only way.

As Myers read through Jessie’s printed-out logs of the Guardians’ innermost communications, her breathing shifted. It was subtle, but she’d been thrown off-balance by Jessie’s initial attack, and as she pretended to re-read the paper over and over, I recognized exactly what was happening. She was using the time to recalibrate—and to think through her next moves.

Jessie had misplayed, but not in the way I’d expected.

Sure enough, Myers handed the paper to Nathan and cleared her throat. “Your sources are very good. You have someone inside the Guardians, too?”

“Too?” Jessie’s eyebrow raised. “No, even better. I’m looking at their unfiltered, raw communications. I know some people who are good at that. Find weaknesses, exploit human nature, get passwords, collect data.”

“Including mine?”

Jessie met Councilwoman Myers’s eyes. “At least once. You’re not immune from making mistakes.”

I cleared my throat. “Councilwoman, if we’re going to waste time, I have other things to do.”

And to my surprise, the hawklike, predatory expression vanished. She rested her hands on the wooden table and stared at me, then glanced down.

“This looks legitimate, Harriet,” Nathan said.

“Councilwoman Myers,” she said automatically. Jessie raised an eyebrow—the councilwoman was lucky she hadn’t stuck her tongue out or burst into laughter instead. A momentary blush crossed her face before she forced herself to regain her composure. “Fine. We’ll table this…spying…thing for now. What do you want, Guildmaster Gerald?”

“First, I want to help you maintain Phoenix political balance. Without the Roadrunners and Coyotes, you’re looking at chaos—whether it comes from the Guardians or from a dozen smaller guilds agitating to fill the power vacuum.”

“Like the Desert Wind?” Myers asked acidly.

“Exactly like the Desert Wind,” Jessie agreed. “I’m blatantly attempting to grab power, and I think I have an offer you won’t be able to overlook. Kade Noelstra alone gives the Desert Wind enough firepower to be a top-tier guild—in the five or six strongest, at the least. He’s that good. I’m also seeing a massive increase in people trying to join. That includes members of both the Roadrunners and Coyotes. We’ll have four delving teams going by the end of the week, and probably seven or eight by next month. That’s not quite what the Roadrunners were pulling, but it’s close. It’s around the Coyotes’ force level.”

“You’re still lacking firepower,” Terrel Young said. He’d been quiet for most of the meeting, and behind him, Ezekiel shifted. A smile crossed the recruiter’s face; those eight or so A-Rankers in the Tyrants put them well ahead of everyone but the Guardians.

“Incorrect. We should have a second S-Ranker within thirty-six hours. Is that enough firepower for you?” Jessie glanced at Ellen.

“How?” Terrel asked. “We’ve hit a dead-end in all our research into core breaks, even with the information you provided us, Kade. It’s just not possible to fix a broken core. Not with the technology we have on Earth.”

I took over. “Six hours ago, Delver Ellen Traynor and I entered a portal world under the control of an SS-Ranked Paragon. We negotiated a deal with the people—not monsters, but people—under his tutelage, and Ellen’s core was repaired from a fractured state. While her core was being repaired, she was able to learn more about the process, and as part of my sister’s negotiations here, she and I are prepared to share everything we know about core repairs with the Portal Tyrants’ research team. Nothing left out this time. All of it.”

That got Terrel’s attention. His eyes flicked from Jessie to Ellen, and then to me. Then he nodded. “Deal.”

“You can’t unilaterally make a deal like that,” Councilwoman Myers interrupted. “This is a full-council decision, not a Portal Tyrants one.”

“Oh?” Jessie said quietly. “Then I guess we shouldn’t tell them about Tanat, huh?”

Silence. Absolute silence, from Terrel, Nathan, and Myers. I suppressed my grin, but it was close. Then, slowly, Ezekiel Elwood broke the silence. “Who…who is Tanat?”

Checkmate.
