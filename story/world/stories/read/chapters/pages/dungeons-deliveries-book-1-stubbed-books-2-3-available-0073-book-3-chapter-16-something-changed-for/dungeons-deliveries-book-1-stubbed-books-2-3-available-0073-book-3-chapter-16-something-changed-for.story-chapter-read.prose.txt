Pandemonium ensued across the region of Toronto.

Everyone knew about Tax Guild Stones in some capacity. Most said they granted multipliers to power, which they did. Some said they were useless except for stealing Credits, which they kind of were. Longstanding holders from the thousands of regions across Earth learned early to fortify themselves, hide their Stones well, and keep their traps shut. Occasionally, which meant hundreds of times over the twenty years or so the System had come, the internet was blessed with footage of a short-term holder losing their marbles, either ranting pasty mouthed and paranoid from a hovel, or claiming themselves ascended gods from the highest building in the region. Famously, one holder walked into a volcano with the stone, but not before telling the locals they were made of fire-resistant jellybeans. That Tax Guild Stone remains unclaimed.

Whatever they truly did, and whatever their holders were granted access to, people, monsters, and beings not quite either all agreed on one thing. Things got terribly ugly when the local Tax Guild Stone was unclaimed. Does it really matter who casts the first punch or beheads the nearest shift-eyed neighbour? What really holds the façade of civility together other than collective agreement?

The game of finder’s keepers began, and the only one who knew where the stone hid was a dying dog. Everything else was hearsay.

Alex was right, he knew that as he could feel his emotions again, and escaping the dangers of rushing Stone searchers was the safest choice, especially with his injuries. The slobbering tongue gave another lick.

Brody could feel the pup reaching towards him with his Core again between the licks. Reaching for what, he couldn’t say, but it was as if the dog was calling towards his being.

What do you want, buddy?, Brody thought as he scratched where he just knew it was most bothersome, though he hadn’t a clue as to how he knew.

Alex flicked the idea that seemed to press into his again. “I’ll…I’ll bring the dog to safety, but I think it’s best Brody if you go back to the—”

An aged growl escaped the dog’s throat, smoky and grinding from being infused with essence and replaced with metal over the decades. He wasn’t any simple dog and didn’t even particularly like his old name. These two didn’t know it, thankfully, so he’d forget it too. The pitbull turned his massive skull towards Alex, hurting something awful inside and out. He hurt, but he’d heard and understood enough and was tired not getting what he wanted.

The dog, nameless and without a master, blinked intelligent eyes at Alex before turning back to the one who had freed him from the itchy connection. His brain felt less foggy, like he’d been hiding behind a curtain, and now he could see. The human who’d freed him was strange smelling, but he could tell his heart was good. That was the kind of master he wanted. That was the kind of master he could bring to the buried goods of his previous master too.

He called out again from his Core and hoped the human would have him. They both needed it; the dog could tell. The human wanted something he couldn’t place words behind, and the dog needed to connect again or fall apart and die. He didn’t want to die, not when he could now see.

Something reached out for the dog’s Core. His tail wagged again, painfully arthritic without the drip of essence to keep him held together from a link, but he managed to look at the human he wanted to connect with.

“Tank,” Brody said as he sat up shakily. “I…think a good name is Tank. Do you like…that name?”

Tank, named once again, tackled the human with the full force of a loving dog.

Tank reached, and reached, and reached again as he lapped at the human’s face, and finally, he felt his new human reach back. It was a familiar connection, but much purer this time.
