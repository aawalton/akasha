The campfire crackled, sending a cloud of embers drifting through the night air as Asher stared hard into the dancing flames. Even now, he could see the flickering fires of the different liches he and his team had gone up against over the past few weeks. The chilling, dark purple fires in Zygrul’s sockets, which turned black whenever the lich pulled upon the power of its Void element. The bright, tender touch of pink fire representing the Love element of Hylm, the first of the liches Asher had slain. The dirty, putrid brown flames that reminded him of rotten blood that had once acted as the King of Death’s eyes, right up until Asher had separated his head from his body only a few hours ago.

And the hard, green fires that danced weakly within the sockets that belonged to Kroult. The lich who had been sealed away within a coffin of soulsteel for the last six hundred and thirty-two years. Freed by Asher after all this time with the intention of slaying him. Only…

He hadn’t been slain just yet.

“Are you serious?” Rulfar asked, the large bloodhound looking at him like he'd been replaced with an imposter. The northerner’s muscular arms tensed as he gripped the great axe lying across his lap, and Asher swore he heard the wooden shaft creaking despite the enchantment giving it strength. “You want to work with him?!”

“I thought the whole point of what we were doing was to kill these things?” Moxy added, stoking the fire with a small air blast as she raised an eyebrow at Asher. Her usual, manic grin she wore during combat was nowhere to be found, replaced instead by a look of genuine confusion. “Aren’t these guys responsible for like every bad thing that’s ever happened to this continent?”

“I doubt we can blame them for everything…” Samantha said, the hunter giving Asher a look that was equal parts worry and anger. He didn’t even have to meet her gaze to know what was going through her mind. Horntho Village, the only home she’d ever known up until a few weeks ago, had been attacked and razed to the ground by the Council of Death’s undead hands. “But we can certainly blame them for certain things.”

“I know the liches have done some seriously horrendous stuff,” Asher started, reaching out and taking Samantha’s hand in his own. “But it was Zygrul and its summoned demons that destroyed Horntho Village. Kroult has been trapped underground, unable to move so much as a single rotting muscle for over six hundred years. It’s not fair of us to push the blame for everything the Council of Death has been doing onto his shoulders.”

“So he was a saint before he got tossed in prison, huh?” Moxy asked, giving him a pointed look. “Just went around kissing babies and handing out flowers?”

“What did Zygrul’s journal say about him?” Rulfar asked. The man was always the first one to come up with a logical solution to any problem despite his size leading many to assume he was nothing but a mindless brute. “What do we actually know about this lich?”

Not too long ago, Asher and his team had led an assault directly onto Zygrul’s hidden mountain base located on the edge of the Noala Kingdom. They’d taken down a horrifying creature from the abyss summoned to act as a guard dog, slain a greater demon, and even took out their first lich. The only problem was that it hadn’t been the lich they had gone there seeking.

Zygrul had revealed only then not only that they had been dancing to its tune the entire time, but that it had a Void element as well. The one and only element banned by every country across the whole world due to its destructive potential. The country of Gostoria, once the largest and most advanced country in the world, had been the first country to ever attempt to research the Void, and had sent out missives informing the rest of the world they’d managed to manifest a Void element.

The next day, the landmass making up the entire country and the fifty million people living within vanished without a trace.

Before taking his whole mountain base into the void with him, Zygrul had left them thirteen journals, each one filled with information on the other liches making up the Council of Death. While Asher could only be left to wonder why exactly Zygrul wanted to throw its fellow liches under the bus, he hadn’t been one to leave such knowledge behind. Some journals were little more than a single half-page of information, while others were the size of small books. He’d been forced to actually read them rather than just tap them with Incorporate due to some potentially unknown enchantment laid into the paper, but even so, they’d already utilized the information from one journal to take down the King of Death.

“The information we were left on Kroult is pretty limited,” Asher admitted, pulling the scattered sheets from his Personal Rift and passing it around. “He’s the youngest of the liches, having only become a lich about seven hundred years ago and joining up with the Council almost immediately. So he was only with them for about sixty years before they began their plan to form the Eternity element and decided to encase him in a coffin of soulsteel.”

“If he’s so ‘young’, maybe that’s why he was against the idea in the first place,” Rulfar said, skimming through the papers. “The other liches would have had centuries, possibly even longer, to grow tired of their undead forms. He, on the other hand, would have only been a lich for a few decades. Makes sense he wasn’t as interested in potentially using the Eternity element to recapture his lost humanity.”

“Still not hearing anything about those babies,” Moxy said, snatching the papers from Rulfar and ignoring his shout of disapproval as she skimmed through them. “Wandering wizard who traveled the lands looking for unique and interesting skills… linked to a number of high-profile thefts and a few prominent murders here and there… What in the realms is this? I’m pretty certain I’ve done more bad stuff than this lich has!”

“Which is exactly why we’re having this conversation in the first place,” Asher said, not having taken his eyes off Samantha and the pain on her face. “I’d gotten so caught up in the excitement over wiping out the entirety of the Council and getting two liches in one shot that I didn’t really stop to think if any of them deserved a second chance. Obviously as part of the Council the rest of them should all burn in the nine hells for all eternity, but again, Kroult functionally wasn’t a part of the Council all this time. You guys should have seen what I did. The tiny room dug a hundred feet below the earth, filled with nothing more than an iron maiden built of pure soulsteel designed to keep him trapped and unable to do a damn thing… Actually, let me just show you.”

Getting nods from around the campfire, Asher reached out with Distortion, laying a finger on one forehead after another as he gently pushed his memory of discovering the bound lich into their heads. Samantha hesitated for a moment before sighing and nodding her consent as well. Using the second-tier effect of Incorporate like this required a willing participant, and Asher gave her a small smile as he handed her the memory.

“Realms…” Rulfar started, his eyes wide as he finished experiencing Asher’s memory. Asher hadn't left anything out, giving them the memory from the time he’d started down that long, freezing tunnel to removing the soulsteel prison and watching the lich collapse onto the floor with hundreds of holes punched all throughout his body where skill-dampening spikes had been moments before. “To think he spent the last six hundred years like that…”

“That’s seriously messed up,” Moxy frowned, shivering as she came out of the memory. “I don’t think even Loratta could have come up with something as evil as that, and I once watched her turn into slime and literally drown a person by flowing down their throat.”

“Where is he now?” Samantha asked, rubbing her eyes as she came out of the memory and asked the most obvious question. “You didn’t just leave him there, right?”

“No… he’s in my Personal Rift,” Asher admitted. “Soulsteel keeps one from recovering tritessence as well as using their skills. I may or may not have stabbed him with the blade I got from Smiles in order to keep him harmless while we figured all this out. Surprisingly, he didn’t even fight me on doing so, though that might have been because he currently looks like he couldn’t even fight his way out of a paper bag. Even for an undead, six hundred years of not using any skills or muscles did a number on him.”

Smiles was one of the King of Death’s deadliest creations, and the only surviving undead that still didn’t draw breath. After learning how Smiles had been created from the remains of a hero who once attempted to slay the King of Death, Asher had brought the undead’s severed head back to Draken as a temporary solution. Without the King of Death’s powerful Death Aura, Draken was the only person capable of keeping Smiles ‘alive.’ That said, Draken’s aura was much weaker than the King of Death’s had been, and he currently needed to hit Smiles with a concentrated blast from his Death Aura every few hours. As such, Draken had informed him he only had a few days to figure out a better solution before he ditched the head for good.

“I want to speak to him,” Samantha said, her eyes cold as her grip tightened around his fingers. “I want to talk to the lich. If I don’t like what I hear… we kill him like we were originally planning to.”

“He could be a powerful asset to our cause against the Council,” Rulfar said, sounding like he’d switched over to Asher’s side after reading about what little crimes the lich had committed and seeing Asher’s memory of finding him sealed in his tomb. “We could certainly use a powerful wizard with what we’re trying to do.”

“Eh, is he really that powerful?” Moxy asked, looking unimpressed as she tossed Asher back the sheets of paper. “He was only a lich for a few decades before being imprisoned, which means he wouldn’t have had centuries to practice magic or hone his craft like the rest of the Council. He’s basically a baby lich. Hardly even worth the effort of killing if you ask me.”

“Regardless, I want to talk to him,” Samantha repeated, still staring hard into Asher’s eyes. “Please.”

Out of all of them, Asher included, there was no question that Samantha had been the one most impacted by the actions of the Council of Death. Sure, Asher had been ripped away from his world and briefly imprisoned, but he’d come to find he enjoyed this world far more than his old one. Back on Earth, he’d just been trudging along, struggling to stay awake through back-to-back shifts at the funeral home as he worked towards finishing his mortician apprenticeship. He hadn’t had a bad life by any means, but he’d been largely alone, far from wealthy, and without any real goals to shoot for. His new life in this world couldn’t be any more different. He had Samantha and her little brother Brian to care for and love, enough shards that he was technically a millionaire, and a mission to give him purpose. If anything, Zygrul had done him a favor.

In stark contrast, the Council of Death had burned Samantha’s family home to the ground and killed just about everyone she’d ever known.

Without a word, Asher nodded, getting up and heading into his Personal Rift to undo the restraints he’d placed upon the lich to keep him from removing the soulsteel sword embedded in his chest. He didn’t have to worry about the lich trashing the contents of his rift as Watcher, one of the King of Death’s powerful Servants, had slipped inside and taken care of that already. Beyond destroying almost all of their potions and shattering a few gemstones, the only real loss she’d caused was the destruction of his final bottle of magadrine powder. Asher had been saving it for a special occasion, and seeing as Eight was quite dead, he wouldn’t be getting any more of the deadly powder anytime soon.

“I hope you’re ready,” Asher said, gripping the lich’s frail arm after freeing him from his bindings. Kroult hadn’t said a single word to him after being freed from his coffin and declaring he would see the Council of Death burn beneath his feet, and Asher could only hope the lich hadn’t gone completely insane during the last six hundred years he had been imprisoned. Focusing on Personal Rift again, Asher nodded toward the floating portal before them that led back to a trio of people who had been training and mentally preparing themselves to slay liches for the past few weeks.

“Time to face the music.”
