Ryland’s world exploded with colours the moment he cast [Omniscience].

He hadn’t used it thus far because his senses had already been effective. His Inscribed Glyph of Detection had already left him certain that Viren and Acrypha’s discovery was sound. This entire structure known as the Crypts was indeed stuffed with Living Mana.

But now that his eyes were empowered with his spell, he saw a great deal more. The walls were crawling with Living Mana. He had spotted the conduits the last time he was here, but now, he saw that those conduits didn’t matter.

More importantly, it was flowing fast. Every iota of the walls, ceiling, and floor was like a rushing river, a tidal flood of Living Mana charging through with incredible intensity.

Ryland was taken aback by how bright it shone in his [Omniscience]-empowered eyes. Now he understood why there were no more of those manasoul fragments. They hadn’t been wiped out as he had assumed earlier. Rather, the Living Mana was simply rushing by so quickly, there was no time for it to leak out and form into something resembling manasouls.

“Sir,” Viren said. His voice was laden with no small amount of concern. “What exactly did you find?”

“Just further proof of your findings,” Ryland said. He had already started scouring his gaze all over the Crypts. The Living Mana wasn’t his main priority right now. First, he had to find Acrypha. “Give me a moment or two, please. Let me see if Acrypha is somewhere here.”

“She has to be, right…?”

Ryland certainly hoped so. [Omniscience] took his sight to every room, every nook and cranny, every passageway, and every single bit of space all throughout the Crypts. He was doing well searching everywhere.

Well, almost everywhere. There were certain locations with extremely thick Inscribed barriers that made his magical questing slip off them. Hmm.

Everything else appeared to be normal. Dead bodies weren’t suddenly coming to life. No magical artifacts were glowing with frightening power. There were a few priests and priestesses, but they stood idly unaware, not attempting something suspicious or malicious. He even found Linsby and Academ Jules, who had clearly spoken with multiple of the clergy without much luck.

And that was the only real point of complaint from Ryland. He had found no sign of Acrypha.

The others hadn’t been successful yet either. Linsby and Academ Jules had clearly interrogated the majority of the priests and priestesses, and their harried expressions clearly professed that they hadn’t found a clue as to the missing student’s whereabouts.

“Viren,” Ryland said. The note of tension in his voice made Viren stand straighter. “I’m going to move [Omniscience] over your eyes.”

“Alright, sir,” Viren said.

“Do not be alarmed by what you see. Simply look at the priestesses and see if any of them appear familiar to you.”

He nodded, then gasped as Ryland’s spell went over his eyes. No doubt, everything revealed by the spell would overwhelm for a few moments. Ryland gently but firmly focused his attention upon the clergy in the Crypts.

“No,” Viren said as [Omniscience] moved on from priestess to priestess. “No. No. No…”

It turned out that the priestess who had more or less abducted Acrypha wasn’t present in the Crypts at all. And she hadn’t informed the rest of her compatriots while doing so. Highly concerning.

“What does this mean, sir?” Viren asked fearfully. “Where have they taken Acrypha?”

They. Whether it was sharpness or plain fear, Viren was probably right in assuming this wasn’t the act of one lone Crypt priestess.

“Well,” Ryland said. “It’s up to us to find out, Viren. Give me a few moments.”

Ryland’s first task was looking deeper into the Crypts itself. He focused on the barriers his initial probes couldn’t pass through. They were quite powerful. The magic imbued upon them prevented him from seeing inside. For the time being, that was. All he had to do was modify a few of the Subglyphs before he was through. Easy.

The sight within had him transfixed for a moment. Now there was the powerful, glowing Relic he hadn’t spotted at the Crypts just yet.

This one was shaped like a ceramic kettle, its spout sucking in the Living Mana when it took on a specific form. Ryland only needed a moment to recognize those were the manasoul fragments. If the Living Mana somehow managed to start becoming Partial Manifestations, then the kettle’s task was trapping those Manifestations.

It was doing a good job too. More and more Living Mana was compressed and stored inside the strange kettle, the mana within turning denser and brighter with every passing second.

Well, that least explained why he hadn’t seen the manasoul fragments outside yet.

Other Relics and powerful Inscriptions were within all the barriers that had initially stopped him. Some amplified the rushing Living Mana. Some moulded and guided it. Some caught the leakages and pushed them back inside the walls and floors and ceilings. Some did even stranger things, like actively changing the conduits in the walls to conduct the Living Mana better.

It formed a strange network Ryland couldn’t completely wrap his head around. Not right now. He was inspecting every individual puzzle piece on its own. What he needed was a proper perspective that would lend him a bird’s eye view of it all.

Something that would be terribly difficult, even for him, just due to how everything was buried so far underground.

“It doesn’t help solve the mystery,” he murmured. “Only adds further questions on top.”

“What’s going on, sir?” Viren asked.

Ryland took a little bit of time to explain everything to him. Viren clearly didn’t understand it all, but he got enough to figure out why Ryland was concerned.

“That doesn’t sound good,” he said. “They’re planning something with the Crypts, aren’t they?”

“Indeed,” Ryland said. “The question remains what exactly that’s supposed to be…”

He was unfortunately on a time crunch, so he decided he’d spend time pondering what it all meant later. Right now, there were other priorities to tend to.

“Come,” he said. “Let’s visit the others. [Translocate].”

Ryland and Viren popped into and out of another gold-edged black rift, appearing right in front of the startled pair of Linsby and Academ Jules.

“Ryland,” the Academ said. “And Viren. I assume you’re here for Acrypha.”

Ryland nodded. “And I assume you’ve found no trace of her yet.”

Both Jules and Linsby shook their heads a little morosely.

“None of the priests and priestess seem to know anything,” Linsby said. His eyes, magnified by his spectacle lenses, made his suspicion even more expressive. “I fear they aren’t being forthright.”

“They have to be hiding something,” Viren said, rather insistently. “I know Acrypha went with a priestess. You can ask Tezar. He was there too.”

Jules frowned. “None of you should have been here in the first place.”

Viren hung his head, swallowing and keeping silent.

Ryland decided to move on. Playing the blame game wasn’t going to help. “I agree with you in essence, Linsby. I don’t know what the clergy are here for, but I have found evidence that there is something going on. Something that goes deeper than any of us are fully capable of fathoming with limited information.”

Linsby and the Academ both looked at Ryland quizzically, who quickly explained everything he had seen and discovered, just as he had done for Viren. If they had been concerned before, once Ryland was finished, they were now very worried.

“What could this all mean?” Jules asked. “Especially now? When the entirety of Rhel is being abandoned?”

Ryland shook his head. That yet another point he had failed to consider. Rhel was due for an end. Yet, the Demiurge and his followers were keeping the Crypts running. In fact, they had changed its function slightly, going by some of those Relics and Inscriptions. To what end?

“This is getting serious,” Linsby said. “I say we head straight to the Demiurge himself. Let’s ask Kindly Mavar what’s happened to Acrypha.”

A bold move. One that Ryland was fond of. But first…

“One moment, please,” he said. “I’ve spotted something strange.”

The trio waited patiently while Ryland investigated his latest discovery. Linsby and Academ Jules were clearly biting their tongues to prevent disturbing him. Viren, who had a great deal more experience in how Ryland worked, just looked patiently expectant.

“What did you find?” Linsby asked a little hurriedly as soon as the sign of [Omniscience] faded from Ryland’s eyes.

“Hmm.” Ryland considered how best to phrase his findings. “I’ve explained already how the physical structure of the Crypts is conducting the Living Mana. Well, the question that has plagued me for a while is where exactly the Living Mana is being conducted to. I think I have the answer now.”

Essentially, the Living Mana was being guided higher up the Crypts. It had taken him a little bit, but Ryland had now discovered that the flow of the Living Mana went outwards from the floor, then rose along the walls, before ascending even higher through the ceiling.

“So,” Academ Jules said, trying to piece together the different pieces of information. “They’re all heading to the surface?”

“Perhaps,” Ryland said. “There is an easy way for me to check. One moment. [Translocate].”

Another rift swallowed up Ryland, spitting him back out far, far above the Crypts. He was now floating dozens of arm spans above those large obelisks he had spotted when he had been fighting against the Order mage, Pious.

And it was to them that the Living Mana was being sent.

Ryland should have been feeling the buffet of the wind, the chill temperature this high up, the thinness of the air itself. Instead, he was being blanketed by what felt like a veritable storm of Living Mana transmitted from the peaks of the obelisks. Tiny, particulate amounts of energy were bombarding him from all directions, like he was floating in the middle of an explosion.

He looked up. The sky was clouded over, and [Omniscience] didn’t reveal anything strange about those clouds. So was the Living Mana being transmitted to someplace beyond the sky? What exactly lay be—

Ryland frowned. Powers were arriving, their sudden appearance a sign of their impressive abilities as much as it was a signal for their intent.

“Marionys Ryland,” said the first Guardian who had appeared from the sky, the column of light he had descended along with now fading into motes. His armour shone the scarlet of dried blood, almost black in certain areas and edged with gold. Twin scimitars of the same gold hung from his waist. “You’ve gone far enough.”

He wasn’t alone. The first Guardian was soon joined by another, then two appearing in a pair, then yet another. Golden spears of light were shooting down from the sky, depositing Rhel’s strongest mages in the airspace around Ryland. In moments, he was surrounded.

Even Guardian Nesmel was there too. He remained silent, but the glower on his face promised that he was relishing the opportunity to get back at a hated foe.

“Did Mavar put you up to this?” Ryland asked.

It stood to reason that everything to do with the Crypts was some sort of conspiracy cooked up by Mavar. No doubt he was keeping an eye on things through the clergy he had forced to remain inside there. They were his eyes and ears, and now that he had seen Ryland was technically on the case, he was playing his most powerful cards in order to prevent his plans from coming to light.

“It matters not,” said another Guardian, the oldest one among the bunch in sky-blue armoured robes. “You should be more concerned with your own survival. Push things too far, and you won’t come out of this unscathed.”

Ryland slowly rose higher into their midst. “You’re making a grave mistake. I bear you no ill will.”

“This is not about ill will,” said a third, her glaive resting on one shoulder. “This is about teaching a wayward mage a lesson he should have picked up by now.”

“If any of you had an ounce of bravery, you’d understand how you were being compelled by the Soul Glyph Inscribed upon your manasouls and act accordingly.” Ryland’s gaze held no mercy, no pity. Not when Acrypha was missing. “But I suppose that’s too much to expect from a group who believe they must act like a gang to claim victory.”

That stung. It was quite obvious that the Guardians didn’t want to attack him all together. Their pride was taking a terrible hit.

But their precious Demiurge, Kindly Mavar, must have compelled them to act as one, to attack Ryland together. Because Mavar of all people would know how hopeless the endeavour would be otherwise.

Yet, it seemed even Mavar had forgotten over the long centuries just how much the situation was still hopeless.

“Looks like there are multiple lessons you need to learn,” said the Guardian with the glaive. She was now holding it aloft, ready to wield with liquid energy twisting around the blade in a roaring whirlpool. “And Mavar the Eternal has sent us as the teachers.”

Ryland was already casting his magic. Because he had no intention of wasting any more time with them than absolutely necessary, he was removing all the chains on his manasoul. His Deific, Calamitous manasoul was coming to life. More and more compressed energy suffused his form, turning his body into a collection of Glyphs so dense that he felt like a magical black hole.

“Is that so?” Ryland said. His voice echoed in the air like thunder, the air shivering under the force of his will, the Glyphs making up every iota of reality now under tension in expectation of his command. “I will warn you one last time. Cease your blind obedience of Mavar Val Arona. It will only lead to your ruin.”

The words incensed the Guardians. Magic flared from them all, their anger overcoming whatever recalcitrance they might have felt upon seeing Ryland assume his Incarnation form.

“You’ve insulted our faith for the last time,” the eldest Guardian said, pulling out a gold-headed mace. “Now, you will pay.”

The blood-armoured Guardian had his scimitars in hand too. “We’ve had our devotion and our determination questioned for the final time. We will end you, Ryland. So I, Enviskar, swear upon the greatness gifted to me by the Eternal.”

Half a dozen manasouls went ablaze around Ryland, each one sparking with enough magic to rain destruction upon the entirety of Rhel.

The next instant, they all attacked.
