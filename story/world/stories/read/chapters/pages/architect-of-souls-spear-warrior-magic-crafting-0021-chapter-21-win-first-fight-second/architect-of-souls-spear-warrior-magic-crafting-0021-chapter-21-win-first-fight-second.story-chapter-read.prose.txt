Ferris woke early the next morning. Last night he’d been exhausted and wrung out, but somehow, he’d slept like a rock and was feeling refreshed. He wasn’t exactly proud of having a breakdown, but it happened. It was time to move on.

Stretching, he felt something warm and comforting on top of him. With growing horror, he realized he’d fallen asleep with Jade in his bed.

Ferris went very still and started speaking over the bond, “Good morning, Jade. Remember, don’t eat me,” along with mental images of himself, and all the bits and pieces of himself that should not be eaten.

Jade started rolling around. Ferris quickly reviewed his safety instructions with the slime, getting more practiced at giving them. It was hard to tell how much Jade remembered from yesterday, but Ferris intended to repeat this several times a day until he figured out the memory problems.

After getting ready, Ferris and Jade found Mother Lily in the kitchen. He asked for a quick early breakfast and a to-go lunch, which she was happy to throw together. Cultivators often had odd hours, and she was prepared for the occasional request.

Food in hand, Ferris sat with Jade in the common room. He had several pieces of new information to consider while eating. Breakfast was just fruit and some juice, which Jade happily began consuming and rating.

The chain demons were attempting to destroy the oath statue. This was the worst possibility he had considered, as it gave him the least amount of time. If they succeeded, it would cause a backlash in his cultivation as he broke a bound contract. Even worse, Elder Harper would know, and ‘my inner demons didn’t like the oath’ would not be accepted as an excuse. Even if she believed him, it would imply that he resented and rejected the contract…

Which is kind of true.

Ferris shook his head. Now was the time to focus, not get lost in his own head.

Either way, she would punish him as an oath breaker. She might show some leniency if he turned himself in and explained. Even then, it showed he wasn’t worth trusting, and he had already received benefits from the clan.

Still, the idea ‘my inner demons didn’t like the oath’ was worth a bit more consideration. Why did the chain demons specifically want to destroy the statue? The demons of the soulspace were a reflection of Ferris, just like everything else in that place. This type of demon was new, which meant the root cause was also new.

Yesterday, he considered, I worried about the bond with Jade controlling my thoughts. I wondered if the oath could subconsciously control me, making me loyal to Hollowfield. It felt connected, as even the chains and awkward movements of the demons seemed to represent a loss of control.

Ferris had been taught that cultivation was a journey of self-reflection and enlightenment. That he would face adversity both in the soulspace, and in the heart. His teachers had warned him, but this was the first time he saw for himself. Thinking about it, he did resent the oath to Hollowfield. A more emotional part of him wanted to go destroy the statue himself.

Of course, he was able to control those impulses. There were a whole host of reasons making that a very bad idea that his rational brain prioritized.

Apparently, his heart hadn’t agreed. It just hated the idea that something like the statue or the core bond could control him.

The core bond… why hadn’t the chain demons stolen the core? His fear of being controlled stemmed more from the foreign feelings of the familiar bond than anything else. The oath statue was related, but not the cause of his anxiety.

He had definitely checked the core last night, and it was still securely fastened on top of its pedestal. The demons could have easily broken it free. Frankly, it would’ve been easier to take the entire pedestal than the statue.

Although… Uncle Winton had emphasized that he should deliberately intend to lock the core in place. Comparing that to the oath statue… Ferris had always wanted to move it, even going so far as to cut the chains every night. He had been told intention mattered in the soulspace. Could that have let the demons steal the large, heavy statue, yet not the small core?

While all these thoughts were important, they didn’t help him with the immediate problem. Even if he let go of his anxiety, demons in the soulspace don’t simply poof out of existence. You had to overcome them both in spirit and mind.

He would eventually need to come to terms with his feelings, or the chain demons would keep hindering his cultivation. But how could he not be anxious about something influencing his mind? Ferris shook his head; he could work on that after he got through this crisis.

He intended to recover the statue that day, so he needed to do anything he could to give himself a better chance.

The chain demons were slow, but vicious at medium range. The chains would grab him before he could reach with his spear, but perhaps a ranged attack would counter them.

Ferris wanted to test throwing Jade’s acid. It would be an ideal counter to the chain demons.

In fact, Jade might be uniquely effective against the demons. They fought by constricting with a chain, which Jade could simply melt or pass through. The slime’s core was weak to direct attacks, but the demon didn’t appear very coordinated. If Jade could get in close, they might be able to attack unopposed. Of course, if the chain had enough speed and control, it could knock the core out of the slime’s body…

He also wanted to visit the merit store and the library, to see if anything could help. Then he would check if Jade had recovered in the soulspace. He needed a new spear as well before attacking the fort.

Ferris had long finished his breakfast, and Mother Lily had dropped off his lunch. Ferris grabbed it and checked the clock. No reason not to keep testing Jade’s memory. After noting the time, he told Jade the names of a dozen different types of fish to remember for later.

To test throwing Jade’s acid, he needed glass vials. The fifth district had alchemists, so they likely had a supply shop. Ferris headed off; Jade held in one arm, crutch in the other, and a bag containing their lunch on his back.

As they left the house, Jade grumbled a bit. The slime distinctly remembered ‘dinner’ having more food than this ‘breakfast,’ enjoyable as it was. No matter that yesterday’s breakfast was larger, they couldn’t remember it.

Jade’s memory loss was starting to weigh on Ferris, beyond the practical difficulty. Will this sadness cause more problems in the soulspace, spawning new demons? He wondered. Would worry over demons also spawn more demons? What would the personified fear of fear itself look like? Do cultivators need to control their emotions, avoiding anything that made them feel? Ferris’ father had been frantic to see him off, but Uncle Winton was a solid rock to depend on. Although… growing up Ferris had memories of his uncle being just as happy and sad as anyone else.

Oddly, his personal experience when he’d lost his leg was both extremes at once. He’d tried for years to unlock his soul unsuccessfully through ‘harmonization of mind and body,’ but then accomplished it at the height of an emotional mess just fading away to emptiness. He thought about Elder Harper, the only tier three cultivator he’d ever spoken to. She was controlled, yet had a focused passion that was intense to witness. Were emotions bad for cultivators, or not?

Soon, Ferris found himself back in the pungent fifth district. There were fewer people about this early, but a friendly stranger was easily able to direct him to an alchemist supply store. The shop had a wide variety of mundane necessities for brewing, concocting, and other crafts beyond Ferris’ knowledge. There wasn’t anything magical, as that would only be sold at the merit store, but there was more equipment than Ferris knew what to do with.

Thankfully, glass vials were a common requirement for several disciplines of crafting. They even had some specifically designed to break when thrown, reasonably priced. Ferris, as a cultivator, would likely not need to worry about the price of mundane items like this unless he purchased in bulk. The money Uncle Winton had left him had barely been touched. Magical resources or knowledge, on the other hand, couldn’t be bought for any reasonable amount of common currency.

He bought a couple vials and metal tongs from a sleepy attendant and took them to the fifth district’s training facility. He was excited, as he had been warned to only practice anything dangerous at one of these buildings. This was the first time he had something that counted.

The outside of the training hall had its own wall within the clan area. Ferris wondered how any building intended for practicing dangerous abilities could stay standing. He remembered the jobs list had mentioned a material reinforcement ability. Perhaps it was magically strengthened, or maybe it was even protected by a formation. There was no obvious magical protection he could see as he entered and walked up to the reception desk.

When he told them he was testing tier one acid with thrown vials, the aide at the training hall directed him to a small private room. It was shaped like a hallway, with sand along the floor. The walls looked and smelled like someone had dumped barrels of something foul around. The aide had also helpfully provided a couple planks of wood as targets.

Ferris examined the glass vials. If this worked, he would need to recreate them in the soulspace. He could probably duplicate the shape using qi forming, but it would be made of solidified qi instead of glass. Ferris thought back to the book recording material corrosivity and corrosiveness. Glass had been rated pretty high for corrosion resistance, but he had no clue how well qi would work. He would have to test both plain qi and demon enhanced qi crafting.

He stuck the glass vial down in the sand deep enough to be stable and called Jade over. The slime had immediately started exploring the room and was currently rolling around the far wall.

“New place!” Jade said, approaching with a small bounce.

Ferris smiled at the slime and glanced at the wooden targets. It wouldn’t hurt to get a baseline example of the slime’s acid. He grabbed a piece and stuck it into the sand. Stepping back, he sent a mental command to Jade to pounce at the target, melting it down to nothing.

Jade, with more excitement than speed, rolled up to the base. There was no pounce, but a little hop got the slime wrapped around the wood. Despite the less-than-terrifying approach, after a couple of seconds the wood inside the slime’s body began dissolving away to nothing. The top of the target fell, being consumed as it dropped down into the slime, until there was nothing left. Interestingly, the part stuck down into the sand was spared, cut off level with the ground.

Staring at his still bandaged hand, Ferris considered Jade’s attack. Slimes were somehow both dangerous, and not. A delver had to be restrained or clueless—like Ferris—to be swallowed. The acid wasn’t instant, and most creatures would likely escape—again like Ferris—and throw the slime off quickly enough to survive. Even so, anything inside the slime’s body long enough would die.

The problem was, any tactic he could think of that took advantage of Jade’s acid would work better with Ferris just stabbing the enemy. If he restrained a monster such that it couldn’t escape or throw Jade off, why not just finish it off with a knife instead? The Jump ability would help Jade approach enemies, but it wouldn’t help the slime hold on. The Grow ability could help, making Jade too large to throw off.

Jade was very pleased with themself, bouncing around where the target used to be. They rolled over when Ferris started setting up a second target, but he directed them over to the glass vial.

Convincing Jade to fill the vial was harder, as he struggled to get the slime to understand what he wanted. The concept of intentionally losing part of their body was foreign to the slime.

Jade reluctantly agreed to try and rolled next to the vial. Ferris asked Jade to imagine a small hole, and push acid out like a spout, but they couldn’t make it work.

Finally, Ferris gave up and went with another approach. He grabbed one of the vials securely with a pair of tongs. He quickly dipped it into Jade’s body and pulled it out. The vial was smoking—a clear sign that something was being corroded—but he had been quick enough that it hadn’t started really melting. Now it had some green liquid scooped up inside.

Acting quickly, Ferris awkwardly flung it at the wooden target. The throw had little power behind it, and the glass didn’t break. More than a little embarrassed, and thankful for the private room, Ferris walked up to the target. He lifted the vial with the tongs and poured the contents over the wooden target.

The acid splattered, but had no visible effect. The wood didn’t melt into a pile of goo like Ferris had hoped. He tried a couple more times, quickly scooping out acid and dumping it directly onto the wood. No matter how fast he was, there appeared to be no magical nature to the acid once it left Jade’s body.

In retrospect, Ferris probably should have seen this coming. The slime ranch chopped off large chunks of slime and collected it in a pit. He’d been told the slime fertilizer was a lot safer to handle. Even the corrosivity tests from the book at the library agreed. He’d just hoped it would still be magically strong if he was fast enough.

Disheartened, Ferris gave up on his dream of tossing vials of acidic death at the chain demons—for now. The whole process of scooping was awkward, but he might have been able to streamline most of it. The real problem was the loss of potency. He still wanted to pursue this, but not today.

Ferris stored the glass vials and tongs in his bag while Jade gleefully ate the remaining wood targets. Ferris could tell he would have little trouble with waste in his future.

This idea wouldn’t help his current situation. It was time to move on to something that might.

Next stop, the merit store, and then the library.
