Kalsin woke to water gently lapping against his thighs. He had to blink for the world to come into focus, once again filled with color as the land stretched around him for miles. The endless field of grass was split up by the fracture weaving and sneaking ahead all the way to the horizon. He welcomed the lush greens below, the azure blues above, having missed the vast world around him. His sweat-drenched coat stuck to him like a second skin under the warming rays of Warmlight.

Summer's first Blindlight had come and gone.

He'd fallen asleep in Sap's saddle as he had done many times in the past, enough so that he trusted his companion to keep him from falling. The water was lukewarm around his submerged feet, feeling the steady movement of scales over his mount's shifting muscles. Hollow swam just ahead with a slumped Jack on his back, his finned tail flicking up and splashing water from time to time. The others were in the boat with Áron at the back as he steered the land-paddles, occasionally propping them against the rocky shore to gain purchase, then push.

Kalsin nudged Sap, steering him to swim beside Hollow. Jack had been dozing off, but straightened when she must've felt the waves stirred by Sap's movements in the narrow river. The bandage on her shoulder had come loose.

"Let me take a look at that," Kalsin said, Sap pulling close enough their feet were practically mushed together. Kalsin ignored his slight nausea from the physical contact, focusing instead on Jack who wore the slightly annoyed look of someone not wanting to be fussed over. Their bahten, as if having a sixth sense for their riders' needs, slowed and steadied underneath them in such a way a regular horse could never imitate. They could've been on land, sitting on a slab of rock and not know the difference.

With quick and efficient motions, Kalsin fully untied the improvised bandage, and let out a relieved breath. The bolt had left a furrow half a finger's width in the side of her shoulder, the powerful shot having only grazed her. It wasn't pretty, but it was significantly better than the alternatives he'd feared.

Jack remained still as Kalsin worked, cleaning the wound and wrapping it anew in a fresh dressing. It was an awkward cast, looping under her arm at an angle that was prone to slipping, so he took his time, wrapping it from different directions and doubling the knots.

Jack kept her cheek turned away, keeping her scar as far away from him as possible. For his sake, Kalsin knew. The restless way her body shifted, from her stolen glances, he could tell she wanted to look at him.

An old memory surfaced, one with his bodyguard looking down at him, pinning him to the ground and gazing at him with fondness that he didn’t deserve. As if his silver eyes were the most beautiful gems she’d ever seen, leaning closer, her lips brushing against his…

"Thank you, old friend," Kalsin said, shaking off the memory.

Jack almost turned back to him, but stiffened when Kalsin pulled hard on the cast. Satisfied it would hold, Kalsin pulled back and instead reached down to give Hollow a pat on the neck. "I was talking to Hollow. Not sure if you noticed, but he saved my ass back there. You've got competition."

Jack only shrugged. Kalsin understood it as 'yeah, well, it's not like that never happened before'.

She looked at him sideways, then gestured with her chin toward his arm.

“Barely feel it now.”

She raised a wry eyebrow.

“No, I’m serious. I’ve had cat scratches more deep than these ones.” He moved to bump his shoulder against hers. He bounced off her tall frame and had to steady himself in the saddle. “When we were kids, Cinder had this theory that if you had a strong physical surge and you wished hard enough, you could spawn a healing manifestation of your own.”

Jack snorted. She then made spooky gestures with her fingers as if casting a spell, then tossed a kiss toward his arm with a big ‘mwah’.

Kalsin clutched his arm to his chest with wide eyes. “Oi, careful, Jack. Keep blowing kisses like that and I’ll need healing somewhere else.”

Her shoulders shook as Jack threw her head back, laughing hard enough to stir waves around them. Hollow endured his rider’s mirth with quiet dignity and a splash of his tail. She tried to signal three times something, only to break down, then eventually give up and settle with a thumbs up and a lecherous grin toward Kalsin.

“Down, girl.” Kalsin muttered, already regretting the joke. Though not without a smile of his own.

Jack was probably the best, most spectacular damn woman he has met his entire life, on all metrics. Now, if only his stupid body also agreed with him, then they’d be having an entirely different, uh… conversation.

His face was probably betraying his thoughts, because he looked over to Jack prodding the inside of her mouth with her tongue, making her cheeks bulge in and out and in and out and watching him with hooded eyes.

Kalsin quickly looked back ahead. Oh gods, oh gods and all the holy entities in the sky.

They let their mounts carry them along the river, enjoying the sun and each other’s presence for long minutes. Kalsin then took a glance back at the boat gliding behind them. He noticed someone was missing. "When did the priest leave?"

Jack thought for a moment with a serene smile still lingering on her face. She pointed toward Kalsin, put her hands together imitating a pillow as if sleeping, and shrugged. Almost as an afterthought, she gestured up at the sun.

The priest had left while Kalsin was sleeping, right after Blindlight ended and Warmlight rose back onto the sky.

While Jack clearly wasn't overly concerned about it, Kalsin found himself disappointed by the news. There was a shroud of mystery around the silent, script-covered girl who might or might not have been a priest in truth. The revelation under that hood had been unsettling, if not shocking, but in a puzzle-to-be-solved kind of way instead of casting a sense of danger.

And if there was one thing Kalsin liked to think himself good at, it was solving puzzles. Having one slip through his fingers felt wrong, like a botched opportunity to peek through a window toward something big. Toward something important he was missing.

"Kalsin." Áron had come to his boat’s front, letting the current do the job for the moment. Erazza sat with her back to them, though Kalsin had no doubts she was listening. "Two more hours and we'll reach a split in the fracture, then a smaller one right after that. Assuming we're followed—which I have no reason to think otherwise—we'll have to change course soon."

"How familiar are you with the surrounding lands?"

"Not very."

"You came this way to Remallon, yeah?"

Áron shifted with the slight rocking of the boat. "Sure. But we weren't exactly going sightseeing."

Kalsin wanted to interrogate them further, but held his questions for now. The collector wouldn't give up so easily, and though they had evaded the ambush during Blindlight, they weren't out of his claws just yet. The good news was that during the good ol’ regular Warmlight, those optics of his would be useless; the playing field had been equalized.

"We'll keep with this fracture until it branches into a particular forest-island. That small fracture you mentioned? I think that's the one. I have someone waiting for me there." He didn't mention specifics. If he wanted the azTéls’ cooperation, Kalsin would have to make sure Cinder remained unseen. Being Clenn and all. "Jack's going to loop back around and scout for our pursuers, keep a lookout. You two join her, and then we’ll meet up in the forest afterwards."

Jack shot a speculative glance at Kalsin, but didn't protest. Áron was about to, but Kalsin cut him off in advance. "Blindlight is over, but we're still blind as to what's happening. And I don't like being one step behind. We don't know how many men the collector has, or how far behind they are. One day? One hour? For all we know, they could be just behind that rise," he waved toward a hill on their left. "Three sets of eyes see more than one."

"You want to fight them?" Erazza spoke up finally, though still didn't turn.

"No.” Kalsin paused, thinking. He then reluctantly said, “No. But at this point, I think we'll have to. We have already killed enough of them to launch a public manhunt after us."

"True enough," she said, looking up at Áron with unspoken words passing between them. Áron nodded shortly after and faced Kalsin again. "Fine. Spot, count, and scurry with murderous Clenn on our tails. Then what?"

"Then you lead them into the forest, right until you reach the waterfall. It's easy enough to find from what I've been told—you'll hear the rumble from a mile away. I'll be waiting there."

"I hope to all spectral hells that you know what you're doing."

Kalsin smiled. "That's the thing about plans getting fucked. You knock one up, and it takes just about nine minutes till a new one is born."
