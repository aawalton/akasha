Chapter 143: Now We're Getting Classified

The silence was deafening. At least, Jack noted that most were caught in amazed fascination rather than any negative reaction. Highfive and Bo had the most to process, utterly gobsmacked that Ira had not quite existed in the way they thought.

But I hope they realize it was Neex inside that they befriended all along. She was constantly peeking out because she’s a terrible spy.

One of the first to say something in the deadness was the ‘doctor aura’ guy, Solvent. His brows were knitted together in an expression of sympathy. “I am so, so sorry for your loss. Those others with you, were they your colleagues? Friends?”

Neex was taken aback. She blinked in surprise and nodded to him, obviously uncomfortable, her pupils shrinking. “Yes. Thank you. It is alright. They pursued their passions to the end. We expected and prepared for death in our pact together, and Memoria very honorably did all she could to recover what remains and articles could be obtained, so that I may return them to their families as tokens of remembrance. Also, to be clear, we don’t hold the actions of a group of miscreants against anyone else. Responsibility taken for error and a just response is more admirable and telling than perfect conduct.”

“Still, it must be hard, being the only one to survive.”

Neex didn’t reply, looking down at the table, tentacles drooping, and appearing quite miserable and uncomfortable. Internally, Jack felt a mix of mortification and guilt spike within her, as one being publicly accused of wrongdoing, or… possibly, some taboo thing discussed inappropriately.

There’s a cultural divide here, isn’t there? No wonder she doesn’t talk about it.

Jack shifted and opened his mouth, but Memoria spoke first, raising her hand, “Let’s move on from the subject rather than delving into the regretful events that brought us here. Don’t discuss this unless she brings it up, hmm?”

In the uncomfortable silence, Highfive suddenly barked a laugh and clapped his hands. As everyone looked at him, Jack realized he had not even been paying attention to the current convo to pick up the subtleties.

“Mmm, mmm, mmm!” Highfive was still shaking his head, grinning, and clapping. Finally, he pointed at a confused Neex and said, “I knew you were weird! It all makes sense! Ira didn’t seem real… because she wasn’t! Ha!”

“You’re such an idiot, Fiver,” Bo muttered, eyes still thoughtful, currently on the center of the table.

Neex, meanwhile, had a sheepish grin as she squeezed her hands together, looking between the two. “I hope you’re not too mad or uncomfortable with me after this, and that you can forgive me for the deception. I cherish our time, and hope we can build a new friendship on such foundations, if… shaky.”

Highfive scoffed and waved his hand. “Eh! You’re good, bro. Hell, you couldn’t stop me from being your friend if you tried.”

Neex beamed. “I’m so happy to hear it!” Her humor faded as her eyes turned to Bo, and new nervousness came. Indeed, this was a whole different can of worms to deal with.

Bo didn’t register the attention at first, but when she did, she met Neex’s eyes and raised her brows. “New friendship? Are you kidding me?”

Neex opened her mouth, appearing stricken and at a loss for words.

Bo continued, “No. All that’s happened is that I found out my friend is even more uniquely awesome. It’s the same friendship. I didn’t help you land a boyfriend just to start from scratch, you know.” Sporadic laughter went around the table, and Neex looked a strange, contrasting combination of mortified and happily relieved, her pupils and tentacles going crazy. Bo continued, “There’s nothing to forgive. You hid what you had to, and I met you when you were undercover. Regardless, I signed up for a life of keeping things from those I’m closest to, and them from me, if necessary.” Her eyes flashed briefly to Jack. “We’re ANPs, and that’s the life.”

“As scintillating as these interpersonal developments are,” Bermuda said sarcastically, “I must insist we get back on point, to numerous more important things at hand.”

“Yes, most of us are stunned right now, I think,” Jetset said, shrugging helplessly and scratching his chin, “but these are big, big revelations. A friendly external Archon and a new species? The capacity to make new ANPs with the promising Agent Ferro as the evident proof?” He met Memoria’s eyes. “I assume these ‘extremely big deals’ are why you’re here.”

“And why I am,” Sato said, eyes burning with intensity — and hope — as he stared at Memoria. “Tell me it’s true, Mother. I’ve got what, a few years at best, and under the knife for how much of it?” His finger traced the ‘line’ in his nearly bare haircut, and Jack suddenly realized what it was: a surgery scar. “Are you telling me I can get cured and fraggin’ upgraded at the same time?”

Memoria met Sato’s eyes, sighed, and finally nodded soberly. “It’s true, Sato. We can’t promise anything, but, well…” She gestured over to Neex.

Neex nodded to Memoria before turning to Sato. “I’m familiar with your file, Agent Sato, and even your biology. That new injection they gave you was something I designed to help tide you over. I believe my people will feel compassion for your plight, and if agreements can be made, you will be bonded and restored, as Jack was. Though the damage is extensive, at a minimum, I believe a vast expansion of capability will be had.”

Sato was definitely a bit wild-eyed. He swallowed hard and replied, “Whatever it is, I agree to it.” His eyes shifted between Memoria and Neex. “I’m game all the way. I’ve got grandchildren to spoil.”

“What is this about damage being related to powers and cures?” Permafrost asked, eyes sliding around the table in confusion. “Is being normal a disease? I don’t understand… and… some of you… do.” She spoke as her eyes scanned various people, seeing their differing reactions.

Memoria winced guiltily and shrugged. “It’s true. This whole matter exposes quite a dirty little detail, one many of you were familiar with, whether by high clearance or special necessity. I knew I’d have to let the cat out of the bag to the rest of you due to mission pertinence. Here’s the rub: I don’t have ‘chosen.’ I attempt to connect with all of my children, all of humanity, but it only works for a vanishingly small percentage. Genes only play a part rather than the whole for this stew, and I’ve squeezed that down into the current crop of humanity.

“My choice of words for what happens to the rest is that their interface is ‘tangled.’ The connections warp before they get to me, jumbled up and contorted into impossible knots I cannot work with. I’ve tried untangling them before, and it wasn’t pretty. It seems I am just not that kind of surgeon. Quallakuloth is such a specialist, though, and it was the means by which Jack’s interface was repaired.”

“It’s an interesting choice of words,” Sato commented. “To me, it’s a disease, a cancer. Literally.”

“Those are just the physical results of something extraphysical, so to speak. Disease-like symptoms of catastrophic damage and abnormality one tiny space over from reality, indirectly connected to you. Sadly, severing it is impossible, though I shudder to think what that would do. All angles of the greater System merely bend around, some hollows greater than others.”

Jack peered over at Sato. “When you introduced me to the Quirked, like yourself, you framed it differently, like it was ‘less of a gift’ rather than this stuff. Did you know the truth, then?”

Sato nodded. “Yeah. I was just feeding you the basic line. There’s a policy for Central to explain things past a certain detriment level, so I had the truth of things, more or less. A nice consolation prize for a time bomb in the brain.”

Another pregnant pause lasted a while, as numerous agents digested the new details internally.

Memoria broke the silence. “The basics of our mission are thus: take Neex back, establish official contact and diplomacy, and hopefully start up an official alliance between our parties, at which point we’re prepared to help them out in all feasible ways, and vice versa. I have a preliminary agreement with Quallakuloth, but that is by no means the end-all, be-all. They have their own PM and their own government and society to deal with. The Deucalians are ultra-cautious people adapted principally to avoidance, flight, and hiding. Neex here is a bolder exception to the rule, by far. Apparently, it wasn’t always so cut and dry, but this is their evolved means of survival.” Memoria stopped and met Neex’s eyes.

“Yes,” Neex said, “we-… A long time ago, we were adapted to bond with a more warrioristic subspecies. They were vanishingly small in number and doomed to perish, but taught many of our own their ways, passing them on. So it was for a time, and our people had very beloved and celebrated warriors, like unto a caste themselves. Still, they remained rare, as our nature conflicted with seeking out violence. In time, war under the water decimated them, especially during certain periods of overly ambitious expansion. The failures set the stage to cripple their numbers, humiliate them in the eyes of the public, and shift society to other, more universally desired strategies.

“As your Archon said… avoidance, flight, and hiding. Our warrior society now is more composed of rangers or scouts, and those who seek glory are unheard of. To expand, to thrive, perhaps even to survive long-term, we could really use an ally, to say the least. In large part, that is the intention of us coming here and making contact.”

Memoria added, “This is the position of Neex, Q-Loth, and a minority within the Deucalian society. There is opposition, and their PM, whom they call the Palanarch, is caught in the middle. Though Neex will be a bit embarrassed by this, I feel it's pertinent to mention that she is the daughter of their PM and is practically part of a royal family of power-users. My words, there — they do not have literal royalty — but her mother possesses social powers and could likely sway our entire fate amongst them if she wanted, both through her husband and influences among the council that advises him.”

Someone whistled, and Jetset leaned over to pat Jack’s shoulder in mock consolation as he said, “You’re dating an alien princess, buddy. Good luck.”

Jack actually felt like he needed it after hearing about her mother and how important impressing her might have just become.

Oh, man…

Neex, for her part, put both hands up to cover her eyes as she turned gray-colored to match the walls behind her. This got a few suppressed snickers. In any case, Neex finally sat back down at this point.

Délight, seemingly brightening at the news, asked, “Is that why I’m coming along? Because social powers are useful, even… celebrated?”

Memoria nodded. “Yes, little as I can spare you here, your primary role is to aid in diplomacy and persuasion. Deucalians are not offended by flagrant charisma plays. Their leaders very often have powers of this nature, even those somewhat like yours. Key people of this nature even turned their society where it has gone, away from their hero-worshiping foundations. From what we’ve gleaned from Neex, the Deucalians are predisposed to this relative pacifism working well among them. It functions so far, but I agree with Neex that it is doomed to be a burst bubble one day by this or that warmongering society. With little exception, the rest of us are constantly honing ourselves stronger and tougher against each other. I think it's plain that the Dreamer has gotten stronger over time, for instance.”

“Fascinating. I look forward to the challenges ahead, then.”

Bermuda cleared her throat and began, “Many of you will have specific instructions which will be given later. Let’s get to the meat of the mission specs.” The hologram in the center of the table blipped and showed what appeared to be a beach, with a military base not far, with a naval extension like a long, giant knife cutting into the ocean. “This is Ft. Sledge on the Weddell Coast, still well within our borders and more or less sheltered by the Calm. It should be a brief stay. Neex will proceed out into the water to make preliminary oceanic communications with her people.

“It is likely she will not receive a response immediately, not within our borders, but they will receive it. We will conduct ourselves via submarine to a closer position and staging ground outside our borders.” The hologram showed a much more compact, solid-looking base built all into a rocky cliff directly leading down into waters as choppy as a storm, yet without a storm in sight. Multiple angles flashed, showing a bleak landscape with truly sorry amounts of dark vegetation. The land was just… different than anything Jack was familiar with. It was dead, spooky, and somehow slightly indistinct, as if caught between definitions. Wrong.

One thing it definitely was: a wasteland.

Reactions around the table showed it wasn’t just him that had never really literally seen such a place, though all understood what supposedly awaited outside the borders. In seeing it, he suddenly understood why few did.

It’s disheartening.

“Welcome to the balmy paradise of Palmer Island,” Bermuda said with a wry grin at their faces. “You thought Antarctica was cool and mild? Think again, tender rookies, when you step outside into the heat at Ft. Elpis, and, while you do, consider what it must be like further north, where even the roaring wind isn’t enough to relieve feeling cooked. Actually, I could show you worse on the tail of Palmer, where we’ve never permanently held territory and don’t bother trying anymore… but let's preserve your virgin minds a little longer, as it won’t be pertinent for this mission.”

The hologram re-focused on the fortress dug into the rock, a truly formidable monstrosity that could easily shrug off hurricanes, and likely even bombing runs. “Ft. Elpis is held by people you should definitely thank while there, as they have one of the most miserable duties in our civilization. Fortunately, your stay will likely be brief before heading underwater. Neex will either receive word granting her request for a diplomatic envoy, or she’ll keep trying.

“Hopefully, the contingencies to follow aren’t necessary, but technically, Neex has both the right to return and the right to assurance of safe passage, i.e., an escort by guards, if not provided. So, we’ll see how it goes from there. This is our basic pattern, and if all works out to our hopes, we’ll get the okay and be following Neex’s lead down below, to a prepared place for such matters, known as ‘Treaty Hollow,’ which I think you’ll all agree, at least sounds reassuring.” She paused, her gaze turning toward Memoria.

Memoria glanced over at Agent Solvent. “Have you guessed anything about our team makeup, yet?”

Solvent blinked, and his gaze swept around the whole table. “Only the obvious, I think? Bases covered, a few circumstantial allowances, layers of buffs as usual, and… Jetset.”

A few chuckles answered this. Jetset smiled winningly and said, “I appreciate being in my own category, bud.”

Memoria gave a subtle acknowledgement to Jetset. “He’s on overwatch, as usual, not on the main team. The unspoken issue is that the Deucalians would be intimidated by anyone at his level of individual physical power, being a foreign thing to them. I worry they can no longer conceptualize it. If he got inside and went on the attack, it would be catastrophic in a short period of time. Not the message we want to send.”

“Wow. Imagine if they realized what our PM could do.”

Memoria gave Jetset a withering look. “Kindly realize you’re veering into classified intel even hinting about that.”

“Oh, I do, Mother, but hypotheticals without details don’t technically violate the rules, do they?” He wagged his eyebrows.

Memoria rolled her eyes at him. “I swear, your generation is composed solely of brats.”

“That would explain why Auntie Stitcher always spoiled me.”

Auntie, seriously?

“Okay,” Bo said, raising a hand politely, “so if I’m guessing right, there is something to weaker members like us being included with powerhouses, isn’t there? Unless we’re included just because we know Jack, who’s situationally essential, but that doesn’t seem quite what is happening here. And when you said you didn’t want to spook the Deucalians, that’s another element, but there’s something else, right?”

Memoria smiled at her. “Very good, dear. Synergy and familiarity have their role, as does the diplomatic function, but we also made the best of it all, in the best way.” She turned back to Solvent and nodded. “Go ahead and explain what you do.” Then, she snapped her fingers. “Wait! You’re too humble and bland about it, actually. Demonstrate, instead.”

Solvent sighed. “As you wish, Mother.” His eyes flashed around the table. “You’ll all need to access your powers and allow my own integration.”

Lattice also sighed. “I know the drill.”

Power interactions? I love these!

Jack obeyed quickly, to summon memorite, and then pointed at himself and cleared his throat deliberately, getting Solvent’s attention. He was aware of Lindsay snickering at him and shaking her head, but he ignored her.

He felt like something threaded into his own branches, entangling with them, wrapping around like vines.

Solvent’s eyes widened as he brightened. “Ooh, metal manipulation! Love it.”

“Thanks, me too,” Jack replied.

“Alright, this will be enough for a demonstration, then. Memoria, could you-”

“Way ahead of you, son,” Memoria replied, and suddenly metal materialized up above the hologram, in the shape of a chrome egg. It was quite strong and dense, even hollow.

“Jack, if you could go ahead and try to crack it on your own, please. Use a metal hammer.”

“Okay,” Jack muttered, and summoned up to about 95% output, forging memorite into a compact hammer, at first liquid but rapidly solidifying. It wasn’t something he relished doing on a dime in crisis situations, but it was still feasible when needed.

Forming something of a dense sledgehammer, he got it as strong as he could and slammed the shit out of the egg with the hammer. There was a loud gong that made a few people wince in annoyance, as the hammer hit and failed to even scratch the egg.

“Alright,” Solvent said, and began to do something extremely complicated with the powers of the others around the table. Someone gasped, another person grimaced, and then Jack felt those ‘vines’ around his Allotment construct thicken and get super-charged with energy, like a powered exoskeleton around a softer body. “Now try it.”

Jack felt such power surging into him that he cackled with glee. That power wanted to go somewhere, too! Without restraint, he wrapped his magnified Allotment more strongly around the hammer and slammed it into the egg, at which point it shattered, sending a vast cascade of shrapnel everywhere.

Many people shrieked and hit the deck, or activated powers in response, to defend themselves.

Oops.
