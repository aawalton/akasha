Contrary to my expectations, Rhogann didn’t actually dodge the blast of searing hot light I’d just loosed at his monstrous head. In fact, he didn’t even seem to try.

The Rendarch took the bolt from the Netherim weapon without even blinking. It just splashed over his flayed, Mythril-woven face without even burning him.

In the split second of surprised silence that rolled over the cathedral at my actions, Rhogann met my eyes…

And smiled at me.

Despite this, I remained unfazed and returned their gaze calmly.

Alright.

If that’s how we’re going to play it, I’m game.

The calm before the battle I always knew was going to happen sudden ended, and the Karvokar surrounding us arrayed all around our hundred man battle-group suddenly surged forward. A tide of half-flayed, dogmatic maddwarves descended upon the defensive formation that my warriors had almost unconsciously settled into during my conversation with the Rendarch.

And my people rose to meet them.

An orgy of blood and death erupted all around us as soldiers of the expedition clashed not only with the Karvokar, who had only moments ago been kneeling in prayer before a flayed altar, but the Skarned sitting in the pews as well. The failed offshoots of the Korvoks' vile practices suddenly jolted awake out of their torpor, as if they were worker bees who had been waiting for instruction. For all I knew, that was how the bond between the Karvokar and the Skarned actually worked.

If I had to guess, we were outnumbered roughly three to one by the Korvoks, here in this hall of desecration and worship. There were roughly even numbers of Karvokar to my people, while there were double that in Skarned alone.

Honestly…

Doable.

It would be a hard fight, but I wasn’t worried that the warriors of the expedition could handle it. And that was because of a specific thing that we had all noticed about the Korvoks. Something we had adjusted our combat tactics to account for. Something that was, in our estimation, the key to actually defeating them.

The Karvokar didn’t fight together. They were all essentially unit commanders, yes. But they were commanders of Skarned. Not once during the grinding battle through the streets of Karag Korvok had we observed a single instance of the Karvokar coordinating with each other as they threw themselves at our advancing lines. They each acted as islands with their own little cadre of brainwashed, failed mutilation victims. It was decidedly odd at first thought, but…

I think I was starting to understand the deeper motivations of these madmen. There was no actual cooperation in this society. They were all competing against each other in their pursuit of their ‘Red Truth’, their belief that their own skin was somehow lying to them. They were a deeply fractured, stratified society, and ultimately?

They just couldn’t compete with the bonds that my people had forged. We were stronger together.

They had them handled.

But Rhogann?

Well.

That was another matter.

There was no point in trying to start slow with this fight. This was a battle, one where my soldiers were counting on this mad priest being occupied while they culled the Karvokar and the Skarned. I was positive that this thing would scythe through my Classers like a hot knife through butter if I let it.

I was not going to let that happen.

I exploded to my full, transformed state and activated Might of the Wyrdwood at sixty percent at the same time. As The Scintillant Blade exploded along the length of Terractus, I wasted no time in blurring towards the patiently waiting, monstrous form of Rhogann, swinging my blade right at its neck as I did so.

This combination had worked fairly well against nearly all of the Karvokar I had personally fought, Djarn included. Their oddly waxy exposed muscle was, in general, very hard to penetrate through normal means. It was as hard as steel armor and typically needed focused effort to penetrate. The Scintillant Blade had no problem cutting right through it, but that was a System-given, purpose-built assassination Skill meant for slaying gods.

Still…

I had a feeling that this wouldn’t go quite as smoothly; there was a problem with Rhogann in particular. Something the Skill’d had trouble with in the past.

My feeling turned out to be true when, yes, my burning blade did cut through the hardened musculature of the Korvok before me…

And was stopped in place by the threads of Mythril twined all around its individual muscle fibers.

As expected.

The Scintillant Blade was a Skill that was meant to pierce defenses, yes. But only mystical defenses. It had absolutely no effect on anything that was a kind of physical defense. Which, of course, implied that whatever they’d done to their exposed, hardened muscle was a kind of mystical treatment. But that didn’t help me much when the actual metal Mythril, or pretty much any kind of true physical armor, was able to stop the force of my blow right in its tracks.

Normally, I used Grinding Crimson Sunder to account for that fact. The chainsaw-like motion of that Skill was typically enough to overcome this deficiency. But it had already shown it wasn’t enough to overcome the waxy muscle defense.

So.

The Scintillant Blade wasn’t enough to pierce the Mythril.

Grinding Crimson Sunder wasn’t enough to pierce Korvokian muscle.

Honestly, it was almost like ‘Rhogann the Unveiled’ was a foe purpose-built to counter me.

Rhoganns and my gaze met once more, there in the middle of the battle raging through the cathedral, and it smiled at me. There was a supreme confidence in those crimson red eyes that grated at me just a little.

I think I was surprised when I smirked right back at it.

If I was alone, and I had encountered this combination, then I probably would have been a little bit concerned. I would have had to get creative if I wanted to overcome it.

But just like my soldiers, I wasn’t fighting alone, now was I?

Rhogann’s head jerked to the right, violently reacting to something that only he could see. The twisted, mutated monstrosity of a Dwarf tried to skip backwards in a movement so quick and nimble that I was honestly a bit impressed.

That was a mistake.

Sylvia quite literally appeared from nowhere at his back, shifting into invisibility from one moment to the next. She was already poised in a lunge, with both of her drawn swords oriented in a scissor position aimed right at Rhogann’s exposed neck. In her right hand, she held the blade gifted to her by her Father, hewn from the same darkly glimmering, azure Stellar Basalt as his own Stellarum, while her left bore the blade she had inherited from the defeated body of Nyx. It was a shorter, thicker blade that drank light not unlike obsidian.

Both of them glowed with an eerie grey light that I knew indicated the presence of Sylvia’s Ki threading its way through both blades.

Sylvia’s primary battle strategy was as an illusionist, yes. But first and foremost, she was a Cultivator.

She was just better than me in melee.

There was a cold, calculating look on my love’s face as the edges of her blades closed in on Rhogann’s unprotected neck. But I wasn’t terribly surprised when the inhuman Korvok seemed to have sensed the sneak attack. Somehow, Rhogann seemed to flow around the oncoming swords, its spine almost looked to dislocate as it twisted its back a full one hundred and eighty degrees. The now furious bloody eyes of the Rendarch were now staring at Sylvia with an outraged look from an upside-down position.

Not for long, though.

A heavy boot slammed right into Rhogann’s face, the momentum from Sylvia’s failed decapitation strike instead turned into a full body kick. The Rendarch flipped end over end from the force of it as he was literally booted away, flying to crash into the back wall of the cathedral and its many burning candles as he did.

Sylvia remained in the position of her kick for a moment, a single leg outstretched parallel to the ground. She caught my gaze as I stood off to the side…and smiled a small, secretive, almost mischievous smile at me.

Even after all the blood we had spilled together today, she still had her spirit. I was almost jealous of that, honestly.

Little managed to rattle her.

I returned the smile as she lowered her foot back to the stone of the cathedral, as the battle raged around us. Fade and Sena had taken on bodyguard positions for the two of us, tearing through any Korvok, Korvokar or otherwise, that sought to interfere in the fight against their leader. Because it was going to be a fight.

A little booting like that wasn’t going to keep a monster like Rhogann down.

I was proven right as the mass of shattered stone and wax beneath the effigy of the splayed man shifted, and a maroon and Mythril fist punched up through the rubble. Both Sylvia and I casually stepped aside as a mass of rock larger than a dinner table flew through the air where we had just been standing, pulverizing a Karvokar Fade had ripped the throat out of. His suffering ended just as the Rendarch pulled themselves up out of the rubble to glower down at us. Not a whole lot of damage seemed to have been done from either the kick or the impact with the candles, but I couldn’t help but smirk at the fact that the diaphanous veils of the ‘Unveiled’ had been burned away by the fires set by the collapsed candles.

For a number of reasons, really.

“I am afraid we shall have to be far harsher in your instruction than I was expecting, Marshal, when it comes time to show you the light of Truth.” Rhogann said, descending the rubble. Displeasure was written on every line of its body as it came to a stop a dozen feet away from where Sylvia and I stood ready. Oddly, the look in the Dwarf's eyes as it turned to stare at Sylvia held a profound disgust within them. "But you...Abomination, I judge you to be unworthy. Your blasphemous form shall never know the warmth of Truth, and for that foundational sin, I sentence you to death."

Slowly, the Rendarch settled into a familiar combat stance, something I had seen dozens of times by now.

The same wide-legged horse stance that Djarn had tried to fight me in. Only, just by looking at the flayed mutant, I could see that Rhogann was far more experienced than the Karvokar had been.

That was fine. I was more experienced dealing with Korvoks now than I had been then.

Sylvia merely scoffed, completely unfazed. "Cease your prattling. The only sin I see is wasted breath on the soon-to-be vanquished."

Almost leisurely, I sheathed Terractus, as Sylvia descended into what I knew she called her ‘crescent’ stance. One blade overhead, the other near her belly, both pointed at the enemy. I meanwhile…

Drew my staff from its place on my back, and transferred the fires of The Scintillant Blade from my own sword, to the amber capstone of my inherited staff.

A coruscating blade of rainbow fire erupted from the tip of it, immediately condensing.

Hardening.

The result was honestly something not dissimilar to a spear, really. I had grown far, far more skilled in manipulating the flames of my primary Precursor Skill, as I had grown in power. It was no longer just an uncontrolled blade of flame at the end of a staff.

No, it was an elongated spearhead of scintillant flame so condensed that it glowed a bright white.

I couldn’t help but notice that the effect was very similar in appearance to Primordium.

I twirled my new spear in the air briefly, mentally thanking the Rafael of the past for crafting such a well-balanced instrument…and sank into a textbook-perfect spearman stance.

“C’mon, then,” I mocked the ‘Dwarf’. “This student is ready for his instruction, if you think you’re hard enough.”

Rhogann’s eyes narrowed at me, and then the Rendarch blurred toward Sylvia and me.

…………………………..

This was really quite draining, to be honest.

Holding Vis Maledicta Exactoris, Might of the Wyrdwood, and this concentrated form of The Scintillant Blade all at once was a powerful drain on my Mana. Even a few weeks prior, I’m not sure I would have been able to do it for more than a few minutes. This evolved form of The Scintillant Blade alone was something I needed to infuse with a constant stream of Mana, or else the compressed form of the fiery spear would collapse.

Still…

It was worth it.

Rhogann actually snarled at me, as I scored a glancing blow along its ribs. To my smirking satisfaction, the resulting wound had not only sliced right through its waxy, hardened muscle, but it had partially melted some of the Mythril threads through it.

That was the thing, really. This form of The Scintillant Blade really was a fire, and not just a coating to sunder mystical defenses. It burned, and it burned hot. In my testing, even brief, glancing blows with steel armor was enough for the impacted area to melt away. Mythril was, of course, far stronger than mere steel, but the intense heat of my conjured spear tip was still enough to weaken the metal.

Just enough for them to become misshapen, and in some cases, cling to each other. The protection they offered was still there, of course.

But that was the thing with doing something as insane as threading your very muscle with metal.

It hurt your flexibility if they were melted together, you see. And Rhogann was all about that flexibility.

I chuckled, a tad maliciously, I’ll admit, when Rhogann had to rapidly backpedal to dodge the following assault of Sylvia, trailing in my shadow. For as skilled at unarmed combat as the Rendarch was, it just couldn’t compete against the easy teamwork of the Sculpted woman and me.

We’d certainly trained together enough, in quiet moments of preparation, to become more than familiar with our combat styles.

I had no problem admitting that against Rhogann, Sylvia was the real powerhouse. Both of Sylvia’s weapons were, to my only chagrin, simply better than my own Oninite sword. Mithril was something that they could easily slice through, with the right level of pure physical might.

And she had that in spades. Even now, with my enhancement and transformation Skills running…

She was still stronger than I.

The stone of the cathedral below shattered from a dodged hammer blow from Sylvia, as Rhogann desperately tried to find a moment to retaliate. He didn’t find it, of course, because I had already been springboarding off of Sylvia’s deliberately bent back, to soar into the air of the tall hall with an assisted flap of my wings. I knew the Sculpted Cultivator probably hadn’t even blinked at momentarily bearing the full weight of my transformed state.

Because I felt the telltale sensation of an illusion settled onto my massive, reptilian form in midair. Not to confuse me, of course.

But Rhogann instead.

From the air, I drove the blade of my shining spear downwards, assisted both by a hard flap of my wings and my full weight. Whatever it was that Rhogann had seen while I was descending, it caused it to dodge towards me instead of away.

My spear carved a hard, burning molten line across Rhogann’s right shoulder. The Rendarch screamed in frustration rather than pain, as it danced away from me once again.

Normally, I think I would have clucked my tongue in disappointment. A blow like that probably would have been enough to kill me if I were on one end of it.

Instead, though, I blinked in surprise.

Because a glowing golden sword suddenly erupted from the center of Rhogann’s chest. Sylvia and I both immediately stopped in surprise, while the Rendarch itself slowly looked down to stare at the claymore that had pierced its heart. And standing behind Rhogann, glowing a similar gold, with his face only barely visible through the glow…

“Walter?” I asked, confused and honestly pretty impressed.

I didn’t know he had it in him.

My Squire nodded at me with a strained smile from behind the Rendarch, and then with a bellow of effort…

The would-be Palatine dragged his sword upwards through both the chest of the mutated, flayed Dwarf known as Rhogann and its skull at the same time.

Huh.

I guess the real danger here had been Walter, and not Sylvia.
