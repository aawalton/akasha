Charlemagne struggled to come to grips with the changes that had taken place inside his body.  His new Core, which the Squiggles called an “Ignition Core”, felt strangely quiet inside his body.  He could tell that it was working: at the very least, it was sustaining his magically enhanced bulk, but it lacked the comforting rhythmic pulses that the rooster had grown accustomed to.

While he examined the changes to his body, he felt the strange lurching sensation that he now recognized as the Squiggles transporting him to a new location.  He looked around to check out his surroundings, finding himself near his companions standing on a tile floor that continued in all directions for as far as the eye could see.

“Ser Charlemagne, you’re finally here,” Josephine exclaimed, sounding a bit peeved at the rooster.  Charlemagne ignored her, because he had more important things to focus on.  There was a message from the Squiggles.

“Congratulations on clearing Floor One of the Delve.  Due to your exemplary performance, you have received an additional award.  The remainder of your awards will not be available until you have left the Delve.

Award granted: One (1) Lifespan Enhancement Token”

The rooster immediately recognized the significance of the Token.  All things, he had learned, had a finite amount of time that they would remain alive.  Even humans, who lived for something like 10 times as long as the average chicken, would eventually age and die, at least according to what Ndiogou had told him.

A longer life was a great boon to the young rooster.  For one thing, it would give him more time to reproduce.  It would also allow him to amass power and grow even stronger.  Finally, it would give him a chance to watch over more generations of his offspring, thus ensuring that his genetic heritage prospered.  Without hesitation, Charlemagne used the Token, eagerly watching with his internal senses to see how it would affect him.

There was a flash of highly concentrated mana as something that looked like a small piece of fruit appeared inside his stomach.  It instantly began to dissolve as the combination of searing heat and potent acid ate away at the Token.  Tiny bits of whatever the System-generated reward was made of were released and passed directly into the rooster’s bloodstream.

Charlemagne watched in fascination as the magical substance began passing through his cells, touching each of them in a minute but meaningful way.  Pushing his awareness to its maximum, he focused on one particle as it circulated through his body, gradually growing smaller as it did.  Eventually, he was able to spot why: as the particle reached the center of each cell, it deposited something onto what looked like a noodle tied into a knot.  But the process seemed to be wasteful.  Sometimes the particles only coated part of the knot, and occasionally they missed the knot entirely.

Stretching his mana manipulation skills to their limits, Charlemagne helped the process along.  There were so many of the little pieces everywhere that he could never hope to assist them all, but he did what he could.  All over his body, his mana nudged the particles in the right direction, helping them land their microscopic payloads where they belonged.  It was grueling, exhausting work, requiring every bit of focus that the rooster possessed.  Thankfully, it was over almost as quickly as it had begun: the Token had completely dissolved.

The Squiggles announced that the process was complete.

But that wasn’t the only message from them, the rooster was pleased to see.

The rooster took a moment to bask in the glory of his latest Achievement, as well as the knowledge that his lifespan had been greatly increased.  He still had no idea exactly how long he was likely to live, but he imagined that, so long as he kept moving forward, the problems of the future would be solved by the Charlemagne of the future.  Having used this particular bit of unassailable logic to put his concerns to rest, the rooster turned his attention back to his group.

“Bawk,” he said.

“Finally!” Phatagin cried, apparently still not used to the rooster spacing out for minutes at a time.  “You must have received some good news from the System, since you were quiet for such a great length of time.”

“Bawk,” the rooster confirmed.  “Bawwk!”

“Oh, those are quite rare.  Was it the only option or did you get a choice?” Phasima inquired.

“Bawk,” Charlemagne explained.

“Well, ordinarily I would be surprised that it wasn’t a stand-alone reward, but these are the Elite Systemic Games, after all.  The prizes are commensurate to the difficulty in securing a spot,” Josephine said.

Phatagin looked like he was about to comment, but, at that moment, reality swirled once again, and Charlemagne found himself standing on a high hilltop or mountain, looking out over a vast plain.  Off in the distance stood two sets of buildings, separated by fairly flat but rugged terrain and a wide, lazy river that flowed through the middle of the plain.  The entire area looked like it had been trampled down multiple times.

Figures that looked smaller than ants from his vantage point swarmed all around the buildings, going about a variety of activities that were completely impossible to distinguish due to the extreme distance.  As the rooster watched, a small detachment of the figures, which appeared to have four legs and two torsos, broke away from one of the sets of buildings and raced toward the other side.  Moments later, a similar group of beings boiled out from the other camp.

Charlemagne’s attention was diverted as the Squiggles arrived to explain what was happening.

Phatagin, Josephine, and Phasima chatted as the rooster considered his next move while watching the two groups run toward each other.  After a few long minutes, they met in the middle of the river, which proved to be quite shallow, and began to fight savagely.  Charlemagne used his enhanced vision to follow the battle, finding that each group actually consisted of armored humans riding horses, rather than some more exotic group of creatures.

He remembered to use his Monocle of Refinement on one of the more powerful-looking combatants.

Next, Charlemagne extended his magical senses, pushing them to their limit in a different way this time.  Isolating the Mook Heavy Cavalry, he estimated its strength using mana alone.  The results were surprising.

Without warning, the rooster spread his wings, allowing them to catch the stiff breeze that pervaded the area.  He soared upward for a moment before diving toward the battlefield below.

Bawk, he told Phatagin through the Party Chat.

“Where’s he going?” Josephine stammered as she watched the rooster streak out toward the skirmish like a black and red comet.

“Charlemagne informed me that he will handle the matter personally.  Furthermore, he instructed me to stay up here out of harm’s way,” the pangolin responded.

“He didn’t say anything about me, did he?” Phasima asked with a chuckle.  Then, without waiting for a response, she launched herself into the air and followed Charlemagne’s trail.

“I wonder what he’s planning to do,” Josephine said to Phatagin as they watched the two figures fly toward the battle.

“Knowing Charlemagne, my wager is that neither side will be left standing once he has finished with them,” Phatagin responded.

“That sounds about right,” the Attendant agreed.  “But since this is Floor 2, I wonder if there are other Contenders with the same quest.  That could complicate things.”

Phatagin gazed out over the plain, his poor eyesight making it difficult to see the two warring factions off in the distance.  His mana sight, however, worked just fine, allowing him to see the outline of an enormous bird descending upon the battlefield like a bomb waiting to go off.

“No,” he finally responded.  “I rather do not think it will.”

Charlemagne, cognizant that the two forces warring over the middle of a river represented only a small fraction of the total combatants, recognized the need to conserve his mana as he dove toward the skirmish.  He was still confused about what was going on with his Ignition Core, which remained strangely quiescent despite the coming battle.  Thus, he had decided to start by relying on his Advanced Fowl Arts Skill to join in the melee.

He first landed on the back of one of the horses, which whinnied and bucked.  The rider fell, aided by a swift peck from the rooster that penetrated their armor just below the armpit.  Having no reason to attack the mount now that the human was out of the picture, the rooster leapt into the air, performing a spinning claw swipe that decapitated another warrior before landing in the thick of the fighting.  A loud splash reminded him that he was fighting in the middle of a river.  While the water itself did not give the rooster pause, the muddy ground was ill-suited for maneuverability.

Leaping up out of the water, the rooster constructed a horizontal force field under his feet.  He leaped again, dodging an incoming lance, before delivering a devastating peck through the helmet of the warrior that had just attempted to skewer him.  Propelled upward by the force of his own blow, Charlemagne flapped his wings before swooping down again, grabbing another armored figure by the shoulders and carrying them up into the air.  The human flailed about with a small dagger, but Charlemagne’s skin and feathers were too tough for the tiny knife to cut.  He dropped the human onto another, causing both to roll across the ground in a crumpled heap.  Neither moved again.

Feeling his heartbeat beginning to accelerate, the rooster dove back into combat, this time taking on a trio of fighters who had already lost their mounts.  Charlemagne took his time weaving around and ducking their awkward sword strikes, noting that the humans were more hampered by the flowing water than he had been.  Once he grew tired of simply dodging, he moved in and released some of the excess heat that his body had built up.  The trio roasted where they stood.

By this point, both sides had realized that something very wrong was happening.  The shouts and screams of the combatants, while expected on a battlefield, were far more panicked than usual.  There were cries to retreat, others to hold fast, and even some to continue advancing.  Very few who had seen the rooster still lived, and those that did not ascribe the strange atmosphere to Charlemagne’s presence.

That was a mistake.

Charlemagne accelerated, using his sheer speed to run over the surface of the water.  Outrunning even the fastest horse by a wide margin, he wove through the melee, jumping into the air to deliver decisive blows as quickly as he could.  Each peck or claw attack dismounted one of the cavalry, and losses on both sides quickly mounted as the rooster reaped lives indiscriminately.

That all changed when a pair of horns suddenly blew almost at the exact same time, spooking both sides equally.  The horses had apparently been well-trained on the desired response to the signal, because they immediately turned toward the river’s banks and galloped off at full speed, whether or not they still carried a rider.  In just a few moments, the entire skirmish had collapsed, leaving Charlemagne the very confused but satisfied victor.  It was not the satisfaction of a hunter who had slain a sufficiently large prey animal, or even the satisfaction of a strong creature that had driven off all comers to claim territory.  The reason Charlemagne was happy was because he had figured out what was going on with his Ignition Core.

Soon, he would find out its limits.
