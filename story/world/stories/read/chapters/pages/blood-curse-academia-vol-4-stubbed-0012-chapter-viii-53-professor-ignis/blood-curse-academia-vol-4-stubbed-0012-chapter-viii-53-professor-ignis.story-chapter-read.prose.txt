Chapter V.III (5.3) - Professor Ignis

Music filled the classroom, spilling out into the hallways beyond. More than just the basic scales or training techniques, this was a true song. Every key of the music thundered with passion. Granted, it wasn’t an original song, but Kizu still put his heart into playing the music. His hands were a blur as he enhanced their speed. Sadly, he lacked the ability to cast mental magic so his mind struggled to keep up with the maximum capabilities of his fingers. More than once, he stumbled, the music getting away from him, but he recovered each time.

His fingers stretched and danced between the black and white keys of the massive piano. Due to enchantments, the music that sounded from within the instrument was even louder than it had any right to be. While not utilizing every single one of the 88 keys, it still felt like he used everything in his musical arsenal while his left and right hands battled the melody and bass off of one another.

Professor Ignis bobbed along to the tune, his eyes closed and appearing entirely focused on Kizu’s song. He never once commented on Kizu’s gaffs.

Kizu hit the last few keys and his foot pressed down on the peddle, letting the final notes linger before drifting off into the edges of the classroom. Kizu did his best to quiet his gasps for air as Ignis remained motionless beside him.

“Well done,” Professor Ignis said. “I cannot state an exact ranking without several notes on hand to reference, but I would currently place you easily within the mid 200 rankings of the academy now. Especially with all of that memorized. It must have been nearly fourteen pages.”

Kizu was indeed beyond needing sheet music for this piece, having memorized it over the last few months on board Owl’s Respite. One of the benefits of having it refurbished by Aoi’s family was that they’d given him a piano to practice with. And while music didn’t take his primary focus most days, Kizu still dedicated a few hours to it every day. That, along with being locked in a time dilation chamber prison with a piano over spring break, had resulted in a steady increase in skill with the instrument.

“Thank you,” Kizu said simply.

“You have a talent.” Ignis sighed and leaned back in his seat, running a hand through his scarlet hair. “I find it moderately embarrassing that the headmaster had to point you out to me. I should have at the very least remembered your sister.”

“You knew…” Kizu let the question drift off and decided to attack from a different angle. “Can you tell me about what songs she liked?”

Ignis chuckled. “I suppose that’s a harmless enough request. She was one of my A students. Not an S tier, so I had little time exclusively teaching her. But she certainly had talent.”

“You meet with me,” Kizu pointed out.

“I don’t normally receive the headmaster’s personal request that I meet with specific students one-on-one.” Ignis stood and stretched. His body popped and he slumped back into a relaxed stance. “Honestly though, if I didn’t enjoy you as a student, I wouldn’t have bothered today. Back hasn’t been feeling right since that Dragon slammed into me. Consider it a compliment. If you were a nuisance, I’d have rescheduled.”

Kizu bit his lip. The professor was complimenting him, but also tactfully shifting the subject away from Anna.

“That’s very kind of you to say,” he said carefully.

“You know, most of the professors believe me to be a manipulator,” Ignis said suddenly. “They think I’m a master of moving people to where they need to be. A puppeteer of emotions.”

“I—what?”

“Not a mental mage. But something tangentially related. And I don’t blame them for thinking so. It’s what my skills are used for by the masters of my craft. Most of the students see illusions and music as a form of art. That’s how I direct my class. But those with knowledge outside my classroom have seen the devastation dealt by lies.” Ignis’s blue eyes met Kizu’s. They were hard, like frozen ponds. “I rarely discuss this with students. But for you, I think it’s important to discuss the true power of my spellcraft. You’re advancing in both subjects too quickly for me to avoid this conversation.”

The professor stood abruptly. He turned away from Kizu and began to walk among all of the other instruments in the classroom, brushing a hand over them as he passed.

“At the center of it all, music is a lie. It covers up the harsh truth of the world, obscures us from that which the world presses on us. It is a way in which we hide from responsibility and make our lives just a smidge more bearable, despite the world of concerns pressing down on all of us at every given moment. If we allow that pressure to fully be realized, we will be crushed. So we escape into a better place. Music, art, stories. Different mediums for the same purpose.”

“Are you saying that art is evil?” Kizu asked. He was still trying to figure out a way to turn this subject back to Anna, but he had absolutely no idea where the professor was going with this topic.

“Evil. No, not at all. Lies aren’t evil. Lies give us hope. They help us live on. And yet…they grant a power to do a great deal of harm. Art can be a balm, or a knife. Lies have collapsed nations and inspired revolutions. And yet, it also is nothing at all. Just words on the wind.”

“You’re saying all art is just a form of lying?” Kizu asked.

“Percisly. All artists are liars, manipulators, and deceivers. Those who advance into magical capacities are often the artists who truly commit to that path.”

“So the other professors don’t trust you? Aren’t they your friends?”

“Last week’s display of my power helped earn me some goodwill. But no. They aren’t truly my friends. They respect me, but they do not wish to get too close. My subjects stray too close to the dreaded subject of mental magic for their comfort. And some like Professor Krimpit are truly antagonistic towards me and my chosen profession.”

“Because he sees himself as someone who teaches the truth while you deal with lies?” Kizu immediately understood the conflict based on his own interactions with Krimpit.

Ignis gave a tired smile. “Professor Krimpit has his own philosophies on life. I don’t believe either of us is truly wrong, we simply have different approaches.”

“I don’t think Krimpit would share that sentiment.”

Ignis chuckled. “The headmaster acts as a good balancing point between our viewpoints,” he said. “Regardless of the drama amongst the academy faculty, do you know why I’ve decided to speak to you about this?”

“Because you’re going to teach me how to incorporate magic into my music?”

“Eventually, yes. But simply speaking, I want to have this discussion with you because we are similar. I see myself in you. You too are a liar.”

Kizu recoiled as if slapped. “I’m not—”

“Kaga Kizu, I barely know you but I still feel the secrets on you as if they’re palpable. Whispers follow you. Everything from your hair to your toes carries a story.”

“Everyone has secrets,” Kizu insisted.

“Not like yours. And not in the same quantity. There are very few others at the academy like you and I.”

As much as he wanted to continue to deny the accusation, a different question slipped out. “Who else?”

“Who else have their lives precariously built upon lies? Well your changeling friend is a contender, though his art is altogether different from yours and mine. And then there is the prince.”

“Akira? I’ve seen him fail at acting. He’s dangerous but not a liar.”

“You need not act to deceive. That boy has a knack for my art of manipulation and deception.”

Kizu shook his head. “But you can’t seriously think everyone talented at music and illusions spend all their time fibbing. What about Sene?”

“An interesting subject to call out. Of all her subjects, Kajima Sene is the worst at illusions. Save for conjuring, of course. And while she plays every piece to perfection, her music lacks creative will or freedom.”

“She’s the best in the academy in both subjects!”

“Only because no one has risen to the occasion to usurp her position. Such an event occurred just last semester. A student with an eye for creativity stepped in and stole her position as the academy’s best student in a certain subject.”

“You mean me taking the brewing position.” Kizu held in a sigh. It hadn’t been like he’d wanted to take the position from Sene and all the headache and responsibilities that came with it. Though she might still insist to this day that she’d arranged for him to take her place, it was false. He just genuinely had been inarguably her superior in the subject. Rather than go into that, Kizu decided to go a simpler route. “All I do is follow a recipe.”

“We both know you can lie better than that,” Ignis admonished. The professor smiled and tapped the metallic center of a gong. The hollow noise rang out at the touch.

“Fine. It’s an art. An art I’m decent at. But you keep changing the subject. What’s your point? I’m not better than Sene in illusions or music. Nobody is.”

Professor Ignis turned and met Kizu’s eye. “But you could be. I see it in you. You’re driven, clever, and intelligent. Your mind doesn’t match Kajima Sene’s in a field like numerology, I’ll grant you that. It would be an uphill battle should you decide to compete with her there. But illusions? Music? You have the potential to be far greater.”

“So do plenty of other students,” Kizu insisted.

“Some, yes. But most lack your drive. Last semester I thought I actually had a potential first year prodigy musician, but he decided to toss aside his talent. I am encouraging you to not follow that example.”

“I was planning to keep up with my studies regardless.” Kizu struggled to understand the point of his conversation. He had never planned to start slacking off. Illusions and music were both subjects he enjoyed.

Rather than respond, Professor Ignis took a seat and raised up a lute. He began to strum the instrument. The constant weariness that always marred his face smoothed as the strings twanged under his fingers. His skin glowed with health and his scarlet hair brightened.

The music sucked in attention. It consumed Kizu’s focus and he felt his mind slipping into the notes. Different from being transported by the words of a story, the lute’s song carried Kizu off into a world of wonder and adventure. The music offered a rough outline of what to seek and his mind scrambled putting together the details like a dream.

Things began to accumulate around him. They solidified, offering Kizu a more concrete understanding of this bizarre world. A place of silvery rocks and a dark empty sky. Creatures darted about, through the holes and craters all in time with the music echoing around them. Kizu reached a hand out, longing to touch one of the critters.

As the song hit a crescendo, it abruptly finished.

The scene before him fell to pieces. The critters died and turned to dust. Without warning, everything ended.

“Wait, what happened?” Kizu blinked and shook his head, trying to clear his foggy mind. “That wasn’t a real ending.” He was back in the classroom. Or…he hadn’t actually left the classroom. He logically knew it must have been a combination between music and illusions.

“Correct. But that was all that was written.” Professor Ignis plucked at the strings of the lute, but they were utterly disconnected from the previous blend of notes. “That song belonged to your sister. She left it unfinished when she departed the academy.”

“My…sister’s.” Kizu’s mind scrambled back, struggling to retain the music he’d been utterly engrossed within a mere minute earlier. Already he felt it slipping from his mind.

“I gift it to you,” Ignis said. “Complete it, and you will ascend higher in the academy rankings.”

“Wait…really? You’re giving this song to me?”

“Yes. Once you complete this song I will teach you to incorporate magic into your notes” Ignis set the lute down and strolled back over to where Kizu sat on the piano bench. With a flick of his wrist, the professor created a leaflet of sheet music and held it out to Kizu.

Gingerly, almost worried it might burst into flames at his touch, Kizu took the pages. A glance at the music showed it to be equally complex to the piece he’d performed earlier for Ignis.

“Thank you,” he said earnestly. He clutched the pages. A rare link to his sister. He wondered if he could use it in a divination ritual to connect himself to her. A song she wrote herself had to hold some connection. But with Anata nearby, likely everything would continue to be redirected to her.

“Later this semester I want to teach you the technique I used.”

“The land with the silver rocks and the little three-legged rodents?” Kizu asked. “You’ll teach me how to combine music and illusions together?”

“I will.” Professor Ignis raised a hand and one of the little critters from the song appeared in an open palm. It scurried around until the tired music professor closed his hand and sighed. “The world is a dangerous place. It needs more liars. For often only they can create new truths.”
