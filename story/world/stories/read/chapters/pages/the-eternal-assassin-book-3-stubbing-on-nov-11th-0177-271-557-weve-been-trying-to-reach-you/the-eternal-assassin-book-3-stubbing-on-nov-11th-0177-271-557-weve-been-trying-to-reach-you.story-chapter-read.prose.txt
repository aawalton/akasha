As the millennia-old lich’s head fell to the ground, quickly followed by the rest of its body and the severed pieces of the corpse he had been puppeting, Asher let his arm go limp as well. Now that the battle had been officially won, he stopped ignoring what his body had been telling him all this time.

And immediately regretted doing so as a wave of nothing but pain washed over him.

Asher did his best to take stock of his various injuries before giving up halfway through as he realized he had too many to count. His ribcage had been all but vaporized, his hip was shattered, half his organs were bruised and the other half felt as though they’d burst, part of his spine had been severed, and he had at the very least a mild concussion. Possibly two.

Or five.

Now, a lesser man may have begun freaking out about the horrific state they suddenly found their body in after coming out on top of a hard-fought battle. But not Asher. He was far too stubborn for anything like that.

Instead, he simply died.

Asher gasped, jerking up from the cold stone floor of the lich’s workshop a few hours later after his Eternal Regeneration had finished putting him back together. A quick pat down confirmed he could both feel and move his legs again, and his ribcage was once again solid, which was quite reassuring. Shaking his head, Asher pushed himself to his feet, taking a look around the workshop and finally looking over his notifications from the fight.

Distortion - Lvl 36

Sever the Thread - Lvl 33

The King of Death’s body and detached head lay exactly where he’d left them, as did the different pieces of the corpse the lich had been controlling. The room was covered in blood, both from Asher and from the giant corpse, and Asher frowned as he spotted where the rotten blood mixed with his fresh blood.

“Why even fill the undead corpses with blood in the first place?” Asher muttered as he walked over to the lich and bent down to examine the body. Other than a few miscellaneous tools that eerily reminded him of his own mortician equipment he used to use back on Earth, the lich only had a single object on him worth noting.

A small, bone key with a gem inlaid in its head.

[Bone Key] - Enchanted

Turning the key this way and that, Asher stood up, already having a pretty good idea of what the key was intended for. They knew from Zygrul’s notes that the King of Death had been tasked with keeping the one dissenting lich on the Council of Death captive. Only a single member of the original fourteen had been against the plan to gather their phylacteries in the hopes of creating an Eternity element, and he’d been unceremoniously bound and held prisoner for the last six hundred and thirty-two years. If Asher could find exactly where he was being held captive, he could take out two of the liches in one fell swoop.

That said, it’s a ridiculously large workshop filled with what looked like dozens of hallways and hundreds of different rooms. And Smiles mentioned that many were sealed as they held dangerous experiments within them.

I’d really rather not spend the next few days going through them all one by one.

Realizing he might have a better solution, Asher reached into his rift and pulled Smiles out. But before he could say anything, he paused, blinking at the state the severed head was in.

Smiles had already looked incredibly pale before, with bright-red, bloodshot eyes. But now, he looked far more like an actual desiccated head. Slowly blinking, Smiles’ eyes found his own as the head grinned.

“Hey there! And here I thought you weren’t planning on saying goodbye!” Even his voice sounded tired, as though Smiles was struggling to muster the energy to speak.

“Smiles? What happened?”

“I told you, your special room is protected from the King of Death’s Death Aura, and I need that energy to survive. Over the past couple of hours, I’ve been leaking vitality like a sponge. I probably don’t have very long now,” Smiles admitted, his eyes flickering down to the lich’s corpse as his grin widened. “But hey, congrats on actually taking the King of Death down! Go team!”

“Oh, right,” Asher said, focusing on Complete Resilience’s third-tier effect. The fact that it was passive after making the initial choice of which element’s energy to ignore was incredible, as that meant he could keep it active twenty-four-seven without draining any of his tritessence. Despite it having made him immune to the lich’s Death Aura, at the time, Asher had still been able to sense the energy trying to harm him. Now, that same overbearing sense of death had vanished. With the King of Death’s own death, the massive, impenetrable Death Aura that had once spread dozens of miles in all directions was officially no more.

“I guess that’s it for the Deadlands then,” Asher muttered, momentarily shocked at the realization that he’d just altered the local geography for thousands of square miles. The Deadlands had been in place for over a thousand years, ever since the King of Death had set up shop and begun making his undead. For some reason, altering the landscape to such an extent with a single slash of his dagger seemed far more grandiose to him than taking down a member of the fabled Council of Death.

“Yeah, no more Deadlands,” Smiles said with a sad smile on his face. “No more Smiles either. Ah, well. I had a good run. Get it? Run? Because I’m so fast and—”

“Yes, Smiles, I get it,” Asher said, unable to stop himself from chuckling slightly at the undead’s joke. After learning from the King of Death that his original twelve Plagues had been created using parts from the very heroes who first ventured into the Deadlands to try and stop him, Asher looked at Smiles in a new light.

If Asher had lost to the King of Death and the lich had managed to place his brain and soul into the undead monstrosity like he’d been planning…

Would he have ended up like Smiles after a few hundred years?

“I’m not giving up on you just yet, Smiles,” Asher said, deciding then and there that at the very least, he’d do what he could to keep the severed head ‘alive,’ at least for the time being. Reattaching the mildly insane undead to his body once more was a different decision for the future, but for now, he had to hope that Draken’s own Death Aura would be enough to keep Smiles going until they could find a more permanent solution. The gang leader’s Death Aura was a good bit different from the King of Death’s constant, heavy aura, but as only a head, the amount of Death energy Smiles needed to survive seemed to be far lower than when he’d been in one piece. “But before we go anywhere, I need your help. Do you know where the other lich is being kept? He would have been brought here about six hundred and thirty-two years back.”

“Oh, yeah, I know about him,” Smiles confirmed. “The King of Death had me and all the other Servants here as a show of force when four other liches all warped in with that one in tow. They all worked together to seal that one underground. Seemed like a bit of overkill if you ask me.”

“And he’s been down there ever since?” Asher asked, shuddering at the very thought. Over six hundred years spent locked in a magical cellar, presumably bound so tightly he couldn’t even move, as they didn’t want him taking his own life and resurrecting back by his phylactery.

“Yep. Though the King of Death may have visited him here and there, I don’t know. I didn’t really spend too much time here in the workshop,” Smiles admitted.

“Can you lead me to him?” Asher asked, his knuckles whitening around his dagger. With Zygrul hiding somewhere within the Void and Asher having already taken out Hylm and the King of Death, killing this lich would bring the Council of Death down to only ten active members. Donvath had assured him that if he could manage to get the Council down to only about half of its original strength, the different kingdoms making up their continent would finally be willing to risk the Council’s ire to come together and eradicate the last of the liches once and for all. That meant after this next lich, Asher would only need to kill three or four more before he had literal armies at his back for the remainder.

“Sure! Let’s go kill another dead thing!” Smiles said as he began directing Asher out of the central room. Before they left, Asher did pause to recollect his scattered daggers, as well as the soulsteel greatsword that the corpse had lodged shockingly well into the workshop wall. He even snagged the supply of gemstone dust and random tools that the King of Death had been using to enchant his corpses, tossing all of it within his rift for the time being.

Following Smiles’ instructions, Asher made his way through the King of Death’s workshop in a far calmer manner than he’d run through it earlier. Somehow, with the complete and utter lack of even a shred of Death Aura hanging over his head, the once-terrifying workshop now just felt like some sort of low-budget haunted house. The sinew-tapestries and partially constructed undead dotting the halls hadn’t changed, but without the bite of the Death Aura behind them, they just felt empty.

While stepping over some of the fallen bones from one of the guardian skeletons, Asher jumped as he felt something in his very soul vibrate. Realizing what it was, he summoned his sending stone from his rift, noting which of his few contacts was trying to reach him before bringing it up to his ear with a smile.

“Samantha? Everything okay?”

“I was going to ask you that!” she said, sighing on the other end of the line. “The Death Aura vanished all of a sudden a few hours ago without warning, so we already figured out you took down the lich. But when I wasn’t able to reach you, we grew concerned. You told us you were planning on coming back before going after the lich!”

“Yeah, sorry about that,” Asher said, scratching his head sheepishly as he kicked at one of the scattered bones and kept walking. “Turns out, it was a trap after all. I was able to Recall in, but I couldn’t Recall back out of the lich’s workshop. He’d intended to just let me die to his Death Aura without even putting up so much as a fight, and then use my soul and brain to create some sort of super undead warrior. But it’s all good! He’s dead now!”

Samantha was silent for a moment, and Asher tried not to laugh as he pictured her confused face staring at the sending stone in shock.

“Well. I’m glad he’s dead! We had a bit of excitement over here ourselves, but that’s not important at the moment. I wanted to give you a heads up that a representative from Nunait is currently heading into the Deadlands to investigate what happened. That’s the country bordering the Deadlands. The one offering the five million shard bounty for its eradication that Rulfar told us about. She stopped by briefly to ensure we were okay, and we told her how you’d gone in to take down the King of Death. She’ll probably be arriving any minute now if she hasn’t already.”

“Thanks for the heads up. Want me to Recall over and grab you guys?”

“No, no sense in showing off what you're capable of if you don’t have to,” Samantha decided. “Though the look on her face when she travelled a few dozen miles only to find the three of us waiting for her again would be pretty funny.”

“Once I finish with her and take out the other lich, I’ll come pick you guys up,” Asher smiled, glancing down at Smiles’ washed-out face. The fact that the severed head hadn’t even once attempted to interrupt the call showed just how close to true death Smiles was, which meant Asher needed to hurry. “...After I make a quick drop off.”

“Alright. Stay safe!”

Promising her he would, Asher hung up, tossing his sending stone back into his rift as he held up Smiles. “Okay, we need to hurry. Someone from the neighboring country is coming by to check out what happened, and if I haven’t dealt with the other lich before they get here, things might get…”

Asher paused, staring at the heavily armored woman who appeared half-way down the hallway in a flash of blinding light. She wore pure white armor emblazoned with a golden sun, and held a sword and kite shield at the ready. Her eyes immediately locked onto Asher and the severed head he was holding, and Asher could only sigh.

“...complicated.”
