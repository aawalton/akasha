Chapter V.LXIV (5.64) - Ione’s Nightmare

With Mort perched on his shoulder, Kizu stepped through the void and into the next dream.

He blinked. He found himself in the Student Council room. But, instead of Faible, Sene, and himself, the chairs were filled by strangers. Random students that looked vaguely familiar, but he couldn’t recall the names of. At the head of the table, stood Ione.

Only, not the Ione he knew. She wore a pressed and wrinkle-free academy uniform and her hair was tied back in a neat ponytail. If not for her expression, he might actually have thought this Sene, rather than Ione. But he’d never witnessed Sene wear such a terrified face.

Ione bit her nails as the Student Council bickered amongst themselves.

“I—I….” Whatever she was trying to say, her words were drowned out by the others. They ignored her.

That disrespect irked Kizu.

He shoved past a student and slammed his hand on the table. The room went silent for several long seconds. Kizu opened his mouth to address them, but was cut-off before the words formed.

“Who do you think you are?” the shoved aside student said. “You don’t belong here! It’s a meeting for the Student Council!”

“Student Council?” Kizu slowly turned, thoroughly. examining each member. He scoffed. “You could have fooled me. You’re nothing but squabbling children. Get a hold of yourselves and—”

“Who are you?” This time, instead of the outraged boy beside him, Ione herself addressed him. But she looked puzzled, rather than angered.

“My name is Kaga Kizu,” he said simply. “And you’re Ione. Where is Sene?”

“Sene? Who is that?”

“Your… sister.” Even as Kizu spoke, he understood. This nightmare of Ione’s was a life without her twin.

“I don’t have a sister.”

“Monkey boy, you’re crazy,” one of the other students said. “Get out of here. Go find Professor Kateshi and get your brain patched back together.”

A few other students chorused their agreement and told him to get lost. Kizu ignored them, deeming solely Ione worth his time. He felt an urge from Mort to leap at the boy and claw his eyes out, but he tampered down his familiar’s irritation.

“We… we need to talk about the water fountains.” Ione shuffled some papers in front of herself. “Or, no. Why was I thinking water fountains? These are notes for the bake sale. We need to work together to supply cookies and—”

“Ugh, can’t you get anything right?” One of the other members rolled her eyes. “We canceled the bake sale two weeks ago. We’re supposed to discuss the new school lunch schedule for next semester. The headmaster put us in charge.”

“Don’t forget the dance,” another member helpfully pointed out. “You only scratched the surface of all that. You haven’t even chosen a caterer, let alone sent out notices.”

“There’s also that discipline problem with first-year bullying.”

“You need to meet with the Hon representative.”

“Professor Krimpit suggested you start an academy newsletter.”

“There’s a beach cleaning next week you need to attend.”

“Did you forget to—”

“Enough!” Kizu shouted. He gritted his teeth and glared at the Student Council. “What good are all of you if you just throw everything onto Ione? Are you willing to do anything yourselves? Might as well just get lost, for all the help you offer.”

The students looked at one another. Then, as a unit, they all shrugged, stood, and filed out the door without another word.

“It’s my responsibility,” Ione said, staring down at the papers spread across the table in front of her. “I’m president, not them.”

“That doesn’t mean you have to do anything alone,” Kizu said.

Mort hopped down from his shoulder and onto the table. He scampered across the surface, scattering papers into the air, then hopped up to Ione. She gave a startled yelp, finally tearing her eyes away from her notes.

“Is this… a familiar? I’ve always wanted to study an animal familiar.” Her face scrunched up in confusion. “Wait… no, I already have that knowledge. How do I know about familiars if I’ve never seen one close-up before?”

“Because none of this is real,” Kizu said. “It’s a dreamscape. We’ve fallen into a trap in the World Dungeon.”

He didn’t expect his words to convince her but thought it worth the try. He was disappointed but not surprised when she shook her head in denial. It could never be that easy.

“Well, if you’re not going to listen to me, then scoot over.” Kizu took a seat beside her and lifted up a page. It was a script for a speech, more than half of it scratched out.

“You’re not allowed to touch any of that,” Ione said, a note of panic in her voice. She tried to snatch the paper back, but Kizu jerked it out of her reach as he continued to read.

“I’m going to help you.”

“It’s my responsibility. I’m Shinzou Academy’s Student Council President.”

“I’m the Student Council Vice-President,” Kizu countered.

“No, you aren’t.” “Then who is? I don’t see anyone else stepping up to the role.”

“It’s… it’s….” Ione set a hand to her temple, as if suffering from a migraine.

“Exactly. It’s me. So, let me do my job and sort through these papers with you.”

“It’s my responsibility,” she repeated, but her heart wasn’t in it. After a few more minutes of prodding, she finally folded and allowed him to assist her.

Kizu spent the next several hours with Ione in that room, slowly taking chunks out of her piles of paperwork. While they worked, he caught Ione flashing Mort glances. The monkey had decided to perch himself at the nearest windowsill and stare at a spider on the other side of the glass. Something about his familiar clearly bothered her. Once Kizu finished up writing a particular tedious response to Professor Ignis about the band instrument security system, he took a break to address her.

“Why do you keep looking at Mort?” he asked.

“Oh!” She jolted to attention. His lackadaisical girlfriend was replaced by a high-stung student. “You see, I’ve always wanted to study familiars. All animals, really. I have a knack for summoning and the familiar bond is supposed to be a unique sort of connection that’s tangentially related to summoning. But I never have the time to devote, since I need to keep up with my other studies. Except, well, it will sound weird but when I look at your familiar, it feels like I already know the information.”

Kizu suspected that it had to do with her real world comprehension leaking into the dreamscape. Her actual memories were removed, but not the bulk of her knowledge. He kept that theory to himself.

“Why do you need to spend so much time studying?”

“I’m my family’s heir. Everything falls on me. If I fail, then everything collapses. There are so many expectations that I need to fulfill.”

“But… why?” Kizu asked.

“Because if I don’t, then nobody else will.”

“How do you know that? If you left the academy today, ran off to become a delver or a shepherd or a cryptozoologist, what do you think would truly happen?”

“Everything would collapse. The academy would be lost without a leader to direct the student body. Professor Oasaji would fall behind in his paperwork without me to act as his assistant. My parents would be left without an heir to take care of the business. And, of course, that means the Hon Empire would be put in a precarious position for the succession of its most high security prison.”

Kizu shook his head. “No. Life would move on. The Student Council would choose a new leader. Oasaji would hire a new assistant. Your parents would choose a new successor to take over the prison. The government has held together for centuries without you. It will survive for many more.” “You—you don’t understand,” Ione insisted. “There’s no one else. I have no one else!”

Kizu set his hand on his girlfriend’s shaking hand. “You have me. It will be fine. Let it all go.”

For the first time, Ione seemed to truly see him. Tears trickled down her cheeks as their eyes met. “Kizu. You came for me.”

“Always. I’ll never leave you behind.”

As Ione fell into his embrace, the room around them melted, deteriorating into a familiar vast nothingness. Mort floated, suspended in the space where the windowsill had been moments earlier. Beside him, a door formed. The passage into their next dreamscape.

They took a minute to let Ione recalibrate and get herself together. This nightmare seemed to take even more out of her than Kizu’s experience with his dead friends and family.

“Would a life without Sene truly be this horrible?” he asked when she finally disentangled herself from his embrace.

“Yes.”
