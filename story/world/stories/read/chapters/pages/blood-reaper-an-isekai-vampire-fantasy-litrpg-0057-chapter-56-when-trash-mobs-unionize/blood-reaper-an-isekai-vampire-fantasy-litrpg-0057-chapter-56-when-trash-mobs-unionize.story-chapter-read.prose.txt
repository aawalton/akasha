Marcus was right—these were death slugs.

Actual, living, breathing death slugs.

The colossal slug loomed over Ace, staring down at him with its two eyestalks focused squarely on him. Spittle rained from its mouth as it roared yet again.

Ace stared up at it, his mind racing through options that dwindled with each passing second. The fifteen-foot monstrosity dominated the clearing and the forty-or-so other slugs, its bioluminescent body pulsing with a sickening rhythm. The smaller, horse-sized slugs encircled their group in a tightening noose of corrosive horror.

Acid from their first round of kills pooled around their boots, eating through the patchy grass with alarming speed. The stench of burning twigs and dirt filled Ace's lungs, sharp enough to make his enhanced vampire senses recoil. He tasted copper at the back of his throat, and his fangs extended instinctively in response to the threat.

"Don’t panic," Ace ordered, his voice eerily calm despite the nightmare looming above them.

“Don’t tell me not to panic!” Marcus hissed under her breath. “I’ll panic if I want to panic!”

Tara notched an arrow in her bow, ignoring Marcus’s outburst. "I count at least forty-two of them. Too many to outrun."

“No shit,” Marcus muttered.

“No escape, then,” Ace said with a frustrated grunt. “No possible retreat. We have to face this one head-on.”

The largest slug's eyestalks swiveled with mechanical precision, focusing on them one by one as if choosing which of them to target first. When those glowing red orbs locked onto Ace, a shiver ran up his spine.

A leader spotting a fellow leader—and the biggest threat.

"That's not in the fucking field guide," Marcus hissed, his bark-skinned forearms raised defensively.

As if on cue, a stats screen appeared with the slug’s information.

———

Alpha Corrosive Slug

Core Racial Trait: Acidic secretion network - interconnected primitive hive mind capable of dissolving organic matter and some metals.

———

“Helpful,” Tara muttered. “What do you think that—”

Before she could finish that thought, the massive slug convulsed. Its body contracted in waves, and suddenly dozens of tiny slugs erupted from pores along its flanks, plopping to the ground with wet, sickening sounds.

"Well, that's just fucking perfect," Ace growled, summoning a Duskblade into each of his palms. He had to be careful, given how much the first fight had tapped into his Soul Meter, but he still had enough SM left to handle this.

Probably.

“Tara,” he said sharply. “You’re our cover. Take out what you can with your bow, but try to get us a healing circle like you’ve been practicing. We’ll need it if the slugs get too close. Marcus, cover us and try to get the things to scatter like last time. Olivia, keep the others safe while I try to take out the big one.”

She frowned, those red lips pouting, but she ultimately nodded.

Good.

“Can anyone figure out how to kill these things?” he asked. “Same as the little ones?”

“As far as I can tell, their anatomy is similar, if not the same,” Tara agreed as her gaze scanned the slowly encroaching creatures. “But if this is a hive mind, then—”

“—killing the big one might kill the others!” Marcus finished.

“Exactly,” Tara said with an annoyed glare his way. “Look! Each time those veins pulse, the others move. It’s controlling them.”

Sure enough, the massive alpha's body pulsed with a sickly glow, and waves of pale green light rippled beneath its translucent skin like electrical signals. Each pulse sent visible tremors through the other slugs, and their bodies jerked around in unison like puppets on invisible strings.

“Good catch,” Ace said with a nod toward their healer. “I hope you’re right, Tara.”

“Me, too,” she muttered.

The alpha shifted its massive bulk, and its skin undulated like a stormy ocean. The ground beneath it blackened and sizzled, any nearby vegetation withering on contact with its corrosive underside. Bubbles of noxious gas formed and popped along its body, releasing a mist that smelled like rotten eggs.

“I’m going to barf,” Marcus said. “That’s disgusting.”

“Well, aim away from the rest of us, then,” Ace replied.

“Thanks, boss,” Marcus said flatly. “Real helpful.”

“Anytime.”

The alpha slug's body tensed, its front end rising up like a cobra preparing to strike. Acid dripped from its underside, hissing where it struck the ground.

"Keep moving, and don't let the acid pool around you," he called out. "And watch for—"

The monster's roar cut him off—part screech, part gurgle, part deafening thunder. At the screamed order from their master, the smaller slugs surged forward in unison.

Ace didn't hesitate. He charged directly at the mountain of toxic goop, his Duskblades raised high.

The first slug hurled itself at him with unexpected speed, its body contracting and expanding to propel its mass through the air. Ace sidestepped it at the last moment, and the creature's slime-slicked body barely missed his shoulder. He launched into the air, jumping as it sailed past, and brought both Duskblades down into its brain. He sliced outward, and chunks of the damn thing’s head hit the ground with wet splats, acid spraying in a fine mist that sizzled against his Thornhide Armor.

He didn't pause to watch it die.

The second slug was already upon him, this one larger than the first. Its eyestalks whipped about frantically as it registered the first slug’s death. It reared back, its body bulging, and vomited a stream of caustic fluid.

“Move!” Ace warned.

The others ducked out of the way as Ace dropped flat against the ground. The acid streamed over him, only to eat a perfect hole through a tree trunk behind him. He rolled, feeling the ground dissolve beneath him, and came up inside striking range. With a single upward thrust, he drove his Duskblades into its jaw and clear into its brain. Acid bit into the back of his hands as the creature flailed in its death throes, and he quickly yanked the blades free so that he could wipe the acidic goop onto his pant leg.

The creature’s death spasm must’ve sent a shockwave through the psychic network, because the third slug approaching from his right momentarily froze.

That hesitation cost it everything.

Ace pivoted, planted his foot against a rock protruding from the ground, and launched himself at the stunned creature. Both blades found their mark, piercing its eyestalks and driving deep into its brain. The slug convulsed violently, its body contorting into an impossible shape before collapsing inward.

The entire exchange had taken just seconds.

Ace yanked his blades free, acidic blood streaming down the shadowsteel and evaporating on contact. His boots sank slightly into the dissolving ground, but he kept his balance. Every breath burned his lungs, but he felt something else too—a savage satisfaction in the slaughter.

This was kind of…

…fun.

The alpha's psychic scream of rage vibrated through Ace's skull as it watched its children die.

It took a moment more for him to recognize their pattern of movement, but once he did, it was clear as day. They attacked in waves, he realized, not random surges. There was a three-beat pattern to their advance—first the scouts, then the flankers, finally the brute-force attackers. Groups of three. The alpha staggered its commands to maintain constant pressure, but the underlying structure remained predictable.

His vampire reflexes, further enhanced by the crystalline wolf and nightmare bear essence he'd absorbed, allowed him to perceive these signals microseconds before the slugs received them. The advantage was slight but crucial—just enough time to position himself where the slugs weren't expecting resistance. His military background recognized this immediately as a command-and-control vulnerability, a single point of failure in an otherwise overwhelming force.

His enhanced senses flared and catalogued everything, his EXP Solute evolutions allowing him to process the alpha's commands as quickly as they came. He anticipated the next wave seconds before it materialized, his body already pivoting to the next position while the slugs lurched toward where he'd been. The alpha's central nervous system stood exposed before him like a flashing beacon—a single, glorious vulnerability in this writhing mass of horror. One clean strike to its brain would collapse their entire offensive, like decapitating a venomous snake.

He knew what to do, now, and he was just getting started.

The next wave of slugs advanced, their bodies undulating in unison. Acid sizzled across the forest floor, the vegetation withering in their path with audible hissing as they collapsed. The air filled with acrid fumes that burned Ace's throat, the chemical tang reminding him of industrial cleaning supplies blended with rot and decay—a wholly repulsive combination.

“Yep!” Marcus shouted above the fray. “Definitely going to barf!”

Their telepath raised his palms toward the nearest cluster of slugs and released a concentrated pulse of magic. A blast of fear shot from his fingers like purple ripples on the air. The forest between him and the slugs distorted visibly, like heat waves over asphalt. The slugs recoiled, their bodies contracting as if he’d punched them, creating a momentary gap in their ranks—but others quickly filled it.

There were just too many of them.

"It's not working!" Marcus shouted, blood trickling from his nose. "Too many minds to influence at once! It's like trying to terrify a puddle!"

“That makes no goddamn sense,” Olivia muttered.

“Keep them at bay!” Ace ordered.

“Right!” the others shouted in unison.

He needed to think of a plan, and in order to do that, he had to buy some time.

Behind him, his team fought with desperate coordination against the seething mass of slugs. Olivia slammed her makeshift staff into brain after brain with devastating force, taking out two of the bastards in a single movement before she pivoted out of the way of a third. Blood streamed from Marcus’s nose and ears as he strained to fracture the hive mind, managing only to turn small clusters against each other in brief, confused melees. Tara let loose a few arrows before raising her hands to form a pseudo-sanctuary—a crimson healing zone that the slugs instinctively avoided, much to Ace’s relief. The ground beneath them glowed with her power, but like Marcus, her face had gone chalk-white. Her reserves were clearly depleting with each passing second.

They were holding, but barely—and they couldn't maintain this defense much longer before exhaustion or acid claimed them all.

He needed to phase into the Shadow Realm, Mark the alpha slug’s brain, and throw Duskblades at the gooey bastard until it died.

A simple enough plan, perhaps, but he didn’t have the time to execute it.

Slugs continued to pour in from every angle, each coming a hair too close for comfort before one of the team inevitably stabbed them through their eyes. Three of them inched closer, their mouths opening as they bore down on Tara.

Nope.

Not going to happen.

Ace jumped and twisted in mid-air, his enhanced reflexes granting him a split-second assessment of what lay before them.

Two Duskblades.

Three slugs, coming close enough to eat his team alive.

One chance to get this right.

Ace didn't aim with his eyes, but rather with an instinct honed through combat and transformed by his new vampiric evolution. Each throw blurred through the air, his body too fast to follow as he launched each weapon at the nearest targets in rapid succession.

The first Duskblade pierced a slug attempting to engulf Tara, and the shadowy blade effortlessly cut through the goopy bastard’s brain. The creature's internal glow stuttered, then extinguished as the weapon's magic disrupted whatever passed for its nervous system.

Boom.

One down.

Still midair, Ace launched the next attack. This blade sank deep into the slug converging on Marcus, and its shadow energy left inky contrails through the air. The slug imploded on itself in a caustic puddle.

Boom.

One left.

He summoned a third Duskblade, and this one caught a larger specimen mid-lunge as it lurched for Olivia. The impact drove the weapon through the thing’s central core, pinning it momentarily to the dissolving stone ground before the slug's own acidic nature consumed both itself and the blade.

A familiar surge of power surged through his body—he was leveling.

Good.

Too bad he didn’t have time to use the AP he had earned.

Ace cataloged their assets with brutal efficiency: Olivia's speed and precision attacks; Tara's depleted but critical healing abilities; Marcus's psychic powers that had proven somewhat effective; his own shadow capabilities; and most importantly, their capacity to function as a coordinated unit.

Against that: a colossal acid-secreting monstrosity with distributed intelligence, regenerative capabilities, and the home field advantage.

Yay.

"The slug alpha's power seems centralized around those larger glowing nodes," Olivia called out, pointing toward three glowing orbs pulsing within the alpha's translucent mass. “Let’s try aiming for those!”

Ace's gaze fixed on the nodes, his enhanced vision detecting subtle differences in their pulsing patterns. He summoned another Duskblade into his palm, but he couldn’t keep doing this. Each one ate away at his Soul Meter, and he wouldn’t be able to regenerate his SM fast enough to take each of these bastards out. The sheer number of opponents was overwhelming, and their carefully planned roles were breaking down under the onslaught.

This wasn't a battle anymore.

It was survival.

The slugs pressed closer, their collective mass now a rippling wall of iridescent horror. Acid sizzled against stone, eating through the very ground beneath their feet. Marcus's terror projections barely slowed them now, as each pulse weakened as his SM reserves depleted. Olivia's strikes couldn't reach enough targets, and even when they did hit, more and more of her makeshift staff melted away. Tara's arrows disappeared into the beasts’ masses with minimal effect. It was like throwing toothpicks at an avalanche.

Time to hit this bastard where it hurt—assuming it had anything resembling pain receptors.

This is fucking stupid, he thought.

He plunged forward anyway.

"Watch my six!" he shouted to Olivia, who nodded sharply. Blood from a run-in with the slug-acid coated her forearms, but the spiraling smoke of her burning skin had faded thanks to Tara’s healing circle.

Ace activated Shadow Realm Immersion, his body phasing into that liminal space between dimensions. The world shifted, sounds becoming hollow and distant as his form faded into the other realm. The slug's pulsing bioluminescence took on an otherworldly glow, revealing internal structures invisible to normal sight. Something that might have been organs glowed brighter than the surrounding tissue—one near what passed for its head, and two more along its midsection.

Target acquired.

He raced through the Shadow Realm like a ghost, passing harmlessly through acid streams and minor slugs. He only had ten seconds before he would be kicked back into reality, and he had to make this count.

The cold emptiness of the realm clawed at him, whispering promises of oblivion if he lingered.

The massive slug's eyestalks swiveled frantically, most likely sensing his presence but unable to track him. He used Duskblade Manifestation to summon three shadow-forged daggers into his palms, and their edges greedily drank in what little light reached them. Each blade hummed with energy, eager for blood.

Time for Shadow Mark.

He channeled his magic through his body, focusing his attention on the three points he had identified, and he took aim. Red light erupted along the creature’s body where he had marked it.

Good.

Ace circled behind it, phasing back into reality at its flank. The transition felt like being ripped from a frozen lake—painful and jarring.

He didn’t care.

Ace launched the first blade at the light cluster near its head. The second and third followed milliseconds later, each slicing through the air with near-perfect aim. Shadows trailed behind the blades and created an illusion of multiple attacks.

The daggers sank deep into the monster’s skin, all three penetrating deep into the slug's gelatinous mass. The creature shuddered violently, its rhythmic pulse faltering.

Then everything went sideways.

Instead of collapsing, the slug's body contracted around the intrusions. Its flesh didn't just accept the blades—it consumed them, sucking them into its body until they disappeared entirely. Instead of weakening it, the daggers were absorbed. Integrated.

Repurposed.

The slug's glowing internal lights shifted, taking on a darker hue as shadow energy corrupted its biological systems. The three glowing organs Ace had targeted didn't dim—they intensified, visible now even outside of the Shadow Realm, and they now pulsed with a sickening combination of crackling light and shadow energy.

“Oh, goodie,” Tara said dryly. “It eats swords.”

The creature's body convulsed, and a shockwave of shadow-tainted acid erupted in all directions. Ace barely managed to activate Shadow Realm Immersion again, phasing through the worst of it, but his SM reserves were getting critically low. The effort left him dizzy, and he stumbled backward toward Tara.

When he fully solidified yet again, the monster was larger. More defined. Almost like it had… evolved.

Fantastic.

Tara’s face paled even further, her face set in the grim determination of someone who recognized death approaching but refused to welcome it. "This isn't looking good."

An understatement, for sure.

"It's absorbing the shadow energy," Olivia said from somewhere behind him.

“Thanks, Captain Obvious!” Marcus yelled back.

The slug's eyestalks locked onto Ace with newfound hatred. It didn’t just observe him, this time—it watched him with predatory focus. Those glowing eyes had developed vertical pupils, and they burned with rage.

The alpha reared back, its mass reshaping as shadow-infused acid pooled beneath it. The creature released a roar that carried harmonics straight from the Shadow Realm itself.

Ace's blood ran cold. He hadn't come close to killing it. Hell, he hadn’t even hurt the damn thing.

He had improved it.
