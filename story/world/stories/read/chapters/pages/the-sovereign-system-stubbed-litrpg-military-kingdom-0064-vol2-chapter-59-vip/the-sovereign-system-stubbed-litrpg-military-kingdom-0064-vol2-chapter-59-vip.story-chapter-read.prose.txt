Luke ascended the stairs quietly, his gaze flicking over the surroundings with caution. This was his first time entering the VIP section of any establishment—let alone a brothel as luxurious as this.

He steadied his breath, quelling the unease building in his chest. There were less than two hours left before his meeting with Ming Hu—a man as ruthless as he was imposing.

Even during their brief interaction, Luke had sensed something primal lurking beneath the surface. A coiled violence. An untamed beast barely restrained.

Intimidating was putting it mildly.

I can see why Hilda warned me to stay away from him... but I didn’t have much of a choice, he reasoned.

After all, it wasn’t his fault that the hulking brute had picked him out of the crowd almost immediately. It was as if Ming Hu had been drawn to him—shoddy disguise and all.

At the top of the staircase, Luke found himself in a modest open space, corridors stretching out in three directions. Each hallway had private rooms branching off—undoubtedly the VIP lounges where the wealthy came to indulge.

He had noticed similar rooms on the lower floor, though those were far smaller and—there was no polite way to put it—much seedier.

Er... what do I do now? He lingered awkwardly, unsure.

Turning to look for guidance, he saw that Fairy Qin had already drifted down the western hall without a word, her flowing robes vanishing around the corner. She hadn’t even glanced his way.

Not surprising. Someone like her was probably used to being ogled and pursued by eager patrons every night.

Thankfully, Hilda hadn’t left him behind. She caught his eye, flashed a wry smile, and pointed toward the eastern hallway.

“The room at the end should be free. Go ahead and freshen up. I’ll be along soon, so sit tight.”

Luke nodded, grateful. “Can you bring some food, too?”

“Sure. We’ve got fresh carp—it was delivered this morning.”

“Not seafood!” Luke cut in, more sharply than intended. The very thought of fish made his stomach churn.

Hilda raised a curious eyebrow but gave a small nod before following after Fairy Qin.

Luke didn’t wait around. He turned and made his way down the eastern corridor. Muffled sounds filtered through the walls—soft giggles, heavy breaths, music that twirled through the air like incense.

It was enough to stoke anyone’s imagination.

His cheeks flushed. He didn’t need much creativity to guess what was happening behind those doors.

Shaking the thought away, he reached the end of the hall and pushed open the final door. The thick wooden panel creaked on its hinges, revealing a room that was simple, yet elegant.

A red-cushioned lounge nestled into the far corner, its curving frame reminiscent of the carriages he’d ridden in before. At the center stood a polished table, large enough for four to dine in comfort.

But the item that dominated the room was the large, luxurious bed. For a brief moment, Luke's weary mind longed to collapse onto it and finally rest—something he hadn’t done properly in weeks since the campaign began.

Then a scream of passion rang out from the adjoining room.

He flinched, the sound snapping him back to reality. Any fantasies of sleep quickly evaporated as he remembered exactly where he was—and the likely purpose of the bed before him.

His expression soured.

Who knows what kinds of bodily fluids are staining that thing...

Turning away, Luke noticed a basin in the corner, filled with steaming water. A silken towel lay neatly beside it, suddenly looking far more inviting than the lavish bed.

Without hesitation, he stripped off his filthy clothes and sank into the basin.

The moment he stepped into the warmth, a shiver ran up his spine. Tension unwound from his muscles, and the herbal scent rising from the water stirred something peaceful inside him.

The water turned dark in seconds as he scrubbed the grime from his body, weeks of sweat and travel washing away under his hands.

I really need to hurry up and bring soap to the market, he thought bitterly. The herbal infusion was nice, but it couldn’t compare to the foamy lather and squeaky-clean feeling of modern soaps he’d once taken for granted.

As he pondered this, the door creaked open behind him.

Luke froze.

Turning his head slowly, he saw a woman standing in the doorway—someone he didn’t recognize. Her long, dark hair spilled over bare shoulders, and the translucent fabric of her robe clung to her curves, leaving little to the imagination.

Without a word, she stepped inside and gently shut the door behind her.

“Master Ming has sent me to serve you, young master,” she said, her voice a sultry purr. “Please... allow me to wash your back.”

A hundred thoughts rushed through Luke’s mind. After a long pause, he gave her a reluctant nod.

I can’t exactly refuse. Ming Hu might take offense, he reasoned.

Still, the faint thrum of his Dragon’s Heart stirred—alert, yet quiet. Luke ignored it, choosing to believe his decision was entirely pragmatic.

The woman approached with a graceful sway, picking up the washcloth. She knelt beside him and began to wash his back with delicate, rhythmic strokes. Her touch was gentle, her presence silent.

At first, the intimacy made him tense. But soon, the methodical motion lulled him into stillness.

When she moved to his shoulders and collarbone, her hands no longer felt intrusive—only warm, soothing, almost tender.

Bliss.That was the only word that fit.

The exhaustion he’d been fighting for days returned in full force. The steam, the warmth, the gentle touch—it all blurred into a soft haze. His body gave in.

Soon, the sound of gentle snores filled the room as Luke drifted off in the basin.

The woman blinked in surprise, her hands instinctively moving to cradle his head. She stared down at his sleeping face, then gave a quiet chuckle. With a surprisingly warm smile, she brushed a strand of wet hair from his brow.

Time passed.

Eventually, Luke stirred. His eyes fluttered open, blinking against the flickering amber of the lantern light.

The first thing he saw was a face—half-lit, close, and unfamiliar.

“Where... where am I?” he asked groggily, still disoriented.

The woman looked down at him and smiled warmly.“You’re in the Jade Silk Pavilion, honey. You should probably get out of that bath before you catch a cold,” she said with a soft giggle.

Luke blinked up at her, still groggy, his brain lagging behind her words. For a moment, he just stared at her face, vaguely aware of the flickering lantern light framing her features. Then, like a dam breaking, everything came crashing back.

He cursed inwardly and stood up abruptly, sending ripples and splashes across the water.

“How long was I out?” he asked, snatching up the nearby silk towel and wrapping it around himself.

The courtesan watched him with a playful glint in her eye, clearly amused.“A little over an hour,” she replied. “But don’t worry—you still have time to enjoy my services.”Her gaze flicked over his exposed body, lingering longer than Luke was comfortable with.

He shuddered, whether from the chill of the now-cool water or her attention, he wasn’t sure. Stepping out of the basin, he clutched the towel tighter around himself.

“Has Hilda come looking for me?” he asked, trying to steer the conversation away from the offer hanging in the air.

“She did,” the woman said lightly. “She said she’d return a little later. Are you two close? I’ve never seen Hilda with anyone other than Fairy Qin.” Her tone turned curious, almost teasing.

Luke didn’t answer. His eyes were busy scanning the room for something—anything—to wear. His old robes lay in a heap on the floor, stained with sweat and streaked with mud. The smell alone nearly made him gag.

There’s no way I’m putting those back on.

As if reading his mind, the woman moved to a nearby table and retrieved a folded white robe.

“Hilda brought this for you,” she said. “Let me help you dress.”

Luke hesitated for a beat, then nodded. The robe was soft and clean, not unlike the one Ming Hu had worn. A bit oversized, but the courtesan expertly folded the sleeves and adjusted the fabric until it rested neatly on his frame.

“Thank you,” Luke muttered, already feeling more human than he had in days. He paused, stomach growling faintly. “If you don’t mind, could you bring something from the kitchen? I haven’t eaten in a while...”

The woman stepped closer, running a finger lightly along his shoulder. Her eyes gleamed with suggestion.

“How about an appetizer first?” she whispered.

Before he could react, she slid the thin straps from her shoulders, letting the sheer fabric slip down her body. It pooled at her feet, leaving her completely exposed.

Luke’s breath hitched.

His gaze was involuntarily drawn downward, caught by the sight of her bare chest. For a moment, his thoughts blurred. He couldn’t help but wonder if his hands could even wrap around one of them.

“I...” he began, trying to force out a polite refusal.

But before the words could form, the familiar groan of a wooden door filled the room.

Luke’s head snapped toward the sound—just in time to see a figure step into the doorway.

Fairy Qin.

She stood in the entrance, her expression unreadable. Her gaze drifted over the scene—Luke in a white robe, the naked woman at his side, the scattered towel and fallen garments on the floor.

Her eyes narrowed, the briefest flicker of disdain flashing across her otherwise impassive face.

Luke swallowed hard.And for some inexplicable reason, guilt surged through him.

I didn’t do anything wrong... so why does it feel like I did?

He cleared his throat. “Can I help you, Miss Qin?” he asked, his voice catching slightly.

But before Fairy Qin could respond, Hilda’s large frame appeared behind her, a steaming bowl cradled in one hand. She scanned the room, taking in the scene with a single glance before letting out a weary sigh.

“That’ll be all, Melanie,” she said, dismissing the woman with a wave.

Melanie shrugged, unfazed. The casual roll of her shoulders caused her ample chest to jiggle slightly, but she made no attempt to cover herself. With practiced ease, she bent to gather the discarded robe and sauntered toward the exit—unhurried, unashamed, utterly accustomed to such displays.

Luke forced his gaze to remain fixed on Hilda. It took effort, but he managed.

Only once Melanie slipped through the door did he allow himself a quiet breath of relief.

“Thank you, Hilda,” he said, his voice tinged with sheepishness. “You saved me from a very awkward situation.” He chuckled, half embarrassed, gesturing toward the small table and chairs. “Is that for me?”

“Awkward situation?” Fairy Qin scoffed behind him. “You came to the Jade Silk Pavilion. Don’t pretend to be some innocent lamb after being caught red-handed. Hilda, I don’t know why you’d call this man your friend. He’s just another lowlife degenerate.”

The venom in her voice caught Luke completely off guard. He froze, turning toward the young woman with a raised brow.

She wasn’t joking.

This girl really doesn’t like me, he thought, bemused.

He shot a questioning glance toward Hilda, who only sighed, her expression caught somewhere between exasperation and resignation. She didn’t defend him. Instead, she quietly set the bowl on the table and eased herself into one of the chairs—doing so carefully, mindful not to crush the wood beneath her weight.

Luke joined her, grateful for the distraction. He pulled the bowl closer and inhaled deeply. A rich, savory aroma rose to meet him—tender chunks of braised beef floating in a fragrant broth, steam curling gently into the air. One spoonful was all it took to banish the lingering chill from his body.

He smacked his lips in satisfaction, warmth blooming in his chest. But when he looked up, Fairy Qin was still glaring at him from across the room.

Something in him snapped. After the brutal weeks on campaign, after everything he had endured, this treatment felt like too much.

“Would you care to sit?” he asked dryly. “Or do you not associate with ‘lowlife degenerates’?”

Fairy Qin harrumphed, arms crossing over her chest. She turned her face away as if the very sight of him offended her.

Why bother showing up at all if she’s going to act like this? Luke mused, irritation prickling beneath his skin. I can’t even speak properly with Hilda while she’s here. How annoying.

He finished the last of the soup and pushed the empty bowl forward. The taste lingered pleasantly on his tongue.

“Thank you for the meal, Hilda,” he said, refocusing. “So—about what we discussed?”

“Mmm.” Hilda leaned back, her expression thoughtful. “I’ve found you a place in the city. Due to your circumstances, it’s a bit... shoddy,” she admitted. “But it should serve. As for your second request, there won’t be any problems.”

Relief flooded through him. A roof over his head and contact with Sebastian and Kayson—that was all he needed to put his plan into motion.

But the moment was cut short by the sound of the door groaning open once more.

Everyone turned.

Melanie stood in the doorway, now fully dressed, her tone casual. “The boss says he’ll see all of you now.”

Luke’s stomach sank.

Ah, damn it. I completely forgot about that...

“What?” Fairy Qin blinked. “Father wants to see this man? Surely not?”

Melanie shrugged. “He asked for the guy by name.”

Hilda stood up, gesturing with her head towards the door, “Let’s go, we don’t want to keep him waiting.”
