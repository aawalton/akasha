“The new missions have been announced!” someone cried out. Everyone exclaimed and rushed for the gym. Gary remained cross-legged on the grass field, rereading the two screens he’d received ten minutes ago.

Battle Brief

Greetings, field bosses. The four coaches and six sector bosses have convened with Mr. Satan Jr. himself and arranged a battle plan which they believe should be shared.

We have spearheaded deep into tentacle territory. Thanks to the enemy’s glaring strategic flaws and our commanders’ valiant performance, we have established a foothold beyond the Three Bridges. Starlight drummed in his veins, making every move stronger and easier, and his core dripped a steady supply of aura into his body, effortlessly supercharging it. He was brimming with power.

“The rogues are here!” a boxer yelled.

Few wore climber robes. Most were dressed in simple armor, either leather or loose metal, with some wearing only shirts or more unique clothing. There was a bride and a groom, dressed accordingly, and Gary could swear he saw a naked man somewhere in the middle of the procession.

This place sucks, Gary thought.

The thousand-strong army crested a hill, and he immediately knew he was wrong. That place hadn’t sucked. This one did.

Wood-Eater Titan, Level 50 (Sector Boss)

This big boy was born as tiny as an ant, but with the special ability to absorb other creatures and grow stronger. He was meant to be a rival to the puppeteer, and there was an entire quest line about stopping this guy like ten years ago, which failed.

Remember that fallen Legion general who spat zombies from his ass? You fought him on the third floor. Well, that guy was part of a campaign to stop this titan, which was foiled by the puppeteer. It got control of both the titan and its attackers, cementing itself as the overlord of the third floor.

In the end, this growth boss, with the potential to become a floor boss, became but a minion. Such is the nature of climbing. Sometimes you fall.

Gary didn’t focus on the sector boss. His gaze scoured the entire grounds, and as it did, he was reminded of the video games he used to play in a time that felt so long ago. Compared to the easy-going, barely organized gyms of the gymnasium, the puppeteer’s efficient base-building reminded him of a tentacle-undead faction from Warcraft, even worse than the Forsaken.

Finally, the swampland titan turned to face them, a hundred feet of wood and muscle. It was made of vine and tentacle intertwined, a grotesque, violent creature. It wasn’t moving in the slow-motion way that Gary expected of such a massive monster, but rather at normal speed, like a magnified human. That alone made it much more threatening.

And the puppeteer is even stronger… Gary thought, licking his dry lips.

The armies sized each other up. Even the monsters had stopped howling and yowling as if gripped by an unknown force.

Six people stepped forth: Boulderus Johnus, Feng Shen, the boxing champ, the wrestling champ, a woman dressed in golden robes, and a man in rags. They stood in front of the army, right at the crest of the hill, staring at the monsters with their chins held high.

Gary stared at the backs of these six people. They were the leaders of their army, six of the eight sector bosses they possessed. Each had led an army to a mission. People like Boulderus Johnus had succeeded twice. They deserved to stand at the front, but how wide their backs seemed.

And how small was Gary’s?

Envy ate at him. He wanted to stand at the front, too. He wanted to exchange barbs with the titan. He wanted to cover the army with his chest, and raise his weapon first towards the enemy. To dive into battle with the highest stakes.

Why can’t I? he wondered. Feng Shen had broken through very recently. He couldn’t possibly be level 50. He only held the position due to his potential, but Gary’s was at a similar level. Why then, couldn’t he stand at the front, while Feng Shen could?

Gary himself had enmity with the puppeteer. The enemy had abandoned two missions just to get him—and failed. Undoubtedly, something similar would happen during this battle. Gary might end up playing a larger role than any of these generals, and his life was in the greatest danger, and his potential was at least as great as theirs, so why couldn’t he stand with them?

He looked around. Nobody moved. No one seemed to share his thoughts. This could only go one way: everyone would stay put, and the people ahead would stay ahead. Even the thought of stepping forward now, under everyone’s gazes, brought Gary under huge pressure.

But he wanted it.

Two paths stretched in front of Gary. They had always been there, but this was the first time he really saw them. One path was quiet. It was what he’d been following his entire life before the tower. Keep your head down, do your best in your given position, study, graduate, get a girlfriend, get a job. Ace the track you’ve been given.

But, sometimes, that track led nowhere. He hadn’t been happy or fulfilled. Ever. His entire life, he’d been a shadow.

The second path was different. It was hard, and embarrassing, and might well fail. He’d step into the limelight. People would mock and judge him. There would be no rules, no safety, no guarantees. The mere act of stepping onto the path required tremendous willforce, because the world itself pushed him away from it, as did every habit and behavior he’d ever learned.

It was a path of defiance, selfishness, and resolve. It was also the only path suited for a climber.

Gary grabbed reality and twisted it to his liking. He stepped out of the lines. He was no longer protected and anonymous in a crowd, but alone in full sight. Every eye rested on him, watched him, bathed in his indecency. He paced to the crest of the hill and stood alongside the man in rags, who glanced his way. All the sector bosses did. Even the titan and the army below. Gary could feel his own army’s doubtful, angry, mocking gazes burning into his back. He met the eyes of the man in rags. They didn’t want him there.

What can you do about it? Gary asked with his eyes, calmly holding the other man’s stare.

And, suddenly, all those gazes were just that. Eyes. They were powerless to stop him and could only pressure, but that pressure was hollow.

The man in rags seemed dissatisfied, but there was nothing he could do. He couldn’t create a scene here, and even if he could, he might not want to. He turned back to the front. The other generals did the same. Feng Shen gave him a slight nod and smile. The thousand gazes burning into his back changed subtly, from mockery, to disbelief, to bitter acceptance. Some admired him. Over time, people would think this had always been his place.

Gary now stood at the front with everyone’s acceptance. He’d carved his own path, simply because he dared. Reality stabilized into a new balance point.

Seven people stood at the crest of the hill, all on the same line. Boulderus Johnus raised his shortsword. “For the gymnasium!” he yelled. “For freedom!”

“For freedom!” the army roared and poured down the hill as one, descending on the endless horde of monsters.
