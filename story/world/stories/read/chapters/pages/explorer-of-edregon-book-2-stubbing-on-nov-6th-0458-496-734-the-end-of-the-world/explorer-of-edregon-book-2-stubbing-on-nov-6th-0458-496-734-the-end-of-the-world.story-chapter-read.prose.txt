The Beacon’s nook did in fact end up getting some use for the first time in a few months, as after Tabetha managed to collect herself somewhat, the four of them sat her down and listened to her story. Shia never let go of the woman’s hand, and Reginald sat on her shoulder, rubbing his furry face lovingly against her own anytime she started looking like she was going to cry again.

“It all started a few years back,” Tabetha began, shaking her head slowly at the memory. “Monsters started popping up more and more often, and settlements started collapsing as warriors were spread thin. Some of our smartest predicted that if nothing drastic was done, our entire race would be wiped from the face of our planet. Rather than let that happen, a drastic plan was whipped up and quickly put into action. The Beacon.”

“A stronghold designed to withstand anything the monsters could throw at it,” Vin surmised, wondering just how strong the outer walls had to be. There was no chance in hell of any regular or even elite monster breaking through into the lighthouse, but he wasn’t sure how the place would fare against some of the epic monsters he’d seen.

“Exactly. It took a few years to build, and entire countries fell during that time as things just kept getting worse and worse. But in the end, it was finished. I wasn’t even supposed to be on the original list of people allowed into the Beacon. The person who’d been allotted my slot had ended up getting killed, and the person chosen to replace them died as well during the attacks. I was the fourth in line for my slot, but I managed to make it to the Beacon alive, which wasn’t something many people could say. Despite being designed to hold upwards of ten thousand divigel, only about six thousand managed to make it before we were forced to seal the doors. That was about two years ago.”

“Two years?” Scule blinked. “Wow… That’s a lot longer than I was expecting to hear.”

“Yeah,” Tabetha nodded. “At first, things were okay. I mean, being trapped within a giant stone tower and unable to fly around and truly stretch our wings was difficult, but we were all in the same roost. We were able to confide in one another and stay strong together. Then… people started dying again. Only not to monsters this time. But to disease.”

Tabetha paused, shaking slightly as the memories came back to her. Shia squeezed her hand and Reginald rubbed against her some more, and the divigel smiled, wiping the tears from her eyes before they could fall.

“Things started small, as they always seem to do. A few people began coughing here and there. Slight fevers and mild aches popped up more and more frequently. Jokes were made about burning through the Beacon’s countless medical supplies in only the first year of living within its walls. Then things got worse.

“The first to die were the elderly, so we still didn’t realize just how bad the disease was. But as it began spreading and people began falling one after another, we quickly realized just how dire the situation was. The Beacon Keeper, a brave man who had fought tirelessly against the monster hordes until losing one of his wings and being forced to retire, tried everything he could think of, even going so far as to declare an internal lockdown and separate the Beacon into quarantined areas. Only by that point, it was already too late.”

“Wait, didn’t you have any Healers?” Scule asked, his brow furrowed in confusion. “Sorry, I’m not trying to nitpick your story, I’m just confused. Curing a disease is a pretty simple thing to do from what I’ve seen. The Citadel has had to deal with a lot of crap, but never a plague.”

“Scule, you grew up in your world’s most populated holy city,” Shia said, frowning at him. “Divine classes don’t just grow on trees. Not to mention certain diseases are far trickier to deal with than others. It’s similar to Regeneration in a way, you need to know what exactly you’re dealing with, or the magic can do more harm than good.”

“Most of our top Healers had died during all the fighting,” Tabetha explained. “And the few who had been chosen to replace them, who actually had managed to make it to the Beacon, were some of the first killed by the disease as they tried to study it like you said. At first, things looked like they were going to be turned around. Only…”

“You got hit by the Great Reset,” Vin gasped, the pieces clicking into place as he stared at the woman in horror. “Your people lost their levels and spells, including your Healers, at the worst possible time. Even their attributes reset, including vigor.”

“Exactly,” Tabetha confirmed. “I could curse the Gods for doing what they did to us, but it’s all sort of just numb at this point. Anyway, that’s why our Healers weren’t able to stop the spread.”

“Damn…” Scule muttered. “Sorry, keep going.”

“After that, the deaths piled up one after another. We went from dozens of people dying each day to hundreds. Yet as the sickness spread and more and more people kept dying…. Throughout it all, I and I alone remained completely unaffected,” Tabetha said, clenching her eyes shut as she shuddered. “Me. Out of thousands of amazing people chosen specifically to be the future of our people for their valiant deeds and talents, I was the one and only person who didn’t get sick. You want to know what I am? I’m a Cleaner. And evidently, not even all that impressive of one, seeing as I was the fourth person in line for this spot.”

“That actually makes some sense,” Shia said, rubbing the woman’s back as she thought. “As a Cleaner, you probably exposed yourself to all manner of germs and disease over the years. You unintentionally made your body more robust and harder to take down from disease and infection without even trying.”

“I did tend to get sick quite a lot,” Tabetha muttered before shaking her head. “Though it seems I’m a special case even in that regard. It’s not as if I was the only Cleaner brought into the Beacon. Not when it was designed to hold ten thousand people. There were a bunch of us at the start, and while the others did manage to last longer than most, one after another they fell ill and died just like the rest. By the end, I was the last person going around and collecting bodies, bringing them down to the incinerator to keep the Beacon from overflowing with corpses. Eventually, after months of death and the world falling apart all over again, six thousand divigel had been reduced down to two. It was just myself and the old Beacon Keeper.”

“He’d probably put his body through the wringer as well from all the fighting,” Vin nodded. Despite knowing how this story was going to end, he realized he was on the edge of his seat. Tabetha seemed to be a natural storyteller, each of her words flowing with emotion that had been bottled up inside her for months.

“I’ll never forget that last conversation the two of us shared,” Tabetha said, her wings drooping behind her as she hung her head. “He praised me for all my hard work keeping the Beacon clean of disease-riddled corpses. Despite the fact that he was hacking up his lungs and barely able to breathe, he walked me through just how important the Beacon was to our people. From the very beginning, when we realized we still had plenty of empty space, he’d turned on the searchlights. The hope was that any divigel fleeing from the monsters might see the Beacon from afar and come join us. He made me promise to stay here and keep the Beacon up and running. To ensure that a chance for our people’s future stood strong. His last words were used to bequeath his title of Beacon Keeper onto me, and I’ve been stuck in here ever since. That was… that was about six months ago. Flying out to retrieve you four was the first time I’ve left the Beacon in all that time.”

“You’ve been stuck in here all on your own for six months?!” Scule gasped. “Why not just fly out for a quick breath of fresh air? Not like anyone was left to yell at you for it.”

“Scule!” Shia snapped, but Tabetha merely gave him a tired smile and held up a hand to calm the Druid down.

“It’s alright. I had the same thought more times than I care to admit since the last Keeper passed. The real reason is because, as it was designed as a fortress, the Beacon can’t be opened from the outside after the runes are first activated. If I were to leave and the door ended up shutting somehow… I’d never be able to get back in. All my people’s hard work, everything they died for, would be lost. There are too many deadly monsters flying around, I wasn’t willing to risk it. Not until you guys showed up. Though I have to admit, I don’t actually understand what any of you are,” Tabetha said, chuckling weakly as she looked between them. “None of you are divigel, that much is obvious. But if that’s the case, what are you?”

“Tabetha… You don’t know anything about Edregon, do you?” Vin realized, sighing as she gave him a blank look. “I’m going to assume your world didn’t have other races? It was just the divigel?”

“Yeah. Wait, what do you mean ‘your world’?”

“The Great Reset wasn’t just something that struck you and your people,” he said, pulling out one of the pamphlets he’d had Terra’s own printing press put together for him. “Nor were the increasingly deadly monster swarms unique to your world. The old universe had some problems, and the Gods did everything in their power to grab what they could and make a new one. You and your people ended up getting dragged along for the ride. It’s all in there.”

Staring at him with wide eyes, Tabetha reached out a shaky hand and took the pamphlet from him. As their fingers brushed, Vin silently cast Comprehend Languages, making it so that she could read the English words without issue. While she read through it, Shia frowned at him and leaned closer to the two of them, casting Still Wind around Tabetha so that she wouldn’t hear their conversation.

“Seriously? The pamphlet? Vin, Tabetha’s been through an incredibly traumatic ordeal. Don’t you think this is just a little impersonal?”

“What? It’s not like I’m throwing it at her and running away,” Vin argued. “I’d like to see you give the ‘welcome to Edregon, your old world is dead and gone’ speech a few dozen times and then complain about the pamphlet. We’ll still be here to answer any questions she has, I’m just speeding things up a bit. I seem to recall even giving you said speech back when we first met. I’ve been doing the same thing for ages now!”

“Woah, touched a nerve there,” Scule said, hopping off Vin’s shoulder and wandering over to the door. “Well, now that we’ve all heard that incredibly tragic tale, I feel like we’ve all gotten to know Tabetha a bit better as a person. As such, I’m fairly confident she wouldn’t mind if I went through the Beacon and helped myself to anything particularly useful. Just shout if you need me. I have a feeling this place will have some fantastic echoes.”

“You two are so heartless,” Shia frowned, going back to rubbing Tabetha’s back as the woman kept reading.

“I can only handle seeing the look on a person’s face when they realize everyone from their old world who didn’t get brought over to Edregon is either already dead or soon will be so many times,” Vin grumbled. “And that’s not any sort of pain magic or even divinity can do anything about.”
