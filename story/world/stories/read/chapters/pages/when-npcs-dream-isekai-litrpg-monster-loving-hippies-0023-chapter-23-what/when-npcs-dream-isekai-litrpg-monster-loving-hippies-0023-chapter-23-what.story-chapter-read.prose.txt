“So you did it?” asked Hyun-woo when we all met back in Juliette’s room.

“Yea,” confirmed Samar proudly. “Dubadilu is now officially the new member of our tribe.”

“Dubadilu…” I repeated, thinking. “I think dūb is ‘bear’ in Arabic, so I’m guessing she did a bear–armadillo word blend. Not that I’m complaining.”

“And where is… ah… Dubadilu now?” Juliette asked. “With Kim Jong-un, I assume?”

“Mm-hm,” Samar nodded. “Kim said he had to do some special training with Dubadilu—teaching him not to wander off when Kim’s not around, and to come when Kim makes this special screeching call. It was taking forever and got really boring, so I left them near the forester’s cabin. I’ll go pick Kim up in a few hours.”

“Wait, near the forester’s cabin?” Hyun-woo frowned. “Isn’t that dangerous? The NPC could think they’re wild monsters and try to attack them.”

“I thought of that,” Samar replied, smug and satisfied. “So I introduced Dubadilu to Elrik and Lori. I stayed there with them until the NPCs got used to seeing them. At first they looked all tense and angry, but after a while their faces relaxed. Now they don’t see Dubadilu as a bad monster anymore. So it’s safe.”

We all blinked at her.

“Wow,” said Juliette, impressed. “You really thought that through.”

“Well, of course,” Samar said, puffing her chest. “I’m smart. I’m learning how all these robot people work.”

I still wasn’t a hundred percent convinced it was safe, but it was a smart move. Samar was turning into a surprisingly dependable part of our little group.

“Well,” Juliette went on, “while you were off in the forest, Hyun-woo and I finished a tavern quest. Earned us twenty silvers.”

“Sweet!” I said, excited. “How did it go?”

“It was…” Juliette rubbed her forehead. “God, it was long and weird. Honestly not sure it was worth the trouble.”

“What was weird?” I asked, genuinely curious.

“Let me tell you,” Hyun-woo said, taking a big breath. “Matilda, the herbalist at the apothecary, wanted to make a ‘Grand Restoration Tonic.’ She was ‘terribly busy,’ so she needed us to run just a few errands. First, we had to fetch a sprig of dawnblossom from Brother Henric at the Chapel of Seven Lamps. But Brother Henric refused to hand it over unless we helped him chase three runaway ducks. Then he insisted we deliver a letter across town to the tanner’s wife. But she wouldn’t even let us in unless—”

“Okay, I get it,” I cut in, laughing. “Sounds… harsh.”

“Not really harsh,” Samar jumped in smugly. “While you were running errands, we were fighting goblins and chasing wild armadillo-bears. That’s what we did.”

That earned me one hell of a glare from Hyun-woo.

“Hey, we didn’t actually fight any goblins,” I said quickly, raising my hands defensively. “We met a group of goblins, and one happened to be the same guy I helped before. Oh—that reminds me. They told us they ran into a group of five humans players.”

“Five bad players,” Samar added solemnly.

I told them everything about the goblin encounter.

“You think they started in another city?” Hyun-woo asked.

“Either that,” I said, “or they arrived weeks before us in Kamatouu and already moved on. Once we reach Kuriolis, we’ll probably find more players and maybe get actual answers.”

Hyun-woo hesitated, then asked quietly, “And… how long do you expect to stay in Kuriolis?”

Ah. Right. He wasn’t coming with us. He planned to stay behind, mostly to help new arrivals. And now that Samar was determined to join us, it clearly weighed heavier on him.

“I honestly don’t know,” I admitted. “Depends on what we find there. Could be two weeks… could be more.”

“I see…” he murmured, thoughtful.

“You know, we could just leave big signs,” I offered. “Put instructions for new players in the central square. That way you could come with us.”

“I thought about it,” he said, shaking his head. “NPCs might remove the signs. And even if they didn’t, new players may not trust them.”

“Yeah, you’re probably right.” I leaned forward, more serious. “But listen—if we end up staying there longer than two weeks, I’ll come back to get you. By then, enough new players should’ve shown up here to take over guiding duties..”

“It’s a two- or three-day trip,” he reminded me.

“I know.” I smiled. “But you’re an important part of this group. And you’re smart, like, super smart. We need you if we want to make sense of this world.”

His face softened into a genuine smile. “Thank you. You’re important to me too.”

Oh no. Embarrassing bro moment. I had no idea how to respond. Someone please say something!

“Too bad there aren’t any phones here,” Juliette cut in. Bless her. “Would make this a lot easier.”

“There might be,” I said quickly. “Some kind of magical device that works like a phone.”

“You mean so we can watch cats on TikTok?” Juliette teased.

“That too,” I smirked.

Honestly, magical accessories were one of the biggest reasons I wanted to reach Kuriolis. Not just for convenience—like magic phones—but because magical artifacts could shed light on the system itself. Like special glasses that let you see stats, for example.

Later, Samar and I went to pick up Kim Jong-un and Dubadilu near the forester’s cabin. I was curious to see the training progress.

When we arrived, we saw the strangest sight yet: a scorpion-suricata clinging to the scaled back of a care-bear armadillo as it trotted around doing awkward gymnastic exercises. The whole piggyback routine looked so ridiculous I burst out laughing. Samar joined in seconds later.

Even Elrik and his daughter Lori stood on the porch watching, looking more curious than hostile. When we laughed, they turned to glance toward us.Then it got weirder—Lori suddenly stepped off the porch and walked straight toward us.We froze as she kept coming. She was… coming right at me!

I took a hesitant step back. What the hell? Was she still angry at me for lifting her out of the pit? Was she about to pull a dagger and stab me? But no — she didn’t look angry. If anything, she looked… a little happy.

She stopped just two feet from me, staring right into my eyes.

“…”

And that was it. She didn’t say anything. Just stood there.

“…What?” I blurted, flustered.

Still nothing. Just staring. Her face really close now.

“Is… is there something you want?” I asked awkwardly.

“…”

Jesus. She was one strange NPC.

“Okay then…” I said finally. “Nice to meet you too. Bye!” I spun on my heel and walked toward Kim and Dubadilu, then glanced back to make sure Lori wasn’t following.

Samar fell into step beside me, smirking. “She totally likes you,” she whispered.

“That’s stupid,” I hissed. “She’s an NPC robot, remember?”

“Well,” Samar said, “if they can hate us when we annoy them, why can’t the opposite be true? You did rescue her, after all.”

Shit. She had a point. And I was pretty sure my cheeks were red.

“Alright—we’re not telling Juliette about this,” I said firmly.

“Sure,” Samar said innocently. Which definitely didn’t guarantee silence. Oh god, if Juliette found out, she’d think… that I would think… uh, whatever.

“Hi Kim! Hi Dubadilu!” Samar called happily.

The two finished their bizarre exercise. Kim hopped down from Dubadilu’s back and immediately climbed into Samar’s arms, purring like he belonged there. They had the weirdest bond.

I placed my hand on his furry back. “So, how’s the training?”

He showed me mental flashes: Dubadilu coming when called, standing guard against a charging cow-deer, but refusing to attack on command yet. That part would need more time.

“Great work, Kim!” I praised him. Honestly, it was impressive. “How about spear training?”

Not yet, he showed, but soon. Good enough.

We didn’t really need a carebear for attacking; not yet. For travel, just protecting us was good enough.

I mentally checked our travel list.

Supplies: food, gear, camping stuff—check.

Money: over fifty silvers now, enough for weeks and maybe a magic trinket or two—check.

One trained armadillo care bear guard—check.

Three brave human adventurers, plus one stubborn teenage scorpion suricata—check.

Strong, fearless, and handsome leader? Definitely check.

Okay… probably I wasn’t the leader. But never mind that — we were ready.

That evening, we all met at the tavern for one last night together before departing—and as a farewell for Hyun-woo.

It was a great night. We swapped stories from home, laughed until our sides hurt, and me and Hyun-woo had a second round of ale and got a little tipsy. Well, I got tipsy—Hyun-woo handled it like a pro.

I didn’t plan to let this be a permanent farewell. I really liked Hyun-woo, but more than that—I believed we needed him. He had this way of spotting details I missed, of thinking logically when sometimes the rest of us were running on impulse. He was crucial for unraveling the mysteries of this world and making real progress.

When morning came, I shot up in bed, heart racing with excitement. This was the day I’d been waiting for! I was ready! I opened my eyes—and immediately wanted to die.

Everything spin. Oh god… what is this? Am I sick?

“That’s called a hangover,” Juliette diagnosed with one look. “You probably overdid it last night with that second glass of ale. Your first hangover?”

“Yes,” I groaned. “Is it… bad?”

She laughed. “No, silly. Just drink water. In an hour or two you’ll be fine. Ah… fine-ish.”

And so, with a heart brimming with excitement , a pounding headache, and a stomach threatening revolt, we set out for our grand adventure.
