After eating dinner, Ferris and Jade settled down in their room. It’d been almost two days since they’d died in the soulspace, and Ferris was excited to return. He wasn’t sure exactly of the exact time it’d happened, but the two days was a loose time frame, anyway.

Ferris laid on the floor next to Jade, with the slime chained to the dresser as usual. He closed his eyes and tried to meditate. He hadn’t felt the oath statue break, but it was hard not to worry about it. He’d made it this long, but would he be headed back into a crisis, or was his theory about the tall demon correct?

His thoughts wandered as he practiced meditative breathing. Jade’s memory problems were more complicated than he’d thought, but the slime’s perfect short-term memory was amazing. Talking with Serena had gotten Ferris excited about Jade’s Adaptation ability all over again—after all, it was the final reason he’d chosen a slime familiar. It was hard not to dream about what it could do for him in the future. The book on cooking… Ferris didn’t even know where to begin unpacking his thoughts on that.

With time, his mind quieted to emptiness. Opening his eyes, Ferris was excited to find himself back in his soulspace. He quickly checked over the area around his Heartfire, but nothing was out of place. His Qi Pool was still depressingly small, little more than a handful of liquid. The only weapon he had was the heavy axe, but it was better than nothing.

Entering the slimespace, Ferris collected Jade. The grey ants and birds were as lively as ever, swarming around yet thankfully still ignoring him.

Jade was energetic as usual, hopping along as they traveled together to the Core Tree. The fruit was more colorful than before, but not quite like the other two had been. Ferris guessed it wasn’t ready, but it would be soon. He really wanted to water the tree to help produce new fruit, but his Qi Pool had nothing to give. He took Jade with him to check the spots he’d planted the two ability seeds, but there was still nothing there.

They both needed more qi and time before they would sprout. Then, it would require dedicating more qi if he wanted either usable in the short term.

“Jade,” Ferris spoke over the bond, “how do you want to fight?”

The slime swirled. The idea of ‘wanting’ things was limited to what was immediately in front of them. Jade wanted food, they wanted to explore. Anything more got confusing.

The two of them had coordinated to fight the demons, but that had been all at Ferris’ direction. He gave commands, and Jade was happy to follow. Without any guidance, the slime would simply charge and try to consume the closest enemy.

Trying something new, Ferris focused on imagining two futures. One, where the Grow ability made Jade larger, the other where Jump allowed them to hop quickly around.

“I’m not that large,” Jade sent back, “I can’t jump like that.”

“No,” Ferris replied, “but you could, if you want.”

Jade continued swirling, and Ferris decided to give his familiar time to think. Time to learn.

After they returned to the soulspace, Ferris had a problem. He needed a lightstone to see in the darkness, but didn’t have enough qi in his pool to make one. He’d used up everything he had assaulting the chain demon’s fort.

This was a problem he’d solved before—with advice from his uncle. After all, he hadn’t had a Qi Pool at all the first time he cultivated.

The Heartfire was a source of fire, and his mental body was fully clothed. Walking around without a shirt or pants wasn’t fun—especially with the demons about—but it was possible.

The first time he’d searched for his Air Passage, he used this fire to provide light. He’d taken his shirt off, tore it into long strips, braided it back together into a wick-like rope, and set the end on fire.

It’d burned at a steady rate, although slower than it would in the real world… perhaps due to his intent? Did intent work without qi? He wasn’t sure.

Either way, this had given enough light to explore the area around his Heartfire for about five minutes. It’d taken him several tries, trying random directions until he found his Air Passage. After each attempt, he’d needed to wait for his clothes to come back the next day, but it’d worked.

He could do that again, but this time he wanted to try something new. First Steps of the Soul had suggested a different way. It’d mentioned Uncle Winton’s method as well, but only as an alternative.

Ferris sat down and closed his eyes. Jade rolled over into his lap, watching his actions through the bond. Ferris focused on his breathing, in and then out. He tried to find a sense of connection to the Air Passage through his breathing technique. That strange falling sensation that existed around the Air Passage should be detectable from further away, if he knew how to look for it.

No matter where he was in the soulspace, Ferris could always point directly at the Heartfire. It was an undeniable presence in his mind. First Steps of the Soul claimed that with practice the same connection could be felt to any open meridian.

It took a while, but Ferris was well accustomed to meditative breathing. He’d been doing it his entire life, after all. Eventually, he felt a sort of resonance with each breath. Almost like an echo too faint to carry. It came from… the direction he was facing.

Well, Ferris had been to the Air Passage many times. He knew its general direction well. A small voice of doubt wondered if the feeling was his imagination.

Standing up, Ferris tied his sack to his back. He carried Jade in one hand and the axe in his other, trying to follow the echo to its source.

It was much better than the first time he had done this, searching randomly until his torch ran out, then running back in the dark. He still didn’t have any light, but the two anchors—the strong beating Heartfire and subtle resonating Air Passage—gave a sense of security. Each step closer made him more sure the feeling was real, and Ferris wasn’t surprised at all to finally see glowing rocks of qi as he found his destination.

Two days ago, Ferris had picked the Air Passage clean. The strange, cloaked demons had stolen some as well, and he could tell they’d been back. There was about half of what Ferris expected the Air Passage to produce normally, but at least they had left him something. Not enough to make another spear, but better than nothing.

Ferris and Jade made it through the loop of the Air Passage without encountering any demons. They only had the dim light of the glowing qi chunks, but he walked with one hand feeling the wall of the trench. It was good enough to see dimly. At least Jade was comfortable in the darkness, used to not being able to see at all. The trip back was even easier with the stronger sense of his Heartfire to follow.

After dumping the paltry collection of qi chunks in the pool, Ferris had to wait for them to liquify. He wanted to go check on the oath statue at the fort, but he needed to wait to make a lightstone. He also wanted a new spear… but even if he had enough qi, his cooking pot was filled with the solid rock of hardened demon remains from his experiment. The axe would have to suffice.

Ferris chipped away a little at the hardened material in the pot. It had been pure liquid demon until it cooled, he wondered if this could be a good material to build with. He would need some way to shape it, maybe make bricks or a mold. Demon corpses were plentiful enough that he could build walls or other structures, although Jade would be sad to lose their snacks. He thought about the fort, somehow created through whatever spiritual process spawned the demons. He would feel a lot safer if he had his own walls protecting his Heartfire, or maybe to keep the cloaked demons out of his Air Passage.

Looking around, Ferris started to plan where he could add defenses. The chain demon’s theft had caused him serious problems. The experience made him wonder if cultivators suppressed their emotions to avoid heart demons.

Maybe I’m looking at it from the wrong direction, he thought. Instead of smothering my emotions to stop them from appearing, just… become so strong they aren’t a problem?

If he had fortifications so large and impenetrable that the demons never stood a chance, then he could protect himself from… himself. Allow himself to feel. The demons would also get more dangerous as he advanced his cultivation, but maybe there was a balance.

Ferris sighed. All he had were guesses. For some reason he doubted ‘just be stronger’ would always work. There was some truth to it, though. I certainly won’t get anywhere without strength.

After enough qi had liquified to produce a lightstone, Ferris and Jade set out for the fort. They encountered two groups of chain demons but ran away both times. Thankfully, neither group had a frog demon attached, as he wasn’t looking forward to fighting the quick things with the heavy axe.

Arriving at the fort, Ferris and Jade were unable to get very close to it due to patrolling groups of chain demons. The fort looked the same, draped in chains with four towers blazing with beacon fires.

Best of all, he could see the oath statue was still intact, hanging on its hook. I knew it, he thought. It looks just like it did two days ago.

The statue was still cracked—it hadn’t magically repaired itself—but it was no longer actively being attacked. It was as good as Ferris could have hoped for, and he let out a long sigh of relief. Despite everything, he’d won time to properly fix this.

Settling in with the lightstone covered, Ferris and Jade spent a while watching the fort. There were fewer demons than before they’d culled them, but the creatures still occupied the area. They sometimes walked along the top of the walls, but they didn’t actively stand guard like before. There was no sign of another tall, lanky demon.

When he had a new crossbow—and some careful patience—Ferris and Jade could wipe them all out. He would then be in possession of a fort far from his Heartfire, and a statue too heavy to move. The demons would likely just wait until he woke up and then retake the fort… unless he could find some way to defend it while not present. There must be a way.

On the way back to the Heartfire—when not running away from demons—Ferris considered his next move. Most of his immediate problems were due to lack of qi. His Air Passage produced a set amount every day. He could, and eventually would, deepen and widen it to increase the flow, but that would only help long term. Uncle Winton had told him improving it would come later, after he started delving.

On the other hand, the slimespace had an abundance of qi; he just needed to find a way to collect it. Even better, he could start actively feeding Jade to produce more. Uncle Winton had told Ferris to collect qi regularly, as it would stop accumulating if you neglected it.

The Digest River was filled with chunks of qi, each one proudly displayed on one of the strange clams in its riverbed. He estimated there was about ten times as much qi as a single day of gathering from his Air Passage, just out of reach.

His next priority was clear: focus on finding a way to gather every single chunk of qi.

Then, when the river was empty, he’d feed Jade up and do it all again.

The slime would feast.
