Alex, in the Cavern with Strange and Grizzle…

As I clutched my fist, staring down at the rock, the tingling in my fingers increased.

“Alpha, we should flee,” said Grizzle, keeping his voice low, his tail sweeping back and forth across the rocky ground. “Whatever is out there is much higher than us. Dangerously so.”

Strange bounced up and down, staring at Grizzle, then chirped, “Rank 3.”

I almost coughed, but swallowed hard instead. While I had just defeated a rank three creature, I wasn’t sure if fighting another one right now was the smart choice. Whatever Noseen had sent me was important.

It made my fingers feel just… like the tooth.

The Devourer tooth that I’d used as a backup knife, then had been consumed during my rank up. Now, he sent me something from a different Devourer… to eat?

Strange’s head twisted from Grizzle, to me, then back without saying anything.

“Just who is following you?” I finally asked, trying to get a better idea of just who Noseen wanted me to eat.

Probably someone with wings, knowing him.

Strange cocked his head to one side. “Three flying bugs, but big. Too big for me to eat.”

This time I swallowed hard.

“Three Rank 3’s,” said Grizzle, taking a step back. “That’s…”

“Not winable,” I added, without even thinking about it. One, sure, maybe, in the dark with lots of notice and setup. Three all at once?

Not gonna happen. At least not yet.

Did Noseen want me dead?

The rock vibrated in my fingers. The rock in my hand had something to do with it, but this wasn’t a safe space, especially not as something dark red appeared on my radar, on the far edges. The dot slowly moved toward the opening at the front of the cavern.

The dungeon entrance behind me pulsed with mana.

“Can you flee into the dungeon?” I asked Grizzle, this time studying him.

“Not until after the Bright,” he grumbled, baring his teeth. “Time passes too slowly, and this might not be cold enough.”

I let out a sigh. This cavern wasn’t dark enough for me to completely vanish in, but the tunnel twisting this way from the waterfall was.

Hopefully, I could persuade the clan assassins to back off. Though, if they were Rank 3, they shouldn’t have anything to do with us. We were too low level for them. I needed to know more.

“This isn’t a fight for you,” I said to Grizzle, then motioned to the water. “Flee, stay safe through the Bright.”

Strange stepped into my shadow and vanished, making it clear where he would stay.

Before Grizzle could respond, I darted to the darkness, pulling on my stealth as I placed the tingling chunk of rock into my inventory. My fingers itched as I pulled on my spear and waited around the second corner for whatever bug had come to join us.

Whatever happened, I couldn’t attack first. If I attacked first, then I was free game, if they were civilized. If they weren’t, it didn’t matter. I’d need to strike hard, and keep the cavern free.

I mentally kicked myself for not meeting with Doc for more explosives.

Those would be handy right now.

The red dot approached the cavern and inched inside. I let every muscle in my body relax as I huddle along the outside of the tight corner.

Grizzle darted to the pool and then dove under. The water didn’t make a sound, and his dot floated across my radar far beneath the entrance.

Nothing appeared in the darkness, but the dot stubbornly peeked around the corner. Then it inched farther into the tunnel, traveling closer and closer to my location.

I didn’t even breathe.

Everything inside me screamed that there was something there, even though I couldn’t see it. My second sight triggered and an outline appeared, along with ripples in the mana floating within the air. The person stood at about my height, and kept close to the opposite wall.

It had two arms, two legs, and something buzzing on its back.

One guess came to mind, but until I could confirm it, I tried not to worry.

The outline inched closer to me, and its head twisted in my direction.

I didn’t dare physically move. Instead, I pulled tighter on my mana signature and made it even smaller. Then, I dropped my second sight.

Who knew if they could read mana in the air? Who knew what any given Rank 3 could do?

Its stealth ranked close to mine, clearly, but something about the buzzing disturbed me. Once I heard it, I couldn’t unhear it.

The red dot crept right at me, but didn’t change its cadence. It got close enough to almost touch me as it hovered right near the inside of the bend.

Air moved ever so slightly as they stayed right where they were.

I pressed myself harder into the outside bend of the tunnel, focusing on staying unseen.

After what seemed like forever, but was probably only a few seconds, the red dot continued down away from me, taking the buzzing with it.

It entered the main cavern and wandered around the area, moving much faster than before. For a second it hesitated near the pool, then moved back in my direction.

I carefully pressed myself harder against the wall again and didn’t dare breathe, but this time the unseen being walked by without a care or sound. I still couldn’t see them, but they didn’t go slow at all.

By the time they headed outside of the cavern, I relaxed just a little.

First things first, I needed to get out of the cavern. Or tuck in and wait out the Bright inside. Hopefully, it would be a good place to wait out the heat.

Even now, the air drifting in from the tunnel cranked up the heat, making me sweat a little.

I let my shoulders drop and took a deep breath.

Strange stepped out of my shadow and nudged my leg.

Soon, we might need to swim, I pushed silently at him through my bond.

Confirmation came from him, along with a rush of determination.

Three dots appeared outside the cavern from the edges of my radar.

Frantically, I pressed myself against the outer bend, yanking on the shadows all around me. Voices drifted in as Strange vanished into the darkness again.

“The sun is too warm,” said a frustrated voice. “This is a suitable location to wait the heat out before we try to trace the trail of the creature.”

“How did it escape us?” asked another.

“Maybe it can swim. There is a water entrance to the cavern.”

The first of the dots stepped into the tunnel. This time my insight worked, since they weren’t even trying to hide.

[Zepyr-Kael, The Tether, Unknown, Potato Chip.]

Potato chip. That meant one thing, Clan Vielether. Seeing the snack food next to the unknown made a fountain of panic bubble up inside me, but I pushed down any idea of movement.

He blinked several times as he entered the dark tunnel. Wide shoulders almost filled the tunnel, as did the glittery wings on his back. Nice, dark armor covered him, but he pulled his shoulders tighter together as he moved through the space. Thankfully he passed without a problem.

“I dislike these tight spaces,” he grumbled, as he entered the main cavern.

[Vyrn-Slyte, The Talon, Unknown, Potato Chip.]

This guy didn’t say anything as he marched behind Zepyr. Two sword handles peeked out from under his armpits. Nothing else stood out, beside the fact that he stayed close to Zepyr.

The third didn’t appear, visibly, but his dot followed the others. The unseen person, again. This time, they didn’t buzz.

Strange followed them into the main cavern without a care, hidden within the shadows.

“The structure of this cavern shows it's not native,” said one of them. “Nothing indicated why.”

“Refuge from the heat?” asked Zepyr in a deeper voice.

How did they miss the dungeon entrance, right there? It softly glowed and highlighted the entire inside of the cavern.

“Maybe,” said a soft voice that moved around the room.

I didn’t dare move from my position. I couldn’t stay here for long. I needed to leave before the Bright started. Not to mention Strange, who wandered about without a care in the world right now. This wasn’t a battle for him.

I pressed on our bond and found myself looking out his eyes.

“I cannot find any trail from the creature we followed,” answered the unseen person.

The two others turned to look at him, and nodded.

Pleasure floated from Strange as he obviously took that as a compliment.

Yet again, I wondered how Noseen thought I could deal with three of them high enough level that I couldn’t see what they were.

“We should set up camp here,” said Zepyr, with a nod of his head. He walked right by the dungeon entrance that even Strange could see, but passed it like it didn’t exist. “We have enough food and water to last several days, until the sun passes by.”

“Hopefully, it's only a couple of days,” grumbled Vyrn with a frown. “I can’t believe Nyx lost it.”

Something flew across the room and Vyrn slammed into a wall.

“Cease!” Zepyr glared at the two combatants. “We aren’t the strongest beings on this planet, by far. Who knows what lurks beneath the water?”

All of them paused at that statement and shivered.

Strange giggled inside his head as he padded away from the pool of water off to one side.

Abby, in Lakeside Landing…

“We will be fine,” I said, trying my best to sound reassuring. “As soon as I find someone to handle things, I will take a turn.”

“How about I take over temporarily, and you go instead?” asked Hellion, with a soft look in his eyes. His hand squeezed mine.

That wouldn’t do.

He wanted to go and explore the new dungeon more than anything.

Something inside him screamed that he needed to level faster.

I shook my head, pushing the impression drifting off of him away from me. “No. I’ll take an additional turn in the dungeon here, and work on my levels that way. I’ll only miss a day or so. Everyone else can handle things.”

Hellion let out a sigh, and glanced away out the open door. “I hope she’s okay.”

“Alex is fine. You and I both know that. She can tackle anything thrown at her.” This time I squeezed his hand before I let go. “Already, folks are picking up their game and working on leveling. Seeing her in person pushed everyone out of the slump folks had gotten into.”

He nodded and stood from where he’d sat at the table. “I better get going, then.”

“You’ll get to meet this Kabi fellow that Lenna spoke about.”

His eyes narrowed. “I plan on having a good conversation with him.”

“They aren’t together,” I said carefully.

“Who said anything about them being together?” he answered, too fast.

“They were only friends,” I added in a reassuring voice. “I think you’ll both have a lot in common.”

“Dad, you coming?” John asked, as he poked his head into the opening. “I want that hovercraft in my workshop, ASAP.”

I chuckled at that. Hellion’s kids were very direct people. None of them beat around the bush, except maybe Benny, and that’s only because he loved plants. Almost as much as he was in love with Cass.

We needed to plan a wedding for those two. Probably for the next time Alex passed through.

But first I had to see these guys off, then head to our dungeon. Hellion wasn’t the only one who wanted to get some levels in. Getting left behind wasn’t fun, and Alex's encouragement to use my class instead of just my profession rested on my shoulders.

Sentinel.

No one else had a class even remotely like it.

Now that things were a little more steady for the survivors, it was time to discover just what I could do.

Mary, Maggie, and maybe even Randy would be a good team to hit the next level in the dungeon with. It was too bad Cass was headed to the other planet with John and Hellion. Hawk had decided to stay behind with me, while Doc took his place.

“Go,” I whispered as I stood.

He leaned in and kissed me on the cheek before he headed out the door.

I couldn’t help myself as I flicked into the town stat screen to check on Alex. Her name rested there, along with her level.

Whatever she was up to, it’d pushed her up… way up.
