Bella didn't wait for Jane to figure out a path. She simply started moving, pushing forward through the crowd with single-minded determination.

"Excuse me! Coming through. Official business."

Jane doubted Bella actually had any authority to claim official business, but she said it with enough confidence that people moved aside anyway. Grabbing onto the back of her friend’s dress, Jane followed in her wake, letting Bella do the hard work of carving a channel through the packed bodies. The crowd grumbled and shifted. Narrow gaps opened, only to close again immediately after the two girls passed. It was slow going, but they did make progress.

They were maybe halfway to the platform when the crowd ahead of them suddenly began parting more easily. Jane looked up and saw a wedge of uniformed servants pushing toward them from the other direction, creating a channel through the masses with the efficiency of people who did this sort of thing regularly. They must have spotted her coming, or Xand had sent them. Either way, the path ahead opened up like magic.

"Well, that's convenient," Bella said.

Moments later, Jane found herself at the base of the platform, looking up at the raised stage where the royal family waited. She grasped a servant’s offered hand and allowed herself be helped up onto the wooden surface. The royal family occupied one end of the platform, surrounded by their attendants. The nearby handful of town officials all appeared to be battling various degrees of anxiety. Xand, in particular, was visibly perspiring. He caught Jane’s eye and nodded his thanks, plainly relieved.

Then the king stepped forward to meet her.

Up close, he was both more and less imposing than she had expected. More, because there was an undeniable presence to him, a sort of majestic aura that couldn’t be ignored. Less, because he was also clearly just a man, middle-aged and tired and frustrated about the broken magical device he was currently holding.

When he spoke, though, none of that frustration seeped through. His voice was nothing but warm and friendly.

"Miss Jane. It’s lovely to meet you. I've heard good things."

"I’ve heard good things about you too," Jane replied. "And I bet for longer."

Jane!

She felt herself blush as she realized how odd she must have sounded. All her nerves were on edge, true, but she’d been in tense situations before. She hadn’t thought she would react to this one by being overly casual.

Don’t be weird in front of the king!

She heard Bella make a small choking sound behind her, but the king just laughed. It was a genuine laugh, surprised and pleased, and it made him look younger than he had a moment before.

"Fair enough. I suppose you have." He held up the golden device, turning it so she could see. "This isn't working. It was in functional condition before we left, but it seems the journey disagreed with it. Can you possibly fix it? Otherwise, I'm going to look rather silly." He glanced out at the sea of faces watching them. "Like I just invited everyone here to admire my shoes.”

Jane took the device from his hands and examined it closely. The craftsmanship was exquisite, probably the work of the academy's magical artificers. The enchantment woven into it was complex, the product of throwing expense to the wind and allowing a whole team of craftsmen unlimited budget and time. She guessed it performed its function uncommonly well… when it was working.

That complexity also made it impossible to fix quickly. She would need to trace the entire spell structure, find what had gone wrong, and carefully repair it. It was a task requiring hours of work, in a situation where minutes counted.

"No," she said, handing the device back to the king. "I can't fix this without more time. But I can do something for you."

Reaching into her bag, she pulled out a piece of chalk. She nearly always carried some with her these days. The king watched with curiosity as she knelt and began to scratch lines onto the wooden platform.

"What is she doing?" The king's voice was directed over Jane's head, towards the spot where Bella had climbed up onto the platform behind her.

Bella's answer was immediate and utterly unhelpful.

"Jane stuff." A beat of silence followed, and then Bella added, "Your Majesty."

Jane could hear the king's amusement even without looking up.

"And who might you be?"

"Bella, Your Majesty. I'm a local breakfast cook."

"A breakfast cook?" Now the king sounded genuinely interested. "That's good work. Important work. There can never be enough breakfast, in my experience."

"That's what I always say, too!"

Jane's chalk faltered for a moment as she processed what was happening. The king of the realm and her best friend were apparently becoming fast friends at an alarming rate.

She pushed the thought aside and focused on the circle taking shape beneath her hands. Thankfully, it wasn’t complicated. This was a simple amplification pattern, the kind of thing she had learned in her first year of magical study. She had done fast-and-dirty versions of the same spell in various emergencies over the years. Here, it was just a matter of taking her time and not ruining things with avoidable mistakes.

She finished the last line and stood, brushing chalk dust from her knees. The circle was about three feet across. She stepped into its center and gestured for the king to join her.

"I'll need to touch you for the spell to work," she said. "Is that acceptable?"

"Of course."

The king stepped into the circle without hesitation and offered her his arm, as if something like this was completely normal for him. For all Jane knew, it was. Royal life probably involved all sorts of strange magical procedures that she’d never had to think about.

She laid her hand on his forearm, feeling the fine fabric of his sleeve beneath her fingers. Then she reached down into herself, found her magical power waiting for her command, and pushed it into the circle.

Jane felt the spell take hold, felt it wrap around the king like an invisible cloak, felt it anchor itself to her through their point of contact. It was a simple enough spell, really. The magic caught the vibrations of his voice and convinced the surrounding world to match them. The hard part was maintaining it, keeping the flow of power steady so the effect didn't waver or cut out at an inopportune moment. Even that was simple enough for someone like Jane, though.

She nodded at the king. "Try it now."

He cleared his throat, a small preparatory sound that nonetheless rolled out across the crowd like distant thunder. A ripple of reaction passed through the assembled people. Heads turned as conversations cut off mid-word.

The king smiled.

"People of Glenfall."

His voice filled the air, not painfully loud, but present. It was impossible to ignore. The words seemed to come from everywhere at once, from the sky and the water and the buildings surrounding the square. Jane could feel the spell working, could feel each syllable vibrating through the circle and out into the world.

"Thank you for this welcome. I am deeply grateful for the hospitality your town has shown and will show my family, both last night and in the days to come."

He paused, letting the words settle over the crowd. Jane kept her hand on his arm, kept the power flowing, and tried not to think about the fact that she was currently providing magical support for the king of the realm in front of the assembled population of Glenfall.

"I know our arrival was unexpected," the king continued. "Your innkeeper was gracious enough to provide us rooms on short notice, and I must say, I slept better last night than I have in weeks. There is something about mountain air that agrees with me, and the beds are as fine as any in my palace."

A murmur of pleased surprise ran through the crowd. Jane suspected the innkeeper would be telling that story for the rest of his life.

"We will be here for several days," the king went on. "I understand there is a showcase planned, an exhibition of your town's finest crafts and goods. I look forward to seeing what Glenfall has to offer. I have heard remarkable things about this place, about its people and their ingenuity, and I am eager to witness it for myself."

He glanced sideways at Jane, a quick flicker of acknowledgment that the crowd probably couldn't see.

"In the meantime, I ask only this. Treat us as you would treat anyone. We are guests in your home. If you see me in the street, feel free to say hello. Treat me as a neighbor. For the time being, that’s exactly what I am."

A burst of genuine delight surged through the crowd. The king had a gift for this, Jane realized. He knew how to speak to people, how to make them feel at ease. It was a skill she had never really considered before, but watching him use it, she understood how powerful it could be.

"Thank you again for your welcome. I look forward to getting to know your town better in the days ahead."

He fell silent, then nodded once at Jane. She pulled her power back into herself and let the spell fade. The circle's glow dimmed and died until it was only a series of chalk marks on the wooden platform.

Suddenly, she realized her hand was still on the king's arm. She let it drop, struck again by the awareness of how strange the whole situation was.

The crowd erupted into applause, the sound washing over the platform like a wave. The king acknowledged it with a raised hand and a warm smile. Then he turned to Jane.

"Thank you.” His voice was normal now, meant only for her ears. "That was impressive work on short notice."

"It was nothing, Your Majesty. Just a simple thing."

"Simple, she says." The king's eyes crinkled with amusement. "I have court mages who couldn't have done that in twice the time. You have a gift, Miss Jane."

"I have practice," Jane said. "And a lot of chalk."

The king laughed again, that same surprised, delighted sound from before. "I can see why people speak so highly of you. We will have to talk more before I leave. For now, though, I should probably go be kingly somewhere. It’s expected, you know."

Jane nodded. She did know. If nothing else, she would have known it from her aunt’s complaints about the demands put on an archmage. Cecelia called it ‘making appearances’, and detested every last moment she had to spend in pursuit of it.

Jane was inclined to agree with her.

The king moved towards the edge of the platform, where his attendants were already forming up to escort him into the crowd. The queen followed. Behind her came the prince, still looking uncomfortable, but at least no longer quite so nervous.

They hadn’t been introduced. There had hardly been time. Jane supposed they would meet at some point, if it was needed. If not, that was more than fine. She was perfectly willing to avoid the whole potentially awkward situation.

Jane watched them go, then turned to find Bella at her elbow, grinning like a cat who had found an unattended bowl of cream.

"You just did magic for the king," Bella said. "On a platform in front of half the city. While I chatted with him about breakfast."

"I noticed."

"He agreed with me about breakfast, Jane. He had to. It was all I could think of to talk about.”

"I heard. I think he really did mean it, though. And that means you’ll be feeding him before all this is done, you know."

Bella's grin widened. "This is the best day of my life."
