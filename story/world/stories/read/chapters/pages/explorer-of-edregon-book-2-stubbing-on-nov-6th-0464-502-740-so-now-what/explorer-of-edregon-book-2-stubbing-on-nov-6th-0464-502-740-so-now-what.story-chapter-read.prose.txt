As much as Vin wished they could just throw themselves at the problem and keep trying until they figured it out, Ovord was limited to a grand total of three minutes in control of his body in a single day. And as far as Vin could tell, the soul mage didn’t so much as even attempt to touch Alka’s soul during those first few attempts, choosing instead to spend the full three minutes simply staring at whatever magical wonders Beyond the Veil was allowing him to see. Their experiment was over before it even began.

“So… that’s it?” Alka asked. Swinging her feet off the stone table, her glowing eyes narrowed in annoyance. “It didn’t feel like he did anything.”

“He didn’t do anything,” Vin confirmed. “Which I’m honestly grateful for. This isn’t something we should take lightly. Ovord wants some time to get used to using Beyond the Veil before he tries actually messing with your soul. He’s already hidden himself back into my mana pool, saying he wants some time to ‘ruminate on these discoveries.’ Whatever that means.”

“Well damn, I was sort of hoping this would be a one and done,” Alka admitted. “So, how far did you guys get without me? You’ve got what, a week before the festival?”

“Three fragments left. Shia is helping someone new settle into Terra, Scule and Reginald are working with Bill on some new reagents they found, and Lumel is still waiting for the Airwalker with the meriders. Any chance you want to keep me company? I’d also like to make sure Ovord gets his three minutes each day to keep working on the problem of getting you back into the System.”

“Sure, sounds fun to me,” Alka shrugged. “After our recent trip, I’m giving the Slayers a little time to rest up and recover. I may have worked them a little too hard.”

“...What did you do?” Vin asked, already dreading the answer. If Alka of all people was admitting something like that, she had to have put them through literal hell and back.

“Nothing too crazy! I just brought them over to the zero gravity fragment and had them practice getting hurled into those dark water bubbles with monsters inside. Good practice fighting their way back out.”

“Alka…” Vin sighed, deciding it wasn’t worth trying to change her ways at this point. “Yeah, I think giving your Slayers a little time off isn’t a bad idea. In which case, let’s head out. The festival is right around the corner, and I need to get back with enough time to physically make this giant banner Spur wants me to put on display.”

With that, Vin grabbed a quick bite to go before taking Alka and heading down to the Underside. As much as he wanted to stop by the meriders and check in on Lumel in person, he’d briefly visited her in her dreams the night prior and made sure she was alright. She was definitely nervous about the upcoming Airwalker conversation, but still overall fine.

While they walked, Vin filled Alka in on everything she’d missed with the last few fragments. She was a bit interested in the concept of the Beacon as a whole, but largely dismissive of what they were trying to do.

“What kind of life would that even be?” she snorted. “They might be safe from monsters, but then they’re trapped for the rest of their lives. And for a race capable of flight? That had to be horrible.”

“I guess enough of them thought it was a better alternative than death,” he shrugged.

Once they were through the subterranean dungeon and back in Tabetha’s fragment, and Alka had gotten her fill of fighting against the strange, burrowing monsters, Vin aimed them toward the next fragment on his list and took off. The fact that two of the last three fragments had only held a single person in them was a bit depressing, and he could only hope he came upon an actual settlement with this next one.

Granted, the turtouns are able to hide from Sense Soul while within their shells. For all we know there were a handful more of them busy napping while we were there.

With just the two of them, it didn’t take long for them to reach the edge of the next fragment, and Vin let out a sigh of relief at the standard, ordinary landscape laid out before them.

“I’ve never been so happy to see boring old trees and grass,” Vin admitted. Walking through the barrier, he smiled when he was hit with the sound of birds chirping.

Third ring fragment discovered! 1,500 exp gained.

As far as he could tell, this latest fragment was similar to the one the Gods had dropped them all off in. The temperature was pleasant, the animals plentiful, and the normal-sized sun shone down on them with its early-afternoon rays.

“I’m waiting for the catch,” Alka said, her sword already in hand as her head swiveled around to take it all in. “There’s always a catch.”

“There is not always a catch,” Vin argued. “Sometimes we get nice, relaxing fragments like this one! Not often, but sometimes! Like the pixies’ fragment. No catch there!”

“Other than the dream mages that chucked you into a coma for two weeks,” Alka snorted.

“First off, I volunteered for that. And second… is that a log cabin?”

With his high focus attribute, Vin was able to see a good bit farther than Alka could. Hidden through some of the trees was in fact what looked like an adorable little log cabin, as if pulled straight out of a fairytale. It appeared to be brand new, which made sense, as most structures tended to be closer to the center of the fragments. Whoever had built it had to have done so after being pulled over to Edregon.

“Looks like a hunting cabin,” Alka said after they’d walked a bit closer. “I assume we’re going in friendly?”

“We’re certainly not going to say hello by throwing a Fireball at them,” Vin scoffed, shaking his head. “Put your sword away! You look scary enough without it!”

Alka grunted but did as he asked, sheathing her sword while they approached the cabin. Vin had already confirmed there was a lone person inside with Sense Soul, and he prepared himself for a good first impression. Yet before he could knock on the door, a blur launched itself out of the upstairs window, landing nimbly behind them and revealing themselves to be an elf with a well-maintained longbow. He wore leather armor and had a few knives strapped to his belt, along with a small handful of potions. An arrow was nocked and pulled back, aimed directly for the two of them.

“Stop right there if you know what’s…”

The elf paused, his eyes going wide as he took in the two of them. Alka already had her sword back in hand and was pointing the blade at him, and the air in front of Vin shimmered from the effect of Wind Wall, prepared to stop the elf’s arrow in its tracks. Neither of them looked concerned or even surprised by the unexpected attack, and the elf frowned while he slowly lowered his bow.

“You two certainly look experienced. Sorry for the rude welcome.”

“Eh, we’ve had ruder,” Alka shrugged, the tip of her sword still pointed at the elf’s chest not wavering in the slightest. “Honestly not even in the top five.”

“Is there a reason you greet people like this?” Vin asked. Letting Wind Wall dissipate, he gestured at the serene landscape around them. “Your fragment seems peaceful enough.”

“I’m one of the people appointed to monitor the fragment boundaries,” the elf explained, spinning his arrow and returning it to his quiver. “I’m Hark. Watcher.”

“Oh, we have a Watcher in our town as well,” Vin said, curious if this man was just as paranoid as John was. “I’m Vin, an Explorer. This is Alka, a Slayer.”

“Slayer? What the demons is a Slayer?” the elf asked, narrowing his eyes at her. “I don’t like that I can’t see your face. Only those with things to hide hide their faces.”

“It means I’m really good at killing things,” Alka said, her voice teasing as her glowing eyes flared.

“Monsters,” Vin said, rolling his eyes with a sigh. “Slayers are a specialized class for killing monsters. Though yes, she is good at killing more than that.”

“What’s an Explorer and a Slayer doing wandering into our fragment?” Hark asked. The elf still looked like he was debating whether or not he should go back to attacking the two of them, despite being clearly outmatched.

“...Exploring?” Vin answered, wondering what kind of the answer the man had expected. “I’m working on creating a map of Edregon. That sort of involves heading into as many fragments as I can.”

“A likely story,” Hark said, not looking the slightest bit convinced.

I suppose that answers the question about Watchers and needless paranoia, Vin thought. John and him would get along great.

“Look, we’re just curious about your fragment,” Alka said, cutting to the heart of the matter. “We’re more than happy to leave you out here in your cabin drinking tree water or whatever it is you do while we search for your town. Actually, what do you even do out here? The fragment you're lined up against doesn’t have anyone in it. Are we the first people you’ve even met from beyond your own world?”

“I don’t have to answer that,” Hark said, narrowing his eyes toward Alka. “Though you two seem like a decent-enough sort. Shifty, but decent. I suppose I can point you in the direction of town. You’re not setting off my class at the very least.”

“We’d certainly appreciate it,” Vin said, trying to give the elf his best smile.

Hark grumbled a bit as he walked between them into his cabin once more, before coming out with a rather poorly drawn map.

“Just follow this path. Should be able to make it to town by mid-afternoon if you can run as fast as you can draw a sword.”

“Got it,” Vin nodded, happily taking the offered map. “Thanks again!”

Vin waved goodbye as Hark slammed the door in their face, before he shook his head and put the cabin behind them.

“Well, that was interesting,” Vin said. “Though I’m not surprised we finally came upon a settlement that decided to post some lookouts along the fragment border. Honestly, I don’t know why more don’t do the same.”

“It’s not like Terra does,” Alka pointed out. “Either you scatter a small number out there and have to deal with them going wacko like that guy, or you devote a significant amount of manpower. Far easier to focus on defenses of the town itself.”

“Fair enough. Still, this gives me some hope that this settlement will be interesting,” Vin admitted, grinning as he peered down at the crude map. “I’m hopeful they only set up those watchpoints because they have lots of people coming through here from other fragments.”

“Guess we’ll find out soon enough,” Alka said. Putting her head down, she began running.

The two of them ran side-by-side for a good bit, following a small dirt path that they discovered soon enough. While there were a number of simple woodland critters running about here and there, Vin was surprised at the complete lack of monsters. It wasn’t often they came upon fragments with so few monsters, and usually that meant the people living within were doing an excellent job of clearing them out, such as the Necromancers and their squads of undead warriors who worked around the clock. Alka clearly noticed as well, because she grunted as they ran.

“At least these people are better at fighting monsters than those divigel were.”

“Try and be friendly if you end up running into Tabetha,” Vin sighed, hoping the poor woman was getting the help she needed. Shia had mentioned bringing her to Terra’s therapist, which Vin thought was a fantastic idea. He’d been putting that meeting himself off for far too long now, but it seemed there was always something more important to take care of.

He and Alka passed a few duos and trios here and there as they ran, mostly consisting of humans and elves. Though there were a few other races that stood out quite dramatically.

“Is that a petian?!” Vin gasped, staring in shock at the five-inch-tall woman standing atop an elven woman’s shoulder while the two of them walked down the path.

“Scule’s not gonna believe this,” Alka laughed, picking up the pace as the town grew nearer. It wasn’t long before they were able to see the town in full, and Vin smiled at the sight of a bustling, hectic town filled to the brim with people. He felt like he was walking back into Terra, other than the fact that the town was clearly old and the proportion of humans to other races was quite different. Despite the fact that it looked a bit larger than Terra was, their walls were far smaller, which made sense if they weren’t constantly going up against sweeping waves of monsters. The guards didn’t even stop them, merely giving them a quick glance before turning away, presumably using some sort of skill to judge that they weren’t going to cause any trouble.

As the two of them entered town, with both of them getting a fair share of curious looks from random passerbies, Alka turned toward him.

“So… we made it into town. Now what?”

“I don’t actually know,” Vin admitted, looking around at all the people walking around them. “Usually something happens which leads to us meeting with the leader, or at the very least there’s someone in need of help who shows us around. This is the first time we’ve just sort of… walked in.”

“That does tend to happen to us, doesn’t it,” Alka mused.

“Maybe we just look around a bit for now? Get a sense of the town and the people inside it before doing anything too crazy?”

“Works for me,” Alka shrugged. “What about that over there? Looks like some sort of bulletin board by the gate with information and requests for aid. If you don’t want to go knocking on the mayor’s door, maybe we can use that to entertain ourselves for a bit.”

“It’s as good an idea as any,” Vin decided, leading them over to it. While the board had plenty of requests for things like herbs or reagents from monsters, there was one flier that caught his eye immediately. Alka must have noticed it at the same time, because she sighed.

“Seriously? Isn’t that a bit pathetic at this point?”

“That remains to be seen,” Vin laughed, happily removing the strip of paper hanging from the flier that had what appeared to be an address listed on it.

“I can’t help but notice how it says introductory lessons for novice mages,” Alka pointed out. “Last I checked, you were a little above that. You know, slightly.”

“It also says the first lesson is free,” Vin said, tapping the flier. “It’s not like we have any of these people’s money, so this is as good a plan as any to get a feel for the town. And who knows, I might learn some new magic!”

“You’re ridiculous,” Alka muttered, grabbing a very different flier and ripping it off the board. “I’m going to go kill this hidden monster that’s been giving people a hard time. Should at least kill a few hours while you go con some magic teachers.”

“Meet back up here at the entrance at sundown?” Vin asked. “If it’s too late to find the dungeon by then, maybe we can use some of your payment to find a room to stay in.”

“Works for me. Try not to embarrass yourself at your introductory magic lessons.”

“No promises,” Vin laughed, already walking toward one of the guards to ask for help with directions.
