Kevin nodded dumbly and said, "Yes, I can. What are you?"

"Oh dear, this has never happened before." The eyes shook, and despite the oddness of the situation, Kevin gathered that whatever owned the eyes was embarrassed. "Nobody but spirits or... Nobody but fairies or natural spirits have ever seen me before. I don't know what to say. I don't know what to do. How embarrassing! I want to run away, but I think I'm too scared. Don't look at me!"

Kevin frowned amd said, "Don't be afraid. In fact, I should be more afraid of you than you are of me. I'm just a goblin."

"The hell you're just a goblin!" said the eyes. "I saw you talking to one of the fairies earlier. That's why I've been watching you. It's fascinating." The voice drawled the last word in a strange way.

"Okay," said Kevin slowly. "If I look down, please don't run away because I think I just used magic to see you, and I'd like to understand what just happened."

The eyes continued shaking, staring back at Kevin, and the voice didn't speak again. Kevin said, "Do you promise?"

"Okay, fine."

Kevin looked down at the ground and said, "Is that better? Now we're just speaking. I'm not looking at you."

"Yes, that actually is better. Thank you very much. I was scared earlier, and I still am, but you seem to be a nice goblin. I’ve never actually talked to a famous person before, or someone who knows a famous person. Or a goblin."

"I thought you just said that I wasn't a goblin."

"You need to stop pointing out the inconsistencies in what I say, or else I'm going to run away. Actually or else maybe I'm going to be more inconsistent."

"Fair enough," muttered Kevin, quickly realizing that whatever he was speaking to was a weirdo. "So what are you?"

"I am a natural spirit. But isn't it more polite to ask somebody's name?"

"Okay, what's your name?" asked Kevin.

"I don't have a name."

Kevin blinked and frowned at the ground. The voice said, "You just frowned, didn't you?"

"No, I was just looking at my feet," lied Kevin.

"Oh. That’s understandable. If I had feet, I would look at them too," said the voice. “Same with a, well you know.” The voice giggled.

Kevin ignored the implication and asked, "Is it okay if I give you a name?"

"Oh, I wouldn't presume to get a name, especially from someone who talks to fairies."

"Okay, what if I give you a nickname then? Like, ‘Tree Voice.’"

"Oh no, now you've gone and done it." The voice shook. "I have a nickname now. Oh my goodness, all my friends are going to think that I'm so arrogant and uppity and trying to get up in the world! And then everybody will stop talking to me, and I won't have friends anymore!"

Kevin paused, not sure how to respond at first.  Then he said, "If it's really that much of a problem, maybe I can talk to your friends and explain the situation so they won't be upset."

"Oh, it's okay," said Tree Voice. "I don't have any friends."

"I see," said Kevin slowly. "So then, what's done is done, Tree Voice, and you have a nickname, but we know that you're not arrogant, and I did it on a whim, so it's not your fault. So would you mind telling me what you are, please?"

The voice harumphed and, in its strange, mostly monotone timbre, said, "I am what's known as a natural spirit. Natural spirits like me generally form out of some sort of high concentration of natural energy. In this case, it's the forest. Most large forests have several natural spirits, and we all live in different areas. Natural spirits have two different paths that we can take if we're lucky and don't fade away over time. One is becoming a natural god, a natural geos, which is sort of like the…overall will of an area or an element in an area. If that happens, we get to live forever, but we also start thinking really slow and can't really talk to anybody anymore."

Kevin resisted the urge to point out that Tree Voice had already said he doesn't have any friends. Instead he asked, "What's the other path that natural spirits can take?"

"Well, it's rare, but some natural spirits can also become fairies. But most fairies, even if they start as natural spirits, don't stay a natural spirit for long. Us spirits tend to believe that fairies are born, not made. It’s all very glamorous."

"Oh, so Branch was a natural spirit maybe before?"

"I would not presume to know," said Tree Voice, "and I would never call the august manifestation by his name. Oh no, that's too far above my station! It would also be far above my station to presume and pass along what I've heard about the fairy Branch before, about how he's considered such a rogue and eccentric individual among fairy kind. Definitely not, because this Tree Voice does not gossip. No gossiping. No way."

"I see," said Kevin slowly. "So…I'm very impressed that you do not gossip. But if you did, what other kinds of things would you perhaps say about the fairy Branch?"

"Well, if I were the type of natural spirit to gossip, and I'm not, of course, I could talk about how Branch, the fairy Branch, is considered exceptional among most blossom fairies, but frustrates other fairies with how strange he is. Apparently, he has turned down several genuine requests to settle down in a region and even start his own fairy court. He's quite the celebrity among natural spirits and fairies. At least, that's what somebody might gossip. And of course, I don't gossip about other natural spirits, and definitely not fairies."

"I definitely understand," said Kevin. He carefully schooled his expression as he spoke to the insane natural spirit. "Is it okay if I look up now?"

"Oh, I'm not sure. It might be too overwhelming. How scandalous!"

Kevin straightened and looked up at the eyes again. They shook, but not as strongly this time.

"Oh, that was very cheeky, Mr. Goblin. Very cheeky indeed. You know, I do not like being surprised like that. No, I don't! When storms come that I don't sense, and then lightning flashes and destroys trees, trees that I'm friends with, well, I tell that lightning, 'You go away,' because it's not fun to have lightning destroy my friends in the first place, but I do not like surprises, too."

Once again, Kevin resisted the urge to point out that Tree Voice had said that he didn't have friends. Kevin felt like since he'd left his parents' cave, maybe he'd been getting better at dealing with strange personalities. This world seemed to be full of them.

"So, I have a question, if you don't mind answering, Tree Voice."

"Oh, goodness, he called me by my special name again. Is this something unique? Is this something magical? Is it…fated? A strange and dashing goblin, friend to the notorious but very sexy Branch, giving me a name and even looking at me eye to eye. Oh, it's so salacious!"

The tree spun in a complete circle around the trunk before meeting Kevin's eyes again, and two pieces of bark actually moved as if they were eyebrows jumping up and down suggestively.

Definitely insane, thought Kevin. He said, "How many natural spirits are there?"

"Oh, I don't know," said Tree Voice. "After all, I never leave my forest, but I can hear their voices on the wind or in the water sometimes. I can't give you a number, but we're out there. What I can tell you is that there are far fewer fairies. I think some mortals think there are many fairies just wandering around, playing in groups, just doing sexy fairy things all day. But there truly aren't that many of them."

"Why not?" asked Kevin.

"Probably because they have too big of personalities and can't tolerate each other for very long, at least not in large groups. Fairies love attention, but again, that would be fairy gossip."

"And you're not a gossiper," finished Kevin. "I understand, Tree Voice. I'm very impressed with your moral character."

"Why, thank you, thoughtful goblin!"

"You can call me Kevin."

"Oh goodness, I wouldn't presume," said the voice. "A companion of the illustrious and very rambunctious Branch being called by name by yours truly? What would all the other natural spirits say? Oh my goodness, I'd be the talk of the town!"

The natural spirit giggled, and the eyes spun around the tree again. It took every fiber of Kevin's being not to roll his eyes.

"Well, I was just trying a new spell, and then I suddenly saw you." Kevin suddenly realized that Tree Spirit had been effectively spying on him for who knew how long, but he chose not to say anything about it. The insane natural spirit might flip, and Kevin still wasn't sure if he could be dangerous or not. "Can anybody ever actually see you?" asked Kevin.

"Not that I know of," said Tree Voice. "We can't show ourselves to mortals the same way that the fairy folk can. But the fairy folk can see and speak to us."

"I see," said Kevin. "So my spell allows me to see you."

"Oh, yes, but only in our natural state. If I wanted to hide…actually, let me try."

Suddenly, the eyes vanished. They reappeared about ten seconds later.

"Did you see me?"

Kevin shook his head. "Nope, you vanished. I couldn't see your eyes anymore."

"Oh, so that does mean I can hide."

"Can you see me while you're hiding though?" asked Kevin.

"Nope," Tree Voice's eyes shook back and forth as if it was shaking a head. "To hide, I need to stop thinking, and when I stop think, it's actually quite pleasant, but it can be very boring too."

"Alright," said Kevin. "So the spell that I used changed my perspective or my perception through my eyes so that I could perceive you."

"I believe that's probably what happened," agreed Tree Spirit.

Kevin nodded slowly and realized that he might have just stumbled upon a very interesting way to use his magic. Since he could alter his vision this way, it was probable that he could change it in other ways too.
