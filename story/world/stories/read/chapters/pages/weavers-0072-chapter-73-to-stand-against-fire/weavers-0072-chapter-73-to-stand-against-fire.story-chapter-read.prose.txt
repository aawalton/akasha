Chapter 73 – To Stand Against Fire.

The forest, once cloaked in mist and ancient hush, now burned with an unnatural silence. Smoke hung in the air like a veil, and the scent of scorched bark mixed with the acrid tang of blood. The Baby Phoenix stood alone in the clearing, a sunspot of molten gold and flame. Around it, the ground hissed and crackled. All life had been cauterized away.

Behind a wide-bellied oak, half-charred and steaming, Ezra crouched low, his chest heaving. His knuckles were pale as he gripped the tree’s scorched bark, eyes flicking toward the clearing. His cloak was already singed at the edges, the threads curling like withered leaves.

Marcel knelt beside him, one hand gripping the bow slung across his back, the other clutching his ribs. His face was ashen, lips pressed thin. “We’re not walking out of this one the usual way,” he said grimly, voice low but steady.

Ezra nodded, exhaling slow. Then a pause, and a small, crooked smile ghosted across his face.

“You wanna try the Meld?” he asked. “It’d at least be a proper fight this time.”

Marcel looked at him sharply. “You sure? If we fail, we’re as good as dead.”

“We need to, if we don’t the whole academy could be in danger, no one else knows this is out here!”

Marcel hesitated for a moment longer, then gave a grim nod. “Alright. Let’s do it.”

They placed their hands together, both drawing in a sharp breath.

Ezra closed his eyes.

Time slowed. The forest grew still, a heartbeat caught in stasis. Ezra’s threads reached out, his weak connection with the Mind being met by the strong, overwhelming power Marcel held. They grasped, their threads intertwining like roots.

The Meld was not painless.

But with it came unity.

A perfect synchronicity.

Marcel’s body slumped gently beside the tree. Ezra, still in control, quickly bent down and dragged him under a nest of ferns and moss, using a broken shield to cover his frame. Leaves and earth did the rest. It was crude, but quick.

Then he stood.

One mind. One body. One purpose.

And he ran.

The clearing had changed. Where once trees crowded together in defiance of the sky, now only a charred basin remained. Bushes were reduced to smoking cinders. Ash rained in soft flakes, gathering in cracks and corners like snow. The Baby Phoenix had reshaped its environment with a single pulse of power.

Ezra stepped into that emptiness, the heat baking his skin. Time ticked louder in his head now, like a pendulum swinging too fast. The Phoenix had sensed him before he arrived.

It turned.

Their eyes met.

And it moved first.

A fire-laced blur, the creature surged forward on taloned feet, wings fanning wide with explosive heat. Ezra dove to the side, rolling behind a blackened stump just as the Phoenix’s flames scorched the earth where he’d stood.

He came up on one knee and slashed the air with his left hand.

The Phoenix’s next motion became faster, it turned its head and shifted its wings. Its fire dimmed, colour bleeding out of the world.

Ezra moved. Sprinting forward, he launched himself toward the creature’s side, blade glinting in the eerie quiet of slowed time which he had given himself.

He reached the base of its wing and leapt.

But the Phoenix reacted on instinct.

Even in frozen seconds, the air shimmered violently. Flame burst outward from its core, a radiant detonation.

Ezra was flung backwards mid-air.

The blast hit him like a wall of hot iron, knocking the wind from his lungs and sending him skidding across the scorched earth. His coat caught flame at the hem. He rolled, slapping it out, teeth gritted against the pain.

“You gotta be more careful there, it’s not just your life on the line, remember?” Marcel said with an annoyed tone.

He groaned, half-dazed, as his mind righted itself.

“Shit, I get it, but how do you suggest we fight this thing. No point in the meld if you’re just sitting back.” Ezra retorted.

“I’m not too sure. I think our best bet would be to try drive it off, there’s no way we’re gonna beat it. We just have to make it think it’s gonna lose.”

He nodded. Marcel’s steadier thoughts pulsed at the edges of his mind. A reminder that he wouldn’t be able to kill it no matter how hard he tried was a necessary warning.

Ezra stood again. “Okay,” he hissed. “Round two.”

He darted right, zigzagging to avoid the beast’s gaze. The Phoenix snarled, a high-pitched, crystalline sound, and launched another gout of fire in a sweeping arc. Ezra skidded under it, rolling and slashing at the creature’s legs. His blade caught on something, a feather? No, a scale-hard patch of heat-warped armour.

“What the fuck? Looks like someone’s had a hand in outfitting it. It’s probably got more armour than us.”

“Just focus on the fight Ezra.”

The Phoenix shrieked.

It spun, wings slamming the air.

Ezra went flying again.

This time, he landed harder, coughing as the impact bruised his ribs. Blood dripped from one ear. His vision swam.

But his grip held.

He blinked, and Time flickered again.

The world slowed. The Phoenix moved slower, just enough. Ezra hurled a spare dagger with all the strength he could summon, not his expensive one, just a stand in until he could afford the second. It embedded in the Phoenix’s shoulder, sending blood spewing from the wound. Not enough to kill, but it was something, and the Phoenix was distracted.

Ezra lunged again.

He used the distraction to leap high, foot planting on a boulder, then another, until he launched himself at the Phoenix’s back.

His blade rose.

He slashed, with his main dagger this time.

And he almost connected, when a burst of flame met him mid-swing.

Another explosive shockwave of pressure. A defence mechanism. He felt it peel the skin from his arms, burn through his sleeves. He screamed, voice lost in the thunder.

Then he hit the ground, rolling hard, twitching in pain.

Smoke filled his lungs.

He couldn’t breathe. Couldn’t move.

His vision dimmed at the edges. Marcel’s voice, his thoughts, kept him conscious.

We can’t win like this. We need a proper plan.

Ezra’s thoughts circled.

The Phoenix was young. Strong, yes, but predictable. That flame-burst, whatever it was, came after he touched its back. After physical contact.

And it wasn’t constant, it only lasted a few seconds at a time.

Ezra forced himself upright.

The Phoenix shrieked again, wings flaring to seem larger.

Ezra moved. He was already climbing the nearest tree, half-burned, blackened, but still tall. He leapt from limb to limb until he was above it.

Now.

He drew the main dagger from his belt again, and he dropped.

Time slowed again, only slightly. Enough for his aim.

He hurled the dagger at the Phoenix’s open wing joint, where fire burned brightest.

It struck home.

The Phoenix roared.

A wild burst of fire enveloped the area, but this time, Ezra had jumped away before it exploded.

He rolled across the scorched earth, coughing, eyes wide.

Smoke drifted upward in chaotic spirals.

The Phoenix flailed, injured now, feathers scorched unevenly, eyes molten with fury.

Ezra stood panting, chest heaving. His body screamed with pain, but he was still alive.

The Phoenix, wounded, turned its golden gaze toward him again.

And took a step forward.

“Still not enough,” Ezra whispered.

“But we’re close.” Marcel replied.

Ezra stood in the heart of the devastation, his body trembling from exertion. His chest rose and fell in uneven bursts, lungs aching with every breath of superheated air. The edges of his cloak had been seared to curls, and his gloves were blackened at the fingertips. Sweat and soot streaked his face.

Around him, the air shimmered with residual heat, distorting the ruined forest into a wavering mirage.

Ezra took a shaky step back. He could feel the weight of its presence pressing against his ribs, could feel his magic flickering beneath his skin like a failing current.

Ezra drew in a slow, ragged breath. “Alright. I say we make it think we’re running. Then we flank it. Go all out.”

Marcel agreed, but there was no need to speak to him, their opinions transmitted immediately, although it needed longer to give detailed explanations.

He retreated slowly toward the edge of the clearing, careful not to draw the Phoenix’s gaze. As soon as he was out of direct line of sight, he broke into a crouched run, weaving between still-smoking logs until he reached the shelter of a half-burnt oak, the same tree where Marcel’s real body was hidden, carefully camouflaged beneath a blanket of damp ferns and ash-darkened moss.

Ezra dropped to one knee beside him, pressing his hand to Marcel’s wrist. The pulse was steady, if faint. The meld had drained them both, but Marcel had taken the brunt. Ezra felt the faint thrum of their connection still lingering, like a thread caught in a breeze.

With a murmured command and a flick of his wrist, two ghostly figures sprang to life. One illusion showed him picking up Marcel from behind the tree, dragging him away through the trees as if in retreat.

The second, a clever mirage of himself, crept away in the opposite direction. Both moved convincingly, flickering just enough in the firelight to blend with the scorched world around them.

The real Ezra bent low and circled wide, ducking under branches and leaping over ash-crusted rocks. His heart pounded in his ears, loud as a war drum. Every movement sent fresh aches through his limbs, but he pushed forward, step by step, weaving a wide arc that brought him behind the Phoenix.

It worked... at first.

The Phoenix turned toward the illusion of the fleeing pair. Its wings flared, scattering cinders across the clearing. It took one step forward, then two.

Ezra kept moving.

Closer.

Then the Phoenix froze.

Ezra stilled.

It turned its head directly toward him.

“Shit,” Ezra whispered.

The creature had seen through the illusion. Or smelled him. Or sensed his magic. Doesn't matter what, its reaction was immediate.

Its feathers bristled. Its chest glowed from within like the forge-heart of a volcano. The air warped, heat rushing inwards, condensing around the Phoenix like it was a dying star pulling the world toward it.

Ezra raised his arm instinctively, warding himself with a weak shield of flesh, although he knew it wouldn’t be enough.

The Phoenix opened its wings and screamed.

A tidal wave of flame burst from its body in all directions. Not a jet, or a plume, a full 360-degree explosion. Fire exploded outward with cataclysmic force, a pulse of white-hot devastation that tore through the forest like judgment.

The shockwave hit first. Trees cracked and bent as if caught in a hurricane. The ground heaved. Then came the fire, a circular inferno consuming everything within 200 meters. Rocks glowed red, then burst. What water remained in the earth hissed into vapor. Illusions disintegrated instantly.

The fire raged, nothing was visible inside. Though even if you could see through, there most likely wouldn’t be anything to see.
