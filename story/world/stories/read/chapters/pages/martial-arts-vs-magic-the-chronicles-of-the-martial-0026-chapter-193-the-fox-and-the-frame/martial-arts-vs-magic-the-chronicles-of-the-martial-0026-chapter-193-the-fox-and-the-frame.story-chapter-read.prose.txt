Princess Jin Ha-Yun woke before sunrise because someone was pounding on the door.

Soo-jin entered before Ha-Yun could answer. She had served Ha-Yun long enough to know better, so Ha-Yun pushed herself upright before the maid said a word.

“Your Highness, forgive me. Something's happened in the Baolian compound.”

Ha-Yun pulled the blanket around herself. “What happened?”

“D-Deputy Secretary Liao Wen. His servants found him dead in his room.”

Ha-Yun threw the blanket aside and reached for her robe.

The Baolian aide had gone to sleep under his own guards, inside rooms protected by powerful wards, both Goryeo ones and the extra ones that Fang Yizhen brought. Said wards never activated. Nor did anybody see an intruder, and nobody had heard a fight. The servants found him after he failed to answer when his morning tea arrived.

Lord Fang Yizhen sent a formal request to the palace before Ha-Yun had finished dressing. He wanted a joint investigation, and he cited the oversight clause from the advisory proposal as precedent even though nobody had signed the damned thing. Ha-Yun had to read the clause twice, mainly because the wording was so calm and annoyingly confident.

Fang had written around the missing seal as if it were a delay rather than a refusal, and if the palace obeyed the clause once, he could point to that obedience the next time.

Within the first hour, she sat through the emergency meeting with her father, General Park, Minister Zhou, and three officials who kept arguing about procedure while a dead Baolian official lay in the guest compound. By the end, she could no longer tell whether they feared Fang's anger or the embarrassment of telling him no to his face.

Her father ordered the royal guards to cooperate with the investigation while refusing to grant Fang’s people extra authority inside the palace. It kept Baolian investigators away from the inner wards, at least for the morning.

Ha-Yun waited for Fang Yizhen to challenge her father's limits, but he accepted the refusal with a simple bow. On the walk out of the council room, she kept wondering which part of that answer he had already planned for.

He had not asked which rooms his investigators could enter. He thanked the Monarch for respecting a Baolian death, then requested the names of Goryeo's investigators before sunset. The exchange had sounded like two courts cooperating.

By sunset, Goryeo's investigators would be working beside Fang's people under a clause the palace had refused to sign. Fang could and definitely would point to that the next time he asked for access, and Ha-Yun suspected he would not wait long.

Ha-Yun had meant to tell Iskandaar what Fang was doing with the proposal and ask what he thought they should do next. After the morning meeting, she did not want to wait for a formal message, so she left the palace before lunch and headed toward the eastern district with two guards trailing behind her. She dismissed them at the branch gate. The guards protested until she reminded them that a royal princess walking into the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult branch was already a public disaster without them standing outside looking nervous.

When she reached, she found the branch had settled into its work.

A porter sat on a bench while Solara checked his shoulder. Some instructors led a small group through breathing exercises upstairs and she also noticed that someone had hung laundry behind the clinic, while a pot of rice boiled in the courtyard.

Nobody stopped working when Ha-Yun entered. The porter tried his arm after Solara finished and smiled when it lifted above his head without pain. Upstairs, one of the students lost the breathing rhythm and started again while an instructor counted for her. The branch was actually helping Goryeo, which made Ha-Yun feel strange.

Ha-Yun found Iskandaar in the rear yard with a broom in his hands.

She stopped inside the gate.

After a morning spent listening to ministers argue over Baolian law, the Princess had not prepared for the Heavenly Demon to be sweeping plum leaves off a stone path with his sleeves rolled to the elbows and whistling to himself. Vyrn sat on a beam nearby, his round eyes following the broom across the stones.

He stopped whistling and looked up after hearing the gate close.

“Ha-Yun?” His grip shifted on the broom. “What happened?”

“Deputy Secretary Liao Wen is dead.”

Iskandaar hummed. She stepped closer. After that morning in the council room, she had no patience left for a careful answer.

“Fang sent the request before I was even dressed. He wants to do a joint investigation, and is demanding an oversight clause. All the typical stuff… The proposal is not signed, Iskandaar, and yet three men in council had to spend an hour debating how much authority we had supposedly given him. It's frustrating. Even though it's obvious that Fang has set up this whole situation, he killed his own servant, we can’t do anything…!”

Iskandaar suddenly looked guilty. “Uh, actually…”

Ha-Yun stared at him. Now that she thought about it, he sounded less surprised by the death and rather sounded a little awkward. For what?

“Actually? Actually what?” Ha-Yun watched his gaze drop to the broom.

He took a breath without looking up. “This time, Fang Yizhen didn’t set something up. This situation is real… Liao Wen died because of me.”

For a moment, the leaves swayed in the air and fell. The wind blew and the world became slow. The cooking pot bubbled in the courtyard. Someone upstairs coughed through a breathing exercise. Then Ha-Yun stepped forward and slapped him.

Vyrn spread his wings and hooted once.

Iskandaar’s face turned with the strike, but he did not raise a hand or reinforce his body. The pain began spreading through Ha-Yun's palm almost immediately.

“You...” Her voice shook, and she hated that it shook. “You did this?”

She wasn’t angry that he killed a person or something, that would be a stupid reason to be angry. But the implications!

He sighed, “I went there for information. Liao wasn't supposed to die.”

“Do not tell me what was supposed to happen, Iskandaar,” She stepped closer. “You of all people know how stressed and frustrated everyone in the palace is, and yet… What you did allowed Fang to walk into my father's council this morning with an unsigned proposal and a body you gave him. Do you understand what that looked like while the rest of us were sitting there blind?!”

His hand tightened around the broom.

“As I said, it wasn’t intentional. Valeria and I went into his dream. We were after Fang’s plans, but the Demon King reached us there.”

Ha-Yun stopped. “The Demon King?”

“Valeria Nocturne, one of the Savage Seven, betrayed the Demon King for me. Now she works for me, but the Demon King doesn’t take betrayal lightly. He left something inside Valeria, a connection none of us found.” Iskandaar set the broom against the wall. “It emerged when we were inside Liao’s dreams and tried to kill her, so I had to fight it.”

“One of the Savage Seven…” Ha-Yun repeated. “What happened to Liao Wen?”

“I had to use my Class Skills to properly erase the Demon King’s influence, and that caused the whole dream to break alongside it. Liao's mind didn't survive the clash.”

Ha-Yun stared at him.

“I’m sorry,” Iskandaar’s face was solemn and serious, which was rare for him. “I had… expected the Demon King to emerge when Valeria would do her dream magic, so ideally I should not have asked her to do her tricks on Fang’s underling. I overlooked that part. It’s my mistake, and I apologize for it.”

His words came so sincerely that she lost the next words she had prepared. He did not reach for some defensive explanation Ha-Yun had expected, he explained what had happened, and apologized for it.

“I knew the dream could turn dangerous, but I thought I could control it. I couldn't.” His gaze dropped to the stones. “Liao Wen is dead because I was wrong.”

When Ha-Yun slapped him earlier, she was ready to fight his arrogance.

Usually he explained why the impossible thing had been necessary, and far too often he turned out to be right. This time, he admitted that he had misjudged the dream and Liao Wen had died because of it.

The admission did nothing for Liao Wen, and it did not keep Fang's proposal out of her father's council room. And yet, it made her feel so guilty. How could she have slapped him?

Ha-Yun looked away from him.

The backyard was small. A cracked water basin sat under the eaves, and leaves from an overgrown plum tree had collected near the wall. The branch had been repaired well, but the yard still looked like the abandoned manor it had been.

“When were you going to tell me?”

“This morning. I was going to come after I checked Valeria. She’s still sleeping.”

“After.” Ha-Yun turned back to him. “So I get to sit across from Fang while he watches us argue over a death that you caused and… and then you wanted to come explain it afterward? I did not even know Liao Wen was dead because of you. You should have come to me last night and told me.”

Iskandaar's mouth tightened, but he let her continue.

“My father told you to leave, and you killed King Tian Shaolong of Xianli anyway and handed us peace. Before that Ao'kai had come because you asked. And now this.” Her palm still stung. “You keep deciding first and leaving me to learn about it afterward. I spent this morning arguing with men who had no idea what happened while Fang used the body. Couldn’t you have warned me? Am I… am I just a pawn to you, to be pushed around on a board whenever you want?”

His fingers touched the reddening mark on his jaw, then dropped when he saw her notice. That made her feel guilty once again, even as she complained. She’d coated foxfire mana on her palms before striking, that was why it managed to hurt him.

She didn’t know what else to say, as silence filled the yard.

Ha-Yun had rehearsed a different conversation on the walk from the palace, one where Fang was the problem and Iskandaar helped her work out what to do next. The part she did not want to examine was that she had wanted him beside her in the council room, instead of maybe arriving later with another decision already made.

Even now, after the slap and the confession, she still wanted his answer.

“Ha-Yun, I built the branch because Shengzong is coming,” he said. The words came slower now. “Because when Goryeo will call for me during an emergency, I want to answer without every court on the continent claiming I invaded you.”

“....”

“The clinic, the food, the instructors, the special martial arts, all of that is real. But, yeah... it also gives me a reason to stand here when any problem comes to Goryeo.”

He looked toward the front hall, where an old woman had started arguing with one of the instructors. Solara walked over with a bowl of soup, and the woman accepted it without pausing her complaint.

“As for why… One reason is something I can’t explain. Goryeo’s culture has a strange place in my heart. Hah. Have I ever told you that the Romani family has eastern roots? Perhaps it's related to that.” Iskandaar fell quiet and took longer with that answer. He rubbed the back of his neck. “That’s the initial reason, I won’t lie. But secondly… It's because I keep thinking about you.”

“What…”

“We had a fun dynamic from the get-go, remember? Your Sakura Blossoms and my Plum Blossoms. Then in Merasca. When I was surrounded by countless people with weapons aimed at my throat, few people kept their weapons low and rather wore an expression of open confusion instead, who trusted that I had my own reasons. I don’t mean this lightly,” he said. “To put it in perspective… Even Professor Amelia had attacked me initially. But you didn't.”

Now she was getting a little embarrassed. Why was he talking like this?

Ha-Yun remembered the scene, and she still felt pain for Iskandaar when she recalled it. Very few had given him a chance to explain himself in their heads, let alone stand beside him. Her fingers loosened at her side.

“W-well… You were barely standing,” she said. “And I just knew… more like I had a feeling that you’re not the type of man to cause the death of so many for no reason. I honestly thought you got framed, that you didn’t cause their death, so don’t give me credit I don’t deserve,” she said. “When I heard confirmation from the others that you indeed did kill all those students and sacrificed their lives, I ended up assuming that the demonic energy of Kurayami, the same sword which I had helped you attain, had caused that. So I felt responsible and guilty.”

“That’s a long explanation to say you trust me.”

“...” Ha-Yun looked toward the front hall because she needed a second before facing him again. “I don’t know why you’re talking like this suddenly, Iskandaar. Do you… do you want something from me?” She looked right into his eyes. “I am not stupid, you know? Are you hitting on me right now? You have women waiting for you in that flying city. What are you trying to do, saying all these sweet stuff to me?”

Iskandaar's mouth opened, then closed. “I don't know how to explain it. It's just… every person is different, you know? They’re special in their own right.”

Neither of them spoke. Iskandaar stayed where he was, the mark from her hand darkening along his jaw. Ha-Yun had hung out with Solara a few days ago, and the phoenix girl was excitedly telling her stories of Iskandaar’s time in Aryavarta. She understood one thing from that exchange. Iskandaar had a lot of lovers, that’s true, however that didn’t mean he chose his women lightly.

He had not chosen Princess Shayura, the Maharani’s daughter, despite the girl clearly having a crush on him. Same with the Naga Princess, Sathari Nezehra. She could easily name a dozen more, and she’d have a whole list left regardless.

What she understood was, while Iskandaar was indeed a manwhore, as she believed it, he had his taste. It wasn’t easy to get him, and he didn’t quite throw himself at women either. Otherwise by now, Iskandaar had countless openings to ask her to be his. Or if maybe he was only after her outer appearance, he could have asked her to spend a night with him in exchange for all his help.

But had he done any of that?

“T-this does not fix what happened to Liao Wen,” Ha-Yun cleared her throat and said, even as her cheeks grew warm and she looked to the side. So does he really like me?!

Somehow, she couldn’t feel her anger anymore. No, it seemed outlandish that she ever felt angry toward this man. After everything he’d done for her and her people, without demanding anything in return.

Right. Liao Wen was still dead, and before sunset Fang would start using the body against her family. Ha-Yun knew all of that, and she was still looking at Iskandaar's mouth.

“You know…” he suddenly said. “My Three Flowers Gather at the Summit lets me read people’s emotions easier than looking for stars in the night sky.”

Her eyes snapped wide.

His hand lifted slowly. Ha-Yun stayed where she was while he brushed the loose hair from her cheek, then leaned down until she could feel his breath.

At that point, what could she do?

She caught the front of his robe and pulled him the rest of the way. His hand moved to the back of her neck as she stepped into him, knocking the broom onto the stones beside them. He smelled faintly of dust and whatever sweet tea he had drunk before she arrived.

Her back met the old brick wall. Iskandaar stopped at once, breathing against her mouth.

“Hey, a slap in exchange for this… A damn good deal.”

She shot him a glare, panting and looking right into his golden eyes. Then she caught his collar again and pulled him back down.

A cane struck the stone path.

“Princess.”

Ha-Yun froze with one hand still twisted in Iskandaar's robe. His forehead rested against hers for another second before he closed his eyes and stepped back, leaving her to turn toward the rear gate.

A short woman stood at the rear gate with both hands resting on a polished cane. She wore a faded green robe, her grey hair tied in a bun, and a small satchel at her hip. Her face was the same harmless one she had shown everyone in the palace medical wing months ago.

The Namja of Eastern Cheonghae.

People often mistook this woman for a local noble who somehow knew too much about the Mountain Gods. Few knew her true identity.

Lady Yueling, the Celestial Fox, looked at them with one brow raised.

“Am I interrupting?”

“...Yes,” Ha-Yun said.

Only then did she realize she still had a fistful of Iskandaar's collar. She released it, tried to straighten the cloth she had wrinkled, and made it worse.

Yueling's gaze moved to Iskandaar’s eyes. “So you see me properly this time.”

Iskandaar had been staring at her with both eyebrows up, before he nodded slowly. “I should have realized the first time we talked, given the fox theme.”

The Celestial Fox tapped her cane once. “Hnn. As much as I’d love to engage in a teasing conversation, the situation is serious. The Mountain Gods are meeting.”

Ha-Yun turned fully toward her. “For what?”

“Ao’kai broke agreements older than your dynasty when he came down during the war. I don’t blame him, for his adopted phoenix of a daughter used his promise to call for him,” Yueling stepped into the yard, talking about Ha-Yun. “He answered the call and involved himself in a mortal war for Goryeo's princess. Now the other gods are wondering whether they should still be here when Shengzong comes.”

Ha-Yun knew Ao’kai had come because of Iskandaar, but to be more specific, he had come to help because Solara, the adopted daughter of Ao’kai, had used his gifted scale and summoned him. She knew this, which was why she loved Solara so much. But she hadn’t understood that his choice broke agreements the other gods still cared about.

Ao’kai had done what he’d done not for Goryeo but for Solara, and now… those gods might abandon it.

“Why now? It’s been months!” Ha-Yun asked.

“Because Fang Yizhen sent word to Ao’kai this morning.” Yueling’s fingers tightened around the cane. “He wants him at a meeting.”

Fang had sent one request into her father's council and another into the highlands before noon. Ha-Yun understood how Liao Wen's death helped Fang's proposal, but she still had no idea what he expected to gain from putting Ao'kai in that meeting.

Iskandaar straightened beside her. “What kind of meeting?”

“Fang called it a clarification of regional spiritual agreements.” Yueling's mouth pulled thin. “He wants Ao’kai across the table while telling him what Shengzong had decided to do with him.”

“So… Fang means to kill Ao’kai?”

Yueling looked at Ha-Yun. “Your father tripled the shrine offerings after the war. He was right to do it, but rice and incense cannot stop the repetition of history, of what happened in Jianghe.”

Jianghe again. Iskandaar had warned her and her father using that as an example, too.

“Jianghe, too, had some local gods. Did you know? They were not as strong as us, but nobody could find those gods after Shengzong erased the kingdom.” Yueling's grip shifted on the cane. “There were three… Having three 9th Ascensions is quite the impressive feat for a small kingdom, but their job limited them. One guarded the western pass, another watched the farming valleys, and the last lived beneath an old cedar where children left fruit every spring.”

The pink petals falling from the trees graced her skin. Until now, she had never thought to ask what happened to blessings when the gods who gave it no longer wanted to remain involved with Goryeo.

“The mountains and the cedar were still there after the Emperor of Sun came, but every shrine stood empty.” Yueling looked down at her hands. “An Arcane King faces little trouble fighting a typical 9th Ascension, and that is no different even if those said 9th Ascensions are worshipped by some few people.”

Iskandaar looked toward the sky beyond the branch roof. “Where is Ao’kai now?”

“Still in the Shan Gui Highlands, with Lady Bai the Qilin. He received Fang’s request but has not answered it. Lady Bai supports his choice.” Yueling looked at him. “Unlike me, a Fox God, Dragon Gods suffer from an incredible ego. Ao’kai did not enjoy being summoned by a man who serves the Emperor of Sun.”

“And exactly because of the ego… he might attend it, right?” Ha-Yun hypothesized. “Just to see if Fang Yizhen truly thinks he can threaten him. But that is the problem. Ao’kai is incredibly strong but are we certain that Fang is weaker?”

“Fang is stronger,” Lady Yueling was direct. “And he’s definitely got items and powers blessed by an Arcane King. He will not lose. There’s a chance that Fang wants to fight Ao’kai and destroy Hwangcheon in the process, using the fight as an excuse.”

Ha-Yun had gone pale by then.

She looked at Iskandaar, and he… he didn’t look any different. He looked as relaxed as he always did. “Ao’kai is Solara’s father. My Solara doesn’t have many family members, so I'm not very keen on letting the proud dragon die. You can relax.”

Yueling studied him, and a scowl of doubt surfaced on her face. “My senses are sharp, Iskandaar Romani, but I can’t sense your current strength, so I’m certain you’ve grown much stronger than before. I understand the confidence. And I appreciate the sentiment. However, even if you stand beside Ao'kai, the chances are it leads to the death of you both. Fang Yizhen has a reputation as a killer even among 9th Ascensions. I sugges–”

Instead of answering, Iskandaar let a thread of his will settle over the yard.

Ha-Yun had felt that pressure when she’d met him for the first time again recently, when he sat in the gazebo with Vyrn on his shoulders. The plum leaves simply stopped moving as it passed over her skin, waking the fox blessing until pink petals lifted around her wrist without any wind.

Then the wind came. It was like a storm but it only existed within this yard, like a huge tornado caught in a bottle. Wind blew, leaves fell, and the air sounded like screams given how fast it moved. Ha-Yun and Yueling’s hair blew across their faces, but their eyes did not close once. They remained wide.

Yueling's false mortal body trembled and her cane sank half an inch into the stone. She pulled the buried tip free and stared at Iskandaar, her eyes wider now and her mouth pressed flat.

“W-what is your Level right now?!” Yueling asked, voice shaking.

“Level 140,” he said. “Gained a few levels last night.”

“Seventh Ascension? Just Seventh Ascension?! How?!”

Ha-Yun understood her Patron Goddess’ shock, for Iskandaar’s aura far outclassed his Level, but even so it was a little embarrassing. Could she at least pretend to be less shocked?

Iskandaar lowered his will, the wind calmed, and the world returned to normal. Yueling stared at him, mouth agape, until the last petal settled. Then she cleared her throat and looked toward Ha-Yun.

“The gods are meeting at the southern ridge tonight,” she said. “As Ha-Yun has predicted, Ao’kai will likely accept the call because of his draconic pride.”

“No worries. When he comes to Hwangcheon, I’ll be there when the royal meeting happens,” Iskandaar said, lacking no hesitation.

Yueling pressed her lips together, then turned toward the gate. “I’m not sure what the future holds, Ha-Yun. If a fight breaks out, either your lover and Ao’kai will meet their end, followed by the entirety of Goryeo, or… well, the Mountain Gods have no choice but to withdraw from Goryeo and limit themselves to the Shan Gui Highlands.” She slowly looked from Iskandaar to Ha-Yun. “Good luck for the days to come.”

She left through the rear gate without another word.

Ha-Yun stood beside Iskandaar and rubbed her stinging palm against her robe. Wasn’t her Patron Goddess a bit too cowardly…?

Then, looking at her side, at Iskandaar, she reminded herself that she shouldn’t judge others by his standards. He was just too strong.

Looking at his side profile, his neck and jaw, she could still taste his tea and wanted to kiss him again, which annoyed her because Liao Wen remained dead and diplomatically she should not be finished being angry with him.

From the front hall, someone laughed. A bowl hit the floor, and Solara complained that the kitchen needed more spoons.

Ha-Yun looked up at the clear afternoon sky above the branch.

Her Patron Goddess had warned her about the days to come. But… looking to the side, as Iskandaar smiled at the sky, she kind of looked forward to it.
