For a brief, insane moment, Henry was tempted to go and have a look at the door by himself, using his actual body and not a clone. Which was likely due to some odd instinct that whispered to him that, for some reason, experiencing the mind-bending construction through his multiple Avatars might not be as accurate or real as what his actual body would experience. Like taking off sunglasses to see something particularly interesting and to be fair, in that scenario, the tint might affect one’s perception, but in this case, there really was no difference between his own sight and his Avatar’s.

In short, the instinct was nonsensical. With his Aspect upgraded, the gap between his own abilities and strengths and those of his Avatars had narrowed even further, and not just when it came to their perceptive abilities. At this point, even two or three of his clones might give him a hard time if he had to fight them. The connection between his mind and that of his Avatars was as close and seamless as the connection between him and his Octominds. Henry could see, hear, and sense just as well as he could with his real body. In fact, his Arcane Avatars were even more sensitive to mana than he was. Through them, he could detect most, if not all the nearby skills that were about to trigger and react before they left their owner.

Henry would know. He was being pelted with hundreds of them right this second. Projectiles, telepathic attacks, and attempts at locking his movements were raining constantly on his visible Avatars. The swarm had many abilities at its disposal, most of them being cast by what he was now thinking of as the “elite” of the swarm. The ones that were different from the Void Lords. Stalkers and their kind were in no way unique, but there were definitely a lot less of them than the chaff that was the Void Lords. At this point, Henry would say there were around twenty Void Lords to every elite. Though that would be a matter for another time because right now, his attention was mostly on the door and what was waiting near it.

“That’s it,” muttered Velistraine, her gaze unblinking. “That’s the door.”

Maurice was equally impressed, and so was Henry. But he couldn’t focus on his companions right now. While he was trying to keep his scout and its supporting crew alive by dodging, dispelling, and Blinking the Avatars out of the way of the hundreds of attacks being sent their way, Henry was noting that the area of the door was not at all empty. In fact, it seemed that there might be even more of the ships around the doorway. A whole armada of them, and some of the ships were gargantuan when compared to his pursuers. These looked more like gigantic, fat, plated caterpillars than anything else. There were three of them, and Henry would have initially compared them to centipedes or something of the sort, but he couldn’t see these things’ legs. He imagined that just like the pillbug ships, these were living beings that had been augmented, but he couldn’t tell from this distance, not yet, anyway.

He was fast approaching with his Avatars, but this might be the end of this scouting mission. Because the area ahead was full of elites circling a particular being he hadn’t thought he’d see again so soon. Next to him, Velistraine had gone still, eyes nearly shining red as Henry heard her teeth grind against each other.

Up ahead, a familiar creature stood. It was the same creature that had peeked at them from the rift, down in Attal’s lab. Or at least, it was the same kind of being. Henry couldn’t tell if this was the same one or not, though it still towered over everything else. Even the largest of the invaders around, an elite subtype covered in a brownish, pearlescent shell that seemed to have some pink hues only came up to the large one’s hip. And though the specific creature was a little hunched, it looked unnaturally sleek and smooth, with two pink, terrifyingly sharp scythe-like arms. Its eyes were large and glittered, as if a constellation of stars had been captured within them, and its carapace shone with a pale purple that veered on pink, as if it had just been polished. It was only half looking back at Henry’s approaching clone, as if he’d interrupted its study of the door, and as soon as their eyes met, Henry sensed the flare of magic right as he cast Identify.

Behind him, the swarm had gone quiet, slowly coming to a halt while Henry’s Lifeblood Avatar froze up mid-flight, and so did all the visible Avatars Henry had sent along with it. His camouflaged ones hadn’t been affected, but Henry left those alone. If they hadn’t been noticed, he’d rather keep them in reserve, though for now, Henry was a little more concerned with the Identify that had failed. It was like it had hit some barrier around the creature and just… bounced off. So Henry plucked a dash of the freshly Unfettered Echoes and cast the skill again.

For the first time in a while, Henry sensed the telltale ache of his skill nexus straining as the power blazed into his soul before flooding his mind with a jumble of impressions.

It took him nearly a full second to parse it all.

The first thing Henry sensed was that this creature was not specialized in warfare. He got more of the impression of a crafter. A creator of some sort. An inventor. Quickly enough, Henry thought of the ships and somehow, he felt that this creature was responsible for those. He didn’t know if it invented them or it just re-created them using some mysterious blueprint, but he felt the connection there. He knew it, just like he knew when Find the Path worked as it should.

For some reason, Henry hadn’t thought the two skills were based on divination.

The next impression he got was that of power, and authority. This thing was beyond everything he’d seen so far. It was, in a way, like seeing the mountain-sized tip of an iceberg, and knowing how much more of it there was below water, knowing that even the bit he was seeing would stomp him flat. Henry couldn’t say for sure, not with the quick and brief exchanges he had, but this creature felt as heavy and dangerous as the sleeping dragon he’d encountered. Maybe a bit less, maybe a bit more. But both of them were so far beyond him that they might as well have been the same.

Finally, the last bit was his own skill, trying to approximate the rank and nature of the being ahead. Without the System adding its poetic grain of salt, it took a beat, giving the creature ample time to slowly approach on its thin, sleek digitgrade legs. Its steps were measured, precise, and Henry could feel both amusement and curiosity from its mind. But that wasn’t what worried him. Being so close, he felt something else below the refined impression, and for a moment, he wasn’t sure if he should call it a drive or a hunger. But it was undeniably an impulse, mighty and absolute, to learn, assimilate, and spread everywhere, and now, a possible vector of that drive had brought itself to its claws.

Identify finally managed to pop something into his sight, and Henry knew his skill wouldn’t be as accurate as it could have been with the System’s input. But it gave him something nonetheless. At this moment, he felt he knew his enemy well enough to glean the rest.

[Architect Queen of the Swarm (?) - ?]

Henry would have been stunned at the confirmation that there was something beyond A-rank, but right now, he was strongly considering detonating all of his Avatars, just in case this thing could take them away and use them somehow. From below the scythe arms, a second pair of arms unfolded, detaching seamlessly from the queen’s sides into two, double-jointed arms that then extended into seven-fingered hands, each with three joints. The hand opened and closed as the queen circled Henry’s Avatar.

“Its eye has healed,” Velistraine muttered to his side, and to Henry’s alarm, the queen’s antennas twitched above its head. It looked down at him, then around at the Arcane and Lifeblood Avatars it had frozen. It reached with a hand, its eyes glowing, right as a voice intruded on Henry’s mind.

“[Like our soldiers. Many shapes, one mind, but better. Worthy of study],” it said, its voice papery and fluttery—even amused, he felt—and Henry found himself wincing at the intensity of the transmission. It had all come through at once, and it was the telepathic equivalent of standing right next to a set of concert speakers. The creature wasn’t yelling exactly, but it was almost as if it was, and Henry would have answered, if it wasn’t for the creature reaching into the air with its human-like hand, and plucking at something—

—and his connection to the Lifeblood Avatar that had been leading the charge flickered in his mind.

Henry immediately detonated it, along with all seven Avatars it had frozen. Instantly, the Avatars exploded, blooming into a cloud of blood and wild mana, only for the creature to wave a hand and all of the explosions were constrained within purple, glass-like geometric structures, while the queen looked up to one of his supposedly camouflaged Avatars. Behind him, hundreds of the invaders stood still or hovered mid-air, silent and placid, like an army of watchful drones, while the queen’s antennas trembled with barely constrained interest.

“[Very interesting,]” it whispered to him, then reached for another thread to pluck.
