"That was the spirit talking through her," Gabriel said before I could do anything dumb in reaction to Aleria insulting my babies.

"And once she actually meets them," Lucy said, "she'll probably love them. But for that to happen, you first have to not kill each other."

Aleria herself hadn't reacted at all to being called a bitch. She just lined up the next shot. This time, I had my wings flexed and used them to assist my jump as she released her shot. Only sparse shrapnel hit me this time. I also noted that after one floor and one load-bearing wall, her arrows did not manage to penetrate into the next floor above me.

While my guides were right, I shouldn't rush her to throw fists; this still wasn't a solid long-term strategy.

"What is your trick, witch? How do you anticipate my attacks? Is your hold on me so strong that you can read my very mind?" Aleria growled. While she stayed as articulate as ever, the way she spoke had very much changed.

"What did they tell you this thing that assists you is? It is tainted to the core. A corruption that matches Nemesis' home, given a mind. Even as it strengthens you, it erodes your real self. You'll turn into the mindless beast you accused me of being. Can you feel it yet? I know all too well what it feels like—"

She had the audacity to shoot at me while I was monologuing. Knowing that she now knew I would dodge, I used my wings to push me backward this time—a correct guess, as she'd led her shot right in front of me.

"Okay, maybe I am reading your mind. Anyway—I know what it's like when your mind doesn't feel like your own anymore. When it becomes difficult to tell what's still you and what's your shape's instincts bleeding into you. This shape is a real doozy, I tell you, she's—"

"Will you stop talking already?! Why won't you fight me? I will kill you!"

Punctuating her screams, she fired off three shots in quick succession, all at the floors underneath me. I ran toward the opposite side of the structure as the building groaned. She couldn't really cause enough structural damage to topple a building, could she?

"Because I don't know how to win yet. I don't want to kill you. You are being played. And I'm also atrociously bad at the whole talk-you-out-of-it thing. As you might have noticed. But I have to try. The moment I hurt you, you'll think of it as confirmation of everything they told you about me."

"Hold still, and the problem will be no more."

I did the opposite and jumped out the window, cutting left immediately. She'd anticipated my flight, but not my mobility. I fucking owned physics. Her shot went wide.

"Have fun landing your hit now, bitch," I said.

Even as she kept up firing, she repositioned herself within her own building.

"You need to keep getting lucky. I only need to hit once."

I stayed behind that same building, my flight as erratic as possible, as she kept peppering it with her overpowered arrows.

"Wrong. You don't have forever," I said. "I will come for you. And I still won't kill you."

There was a trick I had been thinking about ever since that first night. Today, I might have to test it.

"Die. Already. Witch."

On cue to her most recent barrage, I heard it—a groan in the structure, on the far side of the building. While the groan rose to more than that, I ascended higher. Support beams began to buckle under the stress; the popping of cracking concrete stretched into a cacophony of collapse.

And as half the bank collapsed into the street, I followed in its wake, diving straight for the opening Aleria's arrows had torn into her own building.

I had hoped that she'd be a bit more stunned or a bit more blinded by the debris. But her bow was at the ready, the string taut, the arrow crackling with red lightning. She released with only the faintest smile on her lips.

Before she shot, I flapped my wings once and turned myself upright—if her aim was true, she'd hit center mass. The moment she fired, I canceled the transformation.

That impossibly fast projectile caught me halfway through.

For a split second, I was the siren, and I was getting torn to pieces. Another split second, and I was my true self, still being shredded by that projectile as it passed through me. The sensation was unlike anything I had ever felt before—utter, certain annihilation.

But there was enough left of me to shift into the gargoyle, and all that pain and destruction was gone. Replaced with the certainty that this memory would haunt me forever.

This shape was also heavier than either the siren or my true form, so I flapped my wings to keep from dropping like a stone.

At least this stunt had stunned Aleria long enough that she didn't immediately shoot again.

"What…" she muttered.

It was the first time I got a good glimpse of her. That same red lightning flickered all over her body, and around her head it took the shape of a wolf head, overlaid on her actual head. Everywhere I saw skin, I saw scorch marks on her.

The lightning wolf head howled at me. And as her hand twitched back toward her bowstring, I flapped my wings again and dove into her.

Once more, that grappling exercise last night saved my life. Even as we tumbled across the ground, I managed to get a lock on her arm and successfully kept it away from the sword at her hip.

She was strong—very strong. But Sarah's words held true. An angry opponent has lost already. And whatever combat training the elf possessed, she clearly hadn't practiced anything this close and ugly. And what the gargoyle lacked in finesse, it made up for with tough skin, weight, and strength.

Before we had come to a full stop, I had her fully locked down, with the wings wrapped around us, even denying her vision. She kicked, struggled, and tried to bite me, but none of it found purchase. Or a soft enough target to matter.

I just held on. Words could wait for a while. And for a while she kept struggling.

On my minimap, I saw that Jamie and Zi'rah were approaching our position.

["Stay at a distance. I got it under control,"] I wrote.

["What happened? Are you okay? Your health bar did… something."]

["She got a new type of threat with her. A spirit. It's handled now. Just gotta keep spooning this hot elf chick a bit longer, until she calms down."]

And I hoped she would calm down soon. While most of the lock worked through leverage and weight, I still needed to maintain tension. My muscles were starting to burn in pain.

She must have been feeling it too, because her efforts gradually weakened. Though I did not doubt that if I gave even a tiny bit, she'd jump at the opportunity.

"Why are you doing this?" Aleria asked.

"I won't discuss anything with you right now. That approach failed. Just know that I care about you staying unharmed, and you need to be the one to reject this being and send it away. It won't go away unless you tell it to."

I was extrapolating slightly, but hoped it was accurate enough.

"It's nothing you reject. Being chosen as a spirit warrior is a great honor. When I was offered this opportunity to redeem myself, to cleanse myself, I was elated."

I really had to bite my tongue not to point out the obvious inconsistency in that statement. She'd have to arrive there on her own.

"Just ask yourself who you really are, what they told you this would be, and what it urged you to do in reality."

"You know nothing about who I really am, witch."

"Not arguing that. I'll go back to silence now."

This time, I actually managed to keep my mouth shut, despite her barrage of questions. Why I had done what I had done. Why I couldn't die. Why I'd go through this pain, only to subdue her. Why I wouldn't respond to her.

The moment she turned those questions inward, I could feel the fight raging inside. And outside, since every time the spirit gained new ground, she'd intensify her effort at escaping again.

Oh, how I wanted to aid her. I knew that there had to be words that would reach her. That would bolster her in this fight. There was no doubt that Jamie would have known them and how to deliver them.

Instead, I held on in silence. The victory came suddenly, just as I was starting to lose hope.

"Go! Leave! Begone!" she yelled.

The red lightning vanished.

And then she wept. I still held on, more because I was dumbfounded than because I meant to.

"Remember how I said you might need to hug her?" Lucy said. "Don't be an ass. Now is the time."

I canceled out of the gargoyle shape and removed my hold, which prompted her to bury her face against my shoulder. I awkwardly put an arm around her.

["Okay, now I might need help. The hot elven chick is sobbing into my shoulder."]

"What is happening to me? What is happening to my people?" she asked. Even through her sobs, she was as articulate as ever.

"A dark force has manipulated you. Has likely infiltrated your people. And another dark force has infected the entire jungle."

"How can I know what's even real? They told me the same about you."

"Well, if I were the mastermind they told you I was, I'd be a very shitty one, don't you think?"

"A horrible one," she agreed—somehow still sobbing. For a short while, which felt like an eternity, we stayed like that, until voices from the street disturbed us. Aleria pulled back with that same snake-like speed I'd noticed during our first meeting. It was one thing to cry into your enemy's shoulder. It was another thing to be seen doing it.

I handed her a tissue.

"We are up here," I called out. "We'll be with you in a moment."

Aleria was dabbing at her eyes and steadily regaining her composure.

"Are you going to take me prisoner now?" she asked.

"What? Hell, no. Why would I do that? I'll need your help with dealing with those who have sent you on this mission. We have to expose them before their rot takes over your entire people."

She considered my words for a moment before she nodded. I turned toward the gash she'd torn into the building. Jamie and Zi'rah were standing down in the street. I took a few tentative steps on the rubble and held out a hand for Aleria to help her with the descent.

She looked at my hand like I had spat on it and descended several stories in three elegant leaps. Fucking poser.

I cleared the drop to the street in a single jump. Except no one paid any attention to me. Jamie was just about to introduce himself and Zi'rah to Aleria.

"Aleria? I've heard of you. I'm Jamie, this is Zi'rah." My jaw just about shattered the concrete when he took her hand in his and kissed it. "A pleasure to meet you."

And she looked pleased as all fuck. What was happening?

The emotions coming through the oath weren't entirely smug self-satisfaction. Jamie was doing this to help her, too.

He was still smug, though. Increasingly so as my bewilderment reached him.

"The pleasure is all mine, Jamie," she said. "And a pleasure to meet you, too, Zi'rah."

At least Zi'rah was still acting normal. She just nodded at Aleria and said, "Yo."

I stepped forward. "There'll need to be a change in plans. Our previously diplomatic mission to the elves might now be a bit more on the wire. Someone in there is not who they claim they are, and we need to hunt them down and expose them."

"Even if we make haste, it'll be hours before we'd get there. You'd be traversing the jungle at night."

I looked between her, Zi'rah, and Jamie. Aleria and Zi'rah together couldn't weigh more than Jamie in full armor.

I attempted a charming grin. "We won't be walking. And I'll be carrying you?"

Once more, she looked at me with disgust. "Carry? Me?"

I restrained myself from sighing. This would be a long day.
