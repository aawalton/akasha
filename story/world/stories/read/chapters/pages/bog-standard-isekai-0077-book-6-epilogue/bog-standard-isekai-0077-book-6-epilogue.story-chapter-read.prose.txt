Lumina walked the halls of the Tower with Master. They'd started in the Fire Circle offices because he'd let her pick, and she really wanted her juniors to stop crowding her personal laboratory space.

Her thoughts kept returning to her cute little daughter, and Lumina almost regretted that duty had called her here instead of her apartments where Brin was watching little Cecetola. How long had it been that she'd rather be in someone else's company rather than Master's? Had it ever happened before? She felt guilty, because this was Master for Solia's sake, but she couldn't help her thoughts from returning to that sweet little girl.

Master casually waved his wand, and a sitting room repaired itself. Shards of wood snapped back into tables, their bent legs unsplintering themselves and returning to their orderly configuration. The stones reassembled and resumed their place on the walls. Scorch marks returned their carbon to the air, and carpets regrew their missing hairs. Through chaos, order. Everywhere she looked, the broken things mended themselves and returned to their ideal state.

With the room mended, Master tapped his wand again and the wall grew up and closed off the room, blocking it from their sight. He stepped forward, on to the next one.

“Cecetildis Lemaire, do you mind that I let the child keep part of your name?”

Tower Message: I do not require the name I had in life. You may address me as Tower Administrator.

“Tower Administrator, what is the status of the repairs on this level?”

Tower Message: Repairs are 20% complete.

Master had definitely been correct on this count. Ghosts were quite valuable. She didn’t know if Brin could’ve organized things half as well without the Tower’s original ghost.

To Master, she said, “I wish you had returned sooner, though whenever you do arrive after an absence, all the problems that had loomed so large seem to simply fall away.”

“I returned as soon as I could, my dear. Matters such as those that I attend to shall not be rushed,” said Master.

She sniffed. She was not annoyed. She just wished that he'd taken the hint and explained where he'd been when the Tower was practically burning down.

“I've spoken to Duarda. She and all the Circle of Fate are quite alarmed. It seems there was some kind of event the night after the Tower attack. Fate changed in ways she couldn't explain. She described it as the threads of Fate pulling back on themselves, as if someone had dropped a weighted ball on a woven rug, tightening and straining the fabric.

“Hm. Indeed,” said Master absentmindedly. With another wave, the glass of a chemistry station swirled back in place. Another swirl replaced the cabinets and removed all traces of blood from the air. He had the remaining beakers and measuring cups dance in a row, to the tapping of his wand, and they marched back to their places on the shelves.

He glanced at her with a cheeky smile, and she smiled back, amused at his antics.

There had been a great deal of speculation around the wand that he used for the bulk of his magic. Some even assumed that it was the source of his tremendous power. She knew the truth. It was nothing but a magic dampener, helping to whittle down his enormous power to something manageable for simple tasks. Yes, rebuilding the Tower strained him, because it was too easy.

She tried again. “I myself felt some ill omen that night, though I accounted it for a nightmare. After speaking with Duarda, I hypothesized that in fact the phenomenon originated not in Fate itself, but in the Wyrd.”

Master sighed. “I suppose it's time to tell you. I... will die.”

Lumina snorted. “Whatever this is, Master, you aren't going to die.”

“No, no, you misunderstand. I am not saying that I fear I will die, or that I foresee a threat that can cause my death. I am telling you of a resolution. I know what it would take to gain the last levels required to elevate myself into immortality, and I have decided that I will not do it. So all that remains is to enter eternity by the usual methods,” said the Master.

“I see,” said Lumina.

“To tell the truth, it is not so terrible a thought. Aside from you, every person I have ever loved has already passed beyond. I picture myself entering that world, as one soul among many, perhaps to find them there, hat in hand, and to apologize for making them wait so long.”

“What would it take?” asked Lumina.

“Hm? What was that?”

“What would it take for you to achieve ascension?” asked Lumina.

“I would be required to take this power and place it in the hands of the common man. Available for money. Bricks for them to build off of, soil for them to grow out of. I have looked into it, you see. I have seen what this world could become. My sweet child, you cannot understand the world that I could create. Great would be the works of my hands, and the works of the hands of men whom I would have blessed.”

The Master paused his work on repairing the Tower for a moment, eyes glazing over. “Quick to do righteousness, flying forth to deliver justice, to feed the hungry and clothe the naked, to slay the devourer, admonish the famine, and deliver admonitions, yes even stern admonitions to the pox and the storm. The arm of man would be strong as steel, firm as a stone, and smooth as flax. Can you imagine that there will be men, in those days? When the power to create and transform is as common as gold and silver, who could ever remain a man? Great races, wise and fair will rise up, until the question of humanity will be answered in a hundred different ways, all of the answers correct, until nothing remains of man but the memory of a dream.

“Cities would be built to cover the entire land, so that a man could walk from the Alent to the sea and never step on an unpaved stone. How grand, you know not. How lovely to see, you know not. How great and terrible, you cannot understand.

“Neither can you understand the evil that would rise in those days. When no barrier exists between could be and is, every vile thing that ever dwelled in the hearts of man would enter in the world in truth. You cannot imagine the atrocity of those days. Sins that we have no words for. Monsters. It would be better to never have been born than to live in a day where those monsters bared their putrid faces.

“Who can balance these concerns? Who can say what is just? All I know is that I have a relatively spotless conscience, for my age. I have less blood on my hands than many who are a tenth my age. I will not turn the entire world on the hinge of my advancement. I will decline, and then die, quietly.”

Lumina couldn't speak for a while for the tears that she held back. The Master walked on, repairing room after room, giving her time to gather herself.

Once she had her emotions under control, she still couldn't speak because she didn't know what to say? He'd obviously been thinking about this for a while. That speech was planned, if not rehearsed. He knew his mind. She could try to dissuade, but should she? The picture he painted was certainly vivid.

If a multitude of people worked together to create the things he spoke of, she wouldn't say it was ill done. But all of it because of the power of one man? It was too much for any one person to bear, even one as great as her Master.

“How much time to do you have?” asked Lumina.

“Time enough to settle my affairs. More than enough time for that. I told you, I do not predict that something will kill me; only that I have decided not to live forever.”

“I just don't know how I would even approach problems like this disturbance in Fate without you here,” said Lumina.

Master laughed. “You won't let that go, will you, my dear? I haven't told you because I don't want you to become overconfident. Just because I can do these things doesn't mean you could, or that you should ever dream of trying.”

“I promise, Master, I won't grow arrogant,” said Lumina, biting down the irony of Master calling her arrogant after the things he had just told her.

Master sighed. “I became cross with Arcaena. She enabled the Motley Shroud and assisted the Easterling incursion. Try as she might to pretend innocence, I call it interference.”

Lumina stopped walking. Master continued on along, repairing the Tower as he went. “You mean to attack Arcaena? Now, when nothing could compel you to do so last year when it might have helped?”

“I intend to do no such thing,” said Master, and Lumina began to relax until he continued and made it worse. “I already visited her and voiced my disapproval.”

Lumina gasped, suddenly out of breath. She had been downplaying the scale of the event to see if she could draw some information from him, but it had been overwhelming. The Fate readers and Wyrd watchers had seen a continent of pain. A world of fear. And most of all, fire. An all-consuming flame.

“She will curse you! She will seek revenge. On all of us!”

Master shook his head. “See? This is why I sought not to tell you. No. Did you think I didn't understand the Wyrd? I do, and I call it quite simple, and if I understand the Wyrd, then I understand its chief servant. She will not seek revenge. There is a certain kind of fear that one can excite in the human mind that moves beyond all possible thoughts of revenge. It evokes an overwhelming acquiescence, an insatiable desire to do anything to make the object of that fear cease its approach. She is interested in nothing but peace and conciliation now.”

“Master. What did you do?”

“I expressed my firm disapproval,” he answered, and continued on his way. “I recorded the event. I will perhaps allow you to view this recording some day, when you are ready for it. We will speak no more of this today.”

They came to a block of offices that had been completely drowned in water. Opening the door should have caused a flood, though of course Master kept the waters back, and with a wave, sent the excess water straight out of existence. Unsummon magic was rare and costly, and if Brin was ever offered such a thing she'd advise him not to take it. That Master could unsummon water surprised her not at all.

He took a lot longer to repair the damage of the water, even going so far as to return the ink to the papers left on the desks. While he worked, she thought about what she might say to the revelation about Arcaena, about what it might change for her. The answer was what he had used to start the conversation to begin with. She was being arrogant. There was nothing she could or should do about this. It was on the scale of monsters. She was better off thinking about the [King's] recent power grab, and the three dukes who were combining forces to oppose his rise into a total monarch.

But sometimes great monsters still stomped across her smaller world, even if she didn't seek them out. “Will you admonish the Easterlings as well?”

“The Easterlings ignored my warning,” said the Master. “I am at war with the Great Green Mother, and with her children who she delights to feed. I will allow you to participate in this. It would be good for your development to see what I will do there. Oh, but there is another matter of more urgency that we should discuss.”

“What could possibly be more urgent than that?” asked Lumina.

“I heard tell that you mentioned at one time that you expected to be married by now, only that you thought events would arrange themselves to make it happen without your needing to go out of your way. Are those your true thoughts?”

Lumina spoke too quickly after the whiplash change in topic. “Those are an... approximation of thoughts that I might have conveyed... off-handedly… in a non-binding sort of way.”

Master nodded. “Good enough. I'd like you to marry Lord Ian of Avion.”
