Chapter II.XII (2.12)

“You believe a leviathan will attack our ship?” the captain asked. He looked rather pale as he dabbed at his forehead with a neckerchief.

“If we go the usual direction,” Mitsuko said. “There must be alternate paths to take to Porcelain Island.”

The captain exchanged a look with his navigator. “There are… however they’ll take several days to complete. Inside the dome and without the assistance of external winds, we are reliant on the archipelago’s unique current system.”

“How long exactly?”

“Perhaps… four days?”

Mitsuko frowned and looked to Sterling. The black cat stood off to the side, grooming himself. With one day spent on Mauve already, that would only leave her a day and a half on Porcelain to investigate the island. Hardly ideal. But she also didn’t like her odds at fighting that leviathan. She might survive and allow them to escape. That was a gamble though. She didn’t know what awaited her on Porcelain Island so nearly two guaranteed days to explore and scout the island had to be set up against potentially more than double that with the high risk of nothing at all.

“Fine. Let’s do that. Getting a basic amount of information now is probably more important. And it will give me more time to practice my spells and get some of these lower levels figured out.”

“What?” The captain stared at her.

Mitsuko sighed. “Nothing. Don’t worry about it. Just talking out loud.”

She departed the captain’s cabin and wandered The Selcouth Sable’s deck. A profound melancholy took her as she stared out at the flat sea. The horizon was blocked by a barrier. The same as always. An unchanging, immovable barrier that refused to dissipate. Mitsuko could take drastic measures to change everything. But by the end of the week, it would all reset and repeat. Nothing ever changed. A cycle of absolute stagnation.

She considered going to Holly. But while her friend knew the timeloops existed, Holly didn’t truly understand. And perhaps it was better that she didn’t. If someone thought too long about the morality of their very existence being erased and reset, it might lead to dark thoughts.

Dark thoughts like the ones currently weighing down Mitsuko.

“I don’t think I enjoy sea travel,” she muttered to herself. “Too much time to think.”

“Have you ever attempted meditation?” Sterling asked. “Personally, I find it helps. Perhaps an hour or two could be set aside for it now that you’ve overcome the bodily need for sleep. Rest serves a purpose beyond refreshing the physical body. The burden of immortality can weigh quite heavily on the psyche.”

“I suppose that’s something to consider,” she mumbled. As she stood there, pressed up against the ship’s gunwales, a thought occurred to her.

She pressed her magic against The Selcouth Sable and cast Deteriorate.

Vertigo immediately struck her and she slumped against the gunwales, teetering over the edge.

“Woah!” Sterling called out. “Did you just—”

Mitsuko’s vision went black for a moment. The next thing she knew she was face down on the deck, several sailors crying out and rushing at her.

“I’m fine,” she said, trying to wave away their concern. “Just got a bit hot is all. I’ll go below deck and have a glass of water.”

Thankfully, the crew still clearly held wariness after her display of magic fighting against the kraken. and didn’t press the issue.

After stumbling down into the ship’s cargo hold for peace and quiet, Mitsuko addressed the sage.

“What just happened?”

“That is a question I should be asking you,” Sterling replied obstinately.

“I tried casting Deteriorate on the ship to speed up our travel time. I thought with an additional speed boost we might be able to arrive without delay.”

“An interesting attempted application of Juniper’s spell,” Sterling said. “And with further levels and strength it is a feasible option, albeit an extremely inefficient one. The sort of spellcraft you want for completing that task is something that belongs to a different sage. But, of course, I can speak no further on the topic. Unless….”

Mitsuko studied the black cat. He flicked his tail under her scrutiny, his glowing green eyes meeting her dark eyes. That was another nugget of information offered to her freely in hopes of her paying for the full meal. Bait on a fishing hook.

“I look forward to meeting the sage,” she said.

Sterling’s tail dipped in disappointment at her refusal to ask the question.

In truth, she could safely ask anything she wanted without much concern since she sat on a wealth of available questions. But this just didn’t seem like something pressing or particularly important. She’d learn about whatever that spell was when she freed the sage in question. Asking about it right now wouldn’t further her goals in any meaningful way.

“What is your plan for the next few days?” Sterling asked her. “You mentioned training. Anything in particular you’re hoping to study?”

“Well, for one thing, I know that Holly collected some atlases while in the city. She’s trying to compile a decent map for us to use and I’ll be assisting her and learning what I can about Porcelain’s layout.”

“And for magic?”

Mitsuko gestured at the cargo hold. “There’s plenty here for me to study with Retrospection. I want to get that up to level 5 at the very least so I can speak to Fuku without fear of retaliation.”

“Well, she could still retaliate,” Sterling pointed out. “With Retrospection – Self, you simply would have the means in which to reverse those changes on your mind and reset yourself.”

“Semantics,” Mitsuko said dismissively. “Anyway, I want that to be a priority. I don’t like fearing for my mind every time I happen to encounter her. The other option is to map out her every movement throughout a loop to better avoid her, but I need to get this spell leveled up regardless so better to focus my efforts on this.

Sterling nodded. “Yes. I agree. Stalking her for an entire loop doesn’t sound like a productive use of your time.”

Mitsuko leaned back and surveyed the crates and barrels that filled the cargo hold. An enchanted lantern hung on the wall behind her, illuminating the small sea of wood before her. While some cargo had been unloaded back in Mauve, new products had been purchased to take its place. She’d need to crouch just to reach the closer cargo and she foresaw a lot of shuffling of crates in her near future to get at the stuff in the back.

“Any recommendations on where to begin?” she asked the cat.

“Hm.” Sterling padded forward and jumped up onto a crate. He raised his head and sniffed the air. “This one smells like firemaking tools. They might have some interesting history behind them.”

Mitsuko, without any better leads, decided to take Sterling’s word for it and used Mend to revert the crate back to a state where the nails had yet to be hammered down. She yanked off the lid and picked up a stretch of red wood. Rubbing a thumb over it, she felt the heat of friction just from that one brief action. It clearly had highly flammable properties.

She cast Retrospection and the item’s history flashed in her mind. She saw it all backwards as it went back into the crate and was sealed away in darkness. A crack in the crate allowed a sliver of light that shook as the crate was maneuvered around. The crate opened and showed a pudgy face of a woman wearing the standard Sailor’s Guild uniform. She shuffled it to the side as she examined the crate’s contents then it was resealed again. The piece of wood then sat in darkness for a long time. Weeks. Until the crate opened once again and it was removed. Mitsuko watched the backwards process as the strip of wood was joined back together with larger pieces. It grew and grew until it was a full log. Then the log rolled off to the side and over to a river. It washed upstream alongside a hundred similar logs until it hit a riverbank where it rolled its way up a hill and came to rest at a stump. A man with an axe reattached each of its branches and then it lifted skyward. The man swung the axe over and over, repairing the damage and sealing it to the stump. The tree stood tall, the striking reds of a sunset on its bark. Other woodsmen did similar actions to the trees in the vicinity and soon the tree was touching branches with neighboring brothers and sisters. All of the woodsmen walked backwards, retreating from the beautiful red grove. Sun, rain, snow, fog, heat, winds. Mitsuko saw it all strike up against the trunk. Fires smoldered on the bark occasionally, but the wood itself remained strong, the heat exiting it but never marring it. Or was it the heat was rejected by the wood? Her backwards point of view struggled with the concept. It shrank as the years went backwards. It was down to a sprout the size of the wood Mitsuko currently held when her vision finally blitzed out and she returned to the present.

“Woah. That was a lot of information,” she said, blinking and readjusting back to the present.

“What did you expect from organic material?” Sterling asked flippantly. “Flora might not interact as heavily with the world as fauna, but it hardly remains stagnant throughout its existence.”

“I…suppose you’re right,” Mitsuko admitted sheepishly. She gingerly set the slab of firestarting wood back into the crate. She now had a basic level of understanding about how the wood worked. It accelerated heat but didn’t actually burn itself. A weird natural resource. She wondered if it was magical or just complicated.

“On to the next item?” Sterling asked. He hopped over to a nearby barrel. “I believe I smell something rather sour inside this one. Who knows? Maybe it will be something you can practice Deteriorate on as well.”

Mitsuko took a second to stare at the little piece of firestarting wood. Then she put it away and went to see what Sterling was going on about.
