At full speed, the flight barely took Ethan five minutes. The more he pushed mana to [Draconic Wings], the faster they tore through the air and by now, he could channel a pretty sizable amount of mana to the spellform. He couldn’t tell exactly when it had happened, but ever since the Brotherhood and Union’s crisis, he felt his control over his mana and how much he fed to the spellform increase. Maybe it had to do with simple stats. Or maybe it had to do with him clashing against another powerful Zenith state user. Clearly, Prime Manifestations had something to do with willpower and resolve, so maybe that was having an impact on how his other spellforms performed? Like a side effect?

All that was to say that once he was far enough from people and from the surface, he could accelerate fast enough for wind to howl in his ears and if it wasn’t for the high Toughness, he’d bet his eyes wouldn’t have been able to manage the flight, but he didn’t have to worry about that.

Soon enough, he entered the pylon’s zone of influence and recovered access to his interface, at which point he typed a quick message to Ember while he slowly approached the walls. Thanks to the recent inclusion of multiple pylons, the access range had spread pretty widely, and by now he’d estimate he could access the controls from within 700 feet from the pylon. It had nearly doubled from before. On the other hand, the detection range had exploded, going from around a mile and a half back with one pylon to nearly 8 miles around each pylon—which was definitely a factor playing into the number of rifts they were finding though at least now, they had plenty of people to start taking care of them.

His arrival was of course noticed, and even though he stayed up high above the settlement as he waited for Ember to respond, he still waved back toward those who had noticed him. As for Ember, she didn’t deign to respond, but a plume of flames drew his attention to one particular roof near the pylon, so Ethan swooped down toward her.

The flames had caused a little agitation from the surrounding citizens, but they soon realized who had been the cause. Ethan found Ember sitting down on her front paws, ears twitching as she watched him come down and when he was close enough, she got up, hopped in the air a few times with her anklets and knowing where she was going with it, Ethan summoned his horns. A moment later, Ember landed on them.

Better she scratches his horn than tangle her claws in his hair.

Ethan gave her a couple of seconds to settle in, but when a handful of seconds went by without a word aside from the general good mood below, Ethan guessed the cat was still in an iffy mood. The tail swishing back and forth might have also been a tell that something was bothering the Ignisra.

“Still pissed you weren’t around for the fight?” he asked, glancing up past the paw that dangled inches from his face.

“[No.]”

Ethan said nothing, just raising a brow at the clearly annoyed tone, and seconds later, a soft growl escaped the cat. “[Yes. A little.]”

A smile pulled on his lips and he looked back down at the crowd. Same as back in New Dawn, the folks of Wyldrock were having a little get together near the central plaza. Only difference was, there were three large cats bobbing and weaving between the crowd, begging for scraps of meat. “Didn’t know you had grown that attached.”

“[Don’t flatter yourselves. I’m just annoyed someone dared attack in my absence.]”

“Right.”

Of course, Ethan wasn’t buying it. Ember proved again and again that she was willing to put her fur on the line when it came to protecting what she most likely saw as her charges. No matter how much she’d grumble about it, so Ethan could empathize. He had been pissed off enough for having arrived late for the attacks. Missing them altogether?

He might have been in a much worse place when he’d gone to confront the Brotherhood and the Union.

“I’m sure you’ll have plenty of occasions to work that frustration out. Have you seen the rift task force in New Dawn?”

“[Audrey told me. I’ll be going there later today. Or tomorrow. But that’s clearly not why you’re here,]” she added, her paw tapping him on his forehead. “[What’s this about?]”

“To cut to the chase, the high council and me are thinking that the accident that had you summoned in our tutorial might not have been unique. And considering the system did not list you in the leaderboards, we’re thinking there might be more… visitors out there. You’re one of us, but I’m not sure these other visitors would be trustworthy and we’re worried that some might have a way to reach out to where they come from.”

Ember was quiet as she listened. Even her tail had stopped swaying for a few moments before it resumed its rhythmic swaying, though slower, as if she was deep in thought.

“[That is… I didn’t consider that. I really thought that mine was a freak accident, but that possibility is too dangerous to ignore. So I’ll go talk to them. Plus, while someone like me might be easy to spot, I imagine a particularly smart human or two that managed to sneak through could do some damage if they’re left unchecked.]”

Ethan listened and this time, it was him who had to stop and think. He had kept picturing the possible visitor as other magical creatures like Ember, but he hadn’t thought of the possibilities of them being human.

But now a possible worst-case scenario was taking shape in his mind.

As Ember said, if they happened to be smart and malicious, they could wreak havoc on them. If they were random people then it might not be as bad. But if they had powerful affiliations or some knowledge on how to exploit a new, naive world that had been isolated from the rest of the universe? And while a non-human would likely receive a lot of scrutiny if they ever managed to get any political power, a human would go unnoticed. They would slip under the radar, planting their seeds, laying the groundwork for an outside force to come by and take over the planet as soon as the System’s protection was lifted.

It was definitely a threat to keep in mind. And it was another reminder to him that he shouldn’t slack and just like him, it seemed that the Ignisra was not content sitting around and waiting for the possible threat to become a reality.

“[I will talk to them. Take me there.]”

Ethan blinked, then frowned. “It’s not that far. I bet it’d take you less than an hour to get there, plus, I got stuff to do.”

Her tail slapped him over the air, but because it was just furry and light, it only tickled him. “[You owe me. For all the meals you did not provide even though we had agreed on it.]”

Ethan’s word caught in his throat, turning into a defeated groan as he ascended in the air. She got him dead to rights. “Yeah, alright. Fine. Also, get down here or you might fly off,” he said, reaching up to grab the cat who only weakly protested.

With her cradled in his arms, Ethan accelerated, forcing an annoyed yowl from the cat who had to tuck her head and ears away from the wind. But he was able to coax her out when he coated her with his flames, at which point she hungrily chomped at the fire magic for the couple of minutes remaining in the flight. Once they arrived, he put her down at Amanda’s feet, quickly communicating the possibility he and Ember had thought of—though he assumed Amanda had probably considered it already. With his work here done, he flew off, finally free to explore his rift token.

Ethan landed near the dock in which he had fought those scarabs, and as the waves lapped at the cement below him, he activated the token.

At first, nothing happened, and he wondered whether his usually extremely responsive interface had somehow lagged, but a couple of seconds later, a prompt popped out, and Ethan’s brows lifted. While this was the first time he’d claimed a rift token, he hadn’t expected the warning.

The rift token activated will take you to a special rift where the opportunities for learning and growth were judged optimal for your skillset and spells. It will come with dangers to your life and might pit you against other participants. Some of your abilities will be restrained, and this might put your life at risk more so than rifts that you might have already experienced.

Do you agree to these conditions?

The text floated in front of him, and Ethan read once, then twice. All the while he considered what this meant. A restriction? What kind? How limited would he be? He didn’t think it was all or nothing. He didn’t think he’d lose his stats. Maybe they’d be reduced if his traits and spellforms were limited, but not the base ones, considering they were tied to him and part of his levels. It could be a restriction on Arcanums, though. Or spells?

The more he thought of it, the longer the list of possibilities was getting. But his eyes kept swiveling back to the learning and growth part. This was an opportunity and unlike the rifts he’d explored so far, this wouldn’t be—at least he thought—a wild rift. This one had a challenge. It was a learning opportunity and well, he did free himself to do it.

With a resolved nod, Ethan reached out to agree, then caught himself.

Hold on a second, I should prepare before I throw myself into an unknown environment, he thought and with a mental push, he tried moving the prompt aside. Thankfully, it responded to his input, and he was able to minimize it and pull up the rest of his interface.

It was time to spend some fragments.
