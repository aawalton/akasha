The Battle of Divine Will is going to begin soon.

Bet-rank divine warriors and above are to assemble before the Rift of Divine Will.

It was the afternoon of the next day after I began my training when these notifications appeared. It was finally time—the Battle of Divine Will had begun at long last.

“It’s begun already?” Boris muttered, staring in the direction of the Rift Square.

Over the past two days, Boris hadn’t solely focused on training. He had also taken charge of organizing and drilling Fantasia’s divine warriors. Capable individuals, mostly leaders of divine warrior factions, were appointed as leaders of smaller units within the army, forming a clear command structure. From what I could tell, he was using a system inspired by modern military organization, blended with medieval and fantasy elements to keep it flexible.

The smallest unit was a party, consisting of five people. Above that was a platoon, made up of twenty-five. Next came a company of one hundred divine warriors, followed by a battalion of five hundred. At the top was a brigade, numbering roughly 2,500 divine warriors.

I had already informed Boris that Fantasia would only be fielding Bet-rank divine warriors and above for the Battle of Divine Will. That was why the total number of participants fell just short of three thousand. Even if Aleph-rank divine warriors were included, the number would barely reach four thousand. Those who weren’t assigned to the main brigade, usually healers or divine warriors without combat classes, would instead be placed in reserve or designated as support units. This kind of configuration maximized the army’s overall effectiveness while allowing it to adapt to a wide range of situations.

With Boris in command, I was more than confident we would be able to prevail in this Battle of Divine Will. Even if our numbers and overall quality were at a disadvantage, an efficient command structure could offset those shortcomings.

As for myself, I had improved quite a lot over these two days of nonstop training.

Aside from that, both my stats and skills had improved considerably. For instance, Basic Cryo Magic had evolved into Intermediate Cryo Magic, which was a huge improvement. On top of that, I had picked up several new spells over the past two days that complemented my existing kit perfectly.

My physical conditioning had also stabilized. Even under sustained exertion, my stamina recovery had improved noticeably, and my reaction speed felt sharper than ever. Combined with my refined weapon handling, I could confidently say that I was operating beyond the limit of what a Gimmel-rank divine warrior should be capable of—and then some.

Still, I wasn’t arrogant enough to believe that preparation alone guaranteed victory. I had little to no information about the enemies I would be facing, and without information, there was no such thing as certainty.

Regardless, I was ready to face whatever may come.

With that in mind, we made our way to the Rift Square. The moment we arrived, the place was already packed with divine warriors. Some stood in disciplined formations beneath their appointed leaders, while others waited in smaller groups, quietly making their final preparations. When they saw us, especially Boris, they immediately saluted.

At the far end of the square, the Rift of Divine Will rested within the central alcove carved into the wall. It continued to shine, with the eyes of the angel statues encircling the plaza igniting with blinding white-gold light. A massive portal of pure white swirled, and it was becoming increasingly tangible with each passing second, and it almost felt like I could see through on the other side soon enough.

“This is it, huh…” Michelle murmured, swallowing hard.

Just as Boris moved to issue instructions to his unit leaders, a familiar voice cut through the tension.

“Oi! Maxim! Everyone!”

Did she? I didn’t remember her ever saying she’d get it done in time. She must have burned the midnight oil to meet the deadline. As expected of a blacksmith maniac…
