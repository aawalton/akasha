Both father and son were kind. I showed them the juvenile Starry Lemurs after showing Kale, and I convinced his father to stay a little longer. As reluctant as the father was, he couldn’t reject his son’s puppy eyes. How could he?

One way or another, I liked them. Kale’s parents were both cooks in the ninth sector. From the sound of it, the mother was so good that even Blessed from other sectors came to visit her in the Bastion’s outskirts. She’d received several offers to work in the restaurants in other sectors, but she always rejected them.

For one, Kale’s mother was unBlessed and wouldn’t be able to stay long in the sectors closer to the main sector. Ether would slowly seep into her, which could possibly lead to mutations. Most of the time, that would lead to undesired effects, including cancer. Rarely, those mutations would lead to positive changes, though it did happen before.

Anyway, only the ninth and eighth sectors had low enough ether density to ensure mutations wouldn’t occur under any circumstances. And that was where most unBlessed lived. Their opportunities to earn enough to make a living and save up enough to purchase a suitable beast were too low to elevate their status.

Kale and his parents didn’t have unique Worlds as far as I knew, but neither his father nor his mother had been able to refine their World for years. They spent most of the day in the kitchen and focused what was left on Kale, trying to offer him all they never had. That reminded me of my parents. My health condition had made matters worse, but the gist was the same.

All that was enough reason for me to accept Kale and his family as my first clients. I needed points to meet my quota anyway. Yeah, that was definitely the most important part. I wasn’t feeling melancholic at all.

“I heard something interesting through the grapevine. A Foundation, I think it was called Rising, was funded not too long ago. The Foundation plans to help unBlessed awaken by funding them financially. I don’t know the exact criteria, but I think your family fits perfectly. If your Worlds aren’t outrageously unique, that is.” I hinted to Kale’s father while the boy was busy staring at the juvenile Starry Lemurs.

The father turned to me, looking hopeful, then suspicious. “The Council is funding?”

He didn’t trust the Council. That much was obvious. From the looks of it, Kale’s father trusted them as far as he could throw them, which wasn’t very far, given that he was a skinny unBlessed.

Shaking my head, I explained a little more. “The Council has nothing to do with that. It’s an organization, a Foundation, with the sole intention to help the less fortunate. Everyone needs some personal power in times like these, don’t you think?”

Kale’s father nodded slowly but didn’t seem convinced.

“I am not going to force anything onto you. Nobody is forcing you to do anything. All the Foundation wants is to make sure Blessed and beasts treat each other well. Like family. That is the Foundation’s only wish and condition; everyone acquiring a Soulkin through their financial support has to accept it into their family. Treat it like their own flesh and blood. In exchange, the Foundation pays for a thorough counseling session, a suitable Soulkin, and a basic package of food and other requirements for the first three months. Alas, you will have to visit the Foundation or their representative in the Beast Temple once a month to let him – or her – inspect both Blessed and Soulkin to ensure the bond grows naturally.”

That sounded almost too good to be true. Nobody sane enough would care about the sole condition attached to such a massive investment. Whoever rejected such an offer was either suspicious of any small printed conditions that weren’t clearly communicated or planned to do something heinous with their Soulkin. One way or another, I was not particularly worried about Kale and his parents. His mother may change my first impression of them, but I highly doubted that.

If anything, I felt like my impression of them would improve.

“That is the only condition?” Kale’s father furrowed his brows and scratched the back of his hand nervously. “And this is a new Foundation? Do you have any records my wife and I could read through to learn more?”

I liked how cautious he was, yet I hated it at the same time. How was I supposed to pull out a bunch of non-existent records? The Rising Foundation had only just been founded. If anything, it didn’t even exist operationally yet. The higher-ups still had to accept a few more permits and other formalities, but that would be over in a few days.

“Unfortunately, there are no records. To be honest with you, you and your family would be the first to receive their sponsorship. The founder of the Rising Foundation is from the ninth sector and lived a harsh life, if I may say so, and he’d been lucky to escape and awaken. His father left the Bastion’s safety, exposed himself to the wilderness’ ether density and beast threats as an unBlessed, and returned heavily injured with a beast egg for him to bind. He wants to help others like his father helped him, hoping nobody would be as desperate as his father. Even more so with Outsiders roaming outside the Bastion.”

Kale’s father was still not convinced and looked suspiciously at me. Something set him off. Was it the way I spoke about the Foundation? Did I promote it too much?

It took effort to keep my groans and curses inside. Being cautious was great and all, but please…

Alas, my lucky star returned, clearly having heard some of what was said.

“Is that true? Can I awaken?” Kale stormed back to us, his eyes sparkling like a cloudless night. When his father didn’t say anything, Kale exclaimed, clearly thinking he was right.

“That is so cool! I want to awaken! Pleasee, Pa!!”

Kale’s father groaned, but didn’t reject his son right away. That was good enough for me. I opened a page on my watch and showed it to them: the Rising Foundation’s Nexus page.

While the page was still relatively empty, it looked official enough. It included several legal documents, the contract I had crafted by a lawyer a few days ago, and the section for submissions. Finding a good lawyer willing to work overtime had been difficult. I was pretty sure the lawyer had extorted me as well, but I’d already accepted the loss. On another note, the submission section was a hassle. I knew that much too. There were dozens of questions that had to be filled out, some of which were quite personal and cumbersome to answer.

Alas, the officials wanted something… well, official. So I did what I had to do. Furthermore, I could sort out a lot of submissions if I disliked their answers…or if I smelled bullshit. Obvious liars weren’t given a second thought; neither were those who felt like they could be jerks or narcissistic assholes. They could take care of their own awakening. I only wanted genuine people. Nice people. People who cared.

“The Rising Foundation is only active in our Bastion? It says Adam Savier is the Foundation’s representative in the Beetle, and to meet him for counseling sessions once we receive an invitation to an official meeting.” Kale’s father looked up, eyebrows raised. “I assume you are Adam Savier.”

Seeing the doubts, or at least some of them, fade from the man’s eyes was a pleasant surprise. It showed he trusted me. A little, maybe. More than he’d trust a stranger’s newly established Foundation.

“I am Adam Savier.” I nodded, pointing at the name tag on my chest. “My position in the Foundation is nothing special. I have a simple task: to counsel potential clients and decide if they’re fit to enjoy the Foundation’s sponsoring.” I waved dismissively when Kale let out a ‘woah!’

“Like I said, my position is not all that great. I only accepted it because I wanted to help a bit. After all, I am originally from the ninth sector as well.” That seemed to surprise Kale’s father. “Yes, I am from the ninth sector, so I feel obliged to help where I can. To honor all my parents sacrificed to support me. To keep me alive and help me awaken.”

It looked like Kale’s father finally understood. His eyes widened as realization dawned, and he could only nod when his son asked him if they were going to become Blessed.

“Thank you for the opportunity,” he said, glancing at me and then at the holographic screen. “For everything. We will check out the Foundation, but I think… I think we will return for the counseling… if the Foundation permits.”

We chatted a little bit more, but Kale’s eagerness to go home and fill out the submission form shattered all thoughts of a proper conversation. His father looked like he had many questions, some of which were about my age and strength, yet he didn’t get answers to most of them.

“We can talk when you come back.”

He accepted my proposal and left, his shoulders no longer slumped, a faint smile playing on his lips.

We returned to the front desk, where Kale and his father departed, looking happy.

“What exactly did you do to them?” Tanja asked. “Please don’t tell me you made any stupid promises. I know you are young, but you shouldn’t do that. Do it once, and everyone from the ninth sector will come. Can you imagine what would happen if thousands of unBlessed rushed to the main sector to visit the Beast Temple, forming lines and waiting for hours, clinging desperately to the low chance of–”

Tanja stopped when I displayed the Foundation’s information. Her worries were not unfounded. They made sense. Well, most of them. I was not particularly worried about the ether exposure from the main sector. A few hours weren’t enough to stimulate any severe mutations. If that had been the case, I would have mutated or died several years ago.

I also doubted anyone would form a line.

“That’s annoying.” Tanja looked surprised, but also… frustrated? “It looks like we will be a lot busier if this actually goes through. The prices for Wild beasts will rise drastically once this Foundation hits the Nexus stream.”

The former didn’t really matter to anyone but me. I had myself assigned as the Foundation’s counselor. Everyone who made it through the first screening would have to meet me. Twice, actually. Once to get to know them better, and a second time to jumpstart their life as Blessed. I would scan their Worlds and come up with the most efficient ways to adjust their lives to accommodate a new member in their family and household.

Anyway, the matter of the prices was something I hadn’t accounted for, and I honestly didn’t care all that much about it. The Beast Temple was currently overflowing with Wild beasts. It should have been a crime to set the price for Wild beasts this high when there were thousands of them kept in the Sanctuary and hundreds more in underground habitats. Luckily for the Beast Temple, they took great care of their beasts, which made it a little less disgusting.

Nonetheless, I felt a lot better knowing that my Foundation would not only help the unBlessed turn their lives around, but also beasts that had been born and raised in the underground habitats. They would live with a family, have someone to take care of them all day, and enjoy love and proper sunlight for the rest of their lives.

That sounded like a perfect solution in my head. Everyone would benefit, even the Beast Temple, which was probably the only annoying part. They’d earn a lot from my Foundation and have a justification to further increase the prices for all beasts. After all, the demand would increase while the supply decreased.

As far as I could tell, the supply would continue to decrease until the war ended, or until their Bastion removed all threats and resumed their hunt for suitable Soulkins. One way or another, I’d probably have to sell a few more of Kazriel’s more valued armaments to keep the funds rolling.
