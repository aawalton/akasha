That night I shivered and tossed. No matter how much wood I piled onto the fire, or how close I crawled to it, warmth could not find me. I slept fitfully, unable to find comfort on the stone floor, sweat beading my brow as I dreamt of distorted faces sucking mana from my veins with screaming mouths. When I woke, I could see them still, until I blinked and they faded with the dawn. I had never been more grateful to see the rising sun.

Sandy eyed and grouchy, I set water to boil over the fire in my very-useful-and-so-far-trustworthy bucket. One of the wounds on my arm was troubling me, twinging whenever I moved, with streaking lines of angry red radiating out from the centre. Some foul-smelling puss oozed out of a cut on my hand, despite my best efforts to keep it clean.

“That’s okay,” I said to the air, as I scrubbed it out as hard as I could with my squishy soap. “I’ll just pop to the doctor and see if I need anti-biotics. There’s no way I will die of sepsis. Absolutely no way. Everything is fine.”  The horrible soap stung, but I figured that meant it was helping.

Clean but depressed I went up to the roof to work on my [Mana Perception] but instead sat and did nothing for a while. In truth, I was too tired to do anything else. A few hours went by, and at last, I stirred myself, inspired by my stony friends patience, and worked on my meditation.

Apparently exhaustion had a calming effect on my normally overexcited brain, and I was rewarded with another level, bringing with it the ability to feel mana consistently like a physical presence instead of just seeing it. The sensation was interesting - like gossamer spider feet trickling across my skin, or puffs of damp air  - still nebulous but clearly felt.

At Level 6 I realised mana had a faint smell. It changed according to reasons I could not yet fathom, but each scent was deeply evocative, reminding me of childhood memories, favourite foods, or sometimes places that stirred my emotions. Eggs said, perhaps I was remembering things I had forgotten. Cryptic, but I was learning patience.

Level 7 took longer - several more sweaty uncomfortable days - but when it did come, I got a pleasant surprise: while in my meditative state, I could see mana everywhere. Veins of the stuff ran through my body. I could see it in gusts of wind, in the grass, in animals and fish in the stream, in rocks and the earth. In the gargoyles. I could see it threaded through the water, and drifting through the sky, especially when the moons shone bright. It connected us all. Eggs called that ambient mana.

The first time I saw it, I lost my concentration immediately, and the soft shimmering lights disappeared as if they had never been, leaving me with mundane, unmagical, disappointing reality. I started my meditation again, only to immediately lose my focus once more at the sight of it. I had to keep my emotions in check, even the positive ones. If I got too lost in wonder, I overloaded my brain and woke up with tears on my cheeks.

Still, a wonder it was. I now understood how the gargoyles could sit for such long hours simply gazing out at the world. Everything else dimmed into insignificance, and from then on mediation was no longer a chore but something I looked forward to, no matter how sore I was. It also eased my sleep a little, but not my aches or the fevers that persistently hounded me at night.

[Mana Perception] was like having a third eye, closed all my life previously and now, suddenly, miraculously, open (at least while in my meditative state). One day Eggs assured me I would be able to see it all the time, if I so chose.

“Can you do that now?” I asked.

“Not yet,” said Eggs. “Soon.” He had heard about it from another higher levelled, older gargoyle, back at the tower. One pitted and pockmarked with age, so old the carvings of her face were wearing smooth. He said to hurry up so I could feed him mana infused eggs, which he was sure would make both of use very happy.

Alas, my infusion attempts remained explosive, but I kept trying, sure perception was the key.

At level 8, I could suddenly see the product of Wistful and Eggs’ own meditations; not just the mana in them, but the mana they were manipulating. Mana wreathed and curled around them as they meditated, the ever-present current flows around their still forms reminded me of water breaking against rock. Egg’s control was inspirational. At times his mana would branch off and rotate into a slow orb in front of him.

“Is that what you have been doing the whole time?” I asked, a little humbled by the display.

He did not bother to reply but kept his concentration on the small ball of rotating energy. Then he grinned and it flew towards me.

“Catch.”

I panicked, reaching out physically with both my hands.

The globe of mana hit them, distorted, then dissipated into nothing, leaving behind only a cool prickling sensation on my palms.

“Soonff,” Eggs said, and returned to his meditations with a shrug.

I was learning, and I was levelling, which was good because I had little energy for anything else. I kept an eye on my wounds and kept myself as clean as I could, which was all I felt I could do with the mad unicorn stomping around outside. (Incidentally the [Night Mare] was threaded through with angry red veins of mana that I had not seen in anything else.)

Watching the gargoyles handle mana gave me an insight along with level 9. That mana behaved a bit like water behaved in space – slow globules splitting off and floating before drifting away on currents I could not yet perceive. With a lot of mental effort, I could buoy them upwards with my fingers. The mana would rise majestically – like bubbles, or floating seedpods. It didn’t actually touch my hand, but the gestures helped me focus.

“Cheating,” said Eggs, grumpily.

Wistful returned that being warm-blooded and impatient had to have some small advantages.

Suddenly, I was no longer fighting mana but working with it. We were influencing each other like the sun and the moon, and I was the sun. Moons, rather, I corrected myself, tearing my eyes away to gaze upwards at the latest addition to the night sky: a new moon, decidedly green, which had joined its fellows the last night or so. This brought the total of moons on this planet to five.

Five moons was a lot of moons, and a lot more light than I was used to at night. It was more like a gloamy twilight than true night, one that constantly shifted as the celestial bodies fought for supremacy in the sky, as each one rose and set. I wondered briefly if the system had sold me [Umbral Cloak] as a lemon but then went back to meditating.

Level 10 I was suddenly aware of mana even when I couldn’t see it, so long as it was close to my body. If level 1 was a third eye opening, level 10 was a sense that required no vision. The scent, the taste, weight, the feel, all of it coalesced into a sensation I had no words for. A shadow passing in from of the sun, someone walking on your grave. Premonition. An instinct. Perhaps all those feelings we had no words for on Earth were merely magic we didn’t understand. Or I really needed antibiotics.

After watching Eggs and Wistful some more, I tried playing with mana outside of infusions. With some effort, and my improved perception, I was able to pass strains of mana around my body without moving, circling myself with wisps of energy.

[Mana Manipulation has reached level 3!]

[Mana Manipulation has reached level 4!]

Clumsily, compared to my stony friends, but I was very pleased, nonetheless.

[Mana Manipulation has reached level 5!

Congratulations! Your ability grows. From this level onwards you gain the ability to ‘downcast’ a spell, effectively the ability to use less mana than the full amount, if you so choose.]

Oh, now that was useful! While it was nice that my spells grew more powerful with each level it seemed silly to have to swat a bug with a veritable truck, not to mention the increased risk of running low on mana. The control of downcasting would improve things immensely.

I tried it out right away, downcasting various different power levels of [Curses] against some unoffensive shrubbery, shrivelling a daisy, a mushroom and then a small tree.

Now I felt like I was really getting somewhere. Still no magic soap though.

Swimming in inspiration I was ready to try infusion again, with my test subject devil’s flight. My epiphany: I would try to infuse a [Ward]. Mundane plant matter seemed too weak to contain my mana, therefore I needed to make it more robust, fortifying it with the essence of magic itself. The trick would be to combine the herb’s natural mana, and my own, without destroying it in the process.

I was now intimately familiar with each perforation of leaf and vein, the mana signature of devil’s engraved on my mind along, with the physical scent and texture. Instead of brute forcing mana into the fragile vein stricture, I would soak the leaf skeleton in a bath of it, formed with utmost concentration. Soaking it, letting the devil’s flight rest within it.

A drop of sweat dripped from my forehead, but I kept going. Too much mana and it escaped into the ether, risking a mana drain, too little and it didn’t absorb. I did not know how long it took but after a good while, the natural mana of the plant (spring green spice threaded with liquorice black) and my own mana (electric blue, the same colour as the system notifications) started to merge.

Slowly, as if the mana was water and the herb a porous sponge. Slowly, slowly enough seeped in until the leaves felt… heavier, and they glowed as brightly as they had before it was cut, brighter, brighter, each precious vein lit up like city lights. The colour was different too, the natural green tinged with my blue.

“Sweet,” I whispered, and wiped my damp forehead with one sweaty palm.

Out of my meditative state the spray of devil’s flight no longer glowed but I was sure I had done it.

[Congratulations! Mana Manipulation has reached lvl 6! You have successfully infused something without exploding it!]

Yusssssss!

I [inspected] the herb in my hand:

[Mana infused Devil’s Flight, healing, warding]

Okay, no way to know how much mana was in it, presumably not until I had levelled [inspect] some more?  Still, it was clearly infused. I checked my mana 5/35! Had I used a whopping 30 points of mana to infuse this little spray of foliage? I eyed it suspiciously. 30 seemed a great deal, more than quadruple the cost of any spell I could currently cast. I was tired, and it had certainly taken a long time judging by the ache in my limbs and the position of the moons. I wondered, briefly, how long a natural [Witch] would take to reach this level? Thanks to my large mana pool I had a stupendous advantage, able to blunt force it rather than refining my control.

Ah well, dying had to come with some advantages.

Carefully, I set the spray of mana infused herbs onto the outer ring of the [Warding Circle]. On top of the normal devil’s flight. The plants were still there, most of them wilted now, since it had been several days since I had laid them out. I cast [Ward.]

Where the mana infused herbs touched, a ripple of green fire raced out, around the circle, gleaming even to my mundane sight.

[Ward has reached lvl 3.]

[Devil’s Flight Ward Established. Lvl 3. Quality: Stable. Power level: Moderate.]

I grabbed a ball of soap and stepped into the circle.
