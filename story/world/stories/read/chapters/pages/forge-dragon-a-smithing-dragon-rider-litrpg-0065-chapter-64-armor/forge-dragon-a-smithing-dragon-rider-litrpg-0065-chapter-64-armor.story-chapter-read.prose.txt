Caleb spent a whole week at home before his small vacation came to an end. In that time he began work on his full suit of armor. He wrestled with the design for most of that time.

He was fairly certain making a ring mail suit with his new abilities in record speed as the tiny rings cold be directly twisted into shape using his Imperium. The low power high finesse work would be ideal for his new skills. Ring mail did have downsides. If he wanted an aspect to manifest, he’d need to purge the mana from every single ring. Normally he did that as he forged the metal into shape. He could do it on the plate he would eventually cut the rings from, but after cutting he’d still need to hammer out some of the mana.

A potential upside to the ring mail was the ability to mix enchantments. He didn’t think he could fit even a single plus one strength onto a ring, but if he got good enough to do so we could create chainmail with a variety of effects. Mettius’ gauntles and Cassandra’s armor had examples of discontinuous enchantments that spread across multiple plates, and he was certain he could copy that method, but doing so across dozens of rings was beyond him.

Next he considered scale mail. The small scales, much like the rings, would be easy for him to forge. The legion had a lorica squamata he studied in one of his trips to the quarter master. The armor was made by riveting metal scales to a leather or cloth backing material. The overlapping scales served better against arrows than ring mail would. The scales themselves were larger than the rings and it was far more likely he’d be able to fit enchantments into each. One large downside was that the armor required a leather or fabric backer, and he was hesitant to use anything but metal in his armor.

Then there were the plate options. The legion supplied loricas to their soldiers along with a helmet. They did not have any standard issue suits of armor. Caleb was only vaguely aware of the concept and had no examples to go off of. Like the lorica, he would be forced to use many plates to cover himself, each plate would be large enough for an enchantment and there would be enough across his whole body that he could greatly diversify his options.

The problem as he saw it was in maneuverability. He could make himself an immobile and impenetrable tank to sit in, but that would not serve him well if he wanted to move.

Briefly his thoughts wandered down the path of some sort of steel construct but he put those aside before he got carried away.

Maybe someday…

He thought over the problem, and began to build and experiment with joints. At first he made the joints out of ring mail with scales linked in, but he moved away from that quickly. If he was going to cover himself with plate, then he was going to find a way to truly cover himself with it.

For the full week he worked at the problem, trying different means of creating hinges in the metal that would allow movement while still covering him until he finally settled on a working solution. If he copied the standard lorica’s design of overlapping plates and the rivets from the lorica squamata using slots instead of round holes, he could make overlapping plates that could slide over one another.

With a solution at hand, Caleb now had to consider what materials to use. Meta steel would serve him best in battle against other people wielding steel weapons, but he had his skills for that. The real danger he faced lay in elemental attacks. Cold aspected steel had the useful trait of both system recognized   cold resistance when purified of its ambient mana as well as the natural property of taking large amounts of heat to get to a forging temperature.  Nothing compared to his quenching steel, but it could mean the difference between life and death—plus he still had the quenching steel he could pull out in an emergency.

Together this armor would give him a strong resistance to cold and a minor one to heat. What he really needed was heat steel, or what was more colloquially called lava steel, but they were far from the fire volcanos of Vestras. Alternating plates would give him excellent protection against both—so long as there weren’t any adverse reactions between the two metals. He’d need to research that more.

Caleb thought the idea of volcanoes of fire absurd, but he’d heard enough reports to know them not to be fanciful tales.

For now, since it was a good choice and the most abundant, he decided to begin with cold steel. Earth still was also available, but he saw little use for it, unless he were to go up against the Tellus and their earth affinity magic.

Caleb asked Mettius to get him what he needed, and what he needed was steel, lots of steel. Caleb was uncertain how much he’d been paid thus far in his brief military career, but he’d been told he received a bonus for each enemy hero he killed. While he hadn’t officially killed Cassandra, Mettius had offered the Caleb the reward in an effort to continue to foster their equitable relationship.

At first Caleb had wanted to refuse, but when he began to think of the price of crafting a full plate of armor, he’d relented. He’d asked Mettius to find source majority cold steel, with some earth for the sabatons—the armored boots he’d wear instead of proper footwear. He’d weighed the options of these versus spats to cover his boots with metal, but if he was going to go all out, he should really go all out.

He lost track of time as he worked, Bog his constant companion, stoking the forge for him and willingly eating any scrap Caleb threw his way. Kelia visited regularly to ensure he didn’t starve to death in his frenzy, and she also rotated out his quenching steel to ensure he always had a charged supply.

Quench hardening armor was a trickier process than a blade. Armor had to be carefully forged with bends to fit each interlocking piece and a warp could cause a whole piece to be reforged. Luckily, Caleb’s skills worked as well with armor as they did with blades. He was not restricted as mundane smiths were to the relatively slow quenching of oil and was quenching his pieces in the icy baths of quenching steel.

As he worked, his skills increased. Not all his pieces were crafted perfectly the first try, but no smith was perfect, even Caleb with all his magical perception and control. Each piece was forged, purified of its ambient mana, and choice parts were enchanted to grant all of the attributes so he’d have the ability to change out his stats on the fly. He could have continued to rely on ingots, but with so much steel he could afford to lower the aspect of a few pieces for the guarantee he’d not loose the tiny bits of steel.

For the helmet, he crafted a pieces that contoured to the base of his neck with a full face visor that pivoted down to cover him completely. He gave himself a wide narrow band to see through and small holes to allow air flow. The helmet sealed shut with metal pins he would press into place with his Imperium. Without the air of his magic it would be impossible to remove the helmet without taking his head with it.

For the chest, he copied the legions lorica design, not because he thought it best, but because he anticipated he’d be improving his metal over time. With the segmented plates he could replace sections as he acquired more magical metals. Once the whole armor was of a desired quality, he could reforge it into something more impenetrable.

He forged tapered cylinders for the armors and legs, joining them together with segmented plates joined with the slotted rivets he’d devised. The joints would be a weak spot, but that would always be the case and his mechanism would be far greater protection than the ring mail typical to covering joints.

The sabatons were the tricky part. He needed something that would bend with his foot. In the end, his device for the joints proved useful once more and he made the armored footwear out of overlapping pivoting rings of steel. The sabatons were forged from the earth aspected steel he’d acquired, and he’s driven rivets through the bottom of the plates so grant him greater traction.

The gauntlets once more used the overlapping cylinders, but he forged a protective cover for the back of his knuckles should he need to punch. The gauntleted fingers were crafted of meta steel. In working with various steels, he’d learned his affinity sense worked best when traveling through the steel aspected closest to match it. With the meta steel fingers he’d have better clarify in sensing flaws in his foes armor as his senses went through his weapon.

When he was done, he looked out at the plates of metal all laid out neatly before him. He extended that extra sense the system gave to link one to items and willed the connection. Suddenly he was overwhelmed with dozens of messages with the aspect of enchantment of each piece of metal.

He looked at one before dismissing them all.

Expert Cold Steel Armor Piece

Type: Armor Piece

Material: Steel

Aspect: Cold (Greater)

Effects: Improved resistance to cold and cold based attacks.

Looking over the armor, he thought back to the time he got his status screen to only show an aspect of itself displaying his mana. He put himself into that same mindset and pictured all the disparate pieces before him as part of a greater whole. It was easy for him, for in his mind he saw how each piece fit together and as far as he was concerned it was a single set of armor, not dozens of disassociated fragments as the system seemed to claim.

Then something inside of him clicked, and he felt the subtle expanded awareness of another increase to his Magical Perception.

Plate of the Forge Dragon Rider

Quality: Expert

Rarity: Rare

Type: Armor Set

Aspects: Cold (Moderate), Earth (Minor), Steel (Minor)

Effects: Improved defense and resistance to cold, earth, and steel based attacks. You may allocate your equipment stat bonuses across all 6 stats until your equipment stat boost limit has been reached.

A mixed flood of emotions filled Caleb as he examined the screen. After the initial elation at his success he felt confused by the name of the armor.

Forge Dragon Rider?

He looked at Bog. He’d wondered what type of dragon he was and had had no luck thus far identifying him, but he had to admit the name fit. But, was it a name the system had just revealed to him due to his elevated access, or a name plucked from Caleb’s subconscious?

Or maybe that’s what Bog calls himself?

Aside from the details of the name, the fact that the armor set had a name to begin with was another layer of surprise. He’d made many items so far and none had been given names.

Next, if his emotions could be said to have an order to them, was interest as the format for his item status had changed slightly.

Next came disappointment. His aspects were far lower than he’d anticipated, but on a hunch he probed the rankings of the aspects with the same part of his mind he used to examine the errors in the previous status menus. He found on doing so that the ratings here were an average. Each plate had a greater aspect to it, unless he had also placed an stat increasing enchantment on it, in which case he’d only reached moderate. Despite no metal having more than one aspect to it before he’d begun to forge his armor, he sensed that the cold resistance extended to some degree over even the parts of the suit that were steel and earth aspected. Similarly, the whole set had a small amount of metal and earth aspect.

The armor is conducting the aspects of the rest of the set. He realized.

New Trait Unlocked: Metallic Synergy
