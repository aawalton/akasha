The two elves glided out of the portal and through the room at an unhurried pace, already in mid-conversation. They were alike enough to look like brothers, with white skin and hair and pale blue eyes, though Thomas thought that might be his human bias speaking.

One was slightly taller and dressed in pale blue and white ceremonial robes. The second was shorter, in more traditional armor, and seemed to be the warrior of the two. He looked distinctly unimpressed as he said, "...third alarm in this dungeon. You'd think after the lesson we gave after the last, these incompetents would check instead of wasting our time."

The taller one said something that Thomas couldn't quite catch. Whatever he said made the shorter one chuckle darkly.

Thomas's stomach clenched in anxiety, and he subtly shuffled out of the way as quietly as he could to let the elves sweep on by. Thank goodness for Jo's enhanced Gift of Hiding, because something about these two set every hair on end. They acted casual but had absolute confidence, as if there was zero question they owned every person, place, and thing that they set their eyes on. Or like it was owed to them.

He felt with a vague sense that came from his core that these two were higher leveled than him. "Through the first gate," as the Dark Elves would have said, meaning people who hit level 5 and got their first Gift evolution. They had classes and a host of skills, and he did not want to tangle with them.

Despite quickly making way, the pair of elves passed by him so close that he could have reached out and slapped one. Thomas held his breath, wishing he could have at least exchanged glances with his teammates. They'd have to figure out what to do with these two once they were out of range. Maybe set up an ambush or something? They could—

Twitch broke stealth without warning. One second there was empty space, and the next Jo's Gift had fallen away from him and he was lunging straight for the shorter one with arcs of electricity snapping off his growing shaft spear.

Zach apparently had the same idea at the same time because orange-black flame bloomed out of what had looked like an empty space a moment ago, heading for that same elf. Maybe he wanted to catch his robes on fire; Thomas didn't know.

Unfortunately, the attack had been uncoordinated, and Twitch had to stop mid-lunge, half-spinning away to avoid Zach's blast.

The evil-looking fire blasted right into the robed elf.

At the same moment, Jo must have dropped the Gift on herself as she became visible while stabbing her sword right at the armored elf's neck.

That elf had a slight heads-up thanks to the fire and managed to parry the weapon with the armored back of his hand. Jo, her expression completely neutral as she was using her Still Waters skill, grabbed her dagger with her other hand and closed in.

Thomas couldn't actively help in this ambush, as it was nowhere near self-defense, but it didn't mean he was useless. There were moving shapes visible in the searing brightness beyond the portal, and if he could see out, then they could see in. He had to shut the portal down before backup came.

Thomas darted to the control panel, and as he did, he felt Jo's Gift slide off him as if he had stepped out from under a blanket. That explained why Twitch and Zach had become visible as they had moved to attack. Her Gift of Hiding was enhanced right now, but it still had limits.

Luckily, the panel was only a few feet away. Thomas located the X button that canceled the last portal out and smashed it.

The image inside the ring immediately turned blue, a little like the blue screen of death. Hopefully, it would stay that way.

Thomas glanced back to his team to see the fight going... uh... not well.

The robed elf, who, despite taking one of Zach's best fire blasts practically to the face, didn't have so much as a scorch mark on his pretty robes. As Thomas watched, he delivered a backhand that sent Twitch staggering to one side.

The other elf was practically looking at his fingernails while parrying Jo's aggressive attacks.

Let's see you block a shield bash, Thomas thought, and started to move around the control panel to help her.

He got two whole steps before it was as if Thomas's own personal gravity decided to increase tenfold.

He couldn't explain it, didn't really have a previous reference for it, but it was as if he were being shoved down by an immense weight on his shoulders, back, and everything while also being squeezed at the same time.

His excess breath rushed out of him, and he staggered, dropping his shield that was suddenly way too heavy. His ears—his brain—were filled with the sound of static so loud he could barely think, and yet he could still hear and see just fine. His Health Sight, which he usually had running on a low level to burn off healing mana and to not be taken completely unawares, suddenly just blinked out. He was only left with his own, and Thomas felt blinded without it.

That was how he realized he was not the only one affected. His entire team was dropping down to hands and knees. Poor Antonin, who had taken himself to the corner and was now entirely visible, was flat on his stomach with his hands over his head, groaning in pain.

To the side, Jo made a winded gasp as she fell, then Zach and Twitch fully collapsed.

The robed elf watched them all with interest, his hands arrogantly clasped behind his back. Somehow, though Thomas didn't quite know how, he knew that he was the one who was doing this.

Also, he remained standing. Barely. He had a lot of practice wrestling with his own mana, and that helped him cling on. Zach did too, but Thomas also had new physical strength. His Wellspring mana was now trickling down to his burning calves and thighs just to keep him upright.

The robed elf cocked his head at him. The pressure increased, and it was like the air itself became semi-solid in his throat, and Thomas couldn't take it anymore. He dropped to his knees.

The moment he hit the ground, though, the smug look fell off the elf's face. He gasped, staggering to the side with one hand covering his core. It was more of an impact than anyone else had been able to make.

The shorter, armored elf pointed to Thomas and said, "That one is a healer."

In an instant, the invisible weight lifted and Thomas started to stand. He was "helped" by the armored elf who hauled him to his feet like he weighed no more than a kitten. So much for his own strength.

His mind spun. What the hell was going on?

A snippet from one of his conversations with Akilah flashed through his mind: The Burden got stronger as people leveled. Maybe the backlash from hurting a healer did too?

If that was the case, he was absolutely taking advantage.

Thomas turned and landed a solid punch right on the elf's jaw, throwing every bit of his new strength into it. Too bad he didn't have his brass knuckles, too.

The elf's head snapped back, but in the next second Thomas found himself spun around with his arm yanked up behind his back in a lock that threatened to dislocate his shoulder, and ow, damn it, that hurt.

"Are you certain this one is a healer?" asked the robed elf, looking amused. "This team does seem to be the cause of the ruckus today."

"Fuck you, let me go," Thomas snapped.

He was facing his team, who were all still pressed to the ground with that invisible weight. They were breathing, though Jo was gasping, and Twitch's arms trembled as he continued to try and fail to lever himself up. Zach just stared at the elves with smoldering fire in his eyes. He looked like he was trying to teleport fire into them, but was suppressed too. Honestly, good luck because Thomas was out of ideas.

The armored elf who still held Thomas must have seen the direction of his gaze because he said, "You see it, don't you? Raveth has famously precise aura control. It is only one of the ways the strong impress their will upon the weak."

"It is quite easy for me to grind beings this low-leveled into paste," the robed elf, Raveth, added calmly. "Would it destroy you to see that?"

Maybe it would take some healers, but Thomas's soul was made for something entirely different. He still made himself relax, playing as if the fight had gone out of him. The armored elf responded in kind, not ratcheting his wrist so high on his back, though he continued to hold Thomas in place as a warning.

"Sir!" a voice called out. "Sir, have mercy! Please!"

For a second, Thomas thought Antonin was the one calling out because it was as if it were coming directly from him, and yet... it didn't sound like him at all.

The elf pair seemed a little taken aback as well. They stared at the prone man. Then the armored one snorted. "Hiding like a coward, I see. Why am I not surprised? Reduce the aura suppression on that one."

Raveth must have done so because a moment later the hint of a shadow under Antonin wiggled, then grew to the side and solidified and Frederic rolled out.

He looked like crap warmed over, still bleeding from the grievous wound taken to his chest, with more blood and what looked like Corruption mana spilling from his mouth, indicating some serious internal bleeding. Thomas didn't know how he was still alive. It must have been a skill or his Gift... and the other one was apparently to hide in people's shadows.

Or was it possession? Because the second Frederic had rolled free, Antonin let out a long wail and then put his forehead to the floor and started to shake with sobs. Poor guy.

Frederic staggered to his feet. "Thank you for coming. We were under attack by this group. I called for you following the terms of the compact, but feared you would not arrive in time--"

"It should not be our job to put down vermin on your behalf," the armored elf snapped, and hiked up Thomas' wrist over his back another degree, making him wince. It might have lashed back at the elf because Thomas heard him take a sharp breath before the grip loosened back down.

"Rest assured, we will take payment for our services in the next shipment," Raveth added. "Nor will we allow you healing. This one," he gestured at Thomas, "is claimed."

"Not like I would anyway," Thomas muttered.

Raveth ignored him. "Now, get out of our way while we deal with your mess."

Frederic nodded rapidly, and quickly took himself off to stand in a corner, shoulders hunched in a servile attitude.

"You can't just march in here from Aether and... and interfere like this," Thomas said, and felt a little pride that his voice came out calm. "The System forbids you messing around on Earth before the integration countdown. It's why you've been using the de'Montvallon family as your patsies."

The armored elf huffed a quiet laugh, and more infuriatingly, Raveth just smiled fondly at Thomas like he was a toddler who had just said something precocious.

"That's why we would take you back through the portal to Aether, first. Perhaps make a sport out of it. Though not all of your team need to die. My, is that little Preston I see over there? You have grown." Raveth strode over to Zach and with one easy kick, he flipped Zach onto his back, so that the invisible weight of aura pressed straight down on his chest. It didn't help when the elf added a softly slippered foot on his ribs and pressed down. Zach wheezed.

"Your father is upset with you," the elf said to him. "Perhaps enough to demote you to branch family status, hmm? You do remember what that means. Forced Corruption mana. I hear it's excruciating if you didn't start with it." He tsked under his breath as if saddened for Zach's fate.

Zach just glared up at him. If there was any fear, he didn't show it.

Thomas had to get their attention off him. He looked wildly around for something, anything, and thought he caught a blink of movement by the door. The aura wasn't pressing down on him, so he tried his Health Sight and saw figures standing just out of view past the hallway. It looked like Tiny and his crew and... Courtney was with them?

Crap, Thomas did not want to eat crow and ask for their help, but it wasn't like the group was rushing to the rescue. No, they were listening in and probably trying to figure out which way the wind was blowing. For now they were on their own and Thomas had to get the elves' attention off his team, and stop them from thinking if anyone else might be lying in wait for them.

"That's not what the Dark elves think," he blurted. "They were worried the System would send a champion after them if they interfered. What, you think you're too good for the System now?"

That got a reaction. He saw Raveth lift his foot from Zach's chest just before the other elf let go of his arm and spun him around to practically snarl in his face.

"What do you know of the Champion, worm?"

"Laveris," Raveth said in a chiding voice. "Be gentle. He is young."

Thomas held up his empty hands in a peaceable gesture. His right wrist, the one Laveris had been clamped onto, had a ring of red forming bruises, which were already fading. "Just that the person chosen is like the T-1000: They don't stop until you're dead," he added, seeing the elf's confusion because apparently they weren't yet acquainted with the greatest of James Cameron's movies, "and if they die, the System just chooses another one again and again until one succeeds."

The armored elf, Laveris, didn't look amused. In fact, he looked downright pissed that Thomas had brought up the idea of the System Champion at all. He had apparently stomped on a nerve.

"You should disabuse yourself of the notion the System wishes to protect you."

"Or that the Dark Elves make for reliable allies," Raveth added, way too close behind Thomas. He'd practically hissed it in his ear.

He half spun around, not wanting either of these higher-leveled elves at his back. Frederic snickered wetly from his corner.

Raveth's lips curved in a small smile as if he read Thomas' discomfort and it amused him. "So, this is the team the Dark Elves have chosen, hmm? That explains why you're here making mischief in our territory. Tell me, are you also the healer who allegedly saved a level fifteen Wood elf whore, while only being a level three at the time?"

"Uh..." Prinna was a what-now?

"That's impossible," Laveris said. "A beginning healer cannot impact a person three gates over them. Not even a Wood Elf."

"Indeed. The Light Ones are said to be intrigued. I dare think he is every bit as valuable as the de'Montvallon scion."

Maybe he shouldn't have brought their attention back to him after all. He didn't like where this was going, and especially didn't like that Tiny, his lackeys, and Courtney were probably hearing every word. At least no one had an inkling of his true secret, but... Christ.

Fuck it. He wasn't going to cower to these guys like Frederic, and the longer they were kept talking, the more time his team had to fight the aura suppression. Meanwhile, Tiny might dig deep, find his balls, and decide to act against enemies of humanity. Maybe Thomas could learn something, too. He'd already learned the Light Ones wanted him. They'd better get in line behind the Brightens.

"Yeah, I've been to your world and healed a few people," Thomas said, giving Raveth a look that said he wasn't impressed with what he'd seen over there. "So, what?"

That only seemed to amuse Raveth further. "What have your Dark allies done for your world? Hm? No doubt they've certainly been busy plotting for a piece of it." He exchanged a small smirk with Laveris. "Just like we all."

"They're planning a colony," he said, sticking to the dry facts. "But you knew that."

"Sir," Raveth corrected. "They're planning a colony, sir."

Thomas had to bite back an instinctive, "No need to call me sir."

He couldn't bring himself to actually call Raveth or Laveris "sir" either, not even to play the part they wanted and expected of him. Instead, he just looked down.

To his surprise, Raveth dropped it. He hummed again and said, "You will need to learn your manners when you serve the Light Ones." He paused. "Do you understand what Integration means? Truly?"

That was a loaded question if Thomas had ever heard one, but he couldn't quite figure out how. He also guessed that answering, "The countdown in the sky" wasn't what they were aiming for, and they might take out any sarcasm on the hide of his teammates. "Magic on Earth?"

Raveth laughed softly and looked at him with such pity in his eyes that Thomas bristled. Elves had a real bad habit of treating people who could handle healing mana like children, and he was getting sick of it.

"That is but one small aspect, though I'm given to understand your kind thought magic were fables and tales before this." He aimed another smug, pitying look Thomas's way. "No, Integration itself is simply erasure. Your planet is slated to be folded into the greater System. But of course, you have little concept of what that means, so start with this: The chances are great your children will not speak whatever tongue you speak now. The System will translate speech into whatever is most prominent on a given world at the time of integration. Everything else will wither from disuse within a generation or two."

As far as Thomas remembered, English was the most prominently spoken language on the planet. Or... wait, was it Mandarin? Would his children and his nephews' children grow up speaking Mandarin Chinese, with the older generations barely knowing unless they really paid attention, thanks to the smoothness of the System's translation? That was a weird thought.

He glanced at Frederic to get his take, but the man only leered back at him. No help there. He apparently knew what this was about and was a'okay with it.

Raveth continued. "Whatever gods you worship will follow soon after as they become irrelevant. And your culture... well, you are surely aware of the great reshuffling of the Ungifted that will occur at the conclusion of the countdown. There will be no nations, no towns, no communities as you know them because most will be torn apart wholesale. All that will be left will be the System and its general rules. Within a few decades, the events of System pre-Integration will be a curious footnote for those who have a hobby of history to read up on. Nothing more."

Thomas would never call himself religious or particularly culturally involved other than the usual small swell of patriotism on Independence Day, where massive fireworks went off over the lake and good beer and good barbecue were to be had on a typical July summer day. Thinking of that gone, of family traditions small and large also gone, all unsettled him. Other holidays, forgotten as silly curiosities of pre-magic societies.

He'd known the government was screwed. He'd just never let himself follow that rabbit hole see it would swallow his entire culture, down to the language and traditions he grew up with and was only half aware of because it was part of him, being obliterated too.

Yeah, sure, part of that was also just the passage of time. Countries and empires rose and fell, and the world spun on. It was just another thing to be present for the end.

It felt like a deep hole was trying to open under his feet. Thomas focused on one fact to keep from falling in: The System, for all its threatened changes, had given him power, wealth, and his team. Those things had let him keep his nephews and himself safe when assholes like this had tried to barge in and enslave him. It was giving him the opportunity right now, through the quest, to set things right. He just had to figure out how to do it.

Raveth and Laveris thought he was a healer, but his Gift was loot, and someway, somehow he'd see these two elfin flunkies' power turn into mana and skill shards he could use or sell.

"Why are you telling me this?" Thomas asked.

Raveth smiled down at him like a father might a child. "Because you will surely be serving in the Light Ones' personal House of Healing, and you must understand how pointless it is to resist. There will be nothing for you to return to."

"Our planet faced a similar integration several elven generations back," Laveris added in a silky voice. "Out of the four species, Light elves were the only ones who retained a semblance of our culture and way of life, and we forced the others to adapt to our way or else be erased as the System wished it. Our dominance on our planet is absolute." He spread his hands in a that's-that gesture. "And we intend to do the same to your planet. Your Earth is already dying. Your so-called countries are too fractured, and this one is further separated into fifty different states, making it impossible to come together. The choice is now the System that caused it, or the Light which will give them something to cling onto."

Thomas couldn't help it. "I'd like to say I'm surprised to hear you say that, but," he shrugged. "Are you done with the pseudo villain monologue, or what?"

Raveth smirked, then turned and made a press-down motion at Twitch, who was still doing his best to shove up on his elbows. In a second, he was flat on his belly, making a grunting noise that sounded like it would have been a scream if he could get more air. Thomas distinctly heard a pop of either muffled snapping bone or a joint. The sound went through him like a knife.

"Shit, sorry. I'm sorry, okay? I spoke out of turn," he said quickly. "Let him go."

Raveth lifted his hand, and with it much of the visible weight off Twitch. "Observe, Laveris. That is how you discipline a recalcitrant healer. Perhaps we will keep the other two alive as well, until this one is properly trained."

Thomas grit his teeth against saying something that Twitch or Jo would regret. Somehow, Zach had managed to crawl to Jo and they seemed to be helping each other endure the aura pressure somewhat. This would be a really nice time for Tiny or Courtney or anyone else to spring in and save the day, but through his Health Sight, he saw they were holding back.

Laveris chuckled in dark appreciation of Raveth's words and walked over to start punching buttons on the portal. They were going to reopen it and drag them all through.

"In any case," Raveth said, clamping one way-too-strong grip on Thomas' shoulder. "You will learn to serve."

With a flash of magic, the portal opened to a searingly bright landscape.
