After losing consciousness, I dreamt of the Wastelands, with Castle Epiales looming darkly in the near distance.

There was an unsettling and empty cold to them this evening, its epicenter containing something so terrible and dense that I could feel a pulling, an insatiable hunger for something, anything that the chained madness within could devour.

And tomorrow, I was going to do everything I could to destroy the herald of destruction itself.

And then everything would finally be complete, and I could disappear.

Oliver was lying on a squat rock on the other side of the party’s magic circle, his arms behind his head as he gazed at the stars. He turned his head only slightly when he heard my footsteps.

“You’re later than I expected.”

“I only found your note just now.”

He looked skyward once again, his emerald eyes glittering. “What took you so long?”

“Relias wanted to pray.”

“Wanted to? Does that mean he finished?”

“No,” I replied quietly. “He’s still going.” I had stopped at the edge of the magic circle to stare at the faintly luminous line in the sand between us. “What did you want?”

He turned, folding his arms. “Your recent behavior is concerning.”

I waited for more information, an accusation, perhaps even a specific question, but was not rewarded for what little patience I could muster.

I’ve been quieter lately, but it’s only natural to withdraw when you’re planning to leave, right?

Instead, I swallowed my thoughts and pulled out my Captain persona. “I assure you, I intend to fulfill my agreement. I know what needs to be done tomorrow, and I know the consequences if I don’t.”

“That’s not what’s bothering me. It’s what comes after tomorrow. When you arrive in the next world.” He paused, his eyes looking for something else to behold. “What are your goals once you arrive?”

“Why does that matter to you?”

He shrugged. “I’ve gathered that humans do well to have long-term objectives along with the short-term ones. So, humor me. What do you aspire to accomplish when you reach the Isle of the Blessed?”

I hadn’t given it any focused thought, to be honest. However, the other heroes in my head all clamored with their own opinions. Some said it would be dangerous to answer the question. Others told me to answer vaguely. One or two were silent, perhaps pondering their own wishes.

“I would endeavor to do what everyone else does there, I suppose.”

“And if you were to choose your own Purpose? Entirely your own?”

“I…”

The line in the sand shouldn’t be crossed. But the stars above twinkled, saying it might be alright, just for tonight.

“I suppose I would seek out a friend.”

He let out a derisive scoff. “What a novel idea.”

“It’s not novel,” I replied with a shrug. “I’ve done so before, and I believe I would do so again.”

He shook his head before settling back on the rock behind him. “And who among your current companions would you name as such?”

I looked at him for a long moment, my face falling. “Are you not my friend?”

He twitched, as if the idea had caused him physical pain. “I am your contractee.”

“Does our agreement preclude friendship?”

“You would be a fool to think of me that way,” he replied sourly. “A complete and utter fool.”

How insufferable. I think I should like to deflate his bravado just this once. Perhaps he might find a new friend in the future, too.

It would be good for him.

“So the time we spent was entirely in service to your personal agenda, then?” I asked coolly. “All philosophical debates included?”

“Such debates have prepared me for future discussions with the host of humanity, which I would remind you will be voracious in its upcoming assault.”

How delusional.

“I call your attention to the arguments we had about soup the other week. The one in which you refuse to acknowledge you lost?”

He groaned audibly. “Not again!”

Oh, but yes.

“You maintained that requesting extra spices on the side is a dreadful insult to the chef,” I reminded him pointedly.

“And my stance does not change!” he said, sitting up with some heat. “Requesting such additions implies the chef has failed in their duty!”

“The chef’s duty to me, the consumer of said soup! It’s my polite request that allows them to rectify their transgressions!”

“Your palate is imprecise! I had also tasted the soup; it was properly spiced.”

“Yet how would you know? Your experience is quite limited; you don’t even require food to exist! But I must eat, and pay for food to eat, so I simply demand my food to be—”

“The soup was adequate! You were the one being unreasonable.”

That much was true, but I had really, really wanted some pepper in it.

Aleph could have done better, blindfolded.

“So, tell me,” I drawled, finding myself grinning at my anticipated victory. “Who is going to plan to defeat the next Demon King through a volatile debate over the proper soup seasonings?”

He paused. “You should go to sleep.”

“What about this?” I asked, looking down at my right hand. “When are you going to take this snake back?” At the mere mention of her, she appeared coiled loosely around my wrist, glowing faintly.

Oliver looked at her for a moment, then deliberately turned his back. “Not now. We may need to talk again before everything is done. When the time is right.”

It was a surprise to get a single answer from him, let alone two or three.

But any further questioning would have truly crossed the line.

“Goodnight, Oliver,” I said sincerely, and turned to go.

“…Goodbye, Miss Rachel,” he eventually replied.

I didn’t look back. But I did stop walking for a moment so I could strengthen my resolve for what lay ahead.

Then, I went to sleep, resolved of all regrets.
