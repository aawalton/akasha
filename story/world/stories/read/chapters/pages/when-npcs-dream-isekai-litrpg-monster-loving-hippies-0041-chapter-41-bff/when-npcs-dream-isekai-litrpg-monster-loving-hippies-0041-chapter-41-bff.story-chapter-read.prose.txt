We decided to go talk to the Baron.

We weren’t going to suggest some clever plan like last time—just warn him about the army, and maybe, through some talking, get a sense of what was about to happen, probably not hours from now.

We truly had no idea.

“The Stormcrows’ representatives request an audience with the Baron,” Juliette informed the guard.

It felt silly, since the Baron was literally just sitting alone in the main hall like he always was. But it was a formality. And from past experience, formality made him less pissy, so we played along.

The guard exchanged a few words with the Baron and then returned to us.

“The Baron will see you now.”

Me, Juliette, and Hyun-woo approached and gave a small bow, waiting for him to address us.

“Leader of the Stormcrows band,” the Baron began, “why have you sought my audience?”

“Our scouts have discovered a large Lithuron army approaching,” Juliette said clearly. “A force of at least a hundred soldiers is expected to arrive within half a day. They’re also accompanied by an adventuring band—a strong one.”

Now we just had to wait and see how he would take the grave news.

The Baron sat in silence for a long time, not the slightest change in his expression.

"May I count on the blades of the Stormcrows?” He finally asked, “Will you fight alongside us, to defend this town with steel and fire?"

“We will not fight this time,” Juliette said simply. “We also believe that you should surrender. There’s no point in bloodshed. Kamatouu is an open town, without walls or fortifications.”

Again, he fell into contemplation.

“Will you act as my messengers to the coming army?” he asked.

Now that was interesting. Yeah, we could do that.

“Sure,” said Juliette. “Should we parley for terms of surrender?”

The baron took a long pause, again!

“Tell them,” he said, “to leave our lands at once—or face the wrath of Kamatouu. For we shall show no mercy to invaders.”

What…?

“We’ll pass on your message,” Juliette said cautiously. “And… if they insist on attacking in full force?”

“We are done, leader of the Stormcrows.” He turned his face away and called for his guards. “Bring me Captain Gordon. Let the town prepare its defense.”

“Let’s go,” Juliette sighed. “He won’t talk to us anymore. He gets annoying like that.”

“It wasn’t meaningless,” Hyun-woo added. “The Baron recognized us as messengers. That means he’ll still accept communication. We’ve increased the chances of a peaceful resolution.”

“We’ll see,” Juliette said.

We waited five tense hours. All the while, we watched the town’s guards get ready—about forty of them, trying to form ranks in front of the town. It didn’t look promising. Kamatouu had no walls, and these guards weren’t exactly elite soldiers. The enemy would outnumber and outclass them.

Then we saw them.

Marching out of the forest in perfect rows, banners flying high. They stopped about two miles from the town and formed into tight squares.

It was terrifying. And honestly… kind of cool.

They looked like a real army. And they weren’t on our side.

At that moment, I knew exactly what I needed to say.

“OH MY GOD,” I turned to Juliette, “we MUST get a banner too for our band! That would be so cool!”

“Really?” she shot back. “You think that’s our problem right now?”

“Well, maybe it can wait,” I admitted.

“Let’s go,” Juliette said. “Do we have the white flag?”

“Santiago went to get it from the Baron,” I told her.

“Okay…” She glanced at me and sighed. “It’s going to take us hours to get there with your crippled ass.”

“Language!” I said, offended. Juliette often had a mouth on her, but that was an insult to the entire crippled community!

“Nayeem,” she said, “carry Geshem please.”

“Sure,” he smiled.

Hey!

“We should all go,” Juliette continued. “We want them to take us seriously.”

And so we marched—all the Stormcrows, Duchess and Kim included—with me being carried like a baby in Nayeem’s strong arms. Utter humiliation.

We stopped about a hundred yards from the enemy line, raising the white flag.

Yeah, we were probably in archer range. But we figured we knew the rules well enough. We weren’t at risk.

Then I noticed something else at the back of their lines. Large cage on heavy wagon hitched to draft horses—inside, prisoners. About eight, I thought. They were filthy, clothes torn, and very thin.

I didn’t know what that was about, but it looked bad. Disturbing. Like the army had been cruel to their prisoners.

Moments later, five players broke off from the army and walked toward us in shining armor. I recognized Felix and Hina immediately. It was the Order of the Phoenix.

“Hi Felix,” I called casually. “What’s up?”

He ignored me. His eyes scanned our group coldly.

“Xīnyán,” he said coldly.

A female Asian player stepped forward, walking alone, confidently. Just a few feet from us, she stopped. Her lips moved rapidly, her right hand drawing small, practiced gestures.

“SHIT!” I shouted. “She’s casti—”

Too late.

The spell circle completed in an instant. A tremor went through my whole body.

My muscles locked. My skin felt like a prison. My mind screamed commands, but my limbs refused to obey.

I could blink. I could breathe. I could think. But I couldn’t move a single inch.

Some kind of paralysis spell. Shit.

I looked around. My friends were frozen too.

“Fucking assholes!” I shouted. Oh. So we could still talk.

The mage turned back to Felix. “You’ve got five minutes,” she said. “I won’t do this again today.”

“What the… fuck,” Juliette muttered.

“Cool,” Santiago added.

“Bungflap-ladlefuck-Naked-Nula!” Duchess cursed loudly.

Felix turned to Samar. “Hina. Kill the Scorpion Dog. Then tie the rest of them up.”

Hina drew a gleaming silver sword—razor-sharp. She walked fast toward Samar and Kim.

My mind was racing. I must make sense of the bizarre situation! Now!

“The Scorpion Dog!” I shouted. “You must stop! It’s not what you think!”

“Continue,” Felix said when Hina hesitated. “It’s standard procedure.”

“Stop! No!” we all shouted. But she ignored us.

She was moment away from slicing Kim neatly in half.

I had to act.

Even paralyzed, I could move the tips of my fingers—barely. Enough to draw a crude fake spell circle. It wouldn’t actually do anything, but it looked real. I placed it right between Hina and Samar.

She stopped instantly and jumped back. For all she knew, that bullshit circle could spit a lightning bolt.

Before they could call my bluff, I screamed at the top of my lungs, “FELIX!!”

He turned.

“COULD YOU JUST FUCKING STOP AND TALK TO US FOR A FUCKING MINUTE!? YOU FUCKING SNUB ASSHOLE!”

Okay, maybe the last part was uncalled for—but it caught his attention.

“Fine,” Felix said calmly. “You have one minute.”

My mind reeled. Okay. The key was what he had said earlier: “standard procedure.” They thought we were compromised—controlled by a scorpion suricata. Standard procedure was probably:

The important part was: they didn’t attack us, not really. They probably were trying to help. The paralysis spell was just to avoid panic and casualties.

“Look,” I said quickly, “I know your standard procedure is to kill the scorpion dog and subdue us. But we’re not compromised. This scorpion dog’s name is Kim. He’s been with us for a long time. He currently controls two Armadillos-Bear. He is a young Scorpion Dog, so he can’t control more than two. If you came to town, you’d see—there aren’t more of his kind around. We can show you the Armadillo Bears too.”

Felix paused, thinking, but quickly shook his head.

“That is not good enough. A tribe of scorpion dogs could be hiding in the forest. For all I know, you take weekly trips to them to get your shot, bring food offerings. I’ve seen it before.”

Damn. It was so hard to prove we weren’t compromised. My time was running out.

Think. Think!

I look around. The prisoners. The cages. That was it. They were also compromised! That’s why they were locked up.

I squinted hard at them. Yes. Yes, damn it—it was actually them.

“Okay,” I said quietly, “your prisoners. They were controlled by scorpion dogs too, right?”

“Yes…” Felix replied.

“We know them,” I said. “That’s Farhad’s group!”

As I said it, I heard a surprised gasp from my friends.

“So?” Felix ask.

“They’re our enemies,” I continued. “We attacked their keep in Kuriolis, killed one of them, and stole their gold. After that, they retaliated—tried to attack Kamatouu. We arranged the guards and goblins to fight them off. After the battle, apparently the scorpion dogs attacked them and took control.”

“I still don’t—”

“All of that,” I interrupted, “you can confirm with your prisoners. And nothing I said fits scorpion dog behavior. They don’t approve long-distance travel. They don’t plan humans wars, rally goblins, or coordinate with town guards. They’d just order us to hunt, or at best, earn gold to buy food for them.”

Felix finally paused.

The rest of his group stood silently. Clearly, he was the one making the call.

“So answer me this,” he said. “Why the hell did you keep one of those nightmare monsters with you?”

“WHO DO YOU CALL A MONSTER!?” Samar exploded. “Kim is my friend! He’s sweet and kind and never hurt a single human! You should apologize to him right now, you… you ASS!”

Felix looked completely thrown off. He clearly hadn’t expected a fourteen-year-old girl to scream curses at him.

“Fine,” he relented. “We’ll confirm it. Xīnyán, you can drop the paralysis.”

“Frickin’ finally,” she muttered.

The spell released like a pressure valve bursting. My limbs returned, slow and shaky, like my body was made of jelly. I flexed my fingers. We were free.

“Abel,” Felix called to a dark-skinned man beside him. “Go to the prisoners. Confirm everything.”

“Sure thing,” Abel replied, heading off.

“You all wait here!” Felix warned us. “Don’t dare to move!”

“Insolent…” Duchess muttered quietly under her breath. “Naked-Nula fools…”

“Yes,” Samar said, answering something Kim apparently asked, “they are bad people.”

A few minutes later, Abel returned.

“It all checks out,” he said. “And uh… well, they really hate the Stormcrows. Said we shouldn’t trust them and that they’d rob and kill us the first chance they get.”

I saw Juliette smile at that. “Well, we had our disagreements.”

“I’m not going to interfere in your conflicts or make assumptions,” said Felix. “We’re taking Farhad’s group back with us to Liliverth after we’re done here. We’ll rehabilitate them, purge the scorpion control, and release them. This has nothing to do with you—or our objective of conquering Kamatouu.”

“Now that you mention it,” Juliette said, stepping forward, “conquering Kamatouu is actually why we came to talk to you.”

“And you are?” asked Felix.

Juliette lifted her chin, suddenly full of presence. “I’m Juliette, leader of the mighty band of the Stormcrows. And you made a real bad first impression on me.”

“Uh…” Felix hesitated, looking a little embarrassed. “Sorry. It was standard pro—”

“Standard procedure. I know,” Juliette cut in. “Look, our band is based in this town, but we don’t intend to participate in the battle. If you conquer it, we’ll live under your rule. But we’re here as messengers from the Baron—to see if an agreement can be reached to avoid unnecessary bloodshed.”

“I see,” Felix said. “In that case, you can tell him we offer generous surrender conditions. We won’t harm anyone who doesn’t participate in the fighting. Anyone who wishes may leave for another city. For those who stay, we’ll treat the citizens fairly.

“There will be a weekly tax, but a fair one—as in all Lithuron cities. Most guards and administrative personnel can remain in their jobs, except for high-ranking officials—the Baron and his top advisors. They’ll be free to leave safely.”

He lowered his voice a little. “Between us, even if they don’t surrender, we won’t execute anyone. I hate killing—especially women. But you can tell them we’ll slaughter everyone if you think it’ll help us avoid this nonsense battle.”

“Wait,” I said, frowning. “Don’t you want to kill enemy soldiers for XP?”

“I’m level four,” Felix replied. “This guards XP is meaningless to me. And like I said—I dislike killing.”

“That sounds… fairly reasonable,” said Juliette. “Honestly, I don’t have experience with NPC wars. I hope the Baron will see reason.”

“It’s worth a try,” Felix agreed. “But even if he doesn’t, it won’t be as bad as you’re imagining. You’ll see.”

I didn’t know what he meant by that, but he sounded confident. He’d clearly done this before. We had no idea how these ‘NPC wars’ really played out.

We walked back to the Baron. And by “we,” I mean everyone else. I was still being carried like a damn toddler in front of an actual army.

On the way, we talked about Farhad’s group.

“So we finally know what happened to them,” Hyun-woo said. “Honestly, I didn’t think Scorpion Suricata could pull this off, not against a strong group like Farhad’s.”

“They can pull it off,” I explained, “if they’re careful, cunning, and take control of them one by one.”

“Serve them pissfunnel-hobknocker right!” Duchess hissed. “I hope they like being in a cage! Chuu! Like when they tied me in that rope! Insolent Naked-Nula!”

“It’s still really sad,” said Samar, hugging herself. “If Geshem didn’t notice it was them, I never would’ve recognized. They look like… dirty skeletons.”

“Well, Felix said they’ll take care of them,” Juliette replied. “In the Lithuron Kingdom, they probably won’t be considered criminals. They’ll most likely stay there. Honestly, that saves us a lot of trouble.”

“Agreed,” said Hyun-woo. “You can tell the remaining Farhad group at the keep next time they call. It’s a solution to their situation, too. They can come here, join whoever travels to Lithuron, and rejoin Farhad. It’ll be easier for them, especially after Kamatouu is conquered.”

He said when Kamatouu is conquered. Not if. I guess that really was a done deal now. The important part was to convince the Baron to back down.

Back at the manor, Juliette presented the offer to the Baron. She made sense—clear, logical, reasonable.

His response was… well, as expected.

“There is reason in surrender.” He said, “But there is also honor in defiance.”

He stood tall, already put on some ceremonial polished armor.

“Should the tide of battle turn hopeless… then, perhaps, it is better to bend than to break.”

That last line was the hint we needed: if things went south, he would surrender to avoid unnecessary death.

“Well,” I said after we left, “he’s a weird one. Hope our peacekeeping effort wasn’t entirely useless.”

“Yeah,” Hyun-woo replied. “Like Felix said—we’ll see.”

About an hour later, we saw the enemy army start marching forward.

They stopped a few hundred yards from the first line of houses. Across from them, the 40 city guards stood in formation, Captain Gordon at the front, the Baron standing behind them in his overly dramatic ceremonial gear.

All of us Stormcrows were standing at a safe distance, watching.

I could’ve made some “we need popcorn” joke—but we were too tense. None of us wanted to watch Kamatouu guards get slaughtered.

“This is…” Hyun-woo began, “weird. And wrong. I’ve read a lot about medieval battles. This isn’t how it’s supposed to go. The guards don’t even have archers. And they’re standing right in archer range. It looks like a video game. Not a real battle.”

“Look,” I said, pointing. “It’s starting. Felix is moving.”

“Archers ready!” Felix shouted. Around 30 archers drew their bows. The Kamatouu guards raised shields.

“Fire!”

Arrows flew through the air. Most missed. A few struck shields. One unlucky guard caught an arrow in the leg and limped behind the line, groaning.

“Second volley!” Felix shouted. “Fire!”

This time, no one got hurt.

“Mage! Firestorm!” he commanded.

But Xīnyán just gave him a “not gonna happen” hand gesture. Felix stalked over, and the two started arguing.

That was extremely lame. A huge anticlimax after the words fire storm.

“Fine!” Felix finally shouted. “All soldiers—charge!”

Hina started to leave the player group and join the marching soldiers, but Felix caught her arm and shook his head. She looked pissed. I couldn’t hear what she said, but it was obvious—she wanted to kill guards. He said no.

The enemy soldiers didn’t break into a run. They closed the distance with long, quick strides.

When the first line of soldiers reached the guards, they began to fight.

And it was… surprisingly orderly. Like a choreographed duel.

No chaos. No blood frenzy. It was the complete opposite of our battle against Farhad’s group.

Soldiers who weren’t actively engaged just stood and waited their turn.

If someone got overwhelmed or injured, they calmly stepped back behind the line.

It was lame.

But in the best way.

So far, no one had died.

But apparently, it was just a matter of time. The fighters did try to kill each other, just in a very NPC-lazy kind of way - too calm and lacking any bloodlust whatsoever

I saw an injured guard who had trouble going back behind the line. The two enemy soldiers didn’t hesitate and stabbed him in the back, killing him.

Another guard died in a similar way, and one enemy soldier went too far forward and was killed by three guards fighting simultaneously.

After ten minutes, only ten guards remained—and they were already being surrounded.

Then, at last, a voice rang out: “Surrender!”

The Baron’s command.

Immediately, the guards dropped their weapons.

That was it.

Just like Felix promised—it really was a relatively simple matter.

I felt an immense wave of relief. This town meant something to me. I didn’t want it harm.

Of course, things could still go wrong.

Felix raised his voice.

“I hereby declare,” he shouted, “that Kamatouu is now a town of the great Lithuron Kingdom, under the just rule of King Azumuth!”

“Captain,” Felix said to one of his NPC soldier, “take some men with you and replace every flag and banner in town.”

I looked at the Baron. He appeared entirely calm, as if nothing interesting had happened in the last hour.

Then Felix and his fellow players walked toward the Baron and Captain Gordon. He turned and waved us over to come.

We followed.

“See?” Felix said as we come close. “Could’ve been worse.”

He turned to the Baron. “Arrange a meeting in the main manor. Every high-ranking official must attend. We’ll announce our new rule and administrative decisions. Make it happen within the hour.”

Then he turned back to Juliette. “Of course, you’ll join. Do any of the Stormcrows hold high administrative roles in the town?”

“I work as a maid in the manor,” Juliette said, acidic. “Does that count?”

Felix laughed. “Not at all!”

“So it’ll be as you said earlier?” I asked. “Anyone who wants can leave, new governor gets appointed, all that?”

“Sure,” said Felix. “Spoiler: new governor is Hina. It’s a quest reward for her.”

“Oh,” Juliette said, glancing at the armored Asian girl.

Hina stepped forward, smiling, and held out her hand.

“Hi!” she said brightly as Juliette shook it. “Sorry about earlier. I think you’re really cool.”

Hina usually had this bored look—but now she seemed genuinely excited.

“You’re French, right?” she asked. “Were you from Paris?”

“Lyon…” Juliette answered hesitantly.

“Oh my god, Lyon!” Hina clapped her hands. “I’ve been there like twice! I love France! This is so exciting! I just know we’re gonna be best friends!”

Juliette had this stunned look on her face.

It was actually kind of hilarious.

Yeah, our leader now had one more annoying things on her plate to deal with.

But to be fair… things could’ve gone a lot worse.

Now all that remained was to make sure the NPCs were treated fairly…

And that no super-players decided to go goblin-hunting in our forest.
