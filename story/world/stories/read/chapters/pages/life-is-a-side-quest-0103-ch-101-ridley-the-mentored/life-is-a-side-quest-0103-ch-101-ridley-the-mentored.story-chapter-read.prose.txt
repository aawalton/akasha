Ridley charged the first gore-covered monster. It was loosely shaped like a praying mantis, but its body bulged in odd places and its limbs were uneven. Not to mention it was covered in sludgy layers of thick, liquid blood. She swung for its alien-looking head, but it swiftly dodged and swiped at her midsection.

“It’s quick, so aim for its legs, you bloo… uh, dandy dame!” Terry yelled, catching his more colorful language as he ran for a second monster.

Ridley nodded without getting distracted. She and Terry had sparred several times, and one thing had become painfully obvious: Ridley was a terrible fighter. Terry had started pointing out her weaknesses ruthlessly, but she wasn’t proud enough to turn down the free advice—when she could understand it. Terry was a monster. They had been fortunate that the mind control seemed to dampen his wits in combat.

Ridley made an obvious backhanded wide swing with her left hand, sending the mantis jumping left. She immediately spun her right-handed bat around as she lunged and cracked its leg before it could regain its balance. With a powerful blow, she managed to cripple some of its mobility. She followed up with a blow to its neck, staggering it. The blood that had congealed around it began to splash onto the ground. She darted forward, leaving the finishing blow to Bru-haha, who played cleanup.

Terry burst left and right in little sonic bursts and hit the jaw of his mantis with a shining fist, downing it in one decisive strike.

They rushed to the next monster, a giant javelina standing six feet tall. It charged straight at her, lowering its head. As she rushed it, three quick rifle shots hit one shoulder, causing it to falter. She jumped and crashed both bats into the side of its head as it passed wide to her right. This only served to daze it, however. It seemed this one was going to be more of a challenge.

When Ridley turned to attack its back leg, she screamed as she felt a stab of pain in her shoulder. She turned her head to see an arrow sticking deep into the muscle, blood spurting out. Terry appeared before her, and she watched as he caught a second arrow aimed straight at her head.

“Keep legging it! Erratic movements! Charge in and leave the boar to Bru!”

She set her sights on a shadow-covered archer standing next to the tall black boulder and charged. She weaved left and right. The pain from the arrow was delayed, but as she charged in, it flared. She felt something familiar then—white-hot rage. It scared her at first, but then she smiled and embraced it.

“I’m going to shove that bow right down your scrawny little throat!” she yelled as she poured on the speed.

The archer lined up another shot, tracking Ridley, but just as he was about to release, his head was rocked to the side by a blurry fur ball. He turned to see his attacker, but Jameson’s hit-and-run was on another level with the boosts from Funk Master.

Ridley took the opportunity to explode forward. She felt a rush of blissful energy at her freedom of movement. Her head was just clear enough to remember to change up her movements so the archer couldn’t easily line up his shot. The archer was frustrated and jumped backward, but Ridley was confident she would catch him—that is, until a giant shield smashed her in the face.

“Oi! Mind yer flanks, yeh numpty!” Terry shouted, jumping in to attack a fully armored knight who was mountainous in size.

“Shut it, you piebald gypsy!” Ridley shouted.

Her anger flared, but she was able to redirect it at the knight. Completely forgetting the archer, she jumped in to attack. Terry was thrown off by her wild flailing and decided to retreat as the shield proved a worthy defense.

Ridley flailed her bats around uselessly, hitting the unyielding shield. Her stamina was quickly draining and she began to slow. The knight stepped forward and shoved her small frame back with the shield, pushing her off balance. After the trade of blows, the knight felt confident in making quick work of Ridley’s unpolished fighting. He opened the shield to swing down with a massive sword.

Ridley smiled, dropped a bat, reached out her hand, and shouted, “THURIZAS!”

A bolt of lightning shot out, catching the knight in the chest and sending him crashing into the black boulder. Terry jumped in and pounded the knight in the head a half dozen times in a blink. Ridley stood up, grabbing her bat, and reveled in her triumph. Then an arrow pierced into her stomach. She staggered and coughed up a mouthful of blood.

Her rage flared at the insult of the cowardly archer. She ignored the wound and started to rush the archer again.

“Back up and heal, you wanky git!” Terry shouted.

Her anger turned on Terry, but some logic managed to creep in at the memory of being hospitalized. She nodded with a sneer and jumped back to a covered position by the hill. Bru-haha met her there and ripped the arrow out of her shoulder.

Ridley cursed but held in an enraged impulse to break his knee. He moved in front of her and yanked out the arrow in her stomach, then handed her his healing potion. She drank it thankfully, the sharp emotions of rage dwindling.

“Nice to see you worthy of the name again, little fury. I think we can clean this up while you heal.”

She looked at the field of battle and realized the archer was all that was left. She watched him dodging bolts from Nerf Kid. Normally in his position you would retreat, but as he was likely a dull-witted thrall—as Terry had described—his mind was only on the command he had been given.

Jameson smashed into the archer’s shin as he attempted to leap into a tree. The archer attempted to get off a desperate shot at Terry, but he just caught it and threw it back into the archer’s bow arm. Terry gave him a few punches to the head and it was over.

Jared ran up at this point to see the commotion and caught sight of Ridley. The look of shock on his face at her bloody visage was satisfying.

“This is what a party is supposed to look like,” she smiled at him with blood-soaked teeth.

Jared shivered before he turned and puked.

Ridley walked up to the knight, followed by a wary Jared. She was still in a lot of pain, but the wounds were no longer serious.

“These potions worked wonderfully,” Ridley commented.

“I wasn’t certain, but it looks like it mended your internal injuries. Usually gut wounds are tricky, but regenerating thin tissue is simpler than invasive surgery.”

She stepped forward and removed the knight’s helmet to reveal a man with a sharp nose underlined by a terrible pencil mustache and shaggy brown hair. Black ooze was leaking from his mouth.

“Dang it, I was hoping for a burly female knight. That would have been awesome.”

“What is wrong with him? Is he an invader like the orcs?” Jared asked.

Funk Master stepped up next to them. “Terry there had a similar thing going on. Black shadows over his face, black ooze leaking from his mouth, mind controlled.”

“They are Chosen, like us. Likely not from here. Terry was in England before he woke up here,” Ridley said, pointing at the tattooed martial artist.

“So what were they trying to do?” Jared asked.

“That is the magic question,” Ridley responded, now looking at the weird symbols etched into the black boulder.

Bru-haha carried the archer over his shoulder and placed him down gently next to the knight. He looked young, likely younger than Ridley.

“So get this. Terry said he recognizes both of them.”

“They’s at the swanky party I’s at whens me mind went numb,” Terry said, pointing at the archer. “That un is supposed to be the nephew of some bigwig in the parliament. The other one is a nob hobbyist of ancient weaponry. A real bookish bloke.”

“So the whole group of Chosen at that party were likely mind controlled,” Bru-haha said, pulling out a little glass bottle and collecting the black ooze from the knight’s mouth.

“So we keeping these two? They can join Terry’s chain gang,” Ridley suggested.

“Until Brian gets back, we don’t really know who to talk to about this. I don’t think we need to rush to have them deported, do you?”

Ridley looked at Terry. “Eh, Terry’s been helpful enough, I guess. I got my rage back.” Ridley flexed.

“And right looney you went with it. We need to work on that. How’s about I knock you about to see if you can keep your head?” Terry grinned.

“Speaking of keeping our heads, we have a guinea pig here,” Ridley said, pointing at Jared. “How about we give him some of the black ooze and see if he goes full mind slave?”

“Hey! No… no thank you,” he said under the contemplative stare of Bru-haha.

“Eww! No! I’m not putting my hands on that!” Jada shouted. She stood next to one of the mantises that had the glow signifying it had a resource. Jameson sat on her head, barking orders.

Darin shot a Nerf dart at the ferret, who dashed around dodging the darts that followed. Then they started their mock battle antics as usual. Darin could turn off his power to enhance his Nerf darts, and the ferret never tried to draw blood as he bit, so there was no actual risk of injury. Ridley found the whole love triangle hilariously adorable.

Bru-haha shrugged before he stepped up to the boulder and placed his hand on the stony surface. Then his monocle lit up as he examined it. Ben circled his drone around it, likely recording a video.

“Hmm, whatever they were doing was interrupted. All I get when identifying it is ‘Incomplete Runic Monolith,’” Bru-haha shared.

“So they were turning this into a magic item?” Ben asked.

“I think the stone itself is made of magic, but I don’t have Alexander’s skill of seeing magic flows.”

“A monolith… a weapon maybe? An area effect? Maybe a portal?” Ben asked.

“No way of knowing what they are up to. We will interrogate these two when they awake. Hopefully there’s a hint,” Bru-haha said as he stared at the runes scratched into the sides of the boulder.

“I know someone who might be able to tell,” Ridley offered. “But she’s a crazy asshole.”

Ridley sat and cleared her mind while everyone stood back. She had practiced meditation enough recently and had managed to sort her feelings from Victrius’s. It was an odd experience for her because her real emotions had been flared, but all of them had been rooted in her actual experiences. She had to wrestle with the added fact that her perceptions of people’s interactions with her had also been built on a false assumption. Even looking back at her current high school interactions, she felt ashamed of her responses.

When she felt she had a handle on her emotions, she removed the hairpin. She braced herself for an onslaught of words from Victrius, but she was silent. She let the silence linger for a moment before she quested out.

“Victrius?”

“Unfilial child. You have managed to break mine rune,” Victrius’s voice responded.

“Hello, insane ghost. I have. I’ll get rid of the rest of it soon enough.”

“Art thou sure this is your desire? Thine powers would be great should thou let me guideth thee.”

“And potentially have me kill my friends? No thank you. Why are you doing this? What revenge are you actually looking for? You never explained.”

Victrius paused.

“I shall grant to you my past, undeserving as you are. I was once Queen of the Iceni. My husband was betrayed and slaughtered. My daughters defiled. I gave myself to the rage and led my people in a slaughter of the Romans in revenge. Nothing would quench my bloodlust and I made them pay the price.”

“I am sorry for your loss, but I am not a vessel for you to take out your revenge on. The Romans have been gone for a long time.”

Victrius’s anger started to boil over and Ridley felt her muscles tensing, ready to spring into action.

“I WILL PUT THIS HAIRPIN IN IF YOU TRY ANYTHING!”

Her muscles relaxed again.

“Ungrateful git. What would you have me do?” Victrius asked.

“Be helpful! Our enemy is not of this world. Take responsibility for the living!”

“An exchange then.”

“For what? You are not getting my body.”

“Find the head of the snake that led the Romans. He is surely still here. I can feel him.”

“Ok, what type of being was he?”

“A sorcerer of unmatched talents, a conqueror of worlds. A mind mage, a twister of souls, a castellan of otherworldly knowledge with no name.”

“…Did he use runes?”

“Dark twisted magics and runes… yes.”

“Runes like this?” she asked, looking up at the incomplete monolith.

She could feel Victrius looking through her eyes, and felt her shudder in… fear?

“Yes.” She felt Victrius shudder on the edge of tears.

“Can you tell us what this is?” She stood to walk around the boulder.

Ridley examined the runes closely and made a full circle around the boulder.

“It is unfinished… but it seems the enemy is making a gateway.”

“A gateway to where?”

“To the banished plane.”
