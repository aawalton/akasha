Velidan hauled me upright. The pavilion spun as I gasped for air, snapped from a dozen dreams at once and half-limp in an iron grip.

“That was hardly,” he snapped, pulling me far too close as I tried to reel back from the baffling anger, “enjoyable.”

Then silver flickered along my bloody wrist. I was released and fell to my knees. My skin felt like a layer of frost had covered it and I spent a moment shivering, trying to calm my racing heart.

Then a warm, soft form knelt and pressed against mine. “I’m sorry, dear one. It should not have been like that.” Gentle fingers smoothed the trails of moisture from my cheeks. The lethian’s voice was as thick and sweet as honey. “Were I stronger, I could have… but no matter. Lean on me, mortal. I have you. Breathe.”

I did so, basking in the warmth and searching my thoughts for… I wasn’t sure what, exactly. Gaps, missing pieces, blank spots. I found nothing amiss and let out a relieved sigh.

“It was an honor to share in your joys, mortal.” The lethian leaned back, and I saw that she was no longer a wispy, trembling thing. A flushed visage framed by thick golden hair knelt beside me. She gave me a sad smile. “And in your sorrows.”

“I’m sorry,” I stammered, stunned by the change in her entire form. She looked… vibrant. Strong and healthy, skin rosy and hands clasping mine with steady warmth. “I… I only tried to think about nice things for you, but-”

“But some wounds have not yet turned to scars.”

I flinched, and didn’t think I could bear to contemplate that statement for long. “I’m sorry.”

“You have nothing to apologize for.” She drew me up, then pressed a kiss to my forehead. It reminded me starkly of the gesture I’d done only moments ago, with my injured pet. “There are treasures to be found in painful memories, too. Like pearls worn smooth by time and sand.”

“I’m so glad it helped you feel better. Was that eno-”

“She overpaid.” Velidan stood still as death, unmoved from the bowl. He was looking at it, and at the silent glints of scenes and faces within. Very slowly, his gaze slid to the two of us. “You will compensate accordingly.”

“Oh, no,” I shook my head and spread both hands wide, as if to show that I was alright, “you don’t need to. Really. I’m just glad I could help.”

“It’s not a matter of need, sweet one. I’m happy to. Here. These,” she lifted two small, ebony raven figurines with shining amethyst eyes from the velvet, “were crafted long ago, by a druidess from another court. Their magic is limited, but precious nonetheless.”

She pressed one of the birds into my palm, then lifted the other to her lips and whispered, “My name is Shar’ai’a’lain, she-who-remembers-light.”

I gasped as the lethian’s voice slipped like a sigh from my raven’s open beak. Its eyes grew bright, then dimmed when the words ended.

“Strange magic, indeed.” Velidan plucked the other figurine from the Fae’s hand, inspecting it closely. “And useful, if someone could replicate it.”

She didn’t respond, but rather searched my eyes as if looking for something important. Then her mouth tightened. She took a thick bracelet made of sheer, dark green metal from the table.

“Another small thing, but with its own magic. This was fashioned from a wyvern scale.”

I took it curiously. The band felt smooth and smelled subtly of smoke. “What are wyverns?”

“Creatures that long to be dragons.” Laughter fell like music from her lips as I wondered what a dragon was. “And often fail to see their own majesty. Myth tells it that a charm wrought from wyvern scales will lend strength in times of need.”

A little thrill ran down my spine. Myth. Right. After tonight, I don’t know if I believe myths are just myths any more!

“This is perfect,” I said, beaming at the lethian. “My best friend gave me a bracelet, once, and now I’ll have one for him. Thank you.”

“Thank you, dear one.”

“Who is this man?”

Velidan’s smooth, even murmur was so startling that I jumped, turning and half-expecting to see a strange man in the tent with us. Instead I saw him standing over the bowl again, looking down at glimpses and flickers of a familiar scene.

Agnes’ wrinkled visage shifted to Thom’s slackened, scorched face. Bile crept up the back of my throat and I turned quickly away.

“Brin?” Velvet again. Soft, but with a hint of something dark beneath it. “Tell me.”

“That w-was… he’s one of the people who… h-his name was Thom.”

“Was?”

Heat pricked at my eyes. I quickly blinked it away. “Renner killed him.” Before he could press for more details, I cleared my throat. “I’m… I’m hungry.” An unreadable glance was cast my way, and I responded with rounded eyes and a hopeful, “Please? I have things for all my friends. I’d like to eat, and you promised we’d talk about magic.”

Velidan flicked his gaze to the lethian, her shadowed tables, then back to the water. I nearly expected a curt refusal, or more questions about the scenes at his feet. But he simply stepped towards the entrance. “As you wish.”

We wound through the field and towards the shadowed tower. Slip scurried by a few times, flashing a spiky-toothed grin before bounding away towards something that had caught her eye. I glanced towards Velidan a few times and was met by unreadable silence.

Once close, I saw that the tower itself was a sheer face of age-weathered stone and dark ivy dotted by pale purple buds. An enormous opening had been half-carved, half-smashed into its base, revealing a vast, wisp-lit circular chamber full of Fae. Narrow steps curved around the chamber’s rim, leading up.

It was so similar to a human tavern- tables formed of stone slabs, a curving bar filled with steaming pots, and a domed hearth of crackling red fire- that for a long while I could only stop and stare. Figures both otherworldly and unsettlingly not milled about. Some talked, some laughed, some argued, some whispered in furtive voices, and some sat alone.

“We’re eating here?” I could scarcely contain my excitement, though it was once again tinged by nervousness. Many faces turned to me as we entered, ranging in their expressions from pleasantly surprised to outright hostile.

“In a sense.” Velidan strode into the din. Figures glanced up and moved quickly out of the way until he reached a tall woman with deer-like antlers. She dipped her head in greeting and they began to speak.

I crept along until I found a mostly-empty table near the hearth. A gaggle of tiny, dragonfly-winged women stood around a bowl of fizzing pink liquid. They were giggling and scooping up handfuls of the drink.

I watched, charmed by the sight, until a hand clasped my elbow. He drew me towards the steps, and once again other Fae quickly shifted out of the way.

“But this looks really nice,” I protested. “And there’s a few tables free-”

“And we are due a conversation that deserves privacy. So either I’ll tell them all to leave, or we’re going upstairs.”

I sighed and climbed the steps.

We curved up, past wooden-floored chambers filled with cooking supplies, sacks, boxes, cauldrons, glassware, slabs of cured meat, and barrels full of strange plants. Savory, earthy, sweet, and nutty smells assailed me at each doorway.

By the time we reached the top my legs were aching, but one glance around made the entire climb feel worth it. We stood on flat rock rimmed by a waist-height wall of stone and rustling ivy. A slab of marble carved into a table, with several uncomfortable-looking chairs around it, sat in the center. Stars glistened like diamonds above, the moon swelled with light, and the market below was a distant haze of voices beneath lavender smoke and the flickering trails of wisps.

I drew a deep breath, staring down in awe. The throng of Fae looked dreamlike from our vantage, and the sky above only made it all feel more magical. Cool air ruffled my hair, carrying music up from the field.

Then I turned around, and my blooming smile wilted.

The landscape beyond the castle was not dreamlike, nor beautiful or anything close to magical. Petrified forests, scorched earth, and blackened stone swept out towards looming mountains. A wide, deep channel of barren rock cut through it all, with river stones and shells scattered within.

I stared at, shaking my head in horror. “Ashes. What happened?”

“That range,” Velidan said flatly, bracing his hands nearby and nodding towards the shadow-wreathed peaks, “is dragon territory. A few came down when the Nightmare descended.”

I pressed both hands to my chest, then glanced back towards the field of scattered Fae. Remnants of species, he’d called them, which made me wonder... I eyed him, feeling suddenly fearful. “Is the rest of this realm like this?”

He was silent for a while. “It varies. There are beautiful places reforming, like my home, and some were untouched. But the majority is… mm. Unpleasant.” One slender finger pointed towards the mountains. “Beyond that you can find a forest where dryads went mad, and the plants there try to eat anything that enters. Further still is a storm that hasn’t stopped in decades.” He turned, nodding in the opposite direction, where stone eventually turned to dark scrubland. “And just down there is where all the basilisks dwelled before they turned on each other. It’s a valley of statues, now.”

“Saints. How awful.”

“It is,” he agreed, then shifted between me and the scene. “But I didn’t bring you here to gaze at desolation. Sit.”

I did as a little creature made of swishing gray fur and white claws scuttled up the stairs. It placed a wooden tray down, then hurried away with a chirp.

My meal looked enticing. Dripping wedges of dark fruit, a nutty-smelling round of golden cheese, and a steaming, fist-sized hunk of bread dappled by fragrant herbs sat before me. Two dense glasses held dark, bitter-smelling wine, and a wooden cup held clear water.

I eyed it all with furrowed brows. There’s no way I’m drinking the wine, but… I mean, the rest certainly looks delicious. And I can’t very well go all night and day without eating. Right?

I took my first bite tentatively. I chose the bread, because that seemed safest, and was pleasantly surprised to find it warm, thick, and rich with flavors I couldn’t name but very much enjoyed. My mouth watered after the first bite.

Velidan sat opposite, watching me eat with a slight curve to his mouth. He seemed to grow lost in thought for some time. When he finally spoke, it was in a solemn tone. “What became of your squirrel?”

“My…” A disbelieving little snort choked its way out, then became a full-fledged laugh. “Hold on. How do you know what butterflies and bears are, but not rabbits?”

One brow rose. “Brin, your village was my first foray into the mortal realm. All my knowledge comes secondhand from other Fae.”

I took another bite, grinning as I chewed. “And no one mentioned rabbits or tomatoes, huh?”

“Likely countless other things. Such as,” a sly smile curved one side of his mouth, “the apparent custom of young humans purchasing each other.”

I nearly choked. “Saints, that makes it sound untoward! Do you… do Fae not have dowries?”

“Dowry.” Each syllable rolled out slowly, as if he was tasting them. “From context, I’ll assume this refers to the price of a human bride?”

“Well, yes, but don’t say it like that!” Heat pooled in my cheeks. I nibbled more bread, then tentatively took a bite of fruit. Tart, sweet, and vaguely spicy flavors erupted from dense flesh and syrupy juice, earning a delighted gasp. I licked juice from my fingers, then met a gaze that felt too heavy. “Look, it’s human custom for men to… well, you know. Find someone they’d like to court.”

The other brow rose, too, and a soft laugh rose with it. “That custom is not exclusive to humans, Brin.”

I took a nibble of the cheese, jolted upright as it melted to thick, faintly sweet cream in my mouth, then stared slack-jawed before taking a larger bite. “Wow. That’s… salt. Wow. I could eat that all night.”

Velidan chuckled.

“And… well, it’s just how marriage works. The man approaches the girl’s father or next of kin, and the two decide on a fair dowry. It’s usually coin, in the larger cities, but in small places like Fellbrook livestock or a period of labor are just more practical.” I took another bite, stifling a whimper as this one left the tang of clover honey and butter on my tongue. “If they agree on a fair price and the father approves, he gives permission for courtship.”

He looked increasingly perplexed as I spoke. “So mortal unions are based on the haggling and greed of men. And does the woman have any say?”

“If her father’s a good man who wants her to be happy, yes. I’m sure there’s lots who are, and some who aren’t. I…” I set the cheese down with a sigh. “Durst’s answer, leaving the whole thing to me, is… very unusual. I was incredibly grateful. Well,” I rubbed the back of my neck, glancing down, “after I got over my shock. It’s… it’s a strange feeling, having your future just given to you.”

And quite different from having it taken away. I swallowed, one hand creeping up to close around the jewel, then quickly chased the thought away.

I took slow bites, firmly keeping any shred of despair at bay. “I’d just started learning apothecary work from our town cleric, and I… I don’t know. My entire life, I always assumed Durst would promise me to Royce. And I was fine with that. But then suddenly there were all these… possibilities. I could marry and have children, apprentice with Brother Clem, or even go to Respite for proper cleric training. I…” my throat closed, and I took a moment to steady myself. “I even thought about going to Lighthold someday. To study runes and meet other runekeepers, though… though of course that was a silly idea.”

He sipped the wine, pondering all of that with more interest than I’d expected. “And what did you decide?”

My shoulders swelled in a heavy sigh. “Well, I decided to break my best friend’s heart. It was awful. And I wanted to keep learning apothecary work, so I’ve spent the past few years helping Clem.”

Velidan swirled the glass, making dark liquid catch and glint beneath the light. “And all this made you so upset earlier. Why? Are you regretting your choice to deny the blonde paramour?” The question lilted under a mocking note, and I felt the weight of his gaze even without looking up. “He does seem quite taken with you. Or was it your choice to stay as a near-pauper in your quaint little village?”

“Royce deserves someone who loves him as more than a friend.” My fists curled. “And that quaint little village means the world to me.”

Dark hair hissed against one shoulder as he cocked his head. “Then why did you become distraught? You said the memory… broke.”

That earned a wince. Ashes, how am I supposed to answer that? ‘Well, on one of the best days of my life, someone told me I could have any future I wanted. Then you took half of them away.’ But it’s not as though he’d care, and I don’t think I can say any of it without either bursting into tears or trying to shove him off the tower.

I pushed my plate away with a sigh. “Ask me some other time.”

“Why?”

I spent a moment searching the sky for an answer. I found none and said wearily, “Because we’re still on pleasant.”

That earned me a puzzled look.

“I mean… sorry. It just… I don’t want… it has been, though. Pleasant. This.” I stood, pacing to the wall with a sigh. “It’s actually been kind of amazing.”

“Mm. Far superior to your ‘nice evening’ of mild injuries and stale food, I imagine.” He smirked.

I mirrored the expression. Mine was lessened, though, as thoughts of Renner’s hands on my waist and his lips a breath from mine brought a blush. “Oh, don’t be so sure.”

He laughed at that. “Don’t be petulant, Brin. I’ve bought you enchanted items, let you roam freely in a place other humans couldn’t even dream of, and I’ll wager this was the finest meal you’ve ever had. You were practically drooling after one bite.” He sipped his wine, looking at me smugly over the rim.

I spent a moment in puzzled silence. My gaze trailed back to the dreamlike throng, then to our majestic canopy, then to my meal. I frowned. “I suppose that’s true. But… why?”

“You said you were hungry.”

“No,” I snorted, then returned to my seat and braced both elbows on the marble. “No, I meant… I just… why? I already agreed to go with you tomorrow. You already put this on,” one hand clasped the jewel at my throat, then swept to our surroundings. “So why are we doing all this? What’s the point?”

Velidan regarded me for a few heartbeats, eyes glittering as bright as the stars framing his face. “You’re more pleasant when you’re enjoying yourself.” He sipped his drink, then added with a smirk, “Much less entertaining, though.”

The words sank in, equal parts chilling and, in a bizarre way, almost reassuring. Finally I found my voice. “I hope you prefer the version of me that’s not… you know. Breaking your furniture. Yelling.” I offered a weak smile. “Trying to stab you.”

“Oh, don’t be so sure.” A low chuckle accompanied the answer, and both sent a shiver down my spine. As I struggled to think of a response, all the languid ease before me stiffened. A shadow fell across the Fae’s face.

“Though now,” he growled, giving me a warning look, “I’ll prefer you silent.”

I blinked, fully at a loss. Then the sounds of footsteps and deep, familiar laughter echoed from down the steps.
