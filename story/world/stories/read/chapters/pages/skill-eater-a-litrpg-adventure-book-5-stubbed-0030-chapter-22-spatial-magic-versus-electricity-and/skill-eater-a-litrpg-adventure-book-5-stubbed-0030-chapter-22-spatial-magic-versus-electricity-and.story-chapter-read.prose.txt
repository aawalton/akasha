Side by side, Edge and Rue entered the tunnel bordering the Association plaza.

The passage ran straight through the butte’s wall and granted access to the piers used by the city’s veteran crews. The last time he’d walked this way was when he joined Widow’s team to hunt the rock eel, which made him wonder how the hunters’ voyage to Puppet Town was going.

They should be able to make considerably better time than we did. They have our map, a ship, and way more experience navigating extreme-threat biomes. I wish I could see the expression on Ander’s face when he receives his share of the resources we shipped. I wonder who Dialla will send when Warrior returns to World’s End.

By the time Edge was done mulling the matter over, they had arrived at the end of the tunnel. “Remember to keep one eye on the sky and stay at least a hundred feet away from open water,” he warned Rue. “This area is patrolled regularly, but the biome’s predators are an ever-present danger. That eel snatched someone right off the docks.”

Rue grumbled his agreement, and Edge could tell the beast was taking the threat seriously. Satisfied that his Companion wouldn’t take any unnecessary risks, they made their way past a gated complex where boats were built and maintained and entered the harbor where resources were processed and packaged before being brought to market.

“Halt.” A guard raised his hand as the duo walked alongside a loading dock filled with crates and barrels being carried onto wagons, beyond which were the warehouses infested by rodents. “This area is off-limits. If you want to go fishing, there’s a nice pier on the other side of the waterfront with a lovely view of the lake.”

“Thanks for the tip,” Edge replied. “That honestly sounds like a nice way to relax when I have a few hours to spare. But we’re not lost. We’re here to deal with your rat problem, or he is, at any rate. I’m just a portable sausage dispenser as far as Rue is concerned.”

They laughed at the way the beast’s ears pricked up when he said the magic word. Edge showed the guard his society badge, and the warrior let them pass. “Good luck. Those little bastards are nothing but trouble, particularly the fart rats.”

“The what?”

“That’s what we call the gas rats. It will make sense when you smell them. I’m glad I’m stationed out here and the breeze is blowing the other way.” Edge chuckled and offered a parting wave. As the duo approached their destination, he conducted a pre-mission briefing before turning Rue loose.

“You’ve dealt with volt rats before, but the stage-twos are quite a bit older. They will have more skills, superior attributes, and considerably more combat experience. You could probably survive a direct hit with that collar, but it will be a nasty shock. Do your best to take them out before they can take a shot.

“The gas rats have some variance in their skillsets, but they should all be packing wide-area, air-aspected spells, which means dodging will be minimally effective once they’ve finished casting. You’re in charge but ask for help if you need it. Hunting prey above your stage is never safe, and discretion is the better part of valor.”

Through his Companion bond, he received an image of Rue blinding a stage-three serpent, saving Edge’s life in the bargain. It was followed by the fox severing the dimensional anchor, protecting everyone living in World’s End from the monsters’ invasion.

He was going to chide the beast for bragging, but he couldn’t argue with the results. “Technically… that’s true. You’ve shown good judgment in dangerous situations and gone above and beyond when shit hits the fan. All right then. Lead the way but watch out for ambush predators. They love to strike the moment you’re going in for the kill, so never lower your guard.”

When they approached the first warehouse, he noticed the beast was quite a bit bigger than he’d been just a few days ago. Edge realized part of the reason Rue was growing so fast might have something to do with how much time the fox spent inside his core on top of the magicyte-rich environments they’d been traveling through and the magic flowing through their Companion bond.

Rue’s situation was unique. While all Companions had a dimensional den provided by the Summon Companion skill, no one he’d spoken to had heard of a beast that could physically enter someone’s inner world.

Edge hadn’t thought about it before, but the intense time dilation inside his core meant the fox’s biological age was quite a bit older than his chronological age. He hadn’t considered the possibility that this strange quirk would hasten the pup’s physiological development in addition to providing countless hours of training with the avatars living in his village.

It was something he might have to curtail at some point, but if the setup let the beast join the crew months if not years faster than would otherwise be possible, it would make them both happy and grant Rue the strength he needed to defend himself in the wild.

Before they proceeded, Edge flagged down a hunter patrolling the perimeter and asked her for more details.

“We don’t know where the nest is,” she explained, “although it must be around here somewhere. Some of the rats can chew through the warehouses’ walls and the containers stacked inside. We set out traps, and those killed a few, but the beasts adapted quickly and the devices stopped working a few days later. The little shits ate the wards my boss placed, so at least one of them is mana sensitive.”

“Thanks.” He tipped his hat. “I appreciate the heads-up.”

With that, they were ready to begin the hunt. There were four structures that comprised the infested facility. The staff had moved the most valuable items into the free city, but the warehouses were brimming over with a vast range of resources arranged in metal racks that ran from floor to ceiling. Some goods were lying in piles, but most were sealed in stone containers with the contents listed on front.

Edge spotted a few ladders, but nothing that resembled a forklift, although he’d seen a few hydraulic lifts mounted to floating wagons on their way in. This time, he didn’t offer Rue any advice on where to begin. He activated Warlord’s Mantle for insurance, switched on the combined mode of All-Seeing Gaze, then shifted into shadow form and followed in the fox’s wake—ready to step in at a moment’s notice.

He nodded his approval when Rue began by taking a lap around the perimeter. While the beast was searching for any sign of his prey, he never stopped scanning the area for prowling predators or other forms of danger, just like he’d been trained. When the golden fox was certain that nothing nasty was lurking nearby, he performed a slower circuit of the space between the warehouses, grumbling to himself as he sifted through the myriad scents of people, beasts, and goods.

Edge was curious what it would be like to navigate this hidden world of smells, something he might explore when he had time to spare. Although his nose would never compare to a beast using sensory skills, being able to track by scent and detect danger from a distance would be a useful tool to supplement his other techniques.

Rue hadn’t had much trouble catching wind of the volt rats the last time he’d hunted the lightning-flinging rodents, but this time around, he didn’t smell them at all. The stage-twos must be better at concealing the pack’s presence, which explains how they can survive in an extreme-threat biome to begin with.

Since there weren’t any trails to follow, the fox entered the first warehouse, exploring the structure row by row with his nose close to the ground. By the time they made their way through the final building, Edge sensed frustration and determination conducting through their bond.

“What now, boy? Do you need to take a break or want me to give you a hint?”

The irate vulpine turned down both offers. Instead, Rue asked Edge to pull some items out of his vault. Under the beast’s direction, he laid out slices of vegetables, fruit, bread, and cheese, placing them in an arrangement where Rue could see everything without forming a suspiciously convenient pile. With great reluctance, the fox asked Edge to add a few pieces of sausage to the assorted treats, which was the strongest sign yet that he was serious about this hunt.

With their lure in place, they hid on an empty shelf that offered a good view of the warehouse and settled down to wait. They would have to come back in the morning if this wound up taking too long, but forty minutes later, Rue’s ears pricked up, and Edge sensed an eager satisfaction thrumming through their psychic link.

At that point, the beast revealed a new trick. Rue teleported into his dimensional den, erasing his presence and piggybacking on the concealment bonus provided by Shadow Play. It was a creative way to enhance his stealth and a tactic that was perfectly viable in the field, so Edge offered his approval as they waited to find out if the ploy would bear fruit.

Sure enough, a green rodent the size of his backpack appeared around the corner, ignoring the food as it methodically swept the area for danger. He felt mana flowing from the creature’s core—some manner of perceptual skill according to his senses. Whatever the critter was packing, it wasn’t a match for Play. After one last look around, the beast began to dine.

Part of the way through, a pair of lemon-yellow rodents appeared. Judging by their size, they were stage-one volt rats and their older cousin was a stage-two gas rat. Some hissing, squeaking, and posturing ensued before the big beast allowed the others to nibble on the less-desirable morsels, keeping the cheese, fruit, and sausage for itself.

Edge expected Rue to make his move at any moment. He was surprised when the fox watched the last slice of sausage vanish between the gas rat’s jaws. When the creatures left the way they’d come, the beast materialized, sniffing at the air while pursuing his prey. Through their bond, Edge could tell the rats’ natural odors had vanished. However, Rue was following the scent of spiced meat on their breath, which he had grown exquisitely sensitive to over the course of his sausage-stealing career.

The duo crept out of the warehouse, passed through a hole in the fence, and approached a private pier that was in disrepair and hadn’t been used in some time. A strip of dirt ran between the woodwork and the water’s edge, which was shallow along harbor’s interior. After asking Edge to watch the lake, man and beast stepped into the shadows below.

He expected to find some manner of hole leading to a burrow. Instead, he came across a door lying flush with the earth. It was old and the wood was rotting. One corner was missing completely, which explained how the rodents passed through. A small magitech device was embedded in the doorframe—so corroded it had fallen apart. He thought was a ward against vermin, but when he took a closer look, it seemed more like one of the illusion generators he remembered from the feed, devices that made objects blend in with their surroundings.

Before they proceeded, Edge turned to Rue and said, “I can open it, but once I do, they’re going to know we’re here. If we let our prey run past us, they will escape, and there might be another way out even if we don’t.”

The fox asked him if he thought there was anything dangerous down there. “I doubt there are any predators inside, since the rats are using it as a den. This is probably an old storage cellar, so there shouldn’t be any traps either, but assume there are, just to be safe. It’s good practice for delving dungeons anyway.”

After creeping up to the hole and sweeping the far side with Gaze, Edge pulled the portal open. Inside was a staircase leading deeper belowground. The walls and stairs were made of stone, which was a lot nicer than he was expecting. Well now, what do we have here?

He spotted some ancient magelights embedded in the ceiling. Most had expired long ago, but a few were dim and flickering, and a couple were still in working order. The overall effect was kind of spooky, but there was enough light for Rue to see. Edge could make out everything with Gaze’s Darkvision aspect, which had considerably longer range than the ocular skill’s combined mode.

The area was surprisingly spacious, far more extensive than the cellar he was expecting. This must have been built back in the early days of World’s End and lost to the ages. The illusion ward on the door failed fairly recently, or the hunters working out of the northern docks would have spotted it already.

As Edge considered their discovery, the duo skulked their way down the tunnel until they arrived at a four-way junction. To the left and right, hallways stretched out about a hundred feet, with one door on the sides and another at the far end. Straight ahead was a passageway leading toward the free city, but the entrance was blocked by a chunk of ceiling that had collapsed and filled the area with dirt.

The feed was rife with rumors of secret tunnels running throughout World’s End. It seems they weren’t tall tales after all. He was curious where the passageway led and might do some exploring when he had time to spare, but for now, he stopped beside Rue and returned his attention to the task at hand.

“All right, boy,” he whispered into the fox’s ear. “How do you want to play this? There are six rooms and an unknown number of enemies.” Edge nodded in response to the images flowing through their bond. “Got it. That’s a smart play, but this might get dicey. I’ll back you up if things don’t go according to plan.”
