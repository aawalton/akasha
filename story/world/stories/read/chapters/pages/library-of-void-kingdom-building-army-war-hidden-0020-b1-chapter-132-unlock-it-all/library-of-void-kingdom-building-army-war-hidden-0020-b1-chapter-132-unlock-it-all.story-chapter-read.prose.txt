“System,” his enthusiastic voice echoed loudly through the Simulation Space. “Scan this rifle…”

His eyes flickered quietly as he stared at the rifle, hoping that the System could recognize it and that the Scanning function could be used.

The familiar sensation of energy appeared within his body, the same as when he had scanned the looted firearms of the Lionheart Empire.

It’s working…

And the much-anticipated voice suddenly echoed in his mind.

[System Notification]

Scanning…

[Target Identified: Firearm — M4 Carbine Rifle]

[Technological Structure Analyzed]

[Scanning Complete]

[Technology Added to System Database]

“Hahaaa….” His enthusiastic laugh echoed through the Simulation Space after the System’s notification appeared. It truly was possible to scan constructs made within the Simulation Space. And it clearly indicated that the Simulation was not merely a simulation in the ordinary sense, but an independent space that possessed the power of creation and destruction.

Otherwise, how could different environments be created with a simple wave of a hand, and objects be constructed out of thin air? Or how could his body disappear from real world and appear in an imaginary world, the Simulation Space. So, it wasn’t imaginary in sense but in words. It clearly was like real world but with its own set of rules different from the real world.

But Vector’s focus was not on this.

Instead, his focus was on the possibilities that had now been revealed.

The next course of action he would take.

The plan he had originally conceived would now need to be completely changed.

Vector let out a quiet breath and then waved his hand.

The surrounding steppe landscape changed. In its place… a vast and boundless ocean appeared. The air turned salt-tinged and humid, and the sky overhead stretched into an infinite pale-blue vault.

Blue water extended toward the horizon, merging seamlessly with the sky above. The surface appeared calm at first glance, its waves rising and falling with a quiet rhythm.

Yet each rolling wave carried the weight of ocean behind it. The endless motion of water hinted at forces capable of crushing mountains and swallowing entire continents.

It was beautiful.

But he waved his hand once again.

Then the water began to divide.

It separated slowly, peeling away on both sides in white-capped cascades, and from the parting sea—something rose.

A metallic structure began to take shape, tearing through the water. Gradually, the structure formed into the shape of a colossal ship. Massive decks layered upon one another began to appear. Towering antenna arrays rose like mechanical forests. Long runways stretched across the upper surface, perfectly aligned with mathematical precision.

The body of the structure carried the quiet elegance of engineering perfected over centuries.

It was a monument to the art of war, representing the epitome of the technology humanity had achieved on Earth—a fusion of mathematics, physics, metallurgy, and mechanics refined across generations of technological advancement.

Only at the end of its slow emergence did its true identity reveal itself.

An aircraft carrier.

A nuclear-powered aircraft carrier.

One of humanity’s greatest war machines.

A floating fortress capable of carrying an entire air force upon its deck while crossing the endless oceans of Earth.

Vector appeared on the runway of the aircraft carrier…

Then—

“System… scan this,” Vector commanded, his voice barely a whisper against the crashing waves.

And the same sensation of energy rose briefly once again, indicating that the System was using its Scanning function.

Just a moment afterward, the System’s response—its notification—echoed in his mind again, as the System interface began displaying a series of technology trees unlocking.

[System Notification]

Scanning…

[Target Identified: Nuclear-Powered Aircraft Carrier — Nimitz-Class]

[Technological Structure Analyzed]

[Scanning Complete]

[Technology Added to System Database]

What followed was not like the rifle.

The rifle had been a single thread.

This was a tapestry.

The information did not arrive in an orderly sequence. It surged. It flooded outward from the carrier like water released from behind a dam—too vast and too dense to arrive in any single coherent stream.

Vector felt the System processing it all—not struggling, never struggling—but expanding, broadening, and deepening its capacity to receive and record as the sheer volume of what the carrier contained made itself known.

[System Notification]

[Analysis complete.]

………

All data stored within System Database.

What the aircraft carrier had contributed was not a line.

It was an entire library.

Many distinct branches of human technological knowledge—each one representing not merely a set of techniques, but an entire discipline. Centuries of accumulated study, failure, refinement, and breakthrough had been compressed into information and transferred wholesale into a System that could now reconstruct any of it on demand.

Nuclear energy.Jet propulsion.Advanced metallurgy.Weapons engineering.Electronic detection.Communication science.Industrial manufacturing at scale.

…

……

All of it—

From a single object.

From one scan.

One command given to a System that had processed the request and returned centuries of collective human achievement.

It had single-handedly opened nearly all of the technology tree within the System’s Database. Much of the knowledge was now at his fingertips. Some of it had been gained directly from the scan, but a large portion of it consisted of derivative knowledge that appeared as a result of the technology tree being unlocked.

With a few more simulations of advanced technological products, the entire technological database of Earth could now be fully unlocked.

Then, without much delay, he began to simulate many products he knew, remembered, or had seen.

He even created the CERN-operated LHC—the Large Hadron Collider—so that even if something remained locked, it could be unlocked with this largest and most powerful particle accelerator: a 27-kilometer ringed tunnel capable of accelerating particles to near the speed of light within a vacuum-like environment.

This instantly unlocked the remaining database.

Now nothing remained in a locked state.

The Simulation function of the System—though it was not originally part of the System’s functions but had been added, or gifted, by the Will of the Void—had erased all the arduous work that would otherwise have been required to unlock the System’s database.

A godlike function capable of transferring him to a sub-dimension, where along with the effect of time dilation he could also create objects out of empty air. And these were real objects, not imaginary constructs.

Yet there was one regret.

He could not take these objects out of the Simulation Space.

No matter how real they appeared here, they remained confined within this dimension. They could not be carried back into the real world.

Still—

Realizing that he could construct items out of the void and then scan them through the Scanning Function, Vector felt as though an entire universe of possibilities had opened before him.

Realizing this, his ambition instantly soared toward the sky.

His mind immediately leapt to something far grander than firearms or aircraft carriers.

Spaceships.

Machines he had once seen only in movies during his previous life on Earth.

Starships from Star Wars and Star Trek. Vessels capable of crossing the vast darkness between stars, traveling from one star field to another.

Images flashed through his mind.

The Millennium Falcon, the legendary fast ship.

The sleek and agile Starfighters.

And the colossal Executor, the Super Star Destroyer—often called a mothership or command ship—so enormous that entire fleets could operate under its command.

If he could scan such machines…

Would it not be possible to turn humanity’s dream of traveling through the stars into reality?

The idea made his heart race.

Without hesitation, Vector immediately tried to construct one.

“Simulation… construct Millennium Falcon.”

He waited.

Nothing happened.

The vast ocean remained unchanged. The aircraft carrier beneath his feet stood exactly as before, waves crashing quietly against its metallic hull.

Frowning slightly, he tried again.

“Construct Starfighter.”

Once again—

Nothing.

The Simulation Space remained exactly as it had been a moment earlier. Vector still stood upon the runway of the aircraft carrier, the endless ocean stretching around him, but no new object appeared.

His brows slowly furrowed.

Why?

Everything else had appeared without difficulty. The aircraft carrier. The Large Hadron Collider. Both had been constructed instantly.

Then why not these ships?

A thought surfaced in his mind.

Perhaps the System’s Scanning Function had a daily limit.

They had rarely used the function before—only occasionally when scanning captured weapons. It was possible that the System allowed only a limited number of scans within a day.

Perhaps today’s quota had already been exhausted.

However—

The next System notification immediately erased that speculation. The reason was made crystal clear.

So that was the reason.

The aircraft carrier and the Large Hadron Collider had appeared because the System already possessed Earth’s technological knowledge.

Vector slowly exhaled.

Even with this limitation…

What he had just unlocked was already enough to change the fate of an entire world.
