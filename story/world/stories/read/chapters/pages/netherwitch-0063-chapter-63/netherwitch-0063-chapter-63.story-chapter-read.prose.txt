Of course, this wasn’t ice cream as the mortals on Earth knew it. To start, the Cloud Island Wilderness didn’t have strawberries. In fact, there was no equivalent to the strawberry in all the netherworld. A great loss to be sure. Therefore, to create this strawberry flavor, three berries had been cooked and blended to create a color and taste that were similar to the original.

Milk, fortunately, was a substance the netherworld had in abundance. Phantasmal beasts often echoed the creatures of Earth and mammals had been around for millions of years. Though there were no cows native to the Cloud Island Wilderness, they had imported several.

With these two ingredients, the right skills, and a lot of trial and error, the chef had successfully created Nether Frost.

Sylvia took her cone, ki flowing into the frozen scoops to soothe the fragile essence. Experimentally, the petite witch extended her pink tongue. A rich, creamy flavor combined with a sweet tartness. There was a subtle wrongness to the ‘strawberry’, but not an unpleasant one.

“You are so adorable,” Emmy praised, accepting her own cone. “And there can be no doubt, green is very much my color.”

She should’ve known better. Esmeralda shared Emily’s predisposition, which was to always choose the outfit for Sylvia first and herself second.

Potions, Talismans, and Dust

Magical Potions

A magical potion is a volatile substance which contains a spell. This is distinct from alchemical potions, such as mana potions, which have an essence useful for the body or the soul. By common convention, most potions are liquid. They can, however, take the form of a solid or a gas.

All magical potions are constructed using three components: a base, a primer, and a stabilizer. In some cases, a single material may serve multiple roles. In rarer cases, a potion might have several bases, primers, or stabilizers, which may be added during different parts of the brewing process.

To create a potion, the brewer always begins with a base. The next step is to bind the spell or add a stabilizer. To bind a spell, the magic is chanted over the cauldron, willfully deprived of the necessary energy. The runes and intent are then stirred or folded into liquid. Generally speaking, the spell should be cast thirteen times for every dose of effect.

After that, the remaining ingredients are added. The stabilizer is used to reduce the volatility of the potion, helping to hold the spell and prevent evaporation of the essence. The primer is the fuel. This serves to drive the contained magic.

All potions decay. Specialized potion bottles are, therefore, used to prevent or slow this process.

Talisman

A talisman is any stable object that contains a spell. The main distinction between potions and talismans is their volatility. To be considered a talisman, the object must endure for at least a month without any specialized containment mechanism.

The most common way to produce a talisman is to first brew a potion, then to absorb that potion in a phantasmal substance. Most often, this is achieved with ‘talisman paper’. However, talismans are not limited to paper. They can also take the form of gems, statues, seeds or other objects.

The invitation letters sent by the Starlight Nether Witch Academy were talismans. First, sheets of talisman paper were dipped in various potions. Then these sheets were forged together into a collective unit.

Elemental Dust

Elemental dusts are generic, consumable ether sources. Though not a potion, elemental dust is likewise a volatile substance which is often stored in potion bottles. This ether can be accessed by opening the bottle, or by drawing it from a specialized container using the mage’s mana.

Dusts are incredibly easy to create. Simply place a common, elemental material in a powerful furnace, then expose it to intense ether. This will cause the essence to supersaturate, then disintegrate into a simpler structure. The challenge lies after. Ordinary furnaces cannot contain dust. As a result, the substance rapidly evaporates into ether.

Thus, dusts can only be produced in an alchemical furnace.

Almost all the dusts sold are for the elements: space, causality, soul, and psychic. For primordial ethers, the relative cost compared to a source just isn’t worth it. Therefore, primordial dusts are only used by the military. Similarly, advanced element materials are typically too precious to render into dust.

As for the elements blood and void? They have their own obstacles. Blood element dusts generally aren’t produced because the blood of high-class phantasms is not only easy to obtain, but easy to transport. As for void dust, it has a nasty tendency to phase straight through any bottle or furnace. The need for highly specialized equipment, therefore, makes it rare and expensive.
