Romulus followed the blood spears in with cold predation and watched the Wardens work to parry or dodge the hurled projectiles. Naturally, they could only do so within limits, given the sheer number he’d made, and of the dozen he’d fired, at least three made impact with each of them. Cries of pain echoed from both Knights as they were pierced, and Romulus took ruthless advantage.

The female Warden was first, and he charged in to trade blows with her using Lightsbane. The woman’s broadsword met the perfect edge of his runeblade in a scream of metal and flash of sparks, and Romulus met her look of determined fury with cold focus. Killing the Wardens was a calculated sacrifice: he needed levels and power. Preserving them would be his ultimate objective, but pragmatism was the fruit of success, and these two ‘Elites’ were his ticket toward greater power.

More sword exchanges followed the first, and Romulus could feel the difference in power with each blow. The Warden was wounded, but she was by no means weak, and her blows rattled his arms like he were being struck by an anvil. Lightsbane’s nature was his advantage, for the blade was all but impervious to conventional damage based on what Mortarius had told him.

She was also just plain better than him at swordplay.

Her blade slashed with staggering speed toward his face, and even moving at maximum speed with his Vampiric senses at full exertion, she managed to slice open his cheek and jaw with the tip of her sword. Romulus bared his fangs at the pain when his hemoforce dropped in relation to the wound, and he instinctively reached up to touch his face—pulling away his hand to look at his blood, which was faintly interwoven with threads of silver.

A snarl of fury mixed with pain left his lips, and Romulus saw the Warden smile at him grimly.

Instead of letting fury consume him, he reengaged with a surge of cold, predatory focus.

Three more rapid exchanges of good steel against enchanted runemetal echoed within the tunnel before Romulus found his opening. After their third echoing clash, his left hand rose from Lightsbane’s hilt, and he swept his hand outward with a surge of Willpower and Intent, draining his hemoforce to manipulate the blood still pooling on the ground.

The vital liquid flooded under the two Wardens’ feet like an oil slick.

The female Warden stumbled when her right leg, pierced by the first of his blood spears already, failed under her due to the blood. Romulus didn’t hesitate, and instead dove in to slam his left hand, curled into a spiked fist, against her cheek in a full-force punch. Her lack of helmet cost her dearly, and his fist tore through her flesh in a spray of viscera.

The Warden, to her credit, stifled the instinctive scream at the wound and came at him with desperate ferocity. Her blade slammed against Lightsbane hard enough to push him back, and her clear advantage in levels showed itself in the force of the blow. Her right leg wobbled, the destroyed right side of her face bled crimson, and Romulus could see her partner attempting to flank him already.

Romulus didn’t hesitate.

His left hand rose, and he burnt hemoforce to manipulate the blood into diagonal blades that slammed into the Wardens’ shins. Only two pierced the male Warden’s sabatons, but it was enough to stagger his advance—and Romulus launched himself forward again when the woman cried out in pain when her left leg was pierced.

Lightsbane swept up from below in an arcing slice, and he caught her off-balance.

The Revenant Runeblade bit through her vambrace like it were made of paper, and severed her sword-hand midway through her forearm. Lightsbane pulsed in his hand, and with a surge of understanding sourced from the sword itself, Romulus truncated the sword’s momentum with a burst of Strength and stabbed it forward to punch through the woman’s throat.

Her eyes widened in shock, and he tore the sword out sideways to partially decapitate her while panting at the pain burning on his slashed face.

The male Warden lurched half a second later when a darkness-saturated arrow slammed into his back and punched partially out of his chest. Romulus threw himself at the man without hesitation when he was off-balance, their bodies colliding with a thud of steel and flesh. He slammed his fangs into the man’s throat without hesitation the moment they crashed together, and their combined weight pulled them down into the gore and blood lathering the stone.

The Warden’s sword screeched off his warplate desperately as they fell, and in response, Romulus tore out his throat a second later.

The male Warden lay writhing on the ground while holding his throat, blade forgotten, and stared at Romulus in a mix of horror and disbelief. Instead of offering a funny quip or savage one-liner, the Sanguine Revenant threw himself forward and gripped the Warden’s hands with supernatural strength, tearing them away and slamming his fangs and mouth home against the blood-pumping wound.

Sweet bliss, power, and sheer pleasure rolled through Romulus like lightning as he drank deep, and he felt his face rapidly healing itself while his hemoforce roared back to full capacity. The Warden’s kicking legs echoed through the tunnel as he spasmed and twitched under Romulus until, ten seconds later, he finally went still—twitching only once more when the fangs that ended his life were pulled free.

Romulus straddled him like a predator atop the kill, and took a moment to relish in the primal joy of the kill.

He had never known greater satisfaction than he had in that moment.

“I dedicate this blood to the Dark Lady,” he said without full awareness of his own words. “Gloria Domina ex Tenebrae!”

The words left his lips like a prayer and offering both, and he felt something like an electric touch on his cheeks when he spoke them. Power and heat suffused his body, and he felt black flames roll across him in a wave when he spoke.

Romulus tapped ‘YES’ and took a moment to push himself to his feet.

“Allocate all points to Strength,” he said while licking his lips, and turned toward the lone guardswoman left in the tunnel. The change took place immediately, and he felt the subtle increase of his power as he locked his gaze on the trembling woman.

“D-don’t come any c-closer!” she said while her blade shook in her hands. “I… I… Please! I’m just a… I didn’t want… I don’t—”

“Enough,” Romulus said firmly, cutting off her words while lifting his Runeblade and peering at it. Lightsbane pulsed happily in his grip, and he reached up to pull away the blood and gore from his face with an application of Sanguimancy—discarding it in the tunnel thereafter.

“Wow,” Kate’s voice said as she approached a moment later, tucking away her bow and staring at him with wide eyes. “You were a total monster, Mister Big Bad Revenant-King. That was scary. I can’t tell if I should be nervous or excited.”

Romulus smiled at her wryly and sheathed Lightsbane.

“Neither. That fight was…” he paused and let out a satisfied sigh, “...something else, for me too. I awakened a new ability as well, and gained two levels.”

“You are so lucky. I gained some levels, too, but I’m still behind you. I’ll catch up eventually,” Kate said with a wistful sigh, and turned back to the terrified guardswoman. “So. What do you want to do with her?”

Romulus also looked at the woman, and she flinched when he did.

“We’ll have to kill her,” he said regretfully. “She’ll go screaming for help if we don’t, and it’s not like we’re looking to win brownie points at the moment.”

“Killing her may not be necessary,” Mortarius put in suddenly, appearing from Romulus’ left and peering down at the woman. “She may yet prove useful.”

“Are you about to drop some ghost wisdom on us?” Kate asked curiously.

Mortarius’ lips twitched into a slight smile, and he inclined his head.

“Romulus has yet to make a Thrall, and this mortal could prove very useful as a test subject. More than that, the information she holds could be quite useful in pursuing your objectives,” the former Autarch said while turning to look at Romulus. “The Priestess and Paladin are unlikely to be anywhere easily accessible by you or Kate, not at this time of night. This woman may be able to assist you in planning your next move, and more than that, she may be able to aid you in creating more thralls.”

The guardswoman looked between Kate, Romulus, and the thin air they were looking at with open alarm—but Romulus ignored that when he turned back to her scared features.

“That’s an interesting notion,” he said while bracing his left hand on Lightsbane, and his right on his hip. “I never even considered that.”

“This is why education is so important, Your Majesty,” Kate said with a sly grin.

Romulus didn’t dignify her teasing with an acknowledgement, and instead stepped closer to the guardswoman.

“What is your name?” he asked her directly. “If you answer my questions, I may have a condition to let you live.”

The woman’s eyes widened, and she licked her chapped lips before swallowing.

“M’name be Bonnie,” she said after only a moment’s hesitation. “Bonnie Gray.”

“Bonnie,” Romulus repeated carefully. “I take it you heard what I said when this little dance started?”

“I ‘eard,” she replied with another nervous lick of her lips. “Yer the Rev’nant-King. Them what was in that big castle.”

“Not quite,” he said with a faint smile at her words. She reminded him a little of Tom and Jerry, though that was no surprise. “I am the former Revenant-King’s successor. My predecessor has already passed beyond life.”

“I—I see,” she stammered. “What’s th’dition fer me not to die, then?”

Romulus raised his eyebrow at her question and glanced at Mortarius for guidance.

The ghost smiled faintly in response and answered the unspoken question.

“You will cut your wrist, and offer her some of your blood. She must give an oath of loyalty to you in the Dark Lady’s name, and then drink. If she is truly willing, the oath will enforce a Thrall compact between you both, via the Dark Lady’s power. If she is insincere… well, you will know it if you see it.”

Romulus nodded at Mortarius’ words and turned back to Bonnie.
