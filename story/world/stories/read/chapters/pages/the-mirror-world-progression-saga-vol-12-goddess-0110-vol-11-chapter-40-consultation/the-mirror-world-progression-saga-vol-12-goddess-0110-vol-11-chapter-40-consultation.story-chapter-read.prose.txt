As soon as she felt it, Azure popped into her head. “Felt it, too. Oh, shit and thunder, I was just having a good day, too…”

“It’ll be okay, Blueberry,” Samantha replied. “You’ll do fine. And I’ll do great.”

Azure scoffed. “I’d tell you to watch the hubris, but that ship has sailed, hasn’t it? In any case, perhaps I shouldn’t go.”

Samantha was brain-smacked. “What do you mean?! But we always go together…”

“Samantha, please! Not everything has to be some sentimental romp together. There are things we’ve done alone. While I wouldn’t trade the time we had together in that awful forest for anything, I don’t want to repeat its like. Besides, Ozzy warned me off the third trip, if you recall. Said my chances were pretty much dogshit.”

“That was then, this is now, so I think-”

“Do I really need any further boosts, Chestnut? I seem to be as powerful as I need to be for the purposes you have for me. Individually, I’m as strong as any of your ‘cards,’ am I not?”

“Yeah, you’re a card, alright…”

“I own what I’m meant to: the sky, half thanks to Deathsting. We’ve obliterated our enemy’s chances there already. What else do I need to do?”

“I-... Okay, let’s consult Ozzy. If she says the same thing as before, you’re off the hook. If things are more encouraging, we will go together. Deal?”

There was the feel in her mind of Azure’s glare… and then she sighed. “Fine. Deal. I’m sure Fate can work with that, mmn?”

“I’m the Goddess of Fate, wife!”

“Don’t give me that — you’re clearly biased on this matter, wife!”

Rolling her eyes and snickering, Samantha disconnected, then began dematerializing to Heavenpeak, communicating her intent indirectly to Azure.

While waiting, Samantha bounced on back to the level-ups she achieved immediately after finishing Glae’s quest, which were a bit interrupted by the ensuing events.

[Bard] going to Level 4 had improved [Musical Talent]... which simply added a line:

You add +2 Effective Levels, as a (Talent) modifier, to the excellence of any musical effort. This includes chances of being heard at further distances while performing.

Samantha shrugged. Okay.

Bard also had one waiting spell selection, of which she struggled to find anything of use, being level 15 in a powerful spellcasting class already. Not to mention, in any group scenario, higher-level bardic classes were already around. [Bard] at level 4 was actually pretty useful in itself, as when it was added as a multiclass to social matters with [Goddess], it was now adding +2 instead of +1 due to the rounding up aspect of multiclass efforts.

She finally found something. Charismatic Aura was hypothetically useful, as Charisma levels stacked. She grabbed it and called it a day on ‘bardic-centric’ time-wasting.

“Fuck [Bard],” she muttered, giggling. “Oh, how I love saying it…”

She also had a [Magineer] class skill to pick, or [Wizard], technically. She decided to part from [Sympathetic Targeting] focus, figuring it was good enough as it was. She selected her level in something else that was pretty specific, but pretty relevant lately, and synergistic to her specialties.

Countermagic

Adds +1 Effective Levels to any attempt to counter or dispel any other spell.

There we go. Stacks with bonuses from [Deific Counterspell]. Huzzah! Maybe it’ll set up Ozzy? Is there an ultimate mage duel in my future to boost this? Heh. Knowing her, I’ll get the fucking opposite.

Momentarily, Samantha was on Heavenpeak, and she zipped over to an open meadow, with Azure quickly appearing next to her. They were both practically bowled over by wild cabbits begging for food. Sighing, Samantha petted them for a few moments as they crowded around her, then told them to go find the goodies she had hidden elsewhere. That got rid of the little buggers very quickly.

Azure still had a small black-furred, blue-eyed cabbit hanging out on the top of her head, for a wonder more curious than hungry.

Comical with the cabbit there, Azure crossed her arms as she gazed cooly at Samantha and said, “Well, let’s summon our girl, then. Knowing my luck, she’ll tell me I’ve got almost a hundred percent chance of surviving her next hellscape torture sim.”

“That’s the spirit!” Samantha called cheerily. She promptly summoned Ozzy.

The gremlin lady appeared next to Azure in her prototypical steampunk garb, her goggles down over her eyes, and pointed at the cabbit on the Naugite’s head. She pulled out a notebook and began scribbling, nodding to herself. “Evolutionary principles breeding abnormal behavioral sapience… yes, yes, very interesting stuff…”

The cabbit blinked down at her. “Green Sammy?”

Ozzy did a sputtering double-take. “Wh-what?! Excuse you, no! I’m Ozzy!”

The cabbit blinked and nodded firmly. “Green Sammy.”

Ozzy deflated exaggeratedly, shoulders sagging downward. “Egads.”

Samantha cleared her throat. “Ozzy, we were about to take the third trip? We’re consulting you beforehand.”

“Ah!” Ozzy brightened up, tossing the notebook and pen over her shoulder and into the grass. She rubbed her hands together. “The third and final trip, then! Well, there’s technically a fourth, but let’s not talk about it!”

Samantha narrowed her eyes. “Ozzy, if you’re suddenly bringing up the fourth, and saying the third is the final, I’ve got to ask for details.”

Ozzy blew air out of her mouth and lifted her goggles. “I don’t have much information for you. It is closed to you — at least in this reality — and that’s a good thing. The door is a very late-stage opener. Very, very, very, very, very late stage, I mean.”

Samantha frowned thoughtfully. “Late?”

“Yes, late. I mean, like, late late.”

“Too late?”

“Precisely.”

“As in, after an Iteration has already failed?”

“I think so.”

“Perhaps it has something to do with the Fortuneteller,” Azure offered.

“Good guess!” the cabbit suddenly cried, clapping its hands.

Everyone stared at it silently, eyes wide — almost everyone, that is, as Azure more so tried to stare at it with her eyes wide. Sadly, her head was in the way.

After another moment, the cabbit looked puzzled and asked, “What’s a Fortuneteller?”

Samantha breathed a sigh of relief. Sweet Heavens, thank you, it’s just a dumb cabbit being random and dumb. I don’t need new weirdness in my life, thanks. “Anyway, yes, good point, Azure. Hmm. So, I likely don’t have access because I’m not late, and maybe I’m too all-in. I either win or die outright, no half-measures.”

Ozzy smiled at her. “Something like that, surely.”

“Come on, Ozzy, you must know something with a reply like that!”

“Oh, I think you’re just mistaking my innate mysteriousness for insider knowledge. I mean, it’s basically programmed in, whether I know anything or not.”

Samantha glowered at her. “Okay, fine. So the third challenge is the last relevant one to the actual contest in front of me.”

“I do believe so, yes!”

Nodding slowly, Samantha glanced at Azure, then looked down at the grass.

Your move, wife.

Azure cleared her throat. “I, uh… have a question.” As Ozzy hopped around to turn to her with a smug smile, Azure grimaced and continued, “Do you advise I go with her this time? Previously, you said it was a low percentage move that would probably fuck me up or something.”

Ozzy nodded, shrugged her hands out wide. “Oh, you absolutely shouldn’t go, dear.”

Azure blinked. “Really?” She breathed a sigh of relief. “So, it’s the same.”

“No.”

“Huh?”

“Well, it’s not that you are low percentage by capability any longer. It’s more that your heart isn’t in it. Without the desire, your chances fall from the typical 75-80% to a big, fat goose egg.”

Azure looked stricken. “I see.” She looked away, her expression sour, as if she were sick.

“It’s alright! Not everyone wants an adventure. Your soul is weary — even your Inner You is weary. In this, you’ve aligned with each other as never before, full of regrets, full of loathing and shame and guilt. Right now, at this pivotal point, you are wounded deeply, requiring healing and sanctuary more than the constant, continual stress we crave. To put it plainly, our souls are not twins of radiance, but near opposites. You and she are on entirely different roads. You should walk out yours along the peaceful trail of recuperation as she races down the wild, unkept path as a trailblazer, so you may meet again where they do, both of you attaining what they require before the final road you’ll walk together, hand-in-hand. You’ll both be better for it.”

As Ozzy began speaking, Azure was getting angrier, her fist clenched, tail flicking, jaw set, about ready to blow, and maybe punch out Ozzy on the spot. But as the latter words set in, Azure’s face changed, and realization dawned. By the end, Azure’s hand was unclenched, and she met Ozzy's eyes for a long moment.

She’s right. Heavens, she is… of course. What happened to her, who she found out she was, still wears on her terribly; it tears up her very heart and spirit. I can’t expect that to unwind so fast. It’s a lot to come to terms with.

Ozzy only continued smiling as Azure stared, with one little nod of encouragement.

Azure finally swallowed, her eyes watery, as she subtly shifted her eyes away from the gremlin. “I understand now. Thank you.”

“Yes, Ozzy,” Samantha agreed solemnly. “Thank you for that. From the bottom of my heart, I am grateful.”

Ozzy beamed with her eyes closed, rocking on her heels. “No problem, boss!”
