Chapter 69 — The Right Pace

ARC IV: THE THINGS THAT STAY

SYNOPSIS: Three days after the war. Livia visits Alondra — not as a therapist, just as someone checking in. The Verdant Expanse is gray from the window. Some things are said that don’t need to be said in a session to be said properly. Then Livia sits alone with her notepad and writes on a page that has been blank for nine months.

EAST WING, B&B — AFTERNOON

Livia knocked and Alondra said come in and that was the whole ceremony of it.

She was sitting up in the chair by the window — not in the bed, which meant she’d decided she was done with the bed regardless of what the healers had recommended. She looked like someone who had given away a very large thing and was now doing the ordinary work of continuing to exist, which was its own kind of labor.

Livia sat across from her. Not the therapist’s chair. Just the chair.

"Can you see it from here?" Livia asked.

Alondra turned toward the window. Over the guild hall’s eastern wall, the Verdant Expanse — gray, all the way to wherever the eye stopped making distinctions. A line of green at the very far edge where the damage had been less complete. And at the center, the pre-server tree, taller than it had been three days ago, catching the afternoon light in a way that was different from everything around it.

"Yes," Alondra said.

They sat with that for a moment.

"I saw it on the way back," Livia said. "Before I reached the guild hall. There’s more gray than I expected."

"I know." Alondra’s voice was even. Not performing evenness — just even. "Ninety-four percent. Tobren told me this morning. He’s been out there since the first day cataloguing what’s left."

"He would be."

"He left a report outside my door. I think he wanted me to see the numbers." She looked at the window. "He was being kind. In his way."

Livia didn’t say anything. There wasn’t anything to say yet.

"Miles came yesterday," Alondra said.

"I know. He told me."

"He said ’I told you this wasn’t something I wanted from you.’ And then he left. And then he came back and said ’Thank you anyway.’ And left again."

"Both things at once," Livia said.

"Yes." Alondra was quiet for a moment. "I’ve been thinking about the ’anyway.’"

"What about it?"

"It means he knows it wasn’t what he wanted and he’s thanking me for it anyway. Because it mattered. Because those two things don’t cancel each other." She looked at her hands. "I didn’t realize until he said it that I was waiting for someone to hold both of those at once."

Livia thought about the six years she’d known from their sessions. The zone Alondra had described to Caelric. The way she’d said it — it wasn’t even a significant conversation at the time. I thought he saw me. Not the healer. Not the Saint of Bloom Lv.91. Me. The weight of that had never been that she’d done something monstrous. The weight of it was that she’d trusted someone completely and the trust had been weaponized against a person she cared about.

She didn’t say any of that out loud. Alondra knew all of it already.

"The Expanse is gray," Livia said instead. Plain. "That’s real. It doesn’t go away because the reasons were good."

Alondra looked at her.

"No," she said. "It doesn’t."

"And what you did yesterday —" Livia paused. "It mattered. That’s also real."

"Both things at once," Alondra said. A small echo of what she’d just said about Miles.

"Yes."

Alondra turned back to the window. The gray expanse, and the one tree at the center that was taller.

"I spent years trying to figure out how to be in the same room as what I broke," she said. "I kept waiting for a way to carry it that didn’t feel like carrying it. There isn’t one. You just carry it differently as time goes on." A pause. "Yesterday I had something the guild needed. I didn’t think about six years. I didn’t think about Miles or what I’d told Caelric or whether this was the moment the scales balanced. I just had something and they needed it and I gave it." She was quiet for a moment. "That’s the first time in six years I’ve done something without the weight of what I did before attached to it."

That’s the thing. Not that she paid a debt. She stopped calculating the debt long enough to just act. That’s different.

"How does that feel?" Livia asked. Not as a therapist. Just as someone asking.

Alondra considered it seriously, the way she considered everything seriously.

"Strange," she said. "Like something I’ve been holding for a long time is a slightly different shape now. Not gone. Just different."

"That sounds right," Livia said.

They sat with the afternoon light for a while. The gray expanse and the one tree. Somewhere in the guild hall below, someone was making tea — the smell of it drifted up, warm and ordinary.

"Is the door still open?" Alondra asked.

Direct. Precise. Alondra had always asked the exact question she wanted answered.

"Always," Livia said.

Not as a therapist. As herself.

Alondra nodded once. Something in her settled.

"Go," she said. "You have other people to check on."

"I do," Livia agreed. She stood. "Rest."

"I’m resting," Alondra said, in the tone of someone who was absolutely not resting but had decided to let it go. "Come back when you have time."

"I will".

Alondra’s expression did something small that was adjacent to a smile.

PLANNING ROOM — 16:00

The planning room was quiet when she came back to it. The lamp on. The armchair in its usual position, steady gray. Her notepad on the desk exactly where she’d left it.

She sat in the therapist’s chair and opened the notepad to the back. The pages she’d never written on. Nine months of sessions documented from the front, working forward. The back had been empty because the back was for her, and she had not been ready for the back.

She wrote at the top:

Patient: L.M.Q.

Then she started.

I remember everything.

Not everything everything. But everything that matters. My parents. The apartment on the third floor with the window that never closed properly. My professor who called my thesis a cry for help in Times New Roman. The bakery. The croissants. The specific sound the motorcycle made before I saw it. Stepping off the curb.

After that: nothing. Not darkness. Not pain. A gap that becomes Aethelgard.

Nine months here and the memories of here are more textured than the last two years of my real life felt. I don’t know what that means. I don’t know if it means something about the world or something about me.

Margo. The laundromat on Ganesha Street — the one with the broken third machine that nobody ever fixed, that everyone knew not to use but tourists always tried. She mentioned it once and I knew immediately. Not because she described it. Just because I knew it. The specific smell of that place. The plastic chairs. I used to go there every Sunday and I knew Margo from those Sundays and I didn’t say anything when she said laundromat because I didn’t know what to do with the fact that I knew.

She came here for me. Which means she knows something about what happened after the gap. Which means someone is waiting.

Someone sent a message. Eleven seconds. Please wake up.

The question I have been not writing down: do I want to go back?

I’m writing it down now.

Do I want to go back?

I don’t know. And I’m choosing to let that be honest instead of making it into something more resolved. I don’t know what back looks like. I don’t know what is waiting there. I know that in nine months here I have felt more present than I felt in two years before the accident. That is either a reason to stay or a reason to worry. Probably both.

I am not going to resolve this today. I am noting that I finally wrote the question down. That is enough for today.

She turned to a new section of the same page.

Miles.

Patient: Miles Vexley. Progress: 32%.

Three days ago he told fifty-four people the truth about Aethelgard, about Meridian, about being outvoted 3-1 eight years ago. He said I was wrong in front of everyone. And the number is 32%.

The same as before the war.

I checked it twice.

I’ve been sitting with this and I think I understand it. What Miles did in that meeting was right and necessary and it cost him something real. But it was Miles Vexley, guild leader, doing the job he should have done earlier. The Therapist Insight doesn’t measure how much of his past he’s revealed. It measures how much of himself he lets someone see without strategy.

He hasn’t done that. Not fully. Not with me.

There is a difference between what someone reveals and what they let you reach. Miles manages the distance between those two things very carefully. He always has.

Maybe the number will move eventually. Maybe 32% is what’s possible when the therapist is also someone the patient is responsible for protecting. Those two things might always be in tension. I don’t know yet.

Maybe,

she writes, and then stops.

Maybe I am not the wrong therapist for him. Maybe I am the right one at the wrong speed. And the speed is not mine to control.

She closed the notepad.

Not finished. The question was still open. Miles was still 32%. The gap was still a gap.

But she had written on the page that had been empty for months. That was something.

The armchair sat in its usual gray and said nothing, which was the armchair doing its job.

Tomorrow: Alaric’s session. Nyx the day after. Bron has been asking every morning.

The door is still open. I’m still here.

Miles found her near midnight.

He had something in his hand — a small folded piece of paper, the texture of something that existed at the margins where the game’s systems met whatever was underneath them.

"From T.S.," he said. "About an hour ago. Channels I didn’t know he had."

"What does it say?"

"The Contract is safe. The door is locked from the inside." He folded it again. "And then: ’she chose well.’"

Livia didn’t ask who ’she’ was.

"What does that mean to you?" she asked. "The door locked from the inside."

He thought about it. Stood in the lamplight with the specific care of someone who had built the thing being described.

"It means whatever the third Contract is doing, it’s doing it by choice," he said. "Not because the system is holding it. Because something in it decided this was where it needed to be."

A Contract that moves on its own. A pre-server tree that keeps growing. A woman in a gray robe who ended up here when she wasn’t supposed to and chose to stay.

The system doesn’t have categories for any of us. Maybe that’s the point.

The armchair sat in the lamplight, steady and gray and ready. Livia let herself feel the warmth of a room that belonged to her.
