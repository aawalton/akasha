Kivamus exhaled, thinking about the decision for a moment, then smiled at her. “Are you free for the next few hours?”

Syrene snorted. “I already told you I’ve been bored these days, since I don't have any more losuvil to process. Yeah, I have more than enough time. Why?”

“Good. Then I think this is the right time for us to talk about something you need to know. But before that, go and lock the outer door. I don’t want us to be disturbed, and that information is very, very sensitive so we cannot afford for anyone else to hear about it even in passing.”

Syrene frowned for a moment, then followed the order and returned back to her chair. “Well? What is it? I assume Teacher Gorsazo also knows about it, and probably even Sir Duvas?”

Kivamus nodded. “Yeah, those two are the only people who know about it, apart from me. And now you will be the third one I’m trusting with this information. But before I tell you, I want you to give me your word that you will not tell anything about this or talk about what I am going to say to anyone who doesn’t already know. That means you can talk about this matter with me, Gorsazo, or Duvas, but no one else—not even your mother. Can you do that?”

“Uh... I guess so...”

“I’m serious, Syrene. If it gets out, it could be a matter of life and death, for me, for you, and even for the village.”

Syrene looked surprised now. “Oh... Of course. I promise... I won’t tell another soul. Not even Lucem or Ma. Now tell me! What is it that needs this kind of secrecy?”

Kivamus took a deep breath, thinking how to put it delicately. “The thing is, I... uh... I am not exactly the person you know. I mean, yes, I am Kivamus Ralokaar, but I also hold the memories of a life lived in another world. A world different from Eranityn, with different geography, different history, and different people. That world—called Earth—was centuries ahead of this place in technology level, and that is why I know about so many things which seem impossible to people here.”

Syrene looked at both their faces for a moment, like she was checking if this was a joke, then she grinned and pumped her fist. “I knew it! I was right after all!”

"What...?" This time it was Kivamus’ turn to be surprised. “What do you mean? How could you know about it? I haven’t told anyone else apart from Gorsazo and Duvas!”

Syrene smiled confidently. “Oh, what is that phrase you like to say, uh... circumstantial evidence. Yeah, that’s it! I've had my doubts since the time you started designing even simple things like wheelbarrows and gromas. I mean, yeah, you are a noble of high birth and you certainly had a lot better education than I did, but I kinda know your teacher, you know?”

Gorsazo snorted. “Yeah, you know his teacher alright.”

Syrene continued with a grin. “After studying from both Teacher Gorsazo and you for a few months, I realized that the knowledge gap between you two was far too vast to be a coincidence. You also kept using unknown words and phrases, and mentioned ideas and tactics not related to technology to Hudan, Feroy, and others. That meant either you have a freakishly sharp mind, or you had some help—like memories from another world, as weird as that sounds to say. There were other things too which added to my intuition. And finally, you were the one who gave me all the instructions and ideas for how to process losuvil leaves, even if I did the grunt work. I would never have thought about that scientific method and using experimentation to find a solution without you telling me of that.”

She shrugged. “Of course, I still didn’t have any proof, but I had good suspicions that you are not exactly what you claim to be—that is, just the third son of a high noble. I knew there was some other reason, whether it was the goddess helping you with ideas in your dreams or something else. So having memories from another world is not very different from what I thought.”

It took a minute for Kivamus to overcome his surprise. He already knew that Syrene was smart, probably smarter than anyone else he knew in this world, but she had already observed him closely enough and made enough deductions to reach a conclusion which was far too close to the truth to be a coincidence. It was kind of scary how close her guess was...

He glanced at Gorsazo, who just grinned and started to laugh, before Kivamus joined him as well. Syrene looked confused for a moment, but she was also smiling.

Eventually, Kivamus looked back at her. “I know you are smart, but you have exceeded even my best expectations from you. I mean, most people in this world would probably say that I am using witchcraft, or that I am a demon, or that I am just delusional. But you didn’t just accept it immediately, you did it because you had already guessed the truth, or at least close enough.”

Gorsazo looked at her too. “I’m proud of you, Syrene.”

Syrene blushed at the praise, then smiled. “What can I say? I guess I had some good teachers to help me!”

Kivamus laughed again, happy that the revelation had gone well. “In that case, let me tell you more about that world. I’m sure you must be curious.”

“You can’t imagine how curious I am!” Syrene grinned. “I’ve been waiting for months for you to say something like this—that there was a reason you knew all that knowledge.”

Kivamus chuckled, before he launched into an explanation of his past. He told about his appearance into this world, holding the memories of lives lived in London and in Ulriga. He told about his previous job as a mechanical engineer on Earth, and what that work entailed. And then he began giving examples of the wonders of life on modern Earth.

By the time he finished his explanation, Syrene seemed beyond amazed. “You have really lived in that kind of a world? For decades? It must have been amazing! To think that a kid in that world gets the kind of education which even a princess can’t even dream of in this kingdom, and if she wants, she can go on to study in that university thing, and then even become a researcher full-time, discovering one thing after another. How fun it must be to have a full treasure trove of knowledge ready to access right in front of you on a screen... It’s difficult to even imagine it....”

Kivamus smiled. “Earth did not get to that stage by chance or instantly. It took centuries of hard work by countless smart minds who gave their everything despite all the opposition to make sure Earth reached that stage. I don’t know if Tiranat or Reslinor will even come close to that level within our lifetimes, but if smart people like you keep improving things step by step, then I am sure the future of Tiranat might not look too different from 21st-century London. Of course, we have a lot of obstacles on the way, from the threats surrounding the village, to all the greedy nobles who would sell their souls to steal that knowledge from us, but we will get there one day.”

Syrene nodded slowly. “Now I somewhat understand how difficult it must have been for you when you arrived in this world without a choice. Going from life in that immense city called London to a poor little village like Tiranat must have been beyond shocking. I don’t know if I would have been able to manage half of what you did for the village despite all the problems.”

Now it was Kivamus’ turn to grin. “What can I say? I guess I had some good advisors to help me! Duvas is an excellent supervisor, and he manages the village well enough with barely any input from me. Gorsazo is an excellent teacher and he has been teaching all the kids and adults in the village superbly, and making our people literate is something that is the foundation of getting a good future for Tiranat. Although recently, I've often been taking the role of his teacher because he's always curious to know more about Earth. But he only wants to know more so he can teach the villagers more—so they can do their jobs better and hopefully some of them can become teachers and even researchers in the future. And lastly, I have an excellent researcher who has already invented a life-saving medicine at a young age of 21.”

Syrene grinned along with Gorsazo, who smiled as well.

Kivamus added, "For the past few months, Gorsazo has been compiling some fundamental information from what I've told him about Earth for easy access as a new book—both for me, and for him to use a limited form of that information in his classes. Things which were basic knowledge on Earth even in primary or middle schools are a valuable repository here, and Gorsazo needs to know more so he can slowly introduce his classes to some primitive maths and science. He was the teacher of the Duke's son, so people won't ask too many questions about how he knows so much. Even so, he'll only start with the very basics for now."

He continued, "However, the existence of that book is top secret and only Duvas knows about it apart from us. It will probably be decades before we can tell other people about it or put that book out into the world, even if it would be better for the world to do it now, but Tiranat's safety comes before anything. The relevant point is that now you are also allowed to read that book—which is just a slowly growing collection of sheets of paper right now—and it'll help you get more understanding about some fundamental principles of science even without me teaching you directly."

"That..." Syrene looked angry for a moment before she exhaled. "I wanted to go into a rant about how you both could hide something like that from me, when it could have helped me so much in processing losuvil or maybe even inventing other things, but... I understand how sensitive such a thing is. I'm just glad that you've decided to trust me with it."

Kivamus grinned. "You've more than earned that trust with the invention of Acelos medicine. It'll probably take years before that book is anything close to complete, but you do have a way to reference information now, even if it's not as handy as a smartphone having internet access."

Syrene laughed. "True enough. At least I'm not going to be bored anytime soon..." Then she looked at Kivamus with a frown. “Actually, now that I know that I have a king's library’s worth of knowledge available here in your mind, I kinda don’t want to go to that encampment anymore... I know how important it is for us to continue making that medicine, but still...”

Kivamus smiled. “It will still take a couple of weeks until the place is ready for you and the other workers to move, so we have all the time in the world until then for you to learn more about Earth. And even then, I know it would be a waste of your talent for you to just work as a supervisor at that place, when you can do so much more. You have already been training the servants and maids in that, right?”

Syrene nodded. “I had told you earlier that there is a maid who can do most of the steps of processing the leaves nearly as well as me. Of course that was here in the manor, and it would be far more difficult to do it out there, but she and the other workers who have been helping me in that process have learned a lot already. I've made sure that no single person knows the full process as of now, but that will have to change in the future.”

“I know,” Kivamus agreed. “If you trust that maid, you can explain more of the process to her in the future. But either way, that is exactly what you need to do to take some load off your shoulders. It still means you will have to stay at the encampment for a while until the whole process is running smoothly, but once you are sure that someone trustworthy can take over the supervisory duty from you, and can ensure that the medicine is made with all the proper precautions, then you won’t have any reason to stay there anymore and can return to Tiranat after that.”

“I will still have to visit the place regularly to check that everything is being done properly, but I guess you're right,” Syrene added. “Once the workers know the process, I won’t have to look over their shoulders continuously.”

Kivamus nodded. “Good. I think it will take a few months for everything to settle down there, but after that you can move back. And it is not just for your benefit. I know that I certainly want someone to talk with about Earth, and not just for nostalgia. I need someone who can brainstorm ideas with me, and you are more than smart enough for that, even if your interests are in botany.”

Syrene smiled. “Now that I know there is a whole mountain load of knowledge that you can share, I’m not sure if I will want to keep myself limited to botany, even if that's what interests me the most right now." She grinned widely. "Well, you asked me if I was free for the next few hours, which means you are free too, right? So tell me more! I really want to know as much about life on Earth as possible.”

Kivamus grinned. “In that case, here we go!"
