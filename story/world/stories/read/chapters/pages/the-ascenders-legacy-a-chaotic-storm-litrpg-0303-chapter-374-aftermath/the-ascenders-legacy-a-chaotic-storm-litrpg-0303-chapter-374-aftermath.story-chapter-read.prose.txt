Sophran Thunderstride stumbled into the viewing deck barely conscious, his body broken, bloodied, and held together by little more than sheer stubbornness.

He looked as though he had barely made it at all.

But it was the bundle of crimson slung over his shoulder that truly stunned everyone.

“Healers!” Elder Ghaltris barked as he rushed forward, his eyes widening.

“Are you alright?” he demanded the moment he reached him, with Nymera and Ashscar only a step behind.

“I’m fine,” Sophran groaned, staggering as he dumped the body onto the floor, a pungent scent of blood wafting from it.

It was the blood transmigrant, but he was barely alive, his soul seemingly tethered to his ruined body by the glowing strand of vitality linking him to Sophran and the familiar pressed weakly against his chest.

Panting heavily, Sophran pointed at him.

The healers hesitated, but a nod from Ghaltris got them moving, hands glowing as they pressed them against the chest of an enemy to keep him from dying.

An enemy they should have finished without question.

Elder Ghaltris stared at the bizarre scene for a moment before turning back to Sophran.

“How?” he asked quietly, voicing the question on everyone’s mind.

Sophran Thunderstride was an extremely talented transmigrant, but compared to monsters like Nymera, Aodhán, and now Ashscar, he often seemed almost ordinary.

Ashscar had been saved by the storm itself, a miracle Ghaltris was still trying to wrap his mind around, and Nymera had held out long enough for High Elder Zarxan to save her.

But Sophran... no one had saved Sophran.

And yet, he had survived.

But not only that, he had returned carrying another life with him. How was that possible?

Sophran slid to the ground, his chest heaving as he fought for breath. His familiar yipped softly, crawled into his lap, and began to glow with a gentle light.

“I barely made it,” Sophran admitted, shaking his head. “The first attack blew me to bits, but Ancestral Continuance pulled me back together, and Halo’s blessings helped me survive the rest.”

He stroked the creature’s fur with trembling fingers. “When I woke up, the Calamities were gone... and this one was dying.” He gestured weakly toward Butcher.

“Halo tried healing him, and I used Living Convergence to trade him vitality, but his injuries were more extensive than anything I could heal.”

Silence settled over the deck.

Then Nymera dropped to her knees and wrapped him in a fierce hug, tears bright in her eyes.

“I’m so glad you’re alright,” she whispered. “You have no idea how glad.”

Sophran hugged her back, shutting his eyes as the weight on his shoulders finally began to ease.

“I’m glad you’re alright too.”

He glanced up at Ashscar, who looked even more different now than he once had. Gone was his pompous smirk and careless arrogance.

In its place was something calmer. Something reverent.

Almost priestly.

Even his eyes seemed older now, carrying a depth that had not been there before.

Ashscar smiled warmly. “I’m glad you’re alright too.”

Sophran nodded, but Ashscar’s gaze soon returned to the screens, his expression sobering.

“We are not out of the woods yet,” he said quietly. “Aodhán is still nowhere to be found.”

Sophran tensed once more, his gaze sharpening as he forced himself to his feet. “He’s not dead, right?”

“We don’t know—” Elder Ghaltris began, but Ashscar cut him off with a firm shake of his head.

“He’s alive. I can sense that he is, but the voice of the storm is too distant for me to pick out clearly.” He grimaced. “It’s almost muffled.”

He shook his head. “The storm is very busy. Too busy to respond to me.”

Several elders exchanged uneasy glances.

Whatever situation Aodhán was in, it was grave enough—or important enough—to command the storm’s full attention.

Just how powerful had that boy become?

“What was his last known location?” Sophran asked.

“Toward the eastern sector of the battlefield,” Ghaltris replied. “He and the old spatial transmigrant fled into chaotic space to escape the Calamities. That’s the last we saw of them.”

“Don’t worry,” Ashscar said, his tone carrying absolute certainty. “The storm will not let him die. Not so easily.”

Nymera looked at him strangely, then exhaled. “We wait, then. We wait and hope and pray. If he’s still alive, then he has to make it.”

Sophran nodded grimly.

The healers finished their work at that moment, and everyone's attention shifted to Butcher, who was groggily stirring back to consciousness.

“Bind him in null chains,” Elder Ghaltris commanded a group of elders. “And put him with the other. Sophran here has just gotten us two powerful bargaining chips.”

He turned to Ashscar and Nymera. “Maybe there’s something to learn there?”

Nymera nodded. “I’ll try to keep it in mind.”

Ashscar, however, simply shrugged. “It’s not up to me, but I can try.”

Ghaltris sighed and returned his attention to the screens, muttering a prayer under his breath.

Aodhán was the greatest driver of the Origin Plane’s expansion. Losing him would be disastrous for the Storm Sect.

Disastrous for Ghaltris personally as well, and for all the plans he had been quietly cooking up.

Sighing, he muttered, “Survive, Aodhán. Far too much depends on you.”

But as glass and lightning split the heavens across the nearest screen, Ghaltris feared his prayer might yet return unanswered.

***

Back in the alliance command center, a heavy silence loomed over the five elders as they stared at the screens in despair.

This entire war was a disaster.

Not only had they failed in their core mission of restoring balance, they had also lost too many transmigrants, skewing that imbalance even further toward catastrophe.

It was a failure unlike any seen in the history of the Balance Wars.

The Origin Plane of Storm had barely suffered any meaningful setbacks. None of their transmigrants had died. None had fallen to the dozens of Calamities scattered across the blood-soaked battlefield.

The alliance, meanwhile, had lost Kael Varros, Volgrim Kaizen, Lysandra Quinn, Ariana Solis, Elias Hale, and Alaric Thorneveil.

Six transmigrants.

Two of them earth cultivators.

And as if that were not enough, Seraphine and Butcher had also been captured—likely to be used as bargaining chips.

Eight transmigrants had entered this war. And they had lost every single one of them.

Not one had returned alive.

It was too unusual. Almost as though they had declared war on fate itself.

Sighing heavily, Elder Mournwind placed his weathered hands upon the table.

“This is it, then,” he said, rising slowly to his feet. “We have lost.”

Aspwick scowled as though the concept of failure set his teeth on edge. He shook his head. “Surely, there must be something else was can—”

“There is nothing else,” Mournwind cut in firmly. “There is nothing else I want to do. We have lost. We have failed.”

A heavy silence followed. And then—

“What a waste,” Sang-Hoon sneered. “Lives. Resources. Power...” He shook his head, grief plain upon his face. “Where did we go wrong?”

Mournwind sighed sympathetically.

He did not blame the man. Of them all, the Earth Sect had suffered the most, falling from three transmigrants to one.

A humiliation so vast that even he felt ashamed by association.

“Any further fighting will only drag us deeper into the abyss,” Mournwind said, meeting each of their gazes. “I suggest we cut our losses and withdraw while we still possess something.” He glanced at Sang-Hoon. “While you still possess something.”

Clearfall nodded but remained silent, keenly aware that the Glass Sect was the only faction yet to lose a transmigrant outright. Seraphine had been taken, yes, but captives could be reclaimed.

“Fear not,” Mournwind continued when Sang-Hoon hesitated. “We are still your allies. Should your enemies stir, you can count on the Serenity Sect's support.”

Clearfall nodded. “The Glass Sect too. We will be there the moment you call.”

Aspwick and Varkas said the same, and Sang-Hoon exhaled. “Thank you. The Earth—”

The rest of his words were cut short as the screen suddenly shifted, revealing High Elder Zarxan tearing his way out of chaotic space alongside High Elder Tharn and Lordstrom Veylmont.

And in their arms were two unconscious boys.

“Crimson damnation!” Aspwick cursed and Elder Mournwind closed his eyes, only then realizing how much hope he had secretly placed on Aodhán dying within chaotic space.

But the boy had not only survived—it seemed another double inheritor they had believed dead had survived as well.

“What a mess,” Varkas muttered, shaking his head. “They are too strong. How are they this strong?”

“Fate,” Mournwind said, bitterness dripping from the word. “Bloody fate and her bloody meddling.”

Turning his back on the screen, he cursed softly. “Always so partial.”

***

While all this unfolded, Zerathis Starless floated high above the battlefield, his expression blank as he watched the destruction raging below.

Once again, his actions had caused a war to implode.

This time, however, Zerathis had not acted solely for the excitement it would bring him.

He had acted from genuine pain.

Meredith Halcyon had been an excellent transmigrant—an astounding architect of spatial magic and the most promising of his disciples.

Her death had stirred something within him. Something dangerously close to grief.

And in that moment, Zerathis had lost his mind, along with whatever restraint he possessed. He had avenged her almost immediately, and despite how things had turned out, he felt not a shred of regret.

Nor a flicker of excitement.

“Master Zerathis?” A Mythic approached, seemingly unconcerned that they had just lost two transmigrants in less than an hour. “What are your orders, sir?”

Zerathis glanced at him. “Have we finished helping the Storm Sect?”

“We’re finishing up,” the stocky man replied. “The battlefield is nearly evacuated, and Master Veylmont has retrieved Gyatso’s body.” His gaze darkened. “It’s a shame.”

“It is,” Zerathis said, his eyes drifting toward the crater Meredith’s body had made. “It certainly is.”

Exhaling softly, he turned his gaze toward the distant Storm Sect.

“This is it, then. The war is over.”

The Mythic said nothing, but he did not need to.

Everyone knew why Zerathis had come on this expedition in the first place. They knew he had no desire to return to the ceaseless void they called home.

But staying had consequences.

Dire ones.

“I think it is time we leave,” Zerathis said. “While there is still enough chaos to hide us.”

The Mythic nodded. “I shall inform the commanders.”

He departed at once, and Zerathis turned his attention back to the Storm Sect.

Perhaps because of the confusion of war, no one had yet realized that the Origin Plane of Space had not shrunk at all—even after the deaths of two transmigrants.

By every measure, they should have been as distraught as the Earth Sect.

But they were not.

Staying, however, would only invite questions they could not answer. Questions that could spark another balance war.

Sighing, Zerathis looked away and teleported upward, where their ship already waited.

He did not look back.

Not at the Storm Sect. Not at the battlefield below.

Not even at Meredith’s body, half-buried beneath broken stone.

This expedition had been far less exciting than he had hoped. Perhaps there was another sect somewhere that needed their help.

***

Back in the Elder Hall, the First Seat leaned into his throne with a smile of deep satisfaction.

Zarxan, Mystral, and Tharn had all returned alive, and though he had sharply reprimanded Zarxan for his reckless decision to step onto the battlefield, he could not deny the favorable outcome of that interference.

Had Zarxan hesitated, they likely would have lost Aodhán, Nymera, and Seamus—who, against all expectation, was apparently alive as well.

“This is it, then.” His smile widened as he watched their enemies retreat toward their sects. “The war is over.”

“For now,” Zarxan replied with a nod. Then he smiled too. “Or for a few decades, at least.”

Laughter rippled through the hall, and High Elder Velora let out a pleased sigh. “Peace at last. A few decades is more than enough time to rebuild and grow.”

Tharn nodded. “And with the pendants, we could easily equip everyone with a familiar.”

“Doubling our combat power.” The First Seat grinned, excitement bright in his eyes. “This calls for a celebration.”

“Indeed.” Kaelthorn nodded alongside the others. “The transmigrants fought magnificently, and our Mythics acquitted themselves with honor. They should all be rewarded.”

“The Calamities as well,” Mystral added. “Everyone who made this victory possible deserves recognition.” She glanced at the First Seat. “Perhaps a grand ceremony, once they have all rested.”

“Quite right,” Tharn agreed. His gaze shifted to the screen. “And then we must deliberate on Damien’s father, as well as the Entropy Sect’s request to join our alliance.”

The mention of diplomacy dulled the mood at once, and Mystral groaned.

“Why must you always do this, Tharn? Could you not enjoy victory for one moment before dragging us back to work?”

“I am enjoying the victory,” Tharn said primly. “I simply understand that pending matters are pending for a reason. They do not resolve themselves.”

Zarxan exhaled.

“As irritating as it is, I fear Tharn is right. Other matters may wait, but this cannot.” He grimaced. “And I doubt this will be the last request we receive—from allies or former enemies alike. A Balance War without meaningful casualties on our side is unheard of.”

“Not to mention the greater truth,” Tharn said with a nod. “We did not merely win this war. We set a standard. We have become the new balance. Every sect in Gordon now wishes to be us.”

“Do not let it swell your head, Tharn,” Velora chuckled. “But you are right. Aodhán’s initiation into the sect brought us tremendous power—far more than the third transmigrant, perhaps even the second. It makes one wonder whether the fourth transmigrant marks some hidden threshold.” She paused. “Something not entirely Aodhán’s doing at all.”

Her words caused a heavy silence to settle over the hall.

A moment later, Zarxan spoke “The other Sects will want to test it. They will seek expansion.”

“Indeed,” Velora said. “How we answer those requests will go a long way toward establishing dominance. Of course, they possess neither the Interface nor Aodhán’s miracle rain, so I believe we may yet remain ahead.”

Their discussions stretched on for hours as they devised a strategy for dealing with their eager would-be friends.

In the end, however, they returned to celebration and triumph, each of them keenly aware of the Origin Plane expanding once more—reclaiming the ground their enemies had ceded through defeat.

It was glorious.

And with every passing hour, the High Elders could almost feel themselves growing stronger.

The heavens, it seemed, had chosen a side this time.

And it was the side of thunder.
