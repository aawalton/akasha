With only a week to go until the big festival, Vin was slowly starting to feel the pressure of what he still had left to accomplish. Before Ovord had unintentionally cost him a good few weeks by challenging him to a battle within that orb, Vin had been incredibly confident in finishing up the third ring before the battle for wave eight. But now that things were getting down to the wire, he was marginally less so. Sure, if he just wanted to sprint his way through three fragments he could probably do that in barely a few hours. But that sort of defeated the whole point of both his class and what he was trying to do. He didn’t want to make a map and just put ‘fragment with trees’ for one of the fragments. He wanted to be able to talk about who lived there, their society, and what kind of people they were.

That sort of thing was a bit harder to rush through.

On top of which, Vin had plenty of other important things to worry about. Lumel was still waiting patiently over in the meriders’ forest for her people’s Airwalker to arrive. Alka might very well be able to be reconnected back into the System after all this time.

And then there was Ovord.

Vin pinched the bridge of his nose and groaned as he laid back on his shimmerwing mattress, struggling to decide what to do with the troublesome mage floating around inside him. He’d tried not letting on to just how freaked out he’d been yesterday to Ovord’s admittance that he’d altered the very souls of those three neilans, but Vin was more than a little worried. His gut reaction had been to order the former Arch Mage to undo whatever he’d done, before deciding that responding to the surprise soul surgery with more soul surgery was probably not a good idea.

Not to mention the neilans will actually help us now…

Vin tried to squash that voice in his head that was unfortunately very much his own. While he absolutely didn’t approve of what Ovord had done, there was no arguing the fact that the neilans were horrible people as a whole. Having witnessed first hand just what monstrous acts they were capable of and how little they cared about the other races, it was sad that Vin hadn’t been overly surprised by Ovord informing him that the neilans were contemplating taking advantage of their request for help to wipe Terra off the face of Edregon.

“Ovord did the wrong thing for the right reasons. Still doesn’t make it right,” Vin muttered, forcing himself up and out of bed after a few hours of restless sleep. He was still planning on trying to get Ovord a body of his own sooner than later, if only to get the man out of his own head, but now he wanted to be a bit more cautious about exactly what sort of body he ended up getting. Vin needed a way to keep the mage on a short leash that didn’t involve agreeing to any sort of soul bond with him.

Just one more thing for the to-do list.

Deciding at least one of those items could be started on easily enough, Vin went out in search of Alka. To his surprise, rather than finding her in the middle of beating up a group of Slayers in the Earther’s forest dungeon, he came upon her sitting underneath one of the trees, her glowing eyes entirely shut off. For a moment, he feared she’d shut down again, like she had before the librarian had tuned up her golem body. Then he realized she’d be lying sprawled out on the ground like a loose ragdoll if that were the case.

“Alka?” he asked cautiously as he approached. “Are you meditating?”

“Trying to,” she replied. Her green eyes flickered back into existence, and she looked at him. “Turns out, Epli and its people don’t sleep either. Not even during the day. They sort of enter this hibernation-like state to conserve energy that mimics sleep. I was wondering if I might be able to do the same thing if I tried hard enough.”

“Huh. Might be worth asking the librarian about next we see her,” Vin said, wondering exactly how he should approach what he’d come to talk about. He didn’t want to get Alka’s hopes up if Ovord’s theory turned out to be impossible after all, but he didn’t know any other way to put it. Deciding to just come out with it, Vin sighed. “...Ovord thinks he might be able to reconnect you back into the System. To give you classes and levels again, the whole thing.”

Alka’s eyes winked out again, before flaring larger than he’d ever seen them before. Slowly, the golem got to her feet, her knuckles clenching as she stared hard at him.

“Vin. The two of us have been through quite a lot together.”

“That’s an understatement,” he replied, swallowing hard as she continued to stare at him.

“I probably respect you more than anyone else in the entire world,” Alka admitted, causing Vin to blink at the powerful statement. “That said… I don’t know if I could ever forgive you if you were to put this hope in me, only for whatever idea it is you have to fall through. I’ve spent almost a year coming to terms with the fact that I’m stranded from the System, forced to watch everyone I know and interact with on a daily basis continue advancing and growing stronger while I’m limited to this body. I didn’t think there was a chance I could get that back, so I made myself be okay with my current situation. If you’re telling me there might be something that can be done to get me back into the System… You better have a damn good reason why you think that.”

“It’s Ovord,” Vin hurried to explain. “He believes that by combining our skillsets, specifically my Beyond the Veil ability and his skill at manipulating souls, we might be able to reconnect you. We really can’t know how well it will work until we try.”

“So I’d have to willingly put myself under the mercy of a powerful soul mage…” Alka muttered, her eyes narrowing into pinpricks of light as she turned away. “That’s a pretty tough decision to make.”

“I can’t promise you anything. But Ovord is definitely as good as he says,” Vin admitted, hoping Alka wouldn't ask exactly how he knew that. “I know I’m not really in a position to suggest what you do at this point. But I think it’s worth a shot.”

“Vin,” Alka snorted, her eyes returning to their regular green glow as she looked at him. “You literally dragged my soul around in your own body for weeks, even letting me take over here and there for stupid things like eating food or stretching your muscles. And then you and the others risked everything getting me this body and binding me to it. If you think this is worth the risk, then I’m all for it. I want to get access to the System again and become a true Slayer once more so much it hurts. Despite the fact I can’t actually feel pain anymore. So yeah, this isn’t really a decision. I want this.”

Vin could only smile at his oldest friend in the world, his golem arm she’d originally sacrificed from her own body to give him flashing blue as he tried not to get emotional. Alka was usually such a straight-faced, matter-of-fact warrior who didn’t really do sappy stuff.

“Alright,” he said, taking a deep breath and glancing down at his own chest. “Ovord, how exactly is this going to work? What do you need?”

“For you to give me control of your body once more,” the soul mage said, having clearly been paying attention to their whole discussion. “Once I do, you will have to focus on allowing me to utilize your class ability. I will warn you, however, I do not expect us to be able to reconnect her today. I am quite skilled, but it is going to take me some time to get used to seeing Beyond the Veil like you do, and I will have to work carefully around the binding ritual. It was created well enough that I will not be at risk of damaging it, but it makes what I am attempting more difficult. Most likely, this will take some time.”

Vin relayed all this information to Alka, who nodded. “In that case, no time like the present. Let’s get started.”

And just like that, Vin found himself standing over Alka, who was now lying back on a stone table he’d conjured up with Stone Furniture. He felt appropriately nervous for someone who was about to be used as a vessel for critical soul surgery, and he quietly muttered under his breath as he got ready.

“Ovord, I know I threatened you a little about doing what you did with the neilans again. But let me make one thing clear. If I discover you did anything to Alka’s soul other than reconnect it to the System, I will figure out a way to get you back in that orb I found you in and bury you so deep underground you’ll never be found again.”

“You have nothing to worry about,” Ovord reassured him. “And might I recommend you leave the threats to others? They do not suit you.”

“Just do what you can,” Vin whispered, steeling himself before triggering Human Vessel. Unlike the first time he’d handed Ovord control, this instance went exactly like how he’d always remembered with Alka. Suddenly finding himself watching his body move like he was an outside observer chilling in the back of his own head, Vin watched as Ovord wasted no time, his eyes going straight to Alka’s torso as he examined her soul.

“This is going to be tricky,” Ovord muttered in Vin’s voice, and Alka’s eyes flared slightly while she looked at him.

“Huh, silver eyes. That’s interesting.”

“Lend me your class ability,” Ovord said, ignoring Alka’s comment. “I need to see what I am working with.”

Vin tried and failed to nod, cursing silently to himself before focusing on looking beyond. He willed his class ability into existence, allowing Ovord to tap into it. Curiously, it seemed Vin himself wasn’t able to see whatever it was Ovord was looking at while the mage had possession of his body. From his perspective, Ovord was simply staring directly at Alka’s core, not moving in the slightest as he concentrated. One second ticked by after another, and before he knew it, an entire minute had passed. As Vin snapped back into control of his body, he blinked, peering down at his own chest.

“Ovord? You good? You didn’t do anything.”

“That was… a lot to take in,” Ovord said slowly, the confident Arch Mage sounding unsure of himself for the first time Vin could remember. “I am a master of the soul and have nothing more to learn in that field. But I have never before seen the connection between the soul and the System itself. Or lack of one, in this case. I do not believe the task to be impossible, but this is going to take even longer than I originally expected.”

“Well, midnight already passed and refreshed the title, so we’ve got two more uses of Human Vessel available today,” Vin shrugged. “Want to keep at it?”

“Yes,” Ovord said, his trepidation quickly morphing into what sounded like genuine excitement. “Not even including the time I spent within that prison, it has been many years since I last encountered a soul-based problem of such difficulty. I promise you, I will do everything in my power to get your other vassal connected into the System once more.”

“She’s not… You know what, forget it. Just keep doing what you’re doing,” Vin said, handing Ovord the reins once more as he let the soul mage get to work. “I don’t care how many days this takes. No matter what, we’re getting Alka back where she belongs.”
