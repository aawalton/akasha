From high up in an enormous Kin’jal skyship, Ira gazed out at the largest army fielded in human history—certainly the most multifaceted and multinational of any army—and barely registered it.

Of all the issues plaguing Ira, it was the waiting that hurt the most. The agonizing days and weeks of being in the dark with no means to learn the Akh Nara’s plan. No way other than to hope that, by the time his ceasefire ended, at least some semblance of sanity would return to the realm.

That the army Princess Ira was building would be rendered moot, and that humans and demons could go back to coexisting in this wonderful new realm Vir had given them.

Vir, who’d saved her life. Who’d allowed her to take the Empire from her father…

What was he thinking…?

Ira simply couldn’t bring herself to believe the person she knew—that she adored—would do such a thing.

Since the moment she’d met him, when he’d saved her from a grizzly death by way of the boiling pit of water her father had subjected her to, Ira had held nothing but awe and gratitude in her heart for that man.

To her, he was a shining paragon of all that was right in the world. A true force for good, and confirmation of Ira’s belief that humanity and demonkind could truly coexist. Not just coexist, but work together to form a world better than either of their peoples had ever known.

Together with Maiya, the pair had been dazzling to her eyes. Ira had been sure they would live for hundreds, if not thousands of years together… guiding and shaping the realm long after she was nothing more than a postscript in the history books.

In truth, it had been love at first sight, and she would be lying if she said she didn’t envy Maiya. To spend a lifetime with such a perfect man? A lifetime that promised to be incomparably longer than any regular human? Ira had swooned at the thought on multiple occasions.

All thanks to Maiya’s training in the Ash, which was, of course, one of the nearly countless advantages she gained by being the spouse of such an incredible being.

More than once over the years, Ira had been sorely tempted to ask Maiya for Vir’s second hand in marriage. That she’d been her best friend had stopped her, as well as the fact that Vir loved Maiya with every inch of his heart.

It was why Ira knew that even now, with Maiya’s death, there would be no space for her.

Which was precisely why she couldn’t fathom him killing her, or what he hoped to accomplish by doing so.

Yes, the reprieve he’d granted them had been a godsend in terms of their army… but one that Ira still hoped humanity wouldn’t need.

Vir had not returned to kill his wife to fight a war in which demonkind would almost assuredly have won even without him.

The man who’d saved her several times over couldn’t have lost his mind.

Right?

“Imperatrix?” Akar asked, looking at her with concern.

“Nothing,” she said with as reassuring a smile as she could muster in this situation. “It’s nothing…”

In truth, she’d registered not a single word of her foremost general, who beamed with pride as he animatedly updated Ira on the latest progress in the civilian training program.

Just ten days remained before this ceasefire ended, and Ira had to admit, her people had come far in the time they’d been given.

Not especially surprising, given her people’s history. For better or for worse, war was in their blood.

Ira shook her head. Never once had she thought that her father’s doctrine or that of any of his predecessors would be so useful so soon after shifting the Empire to a new course.

She hadn’t thought her best friend would be murdered in cold blood by her own husband, either, but she had, once again, been proven wrong.

“I beg your pardon, Imperatrix?” the general asked, raising an eyebrow this time.

“No, it’s nothing, Akar,” she said, giving him a reassuring smile. What you have worked over these past weeks has been nothing short of a miracle. When I agreed to send Ira a hundred thousand civilians, I had not thought they would resemble anything even close to a fighting force over this short reprieve we’ve been granted.”

“Thank you, Imperatrix,” Akar replied, bowing his head in deference. “We have all worked ourselves to the bone in your name. To hear your praise is the highest honor any of us could possibly receive.”

The gesture, she knew, was little more than a facade. So long as Ira ordered war, he, and the rest of the military, were happy.

Ira nodded regally. “I have full faith in your capacity, General.”

It was no lie, either.

Ira thanked all the gods that she’d been denied the time to oust all of her father’s existing military command structure before the Ash Tears began. She’d been about to do so, thinking the loss in military experience was more than made up for by commanders loyal to her and her alone.

Their aptitude was put on full display when the Ash Beasts had ravaged the realm, and Kin’jal, despite its size and proximity to the Ash Boundary, had emerged better off than nearly every other country.

That decision to keep them in place was biting her now. Until now, they’d begrudgingly accepted her and her unorthodox doctrine in fear of her popularity.

Years of sweat, blood, and toil, all undone in a single night.

Khantaghati Pass had taken her immense popularity and torn it to shreds. Now, there wasn’t a single commander in her army who wished nothing more than for Ira to make a decision they found unpalatable.

What better excuse with which to launch a mutiny?

Ira knew this, of course, and so she played her role. If they survived this war to come, there would be time to mend her reputation. To slowly oust her opponents and replace them as she’d always wished.

Right now, they had a role to play, and so did she.

The skyship finished its circuit of the amassed forces before setting Ira down near her quarters. An entourage of guards accompanied her back to her tent, each taking their place at the entrance, while Ira proceeded inside.

She made it two steps before freezing in place, though not out of fear. Out of elation.

Because finally, at long last, her burning questions would be answered.

On the opposite side of her especially tall tent stood a half-naked red monster with four arms.

“Please,” the Ravager said, extending all his palms. “Do not be alarmed. I am not here to hurt you.”

Ira snorted, letting the tension dissipate. “You hardly need to mention it,” she said. “We both know that I wouldn’t be standing here if you did. If you do have to kill me, however, I merely ask that you make it as painless as possible? As much as I’ve grown from your training in the Ash, I must admit, this Imperatrix is still frail on the inside, where it counts.”

Cirayus’ expression softened, and before she knew it, he was in front of her, placing a reassuring hand upon her shoulder. “I see you still suffer from misplaced insecurity,” he said, looking genuinely worries.”

Ira shook her head. “Come now,” she said. “You can’t be showing such empathy for your enemy now. We’re at war.”

“A war I do not understand and in which I never wished to participate,” Cirayus muttered, keeping his hand on her shoulder. “You know that Maiya was like a daughter to me. You were her dearest friend. Do not push me away so.”

“I’m sorry,” Ira sighed. “It’s just…”

“I am aware,” Cirayus replied with a sigh of his own. “Believe me, lass, I am fully aware,” and in his expression, Ira saw all the anguish she herself had been drowning in these several weeks.

“You don’t know,” she blurted out.

“I do not,” Cirayus replied. “And believe me when I say that, in all my five centuries of life, I have never once been more hurt or more helpless than I am now.”

Ira didn’t know what shocked her more. That she felt no ill will towards Vir for assassinating her best friend… or that Cirayus, of all people, had been left in the dark.

“I…”

“You needn’t shower me with words of pity, lass,” Cirayus replied. “What this desperate old soul came here to ask is whether you know any more than I.”

“I was about to ask the same of you!” Ira said. Her frustration, angst, and anger must have come through, for Cirayus, of all people, shirked back.

“I truly wish I had the answers, but even ancient souls like myself get desperate. The lad told me nothing. Nothing at all. All of the demon realm is in chaos. Whatever he did…” the Ravager choked out the words, “he did on his own. Without consulting a soul, it would seem.”

“I don’t believe it,” Ira shot back. “Vir would never do something this rash. Not without ample reason…”

“And yet, Maiya is dead,” Cirayus said. “And none of the Samsara can sense her. I had hoped… hoped that Vir had done this foul deed to bring her back as an immortal, but now…”

“No…”

Darkness encroached upon Ira’s vision, and the next thing she knew, she was in Cirayus’ arms, stumbling to her feet.

“No!” she wailed. “No no no no no!”

The rest of her outburst was covered by Cirayus’ palm, and good that he did. Should any of her guards find him here, there would be war.

But the Ravager knew that better than anyone. Far be it for Ira to remind him.

“I just…” she whispered, tears streaming unbidden down her cheeks. “I just wish I knew that everything will be alright. That he acted with a reason beyond the understanding of us mortals.”

“Believe me, lass,” I would like nothing more. “And yet, what can we do aside from wait? Wait for the day the Akh Nara promised, and trust in the one who united our realms. Who saved your life and who has relentlessly toiled for the greater good of the realms.”

“Do… do you think he succumbed?” Ira asked despite herself. “To the stress Maiya said he’d been subjected to?”

“I do not wish to believe it, lass,” Cirayus said, wrapping the weeping Ira in his arms. “But I fear I cannot deny the possibility.”

It was only when something wet and cold fell upon Ira’s arms that true hopeless dawned upon her.

The last of her dams broke, and at last, every pent up emotion broke through. Ira shook and wailed in despair.

At the fate of the realm. At the true death of her dearest friend.

Cursing Fate. Cursing the cruelty of the universe.

She was not alone.
