Mia sneered when Aysha turned the corner of the Coral apartment complex. “This is surprising,” she said. “I thought you’d be wearing fishnets.”

Aysha looked at her classic boxing robes. “No. I think most would expect that I’d be wearing this, just like… most would expect a conniving woman like you to crawl back to a man she cheated.”

Mia took a step forward, but Kayline interrupted. “Can you two go upstairs?” she said as she turned the corner with Lina. “People could see you.”

The sisters wore hooded cloaks, something that was a good idea in retrospect.

Mia huffed and led them up the stairs. Once she reached Room 326, she knocked as hard as she could without drawing attention. A man wearing ear muffs opened the door. Mia would soon know him as Rosen Borsia, and the second he saw Lina and Kayline, his face became sickly pale and anemic.

“What’s going on?” he asked, breaths shallow.

“What do you mean, What’s going on?” Kayline asked. “Isn’t Hayden in there?”

“Let them through, Rosen.”

Hayden gave the order, and Mr. Earmuffs complied obsequiously.

This is definitely a trap… Mia thought. She was even more convinced when she saw a long table in the center of the messy living room.

Lina had the same thought. “If you’re expecting me to get on that thing, you’re wrong.”

Hayden leaned against the table and smiled. Nothing he did could be categorized as charming, but attractive people could make anything magnetic, and Hayden was so attractive now that he was practically glowing. Mia found it frustrating.

“Mia,” Hayden said, making her jump.

“What?” she asked nervously.

“Look at Lina’s core and describe it.”

“Are you sure you want to ask her?” Aysha asked. “The last time someone asked for her input, she lied—on video.”

Mia’s stomach sank.

“While that’s true, I won’t fault her for her curiosity. Besides…” Hayden lifted a keychain NFC storage disc, indicating that he had official documentation on the subject. “She can’t lie to me.”

Mia swallowed, throat scratchy as she activated Divine Eyes. The moment she saw Lina’s core, her hesitation and anxiety mixed with bewilderment. “Wait. You have a Haeta core?”

“I can’t do this,” Lina said, trying to walk away.

Kayline grabbed her. “Just… hold still.” She shot a warning glare at Mia.

Mia shivered. “Uh… It’s three times the size of a normal core, but it’s… hollow. No, there’s a tiny core in the center, and it has gates but no locks. Maybe one, actually. It’s just… strange.”

Cores were supposed to be the size of small marbles—gold—and marked with rings and circles which indicated open gates and spirit locks. This one was different. One textbook described it like a lightbulb from an Ancient Earth museum.

“Correct.” Hayden looked at Lina. “What did the doctors tell you?”

“They…” Lina sighed. “They said I had Haeta Core Disease. It’s… a condition that causes the core to expand outward instead of becoming denser, and it restricts me from having a spirit because spirits live in the kebrital anima, and mine is one-tenth the size of a normal… one. Technically, I can have one, but there would be complications using it.”

Hayden stared at her with an emotionless expression that Mia could tell was exasperated somehow. “Please continue,” he said. “What do they say happens if you activate it?”

Lina folded her arms defensively. “They say that the energy floods into the outer core, building pressure that violently attacks my coran channels like a broken dam. So not only is it useless, it also hurts me.”

“Well, that’s all aggressively false,” Hayden declared. “You don’t have a disease—you have an augmenter core. It’s an extraordinarily rare gift I’ll explain to you in private. Right now, you’re just struggling because your release gate is clogged, and your cora channels have been severely damaged. We’re fixing those problems today, as well as unlocking your core.”

A cool silence passed through the room as the group struggled to process his words.

“Now if you’ll kindly lie on the table.” No one believed Hayden until he pulled out a glowing bottle and a balm tin.

Lina sat on the table with the speed of a dying turtle, “What’s that?”

“It’s a channel healing balm, as well as a core expansion elixir.”

“You’re using your elixir on me?”

Hayden laughed. “Not on your life.”

“Then how’d you get it? I thought there was a restriction on getting resources?”

“I have a restriction on using them. You wouldn’t believe how easy it is for me to get anything as long as it doesn’t go into my body. Now lie down.”

She hesitantly lay down as Hayden turned to Mia.

“You can see her strained channels, yes?” Hayden asked, poking spots on Lina’s arms and legs, and stomach. Lina squirmed and yelled, “Stop!” He ignored her.

Mia felt like it was a trap. He was pointing out coran bruising throughout Lina’s limbs with pinpoint precision. “Yes,” Mia said hesitantly.

“Good. Can you also see the frayed channels in these areas?”

He pointed at areas on Lina’s ribs and thighs. Mia studied Lina and saw that her auxiliary channels had indeed frayed. She ignored such channels because they were hard to find and harder to interact with. Yet Hayden had identified their problem without a seeing spirit.

“How do you know that?” she asked.

“Observe,” Hayden said.

Mia had lived twenty years, and in all that time, she had never seen something as shocking as what Hayden did a moment later. The cora around him wisped in complex and intricate patterns, like watching elegant cyclones entering different pressure points. She watched it cycle around him in areas like a halo, then move in and out of channels in beautiful streams.

Then he suddenly changed his breathing, and new patterns formed, each equally complex.

“What are you doing?” Aysha cried.

“Many people believe that spirits are the entities that perform arts,” Hayden said, “but that’s untrue. A spirit is a transformer, an attractor, and an amplifier. It comes with unique characteristics, and a body suited for using them. But once you assimilate, the spirit becomes a part of your body. If you cannot control the energy in your body, how can you use it properly? You can’t. Therefore, you cannot be satisfied with breathing randomly, or doing what’s natural. You must circulate energy to meet the situation. That’s why my muscles are far more infused than yours. That’s why my core is purer than yours. And that’s why my spirit beast can perform incredible feats.”

“Oh, yeah, you’re so good,” Lina said sarcastically.

“Precisely,” Hayden said. “I’m also good at reading breathing patterns. Just because the auxiliaries don’t breathe cora doesn’t mean that I can’t see how they're interacting with the world. That sounds abstract, but it’s actually quite simple. If you dam one section of a river, it flows differently. That's an obvious example to anyone, but what I'm seeing is equally obvious to me. My only problems are that I cannot see precisely without a seer spirit and I lack the spirits necessary to perform the operation safely on my own. Now, if I've satisfied your base curiosity, can we begin?”

Mia had a thousand questions, but Hayden’s eyes said that he wasn’t asking, so she nodded nervously. “Yes.”

Aysha swallowed. “I’m ready.”

Kayline bit her lip and nodded.

Lina grimaced and blinked slowly.

Hayden noted their compliance, put healing balm onto his fingers, and handed Aysha the rest. “Prepare to administer this.” He looked at Kayline. “Hold her down.”

“Hold her—what?” Rosen looked between them nervously. “She won’t die, will she? ‘Cause if she dies in my place—”

“No one’s going to die,” Hayden said. “Now, let’s get to work.” He lifted his finger, and a small line came out of it—a spirit line. It was unmistakable. Under Divine Eyes, it was clear, transparent, colorless forms. Hayden’s spirit, Clear Line, could create tendrils of different sizes or shapes, making it extremely flexible. At present, the line was almost as thin as thread—but its power was enormous.

A gust of aura swirled around it, warping and twisting and morphing the atmosphere in hypnotizing patterns.
