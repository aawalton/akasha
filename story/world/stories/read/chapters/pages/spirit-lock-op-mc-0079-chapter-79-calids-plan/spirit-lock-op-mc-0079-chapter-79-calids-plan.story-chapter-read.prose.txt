“Should we help?” Lina asked Hart.

“First off, you’re not helping anything, so watch your verbiage,” Hart said. “And no. Until we see Hayden, we’re not moving. Those were his orders.”

Lina shut down. She was just as scared as Kayline and half the team. The whole situation seemed hopeless. Hayden had been gone for days, and there had been sixty rifters that passed their concealment cave on the way to the mountain—some famous for their ruthlessness. Now, a battle was raging, and Hayden was nowhere to be seen, and everyone’s minds were playing tricks on them, making them think of a thousand and one situations in which he was dead or had abandoned them.

Kayline didn’t mean to doubt him—but she had also fallen into despair.

“It’ll be okay,” Miki said. She was one of the healers, and she was very sweet. She helped to calm Kayline and Lina throughout the trip.

Kayline winced, pained to contradict her, but it felt like a ghost was squeezing her lungs and refusing to let go. “I don’t mean to doubt you, I really don’t. If anyone could do anything, it’d be Hayden. And it’s pretty clear he had a plan coming. But… Ella? Calid? That’s just who was on the teams that passed us. How many people did they send after Hayden directly? I can’t stop thinking about it. And now—”

She jolted and squeezed her eyes shut when the ground shook, and rocks fell off the ceiling onto them.

“That,” she said. “I just can’t see how things are fine.”

-

Miki rubbed Kayline’s back and then fell silent herself. She was also feeling down, dreading the possibility of getting involved. They were the ambush team, but even if they fought together, all of them would die if Calid or the Frost Witch attacked them.

Miki closed her eyes, too, and secretly prayed that Hayden would die so they never had to get involved.

That was her silent wish when an object crashed in front of the cave.

There was an illusory barrier around the cave that tricked both the eyes and the seeing spirits into believing there wasn’t a cave entrance, and their illusionist, Rico, had used suppression and stealth spirits to mask their presence. The fact that someone ended up in front of their place couldn’t be a coincidence, right?

Everyone held their breath as a man came into view. He had wings, and he was despondent as he held a comrade.

“Shit! Don’t die. Don’t die… if you die, everyone’s screwed…” He looked around. “Fuck! Where the hell’s the cave?”

It was only then that they saw who was in his arms. Hayden.

Gluttony was hanging onto him, and the two were sweating as if they had been poisoned.

Kayline tried to get up, but Hart grabbed her and pulled her down. He put up his fingers, and they kept still, waiting to see if the man was baiting them. It was stressful.

“Shit!” He put down Hayden and said, “You said you could handle it—so prove it, asshole!” He lay Hayden down. Then, he looked around, gripped his fists, and screamed in a suppressed voice. He turned to Hayden. “I’ll be right back. Okay? So just stay… I guess I don’t have to worry about you moving.” He chuckled darkly and flew away.

Everyone took a deep breath.

Their tracker put their hands on the ground and closed their eyes.

“Well?” Hart asked.

“There’s a team running toward us, but they’re keeping their distance. There’s another team following them.”

“Should I check him?” Miki asked.

“Yeah,” Hart said. “Make sure he’s not going to die, but don’t bring him inside.”

“Got it.” Miki left to do her job.

-

Lina didn’t think it was enough. She glanced between Miki and Hart. “Wait! Why aren’t we bringing him back in?”

“‘Cause if we did, they’d find us, and if they find us, we’re useless,” Hart said. “You can’t casually kill someone like Ella or Calid. They’ll just attack from half a mile away. If we wanna win, we gotta ambush the leaders. So just stay calm and let us do our work.”

Lina bit her lip, watching Miki nervously. The healer looked equally rattled, and her face kept pulling taut as she touched his chest. Not a minute later, she returned to the cave with a dark expression.

“What’s wrong?” Kayline asked.

“Cut the suppression spirits and activate your seers,” Miki said. “You need to see this.”

-

The second the team removed the security layers, Hart collapsed against a wall, his mind swirling. “What the… How…?”

There was an expansive cloud of tora hovering around Hayden—the largest he had ever seen. It surrounded him like smog, and a vortex of it was sucking into Hayden’s spirit beast before feeding it into him. The fact that Hayden’s entire body hadn’t exploded was a mind-bending phenomenon.

Hart turned to Miki and the rest. “Can any of you do anything?”

“I’m afraid to do anything,” Miki said. “I don’t even know what Hayden’s doing. If we touch him, we could break something and kill him. I mean… I’ve never seen that much tora.”

A ripple of agreement passed through the team in the cave.

“What’d he kill?” Rico asked.

“Something he shouldn’t’ve,” Hart said.

-

Kayline had to suppress her desire to run out to Hayden’s side, but she kept calm, holding Lina back. Then, she closed her eyes to avoid the grave expressions of everyone present. They were the only people who couldn’t see Hayden, and that looked like a blessing. Everyone looked like doctors staring at MRI images that dramatically showed the last stage of a malignant disease. She couldn’t stand the look, so she closed her eyes and waited for someone to give her the news.

-

Calid turned to the mountain and saw Hayden from the skies. His mind flashed back to it as he circled the mountain, avoiding Living Arrows and wind attacks from the others. He had to think.

There’s no way that he’s going to survive that? he thought, eyes trembling as he examined the cloud of tora above Hayden. And when he dies…

Calid suddenly heard marching. Mackerel and the survivors were running toward him, followed by Carian, who was riding a massive shadow cat. Calid turned to his team. “Pull back! We’re regrouping!”

The group flew to meet Mackerel and Carian.

“Is this it?” Carian asked with shaking eyes. “And your arm…”

Calid looked down, realizing that his arm was still missing. If his body didn’t stop the bleeding, he could be dead.

“It’s fine,” Calid said as a healer rushed forward to help him.

“What happened?” Mackerel asked. “Ambush?”

“Yeah, ambush. We severely underestimated who we were up against. Those bastards got Acid Rain somehow, and some guy with a five-mind split. God knows what the others can do. One’s probably close quarters, but…” He coughed up phlegm.

“Where’s Sylvia?” Carian asked quietly.

“Her core overheated after that meteor spell. So her barrier broke after an arrow barrage. Oh God…”

“What are we gonna do?” Caro asked. He was on Carian’s team, and his clothing was torn to shit—probably from a landslide. God, it was a catastrophe everywhere.

“I don’t know…” Calid said. “We probably have to kill him.”

“What?” Carian snapped. “If we do—”

“He’s dying,” Calid interrupted. “He killed something he shouldn’t’ve, and now he’s threading a colossus’ worth of tora into his core. It’s a literal cloud.”

“Then how’s he not dead already?” Mackerel asked.

“His Horix. It’s acting as a filter, but he’s bright red and sweating, and the spirit’s shaking. He’ll be dead soon, and when he dies, we’re screwed. But if we kill him and lead the colossus over there…”

“It’ll destroy the evidence,” Carian said.

“Yeah. That thing killed at least half of us. Ella. Sylvia. If we spin it right, we can just say it killed Hayden. I’m sure they’ll still blame us, but I’m not sure if Stella will dismantle her top guild for a natural calamity. We’re still needed for Celestial Security.”

Mackerel pursed his lips but nodded. He was caked in sweat and dirt, so when he rubbed his eye, it exposed clean skin. He was a total mess, like the rest of them.

“Shouldn’t we…” Carian paused. “Try to capture him? I mean, we have the forces now, and you can always kill him later.”

Calid’s body felt heavy. He was tired. So tired… He nodded. “Let’s march.”
