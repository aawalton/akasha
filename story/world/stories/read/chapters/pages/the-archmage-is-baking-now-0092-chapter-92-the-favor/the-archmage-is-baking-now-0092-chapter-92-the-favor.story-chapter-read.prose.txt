The king's expression flickered. For a moment, Jane thought she saw anger in his face, or something close to it. He had experienced a few stressful days, and couldn’t be used to people telling him ‘no’ or talking back to him. Before he could respond, in anger or otherwise, the queen reached over and placed her hand on his arm.

The king looked down at her hand and took a long breath.

"Is it necessary that everyone be present for this conversation?" he asked.

Brand shook his head. "No. They should all be allowed to go home. They've earned it."

"Very well." The king raised his voice slightly. "Thank you all for your service. You are dismissed. Please take whatever rest you need."

The guards along the walls began to file out. Harris bowed again and turned to leave, gesturing for Dave to follow him. Jane started toward the door as well, with Cecelia suddenly at her side. Cecelia looped her arm through Jane’s and dropped her head a bit to rest on Jane’s filthy, tangled hair.

"Well," Cecelia said quietly, steering Jane toward the exit, "you do have a talent for getting into trouble, don't you?"

Jane allowed herself to be guided. "I suppose I do."

“I’m proud of you.” Cecelia confirmed Jane’s earlier guess about how she would have felt about Jane as a rabblerouser instead of a reasonably obedient, calm student. “Keep it up.”

They were almost at the door now. The other guards had already filed out, and Harris and Dave were just ahead of them. In a moment, they would be in the hallway, and the door would close, and whatever conversation Brand wanted to have with his parents would happen in private.

Jane stopped walking.

"Actually," she said, "I'm going to get into a bit more trouble now."

Before Cecelia could respond, Jane turned and caught the edge of the door just before it swung shut. She pushed it back open and stepped into the gap.

"Your Majesty?"

The king looked up. Brand had already begun speaking, but he fell silent at her interruption.

"Lady Jane?"

"About that favor you granted me. I'm calling it in."

The king's eyebrows rose. "Now?"

"Yes, now." Jane met his gaze. If she was going to use up the favor, she figured she should get her money’s worth out of it. "I want you to listen to him. Carefully."

"Do you have any particular reason why?” the king asked. “Forgive me. I’m just curious.

Jane thought about the long carriage ride, about Brand staring out the window with red-rimmed eyes. She thought about the way he had insisted on stopping to deliver the news to the laundress himself, the steel in his voice when he had commanded the captain to let him go. She thought about a young man who had done the right thing and was now dreading the consequences.

"I suspect I might agree with him."

Jane bowed and stepped back into the hallway. The door swung shut behind her, cutting off her view of whatever was about to happen. Cecelia was waiting for her when she turned.

"That was funny,” Cecelia said. “Perhaps insane, but funny.”

"Probably both."

"Probably. Come. I'll walk you home. Some soldiers were kind enough to set up a cot for me there. You can tell me everything that happened, and I can pretend to be surprised by how much trouble you managed to cause. It will be very normal."

Jane nodded, and they set off across town. Luckily, as taxing as the trip had been, it had not asked much of her legs or feet. She was fresh enough to walk under her own power without too much discomfort.

"How did you get back so quickly?" she asked as they walked. "You were supposed to be in the north."

"I received word that the prince was missing. Royal word. You understand how it is."

“I do.”

“And, of course, you’ve had a busy time besides that. I thought it was about time for a visit anyway. I just hurried a bit more to arrive than I would have.

"Thank you."

"Don't thank me yet. I fully intend to interrogate you about every decision you made over the past few days." Cecelia's voice was dry. "Starting with your trip to meet your mentor, I believe."

"That's a long story."

"We have time."

Jane found her shop guarded when she arrived, safe and sound under the watchful eye of a sleepy-looking guard who snapped to attention as soon as he noticed the approaching archmages. Cecelia laughed and began to dismiss him, then thought better of it.

“Food.” She shoved a handful of coins into the poor man’s hand. “Find us food. I don’t care what.”

“Of course, ma’am.” The soldier bowed and took off at a trot. “I’ll be back soon.”

He was as good as his word. Ten minutes later, they were standing and eating at Jane’s bakery counter, not a guard in sight. Jane found it refreshing, after the week she’d had.

She and Cecelia ate more slowly than was often the case. Neither of them were starving. Jane could tell her aunt had questions to ask, but Cecelia was decent enough to keep it down to small talk until they had eaten the bulk of their food.

"You want to know about my mentor."

"When you're ready."

Jane nodded. She hadn’t had much of a chance to sit with the story, since she got back to Glenfall. Her aunt was a big part of how she thought about things, anyway, and she knew it would be helpful to tell her about it.

"Felicity Cast was quite a bit. Even in person. The details don’t matter, but we baked together, she taught me a lot, and she was great. Then she died. Just like that. She was old, and she ran out of time.”

Cecelia nodded and kept listening, without comment.

“I showed up the very next day after meeting her, and she had collapsed.”

"And then?"

"And then she died." Jane picked at the crust of her pie. "In her own bed, in her own house. I sat with her, so I was there. It was peaceful, I think. As peaceful as these things get."

"I'm sorry that happened."

Jane looked up at her aunt. "Of all people, you shouldn't be sorry. You were exactly right. If you hadn't told me to go right away I would have missed everything. I would have showed up a month later to an empty house, and that would have been it."

"I'm glad, then." Cecelia picked up a piece of cheese and examined it, then put it down. “Glad you got to meet her.”

"So am I."

Jane stepped away to make their evening tea, only turning back to her aunt once the water was set to boil..

"Why do you think she chose that moment to die?" Jane asked.

Cecelia looked at her. "Your mentor?"

"Both of them, really. My mentor and the laundress’s sister. Why that day? Why then?"

"People don't want to die alone." Cecelia's voice was matter-of-fact. "For all the friends your mentor had in town, you talk about it like none of them really understood who she was, all over. They knew the baker side of her. But they didn't know the expert or the writer."

"And I did?"

"And you did. Or at least it sounds like you did. She made one last friend, an unusual friend who could listen to her story one last time. And maybe that was enough. I’m not sure, but it sounds like the likely thing.”

"And the laundress’s sister?"

"A similar thing, probably, though I’d say almost assuredly not the same. From what you said, she was facing the end either way. Then help came, got her to her bed, and made her comfortable. It was worth waiting for in a different way. She could have died alone, otherwise.”

The food was all gone now. Jane gathered up the empty wrappings and crumbs while Cecelia wiped down the counter with a cloth.

"What do you think will happen with Brand?" Jane asked.

Cecelia paused in her wiping. "I certainly don't know. I hoped you could tell me. I don't even know what he's talking to his parents about."

"Neither do I. Not exactly." Jane sat on a stool she pulled up to the counter. "But I have a guess."

"Tell me."

"I think it's about being normal," Jane said. "Brand tried to take a trip out of town. And when he didn't contact his parents for a few extra days, they diverted half their guards and two archmages to find him."

"That does seem excessive."

"It is excessive. It's not just that his parents would be upset if he got hurt. It's that he's inconveniencing real people and using up resources that could be used for better reasons. Every time he wants to do something as simple as take a walk outside the city, he has to think about what it would cost."

"That's the burden of royalty."

"Is it, though? I understand that he's important to his parents, but he’s not going to be king. There has to be a middle ground somewhere. A way for him to live something approaching a normal life without the entire kingdom holding its breath every time he leaves his bedroom."

"You've thought about this a lot."

"I've had time."

"And you think that's what Brand is talking to his parents about right now? Asking for more freedom?"

“Yes.”

"The world isn't fair." Cecelia sighed and went to fetch the kettle. “He’s never going to be exactly normal, you know. Not in the way you would choose for him or he’d choose for himself.”

"No. But it could be fairer." Jane echoed her sigh. "I don't know. I got more out of life than I thought I could. Friends, and some version of being normal. Of course, I’ve hardly been in my shop for what feels like weeks now, but I have a shop in the first place. It seems like he could at least be closer than he is to what he wants.”

"You like him.”

Jane blinked. "What?"

"The prince. You like him."

"Not like that," Jane said. “Not like that at all.”

"I didn't say you were romantically interested in him. I said you like him.” Cecelia's expression was unreadable. "You care about what happens to him. You used your favor with the king to help him. That's help you didn’t have to give someone you hardly know."

“He's a good person. He tried to do the right thing. I watched him stop a small army to tell a woman her sister had passed. I don’t think he wanted to do either thing. I don’t think everyone would have done that.”

"It is rare." Cecelia said. “I like him too, for the record. I have since he was small. I just hope your favor is enough.”
