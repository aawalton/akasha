Someone was calling my name.

I opened my eyes to near-darkness. Pale moonlight trickled in from the window above my bed. I took a deep breath. Pleasantly cool air and the faint, woody smell of smoke flooded my nostrils. Odd… had Durst forgotten to bank the fire? That wasn’t like him.

A sharp rap at my window made me jolt upright. A spindly silver branch pressed against the glass. Its dark leaves slithered down, as if reaching for the wooden frame. My eyes rounded at the sight. I half-wondered if I was dreaming until the front door slammed, sending my heart straight into my throat.

“Brin!” Royce crashed down the hall and into my bedroom, mouth ajar and blue eyes wild. Blonde hair clung to his temples.

“Royce?!” I clambered out of bed as another crack sounded from behind. “What’s wrong?”

He wrapped me in a tight embrace. The smells of smoke and something metallic clung to his sweat-soaked tunic. “You’re okay!” His muscular arms were trembling. “Saints, you’re okay. Of course you’re okay, this is probably the safest place in town!”

Royce stood a full head taller than me, but I was just able to peer over his shoulder. From beyond my bedroom and our open front door I saw… light? Distant light, orange and dancing, casting buildings and trees into masses of flickering shadow. Stars, is that the bonfire? It shouldn’t be that big!

“Royce, what’s happening?” I pulled back, eying the spreading light in horror. “Is there a fire? We need to go help!” Was Durst already in town, getting people to safety? I didn’t see him in our kitchen. He surely would have appeared, probably with a hunting knife in hand, when the front door had burst open.

Another crack came from behind, alongside the sound of splintering glass.

Royce held me at arms’ length. “It’s not the fire. I mean, it is, but that’s not…” He shook his head as if to clear it, skin glowing white with moonlight and a thin sheen of sweat. “It’s Fae, Brin. Monsters. They’re in Fellbrook.”

I stared. The words made sense, but they also… didn’t. “That’s… not… possible. The witchwood… Durst says they can’t come back to Fellbrook. They can’t get through the grove. It’s impossible.”

“That’s probably why they’re burning it.”

Ice spiked in my chest at the thought of the trees, my trees, in flames. I gave a cry of dismay, rushing forwards.

Royce blocked my path. “It’s safest inside, Brin!” His baritone, usually rife with levity, was several octaves higher than normal. “I know you care about the woods, but you don’t understand, you didn’t see what-!”

“Durst is out there!”

Royce’s face took on a horribly pained expression.

We stared at each other for an impossibly long moment. Just as I was about to push past him, a deep growl came crawling from beyond the doorway. Something guttural. Feral. Hungry.

I peered past his shoulder, fingers twisting nervously at the hem of my nightdress. Was it a Fae? An otherworldly monster, come to drag us away into darkness? The thought was unreal, impossible, and yet…

Distant firelight made shadows dance erratically across the hallway and flit with abandon through the kitchen, but the house beyond my bedroom appeared empty.

Crack. Glass splintered behind me.

“R-Royce?” I squinted into the hallway. Nothing was there, but the pit in my stomach screamed to run.

The growl came again, from so close- just there, beyond my door, but there was nothing- that I jerked backwards.

Royce lunged forward, slammed my bedroom door shut, and drew the bolt.

We stood in breathless silence until movement from the floorboards drew my gaze. My jaw dropped in wordless terror as from between Royce’s mud-caked boots there came a shadow. A shadow with nothing to cast it, pooling in beneath the crack in the door.

It slid into the room like spilled oil. Shapeless, silent as death. It glided across the floor, then drifted upwards, spreading across my bed. Shapes churned within the blackness; gangly, bent limbs and a long snout.

Crack. The branch snapped again, black leaves flush against the glass. Fractured moonlight spilled forth, illuminating the creature forming before us as its edges sharpened, hardened, turned solid. Black, jagged claws stretched out from large paws. A vaguely canine muzzle bared uneven teeth, sharp as shards of broken glass.

The Fae swung its head towards me. Teeth bared, muscles coiled, eyes shining as my scream rang off the walls.

Royce lunged. His fist smashed into solid dark, knocking the monster sideways. Its head twisted and razored rows of teeth sank into his shoulder, sending a spray of crimson across my bed.

Royce tumbled to the floor with a cry. My nightstand and runebook crashed down beside him.

The Fae snapped its gaze back towards me. Its nostrils flared and its night-black muzzle gleamed with slick, visceral red.

Crack. Glass tinkled onto my bed.

Royce heaved himself upright and, roaring like a bull, tackled the monster straight through the window.

"No!" I rushed forward and grabbed the sill, trying to heave myself through. Hot pain blossomed in my hands and I fell back with a cry.

The pair thrashed across matted grass. Shattered glass glittered around them. Familiar hissing, louder and fiercer than ever before, echoed from the trees.

“Run!” Royce’s scream was followed by snarling and the slow, wet rip of flesh. Then a breathless moan, like something from a nightmare, and he stopped thrashing.

I grabbed the sill, swallowed the scream, and dragged myself through. My palms burned as I fell onto the cold grass. "I'm coming! I'll... I'll..." I looked around desperately, but there was only mud and broken glass.

And me. My magic was nothing capable of harm, at least not normally, but perhaps against this monster of shadow… Shaking, bleeding fingers traced. “Vir.” The little symbol hung in the air, smoldering… then coiled into a drop of sunlight.

The Fae tore away from Royce, took a step back, and bared dripping teeth towards the glow. The hissing around us became a buzz, a frenetic roar that swallowed up every other sound. All around me, before me, above me…

I looked up.

Just as the tree nearest my window stooped, actually twisted its graceful trunk down… and a hundred thin silver branches, gleaming like knives, sank into the monster.

I watched, struck mute, as the Fae snarled, spasmed… and went limp with a distinctly canine whimper.

The tree stayed there for a moment, flexing its piercing branches. Then, apparently satisfied, it righted itself. Soft, blood-stained leaves stretched down to brush my cheeks.

“Th… thank… thank…” I couldn’t seem to make my mouth work.

The leaves pulled away, unperturbed.

Royce moaned.

The sound snapped me into action. I scrambled forwards, fighting back bile at the sight of the mangled monster.

"Royce! Are you... it’s dead, they killed it… they killed it… salt, there's so much blood..." Everything was stained red. The grass, so much of Royce… but he was moving, heaving himself upright with one arm.

He stared, glassy-eyed, at the nearby tree. Streaks of crimson slid down the silver.

Royce clutched at my shoulder, then my cheek, with one heavy hand. “Did it h-hurt you?"

I felt the wild urge to laugh. Me, hurt? The monster hadn’t touched me. I shook my head. Then the pain in my hands registered and I looked down. My palms wept blood where they’d been pierced a dozen times by glittering shards of broken glass. Several stuck from the skin like jagged, bloody teeth.

Royce and I staggered inside in a daze. In the distance Fellbrook loomed, haloed by orange light and plumes of smoke. I wanted to yank my boots on and sprint down the dirt path, go see if Durst was alright- of course he was alright, he was Durst, strong and calm and unshakable, he had to be alright- and try to help.

But Royce’s shoulder was torn and he was limping badly. One side of his trousers was slowly turning dark. My own shaking hands burned with pain.

We wouldn’t be able to help anyone in this shape.

Royce stumbled inside and collapsed into a chair.

I elbowed the front door closed and rummaged, with much wincing and whimpering, through our kitchen cabinets until I had found some clean rags and a small pitcher of water.

Royce mumbled something unintelligible. He was slumped half-out of the chair, gaze hooded and glassy.

“What?” I hurried over. My right foot landed in something warm and sticky. The floorboards beneath Royce were turning dark. The sight sent a little trickle of ice down my neck. “Royce?”

No response.

Ashes, that’s a lot of blood. I knelt, sucking in a breath. The fabric of his thick woolen trousers was heavy and wet. Jagged holes pierced the cloth in a vaguely maw-shaped pattern, all of it smeared crimson. Mangled flesh glistened beneath the cloth.

I pressed rags and my forearms against his thigh as hard as I could, trying to keep myself calm. I knew this, I could do this, Clem had taught me how to do this. “Royce, you have to… I need you to s-stay awake, okay?”

His head lolled to one side. He moaned a bit, but his eyes remained closed.

I bit back a sob. My hands were shaking. This was too much blood. “Please?”

The rags grew dark and wet beneath my skin. The ice swept down my spine, flooding my veins and belly.

Okay. Okay, breathe. I know this. Pressure won’t be enough. Tourniquet. A quick grab towards more cloth brought hot spikes in my palms. I ground my teeth through the pain, clenched both hands to pull the fabric around his thigh, and nearly fainted as the flex of muscle made pain swell to agony and a choked-out scream. I fell back, gasping.

Vaguely, I heard hissing and rustling from beyond the front door. It swelled to a vicious crescendo as I knelt there, frozen with fear at the thought that another monster might come bursting it. Outside the kitchen windows I saw branches and leaves swaying, stretching out towards something beneath them…

Abruptly, they stopped.

And there was a very gentle knock at the door.
