Appearing in the main hall of guild headquarters, right next to Milla, Luke was glad to see both Elijah and Priyah up and about. He wiped off the blood coming out of his nose and healed the internal injuries from the jump, then went over to them. The mood was somber, so the news of Nash and Mas must have reached them. Priyah's eyes were wet with tears when Luke sat down next to her.

"If I'd been there, maybe," she began.

Luke put a hand on her shoulder. "It wouldn't have made a difference."

"I'm stronger than you know," she said, voice wavering.

"There were too many Singers. None of them should have been there."

She wiped the tears off on her stained long-sleeved T-shirt. "Where were you, Luke? Couldn't you have healed them?"

His silence made her realize what she'd said, and she stammered, "I-I didn't mean it like that."

"You're right to blame me. They wanted to go, but it was my call, and I let them. You can't heal death."

"Luke," Milla said, coming to stand over them. "We need to talk."

"In a second," Luke said as he gave Priyah another nod, squeezed her shoulder, and stood. Walking over to Elijah, who was even sitting up, Luke brought out a gift. He held it out. "I brought a gift."

"For me?" Elijah asked, his face twisting in what looked like pain.

"Want me to take a look?" Luke asked, dropping the weapon into Elijah's lap.

Elijah shook his head. "It's nothing."

"Nothing serious," Luke agreed. "Just a bruised rib, but I can fix it."

"A little pain can be a good thing sometimes," Elijah said, picking up the gift. He turned it around, frowning. "What is it?"

"It's a-"

Elijah's eyes widened, and he beat Luke to it. "Wait, is this a...?"

He pressed a button, and a blade made out of some sort of light energy rose from the hilt, making a swooshing sound that brought Luke back to when he was a kid and watched the movies with his family, scarfing down popcorn and drinking way too much soda.

"It is," Luke said with a grin. "Does this mean some of your memories are returning?"

"This sword is something special," Elijah said, not taking his eyes off the blade, and he spoke in a reverent whisper, "It works in tandem with my skills." Blinking, he turned to Luke. "Sorry, what?"

"Your memories?"

"I get glimpses sometimes. Nothing big, but this brought something back. 'Now this is pod racing'?"

Luke laughed and groaned at the same time. "Really? That's the one you remember?" He stood. "Ah, well. Good to see you awake. I'll be leaving for a little while soon, and Curtis will need your help." Luke whispered the next part. "You're one of the few who can keep up, I think."

"I'll be ready," Elijah promised. "That's twice you've brought me back. Whatever you need, all you have to do is ask."

"Good man," Luke said, turning to face Milla.

"Let's step into my office," she said, turning to walk over to the other side of the room.

"You have an office?" he asked, following.

Once he'd made it to the wall, she took his hand and touched a small plaque, and they appeared in a small room with nothing more than a desk and two chairs. Luke sat down in one with a tired thud, massaging his temples. "The Singers who were coming to attack the city are gone."

"Martin gave his report," Milla said, taking the other seat. "What the hell happened out there?"

"Luom happened."

"She was there?"

Luke nodded. "And she had healed me at some point, so she knew my position. The ambush didn't work, so Curtis and I had to split up. It was a mess."

"What did you do with Luom?"

He gave her a tired look. "She's dead. That Singer who could resurrect his friends, too."

Luke explained what he'd learned, and Milla, who at first had looked like she wanted to scold him, leaned back in her chair, shaking her head. "A mess, yeah. You said it. If they're going to attack us with more of what hit this place, we'll have to move fast."

"I'm going there."

"Where?" she asked.

"The Singers."

That made her frown. "And do what, exactly? Kill them all?"

"No," Luke said, his tone a little sharp. He didn't like being talked down to, even though his sister had done that for most of his life. The joys of being the younger sibling. "I can close the portals, remember?"

"Do it from this side."

Luke shook his head. "I can't go around the globe and close them all from this side."

"Why not?"

He leaned forward in his chair. "What do you mean? They must have portals in almost every country. How am I supposed to get there? Like you said, we're running out of time, and my profession is still only level three!"

Milla blinked. "Your profession? What are you talking about?"

"Nothing, nothing. I'm just saying I'd like some downtime at some point."

"Well, sack up, little brother," Milla grunted. "I'd like to chill, too, but we've got to play the hands we're dealt, yeah?"

"I still don't see how I'd get to all the portals. Even if I did, they're all protected."

"I've talked it over with the EDP guildmasters, and most of them have people who can get places fast. If you heal them, you'll be able to follow, right?"

"I don't know..."

"And what about if you go through? Why do you think that would help? I'm guessing the portals are just as spread out on their side. Actually, I know they are."

"How?" Luke asked.

Milla gave him a look. "I asked Ray."

"Right," Luke said with a sigh.

"Come to the meeting with me tomorrow, and if you don't like the plan... you'll still do what I say."

Luke raised an eyebrow. "Because you're the guildmistress?"

"No," she said, "Because I'm your big sister, dummy."

"Where is it?" Luke asked without committing to saying yes or no.

Milla eyed him. As if to say his shenanigans didn't pass unnoticed. "It's not a place. These people are all over the world, remember? It's virtual. If you can call system stuff that."

"I don't get it. It's a chat?"

She shook her head. "There's an item in the Integrated shop for it. Kind of like conference calls with webcams, except a lot more realistic. You'll like it, I'm sure."

"Not much use for conference calls when making burgers, but I see your point. You have one of these items?"

"Two, actually."

Luke gestured to the small, sparsely furnished room. "How can you afford all this grandeur?"

"I've got my ways."

Narrowing his eyes, Luke stood to look down at her. "Fine, keep your secrets. I'll be going then. Just shout when you need me tomorrow, and I'll meet you here?"

Milla flicked something at him, and he caught it. The item looked like a small coin, except that both sides were smooth. "What is it?" he asked.

"The item you'll need. Just pop that onto here when I tell you," she said, pointing at her left temple, "And you'll be transported to wonderland. Better find a safe spot first, though."

"Fine," Luke said, pocketing the thing as he headed toward the plaque that would take him out of Milla's office.

She sighed. "One more thing, Luke."

He turned to look at her over his shoulder.

"I'm demoting you."

"Demoting me? How do you mean?"

"You were a Lieutenant in the guild. Now you're not."

"Because of Nash?"

She nodded. "And Mas. No one blames you, but they shouldn't have been there. After the block disappeared, people were rattled, and now they're overcompensating, doing things they aren't ready for. It's too dangerous."

"We're in agreement there," he said. "Demoted. Sounds good. I won't be taking people into danger again if I can help it. The members have to get stronger, Milla. They need to keep up."

"They're doing their best, Luke. Most of them are in parties and clear a dungeon every day. And that's despite the Singers."

"Have them do two dungeons. Or three. We'll beat the Singers back soon enough, but it's only going to get more dangerous after that. As you said, we have six months."

"We'll be ready," Milla said, a hint of doubt creeping into her words.

Luke nodded as he touched the plaque to leave. "I hope we will."

Not feeling like he wanted to partake in the gloom of the main hall in the guild, Luke left. Still not feeling Hannah's presence, he sighed and headed off, walking in a random direction, thinking he might do some enchanting now that he had almost a full day to relax before the big meeting with the EDP people. The prospect excited him. It had been too long since he had the time. A day wasn't much, but it was more than what had been on offer for about as long as he could remember. Nodding to himself, he plopped the Shelter down in an alley and entered, picking out things he could enchant.
