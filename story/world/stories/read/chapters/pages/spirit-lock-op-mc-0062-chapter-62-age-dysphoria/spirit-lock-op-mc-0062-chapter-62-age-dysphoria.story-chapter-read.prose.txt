“What’s that face for?” Stella demanded.

“Uh… It’s…” He took a deep breath. “It’s actually likely that he held that photo-op to destabilize you before your meeting.”

“You can’t be serious.”

Ritrix shifted uncomfortably, noting the people watching them. “Come with me.” He led her to a private room and said, “Stella. Your whole career, you’ve abused your appearance to make people underestimate you.” He presented her dress. “Don’t fall for it yourself.”

Stella pinched the bridge of her nose and squeezed her eyes shut. “That’s what I’m doing, but I can’t help it. If I could, I wouldn’t have filters that blur faces. That said, I’m about to meet him and now this happened.”

Ritrix pondered her words with a subtle grimace.

“Be honest,” she said, ”how skilled do you think he is, really?”

“He terrifies me,” Ritrix said bluntly. “It might do you good to see him as an enemy.”

Her eyes narrowed. “Are you trying to get rid of him?”

“Absolutely not. He’s done right by my daughters, so I’m indebted to him. I’ve also seen the profound change in their skill, and gained glimpses of his knowledge. If we brought him under our wing, we would prosper. That said, I hope he never speaks to my daughters again—because I fear him. And I think you should, too. Because if you don’t treat him like an enemy, I think he’ll abuse your weaknesses and eat you alive.”

Stella listened and nodded. “Thank you for the warning. I needed that.” She left and returned to the ballroom, confident that she could gain the upper hand by being ruthless.

That mindset was short-lived. The second she returned to the ballroom, the entire room fell silent.

What? she thought.

She followed their eyes to her left and saw Hayden Reskco with a champagne glass in his hands—speaking to no one.

“Oh thank the heavens,” he whispered. “I thought I’d be the only young person here.”

Her lips quivered, “I’m not…”

“I know.” He turned to her with a slight smile. “Hello, Stella.”

Her eyes flickered with rage. “You know who I am?”

“It appears so.”

She flashed a cruel, vibrant smile. “Well, you’ve certainly made a bold first impression.”

Hayden pointed at the musicians, who began playing ahead of schedule, then snapped his fingers at his guests and swiped his index finger horizontally. His gesture and cold eyes told everyone to mind their business. To her shock, they did so.

“You better hope your demonstration goes as expected,” Stella warned. “If you fail, these people are gonna tan your skin and use it as a carpet.”

Hayden scanned the room and said, “You know… I’ve found that powerful emotions are the only way to break age dysphoria symptoms.”

“Hmmm… so you’ve been acting this way for my own benefit?”

“No, I’ve done it for mine because it’d be impossible to have a serious conversation otherwise.”

“Oh, I see… If that’s true, why’d you hold that little photo-op?”

“Because rudeness isn’t enough to overcome social dissonance. It requires sharp emotions: hatred… resentment… jealousy.”

“Well you earned that.”

“And we’re speaking.”

Her cheek twitched. “What do you want?”

“Your patronage, starting with a request.”

“And in exchange?”

“I’ll cure your age dysphoria.”

Stella laughed. “You’ve already made me dislike you—I suggest you stop here.”

“I can change your body constitution,” he said emotionlessly. “It will allow you to obtain a mature appearance without losing your youth. On the contrary, it will vastly increase your lifespan.”

She rolled her eyes. “And you haven’t done this… because?”

“Because I’m eighteen, Ms. Ingsley. Any other age would line Nebula Auction House up for bid-rigging and market manipulation lawsuits.”

Her lips curved into a shrewd smile. “I can see your business concerns, but what about your political concerns? No one takes teenagers seriously.”

He eyed her boredly. “Do you take me as a teenager, Ms. Ingsley?”

“It’s rude to answer a question with another question.”

“It’s also rude to waste another’s time.”

Her neck tightened. “I suggest you impress your guests before you speak to them. If you offend all your patrons, you’ll find yourself alone.”

“I suggest you meet me past the south hall barricade at 7 pm,” he countered. “I’ll be in a private room in the Sarlimo.” He glanced at her sharply. “Don’t be late.”

Hayden walked into the crowd and headed directly to representatives of the Morine Domain. To her horror, he had them laughing within the first twenty seconds—and it was genuine laughter. It wasn’t just them. He somehow had charisma that charmed anyone he spoke to easily.

Stella didn’t stay idle. She also charmed her guests, as usual. It felt normal, but it apparently wasn’t.

“It seems my worries were unfounded,” Ritrix said.

“What do you mean?” she asked.

“I was worried you’d lose your edge earlier. You were in rough shape.”

Stella immediately remembered Hayden’s words.

I’ve found that powerful emotions are the only way to break age dysphoria symptoms.

“Is there anything you have to warn me about?” she asked tersely.

“Yeah,” Ritrix said. “I think—”

Hayden tapped a champagne glass with his fork, and somehow the ringing sound spread through the entire ballroom. He stepped onto the stage, waiting for the room’s full attention before speaking.

“Distinguished guests, from home”—he glanced at Stella—”and beyond.” He swept his gaze over the rest of the ballroom. “We have entered a new era—an era of spirit arts—rather than simply spirits.”

He lifted his hand and summoned fire above his palm.

“An era of magic. An era so unbelievable that AI analysis will reject the very idea that a human could create fire with their fingertips. I’m sure it’s hard for you to believe that this isn’t fake; I’m sure you think it’s a magic trick, an illusion, a prefabricated setup I’ve executed with flawless accuracy. I’m sure you’ll scoff and remind each other that I’ve allowed no recordings here today”—he made eye contact with major leaders—”but this is no trick. I will prove it to each and every one of you.”

Murmurs spread through the room as the guests studied the fire on Hayden’s palm.

“I’ve asked you to bring your own equipment—your own experts and specialists. I will—with great care—use your equipment to prove this technology exists. Whether you believe it after is none of my concern. You’re free to join my auction—or deny your senses. With that said, let’s begin.”

Hayden’s technology may have been legitimate, but it felt like a magic trick. He removed his shirt to dispel claims of him hiding things in his sleeves, and had every verifier check his workstation. All he had were two paint brushes with normal paint in one and heavy tar in the other—items that verifiers brought themselves. He then took model ships or other materials and painted them underneath a screen. When the item returned, it had a thick layer of tar that covered whatever he painted—but once he glued a cora crystal to the top of the model, they flew.

No, they didn’t just fly—they swooped through the room, flying over the guests with ease. It was as if he controlled it with his mind.

Stella heard patrons scoff all around her.

“This is bullshit.”

“Yeah, I don’t believe it. You can’t make things fly with paint.”

“This is absurd.”

Yet one after the other, those same people checked the station with their own paint, tar, brushes, and crystal—only to leave stupefied.

Stella did something different. She brought Hayden the largest personal shuttle that they could get into the ballroom. It was over a metric ton once assembled, and to her shock, it flew around as effortlessly as the small models. The shuttle the Morine Domain brought did the same.

There was no doubt in anyone’s mind: the technology was real. For that reason, once Hayden collected and destroyed the models, he was swarmed by people trying to court him. He told them that he would speak to everyone on the second and third days of the expo, then disappeared like a magic trick.

Stella did the same, following guards through a militarized barricade in the south hall to the Sarlimo restaurant, which was empty save for the hosts and servers.

Hayden was waiting for her in a private room, sitting before a bottle of wine.

“Hello, Ms. Ingsley,” Hayden said. “Did you enjoy the show?”

He had a model shuttle fly to the candles. It shot tiny fireballs to light the wicks.
