To Vir’s surprise, it wasn’t the blast that hit him first, but a shockwave of pure force that slammed  into Iteration IV hard enough to send Vir flying.

Cycling his prana to the maximum and slowing time with Haste, Vir flared Balancer of Scales to multiply his weight tenfold, slamming back down against the metal hull so hard it groaned under the strain.

Saunak's contraption held together, but only barely. Vir could feel the structural inscriptions straining under the combined weight of his amplified mass and the residual pressure of the shockwave, and he prayed that the genius Thaumaturge’s craftsmanship held for another minute. That was all he’d need, because one way or another, all of this would be over by then.

Above him, the weapon’s prana accumulation reached levels that warped the surrounding air, becoming visible to the eye. Not with flames as it did with Vir, but a writhing mass that reminded him disconcertingly of a Prana Swarm.

The beam unleashed its torrent of energy, descending like a falling wall, and while the supercompressed prana might only have started as wide as the panel, it grew and grew as it approached.

Vir didn’t even think about taking it head-on. Even if he did survive—an extremely uncertain outcome—the airship he was standing on would definitely disintegrate.

Thankfully, he didn’t need to.

The core in his chest generated prana endlessly, forcing Vir to dissipate it into Siya’s orb… under normal circumstances.

It was about time he put all the energy that had plagued his every waking moment to actual use.

A defensive use, though that was easier said than done.

A few Aspect Arts Aspect of the Final Sanctuary and Aspect of the Stone Sentinel could create barriers, but in both cases, they used inscriptions to convert prana into physical force and walls of Earth, respectively.

Raw prana simply could not be made to function as a wall, as the quantity required to even attempt it would drain a Prime Mejai in seconds.

Such rules, however, could be bent in the face of infinite prana.

Vir poured the core's output into every pore of his body and fired.

A hemispheric dome of compressed Ash prana formed around him and Iteration IV, dense enough to blacken the air, yet wide enough to defend against the incoming attack.

The energy was so thick that blotted out the sun and everything else around him, plunging Vir into total darkness.

It was good, then that the Akh Nara did not need eyes to see. The Third Eye Chakra and Iksana's Clarity showed him far more than his eyes ever could, feeding him glimpses of the future. Showing him the Vimana’s beam as well as the state of his own body, already struggling under the strain of channeling so much prana.

The beam struck the shield, the impact rattling every bone in Vir's frame as though it were a physical thing and not pure magic.

Yet despite a level of output that would have drained his own oceanic reserves in a few seconds without the Prana Core, Clarity showed him that the shield would not hold.

Though the prana currently flowing through him was incomprehensibly vast, the beam was on another level entirely. A stark reminder of the dizzying heights that civilization had reached. To think this wasn’t even their most fearsome weapon…

Vir flared Yuma's Embrace to its absolute limit. The Ultimate Art, inscribed by the most capable Thaumaturge to ever exist, strained against the load, and he could feel the very inscription itself beginning to fray attempting to keep up.

The Ultimate Arts, despite being designed by Janak himself, were never meant to bear such a load.

Despite that, Vir drew deeper from the core. Deeper than he ever had before, and yet, that roiling vortex in his chest did not diminish. Instead, it grew even more dense, pulling prana out of the very energy that formed reality itself. Like a wound bled faster when squeezed.

In any other weapon, that quality would have been a gift. A route to invincibility, even.

Yet all those who wished for ultimate power couldn’t understand the cost that came with it.

Vir did. He knew immediately what it meant.

The fabric of reality around him warped, straining under the crushing weight of his existence.

With the aid of his Crown-Chakra-amplified Third Eye, he could almost see the microfractures develop in real time, the local prana density climbing far past anything normal, the air around him destabilizing as it did just before an Ash Tear opened.

If the core kept accelerating, it would not be long before reality itself buckled and the Tears that had plagued the realms began anew. Sucking Vir into some broken realm between universes.

Was this, too, the work of their enemy? He wondered. It would be the height of irony if it were. The most powerful weapon in the world causing its own doom.

But at least this time, the core was within Vir, and as such, he had a semblance of control, if only just.

The shield held. Barely.

Yet merely enduring the onslaught was no path to victory. If he simply absorbed this attack, the Vimana would fire another. And another after that.

The humans aboard clearly had no compunctions about causing casualties, and there was every reason to expect they would continue until the city below was reduced to rubble.

Then, when the second volley hit, Vir would be left maimed and burned. Miracles never happened twice, after all.

Even now, his skin cooked inside his armor, his arms burning in agony, and Yuma's Embrace barely kept pace with the damage. It reminded him too much of being back in  Janak’s machine. Back then, however, the incredible contraption had multiplied his abilities a thousandfold.

No, Vir would have to strike back, and soon.

Luckily, he did not need to double his prana output for it. A counter beam would be concentrated. A tight lance, fired upward through a small aperture in the shield, requiring far less output than the barrier itself cost to maintain.

And so, against every voice in his head advising against it, Vir called forth yet more prana from the core.

He focused on a tiny sliver of the shield and amplified the prana he sent out at that spot.

Clarity told him the result the instant the lance fired.

The prana dissipated, scattering apart in the heated air, fizzling out a mere third of the way to its destination.

It wasn’t enough. He needed more.

And so, left with little choice, Vir pulled even more from the core.

The strain on reality climbed past any threshold he had ever experienced, and he felt the boundary between dimensions thin around him.

He kept drawing, charging up an attack he was sure not even the Vimana could resist.

If only his right arm wouldn’t fail first.

He saw the muscles break down in Clarity’s vision moments before they did. He felt the bones beneath crack from the sheer prana pressure forcing through his blood vessels, and when he glanced at it, the skin had boiled over, the flesh beneath starting to char.

The pain hit him an instant later, delayed by the body's inability to process so many simultaneous catastrophes at once.

When it arrived, it consumed him.

He held on by will alone, barely managing to focus long enough to fire the incredible mass of prana he’d accumulated.

The second lance was a column so dense it stripped paint from the airship's hull as it left him, and before, Clarity told him the result before it happened.

The lance had scored a direct hit, crashing into the panel head-on.

The white column wavered for a moment, then lost coherence, vanishing in an instant.

For one long second, there was only silence…. and then the weapon emplacement exploded.

The result of the discharge being interrupted mid-fire, all the accumulated prana that had been about to leave the weapon was redirected outward in a chaotic blast, sending slivers in every direction.

Some struck the Vimana's own hull. Some careened harmlessly into the sky, while most were vaporized by Vir’s prana barrier.

But easily a dozen of them missed the barrier entirely, streaking down to the ground.

He watched through the dimming edges of his vision as the slivers plummeted onto the city below.

Tiny specks, barely a thousandth of what the main beam would have delivered. A negligible fraction.

And yet, even a tiny part of something so vast was absolutely devastating.

The first one leveled an entire block. Vir watched in disbelief as buildings just ceased to be and towers that had stood for centuries were reduced to dust.

That, however, was just the opening act.

The slivers that followed destroyed Asura airships, splitting their hulls, causing demons to fall to their deaths. They took out markets, houses, and arenas.

And yet, despite the unprecedented tragedy unfolding before his eyes, the horror of the city below was distant to Vir’s mind.

For there was a far, far more pressing concern occupying Vir’s thoughts at that moment. More terrible than the destruction of Camar Gadin. Than even the deaths of all those demons.

The core had not slowed when he stopped drawing from it. Instead, it continued to grow, expanding past any limit even a thousand of Siya's prana absorbers could ever hope to contain.

The fabric of reality around his chest buckled.

The core that contained the prana of the Ashen realm… began to rupture.
