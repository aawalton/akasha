Tired, beaten, and recovering from their previous injuries. It took Levi and the mage five days on foot to find a faster way to reach the next town.

[ Dungeon Rank: E

Requirement: None

Warning: The Dungeon will close once you step in, and you will not be offered a choice of exit until you clear the dungeon.

]

The entrance to a dungeon was right in front of them, and the pair was left arguing about whether they were ready to hit an E-ranked dungeon.

“Come on! It’s just an E-ranked dungeon,” Xara said, pulling at her hair in frustration. “Didn’t you say that you handled a Krupi all by yourself?”

“Yes, but that was different–”

“Different how?” Xara said exasperatedly. “An E-ranker is an E-ranker. You’ve taken down one by yourself, and this shouldn’t be any different.”

“But it is, and you know it,” Levi argued, “We are both F-ranked synced, albeit high-ranking ones, but we both know that we are going to struggle against a single E-ranked monster. That dungeon is definitely going to have more than just one E-ranked monster.”

“Yes, but we need to get out of this forest. My legs are killing me.”

“No, they’re not, but you know what could actually kill you? The monsters inside the dungeon.”

“You’re insufferable, come on! Take a risk with me!” She said.

Levi couldn’t help but stare at the mage. Her insistence on jumping into the dungeon, which was nothing more than an accredited cerpital death trap, was confusing to him. If Levi had to guess, he’d say that the mage was going through some sort of phase that made her seek death or something like that.

He should’ve known that anyone who was willing to follow him despite the number of people coming from his head had to have a touch of madness, but at the same time, Levi had to admit that the mage was winning him over with her words.

Dungeon diving alone back at Gantus had been hell. He’d only survived due to sheer will and the need to live, but as the days had passed with them walking in the forest, he’d begun to lose some of that zeal to live. Right now, the mage was making him see glimpses of the him that had fought through the pain in the gremlin dungeon.

I’m not alone anymore. I have her. Levi paused as he considered the thought. The mage had proven to be a valuable person to have, contrary to his earlier belief.

Xara had kept him grounded. Given him reasons to live, even if it involved fighting off a monster and a ranger that were both higher-ranked than him.

Levi had been so motivated after killing the Krupi in its cave that he felt that he was ready to take on more dungeons, but at the moment, he wasn’t so sure of his decision, because the dungeon in front of him was E-ranked.

It seemed like he’d come this far through sheer luck and willpower, but how far was that truly going to take him? Already, he was hesitating to hit the dungeon. The threat of Ghost finding him was a distant echo in his mind because of how long ago he’d last seen the rogue.

One week. It’s been one week since I last saw him, and the lack of imminent danger is bringing back my sense of self-preservation.

Levi wasn’t a fool. He knew that he needed to get into this dungeon if he wanted to level up his skills and improve his odds of holding up in a fight against the rogue hunting for the stone. Still, his every being rebelled against the idea of going into the dungeon.

His skin crawled at the thought of having his shoulder popped by a cudgel or by an errant claw tearing into his side. Levi knew that he was only a thief. He wasn’t prepared to become a killer, but what choice did he have in that matter? His life was hanging by a thread, and it was moments like these that decided if that thread was going to be snipped or not.

“I’ll do it!” Levi said, the words escaping through his lips faster than he could stop them. “But if I die, I’m going to kill you.” He said the last part as a joke to cool himself from the heat spreading through his brain at the weight of the decision that he’d just made.

“You can’t kill me, you'll already be dead,” Xara chuckled. “But I’ll do my best to make sure we get out alive. “

“Sure, you will, “ Levi rolled his eyes.” Come on, let’s get in before I change my mind.”

The first thing that Levi noticed about the dungeon as soon as he stepped into it was that it was coloured differently from the one that he’d entered back in Gantus, and the spacing was different.

Even with his enhanced perception, he couldn’t see where the walls of the dungeon were, and by all indications, it seemed like they’d just stepped into an open field, but Levi couldn’t be too sure, considering how dark it was down in the dungeons.

He constantly scanned around, and the moment he tried to focus on the darkness surrounding them, it seemed like his head was about to fall off.

Almost as if something was preventing him from looking too deeply into the darkness. The feeling was very familiar, and Levi was trying to remember where he’d felt this kind of feeling until it suddenly clicked for him. Distortion.

The darkness in this dungeon followed the same principles as distortion. He couldn't really seem to get a lock on anything in his eyes. It almost seemed like his eyes wanted to retreat into the back of his head from all the unnatural darkness that surrounded them.

“Launch a fireball,” Levi said.

A bright flare lit up to his side as a fireball manifested on the open palm of the mage. Without another word, Xara launched the fireball, and Levi followed the trail of fire and noted with disappointment as the fireball seemed to flicker until it disappeared.

It hadn’t hit anyone or anything, the fireball just kept going until it was no more, which meant that Levi was either right in his assumption that this was an open field or there was someone or something that was messing with their heads.”Again! Go up this time.”

Xara grunted in response as another fireball appeared in her palm, and then she threw it upwards just as he’d asked.

Levi received similar disappointment as the fireball seemed to be swallowed by the darkness just like the previous one.

Just what was this intangible darkness that they had found themselves inside? Just what exactly had brought them here just to kill them in this oppressive darkness ? Levi didn’t know, but to be honest, it wasn’t making much sense to him.

They hadn’t been attacked from the moment they stepped in here, which meant that whatever was here was either gauging them or at least watching him from afar, which made much more sense to him. At the moment, it was more than unbelievable that his entire being seemed to resonate with this darkness; it seemed to resonate with this void.

“Any other bright ideas?” the mage asked.

“No, you?”

“No”

“Then I suggest that we start walking. The earlier we get out of here, the better for us.”

“After you,” she said.

Levi nodded even though she couldn’t see him and began moving north. He had no idea what he was supposed to expect, so he tightened his grip on his dagger. Over the recent days, he’d started to get used to the emptiness in his other hand.

Losing that dagger to the mage’s enchanted fire had affected his means of attack, but he’d needed to make that sacrifice at that moment.

Levi wrinkled his nose at the thought of the burning ranger, and as he did that, something odd happened. It seemed as though a fog had lifted over his head, and his brain had screamed one thing to him. You’re in danger, wake up.

He struggled to understand what those words meant. By stepping into the dungeon, he’d known that he was in danger, and so what exactly was this type of danger, and more importantly, why was his subconscious telling him to wake up?

Or was he misinterpreting what had echoed in his mind? Was his subconscious telling him to push the errant thoughts away and focus on the darkness? He didn’t know, and he wasn’t about to spend the majority of his time trying to figure out what exactly his mind… his mind…

My mind.

What if I’m thinking about this all wrong? What if the monster that I’m supposed to fight is right in front of me, but I just can’t see it?  A chill crept up Levi’s spine at the thought. It was very well within the realms of possibility that the monster that he was supposed to fight was right in front of him, but he didn’t know just what exactly he was supposed to do.

Fighting in this kind of darkness was quite the task; he couldn’t see more than a few feet in front of him, which was inconvenient for him. The E-ranked dungeo definitely had a dangerous atmosphere, and he’d have to figure out how he was going to handle it if he was going to have any chance at surviving.

“Stop,” Xara called out. “I see something”.

“What?” He turned to the mage askance. There was no reason for the mage to have better perception than he did at this point, so it was a bit unnerving that she’d picked up something before him.

“The ground, it’s made of darkness, look!” She conjured another fireball and hurled it at the ground.

Levi watched as the fireball travelled farther than whatever his feet seemed to be standing on, going lower and lower until it turned into nothingness. It seemed as though the darkness was endless from all directions, and he wondered just how exactly they were supposed to solve the mystery behind where they were.

“This isn’t real, it can’t be!” she blurted. Xara turned to him, a flicker of fear in her eyes.”Are you… Are you real?”

“Of course, I am,” Levi couldn’t understand why the mage seemed to have forgotten that he was real. It seemed like just a second ago that they were outside the dungeon, contemplating whether they were going to go into the dungeon or not, and now, they were contemplating whether the other person was real or not.

Everything seemed to have fallen apart since they’d walked into the dungeon. The reality of their situation just didn’t make much sense to him. If anything, it was one of the most annoying things that he’d ever witnessed, and his mind was running wild trying to figure out what exactly was going on.

My mind.

She was right, this wasn’t real, it couldn’t be. His mind had tried to warn him to wake up, but he hadn’t understood why at the time. But now, he was beginning to figure out the potential consequences of being asleep while his real body fought with whatever monster was in the real world.

“You were right, it isn’t real. None of this is,” Levi said.

“So, what are we going to do? How are we supposed to end this?”

How? The words tumbled in his head until everything came to a standstill. “We’ll have to kill each other.”
