A glaive shimmered into existence in my dominion before V’s outstretched hand and floated toward the God of Velmoryn.

It was a beautiful weapon. Its shaft was dark and etched with runes. At one end rested a large blade, edges dark crimson, the color of blood as it begins to dry. The other end bore a pommel shaped like a dragon’s open mouth, teeth and all.

Guidance.

[Claw of Death (Unfinished) – Celestial]

A glaive forged with the divinity of the greatest blacksmith ever to walk the mortal realm, who later ascended as @!$@#$!$. The materials used in its creation cannot be identified, as you have yet to encounter any creature capable of yielding them.

Claw of Death remains unfinished. Its creator forged it with a single purpose: to be wielded only by its rightful owner. Only after the glaive absorbs the true owner’s blood will its true nature and purpose be revealed.

Seeing that the glaive was Celestial Tier already left me pleasantly surprised. When V had told me it might be Celestial, but would at least be Relic Tier, I had assumed it would definitely end up being the lower option rather than the best possible one.

However, just as the tier was unexpectedly good, the description was equally confusing.

I had already gotten used to Guidance not revealing the titles of deities. From my own experience, I knew just how coveted that information was, and even when my rank grew, that part would likely never change. It would probably always hide the titles, unless I knew who I was dealing with, of course.

But this time, it had not revealed even the materials or the weapon’s effects. And worst of all, the glaive needed my angel’s blood to show its true form.

It all sounded a little too suspicious.

I knew full well just how dangerous it was for one’s blood to fall into someone else’s hands. Avenor had made that mistake when he allowed Elisabeth to get her hands on his blood. Noah had even used it to track Avenor and find out that he was connected to me.

In a way, this entire situation I was now so desperately trying to solve had started with that one mistake.

And my angel’s blood sounded even more dangerous. Who knew what it could be used for? It might even reveal some information about me.

Although I had to admit there was no reason to think that V, the God of Merchants, or that mysterious blacksmith had any means to obtain Aurelion’s blood after he allowed the weapon to absorb it, I couldn’t help myself. I was always inclined to imagine the worst possible scenario and consider my actions with that in mind.

As my hyper-cynical personality continued acting up, V eventually grew tired of the silence and spoke.

“The blacksmith warned me that the glaive requires Your Highness’s angel’s blood to show its true strength,” he said, rubbing the thin ring on his pinky as he muttered, “Celestial Tier artifacts are demanding that way…”

After seeing the weapon, my first impulse had been to summon Aurelion immediately and check if the glaive was truly as valuable as V made it sound. Without testing it through my angel, I had no real way to confirm its worth. After all, despite somewhat gaining a form, I was still nothing but a figure made of energy. I could not actually hold things.

Not that I would ever touch this particular glaive.

If I was this worried about letting it absorb Aurelion’s blood, I was even more protective of my own essence.

“I’ll confirm its value later,” I said, moving the glaive out of my dominion.

“Oh, its quality and function are something I can guarantee with my own life,” V said firmly. “The blacksmith who forged it is someone even high-ranking gods struggle to commission custom items from. And he put extra effort into this one…”

V stopped, his mouth blurring as he communicated with the God of Merchants. It lasted only a short moment.

“I must apologize, Your Lordship. I am not allowed to reveal the details,” he said, bowing slightly before producing a small parchment that shimmered silver. “Because of the late delivery of the item, we will only take half of what we agreed on. My Liege places great importance on holding to the promises we make.”

“I see,” I said.

Despite my suspicions, I could not deny that I had never been tricked by the God of Merchants. If anything, I had swindled him a couple of times.

The parchment shimmered silver once more as I made up my mind.

[You’ve paid 5,000 Divinity Points to God of Merchants!]

I was about to ask V about humanity’s technology and powers when he suddenly bowed after receiving my payment and bid me farewell.

“Your Excellency, my Liege has summoned all of us. I must leave,” he said.

Before I could try to stop him, he disappeared from my dominion.

That was weird.

This was not the first time V had shown a desire to leave, but never so soon after visiting me.

Could he have realized I was going to ask for information that might prove useful in the war? If so, then the God of Merchants was no longer neutral and had sided with Noah. And if that was the case, the glaive now hovering in my realm was dangerous, and I had been right to hesitate before letting Aurelion use it.

Or I was completely wrong, and the God of Merchants was dealing with a problem that had nothing to do with me. Or… perhaps he summoned all his avatars like this from time to time, and V had simply happened to be in my realm when he did…

I’m overthinking this. I need to stop being so paranoid…

I reined in my thoughts and summoned Aurelion once again.

“My Liege,” he said, repeating the usual routine.

“What do you think about this?” I asked, making the glaive float toward him until it stopped a short distance away.

Even without feeling his emotions, I could clearly see excitement ignite in his scarlet eyes. He reached for the weapon, and I nearly stopped him before his fingers touched the shaft, but then I noticed that his hand was covered in divinity.

“It’s calling to me,” he said, almost in a trance.

Just in case my suspicions were right, I made my divinity surround us, forming a thick shell that would not allow anything to pass through without my permission. If the glaive proved even remotely suspicious after absorbing Aurelion’s blood, I would destroy it immediately.

Holding the glaive, Aurelion swung it several times, its crimson edge leaving red afterimages in its wake.

He seemed content at first, but then his eyes narrowed as he studied the weapon.

After a brief moment, he brought the glaive’s blade to his palm and cut into it. He had to apply so much force to draw blood that I could not help feeling proud of my angel.

Even more so when the glaive shimmered and grew slightly longer, adjusting itself to better suit Aurelion’s height. The ribbon beneath the blade gleamed gold, while the runes etched along its shaft began to shift, changing their shapes and positions.

“My goal… My goal is to cut down everyone who might oppose my Liege, whether mortal or god.”

I felt Aurelion’s thoughts as his gaze turned fierce.

The glaive shimmered more brightly than before. Then, as though someone had flipped a switch, its light vanished.

Guidance.

[Claw of Death – Celestial]

A glaive forged with the divinity of the greatest blacksmith ever to walk the mortal realm, who later ascended as @!$@#$!$. The materials used in its creation cannot be identified, as you have yet to encounter any creature capable of yielding them.

Effect: Once enough divinity is channeled into it, it may cut through the energy itself.
