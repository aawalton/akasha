Chapter II.XIV (2.14)

While the temperature did lower as Mitsuko, Sterling, and Holly followed the path, the snow under their feet remained slushy and wet. Mitsuko cast Mend their boots several times to keep their feet from rotting as they trudged forward. Sterling got carried on Mitsuko’s shoulders as they trudged on. Pine trees spotted the landscape around them, the base of their trunks surrounded by a ring of wet dirt rather than snow. Birds that shouldn’t belong on the island were settling in on the tree branches and Mitsuko spotted a few wild juhyo huddled in the shade.

It took nearly a day of travel until they finally arrived at a small village. The hike up the mountain would have been momentous if not for the boundless renewal of energy at Mitsuko’s command. As they entered the village, they finally saw some signs of civilized life.

A few smaller sculpts darted around, throwing wet snowballs at one another. Mitsuko suffered at the hands of one such assault as she was unlucky enough to be behind a nimble dodger. Rather than apologize, the children ran away when they realized the strike had splattered against Mitsuko’s jacket.

As the snow sculpt children fled, two furry heads poked up from behind a mound of snow.

“They’re retreating!” a little girl cried out. “Now’s our chance! After them Tomlin! VENGENCE!”

Two half-Kemon children raced past Mitsuko. Mitsuko recognized them from the many pictures Jay had shown her right before their departure. The sight of children made her pulse race. She dearly hoped that whoever the guardian was on this island, that they didn’t use children as puppets like her last few experiences. She was so tired of having to fight innocent people.

But if the guardian had any hooks in these kids, there was no sign of it as they reengaged in a snowball battle behind one of the wooden huts. There were squeals of surprise and outrage, but nothing more sinister than a betrayal made of snow.

“Do you think bear Kemon children are referred to as cubs?” Holly asked Mitsuko as they strolled forward.

“Please don’t say offensive things in front of their mother,” Mitsuko warned her. While she knew Holly was too personable and self-aware to actually offend someone, she still felt like it was worth saying.

“Is ignorance offensive? In that case, people offend me every single day.”

“Please don’t be obtuse and argue with me for the sake of arguing.”

“Psh, you’re no fun today,” Holly pouted. “This timeloop has changed you for the worse. Back when I first met you, you used to be so easy to fluster.”

Mitsuko rolled her eyes. “A lot happened in my life between meeting you and the timeloops starting.”

“Oh yeah. I guess that’s true. But now that you’re a powerful mage there is something different about how you hold yourself.”

“That’s true,” Sterling commented from his perch on her shoulder. “You are far more confident than you once were. Granted, when I first met you, you did throw away your life in an attempt to kill Rodrick. So some measure of confidence always existed. However, I fear your self-preservation skills may have suffered. If they ever even existed from the start.”

An adult sculpt stumbled out of a wooden building nearby. Half of his face was warped, as if melted and refrozen. The man had a long beard made from icicles that reached his chest. He was bundled up in a fur cloak. Mitsuko recognized them as juhyo pelts, designed to keep the wearer cold.

“Welcome, travelers, to Porcelain Island,” he greeted them. His voice was soft and a bit slurred, as if speaking around a numb tongue. “I am afraid you’ve found us at an unfortunate time. What brings you here?”

“Hello!” Holly waved at the man. “We want to try to help! We were wondering if any weirdos have appeared on the island since the dome appeared. I know a lot of rather odd occurrences have probably happened in the last few days, but does anything not directly caused by the horrible heat stand out?”

The normal side of the sculpt’s face scrunched up while the deformed half twitched. “Are you suggesting that the culprit of this atrocity might be hiding on Porcelain Island? I find that an unlikely prospect. We are all suffering. Your efforts would be better spent investigating the rogues on Verdant. Who set you on our path? Those accusations are unfounded.”

Holly shrugged. “I meant no offense. We’re just two people investigating. People are on all of the islands looking for any clues or hints to what might have happened. We’re seeking the truth and don’t want to leave any avenue for success unexamined.”

Mitsuko kept her face neutral as Holly delicately danced around the truth. There certainly were people on the other islands investigating, but they had nothing to do with them. The gnome’s words though soothed the man’s concerns. He visibly relaxed once he believed his island wasn’t being singled out by them.

“Hm.” The sculpt tapped his icicle beard as he considered. “Normally, I would invite you inside, though I fear the temperatures in our homes may not be suitable for flesh company. And I suppose I should first introduce myself. My name is King Lars Sturluson. I rule over this small kingdom. Welcome to Porcelain Island.”

Mitsuko paused for two seconds, processing the information, and then immediately bowed deeply to the king. Beside her, Holly did the same. Neither took a knee, as that might be seen as too subservient and they were meant to be emissaries of Emperor Sasaki, but they still kept their bows low.

“I apologize for our blunder and uncouthness earlier in our conversation,” Holly said. “We were ignorant that a line of royalty was present on the island.”

“None of that. My nation is small compared to those of the wider world. Please raise yourselves back up and meet my eyes. I ask that you speak to me as you might anyone else.” Despite his words, Mitsuko could tell through his pleased tone he liked the gesture of respect.

However, with his rank now revealed, Mitsuko was on high alert. If the guardian was going to take control of anyone on this island, this sculpt would be a prime candidate for an acolyte. A monarch with influence over the populous.

“A bit full of himself, don’t you think?” Sterling said. “Back in my day the sculpts were the ones who bowed to me. I still remember a princess over here throwing herself at me. Pretty thing.”

Mitsuko resisted the urge to tell the cat to shut up. It wasn’t as if anyone had asked him to bow.

“As for your question,” King Lars said. “I don’t know of any suddenly suspicious new arrivals. The only ones to come here in the last two weeks were the Kemon ambassador and his wife and children. I highly doubt they were complacent in any conspiracy or scheme to end us.”

“I agree,” Mitsuko said, speaking for the first time since their dialogue started. “Any strange happenings?”

“It is hard to say, as our island continues to melt all around us.” Then King Lars’ good eye sharpened and focused on her. “Actually, there is one thing. There are reports of strange noises from the ice caves at the island’s center.”

That sounded exactly like what Mitsuko was looking for. She resisted the urge to grin.

“However,” King Lars continued, “I don’t believe access will be simple.”

“What do you mean?” Holly asked. “Are there topographical obstacles?”

“That is one way in which to phrase it. The island is melting all around us. And the ice caves are not immune to that. While I believe deeper in the caves might retain some of the cold of winter, you will find them difficult to enter. Even before the dome, we could only step foot within during the dead of night.”

“You mean they’re flooded by snowmelt?” Holly asked.

“Precisely.”

Mitsuko crouched down and scooped up some snow into her palm. It slowly melted, leaving her open palm wet. Then she smiled and cast Mend. All of the sudden, snow once again returned to her hand.

“I don’t think that will be an issue for us.”
