Nevaramis smelled like fresh soil and engine oil, which was impressive for a flying city from a lost civilization.

This happened after Rafin the farmer got his hands on the agricultural platform. The man had turned a section of ancient Architect-designed biodome into something that looked like a farming village from the Al-Qasir delta, complete with irrigation trenches, hand-built rain shelters, and finally a small army of maintenance drones he'd somehow bullied into following a planting schedule.

I appeared in a flash of Bifrost light at the edge of his eastern field and found him kneeling between two rows of eggplant, arguing with a drone.

Today was a long day, but it was nearing its end. I'd come straight from Goryeo, which I'd reached straight from Amravati, with a two-hour detour in between spent talking Amelia out of accompanying me to Ha-Yun's garden.

The Dragon Princess pointed out, correctly, that she could arrive and depart whenever she chose. After some physical reassurance from me, she'd eventually decided she had council reports to review back in Nevaramis that had been waiting long enough.

"No, no, not there! I already said, the spacing is wrong." Rafin, father of Lailah, a Level 41 [Farmer] now compared to his Level 22 self, whom I’d met months ago, and the single least impressed person on Nevaramis, waved a dirt-caked hand at the hovering silver orb. "You'll crowd the roots. Move it three inches left."

The drone beeped. Rafin squinted at it.

"Don't give me that tone."

"Hello, Rafin."

The middle-aged man looked up. His desert-sun-weathered face split into a grin, which was something I'd learned to appreciate about him. In a city full of demigods, vampires, werewolves, and a sentient android, Rafin was one of the few people who consistently smiled at me without calculating anything first. I did not take that for granted.

"Iskandaar! Ha, look at you dressed like a king today," he slapped dirt off his knees and stood, groaning at the motion. "What brings? Lailah isn't home. She's out with Senna checking the southern water seals. Something about filtration maintenance."

"That's fine. Mind if I wait here?"

"Wait here? In the field?" He looked around at the eggplant, the drones, and the muddy trenches like I'd asked to wait in a palace. "Well, sure. Help me move those seedlings while you're standing there."

So I did.

I could have found Lailah directly. The Demonic Sphere had her at the southern water seals, twenty minutes out. But the Goryeo palace had left a specific kind of weight on me, the weight of political rooms where everyone is calculating, and of conversations where no one says the first thing they mean. I'd spent the last three hours being very aware of every move I made.

Rafin's field had none of that in it. Twenty minutes of someone who thought of me as the young man who helped his daughter was worth more than I was comfortable admitting out loud.

For twenty minutes I knelt in the dirt of a flying city and transplanted seedlings while Rafin talked about how he’d been studying “pH levels” as per Stratos’ instructions to understand soil better, the drone calibration problem he'd been fighting for a week, and lastly whether the biodome's artificial sunlight was providing enough ultraviolet for his tomatoes.

The drones hummed around us. One of them was carrying a watering can that was too heavy for it, bobbing slightly in the air with the effort. Somewhere to the west, past the edge of the farming platform, Nevaramis's spires caught the late light, all ancient stone and old height, not caring in the slightest that they'd outlasted everyone who built them.

"You're quiet today," Rafin said.

I looked at him. "Lots on my mind."

"Hm." He patted a mound of soil into place. "You know, when Lailah was little, she'd go quiet like that before doing something her mother would've scolded her for."

I gave a short chuckle. "Yeah, that tracks."

"Ha!" He laughed and shook his head. "Alright, keep your secrets. You'll tell Lailah anyway, and she'll tell me because she can't keep her mouth shut around her father."

That’s not very smart for an aspiring spy mistress, I noted. Just then, a voice carried from the path behind us.

"Dad! I brought the filtration report, and Senna says the southern..."

Lailah stopped.

Lailah stood at the edge of her father's field in practical work clothes, a stack of reports tucked under one arm. Her silver eyes widened as she found me kneeling in the dirt with soil on my sleeves.

"Iskandaar?"

"Hey, what’s up."

"What are you doing in my father's field?"

To be fair, I own this place, I didn’t say that. "Transplanting seedlings."

She stared at me, moving to Rafin, and then back at me. Her brow furrowed as the confusion began to change shape. "You don't transplant seedlings."

"Your father is a very persuasive man."

Lailah stared at me for a moment and then asked, "So what happened?"

Our eyes stayed locked. I looked away, dusting off my hands as I stood up. Looking at the sky where the artificial sun shone, I heaved out a breath. The dome dissolved as I transmitted a mental command. The true sky beyond came visible, full of clouds as it shone brighter than the artificial sun.

Rafin shielded his eyes with his hand, whistling. “I didn’t know you could do that,” he said. I did. Among other reasons, I’d hidden the true sky in this part of the city so that Rafin could continue working without worry, in case the Arcane King of Dominion appeared in the sky.

However, it was time to stop lying to people about the danger. I’d decided to face all danger head-on.

“Iskandaar…” Lailah muttered.

I turned to her. "It’s going to get really dangerous from now on, Lailah. So tell Senna to prepare… The Heavenly Demon Divine Cult is opening a branch in Goryeo."

Lailah's hand tightened on the reports. Her silver eyes searched my face, looking for the joke, and found none.

"You're lighting a fire," she said.

Shengzong had already decided to look at Goryeo. Better he has somewhere specific to look. I'd had three hours to sit with the decision. It had been the right call for all of them. I was still not entirely sure it would stay that way.

"I guess.”

Twelve thousand miles to the east, General Qin Zhenyuan of Baolian Dominion walked through the Halls of Eternal Sunrise.

The corridor was three hundred meters long and wide enough for a battalion to march through shoulder to shoulder. Its walls were compressed sunlight, hardened into a crystalline lattice that radiated a constant golden warmth. Inscriptions covered every surface, millions of characters in Old Baolian script that cataloged every edict and every conquest rendered to the Throne of the Sun since the founding of the Dominion.

The script glowed faintly, and none of it was decorative.

Each character was a binding anchor for the wards that protected the imperial palace complex, and together they formed a defensive matrix dense enough to resist a direct assault from anything below the 9th Ascension. General Qin knew this because he had helped design three of the outermost layers himself.

He walked beneath floating censers that burned perpetually without fuel, their smoke carrying a preservation enchantment that kept the air clean and the temperature regulated to exactly twenty-two degrees. Autonomous guardian constructs flanked the corridor at regular intervals, six-armed golden figures standing at attention with weapons that could level a city block.

The constructs did not react to him. They knew his spiritual signature. After thirteen hundred and sixty years of service to the Emperor, they had better.

A servant appeared from a side passage and fell into step beside him. The young man's robes were immaculate, his posture practiced, and his face was caught between urgency and the professional stillness of a man paid to deliver bad news without dying for it. Qin had learned to read that face over a millennium of service. It meant the information was important.

"General Qin. Reports from the western intelligence division."

"Summarize."

"The individual designated as the Heavenly Demon was confirmed active in Goryeo five days earlier. He entered the royal palace in Hwangcheon and met with Monarch Jin Ha-Im in a private audience. Latest news says…”

General Qin did not slow his pace. "I'm aware."

The servant hesitated. "You are, sir?"

"The Third Bureau flagged his Bifrost activation three days ago. His meeting with the Monarch was expected."

"I see. Shall I prepare a briefing for..."

"No. I will deliver this myself. You're dismissed."

The servant bowed and disappeared. General Qin continued walking. This report included more than just an appearance, so he had to do this himself.

The corridor ended at a door.

It was the only door in the palace that bore no inscriptions, no wards. Plain dark wood, polished smooth by time, and that plainness was the only warning the palace had thought it necessary to provide.

General Qin stopped.

He knocked twice. No voice invited him, but he felt the permission through the Aura in the air. He drew a slow breath, then began layering protections. Nothing he could build would survive what waited on the other side if it chose violence, and he’d never pretend otherwise. What he applied to himself were environmental barriers.

Heat resistance first, then pressure compensation, then spiritual insulation against the passive weight of a presence that could flatten a mortal mind by existing in the same room.

He'd performed this ritual thousands of times. And yet, he checked every layer twice. Because he remembered what had happened to General Duan Zhi, who'd been careless once and entered this room with an incomplete barrier.

Duan Zhi's lungs had boiled from proximity alone.

General Qin pushed the door open.

The heat hit him first, despite the barriers. The room beyond was vast, domed, with walls of volcanic basalt threaded with veins of molten gold. The air shimmered.

In the center of the chamber sat a pool.

It was not water.

Liquid stone moved in slow, lazy currents, glowing white-orange, and the temperature alone would have reduced an unprotected human to vapor before they registered pain. Steam rose from the surface in dense columns that collected against the domed ceiling and fell back as superheated condensation.

In the center of the pool of lava, the Emperor bathed.

Shengzong of the Baolian Dominion did not look like a man who needed a crown to prove anything.

He was tall even at rest, and the word "muscular" would fail to describe him for muscles implied effort and limitation. His build was something that had never needed either.

His skin was dark bronze, lava trailing down the grooves of his frame as he sat with his arms spread along the pool's edge. His beard was thick, squared at the jaw and carved close along the cheeks, a style that Baolian emperors had worn since forever.

His eyes were green, like old jade gets after centuries of handling, deep and patient, and not interested in moving for anything. Those eyes were half-lidded, staring at the ceiling. General Qin did not call it relaxed.

General Qin had served this man for thirteen hundred and sixty years. He knew better.

"O, Emperor of the Sun."

General Qin knelt at the pool's edge. The heat pressed against his barriers in waves. He kept his breathing even.

"The Goryeo matter we discussed previously, there’s an update. The Heavenly Demon has surfaced in Goryeo. He met with Monarch Jin Ha-Im in a private audience. Our sources indicate he is establishing a permanent presence in the kingdom. Perhaps a residence, given his closeness with the Crown Princess of Goryeo.”

The Emperor did not move.

General Qin waited. He had learned, during his long serving, that silence in this room was not nothing. Shengzong thought faster than he spoke. And he rarely bothered to speak at all.

Thirty seconds passed.

The lava moved.

Shengzong rose from the pool in a single unhurried motion, the liquid stone running off his body in slow streams, finding channels between muscle groups, dripping from his arms in threads that cooled to dull orange before they reached the floor. He stood at his full 9 feet of height.

He tilted his head. A slow roll of his neck produced a sound like a boulder settling into place.

Then he smiled.

It was a small smile. Almost kind. The expression Qin associated with Shengzong getting exactly what he had planned for.

"That boy," the Emperor said. His voice was low and vast, and the room didn't dare to echo it. "He has made me wait."

General Qin's expression did not change. He had watched Shengzong destroy kingdoms in a single afternoon over provocations smaller than a garrison bet. He had watched the Emperor wait thirty years for a particular defection to ripen into an example worth the trouble of making. He knew the patience before punishment, fast and arriving without announcement. He knew the patience of administration.

Which was always worse.

In thirteen hundred and sixty years, General Qin had seen this specific stillness twice before. It belonged to the moments when Shengzong had been watching something grow from a distance, watching it become, slowly and precisely, into what he had intended to use it for. The boy had obliged.

Whatever Iskandaar Romani had been a few months ago, he was something different now, and Shengzong's patience had been keeping pace with him.

Qin had always preferred the Emperor's anger. Anger was fast, survivable, and when it was finished, it was finished.

His green eyes opened fully.

"Prepare my court, Zhenyuan."

He bowed. "Yes, Your Majesty."

"And send word to Goryeo's Monarch. Tell him I wish to send a diplomatic delegation. A gesture of goodwill between neighbors. Surely, he will not reject such goodwill."

General Qin's expression did not change. The last kingdom that had received one no longer existed.

"It will be done," General Qin said.
