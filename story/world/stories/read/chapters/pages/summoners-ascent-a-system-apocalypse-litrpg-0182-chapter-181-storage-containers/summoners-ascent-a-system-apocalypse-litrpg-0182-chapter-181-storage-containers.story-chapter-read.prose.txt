Ashton continued moving for a while as he tried to properly adjust to his slightly weakened wisp-sight. It didn't change things too much, all things considered, but it was just a bit annoying that he suddenly had this drop.

On the other hand, it was pretty nice that he could see an immediate, tangible change in the power output of the summons, even if he put in just as much concentration and magic as he did just earlier. It felt like a fair exchange, at least.

But this wasn't the last thing that Ashton had to deal with before getting to Market Street. The Backpacker's advancement was also something that he still had to deal with. Just like the elementals, the spirit had given up the right to choose the advancement to Ashton, just that there was one difference.

The Backpacker didn't seem to have any particular preference. He had actually tried to discuss it with them earlier; with the rest of the summons, too.

After all, no matter if they left the choice up to Ashton, he would always choose the thing that they were pulled toward or wanted to advance to anyway, so there was no need for them to concede this right to the choice to him.

And sure, having particular things they were drawn toward with their very existence was maybe a slightly stronger factor with the elementals, beings of 'concepts', but that didn't mean that spirits didn't usually still have preferences.

But in this case, the Backpacker had tried to reassure him that he could make the choice however he wanted to. And maybe Ashton had to concede to that... after all, the choices presented as advancements came from the actions they took together with their summoner. And at the end of the day, Ashton was the one that was making all the choices about what not just the Backpacker, but also the elementals would do in the first place... whether he wanted to admit it or not, he was already affecting their paths massively. So why stop there, when they were already giving Ashton free reign anyway?

[Spirit Advancement | Backpacker]

[Time Dilation]

[Environmental Adaptation]

[Personal Access Link]

[Storage Containers]

Now, since advancements didn't have the same descriptions that skill refinements did, he mostly had to guess what each was. Luckily, this time around, it seemed pretty straightforward.

Time Dilation would, as implied, allow the Backpacker to change the passage of time within the package. This was particularly useful for keeping rations for a longer time, and though that wasn't an issue right now, this would only keep getting more and more useful as time went on. Especially once region borders were irrelevant.

Environmental Adaption was useful for similar, but different reasons. It would probably allow the spirit to adjust temperature and moisture within the Backpack to make sure that everything was at peak condition. This would be especially useful for certain potion ingredients that might be a bit more prone to heat or moisture damage.

The Personal Access Link advancement most likely just let Ashton grab things from the backpack directly without the spirit as an intermediary. Maybe he could just push his hand into his pocket and grab it, but that didn't seem particularly appealing. The Backpacker was already getting extremely quick at pulling out requested items. It could maybe save a few moments, especially in a fight, but it might actually be slower than just keeping important items physically on Ashton's body where he could access them directly.

And then, there was the last advancement—Storage Containers. Frankly, this one did make Ashton guess the most out of the four options, but when the Backpacker stood there and packed away some magic stones, and Ashton saw the small, dangling item chest charms hanging off the side of the bag, he actually understood what this was.

It was most likely; no, most definitely, something that branched off the new ability the Backpacker gained after absorbing that item chest. After all, if the spirit could now already create different smaller, detachable pocket-spaces, then why wasn't it possible to create them in other forms beside just the item chests?

While Ashton didn't know exactly what form those items would take, he knew that this would come in extremely useful.

Again, since he was nervous about just choosing something from his own gut, as this would affect the spirit long-term, Ashton made sure to ask the Backpacker multiple times over if they were happy to walk this route, and they seemed very excited to. So, that was the choice Ashton made.

[You have chosen for the [Backpacker] to advance through [Storage Containers]]

[Contract | Backpacker's ability [Item Chest] has become [Storage Item Creation]]

As Ashton decided to take a brief break to explore this change, he could almost immediately see a change on the actual bag that the spirit carried.

Before, after they had absorbed the item chest, small keychains appeared dangling off their backpack. They looked almost exactly like the chest that Ashton had gotten through the quest reward, but the singular one that was now left was slightly different. Somehow more 'in tune' with the Backpacker's style, according to how they had altered the Backpack upon acquiring it.

The chest was simpler, but also more playful, covered in new patterns. Though there was just one left, there were others alongside it now. There was a small glass bottle, a pouch, a small backpack, and for some reason—somewhat embarrassingly actually—something that looked like a cigarette pack.

"So what, can you make all of these now?" Ashton asked, squatting down to grab the keychains and hold them between his fingers. There was something weirdly comforting about touching things, and being able to confirm that the world he saw through the wisp was real. Not that he doubted it, but magic was weird sometimes.

"Hm, in that case..." Ashton thought for a moment, channelling the Backpacker to get an overview of what was in the bag. The moment he did, he was actually pretty surprised. The space within the bag had expanded quite a bit. Now that a path had been chosen and the spirit had officially reached level 10, they had probably gotten a small power boost. The same way that people got a stronger boost than usual when one of their classes reached level 10. That's what Ashton figured, at least.

He didn't know what his own level was back when the system came to Lumia, but he did know that the Summoner couldn't really level up properly, due to the sheer power that they already possessed.

But more importantly, Ashton figured he should give this a try. Those smaller, extra pockets of space that were at the side of the backpack were gone, but now, instead, he could feel the different keychains hanging off the side.

He picked out one of the bigger potion bottles that he had and tagged it, connecting it to the glass bottle keychain, and the Backpacker immediately got to work.

They set the pack down and pulled the glass keychain off, though it was immediately replaced by a new one that grew like a fruit off a branch. The Backpacker enclosed the keychain in both of their hands, and as they pulled the back apart, they were holding a different bottle. It was fairly small, really just like a tiny vial that could barely hold more than a shot of liquor. But soon, the bottle filled up, as the plastic bottle that Ashton tagged was becoming empty.

He could tell immediately, as he was still channelling the Backpacker, that the entirety of the one-litre bottle of the potion was now within that tiny potion vial.

Happily, the Backpacker handed it to Ashton, and he grabbed it. It was light. Heavier than a bottle this size should be, no matter what it was filled with, but lighter than a one litre bottle of the healing potion. It was closed with a small piece of cork.

"Is one litre the limit?" Ashton asked, but the Backpacker shook their head. It didn't seem like they could make bottomless bottles, but certainly ones with may more capacity than this one.

As Ashton focused his channelling range on the bottle, he could tell that it was made completely of magic. Like the item chest, it would surely disappear once it was completely empty. But that was fine; Ashton could just make a new one.

It also didn't seem like there was a limit to how many of these he could create at a time. The only thing he could feel was that it drained his concentration and mana pretty fast, but that could be solved with just a bit of rest.

Either way, this was perfect. He had been collecting more and more bottles of water whenever he could, not only so that he simply had as much water as he needed, but largely so that he had a stockpile of bottles for potions. But if this solved that issue, he could get rid of those.

He'd rather not keep too much plastic around, no matter how useful it was in a world like this. Especially if he was going to keep being around more fae, it was extremely, extremely dangerous.

Iron was already known to be harmful to fae, apparently because of how processed iron was a 'bastardization of the natural world', or at least that was how he understood it after trying to learn from different fae. But when Ashton was summoned, the things he had on him were recreated alongside him.

Including the random plastic trinket that he had on his keychain. It was something small and harmless, so even the Summoner didn't seem to care for it, but the fae that were around Ashton were absolutely terrified of it. One of them was curious and tried to touch it, and like she held it in a vat of acid, the skin of her hand began to bubble and melt away. Since he didn't want the Summoner to use this for something nefarious and violent, he secretly buried the trinket in the ground someday.

So if possible, Ashton wanted to avoid using any sort of plastic. Especially since fairies could still drink potions. If something happened to a fairy hiding out somewhere, and the only way to save them was a potion, it would be absolutely fatal to try and feed them one from a plastic bottle. He did already collect some glass bottles for that purpose, though, not that he would need them anymore now.

Ashton gave the bottle to the Backpacker again, and the object was stashed away. Usually, it wasn't possible to put one spatial storage item into another, which was why the Backpacker absorbed the item chest in the first place, but now it was different. Since this was basically just a chunk of the Backpacker's ability, the bottle could be kept within the bag.

This ability was definitely going to clear up a bunch of space within the backpack, and allow Ashton to collect even more before having to stash things away, so this was perfect. There were very clearly limits, though, even if Ashton couldn't see those yet. But again, he had time to figure this out later.
