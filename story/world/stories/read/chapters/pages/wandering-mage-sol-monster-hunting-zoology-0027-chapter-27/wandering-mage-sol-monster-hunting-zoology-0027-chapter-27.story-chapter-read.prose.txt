Tressa gave her feet a break, floating with her toes barely off the ground as her lights from Lumas floated around her, helping to light their way.

The bridge had periodic basins of fire, but now in the dead of night it was quite dark between them.

“Say, elf… errr, Tressa?” Luke asked, his ox clopping along beside him. “Is something the matter with your feet? I noticed when you were actually walking you were limping slightly.”

Lisha nodded. “Yes, she didn’t get new shoes before we left, and now her feet have sores.”

“Elf and human feet are both far too soft,” Braidon chimed in.

“You’re feet would hurt too if you had them!” Tressa said, turning and floating backwards momentarily. “You shouldn’t talk. After all, remember when you got that rock lodged in your hoof? You complained all day!”

“That’s different! I weigh a lot more than you and that rock was sharp! I couldn’t get it out!”

Tressa stuck out her tongue and put up a middle finger.

“Why you!” he stomped after her but she giggled and floated ahead out of his reach.

Luke and Mitchel looked at each other and laughed as Lisha covered her face and shook her head.

“Are they always like that?” Mitchel asked Lisha quietly.

“Pretty much,” she confirmed with a toothy smile.

Tressa looked ahead and slowed as she saw a dense fog had come onto the bridge, heavily muting the light of the next fire basin. Her feet touched carefully back down on the ground. “That’s very foggy…” she said under her breath.

“Hm, yeah, it happens especially on cooler nights as the seasons change,” Mitchel said. “Haven’t seen it quite that thick, though.”

Tressa pulled her staff out of spatial storage and clasped it in her hands as she walked along normally, ignoring the pain for the time being.As they continued on, the fog only got thicker and they couldn’t even seen the glow of the fire basins anymore. Even with brightening her lumas and summoning some more orbs it just wasn’t enough to cut through the thick fog.

She tried a wind spell, but all that did was cause the light from her lumas spell to cast sweeping shadows towards her that made her shriek in alarm. “Did you see that!?”

“What was it!?” Briadon asked, reaching back and gripping his club.

“Relax you two, it was just a shadow from her spell,” Lisha said.

“Are you sure?” Briadon asked. Then he huffed. “I-I mean, lets just keep moving, damn it! The island can’t be far now!”

“Don’t tell me you’re afraid of the dark, minotaur,” Luke said, staring up at the towering minotaur.

“No! Of course not!” Braidon said, coughing into his hand.

Tressa snickered, knowing that he couldn’t see very well in the dark in the first place and he really didn’t like it. But she wasn’t going to make fun of him for that, especially in front of these other two.

Something caught Tressa’s eye and she shrieked once again, blasting wind towards it and clearing the way for Lumas. She stood with her staff in shaky hands as the fog filled in the space her spell made.

“Tressa? Did you see something?” Lisha asked.

“I don’t know,” she said, shaking her head. “It was something out there.” Her finger pointed into the thick fog to her left. “It was huge; I don’t know what I saw, but it was out there.”

“It was probably a trick o’ the light again,” Luke said. “Not to mention we’re all weary and have been crossing this bridge for hours.”

“Yes, we’re all getting tired,” Lisha agreed.

Tressa frowned. “Maybe you right.”

She began walking again with the group, but something in her gut told her she had seen something. What it was, she had no clue. Shaking her head and slapping her cheeks, she took a deep breath and kept walking, subconsciously putting herself between Lisha and Braidon.

“You good?” Briadon asked.

“Yes, just trying to shake off the weariness,” she chuckled. She couldn’t help but glance at the dark once more. This time, she saw nothing. Maybe they were right, and it was just the combination of the light and her tired eyes playing tricks on her.

“I wonder how much further to the island,” Luke said after another twenty minutes. “We’ve been walking since sundown.”

“Well, the gate guard did say it would be late into the night by the time we got there,” Lisha said. “This bridge is massive… I’m just amazed they were able to craft something like this.” She tapped her claw on the thick railing, making a metallic ding ring out in the silence.

“I heard the giants helped them with the heavy lifting,” Mitchel said.

Tressa’s head snapped up. “Giants?”

“Yes, giants.”

“Are giants not extinct?” Tressa asked, confused.

“Extinct?”

“Yes. In my people's records, they state they died out centuries ago.”

“How cut off are your people from the rest of the world?” Mitchel asked.

“Well, we have always been a solitary race but… our scholars had filled our libraries with so much we knew of many things. But over the Mad King’s rule of 700 years, he destroyed anything that he did not like. He burned whole sections of our great library. The damage has not been recovered, and we have sages who do their best to restore what they can but it is impossible that it will ever be restored.”

The group shared glances.

Luke cleared his throat. “Well, many have died out, but they still exist. In the furthest northern sections of the Thimberman Mountains. They are the only beings I can imagine surviving there, as their skin is so thick and only they could deal with the monsters that thrive there.”

“Have you met a giant?” Tressa asked excitedly.

“Me? Ha, no,” Luke laughed. “You’d have a better chance asking a dwarf. Helping the dwarves build this bridge is one of the few times a giant made it this far south, as far as I know.”

Tressa looked stunned, but then, as she stared into the fog, a smile touched her lips. “The giants still live. I wonder what stories they could tell.” Then she turned to Lisha and Briadon. “Can we go!” She pointed. “To the Thimberman Mountains!?”

“Hell no, you crazy elf!” Briadon said.

“Why not?” she pouted.

“Tressa, the guild will not even approve a hunter to the northern parts of the mountains,” Lisha said. “Well, other than the most elite of the elite.”

“Then we can become elite! Is it hard?”

Briadon laughed. “What do you think? We’re only silver rank!”

“So how many more ranks?”

Lisha’s tongue flicked. “Tressa, did you read the guidebook as you were supposed to?”

Tressa’s eyes shifted away as she poked her fingers together. “Maybe not. But I don’t speak common.”

“You know enough to read the guide by now,” Lisha admonished. Then she sighed and explained. “There is copper, bronze, silver, gold, platinum, and finally mithril. Mithril will be recruited to go to all the most dangerous areas in the world, but there is even an elite class above them who are the best of the best,” Lisha explained, raising a claw. “It’s like, Mithril Plus.”

“So I must become Mythrial Plus?” Tressa asked. “But why does the guild have a say? I can go where I want.”

“Well, yes, but if you go somewhere the guild has deemed beyond your rank, you do so without their protection,” Lisha explained. “They won’t approve contracts for the area, and they won’t send a rescue party after you, and they certainly won’t aid you with any medical expenses. If you die there, they are not responsible for recovering your body.”

Tressa blinked… her eyes lowering. “I see…” she said solemnly.

Briadon snorted. “Still wanna go?”

“Yes!” Tressa answered immediately.

“Tressa!” Lisha yelled.

Luke whispered to Mitchel, “I see why the minotaur always calls her crazy.”

Mitchel nodded in agreement.

“Honestly, one way or another, you might want to stay away from the giants,” Mitchel warned. “I hear they aren’t keen on elves.”

Tressa’s face fell as she knew they were right in a way. They’d either be kind or they would stomp on her, cutting her long life short. “Yeah, never mind.”

A laugh spread through the group as they saw her face go from excitement to horror to understanding.

But still, she understood that she couldn’t go to such a place now, but who knows, that could change one day. It had been almost 200 years since the Mad King, and she could only hope the world would forget him more and more. Maybe one day she could meet the giants.

She opened her mouth to explain this to her friends, but then closed it again. A memory of the merchant from Falidorn she had been friends with surfacing.

She remembered how she met him when he was very young, but whenever he would come to visit again, he would grow older and older.

Those first meetings, she found him handsome, and she enjoyed his company in a way other than just hearing stories or seeing what gift he brought her. She never stopped looking forward to seeing him, but he only changed more and more over the years. Not just physically as he became an old man, but his mental state. How he would look at Tressa and speak to her. While he changed, she just always stayed the same.

When he was young, she had flirted with him and cracked different kinds of jokes. On his last visits he’d merely sit and tell her stories, holding a cane under his hands, which she still loved to hear, but he put it in perspective just how different the life of a human and the life of an elf was. They truly couldn’t be much more to each other than friends.

Once he spoke reflectively of a voyage that kept him away for many years.

“Frankly I’m quite envious of you elves,” he had said. “But I love to come here because just for a little while I can feel as though time actually has stopped.”

“What do you mean?” Tressa asked.

“For a human, our time is so limited we must be cautious with how we spend it. If we spend 15 years on a pursuit that never becomes what we thought it would, then we’ve wasted a large portion of our existence. You only get a pursuit like that maybe twice in your life if you’re lucky.”

Tressa's head turned, her ears lowering slightly.

“Meanwhile, you could chase some random whim for decades or even a hundred years, and if it turns into a waste of time, you still have so much of it left to spare.”

Tressa hadn’t thought too much of it at the time, but when he stopped arriving at Falidorn to trade, she knew his time was up, and he was gone.

What about Lisha and Briadon? That thought made her sad as she looked at them. Minotaurs lived about the same lifespan as a human, and a very very old Statiki could live to be 300. Elves easily lived past 3000 and could even live up to 10,000 years, though most don’t.

She wanted to see the world, but realizing she would eventually be seeing it without her two friends, and that made her feel quite sad. Falidorn would still be there, as would her parents, Lemia, Maikia, Narella, most of the other elves, and even Aldmern. But Lisha and Briadon would not.

Tressa gripped her staff, but then Briadon grabbed her attention.

“Hey, what’s that?” he said, pointing ahead into the fog.

They looked closely and could see a shape coming into view.

“Is that light?” Mitchel asked.

“Yes! I think we are here,” Lisha said with relief.

With that statement the group quickened their pace and the ox also seemed eager to end their long journey over the massive bridge. As they grew closer, the light became brighter and brighter until, finally, they exited the fog and found themselves at the edge of a large town.

“Wow,” Lisha said.

“It’s amazing,” Tressa said.
