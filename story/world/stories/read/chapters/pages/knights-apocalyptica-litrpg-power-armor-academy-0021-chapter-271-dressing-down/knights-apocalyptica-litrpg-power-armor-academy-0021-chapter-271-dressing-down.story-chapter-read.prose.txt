Enide paced back and forth as the rest of the Knights arrived, coming in her Pack’s cars.

Garin, of course, scrambled off the hood of the car and gave her a panicked look, like she had the solution. The two other Knights had seen him, along with Bedwyr and Colin. It was safe to say his cover was blown to shit. She liked the guy and was currently thinking through everything in her power to help him out… but it was fucked.

There was no way out of here. She could teleport him, but the idea of dragging them both through the void with the voices in her head and the stares… well… she didn’t even want to go by herself, and that was much safer than the few times she brought a passenger.

Why the fuck did you get out of the trunk?

And why was he such a good shot—she’d been struggling to line up her las-rifle with the monster when he fired. Which meant that during the fight, he'd crawled out of the car and pulled off the perfect shot from very far away, and done it in a space of time she hadn't even seen.

She stopped her pacing. The cars were closing in, and she whispered, “What’s the plan here, Garin?”

He didn’t respond. He was staring at the cars, his jaw going slack. She traced his line of sight and noticed that in the lead car was Boldwick. And they were in clear sight.

“Oh. You’re so fucked. Oh. We’re so fucked.” She admitted that there was nothing to do since they’d been outed in all the ways possible. For a Knight like that, he most certainly would not miss his student with his rifle, right near the battlefield. A student who should be buried behind the Kingdom walls, safe and sound, rather than risking his life here.

So? You claim to be free. Spirit him away, if that’s all there is to life, silly. Unless, maybe it isn’t. The voice invaded her head, a breathless whisper, but, paradoxically, so loud. Every day, it got louder. Got clearer. Being around people wasn’t even suppressing it anymore. Enide shuddered.

And it was mocking her?

Fuck that voice. Maybe she would do just that. They could vanish away, then come back with their plan. Garin would approach on his terms; he was Erec’s best friend. She had convinced him into this plan, in a way, by encouraging him to be free. And she’d be fucked if she wasn’t a woman who stuck to her convictions, mocking voice or otherwise.

Garin looked down at his still-smoking gun, then at Boldwick. He shoved it on his back.

“We get you away. Run off into the wasteland, figure out your approach or—“

“No, Enide.” Garin took in a deep breath.

He circled the car, Fido at his heels, Munchy scrambling up his leg and up to his shoulder. He stepped away from both Enide and the car, squared his shoulders, and waited like a damn idiot.

Enide shook her head.

Boldwick didn’t even wait for his car to come to a stop; he simply hopped off the side, kicking up a plume of dust as both his Armor and body bore the force of 60 miles per hour, grinding against the wasteland as he sprinted, and then eased to a walk as soon as he got within distance.

Enide gave one last look at Erec, knowing he was fine, then back at the other vehicles. The two Knights who’d been here before—Sir Able, and that weird guy with bandages—were greeting the stopping group of vehicles. Enide spotted her father in one of them, escorting a duo of Knights. Yniol gave her a quick wave as he killed his engine. Boldwick was headed to the two Knights, but he kept staring at Garin and at her.

Thankfully, a few of them went further, to help out with Erec, Bedwyr, and Colin, who were limping their way over on the glassed wasteland.

…she paused, the horror of the looming confrontation going away as she saw him, held up on the other side by his brother and friend. She felt a rush of warmth in her; the way he’d swiped down with his axe…. The heat of the fires spread even this far. The man never stopped growing, and never stopped gaining power… to see him in action? It was in a way that she loved him. Why she craved him, but also why she feared one day he was going to push too far. Just like the way she pushed her new car until the engine exploded. Only, when a person’s engine exploded, it wasn’t just something that they could repair.

Pssh. He’s capable of far more than that. Handsome though when he’s fighting like that, don’t you think? Oh, well, of course you do. Look at your blush, dear.

She startled. The warmth and mirth in the tone, the range of emotions the voices kept going into kept throwing her off. She clutched her head, feeling as if she were going mad.

Enide could handle insults. She could handle it, whispering creepy shit.

But it was telling her that her boyfriend was handsome, and mocking her? It… it was too much, crossing too many lines… her theories were only intensifying; was it some type of void parasite—or—

"Enide. I'm going to go talk to Boldwick. Thank you for everything," Garin said, stepping forward, leaving her at the car alone. She hadn’t even given him any last words of encouragement or anything. The voice had been too distracting—she had been losing it. Damn it.

She watched his back as he and the animals left, and was surprised to see Yniol leaving his car and heading her way.

Yniol paused as he saw her expression. “Ya all right?”

“Hey, Pa,” she said, taking a seat on the trunk of her car, her whole body giving a shiver.

The Knights were doing their thing. They’d reached Erec. Her boyfriend was supported by his brother, so he would be alright. Part of her wondered if she should go over there and try to help in some way, but with herself falling apart? Well, that and another part of her knew that he’d be fine and would rather handle his Knightly business independently.

Wait. She frowned. That instinct felt wrong, somehow.

Where is that coming from?

She forced herself to examine it.

It was as if sensations and intuitions filtered into her head and yanked her emotions and reactions wrong. A part of her, right now, wanted to teleport over, give him a kiss, and make sure he got into the right hands. But the pull in the other direction wasn't foreign—it was her own instinct to let him handle it, only felt from so many angles at once that it drowned out the original. It was more than her, a force outside of her, pulling and reassuring in the same breath.

Why?

Was it the influence of that voice? Every day, it felt like she lost a bit more of herself. She’d been keeping it silent, not wanting to scare him or anyone else around him. But now? She wanted to scream from the horror of it all. She couldn’t even focus. Not on the fact that Erec had just hurt himself fighting, nor that her friend was about to face off against Boldwick. And here she was, on the trunk of her car, literally too busy losing her mind to help anyone.

"Are ya all right?" Yniol repeated, and Enide startled.

She gave him a fake smile. “Fine, Pa, worried about Erec.”

“Bah, don’t give me that shit.”

Yniol glared her down, and Enide looked away. "I don't know what you're talking about." Better her pa wandered off. Let her maybe get a handle on her shit again. Why was she losing it like this?

“It’s been at least a month of this. Now, I know ya go around like the wind, and ain't much to be tied down, but how many days have you sat here and talked to your father? Barely ever. Before all this, ya did. I know it’s cause you’re avoiding something. Scared I’ll be the one to dig it out of you.”

She gaped at him, stunned. She’d been careful.

“I’m your father,” he explained.

“Nothing is bothering me.” She tried to lie again. Pa had enough going on with Uncle. If she started losing her mind too, well, if he knew she was losing her mind, where would that put him?

“Bull. Shit. Now, we’re going to watch these Knights have their little moment, make sure your Erec is back on his feet, then you and I are gonna have a long drive. I can see you’re hurting, but if you want help, you’ve got to open yourself up to it. And if you don’t want help? Tough shit, I’m your father, and I’m going to help you no matter what you want. It’s my job.”

He's right. So closely guarded, that's not really being free, is it? More like chained down by yourself. The voice said from inside her, and Enide shuddered.

She could tell by Yniol’s tone that there was no getting out of it. The problem with running away from problems was that eventually, if you wanted to stick around somewhere, those problems would catch up with you. Her fingers tapped the trunk of the car, clicking against the metal, as she tried to focus on Garin, Erec, and the rest of the Knights, and tried pointedly not to focus on her upcoming chat with her father, nor the fact that her condition was most definitely getting worse.

— - ☢ - — - ☼ - — - ☢ - —

Garin stood still, back as rigid as any piece of steel in the Kingdom. And he’d keep it that way, he’d face down the justice that he’d earned, and do it with a straight spine. That much would allow him to maintain his dignity.

The worst part of this all was the way the Armor hid the Knight's face. From the moment Boldwick saw him to even now as he ordered him to join both Sir Able and Sir Helvek in a line, Garin was left in the dark. The order was sharp. But that was it. A split-second tone of voice that had been controlled by a man with years of experience giving orders. Nothing to work off of. And, looking at the other two Knights in the line with him, he got nothing from them either.

Sitting there with no information to work off of, waiting for judgment to rain down on his head was hell.

Colin and Bedwyr reached Boldwick—the Knights took Erec to a car, and then Boldwick ordered those two to the line as he inspected the battlefield. Within those long, silent moments, Garin had nothing to do but watch his friend and Erec’s brother march right up to him.

Thankfully, Colin broke the silence that had begun to hang like an executioner’s axe, as he lined up right next to Garin. He said, not bothering to contain his voice, “What in the hell are you doing out here? Have you lost your mind, Baron's son? He’s going to flay you in front of a fire.”

“I—I know,” Garin admitted in shame and winced as the two elder Knights looked at them.

He readjusted, making sure to keep his spine straight. Goddess, what he wouldn’t have given to have his Armor to hide his reddened face. To not let his face show the world what he felt in his heart.

Boldwick crouched in the distance, poking around with monster corpses. A few of the Knights used their spells or otherwise formed a barrier between the transport vehicles and the battlefield, readying themselves in case anything else erupted from the ground and decided that they still wanted to fight. Truth be told, that fight had awed Garin in a way that he didn't wish to admit to himself. From the way Bedwyr wielded that stone body, to how Colin threw around his lightning and seared things where they stood… how both of them were standing next to him now, tired, but not exhausted.

Garin clenched his fist. His fear was confirmed now more than it ever had been before; Boldwick rose in the distance and marched at them.

Hold on to your resolve, man. It’s the only thing you have left. No matter how Boldwick yelled at him. No matter the screaming. If he’d stayed in the Kingdom, his friends would have skyrocketed past him. He would’ve stagnated—that was why he’d come out here. That was the resolve he needed to maintain. This expedition was the journey, the exodus that would either make him a Knight or allow him to fall. A trial. Maybe it was just a childish feeling in him, but the dreams were insistent. So, he needed to hold onto that resolve, no matter what.

Fido circled his leg and let out a low growl as Boldwick stopped in front of them. Even Munchy let out an angry grunt.

“Hush,” Garin commanded his animals, trying to keep them out of it.

Boldwick kept a hand on the hilt of his weapon, his helmet slowly swiveling between all five of them, lingering on each.

“How do I begin this?” he asked, anger barely restrained. “How am I meant to address this?”

Sir Able was the one who broke the silence, pointing a finger at Sir Helvek, “This insane fuck stopped me from extracting the Knight Errants—he’s the one who let them go against the army alone. I tried to stop them. But he stopped me.”

Helvek simply turned his head to the other Knight, and Sir Able shut his mouth so loudly that Garin heard it outside the old man’s helmet.

Boldwick continued to stare, the silence growing uncomfortable.

Sir Able looked like he wanted to break, but Sir Helvek’s stare kept him from doing so. The man was trapped between two Master Knights. Though Garin knew little of Sir Helvek, from the rumors he’d gathered, he was not a man to be crossed.

The tension ratcheted, and the old man trembled in his Armor. Garin leaned foot to foot, bubbles rising in his chest as he tried to make sense of it. As he waited for that anger in the Master Knight to turn his way, rather than towards those two.

“I will take that into consideration,” Boldwick stated, once it became clear that he wouldn’t get more out of Sir Able. “Well? What do you have to say to that?” Boldwick asked Sir Helvek.

“I was not alone in bringing my Knight Errant out here to fight. I wished to evaluate them more closely. You expressed concerns earlier on the sparring field over letting them go too far, given the conditions at the time. The two of us agreed that if we were to take them to an isolated battlefield, we would be able to better understand the upper range of their capabilities. Once the monsters arrived, I denied us the right to intercede right away, since it furthered the point of this training exercise.”

“And you decided this without consulting the expedition lead, who’d expressly presented concerns over that use of power in the wasteland?”

Helvek shrugged. "He is my Knight Errant, as Colin is Sir Able's Knight Errant. If I were to consult you over every training decision of my Errant, then I'd be violating what it means to be a leading Knight."

Boldwick stepped closer, his hand tightening around the handle. Helvek stood like a stone, not even swaying an inch as he stared directly at the other Master Knight.

“You know damn well that conditions for training are different out here in the wasteland.”

Sir Helvek took a moment, then responded coldly, “I do not. I will remind you, Sir Boldwick, that I am from the Kingdom of an Order that has limited experience with operations so far outside of our walls. It was an error on my part to have chosen this battleground, as we now can see by the corpses strewn across it. I could not have predicted that monsters would rise from it, and I will consider it in the future. I will not consult you over my training methods, but I will be sure to consult you on the environment we engage in for training in the future. Thus, I have conceded my own error, and would like it if you were to admit you do not have a say in our training methods, and are overstepping your bounds by insinuating I went too far with my Errant.”

The two stood at an impasse.

Garin didn’t move an inch; it was as if the very air around him was tensed like coiled muscles. All of the surrounding Knights watched, rapt. He swore to the Goddess above that they were seconds from launching into another fight on the level of Colin and Bedwyr earlier. Blood practically flooded the air, just from two oversized Armored men glaring at each other.

Boldwick took a step back. Didn’t apologize, and didn’t address Helvek’s comment further.

He turned to Sir Able. “You knew better,” he accused.

Sir Able wilted. “I did. I will endeavor to contain my enthusiasm… I—you know this is my first student. And he’s…” the old man stopped, and glanced at Colin, then gritted out his next words carefully, “…a very unique student. I’m still trying to determine the best ways to bring out his potential. You have my sincere apologies, Boldwick. Nothing like this will happen again.”

With that, Boldwick looked between the two of them, weighing how much he could say, what kind of punishment to levy. The social dynamics alone made Garin’s head whirl. On one hand, you had Sir Able admitting responsibility, which demanded a response. But given that Helvek was side-stepping anything like that, and the fact that it set the tone for how other equivalent ranking Knights of different Orders would be handled on this expedition… Garin didn't know the right play here. He didn't envy Boldwick, but he didn't envy himself either. This would only piss the Master Knight off more. And Garin was next on the chopping block.

"We will have a debrief later once I've had time to gather more facts and consider things. Then we will discuss. Both of you are dismissed," he commanded with a tone that made it very clear he didn't want to see either Sir Able or Sir Helvek any longer. The two quickly retreated away from the lineup. For all that Sir Helvek pushed back, the senior Knight did not delay his leaving.

That left Bedwyr, Colin, and Garin.

The mood changed.

“Explain to me, just what the hell I’m looking at here,” Boldwick asked.

Bedwyr spoke up, “I… I’m unsure what to say. They told us to spar, and to go at it, and as our ranking Knights, that is what I did.”

Boldwick sighed and then gestured at Garin, “I’m aware of that. I’m not holding you responsible for that. If anything, I’m proud of your capabilities to handle that threat alone. I mean him. Why is one of our Academy students out here on this expedition, without approval, and without Armor?”

Bedwyr cleared his throat. “I know nothing about that.”

Boldwick leveled his attention on Colin.

Garin shivered. Master Knight was keeping his voice very tightly controlled, but beneath that, he sensed an edge. He’d seen Boldwick in many circumstances. In the middle of battlefields, directing swaths of Knights, delivering critical reports, negotiating with mages whose powers rivaled those of grandmasters. But he’d never heard a tone like this. It was as if he were straddling the desire to strangle Garin right here and trying not to cause a bigger scene. Worse. He hadn’t even addressed Garin directly yet. The wait… it tore him apart inside.

Slouching. Stand straight. Be a man. Garin commanded himself, once more fixing his posture, fighting the dread in his gut that made him lean.

Colin scoffed. “I also know nothing about this. But I will say, in his defense, he did help dispatch the monsters. At the end, we were all spent, and one crawled out of the ground. He killed it before it could lay one of its disgusting mandibles on us. Horrible creatures. Excellent shot, Garin. It would have hurt us, had he not taken aim and fired. Goddess knows how many of the dreadful things he slew.”

Garin went stiff. Wait. Did Colin just exaggerate his part in the fight to save him?

Boldwick stared at the two of them.

Neither flinched, because they were telling the truth. Well, mostly, aside from Colin embellishing details.

Boldwick gestured at them, “You are free to go. Check in on Erec. Excellent fighting the two of you, but I do not want to see you unleash that level of firepower upon one another anymore out here. Leading Knight’s orders or otherwise, and I’ll hold you responsible for that as the leader of this expedition. It is a security risk, unless we are facing enemies.”

“Understood,” both said, giving a quick salute, then marching off.

That left just Garin and Boldwick.

Boldwick let the silence linger. His face was hidden beneath steel. Each second, the silence grew in weight, starting as a mere crushing stone on Garin's chest, becoming that of a mountain in mere seconds. Yet still it dragged.

Boldwick didn’t know. The Master Knight couldn’t know. How could he understand all of those sleepless nights the last month, seeing his friends push into a place he couldn’t follow? Was there a way he could put into words that some part of him, deep down, knew this was his one last chance to go with them? That if he failed here, he’d amount to nothing but a liability to them all? No. He'd think him a foolish kid. Another young Knight, with stupid ideas and more courage than anything else. Worse, part of Boldwick would be right, but Garin couldn't have lived with himself had he made any other choice.

“What do you propose I do, were you in my place?” Boldwick said softly.

“I…” Garin could suggest letting him off, letting him join the expedition. But that was unfair. It… it wasn’t what he deserved, after going behind Boldwick's back and letting things come to this. "…I would likely strip you of your rank and discharge you from the Order of the Verdant Oak," he admitted.

“And then?”

“Then… I’d have to figure out how to get you back. Have to spend time and resources to transport you, now a civilian, back to the safety of the walls.”

“What does this do to the expedition, Garin?” Boldwick prodded softly.

“…It jeopardizes it. All because a foolish Academy student smuggled himself out and put both himself and others in a dangerous position. You end up delaying either the expedition or risking its members unnecessarily to provide transport. Or, you drag a civilian into a battlefield. If you kept me as a Knight, I’d be without Armor, and yet another body to protect.”

Boldwick was quiet, and then said, “If you’d thought I’d do all that, then tell me, Garin, why you would go so far knowing the results? This is an unforgivable act, not just in your role as an Initiate, but also a violation of the trust I’d put in you. We talked about this. I told you why I didn’t want you out here, yet you ignored that and selfishly put this expedition at risk.” The man didn’t even raise his voice. The anger that had been there moments before when dealing with the other Knights was gone. In its place was something far worse.

Boldwick was disappointed.

There were a thousand justifications Garin could give. Hundreds of excuses he could make, but all of them were hollow in his head.

But it didn’t matter. The anger he was ready for. This?

Garin crumpled, the steel in his spine gone, the shame rampaging through him. He hung his head, as his heart was stabbed through.

“So why do this?” Boldwick asked.

"Because I had to," he said. It wasn't even really an excuse or anywhere near a justification that could outweigh the risks he'd taken. But it was the truth. No justification, just the need he felt deep in his gut, to which he forced everything else aside. A silly feeling. A dream. A vision of a table that seemed ever out of reach. It was stupid to say, and even worse, it was nothing he could ever explain.

Boldwick looked up at the sky, “If I were to try to send you back?”

“I’d renounce my position as a Knight, and ask the Pendragons to take me on.” It was the only recourse that allowed him to stay on the expedition. In the absence of being able to explain himself, and given the fact he'd done what he had, it was the only action he had left.

Boldwick let out a small growl at that, then shook his head.

“So that’s how it will be then.”

“I am sorry.”

“You are, I know that. But you don’t regret what you’ve done. All of you are simply rushing to throw your lives into danger… Goddess, what has this world become? I don’t have much to say, since you understand what I’ve been forced to do. Consider yourself stripped of your rank. If you insist on joining the Pendragons, then your presence here will be their responsibility.” Boldwick turned away from him, not with a dismissal, not with another complaint. After all, could he dismiss someone who was no longer a Knight?

Garin watched him disappear. His spine broken, his heart torn.

And he was left with one thing, a hope that the dream he’d chased out here—it wasn’t for nothing. He couldn’t let it be for nothing.
