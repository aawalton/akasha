Chapter 47 – lumiere_27

ARC IV: THE THINGS THAT STAY

SYNOPSIS: Day 4 post-declaration. lumiere_27 has survived her first three days in Aethelgard through a combination of warrior HP, accidental apple hoarding, and the specific stubbornness of someone who has a reason to be here. She finds Livia. She says too much and too little at the same time. And then she says one thing that makes Livia go very still.

[lumiere_27: Warrior Lv. 3. Deaths: 7. HP: 847/1200. Inventory: 47 apples, 3 copper coins, 1 broken tutorial sword, 1 Mystery Pouch (contents: 2 apples, 1 Low-Grade Mana Crystal, 1 item the system has classified as 'miscellaneous'). Current location: Eastern Residential District. Intended location: anywhere Livia Quinn can be found. She has been walking east for forty minutes. Livia is west.]

[The system notes: the system considered sending a waypoint. The system decided against it. She will find her own way.]

The thing about being a level three warrior in a server preparing for guild war was that nobody paid attention to you.

This was, in theory, exactly what she needed. Get in quietly. Find one specific person. Have the conversation she had been composing and recomposing in her head for three weeks. Get out without triggering anything.

The problem was that ‘quietly’ was harder than anticipated when your armor made a sound like a collapsing cutlery drawer every time you walked, and you had 47 apples, and you had been stuck in a fence for two minutes on your first day, and the global chat still occasionally surfaced the message ‘I am stuck in a fence’ as a meme.

She had checked. It was being used as a meme. Her first three words in Aethelgard Online were now a meme.

She opened the map for the fourth time that morning. The dot labeled ‘You’ sat stubbornly in the middle of ‘Eastern Residential District.’ To the west, Thirty minutes away: ‘Guild Hall: Bloodbath & Beyond.’

She had walked past it. Forty minutes ago. Going the wrong direction.

She turned around. Her sword clanked against her hip. Somewhere in her inventory, an apple rolled.

She found Livia outside.

Not at the guild hall — she had stood outside it for six full minutes. The door was large and reinforced and had a guild crest on it that glowed faintly, which felt like it meant something about who was and wasn’t allowed to knock. She had been working up to it when the door opened from the inside and Livia came out carrying a notepad under one arm and a mug in her other hand, already looking at something in the distance, and almost walked directly into her.

They ended up thirty centimeters apart. Close enough that Lumiere_27 could see the ink stain on the side of Livia’s left hand — the outer edge of the palm, where it dragged when she wrote.

Left-handed. She was left-handed. That detail, specific and small, hit harder than She expected.

Livia looked at her with the particular attention of someone whose job was reading people and who had just clocked something. She didn’t step back. She didn’t step forward. She just looked, the way a person looked when they were deciding how much space to give something.

lumiere_27 opened the chat function out of reflex and typed: ‘hi’

Then remembered. She was in the game. She had a voice. She’d practiced using it in the character creation lobby for forty seconds before the embarrassment of hearing herself in a fantasy RPG had made her turn it off and then back on again.

She said, out loud, in the register of someone whose rehearsal had just collided with reality:

“Hello. I am a player. I am new. I…” she stopped. Started again. “I would like to speak with you. If you’re available for that.”

A beat. Livia’s mug tilted slightly in her hand — not enough to spill, just the micro-adjustment of someone recalibrating.

“I’m generally available,” she said. “Who are you?”

“lumiere_27. Warrior. Level three.” Then, because that was just a character name: “I arrived recently.”

Livia glanced at the small inventory display visible at low player levels. “You have 47 apples.”

“I pressed E on a lot of things.”

Something shifted in Livia’s expression. Not amusement exactly. More like recognition of a specific category of person.

“There’s a bench near the east wall,” she said. “Quieter than standing in the doorway.”

The bench was wood, guild-issue, worn smooth at the edges from use. It sat against the east wall of the guild hall where the morning shadow had just started to pull back, leaving the stone warm. A small detail: someone had left a folded piece of paper tucked under one leg to stop it wobbling. It had been there long enough that the paper had compressed into the shape of the gap.

Livia sat at one end and set her mug on the ground beside her. She opened her notepad to a fresh page but didn’t write anything yet. The pen was in her left hand.

lumiere_27 sat at the other end. The armor clanked when she lowered herself — the particular sound of someone who hadn’t yet learned that you lean forward first, then lower. She adjusted twice. Gave up. Went still, like if she stopped moving entirely the armor would forget it was supposed to be uncomfortable.

“First time playing Aethelgard?” Livia asked.

“First game. I don’t usually play games.”

“What made you start?”

The question was simple. Lumiere_27 had known it was coming. She had a rehearsed answer — a careful, vague one that didn’t give too much. She opened her mouth to give it and heard herself say instead:

“I wanted to speak with someone in this server. The normal methods weren’t working. This seemed like the most direct approach.”

Livia wrote something in her notepad. Lumiere_27 couldn’t see what. Then Livia looked up.

“You wanted to speak with me specifically,” she said. Not a question. A door, left open.

“I’ve been following what you do here,” Lumiere said. “From outside. Forum threads, clips.” She looked at her gauntleted hands, which were not her hands. “But it’s different for me. I…”

She stopped. The careful answer was right there. She could still give it. She looked at Livia — at the pen in her left hand, the ink stain on the outer palm, the way she was holding the notepad like she’d been holding it for nine months and had stopped noticing the weight of it.

“I know you,” she said. “Actually. Not like the forum people.”

She kept going before she could stop herself.

“I know your favorite tea is the kind that doesn’t taste like tea. I know you write in the margins of things when you think nobody’s looking. I know you’re left-handed but you eat with your right because your mother thought it was better table manners. And you just kept doing it.”

Silence.

Livia’s pen had stopped moving. Her hand was still on the page but not writing. She was looking at Mar with an expression that was not fear, not confusion. Something more specific than either. The stillness of someone who has just heard a door open that they didn’t know existed.

The fountain in the Tessarae was audible from here if the wind was right. It was right.

“What’s your name?” Livia asked. Not lumiere_27. The other one.

“You can call me Mar.”

Small. One piece of something larger, held carefully. Not the whole thing. Not yet.

Livia wrote it in her notepad. The ink went down cleanly. ‘Mar.’

“You’ve sent messages before,” Livia said.

Mar’s armor made a small sound as she went still. “You got those.”

“Briefly.”

“They were deleted.”

“Yes.”

“I didn’t know how much you got.” She looked at the ground in front of the bench. There was a line of ants doing something purposeful between two stones. She watched them. “I’ve been trying to find a way to talk to you that would actually reach you.”

“This is quite direct,” Livia said.

“I’m not very good at indirect.” Then, from somewhere, the sound that was almost a laugh: “Apparently I’m also not very good at video games.”

Livia’s expression did something. Not a smile exactly. The precursor to one. She wrote something in the margin of her page.

They talked for forty minutes.

Not about the important things. Mar’s armor was still too large and she had 47 apples and she was holding herself together with both hands. So instead they talked about the sword.

“It broke in the tutorial,” Mar said. “I didn’t realize it was broken until someone in the main hub told me. I’d been using it.”

“On what?”

“A fence post, mostly. Also some slimes. Also I tried to use it on a locked door, which in hindsight—”

“The Goldmere blacksmith repairs tutorial equipment for free,” Livia said. “First time only. Second left from the Tessarae fountain through the Goldmere archway. It won’t be a good sword. But it’ll work.”

“Better than what I have.”

Mar looked at the mug on the ground beside the bench. The server rendered steam rising from it in small lazy curls. “Is that tea?”

“Yes.”

“The kind that doesn’t taste like tea.”

“The server generates it from a flavor profile I set in month two. I described what I wanted and it built something close.” Livia picked it up, turned it in her hands. “Not exactly right. But close enough that I stopped noticing the difference.”

Mar looked at that for a second. The mug. The steam. The way Livia held it like muscle memory.

“Close enough that you stopped noticing,” she said.

“Yes.”

Then Mar said, not casually enough to be casual: “Is it safe for you here? With what’s coming.”

Not ‘is the war dangerous.’ Not ‘what happens next.’

Is it safe for *you.*

Livia set the mug back down. She looked at Mar — at this person in armor that was too large, with a broken sword and 47 apples, who had walked into a game she’d never played to ask that specific question first, before anything else.

“I don’t have a combat stats. So I won't be on the front lines.”

“That’s not what I asked.”

“I’m okay,” Livia said. She meant it. That was the thing — she actually meant it. “I have people here. Work to do. Somewhere to be.”

Mar looked at the guild hall behind them. At Lumen, which had drifted out from somewhere and was hovering at the far end of the east wall, its cloud-body moving slowly, its lightning the color of late afternoon. It hadn’t been called. It had just… come.

“Okay,” Mar said.

That one word carrying: I needed to hear that. I’ve been carrying the not-knowing of it for longer than I want to say.

Livia heard it. She wrote something in the margin of her notepad. Smaller than usual. The kind of writing that was for her, not for a record.

When Mar stood to leave she had the stiffness of someone whose armor had settled into one position for forty minutes and was not pleased about being asked to change it. She got upright in stages.

“I’ll come back,” she said. “If that’s alright.”

“Same bench. Same time.”

“I’ll probably get lost.”

“Get the sword repaired first. Second left from the Tessarae fountain through the Goldmere archway. If you go right, you’ll end up in the crafting quarter. It smells like burnt copper. You’ll know.”

“Second left,” Mar said. She filed it. “Okay.”

She started walking. Her armor settled into the rhythm of it. She got to the east gate and took the wrong turn — right, not left — walked for about ten steps, stopped, came back, and took the correct turn without looking back to see if Livia had seen.

Livia had seen. She did not say anything.

Mar disappeared around the corner toward the Goldmere archway. A few seconds of armor sounds, then nothing.

Footsteps. A pause at the corner — just a beat, the length of someone stopping without deciding to stop. Then continuing. Getting quieter. Gone.

Livia sat on the bench a little longer. The afternoon light had moved. The folded paper under the bench leg was still holding its gap. Lumen pulsed once near the wall and went quiet.

She did not write anything else.

Eventually she stood, picked up her mug, and walked back inside. Miles was in the doorway — the planning-something posture, been there a few minutes, looking at nothing in particular. He glanced at her. At the bench. At the space where someone had just been.

“New patient?”

“Something like that.”

He didn’t ask more. She didn’t offer more. They went inside. The east gate closed.

The bench sat empty in the afternoon light, the small folded paper still holding its gap, doing the quiet work of keeping things level.
