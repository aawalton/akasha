Seconds that passed like minutes saturated Harry's mind as if time had substance. Transparent waves circulated through his body as he felt an instant adrenaline rush. As if under the influence of hallucinogens, a sudden dizziness was inflicted upon him as if he had licked a strange frog. Hundreds of colors filled his iris as his fingers tingled and his tongue exploded a million flavors.

And then it was over. Just like that.

The gel like substance was quickly going away as he felt his muscles move again. Out of balance, he was tumbling over himself and ready to fall. Strangely, however, when he tried to use his Emblem, it didn't work. With his doors nullified, he fell to the ground.

Slightly panicked, the Locksmith kept forcing his powers, unsure on what to do next if he lost them permanently. No. It can't be! And after a few seconds, dozens of doors appeared all around the room, with varying sizes.

"Hey, hey!" Brun calmed the man, touching his shoulder. "Don't force it. You know how these things go. One moment you're a healthy dancer and the next your body shuts down. Don't force it."

Don't freak out if they don't work... Harry recalled what the old woman had said. Why couldn't Emblems work after a loop? Could this also be linked to the Astronomer? And then he felt stupid for thinking that. Of course it did; everything in the Logos Tower was related to the Astronomer. How could it not be?

"...Why is that a thing?" The Locksmith asked, getting up, feeling reinvigorated.

"You don't need to dwell so much on it, Mr. Locksmith." Vela said, his candelabra still in his hand.

"Wait, how are you holding your... weapon?"

Harry then groped the insides of his coat, frantically searching for his personal object. Oh, shit. It's here. Nice, so it saves all of our belongings.

The journal didn't have a practical use, outside of being a source of paper, which Brun could benefit. However, it had been with Harry for so long it could as well be part of him.

"It saves anything that you have with you, at the moment of the throwing." Brun said in behalf of Vela, who simply nodded. "But don't worry, your C-Coin will always be with you, even if you don't want to."

And then Harry felt it, the small Emblem tucked away in one of his many pockets.

"Thank the gods!" Prism exclaimed to the group, barging into the door. The sudden entrance made Harry turn around quickly. "Now can we please, please, leave this fucking floor?"

"...Are you sure about that?" Harry inquired as he stared deeply into the seemingly infinite pit of darkness that seemed to engulf the whole floor. "Shouldn't we — you know — climb upwards if we wanna reach the upper floor?"

"I already explained it to you," Brun replied, visibly irritated. "It is the only way! I thought you were supposed to be a fearless thief?"

"Where did the fearless part come from?" Then he remembered the woman's Emblem and rapidly changed his stance. "Actually, you're right! I, um, do not fear death!"

The old woman rolled her eyes as the Locksmith straightened his tie and adjusted his glasses. Vela, right next to the young thief, held his extinguished candelabra as he stretched himself. Prism, copying Vela, kept doing little jumps with a huge grin on her face.

"How long until we can do it?" Harry asked.

The old woman scanned the empty piece of paper and, after a few moments, replied. "2 minutes and 31 seconds."

The loop experience was, in itself, a pretty traumatic experience; a monochromatic shade of the heat death of the universe. At least that's what it felt like to Harry. His fingers shuddered at the thought of it. Brun said it would get better, eventually.

What mattered in that moment, however, was that different floors had different timed loops. According to the old woman, the loops were probably of the same length in time, but started differently. If they were to enter the hatch and arrive at the end of a loop in the second floor, the group would simply return to where they last set their C-Coins. To avoid lost time, however, they would need to wait for the loop on the other floor to reset.

Thanks to Brun's Manuscript Emblem, she could roughly guess when the next floor's loop reseted by analyzing the hatch. How is she seeing it... She's older than me! Shouldn't my vision be better? Harry narrowed his eyes, hoping it could help him see the outline of the hatch that Brun was supposedly seeing.

What if it was a trap? What if there was no hatch and this was simply the play of an organized, torture group? But then the Locksmith quickly threw the thought away. They could've tortured me in worse ways. Which means I'm going to actually have to jump just before a worm eats me. I seriously don't know which is worse.

A rumbling could be felt upwards as everyone prepared to jump down into the pit. They would have to be fast.

"...Why aren't we down there already? Why do we have to jump exactly now!?" Harry said. "This is nonsense!"

"I thought you liked adventures?" Brun raised an eyebrow. "You know what? You actually are special; you're the first individual to ever defy my Emblem."

"I've never said I was fearless!"

"You literally just did!"

"I was insecure!"

And before they could continue bickering, she ordered Vela to light his long candelabra. Like a spear, the man pointed his weapon to the ground, scaring away the darkness. In the middle of it, a small hatch stood, surrounded by dirt and a few corpses. Poor souls who used their C-Coins on the wrong time...

Swallowing his fear, Harry jumped as he created direct passages with his door to the hatch. The rumble of the worm, however, only kept getting louder. Fuck! I hate this! The Locksmith glanced back, glad to see them following him as they fell.

Crack! The sound of the hatch being forcefully opened could be heard as Harry entered it.

Sliding through the penumbra, the young thief felt disorientated as the gravity was constantly changing. The slide was narrow and the texture felt so rough he wondered if he wasn't getting reborn.

Thump! His head then hit something solid. Confused, he pushed it, only to see light once again. The gravity was, however, completely opposite to before, as if he just crossed Earth. Harry got up and held the hatch open for his companions, which thankfully, arrived at the same place as him. Also confused, they got out.

They were inside a tunnel, candles poorly lit surrounded the place, giving a weak light. The stone slabs covering the place looked worn and, in some places, nonexistent. Despite this, they were alive; they were on the second floor.

"It wasn't half as bad as I expected." Prism was the first to comment.

"You expected worse!?" Harry replied.

She simply shrugged it off, her brown hair bouncing off of her shoulders.

Behind them, the trail ended in rubble. The only way, therefore, was following the direction of the candles as they led to an even more concerning darkness; the one that came from the night.

"Are we all okay?" Brun asked, checking each member individually. "Is anyone feeling anything different?"

"I bet if the candles were brighter, we'd all feel better." Vela replied, a strange anxiety creeping up his normally calm voice.

"Vela, no." Brun was firm. "Who knows what we'll find out there. If by some chance they see that this place is brighter than it should be, we'll be found out."

"Imagining there's someone out here." Harry said.

"There is." The old woman looked at him. "My father and Goldman are alive. They have to be."

The Locksmith swallowed his words as he gave her an apologetic smile. Although he didn't really care that much for the two missing people, it wouldn't do any good in shattering her hopes. After all, wasn't he in a similar spot?

Prism and Brun attentively followed Harry and Vela as they made way towards growing penumbra that loomed over the floor. The candles, as if giving way to the darkness, faded away as they reached the exit. Harry could even see Vela eating his fingernails as he seemed to force himself in only looking forward. Hang on, buddy, we're almost done.

Vela wasn't the only one that was fighting against his own desires. Harry also felt compelled to jump dozens of meters with his doors as he was accustomed to, however, it felt reckless in this new place. What if someone was already watching them, and then Harry presented them with a demonstration of his Emblem? The young thief, back in New Cavebrook, soon realized the power that the unknown had over people. Especially in fights.

"...Can't you see anything with your, um... thing?" Harry asked, glancing at Brun. "Like, the number of people in the floor or something?"

"It isn't that broad." Brun replied. "There isn't a single, focused point on the floor besides the hatch, and the only notable thing I can take from it is the loop's time to reset."

The Locksmith nodded and kept going forward, scanning for even the smallest of gaps in the stones. Then, calmly, they approached the end of the tunnel, whose candles barely brightened anything. Outside, only the sound of the wind could be heard as Harry seemed to notice the outlines of trees in the distance. Looking upwards, a set of stars watched the group.

"Are we in a forest?" Harry asked, absorbed by the stars in the sky.

"...Most importantly," Vela said, looking to Locksmith's right. "We're in quite the predicament, Mr. Harry. Or heaven, it depends, really."

"What are you..."

What Harry saw almost made him jump. Stretching into the night sky, a condensed flame appeared to burn the stars as it stretched into the sky. Gigantic hands constructs protruded from the worn out planks of old cabins as if they pleaded to the mother flame. All of the cabins were exactly the same, with the only change being the direction.

Behind the fire, a crooked church stood watchful. With a blank, white mask atop it, the mansion seemed to observe all the other cabins, like a priest discerning the devout from the unfaithful.

Looking at the strange scene before him, Harry could only imagine the reason for the church, the huts, and the great fire rising from the ground.

"Fuckin' hell." Harry said, breaking the silence. "Don't tell me I'm going to have to break into a church."

"...Maybe the people in there are nice?" Vela offered an optimistic vision.

"Let us hope so." Harry added. "Because I'm not in the mood for collecting tithes."
