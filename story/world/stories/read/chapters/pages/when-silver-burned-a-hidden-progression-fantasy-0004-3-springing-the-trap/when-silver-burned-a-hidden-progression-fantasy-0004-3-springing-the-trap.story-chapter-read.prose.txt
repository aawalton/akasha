The pub was a neat little establishment, balcony and all, Kalsin thought as he ascended the stairs.

Jack kept thoughtfully chewing away at a piece of brickleaf with no outward sign that she had noticed him. She was lightly geared with her vambraces slung over her neck on a string like a steel-clad scarf. The man shifted in his seat as Jack spit the spent leaf to the floor, placing a fresh, rust-colored piece in her mouth. Her teeth were tinted pink.

Having had enough entertainment for the moment, Kalsin took the empty seat next to Jack facing the man. "Apologies for the delay. A city so large, I can rarely tell one street from the other. Sinew, yes?" Kalsin narrowed his eyes, recalling the transaction code. "Six three nine seven seven one four, blue scimitar."

The man currently wearing the alias Sinew, had been fiddling with a spoon, but the motion stopped as he scanned Kalsin's face. His eyes clouded for long seconds. Kalsin patiently waited. When the rot's memory-scouring surge took its full course, he blinked and his fingers continued their dance. "You're one Blindlight out of date, so that'll be another silver on top of the standard fee. Savvy? On hand, before you ask your questio– Good, 'priciated. Now, I've got people to see in a quick hour so be hasty about it."

"Naturally," Kalsin said, drawing the string of his coin pouch closed. It was beginning to feel a touch light. He stretched his neck to look around. "But, ah, are we entirely safe here? No ears listening in? I'd appreciate it if you put our minds at ease. It's the first time we're deviating from the lanes of law."

"Simple. On your part, that is. Ask your questions, and then I'll see what I can scour up. Keep my name off your lips while I'm away. Pay the second half of the fee when you receive your answers, and that includes a twin brother of the additional silver on top for keeping me waiting." He punctuated each of his points with tapping the spoon on the table.

Kalsin made a face of surprise. "Your name? But I thought it was just—"

"'Course it's just an alias, kid. But that's how I'm known around these parts, and I don't need no guards kicking up the nest I cobbled together for the duration of my stay here."

Jack bristled, and Kalsin wasn't sure if she was exasperated by his show of cluelessness, or amused by him being called a kid.

"Remedies," Kalsin said. He eyed the scar parting the rot's lips. "Physicians. Healers. Are you acquainted with any of those circles?"

"Depends. If it’s classified info, it could fall in line with my expertise, yes."

"What I need isn't something you find yourself tripping over by chance. Even for someone like you. It's rare. Delicate. Elusive. Something of a legend, really, but you've heard of it. Must have, sure as Warmlight rises with the morning."

"Spit it out."

"A cure for the Scarrd."

The spoon in Sinew's hand clattered on the table from nerveless fingers.

Kalsin continued. "They say many things in dark places, whispers only, but they say it anyway. Hope. Redemption. A salve for the soul."

"No," Sinew said, interrupting. He laughed, then stopped short just as abruptly. "None of that. You flogged in the head, boy? Blindlight sear the senses out of you? There is no cure."

"Is that what you believe? Why? Doesn't sound any more unlikely than preaching in the name of the gods. But I'm not here to change your mind. Stop with that damned spoon. I'm not paying you to indulge in my views, I'm paying you to lead me to another who might. So? How about it?" Kalsin smiled. "Humor the crazed wish of a youth, eh?"

Sinew was nervous. Kalsin could see it from the fervent snaps of his eyes, the accelerated restlessness of his body. A sheen of sweat on his forehead. It confirmed Kalsin's suspicions; the rot wasn't as clueless as he wanted them to believe. Just the mere possession of some knowledge seemed to terrify Sinew.

"A cure for my scar," Kalsin repeated. "Give me a name."

"We're done," Sinew said.

Just as he made to stand, Jack surged. In a blink, she was behind the rot, a muscle-corded arm on his shoulder pushing him back in his seat. With her blade sheathed, she wasn't threatening enough for the inn's inhabitants to interfere—but threatening enough to make them look the other way.

"It's truly a lovely city," Kalsin looked out the balcony, all the way up to the giant wall separating them from the overworld. "A part of it must be, if only to balance the miserable, rank filth of your very own Eastside."

"There's not a thing you can do to me," Sinew said, baring teeth and contempt. "You put a blade to my neck, I'll tell a lie of what you want to hear, and then you'll never find me again."

"Walking the streets has become a dear pastime of mine; don't ask why. Just walking. Watching. Have you ever noticed the way people act when they feel like they're not being watched? Like the consciousness of others is a physical weight attached to their limbs and wearing them down until they either crack, or become desensitized of it."

Jack clicked her tongue.

"Right. One thing led to the next, I'm talking to a bunch of dirts looking for a bloody job. Common thugs. I paid them for their services, as well as for their silence."

"You're not going to get either of those," Sinew said.

"Precisely what I was hoping for." Kalsin let the suggestion of a grin surface. "A man, his face covered by his dark and ominous hood, recruiting muscle for a shakedown with his purse full and generous? News couldn't have spread quicker if I torched the local leechouse. A hooded man, who incidentally, called himself Sinew."

A dark cloud rolled over Sinew's expression. He pried away Jack's grip from his shoulder, but was content enough to stay in his seat otherwise. "Listen here, you little guttershit. Think yourself smart? The authorities will spend the next month looking for a man called Sinew in hopes to prevent—whatever lawlessness it is you rumored to start. My contacts will deny any and all connections with me. People will stop associating with me. That's fucking inconvenient, but that's all it fucking is." He shook his cheeks, like a dog shedding wet droplets. "Trouble will pass. I don't like lying low, and then having to go through the busywork of changing identities and building up a fresh network of informants. But you have nothing on me. Your machinations accomplished nothing, done nothing but cost me time and money. Bloody amateur."

Kalsin listened silently until he pulled out his notebook, opening it.

Sinew's life-like portrait stared back from the paper, a drawing he’d personally penned down just minutes ago while blending in with the priest’s followers. The black-and-white face of graphite was awfully identical in its awfulness to that of the real person.

Sinew's lips parted in the most satisfying incredulity. Before the rot had a chance to get ideas and tap physical surge, Kalsin made the notebook disappear in the safety of his inner pocket. "Would be a screamin' shame if this little piece of art found its way to a lawman. I'd wager even the local Collector himself wouldn't say no. Changing names is easy. Changing faces—not so much. Both? Improbable. If not impossible. We'll see how you manage to disappear afterwards. You will, of that I have no doubt... disappear into the bottom of a deep, dark cell."

Jack carelessly strolled around the table and planted herself back in her seat, shooting Kalsin a red grin. At this point, Sinew wouldn't hope to gain anything by escaping; a fact obvious to all three of them.

"Scars. Talk," Kalsin said.

Whatever had fueled his twitching had evaporated from Sinew as something different, more certain took its place. But even as he acquiesced, he didn’t give off an air of defeat. The man had a rich reserve of professional dignity. "I wasn't lying. There's no cure to my knowledge. None. You could ask me what lies on the bottom of a fracture with the same breath."

"But you know of someone who could give me an answer."

Sinew licked his lips. "Out of my reach. Clenn business."

"Make an effort," Kalsin said. "Sneaking letters under closed doors, donning a decoy mustache to cover yourself, I don't care how, but you will send word to this person. You will tell him or her that I want to meet. One-on-one, sometime before this month's Blindlight. The place—hell, I'm not familiar with this city, anything private will do." Kalsin inclined his head a notch. "I'm not unreasonable. A lowly Scarrd such as myself would never dare tarnish the reputation of a Clenn by asking for public association."

Sinew watched Kalsin for a long while. "You're setting the board for a dangerous game, kid." If Kalsin wasn’t imagining it, there might’ve been a note of respect coloring his voice. He was probably imagining it, though.

Sinew slowly stood, keeping a wary eye on Jack, as if to avoid spooking an animal. "I'll do what I can. But there's no guarantee my contact will agree to the meeting."

Kalsin patted the notebook under his coat. "For your sake, I hope they will. Be here at noon to give me the location. I'll check in daily."

The wind picked up as Kalsin watched Sinew leave the inn, howling in his ears and ruffling his hair. He rested his eyes, allowing a serene moment of satisfaction. A chance. He grasped at that small branch of hope like a man being pulled into the depths of a fracture, drowning. For too long he'd been chasing a mirage, a torturing what-if.

What if there was a way for a tarnished, filthy Scarrd who deserved nothing but scorn to rise back into grace? Was it the gods who decided his path? Did they scar him as punishment for crippling Cinder? If so, he'd defy them. He'd cut a burning hole through the heavens to stand among the Clenn again.

To stand with his sister again, shoulder to shoulder, not scorned but accepted.

Kalsin became aware of Jack having leaned closer, staring out in the distance and chewing in an exaggerated manner that assaulted his ears. He flinched away. "I thought you wanted to stay off brickleaf."

She held out her little finger and thumb, shaking them. She thought the same, apparently.

"You're not doing anyone a favor, least of all yourself. You know I hate the thing." The stress-reliever was all the rage back when they visited the exotic region of Határ, and Jack was quick to follow fashion. A taste of brickleaf, and it had stuck with her ever since.

As Warmlight colored the sharp edged buildings of Remallon in dusk-red and the first stars appeared in the sky, Kalsin talked about the near future.

His friend's addiction notwithstanding, he was glad for the effects of the southern herb. She might've strangled him otherwise.
