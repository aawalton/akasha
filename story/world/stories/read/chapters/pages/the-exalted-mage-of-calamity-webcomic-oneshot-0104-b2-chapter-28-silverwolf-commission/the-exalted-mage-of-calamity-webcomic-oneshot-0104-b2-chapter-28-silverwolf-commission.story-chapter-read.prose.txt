A shadow chasing the reflection of pale moonlight.

That was what Nasha looked like as she swiftly weaved around the lunging silverwolves. Or perhaps, with her red hair, it was more like the shadow of a blood moon chasing the silver glow.

Around her neck, hidden beneath her tunic, was the small necklace Veronica had gifted her. The power of Selene’s Tear was alive and active, fueling her body. Not only were her movements quicker, but her body had become more nimble as well, stretching and twisting easier than before.

Along the creek, seven wolves took turns attacking her, lunging, biting, and clawing once, sometimes twice at a time.

The goal wasn’t to defeat the monsters. That would have been easy enough. No, the goal was for Nasha to become familiar with spiritual energy. To channel it through her limbs and grow accustomed to the new power. Because within their party, it seemed only an aura user was compatible with spiritual energy. Veronica had tried it herself, but the Tear rejected her mana. The same had happened with Gwen.

Instead, the two of them resigned themselves to another task.

Off the embankment, up on a cliff’s edge farther along the stream, Veronica and Gwen stood together, engrossed in their own tasks. Specifically, training meant for Gwen.

“Alright. List off the spells you currently know for me,” Veronica said.

“Gentle Touch, Antidote, Renew, Mend, Reinvigorate, and the newest one you taught me, Lesser Cleanse,” Gwen replied.

“Mm. A good set of starter spells. Magic that can mostly soothe inflammation, encourage muscle healing, fit bones back together, and remove curses. However, since you’re only Tier-2, most of these effects are still slow and weak.”

Gwen nodded, already aware of the problem. “Reinvigorate works the best for healing muscles and small tears, but it gets slower if there are a lot of injuries.”

“That’s right. While you do have plenty of spells for different situations, like Gentle Touch for blood flow or Mend for fitting bones back together, everything you have so far is still quite specific. They’ll work well enough if we have downtime after a fight, but during a fight, they’ll be similar to constantly applying bandages over a wound.”

“Then are you going to teach me a new healing spell?”

Veronica shook her head. “No. Mainly because any stronger, more potent healing spells you’ll learn are restricted to higher tiers. Variables that you can’t access just yet. There’s no point in teaching you something you physically can’t cast.”

Gwen furrowed her brows, her lips forming a thin line. “Then… how am I supposed to heal you guys better?”

Veronica smiled. “Simple. You won’t.”

“What?”

“Nasha is here to get used to wielding Selene’s Tear in combat. You, on the other hand, will be focusing on combat-related lessons. Healing magic will be postponed for the time being.”

“But… aren’t I just a healer? I’m not a Path of Ruin mage like you. How am I supposed to learn offensive spells?”

Veronica turned from her and looked at the scene below, watching Nasha jump and weave around the silverwolves. “There’s more to combat than offensive spells. Especially for a mage like you.” She focused her gaze on the seven wolves. “Do you know the strongest trait of silverwolves?”

Gwen looked over at the wolves. “Um… they are strong in packs?”

“Yes, but what else?”

Her eyes narrowed as she thought. “Uh… keen sense of smell?”

“Correct,” Veronica said plainly. “Based on our commission, these silverwolves have been roaming around quite a lot. This entire creek is their territory. Of course, that means they’ve also marked it.”

Just as Nasha kicked off a large rock and landed back on the ground, Veronica called out to her.

“Nasha! Lead them into the forest and circle back to the creek, then jump up here!”

Nasha glanced toward them for only a moment.

Then she turned and sprinted away from the creek, boots finding every raised rock before she passed a muddy bank. The silverwolves immediately chased after her. Seven gray bodies rushed after her in a blur, splashing water and dirt as they followed her into the trees.

“Alright,” Veronica said, turning around. “Go ahead and cast Lesser Cleanse on the area down there.”

Gwen stared at her. “At… the rocks?”

“And the water next to them. Start from where Nasha was standing, then spread it along the path she used to leave the creek.”

Gwen’s brows drew together. “Is… there a curse down there? Or a poison?”

“That’s not the only thing cleansing magic can remove,” Veronica replied. “Now hurry, before Nasha comes back.”

Gwen was still hesitant, but she lifted her staff anyway. Pale green mana gathered around the wooden shaft, soft and warm, flowing upward toward the crystal at its tip. As she pointed the staff down toward the creek, her magic flowed out in a steady stream, enveloping the area below.

A faint shimmer spread across the creek as her magic imbued itself into the earth and water. The spell finished after stretching across the route Nasha had taken into the woods.

A short while later, Nasha came running out of the trees. The silverwolves weren’t behind her yet, but they would appear soon enough.

She reached the creek first, jumping over rocks and mud before catching the narrow ledges along the cliff. She kicked herself upward, grabbing purchase on each open stone. One final leap sent her landing near Veronica and Gwen a moment later.

“So? What was that about?” Nasha asked.

“For now, just stand behind us. Don’t let the wolves see you,” Veronica said.

Nasha did as told.

After a few more seconds, the silverwolves returned to the center of the creek.

Both Veronica and Gwen looked down, noticing the strange expressions and movements the wolves were making.

“What… are they doing?” Gwen asked.

“They’re confused,” Veronica said.

The silverwolves were looking in every direction. Their noses lifted as they tried to sniff the air. Their eyes searched the nooks and crevices, checking each shadow inside the creek and along the rockbed. A few even looked up toward them, though none kept their gazes there for long.

“Alright, Nasha. You can go back down there and continue getting used to Selene’s Tear.”

Nasha nodded and jumped past them, somersaulting back down.

She landed in front of all the wolves, startling them. Some growled, while others jumped almost like cats.

After realizing it was Nasha, they began attacking her once again.

Back at the top of the cliff, Gwen looked over at Veronica. “So… what was all of that for?”

“Didn’t you notice?” Veronica said, glancing at her for a moment. “Silverwolves hunt through scent as much as sight. Based on the commission, this creek is their territory, so every stone, every patch of mud, and even the air carries information for them. Nasha’s path, her direction, everywhere she stepped or breathed.”

Gwen looked back down below, watching Nasha continue to weave through the wolves. “And you’re saying Lesser Cleanse removed all of it?”

“It purified it,” Veronica corrected. “It reverted the area back to a clean state, before those scents corrupted it. You didn’t erase the territory, but you did clean it. The moment Nasha passed through the creek, she began leaving traces of herself. By removing all of that, the wolves became confused. That’s why they didn’t know where she went after coming back. Your lingering magic began removing her presence the instant she jumped up here.”

Gwen stared at the wolves below, humming softly to herself.

“This was just one example of how you can take a more active role in fights,” Veronica said, turning to face her. “There will be various ways you can help, even with simple healing magic. You’ll just have to recognize them. Be creative.”

“Right,” Gwen said, still unsure of herself.

Veronica studied Gwen a little closer.

“You have quite a good affinity with fire magic, from what I’ve seen,” Veronica said, changing topics.

“Don’t you mean nature or light?” Gwen asked. “That’s the element for all of my spells.”

Veronica shook her head. “All of your healing spells.”

“Huh?”

“Back in the Valley of Silence, what was the one thing you did to help us fight against the carrion beasts, aside from casting healing spells?”

Gwen furrowed her brows, thinking back. “I… threw fireballs?” she asked, almost questioning herself more than answering. “But fireballs are a simple spell. Anyone can learn them.”

“That is true,” Veronica said. She raised a single finger in front of her and conjured a small flame above it. “Fireballs are a staple mage spell. Normally, people wouldn’t classify them as a specific pathway spell, just like most null magic, such as levitation, flight, or teleportation.”

“Then what is this all about?”

“Just from how you used them in the valley, I could tell you have a higher affinity with fire. You essentially cleared the way out for us as we left the sanctuary using purely fire magic.”

“Well, sure,” Gwen said, flicking her hand in a nonchalant gesture. “But that’s also because we killed most of them on our way in. And you helped out with fire spells here and there, too.”

“Don’t degrade yourself like that,” Veronica said sharply. “It was still an impressive feat. Normally, a mage wouldn’t be able to cast fire spells that easily, even if you can absorb ambient mana.”

“Really?” Gwen asked, skeptical of her claim. “I guess I haven’t really seen many other mages, since I’m self-taught, but…”

“If you can’t believe a 10th-tier mage, then who will you believe?” Veronica said, teasingly. “Anyway, with that being said, I do have a set of spells I can teach you, one of which you can learn right now at the second tier.”

“How exactly do you know spells from other tiers? I thought it was much harder to learn other path spells because of our mana cores being reforged. That, and the advanced variables that come with each tier.”

“You are under a misunderstanding there, Gwen,” Veronica said. “For instance, tell me: how did you learn your starting Blooming spells? The ones from before I met you?”

“From a spell book. The Blooming Mage’s Guide to Healing, Sixth Edition.”

Veronica nodded. “Mm. Right. Now, let’s say you weren’t a Blooming mage. Would the equations in the book just be invisible to you?”

Gwen opened her mouth to answer, but froze. “Ah… I see your point.”

“I know the equations for a large number of spells, not just Ruin, but nearly every pathway. The problem is my ability to cast them efficiently. I actually know a 6th-tier healing spell that I can cast once I become strong enough, but mainly because I was taught by some of the greatest mages alive in this century.”

“So… you even know variables up to the sixth tier? For Blooming mages, that is?” Gwen asked.

“That is correct. I know many equations for a variety of Blooming spells, but I’ve only truly learned a few of them. That’s because I’ve only comprehended the variables for certain spells. For the ones where I don’t know the variables, I can still teach the equation. I just won’t be able to cast it myself.”

“I think I get it,” Gwen said. “Basically, you know the equation as it’s written down. Some pieces are missing variables. For certain spells, you’ve learned what those missing variables are. But for other equations, you might not know the specific variables they require, meaning you can’t cast them. But you technically still know how the spell is formed, so you can teach them.”

Veronica smiled. “You’ve got it. Good job.”

Gwen blushed with a nervous giggle. “It feels weird being told that by someone only a year or two older than me.”

“I’m several years older than you.”

“Oh, you know what I mean. You’re currently twenty years old now.”

Veronica shrugged, passing off the comment. “Anyway. To return to what I was saying earlier, I think I’ll teach you your own set of specialty spells.”

“What do you mean?”

“You know how I have my Heavens Armoury set of spells? Each one I created myself? That’s what I mean. You’ll have your own set.”

Gwen’s eyes lit up. “Okay, now you’re talking. My own set of exalted spells? That sounds suuuper mage-like.”

Veronica raised a single brow. “Exalted spells?”

“That’s not what you call them?”

“No,” Veronica said, crossing her arms. She leaned back slightly and tilted her head. “Though, that’s not a bad name for them. I’ll think about it.”

Gwen hummed. “But my specialty spells, what will they be? Like, resurrection magic? Or maybe something to do with your god tree, the Yggdrasil thing?”

“Close. But I will say, they might not exactly be unique.”

“What do you mean?” Gwen asked, tilting her head.

Veronica shrugged. “Technically, I’m stealing these techniques from another mage who created them.”

Veronica lifted her palm once more and formed a flame above it. It wasn’t just a ball of fire. Instead, it morphed into a specific shape.

The shape of a bird.

“I’ll be teaching you a set of phoenix spells.”
