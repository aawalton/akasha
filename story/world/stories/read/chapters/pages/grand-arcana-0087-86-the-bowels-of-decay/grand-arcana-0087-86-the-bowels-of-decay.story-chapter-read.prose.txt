The only thing worse than fighting a Gatekeeper that had a sword was fighting a Gatekeeper that had a bow as well.

Every second the list of restricted actions constantly grew to the point that I was having trouble keeping up with what I could do. On a few occasions my concentration had slipped, causing me to attempt strikes my mind was no longer capable of. Each hesitation was an opening, half of which the aelf took advantage of.

“Why are you alone?” I asked, aiming to distract her. “Did you draw the short straw?”

She took two steps back and shot three arrows in my direction.

I used a butterfly strike to deflect them, then jumped off the railing. Spell circles appeared beneath my feet, releasing a gust of wind.

Much better than bone strengthening, I thought.

More arrows flew my way.

I dashed to the tower door. As Avon used to say, the best way to win a fight is not to fight at all. Spell circles formed in my footsteps, erecting pillars of fire.

All I needed was to reach the door. The rest of the group could deal with the Gatekeeper and then join me.

“I’ll be relying on you, Serp,” I said beneath my breath.

Don’t you always? the serpent blade asked in turn.

“I’m relying on you to kill it before you kill me,” I clarified.

The implication was clear. I’d be using my blood and flesh to have him grow again. My only concern was that, unlike the last trial, Harlequin hadn’t explicitly said that wounds would be healed. I had to be careful not to end up in a condition from which I couldn’t continue.

A new stream of arrows descended onto the floor fifty feet ahead.

What the hell? I stopped.

These weren’t the aelf’s arrows. The glow surrounding them made it obvious that they belonged to Cathy. Why was she focusing here? There was no way I could be the target. She was too good a shot for that. It couldn’t be the aelf, either…

“Shit!” I hissed, leaping back.

My suspicions proved correct, as another Gatekeeper stepped out of the tower’s blind spot. Unlike the one I had engaged in, this one was male, though he seemed to share the same cards and specials. Even its name was identical.

Two of them? I wondered. That was overkill for a sixth trial, even if it were a special one. It was normal for powerful monsters to protect the alternative trial victory condition, but this was way too much.

“More statues on the way!” I heard Reginald shout, followed by another loud blast, likely courtesy of Eleonor.

“Forget the statues!” I ordered. “Kill the Gatekeepers and—”

With a somersault, the Crimson Gatekeeper landed several feet in front of me. It was naive to think that I had given her the slip, although I had hoped that Sparta and the others would have kept her occupied long enough for me to slip through the door.

Time slowed down to a crawl. At this distance, she held a slight advantage. If I were to have a chance, I had to reduce it.

Gripping my swords, I dashed at her. Just as fast, the aelf readied her bow and aimed straight at me.

I kept on running, twisting my body as I did. The arrow flew past my head, missing my cheek by an inch.

Restrict! I struck the aelf’s bow arm.

Using the Ten of Swords, I performed a horizontal slash with Serp. If I could only nick her, the saber would do the rest.

Predicting my attack, she arched back, evading the blade, then aimed an arrow right at my face.

My entire life flashed before my eyes. There was no escaping this. Desperately, I used every ounce of strength to force my body away from the arrow tip, even though I knew there was no point. Then… the arrow didn’t release.

You can’t shoot? I thought in disbelief.

This changed the dynamics. Both of us realized it instantly, though reacted in dramatically different ways. Aware that the bow had turned into a handicap, the aelf tossed it away.

Meanwhile, I swapped the positions of both my blades. Serp flew into my right hand, while the divine blade ended in my left.

Grow! I brushed my thumb along Serp’s edge as I thrust it at my enemy.

The aelf’s reaction was immediate, parrying the strike. Serp pierced the air to the side, then extended along a heavy curve. Maintaining its momentum, the blade kept on growing until it struck the aelf in the back.

The creature paused, though this wasn’t a pause caused by a restriction skill. I could almost see my blade wreaking havoc in her body. Her mouth moved in an attempt to say something. Thankfully, before she got the chance, the aelf disappeared in a puff of smoke.

Three cards floated down, yet no booster sphere. Based on the glow surrounding Serp’s twisted and tangled shape, the reason was obvious.

Sorry, brother, there was no way for me to avoid it. He didn’t sound at all heartbroken by the result, though.

Damn it, Serp! I yelled in my mind. I needed that for the Observer!

I said sorry, okay?! Just go kill the other one.

Normally, that would have sounded like a solid plan. Sadly, aelves weren’t the only enemies that we were facing. Dozens of statues had crawled up the corridors leading to this section of the castle. For the moment, the rest of the group was keeping them engaged, but that was a temporary measure. The Stoneweaver was aware of our presence and was sending everything he had our way. In half a minute we’d be drowning in statues.

Reaching down, I grabbed all three of the cards.

THE GATEKEEPER has been improved

Skill 2: ISOLATE ITEM: restrict the usage of a single item only to entities you approve of

In different circumstances, that would have been rather useful, though not for the upcoming battle. The Archer card was worthless when it came to skills. The Thief had a few tricks to offer, even if it only granted me the silent step skill for now.

Collecting all exceptionals of the initial Houses has granted you the special Enduring.

I stared into the air for a second. There was a reward for this as well? According to the specification, enduring made me slightly more resistant to psychological effects. It wasn’t much, but it still helped. In the previous run, there had been participants who gathered all sorts of crap cards just for the sake of the specials they provided. The logic, as they said, was that given enough cards, they were bound to eventually reach a point at which the combined benefit of all specials would outweigh any individual card combination. The fact that none of them had lasted as long as I had cast severe doubt on that theory. Of course, I wasn’t one to complain about getting a free gift.

“You dropped this.” Sparta appeared a step away, holding my second divine blade.

Instinctively, I pointed Serp in her direction before relaxing my hand.

“Thanks,” I muttered.

Taking it from her, I placed both divine blades in my inventory.

Just as I was about to add something more, a statue leaped from the upper floors, landing on our level with a loud bang.

“We must go!” Eleonor appeared beside me as well.

What’s with all the Thief skills? I suppressed the urge to strike at her.

I didn’t appreciate the use of the skill, but she was right. If we remained here any longer, we risked getting overrun.

“Marcus, Cathy, Reg!” I shouted. “Get down here!”

“No.” Eleonor stepped closer. “We must run. If we miss the moment, you’ll lose an arm and I’ll end up dead.”

Another prediction? That was rather specific, not to mention convenient. In terms of personal gain, it was best if I went to face the boss alone. In terms of overall chances—it was best to go there with the entire group.

“Go,” Sparta said. “We’ll hold them off.”

“We?” I never expected her to react in such a fashion. It was obvious that she didn’t like Eleonor. In fact, I was pretty sure that she hated her, and still she was unusually accommodating right now.

“Never mess with predictions,” Sparta whispered. “I’ll join you soon enough.”

“We must go! Now!” Eleonor urged.

There was nothing in my vast experience that told me which was the best way to react in the situation. Everything was based on my judgement.

“Join us when you get the chance!” I shouted for the rest of the group to hear, then dashed towards the door.

Three spell circles appeared in front of my left palm as I used a combination of water, stone, and fire to break it open. Only the very strong or the very dead meddled with handles within anything aelf made. The monsters had the nasty habit of setting traps to weaken or outright kill any invaders. Most of the red curses I had seen had been obtained through such tricks.

The inside of the tower was a lot more bare than I expected. The whole place looked more like a garrison than the heart of the palace. A few large paintings were visible in an attempt to add a touch of aristocracy to the area, though with no success.

“Down to the cells.” Eleonor rushed directly to a staircase to the left.

“Not up?” I asked.

Being in a position where someone knew more than me was unusual. After our experience in the well, I knew I could trust the goth, but also knew firsthand that her predictions weren’t always a hundred percent accurate.

“The target is—”

I grabbed her hand, pulling her back. Thankfully, Eleonor hadn’t built up a lot of momentum, or the action might have ended up a lot more painful for her.

Emerging from the very staircase she was eager to go down was a creature… yet not one I expected to ever see again.

Undead Aelf

Level: 20

Cards: Undead Paladin (variant)

Specials: Poison Breath, Undead

Weapon: Aelf Rapier

“Shit,” I hissed.

During my past run, I had seen a lot of undead. For the most part, those were former humans, though there were instances of other species as well. Undead dragons were the greatest blight. There had been several trials in which our goal was to kill one of those. There had been a hundred of us, all from different rule-masters, setting off to do the task. Usually about a third returned.

Aelves weren’t the sort of beings that were prone to get zombified. Something about their abundance of magic made the practice unsustainable. Yet, what was impossible for common cards was possible for a Grand Arcana.

“What does your prediction say about that?” I asked.

Eleonor didn’t reply. The expression of horror on her face told me that she hadn’t seen this coming, which was a terrible sign. Not even deities were supposed to be able to shield themselves from prophetic visions. Either the goth wasn’t as strong as she made herself out to be, or we were facing more than a single threat.

The girl pulled herself free from me and extended her hands, unleashing a cascade of aether projectiles at the approaching creature. Holes covered the scant furniture of the room and a few columns that found themselves between us and the aelf. The creature itself disassembled in real time as parts of its body were systematically torn off.

“Easy,” I said. “Don’t lose focus.”

“That’s not supposed to be here!”

First time? I chuckled internally. Having an expected future change could have that effect.

“Are you sure he’s down there?” I asked.

“Yes,” she replied. “I think so,” she added, a note of hesitation in her voice. “We were supposed to run down the stairs to the cell section of the dungeon. There’s a ritual chamber there.”

Another ritual chamber… just great.

“Alright. I’ll take the lead, you offer support.”

“You think there’s more of them?” Her voice trembled in untypical fashion. I couldn’t tell whether she was afraid of the creatures or the fact that her prediction had ended up being partially false.

“Undead often move in packs. We didn’t come across any aelves in the castle, so there’s a chance all of them are here…” I paused for a moment. “In this state,” I added.

“But… how?”

I didn’t reply. Eleonor had killed the creature too fast for me to tell, but the whole scene reminded me of the previous challenge. There had been a demigod who used Anubis’ power to do the exact same thing. There was only one conclusion I could come to, yet if I were right, that meant that the deity that had broken the rules was none other than Anubis himself.

No, I had to be wrong. Nothing in my previous run suggested that a high deity could blatantly challenge the rule-masters to this degree. I hadn’t performed any tasks for Anubis, at least not directly, but I knew a few participants who had made a pact with him, and they were no different from the rest of us. Could something have caused such a drastic change this time around? The answer could well be waiting for us a few floors below.
