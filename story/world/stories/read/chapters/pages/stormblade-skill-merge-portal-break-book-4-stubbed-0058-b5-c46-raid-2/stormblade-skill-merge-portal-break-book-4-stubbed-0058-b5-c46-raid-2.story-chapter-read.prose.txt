Jeff never abandoned his people.

Blossoms of electricity ten times what he’d ever seen Kade’s Stormbreak create bloomed around his teammates. His Mana dropped like a rock, disappearing instantly. He knew what was coming.

And he knew that, this time at least, he had an answer.

He activated Split-Second Shield. Or he tried to. Instead, an aura erupted from his core. A wall of Mana ten yards thick. It slammed into the oncoming Stormbreak, bubbling over it and pushing it away from his allies and himself. Six bubbles of impenetrable force formed instantly.

Stormbreak ripped across the God of Thunder’s world a split second later. The sheer force slammed against Jeff’s barrier aura, ablating it like a shield protecting a re-entry capsule. Jeff gritted his teeth and pushed his will against the storm. It felt pointless. In many regards, it was. His aura was already used. The die was already cast. But in another way, he had to throw his will behind it. To do anything else would be abandoning his people.

And Jeff never abandoned his people. Not while he could do anything to help them.

As Stormbreak pulsed against his temporary aura, Jeff’s core strained and rippled. The sheer force arrayed against him was overwhelming—not from the God of Thunder alone, but from inside his own breaking body. He felt significantly weaker than he’d been just a second ago, and he’d already estimated he was at mid A-Rank in power. Was he at B-Rank now? C-Rank already?

No, not that low.

But dropping fast.

Stormbreak finished. Jeff let his aura go. Then he raised his hand in a wave and turned for the portal.

He’d done everything he could. With his core degrading, it was increasingly dangerous for him to stay here. But even as Kade returned his wave, lightning crackling around his fist, Jeff felt the urge to throw himself back into the fight. He had more to give. Kade needed him. His people needed him.

Instead, he stepped through the portal, then into the one to Earth. He dropped into a meditative stance and let his core continue to decay.

Jeff never abandoned his people. But right now, he had no choice. His people needed him to leave.

For exactly a single second, the God of Thunder went still.

I knew exactly what he was thinking. Stormbreak should have annihilated my friends and allies. Instead, Jeff had used every scrap of power he had, and he’d blocked it. The God of Thunder had gone all-in, and we’d survived it. We couldn’t do it again, though.

But even so, Jeff had bought a second.

And in that second, a massive pulse of power rippled out across the portal world.

The shockwave came first. It blew across the platform as a wall of force that leveled the four of us and even drove the God of Thunder to the ground. Then the Mana exploded out behind it. Where the wall of force had been strong, this burst of energy dwarfed it, looming over the platform and smashing down on it in the time it took to blink. My core strained under the pressure as it refilled instantly and excess Mana poured across it.

Either Ellen had destroyed one of the towers, or Terrel had. One of our objectives was closer to completion.

And Sarah was still on the move. She pushed herself up before the Mana storm had even stopped, sprinting toward the figure of Tanat on the portal world’s floor. Even as the God of Thunder recovered, she waved twice. A heartbeat later, she started casting.

That could only mean Tanat was alive. Relief hit me harder than the wall of Mana or shock wave had. I almost collapsed from the sheer relaxation of it.

Just like that, we’d met every main objective. Tanat was safe—or would be, soon. One Mana spire had shattered, and Ellen was working on a second now. We’d set the God of Thunder back in a very real way. Perhaps a dozen disciples’ cores had gone into each spire, and we’d released their power before Eugene was ready.

Now, all that remained was to get out. But the God of Thunder wasn’t done.

“Kade Noelstra.” His voice echoed like thunder as his hundred-yard-long body whipped into the air. “Kid, you don’t know what you’ve just done. You’ve doomed your world.”

“My world was already your target, indirectly or not,” I said quietly. My voice carried through the whole portal world, echoing off the Mana spires and filling the red and blue sky. It stank of ozone after both my Stormbreak and the God of Thunder’s version, and Mana hung thick in the air. “I’m not stupid. If the Placid King’s ascension would destroy Earth, so would yours. If anything, we’ve delayed that fate here.”

Ellen’s target ruptured. Instead of the explosive wave of force the tower Terrel had shattered had unleashed, this one fired beams of hyper-concentrated Mana through the portal world like the universe’s biggest disco ball. That lasted for all of three seconds before it exploded, but in those three seconds, the God of Thunder finally shifted into action.

A beam of lightning washed over the battlefield as he roared. It surged from his jaws, dwarfing even the empowered Hand of the Thunder God I fired off a moment later. My beam all but vanished inside his, but he recoiled, and it sliced a twenty-foot gap in the platform, inches from the Spark of Life and Tanat. Sarah Cullman bundled Tanat in her arms and threw herself across the gap. “Let’s go, people!” she yelled.

It was time to leave.

The God of Thunder’s spells slammed down on me. Mist Trance dragged me through it, and as his tail flailed toward me, I threw myself over the whip-thin end. It cracked with the sound of thunder. The shockwave lifted me into the air. Before I could hit the ground, his jaws opened. Then they snapped shut. Black clouds pooled around him, and green-black lightning rippled through them. His long torso flailed toward Ellen, who backpedaled toward the portal even as I hit the ground.

I wasted no time. My stance shifted from the defensive Torrentborne to a purely offensive Cyclone even as magic welled in the Paragon’s jaws. Lightning crackled, and wind collapsed around his head in a massive vortex. The spell, whatever it was, was going to be massive.

Cloudburst. I rarely used either it or the snaring effect in Cyclone Forms, because I rarely needed to defend myself while casting. But Cloudburst was ludicrously powerful.

The God of Thunder’s magic failed. The wind stopped howling. The static stopped building. Behind me, Ellen slipped into the portal right behind the Spark of Life. The Portal Tyrant was right behind them, and after a quick second, it was just the God of Thunder and me.

He stared at me, eyes narrowed. Then he laughed, the thunderous sound echoing across his portal world. “Well played, Kade Noelstra. I can’t lie—I was hoping for this result. I won’t say I’m not disappointed. You set me back by centuries, but you’re worthy prey. Just be prepared, because when I respond, it will be swift and painful.”

I nodded. “We’ll be ready.”

“Oh, I don’t think you will be.”

“We’ll see about that,” I said. Then I backed through the portal and prepared myself for the God of Thunder to follow me through it and keep up his assault. If he attacked right now, we had a surprise ready for him.

Overhead, high above the Lightning Tower, the Eye of the Storm finished opening.

But instead, the portal closed, and I dropped to a knee. That minute or so of fighting had been exhausting.

Then I looked around. Ellen and Terrel Young were there, but I couldn’t find the Spark of Life or Tanat.

After a moment to think, it was obvious. Just like Jeff couldn’t stay in my portal world while his core finished…dissolving? Decaying? Whatever was happening to it. Just like that, Sarah Cullman couldn’t risk treating Tanat in a world that the God of Thunder was almost certain to appear in soon. She’d dragged her patient to Earth.

I shot Ellen a quick look, and she nodded. “Terral and I will stay here. We’ll leave this portal open, and if anything happens, we’ll let everyone on this side know. I’ve got work to do here, though.”

“Understood. If she’s conscious, want me to say anything?”

“No. I’ll have a chat with her down the line.”

“Got it.” I hesitated. “That went shockingly well.”

“I agree, Kade,” Ellen said quietly. “Get moving. We don’t have forever.”

She didn’t have to tell me twice. I hurried through the portal between my world and Earth and arrived to both Sarah Cullman and Sophia Walker working frantically. Sarah had her hands in a deep, cauterized wound that had bored through Tanat’s stomach and out the other side, then seared itself shut. Meanwhile, Sophia was slowly healing a gash that flooded blood across Terrel Young’s back. I didn’t even realize he’d taken a hit.

“Is she going to be okay?” I asked.

Terrel glanced at me, one eye swollen shut, and laughed. “Good to see you too, Kade. I’ll be fine. Thanks for asking.”

“I know you’ll be fine. What hit you?”

“Shrapnel. When the spire went, I turned and caught a chunk in the shoulder blade. No biggie, though. I got first blood against an SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon. No, your bat across his snout doesn’t count.” The Portal Tyrant seemed in good spirits, and I nodded to him, then focused on Tanat again.

Sarah didn’t even look up. “She’ll live.”

“Is she going to be okay, though?” I asked again.

“I said she’ll live.” The healer sighed. “Look, Kade. She’s got bruises all the way from her forehead to her knees. Half-healed cuts that your friend over there sealed shut with lightning. The stab wound in her stomach—that looks like she was impaled, and judging by the tension it put on her muscles and organs, she got carried around like that for a while. Her core’s undamaged, thank God, but just about everything else is either broken, lacerated, or bruised. This isn’t going to be a simple job, and she won’t be one hundred percent before the God of Thunder attacks again. She’ll be even less well-off if you keep distracting me, so focus on what you can do, and I’ll do what I do.”

I closed my eyes, swallowing down fury. It wasn’t Sarah’s fault. She was doing her best. No, this was the God of Thunder’s doing, and whatever move he’d planned next, I needed to be ready for it.

So, instead of watching as Sarah and Sophia—who’d finished up with Terrel—slowly alternated between stripping Tanat’s armor and clothes away, revealing a shockingly thin body underneath, I sent a quick text.

Kade: Jessie, how’s it going?

Jessie: Preparations are in progress. We need more time, though. It takes a while to mobilize an entire city, Kade.

Kade: We don’t have more time. This thing’s going to kick off any minute.

Jessie: You worry about your side of things, oh great and powerful Stormblade, and I’ll worry about mine.

Kade: Fine, sixteen-year-old guild master.

But I was right. We didn’t have time. The God of Thunder would retaliate, and we needed to be ready when it happened.
