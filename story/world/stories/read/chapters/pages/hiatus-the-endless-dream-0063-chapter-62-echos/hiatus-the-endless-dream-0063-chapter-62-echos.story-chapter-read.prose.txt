Moonlight - Dorian

Maerin sat across from me on a tiled marble floor that hadn’t been there a minute prior. It irked me that she had already gotten a hold on all of this. The rules, or lack thereof, continued to confuse me and that wouldn’t do. I strived in the gray areas and catching people unaware. My brief time in the Dream had sharpened that edge. I couldn’t use any of those hard-earned skills if I didn’t know the game I was playing.

“So, I can use or not use Presence to create things.”

Maerin sighed, her elbows resting on her knees as she thought, “Yes. It’s about perception and paradox. The immortal example you gave me before. The core question is how someone can use belief to become immortal if their belief is the only thing powering that change. Or rather, how can someone believe they're immortal if they’re aware that their immortality is conditional?”

I nodded. That was the sticking point I couldn’t wrap my head around. If someone knew that their powers came from belief, then none of their powers really existed, and how could anyone think they were immortal if someone with more Presence could come around and overwhelm their immortality? I also needed to know how the other stats affected all of this. They were part of Ananke’s system, so they likely fell under the umbrella of Boons, Restrictions, and Items, but I couldn’t exactly ask a pre-recorded tape or the eldritch Dream entity that threatened me every time we met.

Maerin, unknowing of my thoughts, continued, “I’d be lying if I said I understood it all. I’m not even sure if the things I know are being influenced by your Presence and perception, but I have a working theory. Tell me something you don’t think I can do. No snark.”

I almost replied on autopilot, but caught myself at her warning. I threw away the first couple of ideas that came to me before landing on one that just seemed ridiculous. “Clone yourself.”

“What?” Maerin asked, taken aback.

“Clone yourself. That’s something I don’t think you can do. I know you can do magic, so summoning rain or throwing some rock at me are all under what I know you can already do.”

She hesitated and shook her head. “Let’s do something else. I feel like that crosses an existential line I don’t want to think about right now.”

I hummed. Yeah, I had half expected that. I started racking my head for another idea. In a situation where anything was possible and belief was the crux of power, what couldn’t be done? I came up with another idea. “I don’t think you can bring someone back from the dead.” I offered.

Maerin looked at me with a scowl and gestured around, “Do you see any dead bodies for me to test this on?” I shook my head, “Now you’re constraining your belief.”

“You literally had to spend Presence to do anything, so shut the hell up,” Maerin said.

I shrugged, she partially had me there, “I mean, I know you can. You told me you did it. I believe you, especially for minor stuff like a firework, but not for the crazier stuff, like immortality.

“Look. I’m trying to get there, but you keep throwing crazy suggestions at me,” Maerin said with growing exhaustion.

I, in turn, just threw up my hands. “All the impossible shit is crazy!”

“I’m going to list some things out, just tell me if you think it’s crazy and if I could do it or not,” Maerin pressed.

“If you give me the list then I know you can do them because they’re on the list and if they’re on the list that means you think you can do them,” I countered.

Maerin closed her eyes, and when she opened them, her gaze drifted dangerously towards the tapes. I didn’t need to read her mind to see the malice directed at this new knowledge that we had obtained. Wait a second …

“I don’t think you can read my mind,” I said.

Maerin considered it for a moment before smiling and nodding. “Yes, that will do.” She began to concentrate, and more than a few times I saw static dance across her gray eyes. “Okay,” she eventually said, “hold some fingers behind your back and I’ll read your mind to see how many.”

I put three fingers behind my back.

“Three,” she said instantly.

I flinched back slightly, but didn’t say anything. Instead, I just put down two fingers.

“One. Don’t flip me off,” she sounded actually offended by that.

I held up five fingers, then pushed them further. Humans had five fingers. That was known. It was just a fact, but Rowan’s words from the tape reminded me. Affecting change on a large scale was one thing. Changing humans to have six fingers would be a lot harder than temporarily making it so I had six fingers. Sure, I was a human, but I was also dealing with insane Dream logic, and I was just one human. I thought of another finger on my hand, another thumb near my pinky, for better grabbing. I held up six fingers. I knew six fingers were there but couldn’t feel the sixth one. I could feel it brushing up against my palm, following my mental nudges, but it felt more like controlling a robotic limb than a natural finger. I shifted my thinking, imagining the nerves there, the connection to the rest of my hand. I left space for the feedback I’d get from the finger; the sensation of touch. I thought how it would feel to have another one, how the skin might stretch if I spread my hand wide.

The immediacy of the Dream would never stop shocking my system. Going from one thing to another in an instant with no in-between gradient sent a shiver down my spine like nothing else I had ever experienced, not even comparing to when I had the werewolf leg attached to me.

“Six?” Maerin frowned before looking at me. “Did you grow another finger?”

I brought my hand around, showing her the wriggling digit. She turned her head and put her hand out, “Ew. Ew gross. Put it away. Away I tell you!”

I laughed, but did as she asked. After a moment, Maerin gestured to my skull. “I’m going to peer deeper now.”

“Actually,” a bead of panic formed. Before, I didn’t think there was a chance in hell she could read my mind. Hell, it didn’t even seem like Ananke could. My thoughts were the one safe spot I had, that I knew nothing could get to, but the more Maerin continued, the more I believed she could. The finger counting could have been smoke and mirrors; she could have been using some other ability, or any number of things, but if she could actually track my thoughts and read my mind … Maerin was weak in the grand scheme of things, as was I. If she could peer into my mind, everyone else could too. If they could, I didn’t want to know, and if they couldn’t, I didn’t want a newfound belief that they could do that implanted into me to give any assailants a foothold.

“No,” Maerin said.

“No?” I asked.

“This is how I’ll use my favor. So you don’t get to say no,” I could feel my mind and body growing cold. There was something vicious there in Maerin, just underneath the surface. It was angry and volatile. I had thought we were getting along. I had enjoyed our banter so far, and maybe we were still friendly, but that friendliness was separate from whatever had just come over Maerin and what was currently taking place—whatever that was. I didn’t like it, but I told myself I owed her. If this is how she wanted to use it, who was I to say no?

“This is the favor. No halves. No bringing it up again. This means everything else is water under the bridge,” I tried to put on more confidence than I had. I didn’t do a very good job, and the discontent and unwillingness seeped into my voice.

That only seemed to bring Maerin into a better mood. Right. She was crazy. I had forgotten. A spoiled noble used to getting what she wanted. One hurt and wronged, sure, but still a noble. I should’ve been impressed by her restraint to go by deals instead of just demanding everything. Fine. I accepted the situation for what it was.

“That’s what a favor is. I’m not some Fey or Djinn. I keep to my word when I say I’m going to do something,” Maerin said.

I nodded, a knot forming in my throat as Maerin focused on me. Her concentration broke, and she pulled a gem-inlaid crystal goblet and a knife out of the ether. She pricked her finger and bled into it. The blood bubbled and multiplied from the bottom of the container until a thimbleful of the red substance steamed there. She slid the goblet over and motioned expectantly to it.

I paled, but drank anyway. I killed my taste buds and sense of smell. A second later, I could feel something snap into place.

‘There,’ Maerin said, but her lips weren’t moving. Her eyes smiled at me, ‘Lovely, you can hear me. That means it worked.’

“How?” I demanded. This wasn’t reading my mind, although maybe she could still do that. I could feel her in there. When she focused her attention on something. She felt like a worm moving through my synapses or a vein being flooded with cold saline.

‘No need to shout out loud. We can communicate like this,” Maerin said, but I just stared at her, waiting for an explanation. She obliged easily enough, ‘Like I said. I had a theory. I just needed to test it. You didn’t think I could read your mind. You have more Presence than me, and typical logic would presume that people can’t read minds, so I shouldn’t have been able to do it. So I did two things. First, I convinced you that I might have been able to guess how many fingers you had up. I just made a sensor that could see behind you for that. That wasn’t enough, so I did the second thing: ritual and sacrifice. How you described the problem with immortality gave me the idea. Those two ideas are common in both of our worlds and within the Dream itself. So I did the things that aligned your belief with my action and then aligned large-scale plausible belief with a ritual. Now I’m in your head.’

I stared at her, dumbfounded for a moment before responding—I spoke out loud, and tried not to mind the feeling of having a voice in my head, “I had a similar thought when I gave myself a sixth finger.” I held up my hand to wiggle the digit but realized it was gone. When I realized that I only had five fingers, I felt the digit snap back into place, and I was back at six fingers. I frowned, the more I experimented with this power the more I started to get a feel for its function. I was told perception mattered, and now I understood how. Unless I focused on something existing that only affected me, it didn't.

“Weird,” I said, keeping a growing thought in my mind as I finished what I was saying, “but I thought about affecting something small, like my brother suggested in the tape. Changing my hand to six fingers instead of all humans' hands to six fingers.”

Maerin nodded and thankfully decided to start speaking out loud again. “So it seems this ability runs off a few things. There are the high-level portions

1) Total Presence of belief.

2) Belief is drawn from everything with Presence.

I’m putting this category down as the base and truest form of it, but because people know about this power, more than just us, and because people believe in a world with rules, there are some lower-level rules. Ones that can be manipulated or changed if only someone had enough Presence or did some manipulations.”

I nodded. That right there was my ballpark. I leaned forward ever so slightly. I thought I already knew most of them, but I was curious about the list she’d make.

Maerin smiled at me, “Yes, your ballpark indeed.”

I balanced the discomforting feeling of someone reading my mind, returning as I tried to stifle any stray thoughts.

She continued, “The lower level portions, as far as I know, are:

1) Don’t mess with Restrictions, Boons or Items.

2) Belief can be over-ridden by spending Presence or some other ritual/sacrifice combination.

3) Altering someone's perception can override belief.

4) The more structured or complicated your belief the harder it is for it to be broken.”

She finished talking. I got all of them except for the last one. I thought about asking Maerin, then I thought about asking about it in my mind, then I thought about screaming it in my mind. I settled on cycling through thoughts as vividly as I could with wildly different emotional bases at multiple values, changing scenes as fast as I could think of them.

Maerin flinched back, letting out a high-pitched whine as she grabbed at her head. Something burst, and blood started flowing out of her nose in a torrent. A second later, something else slammed into me with just as much force. I recognized it as my own thoughts, except slightly distorted and at a higher pitch than what I had sent them originally. Soon enough, I was also on the floor, clutching my ears.

I thought about forming a mental barrier and paid five Presence for it just in case. The screaming remained, but it was dampened, and I could actually think now.

Maerin sat across from me, still, blood continued to pour down her nose and onto her lap, but she just stared at me with that wide-eyed, partially crazed look. “Are we done with that?”

I wiped my own nose, and the growing feeling of discontent continued to bubble up. I didn’t like that she was in my head. I agreed to it, but I didn’t like it, and really, that’s all I agreed to. I wasn’t going to attack her. Annoying her a little and getting a few licks back was fair game as far as I was concerned, but I couldn’t abide by a one-sided power discrepancy like this. I dropped the barrier a bit, only allowing a small flow of the icy saline feeling into my brain. I grabbed onto the tendril I felt, and simultaneously, I cleared some of the blood from my nose and flicked it at Maerin. The second it touched her skin, I pushed hard, trying to follow the mental stream back and plant my own connection.

I saw Maerin’s eyes widen as I felt the connection plant itself deeper. The two streams of thought broke down and reformed as one single connection.

‘Yeah done with that,’ I spoke into her brain. It echoed back to me, then to her, and started growing in volume rapidly. We stared at each other as the words grew before scrambling to fix the issue.
