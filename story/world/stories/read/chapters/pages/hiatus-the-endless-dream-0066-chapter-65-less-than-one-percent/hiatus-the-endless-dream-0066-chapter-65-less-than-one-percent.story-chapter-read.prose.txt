“Hello, my name is Minerva. I’m looking for some people. Would you have happened to see them?”

My eyes didn’t leave the woman. She looked older than us, like someone who had been in the work force for a couple of years, if the bags under her eyes were anything to go by. Or, rather, they would have if it weren’t for the completely ridiculous fantasy leather armor she wore. It was practical, but she looked like she had just come out of a Renaissance festival. I considered that possibility and found it very unlikely. The woman looked on edge, her eyes flicking between the car and our group, specifically me. It made me narrow my eyes at her as I started considering how to use belief here. Hmm, I also needed to come up with a better name for it than just belief, since that name seemed like that missed the forest for the trees.

Minerva wilted back slightly, angling her body to dash down the alley she had been running through. She was jumpy, and jumpy people were dangerous. Joey and Mina were whispering to each other, no doubt trying to figure out what to do. By the time they decided, it might be too late. I started preparing to trap the woman when a hand clapped onto my shoulder, making me jump.

“You’re mean mugging, Dorian. It’s making her nervous.” Joey whispered.

Ah, right.

Maerin spoke up imperiously, directing her words towards the woman, “I consider your patience and control amiable while we prepare our bearings. We were expecting company, just not from an unknown party. I see we are of similar tasks. We too are looking for people.” I blinked and stared at Maerin. The casual gym wear highlighted the dissonance between the words and her current appearance. The new woman, Minerva, didn’t seem to mind. If anything, she relaxed greatly as we started talking and didn’t immediately jump to violence.

“Er- yes. I believe our goals are similar then,” Minerva started, and it was hard to ignore how she fumbled over her words. It looked more like she was speaking from a poorly memorized script than anything else. “I do not … presume to know who else you’ve seen. I’m looking for three others, named Carlson, Barrow, and Cammie.”

While Minerva and Maerin continued talking, Joey and I exchanged dubious looks. He leaned closer, conspiratorially blocking his words from the newcomer. “Bets on our world or another?”

“That’s the worst thing to bet on,” I countered, “she’s obviously not from ours. Hmm, ally or enemy. Let’s bet on that.”

“What? Uhh, hmm, 4:1 odds?” he was trying to sound confident and aloof, but the blasé statement seemed to throw him off a little.

“Generous odds, I’ll take enemy though. I’ll put down lunch and 2 homework assignments.”

Joey considered the offer. If he took me up, he owed me 4 lunches and 8 homework assignments, not a small amount. For him. I could just will things into existence now. He pursed his lips in agitation, but I paid it little mind. He shouldn’t have set the odds like that if he didn't want to play. “I’m in.” He finally decided.

In the background, the talks continued, and I felt conflicted at the levity the aside brought me. Since I entered the Dream, everything had been non-stop. In a way, things still were, but things were calmer and less intense. Maybe it was my reaction to it that felt less intense? If I thought about it, I could’ve taken the hits for the other two Instances. It would’ve cost me some Presence and “a person” respectively. Things had only seemed desperate and panicked because I wanted to win against cheating Instance Holders, and there had been larger consequences. I was now in a place where I could actually consider things.

Things like that while in the Dream, I was functionally immortal, and next to anyone but Maerin, I was essentially a God. A fledgling God, but with enough power, I could probably spawn a fireball into the woman in front of me or just convince myself that I could take her bones from twenty feet away. That even after all of that, I could probably keep her alive and bring her back to health, too, didn’t escape me. I wouldn’t try it out on this woman unless she became aggressive, but it was something to keep in mind if push came to shove.

That was one such consideration; another was that if I was immortal within the Dream, why couldn’t I be in my own world, and I meant the type where if I died, I’d come back. There was some film between the Dream, Worlds and Instances, but that barrier was exceedingly thin on my end if the spiders from before were anything to go off of. It would come in handy if an Instance managed to break in, and I had to fight outside the Dream. It irked me that I was simultaneously all powerful and had no power what so ever. I could instantly kill someone, but someone with more Presence could do the same to me.

Nate's words echoed, probably the best lesson he taught me before he died: "Presence is everything." I doubt he had any idea about how right he was.

The last thought of consequence was that while talking to Joey, I had missed the conversation between our negotiator and the newcomer, and I would rather not have. My little practice session with Maerin made me think of more possibilities. I still didn’t know how she took to it so easily or how she could fathom any of the things she did being free, but I knew that we could do more with it than emulate fantasy magic, like creating a copy of myself or, at the very least, splitting my consciousness. I hadn't believed it possible at first, but now I was starting to think differently.

In truth, the only thing stopping me from trying at the moment was how disastrously the link between Maerin and me was … and shit.

I looked to Maerin, who was now next to the newcomer, the two giving details about the people we were looking for. Her expression was strained, and in the moments when Minerva looked away, Maerin kept shooting me scowls. Right. She was in my head still. She got all of what I was just thinking. It was invasive and I hated it, but a deal was a deal. We only had time to set up her half of it. Next time we stopped for a second, I’d get my half of our bargin.

I heard Maerin’s voice in my head a few moments later, ‘Yes, as much as I blocked you out I did get a lot of that. Be discrete, we’re pretending to be from her world. Something bad curse is spreading. They can’t leave the Dream. We’ve scavenged and found these clothes. You and Joey are from a city in the south called Bellthrone. Arid, think of it like Ossalyn. I’m a noble from the same city. We’re pretending to be looking for a particular Visitor’s Instance.’

I focused on my pure confusion and projected the question in my head. ‘What the hell is Ossalyn.’ A moment later, I felt a dense bundle of information fall into my mind. It felt separated from the rest of my thoughts, like a box tied to a string inside my house instead of a piece of furniture. I opened the metaphorical box and began rooting around. Okay, got it. Fantasy Florida, with just as many nut jobs. I’d never been to Florida, but I had heard much about it. Very few good things.

I leaned back towards Joey and conveyed the guise we were to wear for now. He raised a questioning brow at my explanation, but I only shrugged, “We have no better option and no reason not to.”

Joey mumbled something about lying and trust, but I ignored it.

Maerin finished negotiating, which resulted in Minerva riding in the back of the car as we continued, this time with Joey taking it at a much slower speed. The drive was filled with songs on the radio and little else. We drove around for thirty minutes, finding nothing and no one, before Minerva frowned, “We should have seen someone by now. If the curse is telling the truth, there should also be at least a hundred thousand other people here searching. I thought my issue would be sorting through people, not trying to find anyone.”

I sputtered at that. “A hundred thousand?” I asked, practically breaking my neck.

‘Careful,’ Maerin said in my mind, to which I sent her a quick ‘shut up.’

“Yes. It occurs I didn’t ask how long you’ve been here. How many were there when you arrived,” Minerva asked.

I continued to do what I did best: lie.

“So many,” I muttered, barely under my breath, hoping it was caught. “We arrived more than a week back. There were only a couple thousand.” Lying’s an art form. There aren’t rules to it, but there are good instincts. Nudging truths slightly or viewing them through a different lens worked best. A hundred thousand Visitors was a lot. A fuck ton in fact, and it had me worried. Just not for different reasons than I wanted it to appear. I had first entered the Dream a few days back. I just stretched a few days to a little over a week. The truth was malleable, as long as everyone else was left in the dark. If someone claimed an identity and acted within that identity to those who didn’t know them, that’s who they were as long as the guise held. Actions spoke louder than words and if they aligned with personality that was just the truth.

To be fair most of those thoughts were rambling pieces I picked up here and there, but when I didn't think about them they formed good instincts for lying.

I began building an identity from these few words. Confused, concerned for the people of his world, subservient to those in power, worried about a growing number of people, and this curse. That was an important point; pushing it was good.

“How did it grow in such a short time. Surely you have more information?”

I shot Maerin a quick mental message. Or rather thought loudly in my head and hoped Maerin picked it up, ’What’s this about a curse? Any details?’

She replied in short order. ‘The Dream. Something’s happening and a curse is putting everyone to sleep and putting them here.’

I gave her a mental nod and quickly tacked on something at the end before Minerva could get another word out. “How far has the curse spread past Bellthrone?”

It seemed those words were a missing puzzle piece for the woman as her face paled, “So I was right.” She sighed and shook her head. “Based on the distance between Novernguard and Bellthorne. The entire world. The fact there are only a hundred thousand is” the words caught in her throat, and her hands shook. “Everyone’s dead,” she whispered.

The weight of those words hung in the air. I shifted uncomfortably. This was getting far too real for my liking. I didn’t like the idea of dealing with someone who had just realized that almost everyone from their world had died. If their world were anything like Terra, there should have been at least 300 million people if they were pre-industrial. To go from that to barely a fraction … I did the math. Far less than one percent.

So much death in so little time.

“The people you’re looking for. They must still be alive. If you just arrived they must be in the same boat.” Joey said, trying to break the tension in the air. He gave me a pleading look, and I couldn’t tell why. I raised a brow.

‘He wants you to back him up.’ Maerin whispered in my mind. I felt the tendril of the thought and mentally batted her bridge back. I didn't want to break it, just show my displeasure.

“Yeah,” I said, “As long as they don’t wander into an Instance unprepared they’ll be fine.”

Joey turned a corner and stopped the car. We all looked up, and in a four-way intersection stood hundreds of people. There were more people beyond. I could see their heads peaking out of windows in nearby buildings. The energy was frantic, and many people pointed as they saw the car turn the corner.

My eyes fed off that frantic energy, and I looked rapidly around, fearing that a riot or chase would randomly break out for no reason, but really it just seemed to be a slight commotion. A voice called out, and people seemed to straighten. Soon enough, not more than thirty seconds, a figure in full plate armor, besides the helmet, stepped forward.

Minerva sat up and a smile broke her face, “Hey dearest Paladin!” she shouted. “Like my ride?”
