Before discussing complicated topics like elemental balance and modifying centuries old techniques to suit them with Magus Hwang, Jia and Eui had something more important to deal with. A time sensitive mission of such grave importance that Jia refused to even entertain the idea of further training until it was finished. Jia’s face was set with rigid determination and laser focus as she walked through the south wing of the campus with Eui.

“I want meat.”

Eui smiled wryly as she watched Jia stalk through the bustling market like an apex predator on the hunt. When the promised supply caravan had been late to arrive, Jia had grown increasingly anxious. It had finally arrived the previous day, and now the normally empty south wing was set up with a market that would remain here for roughly a week.

The first thing on Jia’s mind had been food. Certainly, their needs were provided for by the academy, however the food they were provided with was nothing but rice and root vegetables, with the occasional ‘treat’ of dried fish. Jia and Eui were both sick of it, but Jia was practically desperate.

Suddenly, Jia’s ears perked up, her tail went completely still, and she began sniffing the air. Her head jerked to the side and her eyes flashed as she stared towards the source of whatever had caught her attention.

“There! Someone’s cooking! Let’s go!”

Eui only chuckled as she was dragged along by Jia until they arrived at a stall selling grilled meat on skewers. Jia stared at the glistening meat skewers with stars in her eyes and drool threatening to leak from the corner of her mouth.

“How much!?”

The seller—an older looking man who appeared to hail from Yamato—was taken aback by Jia’s eager request, but quickly became the model salesman.

“One each for a skewer, Miss Customer, or three for two. Or if you’ve got low grade mana stones or cores to trade, I got some youkai cuts I can grill up special for ya. Packed full of ki!”

Lee Jia quailed a bit, but the man quickly realized the issue and clarified.

“Ah, not yer kinda youkai, miss. Just regular magic beasts—fire-hooved bull, stone-scaled ram, lightning-horned almiraj—that kind of thing. Guaranteed, nothing I sell ever had an intelligent thought in its life.”

Lee Jia relaxed. She had forgotten that ‘youkai’ could refer to magical beasts rather than just half-spirits.

“Oh, ok good. Um, I think I know what a mana stone is, but what’s a core and how do you tell which grade it is?”

The old man scratched his head, grinning at Lee Jia.

“Bit odd for you to be askin’ me miss, seeing as you’re the immortal here, but lemme see… Well, mana stones—we call ‘em ki crystals back home, but when you get around as much as me, you pick up the lingo—they’re just bits of energy that built up and crystalized naturally.

“A core’s the same thing, ‘cept it were formed inside a youkai—er, that is a magic beast. I heard some people can do it too, but that’s how you get oni—demons. Anyway, since you immortals don’t got as much use for money as we regular folk, it’s usually easier to just deal in cores and stones.

“As for grade, I think you immortals have a way of just knowing but I don’t know what it is. For us regular folk we just gotta look real close. Size and clarity are usually a good indication. You develop an eye for it, if you’re in the business of working with immortals.”

Lee Jia exchanged a glance with Eui and blinked at the old man. That was a surprisingly comprehensive answer.

“Thanks for that, mister—I’m kinda new to this. I’d like to buy your non-magic meat skewers.”

The old man’s face lit up in a bright smile.

“Certainly miss, much obliged. How many can I get you?”

“All of them, please!”

His smile froze on his face as he stared at her. She beamed at him and, without breaking eye contact, reached into her robes and held out a stack of gold coins bound together with a bit of silk cord. He glanced down at it, and then back at Lee Jia before accepting it with a nod.

“I’d best get cooking then.”

Jia had a look of ecstasy on her face and two skewers in each hand as she wandered slowly through the market with Eui. With Jia’s mission complete, they were taking their time to shop around and explore.

“I can’t believe you bought all that. You’re never going to finish it.”

Eui had also partaken of Jia’s bounty, but had been satisfied after just one skewer. That had been over an hour ago, and Jia showed no signs of stopping.

“Dae said that food wouldn’t go bad inside the ring, and it’s been over two months since I last had meat! These are so good, too!”

Jia had always had a bit of a weakness for food. At the orphanage, she had been taught to go after only valuables to bring back to the boss, but she could never resist snagging a tasty treat from street vendors. That habit had only gotten worse after she fled and had to fend for herself. While it was much safer to quietly sneak off with a bag of grain or dried vegetables from a storehouse, she would always take the risk for some freshly cooked and properly prepared meat.

“Sure, just remember you have to make those last until the next time the market is in.”

Jia looked sadly at Eui, then down at her snacks. Then, with grim resolve, she placed one of the four skewers back into her ring. Eui gave her a blank stare.

“Ancestors, Jia, you’re hopeless.”

They explored the market and purchased a few luxuries like raw meat, fruit, and even a few gourds of wine, which Jia had never tried before. Jia’s gold funds were effectively limitless as long as she wasn’t trying to buy out every single vendor in the market.

They each purchased a few new sets of non-magical clothing so that they had something other than their uniforms to wear when relaxing at home. They also purchased some basic writing materials so that Jia didn’t have to keep borrowing from Dae and Eunae.

They made a few discoveries about trade between immortals while they were at it. Many if not most of the vendors had goods that were available only in exchange for mana stones, cores, or other ‘immortal’ goods. There was no standard exchange rate between cores and gold, and it was exeedingly rare for such a trade to even happen, making the mortal and immortal markets almost completely insular.

The reason for this was a difference in value between the two groups. Many of the artifacts, elixirs, and magical materials that immortals treasured were of little to no use for mortals, and the few that mortals could make use of were completely priceless. Likewise, gold and mortal goods were pure luxury for an immortal and, while they weren’t always rich, it was generally quite trivial for an immortal to acquire any mortal goods they desired.

Mana stones and beast cores became an easy substitute currency because they were always useful as cultivation aids and materials, but trade between immortals was essentially a barter system. It turned out that Jia’s newfound wealth was nothing to sneeze at in that realm either.

While she had only a few dozen low-grade mana stones, the elixirs she had were all cultivation aids and medicines which were valuable for trade, though she didn’t feel comfortable trading them away until she knew what they were. Even the weapons, though they were not artifacts, were all made from magical materials which made them worth trading.

Still, without learning more about the value of what she had and what she was looking at, Jia was hesitant to do more than window shop and learn about what kind of things immortals traded. The most common use of mana stones was to simply crush them to disperse their energy into the air and then absorb it through meditation. This would allow for longer cultivation sessions without the diminishing returns that would occur as the mana in the area grew thin.

Too much use of a single element of mana stone would eventually lead to elemental imbalance, but even that was considered by some to be a benefit. Since most cultivators never advanced past the first or second stage anyway, intentionally shifting their attunement towards their favored element offered an alternative path to power.

The difference between the common knowledge of immortals, and what Jia and Eui had learned in the academy was surprising. They were once again reminded that this was a place for elites. The disciples here were being groomed not just for immortality, but to be the best among immortals. As they returned from their shopping trip, Jia was once again burdened with the feeling of being severely out of place.

“Ohoho! At last! I’ve been waiting for a chance to study some omnidisciplinary practitioners! Young Master Hyeong has been insistent that his own notes and studies on his new practice are for his master’s eyes only. How vexing! But you two have no such compunctions, yes? Come, come, we have much to discuss!”

Jia and Eui both cringed at Magus Hwang’s excessive enthusiasm. They had approached him after magic lessons and he had immediately agreed to assist them on the condition that they agree to help him study the effects of interdisciplinary practice. In truth, the only reason they hadn’t spoken with Hwang sooner was Dae’s warnings about this exact scenario. Hwang would just as happily treat them as specimens rather than students.

“You two are the ideal subjects, you know! No prior cultivation experience, totally unbiased! Master Hyeong would be a close second since he could double as a lab assistant, but the others just aren’t good options. Princess Seong’s spiritual bloodline is far too strong—not to mention she’s somewhat unapproachable—and there’d be a number of headaches involved with trying to work with the foreigners.”

They listened to Magus Hwang’s ramblings as he led them through the campus. Lee Jia frowned—she had been intimidated at first by Eunae’s presence and status, but she was one of the most aggressively friendly people Lee Jia had ever known.

“Ah, here we are, come on in!”

Hwang ushered them into what appeared to be some kind of private residence. The inside of the house, however, could only be described as a magical laboratory. Magic circles, arcane artifacts, and talismans covered every surface. You almost couldn’t move without stepping on a formation of some kind.

“Don’t mind the formations! They’re not going to break from being tread upon and none of them are active. At least I don’t think they are—don’t step on any glowing ones.”

Lee Jia tread carefully as Hwang directed her and Eui to some seats around a terribly messy table that was covered in research papers and talismans. Hwang took a seat across from them after moving the stack of papers covering the seat, setting them on top of a precarious looking stack nearby.

“Now, before we begin I have something rather important to ask you both. How are you doing? I’m sure it hasn’t been easy for you, but I hope you’ve managed to settle in nicely?”

Jia and Eui were both taken aback by the sudden warmth of Hwang’s tone as he addressed them both with a smile.

“It’s been an incredible experience, so far.”

Lee Jia hedged her words. She was certainly happier here than she’d been in a long time, but she couldn’t really say that it had been a wholly pleasant experience. Eui nodded as she agreed.

“It’s not what I expected. For the better.”

Hwang beamed at them, the feathers in his hair puffing out a bit as he did.

“Good! Good! In truth, I was quite concerned about you both but I couldn’t show too much favoritism. It pleases me greatly to see you both thriving here. I am rather surprised at the incredible talent you’ve displayed thus far, but as I alluded to when we first met, neither of you would likely have made it here without such latent talents.”

Lee Jia hadn’t expected this. Magus Hwang had always been affable, but she hadn’t spoken to him outside of a classroom since the first day she’d arrived. She hadn’t really expected Magus Hwang to give her a second thought after that.

After a few more pleasantries, Magus Hwang listened as they described their elemental balance concerns and the issue of their available manuals being written with the wrong soul alignment in mind. Hwang listened intently, stroking his chin and nodding along. When they finished he hummed thoughtfully.

“Hmm, that’s certainly a conundrum. However, while Qin Zhao’s expertise in cultivation is unquestionable, his knowledge on certain scholarly matters is clearly incomplete. He’s only half-right on a number of issues. Firstly, I rather doubt that Miss An has much to worry about regarding soul alignment.”

Jia and Eui exchanged a curious look. Eui pursed her lips before questioning Hwang.

“What do you mean by that? It’s based on gender, right? What makes you think I have one of those rare flipped alignments, and why me specifically?”

Hwang nodded amiably and chuckled.

“Hoho, yes indeed. Yet, not quite. The soul is a reflection of the body, and vice versa. It is possible to influence one through the other. Your magical scar Miss An—a terrible practice which I have always been vocal against—is a formation branded on your soul. As such, it cannot be removed by physical healing. Likewise, permanent alterations to the body can influence the soul as well—the rites Elder Qin mentioned, for example.”

Jia and Eui both shuddered as they recalled how casually Elder Qin had suggested physical mutilation. Hwang smiled ruefully and shook his head.

“Try not to hold it against him. Sacrificing the body in favor of the soul is very much the doctrine of imperial cultivators. In any case, while gender is one indicator of soul alignment, it’s hardly the only one. One is simply being a half-spirit. Due to our altered physiology, there is a much higher instance of non-standard alignments among our population.

“Another indicator is elemental affinity. This one is almost entirely overlooked by Qin’s scholars, so I can forgive Elder Qin for missing it. It’s exceedingly rare for elemental affinity and soul alignment to match. While I can’t calculate the exact likelihood, not a single subject has ever been recorded as having the same yin-yang alignment in both body and soul.

“With all this in mind, I would wager a small fortune that Miss An’s soul alignment is Yang, in opposition to the Yin-aligned destruction elemental affinity. It could be neutral, of course, but Qin was correct about the rarity of such cases, so smart money is still on Yang.”

As usual, Hwang was long-winded but extremely informative. If he was correct, then this was excellent news for Eui, although—

“Wait a minute, how do you know all this!?”

The elderly magus laughed heartily at Lee Jia’s outburst.

“Ohoho, through research, of course! Did you think we went into this endeavor blind? Decades of research, negotiations, treaties, and trades went into this place’s conception. Ienaga and Qin are here for political reasons, but for me this is my life’s work! You needn’t take my word for it, however. There’s a simple way to check.”

Magus Hwang rummaged through his horrible mess of a lab, tossing expensive looking artifacts around like garbage and muttering to himself.

“—know it’s around here somewhere...aha!”

Hwang returned with a small engraved rod of jade that reminded Lee Jia of the attunement rod they had used to discover their elemental affinities. He held it out towards Lee Jia.

“Don’t take it from my hands, try to direct some qi into it, please.”

Lee Jia regarded the artifact curiously, but did as asked. As she did, the rod grew colder in her hands.

“Cold! That would be Yin-aligned qi. Next…”

Hwang held out the rod toward Eui. Lee Jia watched Eui take the other end of the rod and concentrate for a moment. Nothing seemed to happen, but then Hwang’s face lit up and he announced the result.

“Warm! As I suspected Miss An, your soul is Yang-aligned. There’s still the matter of elemental balance, but you can rest assured that you will be able to practice your available manuals unaltered.”

“Uh, that’s great but what about Jia? We still need to alter the manuals for her, right?”

Hwang scratched at his chin for a moment then shrugged.

“Yes and no. We will have to examine the techniques to determine their elements and how their manifestations will be affected by the change in alignment. Any changes necessary—if any even are—will be impossible to predict without taking a look first. I trust you brought the manuals with you?”

Lee Jia nodded and summoned the jade slips from her ring onto a relatively clearish spot on the table.

“Oho! A dimensional storage artifact, eh? You really have been thriving here. Even I don’t have one of those—best be discreet about it. Now then, let’s take a look at these manuals.”

Hwang rubbed his hands together greedily, like a hungry man sitting down to a feast. Lee Jia had a look of concern on her face as he picked up the first manual.

“They’re kind of hard to read without qi. A lot of it is sort of feeling-based.”

Magus Hwang chuckled at her concerns.

“Hoho, not to worry. I’ve dealt with these before. I worked directly with that damn politician Do Hye to create a technique for transcribing them. That shouldn't be necessary here, though. The parts that I need should be entirely comprehensible.”

Lee Jia watched anxiously as he read each jade slip in turn before setting the last one down and frowning in consternation.

“Well, well. This is quite the conundrum indeed!”
