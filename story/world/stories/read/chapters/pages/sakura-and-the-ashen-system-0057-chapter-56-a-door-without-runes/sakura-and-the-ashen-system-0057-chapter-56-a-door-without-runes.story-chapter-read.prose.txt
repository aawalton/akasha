The corridor of the intake block was narrow and poorly lit. Lines of runes stretched along the floor, as before, completely suppressing any natural magic and sustaining only artificial magic, the kind necessary within these walls to maintain order. Gilbert’s neighbor was either still running through the prison like a rat in a maze, or he had already been caught. The mage did not know that yet, but he did know that the noise the big man had drawn to himself was very much to his advantage.

Gilbert lowered his head, raised his collar higher, and went left.

The mage remembered that the old runic warden was supposed to be at the passage between the block and the corridor to the infirmary. If he had been replaced, the plan would become worse and force him to improvise. Then again, the mage had already gotten used to improvising during his imprisonment.

Gilbert reached the first turn when a young servant boy with an empty bowl in his hands rushed out of a side passage, and they almost collided. The servant boy raised his eyes, saw the guard uniform, and immediately stepped back.

"There... there’s a prisoner..."

"I know," Gilbert said in another man’s low voice.

The mage did not even stop. Sometimes the main thing about a uniform was not how it fit, but the fact that people did not want to argue with someone who looked busy and important.

Around the next turn, the noise grew louder because of the shouting and because someone was demanding that the passage be closed. Gilbert heard the phrase "that animal ran to the stairs!" It seemed his neighbor had chosen the most noticeable route possible.

Gilbert once again confirmed that when they were handing out brains, this man had stood last in line. But right now, his stupidity was still absurdly useful. In the chaos, few people paid attention to Gilbert. And that was exactly what he needed. To be unnoticed. To blend into the crowd. To make sure no one wanted to check his name through the System.

Then Gilbert finally saw the old runic warden. He stood by the service door leading out of the intake block and farther toward the infirmary corridors. Short, dry, with the irritated face of a man whose shift had been ruined yet again. A ring of keys hung at his belt, and among them was a white bone key used to work with runes. No one stood beside him. Gilbert approached the old man with an ordinary step, like a guard sent to clarify an order.

"What is it?" the warden asked irritably, not even looking at him right away. "Another fight?"

"Escapee," Gilbert said.

"I heard that. Where did he run?"

Gilbert pointed toward the side corridor.

"That way. To the stairs."

The warden sharply turned his head, and that one moment was enough. Gilbert stepped in close, grabbed him by the collar with his left hand, and pressed the service knife under his ribs with his right, to the place where cloth could not protect the body from a blade. The old man drew a sharp breath, but Gilbert had already pushed him back into the shadow between the door and the wall.

"Quiet," Gilbert said.

The warden went pale. He recognized him. First confusion flashed in his eyes, then horror and anger at himself. The old man had seen him in a prison shirt, hunched, bloodied, and weak. But in another man’s uniform, with a confident step and a straight back, Gilbert was very different from the prisoner the warden had managed to remember.

"You..."

Gilbert pressed the knife a little harder.

"Quiet."

The warden fell silent. Gilbert lowered his gaze and noticed that a thin cord with a small bone pendant had slipped out from under the old man’s collar. Two roughly carved children’s figures, worn smooth by frequent touch. A very personal thing. Now that was interesting.

Gilbert had already seen how the old man touched it when he thought no one was watching. While the mage had been studying him, he had already understood that the runic warden did his job lazily, did not double-check locks, grimaced at every extra shout, and counted the minutes until the end of his shift. He was tired of his service.

Men like him did not want to die. They wanted to finish their service, leave this place, and sit somewhere by a warm stove while grandchildren pulled at their sleeve and demanded a story.

"You’re tired of this place," Gilbert said quietly. "And too old to die for someone else’s prison."

The old man snorted softly.

"You will open the doors I tell you to open," Gilbert said very calmly. "One extra sound, one attempt to warn anyone, and you won’t have time to regret it."

The warden said nothing.

"Do as I say, and tonight you will still be the kind old man whose grandchildren run to him."

Gilbert leaned slightly closer.

"But if you try to warn someone, they’ll be waiting for you for nothing."

The old man swallowed.

Gilbert felt his own hand tremble slightly because of the side effects of the core. His body had been working on borrowed time for too long. He pressed his elbow to his side to make the trembling less noticeable. The warden noticed it anyway. That was bad. But because of the knife, the old man was in no hurry to do anything stupid. So for now, everything was under control.

"You won’t get out," the runic mage whispered. "They’ll seal every passage."

"Then we won’t waste time. Lower level," Gilbert said. "Young valuable prisoner. Lir."

The warden’s eyes flickered in a way that immediately told Gilbert the old man knew who he meant.

The mage leaned a little closer.

"Lead."

The old man did not move at once. Gilbert did not repeat the order. He only changed the angle of the knife slightly, and the warden understood everything without further words. Slowly, with the wooden caution of a man who did not want to accidentally breathe too deeply, the old man turned toward the service door. They started walking.

"And while we’re taking this walk, tell me where the emergency passage is," Gilbert ordered.

The warden froze.

"What?"

"The nearest emergency passage without runes. Where is it?"

The old man was silent for a little while.

"There are no such passages."

Gilbert did not even smile.

"There are."

"There are no exits without runes in the Castle of Justice," the warden insisted.

"At the main entrances and passages, yes," Gilbert said quietly. "But an emergency passage would not be runic. If a fire starts, or an earthquake, if something happens to the runic mages, there has to be another way out. So everyone doesn’t die in this concrete box."

The warden said nothing. Gilbert nudged him forward slightly so they would not stand still for too long. A stopped guard with a runic mage near a service door looked suspicious. A walking guard and runic mage speaking quietly looked like part of the local order.

"Where is it?" Gilbert repeated.

The old man breathed shallowly and very carefully. The knife was still hidden under the edge of the borrowed cloak, but the warden had already noticed the trembling in Gilbert’s hand. Maybe he was waiting for Gilbert’s weakness to become stronger than Gilbert’s knife. But that had not happened yet, so he had to play by Gilbert’s rules.

"Behind the healer’s utility room," he finally said. "Downstairs. Old fire exit. Leads to the inner service yard. The lock there is mechanical. Iron wheel and bolt. The door is heavy. And it hasn’t been opened in a long time."

"Opens from inside?"

"It should."

"I don’t like that word."

"And I don’t like having a knife poked under my ribs."

Gilbert pressed the blade a little harder.

"We’re both having a difficult day. Where’s the key?"

"No key is needed if the bolt isn’t stuck. The wheel opens it from inside."

Gilbert thought. That sounded too good, good enough that he had to prepare for a catch. If the lock was more complicated after all, if the runic mage had lied, or if the wheel was completely stuck, then he would have to return to the runic passages. Which meant returning to the runic mage. And that was exactly what he wanted to avoid. Gilbert’s plan should not depend on one spiteful old man.

"Fine. How many runic doors to Lir?"

"Two."

"The first is this one?"

The warden gave a short nod toward the service door.

"Yes. The second is before the lower block."

"You open both. Then you lead me to the fire exit."

The old man said nothing, only raised the bone key to the door. Gilbert watched closely. Thin pale-blue lines appeared in the air beside the lock, and the air by the door trembled. The old man traced a short curve with the key, then a second one, very small. A dry click sounded inside the mechanism, and the door opened.

Gilbert pushed the warden forward and followed him in. Beyond the door was a service corridor. It was no longer as noisy as the intake block, but it was not empty. Somewhere ahead, footsteps thudded hollowly, someone cursed behind a wall, and shouts about the escaped prisoner still carried from behind. Runic lines ran here along the lower part of the wall and the corners of the floor.

The core under his skin suddenly jerked again, and pain ran from his collarbone to his ribs, then struck sharply into his temple. For a moment, the world became too narrow, as if the corridor had squeezed around him. Gilbert did not stop, but his step faltered.

The warden felt it immediately and suddenly lunged. He desperately slammed his shoulder into Gilbert’s side, twisted out from under the knife, and rushed toward the turn, already drawing breath to shout.

"Guar..."

Gilbert was half a step faster. He seized the old man by the collar, jerked him back, and slammed his back into the wall. The warden wheezed. Gilbert pressed a hand over his mouth and leaned his full weight onto him, though his own body immediately answered with a fresh flash of pain. This time, his vision darkened more sharply.

Someone stopped around the turn.

"Hey?" a voice called. "What’s there?"

The warden froze under Gilbert’s hand. His eyes were wide open, and there was not only stubbornness in them now, but real fear.

Gilbert forced himself to straighten. His voice had to sound natural, without a tremor.

"The old man tripped," he threw toward the turn in another man’s low voice. "Go to the infirmary. Every extra pair of hands is needed there right now."

A pause.

"I was ordered to hold the passage."

"Then hold it from that side and don’t stand in the middle of the corridor," the mage answered with confident irritation.

The person around the turn cursed quietly.

"Understood."

The footsteps moved away. Gilbert waited a few more seconds and only then removed his hand from the warden’s mouth, but he moved the knife from under the ribs to the throat.

"That was your last attempt. After the next one, your grandchildren will be left without a grandfather. I swear."

The old man breathed heavily, hoarsely, with hatred, but said nothing.

"Walk." Gilbert pushed him in the shoulder, changing their position.

This time, the old man walked at once. The corridor continued along the utility rooms of the intake block: narrow storerooms and doors behind which it smelled of wet rags and cheap oil. By one wall, a low door to a small storeroom stood slightly open. Gilbert noticed shelves inside with old rags, empty vials, and an oil lamp on a hook.

He stopped. The warden stopped too, but no longer risked turning around sharply.

"What now?"

Gilbert looked at the storeroom, then at the corridor ahead. He knew the neighbor had either already been caught or was about to be caught. One stupid big man could not run around the prison for hours. So they needed something else that would raise a commotion here. Besides, the place was excellent, not too close to the emergency exit without runes that Gilbert was keeping as Plan B.

"Inside," he said.

"Why?"

"We’ll give them more work."

The old man went even paler.

"That is a utility storeroom..."

"Then there’s nothing valuable."

Gilbert pushed him inside. The storeroom was cramped, and the warden nearly hit his shoulder on a shelf. Gilbert took the lamp off the hook, tore the cover from a basket of old rags, and overturned the lamp into it. Oil splashed over the cloth. For several moments, nothing happened. Then a thin orange tongue ran along the edge of a rag.

"You’ll burn the block down," the old man breathed in panic.

"Don’t worry. Stone burns badly."

Gilbert waited until the fire caught on the oiled cloth, then pushed the basket deeper under the shelf with his foot, but not too far. He did not need a large fire. Only smoke and panic.

He left the storeroom door slightly open, and the first gray streams were already crawling out toward the intake block.

They did not wait for people to run here. Instead, Gilbert and the runic mage continued on, passed the infirmary, and turned toward the lower passage. The second runic door was heavier, larger, and more imposing than the first.

Behind them, people were already shouting:

"Smoke!"

"Smoke’s coming from the utility room!"

Gilbert nudged the warden toward the door.

"Open it."

The old man raised the bone key. His hand was trembling.

"Faster," Gilbert repeated.

The warden exhaled angrily and brought the key to the lock. Pale-blue lines appeared in the air again. The rune formed slowly and carefully. The old man worked well even when he was afraid.

Behind them, the shouts grew louder.

"Water here!"

"Who left the lamp?!"

"Why is it smoking so much?!"

The second runic door on the way to Lir finally opened, and the cold of the lower level pulled from inside. Gilbert pushed the old man in and followed him. The lower corridor was damper and darker. Ordinary iron cell doors stood along one side. Like the other cells, they had mechanical locks and viewing slits. No runes on every door, because there was simply no need for that. One still had to get here first.

At the far end of the corridor stood a guard. He turned at the sound of the opening door and frowned.

"What’s going on?"

Gilbert did not let the warden answer.

"Smoke in the utility corridor," he said. "Get water upstairs. Quickly."

The guard looked skeptically at Gilbert and the runic mage, but he had already noticed the gray strip of smoke seeping through the open passage.

"This isn’t my post. The upper guards are supposed to deal with that."

"The upper guards are sealing the intake block right now," Gilbert answered with a confident, grim look. "And the smoke is coming here."

"I’m ordered to hold the lower corridor."

"Yes. Except if the smoke gets down into the cells, there won’t be anything left to hold. Then you can explain yourself why a valuable prisoner inhaled smoke while you stood there waiting."

The guard frowned.

"And you?"

"I’m checking the lower passage with the runic mage and staying here. You need to reach the nearest people, get water, and bring two more."

The guard looked at the smoke again and cursed.

"Fine."

The guard jerked his shoulder angrily and ran toward the passage. Gilbert knew people liked arguing with orders, but not with the chance of becoming guilty. The mage waited until the footsteps moved away, then turned his head toward the warden.

"Now to Lir."

The old man silently nodded toward the third cell from the end. Gilbert brought him closer and first removed the borrowed guard’s key ring from his belt. The keys jingled quietly in his fingers. He quickly found the most suitable one: a long, dark key with a square head. It looked as if it should fit this cell’s lock. Only it turned out too thick for the keyhole. Gilbert began sorting through the keys, trying to unlock the door. Metal scraped against metal, but not one key fit properly.

The warden stayed silent. Gilbert slowly turned his head toward him.

"Talk."

The old man licked his dry lips.

"Valuable prisoners have separate keys."

"Where?"

"With the shift chief. Ordinary guards and people like me don’t have them."

Gilbert looked at the key ring on the warden’s belt.

"We’ll check."

The old man did not argue. Gilbert took his keys and quickly went through them one by one, but again none of them fit.

Somewhere outside, coughing could be heard, and smoke crept along the corridor in a thin gray strip.

Something like hope flickered across the old man’s face, but Gilbert pinned him with a look.

"Don’t be happy."

He pushed the warden toward the wall.

"Face the stone. Hands higher."

The old man froze.

"You won’t open the cell without a key. Your plan is over."

Gilbert did not even waste strength on an answer. Instead, he pressed the old man against the wall with his shoulder and closed his eyes for one second to ride out a new wave of pain that rose under his skin and squeezed his chest from the inside.

Then he lowered his hand to his left forearm. Under the sleeve, just above the wrist, was a thin scar. One of those marks no one paid attention to during a quick inspection. Prisoners in the Castle had their clothes changed and were searched by hand, but they did not cut open every old mark on the skin. And that was their mistake.

Gilbert hooked the edge of the scar with the tip of the service knife. The pain was sharp and unpleasant, but nothing compared to everything he had already managed to feel during his time in the dungeon. He clenched his teeth and pressed with his fingers from both sides.

A thin dark edge appeared under the skin. The warden sharply turned his head. Gilbert immediately pressed the knife to his throat.

"Look at the wall."

The old man turned away. Gilbert pulled out the first lockpick. Then the second. They were thin, almost flat, dark with dried blood and too small to draw attention even in good light. Hiding metal under the skin was not the best idea, but bad ideas sometimes saved lives. Gilbert wiped the lockpicks on the edge of the borrowed cloak and crouched in front of the lock.

"Keep your hands on the wall," he told the warden. "If I hear movement, I’ll break your fingers. All of them. One by one."

Gilbert inserted the first lockpick into the lock. The mechanism was heavy, old, and crude. The mage worked quickly, but without sudden movements. A sudden move could ruin the whole attempt.

Gilbert had learned to pick locks as a child. Back then, it had not been entertainment, but a way to get what no one intended to give him: food from storerooms or things from locked boxes. At first, the locks had been simple, then they became more complicated. That ability had eventually become one of the most useful in his life.

Behind him, footsteps, shouts, and commotion were still audible. People were clearing up the aftermath of the small fire, and at any moment one of the guards could enter this corridor. But Gilbert did not take his eyes off the lock.

The first pin yielded to him with a quiet, dry click. Then Gilbert managed to coax the second. But the third was stuck. Gilbert exhaled through his nose and slightly changed the angle. His hand was trembling because of the pulsing foreign core. Gilbert pressed his elbow to his side, as before, forcing his fingers to obey. A little more. A little more.

The third pin finally shifted, and behind his back, the warden whispered barely audibly:

"You won’t make it."

"Be quiet."

The fourth clicked too, while the fifth resisted the longest. Gilbert could already hear footsteps somewhere above, and they were gradually approaching.

Finally, something inside the lock turned with a dull sound. Gilbert turned the lockpick together with the tensioner. The lock opened.

He hid the tools in his fist, stood, and pushed the door with his shoulder.
