Having Tate’s voice in Lexie's head grounded her, beating back some of the panic.

Though the voice wasn't really in her head.

More like...her soul. It was the same place Yasycht talked to her from.

Tate? she asked, to confirm that this wasn’t yet another trick from Mikhail. He didn't seem to be aware of what was going on, now looking at her father smugly, like he’d gotten away with something. And in a way, he had. After all, he’d somehow tricked Aiden into signing a contract with him, binding them forever.

“The contract isn’t with you,” Aiden said. “It’s with the golem. If your souls are different, I fail to see how you're now connected to me.”

“Our souls split a long time ago,” he said. “And I left him with Neqal to test the true limits of chaos on the human body. After he was brought into this world, he used his unique features to grow his powers and those of those around him. He became his own man. Yet I’ve always had a, shall we say, remote access point.” He said, “There are triggers I can switch to enable him to become a puppet again. There are things that I have made him do that he himself does not understand. This manifests as madness on his own end, a drive that he doesn’t fully comprehend. But he's come to accept that madness as a part of him, the same way I would have if I were him."

"Looks like you already have," Aiden murmured, which Lexie had to agree to. All of this was insane.

He sipped his tea. "He has served me well over the years in accumulating knowledge. However, if his use to me has come to an end, I can simply end him and make sure that he ends your father too. You wouldn’t want that, right?”

“You would have to sign a contract with him first, wouldn’t you?" Aiden argued. "And that wouldn’t be a direct contract with me, as I have not agreed to the terms. Our lives aren't linked indefinitely, and I don't die if he dies, only if he dies at the hands of someone in my party, like my daughter, my friends, or a few choice heroes. If you harm him, that doesn't reflect on me."

“Do you really think I can’t find a loophole?”

As they spoke, Lexie tuned it out while listening to Tate in her soul.

I used the card you made for me to find you through the entry point of the temple. You'll be able to make it out if you go to the entry point.

What’s the entry point? She didn’t know if he could hear her, but he continued by saying, The entry point is the mug on the table. Pick it up, and it will show you a way out.

She glanced at it. The mug was currently in Mikhail's hand, and he cradled it as he spoke to her father. She needed to take it from him.

Tate kept repeating himself, which told him that he didn't necessarily hear Lexie when she was speaking to him in her mind, but he simply hoped that she heard him.

She needed to learn how to communicate with her soul, but first, she needed to get them out of here.

“I understand your frustration, Archmage,” Mikhail was saying to Aiden. “But I assure you that we’re on the same page here, and this will be so much better if we could just do this the easy way.”

“Unfortunately, you prevented us from doing things the easy way when you trapped us in this hell and let your animals loose."

With that, while Mikhail was about to respond to Aiden's statement, Lexie lunged at him and touched the mug.

She immediately heard the gongs start sounding in her head, louder and louder, clamoring. The room gave way to darkness even as Mikhail tried to wrestle the mug back, laughing while he did it, and saying, “You finally figured it out, huh? Did you realize who you are now?"

Lexie didn't listen to him. He was surprisingly stronger than he looked, but she held on. The room was coming apart, stretching at the seams.

In the midst of the darkness, she heard voices. Conversation.

“Is it working?’

“Just give me a minute instead of asking me that every five seconds.”

Tate and Torin. Relief flooded Lexie’s system. They were there. She wanted to go to them, but she didn't know how to steer it. Mikhail wasn't just fighting for the mug back; he was fighting to reestablish his domain by stitching it back together, while Lexie fought to blow it apart.

She closed her eyes and inhaled, reaching for Neqal. With the domain coming apart, she could just about feel him.

She just needed a little bit, just needed a little bit more to get to him...

She felt the hints of something, and then she reached out to her dad. "Dad!"

Luckily, he understood and grabbed her hand.

They both vanished and reappeared in reality.

“Oh, thank God.” Tate bolted to his feet and, very uncharacteristically, ran and gave Aiden a big hug. Aiden returned it, patting him on the back, and by the time Tate turned to Lexie, he’d gotten himself back enough to not just jump into the hug. But Lexie hugged him anyway because she was happy to see him.

Then she let go and said, “We need to go.”

“Where?” Torin asked. “What happened?”

“I think he's unleashed Saint-0 already. We need to figure out where and stop it.”

“Okay.”

“Dad, you go to Indigo first and take care of your golem. Torin, you need to go to your family and protect them in case they’re a target. Tate, go back to Hovelton. I’ll go to the association.”

They nodded, and after distributing the teleportation orbs, Lexie disappeared.

And appeared in a ghost town.

She gasped as she took in the aftermath of a massacre, albeit an odd one.

Bodies were strewn on the ground, unconscious. Not bleeding but not obviously alive either. Their eyes were blank, mouths slack, their bodies arranged like puppets cut off a string. Like vacant vessels without a soul. Lexie’s stomach tightened, and she felt nauseous as she observed them, dozens strewn outside the hero's association.

Reporters mostly, their PHORBs still buzzing in the air.

A few civilians.

More than a few heroes.

God. What happened?

Internally, she knew what had happened, but it was too much for her brain to comprehend.

She felt frozen, overtaken by the violent need to retch. She almost went home, but she didn't let herself.

She had to see more, figure out if there was anything left to salvage.

If the culprit was still here.

She swallowed her bile as she picked her feet over them, turning to analyze the depth of the damage. It was the most eerie thing she’d ever seen.

What got to Lexie was the distinct lack of violence in the atmosphere. She was used to scenes like this, in the dungeon, but there was always more violence, torn limbs, bullet-riddled bodies.

She'd expected, once Saint-0 activated, that there would be bombs and brutality and that the city would burn.

She wasn't expecting quiet devastation.

She didn’t expect that people would just be gone, their souls snuffed out without even giving them a chance to fight back.

That was just...awful.

She walked into the hero’s association headquarters, seeing more and more bodies on the way in. Some were heroes she recognized. Even those she didn't, seeing them in this state made her sick to her stomach. The horror slammed into her like an avalanche, drowning her in a wave of guilt.

It was all her fault.

She should have done something. Warned people earlier. Told the association even if they wouldn't have believed her.

But would that have made things better or worse?

It was hard to tell right now. Her brain could not come up with a single sure way to avoid this scenario.

Probably why Mikhail chose it.

She finally came upon a body that was different from the rest. She recognized Kya Wavelost, lying on the floor, bleeding from a hole that had been pierced into the middle of her forehead. Her face was frozen in a horrified expression of shock, anger, and indignation that she, of all people, could be killed.

There was one more like her, a man Lexie didn’t know, and then the rest were simply soulless bodies.

It appeared that when the soul of Saint-0 jumped around, it left a husk in its wake.

Lexie needed to find it and put a stop to its reign of terror.

She enhanced her senses with [All Eyes on Me] and listened for any noise. She thought she had picked up something on the top floor and headed in that direction, staying alert so it didn't get the jump on her.

She was terrified and human enough to admit it.

Yet, there was some comfort in knowing that she had a sturdier soul than most and that Mikhail didn't want her dead.

He simply wanted her to be his Chosen.

Yet she kept asking the question….why her? Why had he chosen her to carry out his evil plans? Was it simply because she was powerful? Because of her unique build. Why not take someone from another world, train them until they were as powerful as Lexie, and then use them against the Fae?

Or create an army of Chosen to stand against the Fae. Why did it need to be a death game? Why couldn’t they have worked together?

Simply because it made Janus happy for them to battle each other to win?

The sound of breathing was coming from a room on the second floor. She slowly pushed the door open and was anticipating an attack. She girded herself even though she had no true idea of what she would face.

The room was empty and bodyless. She was tempted to think that nothing was here, but the sound of breathing told her otherwise.

She monitored some more and called out, “Hello?”

There was no response to start with. Then a familiar voice said, "Lexie."

She instantly recognized it. “Luther.”

He emerged from a coat closet. He looked haggard, his eyes wide, his hair messed up, and his skin giving off a definite heat.

He looked so much like Torin that she did a double-take.

But as he peered at her, he asked hesitantly, “Are you…Are you that thing...of course not, you wouldn’t be talking to me if you were.”

Lexie watched him, trying to decide if he was a carrier or not.

“Did it enter you?"

“No. It entered Jerry, and then he ran somewhere, and I couldn’t follow him."

"Why are you hiding? You should be stopping it?"

"Stopping?" He gave her an incredulous, crazed laugh. "I barely stopped it from taking my life. I’ve called the Fae. They’re coming.”

"Coming here?" She spread her arms wide, her voice breaking, feeling on the verge. "Everyone’s already dead."

“This isn’t the only place affected. There might be more places we can save.”

Just then, Lexie’s phone started to ring. She opened it and saw it was Tate. She steeled herself before she answered.

“You should come here.”

“How bad is it?” she asked.

“It’s Max.”

Her ears rang.

She couldn't breathe for what felt like eternity.

She didn’t even know she screamed and teleported right outside of Max’s home. She saw him lying on the floor, his eyes unseeing.

“No.” She shook her head, and she grabbed him by the shoulders, shaking him, "No! Please! Max!’

She was keenly aware of Tate’s presence as she wailed and screamed and felt the clouds shifting over her. The village sounded eerily silent, no one running out to investigate why she was shouting. Hovelton was quiet on the regular, but this was different. This was a silence that spoke of death and decay.

It didn’t take a genius to learn that everyone was gone.

“What do we do?" Tate asked quietly.

She didn't know what to say, too busy crying.

This never should have happened.

In the blink of an eye, everyone was gone.

In the blink of an eye, her entire world had shifted, and it was all her fault. No.

It was all his fault. Mikhail.

He had eroded the trust between her and the heroes.

He'd gotten rid of Vacek, the only man who could stop this, even though he was a Chosen too.

He'd used Vulcan and Lexie and all the Chosen for his nefarious plans just to get the world where he wanted it to be, under his regime.

She wouldn't let him get away with this. Whatever happened, she wouldn't let him have his way.

But she had to go to the Tower and turn this whole thing back. She had to set things right.

“Tate,” she said, her voice tight from the tears, “What do you know about the Dark Tower?”
