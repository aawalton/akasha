After dinner, Ferris locked himself and Jade in his room, eager to return to the slimespace.

The slime had been well behaved at the meal, and Ferris was growing more confident they would follow instructions.

The burn on his hand was starting to ache, so he carefully unwrapped it to add more of the salve that Foreman Carter had given him.

The sight of his blistering skin made Ferris stop and think. I could have lost my hand today, he thought. Why am I so quick to trust carrying them around so soon?

Before dinner, his concerns over safety had started to feel odd. But shouldn’t it be the other way around? Shouldn’t his trust feel odd?

Ferris looked down at the slime. They were sitting in his lap, contentedly vibrating with a slow ripple. Why didn’t he immediately tie them back up to the dresser, so that he could focus on his hand? Foreman Carter would’ve not been happy.

Continuing that line of thought, had it truly been safe to take Jade to dinner without a hand on the leash? It’d made sense at the time, but in retrospect he questioned his actions. He hadn’t liked the idea that the chain could be used against Jade, but wasn’t that half of the reason he had it?

The primer on familiars clearly stated the bond would prevent the familiar from harming him. Ferris believed it. I still believe it, he realized, even sitting here taking care of my injuried hand, I know Jade would never hurt me.

Why had the slime hurt him? Ferris wanted to just ask, but he wasn’t sure Jade would understand. He tried to condense the question, focusing on the mental images.

“Why eat my hand?” he asked, instinctively patting the slime’s head with his good hand.

“No eat you!” Jade responded, swirling a bit in confusion. The slime’s emotions were laced with aversion and regret.

“Earlier, you ate hand.” Ferris tried again, thinking of the pain. The memory of placing his hand on the slime, only to see it engulfed into their body.

The thought seemed to trigger something, as Jade’s swirls intensified. Some unhappy bubbles appeared, getting spun around inside the slime’s body.

“Was eating not you, then was eating you.” Jade responded slowly; thoughts mired in confusion.

Ferris could feel some of the slime’s feeling. Perhaps… they hadn’t known that Ferris’ hand was part of him? As he was learning, slimes saw the world differently. They relied heavily on touch but had no way to see. How would a slime know that the hand was connected to Ferris? Once Jade understood, the bond prevented them from hurting him.

Perhaps that explained why Jade had hurt him, but not Ferris’ lack of common-sense precautions since then.

The primer on familiar bonds had stated that a familiar would not hurt the cultivator that bonded them, but in retrospect that was a scary power to claim.

If the bond had such power over Jade, did it… go both ways?

With a chill, Ferris remembered reading about delvers that couldn’t send their familiars into battle. It had sounded like a silly problem to him at the time, but what if there was more too it? What if the bond was influencing them, making them irrationally protect their familiar?

Thinking about it, Ferris felt the same instinctual aversion to hurting Jade as he would to putting his hand in fire. He’d assumed it was natural, like knowing not to random attack another person. If Jade died, Ferris might die as well, so it was logical to avoid.

But this feeling… it was more. He was being manipulated.

Ferris felt sick to his stomach. If he couldn’t trust his own mind, what could he trust?

If it was true, what could he even do about it? Destroy the bond?

A wave of revulsion passed over him. While he didn’t know which parts of the feeling were himself, or influenced by the bond, he knew he hated the idea. He’d wanted the bond his entire life. It was his path to being a delver. He could at least trust that.

If he was stuck with it, how could he control it? He was having trouble even noticing it.

The effect was subtle and pervasive; he wouldn’t have suspected anything if his actions hadn’t gone directly against his resolution to be careful. Ferris could’ve easily self-justified his protective attitude, as hurting his bonded core could actually kill him. Without clear evidence that the slime could and would hurt him—and then noticing he ignored the experience—he may never have questioned if his own trust in Jade.

The soulspace was not just a metaphor, but a spiritual connection. Bringing the slime’s core into it gave access on a deep level. It must be possible to influence him through the soul.

Ferris frowned. Would the oath statue from his contract to Hollowfield have a similar effect? Uncle Winton had claimed it would only hurt him if he broke the contract, but what if it subtly changed him? Would he unconsciously start to grow loyal to Hollowfield, against his will? Ten years was a long time for those chains to dig deep.

The core and the statue were already inside Ferris’ soulspace, but that didn’t mean he was completely vulnerable. He already had to protect himself from demons, maybe he could find a way to protect his mind.

There’s really only one solution, he thought. Work on my cultivation, until I can build something in my soul to counter the influence.

Resolved, Ferris wanted to get back to work. He reattached Jade’s chain to the dresser and laid down on the floor. The slime bubbled a bit but went along with it. Meditation wasn’t easy, with new fears swirling around his head, but eventually Ferris found himself back in his soulspace.

While eating, he had decided on three goals he hoped to accomplish that night, beyond his nightly cultivation routine. He may lose some sleep, but bonding the core opened so many possibilities… and with those possibilities came work.

First, harvest the fruits of his Core Tree. The abilities they provide would be random and take a long time to develop, but he could find out what he had available and begin planning around them.

Second, investigate the Consume Pool and Digest River abilities. See if he could find a way to harvest the qi chunks.

Third, see if he could determine the nature of the Adaptation ability. He couldn’t plan out how to use it until he knew how it worked.

While he worked, he would consider ways to protect his mind. It would likely be a long-term project, beginning with trying to recognize the influence when it happened. The book on familiars had implied that delvers could get over an aversion to putting their familiars in danger with mental exercises, perhaps he could start there.

Moving from his soulspace over to the slimespace, Ferris found himself once again on top of the first tower, next to the pedestal and Heartfire. Thankfully, Jade had waited for him this time. It was a short trip over to the tower holding the Core Tree.

As soon as they approached the Core Tree, Jade attempted to reach the fruit. Panicking a little, he called the slime back.

“No eat fruit,” he scolded Jade, trying to convey disappointment.

“Fruit delicious! Must eat!” Jade obediently stopped, but their words did not inspire confidence.

Ferris looked at the fruit, hanging from the tree branches. How had the slime even known the fruit was there without eyes? Jade’s descriptions of the strange sense were as confusing to Ferris as his explanations of sight had been to the slime.

Did the slime have special awareness inside the slimespace? What would happen if Jade ate one of the fruits? Ferris wanted to experiment, but there would be time later. For now, he wanted to secure the three seeds.

Carefully, Ferris inspected the fruit. Each one looked unique, as if they had grown on different trees. Picking them too soon would be a waste, but he wasn’t sure how to tell if they were ready. Uncle Winton told him a mature fruit would visibly change, gaining color and details, but he’d expected to watch the process from a bud.

Two of them were bright and colorful, while one was smaller and more muted. Ferris guessed that it was still growing, while the first two were mature. It would hurt to make a mistake, but the personal nature of cultivation meant there were never guarantees. Even the most common truths could turn out wrong for some. He would have to learn as he went, balancing experimentation with whatever advice he could get.

Ferris reached up and grasped one of the colorful fruits. The bright red skin felt firm, with a velvety fuzz. At the barest touch it broke free from the branch and fell into his hand.

The seed of this fruit could be planted either in his soulspace or here in the slimespace. With enough time, qi, and precious resources the seed would grow into a new ability. This ability could be anything: breathing fire, enhanced muscles, better eyesight. It could allow him to breath underwater like his uncle or create the binding contract like Elder Harper. Some abilities were more internal and only affected his body or the soulspace, but others could use a meridian to affect the real world. Eventually, Ferris hoped this tree would provide the seed for Acid Reconstruction, regrowing his leg.

Unfortunately, the type of seed the tree provided would be random. It could be influenced—Hollowfield had manuals to help find known abilities—but there would always be a large amount of chance. Bonded weapons usually provided abilities related to their shape, and the environment of the soulspace could unlock new types of ability seeds to grow. The slime’s Adaptation ability should also help Ferris here, once he figured out how it worked.

While the seed had to be planted, the flesh of the fruit could be eaten to learn what ability would grow, and what resources it would require. Closing his eyes, Ferris took a bite out of the fruit.

The experience was odd. The fruit was sweet and juicy, but the way knowledge flowing into his head as he chewed was like nothing he had ever felt. Information appeared in his head: the seed would create the Grow ability, letting a slime grow many times larger than before.

Ferris suddenly felt a wave of unhappy emotions from Jade over the bond. There were no words, just intensity of anger from the slime.

Jade started climbing up Ferris’ leg, bubbling intently as they tried to reach the half-eaten fruit in his hand. Thankfully his familiar seemed to be having trouble climbing without encompassing his leg in acid.

“Stop! Stop!” Ferris yelled, both aloud and in his head. He started dancing around, trying to keep the rest of the fruit from the slime. It was impossible to get a grip on their slimy body as it flowed around his hand any time he tried, but Jade was having trouble holding on as he swung about.

Eventually, with a less than graceful twist, Ferris flung Jade off before they could reach the fruit.

“No fair! Greedy eat fruit!” Jade bubbled unhappily on the ground, but thankfully didn’t try to climb up him again.

Ferris sighed. How was he supposed to explain this? He needed to eat the fruit to know how to grow the seed. He had no idea what would happen if Jade ate the fruit, much less the seed. The slime was smart when it came to some things, but other concepts and goals were still foreign.

“The fruit is special,” Ferris began, “we plant seed, grow strong.”

“Fruit special,” Jade agreed, but also missed the point entirely, “eat fruit.” It rolled directly underneath him and began to pout, bubbling up a storm.

Ferris was worried, but at least the slime was no longer trying to climb his leg. He wasn’t sure what to do but figured it would help to remove the temptation. Cautiously, Ferris quickly ate the rest of the fruit, storing the large seed in a pocket.

More information on the Grow ability flowed into his mind. It was a slow, passive ability. It needed to be planted in the slimespace inside the wall of the dome, watered with liquid qi, and then it would… force the dome to expand. Ferris could use any food from the Consume Pool to help the ability grow faster, somehow gathered before it melted. Growing larger wasn’t flashy or quick, but it took the slime’s main strength—an invulnerable acid body—and multiplied it.

Ferris frowned. Grow was a perfect ability for a slime. The slime journal had described how a giant Gumly would roll on top of enemies, swallowing them whole. In comparison, Jade could only cover a hand or foot. Partially covering a head was a good option as well, but the slime could also be flung off.

The real question was, would this ability support Ferris’ fighting style? Currently, Ferris didn’t have a style, but he knew he didn’t want to follow the same path as Janice. Clan Bluereef delvers relied on hit and run tactics, focusing on agility underwater. A larger slime would be slower, especially in water, although Ferris could solve that problem later. Grow was simple but strong, and it played to the slime’s strengths.

Perhaps the other fruit would give a better ability to focus on. Ferris wasn’t free to be too picky, but the wrong path could cost his life. If none of the fruit were suitable, he would have to wait for more options… or work with what he had; this was a common trap cultivators fell into. A sub-optimal choice could be better than nothing, but not if it got them killed in a rift.

This was where a martial tradition helped, with useful abilities shared between members and knowledge of how to best use them. Clan Bluereef knew many common abilities a water core spear could have, and special ways to develop them. Some ability combinations worked well together, and others should be avoided. All abilities could be improved over time, but experimentation was required to learn how. There would be some variation within every cultivator’s personal soulspace, even with the same ability, but there were enough similarities to be worth it.

Ferris knelt before Jade, the slime still bubbling unhappily. The other fruit could wait until he had cheered the slime up, and hopefully better explained the situation. He considered feeding Jade the fruit. It was a worthwhile experiment, but he decided it was too early to risk losing an option.

Ferris started with a few mental images of why he needed to eat the fruit, but the concepts were too abstract. That, and the disgruntled mood, made anything he said just confuse and upset the slime more. Jade didn’t seem able or willing to understand, but maybe he could distract them.

His younger sister Ferra had been born six years after him. According to his mother, he hadn’t reacted to a younger sibling well at first. Over time, Ferris felt he’d grown pretty good at getting along with her. The trick was in how you talk to them. A child didn’t want to be mad, they just didn’t know how to stop.

Ferris picked Jade up, the slime allowed him even though they were still angry. Frustratingly, the bond’s pervasive influence was likely to help him. Trying not to let his irritation at the thought bleed over the bond, he climbed down the tower. As he walked toward the winding river, he considered how to improve the general mood. So far, the slime was very food motivated, maybe there was something else he could give?

“What do you like to eat?” He asked, looking down at Jade still bubbling in his arms.

A swirl of confusion appeared briefly, but then went back to bubbling. The slime ignored him, shifting slightly in his hands.

Ferris decided to tell a story as he walked. He was still wary of the grey stone-like ants that swarmed about, but they had completely ignored him so far.

“The day before I left home, my family cooked an entire feast of my favorite foods,” Ferris tried sending pictures of the food, focusing on the sensations of smell and taste. “There was pasta in a rich hearty tomato sauce, a fish stew with spicy peppers, fried dumplings dipped in salty oil, fluffy rolls covered with sweet jam.” The sentences were probably too complex for Jade, but the memories transferred just fine over the bond.

Soon, the bubbles grew less and less as Jade got more interested in Ferris’ story.

“There was even something only saved for special occasions. There isn’t enough space in Bluereef, or any bastion really, to grow livestock. Monster flesh is poisonous to mundane people, but not everything in a rift is a monster. Rifts have mundane animals that delvers can hunt and bring back, but Bluereef most often connects to underwater areas. My uncle went farther out than normal to hunt a large boar just for the occasion.” Ferris was making himself drool at the memory, even though he had already eaten that night. “He cooked it slow, for an entire day, and then lathered it in a sweet sauce. The smell alone was incredible, and drew all the neighbors to a party.”

“Greedy! Give me food!” Jade broke their silence, unable to control themself any longer listening to Ferris talk about food.

Ferris had arrived where the river was fed from the pool. There was no sign of the food Jade had eaten for dinner, although the acid level was slightly higher than before. The magical chain cage was still sitting in the pool, massive as ever compared to the real world. If the same enlargement happened to the food that Jade ate, it could account for the higher level.

Everything else looked the same. The ranchers had claimed that slimes would never stop eating, but Ferris wondered what would happen if the river overflowed. Another thing to test.

“Okay, let’s go find some food.” Ferris carried Jade back to his Heartfire and left the slimespace.

Waking up, Ferris untied Jade from the dresser. Holding the chain as a leash once more, they left for the kitchen. Mother Lily was still cleaning up after dinner but was happy to provide a snack. Once he had extracted himself—and then Jade—from her care, he returned to his room with a plate full of crackers, fruit, and sweets.

Back in the room, Ferris continued his quest to bribe his way back into the slime’s good graces with a game.

“We try each food, and then rate it,” Ferris explained.

Jade swirled a bit. “Rate? Want eat!”

“Let’s try.” Ferris took a cracker and dropped it onto Jade. The slime immediately consumed it, finally starting to ripple again—although slowly.

“That was a cracker, was it good or bad?”

“Cracker good!”

“How good?” Ferris sent the image of a rating scale, with numbers going from one to ten. He went slow, focusing on the feeling as he went from bad to good. Jade had reacted much better to ideas described with thoughts than explained with words, and Ferris wanted to practice explaining a concept.

“Cracker ten!” Jade declared, catching on.

“What about a cookie,” Ferris fed Jade one of the sweets, “good or bad?”

“Cookie ten!”

Ferris tried several different snacks, asking for a rating each time. He thought Jade understood the scale, but the slime resolutely gave everything a ten.

Once the plate was half empty, Ferris pulled out his secret weapon: the last remaining berry from the Slime ranch. He remembered that the slimes would go for these berries over any other food. So far Jade hadn’t shown any preferences for one food over another with the rating scale, but he thought the berry would be different. The ranchers had warned him it would lose potency over time, so Ferris didn’t want to wait too long before feeding it to Jade.

“What about this berry?” he asked, casually tossing it to Jade.

The slime instantly exploded in ripples. “Berry ten! Berry ten!”

Everything after that was rated lower, confirming Ferris’ belief that the berry was special.

“Cracker four.” “Cookie five.” “Apple four.”

Foreman Carter claimed the berry contained a very small amount of magic. Ferris wasn’t sure how food could have magic, or how to get more, but Jade liked it. As part of his retainer contract, Mother Lily would likely provide as much mundane food as Ferris could eat, within reason. Thankfully, the woman appeared to extend that to providing meals for Jade. It wasn’t technically in his contract, after all.

He wanted to find a supply of magical food for Jade, as he hoped the Consume Pool and Digest River would benefit. It wasn’t guaranteed, but with the slime’s natural desire for magical food it was a good bet. Even if they didn’t work that way, he could develop them in that direction.

It was the same with his fears about the bond influencing him. My only option is to press forward, he thought. There was no way he’d destroy the core. Even if he survived, he’d lose his path to becoming a delver.

I don’t like how it works, he thought, I just have to put in the time and effort to change it. The real world was full of shackles, but his soul had unlimited potential. It might not be easy, but it was possible.

That was the essence of cultivation, after all.
