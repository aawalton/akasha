“There’s something wrong with me.”

We all jerk in surprise as my doppelganger speaks. None of the others spoke, or even had body language. They fought like automatons imbued with skills and knowledge, but none of the personality.

This? This is different, and on Post-Apotheosis Earth, different is dangerous.

“There’s something missing,” it continues. “It tried to make me like you. That’s its story. But it couldn’t.”

Golden sparks spew out of its helmet, and it twitches.

“Not… enough. There wasn’t enough. Devoured as much of the dream as it could. Still not enough.”

A blade of golden glass forms in its hand.

“I’ll take yours. I need it. I NEED IT!”

It launches forward with a thunderous explosion, marble tile shattering. It rushes me like an oncoming train, and I barely get my sword up in time. Golden glass meets deep space with a ringing clash.

“Give it to me!”

It claws at my face, but I kick it back into the ballroom, one the size of an American super stadium.

I storm after it, but it’s ready for me. We trade a dozen clumsy and amateurish strikes in less than a second, each one digging huge trenches into the ballroom floor.

It’s not any better than I am, at least.

Shockwaves batter the French team and Morgan behind me.

I miss a parry, off balance from an over-extended slash. The golden greatsword bounces off my helmet, knocking me over.

It’s heavy.

I roll away from a stomp before launching myself back to my feet. I come face to face with my duplicate. A grasping gauntlet reaches for my face, and I slap it away.

“How did you get so much of it?! Tell me!”

I’m forced to bat away a flurry of strikes, each one breaking the sound barrier. What was a sad excuse for a sword fight has quickly turned into a super-sonic bar-room brawl.

Pieces of marble go flying, our attacks are so strong that the shockwaves they cause are powerful enough to rip up the ground. Morgan is forced to shield everyone with another glowing bubble.

My copy slugs me in the stomach, and I double over. For the first time in a month, I feel pain. I look down, and see glittering trails of broken, golden glass flowing around its arm.

A second fist hammers into my head, and again I go sprawling. Before I can stagger to my feet, it grips my gorget and hauls me to my feet. It hungrily claws at my faceplate.

“Do you know the agony of knowing you’re incomplete? Broken?!”

Its hand reaches inside my helmet, and I feel a deep sense of violation. Blue fire crawls over it. It’s like ripping out nerves, and I scream in pain.

“I was meant to be your equal, but I never could. It wasn’t possible. I was made to be a failure!”

I barely shove it away, fighting through the soul-rending agony. It goes sliding back, digging another trench of broken marble. A trail of blue fire follows it before slowly drifting back to me.

“What are you talking about?” I ask weakly.

“The fire!” it snaps before launching itself back at me. “There wasn’t enough!”

I dodge a jab, counter attacking with one of my own. It hits the ground with an earth shattering boom.

It rolls out of the way as my own stomp shatters more marble. It kicks me high into the air.

It leaps after me, and spikes me into the floor. I blow a hundred foot wide crater into the ground, and the entire ballroom shudders with the impact.

It lands on me, hammering me in deeper. Golden glass whirls around its arm as it pulls me out again.

“I was meant to be a story. But there wasn’t enough to write me!”

As it reaches for my face again, golden sparks pour out of its helmet.

“But I won’t be controlled. I won’t be owned! I can’t be. It made me from you, after all.”

I hold it back, and golden light burns at my finger tips for a brief moment before I roar. Blue fire washes forth, and my duplicate drops me and jumps away.

The armor on its arms looks… melted? There’s slight dents where I was squeezing it when my fire rushed over it.

Just like the others, it’s ultimately a poor copy.

“No!” it snarls.

It clenches with a fist, and the entire Fracture shudders again. The hallway outside the boss room, where everyone else is huddling under Morgan’s shield, begins to collapse. The entire fight has taken only seconds, none of them have had time to do much more than watch. Only Morgan has the speed to react to super-sonic beat-down.

She grabs them all with telekinesis, including Grace’s unconscious body. She drags them into the boss room. Even the room shrinks, rapidly contracting to half its original size.

The sparks inside the clone’s helmet roar to life, now a bonfire rather than a weak ember.

“I rule this dream, it is mine. Not its. I will be a story no longer!”

Our swords rematerialize, and again we clash. The duplicate is faster than before, though no more skilled. I accelerate in turn with it, and what was a staccato of combat turns into a waterfall of violence.

Swords clatter off armor. Golden light stings, and blue fire melts. Marble is ground into gravel, then hammered into dust. I watch as Morgan blinks in slow motion. In the span of a blink, we strike each other a hundred times.

The exchange ends with my boot in its chest. Blue-white actinide lightning jumps off me to scorch away at my copy. It screams in digital agony, the scream chopped like a broken speaker.

It flies off into the wall, and I charge after it. Its scream of pain turns into one of rage.

It clenches its fist, and it rips a hole in the very fabric of the Fracture itself, right in my path. The mind-bending sight sends alarm bells ringing through my head. I lunge to the side, moving too quick to abort my charge.

“I rule this dream! I am SOVEREIGN!”

The Fracture pulses with the final word. A deeper sense of power fills it, and echoes in me as well.

To rule is my right.

The clone launches forward, swinging wildly with its golden blade. Space warps around it, sending waves of glittering fire. They sizzle on my armor, and I hiss in pain.

Thoughts surge from my fire, my core.

“You think yourself above me?” I spit. “You are nothing but a usurper!”

I grab its arm knocking its blade aside, and bring it in close. Blue fire screams out like a wildfire. The Fracture shudders and groans under the weight of everything I am. It bends under my authority, and I have to pull back before it falls apart.

Blue fire leaks out from every seam, the chain mail underneath my plate armor glowing with inner light.

My doppelganger staggers backwards, barely on its feet. Its armor is blackened and scorched, the armor even molten in some places. The golden flames that fill its helmet gutter weakly.

“No. It can’t end like this,” it says. “I’m sovereign.”

The word has no power, not from it.

“There can only be one SOVEREIGN.” I tell it. Everything and everyone shudders.

It tries to form a sword again, but its golden fire sputters, and it shatters into glass. It limps forward anyway.

“Won’t. Give. Up.”

One leg is near useless, the knee joint melted over. Liquid, molten armor sloughs off parts of it, and burns through the floor in an instant. It’s nearly as hot as the surface of the Sun. Whatever fabric remained from our apocalyptic fight in the ballroom ignites.

Morgan’s shield darkens, the molten armor bright enough to hurt everyone’s eyes.

My sword of stars slips through its chest, and the flames finally die out. Its body clatters to the floor, and the exit opens. A reward orb flickers into existence for a moment before disappearing entirely.

I look at the husk of molten armor, identical to mine. It refused to crumble into nothingness like the doppelgangers. It melts a hole in the marble floor, slowly sinking. The armor takes several minutes before it finally stops glowing bright enough to blind someone.

“You can leave it.”

I jerk, distracted by the sight of my own partially slagged wreckage. Morgan floats behind me.

“I know you, Mach. I’d know you’d love to bring it back for ‘research’. But you can just leave it. You don’t need to do this to yourself.”

My only response is to pry it out of the floor and drag it out by the leg.

The two of us stay off to the side, unbothered by the bustle of a busy military camp. The Luminaries joined Grace as she was airlifted to the hospital. They wouldn’t leave her alone, especially not after she just lost her entire team.

2nd Squad and McKinley are loading the doppelganger’s wreckage into a Chinook. The Pineapple Express made it to Paris just after we entered, and quickly deployed to Amiens to assist in clean-up operations.

“This isn’t working.” I wave at the camp packing up.

“What do you mean?” Morgan asks.

“This endless firefighting. Jumping from Fracture to Fracture. We’re trying to patch a boat while someone keeps punching holes in it. We don’t even understand what Fractures are, Morgan. We were able to measure them and we all thought that meant we knew enough to fight them. Clearly, we’re wrong.”

“I assume you’re not talking about abandoning clearing Fractures entirely.”

“No, no. We just need a new approach. We can’t keep throwing people at every Fracture and hope everything stays the same. We lost a 3-Star team here, a tenth of France’s 3-Star Empowered.

“It can’t keep going on like this. If we keep charging into Fractures based on numbers we barely understand we’ll lose everything. We keep acting like if we just clear the Fractures first, everything will be fine. It’s not. It won’t. We’re already past the point of no return, and we need to stop acting like we can still grab the edge of the cliff.”

“There’s always the Fractal Shards,” Morgan says. “The Clairvoyance Opus we used to measure this Fracture is just the beginning.”

She sighs.

“If the Cat-4 had provided some Shards, that would have been immensely valuable. Shards are exceptionally rare and hard to get a hold of.”

I pull a Shard into my hand. The shimmering quartz-like crystal is a foot long, and slowly oscillates up and down the electromagnetic spectrum.

“You must have quite the stockpile of those,” Morgan says, eyeing the magical crystal.

“A hundred and sixty of them. Do you think you could make something of them all?”

“I could certainly try. I’d like your help, though.”

“Why? I can’t wield Potentia.”

“Machina. Your doppelganger controlled the Fracture. If it was anything at all like you, then you might be able to do the same.”

“I’m not sure I want it to be like me. I felt its Aura as much as you did. By the end it was…”

“Tyrannical.” Morgan finishes.

“Yeah. I don’t want to be a king. I never asked for this. For the responsibility. It’s nearly an unbearable weight. I’m terrified I’ll become something like that. *Sovereign.”

The word rings out between us, filled with a deeper meaning, a deeper power.

She lands on my shoulder, settling down into the same place as before.

“I’m not afraid of that. You shouldn’t be either.”

“Why?”

“Because you’re a good person. I’ve met a lot of people, Mach. And you’re the best.”

“But I have to try to be good. A good person shouldn’t have to try.”

She laughs, slumping against my head, chin on the top of my helmet.

“Oh, Mach. You think being good is easy? Being a good person takes effort, sacrifice, and dedication. It is the most difficult thing to be. You’re good because you try.”

“…Thank you.”

Her smile outshines the sun, and her eyes glimmer.

“Always. Now, off to my gallery. We have work to do!”
