Fay's eyes traveled between the thermos in her hand and the Staging Point portal. Briana had been waiting when she got there, coffee in hand. She hadn't argued, she hadn't asked questions that Fay didn't want to answer. She'd only asked for two things. Two things that Fay had no trouble agreeing to, no trouble promising even if a small part of her knew they might ultimately be lies.

Her scalp hurt, pulling her hair into a bun was never pleasant, especially after having gotten used to leaving it loose. She had her change of clothes, a day or two's supply of food, and her coffee. She was going to step into the Depths, she was going to find her family.

She didn't move. She couldn't. This was the Depths. She wasn't a Combat Pather. She knew what to expect, she could probably have painted the other side of the portal after all of Seb's descriptions. She knew what she was doing.

She would step through. She'd be careful, the first promise. She'd take her time, pay attention despite the lack of risks. She'd follow the path until she reached the right level, then she'd start looking for signs. She'd find them and she'd come back, her second promise, with them.

She still hadn't moved. She'd been going through Portals for years, what was another? She couldn't find them if she didn't step through the portal. She couldn't re-unite with Seb and Anya, she wouldn't be able to scold them, tell them how they'd terrified her, exact her revenge on them for putting her through this. She wouldn't be able to force them to stay home for days, to just be family until everyone felt normal again.

She stepped through the portal.

~ * ~

Fay knew from past discussions that it would take Seb and Anya a couple hours to make it from the portal at the 10th level down to the 15th, or now 16th, level of the Depths. They could do it in less if they rushed, sometimes it took them longer because they got distracted, but two or so hours was their usual pace.

She was higher level than them, able to move faster than them. It took her eight to reach the 15th, hesitating at every echo, every glint of light she hadn't expected. She spent another hour glancing around, getting a feel for the most recent area they'd described to her. She knew where they had camped, what they found and where.

She could have done this at any of the previous levels, but this one was freshest in her mind. And if for some reason they hadn't gone deeper, done what they had planned? This is where they'd be.

She didn't find any fresh traces. She found evidence of their past stay exactly where she expected. She found everything she was looking for, except for them.

She'd been up for- she honestly didn't know how long she'd been awake at this point. Hours? Days? It didn't matter. She'd sleep later. She was exhausted but her mind was still sharp. She hadn't pushed to that point yet, she knew the dangers of that point, she knew she probably wasn't at her best, but she could see clearly, think clearly, reason clearly about the untouched dust she'd walked through.

The last descent, the passage to the 16th level, took as long as any other two, maybe even three. Each step was heavier than the last, each movement slower. Not because the pressure affected her, not because the Depths were slowing her down. She'd have to go down another ten or more before that became the issue. She didn't know what she'd find, she didn't know what she'd see. They hadn't been to this level, this was their first trip since they advanced.

She knew she was drawing close when the lighting changed. One more bend and she'd be able to see the gap that led into the level itself. One more bend and she'd be able to start looking.

She stepped into the cavern and paused. She'd made the trip, now she had to-

"Fay!? Run!"

She didn't hesitate, she ran towards family.

~ * ~

Back to camp! Seb sent as Fay came running towards them.

He knew Anya understood, that she'd lead the way. He had no idea why Fay was there, no idea why she was coming further into the depths and not listening when Anya had told her to run.

She was coming towards them and there wasn't time to figure that out. There wasn't time to stand there talking, to let Fay come to a halt. They had to get out of the tunnel and into a cave that had a small enough entrance. Their camp, not their first, but the one they'd used for the last month, fit the bill.

As Fay started to slow he pointed at Anya, already partway down the tunnel.

"Follow."

She fell into step beside him, matching him even as he pushed himself faster. She barely seemed to put any effort into it, the difference in levels enough that she wasn't slowed by the pressure.

He could hear the beast behind them, it was faster than him, but not by enough. It was too big to get into their camp, too dangerous for them to deal with directly, too determined to nest next to the landing for them to sneak past. They'd tried once, spent a week actively recovering after managing to escape.

Anya turned into their campsite and Fay followed on her heels. He was only steps behind her when he jerked to a stop rather than crash into them both. The thud against the wall outside told the tale of how close they had cut it.

"Fay," he began. "What are you-"

He felt Anya crash into his side as Fay swept them both into her arms. She didn't squeeze too hard, she didn't hurt them, she simply kept them pinned as she buried her head between them and sobbed.

"You're alive, you're not hurt, you're not in danger."

He felt her flinch at a renewed thud at the entrance. She pulled away from them, her face pale as she realized they were in a cave that was smaller than her bedroom in the house.

"Are we safe?"

"It can't fit. It can't reach us," Anya said. "It'll wander back to its nest in an hour or so when it remembers to."

"An hour?" Fay said, her eyes darting to the entrance at every sound.

"Fay!" Seb said before touching her shoulder gently. He would have grabbed Anya, turned her to face him, but having Fay react in a panic would be horrible.

"Seb? Anya? You're late. You didn't come back on time and you're late."

Tent? Anya sent.

Really cramped with three.

We can put stuff outside, but it isn't a cave, it isn't as noisy.

"Fay," he said gently. "We can sit in the tent, it's warm and feels like home."

"Home? Home is safe."

He didn't have to guide her. She barely let Anya lead the way, pressing into the tent and sliding all the way to the back before Seb even got to the entry.

It was very cramped. He didn't even bother trying to join them. He started pulling the bulkiest items out, those that might be a nuisance to replace but took up room that a third human would need.

He got about a third of the supplies out when Anya's emotions shifted and he decided it would have to do. Joining them made the space cozy, they each had space to sit, but sleeping would leave all three of them as close together as he and Anya usually were.

The moment he sat down he both saw and felt Fay relax. The beast was still there, still trying to reach them, but the sound seemed muted. When Fay looked at him the panic was gone, the confusion was clearing up. One moment she seemed business-like, her attention moving from one to the other to confirm what she knew. The next she was slumping against Anya, eyes flickering shut.

Is she..? he asked.

A faint snore set Anya giggling. The answer didn't need to be given.

They did their best to move her gently, shifting her until she could lie down. They tried to give her space, to let her settle comfortably. They couldn't. She started to wake up the moment they moved away, settling back down instantly when she touched them.

Did she say anything while I was making space?

Just kept repeating that she found us, that we were safe.

What do we do? he asked.

He felt Anya trying not to laugh at his question. Fay had decided her lap was a pillow at the same time she'd latched on to his ankle. There wasn't anything they could do, not until she woke up.
