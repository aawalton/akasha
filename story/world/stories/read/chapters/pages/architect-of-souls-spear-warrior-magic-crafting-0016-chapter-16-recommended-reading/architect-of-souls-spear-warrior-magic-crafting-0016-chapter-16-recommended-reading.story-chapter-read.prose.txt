The next morning, Ferris awoke to Jade eating his shoe.

“Not food! No eat shoe!” Ferris scolded Jade, watching the last of the shoe dissolve. Luckily, he had a spare set, but that shoe had been practically new.

Also, why couldn’t they have eaten the other one, instead of the one he actually had a foot for. It was right next to it, waiting for him to regrow his leg.

“Greedy feed me more!” Sending confusion, the slime swirled at being scolded. “Shoe six out of ten!”

Sighing, Ferris asked Jade to move away from his things, but the slime just sat there. Jade had grown adept at following his mental images to know where to move, but now they just swirled a bit. The slime kept sending back confusion over the bond, rather than any desire to disobey.

Ferris frowned. Foreman Carter had mentioned that slimes could learn… but would forget all their training after a day. Had Jade forgotten how to process the mental images he sent? Would he have to re-teach the slime every morning?

The slime journal observing Janice Landsworn hadn’t mentioned anything like this for Gumly, but it had only contained what she was willing to tell others. A familiar with long term memory loss would be a weakness, and Ferris doubted any cultivator would willingly discuss it.

Ferris tried to determine how long Jade’s memory could be. He’d told his familiar not to eat things in his room yesterday before dinner. That’d lasted until he went to sleep but was forgotten when he woke up, giving a range of somewhere between seven and fourteen hours.

Jade had also lost their mental body yesterday, and Ferris wasn’t sure if that was related or not. He hadn’t lost any memories—that he knew of—when he had died in the soulspace, but perhaps slimes are different.

Jade did seem to remember some things, like the rating scale. And why, Ferris grumbled to himself, is my shoe rated higher than a cookie? The slime also remembered to call Ferris ‘greedy,’ which was a clear reference to when he ate the Grow fruit.

Thinking about it… how long ago had he told Jade his name? Communicating over the bond was more mental images than words, and he hadn’t needed to refer to himself much. He only really mentioned it when trying to teach about names.

“Good morning, Jade,” he sat down near the slime—although outside the reach of the chain. Ferris didn’t trust any of the training from yesterday. “My name is Ferris, please don’t eat me.” He sent mental images of himself, attempting to communicate that his hands were part of his body, hopefully preventing a misunderstanding like yesterday.

“Ferris greedy! Feed me now!” The slime seemed to agree, but also ignored the point entirely. They definitely remembered the plate of snacks from the night before and sent mental images of Ferris feeding them.

“First, we practice moving, then we have breakfast,” Ferris responded.

“Breakfast?” Jade swirled a bit.

Ferris sent an image of plates of food, and the slime rippled in joy.

“Breakfast!”

Ferris ran through the same training exercises as when he’d first bonded Jade. This time the slime was much faster at understanding the mental pictures, although that could be because Ferris was better at communicating over the bond… or perhaps Jade’s forgotten experience coming back.

Soon Jade was once again able to move to the precise spot Ferris was thinking of, based on a mental picture and an innate spatial awareness of Ferris’ position from the bond. It would be much easier if the slime had eyes, or could detect anything beyond touch and taste, but it worked.

While training, Ferris considered the problem of Jade’s memory. The primer on familiar bonds claimed the slime would gradually grow more intelligent. That might solve the problem, but there was no guarantee. Most familiars didn’t have memory loss to begin with.

Ferris needed more information. The slime was already smart, just in a different way. He devised a plan to test how long Jade’s memory lasted. If he checked every day, he could confirm the memory loss wasn’t a one-time problem and determine if it was improving or staying the same.

Ferris didn’t have a clock, but he remembered there was one in the common room. Freeing Jade’s chain from the dresser, he led his familiar out on a leash to check. After noting the time, Ferris asked Jade to remember ten names of children he had grown up with. He intended to check a single name at various times, until he got a wrong answer. There was a chance of Jade simply forgetting a name, but if that happened, he could verify with the other names. Ferris would need to return to the common room near his estimated time window, but hopefully this would narrow down the time he needed to check.

Mother Lily soon called them to eat. Jade remembered her from last night, which suggested the slime’s memory was near the longer end of his window.

She pulled out all the stops for breakfast, giving Jade a heaping plate of food. It was enough for three people. Ferris was happy, as more food would mean more qi. At least, once he could harvest from the Digest River. Jade was thrilled and declared Mother Lily the most wonderful person ever. He tried not to be too hurt as Jade droned on and on about how ‘not greedy’ she was.

Karl was sticking his nose in the air, not-so-subtly offended to share a table with a slime. Cyrus watched Jade with the intensity of a scholar, his eyes sometimes flashing as he used some special ability. Ferris was curious what it was, but it would be rude to ask. Brix missed breakfast, likely focused on her cultivation. It was uncommon for every resident to be present for a meal.

After eating, Ferris and Jade set out to report back to Elder Harper. He’d successfully bonded and was looking forward to the next step to acquiring Acid Reconstruction.

Before they left, he decided to re-wrap the magical chain around the cage covering Jade’s core, rather than use it like a leash.

There was still a risk that Jade would forget the instructions and randomly start to eat everything around them, but Ferris felt he was gaining a better understanding of how the slime worked. As long as he kept repeating the safety lessons beforehand, Jade was well behaved.

It was hard to know if his judgement was compromised, but Ferris did his best to consider and reconsider his decisions.

The problem was, showing up to meet Elder Harper with his familiar on a leash was also a risk. He was here all by himself, both stuck with and dependent on Clan Hollowfield for the next ten years. They expected to get a competent retainer out of the deal. If Ferris didn’t live up to those expectations, he had no one to fall back on.

He wasn’t sure at what point Elder Harper would decide he was incompetent and cut her losses, but Ferris wouldn’t trust anyone that needed to leash their own familiar. At the very least, it would make it harder for him to earn better opportunities.

So off Ferris went, crutch under one arm and Jade in his other. It was a bit awkward, but he was well accustomed to it by now. There was no way he would let his familiar simply roll along beside him. He didn’t want anyone to mistake the slime for a random monster and kill them.

Arriving at her office, Hemon met with him. Elder Harper was busy, but Ferris was able to make an appointment for that afternoon.

Left with the morning to himself, Ferris considered his priorities. He had several goals to work on in the soulspace, but he would be limited until Jade reformed their mental body. He could only enter the slimespace when touching Jade, and Ferris wasn’t willing to trust leaving the slime awake with access to him while he meditated. The ache of pain every time he moved his bandaged hand wouldn’t let him consider being that vulnerable. Maybe after he better understood Jade’s memory loss, but for now Ferris wanted to wait until they could enter the soulspace together.

Plus, he had plenty to do outside of cultivating. Clan Hollowfield had a lot to offer, and he was still discovering it all. Eventually, he wanted to visit the first district at the center of the clan’s compound, which hosted the clan’s merit store and organized delving groups. But before Ferris went shopping, he needed information—and likely more merit points. What better place to get information than the library right next door?

Hollowfield’s fourth branch prided itself on knowledge and research, and Ferris intended to take full advantage of it. Entering the library once more, that pride could be clearly seen in the grand scale of the foyer. The line to speak to an assistant moved quickly, and he did not have to wait long.

After confirming Ferris’ identity token, an older man greeted him.

“Welcome to the library, Adept. My name is Gilbert Booker Hollowfield. How may I be of service today.”

Ferris noted that Gilbert gave a family name, but also specified his last name was Hollowfield. That meant he was a full member of the clan, either through birth, marriage, or the more rare adoption. Ferris thought the clan name was a bit obvious, given where they were, but there were enough non-clan members around it must be warranted. At the same time, he granted Ferris his family name, not fully treating him like an outsider.

The family name of ‘Booker’ was also a bit blunt for someone that works at the library. The man, or his family, must want everyone to know how proud they were of the occupation. Perhaps, rather than welcoming, he was just boasting?

“Greetings, Gilbert. I am Adept Ferris Bluereef, retainer of Hollowfield. I have recently bonded, and I am interested in any books related to acid cores, the study of acid in general, slimes, and familiar bonds.” Elder Harper had already shown Ferris the most important books, like the familiar bond primer and the journal detailing Janice and Gumly, but it didn’t hurt to see what might be available for a newly bonded Adept.

Gilbert led the way to a private room. It was noticeably smaller than the one when Ferris was working on Elder Harper’s research project, yet still nice and cozy. There was a table, two chairs, and a cot tucked off to the side. The man left Ferris and Jade alone for a bit to assemble a list of books.

While waiting, Ferris practiced his coordination directing Jade around the room. Communicating over the bond was like flexing a muscle Ferris never knew he had. Jade was happy to follow direction, reveling in the freedom to explore new places. Ferris, bored with simply directing the slime around, started to make the exercises more complicated.

When Gilbert returned, he found Ferris, with his eyes closed, tossing Jade up into the air and playfully catching the slime. The man closed the door a bit loudly, causing Ferris to jump a little. He quickly opened his eyes, trying to cover up a blush. Gilbert Booker, the consummate professional, pretended nothing had happened and set a stack of three books on the table.

“First, we have a compilation of Hollowfield’s purchasable basic tier one abilities for acid cores.” Gilbert presented the first book. “It will list the ability’s name along with a short summary. Any other details will be in the ability manual, which will cost merit points. The minimum fee for a tier one ability manual is twelve merit points, although there are many that cost more. This catalog is provided as a service for any retainer with the corresponding core aspect, but please understand that most information related to cultivation cost merit.”

Ferris recognized the book as an acid core version of the fire and plant core primers Elder Harper had him read when researching potential healing abilities.

“For research studies on acid, I would recommend starting with these two volumes. The first is a general scientific reference titled A Study of Acid, and the second is Material Tests Logs for Corrosivity and Corrosiveness.” Gilbert handed over the final two books he had brought. “Both of these are primarily mundane in nature, and thus won’t cost merit points. If I may be so bold,” Gilbert looked Ferris in the eyes, “may I offer a few words of advice?”

Ferris nodded; he had traveled all this way for Clan Hollowfield’s knowledge. He didn’t care who delivered it.

“Information like this is almost always sought by Adepts seeking to understand and develop their magic. Often, they are successful. The clan gathers mundane studies like this primarily for this reason. There are two potential downsides to this approach. First,” the man raised a finger, “these represent our current understanding, but we don’t know everything. Many times, I have seen ‘fact’ be overturned by new research.”

Gilbert didn’t say it, but Ferris could also imagine some factions either hiding or deliberately falsifying such facts.

“Second,” the man continued, “we supplement mundane research with mundane researchers. This allows us to offer books like this free of charge. However, the study of magic requires an Adept, and with magic you will find many ironclad mundane rules change or disappear. Not everything directly relates. The library contains several volumes focused on specific magical acids, but they require merit points.”

Ferris nodded, happy to at least have the free information.

“For a slime bestiary, you may need to be more specific,” Gilbert said. “The library has an extensive collection of tactical treatises on fighting different monster types around Scalefort Bastion. There are seven different variations of slime evolutions covered, although each has a cost to view.”

“I was hoping for something studying the base nature of a slime, rather than a combat guide,” Ferris said, glancing down at Jade.

“Unfortunately, the library does not have such a book,” Gilbert appeared displeased to admit the lack. “Nor do we have a tactical treatise on slimes without specific evolved abilities.”

Left unsaid was why the library didn’t have a guide on how to fight a common slime. Ferris could make a guess: no one considered it necessary. The book would start and end with Foreman Carter’s boot.

They did consider evolved slimes dangerous, though. When he had merit points to spare, Ferris wanted to study those guides for potential ideas Jade could use.

“The library does have an in-depth guide to slime ranching. It is restricted, accessible only subject to an Elder’s approval.”

Ferris smiled at the words. Foreman Carter had probably explained much of what that book contained during his training orientation. The library may treat the knowledge as clan secrets, but it was human nature to talk when someone shows interest in your job.

“Next, we have an introduction to familiar bonds. Our records indicate that you already have permission to read this information. Unfortunately… Hollowfield does not have a more detailed manual dedicated to familiar bonds.”

Ferris was expecting this, but it was still unfortunate to hear. Elder Harper had warned him that the clan didn’t have much aid for familiar bonds. The book that Gilbert did bring was either a copy or the same one that he had read before, provided by Elder Harper before he bonded Jade.

“Lastly, it is my duty to mention these as recommended to all newly bonded retainers of the clan. They contain knowledge helpful for all new Adepts, regardless of the bond or aspect.”

Three names were written on a piece of paper: First Steps of the Soul, Spiritual Harmony, and Mental Materials.

“First Steps of the Soul is provided free of change to all new Adepts. The other two are priced at six merit points each,” Gilbert continued, “the minimum price allowed for tier one technical manuals.”

Ferris re-read the names, a bit hungrily. Uncle Winton had guided Ferris with advice and instructions so far, but the idea of Clan Hollowfield’s experience and knowledge at his fingertips was exciting. Many retainers agreed to trade a decade of service just for this kind of access.

Ferris had spent six merit points on the chain, but he had nine remaining. He could afford either Spiritual Harmony or Mental Materials now… but it would be better to wait until he had talked to Elder Harper. He wanted to speak to her about how to work towards healing his leg before making any expensive decisions.

Reading through First Steps of the Soul might also direct him to one or the other, but he didn’t think he would have time before his appointment. From its name, he guessed it covered information Uncle Winton had already helped him with, but he hoped it would contain more details or go farther.

“Thank you,” Ferris said, giving Gilbert a slight bow, “I will consider these options carefully.”

However, his eyes couldn’t help but be drawn to the book on acid core abilities. It contained dozens, maybe more, magical abilities that he could learn.

It was all he could do to wait for the man to leave the room before rushing over to it.
