Chapter II.XXVIII (2.28)

Mitsuko sipped a sour wine made from local grapes. Like everything that grew on Mauve Island, it was a shade of purple. Mitsuko didn’t love the flavor, but at least it was alcohol. She flagged down a serving girl and ordered another. As appealing as another of the same drink was to her, she shuddered at the thought of facing this next challenge sober. That would be a recipe for disaster.

Sterling wasn’t allowed inside the building so she sat alone at a table while he instead performed a reconnaissance mission for her.

“Mitsuko. What a lovely turn of events. You’ve come running to my side.” Akuto’s tall figure loomed over her. His arms crossed, he was clearly attempting to subtly flex his biceps. Mitsuko caught sight of a band of metal on his left ring finger. A vein of gold through an otherwise steel ring. While she lacked the ability to inspect it for magic, she had no doubt it was enchanted.

“Yes, Akuto. I heard you were in town and I… couldn’t help myself.”

Her ex-fiancé nodded with a smug smirk. “Good. I assumed I’d have to pull you home by your hair.”

“You’re an accomplished elementalist,” Mitsuko replied, keeping her voice flat. “Even if I wanted to, how could I resist?”

“I’m happy you’re sensible about your place in the world. I was worried when I heard those filthy rumors. But they were so stupid that I should have known they couldn’t be true.”

Mitsuko was tempted to ask which rumors he had heard. She had spawned hundreds of small ones at this point. The nobility loved to gossip and she’d been the subject of their scrutiny ever since the ghoul attack on Hon and her mysterious placement at Sasaki’s side. However, her current interlocutor damped the temptation to ask. Every word spoken to Akuto filled her insides with a bit more disgust. Like pouring pondscum into a clean bathtub.

“Your father is here as well,” Akuto said. “He and some witch he dredged up from the swamps near our home.”

“I know.”

“Oh, do you?” Akuto sneered, his posture turning hostile at the interruption. “I’m sorry, is speaking to me a chore?”

The honest answer was ‘yes’ but Mitsuko bit back that response. “You never liked smalltalk,” she said instead. “I wanted to let you know so that you don’t feel obligated to explain.”

“I’ll talk when I want to talk, bitch.”

Mitsuko’s hand twitched but she held it back from a full flick of the wrist. After years of socializing with normal people it was easy to forget how temperamental this man was. Sometimes it was hard to believe that men like Akuto and the clown Sam could co-exist in the same world as someone like Wan. Even Jay, who she’d only known for an hour, was by far a superior person to Akuto. His relationship and love for his family was a stark contrast to Akuto’s greedy and selfish nature.

“You’re right,” she said, keeping her voice meek. “Now what comes next? The dome is cutting off our escape from the island so we’re stuck here for a while. Should we go to your room and… reunite? It could help pass the time.”

While Mitsuko never considered herself very adept at deception or nuanced manipulation, she was quite proud of herself for just managing to get those last couple words out. Judging by how Akuta’s face lit up at the implied suggestion, they’d had the desired effect on him. That man could flip through emotions faster than a tumbler through the air at the circus.

“Yes.” Akuto licked his lips and glanced over his shoulder. Likely checking for her father. Then he strode away, clearly expecting her to follow after him like a lost puppy.

Mitsuko held back a sigh as she pushed back her chair and shadowed his path. Nobody else in the common room even glanced in their direction. They remained oblivious as they chattered amongst each other.

The moment the bedroom door closed behind her, Akuto dropped his pants. Mitsuko, very much not interested in that, cast Mend and the pants shot back up to his waist and the belt rebuckled.

“What the….” Akuto yanked on his belt but whenever he unbuckled it and yanked the ends apart, Mitsuko brought them back together.

Despite how humorous Mitsuko found his frustration, she needed to finish up before Akuto could wise up and realize that she was responsible for the sudden esoteric nature of the belt. She flicked a wrist and drove the point of the blade into his chest. It slid through his ribs with satisfying ease.

His hands rose from his waist, up to the blade skewering him. “Bi…tch.” Blood dribbled out of his mouth alongside his final word. Then his hands dropped back down and he slumped to his knees. He tried to cast an elemental spell and rocks formed around his fists. But they crumbled as the light went out of his eyes.

Mitsuko bent down and slid the magic ring off his wet, blood hand. As she stepped back and examined the corpse, Mitsuko felt a tension ease from her shoulders. She smiled and took a deep breath. Killing Akuto was certainly therapeutic. Definitely not a healthy way to release stress, but she couldn’t discount the results.

She cast Mend on the new ring, holding onto it tight as the blood parted from the metal and returned to Akuto’s lifeless body. Then she pocketed it.

“Maybe I’ll come meet you in future loops,” she said to the corpse. “Who knows, maybe you’ll manage to surprise me in one of them and convince me to let you survive the final loop.”

The corpse said nothing. And she knew it wouldn’t surprise her. The thought made her fingers twitch. Then she frowned and shook her head.

“Damn. These loops are making me numb to death. Akuto has always been a piece of shit, but what kind of person takes pleasure in killing?”

She cast Mend on Akuto’s corpse and watched as the blood spilled seeped back into his wound and both skin and fabric resealed itself. The corpse stood back up on its feet and took a backwards step in the direction of the bedroom door. Mitsuko released her spell and Akuto slumped back to the floor.

A nudge of her foot revealed him to be as lifeless as ever. A perfectly healthy body sans soul. It would be completely impossible to figure out his cause of death.

She lifted him up and tucked him into bed. With his eyes closed, anyone checking in on him would hopefully assume him asleep. By the time anyone realized he was dead and linked his passing to her, she should be safely sailing away.

Next, Mitsuko turned her attention to Akuto’s luggage. Her father had mentioned a matching ring worth a lot of money. That implied that Akuto’s wasn’t the only ring.

After a few minutes of rummaging, she recovered a small sack of coins as well as a wooden jewelry box. It was sealed closed with magic, but she bypassed the enchantment by Mending it back in time to a point of time before the spell had been cast on the wood.

Sure enough, inside she found a matching ring. This one was gold with a vein of steel. Gingerly, she plucked it from the soft velvet padding and dropped it in her pocket to accompany the first ring. Then she snapped the box closed and replaced it in the luggage.

“Nothing else here,” she said to the body. “All that’s left is for me to reunite with Sterling. Thanks for the assistance, Akuto. I’ll put your money to good use.”

Once again, she received no reply from the corpse.

She strolled out of the room and did her best to act casually indifferent as she walked through the inn’s dining room. The more boring and uninteresting she appeared, the harder it would be for anyone to match her face to Akuto.

“Mitsuko?”

The sudden call out from across the room made her stumble over her feet. She closed her eyes and took a deep breath. Apparently she wasn’t quite in the clear just yet.

“Pa,” she said, turning slowly to the middle aged man addressing her. While she’d been cold with Akuto, a flaming anger gripped her heart as she met eyes with her father. She’d hoped to avoid this confrontation. Something about her father in particular made her less rational.

“I can’t believe my eyes!” He scrambled across the room and embraced her in a hug. “You’re truly here! Oh, Mitsuko. You’re trembling. It will be fine now, my dear daughter.”

“I’m just… overwhelmed to see you,” Mitsuko said through clenched teeth as her mother’s killer held her close.

He released her from the full embrace but still clutched her shoulders, as if scared she’d scamper off. “It’s been so long,” he said while a tear trickled down his cheek.

“It feels like I just saw you a few weeks ago,” she said.

“Ha! Like no time has passed at all. Oh, I’ve missed you, Mitsuko. I don’t know how you’ve survived so long without support. But it’s time to come home now.”

“That might be difficult while we’re sealed within a barrier dome,” Mitsuko replied dryly.

“Oh, that’s true.” He took a deep sigh. “It’s unfortunate. I wanted to introduce you to your new sister.”

Mitsuko took a step back, freeing herself from her pa’s grasp with a slight push. “My… sister.”

“Yes. She’s three years old now, can you believe it? I sent you letters while you were in Hon but they must not have reached you.”

Mitsuko had requested to Emperor Sasaki’s servants that all letters addressed to her by her father be burned. Maybe not the most rational decision in hindsight. Still, she wanted as little contact with this murderer as possible. A clean cut. Honestly, he should have just considered himself lucky that she didn’t send an envoy to the village for a proper investigation on the death of her mother.

Her pa’s face darkened. “And then I went to the city to find you in person and the guards laughed in my face. I was turned away. They refused to listen to reason.”

Another request of hers. She’d asked them to treat anyone matching her father’s description as a madman and send him away. A satisfied smile threatened to pierce her stern expression as she imagined the exchange between the guards and her pa.

“Half-sister,” Mitsuko corrected.

“What?”

“I have a half-sister.”

“Well, obviously. I’m not a necromancer.” He chuckled nervously and Mitsuko’s anger flared.

“I’m glad Piaki survived the experience.”

“She’s young and full of life. I don’t think this will be your last sibling!” Her father beamed completely oblivious to Mitsuko’s implication.

In that moment, she hated this man with the entirety of her being. Far more than a womanizer like Akuto or Sam. Her loathing ran deep with roots in every happy memory she’d experienced with her pa. Every silly conversation and game she’d played with him as a child. Every meal they’d enjoyed together. Every moment he’d held her. It was all corrupted. Tarnished by the truth.

“I’ll never forgive you,” she realized out loud.

He blinked. “For marrying Piaki? Please, Mitsuko, I thought you would be over this childish jealousy of your stepmother.”

Mitsuko shook her head and barked a humorless laugh. “Even now, years later, you refuse to even acknowledge what you’ve done. You won’t ever change. Cowardice is an integral part of who you are. And I can’t forgive that. Not even if I had a thousand more loops.”

His face scrunched up in confusion. “I—”

“This conversation is over,” Mitsuko cut him off. “Thank you for telling me about the existence of my sister. Rescuing her from you adds to the long list of reasons for me to successfully escape this archipelago."

“Rescuing… wait, Mitsuko, what are you talking about?” He attempted to chase after her but she cast Mend on his pants. He yelped in surprise as the spell dragged him away from her.

As she reached the inn’s exit, she turned around. Every eye in the inn was now on her. Food forgotten and tankards of drink frozen halfway to patrons’ mouths. So much for being discreet.

“I am the most powerful mage on this island,” Mitsuko announced. “I’ve slain legends that you’ve heard of only in fearful whispers. I fraternize with kings and emperors. No mere blade can slay me or fire burn me. Spells crumble in my presence. I am an immortal being unlike any you’ve ever seen. And I will free you all from this cage.”

Silence.

“You’re mad,” her pa whispered.

Mitsuko smiled with teeth. “Pray that I am not, for your lives are forfeit to my whims.”

She let the inn’s door swing shut as she departed.

“Nice speech. A bit pompous and self-aggrandising while remaining mostly accurate. I approve of a bit of exaggeration for dramatic effect.”

Mitsuko glanced at a nearby fence where a black cat with glowing green eyes groomed himself.

“Did you get the information?”

“Is that a question?” Sterling winked at her.

“Yes. You know it is.”

“Follow me.” Sterling hopped off the fence and padded down the purple cobblestone road. “You do know that a normal blade could still kill you? And fire still does burn you if it touches your skin. And most people don’t whisper about Anemone and Rodrick. At least, not unless some wild rumors have taken root in the last thousand years.”

“I am aware,” Mitsuko said to the cat.

“Just wanted to make certain. I do believe though that you are likely the most powerful mage on the island. That seems to be an accurate assessment from all I have seen.”

They strolled through the town’s streets. Away from the inn, nobody gave her and the cat a second glance. Most were still absorbed by the dome’s appearance overhead. She had a suspicion her anonymity would be short lived.

Sterling guided her to an unassuming home tucked between two more grandiose buildings. Unlike the location of the inn and the streets leading this direction, the people here dressed in pristine outfits and avoided her on principle, often crossing the street to avoid her.

She lifted the metal door knocker and let its weight clack against the door. No response. She tried again. Still nothing.

“You’ve certain this is the right house?” Mitsuko asked. “You didn’t get mixed up?”

Sterling raised his nose into the air and sniffed. “She was in the bobble shop, just the same as when you saw her in the previous loop and I followed her until I saw her enter not half an hour ago.”

This time when Mitsuko knocked, she simultaneously cast Deteriorate on the metal door knocker. The impact left a gash in the door and nearly threatened to knock it from its hinges. The clang this time turned heads across the street.

A few seconds later, the door swung open, revealing a human woman dressed in a purple silk gown.

“Hooligan! What are you doing to my home? I will call the guards and have you locked in stockades for months!”

“I’m here to sell you some enchanted rings.” Mitsuko tossed the jewelry at her.

The woman fumbled and caught one while the other fell to the door step and rolled away. Mitsuko cast Mend and it snapped back up to her hand.

“What is…” The woman trailed off as she leaned in close to examine the enchanted ring. She turned it over in her hand. “A ring of dominionation? This is quite dangerous. And I assume the matching one is a ring of subjugation?”

Mitsuko frowned down at the ring in her palm. So that was the expensive gift Akuto wanted to bless her with. Lovely. How thoughtful of him.

“Yes. I’m here to sell them. They’re rare.”

“You don’t have the look of a local. Where did you acquire these? They’re illegal in most countries. Edgeland likely lacks the regulations but other than that….”

“So, you won’t buy them?” Mitsuko asked, resisting the urge to sigh.

“Well, they’re not illegal here on the archipelago. There aren’t any specific laws regarding them as there’s never been any reason to create such a law. And I’m simply a collector. No one will protest me holding onto a pair.” She licked her lips and glanced over Mitsuko’s shoulder. “What’s your starting price? I have no doubt we can barter it down to something reasonable.”

“I don’t want coins. I simply need enough money to convince The Terror to take me to Porcelain Island.”

“The Terror. Is that a ship? The Sailor’s Guild have locked down all trade and are heavily regulating all departing vessels.”

Mitsuko did her best to keep annoyance from showing on her face. She’d visited the guild after her arrival in this loop. It took only a few minutes for her to realize the bureaucracy would wall her off from any meaningful progress from there.

“You’re claiming you can’t do it?” Mitsuko asked. She extended a hand for the return of the ring.

“No!” The woman clutched the ring. “I said nothing of the sort. It simply won’t be an easy endeavor. However… in exchange for both rings I will send me servants to care for the task.”

“Deal,” Mitsuko said instantly. “However, I’ll keep the second ring until after I step onto the ship. I’ll send it back to you with whatever errand-boy you send.”

The woman blinked and then smiled. While Mitsuko had likely been swindled, it didn’t matter much so long as she got where she needed to be in this loop.

The ring seller glanced at Mitsuko’s other hand with greed glinting in her eyes as she examined the ice ring. “About th—”

“Not for sale.”
