“On your left!” Samantha shouted as she fired a glowing arrow straight through the gaping maw of a giant snake that was lunging for her. Not even waiting to see if her arrow had the effect she wanted, she spun to the side in the air, snapping one of her wings out to slice clean through the snake’s head as it missed her by mere inches. Of course, the monster they were currently fighting had more than just the one head. A fact that was readily apparent as Asher slipped into the astral to dodge a powerful bite from behind.

“Thanks!” he called out after returning and elongating Crescent to stab directly into the snake’s brain. “How many heads are left?!”

“I don’t know!” Samantha admitted, twisting and maneuvering in the air to dodge out of the way of four more heads that trailed after her. Flying directly into a tree, Samantha melded into the thick bark, narrowly avoiding having her legs bitten off as the heads all crashed into the solid tree at the same time. Rather than their immense mass knocking the tree down, instead, their teeth shattered as they tried and failed to bite through it, coming to a dead stop.

Samantha’s Tree Mender skill didn’t just let her heal trees. With a quick flex of her will, she could temporarily make them indestructible. Combined with her Woodland Stride skill allowing her to physically phase into and through trees, she had the perfect emergency escape combination anytime a monster too powerful for her to take on reared its ugly head.

Or heads, in this case.

Hurling two of his daggers, Asher grinned as each one sank deep into the eyes of two of the heads, putting them down for the count and causing the other two to roar at him as they turned their attention toward him instead of Samantha. Yet the moment they did so, the hunter shot back out of the tree she’d been hiding within, easily flying straight between them and slicing through their necks with each of her wings. As the last two heads fell to the ground, Asher motioned for her to come down.

“I think that’s the last of them,” he admitted, carefully scanning their surroundings as he waited for another head to lunge out toward them. The monster they’d just taken on had been like a hydra without an actual body. It had slithered through the treeline toward them with nearly a dozen heads all snapping at the same time, which had made for a pretty interesting fight. Somehow they’d managed to take it down without either of them getting bitten, which had been a small miracle.

“These monsters are just getting weirder and weirder,” Samantha muttered as she yanked free the few arrows she’d shot into its scaly hide. “And it’s a lot harder to fight with this thing on my head. Honestly, I think that’s the real reason Moxy was so fine with sitting this one out.”

“I’m with you there, it’s pretty cumbersome,” Asher admitted, tapping at his own helmet with a finger. “I’m just glad they’re as sturdy as Opal promised.”

He and Samantha had spent the last day and a half running around within the heart of the forest, doing their damndest to find the lich. Similar to how Zygrul had set up magical defenses to prevent Rulfar from tracking it to more than just a rough location on the mountain, it seemed Jormon had done something similar. Samantha could tell thanks to Natural Guide that there was a structure somewhere within this particular chunk of the heart of the forest, but that still left them with about a hundred square miles of terrain to explore. All they could do was wander up and down the forest, winding through the dense foliage as they hunted for the lich’s base. All the while fighting against the diseased monsters that littered the forest.

“Almost twenty thousand shards from that one,” Samantha said, her eyebrows raised as she looked toward him. “Split it?”

“You take those. You took out more of the heads anyway,” Asher said, glancing at his latest notifications from a day and a half of fighting deadly monsters. Even if it wasn’t nearly as fun as fighting against people, at least he was getting something out of all this.

Permanence - lvl 30

Spatial Lock - lvl 33

Astral Dip - lvl 38

“Think we’re getting close?” he asked as he dismissed his notifications. “I’m leaning on Secret Seeker as much as I can, but I think finding the lair is going to be all you this time around. Your Forest element has already proven to be invaluable.”

“It does feel nice to get a chance to stretch my wings,” Samantha smiled, wiping her hands free of snake blood. “But just like the last time you asked, I don’t know. I can tell we're close, but that’s about all Natural Guide will give me.”

“Wizards,” Asher sighed, shaking his head. “Well, let’s get back to it.”

With that, they took off once more, doing their best to keep an eye on their surroundings as they made haste through the woods. Samantha was able to run in a straight line, ignoring trees, underbrush, and basically anything other than monsters themselves thanks to Woodland Stride. Asher had a slightly tougher time following her, having to time Astral Dip correctly, but a quick use of Distortion here or there to compress space was all he needed to catch up to her when he started lagging behind. As much as he wished they had the full team with them, the two of them really were able to make far quicker time all on their own.

The hours passed, and Asher and Samantha continued carving their way through the heart of the forest. They took down a gecko with poison-filled sacks that was all but invisible, saved from its ambush thanks solely to Asher’s Shard Detection skill he’d borrowed. They wiped out a swarm of far-too-large bees, with Asher dragging the entire hive into the astral realm and letting them quickly tire themselves out as Samantha guarded the two of them in a cocoon of her wings. But it wasn’t until they finished up what had to have been somewhere around their twentieth fight of the day that Asher made up his mind.

“I think that settles it,” he said, poking at the lizard that had sprinted out of the forest at them at top speed, nearly biting his feet off before he even sensed its presence. If it hadn’t been for Samantha’s quick shooting, he might have gotten that level up he’d been waiting on all this time for Eternal Regeneration after all. “This lich has to have a Poison element.”

“I thought it was Disease we were dealing with?” Samantha asked as she retrieved her arrow. “You think he has both?”

“It makes the most sense,” Asher said, thinking back to the sight of Rulfar’s infection. “The speed of which that disease took hold of Rulfar… he said it himself it felt almost like poison. I think the lich has both elements, and we’re probably dealing with a blended skill. Or worse. I mean, think about the kinds of monsters we’ve been attacked by so far.”

“After that strange wolf with the quills? Snakes, bees, various reptiles… all things known for their venoms,” Samantha said, giving him a startled look. “Wait, that would mean Jormon is influencing the monsters that spawn in the heart of the forest. How is that possible?”

“Not a clue. But I think we’ve already established that it’s best to assume the rules as we know them don’t matter much when it comes to the Council of Death. If they were able to find a way to rip an element out of someone’s soul, I wouldn’t put figuring out how to influence monster spawns to be beyond them by any means. And that still begs the question, what’s the third element we’re dealing with here? If we’re lucky, it’s something completely unrelated to his disease-poison combination. If we’re unlucky… Well, let’s hope we’re lucky.”

Asher had already preemptively switched his Complete Resilience immunity over to Poison, but he didn’t know how much of an effect that would end up having. The one downside to his skill’s third-tier effect was that it only worked against energy related to the elements. Poison was an actual, physical thing. It would probably save him from any skills like Loratta’s Compound Toxicity she’d used against him ages ago, but not so much from the initial poison itself. Thankfully, that was what he had the purge effect for, so he wasn’t too worried.

A few more hours passed as they kept hunting, and the sun slowly began to set on their second full day within the heart of the forest. Just as Asher was preparing to suggest they call it a day and head back to the capital, Samantha gasped.

“We’re close!” she said, grabbing his hand and dragging him forward. Thankfully, she didn’t try and drag him physically through any trees, as that would have been a bit painful. “Look!”

After pulling him for a few hundred feet, the two of them paused as they found themselves looking at the strangest fort Asher had ever seen. Constructed almost entirely from wood, the fort stretched right up to the tops of the trees making up the forest without breaking through the canopy. It looked to be almost as large as the royal palace itself, though its thick, outer walls hid the interior of the fort from sight. There was a simple but gigantic door set in the side of the fort, looking like it would need a team of people with Strength elements working in unison in order to open.

“We actually found it,” Asher muttered, careful to keep his voice low. He didn’t see any guards or monsters, but this close to their prey, there was no sense in risking anything. “What do you think? This has to be it, right?”

“There aren’t any other structures within the heart of the forest,” Samantha confirmed with an excited grin. “Jormon is somewhere inside there.”

“Unless he figured out we were coming and already ran away,” Asher pointed out, wincing as Samantha’s excitement about finding the lich for them died down a bit. “But either way, this is fantastic! Seriously, your Forest element has been insanely useful! What do you think, head back and grab the others? We could launch an assault on the fort within the hour if we wanted. I’d offer to scout ahead, but after the King of Death managed to disable some of my Spatial skills from working… I’d rather not get trapped in there without you guys.”

“I appreciate you learning from your past mistakes,” Samantha said, her smile coming back in full force as she took his hand. “Let’s head back and prepare. Honestly, while striking under the cover of night isn’t a bad idea, it might be smart to wait until tomorrow so we can see better. Darkvision potions are all well and good, but I prefer being able to see in color.”

“Fair enough,” Asher chuckled as he shifted his Eternal Mark to a nearby tree. “Maybe we could—”

A wave of sound unlike anything Asher had ever before washed over him, triggering some sort of ancestral reflex to shrink back and cower as the roar of a creature countless times larger than himself erupted from within the fort. Before Asher could even ask what the hell could possibly make a noise like that, something smashed into the other side of the wall that they were on hard enough to shake the entire structure and the very trees they were standing between.

Asher’s heart skipped a beat as his Shard Detection skill lit up, and he swallowed painfully as he glanced up at the tip of the six-story wall, fearing what he would see at the top. He wasn’t able to tell how many shards something held, but the fact that he was getting a reading at all was a dead giveaway as to what was on the other side of that wall. Jormon’s base wasn’t quite as undefended as they’d first hoped. Based on the power of that roar and the size of the quake…

Jormon had the mother of all monsters tucked away within his fort.
