The entire cavern, or rather, what was left of it, writhed and thrashed as Tillienne continued her assault.  Skippy had dealt grievous damage to the fungal constructs, but the sheer power thrumming in the air made it clear that the fight was far from over.  And with Kyle performing emergency triage on their overpowered companion, that left her alone to face the tide of attacks, at least for a while.  Just like Tillienne wanted.

Back home, she’d been hailed a genius.  Her quick studies and combat ability earned her recognition from the Queensguard, and she was put on a path to become one of the newest Royal Guards.  Once she had enough experience, at least.  Beginning her Practicum, she’d only affirmed that belief, rising to sit among the elite of the elite.  While Arhades stood a rung above, she felt confident that she rested at the number two spot, until she laid eyes on Kyle.

At first, she questioned her identification skill.  How could a nobody be a threat to her?  It didn’t make any sense.  In talking with him, her confusion had only grown.  She’d been convinced he had to have either been a hidden heir to a powerful legacy, or otherwise scooped up and trained by a powerful backer.  But no, he truly was just a random wanderer from a backwater world.  And somehow, he was stronger than her.

Their sparring session was jarring, and Tillienne was still embarrassed by her reaction to the loss.  Then the word came out about his Phase Three, followed not long after by the news circulating through the Verdant Republic about Er’Mithren.  Kyle was fighting for his life against C Grades, while Tillienne was taking safe, calculated risks along her prescribed path.  By the time they finally met again for the job on Kertan Six, Kyle had well and truly left her behind.  He’d been hunting C Grades with Arhades, refining his skills to the absolute apex for D Grade.  She brushed it off as an anomaly, until she met the diminutive skrell.  He, too, was a bona fide monster, somebody who exceeded every expectation for what should have been possible for a D Grade.

When they fought Reynolds, she had been the weak link.  When the floating settlement crashed on them, she had been the one to fall.  When Kyle was ambushed by the man known as Valentine, she was the one who needed to be saved, leaving Kyle to face him alone.  She had simply been too weak.  But no longer.

Tillienne let the frustration build inside her as she shifted her grip on the axe to hold it in one hand, another axe appearing from a charm in her free hand.  The lance and shield shimmered and flowed, their shape changing into short, thick blades as well.  At the same time, her armor thickened around her, leaving no exposed skin at all.  Fungal spears leapt toward her, but they were cut to ribbons as the methodical swings of her weapons sped up with every passing moment, creating an area of carnage surrounding her on all sides.

Her primary boosting skill, Scarlet Ascent, was already active.  It was a quality skill that gave strong overall increases, and enabled her flight.  Now, she activated her second.  The moment she did, her head swam as her frustration at her own impotence rose to a fever-pitch.  At the same time, her muscles screamed at her under the strain of two boosting skills at once.  Berserker’s Might improved all of her physical attributes to incredible levels, but made her thoughts cloudy, harder to focus on anything other than the fight.

Fortunately, she maintained enough presence of mind to activate one other skill, courtesy of her second class.  Greater Regeneration took the bite away from her screaming muscles as it fought to reduce the damage from the overlapping boosting skills, though Tillienne hardly noticed.  Her eyes were laser-focused on the bisected remains of the fallen central pillar.  Even now, they gave off an impressively powerful aura; a problem she intended to rectify.

More tendrils sought to slow her advance as the fallen pieces tried to reconnect, but Tillienne was at the eye of a tornado of flashing blades and crimson mana.  Anything and everything that approached was summarily shredded as she continued her dive toward the nearest half of the fallen pillar.  She hardly registered as the cloud of toxins in the air began to shift, changing from a deep violet to an almost sickly yellow, until a sizzling sound finally broke through her berserker haze.  She noticed with an odd sense of detachment that the new spores seemed to be highly acidic, slowly trying to eat through her armor.

A race to the finish it is, she thought, letting out a primal roar as dove toward her target.  The pillar released a twister of mana to try and blow Tillienne back, but her forward momentum was too much to easily overcome.  One axe carved a deep furrow into the pillar’s wriggling flesh, the short swords following after to widen the cut and create an opening for another devastating cleave with the second axe.

Another bone-rattling screech filled the chamber as she continued her butchery, soon finding herself nearly through the first portion of the pillar.  Waves of razor-sharp crimson energy continued to slice away at the fungal mass, inhibiting its regeneration as the DeRosa’s aura in the room decreased appreciably.  One down.

She turned to face the second half of the fallen pillar, which was almost entirely wrapped in fungal tendrils rising from the cavern floor.  She flew toward it, until her Predator’s Intuition warned her of an attack.  She dodged to her left, just in time to avoid being skewered by a dark shape falling like a meteor from above.  Tillienne turned to face it, just as a fungal spear faster than any before slipped past her defenses, breaking through her brittle armor and piercing her left shoulder.

The pain barely registered as she squared up to the shape, only now visible within the cloud of spores.  Four massive tendril legs supported an oversized, bulbous torso, two thick, claw-tipped arms visible on either side.  The skin of the fungal creature was jet black, though two piercing violet eyes shone through the haze of spores.  Tillienne could practically feel the figure before her resonating with the remaining piece of the pillar, and it all clicked into place.   That’s DeRosa, she thought, wincing at the burning in her shoulder.

Wait, why does it hurt?  The thought flickered across her mind for just a moment as she narrowly dodged another spear, then deflected a third off her axe.  Ignoring the growing discomfort, Tillienne released a wave of crimson energy at DeRosa, which he blocked with a simple mana barrier, entirely neutralizing the attack.  Undeterred, she launched several more attacks as she slowly circled DeRosa, who blocked each one methodically.

A pathetic attempt – DeRosa began to rasp, but before he could finish, Tillienne was off like a shot toward the fallen piece of the pillar.  Her attacks had been little more than distractions to get into a better position to bypass the monster, and she certainly wasn’t going to stand around and trade blows while the madman had a passive amplifier active.  Especially not with the throbbing ache in her left side.  Did I get hit again? She wondered, though as her target got in range, she refocused.

She could feel mana building behind her, so she’d need to make these attacks count.  She made a massive horizontal swing with the axe in one of her right hands, slashing through most of the connecting tendrils on the floor.  The axe in her left hand was supposed to follow, but at the last minute her shoulder locked up as a searing pain wracked her body.  Both of her boosting skills forcibly deactivated, and she looked down in horror as she saw inky-black tendrils of fungal flesh erupt from her wound, fragmenting her armor further as the acidic spores began to sizzle against her flesh.

Gritting her teeth, she reverted her short swords and the axe in her left hand back into charms, quickly grabbing a bottle of the prepared antifungals and smashing it against the injury.  The sizzling sound redoubled as the liquid fought against the fungal infestation.

She whipped her head backward as the mana behind her hit a crescendo, and barely managed to get a shield raised in time before she was blasted into the ground beside the remaining piece of the central pillar.  Another condensed explosion of mana struck her, cracking her armor and causing her to stagger back.

As I was saying.  A pathetic attempt from impudent whelps such as you.  To arrive here, in my domain, and expect me to simply die?  A foolish notion.

He continued to talk, but Tillienne’s attention was drawn by the sharp pain all through her left side.  It’s spreading, she realized.  And with DeRosa’s control of the fungus, time wasn’t on her side.  And, with that, she knew exactly what she needed to do.  Her right arms fell limp to her side, the battleaxe returning to its form as a charm around her wrist.

Good, DeRosa rasped.  You realize your folly, and your fate.  Soon-

Before he could finish, the lance reappeared in Tillienne’s upper right arm.  She took as firm a stance as she was able, and launched a flurry of thrusting attacks toward the damaged pillar, releasing piercing blasts of crimson energy directly into it.  Barriers appeared to intercept, but many were simply too late.  The amplification pillar was already on the brink thanks to the accumulated damage from their assault, and these final strikes were enough.  DeRosa’s aura noticeably weakened once more, though that hardly mattered as she was lifted into the air and slammed against one of the walls nearby.

Grasping fungal tendrils erupted out of the wall, constricting around Tillienne’s already damaged armor as the pain across the left side of her body began to spread.  The lance reverted to a charm that dangled from her wrist as the dark form of DeRosa approached, malice behind his glowing, violet eyes.  Truly, impudent to the very end.  At least you can be satisfied that you caused me great inconvenience – a larger setback than I had anticipated.

Despite herself, Tillienne smiled.

“You really don’t know when to shut up, do you?” she asked, looking over his shoulder.

DeRosa looked to be about to respond, when he finally turned to see the golden comet flying his way.  Violet energy sprang up around DeRosa just before a blast of force tore up the entire area around him.  You’re not the only monster in this cavern, Tillienne thought, allowing her eyes to close.  We’re leaving the rest to you, Kyle.
