In Vector’s vision, the Earl’s Estate rose ahead—imposing and luxurious, a silent testament to the Lionheart Empire’s wealth and militaristic authority.

The Estate was encircled by six-meter-high, one-meter-thick stone walls, reinforced with metal plating at critical structural points, especially the corners. A metallic glint caught his attention. His gaze shifted toward the ramparts, where the source revealed itself—a roaring lion etched into a soldier’s chest plate. The emblem gleamed far brighter than the rest of the armor. They had taken far greater care in polishing the symbol than the protection itself, and the shine made that fact unmistakably clear.

The guards, as usual, were alert yet relaxed, patrolling the walls and occasionally glancing below for any unusual activity.

But Vector’s focus was not on their mildly lazy behavior. It was on the rifles slung over their shoulders—the flintlock muskets. In peacetime, he reasoned, such laxity was inevitable.

Maintaining a steady pace, he reached the main gate. It was fortified with steel and embedded with sharp spikes jutting outward. What purpose did these metals and spikes serve, other than creating a spine-chilling appearance? Frightening. Intimidating. That was the conclusion forming in his mind.

A needless waste of money and resources.

Four guards stood at the gate, disciplined and unmoving, the real deal. All of them were First-Stage Warriors. Vector greeted them and raised his identity token for routine inspection. One of the soldiers waved him through impatiently, gesturing for him not to waste time on trivialities. As a gardener, Vector had long since become a familiar face among the guards.

With firearms, the metallic dawn of civilization, resting on their shoulders and short swords strapped to their left waists, he passed through the smaller gate embedded within the massive main doors.

Inside, a cluster of structures came into view. Plumes of smoke rose from several buildings, while others—two stories tall—were clearly barracks for soldiers. The smoke-emitting structures housed weapon production facilities, but only for cold weapons. Firearms and other strategic siege weapons were not produced here. In a border city, placing such facilities openly would have been foolish.

Surveying the scene, it hardly resembled an Estate. From within, it felt more like a fortress—an unspoken threat to anyone who dared rebel.

His expression remained calm, but his mind was alert. Better to be prepared for unforeseen circumstances than to be caught off guard later. Wearing a servile demeanor, he moved through the densely packed buildings, paths he had traversed countless times. He knew every nook and corner so well that he could have walked them with his eyes closed.

Everything appeared routine. Guards stood at their posts. Workers went about their duties. No one spared him a second glance. No suspicious looks. No heightened vigilance.

It seemed his cover remained intact.

The attacker truly had acted on a sudden whim—only after discovering his identity as the gardener.

Under the morning light, a mighty and luxurious palace caught his eyes, gleaming brilliantly. A legacy of the Novastra Empire—now serving the Lionheart Empire. And his destination. The City Lord’s mansion.

Before Lionheart’s occupation, the City Lord’s Estate had consisted of only this palace. But now, measuring the distance from the main gate to the palace’s lattice gate, the additional structures erected after the occupation expanded the Estate to a scale comparable to an entire commercial district. For a mid-sized city, it was an enormous tract of land—claimed exclusively by the Lionhearts.

The slums, too, had been born as a consequence of this land acquisition and the displacement of the general populace surrounding the City Lord’s mansion. The wealthy, who once lived near the palace, had moved elsewhere, while the displaced commoners were pushed into overcrowded districts—or worse, into the slums.

The mansion itself was surrounded by boundary walls, though these were largely decorative, designed to enhance its grandeur rather than defend it. Passing through the lattice gate—where two soldiers stood stationed at either side, casting him only a brief glance before resuming their guard—Vector made his way toward the gardener’s shed.

It was a small room tucked into the servants’ wing, closer to a storeroom than a workspace.

Inside, neatly arranged tools hung from iron hooks—shears, trowels, pruning saws, and other implements of varying sizes. Some were already missing, taken by his fellow gardeners—ordinary workers, not Resistance members.

Selecting what he needed, a pair of small and medium-sized shears, he joined the others to prune the plants and trees.

His first stop was near the study room of the City Lord, Earl Varent Kaelthorn.

He began trimming, his hands moving methodically while his focus remained fixed on any murmurs or conversation drifting from inside. Kaelthorn’s mornings were always the same—immersed in poetry or official documents. The study served as both his private refuge and his office.

That routine was precisely why Vector always worked here.

The pine trees had already been trimmed, yet his shears passed over them again and again, snipping at leaves so insignificant they scarcely needed removal. A performance—nothing more.

If he failed to gather any intelligence today, there was always tomorrow.

On Solasra, the Earl often went hunting near the Naga Forest to the north of Carnatic—one of the few forests known to house beasts as powerful as Fourth-Stage Warriors. Its core region had even been declared forbidden land.

Solasra reminded Vector of Earth’s weekdays. It was closest to what Earth would have called Sunday. But in Ethereum, the concept of holidays did not exist. Solasra was treated like any other day.

A working day.

After finishing the pruning around the study room, he deliberately made his presence known in several areas of the Estate before appearing near the butlers’ workplace in the early afternoon.

The second spot.

There were only two locations within the Estate from which he regularly obtained critical intelligence regarding military deployments or actions against the Resistance. Information that allowed them to anticipate movements, intercept convoys, loot firearms, and seize resources.

The study room. And the butlers’ workplace.

In the mornings, the butler and the eunuchs—the servant-guards of the Kaelthorn household—were usually occupied attending Varent Kaelthorn directly. But by early afternoon, they gathered in their office, immersed in administrative work.

Carefully trimming excess leaves nearby, Vector kept his posture relaxed—servile, unremarkable.

But his ears were sharp.

Waiting. Listening. Hoping for even a fragment of information—especially anything related to the attacker.

Since his baptism by Chaos Energy, his five senses had become razor-sharp—far beyond what they had been before. He could now hear whispers through brick walls, detect footsteps from distances that would have been impossible for ordinary Blood Energy awakeners.

Yet the afternoon yielded nothing.

Only mundane whispers. Petty gossip. Trivial complaints.

That was all the fruit of his labor today.

Later that night...

Vector sat cross-legged in his apartment, already repaired by members of the Astral Alliance.

He was preparing to practice Body Refinement using the Simulation Space of the System.

The Knowledge Database interface hovered before him, displaying Martial Arts and Body Refinement manuals. But as he skimmed through them, realization dawned quickly—every single one matched the manuals stored in his family’s archives.

Nothing new.

Frowning faintly, he questioned the System.

The System’s calm, neutral voice echoed in his mind as the interface reshaped itself before his eyes.

--- [System Notification]

[The Martial Art and Body Refinement Manual knowledge provided is based on the Host’s learned manuals and memories in this life.] ---

As expected.

Everything available mirrored what he had already seen within the Astrum family archives. No additional arts. No hidden techniques. No mysterious manuals waiting to be uncovered.

Despite being called a Library, the System contained nothing truly new—aside from Earth’s knowledge, which remained completely locked, requiring the creation of initial-tier technologies to unlock.

For a moment, he felt the urge to curse the System.

But he restrained himself.

After all, it still possessed heaven-defying functions—chief among them, Simulation. And that was precisely what he intended to use now.

Shifting his focus to the Function tab, he selected Simulation.

Begin simulation.

The Simulation initiated—his very first experience within the Simulation Space, a sub-dimensional construct rooted in the Void Domain.

His physical body vanished from the real world, replaced by a holographic projection. If someone attempted to touch it, their hand would pass straight through—encountering no resistance, no sensation.

Yet the System was not without safeguards.

If any disturbance occurred—at his door, or anywhere within his room—the System would immediately alert him. The Simulation would be terminated instantly, and his real body would be returned to its original position without delay.

Only then did Vector fully relax. And step into the Simulation Space.

…

He appeared in a vast grassland, the default simulated environment. In the distance, nothing was visible except endless stretches of grass, rolling gently beneath an open sky.

To test the Simulation, he formed a thought.

The world changed instantly.

He now stood within a canyon of towering glass and steel; every surface wrapped in cascading LED screens. The road beneath his feet was a patchwork of dark granite and gray asphalt, reflecting the jittering glow of advertisements and the cold blues of massive billboards. Above, white clouds drifted across a sky washed in clean shades of blue.

Times Square.

In his previous life, he had never been there—only seen it through screens. And yet now, he was standing within it, experiencing everything firsthand.

Silent.

Empty.

A place that was always overflowing with people—day and night—now stood completely devoid of life.

The absence was unsettling.

But he did not emerge in this simulated scene any more.

Without wasting time, he dismissed the scene and returned the environment to its default state—the endless grassland.

He sat cross-legged upon the earth.

It was time to experience the true power of the Simulation.

He closed his eyes.

The grass brushed softly against his palms, cool and unreal in a way only the Simulation could produce. The moment his breathing steadied, a faint sensation stirred within him.

At first, it was barely perceptible.

A thin stream. No—thinner than that. More like mist.

Something was seeping into his body.

Not forcefully. Not violently.

Slow. Gentle. Persistent.

This sensation was different from Blood Energy.

It wasn’t circulating through his veins in the familiar way. It didn’t surge or respond to intent. Instead, it slipped through his skin, passed through muscle and bone, and drifted inward—as if his body itself were breathing it in.

His eyes snapped open.

If not for his heightened perception—if not for the Void Body—he would never have noticed it at all. Even now, it was subtle enough that an ordinary Blood Energy awakener would have dismissed it as imagination.

System. He asked the question. There’s an energy entering my body, It’s faint, but constant. What is it?

The reply came without delay.

[System Notification]

[The energy fusing with the Host is Chaos Energy. It is passively fusing due to the Host’s physique: Void Body.]

[The Simulation Space contains a high density of Origin Energy. Chaos Energy occupies a major portion of Origin Energy as consideration to Host’s physique.]

His forehead creased as he absorbed the System’s response.

“Chaos Energy…” he murmured—though no one was there to hear him.

Chaos Energy, a form of Origin Energy, was ubiquitous. His Void Body was naturally absorbing it, refining itself little by little, even without deliberate cultivation.

But then—

Why didn’t he feel the same sensation in Ethereum? Was Chaos Energy not supposed to be present in abundance?

He voiced his question.

Why don’t I sense this in the real world?

[System Notification]

[The System has detected no presence of Origin Energy in Ethereum.]

Vector’s expression darkened.

Wasn’t Origin Energy supposed to be omnipresent throughout the universe? That was what the inheritance had taught him—that Origin Energy permeated every corner of the Void, every universe, every existence.

And yet…

[System Notification]

[Unknown. However, System has determined that Origin Energy has been sealed or blocked across the entirety of Ethereum.]

A chill ran down Vector’s spine.

Sealed? Who could possibly seal off the foundational energy, Origin Energy… from an entire planet?

His mind reeled with possibilities.

The implications were staggering.

Without Origin Energy, the entire power structure shifted. He knew from the information he received through the Will of Void that the Origin Energy, fundamental energy, is the laws of the universe. That's why there are no Origin practitioners in Ethereum. Only martial arts and Body Refinement Manuals remained.

And then—

As a realization struck him.

His mind turned back to the planet’s puzzling past. Ancient Ruins, architectural wonders—predating recorded history… everything hinted at a forgotten age. A history wiped from the world’s memory.

This planet’s history… Someone didn’t just erase it. They also sealed the Origin Energy.

If that was true…

Then Ethereum’s past was far more dangerous—and far more mysterious—than he had ever imagined.

What kind of war could lead to such a result?

What kind of power?

Who could do something like this?

Vector stood silently, the weight of the unknown pressing down on him like an invisible force. But now wasn’t the time to dwell on ancient secrets. He was still too weak, too far from the answers.

He took a deep breath and pushed the thoughts aside.

Yet, something nagged at him.

Then how does the System have access to Origin Energy in the Simulation Space?

[System Notification]

[Simulation Space exists outside Ethereum’s sealed framework.]

[It is anchored within a sub-dimensional domain of the Void. While Origin Energy is sealed from the physical world, it remains accessible within sub-dimensional domains.]

[Access to Origin Energy is limited to Simulation Space. Direct manipulation outside the Simulation Space is currently beyond the System and Host's capabilities.]

Vector exhaled slowly. So, that was it.

And if it had been sealed…

Then it could, theoretically, be unsealed. Another task quietly joined the ever-growing list in his mind.

He thought silently.

Find the reason for the sealing. Find who did it. Find whether it can be broken.

But not now. This was not the time to chase cosmic conspiracies.

Vector pushed his thoughts aside.

For now, he had a body to temper. A foundation to solidify. Chaos Energy continued to fuse with him at a passive rate, but he did not interfere with it. The Void Body would handle that on its own.

Then he shifted his focus to Blood Energy. The foundation of Body Refinement.

But the instant he started he frowned.

The moment he attempted to circulate his Blood Energy through his body, he encountered—

No.

Not resistance.

Absence.

He couldn’t sense it at all.

It was as if he had never awakened Blood Energy in the first place. Before doubt could spiral further, the System’s interface materialized, cold and precise, dispelling the confusion.
