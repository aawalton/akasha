My counterpart hesitates. Just a fraction.

I’m not quite sure why. Getting the opportunity to dance with someone as beautiful and graceful as me is a privilege. One worth dying for, even.

Since he seems a little overwhelmed by the honor, I suppose I’ll be gracious and take the lead. I am, after all, magnanimous.

I move in swiftly, fully intending to keep our second dance a short one.

After all, there’s an entire forest clamoring for my attention, and it would be terribly unprofessional of me—as a dancer—to fixate on a single partner for too long.

He responds in kind.

Tendrils lash out toward me in a flurry of motion utterly devoid of grace.

Annoying, but acceptable.

I am, after all, a genius. I can lead even the most hopelessly clumsy of followers.

A simple spin carries me through the gap between two tendrils, my blade flicking out to guide a third safely away from my body. I step lightly to the side, letting another strike cut harmlessly through empty air.

His lower two tendrils spear into the ground as he lifts his mace.

The same setup again.

Boring.

As if something like that would ever hit me now that I’m setting the beat.

Sure enough, the moment I enter range, the weapon slams down toward me in a brutal arc.

I orbit around it, smooth and effortless, ending up at his back as dirt and grass erupt where I used to be.

He turns—

—and his tendrils burst from the ground in response, angling to turn me into a very stylish Lyn-kebab.

Unacceptable.

I hop cleanly over the wooden spears and let the motion carry me into a loose, almost-barrel-roll of an attack, my blade sweeping toward his head—

—only for something new to happen.

A wall of solid wood erupts from the earth, intercepting my strike in a violent spray of splinters.

Huh.

I suppose it makes sense that a miniboss would have more than two tricks tied to his Aspect.

Still, I’ll never understand the idea of keeping cards hidden in a fight like this.

…

I also politely ignore the fact that I haven’t used Starlight or Starfire even once.

That’s different.

I’m special.

Obviously.

Four tendrils coil around the newly raised wall and surge toward me all at once.

My posture shifts instantly, my defensive state settling in immediately.

I sidestep one.

Receive another.

Then retreat just far enough to let the last two miss.

Hm.

I really should stop calling these “offensive” and “defensive.”

My expression goes distant, thoughtful.

This is, after all, a crucial moment. One that may very well define the rest of my life.

Choosing the perfect names.

Far more important than the Warden, who is actively attempting to violently end my existence.

Naturally, I think.

I ponder.

Thousands of potential names cascade through my mind. An endless river of elegance, menace, and just the right amount of dramatic flair.

Then my feral smile snaps back into place.

Got it.

Unstoppable momentum, paired with ever-burning Starfire.

Blazing Star Dance.

Unshakable stillness, guided by illusory Starlight.

Guiding Star Dance.

Another notification blooms quietly at the edge of my vision.

Ignored. Again.

I’ll check it later.

Preferably when there isn’t a wooden tentacle aiming to punch a golf-ball-sized hole through my throat.

My sword meets it midair and turns it aside.

Behind it, the wooden wall separating me from my partner finally collapses. Apparently it has a duration limit.

Unfortunate.

The Warden looks far more worried than before.

As he should.

After all, I have no further use for him.

I’ve achieved my goal. There’s no reason to hold back anymore.

I drop into a low crouch, posture coiled, and Blazing Star rises to the surface.

Then I Dash.

The distance vanishes in a heartbeat.

His mace sweeps in a brutal horizontal arc, aiming to reduce me to decorative paste across the forest floor.

At the same time, all six tendrils strike.

It doesn’t matter.

I reach him anyway, my blade cutting for his torso.

He ignores it, choosing a damage trade. One I would absolutely lose, considering that mace alone probably outweighs me.

A shame, then.

Because it passes cleanly through the illusion he thought was me.

So do the tendrils.

Reality snaps back into place.

I’m airborne.

My blade is already descending, starfire blazing along its edge, aimed straight for his neck.

His eyes widen.

I give him my most beautiful smile.

He wasn’t the greatest of partners, but his existence let me rediscover myself.

My identity. My passion.

So I’ll give him the respect he’s earned.

The runes along my blade flare. Starfire intensifies. Sapphire light sings along the edge.

“Horizon Slash.”

The wooden wall surges up once more, desperately trying to intercept my finale.

I carve through it. And through his neck.

I land lightly, moss compressing beneath my boots as the Warden’s headless body collapses behind me, already beginning to crumble back into the forest.

Yes.

That will do.

That will do nicely.
