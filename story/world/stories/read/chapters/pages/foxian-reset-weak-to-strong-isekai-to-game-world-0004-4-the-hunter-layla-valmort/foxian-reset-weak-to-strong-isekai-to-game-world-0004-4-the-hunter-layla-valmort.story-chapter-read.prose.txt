Kae had killed King Blue more times than she could count. His mobs weren’t giving nearly enough EXP to level anymore. Normally, a player would be out of Onyx Forest by level 7 at most. Being level 12, anyone could see how the struggle had begun.

Still, thanks to her relentless farming of King Blue, she had earned plenty of a certain item that was low-level but powerful for someone like her.

“You horrible Foxian beast!”

“Shut up already.”

Kae poked him again with her knife, causing him to explode in a blaze of blue slime glory. Being [Sentient] had gone from annoying to downright funny. The Goddess had probably given up on him a long time ago. She hadn’t appeared since the day she made her declaration to kill one another.

“This… and that…”

As the fox was doing her normal pick up, a bright shine appeared in the middle of the loot.

“What the?!”

When Kae gazed over at it, an icon popped up, and her heart nearly leapt from her throat.

[Blue King’s Core]

“Fallen Angel core drops do exist!”

The happy fox reached for it only for an eagle to swoop down, snatching the core in its mouth and soaring skyward. At the top of a tree, the eagle perched on a girl’s arm. She had short red hair and wore the second ascension of Archer, the Hunter’s outfit. Her outfit was practical but stylish: green gloves, a cropped jacket that stopped at her stomach, and short brown pants and boots.

“I can’t believe I got one! Nice find, Dynomight!”

The looting hunter held the [Blue King’s Core] in her hands, admiring Kae’s hard work. Kae had always liked the female hunter aesthetic, but admiration was the last thing on her mind right now. She felt cheated as the annoyed fox approached.

The idea of that caused Kae to want to go mad. Wasting a precious core on a garbage helmet was like committing murder.

Layla danced on the branches above. On her screen, she was probably just spinning her character, but in this world, she was super lively as Kae got closer.

“Hey, that’s looting! Clearly, that was my core! I can report you for that!”

Kae called out. Her ears were even spiking up, but Layla just rubbed her nose and stuck out her tongue.

“I’ve been farming that guy for weeks! I deserve it.”

She crossed her arms, proud of herself.

“But you weren’t here when I stepped in and worked my butt off! Just because it dropped while you were sleeping doesn’t mean you can steal it from others!”

The hunter jumped from the branch and landed in front of the fox. That cocky smile only made Kae’s tail lash back and forth in irritation. Layla circled around her, teasing her with the core she had worked so hard for.

“Level 12? Hm, you wanna pvp for it, newbie?”

The hunter grinned, trying to intimidate her. She sounded like a school bully, but Kae wasn’t about to be pushed around.

“Sure, I’ll beat you for it.”

Without hesitation, Kae selected her, agreeing to a PvP duel. Even with her level 1 weapon and eggshell headgear, she was confident that she could win.

“Wow, you think you can win against someone over double your level? You really are a novice at this game.”

She agreed, and Kae jumped back, dodging her [Rapid Arrows] strike, a basic skill low-ranked Hunters always spammed in pvp.

Kae hadn’t messed with her skill tree much yet. The Foxian had no ranged skills either. But that didn’t deter her. If Kae lost here, in this PvP match, though, she wouldn’t respawn as Layla would.

[Hawk Raid]

That annoying hawk dived at Kae, dazing her and stopping her in place for a short time.

[Rapid Arrows]

A hail of arrows nearly struck the fox, but Kae broke out of the daze and dodged. Another effective Hunter combo, but she had faced better PvP players before and knew how to counter it.

“Oh, that was close.”

Kae murmured.

“You sure you don’t want to give up? You lose EXP, and I’ll be able to loot you if you die!”

Layla taunted, firing more arrows. The nimble fox ran in a circle, dodging her relentless arrow spam.

Kae opened her inventory and hot-keyed her trump card. Killing King Blue countless times had earned her an advantage.

[Ice Spear Spell Scrolls].

Dozens of them.

[Ice Spear Spell Scroll]

The spell is instantly cast on the hunter, dealing -200HP.

“Huh?”

Kae spammed the scroll until the hunter collapsed, defeated. The best part about scrolls was that INT and DEX didn’t matter. The damage was flat and instant.

“Come on, stop taking things from me!”

Layla whined.

“You made the mistake of dueling me. I’m just taking what’s rightfully mine and a little extra for my trouble.”

Kae spared the rest of her gear, leaving Layla to revive with 1HP. It was time to leave Onyx Forest anyway. The fun grind was over. If she wanted to kill the Goddess, she had to move on.

“You should learn your lesson. That’s bannable if you steal someone else’s loot. And it’s just mean to other players.”

Kae crossed her arms and shook her head like a disappointed parent.

“We shouldn’t be mean to each other like that. It makes the game less fun. I hope you learned your lesson, Layla.”

She waved to the defeated hunter and turned to leave.

“Hold up! Are you a vet player on a new account?”

Layla ran up, guzzling potions to heal.

“Huh? Well, something like that. My old account got destroyed by an unruly Goddess, Rexia, and I’m trying to murder her and become a God.”

Layla stared like Kae had twelve heads. The way she was staring at the fox made her a tad self-conscious. She grabbed her fluffy tail in her arms and squeezed it.

“What a strange back story for your character. You’re weird, Kae.”

Her ear twitched, and she gave it a scratch, another Foxian tick she hadn’t quite broken.

“I’m not weird. I’m telling the truth. You’re weird if you don’t believe me.”

Kae continued through the forest, still holding her tail. Layla hovered nearby.

“I just started playing a few weeks ago. Sorry about being a jerk! Ah, do you want to be friends? I don’t have any yet…”

Just a minute ago, she had tried to steal from the Foxian. But she was new to the game.... And being mean would make her no better. So, she put her fluffy tail down and turned back to the misguided hunter.

“No worries, I forgive you, Layla. And sure, let’s be friends.”

Kae extended her hand. Layla took it. The friendship animation of Keostar Online played.

Kae became friends with Layla.

A short jingle rang out that Kae hadn’t heard in a long time. But that act of touching her hand, gripping it firmly, and solidifying her friendship felt real, compared to seeing it happen on a screen.

“Yes, I made my first friend!”

Layla hopped, celebrating with a spin.

Layla really was green. And Kae was a veteran, not a jerk. Just seeing how happy she got from this small interaction made the fox smile softly. It took her back to a time she had forgotten, one long ago, lost to the endless quest and battles she faced on a screen.

“Ah, now that I think about it, do you have a merchant account, Layla?”

Kae was more crippled than she thought. Multiple characters were vital in Keostar Online, especially merchants. If she were stuck inside of Kae Loredom, unless she became a Merchant, she wouldn’t be able to sell items to other players in shop format.

“I do have a trader character. But he’s super weak, and I only use him for shop setup.”

But to the vet, that was good enough.

“How about we both sell the BK core, and you get 20% of the sale? And I’ll mentor you on how to build your hunter, too, if you want.”

Kae suggested.

“Well, it was your BK core in the end. Better than getting reported, I guess. And I still get cash in the end.”

Layla looked embarrassed as she scratched her cheek.

“I don’t get your situation, but I can use my merchant for both of us. And yeah, that would be cool if you could help me build my hunter too.”

She hesitated as they both walked towards the exit of the Onyx Forest. Kae caught on to this strange reaction and perked up.

“What’s wrong?”

“How can you trust me not to just take the core, though, Kae?”

“I can’t.”

Kae admitted.

“I have to put my faith in you and others if I want to accomplish my goal to kill Rexia. So please, don’t betray my trust... I’d be sad.”

Kae hugged her tail again, a comforting motion she’d picked up since becoming a Foxian. Layla rubbed the back of her head, maybe her own human tick.

Not only that, the “I’d be sad” comment at the end made Layla feel even more guilty than she already did.

“You really are a weirdo. But I won’t steal it, I promise. Being beaten by a level 12 novice was embarrassing enough.”

She rubbed the back of her fiery hair, and it even made Kae smile. They both laughed, and a tingling of nostalgia touched Kae’s chest. A feeling that she hasn’t experienced in quite a while.
