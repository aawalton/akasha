Jianyi was lost.

Sort of.

When Minghao had told him the day before that he would have to cover his eyes and be taken deep into the wilderness by him to find his way back to camp, he had thought it was a joke.

It wasn’t.

Before leaving, his teacher had told him that the camp was to the east and that he would now be on his own.

So he roughly knew which direction to take, but he was farther out than he had ever gone on his forays into the area around their camp—and this was truly the direction that had a higher qi density and therefore carried the risk of higher realm beasts.

Minghao had set him a few goals to pass the test: first, find his way home within a single day; second, do so without attracting the attention of any spirit beast; third, hunt a mortal animal or forage for food.

It sounded sensible, but given the episode with the leopard, he wasn’t sure how good he really was at masking his presence from creatures that much more powerful than him.

However, the time for deliberations was over. He needed to move.

Turning east, he was pleased to see that there was little underbrush between the trees, likely due to the dense canopy shielding the ground from sunlight for most of the year. The dry leaves on the ground would make silent movement difficult, though.

He moved forward.

A few hours later, he stopped for a brief break. Masking one’s presence while paying attention to the environment was exhausting. Not so much physically, but mentally.

Now that he was resting, he thought he heard the faint sound of a stream in the distance. That was definitely worth a short detour. He hadn’t used much of his water yet, but one of Minghao’s lessons had been to always refill one's supplies when the opportunity arose—you never knew when the next chance would come.

Fifteen minutes later, he had refilled his water skin and was following the shallow little stream when he came across a patch of spirit grass. It was, without a doubt, the most common natural treasure, and no experienced cultivator would stoop so low as to collect it.

But Jianyi still knew the value of a silver, and while he wouldn’t get a lot of contribution points for the thin green blades of grass, some were better than none, and it would take him just a few minutes.

However, he had learned his lessons and did spend a few moments observing the area to make sure he did not step into a trap.

Fortunately, that wasn’t the case, and he was quickly back on his way.

The forest felt oddly quiet.

Jianyi was usually busy. Busy with training, busy with thinking about the next training, busy with researching how to improve the next training.

In that moment, Jianyi didn’t really have to think about much but making sure his next foot placement was correct.

He had just one more thing to do after this excursion, then his time in the wilderness would come to an end.

What would he be doing if he weren’t training all day, every day?

It was a useless thought—how could he defeat the demons if he weren’t training?

Was there a different life?

He abandoned that line of thinking.

The forest felt really quiet.

What did other people think about?

He thought of a caravan travelling down a road…

No.

How to get to the second form of the Eternal Spear Meditation? Yes, that was a worthwhile thought.

Based on Minghao’s feedback, he would need another couple of weeks to master the Eternal Ray of Sun, but in the evenings, he could already start visualizing how the next form would look and feel.

There was also the project he had set himself of learning how to extract cards from spirits without a spirit needle.

He had been a bit lax in his training of activating techniques with his intent only—he had made some progress, but there was always more.

Always.

The birds singing in the background were quite beautiful and relaxing.

A few hours later, he stopped as he came across two sets of tracks.

Definitely from different species—based on the size, probably beasts.

They looked quite fresh to him.

Most intriguingly, they seemed deep, hasty…

They ran perpendicular to his path. He could just move on. Should just move on.

He had planned his path up to the fourth form of the Eternal Spear Meditation.

He followed the tracks.

Carefully, though.

It didn’t take long for him to start hearing something. At first, it was difficult to identify, but quickly the hisses, crashes, and thumps started to paint a picture. The picture of a fight.

He hesitated again.

Was it wise to move forward?

He was honest enough with himself to know that the answer was no, but for some reason, he still felt an itch.

He wanted to see, wanted to experience, what else the wilderness had to show him.

Slowly, he moved in the direction of the fight.

First, there was nothing, then there was… too much.

Too much movement.

Too much noise.

Too much qi in the air.

After a moment of adjustment, he was finally able to make heads and tails out of what happened.

Two beasts, fighting.

From what he could tell, one of the combatants was a snow fox with two bushy tails. The beast was a bit smaller than a mortal wolf would be. It wasn’t just moving quickly; no, it just was everywhere.

For a moment, Jianyi thought there was more than one, but all of them had the thin blue strip on their back on an otherwise perfectly white fur.

Its opponent made itself known by the constant supply of quills flying through the air at alarming speeds.

The porcupine was not that much bigger than a mortal variant would be, but based on the color of the quills, it was Metal-aligned—rare in the forest—and Jianyi was certain that he did not want to get hit by one of these weapons.

He remained as far away from the confrontation as possible while still being able to follow it, so it was hard to get a good sense of the strength of the creatures, but he was fairly sure that both were at least in the upper range of Qi Condensation.

Maybe even Foundation Establishment.

The fox suddenly appeared beside the porcupine, clawing at its less protected head, only to get almost stabbed by a handful of quills appearing out of nowhere.

It reappeared a good ten paces from the porcupine, unhurt, but breathing hard.

The longer he followed the fight, the more he adjusted his assessment of the creatures’ realm upward. He just couldn’t imagine anyone just one realm above him firing off one technique after the next.

Then again, what did he know…

The one thing he was certain of was that this fight was way beyond him.

It wasn’t quite as dramatic as Elder Sun, whom he could actually not see move when he went all out, but it was bad.

Coming here had been a mistake.

He felt a prickling on the back of his neck, his hair standing up.

Was that from the fight ahead of him?

He cowered in the shadow of the tree before him, slowly trying to confirm that he was alone.

There was nothing he could see.

It was time to leave.

With a final look at the fight—both opponents seemed to be exhausted—he slowly and very, very carefully withdrew.

The feeling of somebody—or something—observing him didn’t leave.

He applied every lesson, every piece of advice Minghao had given him, while making his way back to his path.

When he had made it a hundred paces or so, he paused to look back. First, there was nothing, then he thought he saw a shadow up in the highest branches of a tree.

A bird?

No, too large.

Surely.

Maybe just a shadow.

He moved on.

***

It had taken him a while to recover from that encounter.

First, the speed, power, and density of techniques by the spirit beasts. It truly was another level than him.

On the one hand, that was frightening—especially when thinking of demons wielding that power; on the other, it was also exciting. If he could get access to that kind of strength in combination with his inherent advantage of the Celestial Core Card… he could, no, he would change things.

Second, he still wasn’t sure whether he had imagined the observer—maybe as a byproduct of the hectic fight before him—or whether it had been real. If so, it likely was so far beyond him that it hadn’t bothered with him.

He wasn’t that deep into the forest, but theoretically, one of the stronger beasts could have come out here. He would likely never know.

Lastly, what had made him go and observe the fight in the first place?

Over the last few weeks, Jianyi had observed a few stray thoughts in himself that were surprising. He genuinely enjoyed his time in the wilderness. Much more so than he would have expected.

Sure, everything he had learned, all the progress he had made, would help him on his singular goal of defeating the demons. But… there was more.

He had enjoyed the adventure of it all.

It had felt much closer to the stories of old that he had grown up with than just fighting demons.

He would have to observe himself. No distractions should be allowed.

***

It was slowly getting dark, and he searched for a place to spend the night, while munching on a few mushrooms he had found that one could eat raw.

They had the consistency of rubber and tasted like earth, but it was better than nothing, and he didn’t want to light a fire.

After a while, he found a small copse of trees that offered some protection from the wind. He didn’t set up a tent as he wasn’t going to lie down that night.

He carefully planted the flags for the alarm formation around him, but he still didn’t trust that he would have sufficient warning to get up in case a beast attacked.

The copse was large enough to go through his cultivation exercises. The way to the next stage of Qi Gathering was long, but every little bit helped.

The rest of the night, he alternated between his various meditations and other exercises. He was frustrated that he still wasn’t able to control three threads of qi outside of his body, so that was the focus of his activities.

It wasn’t long after sunrise the next morning that he saw a large, gnarled birch tree in the distance, which he had come across on his previous outings.

He was close to the camp after all.

The trip had not been very challenging, but interesting nonetheless.
