Aodhán floated in a vast expanse of white.

He heard nothing, felt nothing, saw nothing.

Yet unlike the darkness he had experienced earlier, this emptiness carried no fear. It was gentle. Welcoming. Like sinking into a warm bed after a long day beneath cold rain.

Instinctively, he leaned into it and let the peace engulf him.

Time passed, but Aodhán barely felt its passing. He had no idea how long he had spent there up till that moment or how long he spent after.

He simply floated, basking in the peace until his consciousness began to stir—threads of red, gold, and violet gathering around him in thin streaks, their colors stark against the endless white.

Slowly, almost cautiously, they drifted closer. Light twisted around him, and deep within his consciousness, a memory bubbled to the surface.

The scent of lilies touched the air. Wet earth followed. And suddenly, there was lush grass beneath his feet.

“Do you like this one?”

The voice came bright and laughing from behind him, and Aodhán—or rather, little Aodhán—turned to find his mother staring down at him with mud streaking her cheeks and dirt staining her clothes.

It should have been unflattering. Yet somehow, it wasn't.

She was still exceedingly beautiful, with golden locks that fell past her shoulders in loose waves and eyes that subtly hinted at age and vast knowledge. She wore drab brown overalls suitable for garden work, yet even those seemed elevated on her. Almost noble.

He himself was dressed in a similar pair of overalls, though he wore them with considerably less grace. Granted, he couldn't have been more than three years old in this memory.

"I love it," Little Aodhán replied, an excited giggle escaping his lips as she picked him up and spun him around.

"Do you want to pluck more for dada?" she asked, and Little Aodhán nodded enthusiastically.

"Dada likes lilies. I'll give him all the lilies in the world."

His mother laughed, and from the back of the garden, a man's voice echoed. "I don't like lilies, son. Your mother is the one obsessed with them."

His father appeared a moment later, dressed in the same drab brown overalls as both his mother and little him. He was handsome, yet obviously ordinary—average, even by human standards. Yet the sight of him twisted something deep within Aodhán's chest.

His father.

His—

Emotion surged through him. Tears pooled in his eyes as he watched the man walk toward them with an easy, loving smile.

"But you buy them every time," Little Aodhán said in confusion. "Nana says you do what you love every time."

His father grinned. "Well, I do your mother a lot, so yeah."

"Sebastian Vale!" his mother scolded through embarrassed giggles.

His father merely grinned wider as he wrapped mud-stained arms around them both.

Little Aodhán didn't understand the joke, but present-day Aodhán did, and it was gross.

"Don't mind your father," his mother laughed, shoving him playfully with her free hand. "We won't give him any lilies because he's a nasty boy."

Little Aodhán laughed, and his father immediately roared, mimicking an angry bear. "Mr. Nasty is coming to get you!"

“No,” Little Aodhán squealed with laughter as his father snatched him from his mother's arms and spun him around, smearing dirt all across his clothes.

“Who’s Mr. nasty now?” his father demanded, planting a kiss on his forehead. "You, Zachary. You are Mr. Nasty."

"I am Mr. Nasty!"

Little Aodhán squealed again, laughter blooming around him as the world blurred into warmth and sunlight.

The memory faded then, yet the scent of mud and lilies lingered. Almost tangibly.

Aodhán basked in it for a long while, tears gathering in his eyes as emotion swelled within him.

For so long, he had known next to nothing about his family. About his parents. But this single memory had revealed more than a hundred explanations ever could.

His parents had loved him. That much was obvious.

But beyond that, they had loved each other.

They had been happy in their little cottage in the middle of nowhere. They had built the simple life his mother had always wanted, and had Az'marthon never found them, Aodhán might have grown up surrounded by that love.

He would have lived the ordinary life his mother had wanted for him, and deep within, he suspected he would have loved it.

After all, he would never have known what he was missing.

But the most important thing he had learned from this memory was his true name.

The name his parents had given him.

Zachary Aurelius Vale.

The name settled heavily within him.

All his life, he had gone by Aodhán Ashoka, the name given to him by the monks. After his transmigration, he had added Brystion in honor of the family that had taken him in when he had needed it most.

Aodhán Ashoka-Brystion.

That was his name.

The name that had survived trauma, hunger, pain, friendship, loss, and victory. The name that had weathered two worlds and countless hardships. The name even the System recognized.

He never wanted to discard it. And yet, he couldn't discard his true name either.

Zachary Aurelius Vale.

It was the name of his bloodline. The name his parents had chosen for him. The name of the father whose laughter still echoed in his memory and the mother who had wanted nothing more than a peaceful life for her family.

Discarding it would feel like a betrayal.

A betrayal of his parents. Of the family that had loved him so deeply.

But discarding Aodhán would be a betrayal as well.

A betrayal of the monks who had raised him and sheltered him. Of the Brystions who had accepted him as their own. Of every hardship and triumph that had forged him into the man he was today.

Aodhán couldn't choose.

And the moment he realized that, a notification screen appeared before him.

IDENTITY CONFLICT DETECTED.

Multiple legitimate soul-lineage signatures recognized.

Primary registered names.

Please select primary identity.

Aodhán stared at the screen with a frown, uncertain which parts of himself he was supposed to discard and which he was supposed to claim.

Minutes passed, and when it became obvious he still couldn’t choose, another screen appeared.

Soul displays incompatible emotional alignment with singular designation.

Reconstructing Composite Identity…

Light flooded the screen. The white space shuddered. And then—

Congratulations! New True Name Registered.

Aodhán Zachary Aurelius Ashoka Vale-Brystion.

Aodhán stared at the absurdly long name for several seconds. Then he smiled.

"That sounds like all of me put together in one place." His smile widened. "I love it."

The light faded then. The white space dissolved, and when Aodhán opened his eyes once more, he found himself in a familiar room—his room—with sunlight streaming through the window and painting the wooden floor in shades of gold and amber.

The sight of it struck him harder than he expected.

The bed.

The scaly walls.

The faint scent of lightning in the air.

Home.

A deep breath escaped him.

He was alive. He wasn’t entirely sure how, but somehow he had made it.

The confirmation of life sent a rush of relief through him, so profound that he felt the storm within him shift in response.

"Hi, Varéc," he whispered, a blissful smile spreading across his face. And from within him the Familiar roared.

He rushed out of Aodhan’s spirit an instant later, a mass of inky black scales and rippling electricity.

Varéc growled, affection flooding their bond as he pressed his massive head against Aodhán's chest.

Aodhán chuckled. "I'm happy to see you too.” His hand slid across the dragon's scales.

"I'm... really happy to be alive."

Varéc's tail swished. Then he bared his fangs, a hint of irritation bleeding through the affection.

Aodhán's smile faltered. "I'm sorry, Varéc. I know I haven't relied on you much lately, but there was very little you could have done against eight Calamities—"

Varéc growled again, and Aodhán rolled his eyes. “Okay, fine. I get it. I’ll do better.”

Varéc's growl immediately became much more satisfied, and Aodhán shook his head in amusement.

Storms, I've missed you.

At that moment, hurried footsteps thundered up the stairs, and an instant later, the door burst open.

“Aodhán,” Crixis stood in the doorway, eyes wide in relief. “You’re awake!”

Aodhán smiled. “I am. I just woke up.”

“Oh, thank the storm,” Crixis shuddered, tension draining visibly from his shoulders. He stumbled toward the bed, tears already gathering in his eyes. "After the battle, I feared—we all feared—"

"I'm alive, Crixis," Aodhán said gently. "I'm fine now. I'm awake."

Crixis nodded repeatedly. “Thank the storm.”

He wiped hastily at his face. "You must be starving. I'll get you something to eat."

"Not yet." Aodhán caught his arm, suddenly concerned by the severity of Crixis’s reaction. “How… how long have I been out of it?”

“Three days.” Crixis swallowed. “Three whole days. You weren’t breathing, and we... we feared you were dead.”

Aodhán’s eyes widened. “Well, I was, but…” he shook his head, dispelling the memory of darkness. “What about the war, the others?”

“They are all fine,” Crixis smiled, his face softening. “After Zarxan saved you, our enemies fled to the Sects. We are all fine. Everybody’s safe.”

Crixis's words chased away the last of his worries, and Aodhán smiled—brightly, genuinely.

"Ah." He leaned back into his pillow. "I think I really am famished."

Crixis chuckled. "I'll get you something immediately."

He hurried toward the door. However, just as his hand touched the handle, he paused and glanced back.

Then he bowed. "Happy birthday, elder brother."

Aodhán stared blankly at him for a moment, and then it clicked. Three days since the war.

Today was his birthday.

His eyes widened as everything that had happened over the last few hours suddenly seemed to fall into place. The memory. The identity conflict. The strange sense of rebirth that had lingered since awakening.

They had been gifts from the System itself.

A true name. A memory.

His vision blurred as tears stung his eyes, but he blinked them back with a sniff. “Thank you, Crixis. I… I’d completely forgotten.”

Crixis chuckled.

"I didn't even know either. Nymera was the one who reminded us." His smile softened. "She'll be ecstatic to hear you're awake. I'll tell her immediately."

He left then, and Aodhán closed his eyes in quiet gratitude.

One year.

His journey since arriving in ÆFLYM had not been an easy one.

He had fought, loved, killed, traveled, and grown.

He had survived wars and started them.

He had made friends and lost them.

But most importantly, he had learned so much about this new world he found himself in. He had found answers to questions that had plagued him for years, and he had grown far more powerful than the frightened boy who had once awakened in Warren Forest could ever have imagined.

And all of it had happened within a single year.

Smiling to himself, he pulled up his status screen to see what else he had to be grateful for.

Unsurprisingly, the list was very long.

Aodhán stared at the screen for a long moment—the titles, the aspect, the skill, the bloodline evolution...

He took it all in and simply smiled, gratitude swelling within him.

It was too much for his mind to fully process in that moment, but he understood enough to know that he had once again broken through the boundaries of what he had thought possible.

Only two months ago, he had fled Lutia to begin a new life in Gordon at Tier 35.

His power had grown tremendously since then, carrying him into waters he would have once called absurd had he not been lying here himself.

Perhaps his power wasn't truly broken yet, but he had certainly become a devastating opponent for anyone in the Advanced Class, regardless of affinity.

A memory of Ariana Solis's death flashed through his mind, and his smile faded slightly.

She had been terrifyingly powerful. Powerful enough to give Nymera a genuine challenge.

And he had killed her with startling ease.

Principal Zatya would marvel at how far he had come if she could see him now.

The thought brought a faint smile back to his face, and he shook his head.

He dispelled the screen and turned to glance out the window as he sensed the approaching cores of his friends drawing closer to the house.

They would reach his room in a few minutes.

But before then, there was something important he needed to do.

Pushing himself into a meditative position, he closed his eyes and breathed.

He had celebrated eleven birthdays with Master Gyatso in the monastery. They had never done anything elaborate, but they had always begun with prayer, and he settled into stillness, the words rose naturally to his lips, carried by nearly a dozen years of repetition.

Sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā.

May all beings be happy.

May I grow in discipline.

May I lessen suffering.

May I waste no breath.

He had no candle, no incense, no rosary, and yet the prayer settled over him exactly as it always had—quiet and steady, like familiar hands resting upon his shoulders.

For a fleeting moment, he could almost smell the old cedar floors, morning ash, and the faint herbal scent that always seemed to cling to Master Gyatso's robes. He could hear the murmur of chanting beyond the paper walls, the scrape of a broom in the courtyard, the distant clatter of bowls from the kitchen below.

It was home, carried not in stone or timber, but in memory.

Aodhán opened his eyes just as footsteps thundered down the hall and laughter crashed toward his door like an approaching storm.

His lips curved into a grin

The door burst open.

And his friends—no, his new family stood on the other side of it, each wearing hastily constructed party hats.

Someone popped a confetti cracker. And together, they shouted, “Happy birthday, Aodhán.”

Aodhán’s heart swelled.

Emotions surged through him with such fullness that for a moment he could do nothing but stare.

One year.

One impossible, painful, beautiful year that had taken far more than he had ever expected, yet given him even more in return.

Surrounded by people who had chosen him just as surely as any blood relative ever could, Aodhán was certain of one thing.

He had never been happier.
